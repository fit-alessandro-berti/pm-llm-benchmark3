## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Shared clock construction.** Let \(t_C\) and \(t_M\) be the calendar timestamps of usable chemistry and microbiology results. The join is

$$
\boxed{t_J=\max(t_C,t_M),}
$$

not their sum. QA queue waiting is then evaluated **at \(t_J\)**. Release requires calendar-aware QA completion plus 0.25–0.75 h of continuously available electronic processing.

A delayed join can encounter a shorter QA queue because earlier work has drained. Conversely, an arrival burst can erase that benefit. Thus, even “branch delay plus a fixed QA waiting mean” is an inadequate approximation.

**Chemistry waiting, service, and closure.** There are only 2.5 laboratory hours left on Friday. The closure from Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00 lasts **62 elapsed hours**.

Under the chosen risk-priority interpretation, the urgent sample normally takes the analyst becoming free first. B-417 then starts around **Friday 16:42–17:30**. Only approximately 0.5–1.3 service hours remain before closure, less than its 1.5 h minimum complete service requirement. Under the comparable-preceding-work assumption, it therefore carries over to Monday.

Its normal chemistry result is then plausibly:

* **Risk-priority:** approximately **Monday 09:00–11:00**, with a wider short-to-long-service envelope of approximately 08:12–12:00.
* **FIFO:** commonly **Monday 13:00–17:00**. Scheduling the two analysts against the bounded preceding workloads gives a broader no-rerun envelope from approximately Monday 10:24 to Tuesday 09:54.

Those wide FIFO endpoints are queue/service envelopes, not likely outcomes. They illustrate why adding four service means as if there were one analyst is wrong, but assuming two analysts eliminate the queue is also wrong.

**Microbiology incubation and staffing.** Incubation began Friday at 14:00. Its technical readiness window is therefore:

$$
\textbf{Sunday 20 September, 12:00–20:00}.
$$

From the forecast origin, that is **44.5–52.5 remaining hours** of primary incubation.

With timely weekend authorization, the first recorded result is approximately **Sunday 13:00–23:00**. Without authorization—a probability band of 55–75%—it is approximately **Monday 08:30–09:30**, assuming prompt access at opening. The latter includes about **12–20 h of staffing wait after technical readiness**, plus readout.

Saturday QA availability cannot help: the primary incubation is not technically ready until Sunday. Sunday readout also cannot produce a normal Sunday release because QA is closed and chemistry commonly remains unfinished.

**QA backlog at the future join.** Between Friday 15:30 and Monday 08:00, QA has:

$$
4.5\text{ Friday hours}+4\text{ Saturday hours}=8.5\text{ opening hours}.
$$

Expected arrivals during that period are approximately

$$
0.35\times8.5\approx3\text{ batches}.
$$

Using the illustrative 2.5 h mean service time, the single reviewer can process approximately \(8.5/2.5=3.4\) batch-equivalents. A rough fluid calculation is therefore

$$
5+3-3.4\approx4.6
$$

batch-equivalents still ahead at Monday opening, before accounting for any currently active review.

This is **not a prediction of exactly five waiting batches**. Long services and overdispersed arrivals create much wider realizations. It does show why assuming Friday’s queue simply disappears over the weekend is optimistic.

From Monday, two reviewers have approximate capacity \(2/2.5=0.8\) batches per opening hour, versus 0.35 arrivals. The reference backlog tends to drain, but not instantly. I use roughly **2–6 working hours of initial QA waiting for an early Monday join**, often less for a later join, with stressed realizations of **8–16 working hours or more**.

**Smooth path.** Chemistry has the queue wait and service described above, without rerun. Microbiology finishes Sunday with authorization or Monday morning without it. Their maximum usually places the join between **Monday morning and late afternoon**.

Two illustrative schedules show the competition:

| Smooth-path illustration     | Usable chemistry | Usable microbiology | Join          | QA waiting and active review                 | Electronic Release |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Effective chemistry priority | Mon 10:00        | Mon 09:00           | **Mon 10:00** | Approximately 4 h waiting, then 1.5 h review | Around Mon 16:00   |
| FIFO chemistry               | Mon 15:00        | Mon 09:00           | **Mon 15:00** | Approximately 2 h waiting, then 1.5 h review | Around Mon 19:00   |

These are schedules, not deterministic forecasts. They also show that the five-hour chemistry difference need not translate into a five-hour terminal-time difference.

There is no optional rework on this path. A long QA service or queue can nevertheless carry acceptance into Tuesday. Electronic Release itself need not wait for the next opening: acceptance at 19:50 can produce release after 20:00.

**Chemistry rerun.** Initial chemistry waiting is unchanged. After the first test, the priority rerun adds **1–3 working hours**, not necessarily 1–3 elapsed hours.

On the risk-priority path, chemistry commonly becomes usable later on Monday. On the FIFO path, a late first result can push the rerun into Tuesday. Under the bounded comparable-work assumptions, the broad latest chemistry endpoint becomes approximately **Tuesday 12:54**, without new overtaking arrivals.

Microbiology is unchanged unless its own indication occurs. The join remains the maximum: a rerun has little terminal-time effect while chemistry still finishes before microbiology, but can become decisive once it passes that timestamp or crosses closing time.

After the join, allow the evolving QA queue and review service. The table’s central range assumes no further rework. Because the rerun raises investigation probability to the supplied 30–55% band, its unconditional tail is longer than “smooth completion plus two hours.”

**Microbiology confirmation.** Chemistry retains its normal or rerun clock; it is not restarted automatically. Microbiology changes as follows:

* **Sunday indication after on-call readout:** first readout around Sunday 13:00–23:00, then 20–30 h continuous confirmation. Technical confirmation readiness spans approximately Monday 09:00–Tuesday 05:00. Staffed readout gives a usable result approximately **Monday 09:30–Tuesday 09:30**.
* **Monday indication without on-call readout:** first readout Monday 08:30–09:30, then confirmation readiness Tuesday 04:30–15:30. Staffed readout gives approximately **Tuesday 08:30–17:00**.

The join therefore often moves to Tuesday, although late FIFO chemistry can still compete with an early confirmation result. QA waiting and review follow; by Tuesday, the original queue may have drained, but future arrivals remain uncertain.

If confirmation rules out a critical defect, QA can proceed, potentially with an investigation. If it establishes a critical defect, rejection recording takes **another 0.5–1.0 h** under the ledger’s disposition assumption. It would be wrong to add a successful-release review and Electronic Release after that rejection decision.

**Documentation Query.** Branch waiting, testing, readout, and the initial join follow whichever laboratory scenario occurred. QA waits and performs its initial review before raising the query.

For a query raised **Monday at 18:00**, the supplied 4–12 working-hour information delay ends approximately:

$$
\textbf{Tuesday 10:00–18:00}.
$$

Only two hours can be used Monday before QA closes. Resumption then requires the reviewer to be available: allow approximately 0–3 additional working hours and 0.5–1.5 h active review, followed by release processing. This commonly means **Tuesday**, with a late resumption spilling into **Wednesday**.

The completeness score does not justify treating the query as certain or excluding it. Nor should its delay automatically be added in full to a concurrent documentation investigation.

**Documentation-only investigation.** Again, the laboratory clocks and join come first, followed by initial QA waiting and review. The new delay is **8–20 working hours**, not 8–20 elapsed hours.

Opened Monday at 18:00, the investigation would finish approximately **Tuesday 14:00–Wednesday 14:00**, before resumed QA waiting and review. This supports a central terminal range from Tuesday afternoon through Thursday.

A simultaneous Documentation Query could be largely contained within the investigation’s document collection. At the optimistic end, use approximately the **maximum** of their delays. Where one requires the other’s output, use a sequential sum. The distinction materially affects whether a second overnight closure is crossed.

**Laboratory investigation.** After usable branch results and initial QA, add **3–7 calendar days** for the distinct, additional investigation. Do not impose an extra weekend suspension on that supplied calendar-day duration unless constructing a different assumption.

QA acceptance after the investigation still obeys its calendar. For example, five calendar days after an investigation opened **Monday 21 September at 18:00** is **Saturday 26 September at 18:00**. QA closed at 13:00, so final acceptance waits until **Monday 28 September**, despite Electronic Release being continuously available.

A confirmation-delayed join, followed by sequential documentation work and a seven-day laboratory investigation, can push completion to **30 September–2 October**. Repeated investigations would extend it further.

## 4. Forecast summary

**For planning, the center is late Monday through Tuesday, not the weekend.** The upper tail is much less stable because laboratory-investigation frequency can cross the 10% threshold.

| Forecast measure                                 | Approximate remaining elapsed time | Approximate terminal clock window                  |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------: | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plausible earliest routine completion**        |                        **66–68 h** | **Mon 21 Sep, approximately 09:00–11:00**          |
| **Central / P50-like range**                     |                        **75–99 h** | **Mon 21 Sep 18:00–Tue 22 Sep 18:00**              |
| **P80-like range**                               |                       **97–145 h** | **Tue 22 Sep 16:00–Thu 24 Sep 16:00**              |
| **P90-like range across credible mixtures**      |                      **115–237 h** | **Wed 23 Sep 10:00–Mon 28 Sep 12:00**              |
| **Severe stacked laboratory-investigation tail** |              **257–341 h or more** | **Tue 29 Sep morning–Fri 2 Oct evening, or later** |

Hours are rounded. These are ranges across alternative coherent assumptions, not a confidence interval around one known distribution. Consequently, the P80-like and P90-like bands overlap; within any one distribution, their percentile ordering remains intact.

An illustrative calendar-aware queue/path calculation under middle assumptions places the P50-like region around **Tuesday late morning**, P80-like around **Wednesday late morning**, and P90-like around **Thursday morning**. The wider table reflects lower/higher anomaly rates, arrival bursts, re-entry uncertainty, and investigation completion near the second weekend.

**Electronic Release is the nominal planning endpoint**, assuming most indications are resolved without a confirmed critical defect. The supplied evidence does not identify its probability relative to rejection. A contamination indication, borderline chemistry result, or temperature flag is not itself a confirmed critical defect.

The earliest row is therefore an earliest **routine-release** forecast, not an absolute lower bound on every terminal event. An earlier confirmed critical defect could yield rejection 0.5–1.0 h after confirmation. Its discovery-time distribution is unspecified.

Finally, these are not hard upper bounds. QA’s two supplied service quantiles do not determine its extreme tail, and repeated rework has no supplied frequency.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**The most valuable near-term observation is engineering’s resolution of the temperature-excursion flag: confirmed or ruled out.**

It updates three connected parts of the forecast before waiting for downstream outcomes:

**If confirmed**, both laboratory anomaly probabilities increase, and the investigation probability enters the supplied 30–55% band even if the test outcomes later look normal. In the illustrative reference model, a confirmed excursion implies approximately **13% laboratory-investigation probability**. That is enough to move the P90-like forecast toward the following week. It does **not** imply automatic rejection.

**If ruled out**, the lower conditional rerun and contamination probabilities apply, and investigation risk falls unless a laboratory anomaly subsequently appears. In that same illustrative model, laboratory-investigation probability falls to approximately **4%**. The long tail becomes less prominent, but Sunday incubation readiness and the working calendars do not disappear: the smooth path still centers on Monday/Tuesday.

**If still unresolved**, retain the mixture. “Unknown” should not be silently converted to either “normal” or “confirmed excursion.”

The **secondary update trigger** is an explicit QA determination that a laboratory investigation is required. That would replace a probability-weighted tail with a committed **3–7-calendar-day investigation clock**, followed by calendar-constrained QA acceptance.
