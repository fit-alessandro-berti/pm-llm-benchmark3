```
senior_service -> escalation_done
manual_done -> close [if no escalation]
escalation_done -> close
close -> decision_outcome
resource_assignment -> triage_service
resource_assignment -> manual_service
U_review_behavior -> decision_outcome
```

Variables used: `underlying_risk` (`U_risk`), `risk_score`, `complexity`, `flag`, `priority_lane`, `arrival_load`, `triage_wait`, `triage_service`, `manual_review`, `manual_queue_wait`, `manual_service`, `escalation`, `escalation_queue_wait`, `senior_service`, `cycle_time`, `decision_outcome`.

### 2.2 Time-ordered process-mechanism diagram (fenced)

```
t0 arrival
  |-- U_risk, complexity, risk_score --> flag (t1)
  |-- priority_lane (t1) --> triage_start (t1')
         |-> triage_service -> triage_done (t2)
  |-- manual_review (t2) [if flag/high U_risk]
         |-> manual_queue_wait (t2') -> manual_start (t3)
         |-> manual_service -> manual_done (t4)
              |-> escalation (t4) [if review indicates high U_risk]
                   |-> escalation_queue_wait (t4') -> escalation_start (t5)
                   |-> senior_service -> escalation_done (t6)
  |-- close (t7) [after last stage]
  |-> decision_outcome (t7')
```

### 2.3 Where interference, exposure-induced confounding, and feedback arise (<180 words)

**Interference**: `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` are shared resources. If flagged cases are routed to manual review (6/10), they occupy reviewers (M1/M2/M3) and can delay unflagged cases (R09, R20) that also enter review by human judgment. Thus one case’s `flag` affects another’s `manual_done` through congestion, violating SUTVA.

**Exposure-induced confounding**: `complexity` and `U_risk` influence both `flag` (via `risk_score`) and `manual_review_need`. Conditioning on observed `flag` opens paths through unobserved case mix. Even if we adjust for `risk_score`, `complexity` is assigned by a separate rule and may omit important dimensions of risk (e.g., regulatory sensitivity).

**Treatment-confounder feedback / cross-world failure**: Manual review may reveal new risk information (e.g., from M2 notes) that updates escalation likelihood or close timing. For natural mediation, we need the counterfactual manual review time under `flag=0`; but if reviewers change behavior when they see the flag (more scrutiny, different documentation), the mediator mechanism itself is treatment-dependent. Cross-world assumptions fail because a case cannot simultaneously be flagged (fast triage, mandatory review) and unflagged (slow triage, optional review) while sharing the same queue state.

**Handling of absent stages**: For cases without manual review (all flag 0 except R05/R09/R17/R20; all flag 1 have it), `manual_start` and `manual_done` are structurally absent and not imputed as zero; the time from `triage_done` to `close` is decomposed only into “residual-to-close” (includes any unmodeled post-triage delay). For cases without escalation, `escalation_done` to close is not defined; `manual_done` to close is the residual.

**Why not automatically causal?** Flagged cases receive faster triage (median 12m vs. 2.5h to start; service 36m vs. 54m). Yet total cycle is ~3× longer because 100% of flagged cases enter mandatory manual review versus 40% of unflagged, with median wait ~6h, plus 60% escalate to a scarce senior team adding ~7h queue + 2h service. Unflagged high-complexity cases (R09, R20) also show long downstream paths (manual + escalation), confirming that underlying case mix—not the flag per se—drives review need. Additionally, overnight batching (e.g., R01 manual starts 15:42 after 08:42 triage) suggests scheduling rather than continuous service; observed gaps may reflect organizational rhythms rather than pure queueing. Shared-queue dynamics mean a flagged case’s manual entry increases wait for others, so the contrast is partly systemic interference.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local-threshold / RD near flag cutoff | Regression discontinuity in `manual_review` and `cycle_time` at `risk_score` threshold where `flag` switches | `risk_score` (near cutoff); `complexity`; `arrival_load` | Continuity of potential outcomes in score; no manipulation at threshold | Check for jumps in `complexity` or `arrival_load` at threshold; placebo outcomes (e.g., `triage_resource`) should not jump | If `manual_review` jumps at threshold but `complexity` also jumps, confounding is severe; if no jump, flag is not causal driver |
| Queue-state-aware analysis | Compare flagged vs. unflagged conditional on concurrent queue length at `manual_start` / `escalation_start` | `arrival_load`; time-of-day; number of active cases in manual/escalation at entry | Queue state is observable and captures congestion dynamics | If flagged effect on wait disappears when queue is empty, mechanism is queue congestion, not case need | If wait remains long even with empty queue, delay is due to scheduling/batching (not queue interference) |
| Policy-change / randomized encouragement | Randomly suppress flag display (P1) for 20% of cases/shifts; compare `triage_start` and `manual_start` rates | Shift-level; case-level `risk_score`; staff ID | Encouragement does not change reviewer knowledge of underlying risk; no spillover across shifts | If suppressed cases have same `manual_start` rate, display/routing is not the driver; if triage slows, priority lane depends on visible flag | If manual review rate unchanged but triage slows, P1 affects only lane; if both unchanged, underlying score drives behavior |
| Mediation-oriented (interventional) | Estimate I2M: fix mediator distribution under `flag=1` to that under `flag=0`; compare to CDE | `risk_score`; `complexity`; `arrival_load`; resource ID | Sequential ignorability of mediator given covariates; no unmeasured mediator-confounders | Sensitivity analysis: if I2M is sensitive to small U_risk association, mediation is unreliable | If indirect effect remains positive across all sensitivity bounds, manual review is a robust mediator |
| Negative-control outcome / exposure | Use stage that should not be affected by flag display: `triage_resource` assignment if only priority lane is affected; or external “close-to-payment” lag | Case-level; shift-level | Negative control is independent of flag mechanism but shares confounders | If “negative control” differs by flag, confounding / interference is present; if not, specific mechanism is plausible | A significant difference in negative control implies broad policy-era changes, not specific flag mechanism |
| Sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk | Bounding analysis (VanderWeele/Ding style) for association between U_risk and both flag and manual_review | Observed `risk_score`; `complexity`; `outcome` | Unobserved confounder is binary with specified strength | If bounds include null at moderate U_risk strength, mediation is not robust; if exclude null at high strength, more credible | If bounds are wide and include zero, any mediation claim is speculative |
| Interference-aware design | Cluster-randomize by day to P3 (protected capacity) vs. P0; measure treatment effects on both flagged (direct) and unflagged (spillover) cases | Day/cluster; case-level covariates; concurrent load | Interference is localized to cluster; no cross-cluster congestion | Compare unflagged wait in P3 days vs. P0 days; if reduced, positive spillover; if increased, capacity theft | If unflagged wait increases under P3, protected capacity harms others; recommendation reverses to P2 or P4 |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (current) | Fast (priority lane observed) | Long (mandatory review + shared queue + escalation) | Long (median ~16.5h f1 vs. ~5.7h f0) | Low (review occurs for flagged) | Negative (congestion spills to unflagged high-risk) | Strong observation; weak causal identification | Status quo; not optimal for speed |
| P1 (suppress display) | Slow (lose priority if lane tied to visible flag); if hidden score drives lane, maybe unchanged | Lower if manual review triggered by display; unchanged if triggered by hidden score | Uncertain: may shorten if less review, or lengthen if triage slows and cases queue differently | Moderate: reviewers may miss flagged cases if display suppresses priority behavior | Positive if less review volume; ambiguous if reviewers detect hidden score | Weak (behavior change unknown) | Not recommended as standalone; useful as experiment |
| P2 (priority lane; remove mandatory manual) | Fast (lane retained) | Lower (manual only by judgment; fewer forced entries) | Shorter (removes ~6h wait + service for many) | Higher (some high-U_risk cases may not enter review; depends on score accuracy) | Positive (less shared-queue load) | Moderate; requires test that judgment catches risk | Pilot only if decision quality is monitored closely |
| P3 (current routing + protected manual capacity for flagged) | Fast (lane retained) | Reduced flagged wait if dedicated capacity; shared queue may improve if congestion drops | Reduced from ~16.5h toward ~10–12h (estimates) | Low (review preserved; possibly faster = less batch delay) | Ambiguous: depends on whether protected resource is additive or pulled from shared pool | Strong for testing isolation of queue mechanism | **Recommended for pilot** |
| P4 (retain manual; change escalation criteria/capacity) | Fast (lane unchanged) | Escalation wait changed; manual unchanged | Variable: shorter if fewer escalate; longer if more escalate to same senior team | Moderate (criteria change affects compliance) | Senior-team spillover; manual spillover unchanged | Weak (criteria unknown) | Secondary; test after P3 |

**Pilot selection and specification**

**Policy chosen: P3 (protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases).**

- **Eligibility**: Cases with `flag=1` arriving during high-load periods (`arrival_load` = High) or with `complexity` = High/Medium and `risk_score`  0.75.
- **Assignment rule**: Cluster-randomized by calendar day (or half-day block, e.g., 08:00–14:00 vs. 14:00–20:00) to “Protected Lane” (a dedicated reviewer M4 or reserved 2-hour manual-review window with capacity cap of 30% of flagged volume that day) versus standard shared queue (P0). Randomization prevents selection; cluster prevents daily spillover contamination.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail**: If protected-queue wait exceeds 2 hours, or if unflagged manual-review wait increases >20% over the preceding 4-week baseline, revert to shared-queue assignment immediately. This protects against capacity theft.
- **Primary outcome**: `manual_done  manual_start` for flagged cases (service + wait combined, because both matter). **Secondary**: total cycle time (`close  arrival`); escalation rate; `decision_outcome`; unflagged manual-review wait (spare measurement for interference).
- **Minimum process data needed**: Per-case stage start/end timestamps; resource ID at each stage; timestamped queue-entry event (even approximated by `manual_start`); concurrent case load at `manual_start`; `risk_score`; `complexity`; `flag`; outcome.
- **Stopping conditions**: (1) Manual wait in protected group  control group at interim (futility); (2) Unflagged wait increases >20% (interference harm); (3) Decision outcome shifts >10% in error direction (quality harm); (4) Capacity guardrail breached >3 days (operational infeasibility). The pilot produces knowledge about queue mechanism without assuming fastest cycle = best.

```python
    # Manual wait (only for those with manual_start not null)
    has_man = group_df_case['manual_start'].notna()
    man_wait = (group_df_case.loc[has_man, 'manual_start'] - group_df_case.loc[has_man, 'triage_done']).dt.total_seconds()/60
    man_svc = (group_df_case.loc[has_man, 'manual_done'] - group_df_case.loc[has_man, 'manual_start']).dt.total_seconds()/60

    # Escalation wait
    has_esc = group_df_case['escalation_start'].notna()
    esc_wait = (group_df_case.loc[has_esc, 'escalation_start'] - group_df_case.loc[has_esc, 'manual_done']).dt.total_seconds()/60
    esc_svc = (group_df_case.loc[has_esc, 'escalation_done'] - group_df_case.loc[has_esc, 'escalation_start']).dt.total_seconds()/60

    # Residual to close (after last observed stage)
    last_stage = group_df_case.apply(
        lambda rr: rr['escalation_done'] if pd.notna(rr['escalation_done']) else
                   (rr['manual_done'] if pd.notna(rr['manual_done']) else rr['triage_done']),
        axis=1
    )
    resid = (group_df_case['close'] - last_stage).dt.total_seconds()/60

    total = (group_df_case['close'] - group_df_case['arrival']).dt.total_seconds()/3600

    print(f"\n--- {group_name} (n={n}) ---")
    print(f"Arrival->Triage start: median={triage_start_del.median():.0f}m, mean={triage_start_del.mean():.0f}m")
    print(f"Triage service: median={triage_svc.median():.0f}m")
    print(f"Manual present: {has_man.sum()}/{n}; wait (median)={man_wait.median() if len(man_wait)>0 else 'NA':.0f}m; service (median)={man_svc.median() if len(man_svc)>0 else 'NA':.0f}m")
    print(f"Escalation present: {has_esc.sum()}/{n}; wait (median)={esc_wait.median() if len(esc_wait)>0 else 'NA':.0f}m; service (median)={esc_svc.median() if len(esc_svc)>0 else 'NA':.0f}m")
    print(f"Residual to close: median={resid.median():.0f}m")
    print(f"Total cycle (h): median={total.median():.1f}, mean={total.mean():.1f}")

# Summarize by flag and by escalation status
stage_summary(df_case[df_case['flag']==0], "flag=0")
stage_summary(df_case[df_case['flag']==1], "flag=1")
stage_summary(df_case[(df_case['flag']==1)&(df_case['escalation_start'].notna())], "flag=1 + escalated")
stage_summary(df_case[(df_case['flag']==1)&(df_case['escalation_start'].isna())], "flag=1 + not escalated")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. WARNINGS (interpretation guards)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
warnings.warn("Performance DFG differences between flagged/unflagged are descriptive, "
              "not causal. Shared queues, case mix (complexity/U_risk), and batch "
              "effects confound interval comparisons. Do not treat shorter triage "
              "service as a direct manipulation of flag without controlling for "
              "resource assignment and concurrency.")
warnings.warn("Absent stages (manual/escalation) are omitted, not zero-length. "
              "Interpreting missing manual review as 'zero wait' would understate "
              "the structural difference between flagged and unflagged routes.")
print("\nDone. All stages computed; absent stages = NA. Check warnings above.")
```

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk / exposure-induced confounding**: If an unobserved dimension of `U_risk` drives both `flag` and `manual_review` need, and suppressing the flag (P1) does not alter that need, then manual review remains necessary and the “direct” triage benefit of priority is the only true effect; cycle time may not improve without also reducing underlying risk.
2. **Queue interference**: If protected capacity (P3) is implemented by pulling reviewers from the shared pool rather than adding staff, unflagged cases may wait longer; if total system time increases, the recommendation reverses to P2 (reduce mandatory review) or P4 (escalation only).
3. **Decision quality**: If manual review is required to catch high-risk errors, and removing it (P2) increases false approvals/rejections, quality costs outweigh speed gains; recommendation shifts to P3 (protect review) rather than P2.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If `triage_done` to `manual_start` gaps are not waiting but scheduled overnight batching (e.g., cases registered at 08:00 batch-start review at 15:42), then “queue wait” is misidentified; mechanism is scheduling, not congestion, and capacity solutions (P3) are less relevant than scheduling changes.
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap**: If no unflagged cases have `complexity` = High with high `risk_score` near the threshold, or if flagged cases always have high complexity, the causal effect of the flag is non-identifiable by overlap; conclusions are extrapolations, and any mediation analysis reverses to “unidentified.”
6. **Behavior change by reviewers**: If reviewers learn of protected capacity (P3) and apply less scrutiny, or if suppressing the flag (P1) causes them to ignore the hidden `risk_score`, then the mechanism is not “queue” but “attention / vigilance”; policy effects reverse if reviewer behavior dominates over structural routing.

---

## 9. Final synthesis (180–260 words)

**What is directly observed**: Flagged cases (`flag=1`, n=10) have dramatically faster triage entry (median ~12 min vs. ~2.5 h) and slightly faster triage service (~36 min vs. ~54 min) than unflagged cases (`flag=0`, n=10). Despite this intake acceleration, total cycle time is roughly three times longer for flagged cases (median ~16.5 h vs. ~5.7 h). All flagged cases enter mandatory manual review; 60% escalate to a scarce senior team. Unflagged high-complexity cases (R09, R20) also exhibit long downstream paths when they enter review/escalation by human judgment, confirming that high case complexity and underlying risk—not the flag magic—drive review need.

**What is a plausible mechanism**: The flag activates a priority lane (direct/separable triage effect) but bundles with a mandatory manual-review rule and escalation criteria. The downstream delay is mediated through (a) occurrence of manual review (100% vs. 40%), (b) waiting time in shared reviewer queues (median ~6 h), and (c) escalation bottleneck (~7 h queue + 2 h service). Interference through shared queues amplifies individual delays.

**What remains unidentified**: The decomposition of the ~16.5 h into “direct flag routing,” “mandatory review,” “queue congestion,” and “case mix” is underidentified because `complexity` and `U_risk` are imperfectly measured and because timestamp gaps may reflect overnight batching rather than continuous service. Natural direct/indirect effects are not credible due to exposure-induced mediator confounding and shared-resource interference. Interventional analogues (I2M) require stronger sequential ignorability assumptions that are unsupported by observed data alone.

**Which intervention produces the most useful next evidence**: A cluster-randomized pilot of **P3 (protected manual-review capacity)** is preferred. It isolates the queue-interference mechanism from the decision-content mechanism: if protected capacity shortens manual wait without altering review occurrence or outcomes, then shared-queue congestion is validated; if wait does not improve, the mechanism is scheduling/batching or reviewer behavior, not capacity scarcity. It avoids the high quality risk of P2 (removing review) and generates spillover data to test SUTVA violations, yielding knowledge even if cycle time does not fall.
