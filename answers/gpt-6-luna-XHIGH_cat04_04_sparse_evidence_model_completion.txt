# 1. Evidence Ledger

Shorthand used below: `R` = Register Order, `C` = Credit Check, `E` = Engineering Review, `S` = Supplier Confirmation, `X` = Expedite Review, `A` = Approve Configuration, `P` = Release Production, `Z` = Archive Case. A listed trace is an observed completion-order trace; it is not itself a statement about when activities started or overlapped.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three models describe complete case traces under event-completion semantics: each activity label is an observed completion, and the listed order is the exported order. The log contains no starts or intervals, so an unconstrained order between C and E is represented as an unordered pair of required completions whose linearizations may appear either way. It does not assert actual overlap. Ties ordered by ingestion index are also not treated as causal evidence.

R is the start, Z is the end, and the main route includes C and E before A, then P, then Z. Every activity other than the explicitly optional X and the S control-flow alternative is assumed to occur at most once per case. X is at most once by D6. S, when present, is before A by D4. Because the external/stocked attribute is missing and some S events may be lost, an S-absent branch conflates at least two possibilities: a legitimately stocked case and a case whose event was not recorded. None of these models can distinguish those states from this log alone.

Optionality here means an XOR control-flow choice between a branch containing S and one without a logged S. It does not mean that S is optional for an external component. The models also do not include an invisible “missing event” transition: missingness is represented only by the same S-absent trace that represents a stocked case.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** This candidate memorizes exactly the seven distinct observed strings and uses the counts only as log frequencies, not as repeated model branches. It gives priority to avoiding any unobserved completion order. This is the least committal model about general rules because it makes almost no generalization at all; it is useful as a replay baseline, but its apparent precision is an artifact of the sample. Repeated activity labels appear in many XOR branches. That is manageable for seven traces but makes the net larger, can complicate alignments and explanations, and scales poorly as the number of variants grows.

**Compact formal expression.** `L_conservative = {R C E S A P Z, R E C S A P Z, R C E A P Z, R E C A P Z, R C S E A P Z, R C E S X A P Z, R E C X S A P Z}`. The corresponding observed counts are respectively `12, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1`.

**PM4Py code.** The code builds a ProcessTree whose language is exactly that set.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

variants = [
    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation",
     "Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
]

def sequence_tree(labels):
    return ProcessTree(
        operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
        children=[ProcessTree(label=label) for label in labels],
    )

model_conservative = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[sequence_tree(trace) for trace in variants],
)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.** Every complete trace other than those seven strings is excluded, including new check interleavings, adding S or X to a previously S/X-absent variant, moving X or S to a new position, and any repeated activity.

**Known underfitting risks.** It rejects reasonable unseen orders of C and E, despite both orders being observed and D7 leaving their relation unknown. It rejects many reasonable combinations of the observed S and X alternatives. It cannot distinguish stocked cases from missing S events, and it treats one-off traces as equally normative as the 12-case variant. The exact-variant restriction is not a sound compliance rule for future cases.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** This candidate uses the highest-confidence boundaries and prerequisites as constraints, while representing uncertain behavior as explicit alternatives. C and E are both required before A, but their mutual relation is an unordered partial order: either may complete first, with no claim about overlap. If S occurs, it must follow at least C or E; three partial-order branches encode after C, after E, or after both. This directly reflects D5 while avoiding a fixed sequence. X is optional; if present, both checks precede it and it precedes A. For S/X, branches encode X after S, or X before S after both checks. S omission remains a control-flow abstraction for stocked cases or missing events. This is broader than the recorded variants but still preserves the medium-to-high confidence rules.

**Compact formal expression.** `R · (⊕ Q) · A · P · Z`, where `Q` is the union of these partial orders: (i) unordered `{C,E}`; (ii) `{C,E,X}` with `C≺X` and `E≺X`; (iii) `{C,E,S}` with one of `C≺S`, `E≺S`, or both; and (iv) `{C,E,S,X}` with either `C,E≺X≺S`, or one of the three S-placement orders plus `C,E,S≺X`. The XOR over partial orders represents disjunctive prerequisites; the unordered checks represent only lack of a precedence constraint in the trace language.

**PM4Py code.** This candidate is a POWL model. Each branch creates fresh transition objects, including duplicate labels across XOR branches.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def partial_order(labels, edges):
    nodes = {name: Transition(label=name) for name in labels}
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes.values()))
    for before, after in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(nodes[before], nodes[after])
    return po


# S absent, X absent; or S absent, X after both checks.
core_branches = [
    partial_order(["Credit Check", "Engineering Review"], []),
    partial_order(
        ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Expedite Review"],
        [("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"),
         ("Engineering Review", "Expedite Review")],
    ),
]

# S present, with one or both checks preceding it.
placements = {
    "after_credit": [("Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation")],
    "after_engineering": [("Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation")],
    "after_both": [("Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation"),
                   ("Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation")],
}
for edges in placements.values():
    core_branches.append(
        partial_order(
            ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation"],
            edges,
        )
    )
    # X follows S, but still follows both principal checks.
    core_branches.append(
        partial_order(
            ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation",
             "Expedite Review"],
            edges + [
                ("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"),
                ("Engineering Review", "Expedite Review"),
                ("Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review"),
            ],
        )
    )

# X precedes S; because S follows X, both checks precede S as well.
core_branches.append(
    partial_order(
        ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Expedite Review",
         "Supplier Confirmation"],
        [("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"),
         ("Engineering Review", "Expedite Review"),
         ("Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation")],
    )
)
core = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=core_branches)

stages = [
    Transition(label="Register Order"),
    core,
    Transition(label="Approve Configuration"),
    Transition(label="Release Production"),
    Transition(label="Archive Case"),
]
model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=stages)
for before, after in zip(stages, stages[1:]):
    model_balanced.order.add_edge(before, after)
```

`SilentTransition` is imported for compatibility with the POWL object family but is not needed: optionality is represented by XOR branches that include or omit S/X.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.** Missing either C or E before A; X before both principal checks or X after A; S after A; repeated S or X; and, relative to the permissive model, S before both C and E. This candidate also does not include loops, rework, cancellation, or alternative start/end activities.

**Known underfitting risks.** D5 is low-to-medium confidence, so the “S after at least one check” condition may be too strong for a supplier process that starts from order data alone. The model treats every S-absent trace the same, despite the likely distinction between stocked and external components and possible event loss. Its at-most-once assumption may reject rework or repeated supplier confirmations not represented in this sample. Its partial order cannot say whether activities physically overlapped.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** This candidate keeps D1–D4 and D6 as hard boundaries but relaxes D5 for sensitivity analysis: S may appear at any point after R and before A, including before either principal check, because the stated “may begin after either” is low-to-medium confidence rather than an explicit universal prohibition on earlier sourcing. The component attribute is unavailable, so S remains optional in the trace abstraction. C and E remain required before A; X, if present, still follows both and precedes A. This admits a broad set of unseen supplier placements without allowing missing principal checks or an early expedite.

**Compact formal expression.** `R · (⊕ Lin({C,E,S?,X?})) · A · P · Z`, where `S?` and `X?` independently mean present once or absent, `Lin` is the set of all linearizations, and if X is present both `C≺X` and `E≺X`. S has no predecessor constraint within the middle block, but always occurs after R and before A. The resulting language is finite under the at-most-once assumption.

**PM4Py code.** The explicit XOR enumeration makes the finite permissive language easy to inspect. It creates 18 middle-block sequences: 2 without S or X, 6 with S only, 2 with X only, and 8 with both. Duplicate activity labels recur across alternatives.

```python
from itertools import permutations
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def sequence_tree(labels):
    return ProcessTree(
        operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
        children=[ProcessTree(label=label) for label in labels],
    )

start = "Register Order"
check_c = "Credit Check"
check_e = "Engineering Review"
supplier = "Supplier Confirmation"
expedite = "Expedite Review"
approval = "Approve Configuration"
release = "Release Production"
archive = "Archive Case"

middle_branches = []
for include_supplier in (False, True):
    for include_expedite in (False, True):
        activities = [check_c, check_e]
        if include_supplier:
            activities.append(supplier)
        if include_expedite:
            activities.append(expedite)
        for order in permutations(activities):
            position = {activity: i for i, activity in enumerate(order)}
            if include_expedite and not (
                position[check_c] < position[expedite]
                and position[check_e] < position[expedite]
            ):
                continue
            middle_branches.append(sequence_tree(list(order)))

model_permissive = sequence_tree([start])
model_permissive = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(label=start),
        ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=middle_branches),
        ProcessTree(label=approval),
        ProcessTree(label=release),
        ProcessTree(label=archive),
    ],
)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.** A trace missing C or E at approval; X before either check or after approval; S after approval; repeated S/X or repeated principal checks; and any trace not beginning at R and ending `A,P,Z` in that order. The permissive boundary is therefore uncertainty about S placement, not permission to violate the more confident control requirements.

**Known underfitting risks.** It still assumes both principal checks always occur, even though D3 says “should” rather than stating there are no exceptions. It cannot distinguish a stocked case from an external case with a missing event. It also excludes retries, loops, multiple suppliers, check rework, and cancellation paths. Its many duplicate-label XOR branches can make the accepting net large and model-based metrics less stable or less interpretable.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on the 30 observed cases | Exact language fit for all 30 cases by construction, assuming conversion preserves the tree language. | High: every observed variant is admitted by the partial-order/XOR branches. | High: every observed variant satisfies its broader supplier placement and hard check/expedite constraints. |
| Expected precision risk | Low apparent in-sample extra behavior, but extreme overfitting; rejects almost every unseen trace. | Moderate: admits justified unseen interleavings and supplier placements, with a few debatable ones. | High: admits supplier-before-check traces with little direct support. |
| Generalization potential | Very low. | Medium to high within the stated control boundaries. | High for exploring S placement; still bounded by the hard prerequisites. |
| Structural simplicity | Low: seven full sequence branches duplicate labels. Easy to state, bulky as a net. | Medium: compact partial-order idea, but nine disjunctive branches and duplicate labels. | Low to medium: simple rule, but 18 enumerated sequence branches and duplicate labels. |
| Explainability | Easy to explain as a replay of the sample; poor as a process policy. | Strongest policy explanation: rules and uncertainty are explicit. | Understandable boundary, but the relaxed S prerequisite needs a clear warning. |
| Robustness to missing S events | Weak outside the exact S-absent variants already seen. | Moderate: S can be absent with either check ordering; cause of absence remains unknown. | Moderate to high for S omission and placement; still cannot identify whether omission is missingness or stocked behavior. |
| Handling of unseen interleavings | None. | Admits linearizations allowed by its partial orders. | Admits all middle-block permutations that respect the X prerequisite. |

# 8. Validation Code

The code below reconstructs the weighted 30-case event log from the seven variants, converts each model to a Petri net, attempts the requested metrics, runs sampled playout, and checks a manually defined trace suite using alignment fitness. It assumes the three candidate definitions above have been executed in the same Python session. PM4Py API names and result keys vary by version, so unavailable calls are reported rather than silently treated as successful. Sampled playout and a finite hand-written suite do not prove language inclusion or exclusion. These candidates are finite under their at-most-once activity definitions, but an exhaustive comparison should enumerate all legal permutations/branches for the exact installed model language or use a dedicated language-equivalence procedure.

```python
import pm4py

observed = [
    (12, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
          "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
          "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (7, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
         "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
         "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (4, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
         "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (3, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
         "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (2, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation",
         "Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration",
         "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (1, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
         "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review",
         "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (1, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
         "Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation",
         "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
]

trace_strings = []
for count, trace in observed:
    trace_strings.extend([",".join(trace)] * count)
log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)

models = {
    "conservative": model_conservative,
    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}

nets = {}
for name, model in models.items():
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    nets[name] = (net, initial_marking, final_marking)
    print(f"\\n{name}: soundness")
    try:
        print(pm4py.check_soundness(
            net, initial_marking, final_marking, print_diagnostics=True
        ))
    except Exception as exc:
        print("Soundness API unavailable or failed:", repr(exc))

    metric_calls = {
        "fitness": lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "precision": lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "generalization": lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "simplicity": lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
    }
    for metric_name, call in metric_calls.items():
        try:
            print(metric_name, call())
        except Exception as exc:
            print(metric_name, "unavailable or failed:", repr(exc))

    # This is a sample, not exhaustive language enumeration.
    try:
        playout = pm4py.play_out(model, no_traces=100)
        print("sampled playout trace count:", len(playout))
    except Exception as exc:
        print("play_out unavailable or failed:", repr(exc))

R = "Register Order"
C = "Credit Check"
E = "Engineering Review"
S = "Supplier Confirmation"
X = "Expedite Review"
A = "Approve Configuration"
P = "Release Production"
Z = "Archive Case"

def t(*activities):
    return list(activities)

observed_positives = [trace for _, trace in observed]
trace_suites = {
    "conservative": {
        "positive": observed_positives,
        "negative": [
            t(R, E, C, S, A, P, Z),
            t(R, C, E, X, S, A, P, Z),
        ],
    },
    "balanced": {
        "positive": observed_positives,
        "negative": [
            t(R, S, C, E, A, P, Z),
            t(R, C, X, E, S, A, P, Z),
        ],
    },
    "permissive": {
        "positive": observed_positives,
        "negative": [
            t(R, C, X, E, A, P, Z),
            t(R, C, S, A, P, Z),
        ],
    },
}

def singleton_log(trace):
    return pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(trace)])

def fitted_fraction(fitness_result):
    # PM4Py versions expose different result keys.
    for key in ("log_fitness", "average_trace_fitness"):
        value = fitness_result.get(key)
        if isinstance(value, (int, float)):
            return float(value)
    value = fitness_result.get("percentage_of_fitting_traces")
    if isinstance(value, (int, float)):
        return float(value) / 100.0
    value = fitness_result.get("percFitTraces")
    if isinstance(value, (int, float)):
        return float(value) / 100.0
    raise KeyError("No recognized fitness field in result")

for name, cases in trace_suites.items():
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = nets[name]
    print(f"\\n{name}: manual trace suite")
    for polarity in ("positive", "negative"):
        for trace in cases[polarity]:
            try:
                result = pm4py.fitness_alignments(
                    singleton_log(trace), net, initial_marking, final_marking
                )
                accepted = fitted_fraction(result) >= 0.999999
                print(polarity, "accepted=" + str(accepted), trace)
            except Exception as exc:
                print(polarity, "check unavailable or failed:", repr(exc), trace)
```

The suite is intentionally small and diagnostic. A complete finite-language comparison can be built by enumerating all linearizations of each partial-order branch and comparing sets against the exact variant set, after confirming that the installed converter preserves those POWL semantics. Metrics are useful summaries, not substitutes for inspecting the accepted behavior.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use the **balanced candidate** as the immediate provisional model: it honors the strongest shared boundaries, accommodates every observed trace, and makes the uncertain supplier and check-order choices visible. Use the **permissive candidate** for sensitivity analysis to measure how much conclusions change when the weak S prerequisite is removed. Keep the conservative model as a replay baseline for reproducing the sample, not as a future-case policy. Before using any candidate as an automated compliance gate, resolve the S meaning and validate the lifecycle semantics.

Collect the following with stable case and event identifiers:

- Component classification (`external`/`stocked`), supplier requirement, component or line-item ID, and the rule version that determined whether supplier confirmation was needed.
- Activity lifecycle start and completion timestamps for C, E, S, and X, including timezone, source clock, precision, and ingestion timestamp/index. Keep raw source timestamps so equal-time ingestion ordering can be separated from business order.
- For S: supplier, confirmation request and response timestamps, integration message ID, retries/errors, and an explicit status distinguishing “not required,” “required and completed,” “required but not completed,” and “event unavailable.”
- For X: authorization/trigger reason, decision timestamp, and links to the C/E information available at that time.
- Case outcome, approval decision, rework/retry indicators, and any cancelled or reopened status, so the at-most-once and complete-case assumptions can be checked.

Prioritize future cases that distinguish the alternatives:

1. Capture actual C/E start and finish intervals for cases with each completion order. If the intervals overlap, that supports S2; if C finishes before E starts consistently, that supports S1. Completion order alone cannot settle this.
2. For externally classified cases with complete integration telemetry, observe whether S is ever absent. Pair those with stocked cases. This separates conditional requirement from missing events and true non-requirement.
3. Record cases with S beginning after only C and after only E, especially `R,E,S,C,A,P,Z`, alongside the already observed `R,C,S,E,A,P,Z`. These distinguish symmetric “either check is enough” behavior from a C-specific path or an S-after-both rule.
4. Record cases where S starts before both checks. This directly tests the permissive relaxation of D5; preserve S start time, since completion order alone can obscure when procurement work began.
5. For cases with X, capture the two principal check completions and S lifecycle times. The observed log already has X after S in V6 and before S in V7; timestamped repeats can establish whether both are ordinary routes and confirm X never starts before both checks finish.
6. Preserve same-timestamp events and ingestion indices while collecting higher-resolution lifecycle times. This can distinguish a genuine ordering constraint from arbitrary tie ordering and provide evidence about concurrency/overlap.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

- **R start, Z end, A before P, and P before Z:** high confidence, supported by D1/D2 and all observed variants. Confidence is about the intended control flow, not every potentially malformed record.
- **Both C and E before A:** medium-to-high confidence from D3 and all 30 cases; moderate confidence as an exception-free rule because D3 says “should.”
- **C/E sequence versus concurrency:** low confidence in any physical concurrency claim. Both completion orders occur, but D7 and S7 prevent treating that as proof. Confidence is high only in the modeling choice to leave their completion order unconstrained in the balanced candidate.
- **Supplier Confirmation requirement:** low confidence at the individual-case level. The 23 recorded S cases and seven S-absent cases, conflicting S3/S4 statements, missing attribute, and possible integration loss do not identify which cases truly required S. Confidence is moderate that an S-absent log path must be represented.
- **Supplier placement after one check:** low-to-medium confidence. V5 and D5 support it; whether the rule is symmetric and universal is unresolved. Relaxing it in the permissive candidate is a deliberate sensitivity choice, not a factual claim.
- **Expedite behavior:** medium confidence in optionality and in the both-check-before-X, X-before-A constraint, based on D6/S6 and the two observed traces. Low confidence in its frequency or any association with S.
- **At-most-once behavior for activities other than X:** low-to-medium confidence; it is a simplifying assumption based on the observed variants, not an explicit domain rule.

A plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation is that all cases represented in the sample had external components and the seven S-absent traces are integration losses, while S is mandatory for every real case. Under that interpretation, the observed S-absent branches should not be used as evidence of business optionality; the model should instead condition S on an external-component attribute and treat the current event log as incomplete. Conversely, if all seven were stocked cases and external cases always log S, the right model is a data-conditioned XOR rather than an unconditional optional branch. Until the attribute and event status are collected, the balanced model is a provisional hypothesis, not a uniquely identified process.
