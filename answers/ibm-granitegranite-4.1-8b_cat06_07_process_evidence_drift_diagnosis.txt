## 2. Competing explanations and timeline  

**January – March:**  
- **Process stability:** V1V3 distribution unchanged; variation around baseline (0.180.19 breach).  
- **Metrics:** High AUROC (0.810.82), low unknown activity (<0.3%).  
- **Interpretation:** Baseline model performs as expected; no drift signals.

**April:**  
- **Pipeline incident:** Renaming of diagnosis label triggered unknown activity share (17%) and a spike in mean predicted risk (0.28) versus observed 0.19.  
- **Evidence:** Massive drop in AUROC (0.67) and increase in Brier (0.184).  
- **Explanation:** Primarily **datapipeline failure**; resolves once April records are backfilled.

**May – June:**  
- **Partial recovery of pipeline:** Mapping patch deployed (6May) reduces unknown activity (2%).  
- **Routing shift:** Remoterouting policy implemented (1June) raises V2 share to 52%.  
- **Performance:** Observed breach rate drops to 0.15 (June) while mean predicted risk stays at 0.20.  
- **Explanation:** Mixed **process drift** (new routing) and **concept drift** potentially beginning; no major pipeline issues remain.

**July:**  
- **Supplier issue onset:** Parts supplier delays become severe; median `Order Part -> Part Received` climbs to 65h.  
- **Metrics:** AUROC 0.76, observed breach rate 0.19 (still near baseline).  
- **Explanation:** **Covariate drift** (parts delivery) begins to affect V3 cases, but overall performance not yet catastrophically altered.

**August:**  
- **Covariate & process drift intensify:** Median `Order Part -> Part Received` spikes to 130h; V3 share rises to 35%.  
- **Metrics:** Observed breach rate jumps to 0.28, mean predicted risk remains low (0.19). AUROC falls to 0.68, Brier 0.197.  
- **Explanation:** Overlap of **covariate drift** (parts) and **process drift** (higher V3). Early signs of **concept drift** as new failure modes emerge.

**September:**  
- **Partner Z onboarding:** Ruralheavy V3 cases surge (42%); Partner Z share climbs to 27%.  
- **Mature breach rates:** V3 reaches 0.61; Partner Z breach 0.45 vs 0.27 for others.  
- **Label maturity:** Only 82% mature, Brier 0.214 .  
- **Explanation:** **Population shift** (new partner), **concept drift** (different failure modes), and **process drift** (new variant mix).  

**October:**  
- **Label maturity crisis:** Only 55% mature; observed breach 0.16 vs predicted 0.23.  
- **Metrics:** AUROC drops to 0.64; Brier 0.205 highest.  
- **Explanation:** Dominated by **label delay / censoring**; pipeline is stable, but immature labels bias any training.  

**Conclusion:** Each month exhibits distinct drivers; treating April, June, AugustSeptember, and October as a single failure would mask critical nuances and mislead remediation.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| **Fixedvintage backtesting** | Recompute predictions for each month using only features from that month (no future updates). | Fixed vintage models for AprilMay should degrade due to missing label “Diagnostic Assessment Completed”; vintage for JuneAug should improve as pipeline stabilizes. | Vintage models for JulySept show drop in performance *after* supplier issue and Partner Z onboarding, confirming real drift. |
| **Calibration & discrimination stratified** | Compute calibration plots (predicted vs observed) and AUROC for each variant, source (Partner Z vs others), month. | Calibration plots for AprilMay will be noisy due to label mismatches; others will be nearidentity. | Variantspecific calibration gaps appear for V3 in AugSept and for Partner Z in SepOct, indicating concept/policy changes. |
| **Processvariant & DFG comparison** | Run `pm4py.discover_performance_df` on each process variant; compare median edge times (`Order Part  Part Received`, `Diagnostic Assessment  Remote Fix`). | Edge times for `Order Part  Part Received` should show only April anomaly; later steady. | Edge times for `Order Part  Part Received` spike in JulySept; `Remote Fix` edge stays constant, isolating supplier issue. |

| **Feature missingness & lineage audit** | Inspect missing “number of events before diagnosis” and any gaps postApril renaming. | High missingness in AprilMay features explains low AUROC; missingness resolves after May patch. | Missingness remains low after May, supporting drift hypotheses for later months. |

These tests differentiate between a technical datapipeline glitch (which resolves with cleaning) and substantive model drifts (which require retraining or recalibration).

## 4. Recalibrationversusretraining decision  

1. **Immediate containment (April–May):**  
   - **Action:** Clean data pipeline – normalize activity labels (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed`  `Initial Diagnosis Completed`), drop duplicates, backfill missing derived features for April.  
   - **Training safe?** No. Retrain only after pipeline issues fixed; use a **staledata holdout** for April only.

2. **Shortterm recalibration (JuneAugust):**  
   - **Rationale:** Process shift (V2 surge, parts delays) but overall label maturity >90%.  
   - **Action:** Apply variantspecific calibration (adjust risk thresholds for V3 vs V2) and monitor AUROC. No full model retraining needed unless concept drift persists beyond August.

3. **Feature repair (AugustSeptember):**  
   - **Action:** Introduce `supplier_delay` feature (latency on `Order Part  Part Received`) and `partner_z_flag`.  
   - **Training safe?** Yes, provided labels mature (80%). For September, wait until maturity >80% before any retraining; otherwise, serve calibrated predictions.

4. **Full retraining (Octoberfuture):**  
   - **Condition:** When label maturity recovers (>80%) and pipeline is stable, run a **segmented** full retraining:  
     - Variantspecific models (V1, V2, V3) to capture distinct failure modes.  
     - PartnerZ specific submodel if disparity >10% AUROC after calibration.  
   - **Training safe?** No until October’s backlog clears; schedule retraining postcutover (Nov).

**Stratification justification:** Evidence shows V3 (and Partner Z) exhibit distinct breach patterns; a single monolithic model risks miscalibration for these segments. Variantspecific or hybrid models improve fairness and predictive power.

## 5. Monitoring architecture  

| Monitor type | Metric / Query | Frequency | Alert logic (dependent) |
|--------------|----------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Predictive performance** | AUROC, Brier, ECE per variant & partnerz flag | Monthly | If AUROC < 0.66 **AND** Calibration ECE > 0.05  alert |
| **Calibration** | Reliability diagram per month | Monthly | If calibration deviation > 0.07  alert (triggers recalibration) |
| **Label maturity** | % of labels matured at cutoff (from outcome API) | Monthly | If maturity < 0.60  pause new model deployment |
| **Dataquality** | Unknown activity share, duplicateevent count, missingfeature rates | Weekly | Unknown activity > 5%  pipeline audit |
| **Processvariant** | Proportion of V1/V2/V3, distribution shifts monthtomonth | Monthly | Variant share change > 10% without business rationale  process review |
| **Performance DFG** | Median edge times (`Order Part  Part Received`, `Remote Fix`) | Weekly | Spike > 2× baseline  supplier/partner investigation |
| **Population** | PartnerZ share, ruraltraveldistance & deviceage cohort | Monthly | PartnerZ share > 20% without weight adjustment  transport check |
| **Intervention/deployment** | Model version rollout latency, featurepipeline job failures | Realtime | Any rollout delay > 2h  deployment hold |

**Alert dependencies:**  
- **Labelmaturity alarm** (if maturity < 0.60) **suppresses** automatic retraining triggers; only allows inference with provisional thresholds.  
- **Dataquality alarm** (high unknown activity) **requires** pipeline fix before any model update is considered.  
- Once both are resolved, **predictiveperformance** and **calibration** alerts resume normal retraining pathways.

### Python code sketch (monthly audit)

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Load events
events_df = pd.read_csv('field_service_events.csv')

# 2. Normalize diagnosis activity name
activity_map = {'Initial Diagnosis Completed': 'Diagnostic Assessment Completed'}
events_df['activity'] = events_df['activity'].map(activity_map).fillna('UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY')

# 3. Remove duplicates (keep first occurrence per case_id)
events_df = events_df.drop_duplicates(subset=['case_id', 'activity'], keep='first')

# 4. Prepare timestamp column for temporal analysis
events_df['timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(events_df['timestamp'])

# 5. Extract event calendar (ordered by time)
event_calendar = pm4py.format_dataframe(events_df, id_column='case_id', timestamp_column='timestamp')

# 6. Extract case start cohort (creation timestamps)
case_start = events_df.groupby('case_id')['timestamp'].min().reset_index(name='case_start')

# 7. Discover DFG for the calendar (ignore performance data for now)
dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_calendar)

# 8. Discover performance DFG focusing on critical edges (e.g., Order Part -> Part Received)
performance_df = pm4py.discover_performance_df(event_calendar, event_key='activity',
                                              performance_column='duration', edge_filter=lambda a,b: a=='Order Part' and b=='Part Received')
median_latency = performance_df.groupby(performance_df.index[1])['duration'].median()

# 9. Get monthly variant distribution
variant_counts = pm4py.get_variants(event_calendar)
monthly_variant = variant_counts.groupby(lambda t: pd.to_datetime(t).strftime('%Y-%m'))['case_count'].sum() / variant_counts['case_count'].sum()

# 10. Assemble auditable monthly summary
monthly_summary = {
    'month': pd.to_datetime(events_df['timestamp']).dt.to_period('M').unique(),
    'variant_distribution': monthly_variant,
    'median_part_latency': median_latency,
    'unknown_activity_share': (events_df['activity'] == 'UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY').mean(),
    'duplicate_removed': events_df.duplicated(subset=['case_id','activity']).sum(),
    'maturity_signal': 'TODO_fetch_from_outcome_api'  # placeholder for actual maturity percentage
}

print(monthly_summary)
```

*The code reads the CSV, normalizes the renamed activity label, cleans duplicates, builds an event calendar and casestart cohort, discovers the workflow DFG and a performancefocused DFG for the key edge, extracts variant usage, and outputs a concise monthly audit table without fitting a predictive model.*

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis  

| Failure mode | How it could arise from misdiagnosing drift | Mitigation |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Retraining on immature labels (October)** | If one treats October’s low maturity as indicative of model decay, a full retrain would be rushed, using biased breach data, worsening predictions. | Enforce **labelmaturity gate**: only proceed to retraining when maturity  80%. Use maturedonly set for any model update. |
| **Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April)** | Assuming April’s AUROC drop is due to concept drift and applying posthoc calibration would mask the underlying labelmapping error, propagating systematic bias. | Conduct **pipeline audit first**: normalize activity names, backfill missing features; only after dataquality fixes apply calibration. |
| **Ignoring genuine supplierdriven concept drift (JulySept)** | If one attributes the rise in V3 breach rates solely to the Partner Z population shift, the partsdelay signal (supplier) would be missed, leading to inaccuracy for V3 cases. | **Feature addition**: introduce `supplier_delay` and stratify analyses; monitor if calibration for V3 improves only after this feature. |
| **Attributing Partner Z shift to unfair model behavior (SeptOct)** | Without examining whether the higher breach rates stem from real process differences or unmodeled rural characteristics, one might implement bias mitigation that harms all Partner Z cases or incorrectly label the model unfair. | Perform **transportstyle checks**: reweight Partner Z cases, compare performance; only act if disparity persists after reweighting. |
| **Overgeneralizing process drift (June) as a universal fix** | If June’s V2 surge is mistakenly seen as a permanent routing benefit, the model may be redeployed without accounting for the later parts delays in V3, causing underprediction for V3 cases. | **Variantspecific monitoring**: keep separate AUROC/ECE trackers for V1, V2, V3; intervene only when each shows degradation. |

**Overall mitigation strategy:** adopt a **layered verification**—first ensure data integrity, then assess label maturity, then examine both statistical and process evidence before deciding on recalibration vs retraining. This prevents premature model updates and preserves fairness across variants and partners.
