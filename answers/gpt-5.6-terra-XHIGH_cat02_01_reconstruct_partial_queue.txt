# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Time origin and units.** I use 08:00 as minute 0 and 14:00 as horizon \(H=360\) minutes. All durations below are in minutes unless stated otherwise.

**A2 — Rounding.** A reported timestamp is treated as a nominal clock time. An observed difference between two independently rounded timestamps has an approximate endpoint-error envelope of reported duration \(\pm 5\) minutes, truncated at zero where necessary. The operational cutoff at 14:00 is treated as exact for elapsed-time reporting. Nominal values are used for the main reconstruction; rounding sensitivity is stated separately.

**A3 — No unreported initial WIP.** I assume the supplied table contains the cases present in the process during the observation window, and that there was no unreported S1 or S2 work already in process at 08:00. This is necessary to reconstruct absolute queue lengths from zero.

**A4 — Simultaneous-event convention.** For events with the same rounded timestamp, I use this deterministic order:

1. service completions;
2. service starts;
3. external S1 arrivals.

An S1 completion is simultaneously an S1 departure and an S2 queue entry, so that S2 entry is processed with completions. Queue and WIP snapshots are the state immediately after all events at the stated time under this convention. Another tie convention can change an instantaneous peak by roughly one case, especially under five-minute rounding.

**A5 — Capacity.** S1 has two nominal single-task resources, I1 and I2, for all 360 minutes: 720 available resource-minutes. S2 has C1 for 360 minutes and C2 for 345 minutes because C2 is assumed unavailable on \([10{:}55,11{:}10)\): 705 available resource-minutes. I do not infer undocumented extra capacity.

**A6 — Resource feasibility.** For imputation, a missing start must be no earlier than the queue-entry time and no earlier than the preceding known completion on the stated resource; it must be no later than its own known completion and the next known resource start where relevant. I do not assume FIFO, because the observed assignment order is sufficient for queue counts but not enough to establish a scheduling policy.

**A7 — Meaning of MISSING.** A MISSING start means that service occurred, but its start time is unknown. A zero-minute service duration is therefore only a mathematical lower limit, not a claim that physical work literally took zero time.

**A8 — Meaning of OPEN and blanks.** For an OPEN activity, elapsed service through 14:00 is observable, but final service and final station sojourn are right-censored. A blank start/end after S1 completion means the case is waiting for S2; a blank S1 start/end means it is waiting for S1. Such waiting times are lower bounds at 14:00, not completed waits.

**A9 — Central imputation.** The central estimate is the midpoint of each feasible nominal missing-start interval. It is a reporting convention, not an assertion that the start is uniformly distributed or statistically unbiased.

**A10 — Notation.** For case \(i\) and station \(j\):

- \(a_{ij}\): station queue-entry time;
- \(b_{ij}\): service-start time;
- \(c_{ij}\): service-completion time;
- \(w_{ij}=b_{ij}-a_{ij}\): queue waiting time;
- \(s_{ij}=c_{ij}-b_{ij}\): active service time;
- \(r_{ij}=c_{ij}-a_{ij}=w_{ij}+s_{ij}\): station sojourn time.

For S1, \(a_{i1}\) is `arrival_s1`; for S2, \(a_{i2}=c_{i1}\). For open or unstarted work, \(H\) replaces the unknown completion or start only when computing elapsed lower bounds.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For a completed station activity:

\[
w_{ij}=b_{ij}-a_{ij}, \qquad
s_{ij}=c_{ij}-b_{ij}, \qquad
r_{ij}=c_{ij}-a_{ij}.
\]

For an open activity started at \(b_{ij}\):

\[
s^{\mathrm{elapsed}}_{ij}=H-b_{ij}, \qquad s_{ij}^{\mathrm{final}}\ge s^{\mathrm{elapsed}}_{ij}.
\]

For a case still waiting with no station start:

\[
w_{ij}^{\mathrm{elapsed}}=H-a_{ij}, \qquad w_{ij}^{\mathrm{final}}\ge w_{ij}^{\mathrm{elapsed}}.
\]

The end-to-end case sojourn for a completed case is:

\[
R_i=c_{i2}-a_{i1}.
\]

For station \(j\), define station WIP and queue length as:

\[
L_j(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}(a_{ij}\le t<c^*_{ij}),
\]

\[
Q_j(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}(a_{ij}\le t<b_{ij}),
\]

where \(c^*_{ij}=c_{ij}\) if completed and \(H\) otherwise. Thus:

\[
L_j(t)=Q_j(t)+A_j(t),
\]

where \(A_j(t)\) is the number actively in service. A case with no recorded start remains in \(Q_j(t)\) through the horizon.

The time-average station WIP is computed directly from residence areas:

\[
\bar L_j=\frac{1}{H}\sum_i \left[\min(c^*_{ij},H)-a_{ij}\right].
\]

Elapsed busy time is:

\[
B_j=\sum_{i:b_{ij}\text{ known/imputed}}
 \left[\min(c^*_{ij},H)-b_{ij}\right].
\]

This gives mean queue length without requiring a queue discipline:

\[
\bar Q_j=\frac{H\bar L_j-B_j}{H}.
\]

Aggregate utilization is:

\[
U_j=\frac{B_j}{\text{available resource-minutes at station }j}.
\]

Arrival and departure rates over a subperiod of length \(\Delta\) are simple empirical counts:

\[
\hat\lambda_j=\frac{N^{\mathrm{entry}}_j}{\Delta}, \qquad
\hat\mu_j=\frac{N^{\mathrm{completed}}_j}{\Delta}.
\]

They are descriptive rates, not claims of Poisson arrivals, stationarity, or long-run capacity.

## Little’s-Law consistency check

A conventional steady-state Little’s Law calculation is not defensible for the raw station populations: arrivals and completions are visibly nonstationary, S1 is draining after noon, S2 is accumulating late, C2 availability changes, and several cases are censored.

A defensible finite-window **closed-cohort identity** can still be checked. Restrict attention to station trajectories that both enter and leave the station during the six-hour window.

For S1, P01–P20 have complete S1 residence trajectories:

\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{20}{6}=3.333 \text{ cases/hour},
\]

\[
\bar W_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{1{,}845}{20}=92.25 \text{ minutes}=1.5375 \text{ hours},
\]

\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{cohort}}\bar W_{\mathrm{cohort}}
=3.333 \times 1.5375
=5.125.
\]

The corresponding cohort-only time-average WIP is:

\[
\bar L_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{1{,}845}{360}=5.125.
\]

For S2, P01–P14 have complete S2 trajectories:

\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{14}{6}=2.333 \text{ cases/hour},
\]

\[
\bar W_{\mathrm{cohort}}=\frac{980}{14}=70 \text{ minutes}=1.1667 \text{ hours},
\]

\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{cohort}}\bar W_{\mathrm{cohort}}
=2.333 \times 1.1667
=2.722,
\]

which equals the cohort-only WIP area \(980/360=2.722\).

This validates the internal arithmetic. It is not evidence of stationarity or proof that completed cases are representative: actual observed mean WIP is higher because P21/P22 remain in S1 and P15–P20 remain in S2 at the horizon.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

## Evidence

1. **Early S1 intake pressure is clear.**  
   In 08:00–10:00, S1 receives 13 arrivals but completes only 6 services. Reported S1 waits rise from 5 minutes for P01/P02 to 75–105 minutes for later cases. S1 WIP rises from 1 at 08:00 to 7–8 by approximately 10:00–10:30.

2. **S1 becomes a drain rather than the dominant late constraint.**  
   From 12:00–14:00, only P22 arrives at S1, while seven S1 services complete. At 14:00, S1 has only two cases remaining: P21 in service and P22 waiting.

3. **S2 becomes the visible downstream constraint late in the window.**  
   S2 completes fewer services than it receives in every two-hour bin: 3 vs. 6, 5 vs. 7, and 6 vs. 8. By 14:00, S2 has six cases in station WIP: P15 and P17 active, plus P16, P18, P19, and P20 waiting.

4. **S2 queue pressure persists even after S1 begins clearing.**  
   At 13:30, S1 WIP has fallen to 3 while S2 WIP is 6. At 14:00, the corresponding counts are 2 and 6. This downstream accumulation is insensitive to the missing-start assumptions because the S2 entries and S2 completions are known.

5. **Aggregate utilization alone does not decide the bottleneck.**  
   Central utilization estimates are similar: roughly 79% at S1 and 77% at S2. The imputation ranges overlap, so utilization by itself is weak evidence. Queue growth, departure imbalance, and terminal backlog are more informative.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports a **time-varying bottleneck** rather than a single stationary bottleneck:

- **08:00 to roughly 10:30:** S1 is under immediate demand pressure because external arrivals outpace S1 completions. This is an input-overload phase; it does not necessarily mean S1 has lower intrinsic capacity than S2.
- **Late morning:** both stations matter. S1 still has a large queue, while S2 has lower completion flow than entry flow and begins accumulating downstream work.
- **After noon:** S1 clears its inherited queue, while S2 receives the released S1 work faster than it can complete it. S2 is the dominant visible bottleneck by the end of the window.

## Maintenance interval

C2 is unavailable for 15 minutes, about 2.1% of S2’s nominal 720 resource-minute capacity. Therefore, the outage alone cannot mechanically explain all of the later S2 backlog.

It is nevertheless temporally relevant:

- P06 occupies C2 until 10:50.
- C2 maintenance begins at 10:55.
- P08 had entered S2 at 10:20 and waits until C2 starts it at 11:15.
- P11 enters S2 at 11:05 during the maintenance interval and cannot begin until after P08’s C2 work completes.

A plausible hypothesis is that a non-preemptive C2 job such as P08 could not sensibly start in the short 10:50–10:55 gap before maintenance, causing a locally important delay. This is not proven by the log: dispatch policy, setup requirements, or logging delay could also explain the gap. The stable conclusion is weaker but defensible: the outage reduced effective S2 capacity at a time when S2 already had ready work.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

## Three missing-start scenarios

The scenarios vary only the four MISSING starts. They do not alter observed arrivals, known completions, final completed-case sojourns, or the number of cases remaining at 14:00.

| scenario | missing-start rule | S1 busy time / utilization / mean queue | S2 busy time / utilization / mean queue | started-case mean waits, S1 / S2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Late-start / minimum-service | use upper feasible start bound; missing service tends to 0 | 535 min / 74% / 4.31 | 515 min / 73% / 2.29 | about 68.6 / 40.0 |
| Central midpoint | use midpoint of each feasible interval | 570 min / 79% / 4.21 | 542.5 min / 77% / 2.22 | about 66.9 / 38.3 |
| Early-start / maximum-service | use lower feasible start bound; missing service is maximized | 605 min / 84% / 4.11 | 570 min / 81% / 2.14 | about 65.2 / 36.6 |

“Optimistic” and “pessimistic” depend on the question:

- For capacity utilization, the late-start scenario is capacity-optimistic because it minimizes inferred busy time.
- For customer waiting, that same late-start scenario is pessimistic because it maximizes inferred queue waiting.
- The early-start scenario reverses those directions.

## Stable conclusions

The following conclusions survive all three nominal missing-start scenarios:

- S1 has 20 known completed services and two remaining cases at 14:00.
- S2 has only 14 completed services and six remaining cases at 14:00.
- S2 ends with four waiting cases plus two active cases; S1 ends with one waiting and one active case.
- S1 is the early input-pressure point, while S2 is the visible late downstream constraint.
- The total completed case sojourns for P01–P14 are unchanged by missing-start imputation.
- The central conclusion that S2’s late backlog is material does not rely on assuming long missing services.

## Conclusions sensitive to missingness

The following should not be over-interpreted:

- exact mean active service time at either station;
- station utilization ranking when differences are only a few percentage points;
- the split between queue time and active time for P05, P07, P11, and P15;
- any claim that a particular resource was idle versus doing a short undocumented activity inside a missing interval.

## Effects of right censoring

Right censoring is operationally important:

- P15 and P17 have known elapsed S2 service, but unknown final service;
- P16, P18, P19, and P20 have lower-bounded S2 waits;
- P21 has lower-bounded S1 service and total sojourn;
- P22 has lower-bounded S1 waiting and total sojourn.

Treating the 14:00 values as final would understate waiting, service, station sojourn, and end-to-end sojourn for these cases. Conversely, dropping them would understate observed WIP and busy time. The reconstruction retains them as censored observations.

## Effects of rounding

Rounding affects several interpretations:

- An observed wait or service difference can vary by about \(\pm 5\) minutes.
- Open-service elapsed time varies by about \(\pm 2.5\) minutes if the 14:00 cutoff is exact.
- Events reported at the same five-minute timestamp may have occurred in either order; this affects instantaneous queue peaks and subperiod rate bins.
- Resource conflicts within a few minutes cannot be inferred solely from rounded times.
- The broad rounding-expanded missing-start ranges are wider than the nominal imputation ranges, but do not overturn the terminal backlog pattern.

The strongest operational findings therefore come from case counts, known completions, terminal station WIP, and the broad shift of backlog from S1 toward S2—not from minute-level distinctions.

df = pd.DataFrame(raw_rows, columns=raw_columns)
origin = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 08:00")
horizon = 360.0

def parse_status_aware(value):
    """Return (Timestamp-or-NaT, status), preserving MISSING, OPEN and BLANK."""
    if pd.isna(value) or str(value).strip() == "":
        return pd.NaT, "BLANK"
    value = str(value).strip()
    if value == "MISSING":
        return pd.NaT, "MISSING"
    if value == "OPEN":
        return pd.NaT, "OPEN"
    return pd.Timestamp(f"2026-05-12 {value}"), "OBSERVED"

time_fields = ["arrival_s1", "s1_start", "s1_end", "s2_start", "s2_end"]
for col in time_fields:
    parsed = df[col].map(parse_status_aware)
    df[f"{col}_ts"] = pd.to_datetime(parsed.map(lambda z: z[0]))
    df[f"{col}_status"] = parsed.map(lambda z: z[1])
    df[f"{col}_m"] = (
        (df[f"{col}_ts"] - origin).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    )

print("\nStatus-aware source extract:")
print(df[
    ["case"] +
    [f"{c}_status" for c in time_fields] +
    ["state_at_14_00"]
].to_string(index=False))

        "entry": "arrival_s1_m",

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Derive missing-start bounds from queue entry, own completion,
#    prior resource end, next resource start, and C2 availability.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
outage_start, outage_end = 175.0, 190.0  # [10:55, 11:10)

missing_bounds = {}
bound_rows = []

for station, spec in station_specs.items():
    missing_cases = df[df[spec["start_status"]] == "MISSING"]

    for _, r in missing_cases.iterrows():
        entry = r[spec["entry"]]
        own_end = r[spec["end"]]
        resource = r[spec["resource"]]

        same_resource = df[df[spec["resource"]] == resource]

        previous_ends = same_resource.loc[
            (same_resource[spec["end_status"]] == "OBSERVED") &
            (same_resource[spec["end"]] < own_end),
            spec["end"]
        ]
        next_starts = same_resource.loc[
            (same_resource[spec["start_status"]] == "OBSERVED") &
            (same_resource[spec["start"]] > own_end),
            spec["start"]
        ]

        prior_end = previous_ends.max() if len(previous_ends) else -np.inf
        next_start = next_starts.min() if len(next_starts) else np.inf

        lower = max(entry, prior_end)
        upper = min(own_end, next_start)

        # For a missing C2 start whose known completion is after maintenance,
        # a non-preemptive execution cannot begin during the outage.
        if resource == "C2" and own_end >= outage_end:
            lower = max(lower, outage_end)

        assert lower <= upper, f"Infeasible missing-start bounds for {r['case']} {station}"

        missing_bounds[(r["case"], station)] = (float(lower), float(upper))
        bound_rows.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "station": station,
            "resource": resource,
            "lower_minute": lower,
            "upper_minute": upper,
            "midpoint_minute": (lower + upper) / 2,
        })

bounds_df = pd.DataFrame(bound_rows)

def minute_to_clock(m):
    return (origin + pd.to_timedelta(float(m), unit="m")).strftime("%H:%M:%S")

for col in ["lower_minute", "upper_minute", "midpoint_minute"]:
    bounds_df[col.replace("minute", "clock")] = bounds_df[col].map(minute_to_clock)

print("\nDerived nominal missing-start bounds:")
print(bounds_df[
    ["case", "station", "resource",
     "lower_clock", "upper_clock", "midpoint_clock"]
].to_string(index=False))

# 4. Pandas station reconstruction under low/mid/high service scenarios
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
scenario_fraction = {
    "early_start_max_service": 0.0,
    "midpoint": 0.5,
    "late_start_min_service": 1.0,
}

def station_records(station, scenario_name):
    """Build station records with observed, imputed, open, and blank semantics."""
    spec = station_specs[station]
    alpha = scenario_fraction[scenario_name]
    rows = []

    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        if station == "S2" and r["s1_end_status"] != "OBSERVED":
            continue

        entry = r[spec["entry"]]
        start_status = r[spec["start_status"]]
        end_status = r[spec["end_status"]]

        if start_status == "OBSERVED":
            start = float(r[spec["start"]])
        elif start_status == "MISSING":
            lo, hi = missing_bounds[(r["case"], station)]
            start = lo + alpha * (hi - lo)
        else:
            start = np.nan

        end = float(r[spec["end"]]) if end_status == "OBSERVED" else np.nan

        rows.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "station": station,
            "entry": float(entry),
            "start": start,
            "end": end,
            "resource": r[spec["resource"]],
            "start_status": start_status,
            "end_status": end_status,
        })

    rec = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    rec["started"] = rec["start"].notna()
    rec["completed"] = rec["end"].notna()

    rec["wait"] = np.where(rec["started"], rec["start"] - rec["entry"], np.nan)
    rec["elapsed_wait_if_not_started"] = np.where(
        ~rec["started"], horizon - rec["entry"], np.nan
    )

    rec["elapsed_service"] = np.where(
        rec["started"],
        np.minimum(rec["end"].fillna(horizon), horizon) - rec["start"],
        0.0
    )

    rec["residence_to_horizon"] = (
        np.minimum(rec["end"].fillna(horizon), horizon) - rec["entry"]
    )
    return rec

def event_sweep(rec):
    """
    Explicit station event sweep. Priority implements:
    completion -> start -> queue entry.
    """
    events = []

    for _, r in rec.iterrows():
        events.append({
            "t": r["entry"], "priority": 2, "event": "entry",
            "dq": 1, "da": 0, "dwip": 1, "case": r["case"]
        })

        if pd.notna(r["start"]):
            events.append({
                "t": r["start"], "priority": 1, "event": "start",
                "dq": -1, "da": 1, "dwip": 0, "case": r["case"]
            })

        if pd.notna(r["end"]):
            events.append({
                "t": r["end"], "priority": 0, "event": "complete",
                "dq": 0, "da": -1, "dwip": -1, "case": r["case"]
            })

    ev = pd.DataFrame(events).sort_values(
        ["t", "priority"], kind="mergesort"
    )

    q = active = wip = 0
    out = []
    for _, e in ev.iterrows():
        q += int(e["dq"])
        active += int(e["da"])
        wip += int(e["dwip"])
        out.append({
            "t": e["t"], "event": e["event"], "case": e["case"],
            "queue": q, "active": active, "wip": wip
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(out)

available_minutes = {"S1": 720.0, "S2": 705.0}

def summarize_station(station, scenario_name):
    rec = station_records(station, scenario_name)
    tl = event_sweep(rec)

    busy = rec["elapsed_service"].sum()
    wip_area = rec["residence_to_horizon"].sum()
    mean_wip = wip_area / horizon
    mean_q = (wip_area - busy) / horizon

    return {
        "scenario": scenario_name,
        "station": station,
        "arrivals_or_entries": len(rec),
        "completed_services": int(rec["completed"].sum()),
        "started_services": int(rec["started"].sum()),
        "mean_wait_started": rec.loc[rec["started"], "wait"].mean(),
        "mean_service_completed": rec.loc[rec["completed"], "elapsed_service"].mean(),
        "busy_minutes": busy,
        "utilization": busy / available_minutes[station],
        "mean_queue": mean_q,
        "mean_wip": mean_wip,
        "peak_queue": int(tl["queue"].max()),
        "peak_wip": int(tl["wip"].max()),
    }

scenario_summary = pd.DataFrame([
    summarize_station(station, scenario)
    for scenario in scenario_fraction
    for station in ["S1", "S2"]
])

print("\nScenario-sensitive station quantities:")
print(scenario_summary[
    ["scenario", "station", "arrivals_or_entries", "completed_services",
     "mean_wait_started", "mean_service_completed", "busy_minutes",
     "utilization", "mean_queue", "mean_wip", "peak_queue", "peak_wip"]
].round(3).to_string(index=False))

def state_at(timeline, minute):
    eligible = timeline[timeline["t"] <= minute]
    if eligible.empty:
        return {"queue": 0, "active": 0, "wip": 0}
    return eligible.iloc[-1][["queue", "active", "wip"]].to_dict()

for minute in range(0, 361, 30):
    s1 = state_at(timeline_s1, minute)
    s2 = state_at(timeline_s2, minute)
    snapshots.append({
        "time": minute_to_clock(minute),
        "S1_queue": s1["queue"], "S1_active": s1["active"], "S1_WIP": s1["wip"],
        "S2_queue": s2["queue"], "S2_active": s2["active"], "S2_WIP": s2["wip"],
        "total_WIP": s1["wip"] + s2["wip"],
    })

def subperiod_rates(rec, station):
    bins = [(0, 120, "08-10"), (120, 240, "10-12"), (240, 360, "12-14")]
    rows = []
    for lo, hi, label in bins:
        entries = ((rec["entry"] >= lo) & (rec["entry"] < hi)).sum()
        completions = (
            rec["end"].notna() &
            (rec["end"] >= lo) &
            (rec["end"] < hi)
        ).sum()
        rows.append({
            "station": station,
            "period": label,
            "entries": int(entries),
            "completed_services": int(completions),
            "entry_rate_per_hour": entries / 2.0,
            "completion_rate_per_hour": completions / 2.0,
        })
    return rows

    subperiod_rates(central_s2, "S2")
)
