# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately
1. **Audit and standardize the event log:** Reconcile why Mira’s 960 events/905 cases disagree with >1,600 outgoing handovers and 224+ subcontracting loops, clarify how `Assign Specialist` is generated and mapped against the 14 canonical activities, and confirm `Mira_Coordinator` represents a single human user rather than a shared queue ID.
2. **Establish formal backup coverage for exceptions:** Eliminate the acute key-person exposure evidenced by the 6-day absence by designating a rotating backup protocol (e.g., cross-training `Alice_TeamLead`, `Bruno_TeamLead`, or senior adjusters `Carla`/`Diego` on exception-desk procedures) and configuring an out-of-office fallback rule in `Bot_Rules` so `exception_flag=True` cases do not stall when Mira is away.
3. **Add lightweight auditability to routing decisions:** Introduce mandatory structured reason codes (and brief escalation notes) whenever any coordinator reopens a claim, resolves a `Bot_Rules` `Fraud Screen` exception, or assigns a specialist. This addresses the compliance reviewer’s discretion concern with minimal operational friction.

## What should be monitored
1. **Decomposed queue and latency metrics:** Track waiting time *upstream* of Mira (after `Bot_Rules` and `Temp_1`/`Temp_2`) separately from waiting/service time at `Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`, `Vendor_Verify`, and `Vendor_Pay`, segmented by `exception_flag` and `priority`.
2. **Routing quality and equity indicators:** Monitor specialist rejection/rework rates on Mira-routed vs. backup-routed claims, adjuster workload balance adjusted for `amount_band` and `exception_flag`, and the frequency/outcomes of `Bot_Rules  Mira  Bot_Rules` `Fraud Screen` loops.
3. **Capacity and fatigue signals:** Monitor Mira’s weekly intake of complaints (currently 71% share) and reopens (64% share), plus post-absence backlog recovery times, to detect emerging overload early.

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available
1. **Do not remove, demote, or bypass `Mira_Coordinator`:** Removing her without first building and validating a replacement coordination mechanism—such as upgraded `Bot_Rules` routing logic, formal regional team-lead ownership of exceptions, direct specialist intake criteria, and trained backup staff—would very likely reproduce the 8.4  13.9-day exception delay seen during her absence and risk specialist overload.
2. **Do not prematurely automate her routing logic inside `Bot_Rules`:** Until attribute-filtered analyses distinguish deterministic automation gaps (Hypothesis 4) from tacit diagnostic judgment (Hypothesis 3), hard-coding rules risks pushing poorly prepared cases directly onto expensive medical and legal specialists.
3. **Do not impose heavy dual-approval or strict SoD blockers on every routing step:** Because Mira does not execute `Approve Settlement` or `Issue Payment`, heavy pre-routing approvals could worsen exception cycle times before compliance audits confirm whether material risk actually exists in her `Fraud Screen` or specialist-selection decisions.

# 6. Short executive summary

Current process data does not support a single simple label for Mira_Coordinator; she appears neither merely a routine "queue cleaner" nor an informal team lead. Instead, the evidence points to a critical, cross-team exception router who bridges automated rules, temporary staff, regional claims teams, and medical or legal specialists. She looks central in handoff networks because she directs difficult claims across silos, yet peripheral in team-collaboration metrics because she touches only 22% of claims briefly and spreads them across many colleagues.

This structure carries real operational risk. During Mira’s six-day absence, ordinary claims were unaffected, but the slowest exception claims jumped from 8.4 to 13.9 days. Simultaneously, her broad routing discretion and several event-log inconsistencies warrant closer review. Management should not remove her role without first building a reliable replacement mechanism. Immediate priorities are auditing the underlying data, establishing formal backup coverage for exception handling, recording simple routing reason codes, and running targeted analyses before redesigning responsibilities.
