## 4. What flattening misses

**Job-flattened DFG**  
- *Visible*: Activity sequence, median times, rework loop.  
- *Hidden*: Waiting due to batch synchronization, tool contention, lot release, shipment consolidation. These waits are not recorded as job-specific events.  
- *Misleading conclusion*: No job duration >1.8 SD above mean suggests no outliers, yet jobs experience hours of hidden waiting.

**Machine-flattened summary**  
- *Visible*: Utilization, activity sequence, gaps.  
- *Hidden*: Reasons for gaps (e.g., OVEN-1 gap due to batch synchronization or RACK-7 cleaning).  
- *Misleading conclusion*: Low utilization may be misinterpreted as no bottleneck, while jobs wait for other resources.

**Batch-flattening** (inferred)  
- *Visible*: Batch cure events and included jobs.  
- *Hidden*: Individual job waiting times before batch start; tool/machine usage context.  
- *Misleading*: Batch appears efficient if measured only by cure time, ignoring synchronization delays.

**Tool-flattening** (inferred)  
- *Visible*: Acquire/release events and associated jobs.  
- *Hidden*: Impact on downstream jobs (e.g., J4 waiting for FIX-A).  
- *Misleading*: Tool utilization may appear low if measured only by hold times, ignoring contention effects.

**ComponentLot-flattening**  
- *Visible*: Quarantine/release events and contained components.  
- *Hidden*: Impact on specific jobs because lot events are not linked to jobs in job-flattened log.  
- *Misleading*: Lot release process may seem timely if not connected to job delays.

**Shipment-flattened DFG**  
- *Visible*: Create Shipment  Load  Dispatch median times.  
- *Hidden*: Waiting of individual jobs for consolidation partners.  
- *Misleading*: Shipment process appears fast (median 0.3h), but jobs wait hours for consolidation.

**Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting**:  
Machines are underutilized because jobs are delayed upstream by non-machine constraints (batch synchronization, tool contention, lot release). Thus, machines idle while jobs queue for other resources, making utilization a poor indicator of system throughput.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):**

1. If J3 were ready much earlier (e.g., 09:00) but B1 still started at 12:05, indicating delay due to machine availability rather than job readiness.
2. If batch start times were independent of job readiness (e.g., fixed schedule), then waiting would be due to scheduling policy, not synchronization.
3. If batch sizes were reduced to one job (no batching), yet delays persisted at similar levels.
4. If jobs showed similar waiting times even when processed individually (non-batched).
5. If batch cure times were extremely short, making synchronization waiting negligible.
6. If multiple batches had overlapping synchronization barriers that canceled out delays.

**Additional data fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**

1. **Explicit wait-for-tool events**: Record when a job is waiting for a specific tool.
2. **Batch assignment timestamps**: Exact time when jobs are assigned to a batch.
3. **Lot linkage to jobs**: Direct relation from lot events to affected jobs in the log.
4. **Shipment packing timestamps per job**: When each job is packed and loaded into a shipment.
5. **Machine queue timestamps**: Time when job arrives at machine queue and when processing starts.
6. **Tool calibration start/end times**: More granular data on tool availability.
7. **Overhead/setup times**: Explicit events for machine setup or tool changeover.
8. **Resource reservation events**: When resources are reserved for future use.

This data would help isolate whether delays are due to synchronization, contention, policy, or capacity.
