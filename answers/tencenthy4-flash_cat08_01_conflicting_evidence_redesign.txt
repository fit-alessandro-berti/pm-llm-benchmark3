# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process is fast in the wrong places and slow in the wrong places. The dominant path is cheap and quick before downstream rework, but it creates the complaints, reopens, compliance deviations, and reshipments that erode the apparent benefit.

- **Frequency DFG shows a strong fast-path bias.** Of 5,000 cases, 3,600 go Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen, 2,550 go Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval, 2,300 go Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement, and 2,320 go Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close. This path looks like the “normal” process by volume. But frequency does not prove it is the desirable process.

- **Performance DFG confirms the fast path’s local speed.** Intake -> Auto screen is 0.8h mean / 0.5h median, Auto screen -> Fast approval is 1.0h / 0.4h, Fast approval -> Dispatch is 3.0h / 1.8h, and Dispatch -> Close is 5.0h / 3.2h. By contrast, manual and evidence paths are much slower: Intake -> Manual review is 7.5h / 5.0h, Manual review -> Repair quote is 22h / 15h, Request missing evidence -> Manual review is 34h / 26h, and complaint/reopen edges are 40-96h mean.

- **The fast path’s downstream rework destroys the cost advantage.** Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. Complaint/reopen occurs in 9.2% of all cases, but 14.1% of Fast approval cases versus only 3.3% of Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device cases. Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen has 260 cases and a 96h mean / 180h p90, linked to incorrect replacement part selection.

- **Conformance results show the fast path is not merely “lean”; it is often non-compliant.** Token-based replay gives log fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces, meaning the high-level model can replay much of the log while nearly one third of traces still deviate. Alignment diagnostics repeatedly show Fast approval before Evidence completeness, Customer complaint/reopen after close, and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices. These map directly to R1, R2, and R3 violations.

- **Low precision indicates unstructured variation, not legitimate segmentation.** The discovered model has low precision because many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy. That matters: the process is not simply offering a few deliberate service tiers; it is drifting into ad hoc combinations.

- **Temporal deviations are segment-sensitive.** Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote is often too slow for Premium cases, while Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen is a long-tail pattern associated with wrong replacement parts. So the slow path is not universally better: Premium low-risk accessory cases on the manual path have 46h median cycle time and high service-credit exposure above 24h, while high-value devices on the fast path have much worse complaint rates.

- **Evidence handling is a major hidden queue.** 22% of cases are unclear defect / missing evidence, with 140h median cycle time and highest abandonment. Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it. The DFG shows Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review for 890 cases and Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned for 320 cases. This is not just slow; it is abandonment-generating.

- **Resources are mismatched to demand.** Manual review has 7 reviewers x 36 productive hours/week, with sharp queue growth above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk has only 2 specialists and bottleneck behavior on Mondays and after campaigns. Repair quote is delayed by part-price confirmation. Quality check is underused except after repair campaigns, even though repair rework is 12.5% for repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% for low-risk devices. The escalation board has strong satisfaction effect but only one daily 45-minute slot, so it cannot scale.

The core contradiction is therefore: the frequent path is locally fast and cheap, but it concentrates compliance violations and downstream pain. The reviewed/repair path has better quality outcomes, especially for high-value devices, but it is too slow and expensive for low-risk accessories and Premium cases with tight service expectations. The redesign problem is not “eliminate the fast path”; it is to decide where fast approval is genuinely safe and where speed must be bought through better front-end design, parallel work, or targeted gates rather than by skipping controls.

# 2. Redesign A: Segmented Compliance Firewall with a Lightweight Dispatch Gate

- **Describe the redesigned process flow.**

  Intake claim is enriched with claim value, customer segment, defect type, evidence completeness, device risk class, and candidate replacement part. The auto screen is replaced with a rules-based **compliance firewall**:

  - **Premium low-risk accessory, complete evidence:** eligible for the existing R2 exception; Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement remains available with minimal added delay.
  - **Standard low-risk accessory, complete evidence:** Fast approval remains available, but with proactive status visibility because these customers tolerate up to roughly 72h if informed.
  - **Any claim above EUR 400, high-value device, high-risk defect type, or unclear evidence:** cannot enter Fast approval without Manual eligibility review and/or Fraud/risk review; replacement dispatch requires explicit evidence completeness confirmation.
  - **Reopened claims:** enter a dedicated R3 lane where Manual eligibility review is mandatory before close or denial.
  - **High-value or part-uncertain replacements:** pass through a lightweight pre-dispatch part-match gate. A low-cost automated SKU/defect/serial compatibility check runs first; if confidence is low, technician or inspector confirmation is added.

- **State which segments, paths, or activities it targets.**

  It targets the leakage from Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval and Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement, especially where R1 and R2 are violated. It focuses on high-value devices, mid-risk devices, claims above EUR 400, unclear defect types, reopened claims, and any replacement where wrong-part selection has high expected cost. It deliberately preserves express handling for low-risk accessories, especially Premium low-risk accessories where 8h fast-path median cycle time is valuable.

- **Explain the mechanism of improvement.**

  The redesign attacks the contradiction directly: instead of treating Fast approval as the default, it makes fast approval a controlled outcome of segmentation. It uses the auto-screen update, deployable in two weeks, to prevent non-compliant fast approvals before they occur. The pre-dispatch gate addresses the specific long-tail pattern Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen caused by wrong replacement selection; this is most valuable for high-value devices where a wrong replacement has EUR 410 expected cost, versus EUR 95 for low-risk accessories. The R3 reopen lane reduces the chance of Customer complaint/reopen after close and gives reopened cases a controlled path back to Manual eligibility review rather than unstructured escalation.

  It also converts “evidence completeness” from an implicit assumption into a checked gate before dispatch. That directly addresses alignment deviations where Fast approval occurs before Evidence completeness and where high-value devices are dispatched without risk review.

- **State what it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.**

  It sacrifices some median speed for high-value and mid-risk cases. The pre-dispatch gate adds 6-18h depending on resource design, and manual/risk routing adds delay compared with current fast approval. It may increase manual-review inflow by 15-35%, pushing reviewers beyond the 82% utilization threshold where queues grow sharply. It also risks overloading the 2 fraud/risk specialists, especially after campaigns.

  It does not solve the repeated-evidence loop or low-digital-literacy abandonment. It also does not redesign the repair quote process, so Premium manual review -> Repair quote may remain slow unless paired with other changes.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-First Completion with Assisted Status Visibility

- **Describe the redesigned process flow.**

  Intake claim begins with a structured evidence checklist keyed to defect type, device risk, segment, and claim value. Evidence completeness is explicitly recorded before eligibility finalization. If evidence is missing, the system issues one consolidated Request missing evidence with defect-specific prompts, photo examples, serial-number capture guidance, and a clear list of what will happen next.

  A new evidence portal supports upload and automatic completeness checking, but it is paired with an assisted channel: callback, SMS link, or agent-assisted upload for customers with low digital literacy. Repeated requests are capped; after two incomplete evidence cycles, the case moves to a navigator/evidence-completion cell rather than looping back through generic Manual eligibility review. Standard low-risk customers receive proactive status updates during evidence waiting time, since they tolerate longer completion if visibility is good. Premium cases get tighter service alerts where service-credit exposure is high.

- **State which segments, paths, or activities it targets.**

  It targets the unclear defect / missing evidence segment, which is 22% of cases and currently has 140h median cycle time and highest abandonment. It specifically addresses Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review, Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned, and the 26% of evidence-request cases that receive at least two requests. It also targets Standard low-risk accessory customers by making longer waits acceptable through status visibility. It is less aimed at high-value technical decision quality or wrong-part selection; those remain separate control problems.

- **Explain the mechanism of improvement.**

  The redesign separates “evidence is missing” from “eligibility is uncertain.” Today, missing evidence appears to feed manual review repeatedly: 890 cases go Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review, and 320 go Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned. A front-loaded, defect-specific evidence process can reduce repeated requests by the expected 30-45% range from the portal, while the assisted channel mitigates the portal’s known risk of increasing abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers.

  This improves cycle time by reducing rework loops before Technical decision rather than accelerating the decision itself. It improves quality because technical and eligibility decisions are made against a more complete case file. It improves customer experience by reducing repeated customer effort and by giving Standard customers visibility during waits. It may also reduce unnecessary manual-review load, because simple evidence gaps can be closed without sending the case back into the general review queue.

- **State what it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.**

  It adds front-end structure to all claims, including cases that would otherwise have been fast approved. If poorly designed, that could slow simple cases. It requires portal investment and an evidence-completion cell, and it may shift effort from reviewers to customer support or evidence validators.

  It does not by itself guarantee R1/R2 compliance for high-value devices. Evidence completeness helps R2, but it does not ensure risk review before Fast approval. It also does not directly fix wrong replacement part selection or repair rework. The portal may increase abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers if the assisted path is under-resourced.

# 4. Redesign C: High-Value Repair Pod with Parallel Decisions and Quality Closure

- **Describe the redesigned process flow.**

  Low-risk accessories remain on a lean express path. High-value devices, mid-risk devices with unclear defects, and reopened claims are routed to a dedicated **high-value repair pod** containing eligibility review, risk/fraud capability, technical quote capability, and inspection capacity.

  After Intake, the pod creates a single decision pack: evidence, claim value, segment, defect type, device history, part availability, and candidate remedy. Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review run in parallel rather than sequentially, reducing the current Manual review -> Fraud/risk delay of 18h mean / 44h p90. Repair quote uses a pre-approved part-price catalog or standing quote rules to reduce the known delay from part-price confirmation. Customer approval is then obtained, followed by Repair device -> Quality check -> Quality/compliance close.

  For high-value devices, replacement is no longer the default when defect evidence is weak; the pod prefers repair or verified part selection. Rework repair is managed inside the pod with feedback to the technician, using the currently underused Quality check capacity. Reopened claims are handled by the pod to satisfy R3 before close or denial. The daily escalation board is replaced by an asynchronous, pod-owned triage queue with explicit authority to approve repair, replacement, or denial; the human board is reserved for genuinely exceptional cases.

- **State which segments, paths, or activities it targets.**

  It targets Premium and Standard high-value devices, especially where current fast-path complaints are 22% and 18% respectively versus 5% and 6% on reviewed/repair paths. It also targets the repair path itself: Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote, Repair quote -> Customer approval, Repair device -> Quality check, Quality check -> Rework repair, and Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board.

- **Explain the mechanism of improvement.**

  This redesign does not make the slow path faster by removing controls; it makes the controlled path operationally viable. It groups scarce skills around the cases where wrong decisions are expensive. Parallel eligibility and risk review reduce queue time. Pre-approved part pricing attacks the main repair-quote delay. Mandatory quality check and structured rework feedback address the 12.5% rework rate for repaired high-value devices and reduce the chance that defects escape to customers.

  It also uses the pod to protect customer experience. Premium high-value cases receive proactive handling despite longer technical work, while Standard high-value cases avoid the high complaint risk of fast replacement. The redesign keeps low-risk accessories away from the pod, avoiding the mistake of applying high-cost quality machinery to EUR-level accessory claims.

- **State what it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.**

  It sacrifices direct cost and median cycle time for high-value cases versus the current fast path. It uses expensive resources: EUR 34/h reviewers, EUR 52/h risk specialists, EUR 45/h technicians, and EUR 38/h inspectors. Even with parallelization, reviewed/repair paths for high-value devices will not match the 20h or 28h fast-path medians immediately.

  It also requires significant operating-model change: cross-functional pods, revised escalation authority, part-price catalog maintenance, and stronger quality-check scheduling. It does not solve low-risk accessory volume, missing-evidence abandonment, or inaccurate SKU master data unless those are handled elsewhere. Over-routing cases into the pod would create a new bottleneck and damage the economics of the whole process.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

- **Redesign A: Segmented compliance firewall + dispatch gate**

  - **Failure mode 1: rule misclassification.** If value, defect type, or risk attributes are incomplete at Intake, high-value cases may still enter the express lane, preserving the current R1/R2 violations. Conversely, too many low-risk cases may be routed to review, increasing manual inflow by 15-35% and overwhelming reviewers.
  - **Failure mode 2: gate becomes delay without accuracy.** The 6-18h pre-dispatch gate only helps if SKU/defect/serial data are accurate. If master data is poor, the gate may add time while still allowing wrong replacements.
  - **Failure mode 3: Premium service erosion.** If the gate is applied too broadly, Premium low-risk accessory claims could lose their main advantage: 8h median fast-path cycle time.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** from Fast approval/replacement to Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, and the pre-dispatch gate. Reviewer utilization could exceed 82%, and the 2 fraud/risk specialists could become the new Monday/campaign bottleneck.

- **Redesign B: Evidence-first completion + assisted visibility**

  - **Failure mode 1: portal increases abandonment.** The prompt notes that a new evidence portal may increase abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers. If the assisted channel is weak, the redesign could worsen the current 320 Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned flow.
  - **Failure mode 2: false completeness confidence.** Automated completeness checks may treat evidence as sufficient when it is not, allowing technical decisions to proceed with poor defect information and creating downstream reopens.
  - **Failure mode 3: duplicated work.** A new evidence-completion cell could duplicate Manual eligibility review rather than reduce it, adding cost without reducing the 890 Request missing evidence -> Manual review loops.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** from repeated manual-review loops to the evidence portal, assisted callback queue, and evidence-validation cell. If evidence arrives faster, the bottleneck may also move downstream to Technical decision or part-price confirmation.

- **Redesign C: High-value repair pod + quality closure**

  - **Failure mode 1: pod under-capacity.** The pod concentrates expensive scarce resources. If demand exceeds capacity, parallel review and quote work may still queue, and high-value cases could experience longer p90 times than today.
  - **Failure mode 2: quality check becomes a queue.** Quality check is currently underused except after repair campaigns; after routing more high-value repairs through the pod, inspectors could become a bottleneck, especially with 12.5% high-value rework.
  - **Failure mode 3: catalog becomes stale.** Repair quote improvement depends on pre-approved part pricing. If part-price confirmation logic is outdated, the pod will reproduce the current 22h mean Manual review -> Repair quote delay.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** from sequential Manual review -> Repair quote to technician capacity, part-price catalog maintenance, quality inspection, and rework repair. The redesigned triage queue could also become a mini-escalation board if authority is not clearly delegated.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

- **Baseline instrumentation before the first pilot.**

  Before changing the process, enrich the event log with segment, claim value, risk class, defect type, evidence-completeness event, replacement-part candidate, service-credit exposure, and post-close complaint/reopen outcome. Re-run the frequency DFG, performance DFG, conformance check against R1-R3, and alignment diagnostics on this enriched baseline. Without an explicit evidence-completeness event and part-selection event, later pilots will not show whether the intervention fixed the real deviation.

- **First pilot: Redesign A for high-value and >EUR 400 claims.**

  Start with the fastest-to-deploy, highest-compliance-value intervention. Use the updated auto-screen rules to block Fast approval for high-value and above-EUR 400 claims unless R1 is satisfied, and require evidence confirmation before replacement dispatch except for the permitted Premium low-risk accessory case. Add the lightweight pre-dispatch part-match gate only for high-value or part-uncertain replacements. Keep low-risk accessories as a comparison cohort.

- **Metrics to monitor.**

  Track by segment and risk class:

  - median and p90 cycle time from Intake -> Close, not only individual edge times;
  - direct cost and total cost after complaints, reshipments, service credits, and rework;
  - complaint/reopen rate, especially Fast approval cases and Dispatch replacement cases;
  - wrong replacement / part-match failures and rework repair rate;
  - evidence request count, repeated evidence requests, and abandonment;
  - R1/R2/R3 deviations, fitting traces, and move-on-log alignments;
  - Premium service-credit exposure and Standard status-visibility satisfaction;
  - reviewer utilization around the 82% queue threshold, fraud/risk utilization, inspection queue, and escalation-board load.

- **Stop/go thresholds for the first pilot.**

  - **Go** if R1/R2 move-on-log deviations drop by at least roughly half and trend toward near elimination for pilot cases; fitting traces improve materially from the 68% baseline; high-value complaint/reopen moves from 22%/18% toward the reviewed/repair range of roughly 5-6% or at least below a 10-12% band; post-rework cost per successful outcome does not materially worsen; and abandonment does not rise by more than a small few percentage points.
  - **Pause or redesign** if manual-review inflow pushes utilization above the 82% queue-growth zone, fraud/risk becomes saturated, Premium cycle-time p90 worsens materially, or compliance deviations shift into hidden abandonment or denial rather than proper review.

- **Second experiment: Redesign B for unclear/missing evidence.**

  Pilot the evidence-first flow on the 22% unclear defect / missing evidence segment, with the assisted channel mandatory from day one. Include Standard low-risk cases for visibility testing but do not force high-value cases through a purely digital flow without R1/R2 controls.

  - **Go** if repeated evidence requests fall materially toward the portal’s expected 30-45% reduction range, Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned improves, median cycle time for unclear evidence moves well below the current 140h baseline, and customer effort measures improve.
  - **Stop** if low-digital-literacy abandonment rises, the evidence cell becomes a new queue, or completeness scoring creates false confidence and more downstream reopens.

- **Third experiment: Redesign C for high-value repair and reopened cases.**

  After A and B clarify compliance and evidence demand, pilot the high-value repair pod. Include Premium and Standard high-value devices, complex mid-risk devices, and reopened claims. Use parallel eligibility/risk review, pre-approved part pricing, mandatory quality check, and pod-owned triage.

  - **Go** if reviewed/repair cycle time improves materially from the current 96h/120h medians while complaint/reopen remains near the current reviewed/repair range, high-value rework falls below the 12.5% baseline, and inspector capacity is not overloaded.
  - **Stop** if direct labor cost rises without downstream complaint, credit, or rework reduction; if the pod merely recreates the escalation board in asynchronous form; or if part-price catalog delays survive.

- **Sequencing logic.**

  Sequence from low-regret, fast-to-reverse changes to high-commitment operating-model changes. A’s rule update is deployable in two weeks and addresses the highest compliance exposure. B addresses the largest abandonment and repeated-work pool but needs portal/support design. C is the most expensive and organizationally complex, so it should be piloted after A and B reveal the true demand for high-value review, evidence completion, and technical capacity.

- **How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream.**

  Use matched comparison cohorts by segment, value, defect type, and evidence status; keep a control group on the current process where ethically and operationally acceptable. Count abandonment and denial as outcomes, not successful exits. Track cases beyond Quality/compliance close into the complaint/reopen window, because the current process’s apparent cheapness is only exposed downstream. Report total good-outcome cost, not pre-rework cost. Re-run alignment diagnostics after each pilot to verify that improvements are not merely moving deviations from Fast approval to reopen, escalation, or abandonment. Finally, review DFG edge changes and performance DFG changes together: an edge that becomes faster is not an improvement if it feeds a downstream complaint or rework loop.
