# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

Each proposed role is delineated by core and peripheral activities:

- **Core activities** represent the primary responsibilities that define the role's essence and directly contribute to the process's success. These activities are frequent and correlate with higher case complexity scores.
- **Peripheral activities** indicate supplementary tasks that the role occasionally performs. These are less frequent and may involve temporary or cross-functional support.

For example, the Front Office Service Agents (R01/R02) have core activities like registering requests, validating entitlements, and implementing solutions. Peripheral activities include configuring solutions, security reviews, and customer negotiations, which they perform less frequently. This distinction helps differentiate between core competencies and auxiliary tasks.

Three shadow or bridging roles are identified:

While he handles these tasks, his authority in commercial approvals (A9) is less evident. - **R13_Mateo (Cross-Functional Escalation Broker)**: Mateo acts as a bridge between technical and commercial teams, coordinating reopen requests (A13) and performing approvals (A9). He facilitates escalation (A13) and collaborates with R05_Erik for security reviews (A6). This role is crucial for maintaining process flow during high incident loads. - **R05_Erik (Rare Critical Specialist)**: Erik specializes in security reviews (A6) and rare but critical interventions. His high activity in A6 and occasional involvement in the entire case workflow highlights his critical role. Benefits include specialized expertise in security, while risks involve over-reliance on his availability and the potential for delays when he is unavailable.

Automation boundary roles and temporary workforce roles also play significant but often overlooked roles, ensuring process continuity during temporary absences.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
from pm4py.objects.log.factory import DefaultEventLogFactory
from pm4py.objects.xml.factory import DefaultXMLFactory
from pm4py.process.discovery import discover_organizational_roles as dor
from pm4py.process.similarity import discover_activity_based_resource_similarity as dasr
from pm4py.process.similarity import handover_of_work_network as hown
from pm4py.process.similarity import working_together_network as wtn
import pandas as pd

# Load data
log = DefaultEventLogFactory().create(pd.read_csv('order_to_resolution_log.csv'))

# Discover organizational roles
roles = dor.discover_organizational_roles(log, case_attr='case', activity_attr='concept:name')
print("Organizational Roles:", roles)

# Discover activity-based resource similarity
similarity_matrix = dasr.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log, case_attr='case', activity_attr='concept:name', time_attr='time:timestamp')
print("Activity-Based Similarity Matrix:\n", similarity_matrix)

# Compute handover-of-work network
handover_network = hown.handover_of_work_network(log)
print("Handover-of-Work Network:", handover_network)

# Compute working-together network
working_network = wtn.working_together_network(log)
print("Working-Together Network:", working_network)
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management can use the proposed organizational role structure for several strategic purposes:

- **Staffing and Training**: By understanding the core competencies of each role, management can allocate resources effectively. For example, the Front Office Service Agents (R01/R02) can be trained to handle more complex cases, while the Technical Analysts (R03/R04) can focus on specialized diagnostics. The Automation Bot (R14) ensures continuous registration and validation, reducing manual workload. R05_Erik's critical security role should be highlighted for training and readiness.

- **Risk Control and Resilience**: Identifying shadow and bridging roles helps manage risks associated with role ambiguity. For instance, R07_Giorgio's commercial shadow role can be mitigated by formalizing his pricing authority, ensuring consistency in pricing decisions. R13_Mateo's bridging role requires robust coordination protocols during high incident loads to prevent delays. R05_Erik's critical specialist role necessitates backup plans and cross-training to ensure process resilience when he is unavailable.

**Cautions**:

1. **Avoid Over-Reliance on Activity Frequency**: While R01 and R02 show high activity frequencies in core activities, this does not necessarily imply superior authority. Their frequent involvement in cross-functional tasks suggests a need for clear role definitions and authority boundaries.

2. **Consider Case Complexity**: The complexity of cases handled by each role should be integrated into the role structure. High-complexity cases (e.g., R03_Chen's high-complexity cases) may warrant specialized roles or additional training to ensure quality outcomes.

3. **Temporary Substitution Limitations**: Temporary agents like R11_Kai and R12_Lina should not be assumed to possess the same competencies as permanent staff. Training and support should be provided to ensure they can effectively handle their assigned tasks without compromising process quality.

4. **Shadow Roles Benefits and Risks**: While shadow roles like R07_Giorgio provide flexibility, they also introduce risks such as inconsistent decision-making. Formalizing their roles with clear guidelines and authority limits can mitigate these risks.

By carefully considering these factors, management can leverage the proposed role structure to enhance operational efficiency, risk management, and overall service quality.
