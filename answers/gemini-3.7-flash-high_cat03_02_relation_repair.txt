## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

In Object-Centric Event Log (OCEL 2.0) repair, missing Event-to-Object (E2O) and Object-to-Object (O2O) relations require an abductive reasoning framework that evaluates observed telemetry, structural constraints, and domain semantics within a calibrated belief model.

### Rule Classification

1. **Hard Invariants (Strict constraints — violations reject candidate repairs):**
   - **HI-1 (Referential Integrity):** Every E2O edge $(e, o)$ and O2O edge $(o_1, \text{qualifier}, o_2)$ must reference existing identifiers in $E$ and $O$.
   - **HI-2 (Metamodel Typing & Directionality):** O2O qualifiers are strictly typed and directed (e.g., `Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, `Payment settles Invoice`). Inverted orientations are invalid.
   - **HI-3 (Temporal Causality):** Processing steps must maintain chronological ordering ($t_{\text{Create Order}} \le t_{\text{Add Item}} \le t_{\text{Pack Item}} \le t_{\text{Dispatch Package}} \le t_{\text{Confirm Delivery}}$).
   - **HI-4 (Attribute Immutability):** Observed attributes (scanner refs, carrier refs, amounts) must not be altered.

2. **Strong Defaults (High-confidence priors — accepted unless contradicted):**
   - **SD-1 (Scanner Telemetry):** Scanner prefixes (e.g., `scanner_ref=PK8-2`) bind directly to the target container (`PK-8`) unless station-sharing is proven.
   - **SD-2 (Carrier Reference Match):** Dispatch references matching carrier IDs (e.g., `external_ref=N-421?` matching `carrier_ref=N-421` on `DEL-Y`) form strong default bindings.
   - **SD-3 (Standard Cardinality Baselines):** Standard execution assumes 1 Order per `Create Order`, 1 Item + 1 Package per `Pack Item`, 1 Package + 1 Delivery per `Dispatch Package`, and 1 Payment + 1 Invoice per `Record Payment`.

3. **Weak Heuristics (Corroborating signals — insufficient in isolation):**
   - **WH-1 (Arithmetic Balance):** Monetary equality ($\sum \text{declared\_value} = \text{invoice total}$; $\sum \text{payment} = \text{invoice total}$) suggests consolidation, but fees, discounts, and taxes make equality evidence, not proof.
   - **WH-2 (Account / Route Context):** Shared customer (`C17`) or route (`NORTH`) raises prior probability of joint handling but does not guarantee co-processing.
   - **WH-3 (Wildcard Patterns):** Wildcards (`external_ref=N-42*`) indicate candidate sets (`N-420`, `N-421`) without uniquely isolating targets.

4. **Evidence Against a Repair (Negative indicators — downweight or reject candidates):**
   - **EA-1 (Cross-Customer Binding):** Associating items, orders, or invoices across distinct customers.
   - **EA-2 (Chronological Inversion):** Upstream operational events occurring after downstream settlements.
   - **EA-3 (Unjustified Cardinality Explosion):** Creating unevidenced $M:N$ structures without multi-scan telemetry.

### Evidence Combination Framework

Candidate relations are scored across orthogonal dimensions: identifier similarity ($S_{\text{id}}$), temporal proximity ($S_{\text{time}}$), account alignment ($S_{\text{cust}}$), route compatibility ($S_{\text{route}}$), and arithmetic balance ($S_{\text{arith}}$). Strong physical telemetry ($S_{\text{id}}$) receives primary weight. Weak financial heuristics provide secondary uplift but cannot justify repairs independently. Crucially, inferred E2O links must not be treated as empirical corroboration for derived O2O edges.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

To preserve calibrated uncertainty rather than forcing artificial completeness, we define two primary bundles and one logistics variant.

### Bundle A: Conservative / Minimum-Change Repair (Selected Baseline)

Bundle A restricts modifications strictly to high-confidence physical telemetry where scanner and carrier references provide direct operational evidence, deliberately leaving administrative, billing, and portal ambiguities unresolved.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepted (P-01, P-02). Telemetry `scanner_ref=PK8-2` at 09:05 links packing of `IT-B1` to container `PK-8`. This models `PK-8` as a multi-order consolidated package containing `IT-A2` (`ORD-A`) and `IT-B1` (`ORD-B`) for customer `C17`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Accepted (P-03, P-04). `external_ref=N-421?` resolves to `DEL-Y` (`carrier_ref=N-421`), establishing that `DEL-Y` carried `PK-8` on route `NORTH`.
- **ev11 and Delivery Objects:** Left Unresolved. The wildcard `external_ref=N-42*` and portal customer `C17` confirm receipt for `ORD-A`, but cannot disambiguate between `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, or portal loss of carrier identifiers.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Left Unresolved. While $130 + $100 = $230 matches the invoice total, arithmetic equality is not proof (Rule 6). `ORD-B` is retained as unbilled to preserve potential unbilled order anomalies.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Left Unresolved. `PAY-2` ($130) is maintained as unapplied cash, preserving visibility over unallocated receipts.
- **Lifecycle Coherence:** Produces complete physical fulfillment lifecycles for items and packages while keeping administrative and financial lifecycles open, accurately reflecting genuine logging gaps.

### Bundle B: Consolidated Order-to-Cash Interpretation

Bundle B interprets the dataset as an integrated multi-order fulfillment and consolidated billing/settlement workflow for customer `C17`.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepted (P-01, P-02). `IT-B1` packed into `PK-8`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Accepted (P-03, P-04). `PK-8` dispatched via `DEL-Y`.
- **ev11 and Delivery Objects:** Resolved as Multi-Object Confirmation (P-05, P-06, P-07). ev11 is interpreted as a portal batch confirmation acknowledging shipments `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` and orders `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` under tracking prefix `N-42*`.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Accepted (P-08, P-09). `INV-44` is modeled as a consolidated invoice billing `ORD-A` ($130) and `ORD-B` ($100) totaling $230.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Accepted (P-10, P-11). `PAY-2` ($130) is modeled as a second installment settling `INV-44`, combining with `PAY-1` ($100) to clear the $230 balance.
- **Lifecycle Coherence:** Achieves 100% closed lifecycles across all 12 objects, but introduces heavy cross-object coupling and risks masking unbilled orders or unapplied cash.

### Bundle C: Single-Carrier Bulk Dispatch & Split Billing Variant

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepted (P-01, P-02).
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Assigned to `DEL-X`. Interprets `N-421?` as an erroneous route inquiry; `PK-7` and `PK-8` are loaded onto `DEL-X` as a bulk delivery; `DEL-Y` is an unused booking.
- **ev11 and Delivery Objects:** Linked exclusively to `DEL-X` and `ORD-A`.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Rejected. `INV-44` bills only `ORD-A` ($130 + $100 freight/fees = $230); `ORD-B` remains unbilled.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Rejected. `PAY-2` is an advance payment for future orders.
- **Lifecycle Coherence:** Isolates `ORD-B` as unbilled and `DEL-Y` as an unexecuted ghost delivery.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

### Object Type Lifecycle Evaluation (Bundle A)

1. **Order (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`):** `ORD-A` achieves a complete lifecycle (`Create Order` $\to$ `Add Item` [x2] $\to$ `Confirm Delivery` $\to$ `Issue Invoice`). `ORD-B` achieves fulfillment through its item `IT-B1` (packed in `PK-8`, dispatched in `DEL-Y`), but its billing and settlement remain open.
2. **Item (`IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, `IT-B1`):** All items achieve complete 2-event lifecycles (`Add Item` $\to$ `Pack Item`). `IT-A1` is packed in `PK-7`; `IT-A2` and `IT-B1` are packed in `PK-8`.
3. **Package (`PK-7`, `PK-8`):** Both packages achieve complete lifecycles (`Pack Item` $\to$ `Dispatch Package`). `PK-8` aggregates two packing events (ev07, ev08).
4. **Delivery (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`):** `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` both execute `Dispatch Package`. Confirmation via ev11 is left unresolved at the object level, consistent with carrier-managed delivery handoffs.
5. **Invoice (`INV-44`):** Executed via `Issue Invoice` (ev12) and partially settled by `PAY-1` (ev13).
6. **Payment (`PAY-1`, `PAY-2`):** `PAY-1` settles `INV-44`. `PAY-2` is an unapplied cash entry with a single `Record Payment` event.

### Orphan Object and Orphan Event Analysis

- **Unrepaired Log:** `DEL-Y` is an orphan object (0 related events); ev08 is a pack event without a package container; ev10 is a dispatch event without a delivery carrier.
- **Repaired Log (Bundle A):** Zero orphan events. All events associate with at least one valid object. `DEL-Y` is actively bound to ev10 and `PK-8`. `PAY-2` has a valid single-event lifecycle as unapplied cash.

### Cardinality and Structural Patterns

- **Multi-Order Packaging ($1:N$ Package-to-Items across Orders):** `PK-8` contains `IT-A2` (`ORD-A`) and `IT-B1` (`ORD-B`), creating an $M:N$ structural coupling between Orders and Packages. This is physically plausible due to shared customer `C17` and route `NORTH`.
- **Split Order Fulfillment ($1:N$ Order-to-Packages):** `ORD-A` items are split across `PK-7` and `PK-8`.

### Independent Support and Circular Reasoning Audit

- **Independent Corroboration:** In ev08, scanner telemetry (`PK8-2`), timestamp sequence (09:05), and container weight budgets independently support the repair. O2O edge `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is an ontological projection of this physical event.
- **Circular Reasoning Identification:** Inferring `INV-44 bills ORD-B` solely from arithmetic ($130 + 100 = 230$) and then using that inferred O2O link to justify adding `ORD-B` to event ev12 constitutes circular reasoning. Bundle A strictly avoids this fallacy.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

### 1. Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph (OC-DFG)

- **Unrepaired Log:** `IT-B1` dead-ends after `Pack Item` (ev08) with no outgoing package edge; `PK-8` stops at `Dispatch Package` (ev10) with no delivery transition; `DEL-Y` is absent from the DFG.
- **Bundle A:** Restores `Add Item` $\to$ `Pack Item` for all 3 items; establishes `Pack Item` $\to$ `Dispatch Package` for `PK-8` (traversed twice); activates `DEL-Y` with `Dispatch Package`.
- **Bundle B:** Adds cross-object DFG transitions `Dispatch Package` $\to$ `Confirm Delivery` connecting `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` to both orders, introduces `Confirm Delivery` $\to$ `Issue Invoice` for `ORD-B`, and introduces a self-loop `Record Payment` $\to$ `Record Payment` on `INV-44`.

### 2. Object Type Graph (OTG) and ET-OT Graph

- **OTG Changes:** Unrepaired has 1 `Delivery -- Package` edge (`DEL-X -- PK-7`). Bundle A adds `Package -- Item` (`PK-8 -- IT-B1`) and `Delivery -- Package` (`DEL-Y -- PK-8`). Bundle B further adds `Invoice -- Order` (`INV-44 -- ORD-B`, $1:N$) and `Payment -- Invoice` (`PAY-2 -- INV-44`, $N:1$).
- **ET–OT Bipartite Graph:** In unrepaired, `Pack Item` connects to `{Item}` for ev08 and `{Item, Package}` for ev06/ev07. Bundle A homogenizes `Pack Item` to always connect to `{Item, Package}`, and `Dispatch Package` to `{Package, Delivery}`.

### 3. Flattening Transformations

- **Order Flattening:** In unrepaired, `ORD-A` has 8 events, while `ORD-B` has 3 events (`Create`, `Add`, `Pack`). In Bundle A, `ORD-B` includes `Pack Item` (ev08) and `Dispatch Package` (ev10) via item-package containment. In Bundle B, `ORD-B` inherits ev11, ev12, ev13, and ev14, creating identical trace variants for both orders.
- **Delivery Flattening:** `DEL-Y` is empty in unrepaired; contains 1 event (`Dispatch Package`) in Bundle A; contains 2 events (`Dispatch Package`, `Confirm Delivery`) in Bundle B.

### 4. Activity Frequency Metrics

- **Event vs. Unique Object vs. Total E2O Counts:**
  - `Pack Item`: 3 events across 3 items and 2 packages. Total E2O links increase from 5 to 6 in Bundle A.
  - `Dispatch Package`: 2 events across 2 packages and 2 deliveries. Total E2O links increase from 3 to 4 in Bundle A.
  - `Confirm Delivery`: 1 event; associated with 1 object (`ORD-A`) in Bundle A, expanding to 4–5 objects in Bundle B.
  - `Issue Invoice`: 1 event; associated with 2 objects (`INV-44`, `ORD-A`) in Bundle A, expanding to 3 objects in Bundle B.
  - `Record Payment`: 2 events; associated with 3 E2O links in Bundle A, expanding to 4 links in Bundle B.

### 5. Equivalent-Execution Clustering around Order

In unrepaired, `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` are assigned to disparate execution clusters (completed order vs. abandoned fulfillment). In Bundle B, both orders are forced into a single consolidated cluster. Bundle A preserves the operational distinction of `ORD-B` (fulfilled but unbilled) while grouping its shared physical logistics.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
"""
PM4Py Validation Script for OCEL 2.0 Relation Repair
Demonstrates table copying, repair concatenation, referential integrity checks,
OCEL construction, discovery of OC-DFG, OTG, ET-OT, and flattened discovery.
"""

import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def validate_and_discover_ocel(
    events: pd.DataFrame,
    objects: pd.DataFrame,
    relations: pd.DataFrame,
    o2o: pd.DataFrame,
):
    # Step 1: Copy original tables before repair
    events_rep = events.copy()
    objects_rep = objects.copy()
    relations_rep = relations.copy()
    o2o_rep = o2o.copy()

    # Step 2: Define selected repair additions (Bundle A: Conservative Repair)
    e2o_additions = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "ocel:eid": "ev08",
                "ocel:oid": "PK-8",
                "ocel:qualifier": "packs",
            },
            {
                "ocel:eid": "ev10",
                "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
                "ocel:qualifier": "dispatches",
            },
        ]
    )

    o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "ocel:oid": "PK-8",
                "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1",
                "ocel:qualifier": "packs",
            },
            {
                "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
                "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",
                "ocel:qualifier": "carries",
            },
        ]
    )

    # Concatenate repair additions
    relations_rep = pd.concat(
        [relations_rep, e2o_additions], ignore_index=True
    )
    o2o_rep = pd.concat([o2o_rep, o2o_additions], ignore_index=True)

    # Step 3: Referential Integrity and Duplicate Validation
    valid_eids = set(events_rep["ocel:eid"])
    valid_oids = set(objects_rep["ocel:oid"])

    assert relations_rep["ocel:eid"].isin(valid_eids).all(), (
        "E2O Violation: Invalid event ID."
    )
    assert relations_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(valid_oids).all(), (
        "E2O Violation: Invalid object ID."
    )
    assert o2o_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(valid_oids).all(), (
        "O2O Violation: Invalid source object ID."
    )
    assert o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"].isin(valid_oids).all(), (
        "O2O Violation: Invalid target object ID."
    )

    assert not relations_rep.duplicated(
        subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
    ).any(), "Duplicate E2O relations."
    assert not o2o_rep.duplicated(
        subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
    ).any(), "Duplicate O2O relations."

    print("Referential integrity and duplicate checks passed.")

    # Step 4: Construct OCEL 2.0 object
    # NOTE: In PM4Py >= 2.7.8, constructor is pm4py.OCEL(events=..., objects=..., relations=..., o2o=...).
    # In legacy PM4Py (<= 2.3.x), constructor was pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL.
    ocel = pm4py.OCEL(
        events=events_rep,
        objects=objects_rep,
        relations=relations_rep,
        o2o=o2o_rep,
    )

    # Step 5: Process Discovery on Repaired OCEL
    ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
    otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
    etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

    # Step 6: Flattening on Order and Delivery
    # Note: Avoid using pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment as proof of business semantics;
    # automated relational closure creates unverified transitive dependencies.
    flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Order")
    flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Delivery")

    return ocel, ocdfg, otg, etot, flat_order, flat_delivery
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Aggressive repairs that force every object into an artificially complete lifecycle introduce severe distortions into downstream process mining:

1. **Masking Revenue Leakage by Forcing Invoicing (False Conformance):**
   Linking `INV-44` to `ORD-B` solely because $130 + $100 = $230 conceals business failure modes such as unbilled orders or billing omissions. If `ORD-B` was never invoiced, aggressive repair fabricates false conformance that blinds revenue audits.

2. **Mischaracterizing Unapplied Cash and Corrupting AR Aging:**
   Forcing `PAY-2` ($130) to settle `INV-44` eliminates unapplied cash balances. If `PAY-2` was an advance deposit or unallocated remittance, forcing the link conceals suspense balances and distorts accounts receivable aging metrics.

3. **Spurious SLA and Lead-Time Compression via Wildcard Delivery Resolution:**
   Assigning `Confirm Delivery` (ev11) to both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` based on `external_ref=N-42*` fabricates on-time delivery metrics. If `DEL-Y` was delayed, assigning the 14:00 confirmation records a premature delivery milestone.

4. **Artificial Synchronization Bottlenecks in Single-Object Flattenings:**
   Linking `ORD-B` to ev11, ev12, ev13, and ev14 forces the flattened trace of `ORD-B` to synchronize with `ORD-A`. In process discovery (e.g., Inductive Miner), this generates artificial parallel splits and joins (`AND-forks`) that do not exist physically.

5. **Self-Fulfilling Confirmation Bias via Automated O2O Graph Closures:**
   Using automated O2O relational enrichment to infer E2O bindings creates circular feedback loops. Once an assumed relation is added, discovery algorithms treat it as ground truth, generating over-coupled models that obscure genuine process decoupling.
