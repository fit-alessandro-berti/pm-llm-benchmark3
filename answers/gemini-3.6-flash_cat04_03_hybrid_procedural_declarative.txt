# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material Assumptions
1. **Case Lifetime and Scope**: Every onboarding case begins with `Receive Onboarding Request` and terminates with `Close Onboarding`.
2. **Event Log Sufficiency**: The event log records activity completions with timestamps and user identifiers (`resource`). Operational context, emergency flags, and external offline authorizations are absent from the event log unless explicitly modeled as discrete logging events.
3. **Data and Resource Scope**: Four-eyes constraints (segregation of duties) rely on the `resource` attribute of each event.

## Conflict Analysis: R5 vs. R6
- **Requirement R5 (Legal Perspective)**: Mandates a strict sequence: if `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must *precede* `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances.
- **Requirement R6 (Operations Perspective)**: Permits emergency activation prior to `Obtain Legal Opinion` provided joint authorization occurred offline and `Obtain Legal Opinion` follows within 24 hours. Because the event log lacks offline authorization attributes, an automated system evaluating control flow cannot distinguish a legitimate emergency activation from an unapproved control breach.

### Possible Conflict Resolutions

| Resolution Strategy | Primary Rule | Policy Handling | Compliance Risk & Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Resolution A: Strict Legal Compliance (Adopted Baseline)** | R5 | Enforce `Obtain Legal Opinion` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor` as an absolute control-flow requirement. Any case activating prior to legal opinion fails control-flow conformance. | **Low Legal Risk / High Operational Rigidity**: Prevents unvetted vendors from being activated, but flags valid emergency overrides as violations. |
| **Resolution B: Operational Flexibility with Audit Flag** | R6 | Allow `Activate Vendor` prior to `Obtain Legal Opinion` *if and only if* `Obtain Legal Opinion` occurs within 24 hours of escalation/activation. Flag the case for post-hoc offline authorization audit. | **High Legal Risk / Low Operational Rigidity**: Prevents operational bottlenecks, but exposes the organization to risk if the offline authorization was never granted. |

### Implemented Model Choice
The **implemented baseline model adopts Resolution A** at the control-flow level (DECLARE / Procedural backbone) to uphold non-negotiable legal gating. Resolution B is supported in the **Resource/Data validation layer**, where T6 is flagged as an operational exception requiring manual audit rather than a hard silent pass.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

| Requirement | Primary Formalism | Exact Encoding | Why This Formalism | Redundancy or Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1**: Reject/Activate mutual exclusion | Log Skeleton / DECLARE | `never_together: {("Activate Vendor", "Reject Vendor")}` / `noncoexistence` | Directly expresses pairwise activity exclusion across the whole trace. | Also enforced by procedural model structural choices. |
| **R2**: Manager vs. Compliance approval per path | Procedural Backbone | Exclusive choice branches ($\text{XOR}$) after `Classify Risk` | Enforces structural path routing and structural absence in rejected branch. | Modelled in DECLARE via `noncoexistence` and `absence`. |
| **R3**: Request/Receive Clarification response | DECLARE | `response: ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` & `precedence` | Enforces that every request is eventually answered before trace end. | Standard process tree cannot easily model arbitrary mid-process loopings without over-generalizing. |
| **R4**: Sanctions Escalation requires Legal Opinion | Log Skeleton / DECLARE | `always_after: {("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")}` / `response` | Ensures eventual completion of legal review if escalation occurs. | Does not enforce order relative to activation (handled by R5). |
| **R5**: Legal Opinion before Activation | DECLARE | `precedence: ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` (scoped to cases with escalation) | Enforces temporal sequence ordering across arbitrary distance. | Conflict with R6 operations bypass rule. |
| **R6**: Emergency activation timing | Resource/Data Layer | Custom Python code measuring timestamp delta $\le 24\text{ hours}$ | Requires timestamp calculation and conditional logic unavailable in standard activity graphs. | Cannot verify offline joint authorization from log data alone. |
| **R7**: Escalation after Sanctions Screening | Log Skeleton / DECLARE | `always_before: {("Sanctions Escalation", "Run Sanctions Screening")}` / `precedence` | Enforces strict causality (cannot escalate before screening). | Also structural in procedural model. |
| **R8**: Mutual exclusion of approval types & EDD | Procedural Backbone | Branching structure: (EDD $\rightarrow$ Compliance) $\text{XOR}$ (Manager Approval) | Structural exclusion across parallel/choice blocks. | DECLARE `noncoexistence` provides secondary validation. |
| **R9**: Four-eyes / Segregation of Duties | Resource/Data Layer | `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` / DataFrame attribute comparison | Requires inspecting the `resource` attribute per event pair. | Control-flow formalisms treat activities as opaque atomic symbols. |
| **R10**: Cardinality constraints ($\le 1$) | Log Skeleton | `activ_freq: {"Activate Vendor": {0, 1}, "Reject Vendor": {0, 1}, "Release First Payment": {0, 1}}` | Directly bounds occurrence counts. | DECLARE `absence2` can approximate, but skeleton `activ_freq` is exact. |
| **R11**: Post-activation bank change & re-verification | Resource/Data Layer | Imperative state machine tracking state transition after `Activate Vendor` | Requires complex stateful re-triggering (re-verification before payment). | Expressing multi-step state loops in pure DECLARE creates heavy vacuity issues. |
| **R12**: Temporal duration expectations | Temporal Profile | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400)` ($\zeta=2.0$) | Encodes mean and standard deviation of time deltas between activity pairs. | Serves as performance/risk warning layer; non-blocking for strict control flow. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## Formal Description
The procedural backbone models the core lifecycle:
1. `Receive Onboarding Request` (Start)
2. Parallel Execution ($\text{AND}$): `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Verify Bank Account`.
3. Join $\rightarrow$ `Classify Risk`.
4. Outcome Choice ($\text{XOR}$):
   - **Path A (Rejection)**: `Reject Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Close Onboarding`.
   - **Path B (Low-Risk Accepted)**: `Manager Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment` $\rightarrow$ `Close Onboarding`.
   - **Path C (High-Risk Accepted)**: `Enhanced Due Diligence` $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment` $\rightarrow$ `Close Onboarding`.

*Note on Approximations*: Clarification loops and post-activation changes (R11) are intentionally omitted from the core procedural tree to avoid unstructured state-space explosion and loop-induced control-flow vacuity. They are handed off to DECLARE, Log Skeleton, and the Data/Resource Layer.

## PM4Py Executable Code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# Define leaf nodes
req = ProcessTree(label="Receive Onboarding Request")
cap = ProcessTree(label="Capture Master Data")
sanc = ProcessTree(label="Run Sanctions Screening")
vbank = ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account")
classify = ProcessTree(label="Classify Risk")

mgr_app = ProcessTree(label="Manager Approval")
edd = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence")
comp_app = ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval")

act = ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor")
pay = ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment")
close = ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding")
rej = ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor")

# Step 1: Initial Parallel Checks
parallel_checks = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[cap, sanc, vbank])

# Step 2: Outcomes
# Outcome A: Reject
rej_branch = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[rej, close])

# Outcome B: Low-Risk
low_risk_branch = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[mgr_app, act, pay, close])

# Outcome C: High-Risk
high_risk_branch = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[edd, comp_app, act, pay, close])

# Outcome Choice
outcomes = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[rej_branch, low_risk_branch, high_risk_branch])

# Complete Backbone
procedural_model = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    req,
    parallel_checks,
    classify,
    outcomes
])
```

## DECLARE Semantics Table

| Template | Scope / Activity Pair | Semantics & Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `existence` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | Mandatory execution: Activity must occur at least once in every trace. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor)` | Mutual exclusion: If `Activate Vendor` occurs, `Reject Vendor` cannot occur in the same trace, and vice versa. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Mutual exclusion: An accepted case must follow either the low-risk path or high-risk path, never both. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Enhanced Due Diligence)` | Path purity: Manager approval cannot co-occur with high-risk due diligence. |
| `response` | `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Obligation target: If `Request Clarification` occurs, `Receive Clarification` must occur later in the trace. |
| `response` | `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Obligation target: Escalation obligates a downstream legal opinion. |
| `response` | `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Mandatory progression: Activation obligates subsequent payment release. |
| `precedence` | `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Pre-condition: `Sanctions Escalation` cannot occur unless `Run Sanctions Screening` occurred prior. |
| `precedence` | `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | Strict Legal Gating (R5): `Activate Vendor` cannot occur unless `Obtain Legal Opinion` occurred prior. |

## Model Explanation and Layer Alignment
- **`always_before` vs. `always_after` Orientation**:
  - `always_before: (B, A)` means if $B$ occurs, $A$ must have occurred *before* $B$.
  - `always_after: (A, B)` means if $A$ occurs, $B$ must occur *after* $A$.
  - Pair `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` appears in `always_after` as `(Escalation, Opinion)` and in `always_before` as `(Opinion, Escalation)`.
- **Handling Post-Activation Changes (R11)**: `Verify Bank Account` has frequency `{1, 2}` to accommodate re-verification following a `Change Bank Details` event.

# 6. Temporal Profile

## Python Dictionary (`temporal_profile`)

Times are converted to seconds ($1\text{ hour} = 3600\text{ seconds}$).

```python
temporal_profile = {
    # Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification: 48h (172,800s), std 24h (86,400s)
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800.0, 86400.0),
    
    # Sanctions Escalation -> Obtain Legal Opinion: 12h (43,200s), std 6h (21,600s)
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200.0, 21600.0),
    
    # Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment: 72h (259,200s), std 36h (129,600s)
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200.0, 129600.0),
}
```

## Operational Rationale for Non-Blocking Temporal Violations
Temporal profile conformance evaluates performance distributions using standard deviation thresholds ($\zeta = 2.0$). A temporal deviation indicates operational delay or unusual speed, but does not invalidate control-flow legality. For example, a legal opinion taking 34 hours instead of 12 hours represents a service-level agreement (SLA) breach, not a process control breach; flagging it as an automatic control-flow failure would distort process audit metrics.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

```python
import pandas as pd
from typing import List, Dict, Any

def validate_resource_and_data_rules(trace_events: List[Dict[str, Any]]) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Validates Resource (SoD/Four-Eyes) and Complex State/Emergency Rules (R6, R9, R11).
    Expects trace_events sorted by timestamp.
    Each event dict must have: 'concept:name', 'org:resource', 'time:timestamp'.
    """
    violations = []
    warnings = []
    
    # Helper to extract events
    events_by_name = {}
    for e in trace_events:
        name = e.get("concept:name")
        events_by_name.setdefault(name, []).append(e)
        
    # Check missing attributes
    for idx, e in enumerate(trace_events):
        if "org:resource" not in e or not e["org:resource"]:
            violations.append(f"Missing resource attribute at event index {idx} ({e.get('concept:name')})")
        if "time:timestamp" not in e:
            violations.append(f"Missing timestamp attribute at event index {idx} ({e.get('concept:name')})")

    if violations:
        return {"status": "FAIL", "violations": violations, "warnings": warnings}

    # R9: Four-Eyes Principle Checks
    # 1. Verify Bank Account resource != Manager Approval OR Compliance Approval resource
    vbank_events = events_by_name.get("Verify Bank Account", [])
    app_events = events_by_name.get("Manager Approval", []) + events_by_name.get("Compliance Approval", [])
    
    for vb in vbank_events:
        for app in app_events:
            if vb["org:resource"] == app["org:resource"]:
                violations.append(
                    f"R9 Violation (Four-Eyes): Resource '{vb['org:resource']}' performed both "
                    f"Verify Bank Account and {app['concept:name']}."
                )

    # 2. Capture Master Data resource != Activate Vendor resource
    cap_events = events_by_name.get("Capture Master Data", [])
    act_events = events_by_name.get("Activate Vendor", [])
    for cap in cap_events:
        for act in act_events:
            if cap["org:resource"] == act["org:resource"]:
                violations.append(
                    f"R9 Violation (Four-Eyes): Resource '{cap['org:resource']}' performed both "
                    f"Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor."
                )

    # R6 Check: Emergency Policy Handling
    sanc_esc = events_by_name.get("Sanctions Escalation", [])
    legal_op = events_by_name.get("Obtain Legal Opinion", [])
    
    if sanc_esc and act_events:
        esc_time = sanc_esc[0]["time:timestamp"]
        act_time = act_events[0]["time:timestamp"]
        
        # Check if Activation occurred before Legal Opinion
        if legal_op:
            legal_time = legal_op[0]["time:timestamp"]
            if act_time < legal_time:
                # Emergency activation path
                delta_hours = (legal_time - act_time).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                if delta_hours <= 24.0:
                    warnings.append(
                        f"R6 Emergency Activation Detected: Vendor activated before legal opinion. "
                        f"Opinion received in {delta_hours:.1f}h (<=24h). Offline authorization requires manual audit."
                    )
                else:
                    violations.append(
                        f"R6/R5 Violation: Vendor activated before legal opinion and legal opinion "
                        f"exceeded 24h window ({delta_hours:.1f}h)."
                    )
        else:
            violations.append("R5/R6 Violation: Vendor activated after Sanctions Escalation without Legal Opinion.")

    # R11 Check: Change Bank Details Post-Activation State Machine
    change_bank_events = events_by_name.get("Change Bank Details", [])
    if change_bank_events and act_events:
        act_time = act_events[0]["time:timestamp"]
        post_act_changes = [cb for cb in change_bank_events if cb["time:timestamp"] > act_time]
        
        if post_act_changes:
            # Must be followed by Suspend Vendor
            suspend_events = events_by_name.get("Suspend Vendor", [])
            post_change_suspends = [s for s in suspend_events if s["time:timestamp"] > post_act_changes[0]["time:timestamp"]]
            if not post_change_suspends:
                violations.append("R11 Violation: Change Bank Details occurred after activation without subsequent Suspend Vendor.")
            
            # Check re-verification before subsequent payment
            payments = events_by_name.get("Release First Payment", [])
            post_change_payments = [p for p in payments if p["time:timestamp"] > post_act_changes[0]["time:timestamp"]]
            if post_change_payments:
                re_verifications = [
                    vb for vb in vbank_events 
                    if post_act_changes[0]["time:timestamp"] < vb["time:timestamp"] < post_change_payments[0]["time:timestamp"]
                ]
                if not re_verifications:
                    violations.append("R11 Violation: Payment released after post-activation bank detail change without re-verification.")

    status = "FAIL" if violations else ("WARNING" if warnings else "PASS")
    return {"status": status, "violations": violations, "warnings": warnings}
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

1. **Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers**:
   - The mutual exclusion of `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` (R1) is enforced structurally in the process tree, declaratively via `noncoexistence`, and via `never_together` in the Log Skeleton. While redundant, this multi-layer defense prevents false passes if one layer is disabled.
2. **Post-Activation Loop and State Inconsistencies**:
   - Log Skeletons and DECLARE models struggle with post-activation bank changes (R11). A global rule like `response: (Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` becomes vacuous or false if bank details change prior to activation vs. post-activation.
3. **Vacuity in Declarative Rules**:
   - DECLARE rules like `precedence: (Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` evaluate as **vacuously true** for cases where `Activate Vendor` never occurs (e.g., rejected cases).
4. **Missing Case Attributes and Offline Operations**:
   - R6 relies on an offline joint authorization. Because this event is unrecorded, pure control-flow models cannot distinguish an authorized emergency bypass from an illicit procedure breach.
5. **Timestamp Precision Issues**:
   - Sub-second execution or equal timestamps across concurrent tasks (e.g., parallel initial checks) can cause non-deterministic ordering in sequence-based metrics like `directly_follows`.

## Architectural Design
1. **Canonical Procedural Backbone**: Serves as the primary reference visual model for operational staff, defining normal process routing.
2. **Authoritative Policy Layer**: Enforced by a hybrid verification engine combining **DECLARE** (for non-linear activity dependencies like clarifications and legal gating) and custom **Resource/Data Python validators** (for Four-Eyes and stateful post-activation checks).
3. **Execution Order of Conformance Pipeline**:
   - **Stage 1: Structural & Data Validation Layer** (Fast Failure): Evaluates resource SoD (R9) and missing event attributes.
   - **Stage 2: Declarative Control-Flow Layer**: Validates mutual exclusion (R1), response cycles (R3), and mandatory gating (R5/R7).
   - **Stage 3: Temporal Profile Layer** (Non-Blocking Performance Audit): Evaluates process duration deviations ($\zeta=2.0$) and flags SLA warnings without blocking case completion.
4. **User Surfacing and Alert Handling**:
   - **Hard Failures (Control-Flow / SoD Breaches)**: Immediate block on payment release; case flagged for Compliance Risk Audit.
   - **Policy Warnings (Emergency Bypass / Temporal SLAs)**: Assigned to Operational Risk Managers with an auto-generated ticket requiring offline authorization attachment within 24 hours.
