def handover_wait_table(d):
  x=d.sort_values([CASE,TS]).copy()
  x['prev_res']=x.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift()
  x['prev_act']=x.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift()
  x['prev_ts']=x.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift()
  x['wait_h']=(x[TS]-x['prev_ts']).dt.total_seconds()/3600
  h=x[x[RES].ne(x['prev_res']) & x['prev_res'].notna()].copy()
  h['edge']=h['prev_res']+' -> '+h[RES]
  n_cases=x[CASE].nunique()
  return h.groupby(['edge','handover_reason'], dropna=False).agg(
      freq_per_100_cases=(CASE, lambda s: 100*len(s)/n_cases),
      med_wait_h=('wait_h','median'),
      p90_wait_h=('wait_h', lambda s: s.quantile(.9)),
      rework_rate=('rework_flag','mean'),
      reassignment=('reassignment_count','mean')
  ).reset_index().sort_values('freq_per_100_cases', ascending=False)

# 2. Scenario forecasts

## Optimistic scenario

**Conditions:** central queues are adequately staffed; local technical liaisons remain available for early advice; documentation standards improve; legal/risk triage capacity is expanded; approval queue has named owners; management monitors p90 and segment effects, not only averages.

- **Handover pattern**
  - Handover-of-work network becomes cleaner: fewer ad hoc cross-team handovers, approximately 6  3 per 100 cases.

- **Early warning signs**
  - Two consecutive weeks of rising central queue age.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency**  
   Average or median cycle time can improve while p90/p95 worsens. Central queues often affect a minority of cases severely: high-complexity, legal/risk, or exception cases.  
   *Measure:* p90/p95 cycle time and p90 wait after central handover edges.

2. **High-complexity case disadvantage**  
   High-complexity cases may wait longer because they are routed more often to Technical Shared Service, Legal/Risk, and difficult QC. A stable average can hide deterioration where expertise need is greatest.  
   *Measure:* filter by `case_complexity=high`; compare DFG loops, waits, rework, and SLA misses.

3. **Local knowledge loss**  
   North and South currently have strong local expertise and stable local loops. Removing embedded specialists may degrade regional-case handling even if central decisions are more standardized.  
   *Measure:* outcomes and rework for `local_expertise_flag=1`, by team and customer segment.

4. **Fairness across teams**  
   East may improve because E_Tech was overloaded at 82% utilization, while North and South may lose responsive local support. A global average can mask divergent team effects.  
   *Measure:* team-stratified p50/p90 cycle time, wait, rework, and SLA misses; East vs North/South difference-in-differences.

5. **Fairness across customer segments**  
   Customers requiring language or regional accommodation may face more handovers and more explanation repeated across queues. Their volume may be too small to move the global average.  
   *Measure:* segment-level handover count, wait after handover, rework, and outcome.

6. **Learning and skill erosion**  
   If all hard cases are routed away, local staff stop developing technical capability. This may not affect short-term averages but reduces future capacity and resilience.  
   *Measure:* decline of local resources in technical activities; reduced activity-based resource similarity; role discovery showing loss of local technical roles.

7. **Accountability and role ambiguity**  
   Removing supervisors and introducing a shared approval queue may improve standardization but weaken ownership. Average cycle time does not show who is accountable when a case fails.  
   *Measure:* `handover_reason`, `reassignment_count`, number of resources per case, organizational role entropy.

8. **Exception handling degradation**  
   Current cross-team cover is poor but limited. Centralization may replace it with queue-based exception handling that is slower and less contextual.  
   *Measure:* variants involving absence cover, exception handovers, repeated Specialist Review, or repeated Approval.

9. **Legal/Risk saturation**  
   Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91% utilization. Broader detection and adding difficult QC to the same queue can create nonlinear delays.  
   *Measure:* utilization, queue age, and p90 wait for legal/risk/security cases.

10. **Detection vs true rework confound**  
   More legal/risk referrals may look like worse rework, or may represent better detection. Conversely, lower rework may mean fewer cases are being identified for correction.  
   *Measure:* combine `rework_flag`, Specialist Review loops, final `outcome`, and customer satisfaction.

11. **Resilience to absence**  
   Average performance on normal days may hide fragility when a central specialist is absent.  
   *Measure:* temporal features and wait variance by day, hour, and resource availability.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope**

- **Phase 0 — instrumentation and baseline, 8–12 weeks**
  - No structural change.
  - Validate event-log fields: `handover_reason`, `reassignment_count`, `local_expertise_flag`, `outcome`, `rework_flag`.
  - Establish baseline handover, queue, cycle, rework, SLA, and fairness metrics by segment.

- **Phase 1 — bounded Technical Shared Service, 8–12 weeks**
  - Route high-complexity technical cases from **East** to the centralized Technical Shared Service because East shows the strongest current strain.
  - Include a limited, stratified subset of high-complexity cases from **North** to test whether centralization harms strong local-expertise teams.
  - Keep N_Tech and S_Tech available as local liaisons for early triage; do not remove embedded expertise yet.
  - Do not merge difficult QC and legal/risk triage into the same queue at this stage.

- **Phase 2 — shared approval, 8–12 weeks if guardrails pass**
  - Introduce shared approval for medium/high-value cases.
  - Retain a named local escalation owner until role clarity is proven.

- **Phase 3 — broader centralization only after evidence**
  - Add difficult QC and legal/risk queue integration only if Legal/Risk capacity and queue SLAs are demonstrated.
  - Remove local supervisor or specialist positions only after lagging indicators confirm no unacceptable fairness, learning, or resilience harm.

**Comparison group**

- Keep **South** as a contemporaneous control using the current embedded-specialist model.
- Use difference-in-differences: East vs South, North pilot subset vs South, and pre/post within each group.
- If operationally feasible, randomize eligible cases in North/South between local and central routing.
- Match cases on `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, `priority`, and `local_expertise_flag`.

**Pre/post observation window**

- At least 12 weeks baseline to cover seasonality.
- 8–12 weeks per phase.
- Exclude a 1–2 week wash-in period from each phase.
- Track lagging outcomes for at least 4–8 weeks after case closure.

**Segmentation**

Segment every metric by:

- Team: North, South, East, TSS, Approval, Legal/Risk.
- Complexity: low, medium, high.
- Specialty: none, technical, legal, risk, security.
- Customer segment: including language/regional accommodation groups.
- Priority.
- `local_expertise_flag`.
- `handover_reason`.
- Absence/peak periods.

**Guardrail metrics**

- p90 cycle time and p90 wait after handover no more than ~10% worse than baseline/control.
- High-complexity p90 cycle time no more than ~10% worse.
- Legal/Risk p90 wait no worse than current 9.6h median baseline unless justified by capacity expansion.
- Central resource utilization generally below 85–90%; sustained >90% triggers review.
- Rework rate not more than 1–2 percentage points worse for any major segment.
- SLA miss rate not more than 1–2 percentage points worse.
- `reassignment_count` not more than ~10–20% above baseline.
- No widening fairness gap between East, North, and South beyond agreed tolerance.
- Role-clarity survey score must not decline materially.
- Customer satisfaction and complaint rates must not deteriorate.

**Staff feedback collection**

- Weekly short pulse survey: role clarity, ownership, handover note quality, perceived customer impact.
- Structured interviews with caseworkers, local supervisors, TSS staff, Legal/Risk, and East staff.
- Review of coded `handover_reason` categories, especially “unclear owner,” “missing context,” and “routing error.”
- Monthly learning check: whether local caseworkers still gain technical exposure and whether specialists understand local conventions.

**Rollback or adjustment triggers**

- Pause rollout if central queue age rises for two consecutive weeks.
- Reopen local triage if high-complexity or `local_expertise_flag=1` rework rises materially.
- Split Technical, Legal/Risk, QC, and Approval queues if one queue dominates delays.
- Restore local supervisor escalation ownership if `reassignment_count` or unclear-ownership reasons rise.
- Add capacity or exclude specific customer segments if fairness gaps worsen.
- Roll back fully if p90 cycle time, SLA misses, rework, and role-clarity scores jointly deteriorate.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: pilot first with modifications; do not proceed to full rollout yet.**

The restructuring has plausible benefits: East may gain relief from E_Tech overload, routing may become more consistent, and central queues can improve specialist utilization. However, the proposed management table itself suggests more listed handovers and more median wait exposure, not fewer. Legal/Risk are already near capacity, and adding triage/difficult QC to the same shared-service model creates a strong bottleneck risk. North and South may lose local expertise that currently reduces rework in complex cases.

Proceed beyond the pilot only if all of the following are observed:

- Handover-of-work networks show fewer **problematic** handovers: fewer cross-team transfers, fewer unclear-ownership reasons, lower `reassignment_count`.
- p90 and p95 waits and cycle times are stable or improved for high-complexity, legal/risk/security, and local-expertise cases.
- East improves without material deterioration in North or South.
- Central queues maintain headroom; Legal/Risk do not become saturated.
- Rework and SLA misses are stable or better after outcomes accumulate.
- Role discovery and staff feedback indicate clear accountability.
- Local learning and resilience safeguards are in place, such as technical liaisons, training, and documented regional conventions.

Management should **not** infer success from early average cycle time alone. Early averages may improve because simple cases are processed faster, because automation changes case mix, or because queues have not yet matured. Rework and quality effects appear late. Central utilization may look high because work is backlogged, not because value is being delivered. East’s improvement should not be generalized to North and South. Most importantly, a lower average can coexist with worse tail latency, weaker fairness, poorer local knowledge retention, and unclear accountability.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import itertools
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Load and prepare event log
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")
raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS])

# Define cutover date or pilot phase start
CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01")
raw["phase"] = np.where(raw[TS] < CUTOFF, "before", "after")

# If outcome encodes SLA miss directly, use it; otherwise compute below
raw["sla_miss_flag"] = raw["outcome"].eq("SLA_miss")

# PM4Py formatting
log_all = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id=CASE,
    activity_key=ACT,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

def as_log(df):
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS
    )

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Filtering by phase, team, complexity, specialty, customer segment
# Use level="event" here; switch to level="case" if attributes are case-level constants
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def subset_log(
    phase=None,
    team=None,
    complexity=None,
    specialty=None,
    segment=None,
    priority=None
):
    d = log_all.copy()

    if phase is not None:
        phases = [phase] if isinstance(phase, str) else phase
        d = d[d["phase"].isin(phases)]

    filters = {
        "team": team,
        "case_complexity": complexity,
        "required_specialty": specialty,
        "customer_segment": segment,
        "priority": priority,
    }

    for col, vals in filters.items():
        if vals is not None:
            vals = [vals] if isinstance(vals, str) else vals
            d = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
                d,
                attribute_key=col,
                values=vals,
                level="event",
                retain=True
            )

    return d

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Handover-of-work, working-together, and subcontracting networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def edge_table(graph):
    return pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {"source": u, "target": v, **attrs}
            for u, v, attrs in graph.edges(data=True)
        ]
    )

def organizational_networks(d):
    l = as_log(d)

    handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
        l,
        resource_key=RES,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS
    )

    working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
        l,
        resource_key=RES,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS
    )

    subcontracting = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
        l,
        resource_key=RES,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS
    )

    return {
        "handover": edge_table(handover),
        "working_together": edge_table(working_together),
        "subcontracting": edge_table(subcontracting),
    }

# Custom handover table with waiting and rework after each edge
def handover_wait_table(d):
    x = d.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()

    x["prev_resource"] = x.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift()
    x["prev_activity"] = x.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift()
    x["prev_timestamp"] = x.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift()

    x["wait_hours"] = (
        x[TS] - x["prev_timestamp"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    h = x[
        x["prev_resource"].notna()
        & x[RES].ne(x["prev_resource"])
    ].copy()

    h["handover_edge"] = h["prev_resource"] + " -> " + h[RES]

    n_cases = x[CASE].nunique()

    out = (
        h.groupby(["handover_edge", "handover_reason"], dropna=False)
         .agg(
             freq_per_100_cases=(CASE, lambda s: 100 * len(s) / n_cases),
             median_wait_h=("wait_hours", "median"),
             p90_wait_h=("wait_hours", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
             rework_rate=("rework_flag", "mean"),
             mean_reassignments=("reassignment_count", "mean")
         )
         .reset_index()
         .sort_values("freq_per_100_cases", ascending=False)
    )

    return out

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Variants, DFG, and performance DFG
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def variant_and_dfg_analysis(d):
    l = as_log(d)

    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(l)

    perf_dfg, perf_start, perf_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(l)

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(l)

    variant_counts = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {"variant": " -> ".join(v), "cases": len(cases)}
            for v, cases in variants.items()
        ]
    ).sort_values("cases", ascending=False)

    rework_variants = [
        v for v in variants.keys()
        if "Rework" in v
    ]

    top_variant = max(variants, key=lambda v: len(variants[v]))
    top_variant_log = pm4py.filter_variants(
        l,
        [top_variant],
        retain=True
    )

    return {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": start_activities,
        "end_activities": end_activities,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "variant_counts": variant_counts,
        "rework_variants": rework_variants,
        "top_variant_log": top_variant_log
    }

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Service time, waiting time, cycle time, SLA misses
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def service_wait_sla_analysis(d, sla_hours=24 * 14):
    l = as_log(d)

    enriched_log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(l)
    service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(l)

    case_stats = (
        d.groupby(CASE)
         .agg(
             start_ts=(TS, "min"),
             end_ts=(TS, "max"),
             team=("team", "first"),
             complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
             specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
             segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
             priority=("priority", "first"),
             local_expertise=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
             rework=("rework_flag", "max"),
             reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
             outcome=("outcome", "last"),
             sla_miss_flag=("sla_miss_flag", "max")
         )
         .reset_index()
    )

    case_stats["cycle_hours"] = (
        case_stats["end_ts"] - case_stats["start_ts"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    case_stats["sla_miss_computed"] = (
        case_stats["cycle_hours"] > sla_hours
    )

    return {
        "enriched_log": enriched_log,
        "service_times": service_times,
        "case_stats": case_stats
    }

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Resource similarity and role discovery
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def role_analysis(d):
    l = as_log(d)

    resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        l,
        activity_key=ACT,
        resource_key=RES
    )

    roles, role_matrix = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
        l,
        activity_key=ACT,
        resource_key=RES
    )

    return {
        "resource_similarity": resource_similarity,
        "roles": roles,
        "role_matrix": role_matrix
    }

def rework_temporal_analysis(d):
    l = as_log(d)

    rework_cases = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(l)

    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(l)

    return {
        "rework_cases_per_activity": rework_cases,
        "temporal_features": temporal
    }
```
