# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

**Material Assumptions**
1. The event log contains only `activity`, `timestamp`, and `resource` attributes. External authorization flags, risk scores, and vendor identifiers are absent.
2. The clarification loop (R3) occurs after `Classify Risk` and before any terminal outcome, as observed in T2.
3. Post-activation changes (R11) are exceptional but must be structurally supported; they are modeled as a loop after `Activate Vendor`.
4. Temporal deviations reflect operational variability, not control-flow breaches, unless they cross hard SLA boundaries defined by the business.
5. Resource constraints (R9) are strict segregation-of-duties (SoD) rules; violations are hard failures.

**R5/R6 Conflict Resolutions**
- **Resolution A (Strict Legal Precedence):** Enforce R5 as a hard control-flow rule: `Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion  Activate Vendor`. R6 is treated as a policy exception requiring an explicit external attribute (e.g., `emergency_authorization=True`). If the attribute is missing, the case is flagged as non-compliant. *Compliance Risk:* Legitimate emergencies may be blocked or require manual override if the external flag isn't logged, but legal exposure is minimized.
- **Resolution B (Operational Flexibility):** Override R5 in the control-flow model to allow `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion`. Enforce R6 via the temporal profile (24h window) and a resource-layer check for joint authorization. *Compliance Risk:* Legal opinion may arrive after activation, increasing regulatory exposure if the emergency override is abused or if the 24h SLA is breached.

**Chosen Resolution:** Resolution A. The implemented model enforces R5 strictly in the procedural and DECLARE layers. R6 is handled as a `policy-dependent` check in the resource layer. The compliance risk is operational friction for genuine emergencies, but it preserves a defensible audit trail and prevents unapproved legal bypasses.

| R2 | DECLARE | `exactly_one` on `(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Captures the "one-and-only-one" normative expectation | Case-type dependent (accepted vs rejected); DECLARE is log-agnostic |
| R3 | ProcessTree / DECLARE | `loop(sequence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification))` / `chainresponse` | ProcessTree natively supports multi-cycle loops; DECLARE captures response semantics | ProcessTree loop placement is approximate; DECLARE doesn't enforce "before final outcome" structurally |
| R4 | DECLARE | `response` on `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Simple eventual occurrence requirement; lightweight declarative template | Overlaps with R5 precedence; response is weaker than chainprecedence |
| R5 | DECLARE | `chainprecedence` on `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` and `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | Enforces strict ordering across non-adjacent activities | Conflicts with R6; requires external auth attribute for exception handling |
| R6 | Resource/Data Layer | Conditional check: if `Activate Vendor < Obtain Legal Opinion`, verify `emergency_authorization` attribute; enforce 24h window via temporal profile | Activity-only models cannot express external authorization or time-bound exceptions | Policy-dependent; requires attributes not present in standard logs |
| R7 | ProcessTree / DECLARE | `precedence` on `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Structural prerequisite fits both control-flow and declarative layers | Redundant with ProcessTree concurrency block |
| R8 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence` on `(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` and `(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | Explicitly forbids co-occurrence of mutually exclusive risk routes | Overlaps with R2; declarative layer catches violations early |
| R9 | Resource/Data Layer | SoD checks: `resource(Verify Bank Account)  resource(Approval)`; `resource(Capture Master Data)  resource(Activate Vendor)` | Activity sequences cannot encode resource assignments; requires data-aware validation | Cannot be expressed in PM4Py declarative/temporal layers |
| R10 | Log Skeleton | `activ_freq` mapping `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, `Release First Payment` to `{0, 1}` | Log skeletons natively support occurrence-count constraints | Support/confidence metadata may mask rare multi-occurrence anomalies |
| R11 | ProcessTree / DECLARE | Post-activation `loop(Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor  Verify Bank Account  Release First Payment)` / `chainprecedence` | Structural post-activation flow; DECLARE captures ordering | ProcessTree is block-structured; post-activation loops are approximated |
| R12 | Temporal Profile | Mean/SD pairs for clarification, legal, and payment intervals | Temporal profiles are designed for SLA monitoring, not control flow | Warnings only; cannot trigger automatic case rejection |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

**1. Compact Formal Description**
Start  `Receive Onboarding Request`  Concurrent block: `Capture Master Data`  `Run Sanctions Screening`  `Verify Bank Account`  `Classify Risk`  Loop: `(Request Clarification  Receive Clarification)*`  Choice: `Reject Vendor`  `Manager Approval`  (`Enhanced Due Diligence`  `Compliance Approval`)  `Activate Vendor`  Loop: `(Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor  Verify Bank Account  Release First Payment)*`  `Close Onboarding`

**2. PM4Py Python Code**
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.process_tree_constructors import sequence, and_, or_, loop, activity

# Activity nodes
a_receive = activity("Receive Onboarding Request")
a_capture = activity("Capture Master Data")
a_screening = activity("Run Sanctions Screening")
a_verify = activity("Verify Bank Account")
a_classify = activity("Classify Risk")
a_request = activity("Request Clarification")
a_receive_clar = activity("Receive Clarification")
a_reject = activity("Reject Vendor")
a_mgr_approval = activity("Manager Approval")
a_edd = activity("Enhanced Due Diligence")
a_comp_approval = activity("Compliance Approval")
a_activate = activity("Activate Vendor")
a_release = activity("Release First Payment")
a_close = activity("Close Onboarding")
a_change_bank = activity("Change Bank Details")
a_suspend = activity("Suspend Vendor")

# Clarification loop
clar_loop = loop(sequence(a_request, a_receive_clar))

# Concurrency block
concurrent_checks = and_(a_capture, a_screening, a_verify)

# Choice block
low_risk = a_mgr_approval
high_risk = sequence(a_edd, a_comp_approval)
choice_block = or_(a_reject, low_risk, high_risk)

# Post-activation loop (approximation: block-structured loop after activation)
post_activation = loop(sequence(a_change_bank, a_suspend, a_verify, a_release))

# Full tree
procedural_model = sequence(
    a_receive,
    concurrent_checks,
    a_classify,
    clar_loop,
    choice_block,
    a_activate,
    post_activation,
    a_close
)
```

**3. Explanation of Modeling Choices**
- **Concurrency:** The `and_` operator captures the parallel execution of the three checks. PM4Py's ProcessTree treats this as a block-structured concurrency, which is sufficient for control-flow validation.
- **Choices:** The `or_` operator models the three mutually exclusive paths. `exactly_one` is enforced declaratively (R2) to handle case-type dependency.
- **Clarification Loop:** Placed after classification to match T2. The `loop` operator allows zero or more cycles. It does not structurally enforce "before final outcome" because ProcessTrees are static; this is delegated to DECLARE `chainresponse`.
- **Terminal Outcomes:** `Reject Vendor` and `Activate Vendor` lead to `Close Onboarding`. The tree assumes `Close Onboarding` follows either path.
- **Post-Activation Behavior:** R11 is approximated as a loop after `Activate Vendor`. ProcessTrees cannot express true post-termination branches, so this is a conservative structural approximation. The resource/data layer enforces the strict ordering and SoD constraints.

**Explanation of Overlaps & Disagreements**
- The log skeleton captures statistical/descriptive constraints rather than prescriptive rules. `always_before` and `never_together` overlap with DECLARE but are weaker (empirical vs normative).
- `directly_follows` assumes strict adjacency, which conflicts with the clarification loop and post-activation changes. It is intentionally conservative: it reflects the most common path but does not block loops.
- `activ_freq` enforces R10 empirically. If a case contains two activations, the skeleton flags it, but the DECLARE `noncoexistence` is authoritative.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),

    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600)
}
```

**Why Temporal Deviations Are Warnings**
Temporal expectations in vendor onboarding are driven by external factors (vendor response times, legal workload, banking processing delays). A `zeta = 2.0` threshold means deviations beyond `mean + 2*std` are flagged as warnings, not automatic control-flow violations. Hard control flow should be decoupled from time to avoid false rejections during peak loads or vendor delays. Temporal warnings trigger operational reviews, not case termination.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def check_segregation_of_duties(log_df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Check R9: Verify Bank Account resource != Approval resource; Capture != Activate."""
    # Extract relevant activities
    verify_df = log_df[log_df["activity"] == "Verify Bank Account"][["case_id", "resource"]]
    approval_df = log_df[log_df["activity"].isin(["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"])][["case_id", "resource"]]
    
    # Merge to find same-resource violations
    violations = pd.merge(verify_df, approval_df, on="case_id", suffixes=("_verify", "_approval"))
    sod_violations = violations[violations["resource_verify"] == violations["resource_approval"]]
    
    capture_df = log_df[log_df["activity"] == "Capture Master Data"][["case_id", "resource"]]
    activate_df = log_df[log_df["activity"] == "Activate Vendor"][["case_id", "resource"]]
    act_violations = pd.merge(capture_df, activate_df, on="case_id", suffixes=("_capture", "_activate"))
    act_sod_violations = act_violations[act_violations["resource_capture"] == act_violations["resource_activate"]]
    
    return pd.concat([sod_violations, act_sod_violations])

def check_emergency_policy(log_df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Check R6: If Activate Vendor < Obtain Legal Opinion, verify emergency_authorization attribute."""
    activate_df = log_df[log_df["activity"] == "Activate Vendor"][["case_id", "timestamp"]]
    legal_df = log_df[log_df["activity"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"][["case_id", "timestamp"]]
    
    merged = pd.merge(activate_df, legal_df, on="case_id", suffixes=("_activate", "_legal"))
    early_activation = merged[merged["timestamp_activate"] < merged["timestamp_legal"]]
    
    # Check for external authorization attribute
    if "emergency_authorization" not in log_df.columns:
        early_activation["status"] = "missing_attribute_flagged"
    else:
        auth_df = log_df[log_df["emergency_authorization"] == True][["case_id"]]
        valid_cases = set(auth_df["case_id"])
        early_activation["status"] = early_activation["case_id"].apply(lambda x: "policy_compliant" if x in valid_cases else "policy_violation")
        
    return early_activation[early_activation["status"] != "policy_compliant"]

# Usage example:
# resource_violations = check_segregation_of_duties(event_log_df)
# emergency_flags = check_emergency_policy(event_log_df)
```

**Required Attributes & Fallbacks**
- `resource`: Present in standard logs. If missing, SoD checks cannot run; fallback is to flag the case for manual review.
- `emergency_authorization`: External attribute. If missing, the system assumes strict R5 enforcement and flags the case as `policy_violation` or `warning` depending on governance policy.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated Enforcement:** R1 and R5 appear in both DECLARE and Log Skeleton. This creates redundant checks but improves fault tolerance. However, it may surface conflicting diagnostics if one layer uses empirical thresholds and the other uses normative rules.
- **Inconsistency Under Loops:** R3's clarification loop interacts with R5 if sanctions escalation occurs during a clarification cycle. The ProcessTree places the loop before the choice block, but DECLARE's `chainresponse` doesn't block escalation mid-loop. This creates a blind spot where escalation could legally precede clarification responses.
- **Vacuity:** `exactly_one` on approvals (R2) is partially vacuous because R8's `noncoexistence` rules already prevent co-occurrence, and the process tree enforces mutual exclusivity. The `exactly_one` rule mainly catches rejected cases where neither occurs, but DECLARE lacks case-type filtering.
- **Missing Case Attributes:** No risk score, vendor tier, or emergency authorization flag in the log. This forces reliance on temporal/resource proxies, increasing false positives/negatives.
- **Timestamp-Only Limitations:** The temporal profile ignores weekends, holidays, and banking cut-off times. A 48-hour mean may be 2 business days, but the profile treats it as calendar time, potentially flagging legitimate delays.
- **Support/Confidence as Normative Truth:** High confidence values (1.0) imply normative strength, but in PM4Py they are empirical frequencies. Treating them as absolute policy can mask rare but critical violations or over-penalize legitimate outliers.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

- **Canonical Procedural Representation:** ProcessTree for the control-flow backbone (concurrency, choices, loops). It provides a clear, executable structure that aligns with BPMN standards and supports automated simulation.
- **Authoritative Policy Layer:** DECLARE combined with the Resource/Data validation layer. DECLARE handles declarative constraints (ordering, exclusion, response), while the resource layer enforces SoD and emergency policy exceptions. This separation ensures control flow and data-aware rules are validated independently.
- **Check Execution Order:** 
  1. Control-flow (ProcessTree)  catches structural impossibilities.
  2. Declarative (DECLARE)  catches ordering/exclusion violations.
  3. Resource/Data  catches SoD and policy exceptions.
  4. Temporal Profile  generates warnings for SLA breaches.
- **Surfacing Contradictions:** Implement a hierarchical dashboard. Hard fails (Procedural/DECLARE/Resource) block case progression and require compliance officer approval. Warnings (Temporal/Log Skeleton) are logged for audit but don't block flow. If R5/R6 conflict arises, the system flags the case as `policy-dependent` and routes it to a manual review queue with both interpretations displayed. Support/confidence metadata should be logged separately from normative rules to prevent empirical frequencies from overriding explicit policy.
