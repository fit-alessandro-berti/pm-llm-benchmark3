# Redesign of the Diagnostic-Lab Sample Process for Disruption-Robust Operation

All quantitative claims below are derived from the supplied DFGs, queue metrics, performance edges, and variant statistics. Because the normal window (8 weeks) and peak window (3 weeks) differ, flow quantities are normalized to **per-week rates** before comparison.

---

## 1. Stress scenarios

| # | Scenario | Demand profile | Disruption type | Primary metric at risk |
|---|----------|----------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| S1 | **Winter wave mix-shift** (observed peak, +10% severity) | Volume +43% (2,3003,300/wk); Urgent +61%/wk; Complex-panel +121%/wk (299660/wk) | Demand *mix* shift, not just volume | Urgent 18h contractual penalty (peak p90 already 33h); Routine 72h target (peak p90 120h) |
| S2 | **Assay-machine degradation / M2 outage** | Moderate-high volume, normal mix | Equipment: M2 soft-failure or 48–72h outage. M2 has faster runtime but higher failure rate; repeats cluster after night shifts and M2 usage | All cycle times; repeat rate spirals above the peak baseline of 14.8% |
| S3 | **Validator / specialist absence** | Normal-to-elevated volume, Complex share 18–20% | Staffing: one medical validator absent for a week, overlapping the recurring second-Friday specialist-review absence | Med-validation queue (22h at peak, 93% util  >110% effective); Hold-for-specialist queue (36h median, 48h holdmed-val edge) |
| S4 | **Courier arrival compression** | Normal volume, distorted arrival curve | Logistics: the 10:00 and 17:00 waves merge or a delayed convoy dumps simultaneously (+30–50% tubes/hour at intake); samples age in transit | Registration/pre-analytical surge; urgent contractual clock burned *before* the lab touches the sample |
| S5 | **Compound tail (S1+S2+S3 overlap)** | Wave-week demand | Machine failure + absence during peak | System gridlock; defines the "survival mode" the design must support |

S1 matters because the peak log shows the stress is **structural, not volumetric**: complex-panel demand more than doubles per week, and complex cases are 3.1× more likely to need medical validation and 2.4× more likely to enter specialist hold. S4 matters because for urgent cases the 18-hour clock includes courier time — no internal speedup can recover it.

## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

**Normalized degradation signals (normal  peak):**

| Signal | Normal | Peak | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-analytical first-pass yield | 93.0% (16,860/18,120) | 87.7% (8,530/9,730) | Quality collapses first under load |
| Recollectioncancel rate | 34.9% (290/830) | 41.6% (370/890) | Loops stop recovering cases |
| Batch-wait incidence (share of prep exits) | 4.4% | 12.7% (393/wk vs 95/wk) | Scheduling discipline breaks |
| Repeat-assay rate (share of assay exits) | 6.7% | 14.8% (420/wk vs 143/wk) | Assay effectively loses ~1 slot in 7 |
| Med-validation share of tech-val exits | 24.5% | 42.3% (3,420/8,080) | Validation mix shifts to the scarce resource |
| Hold-for-specialist incidence | 1.8% (36/wk) | 7.6% (203/wk, 5.6×) | Specialist becomes a serial blocker |
| Urgent bypass of Batch wait | 74% | 58% | Priority rules erode exactly when needed most |

### S1 — Wave mix-shift
Migration chain: **Pre-analytical check (96% util, 9.5h queue)**  **Accession correction (2 specialists, 91%, 14h)**  **Prep batching (batch-wait 4.1×/wk)**  **Assay (98%, 18h queue, amplified by 14.8% repeats)**  **Technical validation (101% — structural deficit, queue grows unboundedly)**  **Medical validation (42.3% of tech-val exits, 22h queue)**  **Specialist hold (48h edge into med-val)**.
Relief traps: I6 (early triage) delivers *cleaner but faster* flow into prep/assay — the assay queue binds earlier unless WIP is capped. I4 (pulling tech validators to accession correction) strips the only >100% station. Strict I1 starves Routine/Complex and inflates WIP; blanket I2 (size-24 batches) collides with the already-eroding urgent bypass.

### S2 — M2 degradation/outage
The constraint snaps to **assay** immediately. Upstream, queues spill into Batch wait and prep within hours (Little's law: 18h assay queue  batch-wait incidence rises further); downstream, tech-val arrivals become bursty after repair. Each repeat costs a *second full run* plus a 26h loop (Repeat assayAssay run), so a degrading M2 acts as a capacity multiplier loss, not a linear one.
Relief traps: dumping all Routine volume on M2 (naive I3) raises repeats (variant evidence: repeats cluster on M2), which reloads assay *and* med-validation. Overtime at prep builds upstream WIP that arrives as a post-repair tsunami. Dedicate M1 to Urgent+Complex and its schedule fragments (urgent bypass), shrinking effective M1 capacity precisely when it is the only healthy machine.

### S3 — Validator absence
Med-validation is the constraint; because tech-val feeds it (8h24h edge) and tech-val is already at 101%, the two stations **couple into gridlock**: tech-val queue swells, med-val starves then floods. Holds pile up behind the absent specialist (HoldMed-val = 48h at peak), and each held case then competes for the remaining med-validator. Critically, **Urgent and Complex-panel cases both demand the same scarce validator and cannot both be prioritized** — any policy must explicitly rank them or split the lane.
Relief traps: an evening med-val shift (I5) idles if tech-val doesn't feed it; borrowing tech validators for med-val empties tech-val; I10 relieves complex-panel specialist load but injects a 12h transport leg and a new reconciliation queue at release.

### S4 — Courier compression
The spike hits **registration** (85% util; error rate rises after 9h shifts)  errors propagate to **accession correction** (2 specialists)  aged samples raise **pre-analytical rejects**  **recollection loops** (60h round trip) that additionally consume clerk capacity explaining errors to physicians (variant note)  mid-day trough, then a second collision at the 17:00 wave. Lumpy arrivals also degrade batch decisions at prep.
Relief traps: I8 (auto-registration) shifts defects downstream to the 2-person accession team; I7 (smoothing) moves load *earlier* into prep/assay — beneficial only if batch plans are adjusted, otherwise the morning glut simply relocates.

### S5 — Compound
Constraints stack: assay + med-val + specialist simultaneously. No sequencing of local fixes works; the process must drop to an explicit **degradation ladder**: (1) protect the urgent 18h contract, (2) formally defer Routine with communicated new targets (72h target gives slack), (3) evacuate Complex panels to the partner lab, (4) manual workarounds and daily executive triage.

## 3. Robust redesign package

Design principle: **buy robustness with quality-at-source, state-dependent control, and decoupling points — not uniform capacity.**

**B1. Clean-intake bundle (I6 + gated I8).** Early pre-analytical defect triage rejecting poor samples within 2h; automated registration only for orders passing hard metadata validation at entry.
*Why robust:* attacks the largest multiplicative waste term — pre-check FY fell 93.0%87.7%, each failure costing a 60h recollection loop, clerk time, and a 3542% cancel rate. Fewer bad samples also mean fewer ambiguous results, fewer repeats, and less load on the 101%-utilized tech-val station.
*Downstream overload:* cleaner flow reaches prep/assay faster — must be paired with B2's WIP caps or the assay queue binds earlier. Auto-registration shifts residual errors to accession correction (2 specialists) — hence validation rules at entry, not just capture.
*Assumptions/failure:* dominant defects are detectable in 2h (hemolysis, labeling); fails for assay-visible defects, and an understaffed triage desk becomes the new bottleneck.

**B2. Adaptive priority-and-batch governor (I1/I2) with WIP caps.** Urgent-first dispatch **with protected Routine reserve slots** (e.g., one routine batch slot per shift per bench, minimum routine share on machines); batch size state-dependent (12 when WIP high, up to 24 when queues short); urgent mini-batches 6 when the urgent queue is long; hard WIP caps per station; when caps go red, **throttle demand-side** (defer elective routine collections, communicate revised targets).
*Why robust:* pure I1 starves Routine/Complex and explodes WIP; pure I2 delays Urgent. A governed hybrid preserves the urgent 18h budget while bounding Routine p90, and WIP caps bound queues in *every* scenario. This encodes the fact that **not all case types can be prioritized simultaneously** — the reserve is the explicit arbitration.
*Downstream overload:* caps push waiting upstream to intake/couriers — requires physician communication or waiting migrates to an invisible external queue (see §6).
*Fails when:* urgent share exceeds ~35% (reserve too small) or telemetry is stale.

**B3. Health-gated machine dedication (I3).** Default mixed routing; under stress, M1 dedicated to Urgent+Complex, Routine to M2 **only while M2's health index is clean** (repeat-share by machine, failure events, time since maintenance); maintenance windows placed after the 17:00 wave; mandatory second-check on M2 night runs to attack the observed night-shift/M2 repeat clustering.
*Downstream overload:* Routine-on-M2 raises repeats  med-val load; M1 dedication fragments M1's schedule and concentrates all critical work on one asset (accepted, monitored risk).

**B4. Conditional float pool (revised I4).** Cross-train, but deploy by a queue-state matrix: accession-correction relief only when the accession queue exceeds threshold **and** tech-val utilization is <90%. Additionally train prep-bench staff toward tech validation (notes say 4-week cross-training is feasible) because tech-val — not accession — is the structurally deficient station at peak (101%).
*Downstream overload:* unconditional I4 would deepen the tech-val deficit; the matrix prevents robbing the constrained station to feed a secondary one.

**B5. Arrival-shaped staffing + triggered evening med-val shift (I7 + I5).** Stagger registration/pre-analytical shifts onto the 10:00/17:00 waves; hard-cap overtime at 9h (error-rate cliff); evening med-validation shift **activated by trigger and paired with a tech-val expedite rule**, because the tech-valmed-val edge stretches to 24h at peak — an unfed evening shift is idle cost.
*Downstream overload:* pulling cases forward to release exposes any weakness at release/reporting; smoothing arrivals front-loads prep/assay earlier in the day.

**B6. Complex-panel overflow standby (I10), pre-contracted.** Partner lab takes *Complex-panel only, never Urgent* (12h transport is intolerable inside an 18h budget but marginal against a 210h complex p90). Requires chain-of-custody protocol and unified final-report assembly so results don't fragment.
*Downstream overload:* transfers load to the partner and creates a reconciliation/traceability workload at release; must be rehearsed, not improvised.

**Explicitly rejected or restricted:** strict I1 (starvation + WIP explosion); blanket I2 (delays Urgent; the 74%58% bypass erosion shows priority discipline already fails under load); I9 (holding Routine releases for batch reports adds waiting to a class whose p90 headroom under the 72h target is already thin — permissible only for non-time-critical reportable panels).

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

1. **M1 concentration fragility (caused by the redesign).** B3 deliberately loads all Urgent+Complex work onto one machine during stress. An M1 failure in P2/P5 mode is strictly worse than today's balanced two-machine load. Mitigation is detection speed and the partner channel — not elimination.
2. **Hidden external queue (caused by the redesign).** B2's WIP caps and demand throttling push waiting upstream to collection sites and couriers, outside log visibility. The process will *look* in-control while patients wait unmeasured. Requires explicit deferral communication and an external-wait register.
3. **Triage over-rejection (caused by the redesign).** Under B1, a pressured triage desk may over-reject borderline samples, converting one rework type (late recollection) into another (unnecessary recollection) and irritating physicians — watch the recollection-request rate after rollout.
4. **Governor oscillation/gaming.** State-dependent priorities can thrash (urgent mini-batch  routine reserve flipping) or be gamed by staff relabeling cases as urgent. Needs hysteresis in triggers and audit of urgent-label accuracy.
5. **Key-person risk persists.** One specialist reviewer with a recurring second-Friday absence remains a single point of failure; B6 reduces frequency of exposure, not the structural dependency.
6. **Partner dependency.** Outsourced complex panels introduce external TAT variance, chain-of-custody failures, and result-reconciliation errors — a new rework category at release.
7. **Tube-level blindness.** The log is flattened by order; batching and dedication decisions act on *tubes*. Order-level KPIs can mask tube-level starvation of complex panels (multiple tubes must co-arrive), so variant-level tracking is required.
