## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The operational benefit of meeting P-884’s deadline is bounded: **€20k–€35k** in avoided operational loss. The direct customer harm from a false rejection or prolonged hold is broader: **€15k–€80k**. The potential regulatory/compliance harm from an unlawful execution is highly asymmetric: **€100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences** such as audit findings, remediation, reputational damage, and future supervisory scrutiny.

This asymmetry creates a dominance argument:

- **Actions A and B are dominated by conformant alternatives** even if they appear to reduce SLA-breach risk. A favorable forecast of deadline preservation cannot compensate for a mandatory-control breach. If the probability of an unlawful execution were even moderately material, the expected regulatory harm could exceed the entire deadline benefit. Given the current risk score of 72, the unresolved fuzzy match, and the prediction that ultimate approval after conformant review is only **0.55–0.75**, there is a nontrivial chance that the payment should not be executed. Therefore, immediate execution or self-certification should not be recommended.

- **Action C** preserves the mandatory control path and gives the best immediate chance of a conformant decision before the 90-minute deadline. Its expected benefit is the deadline benefit multiplied by the probability of timely independent approval, but it carries a real spillover cost to P-901. The spillover is not a compliance violation for P-884, but it is an operational decision cost that must be considered.

- **Action D** can reduce uncertainty and may avoid the P-901 interruption if S4 becomes available quickly. However, it is timing-risky. If S4 is available near 35 minutes and the second review can be expedited, Action D becomes very attractive. If S4 is available near 65 minutes, Action D likely misses the deadline unless S2 is used, in which case the P-901 spillover returns.

- **Action F** is conservative. It avoids the tail regulatory harm, but it may impose a large false-rejection cost and is only conditionally conformant. It should be used when the documented evidence threshold for rejection is clearly satisfied, or when no conformant review can be completed before the deadline and the organization prefers to avoid unresolved sanctions risk.

- **Action E** is process-conformant but operationally weak for the current deadline. It is appropriate when evidence is likely to resolve the fuzzy match and the case can tolerate a multi-hour delay. For the 90-minute deadline, it is inferior to a timely second-review path, but superior to nonconformant execution.

- **Action G** should not be treated as a primary operational plan because the exception is not currently enabled. Waiting for an override can consume the remaining time and crowd out the conformant second-review options. It becomes relevant only if a genuine incident identifier and Compliance Director authorization can actually be obtained.

Because the predictions are observational and may not transport unchanged under intervention, the ranking favors robust, control-preserving actions rather than shortcuts that depend on optimistic counterfactual behavior.
