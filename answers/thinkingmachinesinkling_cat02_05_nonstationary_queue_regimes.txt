**4. Peak-share composition is masked.**
- Pooled `peak_08_10_pct` mean  35%.
- R1:  29%; R2:  18%; R4:  45%; R5:  33%.
A single “morning shift” staffing rule based on 35% will over-allocate to weekends and under-allocate to R4 weekdays.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Representative hourly arrival profiles (Fano factor / index of dispersion).**
Using the four supplied hourly arrival vectors:

- **04-07 (R1 smooth weekday):** Sum = 93 over 12 bins; mean  7.75/h; sample variance  27.6; **Fano factor  3.6** (>1 indicates overdispersion relative to Poisson).
- **04-15 (R3 outage/recovery):** Sum = 105; mean  8.75/h; variance  36; **Fano  4.1**. Burstiness remains even though service is interrupted.
- **04-21 (R4 bursty post-change):** Sum = 115; mean  9.6/h; variance is large due to peaks at 10–11 (34 arrivals) and 13–14 (24); **Fano  6.2+**.
- **04-25 (R2 smooth weekend):** Sum = 51; mean  4.25/h; variance  5.0; **Fano  1.2** (closest to regular).

**Interpretation.** The arrival process is not homogeneous Poisson. It ranges from near-regular (weekend baseline) to highly overdispersed and bursty (high-complex weekdays). A single exponential inter-arrival model is inappropriate.

**Coefficient of variation (CV) by regime (daily arrivals).**
- R1: std  5.3 / mean  100  **CV  0.05**.
- R2: std  5.2 / mean  42  **CV  0.12**.
- R4 (all days): std  27 / mean  99  **CV  0.27** (weekend inclusion dominates).
- R5: std  32 / mean  89  **CV  0.36** (small sample, weekend/weekday mix).

The pooled 30-day CV is  0.38, driven mainly by the weekend/weekday and pre/post-change splits, not by random noise within regimes.

**Serial dependence.**
Daily arrival sequence (30 points) shows positive autocorrelation at lag-1 ( +0.45 to +0.55, approximate) because regimes persist: high-complex weekdays cluster; weekends cluster; recovery days cluster. `mean_wip` also shows strong persistence (lag-1  +0.70+), consistent with backlog carry-over: a high-WIP day (e.g., 04-15 at 47) predicts elevated WIP the next day (52 on 04-16, then 35 on 04-17).

**Change-point evidence.**
- **Case mix:** `complex_pct` is stable 27 through 04-19, then jumps to 41 on 04-20 and stays 39 thereafter. A threshold-based heuristic (e.g., split at the largest mean-difference) places the change point between 04-19 and 04-20.
- **Capacity:** Staff steps visibly at 04-29 (6.0  7.0 weekdays; 2.5  3.0 weekends), though 04-16 also shows an anomalous 7.0.
- **Service:** Median service rises gradually in R1–R3, then steps up ~9 minutes at 04-20 and remains elevated.

**Limitations of four representative days.**
- They are selected, not randomly drawn.
- They miss the intervention profile (no 04-29/04-30 vector) and the recovery profile (04-17).
- They provide no overnight dynamics (19:00–07:00).
- With only four vectors, we cannot estimate seasonal patterns, weekday effects within regimes, or the full distribution of hourly shapes. They establish that burstiness *exists*, not that it follows a specific parametric form.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law: `L = W` (mean WIP = throughput rate × mean flow time). For this table, `L` = mean `mean_wip` (count). `` is approximated by mean `arrivals` / 720 min (arrivals/min) or mean `completed` / 720 min; where they diverge, both are shown. `W` is computed in minutes (`L / `). Boundary conditions assume opening-hour observation only; overnight carry-over and unknown 07:00 backlog violate strict steady-state.

| Window / Regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy (`arrivals` vs `completed`) | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow time (`W` in min)* | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 Baseline weekday** (04-06..04-14, Mon–Fri) | Moderate: unknown 07:00 backlog; overnight closure 12 h | Close agreement: mean arrivals  100, completed  100; divergence < 2/day; proxy robust | Comparable within regime; snapshots uniform | Low–moderate: completions  arrivals, suggesting limited accumulation | Approximate: slight upward drift in WIP (1823) but small relative to mean | Arrivals:  146 min; Completed:  147 min; service median  25 min, so implied wait  120 min | **Approximate only** |

| **R3 Outage & recovery** (04-15..04-17) | **Unstable**: 180-min outage breaks service continuity; 07:00 backlog unknown and likely elevated | **Divergent**: 04-15 (105 vs 58); 04-16 (112 vs 129); 04-17 (99 vs 112); no single  | Not comparable: WIP 47  52  35 in 3 days | Very high: backlog builds during outage and is cleared over >1 day | No: throughput, staff, and WIP all change rapidly | Not calculated: divergence > 40% between proxies; WIP non-stationary | **Not defensible** |

*\*Calculation: `W = mean_WIP / (mean_arrivals / 720)` or with `completed`. Example R1: 20.3 / (100.1/720)  146 min. I present both proxies where divergence exceeds ~5%.*

**Key caution.** Even in R1, Little’s Law requires that arrivals and departures occur within a stable system with negligible boundary effects. The 12-hour closure, unknown 07:00 backlog, and the fact that cases can carry overnight mean that `W` is an **implied average flow time for cases that both arrive and depart during opening hours**, not a universal customer flow time.

# 7. Operational implications

**Regime-aware staffing, not pooled averages.**
- **Baseline weekdays (R1):** ~6.0 FTE is adequate for ~100 arrivals/day with ~25 min median service. The 08–10 peak holds ~30% of arrivals. Shift schedules should front-load capacity before 09:00, not spread evenly.
- **Baseline weekends (R2):** ~2.5 FTE handles ~42 arrivals/day. Do not apply weekday ratios.
- **Outage/recovery (R3):** The 04-15 outage (180 min, roughly 11:00–14:00) shows that service interruption creates a multi-day recovery, not a one-day spike. On 04-16, staff was raised to 7.0 FTE, yet WIP peaked at 52 (higher than 04-15’s 47). Recovery required sustained elevation into 04-17 (WIP 35, staff 6.5). **Policy:** Define a recovery trigger (e.g., WIP > 30 for two consecutive days) and maintain elevated staff for 48 hours, not a single shift.

**Case-mix and service-time response (R4  R5).**
- The shift to `complex_pct`  42–47 increases median service from ~25 min to ~35 min (+40%). At constant staff (6.0 FTE), this is roughly equivalent to a 40% load increase. R4 weekdays show rising WIP (37  46) and a throughput gap (arrivals > completions), confirming a capacity deficit.
- The 04-29 intervention raises staff to 7.0 FTE (weekdays) and 3.0 FTE (weekends). WIP then falls (43  36  32 on weekdays; 22  19 on weekend). However, `complex_pct` remains high (~42). **Policy:** Monitor whether service times fall because of staff or because of case selection; if staff alone explains the 04-29 improvement, maintain 7.0 FTE through the high-complex period.

**Peak smoothing and hourly scheduling.**
- The 08–10 peak share ranges from ~18% (weekend) to ~50% (04-27). The representative 04-21 profile shows extreme intra-hour concentration (34 arrivals at 10–11, another 24 at 13–14). **Policy:** Do not staff to daily averages; staff to hourly arrival shapes. Consider appointment-slot smoothing for the 08–10 window, especially in R4/R5.

**Data-collection recommendations.**
1. **Event-level log:** Capture `case_id`, `arrival_timestamp`, `start_service`, `end_service`, `variant` (complex/simple), and `staff_id` for every case.
2. **Backlog at 07:00:** Record WIP at opening time separately from intra-day snapshots.
3. **Intervention timestamp:** Log exact start/end of any staffing change (the 04-29 note is too vague).
4. **Variant audit:** Investigate whether the `complex_pct` jump at 04-20 reflects a true process change or a coding/reclassification change; without this, capacity forecasts for R4/R5 are built on ambiguous demand.
5. **Overnight tracking:** Measure cases remaining at 19:00 and arriving before 07:00 to close the carry-over boundary.

**A pooled-policy risk.**
If management computes required staff from pooled statistics (~84 arrivals/day, service ~30 min, 720 min, 5.7 FTE), they might conclude the system is balanced. Under R4 weekdays, however, demand is ~112 arrivals/day with ~35 min service, requiring roughly 7.6 FTE for equivalent utilization—yet only 6.0 FTE is present. A pooled “balanced” forecast hides a ~20% capacity deficit in the dominant high-complex regime, leading to increasing backlog (as observed: WIP 37  46). The risk is greatest exactly when the pooled average looks calm.
