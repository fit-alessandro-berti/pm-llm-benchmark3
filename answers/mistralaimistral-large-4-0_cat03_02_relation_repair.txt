## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the domain semantics as defeasible, not as axioms. The repair is abductive: I look for the most plausible completion of missing relations while preserving uncertainty.

### Hard invariants

- **H1 — Referential integrity.** Every E2O relation must reference an existing `ocel:eid` and `ocel:oid`. Every O2O relation must reference two existing object IDs.
- **H2 — No duplicate identical relations.** Adding the same E2O or O2O triple twice is a data-quality error, not a repair.
- **H3 — Directionality of O2O qualifiers.** `DEL-X carries PK-7` does not imply `PK-7 carries DEL-X`. I do not add reverse edges.
- **H4 — No invented events or objects.** Only relations among the given events and objects may be added.
- **H5 — Fixed event timestamps.** Repairs cannot change event timestamps or activity labels.

### Strong defaults

- **S1 — `Create Order`** normally relates to exactly one Order.
- **S2 — `Add Item`** normally relates to one Order and one Item.
- **S3 — `Pack Item`** normally relates to one Item and one Package, but a scanner outage can leave an item-only row.
- **S4 — `Dispatch Package`** normally relates to one Package and one Delivery, but bulk dispatch may cover multiple packages.
- **S5 — `Confirm Delivery`** may refer to one delivery, several deliveries acknowledged together, or only a customer/order when the portal loses the carrier identifier.
- **S6 — `Issue Invoice`** relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders.
- **S7 — `Record Payment`** relates to exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice; unapplied cash is possible.

### Weak heuristics

- **W1 — Identifier similarity.** `external_ref=N-421?` resembles `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421`; `scanner_ref=PK8-2` resembles `PK-8`; `order_hint` and `route_hint` are contextual.
- **W2 — Timestamp proximity and lifecycle order.** Packing should precede dispatch; dispatch should precede delivery confirmation; invoicing/payment should follow order creation.
- **W3 — Amount equality.** Invoice totals and payment amounts are suggestive but not probative because of fees, discounts, taxes, partial payments, and unapplied cash.
- **W4 — Customer equality.** `customer=C17` links orders, invoice, and portal confirmation.
- **W5 — Route equality.** `route_hint=NORTH` and `route=NORTH` link packages and deliveries.
- **W6 — Existing O2O structure.** `DEL-X carries PK-7` and `PAY-1 settles INV-44` provide patterns, but they are not proof for new edges.

### Evidence against a repair

- **A1 — `PK-8` already packs `IT-A2`.** Adding `IT-B1` to `PK-8` creates a one-to-many package-item relation. That is possible, but it is not free.
- **A2 — `DEL-X` already carries `PK-7`.** Adding `DEL-X` to `ev10` would conflict with the existing dispatch pairing.
- **A3 — `INV-44.total=230` vs `ORD-A.declared_value=130`.** If `INV-44` bills only `ORD-A`, the mismatch needs explanation. If it bills both orders, the sum matches exactly. Both are possible.
- **A4 — `PAY-2.amount=130`** equals `ORD-A.declared_value` and also equals `INV-44.total - PAY-1.amount`. This is suggestive but not decisive.
- **A5 — `ev11.external_ref=N-42*`** is a wildcard-like value that could match `N-420` or `N-421`. The event already relates only to `ORD-A`.
- **A6 — `ev10.external_ref=N-421?`** contains an explicit question mark, so identifier matching is weaker than it first appears.

### How evidence is combined

Hard invariants act as filters. Strong defaults provide the prior shape of a repair. Weak heuristics raise or lower confidence. Identifier similarity plus lifecycle order plus an existing O2O pattern can justify a provisional edge. Amount equality alone is never enough. Customer and route are contextual support.

I explicitly avoid circular reasoning: if I add `ev10 -> DEL-Y`, I cannot then treat the derived O2O edge `DEL-Y carries PK-8` as independent confirmation of `ev10 -> DEL-Y`. The O2O edge is a consistency consequence, not a second witness.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A adds only **R1** and **R2**.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** leave unresolved. `ev08` remains an item-only `Pack Item` row for `IT-B1`. I do not add `ev08 -> PK-8` or `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. The scanner-outage explanation is acceptable, and `scanner_ref=PK8-2` is too weak because `PK-8` already packs `IT-A2`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add provisional `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. This gives `PK-8` a delivery. `DEL-X` remains paired only with `PK-7`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** leave unresolved. `ev11` remains an order-level confirmation for `ORD-A`. I do not assign it uniquely to `DEL-X` or `DEL-Y` because `N-42*` is ambiguous and `ORD-A` may span two deliveries.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** leave unresolved. `INV-44` remains billing `ORD-A` only. The total mismatch is explained by fees, taxes, discounts, or data quality, not by forced arithmetic.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** leave unresolved. `PAY-2` remains unapplied cash.

This bundle yields a coherent but incomplete lifecycle: `ORD-A` is mostly complete; `ORD-B` stops after `Add Item`; `IT-B1` is an orphan after `Add Item`; `PAY-2` is an orphan payment; `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` lack delivery confirmation. That is acceptable.

### Bundle B — plausible alternative interpretation

Bundle B adds **R1–R10** as provisional edges.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. This reconciles the scanner-outage row. `PK-8` then packs both `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`. The one-to-many package-item relation is plausible, though it should be flagged.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, as in Bundle A.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** add `ev11 -> DEL-X` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y`. This is not a unique assignment. The rationale is that `ORD-A` has items in both `PK-7` and `PK-8`, and `N-42*` could match both `N-420` and `N-421`. Rule 5 allows several deliveries to be acknowledged together. This is more coherent than assigning `ev11` to only one delivery, but it is still provisional.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** add `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The invoice total 230 equals the sum of the two declared order values, and the customer matches. This makes `INV-44` a consolidated invoice for both orders.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** add `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. The payment amount 130 equals the remaining invoice balance after `PAY-1`.

Bundle B produces a nearly complete happy-path lifecycle: both orders are created, their items are added and packed, packages are dispatched, `ORD-A` delivery is confirmed for both deliveries, both orders are invoiced together, and the invoice is paid by two payments. However, it relies on several weak inferences. `ORD-B` still lacks its own `Confirm Delivery` event because `ev11` relates only to `ORD-A`.

### Bundle C — delivery-complete but invoice-conservative

Bundle C adds **R1–R6** and **R9–R10**, but leaves **R7–R8** unresolved.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** add `ev11 -> DEL-X` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y`.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** leave unresolved; `INV-44` bills only `ORD-A`.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** add `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`.

This bundle shows that delivery repair and invoice repair are separable. It completes the delivery/payment side while preserving uncertainty about whether `INV-44` covers `ORD-B`.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

The selected repair is **Bundle A**.

### Order

- **ORD-A** has a plausible lifecycle: `Create Order`  `Add Item` twice  items packed into `PK-7` and `PK-8`  `PK-7` dispatched via `DEL-X`  `PK-8` dispatched via provisional `DEL-Y`  order-level `Confirm Delivery`  `Issue Invoice`  partial `Record Payment`.
- **ORD-B** is an orphan after `Add Item`. It has no pack, dispatch, delivery, invoice, or payment. This is acceptable; the log does not force every order to be complete.
- **Suspicious relation:** `INV-44 bills ORD-A` with `INV-44.total=230` vs `ORD-A.declared_value=130`. This is not repaired in Bundle A. It may be fees/taxes/discounts or a data-quality issue.
- **No circular reasoning:** I do not use `INV-44 bills ORD-A` to prove `ev12 -> ORD-A`; that edge is already observed.

### Item

- **IT-A1** and **IT-A2** have complete lifecycles: added to `ORD-A`, packed into `PK-7` and `PK-8`, respectively.
- **IT-B1** is an orphan after `Add Item`. `ev08` is an item-only `Pack Item` row. In Bundle A, I leave it unresolved.
- **Suspicious relation:** `ev08` relates only to `IT-B1`. This is explicitly allowed by the scanner-outage rule, so it is not an error.

### Package

- **PK-7** has a complete lifecycle: packs `IT-A1`, dispatched by `ev09`, carried by `DEL-X`.
- **PK-8** has a plausible lifecycle: packs `IT-A2`, dispatched by `ev10`, provisionally carried by `DEL-Y`.
- **Suspicious relation:** In Bundle B, `PK-8` would pack both `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`. That is a one-to-many package-item relation. It is plausible, but it should be monitored because the domain rule says `Pack Item` normally relates one Item and one Package.

### Delivery

- **DEL-X** is dispatched with `PK-7` but has no `Confirm Delivery` link. In Bundle A, `ev11` is not assigned to `DEL-X`.
- **DEL-Y** is provisionally dispatched with `PK-8` but also has no `Confirm Delivery` link.
- **Orphan objects:** both deliveries lack confirmation. This is acceptable because `ev11` may be order-level only.
- **Suspicious relation:** `ev11` relates only to `ORD-A`. The wildcard `N-42*` makes delivery-level assignment ambiguous.

### Invoice

- **INV-44** is issued for `ORD-A` and partially paid by `PAY-1`. `PAY-2` is unresolved.
- **Suspicious relation:** `INV-44.total=230` vs `ORD-A.declared_value=130`. In Bundle A, this mismatch remains. It is not repaired by arithmetic alone.

### Payment

- **PAY-1** has a complete lifecycle: recorded and settles `INV-44`.
- **PAY-2** is an orphan/unapplied payment. Its amount 130 matches `ORD-A.declared_value` and the invoice remainder, but that is weak evidence.

### Events

- **ev08** is incomplete: `Pack Item` with only an item.
- **ev11** is ambiguous: `Confirm Delivery` with only an order.
- **ev14** is incomplete: `Record Payment` with only a payment.
- No event is completely orphaned, because every event relates to at least one object.

### Independent E2O and O2O support

- `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is **not** independent evidence for `ev10 -> DEL-Y`. It is derived from the proposed E2O edge and the existing `DEL-X carries PK-7` pattern. Treating it as independent confirmation would be circular.
- In Bundle B, `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is derived from `ev08 -> PK-8`; `INV-44 bills ORD-B` is derived from `ev12 -> ORD-B`; `PAY-2 settles INV-44` is derived from `ev14 -> INV-44`. None of these O2O edges is an independent witness.
- The existing observed O2O edges, such as `ORD-A contains IT-A1` and `PAY-1 settles INV-44`, are independent observations, but they do not directly confirm the new edges.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **False package-item completion from `scanner_ref=PK8-2`.** Adding `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` may invent a package assignment. If `PK8-2` is only a scanner device ID, this creates a false `Pack Item` relation and a false `Pack Item  Pack Item` self-loop on the Package flattening.

2. **False delivery assignment from `N-421?`.** Adding `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` relies on a questionable identifier match. If the external reference is corrupted or the dispatch was bulk/unrecorded, this creates a false delivery lifecycle for `PK-8` and a false `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` path.

3. **False carrier-level confirmation from `N-42*`.** Adding `ev11 -> DEL-X` or `ev11 -> DEL-Y` turns an order-level portal confirmation into a carrier-level delivery confirmation. If the portal lost the carrier identifier, this fabricates delivery evidence and adds a new `Confirm Delivery  Delivery` edge in the ET–OT graph.

4. **False consolidated invoice from arithmetic.** Adding `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because `130 + 100 = 230` treats arithmetic equality as proof. Fees, discounts, taxes, or data errors can produce the same total. This would falsely give `ORD-B` an `Issue Invoice` event in the Order flattening and change equivalent-execution clustering.

5. **False payment settlement from remainder arithmetic.** Adding `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` because `230 - 100 = 130` ignores unapplied cash. If `PAY-2` is unapplied or belongs to an invoice outside this log, the repair falsely closes `INV-44` and creates a `Record Payment` self-loop on the Invoice flattening.

6. **Circular O2O confirmation.** Using a newly added O2O edge, such as `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, as independent evidence for the E2O edge that produced it overstates confidence. The O2O edge is a consistency consequence, not a second witness.

7. **Forcing complete lifecycles.** Repairing the log so that every object has a full lifecycle hides real exceptions: scanner outages, unapplied payments, missing delivery confirmations, and uninvoiced orders. A conservative repair should leave plausible orphans rather than manufacture a happy path.
