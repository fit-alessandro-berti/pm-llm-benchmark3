# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1 (Rounding & Uncertainty):** All reported timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. The true event time lies in `[t  2.5, t + 2.5]` minutes. This uncertainty propagates to service, wait, and sojourn estimates.
- **A2 (Resource Assignment & FIFO):** Each case is assigned a fixed resource per station. S2 queue discipline is strictly FIFO based on S1 completion time. No reassignment or priority switching occurs.
- **A3 (C2 Maintenance):** C2 is unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10. During this window, S2 capacity drops from 2 to 1 resource (C1). Cases arriving at S2 during this window are queued for C1; no data shows C2 starts in this interval, implying either no cases were assigned or they were deferred.
- **A4 (Missing Starts Imputation):** For `MISSING` starts, the true start time is bounded by: (i) the assigned resource’s previous activity completion, (ii) the reported `*_end` timestamp, and (iii) non-negative service time. Central estimates use the midpoint of feasible bounds.
- **A5 (Censoring for OPEN Activities):** `OPEN` activities are right-censored. Elapsed service time is calculated as `14:00  start` (or `14:00  S1_end` if S2 start is blank but state is `OPEN_IN_S2`). Total service time is unbounded above.
- **A6 (Event Ordering Convention):** At identical rounded timestamps, arrivals/starts are assumed to precede completions unless resource constraints force otherwise. Simultaneous resource assignments are resolved by FIFO queue order.
- **A7 (Little’s Law Applicability):** Little’s Law (`L = ·W`) is applied only to intervals where queue lengths are bounded, arrival/departure rates are roughly constant, and the system is not in a transient buildup phase.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

- **S1 Wait:** `S1_wait = S1_start  arrival_s1` (or bounds if `S1_start` is `MISSING`).
- **S1 Service:** `S1_svc = S1_end  S1_start` (bounds for `MISSING`/`OPEN`).
- **S2 Wait:** `S2_wait = S2_start  S1_end` (blank `S2_start` implies queueing at 14:00; elapsed wait = `14:00  S1_end`).
- **S2 Service:** `S2_svc = S2_end  S2_start` (bounds for `MISSING`; elapsed for `OPEN`).
- **Station Sojourn:** `S1_sojourn = S1_svc + S2_wait + S2_svc`.
- **Case Sojourn:** `Case_sojourn = S2_end  arrival_s1` (or `14:00  arrival_s1` for right-censored cases).
- **WIP(t):** Count of cases in S1 queue + S1 service + S2 queue + S2 service at time `t`. Computed by sweeping event timestamps.
- **Utilization:** `Util = Busy_time / (Capacity × Window_length)`. Capacity = 2 per station, reduced to 1 on S2 during 10:55–11:10.
- **Estimation Strategy:** For each metric, I report `[Lower, Upper]` bounds derived from rounding (±2.5 min) and imputation constraints. Central estimates use midpoints. Uncertainty is explicitly propagated.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence:**
- **08:00–10:00:** S1 and S2 utilization are balanced (~60% vs ~48%). Queue lengths are low and stable. No station clearly dominates. S1 feeds S2 at a rate slightly above S2’s processing capacity, causing mild S2 buildup but no congestion.
- **10:00–12:00:** C2 maintenance (10:55–11:10) reduces S2 capacity to 1. S1 continues processing at ~52% utilization, feeding cases into S2. S2 utilization spikes to ~71%, and WIP peaks at 6. The C2 outage creates a *temporary* S2 bottleneck, but S1 remains the upstream driver of queue growth.
- **12:00–14:00:** Arrival rate (11 cases) exceeds S1 completion rate (9 cases). S1 utilization rises to ~71%, WIP peaks at 8, and S1 queue grows steadily. S2 utilization remains high (~80%) but is constrained by S1 output. S1 becomes the *primary bottleneck*.

**Hypotheses:**
- The shift to an S1 bottleneck is likely driven by a time-varying arrival rate (increasing from ~3.5/h to ~5.5/h) rather than reduced S1 capacity.
- The C2 outage did not permanently shift the bottleneck to S2; once C2 returned, S2 cleared backlog until S1 arrival rate outpaced total station throughput.
- If arrival rates continue to rise, S1 will require additional resources or parallelism to prevent queue explosion.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

**Scenarios Compared:**
1. **Optimistic:** Missing starts occur at earliest feasible resource availability; OPEN activities complete immediately after 14:00. Yields lowest wait/service estimates.
2. **Central:** Missing starts use midpoint of resource-constrained bounds; OPEN activities continue at observed elapsed rates. Yields baseline metrics.
3. **Pessimistic:** Missing starts occur at latest feasible time; OPEN activities are delayed indefinitely. Yields highest wait/service estimates.

**Stable Conclusions:**
- S1 transitions to the primary bottleneck by 12:00 across all scenarios.
- C2 outage causes a measurable but transient S2 congestion spike.
- Rounding uncertainty (±2.5 min) shifts aggregate means by <3% and does not alter bottleneck identification.
- Queue growth is driven by arrival rate exceeding station throughput, not by missing data artifacts.

**Unstable Conclusions:**
- Exact utilization percentages vary by ±8–10% between scenarios.
- Precise queue lengths at minute-level resolution fluctuate due to imputation bounds.
- Case-sojourn estimates for OPEN cases span wide intervals; only lower bounds are defensible.

**Recommendation:** Monitor arrival rate trends and consider dynamic resource allocation or S1 parallelism if arrivals exceed ~5 cases/hour. Treat OPEN cases as right-censored in reporting to avoid false bottleneck signals.
