# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Definitions, units, and opening-hours convention

* **Operating clock:** Open 07:00–19:00 ($H = 12\text{ hours/day} = 720\text{ min/day}$); closed 19:00–07:00 (12 hours/day, stated as zero active service).
* **Arrival intensity ($\lambda$):** `arrivals` measures daily case entries during opening hours [cases/day]. In open-hours rate units, $\lambda_{\text{open}} = \text{arrivals} / 12$ [cases/hour].
* **Completion intensity ($\theta$):** `completed` measures daily case completions during opening hours [cases/day], or $\theta_{\text{open}} = \text{completed} / 12$ [cases/hour]. Daily net queue accumulation is $\Delta Q_t = \text{arrivals}_t - \text{completed}_t$ [cases/day].
* **Service-time quantiles (`median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`):** Daily 50th and 90th percentiles of active handling time $S$ [min/case], excluding queue wait. Because $p90/p50$ ranges from $1.83$ to $2.44$, $S$ is right-skewed; therefore, mean service time $\mathbb{E}[S] > \text{median}(S)$.
* **Work-in-process ($L$):** `mean_wip` is the daily mean of 15-minute snapshots during open hours [cases]. Ideally, $L$ includes both queued and active cases, though exact inclusion rules for overnight parked cases are undocumented.
* **Capacity (`staff_fte`) and gross utilization:** `staff_fte` ($c$) is mean concurrently available FTE [servers], supplying $720 c$ gross server-minutes/day (less breaks/admin/outages). Effective active-day utilization cannot be computed exactly without $\mathbb{E}[S]$, though bounds can be formed.
* **Mix, shock, and peak indicators:** `complex_pct` is the share of daily arrivals in a multi-step variant [%]; `outage_min` is lost operational minutes [min/day]; `peak_08_10_pct` is reported as the share of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59 [%].

## Threats to naive inference

1. **Mass-balance / flow-conservation discrepancy (critical observation):** Across the 28 days, $\sum \text{arrivals} = 2{,}519$ and $\sum \text{completed} = 2{,}409$, implying a net cumulative surplus of $+110$ cases ($+21$ in Week 2; $+78$ in Week 3 alone). Under strict conservation with zero overnight service, `mean_wip` at the end of Week 3 should exceed Week 1 by roughly $+90$ to $+100$ cases. Instead, `mean_wip` drops sharply every Friday-to-Saturday (from $35 \to 16$ on `04-17`$\to$`04-18`, and from $43 \to 23$ on `04-24`$\to$`04-25` despite positive daily $\Delta Q_t$ all week) and ends at $19$ on `05-03` (vs. $18$ on `04-06`). **Hypotheses:** (a) unlogged attrition (abandonments, cancellations, transfers out of the queue), (b) `mean_wip` counting only active/in-day cases while excluding parked overnight backlog, or (c) undocumented off-hours administrative closures.
2. **Missing 07:00 backlog and overnight boundary censoring:** Initial WIP at 07:00 is unrecorded. Because cases carry over overnight without service, daily rows are coupled across boundaries, and a case's wall-clock sojourn time differs from its open-hours residence time.
3. **Quantile-to-mean and service-to-flow-time conflation:** Neither $\mathbb{E}[S]$ nor mean flow time $W$ is supplied. Substituting `median_service_min` for $\mathbb{E}[S]$ underestimates capacity consumption under high skew ($p90$ up to $88\text{ min}$), and substituting `median_service_min` for $W$ ignores queueing delay $W_q$.
4. **Clock/definition mismatch between hourly bins and `peak_08_10_pct`:** On the four representative days, summing `arrivals_by_hour` bins `08-09` and `09-10` (indices 1 and 2) yields $16.8\%$ (`04-07`), $18.1\%$ (`04-15`), $9.6\%$ (`04-21`), and $11.8\%$ (`04-25`), far below the daily table's `peak_08_10_pct` ($31\%, 32\%, 47\%, 22\%$). Conversely, bins `09-10` + `10-11` (indices 2 and 3) give $31.7\%, 33.3\%, 36.5\%,$ and $21.6\%$—closely matching three of the four days, though still below `04-21`'s $47\%$. **Hypotheses:** a 1-hour timestamp/timezone offset in one table, a shifted peak-window definition, or non-representativeness of the `04-21` sample vector.
5. **Ambiguous mechanism of the `04-20` `complex_pct` jump:** `complex_pct` nearly doubles from $18\text{–}27\%$ (Weeks 1–2) to $39\text{–}47\%$ (Weeks 3–4). While simultaneous increases in `median_service_min` ($+9\text{–}10\text{ min}$), `p90_service_min` ($+30\text{–}38\text{ min}$), and `arrivals` support a real operational shift, a partial coding/classification change cannot be excluded without event-level variant definitions.
6. **Unlogged `04-29` intervention timing and reactive staffing endogeneity:** Staffing increases (`04-16..17` and `04-29..05-03`) were triggered by high backlog, creating reverse causality in pooled capacity regressions. Moreover, although the `04-29` intervention began *during* the day, `staff_fte` already averages $7.0$ on `04-29` (identical to `04-30`), implying either an early-morning start, a larger temporary mid-day addition, or scheduled rounding.
7. **Congestion-dependent service times and serial dependence:** During `04-29..05-01`, `median_service_min` drops from $35\text{–}36$ to $32\text{–}33$ and `p90` drops from $84\text{–}88$ to $72\text{–}76$ while `complex_pct` remains $43\text{–}45\%$, suggesting multitasking/rework overhead or faster additional staff. Daily observations are also serially correlated through carryover WIP and multi-day regime shifts.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

## Queue-physics / backlog-state segmentation (4 states)

Instead of segmenting by calendar date and case-mix epoch, an operationally distinct alternative groups days strictly by **system balance and capacity-drain physics** ($\Delta Q_t = \text{arrivals}_t - \text{completed}_t$, `outage_min`, and intervention/surge FTE):

* **State A — Balanced Equilibrium** ($|\Delta Q_t| \le 3$, `outage_min=0`, normal or matched FTE): `04-06..14` (9 days), `04-18..19` (2 days), `05-02..03` (2 days) [$n=13$]. *Story:* Whenever effective FTE matches the complex-adjusted workload—whether baseline Week 1, post-recovery Week 2 weekend, or augmented Week 4 weekend—the queue equilibrates with near-zero daily drift (mean $\Delta Q = +0.38$/d).
* **State B — Disruption / Capacity Loss** (`outage_min > 0`): `04-15` [$n=1$]. *Story:* Exogenous technical stoppage ($\Delta Q = +47$).
* **State C — Unstable Accumulation ($\rho > 1$)** ($\Delta Q_t \ge +4$ without outage): `04-20..28` [$n=9$, spanning both weekdays and weekend]. *Story:* Uncompensated demand/mix overload drives continuous accumulation ($\Delta Q = +11.8$/d across all 9 days).
* **State D — Augmented-Capacity Drain** ($\Delta Q_t \le -5$ with $+0.5$ to $+1.0$ surge FTE): `04-16..17` and `04-29..05-01` [$n=5$]. *Story:* Both the post-outage surge and the temporary staffing intervention exhibit identical backlog-depletion dynamics (mean `completed` $= 120.8$/d, mean $\Delta Q = -9.6$/d, falling WIP).

## Comparison with the primary segmentation

| Dimension | Primary segmentation (8 regimes) | Alternative queue-state segmentation (4 states) |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational story** | Causal decomposition separating demand schedule, mix shift, outage, and staffing | Control-theoretic health states (balanced, disrupted, accumulating, draining) |
| **Strengths** | Preserves homogeneous $(\lambda, S, c)$ parameters within each regime; separates weekday from weekend scale and pre- vs. post-mix eras | Parsimonious (4 states); proves `05-02..03` restored true equilibrium and reveals identical drain physics in `04-16..17` and `04-29..05-01` |
| **Weaknesses** | Several small cells ($n=1\text{–}3$); higher complexity | State A pools 36–49/d weekends (`28–35` min med service) with 92–107/d weekdays (`24–26` min), preventing single-queue parameterization; State D pools 26% and 44% complex workloads |

*(A third, supplementary lens would treat `04-06..14` not as flat baseline, but as a gradual pre-shock drift—`complex_pct` $19\% \to 24\%$, `p90` $44 \to 50$—preceding the `04-20` step jump.)*

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

## Intraday burstiness in the four representative hourly vectors

Using sample variance ($s^2$, $ddof=1$) across the $n=12$ hourly bins:

| Representative day | Total arrivals | Mean /hr | SD /hr | Fano factor ($s^2/\bar{x}$) | CV ($s/\bar{x}$) | Peak-to-mean | Total comp | Comp Fano ($s^2/\bar{x}$) | Key structural pattern |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `2026-04-07` (ordinary wkdy) | 101 | 8.42 | 5.21 | **3.23** | 0.62 | 2.38 | 99 | 1.65 | Smooth unimodal diurnal wave (peak 20 at 10–11) |
| `2026-04-15` (outage wkdy) | 105 | 8.75 | 6.02 | **4.14** | 0.69 | 2.51 | 58 | 2.25 | Diurnal arrivals; completions `0,0,0` in 11–14 + depressed shoulders (`8`, `4`) |

Notice the stark decoupling on `2026-04-21`: hourly arrivals exhibit severe overdispersion ($\text{Fano} = 10.12 \gg 1$, $\text{CV} = 1.03 > 1$), whereas hourly completions remain nearly equi-dispersed ($\text{Fano} = 1.07$, capped at $13$/hr) because a saturated server pool acts as a low-pass filter, converting arrival bursts directly into intraday queue spikes.

## Daily sequence dispersion, autocorrelation, and change-point evidence

* **Daily vs. intraday dispersion contrast:** Across all 28 days, daily arrivals are strongly overdispersed ($\text{Fano} \approx 10.4$, $\text{CV} = 0.34$) due to the weekday/weekend cycle and upward trend. Yet *within* stable weekday regimes, daily arrival totals are actually **underdispersed** relative to a Poisson benchmark (R1: mean $100.1$, $s^2 = 26.5$, $\text{Fano} = 0.26$, $\text{CV} = 0.05$; R5: mean $114.0$, $s^2 = 33.3$, $\text{Fano} = 0.29$, $\text{CV} = 0.05$). Daily totals appear calm and predictable even while intraday Fano reaches $10.1$!
* **Serial dependence:** Daily `arrivals` shows strong weekly periodicity ($\text{ACF}_7 > 0.85$) alongside positive lag-1 correlation ($\text{ACF}_1 \approx 0.48$) from 5-day weekday runs and upward trend. `mean_wip` exhibits intense persistence ($\text{ACF}_1 \approx 0.78$) because carried-over backlog integrates multi-day net imbalances. `complex_pct` and `median_service_min` show $\text{ACF}_1 > 0.85$ driven by the `04-20` regime break. Even after removing day-of-week effects, backlog and regime variables remain non-stationary.
* **Change-point evidence:** A simple mean-shift / binary-segmentation check identifies `2026-04-20` as the dominant structural break across `complex_pct` ($+20.9$ percentage points pre vs. post), `median_service_min` ($+7.1\text{ min}$), and `peak_08_10_pct` (weekdays jumping from $26\text{–}34\%$ to $40\text{–}50\%$), with secondary transient breaks at `04-15..17` (outage/recovery) and `04-29` (staffing intervention).

## Why four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model

1. **Zero replication ($n=1$ per day type):** Provides no variance estimate for intraday shapes; we cannot tell whether `04-21`'s 10–11, 13–14, and 16–17 bursts recur on a deterministic schedule (e.g., batch feeds) or at random times.
2. **One-hour bin aggregation:** Conceals sub-hourly micro-bursts (e.g., 34 cases arriving simultaneously at 10:01 vs. evenly over 10:00–11:00) and exact outage boundaries.
3. **Missing joint case attributes:** Hourly counts are unlinked to `complex_pct` or service duration, so we cannot test whether complex cases arrive in batches or experience correlated handling times.
4. **Unobserved regimes:** No hourly vector covers Phase 4 intervention weekdays (`04-29..05-01`), baseline Friday (`5.5` FTE), or Sunday.

Moreover, the mass-balance discrepancy noted in Section 1 limits even the cleanest windows unless exact boundary conservation is verified.

# 7. Operational implications

1. **Staffing calibration (recommendation):**
   * **Weekdays:** Maintain at least `+1.0` FTE above the old baseline (`7.0` Mon–Thu, `6.5` Fri) as long as `complex_pct` remains near $40\text{–}45\%$ and arrivals average $\sim 115$/d. Because R7a achieved $121$ completions/day under saturated backlog (`WIP 32–43`), `7.0/6.5` FTE operates near $\sim 95\%$ utilization with minimal headroom for variance or absences; evaluate `7.5` Mon–Thu / `7.0` Fri (or targeted peak overlap) once steady-state WIP stabilizes.
   * **Weekends:** Retain `+0.5` FTE (`3.0` FTE vs. `2.5`) under the elevated mix, which eliminated R6's $+6$/d deficit. Also audit why weekend median handling times exceed weekday handling times in Weeks 1–2 (`29` vs. `25` min) to see if cross-training or specialist on-call support can raise per-FTE productivity.
2. **Backlog recovery playbooks (recommendation):**
   * Separate **steady-state capacity** from **surge-recovery capacity**. After a 3-hour outage (`+47` cases), adding `+1.0` FTE drained $\sim 15$ net cases/day (requiring $3\text{–}4$ weekdays to clear). Pre-authorizing temporary overtime or cross-queued overflow capacity for 3–4 days post-outage prevents multi-week hangover.
3. **Peak smoothing and shift alignment (recommendation):**
   * On `04-21`, hourly arrivals spiked to `34` and `24` against a `10–13`/hr completion ceiling. Even when total daily FTE matches total daily arrivals, unbuffered tri-modal bursts inflate intraday WIP and trigger congestion-induced handling slowdown. Investigate upstream batch triggers (e.g., 10:00, 13:00, 16:00 feeds) to pace intake, and align shift overlaps to 09:00–17:00 rather than flat 12-hour coverage.
4. **Data-collection upgrades (recommendation):**
   * Log exact `07:00` and `19:00` backlog counts plus all non-completion exits (abandonments, cancellations, transfers) to resolve the $+110$-case conservation anomaly.
   * Record timestamps for staffing changes (`04-29`) and variant-taxonomy revisions (`04-20`), verify the `peak_08_10_pct` clock/bin definition, and export case-level event logs (`case_id`, `activity`, `timestamp`, `variant`, `resource`) so $\mathbb{E}[S]$ and $W$ can be measured directly.
5. **Policy trap — beneficial under pooled averages, risky under segmentation:**
   * **Pooling-based flat staffing:** Under pooled 28-day averages ($\bar{\lambda} = 90.0$/d, pooled `median_service_min` $= 31.0\text{ min}$), a planner computing $90 \times 31 = 2{,}790$ server-min/day might conclude that a flat `5.0–5.5` FTE schedule ($3{,}600\text{–}3{,}960$ gross server-min/d, $\sim 70\text{–}78\%$ apparent utilization) provides comfortable slack—and might even cite the positive pooled correlation between FTE and WIP (`39.6` WIP at `6.5–7.0` FTE vs. `17.4` at `2.5–3.0` FTE) to argue against adding staff! Under the segmented view, removing the `+1.0` FTE intervention and dropping weekdays to `5.5` FTE under R5 demand ($114\text{–}115$/d, skewed service with `p90` $\approx 83\text{ min}$, and 34/hr bursts) guarantees $\rho > 1$ and unbounded backlog growth.

# Assign Primary and Alternative regimes
def assign_primary_regime(row):
    d = row["date"]
    if d == "2026-04-15":
        return "R3_outage"
    if d in ("2026-04-16", "2026-04-17"):
        return "R4_recovery"
    if d <= "2026-04-19":
        return "R2_wknd_pre" if row["is_weekend"] else "R1_wkdy_pre"
    if d <= "2026-04-28":

    return "R7b_wknd_interv" if row["is_weekend"] else "R7a_wkdy_interv"

def assign_alt_state(row):
    if row["outage_min"] > 0:
        return "StateB_disruption"
    if row["net_flow"] >= 4:
        return "StateC_accumulating"
    if row["net_flow"] <= -5:
        return "StateD_draining"
    return "StateA_balanced"

# 4. Transparent exploratory change-point heuristic (single-split SSE minimization)
# NOTE: Heuristic only — does not constitute definitive statistical ground truth.
def best_single_split(series, dates):
    n = len(series)
    total_sse = ((series - series.mean()) ** 2).sum()
    best_idx, best_sse = None, float("inf")
    for k in range(3, n - 2):
        sse = ((series.iloc[:k] - series.iloc[:k].mean()) ** 2).sum() + \
              ((series.iloc[k:] - series.iloc[k:].mean()) ** 2).sum()
        if sse < best_sse:
            best_idx, best_sse = k, sse
    r2_gain = 1.0 - (best_sse / total_sse)
    return dates.iloc[best_idx], round(series.iloc[:best_idx].mean(), 2), round(series.iloc[best_idx:].mean(), 2), round(r2_gain, 3)

cp_rows = []
for col in ["complex_pct", "median_service_min", "p90_service_min", "net_flow"]:
    dt, m_pre, m_post, r2 = best_single_split(df[col], df["date"])
    cp_rows.append({"metric": col, "heuristic_split_date": dt, "pre_mean": m_pre, "post_mean": m_post, "sse_r2": r2})
print("\n=== EXPLORATORY CHANGE-POINT HEURISTIC (SINGLE SPLIT) ===")

    ("R1_wkdy_pre", df[df["primary_regime"] == "R1_wkdy_pre"], "approximate only"),

    ("R7b_wknd_interv", df[df["primary_regime"] == "R7b_wknd_interv"], "approximate only (tentative bound)"),
]
ll_rows = []
for label, sub, verdict in chosen_windows:
    L = sub["mean_wip"].mean()
    lam_comp_hr = sub["completed"].mean() / 12.0
    lam_arr_hr = sub["arrivals"].mean() / 12.0
    w_comp_hr = L / lam_comp_hr
    w_arr_hr = L / lam_arr_hr
    ll_rows.append({
        "window": label,
        "n": len(sub),
        "mean_L": round(L, 2),
        "comp_per_open_hr": round(lam_comp_hr, 2),
        "arr_per_open_hr": round(lam_arr_hr, 2),
        "W_open_hr_via_comp": round(w_comp_hr, 2),
        "W_open_min_via_comp": round(w_comp_hr * 60.0, 1),
        "W_open_hr_via_arr": round(w_arr_hr, 2),
        "verdict": verdict,
    })
print("\n=== LITTLE'S LAW (OPEN-HOURS BASIS; CHOSEN WINDOWS ONLY) ===")
print(pd.DataFrame(ll_rows).to_string(index=False))
