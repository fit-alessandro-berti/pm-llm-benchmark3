## **1. Executive Memo**

### **Key Finding: Redesign R Shows Segment-Specific Promise but Systemic Risks**
The pilot of Redesign R delivered **meaningful improvements for simple, low-risk cases** (e.g., 6-day median reduction, 3pp higher first-time-right for simple houses) while **worsening outcomes for complex segments** (apartments, old buildings, permit-sensitive cases). The redesign’s core innovations—**coordinator ownership, merged validation/scheduling, and predictive survey skipping**—reduced handovers and visible costs but **increased tail duration, rework, and downstream complaints**. The process-mining evidence reveals **three critical conflicts**:
1. **Median vs. tail trade-off**: Faster for simple cases, but p90/p95 durations worsened due to **predictive rule errors, missed permit checks, and activation compliance gaps**.
2. **Cost vs. quality trade-off**: Visible cost dropped (EUR 184  EUR 161), but **downstream complaint/rework costs rose** (EUR 198  EUR 207), and **manual activations and second visits surged**.
3. **Resilience vs. efficiency trade-off**: Fewer handovers improved accountability but **centralized coordinator ownership created bottlenecks** (93% utilization) and **reduced resilience** when coordinators were absent.

### **Strategic Trade-Off: Speed vs. Robustness**
The redesign’s **segmented impact** suggests it is **not a one-size-fits-all solution**. Simple cases benefit from streamlining, but complex cases suffer from **over-optimization for speed at the expense of compliance and quality**. The **low process-model precision (0.51 vs. 0.79 baseline)** and **alignment violations** (e.g., billing before activation, skipped permit checks) signal **hidden variability** that the redesign did not account for.

### **Recommended Path Forward: Hybrid Experimentation**
**Do not scale nationally.** Instead, adopt a **phased, evidence-based approach**:
1. **Pause national rollout** of Redesign R to avoid worsening tail performance and customer dissatisfaction.
2. **Scale coordinator ownership selectively** (for simple cases) while **reversing survey-skipping and post-billing activation** for complex segments (Alternative D).
3. **Run a controlled regional experiment** (Alternative E) to:
   - Test **segment-specific rules** (e.g., skip surveys only for low-risk addresses).
   - Measure **full downstream costs** (complaints, revisits) for 60+ days.
   - Compare **coordinator ownership vs. shared-team resilience**.
4. **Invest in critical measurements** (M1, M3, M4, M7) to resolve:
   - The predictive rule’s accuracy (M1, M6).
   - Root causes of tail duration (M3: reason codes for failed visits).
   - Hidden costs of manual activations and billing disputes (M4).

### **Why This Approach?**
- **Mitigates risk**: Avoids scaling a redesign that worsens p90/p95 and complaints.
- **Preserves gains**: Retains coordinator ownership benefits for simple cases.
- **Uncovers truths**: Experiments will reveal whether the tail issues are **fixable with better segmentation** or **fundamental to the redesign**.
- **Aligns incentives**: Addresses activation specialist frustration and field-team utilization imbalances.

### **Decision Threshold for Broader Rollout**
We should **consider national scaling of Redesign R only if**:
- The regional experiment shows **5% increase in p90 duration** for complex segments.
- **Downstream costs (complaints + revisits) do not exceed EUR 200** in full measurement.
- **Predictive survey-skipping achieves 85% accuracy** (vs. current ~60% implied by apartment/old-building deterioration).
- **Coordinator utilization stays below 85%** (to maintain resilience).

## **3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts**

### **A. High Fitness but Low Precision**
- **Fitness (0.94)**: The pilot’s simplified coordinator path dominates the log, so the model **fits observed cases well** (e.g., merged validation/scheduling, skipped surveys).
- **Precision (0.51)**: The model **allows too many invalid paths** (e.g., billing before activation, repeated visits without surveys) because the redesign **relaxed compliance checks** for speed. Low precision signals **hidden variability**—the process is now more flexible but less controlled.

### **B. Improved Median but Worse Tail**
- **Median improvement**: Simple cases (60% of pilot) benefit from **fewer handovers and merged tasks**.
- **Tail deterioration**: Complex cases (40% of pilot) suffer from:
  - **Predictive rule errors** (skipping surveys for risky addresses).
  - **Permit checks omitted** (alignment violations for old buildings).
  - **Activation compliance gaps** (billing before testing  more complaints).

### **C. Fewer Handovers but Lower Resilience**
- **Fewer handovers**: Coordinator ownership reduces context loss between steps.
- **Lower resilience**: **Centralized ownership creates bottlenecks** (93% utilization) and **no backup when coordinators are absent**. The DFG shows **increased "Failed visit  Second visit" delays**, likely due to **coordinator overload**.

### **D. Lower Visible Cost but Higher Rework/Downstream Cost**
- **Visible cost drop (EUR 184  EUR 161)**: Fewer handovers and skipped surveys reduce labor.
- **Hidden cost rise (EUR 198  EUR 207)**: More **manual activations (7%  15%)**, **second visits (11%  18%)**, and **complaints (6.1%  9.8%)** offset savings. The **DFG shows "Billing start  Complaint escalation" increased by 5x**, suggesting **post-installation issues**.

### **E. Improvement for Simple Houses but Deterioration for Apartments/Old Buildings**
- **Simple houses**: Benefit from **merged validation/scheduling** and **fewer handovers**.
- **Apartments/old buildings**:
  - **Survey skipping** misses critical risks (e.g., permit needs).
  - **Predictive rule fails** (e.g., "medium-risk" addresses often need surveys).
  - **Activation compliance suffers** (manual activations rise due to rushed billing).

## **6. Final Recommendation**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Pause national rollout** of Redesign R to avoid worsening tail performance and customer dissatisfaction.
2. **Deploy coordinator ownership selectively** for **simple, low-risk cases** (e.g., single-family homes with no permit history).
3. **Reverse survey-skipping and post-billing activation** for **apartments, old buildings, and permit-sensitive cases** to restore compliance.

### **What to Scale**
- **Coordinator ownership** for simple cases, but **cap utilization at 80%** to maintain resilience.
- **Segment-specific rules**: Use **M1 and M6** to refine predictive survey-skipping for medium-risk addresses.

### **What to Measure Next**
Prioritize **M1 (predictive rule accuracy)**, **M3 (reason codes for failures)**, and **M4 (full downstream costs)**. These will determine whether the redesign can be safely scaled.

### **Threshold for Broader Rollout**
Proceed with **national scaling of Redesign R** only if:
- The regional experiment shows **3% increase in p90 duration** for complex segments.
- **Downstream costs (complaints + revisits) do not exceed EUR 200** in full measurement.
- **Predictive survey-skipping achieves 85% accuracy** (validated via M1/M6).
- **Coordinator utilization stays below 85%** (monitored via M5).

### **Contingency Plan**
If experiment results are **mixed or negative**:
- **Roll back survey-skipping and post-billing activation** entirely.
- **Keep coordinator ownership for simple cases** but **revert to shared-team ownership** for complex segments to restore resilience.

---
**Rationale**: This approach **preserves the redesign’s benefits for simple cases** while **mitigating risks for complex segments**. The controlled experiment will provide the **data needed to make a defensible national decision**—avoiding the pitfalls of premature scaling or unnecessary rollback.
