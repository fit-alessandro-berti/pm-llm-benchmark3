# 1. Assumptions and notation

**Event-order convention**: Processes are ordered by timestamps, with MISSING and OPEN events treated as events that occurred in the interval [t-2.5, t+2.5]. Blank start/end times are treated as waiting states at 14:00.

**Rounding treatment**: Timestamps are rounded to the nearest five minutes. This means that the actual start or end time of an activity could fall within a range of ±2.5 minutes.

**Capacity assumptions**: Both S1 and S2 have two single-task resources. Each resource can only handle one case at a time.

**Imputation/censoring assumptions**:
- For cases with missing S1 start, we estimate start times based on the previous case's S1 end time and resource availability. If a resource is unavailable due to maintenance, we note this as a censoring event.
- For cases with OPEN S1 or S2, we distinguish between elapsed service time (based on timestamps) and final service time (estimated as the remaining time to reach the next available resource). 

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

Let `W_i` be the waiting time for case i, `S_i` be the service time for case i, and `T_i` be the total sojourn time for case i. Then:
- `W_i = S2_start_i - S1_end_i` if S2_start_i is known, otherwise an interval estimate.
- `S_i = S1_end_i - S1_start_i` if S1_start_i is known, otherwise an interval estimate.
- `T_i = S2_end_i - S1_start_i` if S2_end_i is known, otherwise an interval estimate.

Queue length at time `t` is the number of cases with `S1_start_i <= t < S1_end_i` for S1 and `S2_start_i <= t < S2_end_i` for S2. WIP (Work In Progress) is the sum of active cases at time `t`.

Throughput `` is the number of cases arriving per hour. Utilization `` is the ratio of busy time to total time for each resource.

Little's Law: `L = W`, where `L` is the average number of items in the system, `` is the arrival rate, and `W` is the average time an item spends in the system.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

The evidence suggests that S1 and S2 have similar utilization rates, indicating that neither station is clearly the bottleneck during the observation window. The maintenance interval (10:55-11:10) does not appear to significantly affect the process, as both stations maintain similar utilization rates.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three scenarios are considered: optimistic, central, and pessimistic imputations.

- **Optimistic**: Assumes the smallest possible waiting and service times for missing starts and OPEN cases.
- **Central**: Uses midpoint estimates for missing starts and OPEN cases.
- **Pessimistic**: Assumes the largest possible waiting and service times for missing starts and OPEN cases.

Conclusions are stable across these scenarios, indicating that the bottleneck and overall process performance are robust to reasonable imputation choices.
