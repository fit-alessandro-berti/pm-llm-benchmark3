# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

To understand `Mira_Coordinator`'s position in the organizational structure, we must evaluate what each network graph type derived from PM4Py captures—and critically, what it omits.

### Handover-of-work network
* **What it implies:** `Mira_Coordinator` exhibits the highest out-degree (0.82), in-degree (0.51), and betweenness centrality (0.77) across the entire process. This implies she operates as the primary traffic hub for non-standard process flows. * **What it does NOT imply:** High handover centrality does not imply that Mira performs deep domain processing or holds formal managerial sign-off authority. Handover networks track sequential event transitions ($R_A \rightarrow R_B$), measuring operational connectivity rather than task duration, decision authority, or value creation.

### Working-together network
* **What it implies:** `Mira_Coordinator` has a remarkably low weighted degree (0.16) and low co-case rates with individual resources (4% to 8%). This implies she does not belong to a dedicated operational micro-team (such as Team North or Team South) and rarely co-handles cases over multiple sustained lifecycle stages with the same individuals. 
* **What it does NOT imply:** It does not imply that Mira is isolated or peripheral to the process. Her low co-occurrence score is a structural artifact of how the working-together metric is calculated: because her 960 events are broadly distributed across 905 distinct cases (averaging ~1.06 events per case), her pairwise co-case overlap with any single adjuster is diluted across the broad workforce, unlike `Alice_TeamLead` and `Carla_Adjuster` who co-occur in 27% of Team North's cases.

### Subcontracting network
* **What it implies:** `Mira_Coordinator` dominates subcontracting betweenness (0.69) with strong $R_A \rightarrow R_B \rightarrow R_A$ loops (e.g., Mira $\rightarrow$ `Omar_Medical` $\rightarrow$ Mira: 126 instances; Mira $\rightarrow$ `Priya_Legal` $\rightarrow$ Mira: 98 instances). This implies a classic "delegate and retrieve" pattern: she initiates specialized sub-tasks, relinquishes temporary control, and re-assumes control to process outcomes or reassign cases upon completion.
* **What it does NOT imply:** It does not imply that she supervises the technical content of the specialist work. Instead, it indicates that external/specialist reviews do not automatically route back to the originating adjuster, but rather pass back through her desk for secondary routing.

### Performance / time statistics
* **What it implies:** Mira has a brief median service time of 4.2 minutes per event, yet cases she touches exhibit high overall cycle-time variance. During her 6-day absence, median cycle times for routine cases remained unchanged, but exception cases accumulated, driving 90th percentile cycle times from 8.4 to 13.9 days. This implies her main operational contribution is active queue management and dispatching; without her, exception cases sit idle in unassigned state queues.
* **What it does NOT imply:** It does not imply that Mira's active execution is slow or inefficient. Her low service time (4.2 min) shows rapid task execution; the delay in exception cases stems from queue waiting times before and after her intervention, not processing time spent by her.

### Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity
* **What it implies:** Vectorizing resource-activity profiles shows Mira clustering in a unique role distinct from both Team Leads (`Alice`, `Bruno`) and Line Adjusters (`Carla`, `Diego`, `Elise`, `Farid`). * **What it does NOT imply:** It does not imply she possesses financial approval authority (she performs no `Approve Settlement` events) or primary investigative functions (`Assess Damage`). Her role profile reflects exception triage and procedural remediation.

# 3. Risk assessment

### Bottleneck Risk
* **Severity:** High (Conditional)
* **Analysis:** Mira is not a continuous throughput bottleneck, as she processes events rapidly (4.2 min median). However, she acts as a structural queue bottleneck for the exception segment (22% of process volume). When workload spikes or when she is absent, exception cases stall in pre-assignment states, directly expanding tail latency (90th percentile cycle time).

### Key-Person Dependency Risk
* **Severity:** Critical
* **Analysis:** The process relies heavily on Mira’s tacit knowledge for routing complex cases, complaints, and reopens. Her high betweenness centrality (0.77) and the performance collapse during her 6-day leave demonstrate that tacit triage rules reside with an individual rather than in documented system logic.

### Coordination Risk
* **Severity:** Moderate
* **Analysis:** Because Mira operates largely outside the formal team structures (low co-case rates with `Alice` and `Bruno`), there is a risk of misaligned priority scoring. Team leads manage capacity for Team North and South, but Mira dispatches work to their adjusters independently, potentially conflicting with team-level workload management.

### Compliance & Control Risk (Segregation of Duties)
* **Severity:** Moderate-High
* **Analysis:** As noted by the compliance reviewer, Mira identifies processing gaps (`Request Missing Information`, `Escalate Complaint`) and subsequently selects the downstream specialist (`Assign Specialist`, handover to `Omar`/`Priya`). Without secondary sign-off or automated routing matrices, this dual responsibility introduces uncontrolled discretion in specialist selection and case reopening.

### Workload Fairness Risk
* **Severity:** Moderate
* **Analysis:** Handover data shows Mira distributing cases to all four adjusters (`Carla`: 350, `Diego`: 330, `Elise`: 290, `Farid`: 280). However, senior adjusters (`Carla`, `Diego`) receive a higher proportion of complex exception handovers than junior adjusters (`Elise`, `Farid`). If dispatching is based on informal perception rather than objective capacity/skill metrics, workload distribution could become inequitable.

### Data-Quality & Logging Risk
* **Severity:** Low-Moderate
* **Analysis:** Mira's activity profile includes generic event names such as `Assign Specialist` and `Request Missing Information`. If event logging fails to capture underlying parameters (e.g., reason for reassignment, specific document requested), analytical visibility into why 48% of her cases carry `exception_flag=True` remains masked.
