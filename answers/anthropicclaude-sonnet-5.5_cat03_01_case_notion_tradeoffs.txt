## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is the choice of object type whose objects each become one trace. A flattened log contains every event directly related to that object through E2O, ordered by timestamp. Ties across different objects (e29 and e30 at 11:05) are treated as unordered.

**Convergence.** One event belongs to several cases of the chosen type. Example: e16 is related to O1, O2 and O3, so Order flattening copies it three times.

**Divergence.** One case contains several events of the same activity that belong to different sub-objects. The flattened trace then shows a loop or repetition that the case notion cannot explain. Example: O1 has two Pack Package events, e11 for P1 and e12 for P2.

**Copied shared events** are both evidence and duplication.
- As evidence, e13 is a real co-involvement fact: O2 and O3 share P3.
- As duplication, it inflates frequencies and waiting times when counted once per case.
- I therefore report raw (copied) and event-deduplicated figures side by side.

**Permitted transformations.**
- The baseline uses direct E2O only.
- Labelled variants may propagate relations along O2O paths. The two used here are OrderInvoice and OrderInvoicePayment.
- Timestamps may be perturbed only in tie tests.
- No events may be invented, and propagated relations are never merged silently into the baseline.

2. Explain every mismatch with a named mechanism, such as copied e16 or singleton M2.
3. Report, for example, Dispatch as 3 events, 4 unique orders, 6 order references and 6 items. No single number is "the" frequency.
4. Prefer the OC view for structure and the lowest-duplication flattening (Delivery for delivery time, Invoice for collection time) for timing.
5. Flag a finding as unresolved if it survives only in one view.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position: B.** Order is the primary reporting frame. Explicit secondary lifecycles are ItemPackageDelivery for logistics, and InvoicePayment for billing and cash. Order is lead only for scoping and coverage, not for counts or timing.

**Decision criteria.**
- Coverage of events and activities.
- Duplication ratio.
- Divergence.
- Fit to the business question "order fulfilment".
- Dependence on entangled structure.

**Strongest supporting evidence.**
- Order is the only flattening with 31 of 31 events and 8 of 8 activities. Item covers 16 events and 4 activities; Payment covers 4 events and 1 activity.
- Its duplication ratio (1.35) is lower than Item's (1.5).
- Its divergence is explainable (O1 and O3 have split shipments).
- Only Order links logistics to billing and closure.

**Strongest counterargument.**
- Every order is entangled: O1 via D2, O2 via P3, V2 and D2, O3 via P3, P4, V2 and D2, and O4 via P4. The whole log is one connected component.
- 9 of 31 events (29%) are shared, and the false edge e14e16 plus the PayConfirm cycle show Order traces are not independent.
- This supports Position C.

**Residual risk.**
- Order counts and performance numbers remain biased in the primary frame.
- Propagation results depend on path choice.
- Without allocation data for V2 and M2/M3, per-order financials cannot be established.

**Conditions for change.**
- **To C:** if down-weighting or event-level dedup changes the top edges or variants, if shared-event share grows beyond about a third in a larger sample, or if stakeholders need per-package, per-delivery or per-invoice cost.
- **To A (Order):** if consolidation is rare (for example under 5% of events) in a larger sample.
- **Lead becomes Item or Delivery:** if the question is line-level or logistics-only.

def propagate(ocel, path):
    otype = dict(zip(ocel.objects["ocel:oid"], ocel.objects["ocel:type"]))
    adj = {}
    for _, r in ocel.o2o.iterrows():
        adj.setdefault(r["ocel:oid"], set()).add(r["ocel:oid_2"])
        adj.setdefault(r["ocel:oid_2"], set()).add(r["ocel:oid"])
    frontier = ocel.relations.loc[ocel.relations["ocel:type"] == path[0],
                                  ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]]
    added = []
    for nxt_type in path[1:]:
        rows = [(e, nb) for e, o in frontier.itertuples(index=False)
                for nb in adj.get(o, ()) if otype[nb] == nxt_type]
        frontier = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]).drop_duplicates()
        added.append(frontier)

        ocel.events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]], on="ocel:eid")

    add["ocel:qualifier"] = "propagated"
    new_rel = (pd.concat([ocel.relations, add], ignore_index=True)
                 .drop_duplicates(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]))
    return OCEL(events=ocel.events, objects=ocel.objects,
                relations=new_rel, o2o=ocel.o2o)

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Remove or down-weight copied events.**
   - Assign each Order-log row weight 1/k, where k is the number of orders on the event. Weighted totals must equal raw event counts (Pack 4, Dispatch 3, Confirm 3, Record Payment 4).
   - Compare edges before and after. Loops that persist (O1's PackPack for P1 and P2, O3's DispatchDispatch for D2 and D3) are real divergence.
   - Edges that depend only on a shared event, such as the false e14e16, should weaken under primary-owner assignment, for example assigning e16 to O1 only.

2. **Direct versus propagated relations.**
   - Run the propagation code and check the predicted counts.
   - For Invoice: V1 12, V2 16 (O2's 9 plus O3's 13 minus 6 shared) and V3 8, so 36 rows with 5 copies (e14, e16, e17, e24, e27).
   - For Payment: M1 12, M2 16, M3 16 and M4 8, so 52 rows.
   - M2 and M3 becoming identical 16-event traces is an artefact, not a finding.
   - If the counts do not match, the propagation code is wrong and the O2O graph is misread.

3. **Change the lead object.**
   - Compare Order, propagated Invoice and Delivery on the same question.
   - DispatchConfirm means: Package 8h54m, Delivery 10h13m, Item 10h33m.
   - Invoice-propagated lead times: V1 26h00, V2 26h03 and V3 26h04, matching Order's.
   - Robustness across lead choices for the 26 h figure mainly reflects the common closure batch (e28–e31), not agreement on fulfilment speed.

4. **Event, unique-object and total-object frequencies in the OC-DFG.**
   - Dispatch: 3 events; orders 4 unique and 6 total; items 6/6; packages 4/4; deliveries 3/3.
   - Record Payment: 4 events; invoices 3 unique versus 4 total, exposing V2's two instalments; orders 4 unique versus 6 total.
   - Discrepancies between unique and total counts mark consolidation or repetition and must be explained rather than averaged.

5. **Timestamp-tie test.** e29 and e30 (both 11:05) are separate orders. They are harmless in the Order log, but in the propagated Invoice log for V2 (O2 and O3) their order determines an arbitrary CloseClose edge. Permute them and check whether any claim changes.

6. **Leave-consolidation-out check.** Analyse O1 alone against O2–O4. O1's involvement is only via D2, not via shared packages. A conclusion that changes when O1 is excluded rests on D2 consolidation rather than on order behaviour.
