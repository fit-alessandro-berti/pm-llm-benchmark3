# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### Pattern 1: Fast PO Approvals & Four-Eyes Violations in Emergency Subgroups (Buyer_Carla / Buyer_Dan)
* **Benign Explanation A (Genuine Emergency Response):** Life-saving medical supplies and urgent facilities repairs require real-time coordination where formal segregation-of-duties controls are temporarily relaxed via authorized emergency override protocols to prevent operational shutdowns.
  * *Supporting evidence:* Concurrent emergency medical dispatch logs, high-priority site tickets, dispatch manifests, and formal emergency classification tags.
  * *Refuting evidence:* Cases marked as emergencies that correspond to standard, non-urgent inventory restocks or routine administrative purchases.
* **Benign Explanation B (Informal Specialization / Pod Working):** High-throughput teams operating under extreme end-of-quarter pressure adopt tightly coupled desk-sharing arrangements where one resource pre-drafts and another immediately counter-signs based on verbal pre-clearance.
  * *Supporting evidence:* Chat logs, shift handover notes, and supervisor authorizations approving desk-pod processing structures.
  * *Refuting evidence:* Systematic absence of pre-approval documentation or management sign-offs for the pod structure.

### Pattern 2: Fast Bank Detail Changes & Payment Approvals (VendorMaster_Lena & AP_Kim)
* **Benign Explanation A (System Migration / Mass Re-banking):** Vendor family V-77x underwent a corporate banking platform migration, generating legitimate batch updates where bank change tickets were processed immediately prior to scheduled payment runs.
  * *Supporting evidence:* Bank notification letters from vendor corporate headquarters, treasury migration project schedules, and bulk ticket reference IDs.
  * *Refuting evidence:* Bank accounts pointing to individual employee accounts or newly created shell companies rather than established corporate entities.
* **Benign Explanation B (Batch Processing Artifacts):** Operational bottlenecks caused bank change requests and invoice payment releases to be batched and processed sequentially in single administrative sessions, distorting timestamp intervals.
  * *Supporting evidence:* System processing queue logs showing batch job execution windows.
  * *Refuting evidence:* Discontinuous transaction timestamps scattered across irregular off-hours without batch processing metadata.

### Pattern 3: Rapid Batch-Entered Retrospective Justifications (Manager_Hugo / Approver_Gita)
* **Benign Explanation A (Administrative Backlog Remediation):** Operational staff deferred administrative paperwork during active emergency handling and subsequently recorded retrospective justifications en masse once the crisis subsided.
  * *Supporting evidence:* Contemporary incident response tickets, operational status reports, and email notifications explaining administrative backlogs.
  * *Refuting evidence:* Justifications lacking any linkage to prior emergency event records or referencing non-existent operational incidents.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

### PM4Py Analysis Code and Pseudocode

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# 1. Format dataframe for PM4Py compliance
df = pd.read_csv("procurement_payment_log.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# 2. Filter four-eyes violations
# Define activity pairs that must be performed by different resources
four_eyes_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity1="Create Purchase Order",
    activity2="Approve Purchase Order",
)

bank_so_d_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity1="Change Vendor Bank Details",
    activity2="Payment Approval",
)

# 3. Discover process networks (Handover, Working Together, Subcontracting)
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
working_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)

# 4. Compare emergency and non-emergency cases
emergency_df = df[df["emergency_flag"] == True]
routine_df = df[df["emergency_flag"] == False]

emergency_variants = pm4py.get_variants(emergency_df)
routine_variants = pm4py.get_variants(routine_df)

# 5. Check timing using path-performance and service/waiting time features
# Analyze transition performance between Create PO and Approve PO
po_path_perf = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    start_activity="Create Purchase Order",
    end_activity="Approve Purchase Order",
    percentile=0.95,
)

df_with_service = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    activity_key="concept:name",
)

# 6. Declarative rule / log-skeleton check for retrospective justification
# Discover Declare constraints and log skeleton to check conformance on emergency workflows
log_skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(df)
conformance_skeleton = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(df, log_skeleton)

# 7. Isolate manual_upload and legacy_erp source-system effects
manual_upload_df = df[df["source_system"] == "manual_upload"]
legacy_erp_df = df[df["source_system"] == "legacy_erp"]
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

### Evidence that would INCREASE concern
* **Vendor Master Changes:** Bank account verification records revealing that multiple accounts across vendor family V-77x point to identical beneficiary names, newly opened digital wallets, or employee-linked accounts.
* **Access Logs:** Active Directory or ERP login session telemetry showing that a single user (e.g., Approver_Gita or AP_Kim) utilized shared credentials or logged into secondary accounts to simulate multi-user approvals.
* **Documentary Audit Trails:** Absence of verifiable physical delivery notes, patient care tickets, or facility repair logs matching the emergency override timestamps for Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo cases.
* **Batch Migration Anomalies:** Metadata proving that spreadsheet uploads for retrospective justifications were created *after* internal audit notification rather than at quarter close.

### Evidence that would REDUCE concern
* **Vendor Master Changes:** Cryptographic verification logs, signed treasury change authorization forms, and independent phone callback verification records confirming legitimate banking updates for vendor family V-77x.
* **Access Logs:** Hardware token logs, IP address tracking, and physical badge swipes confirming that distinct individuals executed tasks despite shared-account naming conventions in reporting layers.
* **Operational Verification:** Validated emergency declarations signed by executive management authorizing expedited processing protocols for critical medical and facility requirements.
* **Data Quality Confirmation:** IT remediation tickets confirming that shared-account naming and mapping table errors in `legacy_erp` and spreadsheet migrations were system-generated artifacts rather than manual obfuscation.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified several procedural exceptions, segregation-of-duties anomalies, and workflow bottlenecks that warrant targeted operational remediation. Specifically, testing revealed 42 instances where purchase orders were approved by the same resource who created them, alongside 9 instances where vendor bank detail modifications and payment approvals were executed by overlapping personnel, concentrated primarily within vendor family V-77x. Furthermore, certain emergency medical supply and facility repair pathways exhibited exceptionally rapid approval times (ranging between 8 to 17 minutes) and circular subcontracting loops.

However, internal audit emphasizes that these findings do not constitute conclusive evidence of fraud or intentional misconduct. The majority of four-eyes exceptions and accelerated approvals correlate directly with documented emergency operational flags (`emergency_flag=True`), reflecting high-pressure response mechanisms for critical infrastructure and healthcare needs. Additionally, technical data-quality evaluations identified significant systemic artifacts, including shared-account naming conventions (`Approver_Gita`), mapping table errors in legacy enterprise resource planning systems, and batch spreadsheet migrations (`quarter_close_upload`) that distorted timestamp granularity.

We recommend management perform a focused review of high-value vendor disbursements associated with vendor family V-77x, validate physical authorization records for emergency overrides, and correct system mapping configurations to ensure unambiguous attribution of approval activities in future audit trails.
