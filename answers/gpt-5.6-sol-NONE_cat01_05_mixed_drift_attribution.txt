## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-fitness collapse, from 0.93 to 0.55, is primarily evidence of a representation mismatch. The migration renamed most `Validate Eligibility` events to `Eligibility Screen` and introduced duplicated technical `Open Case` events. Mapping the renamed activity and removing those duplicates restores March fitness to 0.90, close to the 0.92-0.93 baseline. Thus, most of the raw decline is semantic/provenance drift, not evidence that workers abruptly abandoned the intended process. It does not establish that all March behavior was unchanged: mapped fitness remains slightly below baseline, and fitness alone can hide changes in frequencies, timing, resources, and case routing.

Label mapping recovers conformance because two labels are treated as the same modeled activity. That is a defensible semantic normalization, but it can mask a real application redesign if the new screen differs in rules, automation, or timing. It therefore requires validation from event attributes and operational documentation.

Duplicated `Open Case` events distort raw DFGs, variants, trace lengths, and alignment costs; removal is appropriate only when duplication is demonstrably technical. Missing resources have less effect on activity-only DFGs, but bias handover, workload, segregation-of-duty, team-comparison, and queue reconstruction analyses. Concentration in migrated activities makes complete-case analysis unsafe.

Post-May changes cannot be dismissed as logging drift. They remain after label and duplicate normalization: rework reaches 29%, mapped fitness falls to 0.86, normalized `AssessRequestInfo` rises from 12 to 29 per 100 cases, and cycle-time tails increase sharply. The supplier disruption, assessor departures, complexity growth, and queue-wait evidence provide operationally coherent mechanisms for genuine behavioral and performance drift.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

For each reported month, let \(r_m\) be the seven supplied relation rates. I use the mean absolute relation-rate difference from January:

\[
D(m,\text{Jan})=\frac{1}{7}\sum_{e=1}^{7}|r_{m,e}-r_{\text{Jan},e}|
\]

This is interpretable as average absolute change in occurrences per 100 cases across the supplied edges. It is not a full DFG distance: omitted edges are assumed unavailable, not unchanged, and no probability renormalization is imposed because loops permit more than one edge occurrence per case.

| month | mean absolute distance | L1 total difference | dominant contributions |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Mar | 0.71 | 5 | Small changes in assessment, senior review, and decision relations |
| May | 6.86 | 48 | Request-info loop +12/+12; decisions -7 |
| Jul | 10.00 | 70 | Request-info loop +17/+17; decisions -11 |
| Sep | 4.57 | 32 | Partial rework recovery; decisions still -4 |
| Oct | 9.86 | 69 | Senior review +18; rework +14/+14; decisions -10 |
| Dec | 5.86 | 41 | Residual rework +9/+9 and senior review +10 |

March is structurally close after normalization. July and October are comparably distant for different reasons: rework dominates July, while seasonal senior review plus rework dominates October.

### 3.3 Cycle-time deterioration

For July-August versus the 31.15 h baseline median, deterioration is about 13-14 h. For October-November, it is about 17-18 h. Event-level counterfactual decomposition is unavailable, so defensible ranges are:

| component | Jul-Aug share of deterioration | Oct-Nov share | justification |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Case mix | 10-25% | 20-40% | Complexity rises to 28-29%, then 41-45%; Supplier Z is non-random |
| Control-flow/rework | 20-35% | 15-30% | Rework rises from 11.5% to 27-29%; repeated collection and assessment add elapsed time |
| Service | 0-10% | 0-15% | Assess service changes only from about 4.8 to 5.4 h; other service activities are unknown |
| Queueing/capacity | 40-65% | 35-60% | Reconstructed waiting rises much more than service; staffing loss, backlog, and seasonal load align temporally |

These are overlapping causal shares rather than additive estimates: rework and case mix create queue demand, so queueing mediates part of their effects.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

- **Logging drift:** March 1, for activity labels, duplicate opens, label count, raw fitness, and missing resources; July, a secondary candidate for peak missingness/duplicate rate, though likely intensity variation rather than a new schema. December is a possible recovery point.
- **Behavioral drift:** May 5, when rework and request-information edges jump; July 1, when priority policy changes routing; October 1, when senior assessment and complex-case pathways rise; December 5 is a recovery/intervention point.
- **Performance drift:** May, when median and p90 first rise materially; late June/July, when capacity loss and queues produce sustained deterioration; October, when seasonal demand creates a second step; December is partial recovery.

The evidence strongly rejects one global change point. March changes measurement, May changes document/rework behavior, June-July changes capacity and prioritization, and October changes population and load. Formal detection should therefore be metric-specific and stratified.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core monthly cohort | registration month | Stable top-line monitoring | At least 200 completed/mature cases; otherwise pool adjacent weeks | Change-point and seasonal comparisons | Completion-based cohorts can exclude slow open cases |
| Weekly operational cohort | registration week and maturity status | Detect onset near dated interventions | At least 75 cases or use control-chart shrinkage | Migration, supplier, staffing, priority, campaign event studies | Week-level calendar and censoring effects |
| Primary process strata | supplier group: Z vs non-Z | Isolate document disruption | At least 100 per comparison window; pool non-Z | Supplier difference-in-differences | Supplier assignment is related to region and complexity |
| Complexity strata | high vs standard | Separate case mix from within-class behavior | At least 100 per stratum/window | Seasonal and priority comparisons | Complexity may be post-treatment or inconsistently recorded |
| Priority strata | priority class, with complexity | Test redistribution of queue delay | At least 75 mature cases per class/window | Pre/post July wait quantiles | Conditioning on policy assignment can induce selection |
| Organizational strata | team; resources hierarchically nested | Diagnose capacity and practice differences | Team-month 50; individual estimates only with 30 and shrinkage | Departures, handovers, missingness | Teams may receive systematically different cases |
| Geographic strata | region group | Control Supplier Z’s non-random footprint | Region-window 75; merge adjacent low-volume regions | Within-region supplier comparisons | Overcontrol if disruption acts through regional operations |
| Process-path strata | straight-through, request loop, senior review | Explain timing and outcomes | Variant family 5% or 50 cases | Variant-specific service/wait decomposition | Path is a mediator, so not suitable for total causal effects |
| Data-quality strata | source system, raw/mapped eligibility label, resource completeness | Identify measurement drift | Source-window 100 events | Migration validation and sensitivity analysis | Missingness-based segmentation selects cases |
| Backlog cohorts | document receipt before/after recovery; queue-entry cohort | Separate old backlog from new work | 75 cases per cohort and sufficient follow-up | Supplier recovery and overtime impact | Receipt date may itself depend on disruption |

The **primary monitoring population** should be all registered cases, assigned to registration cohorts and followed with explicit maturity/censoring indicators. It preserves the operational denominator and avoids selecting only completed, supplier-specific, or path-specific cases. Supplier, complexity, and priority should be reported as standardized secondary strata rather than replacing the primary population.

Seasonality should be handled with prior-year renewal baselines where available, calendar regressors, and within-season comparisons; until multiple years exist, use wider uncertainty bands and label October effects as provisional recurrence. Multiple testing should be controlled by monitor families using false-discovery-rate procedures. Alerts should require both statistical evidence and a minimum operational effect, except hard schema and safety constraints.

# 1. Validate data
assert monthly["month"].is_monotonic_increasing and monthly["month"].is_unique

    "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness",
    "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct"
]

      .round(3).to_string(index=False))

# 5. Explainable candidate change-point rule:
# Flag a month if its month-to-month change exceeds 3 times the median
# absolute month-to-month change for that metric, with a small floor.
# This is a screening rule, not formal inference.
cp_metrics = [
    "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness", "rework_variant_pct",
    "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "high_complexity_pct",
    "supplier_z_pct", "missing_resource_pct"
]
floors = {
    "raw_model_fitness": 0.05, "mapped_model_fitness": 0.015,
    "rework_variant_pct": 4.0, "median_cycle_h": 3.0, "p90_cycle_h": 8.0,
    "high_complexity_pct": 5.0, "supplier_z_pct": 8.0,
    "missing_resource_pct": 1.0
}

candidate_rows = []
for col in cp_metrics:
    delta = monthly[col].diff()
    robust_scale = delta.abs().median()
    threshold = max(3.0 * robust_scale, floors[col])
    for idx in monthly.index[delta.abs() >= threshold]:
        candidate_rows.append({
            "metric": col,
            "month": monthly.loc[idx, "month"].strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "delta": float(delta.loc[idx]),
            "threshold": float(threshold)
        })

candidate_cps = pd.DataFrame(candidate_rows)
print("\nCandidate change points (robust jump rule):")

import pm4py

def prepare_event_log(
    events,
    case_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    start_timestamp_col=None
):
    """Return a formatted copy; never mutate the caller's dataframe."""
    df = events.copy(deep=True)
    df[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df[timestamp_col], utc=True)
    kwargs = {
        "case_id": case_col,
        "activity_key": activity_col,
        "timestamp_key": timestamp_col
    }
    if start_timestamp_col is not None:
        df[start_timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df[start_timestamp_col], utc=True)
        kwargs["start_timestamp_key"] = start_timestamp_col
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(df, **kwargs)


def normalize_event_log(
    formatted_df,
    activity_col="concept:name",
    mapping=None,
    technical_duplicate_col=None
):
    """
    Preserve raw labels in 'activity_raw'. Apply semantic mapping to a copy.
    Optional duplicate removal requires a prevalidated technical-duplicate flag.
    """
    mapping = mapping or {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    normalized = formatted_df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized["activity_raw"] = normalized[activity_col]
    normalized[activity_col] = normalized[activity_col].replace(mapping)
    if technical_duplicate_col is not None:
        normalized = normalized.loc[
            ~normalized[technical_duplicate_col].fillna(False)
        ].copy()
    return normalized


def slice_window(formatted_df, start, end):
    """PM4Py time filtering; specify interval strings/timestamps consistently."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_df, start, end)


def monthly_process_artifacts(formatted_df, timestamp_col="time:timestamp"):
    """
    Discover a DFG, variants, and temporal profile for every calendar month.
    Returns dictionaries keyed by YYYY-MM.
    """
    work = formatted_df.copy(deep=True)
    months = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp_col], utc=True).dt.to_period("M")
    artifacts = {}
    for period in sorted(months.unique()):
        start = period.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        # PM4Py's range boundary semantics can vary by version, so use the
        # next-month boundary and verify counts in production.
        end = (period + 1).start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        window = pm4py.filter_time_range(work, start, end)
        artifacts[str(period)] = {
            "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(window),
            "variants": pm4py.get_variants(window),
            "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window)
        }
    return artifacts


def optional_behavioral_models(formatted_df, discover="log_skeleton"):
    """
    Discover a declarative model when supported by the installed PM4Py version.
    """
    if discover == "log_skeleton":
        if not hasattr(pm4py, "discover_log_skeleton"):
            raise AttributeError("Installed PM4Py lacks discover_log_skeleton.")
        return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(formatted_df)
    if discover == "declare":
        if not hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare"):
            raise AttributeError("Installed PM4Py lacks discover_declare.")
        return pm4py.discover_declare(formatted_df)
    raise ValueError("discover must be 'log_skeleton' or 'declare'")


def compare_raw_and_normalized(
    events,
    case_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    mapping=None,
    technical_duplicate_col=None
):
    """
    Format raw and normalized copies independently and return comparable
    DFG/variant/temporal artifacts without overwriting the original event log.
    """
    raw_source = events.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_source = events.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_source["activity_raw"] = normalized_source[activity_col]
    normalized_source[activity_col] = normalized_source[activity_col].replace(
        mapping or {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    )
    if technical_duplicate_col is not None:
        normalized_source = normalized_source.loc[
            ~normalized_source[technical_duplicate_col].fillna(False)
        ].copy()

    raw_log = prepare_event_log(
        raw_source, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col
    )
    normalized_log = prepare_event_log(
        normalized_source, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col
    )

    return {
        "raw_log": raw_log,
        "normalized_log": normalized_log,
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(raw_log),
        "normalized_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(normalized_log),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw_log),
        "normalized_variants": pm4py.get_variants(normalized_log),
        "raw_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(raw_log),
        "normalized_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(normalized_log)
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion, at **very high confidence (95-99%)**, is that the March raw-fitness collapse is primarily logging-semantic drift caused by migration. The timing is exact, the expected artifacts appear—new labels, duplicated technical opens, and missing resources—and normalization restores fitness from 0.55 to 0.90. This does not prove complete behavioral stability, because mapped fitness remains slightly below baseline and resource-based analyses are impaired.

The post-May rework increase is attributed to the Supplier Z disruption with **high confidence (80-90%)**. Supplier exposure rises sharply, request-information loops approximately double, fast variants decline, and partial recovery follows the supplier’s September recovery. Attribution is not complete: Z already served more complex cases, and aggregate supplier share may reflect changing intake rather than only disruption. Persistent rework through December could represent backlog, seasonal complexity, or learned risk-averse behavior.

The June-August cycle-time deterioration is attributed mainly to queueing under reduced assessment capacity with **high confidence (80-90%)**. Assess service time moves only modestly while reconstructed pre-Assessment waiting and the p90 rise substantially. Staffing cuts are therefore more consistent with waiting than slower execution. However, supplier-driven rework increases workload and mediates part of this effect; staffing cannot be assigned the entire delay.

Priority-induced redistribution after July is **plausible but not established (55-70%)**. The policy predicts improved waits for high-complexity cases and worse waits for standard cases, but monthly aggregates cannot reveal that contrast. October-November deterioration is attributed to renewal-season volume and complexity with **high confidence (85-95%)**, interacting with unresolved capacity constraints. The Senior Assessment rise likely reflects real case mix, but improved recording remains plausible.

December recovery has **moderate confidence (65-80%)** as a joint effect of overtime, document-quality intervention, supplier recovery, backlog reduction, and lower seasonal load. Clean attribution is impossible at monthly resolution.

I would revise this account if event-level, complexity-standardized supplier comparisons show no post-May differential rework; if queue waits did not rise within stable strata after June 15; if priority-class waits moved together after July; or if source audits show that request, senior-review, or timestamp changes were also migration artifacts.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF raw conformance falls sharply while mapped conformance, normalized DFGs, and temporal profiles remain within baseline bands, THEN route the alert to data engineering and suspend business-process escalation, BECAUSE semantic labels or technical duplicates are the more likely mechanism.`

2. `IF AssessRequestInfo and RequestInfoCollectDocs rise mainly for Supplier Z after adjustment for complexity and region, THEN initiate a supplier document-quality review and cohort-level backlog analysis, BECAUSE incomplete inbound bundles predict repeated collection and assessment.`

3. `IF pre-Assessment median or p90 waiting rises while Assess service time remains stable, THEN review capacity, WIP, arrivals, and assessor allocation rather than coaching execution speed, BECAUSE queueing rather than task performance is driving elapsed time.`

4. `IF high-complexity waits improve after July while standard-case waits worsen and service times remain stable, THEN audit priority fairness and reserve capacity for standard cases, BECAUSE the priority rule is redistributing delay rather than removing it.`

5. `IF October-November drift matches prior renewal-season volume, complexity, and pathway patterns, THEN apply the seasonal reference and planned surge capacity instead of declaring a novel process drift, BECAUSE recurrence with matched case mix indicates seasonal demand pressure.`

6. `IF normalized rework or conformance remains abnormal within supplier, complexity, priority, and source strata after the triggering disruption has recovered, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation and update or retrain the process model only after governance review, BECAUSE composition and logging no longer explain the residual drift.`

7. `IF December performance improves only among completed cases or alongside falling intake and complexity, THEN withhold intervention-effect claims and run maturity-adjusted daily cohort analysis, BECAUSE censoring and seasonal relief can mimic overtime or campaign benefits.`
