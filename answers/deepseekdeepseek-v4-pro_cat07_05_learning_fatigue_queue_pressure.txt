# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance patterns cannot be reduced to a single mechanism. Rather, they emerge from the interaction of case-mix dynamics, queue pressure, worker adaptation strategies, and structural features of shift-based work organization.

**The shape of the service time curve** is dominated by case-mix selection operating at multiple levels. Early in each shift, workers face the accumulated backlog from the previous shift—cases that were not cleared, disproportionately complex (complexity 0.72 vs 0.43 late), and more likely high-priority (41% vs 19%). Senior resources actively select into these complex cases early, as evidenced by the early shift pattern where 48% of first-hour cases are high complexity. This selection is rational: complex cases require fresh cognitive resources and senior expertise. As the shift progresses, the case pool shifts toward lower-complexity, routine work. The apparent "learning curve" from 18.8 to 11.4 minutes is largely a composition effect—workers are not necessarily learning; they are working on progressively simpler cases.

However, composition alone cannot explain the full 40% reduction in service time, nor does it explain the within-complexity-band patterns. Here, queue pressure emerges as the dominant mechanism in the late-shift period. When WIP exceeds 70, service times drop sharply even within the same complexity band: low-complexity cases fall from 12.7 to 9.4 minutes, high-complexity from 22.4 to 16.8 minutes. Workers visibly accelerate throughput. This is not passive fatigue but active strategic adjustment—a speed-quality tradeoff. The mechanism is likely dual: workers truncate deliberation, skip secondary checks, and compress interaction time when they perceive mounting backlog. The fact that service time falls while error rates rise (from 2.1% to 5.8% for low complexity; 4.2% to 10.9% for high complexity) is the signature of this tradeoff.

**Why errors rise only under specific conditions** is critical. Under low WIP, error rates are relatively stable across the shift (baseline 2.1% for low complexity, 4.2% for high). The error escalation appears primarily when queue pressure and shift progression coincide. The late-shift period (pos 51+) combines high WIP, accumulated mental fatigue from sustained work, and—in day and late shifts—circadian trough effects in the afternoon/evening. The interaction is multiplicative rather than additive: juniors under high WIP late in shift show 12.4% error rates, substantially higher than either late-shift baseline (4.4%) or high-WIP baseline alone would predict. Mid-level workers similarly degrade from 2.9% to 6.8%, and seniors from 2.2% to 4.9%. Experience provides partial insulation but not immunity.

**Batching and workarounds** amplify these patterns structurally. Quality Check deferral to shift end creates concentrated correction bursts that both increase measured rework and potentially mask earlier error generation (corrections attributed to QC activity rather than the original error source). Bot_Risk corrections rising under high WIP suggest that either automation reliability degrades with case characteristics during peak periods, or—more plausibly—human monitoring thoroughness drops when queue pressure is high, allowing bot errors to propagate further before correction.

**Circadian effects** are real but operate primarily through the shift structure rather than as an independent clock-time effect. Night shift's higher service times (15.2 min) reflect the different case mix (56% high complexity, more urgent exceptions) and lower queue pressure allowing deliberation, not necessarily circadian impairment. The day shift's late-shift error peak (7.5%) occurs at 15:00–17:00, which is both a circadian post-lunch dip window and the period of maximum demand and queue accumulation—disentangling these requires within-shift clock-time controls.

**The net pattern** is: early shift = complex cases + fresh resources + moderate pressure  good quality, moderate speed; mid shift = simpler cases + warmed-up resources + low pressure  best efficiency; late shift = residual complexity + queue pressure + cumulative fatigue + circadian dip  speed maximization with quality sacrifice. The system-wide error rate rises not because workers deteriorate uniformly but because the combination of high pressure, sustained work duration, and reduced cognitive resources creates conditions where the marginal cost of quality (in time) exceeds the perceived marginal benefit, given that throughput is the salient performance metric.

## 3.1 Within-resource comparisons

The core confound in aggregate data is that different workers handle different cases at different times. Within-resource analysis isolates individual adaptation.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
import statsmodels.api as sm

# Assuming log with standardized format
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(log, case_id='case:concept:name', 
                              activity_key='concept:name',
                              timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                              start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')

# Compute service time per event
log['service_time'] = pm4py.get_service_time(log, start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp',
                                               timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

# Within-resource models
for resource in log['org:resource'].unique():
    if 'Bot' in resource:
        continue
    resource_log = log[log['org:resource'] == resource]
    
    # Control for complexity
    model = sm.OLS(
        resource_log['service_time'],
        sm.add_constant(resource_log[['minutes_since_shift_start', 
                                       'case_complexity_score',
                                       'queue_length_at_start']])
    ).fit()
    
    print(f"{resource}: sequence_position effect = {model.params[1]:.3f} min/min")
    print(f"{resource}: complexity effect = {model.params[2]:.3f}")
    print(f"{resource}: queue pressure effect = {model.params[3]:.3f}")
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Worker-level conclusions can be profoundly unfair when drawn from observational event logs.** The data reflect a complex socio-technical system where workers respond to conditions they do not control. Several specific cautions apply:

**Selection bias is pervasive and difficult to fully adjust for.** Senior resources select complex cases early; juniors receive simpler cases; night staff handle urgent exceptions. Any comparison of raw error rates or service times between workers reflects these assignment patterns as much as individual capability. Even within-worker comparisons over time are confounded: the cases a worker sees at 4 PM are not a random sample of the cases they saw at 9 AM. Statistical adjustment helps but cannot recover true counterfactuals—we never observe the same worker handling the same case under different conditions.

**Measurement error in error and rework flags is likely substantial.** Not all errors are detected; detection probability varies with QC thoroughness (which varies systematically across shift); some "errors" reflect ambiguous judgments where reasonable professionals disagree. Rework flags may capture legitimate second looks rather than true corrections. If error detection is less thorough under high WIP (because QC is rushed or deferred), the observed error rate may understate the true quality degradation during peak periods—the problem may be worse than it appears.

**Queue pressure is created by management decisions about staffing, shift boundaries, and performance targets, not by workers.** When WIP exceeds 70, workers face a structural dilemma: maintain quality and let the queue grow (potentially facing criticism for slow throughput) or accelerate and accept higher error risk. The speed-quality tradeoff reflects system design, not worker negligence. Punishing workers for errors under high WIP punishes them for adapting rationally to the throughput-quality tension that management has created.

**Automation quality is not neutral.** Bot_Risk classifications that require more corrections under high WIP may indicate that the bot itself performs worse on case types arriving during peak periods, or that rushed humans override correct bot decisions. Treating bot corrections as "errors" without analyzing bot decision confidence confuses human error with automation failure.

**Punitive use of error rates (Intervention 8) is particularly dangerous.** It creates incentives to: (a) slow down when queue is high, worsening the backlog problem; (b) avoid complex cases; (c) over-document to create defensive records; (d) shift errors to downstream activities where attribution is harder. More fundamentally, it treats a systemic output as an individual failing.

**Worker load fairness** requires examining not just service times and errors but the distribution of complex cases, high-WIP periods, and shift timing across workers. If some workers are consistently assigned to high-pressure periods or complex-heavy shifts, apparent performance differences reflect structural inequity.

# Example: build dashboard data pipeline
import pm4py

def compute_dashboard_metrics(log):
    metrics = {}
    
    # 1. Service time distribution by shift hour and complexity
    log['shift_hour'] = log['minutes_since_shift_start'] // 60
    metrics['service_time_distribution'] = log.groupby(
        ['shift_hour', pd.cut(log['case_complexity_score'], bins=3)]
    )['service_time'].describe()
    
    # 3. WIP alert frequency
    metrics['wip_alert_frequency'] = (
        log[log['queue_length_at_start'] > 70]
        .groupby('shift_id')['time:timestamp']
        .count()
    )
    
    # 4. Rework cycle from performance DFG

        performance_attribute='rework_flag'
    )
    metrics['rework_dfg'] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(rework_cases)
    
    # 7. Batching indicators
    metrics['batches'] = pm4py.discover_batches(log)

        log[(log['concept:name'] == 'Quality Check') &

    )
    
    # 8. Handover volume
    metrics['handover_volume'] = log[
        log.groupby('case:concept:name')['shift_id'].transform('nunique') > 1
    ].shape[0]
    
    return metrics
