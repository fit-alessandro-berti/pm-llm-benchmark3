The plausible shared mechanism is a partial-order change: once registration is complete, two independent checks can start without one waiting for the other, while assessment still joins their outputs. Removing an unnecessary precedence edge can shorten the critical path and the queue before assessment. It can also shift arrivals into the assessment queue. Site A's observed fall in that wait supports the mechanism descriptively, but does not isolate its cause.

At B, document readiness is governed by originals law for 62% of cases. In that branch, identity can overlap receipt, but document checking cannot precede receipt. Separate reviewer pools, a one-owner case lock, fewer downstream staff, and mandatory legal review create additional queues and implementation constraints. These are site-specific paths from the intervention to actual overlap and final cycle time. Digital availability, originals requirement and delay, complexity, legal-review status, role specialization, locking, and capacity utilization are effect modifiers. They change either whether overlap can occur or whether saved verification time survives to completion.

Selection nodes mark where A and B differ; they do not make A's after-period an experiment. A's API upgrade, document staffing, huddles, and mix shift also affect outcomes. The 71% recorded overlap may partly be better logging. A visually similar control-flow diagram omits statutory guards, resource contention, durations, and measurement error. Consequently, the A contrast cannot identify B's effect without B-specific support and a test of implementation fidelity.

The service-time overlap available in a ready digital case is bounded roughly by the shorter check's 3.4-hour B median if there are no queue effects; larger gains require removing queue or handoff waiting. A's median fell nine hours while its median pre-assessment wait fell six, but its service, staffing, API and mix also changed. B's 62% originals share limits direct transfer; the 38% complement is only an upper bound on immediately ready cases. Multiplying 38% by nine hours would not estimate B's median because medians are nonlinear and the eligible cases may lie in the faster half. For originals cases, an 18-hour median receipt delay may swallow a 3.4-hour identity check if identity already runs early. B has 1,360 legal reviews and 360 supplement requests (about 26% of legal cases), with 345 recorded returns to document checking. Those repeated arrivals can make a lower average or median coexist with a worse p90. DFG adjacencies describe observed order, not durations, unique paths or lawful counterfactuals.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Eligibility and execution.** After Register, screen for statutory originals, actual document readiness, data dependence, legal-review status and complexity. For no-originals cases with ready documents and independent checks, release Identity Check and Document Check to their respective role queues together. For originals-required cases, start Identity Check and the Originals Receipt request/tracking process together; create a pending document task if useful for planning, but prohibit its execution and completion before verified receipt. Once originals arrive, release Document Check even if Identity Check remains underway. Assessment starts only when both checks finish. Pilot low-complexity, non-legal, ready-document cases first; add other strata through separately measured stages.

**Control and responsibilities.** Identity, document, assessment and legal staff retain separate sign-offs. Configure genuine parallel ownership or an auditable task-level workaround before treatment; avoid retrospective event entry as evidence of work. Begin with a cap of four active verification tasks per reviewer, subject to observed workload, and a six-case ready-for-assessment WIP alert for three assessors. Keep queue huddles and priority rules identical across study arms. Use FIFO within priority/age bands, with escalation for statutory deadlines and aged originals. Monitor each role's queue and pause new releases if assessment or legal backlog breaches prespecified limits.

**Legal and records.** After Assessment, route required cases to Legal Review; others go to decision. A supplement request opens a new document-check version, and a new originals receipt gate if the supplement legally needs originals, followed by repeat Assessment and Legal Review. The final Approve or Reject precedes Notify. Log immutable case and task IDs, role, assignment, ready/start/end timestamps, originals proof, lock state, document version, supplement cause, decision and appeal linkage.

A compact partial-order specification is:

~~~text
Register ≺ {Identity Check, Document Check} ≺ Assessment
    if documents are ready and originals are not required;
Register ≺ {Identity Check, Originals Receipt};
Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check;
{Identity Check, Document Check} ≺ Assessment
    if originals are required;
Assessment ≺ [Legal Review if required] ≺ (Approve XOR Reject) ≺ Notify;
Legal Review ≺ Request Supplement ≺ Document Check' ≺ Assessment'
    ≺ Legal Review'  (zero or more supplement repetitions).
~~~

Braces denote unordered siblings. The receipt condition and legal routing are case-data guards, not permission to bypass statutory prerequisites.

Mechanism failure and implementation failure require different evidence. Audit a sample of task intervals against worker activity and lock events. If V1 assignment produces little verified overlap, investigate task release, ownership, document readiness, and role queue availability before judging the causal mechanism. If verified overlap rises but pre-assessment waiting does not fall, the checks were not on the critical path or independent work still waited elsewhere. If that wait falls while assessment or legal wait rises, the mechanism operated but the bottleneck moved. Compare assigned groups for the primary estimate; use fidelity and mediation diagnostics to explain it, not to replace randomization with a selected “successful cases” comparison. Inspect loop frequencies and 90-day appeals before expansion.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

~~~python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', "
    "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)
a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), "
    "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

def order(nodes, edges):
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for predecessor, successor in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(predecessor, successor)
    return model

# No-originals branch: both checks precede Assessment, with no order
# between the checks.
id_d = Transition("Identity Check")
doc_d = Transition("Document Check")
ass_d = Transition("Assessment")
digital = order(
    [id_d, doc_d, ass_d],
    [(id_d, ass_d), (doc_d, ass_d)]
)

# Originals branch: identity can overlap receipt; the document check
# waits for receipt; Assessment explicitly waits for both checks.
id_o = Transition("Identity Check")
receipt = Transition("Originals Receipt")
doc_o = Transition("Document Check")
ass_o = Transition("Assessment")
originals = order(
    [id_o, receipt, doc_o, ass_o],
    [(receipt, doc_o), (id_o, ass_o), (doc_o, ass_o)]
)
verification = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [digital, originals])

# A legal review can request any number of supplements. Each repetition
# rechecks documents and repeats Assessment before another Legal Review.
request = Transition("Request Supplement")
doc_repeat = Transition("Document Check")
ass_repeat = Transition("Assessment")
redo = order(
    [request, doc_repeat, ass_repeat],
    [(request, doc_repeat), (doc_repeat, ass_repeat)]
)
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.LOOP, [Transition("Legal Review"), redo]
)
legal_optional = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), legal_loop]
)
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [Transition("Approve"), Transition("Reject")]
)
register = Transition("Register")
notify = Transition("Notify")
site_b = order(
    [register, verification, legal_optional, decision, notify],
    [
        (register, verification),
        (verification, legal_optional),
        (legal_optional, decision),
        (decision, notify),
    ]
)

for name, model in (
    ("A before", a_before), ("A after", a_after), ("B candidate", site_b)
):
    net, initial, final = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    workflow = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
    sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net, initial, final)
    print(name, "workflow net:", workflow, "sound:", sound)
    if not sound:
        print(diagnostics)

# POWL captures control-flow choices and partial orders here. The XOR
# branches are abstractions: this model cannot enforce data-dependent
# originals or legal rules, resource capacity, case locks, task durations,
# document versions, or accurate event timestamps. Those need guards,
# scheduling rules and event-log validation outside this model.
~~~

## 9. Recommendation

conditional go

Transfer the structural idea of removing an unnecessary precedence edge, subject to B's document-readiness rule. The first intervention should release identity and document checks together only for low-complexity cases that have usable documents at registration, require no originals, and have independent checks. Keep the join before assessment. For cases requiring originals, the lawful analogue is to run identity work alongside receipt and to release document checking only after receipt. Required legal review, supplement loops, final decisions and notification retain their prescribed order.

Do not copy A's universal immediate task execution, its cross-trained staffing assumptions, or its six-open-task limit. B's case lock must permit genuine parallel ownership before the pilot can test the mechanism. An open task or a back-entered timestamp is not evidence of concurrent work. Keep queue huddles, staffing, priority rules and API versions stable across randomized blocks so their effects do not get mixed into the estimate.

The expected benefit is modest and concentrated in ready-document cases. A's nine-hour median reduction is an upper-level descriptive contrast, not a B forecast: A gained document reviewers, a faster API, huddles and a simpler mix. For B's first subgroup, a three-to-seven-hour median improvement is plausible if queue waiting also falls; the whole-site median may move little because at most 38% avoid mandatory originals, and some of those still lack ready documents. The main risks are lock failure, extra verification WIP, assessment and legal congestion, repeated document work, and worse tail or quality outcomes. B's three assessors and two legal reviewers make the downstream queue especially important.

Expand first to other ready-document complexity strata only if the randomized intention-to-treat estimate shows a useful cycle reduction, audited overlap rises, statutory conformance remains perfect, and p90, rework and mature appeals stay within prespecified harm bounds. Test the originals pathway in a separate stage; do not infer its effect from the digital subgroup. Modify the release cap or add downstream capacity if verification waiting falls but assessment or legal waiting rises. Abandon the rollout if the lock prevents reliable concurrency, legal prerequisites are violated, or replicated quality and tail deterioration outweigh the measured gain. Report uncertain strata as uncertain rather than extrapolating A's aggregate result to them.

## 10. Assumption register

1. No-originals cases with ready digital documents constitute a useful subset of the 38% complement — needed for an immediate two-check pilot — audit document readiness at registration — if false, eligible volume and benefit shrink.
2. Identity and document checks in the first subgroup use sufficiently independent evidence — needed for useful simultaneous work — review data dependencies and invalidated checks — if false, restrict eligibility or retain an order.
3. Identity may lawfully start while mandatory originals are in transit — needed for the originals branch — obtain B legal sign-off and audit case rules — if false, remove that branch.
4. B can support genuine concurrent task ownership with reliable start and end events — needed to implement and measure treatment — test lock configuration against worker activity — if false, defer efficacy claims and repair the system.
5. Assessment and legal queues have enough headroom, or can be controlled, when checks complete earlier — needed for end-to-end gain — measure utilization and queue age by role — if false, throttle releases or add capacity.
6. Pilot staffing, API performance, huddles and priority rules can be kept comparable between arms — needed for causal attribution — log operational changes by block — if false, adjust the design or limit causal claims.
7. Complexity, legal status and originals requirement can be recorded before assignment — needed for safe routing and subgroup estimates — validate intake fields against files — if false, use conservative routing and postpone subgroup inference.
8. Rework and 90-day appeals capture material quality harm with consistent definitions — needed for expansion decisions — audit coding and complete follow-up — if false, add independent quality review before rollout.
