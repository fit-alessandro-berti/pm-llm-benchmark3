## 1. Feasibility model

Partial-order relation set:

```text
Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check task creation
If originals are required: Originals Receipt ≺ each corresponding Document Check
Identity Check ≺ Assessment
Document Check ≺ Assessment
Assessment ≺ Decision ≺ Notify
For every Request More Info event: (Assessment or Compliance Review) ≺ Request More Info
Request More Info ≺ new Document Check ≺ new Assessment ≺ Decision
For high-risk or high-value cases: latest relevant Assessment ≺ final Compliance Review ≺ Decision
If a high-risk/high-value case has a post-request Assessment: that Assessment ≺ new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review ≺ Decision
Final Assessment ≺ final Compliance Review, with different people performing those two activities
```

Conditional and declarative constraints:

```text
If originals are not required, Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently when task ownership and resources permit.
If originals are required, Identity Check may run before Originals Receipt; Document Check may not.
A preliminary compliance screen may precede Assessment and run alongside verification, but never substitutes for final Compliance Review.
After a Request More Info event, a fresh Document Check and Assessment are mandatory before Decision.
High-risk/high-value cases always need a final Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
A person or resource cannot perform overlapping service intervals.
Assessment or Compliance Review may initiate Request More Info; if a loop is prohibited, the resulting Decision must explicitly account for unresolved evidence.
Decision precedes Notify.
Queue estimates are uncertain forecasts, not service guarantees or reserved start times.
Concurrent work must have genuine, auditable overlapping service intervals; batch timestamps do not establish concurrency.
```

K-104 is a timing and preparation intervention: early advice and a provisional slot can shorten or better prepare final review, but do not change the decision rules. K-219 changes routing from D1 to D2. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by forbidding a second information request and requiring a decision on incomplete evidence. K-411 changes task creation and routing around a legacy lock to permit parallel verification; its benefit depends on genuine concurrency and downstream capacity.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage recognized the sanctions ambiguity at 08:50 Monday, but the first specialist advice followed A1's Assessment on Tuesday evening. Verification itself continued through the long D1 queue, so the case was not ready for Assessment until Tuesday.

**Minimal intervention:** Send only the known ambiguity and available case details to S1 at Triage, and tentatively reserve C1 time. Let identity and document work proceed normally. Treat any C1 work before Assessment as preparation, not as the final review.

**Temporal propagation:** S1 can advise during the verification wait, and C1 can pre-read material that would otherwise be encountered from scratch. Once A1 completes, C1 still has to review the final assessment and current evidence. The 20-hour Monday morning C1 queue estimate and later urgent arrivals make the reserved slot's practical value uncertain; the estimate cannot be subtracted mechanically from the case duration.

**Process-feasibility check:** Register and Triage remain first; identity and documents may overlap because originals are not required; both precede Assessment. A1's final Assessment precedes C1's final Compliance Review, and A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes separation. M1 decides only after C1, then notifies.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: reusable preparation and a protected slot produce a materially earlier final review, with conditions adjusted using earlier advice. Base: same approval with conditions, perhaps several hours sooner. Worst: sparse early evidence is reread and C1's protected slot delays urgent cases while K-104 gains little.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If early packets usually lack the decisive evidence, preliminary review is not reusable, or reserved slots are frequently overtaken by urgent work, the time benefit is weak. If early S1 advice repeatedly prevents missed sanctions issues, the quality value rises even without cycle-time savings.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Receipt at 17:30 Monday satisfied the original-document prerequisite, but D1's queue deferred the check to Tuesday. D1 completed an accurate check in four hours; afterward A2's queue, rather than document readiness alone, governed much of the wait to Assessment.

**Minimal intervention:** Assign this one standard handwritten packet to D2 after receipt, retaining I2's existing identity check and all downstream gates. Do not assume D2 begins at receipt merely because it is assigned then.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's Monday 16:00 queue forecast was five hours, versus eleven hours for D1. That supports earlier possible service, but the estimate is not a guaranteed start and D2 may take longer on difficult handwriting. If D2 completes before A2 can accept the case, the time moves into the assessment queue instead of reducing total cycle time. A clarification adds external response time, a new document check, and a new Assessment.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt precedes D2's check; identity may occur earlier; both checks precede A2's Assessment. This is not a high-risk or high-value case, so no compliance review is required. If D2 issues Request More Info, the fresh check and Assessment are mandatory before M1's Decision and Notify.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 starts earlier, reads the handwriting correctly, and the case reaches assessment a few hours sooner. Base: document readiness improves but A2 absorbs most of the gain. Worst: D2 requests clarification, adding an 8–36-hour external-response interval plus queueing and rework; a material ambiguity changes the approval decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Case-level D2 start times, handwritten-document error and clarification rates, and A2 start times after early readiness would determine whether D2 substitution helps. If D2 commonly needs clarification on this handwriting type, D1 remains preferable.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Assessment 2 still found one unresolved inconsistency. The second external request then crossed a weekend, followed by another document check and Assessment before the final compliance review. This loop changed the evidence available for the eventual approval.

**Minimal intervention:** At Assessment 2, record the unresolved fact and block Request More Info 2. Continue directly to final compliance review and a reasoned decision. The existing first request and its required new check and Assessment remain intact.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing the second request avoids its supplier wait and the subsequent document and assessment services, but it does not guarantee an equally early decision: C1 is the sole certified reviewer, must review after Assessment 2, and can remain the bottleneck. In the factual path, the avoided loop also supplied the confirmation that supported approval. The shorter path therefore trades evidence acquisition for a different decision under uncertainty.

**Process-feasibility check:** Request More Info 1 is followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. With no second request, no third check is required. The unresolved discrepancy must be explicitly documented; C1's final review follows Assessment 2, with A1 and C1 distinct, and precedes M1's decision and notification.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: C1 reviews promptly and M1 approves with enforceable conditions that contain the uncertainty, saving several days. Base: a faster conditional approval or rejection with a clear record of the gap. Worst: the evidence is insufficient to bound exposure, yielding a poorly defensible approval or a costly rejection and appeal.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If the second supplier confirmation is usually decision-critical or predicts fraud/loss, suppressing the loop is unsafe. If later confirmations rarely alter decisions and conditional controls reliably contain the uncertainty, the speed-quality trade may be more acceptable.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The legacy system created only an identity task at Triage. A case lock prevented document ownership until identity finished, so D2 did not start until 15:00 Tuesday. The eventual Assessment began the next morning after an assessment-queue wait.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create both tasks and use an authorized, logged ownership workaround. Preserve the real order constraints and record actual service starts and completions, not just task creation.

**Temporal propagation:** The Tuesday 08:00 forecasts suggest I2 and D2 both have queues, so neither necessarily starts at 09:35. Parallel task ownership can nevertheless let D2's wait and service overlap I2's interval, making both checks ready earlier. If A2 has no earlier slot, readiness advances without moving the final completion. With many cases, earlier arrivals can increase A2's wait.

**Process-feasibility check:** The case has no originals requirement, so checks may run concurrently. Both must finish before A2's Assessment, then M1's Decision precedes Notify. No compliance review is required. The logs must prove real overlap and preserve access controls; concurrent task records alone are insufficient.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 starts while I2 works and saves some of the sequential wait. Base: the case is ready sooner but reaches the same A2 slot. Worst: workload congestion or an unreliable bypass removes the gain and creates misleading audit timestamps or excess assessor queueing.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If earlier-ready cases consistently obtain earlier A2 starts without worsening other cases, the intervention is stronger. If assessment starts stay fixed or the lock workaround produces ownership or timestamp exceptions, the benefit is small or the control design needs revision.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 | Adds early S1 advice and C1 preparation; may save 1–3 hours of later C1 effort, while reserving a scarce slot. | K-104's effective final-review wait if its reservation is usable. | C1 queue for urgent and other high-risk cases if reserved time is idle or displaces them. | Cases awaiting the only certified C1 reviewer, especially urgent cases. | Mixed; possible quality gain, with neutral or negative throughput if reservation displaces work. | Early evidence completeness and the timing of urgent arrivals determine whether preparation is reusable and the reservation productive. |
| K-219 | Moves standard handwritten work from D1 to D2; rework can add D2, external, and assessment demand. | D1 queue, particularly for complex corporate work. | D2 queue and, on clarification, external-response and rework queues. | D1-dependent complex cases may benefit; other D2 cases may wait longer. | Mixed and skill-mix dependent. | Clarification probability can turn a modest queue shift into a long loop; assessment congestion can absorb time saved. |
| K-337 | Avoids a second external request, document check, and Assessment; increases unresolved-risk and possible later appeal work. | D1, A1, and supplier-response queues. | Potential future appeals, disputes, loss handling, or remedial review. | Other cases may move sooner through D1/A1; affected applicants or downstream reviewers bear decision risk. | Faster short-run throughput; uncertain quality-adjusted effect. | Whether the missing confirmation changes the decision or exposure is case-specific and potentially high impact. |
| K-411 | Same verification services, shifted earlier and overlapped; possibly more simultaneous I2/D2 workload. | Cases waiting for sequential task creation/ownership. | A2 assessment queue if early-ready cases arrive in a burst; potentially D2 at peak overlap. | Other cases competing for D2 and A2 slots. | Likely small local gain; system sign depends on whether assessment capacity absorbs the shifted arrivals. | Queue displacement and synchronized readiness can erase the local gain; timestamps can misstate concurrency. |

At 100 similar cases in a week, K-104's reservations become a capacity allocation policy for the only fully certified reviewer. Even if each preliminary packet saves one to three hours of final reading, C1 time is not necessarily saved at the same hour the work arrives. Reservations made before evidence is ready can strand capacity or push urgent cases behind protected slots. A pilot must count both target-case waits and C1 waits for non-target cases.

K-219 shifts work rather than creating document capacity. D1 may clear complex corporate cases faster, while D2's queue grows. If D2 clarification rates rise on handwritten packets, each clarification adds external waiting plus repeat document and assessment work; if the rate is low, substitution can relieve the D1 bottleneck. K-337 reduces expected second-loop work and can free D1 and A1 time, but faster decisions are not a free capacity gain if unresolved cases generate disputes, appeals, or loss work downstream.

K-411 may make verification completion earlier without making final completion earlier. One hundred cases ready together can move the bottleneck to A1/A2, where assessors may have less flexibility than the verification pools. Demand also varies by day: a queue delay that fits within a weekday can cross a weekend, and slow supplier responses have highly skewed durations. Thus avoided loops can save a whole weekend in some cases, while a small queue shift may save almost nothing in others.

Finally, task creation time, claimed service time, and actual service intervals must be kept distinct. A batch-entered document task can look concurrent with identity work even when no work overlapped. Realtime start, pause, ownership, and completion events are essential both to assess K-411 and to estimate the real capacity transferred by all four policies.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411-like digital, standard, no-original cases for which identity and document checks are independently permissible and both pools have authorized task access. | K-104-like high-risk/high-value cases with an identifiable early ambiguity, no originals requirement, and a preliminary packet that can be assembled at Triage. |
| assignment rule | Randomize or alternate eligible cases within day and workload bands between controlled parallel task creation and current lock behavior. | Stagger eligible cases between early S1 packet plus provisional C1 reservation and current triage handling; retain an urgent-case override. |
| sample/support logic | Begin with a small staged cohort, for example 20–30 treated cases and contemporaneous matched controls; extend if queue and quality estimates remain imprecise. Balance by weekday and pool load. | Use several C1 scheduling windows with treated and comparison cases; record every displaced or expedited case. Expand only when enough completed high-risk cases support review. |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify elapsed time, with both case-level change and same-week peer queue effects. | Register-to-Notify elapsed time and time from final Assessment to final Compliance Review, alongside C1 delay for other cases. |
| process-path outcome | Fraction with verified overlapping service intervals; time from both checks complete to Assessment start. | Share of preliminary work reused, final-review duration, and proportion with final Compliance Review after the latest Assessment. |
| quality outcome | Audit sample of document/identity exceptions, decision reversals, and control violations. | Independent audit of sanctions handling, conditions, escalation completeness, and subsequent decision changes. |
| queue-spillover outcome | D2 and A2 wait-time distributions for treated and non-treated cases; count concurrency peaks. | C1 waits, urgent-case waits, unused reserved time, and displacement of cases outside the pilot. |
| logging validation | Reconcile task creation, ownership, start, pause, and completion events; confirm actual simultaneous service intervals. | Audit packet version and timestamps; distinguish preliminary work from final review and confirm final reviewer identities. |
| stopping rule | Pause on any unauthorized access, false concurrency record, or material deterioration in A2 or D2 peer waits; otherwise review after the staged cohort. | Pause if urgent C1 cases are delayed beyond the agreed operating threshold, reserved slots go unused repeatedly, or an audit finds a compliance-control failure. |

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust because it preserves the same verification and decision requirements while removing a technical barrier to work that is already allowed to run in parallel. Its benefit is modest and may disappear at the assessment queue, but the intervention is reversible and a well-logged pilot can show whether genuine overlap advances final completion or merely shifts waiting downstream.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential speed. It removes the supplier confirmation that resolved the last discrepancy, so the shorter path is a different evidence and decision policy. The case must not be described as an ordinary cycle-time reduction: the unresolved fact can change approval, conditions, rejection, exposure, and defensibility. Any use needs explicit documentation and governance of the acceptable decision under uncertainty.

K-411's local counterfactual is least transportable to a 100-case rollout. One case can overlap two checks without noticeably changing A2 demand; a cohort can synchronize readiness and move the bottleneck into Assessment. K-104 also has major interference risk because all cases compete for one certified reviewer, but its queue impact is directly observable.

The most valuable additional data are event-level timestamps for task creation, ownership, actual service start and stop, pauses, and queue entry/exit; resource-specific workloads and concurrent assignments; and C1 reservations versus actual use. For quality and rework, link each Request More Info to its initiating assessment, external response duration, new checks, and decision changes. Stratify handwriting and document complexity for D2 comparisons. Track non-target cases too: without peer queue waits, apparent target-case gains cannot reveal displacement. Record calendar and weekend boundaries and distinguish queue forecasts from actual service starts.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — Identity and Document Checks are both prerequisites for Assessment, with concurrency permitted only under the stated original-document and ownership rules.
2. **structural** — A high-risk or high-value case requires final Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
3. **temporal** — Queue estimates give rough ordering and likely waiting pressure, but not guaranteed start times or service durations.
4. **resource** — C1 remains the only fully certified compliance reviewer during the relevant window.
5. **behavioral** — Early S1/C1 preparation is partly reusable when later evidence arrives, but may need updating or rereading.
6. **quality** — D2's higher clarification risk on difficult handwriting is plausible but not quantified for K-219.
7. **behavioral** — If K-337 must proceed without a second confirmation, M1 records the unresolved discrepancy and makes an explicit, reasoned decision.
8. **interference** — At 100 cases, increased or shifted work changes shared queues; queue displacement and bottleneck migration are possible even when a target case improves.
