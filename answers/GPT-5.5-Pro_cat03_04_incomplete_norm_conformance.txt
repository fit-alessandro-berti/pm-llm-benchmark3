## 1. Conformance semantics

I would not interpret the observed graph as a closed-world compliance checklist. The norm is explicitly **closed-world only for forbidden relations** and for **mandatory relations inside their stated scope**. Everything else is open-world: an edge that is not listed may be legitimate, incompletely modeled, governed by another policy, or merely surprising.

**Forbidden behavior** means an observed relation contradicts an explicit prohibition. These are the strongest findings because absence of modeling coverage is not relevant: N3 forbids `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, T6 forbids `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`, and E10 forbids `Sensor Heartbeat` linked to `Invoice`. Even if rare, these require containment or data repair.

**Missing mandatory behavior** means a required relation is absent within its stated scope. However, the scope matters. N4 applies only to packages intended for external shipment. E8 applies to `Record Payment` events except those explicitly typed as unapplied cash. N1 says every newly created standard order should eventually enter validation, but the observed OC-DFG approximates this with directly-follows behavior, which is stricter than the business rule.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** means an observed relation is not specified but also not forbidden. Examples include `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)`, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order`. These may be important operational signals, but not violations under this norm.

**Typical-pattern absence** means a desirable or common relation is missing, but legitimate alternatives exist. N2 and N5 are typical, not mandatory. Therefore `Issue Invoice` without observed `Record Payment` is not automatically nonconforming because write-offs, credit notes, credit terms, and label immaturity may explain it.

**Likely data-quality error** means the graph contains a relation that probably comes from extraction, joining, typing, or identifier problems rather than real process behavior. The `Sensor Heartbeat`–`Invoice` edge is the clearest case because the context says it was produced by a join on a reused numeric identifier.

**Abstraction mismatch** means the graph abstraction is too coarse or too local for the norm. The main example is N1: “eventually enter validation” has been approximated as `Create Order -> Validate Order`. If `Manual Compliance Review` or another review event occurs between creation and validation, the directly-follows edge disappears even though the lifecycle may still comply.

**Insufficient evidence** means the summary counts are not enough to decide whether the behavior is compliant. Graph counts rarely tell us object-level ordering, event identity, event attributes, terminal states, age of open cases, or whether one anomalous edge and another anomalous edge refer to the same event.

Open-world interpretation prevents over-classifying unlisted edges as violations. Closed-world interpretation is still appropriate for explicit prohibitions and scoped mandatory obligations. The main audit task is therefore not “is this edge in the norm?” but “does this observation contradict a forbidden rule, miss a mandatory scoped relation, deviate from a typical pattern, expose a data problem, or reveal that the norm is incomplete?”

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can establish object-type-specific directly-follows relations: for a given object type, it summarizes which activities immediately follow which earlier activities along that object’s projected lifecycle. It is useful for detecting suspicious local sequences such as the 2 `Order: Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` observations under N3. Since N3 is explicitly forbidden, the presence of this directly-follows relation is a strong alert.

However, an OC-DFG cannot by itself establish the full business semantics of “eventually,” “legitimate exception,” or “same business case.” N1 is the central example. The norm says every newly created standard Order should eventually enter validation, but the summary approximates this as `Create Order -> Validate Order`. That approximation is risky. If an order follows `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review -> Validate Order`, then the direct `Create -> Validate` edge is absent, while the eventual validation obligation may still be satisfied.

Counts and scopes are also critical. `Create Order -> Validate Order` appears for 98 of 100 newly created Orders. This is not automatically two noncompliant orders; it is two orders lacking the direct-follows proxy. Similarly, `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` appears for 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages. Because N4 is mandatory for externally shipped packages, those two cases require investigation, but the OC-DFG alone cannot tell whether the pack event was missing, mislabeled, separated by an intermediate event, or outside the intended scope.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction and suspicious at another. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` is not listed in the norm, but N2 explicitly allows intermediate invoice, review, or fulfilment events before close. Therefore it is not an OC-DFG violation. Conversely, `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` is suspicious precisely because it contradicts an explicit forbidden sequence for the same Order.

### OTG

An OTG summarizes object-type relationships such as interactions, cobirth, and codeath. It can show whether required object-type interactions are present at an aggregate level. For example, `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` with weight 1450 broadly supports T1, and `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` with weight 200 broadly supports T2.

But an OTG cannot prove that every required event had the required object link. The aggregate `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` edge has weight 200, yet the ET–OT summary says only 198 `Dispatch Package` events link to Delivery. Both can be true if Package–Delivery interactions occur in other events, if weights count object pairs rather than dispatch events, or if carrier attributes produce related but not equivalent evidence.

The OTG is strongest for explicit forbidden object-lifecycle relations. `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` with weight 1 violates T6 unless the birth inference is wrong. Still, the OTG cannot tell whether the same raw event truly created both objects, whether timestamps were collapsed, or whether an ingestion batch made two lifecycles appear co-born.

Unspecified OTG edges must not be treated as noncompliance. `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` is explicitly outside this norm under T4. `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` is outside this norm under T5. `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` is not governed at all, and the context gives a plausible explanation: both lifecycles end in the same confirmation event.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT view links event types to object types. It is the best of the three families for detecting missing mandatory object participation in events. E2 and E3 say every `Add Item` event must relate to both Order and Item. The summary has 1450 `Add Item`–Item links and 1448 `Add Item`–Order links, so at least two item interactions may be missing an Order link, unless weights reflect different cardinalities per event. E6 similarly flags the two `Dispatch Package` events lacking Delivery relations. E8 flags one `Record Payment` linked to Payment but not Invoice, unless it is explicitly typed as unapplied cash, which this snapshot says it is not.

ET–OT cannot establish temporal lifecycle order. It cannot detect `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`; that is an OC-DFG-style temporal relation. It also cannot decide whether an unlinked invoice is unpaid, written off, outside the extraction window, or wrongly joined.

A relation can be conforming in ET–OT but still suspicious in OC-DFG, or vice versa. `Dispatch Package`–Package is fully present at 200 under E5, but `Package: Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` is only 198 of 200 under N4. That means dispatch events are well linked to packages, while package lifecycle sequencing may still be incomplete. Conversely, `Manual Compliance Review`–RiskCase is explicitly unspecified and legitimate under E9, but when inserted between `Create Order` and `Validate Order`, it can make the OC-DFG direct-follows proxy for N1 look nonconforming even if the eventual validation rule is satisfied.

The main methodological danger is treating an “eventually expected” business rule as a directly-follows rule. Directly-follows is a local adjacency condition; eventual validation is a lifecycle reachability condition. A conformance check should therefore ask: for each created standard Order, is there any later `Validate Order` before terminal closure or cancellation, subject to allowed exceptions? It should not rely only on the direct edge `Create Order -> Validate Order`.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory behavior**

* N1: `Create Order -> Validate Order` observed for 98 of 100 newly created Orders. Potential mandatory gap only if the two Orders never eventually validate and are in scope as standard Orders.
* N4: `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` observed for 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages. Potential mandatory gap if those packages were externally shipped and no legitimate packing record exists.
* T1/E2/E3: `Add Item` should link Order and Item. The 1450 Item links versus 1448 Order links suggest two possible missing Order links, subject to cardinality confirmation.
* T2/E5/E6: `Dispatch Package` should link Package and Delivery. Package links are complete at 200; Delivery links are short by 2 at ET–OT level, despite carrier attributes.
* E7: `Record Payment`–Payment appears complete at 93.
* E8: `Record Payment`–Invoice is missing for one event unless that event is explicitly retyped as unapplied cash.

**Forbidden behavior**

* N3: 2 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` Order relations are violations unless caused by wrong timestamps, wrong object links, or an unmodeled cancellation reversal.
* T6: 1 `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` relation is forbidden unless the cobirth inference is an extraction artifact.
* E10: 1 `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice relation is forbidden and is likely a data-quality error from reused numeric identifiers.

**Typical behavior**

* N2: `Validate Order -> Close Order` appears for 83 Orders. Its absence in other cases is not a violation because invoice, review, or fulfilment events may intervene.
* N5: `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` appears for 92 of 110 Invoices. The 4 write-offs and 14 no-successor invoices are deviations from the typical payment path, not automatic noncompliance.
* T3: `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` appears 92 times. Missing invoice-payment interactions may reflect unapplied cash, open terms, write-offs, or observation-window truncation.

**Unregulated behavior**

* `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` for 72 Orders is unregulated and plausibly legitimate.
* `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` for 3 Orders is unregulated; it becomes relevant to N1 only if those Orders never eventually validate.
* `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` is explicitly unspecified under T4.
* `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` is explicitly unspecified under T5.
* `Manual Compliance Review`–RiskCase is explicitly unspecified under E9.
* `Manual Compliance Review`–Customer is unregulated by this norm; it may be governed by customer-data or compliance-review policy.
* `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` is unregulated; it would change classification only if lifecycle co-death is later forbidden or constrained.
* `Reopen Order -> Close Order` is explicitly unspecified under N6; it would change classification only if a reopen policy defines allowed triggers and required follow-up steps.

def semantic_class_for(rule: NormRule, observed_count: Optional[int]) -> str:
    """
    Adds a business interpretation layer on top of raw PM4Py output.
    In production, observed_count should come from robust adapters over
    observed_ocdfg/observed_otg/observed_etot, plus object/event-level checks.
    """
    if rule.status == "forbidden":
        return "forbidden_observed" if observed_count and observed_count > 0 else "forbidden_absent_ok"

    if rule.status == "mandatory":
        if observed_count is None:
            return "needs_scope_and_event_level_check"
        if observed_count == 0:
            return "missing_mandatory_candidate"
        return "mandatory_observed_but_scope_or_cardinality_may_still_need_check"

    if rule.status == "typical":
        if observed_count is None or observed_count == 0:
            return "typical_absent_not_violation"
        return "typical_observed"

    if rule.status == "unspecified":
        return "unregulated_by_this_norm"

    return "unknown_status"

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**

First, contain the two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` cases under N3. Retrieve the exact Order, Package, Delivery, cancellation, and dispatch events. If dispatch is pending, stop it. If shipment already happened, assess customer communication, inventory reversal, billing exposure, and whether the cancellation was later reversed.

Second, quarantine the forbidden financial-object data anomalies: the `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice edge under E10 and the single `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge under T6. The sensor relation should be removed from any compliance report until the join-key defect is fixed. The cobirth event should be inspected before deciding whether it is true process behavior or extraction artifact.

**Data repair**

Repair namespacing for sensor and financial identifiers. Backfill or create Delivery objects for the two dispatches that have only carrier references, unless the norm is revised to allow carrier-reference-only dispatches. For the one `Record Payment` without Invoice, either link the correct invoice or relabel the event as unapplied cash with an explicit subtype. Reconcile the two `Add Item` events lacking Order links.

**Process investigation**

For the two Orders missing direct `Create Order -> Validate Order`, run an object-level reachability check: did validation occur later after manual review or another event? For the two packages missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, inspect package traces to determine whether packing was skipped, mislabeled, or separated by an intermediate event. For the 18 invoices without typical payment successor, perform aging analysis rather than treating all as exceptions.

**Norm revision**

Revise N1 to distinguish eventual validation from direct adjacency. Clarify cancellation/reopen semantics. Add explicit exception labeling for unapplied cash. Decide whether carrier references are acceptable substitutes for Delivery objects. Cross-reference customer and risk-review policies instead of silently treating those edges as violations.

**Monitoring**

Create separate dashboards for forbidden relations, mandatory scoped gaps, typical-path aging, and open-world unregulated relations. Forbidden relations should trigger immediate alerts even at count 1. Mandatory gaps should trigger case-level investigation with scope checks. Typical deviations should be monitored by age, value, and business materiality, not merely by frequency. Unregulated rare behavior should be trended but not escalated solely because it is absent from the normative graph.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The robust conclusions are that `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` under N3, `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` under T6, and `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice under E10 contradict explicit forbidden rules if the extracted relations are correct. The mandatory-edge gaps around N1, N4, E2, E6, and E8 require investigation, but their final classification depends on scope, event-level cardinality, and whether the graph abstraction is too strict. The `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice relation strongly depends on a suspected data-quality issue, and the payment-without-invoice case depends on whether unapplied cash is formally labeled. The graph summaries alone cannot decide object-level causality, exact event identity, legitimate business exceptions, observation-window maturity, or whether unlisted relations are governed by another policy.
