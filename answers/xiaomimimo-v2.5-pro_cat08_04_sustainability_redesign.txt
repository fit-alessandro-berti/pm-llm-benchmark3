Here is a comprehensive process-redesign proposal grounded in the event-log evidence, aiming to balance environmental sustainability with service, quality, and resilience.

### 1. System boundary and assumptions
**Environmental Scope (Inside Boundary):**
*   **Gate-to-Gate Manufacturing:** Energy consumption (`energy_kwh`), material scrap (`material_scrap_kg`), and machine utilization (CNC/Heat Treat) associated with Made-to-Order (MTO) and Emergency production.
*   **Logistics & Packaging:** Transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`), packaging materials (`package_material_kg`), and rework materials/energy for both packaging and production.
*   **Upstream & Downstream Impacts:** Return trips (e.g., empty dedicated vans) are partially allocated to the delivery emissions.

**Outside Boundary:**
*   Customer use-phase and end-of-life (spare parts prolong the life of industrial machinery, generating an overwhelmingly positive lifecycle impact, but this is outside the distributor's direct operational control).
*   IT infrastructure and administrative overhead.

**Uncertainties & Assumptions:**
*   It is assumed that `transport_kgco2e` includes upstream carrier emissions (Well-to-Wheel).
*   We assume demand forecast accuracy remains constant; thus, larger production batches inherently carry a higher risk of obsolescence.
*   Rework loops (`Rework production`, `Rework packaging`) include the full energy and material footprint of the repeated steps.

### 2. Sustainability-related process indicators (SPIs)
To monitor trade-offs, we define a multi-criteria indicator set directly mapped to event attributes:

*   **Energy Efficiency:** `Avg energy_kwh` per *on-time* delivered order. (Focuses energy spend on value-added deliveries).
*   **Transport Intensity:** `Avg transport_kgCO2e` per order, filtered by `route_mode` and segmented by `case_type`.
*   **Material Yield:** Total waste per order (`material_scrap_kg` + `package_material_kg`), monitored specifically for paths containing `Rework flag = true`.
*   **Rework Rate:** % of orders traversing `Rework production` or `Rework packaging`, stratified by `machine_group` and `route_mode`.
*   **Batching Efficiency vs. Obsolescence:** Average `Batch wait` duration vs. % of batched items unsold/unshipped after 14 days.
*   **Service Conformance:** % of orders meeting `promised_lead_time_hours` (Late delivery %).
*   **Resilience Buffer:** % of Service-contract orders with active alternative routes during simulated carrier disruption events.

### 3. Intervention options
**1. Strict SP1 Enforcement & Smart Routing (S4)**
*   *Target:* Standard-stock Service-contract (16% of volume).
*   *Mechanism:* Automatically route to `parcel_ground` with priority picking if `promised_lead_time_hours` allows an >85% probability of on-time delivery. Reserve `parcel_air` only for insufficient slack.
*   *Effect:* Massive drop in transport emissions (currently 7.8 kgCO2e/order, mostly air).
*   *Evidence Needed:* Compare `promised_lead_time_hours` vs. historical ground transit times for the specific destination.

**2. Dynamic Batching with Time Caps (S2)**
*   *Target:* MTO Regular & Service-contract (`Batch wait` -> `CNC production`).
*   *Mechanism:* Allow batching to reach optimal machine energy levels (e.g., batch size  12 for CNC_A) but impose a strict maximum wait time (e.g., 24h) to protect cycle times.
*   *Effect:* Reduces `energy_kwh` per part while bounding late-delivery risk (currently 18-21% for batched MTO).
*   *Evidence Needed:* Simulation of batch arrival rates to predict wait times under capped conditions.

**3. CNC Machine Shift (S3)**
*   *Target:* MTO production currently routed to `CNC_B`.
*   *Mechanism:* Shift production to `CNC_A` when queue lengths and due-date slack permit. `CNC_B` has a 18% rework rate vs. 9% for `CNC_A`.
*   *Effect:* Halves production rework, significantly reducing hidden `material_scrap_kg` and `energy_kwh`.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Real-time queue lengths for CNC_A vs. CNC_B and remaining slack to `promised_lead_time_hours`.

**4. Consolidated Trucking for Stock (S5)**
*   *Target:* Standard-stock Regular orders (39% of volume).
*   *Mechanism:* Replace individual `parcel_ground` with `consolidated_truck` running twice daily.
*   *Effect:* Reduces transport emissions by 35-55% per order.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Order clustering analysis to ensure the 12-24h consolidation wait does not violate Standard-stock SLAs.

**5. Packaging & Route Compatibility Check (S6)**
*   *Target:* All segments, specifically paths with `Rework packaging`.
*   *Mechanism:* Implement a pre-shipment digital check ensuring `package_material_kg` and reinforcement match the assigned `route_mode` and part geometry.
*   *Effect:* Eliminates the 620 rework-packaging events, saving materials and preventing damage-related returns.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Root cause analysis of the current 620 `Rework packaging` instances.

**6. Inspection Quality Gate (S7)**
*   *Target:* `Inspection` -> `Rework production` loops.
*   *Mechanism:* Add a rigorous quality protocol at the `inspection_cell` for high-rework machine/part combos to catch defects before packaging or shipment.
*   *Effect:* Prevents downstream customer returns and the massive hidden environmental cost of return logistics and total rework.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Pareto analysis of defect types causing post-inspection returns.

**7. True vs. Precautionary Critical-Downtime Split (S9)**
*   *Target:* Critical-downtime Service-contract (11% of volume).
*   *Mechanism:* Split the category. "True Downtime" gets `dedicated_van` or `parcel_air`. "Precautionary Urgent" gets priority `consolidated_truck` or ground parcel.
*   *Effect:* Reduces the 52.0 kgCO2e/order footprint of dedicated vans (often returning empty) without jeopardizing active industrial outages.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Upstream data or order-entry flags distinguishing active machine failure vs. inventory replenishment urgency.

**8. Validated Exception Logging for Dedicated Vans (S8 & S12)**
*   *Target:* Dedicated van usage.
*   *Mechanism:* Enforce SP2 by requiring a mandatory justification event (e.g., `active_line_down = true`) before a van can be dispatched. Unjustified vans default to shared emergency couriers.
*   *Effect:* Curtails the 31% SP2 violation rate, reducing unnecessary high-emission transport.
*   *Evidence Needed:* Post-implementation conformance checking of the `justification` attribute.

### 5. Rebound and displacement risks
1.  **Batching & Obsolescence:** Pushing MTO batches to larger sizes (e.g., >12 items) to save machine `energy_kwh` directly increases the 6% risk of items becoming obsolete if customer demand fluctuates, displacing energy savings with complete material waste.
2.  **Transport Consolidation & Emergency Expediting:** Shifting orders to `consolidated_truck` to save `transport_kgCO2e` introduces a 12-24h wait. If an order misses the consolidation cutoff, the panic response will force the next order onto `parcel_air` or `dedicated_van`, causing a massive rebound in emissions.
3.  **Packaging Policy & Over-Wrapping:** Strict enforcement of Quality Policy Q1 (reinforced packaging for MTO > 8kg) prevents damage returns, but may lead to over-packaging lightweight or low-risk MTO parts, displacing transport emissions with high `package_material_kg` waste.
4.  **CNC Routing & Capacity Crunch:** Shifting orders from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` to reduce rework (`material_scrap_kg`) could overwhelm `CNC_A`, drastically spiking cycle times and forcing orders into `Emergency production` (which uses high-energy, rapid processes).
5.  **Partial Shipment Reduction & Customer Downtime:** Eliminating partial shipments reduces transport emissions and packaging waste, but delays the delivery of available critical items. For a `Critical-downtime` customer, this could force *them* to halt production, displacing the distributor's footprint onto a much larger industrial footprint.
6.  **Customer Behavioral Rebound:** If standard-stock customers know that the distributor defaults to slow, consolidated, green shipping to save emissions, they may hoard spare parts or artificially inflate order sizes, destabilizing demand forecasting and increasing upstream inventory emissions.

### 6. Balanced recommendation
We recommend a phased, data-driven package that prioritizes **high-impact, low-risk conformance fixes first**, followed by **smart routing and capacity management**.

**Phase 1: Zero-Emission-Cost Service Improvements (Weeks 1-4)**
*   **Enforce SP1 and SP2:** Implement strict conformance rules. Only allow `parcel_air` for Standard-stock if ground transit probability is <85%. Mandate digital justification for `dedicated_van` (Interventions 1 & 8). *Why:* This cuts massive transport emissions without hurting true service levels.
*   **Deploy Packaging Checks (S6):** Eliminate incompatible packaging before it hits the ship station. *Why:* Saves materials and prevents quality-driven returns.

**Phase 2: Smart Production & Logistics Balancing (Months 2-3)**
*   **Dynamic Batching with Time Caps (S2):** Implement algorithmic batching for CNC and Heat Treat. If a batch hits the time cap (e.g., 24 hours) before reaching optimal size, it triggers a smaller, immediate batch. *Why:* Balances machine energy savings against late-delivery risks.
*   **Conditional CNC Routing (S3):** Route MTO parts to `CNC_A` *only* if the current queue is below a specific threshold. If `CNC_A` is congested, route to `CNC_B` but trigger the enhanced **Inspection Quality Gate (S7)**. *Why:* Mitigates the capacity crunch rebound risk while prioritizing lower rework.
*   **Consolidated Trucking with Priority Tiers (S5):** Consolidate standard stock but create an "Express Ground" tier for orders nearing their SLA limit.

**Phase 3: Strategic Process Redesign (Month 4+)**
*   **Segment Splitting (S9):** Create an intake questionnaire to separate "Precautionary Urgent" from "True Critical-Downtime". Precautionary orders use priority ground shipping; true emergencies keep dedicated vans.
*   **Carbon-aware Promise Dates (S10):** For Regular MTO customers, present a "Green Option" with a slightly longer lead time that guarantees ground shipping and optimized batching, rewarding them (via discount or loyalty) for accepting a lower environmental footprint.

### 7. Validation and governance plan
To ensure this redesign achieves its goals without degrading operations, we will utilize continuous process mining:

*   **Conformance Monitoring:**
    *   *SP1/SP2/Q1 Checks:* Real-time alerts if air transport is selected with >24h slack, if a van lacks a justification event, or if reinforced packaging is skipped for MTO parts > 8kg.
    *   *Exception Rates:* Monitor the `Cancel due to delay` and `Rework` frequencies. If they exceed the baseline (530 cancels, 830 reworks), the specific intervention triggering the spike is automatically paused.
*   **Segment-Level Service Monitoring:**
    *   We will track the p90 cycle time for Standard-stock Service-contract closely. If the shift from air to ground causes the p90 to breach the `promised_lead_time_hours`, the algorithm will dynamically loosen the SP1 constraint for that specific region/segment.
*   **Sustainability Exception Governance:**
    *   Implement the exception log (S12). Any human override of a "Green" rule (e.g., forcing Air for a standard stock order) requires a reason code. Monthly audits of these exceptions will distinguish between valid resilience actions (e.g., carrier strikes, actual emergencies) and policy drift, ensuring that sustainability gains are not quietly eroded.
