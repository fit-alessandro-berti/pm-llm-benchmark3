# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

### 1.1 Material Assumptions
1. **Case Boundaries & Lifecycle**: Every onboarding case represents the evaluation of a single prospective vendor. The process is strictly instantiated by `Receive Onboarding Request` and terminates upon reaching `Close Onboarding`.
2. **Cardinality & Exclusivity**: A case results in either the commercial onboarding and enablement of the vendor (`Activate Vendor`) or formal rejection (`Reject Vendor`). Under normative governance, these terminal outcomes are strictly mutually exclusive.
3. **Concurrency Semantics of Initial Triage**: `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute concurrently (interleaved or in parallel) following receipt of the request. All three checks must complete prior to the execution of `Classify Risk`.
4. **Clarification Loops**: Clarifications may be requested dynamically during onboarding (typically during initial validation or prior to risk classification). Each `Request Clarification` must be paired with and resolved by a `Receive Clarification` before another clarification request can be issued and before any final outcome is reached.
5. **Observability Gap**: The event log records control-flow activities, execution timestamps, and user identifiers (`org:resource`). It does not contain an explicit payload attribute denoting "emergency status" or external joint management approvals.
6. **Temporal Semantics**: Timestamps denote the completion instant of discrete tasks. Elapsed time between activities represents latency (dwell plus processing time).

---

### 1.2 Analysis of the R5/R6 Policy Conflict
The scenario presents a direct normative contradiction between two organizational stakeholders:
* **Legal Department Policy (R5)**: Asserts a strict, non-negotiable compliance constraint. If `Sanctions Escalation` is triggered, an `Obtain Legal Opinion` must strictly precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances. From Legal's perspective, activating an entity subject to sanctions scrutiny without written legal clearance introduces catastrophic statutory and criminal liability.
* **Operations Department Policy (R6)**: Asserts a business-continuity override. Under emergency conditions, `Activate Vendor` may precede `Obtain Legal Opinion`, provided two conditions are met:
  1. The bypass is jointly authorized outside the event log;
  2. The legal opinion arrives within a strict deadline of 24 hours post-activation.

#### Resolution Option 1: Strict Compliance Enforcement (Legal-First)
* **Mechanics**: Enforce R5 as an immutable hard constraint within the procedural backbone and declarative rules. Any case where `Activate Vendor` occurs without a prior `Obtain Legal Opinion` (when `Sanctions Escalation` has fired) is flagged as a fatal conformance failure (`fail`).
* **Compliance & Operational Risk**: Eliminates regulatory risk regarding sanctions breaches. However, it creates severe operational rigidity: legitimate business emergencies (e.g., critical supply shortages) are blocked or flagged as non-compliant, generating false alarms and process friction.

#### Resolution Option 2: Operational Permissiveness with External Audit Gate (Operations-First)
* **Mechanics**: Relax the control-flow order between `Activate Vendor` and `Obtain Legal Opinion`. The automated engine verifies that the time difference $\Delta t = t(\text{Obtain Legal Opinion}) - t(\text{Activate Vendor}) \le 24\text{ hours}$. Because the event log lacks the emergency attribute and joint authorization proof, the case cannot automatically pass; it is assigned a conditional status (`policy-dependent` or `warning`), halting `Release First Payment` until an out-of-band compliance audit validates the offline authorization.
* **Compliance & Operational Risk**: Preserves operational agility. However, the compliance risk is substantial: activating a vendor prior to legal clearance creates an interim period of unmitigated legal exposure. If the legal opinion ultimately determines that the vendor is sanctioned, the organization has already legally onboarded an illicit entity, risking severe regulatory sanctions.

---

### 1.3 Chosen Resolution for the Implemented Model
**Resolution 1 (Strict Compliance Enforcement)** is adopted as the canonical rule for the baseline procedural and DECLARE models. In anti-money laundering (AML) and international trade sanctions compliance, regulatory penalties are strict-liability offenses. An automated system must not assume out-of-band authorization when no log artifact exists to prove it. 

To prevent operational blindness, the hybrid architecture deploys Resolution 2 inside the **Resource- and Data-Aware Validation Layer** as an exception-handling audit gate. When an emergency activation occurs within the 24-hour SLA, the system diverts the case into a high-priority "Emergency Audit" queue rather than dropping it silently.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

### 3.1 Compact Formal Description
The procedural backbone is formulated as a block-structured Process Tree $Q$, which maps directly to a 1-safe, sound Workflow Net:

$$Q = \rightarrow \Big( a_{\text{rec}}, \; + ( a_{\text{cmd}}, a_{\text{rss}}, a_{\text{vba}} ), \; * ( \tau, \rightarrow ( a_{\text{req\_c}}, a_{\text{rec\_c}} ) ), \; a_{\text{class}}, \; X \big( a_{\text{rej}}, \; \rightarrow( a_{\text{mgr}}, a_{\text{act}}, a_{\text{pay}} ), \; \rightarrow( a_{\text{hr\_route}}, a_{\text{cmp}}, a_{\text{act}}, a_{\text{pay}} ) \big), \; a_{\text{close}} \Big)$$

Where:
* $a_{\text{rec}} = \text{Receive Onboarding Request}$
* $a_{\text{cmd}} = \text{Capture Master Data}$, $a_{\text{rss}} = \text{Run Sanctions Screening}$, $a_{\text{vba}} = \text{Verify Bank Account}$
* $a_{\text{req\_c}} = \text{Request Clarification}$, $a_{\text{rec\_c}} = \text{Receive Clarification}$
* $a_{\text{class}} = \text{Classify Risk}$
* $a_{\text{rej}} = \text{Reject Vendor}$
* $a_{\text{mgr}} = \text{Manager Approval}$, $a_{\text{cmp}} = \text{Compliance Approval}$
* $a_{\text{hr\_route}} = X \big( a_{\text{edd}}, \; \rightarrow( a_{\text{esc}}, a_{\text{edd}}, a_{\text{leg}} ) \big)$
* $a_{\text{edd}} = \text{Enhanced Due Diligence}$, $a_{\text{esc}} = \text{Sanctions Escalation}$, $a_{\text{leg}} = \text{Obtain Legal Opinion}$
* $a_{\text{act}} = \text{Activate Vendor}$, $a_{\text{pay}} = \text{Release First Payment}$, $a_{\text{close}} = \text{Close Onboarding}$

### 3.3 Structural & Behavioral Analysis
1. **Concurrency**: An AND-split (`+`) immediately forks execution after `Receive Onboarding Request` into three parallel branches: `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account`. An AND-join synchronizes all three branches before downstream execution.
2. **Clarification Loops**: Modeled via the loop operator `* ( tau, -> ( Request Clarification, Receive Clarification ) )`. The `tau` child permits zero clarification cycles (skipping directly to classification). If a clarification is needed, it strictly obligates `Request Clarification` followed by `Receive Clarification` before looping back or proceeding. This prevents dangling, unanswered clarification requests prior to risk classification.
3. **Primary Choices & Route Isolation**: An XOR-split (`X`) after `Classify Risk` routes the case into three mutually exclusive pathways:
   - *Rejection Route*: Directly fires `Reject Vendor`.
   - *Low-Risk Route*: Fires `Manager Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment`.
   - *High-Risk Route*: Requires `Enhanced Due Diligence` (with optional preceding `Sanctions Escalation` and mandatory `Obtain Legal Opinion` under Resolution 1), followed by `Compliance Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment`.
4. **Terminal Convergence**: All routes converge at `Close Onboarding`. Rejections bypass payment and activation; accepted routes must execute both activation and payment before closure.
5. **Deliberate Exclusion of Post-Activation Behavior (R11)**: `Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` are deliberately excluded from the procedural backbone. In banking architectures, mid-lifecycle profile changes represent asynchronous, event-driven interrupts rather than standard forward-progress steps. Incorporating them into a block-structured tree would necessitate an infinite redo-loop around `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment`, introducing severe deadlocks and false concurrency. R11 is therefore delegated to the declarative and resource/data validation layers.

### 4.2 Semantic Rules and Distinctions

| Template | Scope / Key | Direction | Formal Semantics & Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | Unary | $\text{Trace}[0] = \text{Receive Onboarding Request}$. Enforces that every process instance starts with the onboarding request. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor)` | Binary (Symmetric) | $\neg (\lozenge a \wedge \lozenge b)$. If `Activate Vendor` occurs, `Reject Vendor` can never occur anywhere in the case, and vice versa. Enforces R1. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Binary (Symmetric) | $\neg (\lozenge a \wedge \lozenge b)$. Prevents combining low-risk and high-risk approval tracks in the same case. Enforces R2. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Enhanced Due Diligence)` | Binary (Symmetric) | $\neg (\lozenge a \wedge \lozenge b)$. Ensures `Enhanced Due Diligence` is isolated exclusively to the high-risk compliance route. Enforces R8. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Reject Vendor)` | Binary (Symmetric) | $\neg (\lozenge a \wedge \lozenge b)$. Guarantees no managerial approval is issued in a rejected case. Enforces R2. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Compliance Approval, Reject Vendor)` | Binary (Symmetric) | $\neg (\lozenge a \wedge \lozenge b)$. Guarantees no compliance approval is issued in a rejected case. Enforces R2. |
| `altresponse` | `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Binary ($a \rightarrow b$) | $\square (a \rightarrow \bigcirc (\neg a \mathbin{\mathcal{U}} b))$. Every clarification request must eventually be followed by a clarification receipt, with no intervening second clarification request. Enforces R3. |
| `response` | `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Binary ($a \rightarrow b$) | $\square (a \rightarrow \lozenge b)$. If sanctions escalation occurs, obtaining a legal opinion is an unavoidable eventual obligation. Enforces R4. |
| `response` | `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Binary ($a \rightarrow b$) | $\square (a \rightarrow \lozenge b)$. If a vendor is activated, releasing first payment must eventually occur before case termination. |
| `precedence` | `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Binary ($b \leftarrow a$) | $\neg b \mathbin{\mathcal{W}} a$. `Sanctions Escalation` cannot occur unless `Run Sanctions Screening` occurred earlier. Enforces R7. |
| `precedence` | `(Classify Risk, Activate Vendor)` | Binary ($b \leftarrow a$) | $\neg b \mathbin{\mathcal{W}} a$. Vendor activation cannot occur without prior risk classification. |
| `precedence` | `(Classify Risk, Reject Vendor)` | Binary ($b \leftarrow a$) | $\neg b \mathbin{\mathcal{W}} a$. Vendor rejection cannot occur without prior risk classification. |
| `precedence` | `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Binary ($b \leftarrow a$) | $\neg b \mathbin{\mathcal{W}} a$. Releasing first payment is impossible without prior activation. |

#### Critical Template Distinctions:
1. **Response vs. Precedence**: `response(A, B)` is forward-looking ($\square(A \rightarrow \lozenge B)$); it mandates that if $A$ occurs, $B$ must follow. `precedence(A, B)` is backward-looking ($\neg B \mathbin{\mathcal{W}} A$); it mandates that $B$ cannot occur unless $A$ preceded it. In R7, `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` allows screening to occur without escalation, but forbids escalation without screening.
2. **Alternate Response vs. Chain Response**: `chainresponse(A, B)` requires $B$ to be the *immediate next event* ($i+1$). This is too restrictive for clarifications because background checks may execute concurrently while waiting for clarification. `altresponse(A, B)` correctly permits concurrent events to interleave while strictly requiring that $B$ arrives before the next $A$.
3. **Noncoexistence vs. Nonsuccession**: `nonsuccession(A, B)` only forbids $B$ from occurring *after* $A$ ($\square(A \rightarrow \neg \lozenge B)$), permitting $B$ to occur before $A$. `noncoexistence(A, B)` is global and symmetric, forbidding both $(A \dots B)$ and $(B \dots A)$. R1 and R8 require `noncoexistence`.

### 5.2 Defense and Orientation Analysis
* **Pair Orientation**:
  - `always_after: (A, B)`: PM4Py semantics define this as $\forall \text{ trace}, A \in \text{trace} \implies B \in \text{trace} \wedge \text{idx}(B) > \text{idx}(A)$. Thus, `('Request Clarification', 'Receive Clarification')` asserts that whenever clarification is requested, receipt must follow.
  - `always_before: (B, A)`: PM4Py checks whether $A$ occurred prior to $B$ ($\text{idx}(A) < \text{idx}(B)$). Thus, `('Classify Risk', 'Verify Bank Account')` asserts that `Verify Bank Account` must have executed before `Classify Risk` is reached.
  - `never_together: (A, B)`: Symmetric intersection check. If both $A$ and $B$ are in the trace, a violation is recorded. Both $(A, B)$ and $(B, A)$ are populated to guarantee invariance under trace enumeration.
* **Equivalence**: `('Activate Vendor', 'Release First Payment')` holds across completed cases: in accepted cases, both occur exactly once; in rejected cases, both occur zero times ($0 = 0$ and $1 = 1$). `Verify Bank Account` is excluded from equivalence because R11 permits re-verification upon bank detail changes (count $= 2$).
* **Defense of Empty `directly_follows`**: Populating `directly_follows` in concurrent processes introduces extreme model brittleness. Because `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute concurrently, their interleavings vary non-deterministically. Asserting strict adjacency (e.g., `Capture Master Data` directly followed by `Run Sanctions Screening`) would cause false rejections on valid concurrent traces.

# 6. Temporal Profile

### 6.1 Temporal Profile Definition
```python
# Keys: (activity_1, activity_2), Values: (mean_seconds, std_deviation_seconds)
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (
        48.0 * 3600.0,  # 172,800.0 seconds (48 hours)
        24.0 * 3600.0   #  86,400.0 seconds (24 hours)
    ),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (
        12.0 * 3600.0,  #  43,200.0 seconds (12 hours)
        6.0 * 3600.0    #  21,600.0 seconds (6 hours)
    ),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (
        72.0 * 3600.0,  # 259,200.0 seconds (72 hours)
        36.0 * 3600.0   # 129,600.0 seconds (36 hours)
    ),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

With $\zeta = 2.0$, the permissible interval $[\mu - 2\sigma, \mu + 2\sigma]$ for each pair is:
1. `Request Clarification` $\rightarrow$ `Receive Clarification`: $[0.0\text{ h}, 96.0\text{ h}]$ ($[0, 345,600]\text{ s}$).
2. `Sanctions Escalation` $\rightarrow$ `Obtain Legal Opinion`: $[0.0\text{ h}, 24.0\text{ h}]$ ($[0, 86,400]\text{ s}$).
3. `Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment`: $[0.0\text{ h}, 144.0\text{ h}]$ ($[0, 518,400]\text{ s}$).

---

### 6.2 Why Temporal Deviations are Warnings, Not Rejections
1. **Nature of Operational Latency**: Control-flow models evaluate logical and legal correctness (e.g., "was sanctions screening performed?"). Temporal profiles evaluate operational efficiency and SLA conformance. A legal opinion that takes 34 hours instead of 24 hours (as in T7) is operationally tardy, but the opinion itself remains legally valid and binding.
2. **External Dependencies**: Clarifications depend on third-party vendor responsiveness; legal opinions depend on external counsel workload; payment release depends on commercial banking settlement windows. Automatically rejecting an otherwise fully compliant vendor due to external latency would cause unnecessary commercial damage.
3. **Statistical Dispersion**: Processing times exhibit right-skewed, heavy-tailed distributions. A Gaussian threshold ($\zeta = 2.0$) flags the slowest $\approx 2.5\%$ of cases for supervisory review and queue escalation, not for cancellation.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

### 7.1 Executable Validation Code
```python
import pandas as pd
from typing import Dict, List, Tuple, Any

def validate_resource_and_data_layer(
    df_case: pd.DataFrame, 
    resolution_r5_r6: str = "strict_r5",
    external_auth_db: Dict[str, bool] = None
) -> Tuple[List[str], List[str]]:
    """
    Executes cross-cutting checks for Segregation of Duties (R9),
    Emergency Policies (R5/R6), and Bank Detail Updates (R11).
    
    Required Attributes:
      - 'concept:name': activity label (str)
      - 'time:timestamp': pandas Datetime or float/int hours
      - 'org:resource': resource identifier (str)
      - 'case:concept:name': case identifier
    """
    violations = []
    warnings = []
    case_id = df_case["case:concept:name"].iloc[0] if "case:concept:name" in df_case.columns else "UNKNOWN"

    # --- 1. Attribute Completeness Validation ---
    for req_col in ["concept:name", "org:resource", "time:timestamp"]:
        if req_col not in df_case.columns or df_case[req_col].isnull().any():
            violations.append(f"AUDIT_TRAIL_ERROR: Missing or null '{req_col}' attribute in case {case_id}.")
            return violations, warnings

    events = df_case.sort_values(by="time:timestamp").to_dict("records")

    # --- 2. R9: Segregation of Duties (Four-Eyes Principle) ---
    vba_resources = {e["org:resource"] for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account"}
    appr_resources = {e["org:resource"] for e in events if e["concept:name"] in ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")}
    cmd_resources = {e["org:resource"] for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data"}
    act_resources = {e["org:resource"] for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"}

    # SoD Check 1: Verify Bank Account vs. Approval
    shared_vba_appr = vba_resources.intersection(appr_resources)
    if shared_vba_appr:
        violations.append(
            f"R9_SOD_VIOLATION: Resource(s) {shared_vba_appr} performed both 'Verify Bank Account' "
            f"and an Approval ('Manager Approval' / 'Compliance Approval')."
        )

    # SoD Check 2: Capture Master Data vs. Activate Vendor
    shared_cmd_act = cmd_resources.intersection(act_resources)
    if shared_cmd_act:
        violations.append(
            f"R9_SOD_VIOLATION: Resource(s) {shared_cmd_act} performed both 'Capture Master Data' "
            f"and 'Activate Vendor'."
        )

    # --- 3. R5 / R6: Sanctions Escalation vs. Activation & Emergency Opinion ---
    acts = [e["concept:name"] for e in events]
    if "Sanctions Escalation" in acts and "Activate Vendor" in acts:
        e_esc = next(e for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Sanctions Escalation")
        e_act = next(e for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor")
        e_leg = next((e for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"), None)

        if e_leg is None:
            violations.append("R4_R5_VIOLATION: 'Sanctions Escalation' occurred, but 'Obtain Legal Opinion' was never executed.")
        elif e_act["time:timestamp"] < e_leg["time:timestamp"]:
            # Activation occurred BEFORE Legal Opinion
            delta_hours = (e_leg["time:timestamp"] - e_act["time:timestamp"]).total_seconds() / 3600.0 if hasattr(e_act["time:timestamp"], "total_seconds") else (e_leg["time:timestamp"] - e_act["time:timestamp"])

            if resolution_r5_r6 == "strict_r5":
                violations.append(
                    f"R5_VIOLATION: 'Activate Vendor' occurred at t={e_act['time:timestamp']} prior to "
                    f"'Obtain Legal Opinion' at t={e_leg['time:timestamp']}. Strict Legal policy prohibits activation."
                )
            else:
                # Operational Emergency Policy (R6)
                is_authorized = external_auth_db.get(case_id, False) if external_auth_db else False
                if delta_hours > 24.0:
                    violations.append(
                        f"R6_SLA_VIOLATION: Emergency activation occurred, but 'Obtain Legal Opinion' arrived "
                        f"{delta_hours:.1f}h later (exceeding the 24.0h limit)."
                    )
                elif not is_authorized:
                    warnings.append(
                        f"R6_AUDIT_WARNING: Legal opinion arrived within 24h ({delta_hours:.1f}h), but the log lacks "
                        f"an emergency attribute; external joint authorization must be verified manually."
                    )

    # --- 4. R11: Dynamic Bank Details Update ---
    if "Change Bank Details" in acts:
        e_cbd = next(e for e in events if e["concept:name"] == "Change Bank Details")
        e_acts_post_cbd = [e for e in events if e["time:timestamp"] > e_cbd["time:timestamp"]]
        acts_post_cbd = [e["concept:name"] for e in e_acts_post_cbd]

        # Suspend Vendor must eventually follow
        if "Suspend Vendor" not in acts_post_cbd:
            violations.append("R11_VIOLATION: 'Change Bank Details' occurred without subsequent 'Suspend Vendor'.")

        # Another Verify Bank Account must precede any later Release First Payment
        if "Release First Payment" in acts_post_cbd:
            e_pay = next(e for e in e_acts_post_cbd if e["concept:name"] == "Release First Payment")
            re_vba = [e for e in e_acts_post_cbd if e["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account" and e["time:timestamp"] < e_pay["time:timestamp"]]
            if not re_vba:
                violations.append("R11_VIOLATION: 'Release First Payment' fired after 'Change Bank Details' without re-running 'Verify Bank Account'.")

    return violations, warnings
```

### 7.2 Required Event Attributes and Missing Value Policy
* **Required Attributes**:
  - `concept:name`: Identifies the execution of control-flow milestones.
  - `org:resource`: Identifies the human or automated actor executing the task.
  - `time:timestamp`: Enables ordering verification and elapsed-time calculations.
  - `case:concept:name`: Correlates events within a business instance.
* **Missing Value Policy (Fail-Closed Governance)**:
  - If `org:resource` is null/empty for an approval or verification task, the check raises a fatal `AUDIT_TRAIL_ERROR` (`fail`). In anti-fraud compliance, an un-attributed four-eyes action cannot be presumed compliant.
  - If `time:timestamp` is missing or unparseable, ordering cannot be verified; the case is flagged for technical data integrity failure.
  - If external authorization data is absent, the system defaults to strict non-emergency assumptions, flagging any emergency sequence as an unverified breach.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

### 9.1 Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers
In our hybrid design, several constraints are intentionally duplicated:
* **Terminal Mutual Exclusion (R1)**: Enforced via XOR branching in the procedural backbone, `noncoexistence` in DECLARE, and `never_together` in the Log Skeleton.
* **Sanctions Escalation Sequence (R4/R7)**: Redundantly captured across the high-risk procedural path, DECLARE `response`/`precedence`, and Log Skeleton `always_after`/`always_before`.

*Trade-off*: Duplication provides "defense-in-depth": if a lightweight streaming filter (e.g., Log Skeleton) scans the data first, it catches fatal breaches (like T8) in $O(1)$ time without constructing heavy Petri-net reachability graphs. However, multi-layer duplication creates significant maintenance hazards: modifying a policy requires synchronous updates across Process Trees, LTL rules, and Log Skeletons to prevent cross-layer specification drift.

---

### 9.2 Inconsistencies Under Loops and Post-Activation Changes
* **Cardinaity & Equivalence Collapse (R11)**: Log Skeleton `equivalence` assumes `Verify Bank Account` matches the case count ($1$). When R11 fires (`Change Bank Details`), bank re-verification occurs, raising the occurrence count to $2$. If `activ_freq` or `equivalence` are defined rigidly, a legitimate post-activation re-verification triggers false deviations.
* **Clarification Iteration Blind Spots**: Simple DECLARE `response(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` is satisfied as long as at least one response occurs somewhere in the trace. If a user issues two requests and receives only one response, naive `response` fails to catch the open request. This requires `altresponse` or explicit procedural loop modeling.

---

### 9.3 Vacuity in Declarative Rules
Declarative implication rules ($A \rightarrow B$) suffer from formal vacuity: whenever the antecedent $A$ is absent, the rule evaluates to true.
* In T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, and T8, `Sanctions Escalation` never occurs. Consequently, `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is vacuously satisfied.
* A naive compliance officer reviewing DECLARE fitness reports might mistake "100% rule satisfaction" for "thorough sanctions scrutiny," even though sanctions screening was never escalated.

---

### 9.4 Missing Case Attributes and Observability Gaps
R6 exposes a critical architectural vulnerability: specifying compliance rules dependent on data that does not exist in the event stream. The log contains no `is_emergency: bool` attribute or `joint_authorization_id: str`. Activity-only formalisms fail silently in this scenario; they either block all emergencies (causing operational paralysis) or allow all unvetted activations (causing regulatory exposure). Conformance engines must recognize the boundary of the event log and invoke external database lookups.

---

### 9.5 Timestamp-Only Limitations
The temporal profile computes $\Delta t = t_2 - t_1$. This metric reflects gross calendar duration, but fails to distinguish:
1. *Active Working Time* vs. *Queue Waiting Time*;
2. *Business Hours* vs. *Weekends/Holidays* (e.g., a 72-hour delay initiated on Friday afternoon may represent zero business days);
3. *System Delays* vs. *Third-Party Inaction*.
Treating raw calendar time as an absolute proxy for employee performance or compliance risk yields excessive false alarms.

---

### 9.6 The Fallacy of Support/Confidence Metadata as Normative Truth
In process mining discovery algorithms, `support` and `confidence` measure statistical frequency in historical logs. In governance and compliance, frequency has zero correlation with legality:
* A fraudulent four-eyes bypass (T4) might occur in 99% of historical cases due to poor local management, showing 0.99 support in discovery. That does not make it legally compliant.
* An emergency sanctions override (R6) might occur in 0.01% of cases, showing near-zero support. That does not make it non-compliant.
Normative compliance rules must have their support and confidence set to `1.0` by policy mandate, never derived from historical log distributions.

### 10.1 Pipeline Execution Architecture
1. **Canonical Procedural Model**: The Process Tree (converted to an Accepting Petri Net) serves as the primary execution engine. It defines the stable, forward-moving operational lifecycle and enforces valid case termination.
2. **Authoritative Policy Layer**: The **Resource- and Data-Aware Validation Layer** is the supreme authoritative gate. In financial onboarding, statutory compliance (SoD, AML sanctions, external approvals) overrides pure activity sequence fit. If a trace passes Petri net alignment but fails SoD (T4), the case is decisively rejected.
3. **Execution Order**:
   - **Stage 1 (Schema & Integrity)**: Verify payload completeness (`org:resource`, timestamps). Fail-fast on malformed events.
   - **Stage 2 (Resource & Data Checks)**: Execute four-eyes filtering and emergency external DB lookups. Immediate hard block if SoD fails.
   - **Stage 3 (Procedural Conformance)**: Run token-based replay against the Accepting Petri Net. Detects broken control-flow structures, illegal bypasses, or skipped mandatory checks.
   - **Stage 4 (Declarative Conformance)**: Run DECLARE checking to validate fine-grained temporal logic (e.g., strict clarification alternation).
   - **Stage 5 (Temporal SLA Monitoring)**: Compute Gaussian temporal profile metrics. Deviations beyond $\zeta = 2.0$ generate non-blocking operational warnings.

---

### 10.2 Surfacing Contradictory Findings to Users
When formalisms yield divergent verdicts, the system surfaces findings via a tiered, severity-based dashboard:
* **Level 1: Critical Regulatory Breach (Red Stop)**: Triggered by SoD violations (T4) or contradictory terminal states (T8). Releasing payment and activating the vendor are automatically frozen at the database level. Process owners receive a blocking compliance alert detailing the specific actors and events involved.
* **Level 2: Policy-Dependent Emergency Quarantine (Amber Hold)**: Triggered when emergency activation occurs before legal opinion arrival (T6). The system assigns a `QUARANTINE_PENDING_AUTH` status, issuing an automated API prompt to the Compliance Officer to upload and verify the offline joint authorization document before `Release First Payment` is unlocked.
* **Level 3: Operational SLA Warning (Blue Notice)**: Triggered by temporal profile outliers (T7). The onboarding process proceeds without interruption, but the case is flagged in the managerial analytics portal with a positive z-score alert ($\zeta = 3.67$) to trigger vendor-management or legal-department SLA reviews.
