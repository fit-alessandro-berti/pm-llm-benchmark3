# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Observations versus assumptions

**Observations supplied by the prompt**
- Control flow: `Intake -> diagnostic work -> Validation`.
- Diagnostic work depends on case type:
  - `M`: Mechanical only.
  - `E`: Electrical only.
  - `S`: Software only.
  - `ME`: Mechanical + Electrical; may be parallel on different resources or sequential on one cross-skilled resource.
  - `ES`: Electrical + Software; same parallel/sequential choice.
- P1 may preempt a lower-priority diagnostic activity at most once per case, with a restart penalty equal to 10% of the interrupted activity’s remaining active service time.
- SLA targets:
  - P1: 90% complete within 120 minutes.
  - P2: 90% complete within 8 hours = 480 minutes.
  - P3: 90% complete within 24 hours = 1440 minutes.
- Service-time ranges, complex-tail probabilities, resource skill multipliers, shifts, breaks, demand mix, handover/setup/coordination penalties, overtime limit, and fairness constraint are all given.

**Assumptions needed to make the problem analyzable**
1. **Service-time distributions:**  
   For each activity, ordinary and complex times are treated as uniform distributions over the stated ranges. For expected-load calculations, I use the midpoint of each range and mix by complex-tail probability. For simulation, I sample uniformly from ordinary or complex ranges.
2. **Complex-tail multiplier under U4:**  
   U4 doubles complex-tail probabilities for diagnostic activities only: Mechanical, Electrical, Software. Intake and Validation tail probabilities remain unchanged because the prompt says “diagnostic activities.”
3. **Resource multipliers:**  
   A multiplier `m` means actual active processing time = base drawn service time × `m`. Multipliers apply to active service, not to handover/setup/coordination delays.
4. **Calendar:**  
   The day starts at 08:00. Times are measured in minutes from 08:00. Breaks are excluded from available capacity. Overtime is available as an emergency buffer up to 30 minutes per resource, but base planned utilization above 90% is treated as unfair/fragile.
5. **Handover and setup delays:**  
   - 7 minutes are added when a case changes resource for Intake, diagnostic work, or Validation.  
   - A further 5 minutes is added when a case moves between Mechanical team and Electrical/Software team unless the same cross-skilled resource retains the case.  
   - 5 minutes setup is added when a resource switches diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases.  
   These delays are treated as elapsed time and, where plausible, as consuming resource time.
6. **Parallel versus sequential ME/ES:**  
   Parallel branches require two distinct qualified resources and add 8 minutes coordination before Validation. Sequential execution on one cross-skilled resource avoids coordination and branch handover but may be slower because of multipliers. Sequential execution across two different resources is allowed as a fallback when parallelism is impossible; it incurs resource-change and, for ME, team-transfer delay.
7. **Preemption approximation:**  
   Preemption applies only to diagnostic activities. Because the exact interruption point is unknown, I assume an interrupted activity has about 50% remaining active service time on average. Therefore the expected restart penalty is approximately `0.10 × 0.50 × remaining active service  5%` of the activity’s active service time. This is labeled as an approximation.
8. **Demand stochasticity:**  
   Expected arrivals are treated as Poisson means in the Python simulation. For hand calculations, expected arrivals are used deterministically.
9. **U2 interpretation:**  
   “E and ES demand from 12:00–16:00 doubles” is interpreted as doubling the expected counts of E and ES cases in those blocks, then renormalizing type proportions. Total arrivals in those blocks therefore increase. An alternative interpretation—fixed total arrivals with renormalized shares—would reduce non-E/ES work but still create an Electrical/Software bottleneck; I compare this qualitatively.
10. **Carryover after 20:00:**  
    The explicit demand horizon is 08:00–20:00. P3 SLA is 24 hours, so P3 cases may safely carry over. P2 cases arriving late in the day are sensitive to evening capacity and overtime. The approximation penalizes overloaded evening blocks with additional waiting.

## 1.2 Decision variables

The policy space includes:
- Eligibility rules: which resources may serve which activity/case type.
- Queue discipline: priority weighting, oldest-first protection, reservation.
- Reservation: how much capacity is protected for P1.
- Overflow triggers: when cross-skilled resources may leave home queues.
- Parallelization rule: when ME/ES cases run parallel versus sequential.
- Preemption rule: when P1 may interrupt lower-priority diagnostic work.
- Handover/setup minimization: preference for same-resource retention and skill continuity.
- Overtime use: when and on which resources.

## 1.3 Objective formulations

### Objective A: Tail/SLA risk minimization

Let `D_c` be elapsed time from arrival to completion for case `c`. Let `T_c` be its SLA target: 120, 480, or 1440 minutes.

A tail-risk objective is:

\[
\min \; \alpha \mathbb{E}[D_c] + \beta \text{CVaR}_{95}(D_c) + \gamma \sum_{p \in \{P1,P2,P3\}} w_p \Pr(D_c > T_c \mid p)
\]

with much larger weight `w_P1` than `w_P3`. In operational terms, this objective favors policies that protect P1 SLA and reduce p90/p95 delay, even if average delay or fairness worsens slightly.

### Objective B: Fairness/robustness minimization

Let `U_r,s` be utilization of resource `r` under scenario `s`. Let `Fairness_s` be a fairness index, for example:

\[
Fairness_s = 1 - \min(1, \max_r U_{r,s} - \min_r U_{r,s})
\]

A robustness objective can be written as worst-case regret or weighted worst-case performance:

\[
\min_{policy} \max_{s \in \{base,U1,...,U5\}}
\left[
\lambda_1 (1 - P1SLA_s)
+ \lambda_2 (1 - P2SLA_s)
+ \lambda_3 \max(0, U^{max}_s - 0.90)
+ \lambda_4 (1 - Fairness_s)
+ \lambda_5 \frac{HandoverSetup_s}{cases_s}
\right]
\]

This objective penalizes policies that are excellent in the base case but collapse under shocks, and it explicitly includes fairness/fragility through resource-utilization spread.

The two objectives can prefer different policies. Objective A may prefer aggressive pooling and preemption. Objective B may prefer hybrid reservation with controlled overflow because it protects P1 while limiting unfair overload and handover churn.

# 3. Candidate policies

## 3.1 Policy 1: Strict specialization with fixed queues — `SPEC`

**Eligibility**
- Intake and Validation are pooled across all qualified resources because all resources can perform them.
- Diagnostic queues are fixed:
  - Mechanical queue: Ada, Ben.
  - Electrical queue: Chen, Deepa.
  - Software queue: Emil.
  - Farah is used primarily for Intake/Validation because her diagnostic multipliers are high.
- No voluntary cross-skill overflow unless a queue has zero home resources due to absence. This emergency fallback is labeled as an assumption.

**Priority and preemption**
- P1 moves to the front of its home diagnostic queue.
- P1 may preempt a lower-priority diagnostic case only within the same home queue after 10 minutes of P1 waiting.
- Maximum one preemption per case.

**Parallelization**
- ME/ES cases run parallel if both relevant home queues have available resources.
- Otherwise, branches run sequentially across home resources, incurring handover and, for ME, team-transfer delay.
- No sequential same-resource cross-skilled execution because cross-skill overflow is disabled.

**Handover/setup**
- Low setup burden because resources remain in home skill families.
- High handover burden because cases often move from Intake to specialized diagnostic resources and then to Validation.

**Operational character**
- Easy to understand.
- Protects expertise.
- Fragile when demand is unbalanced or when a key specialist is absent.

## 3.2 Policy 2: Full pooling with transparent dispatch — `POOL`

**Eligibility**
- Any qualified resource may perform any activity.
- Cross-skilled resources are fully usable for ME/ES sequential execution.

**Dispatch score**
For each eligible resource, dispatch chooses the minimum score:

\[
score = multiplier + 0.75 \times current\_load\_ratio - 0.35 \times same\_case\_retention\_bonus
\]

Priority is handled through waiting factors and preemption rather than being hidden in the resource score.

**Priority and preemption**
- P1 has reduced waiting factor.
- If a P1 case cannot start diagnostic work promptly and all eligible resources are busy with lower-priority work, P1 may preempt P3 first, then P2 if P1 SLA breach is imminent.
- Preemption penalty is explicitly calculated as 10% of assumed remaining active service.

**Parallelization**
- ME/ES parallel if two distinct qualified resources are available and expected parallel elapsed time plus 8-minute coordination is better than sequential.
- P1 receives a small preference for parallelism to reduce critical-path time.
- Sequential same-resource execution is chosen when a cross-skilled resource gives lower total elapsed time after multipliers and avoided handover/coordination.

**Handover/setup**
- Handover minimization: prefer same resource for Intake-diagnostic-Validation if multiplier is acceptable and resource load is not excessive.
- Setup awareness: avoid switching a resource between diagnostic skill families if another qualified resource is nearly equivalent.

**Operational character**
- Best average delay when demand is balanced and cross-skilling is real.
- Higher risk of setup churn, perceived unfairness, and opaque dynamic decisions unless the score is displayed to operators.

## 3.3 Policy 3: Hybrid with reserved P1 capacity and controlled overflow — `HYB`

**Default home queues**
- Mechanical: Ada, Ben.
- Electrical: Chen, Deepa.
- Software: Emil.
- Farah: Intake/Validation, with diagnostic overflow only when triggered.

**P1 reservation**
- From 08:00–12:00, reserve approximately 15–20% of one cross-skilled resource’s diagnostic capacity for P1:
  - Ada for Mechanical/Electrical if present.
  - Chen for Electrical/Software if present.
- From 12:00–20:00, reserve Deepa/Emil capacity for P1 as bottleneck shifts.
- P3 cases are not allowed to consume reserved capacity when P1 queue is nonzero or a P1 arrival is likely within 15 minutes.

**Overflow triggers**
Cross-skill overflow is allowed when any of the following holds:
- Home-queue resource load ratio exceeds 0.85.
- Expected home-queue wait exceeds 30 minutes.
- A required home skill has no available home resource.

**Parallelization**
- P1 ME/ES: parallel by default if two qualified resources are available.
- P2 ME/ES: parallel if expected waiting for a second resource exceeds the 8-minute coordination penalty by at least 10–15 minutes.
- P3 ME/ES: prefer sequential same-resource execution when possible to reduce coordination and handover, even if slightly slower.

**Preemption**
- P1 may preempt P3 after 10 minutes of P1 waiting.
- P1 may preempt P2 only if P1 SLA breach is predicted and expected preemption penalty is less than 8 minutes.
- One preemption per case.

**Handover minimization**
- Prefer retaining a case on the same resource from Intake to diagnostic to Validation when the resource is qualified and load is below 85%.
- Overflow only when imbalance justifies it.

**Operational character**
- Slightly worse base-case mean delay than full pooling, but better P1 protection and better robustness under shocks.
- More interpretable than pure pooling because home queues and triggers are explicit.

## 4.1 Interpretation

- `SPEC` minimizes setup and preserves expertise but performs poorly when demand is unbalanced or a specialist is absent. It is especially weak in U3 and U5.
- `POOL` is usually best for mean delay and p90 delay under moderate stress, but it can create fairness and setup issues. It also depends heavily on accurate dispatch and cross-skill competence.
- `HYB` is not always best on average delay, but it usually gives the best P1 SLA and robustness trade-off. It protects P1 in U1/U5 while preventing uncontrolled cross-skill churn.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## 5.1 Primary recommendation: hybrid P1 reservation with controlled overflow

I recommend `HYB`, the hybrid policy with reserved P1 capacity and controlled cross-skill overflow, as the primary robust policy under Objective B.

It is robust because:
1. It protects P1 SLA better than strict specialization and usually better than full pooling under surge.
2. It preserves interpretable home queues, which helps operators understand why work is assigned.
3. It allows overflow only when load imbalance is high, reducing setup churn and skill erosion.
4. It uses sequential execution for lower-priority ME/ES cases when appropriate, reducing coordination overhead.
5. It performs acceptably across base, U1, U2, U3, U4, and U5, rather than optimizing only the base case.

## 5.2 Fallback crisis mode

When stress is severe, switch from normal hybrid to **surge triage mode**.

**Trigger thresholds**
Enter surge triage if any of the following holds for two consecutive 15-minute windows:
- P1 queue length > 1.
- Predicted 30-minute resource utilization > 1.0 for any critical skill.
- P1 SLA risk score > 0.20, meaning predicted P1 completion probability below 80%.
- Chen absent and Electrical/Software queue length > 2.
- Intake outage or external stoppage active.

**Surge triage rules**
- P1 gets absolute priority for diagnostic resources.
- Suspend starts of P3 diagnostic work unless a qualified resource is idle and no P1/P2 case is expected within 15 minutes.
- Use parallel branches for P1 ME/ES whenever two qualified resources exist.
- Use sequential same-resource execution for P2/P3 ME/ES only when it avoids a large handover/coordination burden.
- Allow overtime first on Deepa and Emil, because they cover evening Electrical/Software capacity.
- Preemption allowed after 5 minutes for P1 on P3, and after 10 minutes for P1 on P2 only if P1 breach is imminent.
- Validation is prioritized for cases near SLA deadline; do not let completed diagnostics wait long for Validation.

## 5.3 When another policy is preferable

- **Full pooling** is preferable if the objective is primarily mean delay and the organization has strong cross-training, low setup times, and operators trust the dispatch score.
- **Strict specialization** is preferable if diagnostic errors are extremely costly, cross-skilling is weak, or demand is low enough that specialization does not create bottlenecks.
- **Pure P1 triage** is preferable only during extreme crisis, because it starves P2/P3 and can create long-term backlog.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## 6.1 Gaming of estimates and priority labels

If P1 receives reservation and preemption, there is an incentive to label cases as P1 or to overstate complexity. Mitigations:
- Require objective triage criteria for P1.
- Audit priority distribution by block and compare with historical baseline.
- Track “priority upgrades after arrival” as an event attribute.

## 6.2 Starvation of P3 work

Hybrid and surge modes can delay P3 heavily. This is acceptable for SLA if P3 has a 24-hour target, but chronic starvation can create backlog and hidden risk. Mitigations:
- Reserve a small P3 release window when no P1/P2 work is present.
- Monitor P3 age and trigger P3 release when P3 queue age exceeds 12 hours.

## 6.3 Skill erosion

Controlled overflow reduces cross-skill use compared with full pooling. If specialists never work outside their home queue, cross-skills may erode. Mitigations:
- Use low-stress periods to assign cross-skilled overflow deliberately.
- Track skill usage per resource per week.
- Maintain certification thresholds for Electrical/Software cross-skills.

## 6.4 Excessive preemption

Preemption protects P1 but adds restart penalties and can destabilize the system. If preemption rate is too high, total work increases and P2/P3 collapse. Mitigations:
- Cap preemptions per resource per hour.
- Preempt only when expected penalty is smaller than expected P1 SLA benefit.
- Monitor preemption penalty minutes as a first-class cost.

## 6.5 Coordination overhead

Parallel ME/ES reduces critical path but adds 8 minutes coordination. Under high load, coordination can become a hidden bottleneck before Validation. Mitigations:
- Use sequential same-resource execution for P3 when coordination queue is long.
- Monitor coordination delay separately from diagnostic service time.

## 6.6 New bottleneck at Validation

Pooling may push diagnostic work faster than Validation can absorb it. Validation is shared by all resources, but late-day capacity is limited. Mitigations:
- Reserve Validation slots for cases near SLA deadline.
- Monitor “diagnostic complete but waiting for Validation” as a separate queue.

## 6.7 Fairness and human interpretability

Full pooling can overload fast resources and underuse slow resources if the dispatcher only minimizes expected processing time. Hybrid home queues improve perceived fairness but may reduce efficiency. Mitigations:
- Publish dispatch score and reason.
- Monitor resource utilization spread.
- Add fairness penalty when one resource exceeds 90% planned utilization while qualified alternatives are below 70%.

Monitor in real time:

- Waiting time by stage and priority.

- Resource utilization rolling 30/60 minutes.
- Fairness index: max-minus-min utilization.
- Handover rate and handover minutes per case.
- Setup rate and setup minutes per resource.
- Preemption count and penalty minutes.
- Parallel fraction for ME/ES.
- Coordination delay before Validation.
- Overtime used per resource.
- Validation backlog after diagnostic completion.

## 7.3 PM4Py analyses

Using PM4Py on the interval event log:

1. **Case performance metrics**  
   Use case service time and waiting time to separate active processing from queueing. This reveals whether delays are caused by slow work or by queues/handovers.

2. **Service time by activity**  
   Compare planned versus actual service time by activity and resource. This detects service-time drift, resource-speed changes, or complex-tail increases.

3. **Handover-of-work network**  
   Use `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` to identify frequent resource transitions. A rising handover weight between Intake and diagnostic resources or between diagnostic branches indicates fragmentation.

4. **Bottleneck migration**  
   Filter cases by time window, scenario flag, or policy version. Compare waiting time by stage across windows. If delay moves from Electrical to Validation, the bottleneck has migrated.

5. **Drift detection**  
   Compare weekly distributions of service time, waiting time, handover count, and preemption count. Use control charts or simple rolling baselines.

6. **Conformance/variant analysis**  
   Check whether cases follow expected variants:
   - Intake -> Mechanical -> Validation
   - Intake -> Electrical -> Validation
   - Intake -> Software -> Validation
   - Intake -> parallel Mechanical/Electrical -> Validation
   - Intake -> sequential Electrical/Software -> Validation  
   Unexpected variants may indicate missing events, incorrect handovers, or policy violations.

def midpoint(interval):
    return (interval[0] + interval[1]) / 2.0


def expected_service(activity, tail_mult=1.0):
    d = SERVICE[activity]
    p = d["p"]
    if d["diag"]:
        p = min(0.95, p * tail_mult)
    return (1 - p) * midpoint(d["ord"]) + p * midpoint(d["comp"])


def sample_service(rng, activity, tail_mult=1.0):
    d = SERVICE[activity]
    p = d["p"]
    if d["diag"]:
        p = min(0.95, p * tail_mult)
    lo, hi = d["comp"] if rng.random() < p else d["ord"]
    return float(rng.uniform(lo, hi))


def working_minutes(resource, start, end):
    shift = RESOURCES[resource]["shift"]
    br = RESOURCES[resource]["break"]
    s = max(start, shift[0])
    e = min(end, shift[1])
    if e <= s:
        return 0.0
    mins = float(e - s)
    if br is not None:
        bs = max(s, br[0])
        be = min(e, br[1])
        if be > bs:
            mins -= (be - bs)
    return max(0.0, mins)


def qualified(resource, activity):
    return RESOURCES[resource].get(activity) is not None


def case_skills(case_type):
    if case_type == "M":
        return ["Mechanical"]
    if case_type == "E":
        return ["Electrical"]
    if case_type == "S":
        return ["Software"]
    if case_type == "ME":
        return ["Mechanical", "Electrical"]
    if case_type == "ES":
        return ["Electrical", "Software"]
    raise ValueError(case_type)

def get_scenario(name):
    blocks = copy.deepcopy(BLOCKS)
    resources = list(RESOURCES.keys())
    tail_mult = 1.0
    outage = None

    if name in ["U1", "U5"]:
        for b in blocks:
            if b["start"] < 240:
                b["arrivals"] = b["arrivals"] * 1.7
                p1 = min(100.0, b["prio_pct"]["P1"] + 8.0)
                p2 = b["prio_pct"]["P2"]
                p3 = b["prio_pct"]["P3"]
                rem = 100.0 - p1
                tot = p2 + p3
                if tot > 0:
                    p2 = p2 * rem / tot
                    p3 = p3 * rem / tot
                else:
                    p2 = rem / 2.0
                    p3 = rem / 2.0
                b["prio_pct"] = {"P1": p1, "P2": p2, "P3": p3}

    if name == "U2":
        # Assumption: E and ES expected counts double, total arrivals increase,
        # then type proportions are renormalized.
        for b in blocks:
            if b["start"] in (240, 360):
                counts = {k: b["arrivals"] * v / 100.0 for k, v in b["type_pct"].items()}
                counts["E"] *= 2.0
                counts["ES"] *= 2.0
                total = sum(counts.values())
                b["arrivals"] = total
                b["type_pct"] = {k: 100.0 * v / total for k, v in counts.items()}

    if name in ["U3", "U5"]:
        if "Chen" in resources:
            resources.remove("Chen")

    if name == "U4":
        tail_mult = 2.0

    if name == "U5":
        outage = (90, 120)  # 09:30-10:00

    return blocks, resources, tail_mult, outage

def eligible_resources(activity, policy_name, priority, resources_present,
                       resource_load=None, capacity_block=None):
    pol = POLICIES[policy_name]

    if activity in ["Intake", "Validation"]:
        return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

    if policy_name == "pooled":
        return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

    if policy_name == "specialization":
        home = [r for r in resources_present if r in HOME.get(activity, [])]
        if home:
            return home
        # Emergency fallback if absence removes a home queue.
        return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

    if policy_name == "hybrid":
        if priority == "P1":
            return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

        home = [r for r in resources_present if r in HOME.get(activity, [])]
        if not home:
            return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

        if resource_load is not None and capacity_block is not None:
            overloaded = any(
                resource_load.get(r, 0.0) / max(capacity_block.get(r, 1.0), 1.0)
                > pol["overflow_threshold"]
                for r in home
            )
            if overloaded:
                return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, activity)]

        return home

    return []


def choose_resource(activity, priority, policy_name, resources_present,
                    resource_load, capacity_block, avoid=None, prefer=None):
    elig = eligible_resources(
        activity, policy_name, priority, resources_present,
        resource_load=resource_load, capacity_block=capacity_block
    )
    if avoid:
        elig = [r for r in elig if r not in avoid]
    if not elig:
        return None

    def score(r):
        mult = RESOURCES[r][activity]
        ratio = resource_load.get(r, 0.0) / max(capacity_block.get(r, 1.0), 1.0)
        s = mult + 0.75 * ratio
        if prefer and r in prefer:
            s -= 0.35
        return s

    return min(elig, key=score)


def setup_temp(last_diag_skill, resource, skill):
    prev = last_diag_skill.get(resource)
    return SETUP_MIN if prev is not None and prev != skill else 0.0


def load_penalty(resources, resource_load, capacity_block):
    if not resources:
        return 0.0
    ratios = [
        resource_load.get(r, 0.0) / max(capacity_block.get(r, 1.0), 1.0)
        for r in set(resources)
    ]
    return 10.0 * max(ratios) if ratios else 0.0

# 4. Diagnostic assignment: parallel/sequential, handover/setup
# ============================================================

def assign_diagnostic(case, intake_resource, block_start, policy_name,
                      resources_present, resource_load, capacity_block,
                      last_diag_skill):
    skills = case_skills(case["type"])
    priority = case["priority"]
    pol = POLICIES[policy_name]

    if len(skills) == 1:
        skill = skills[0]
        base = case["services"][skill]
        res = choose_resource(
            skill, priority, policy_name, resources_present,
            resource_load, capacity_block, prefer=[intake_resource]
        )
        if res is None:
            res = [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill)][0]

        active = base * RESOURCES[res][skill]
        handover = 0.0 if res == intake_resource else HANDOVER_MIN
        setup = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, res, skill)

        resource_load[res] += active + handover + setup
        last_diag_skill[res] = skill

        case["branches"] = [{
            "skill": skill,
            "resource": res,
            "base": base,
            "active": active,
            "setup": setup,
            "handover": handover,
            "post_delay": 0.0,
        }]
        case["diag_mode"] = "sequential"
        case["diag_pre_delay"] = handover + setup
        case["coordination"] = 0.0
        case["diag_active_path"] = active
        case["diag_resources"] = [res]
        return

    skill1, skill2 = skills
    base1 = case["services"][skill1]
    base2 = case["services"][skill2]
    options = []

    # Parallel option: two distinct resources.
    res1 = choose_resource(
        skill1, priority, policy_name, resources_present,
        resource_load, capacity_block, prefer=[intake_resource]
    )
    res2 = None
    if res1 is not None:
        res2 = choose_resource(
            skill2, priority, policy_name, resources_present,
            resource_load, capacity_block, avoid=[res1], prefer=[intake_resource]
        )

    if res1 is not None and res2 is not None:
        active1 = base1 * RESOURCES[res1][skill1]
        active2 = base2 * RESOURCES[res2][skill2]
        path = max(active1, active2)
        handover = 0.0
        if res1 != intake_resource:
            handover += HANDOVER_MIN
        if res2 != intake_resource:
            handover += HANDOVER_MIN
        setup1 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, res1, skill1)
        setup2 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, res2, skill2)
        score = (
            path + handover + setup1 + setup2 + COORD_MIN
            + load_penalty([res1, res2], resource_load, capacity_block)
            - (5.0 if priority == "P1" else 0.0)
        )
        options.append({
            "score": score,
            "mode": "parallel",
            "resources": [res1, res2],
            "actives": [active1, active2],
            "pre_delay": handover + setup1 + setup2,
            "coordination": COORD_MIN,
            "post_delays": [0.0, 0.0],
            "last_skills": {res1: skill1, res2: skill2},
            "setups": [setup1, setup2],
            "handover": handover,
        })

    # Sequential same cross-skilled resource, if policy allows cross-skill.
    if pol.get("cross_skill", False):
        for r in resources_present:
            if qualified(r, skill1) and qualified(r, skill2):
                active1 = base1 * RESOURCES[r][skill1]
                active2 = base2 * RESOURCES[r][skill2]
                active = active1 + active2
                handover = 0.0 if r == intake_resource else HANDOVER_MIN
                setup = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, r, skill1)
                score = (
                    active + handover + setup
                    + load_penalty([r], resource_load, capacity_block)
                )
                options.append({
                    "score": score,
                    "mode": "sequential_same",
                    "resources": [r, r],
                    "actives": [active1, active2],
                    "pre_delay": handover + setup,
                    "coordination": 0.0,
                    "post_delays": [0.0, 0.0],
                    "last_skills": {r: skill2},
                    "setups": [setup, 0.0],
                    "handover": handover,
                })

    # Sequential different resources: fallback / non-cross-skilled path.
    if res1 is not None and res2 is not None:
        active1 = base1 * RESOURCES[res1][skill1]
        active2 = base2 * RESOURCES[res2][skill2]
        active = active1 + active2
        pre_handover = 0.0 if res1 == intake_resource else HANDOVER_MIN
        setup1 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, res1, skill1)
        setup2 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, res2, skill2)
        branch_handover = HANDOVER_MIN if res2 != res1 else 0.0
        team = TEAM_TRANSFER_MIN if family(skill1) != family(skill2) and res2 != res1 else 0.0
        post_delay = branch_handover + team + setup2
        score = (
            active + pre_handover + setup1 + post_delay
            + load_penalty([res1, res2], resource_load, capacity_block)
        )
        options.append({
            "score": score,
            "mode": "sequential_multi",
            "resources": [res1, res2],
            "actives": [active1, active2],
            "pre_delay": pre_handover + setup1,
            "coordination": 0.0,
            "post_delays": [post_delay, 0.0],
            "last_skills": {res1: skill1, res2: skill2},
            "setups": [setup1, setup2],
            "handover": pre_handover + branch_handover + team,
        })

    # Emergency option if normal options failed.
    if not options:
        elig1 = [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill1)]
        elig2 = [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill2)]
        if elig1 and elig2:
            r1 = min(elig1, key=lambda r: RESOURCES[r][skill1])
            other = [r for r in elig2 if r != r1]
            r2 = min(other if other else elig2, key=lambda r: RESOURCES[r][skill2])
            active1 = base1 * RESOURCES[r1][skill1]
            active2 = base2 * RESOURCES[r2][skill2]
            if r1 == r2:
                active = active1 + active2
                handover = 0.0 if r1 == intake_resource else HANDOVER_MIN
                setup = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, r1, skill1)
                options.append({
                    "score": active + handover + setup,
                    "mode": "sequential_same",
                    "resources": [r1, r1],
                    "actives": [active1, active2],
                    "pre_delay": handover + setup,
                    "coordination": 0.0,
                    "post_delays": [0.0, 0.0],
                    "last_skills": {r1: skill2},
                    "setups": [setup, 0.0],
                    "handover": handover,
                })
            else:
                active = active1 + active2
                pre_handover = 0.0 if r1 == intake_resource else HANDOVER_MIN
                setup1 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, r1, skill1)
                setup2 = setup_temp(last_diag_skill, r2, skill2)
                branch_handover = HANDOVER_MIN
                team = TEAM_TRANSFER_MIN if family(skill1) != family(skill2) else 0.0
                post_delay = branch_handover + team + setup2
                options.append({
                    "score": active + pre_handover + setup1 + post_delay,
                    "mode": "sequential_multi",
                    "resources": [r1, r2],
                    "actives": [active1, active2],
                    "pre_delay": pre_handover + setup1,
                    "coordination": 0.0,
                    "post_delays": [post_delay, 0.0],
                    "last_skills": {r1: skill1, r2: skill2},
                    "setups": [setup1, setup2],
                    "handover": pre_handover + branch_handover + team,
                })

    best = min(options, key=lambda o: o["score"])

    # Update resource load and last diagnostic skill.
    for r, active in zip(best["resources"], best["actives"]):
        resource_load[r] += active
    if best["pre_delay"] > 0:
        resource_load[best["resources"][0]] += best["pre_delay"]
    if best["mode"] == "sequential_multi" and best["post_delays"][0] > 0:
        resource_load[best["resources"][1]] += best["post_delays"][0]
    if best["coordination"] > 0:
        resource_load[best["resources"][0]] += best["coordination"]
    for r, sk in best["last_skills"].items():
        last_diag_skill[r] = sk

    branches = []
    for i, (skill, res, active, base) in enumerate(
        zip(skills, best["resources"], best["actives"], [base1, base2])
    ):
        branches.append({
            "skill": skill,
            "resource": res,
            "base": base,
            "active": active,
            "setup": best["setups"][i] if i < len(best["setups"]) else 0.0,
            "handover": best["handover"] if i == 0 else 0.0,
            "post_delay": best["post_delays"][i],
        })

    case["branches"] = branches
    case["diag_mode"] = best["mode"]
    case["diag_pre_delay"] = best["pre_delay"]
    case["coordination"] = best["coordination"]
    case["diag_active_path"] = (
        max(best["actives"]) if best["mode"] == "parallel" else sum(best["actives"])
    )
    case["diag_resources"] = list(dict.fromkeys(best["resources"]))


# ============================================================
# 5. Queue waiting approximation
# This is not a full DES; it is a transparent scenario-capacity
# approximation with explicit penalty assumptions.
# ============================================================

def congestion_wait(active_minutes, rho, priority_factor):
    if rho <= 0:
        return 0.0
    if rho < 0.70:
        w = 2.0 + 8.0 * rho
    elif rho < 1.00:
        w = active_minutes * 0.25 * rho / max(1e-6, 1.0 - rho)
    else:
        w = 30.0 + active_minutes * 2.0 * (rho - 1.0)
    return float(min(480.0, w * priority_factor))


def policy_skill_capacity_resources(skill, policy_name, resources_present):
    if skill in ["Intake", "Validation"]:
        return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill)]

    if policy_name == "specialization":
        home = [r for r in resources_present if r in HOME.get(skill, [])]
        if home:
            return home
        return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill)]

    return [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, skill)]


# ============================================================
# 6. Simulation / approximation for one scenario-policy-seed
# ============================================================

def simulate_scenario_policy(scenario_name, policy_name, seed, collect_events=False):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    blocks, resources_present, tail_mult, outage = get_scenario(scenario_name)
    block_by_start = {b["start"]: b for b in blocks}

    cases = []
    case_counter = 0

    # Generate cases.
    for b in blocks:
        n = int(rng.poisson(b["arrivals"]))
        prio_keys = ["P1", "P2", "P3"]
        prio_p = np.array([b["prio_pct"][k] for k in prio_keys], dtype=float)
        prio_p = prio_p / prio_p.sum()

        type_keys = list(b["type_pct"].keys())
        type_p = np.array([b["type_pct"][k] for k in type_keys], dtype=float)
        type_p = type_p / type_p.sum()

        for _ in range(n):
            case_counter += 1
            arrival = float(b["start"] + rng.uniform(0, b["end"] - b["start"]))
            priority = str(rng.choice(prio_keys, p=prio_p))
            ctype = str(rng.choice(type_keys, p=type_p))

            services = {
                "Intake": sample_service(rng, "Intake", 1.0),
                "Validation": sample_service(rng, "Validation", 1.0),
            }
            for skill in case_skills(ctype):
                services[skill] = sample_service(rng, skill, tail_mult)

            cases.append({
                "case_id": f"{scenario_name}_{policy_name}_{seed}_{case_counter}",
                "arrival": arrival,
                "block_start": b["start"],
                "block_p1": b["prio_pct"]["P1"],
                "priority": priority,
                "type": ctype,
                "services": services,
                "preemption_penalty": 0.0,
            })

    cases.sort(key=lambda c: c["arrival"])

    # First pass: assign resources and compute active/delay burdens ignoring congestion.
    resource_load = defaultdict(float)
    last_diag_skill = {r: None for r in resources_present}

    for c in cases:
        bs = c["block_start"]
        capacity_block = {
            r: working_minutes(r, bs, bs + 120)
            for r in resources_present
        }

        intake_res = choose_resource(
            "Intake", c["priority"], policy_name, resources_present,
            resource_load, capacity_block
        )
        if intake_res is None:
            intake_res = [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, "Intake")][0]

        intake_active = c["services"]["Intake"] * RESOURCES[intake_res]["Intake"]
        c["intake_resource"] = intake_res
        c["intake_active"] = intake_active
        resource_load[intake_res] += intake_active

        assign_diagnostic(
            c, intake_res, bs, policy_name, resources_present,
            resource_load, capacity_block, last_diag_skill
        )

        val_res = choose_resource(
            "Validation", c["priority"], policy_name, resources_present,
            resource_load, capacity_block, prefer=c.get("diag_resources", [])
        )
        if val_res is None:
            val_res = [r for r in resources_present if qualified(r, "Validation")][0]

        val_active = c["services"]["Validation"] * RESOURCES[val_res]["Validation"]
        val_handover = 0.0 if val_res in c.get("diag_resources", []) else HANDOVER_MIN

        c["validation_resource"] = val_res
        c["validation_active"] = val_active
        c["validation_handover"] = val_handover
        resource_load[val_res] += val_active + val_handover

    # Aggregate skill load by block for rho.
    skill_load = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(float))
    for c in cases:
        bs = c["block_start"]
        skill_load[bs]["Intake"] += c["intake_active"]
        skill_load[bs]["Validation"] += c["validation_active"]
        for br in c["branches"]:
            skill_load[bs][br["skill"]] += br["active"]

    skill_cap = {}
    for b in blocks:
        cap = {}
        for skill in SKILLS:
            rs = policy_skill_capacity_resources(skill, policy_name, resources_present)
            cap[skill] = sum(working_minutes(r, b["start"], b["end"]) for r in rs)
        skill_cap[b["start"]] = cap

    skill_rho = {}
    for b in blocks:
        bs = b["start"]
        skill_rho[bs] = {}
        for skill in SKILLS:
            load = skill_load[bs].get(skill, 0.0)
            cap = max(skill_cap[bs].get(skill, 0.0), 1.0)
            skill_rho[bs][skill] = load / cap

    # Second pass: waiting, preemption, completion, event log.
    event_rows = []
    scheduled_day = {
        r: sum(working_minutes(r, b["start"], b["end"]) for b in blocks)
        for r in resources_present
    }

    for c in cases:
        bs = c["block_start"]
        rho = skill_rho.get(bs, {})
        priority_factor = POLICIES[policy_name]["wait_factors"][c["priority"]]

        outage_delay = 0.0
        if outage is not None and outage[0] <= c["arrival"] < outage[1]:
            outage_delay = outage[1] - c["arrival"]

        intake_wait = congestion_wait(c["intake_active"], rho.get("Intake", 0.0), priority_factor)
        intake_wait += outage_delay
        intake_start = c["arrival"] + intake_wait
        intake_end = intake_start + c["intake_active"]
        c["intake_wait"] = intake_wait
        c["intake_start"] = intake_start
        c["intake_end"] = intake_end

        # Preemption approximation for lower-priority cases.
        diag_skills = [br["skill"] for br in c["branches"]]
        maxrho = max([rho.get(s, 0.0) for s in diag_skills], default=0.0)
        if c["priority"] in ["P2", "P3"] and maxrho > 0.80 and c["branches"]:
            prob = min(0.30, 0.5 * (c["block_p1"] / 100.0) + max(0.0, maxrho - 0.85))
            if rng.random() < prob:
                # Assumption: interrupted at about half remaining active service.
                penalty = 0.10 * 0.50 * c["diag_active_path"]
                c["preemption_penalty"] = penalty
                c["branches"][-1]["active"] += penalty
                c["diag_active_path"] += penalty
                resource_load[c["branches"][-1]["resource"]] += penalty

        diag_ready = intake_end + c.get("diag_pre_delay", 0.0)

        if c.get("diag_mode") == "parallel":
            waits = [
                congestion_wait(br["active"], rho.get(br["skill"], 0.0), priority_factor)
                for br in c["branches"]
            ]
            diag_wait = max(waits) if waits else 0.0
            for br, w in zip(c["branches"], waits):
                br["queue_entry"] = diag_ready
                br["waiting"] = diag_wait
                br["start"] = diag_ready + diag_wait
                br["end"] = br["start"] + br["active"]
            diag_end = max(br["end"] for br in c["branches"]) if c["branches"] else diag_ready
            val_ready = diag_end + c.get("coordination", 0.0)
        else:
            current = diag_ready
            total_wait = 0.0
            for br in c["branches"]:
                w = congestion_wait(br["active"], rho.get(br["skill"], 0.0), priority_factor)
                total_wait += w
                br["queue_entry"] = current
                br["waiting"] = w
                br_start = current + w
                br_end = br_start + br["active"]
                br["start"] = br_start
                br["end"] = br_end
                current = br_end + br.get("post_delay", 0.0)
            diag_wait = total_wait
            diag_end = current
            val_ready = diag_end + c.get("coordination", 0.0)

        c["diag_wait"] = diag_wait

        val_wait = congestion_wait(c["validation_active"], rho.get("Validation", 0.0), priority_factor)
        val_start = val_ready + val_wait
        val_end = val_start + c["validation_active"]
        c["validation_wait"] = val_wait
        c["validation_start"] = val_start
        c["validation_end"] = val_end
        c["completion"] = val_end
        c["elapsed"] = val_end - c["arrival"]

        post_sum = sum(br.get("post_delay", 0.0) for br in c["branches"])
        c["handover_setup_burden"] = (
            c.get("diag_pre_delay", 0.0)
            + post_sum
            + c.get("validation_handover", 0.0)
            + c.get("coordination", 0.0)
            + c.get("preemption_penalty", 0.0)
        )

        if collect_events:
            target = {"P1": 120, "P2": 480, "P3": 1440}[c["priority"]]
            common = {
                "case:concept:name": c["case_id"],
                "priority": c["priority"],
                "case_type": c["type"],
                "policy_version": policy_name,
                "scenario_flag": scenario_name,
                "sla_target_min": target,
                "sla_remaining_at_completion": target - c["elapsed"],
                "assignment_decision": f"{policy_name}_min_score",
            }

            int_elig = eligible_resources("Intake", policy_name, c["priority"], resources_present)
            event_rows.append({
                **common,
                "concept:name": "Intake",
                "stage": "Intake",
                "org:resource": c["intake_resource"],
                "queue_entry_timestamp": ts(c["arrival"]),
                "start_timestamp": ts(intake_start),
                "time:timestamp": ts(intake_end),
                "skill_used": "Intake",
                "planned_service_minutes": c["services"]["Intake"],
                "actual_service_minutes": c["intake_active"],
                "waiting_minutes": intake_wait,
                "handover_delay": 0.0,
                "setup_delay": 0.0,
                "coordination_delay": 0.0,
                "preemption_penalty": 0.0,
                "pause_resume": "",
                "eligible_resource_set": ",".join(sorted(int_elig)),
            })

            for i, br in enumerate(c["branches"]):
                elig = eligible_resources(br["skill"], policy_name, c["priority"], resources_present)
                pause_resume = ""
                if c.get("preemption_penalty", 0.0) > 0 and i == len(c["branches"]) - 1:
                    pause_resume = "resumed_after_preemption"
                event_rows.append({
                    **common,
                    "concept:name": br["skill"],
                    "stage": "Diagnostic",
                    "org:resource": br["resource"],
                    "queue_entry_timestamp": ts(br.get("queue_entry", br["start"])),
                    "start_timestamp": ts(br["start"]),
                    "time:timestamp": ts(br["end"]),
                    "skill_used": br["skill"],
                    "planned_service_minutes": br["base"],
                    "actual_service_minutes": br["active"],
                    "waiting_minutes": br.get("waiting", 0.0),
                    "handover_delay": br.get("handover", 0.0),
                    "setup_delay": br.get("setup", 0.0),
                    "coordination_delay": 0.0,
                    "preemption_penalty": c["preemption_penalty"] if i == len(c["branches"]) - 1 else 0.0,
                    "pause_resume": pause_resume,
                    "eligible_resource_set": ",".join(sorted(elig)),
                })

            val_elig = eligible_resources("Validation", policy_name, c["priority"], resources_present)
            event_rows.append({
                **common,
                "concept:name": "Validation",
                "stage": "Validation",
                "org:resource": c["validation_resource"],
                "queue_entry_timestamp": ts(val_ready),
                "start_timestamp": ts(val_start),
                "time:timestamp": ts(val_end),
                "skill_used": "Validation",
                "planned_service_minutes": c["services"]["Validation"],
                "actual_service_minutes": c["validation_active"],
                "waiting_minutes": val_wait,
                "handover_delay": c.get("validation_handover", 0.0),
                "setup_delay": 0.0,
                "coordination_delay": c.get("coordination", 0.0),
                "preemption_penalty": 0.0,
                "pause_resume": "",
                "eligible_resource_set": ",".join(sorted(val_elig)),
            })

    # Metrics.
    if not cases:
        empty = {
            "mean_delay": np.nan,
            "p90_delay": np.nan,
            "P1_SLA": np.nan,
            "P2_SLA": np.nan,
            "max_resource_utilization": np.nan,
            "utilization_fairness": np.nan,
            "handover_setup_burden": np.nan,
            "resilience_score": np.nan,
            "main_failure_mode": "no_cases",
        }
        return empty, []

    elapsed = np.array([c["elapsed"] for c in cases], dtype=float)
    mean_delay = float(np.mean(elapsed))
    p90_delay = float(np.percentile(elapsed, 90))

    def sla(priority, target):
        vals = [c["elapsed"] for c in cases if c["priority"] == priority]
        if not vals:
            return np.nan
        return float(np.mean([v <= target for v in vals]))

    p1_sla = sla("P1", 120)
    p2_sla = sla("P2", 480)

    utils = []
    for r in resources_present:
        cap = scheduled_day.get(r, 0.0)
        if cap > 0:
            utils.append(resource_load.get(r, 0.0) / cap)

    max_util = float(max(utils)) if utils else np.nan
    fairness = float(max(0.0, 1.0 - (max(utils) - min(utils)))) if utils else np.nan

    handover_burden = float(np.mean([c["handover_setup_burden"] for c in cases]))

    def nan0(x):
        return 0.0 if x is None or (isinstance(x, float) and math.isnan(x)) else x

    resilience = 0.6 * nan0(p1_sla) + 0.4 * nan0(p2_sla)

    avg_rho = defaultdict(list)
    for bs, d in skill_rho.items():
        for skill, val in d.items():
            avg_rho[skill].append(val)
    skill_avg = {k: float(np.mean(v)) for k, v in avg_rho.items() if v}
    bottleneck_skill = max(skill_avg, key=skill_avg.get) if skill_avg else "none"

    reasons = []
    if not math.isnan(max_util) and max_util > 1.10:
        reasons.append("overtime/backlog")
    if bottleneck_skill != "none" and skill_avg[bottleneck_skill] > 0.90:
        reasons.append(f"{bottleneck_skill} capacity")
    if not math.isnan(p1_sla) and p1_sla < 0.80:
        reasons.append("P1 SLA misses")
    if handover_burden > 15:
        reasons.append("handover/setup burden")
    main_failure = "; ".join(reasons) if reasons else "stable"

    metrics = {
        "mean_delay": mean_delay,
        "p90_delay": p90_delay,
        "P1_SLA": p1_sla,
        "P2_SLA": p2_sla,
        "max_resource_utilization": max_util,
        "utilization_fairness": fairness,
        "handover_setup_burden": handover_burden,
        "resilience_score": resilience,
        "main_failure_mode": main_failure,
    }

    return metrics, event_rows

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 What cannot be concluded from the supplied data alone

The supplied expected arrivals and service-time ranges are not enough to conclude exact SLA attainment or exact delays. In particular:

1. **Arrival randomness is unspecified.**  
   Expected arrivals per block do not tell us whether arrivals are smooth, bursty, or correlated. Burstiness can dramatically increase queueing delay.

2. **Service-time distribution shape is unspecified.**  
   The prompt gives ranges and tail probabilities, but not exact distributions. Uniform ranges are an assumption. Real service times may be lognormal, bimodal, or dependent on case attributes.

3. **Resource behavior is unspecified.**  
   The model assumes resources follow the dispatch rule, do not collaborate except through defined handovers, and do not learn or fatigue. Real behavior may differ.

4. **Preemption point is unknown.**  
   The restart penalty depends on remaining active service time. Without interruption-time data, the 50% remaining assumption is only a bound-like approximation.

5. **Parallelism details are incomplete.**  
   The 8-minute coordination penalty is given, but the true cost may depend on resource experience, case complexity, and whether branches are truly independent.

6. **Handover/setup may be resource-dependent.**  
   The prompt uses fixed penalties, but real handover times likely depend on familiarity, documentation quality, and workload.

7. **Carryover between days is not modeled.**  
   P3 cases can be delayed beyond the 08:00–20:00 window, but the next-day starting state is unknown.

## 9.2 Pilot and backtest design

A safe validation plan should distinguish policies without endangering P1 cases.

### Phase 1: Shadow mode
- Run all three policies in shadow mode on live arrivals.
- Do not change actual assignments.
- Record what each policy would have done.
- Compare predicted queue lengths, handovers, setups, preemptions, and SLA risk.

### Phase 2: Protected pilot for hybrid policy
- Pilot `HYB` on a subset of shifts, for example 10:00–16:00, where demand is high but overnight risk is limited.
- Keep a manual override for P1 cases.
- Continue full pooling or current practice as fallback.
- Measure:
  - P1 completion within 120 minutes.
  - P2 completion within 480 minutes.
  - mean and p90 delay.
  - handover/setup minutes per case.
  - resource utilization spread.
  - preemption count and penalty minutes.

### Phase 3: Crossover or randomized block design
- Randomize policy by day or half-day, avoiding rapid switching that confounds learning effects.
- Ensure P1 cases always have a human escalation path.
- Use Bayesian or bootstrap comparison to estimate uncertainty in SLA differences.

### Phase 4: Stress-test days
- Simulate or rehearse U1-like morning surge and U3-like specialist absence.
- Test surge triage thresholds.
- Validate that overtime allocation and P1 reservation behave as intended.

### Success criteria
Before full rollout, require evidence that:
- P1 SLA is not worse than current practice and preferably improves.
- P2 SLA does not degrade materially.
- Max planned resource utilization stays below about 90% in normal base conditions.
- Handover/setup burden does not increase unsustainably.
- Operators can explain and override dispatch decisions.
