```python
def mean_shift_changepoint(x, min_seg=3):

       left=x[:t]; right=x[t:]
       cost=((left-left.mean())**2).sum()+((right-right.mean())**2).sum()
```

```python
def mean_shift_cp(x, min_seg=5):
    x=np.asarray(x, float)
    n=len(x); best=None; best_cost=np.inf
    for t in range(min_seg, n-min_seg):
       left=x[:t]; right=x[t:]
       cost=((left-left.mean())**2).sum()+((right-right.mean())**2).sum()
       if cost<best_cost: best_cost=cost; best_t=t
    total=((x-x.mean())**2).sum()
    reduction = 1 - best_cost/total if total>0 else np.nan
    return best_t, reduction
```

# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention.** The service center is assumed to operate only from **07:00 to 19:00**, i.e. **12 hours = 720 minutes** per observed day. `arrivals` and `completed` are therefore interpreted as counts during this 12-hour window. Where an intensity is needed, I use:

- arrival intensity: `arrivals / 12` cases per open hour  
- completion/departure intensity: `completed / 12` cases per open hour

This is an **open-hour intensity**, not a 24-hour intensity. No service is assumed outside opening hours, although cases may remain in the system overnight.

**Queueing estimands used or attempted.**

| Quantity | Working definition | Units / notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Arrival intensity | average arrivals per open hour | cases/hour |
| Completion intensity | average completions per open hour | cases/hour; used as departure throughput where defensible |
| Service-time behavior | supplied `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min` | active handling minutes, excluding waiting; medians/p90s are not mean service times |
| WIP | supplied `mean_wip` | cases in system from 15-minute snapshots; assumed to reflect opening-hours snapshots |
| Capacity availability | `staff_fte * 720` | FTE-minutes per day, ignoring breaks unless already embedded in `staff_fte` |
| Capacity-pressure proxy 1 | `mean_wip / staff_fte` | cases per concurrently available FTE |
| Capacity-pressure proxy 2 | `completed * median_service_min / (staff_fte * 720)` | crude median-based served-work index; not utilization because mean service time is unknown |
| Capacity-pressure proxy 3 | `completed * p90_service_min / (staff_fte * 720)` | crude tail-stress index; not utilization because p90 is not mean |

**Key limitations and threats to naive inference.**

1. **Nonstationarity and regime shifts.** The daily sequence contains an outage on 2026-04-15, post-outage recovery on 2026-04-16/17, a large step-up in `complex_pct`, median service time, p90 service time, peak concentration and WIP around 2026-04-20, and a staffing intervention beginning during 2026-04-29. Pooling these periods mixes different operating systems.

2. **Open/closed boundary and carry-over backlog.** Cases may arrive one day and complete on a later day. The backlog at 07:00 is not supplied. Therefore daily completions are not necessarily equal to daily arrivals, and `mean_wip` may contain previous-day cases. This makes direct substitution into queueing formulas risky.

3. **Median/p90 service times are not mean service times.** The table gives medians and p90s of active handling time. The mean service time is not supplied and cannot be recovered without assumptions. Using median service time as if it were mean service time or mean flow time would be incorrect.

4. **Outage and recovery are not steady random variability.** The 2026-04-15 outage suppressed completions, especially approximately 11:00–14:00 in the representative hourly vector. The following days’ high completions are at least partly backlog drainage, not evidence of a new stable capacity level.

5. **Measurement and definition ambiguity.** Several fields have uncertainty: exact intervention time on 2026-04-29 is unknown; `complex_pct` may represent a real case-mix shift, a coding change, or both; the precise snapshot window for `mean_wip` is not stated; and the daily `peak_08_10_pct` field is not exactly reconcilable with the stated hourly bin convention for at least one representative day. For example, the 2026-04-21 representative vector has only 11 arrivals in the 08:00–09:59 bins under the stated bin labels, whereas the daily table reports `peak_08_10_pct = 47`. This suggests either a different peak definition, a stylized representative vector, or a data quality issue.

6. **Representative days are not a full stochastic sample.** Only four representative hourly vectors are supplied. They are useful for qualitative contrast but cannot support a full arrival-process or service-process distribution. Daily rows are also serially dependent and not independent observations.
