from pm4py.filtering import filter_four_eyes_principle

# Load and format event log
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
event_log = log_converter.apply(log)

# Define four-eyes rules
rules = [
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    ("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification")
]

# Filter violations

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Internal Audit Report: Procurement-to-Payment Control Exceptions**

**Summary of Findings**
The review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified control exceptions in four-eyes principle adherence, approval timing, and vendor concentration. While no direct evidence of fraud or collusion was found, the patterns observed warrant targeted follow-up to rule out misconduct or systemic control weaknesses.

**Key Observations**
1. **Four-Eyes Violations**: 42 cases (1.8%) violated the segregation of duties between PO creation and approval, primarily involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo. An additional 9 cases (0.4%) violated the vendor bank change/payment approval rule, concentrated in vendor family V-77x.
2. **Unusually Fast Approvals**: Median approval times for Buyer_CarlaApprover_Gita (8 minutes) and Buyer_DanManager_Hugo (10 minutes) were significantly faster than the global median (2.1 hours). While these may reflect emergency handling, the speed and frequency raise questions about pre-approval or rubber-stamping.
3. **Vendor Concentration**: 72% of VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cases involved vendor family V-77x, with 83% processed via manual uploads. This concentration increases the risk of favoritism or collusion, particularly given the four-eyes violations in this subgroup.
4. **Data-Quality Issues**: Shared-account usage (e.g., Approver_Gita) and source-system mapping errors (e.g., Bot_PO/human approvers) obscure accountability and may hide additional control exceptions.

**Most Concerning Subgroup**
The VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cluster stands out due to the combination of four-eyes violations, vendor concentration, manual source-system usage, and fast approvals. While these may reflect legitimate vendor onboarding or banking changes, the lack of clear linkage between maintenance tickets and procurement cases limits assurance.

**Recommendations**
1. **High-Priority Review**:
   - Sample 100% of VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cases for vendor bank change tickets, payment approval logs, and manual upload documentation.
   - Review all Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo cases with four-eyes violations or fast approvals, focusing on emergency justification narratives and case comments.
2. **Medium-Priority Review**:
   - Investigate shared-account usage (e.g., Approver_Gita) to confirm entered_by fields align with org:resource.
   - Validate source_system="legacy_erp" mapping errors to rule out intentional overrides.
3. **Process Improvements**:
   - Strengthen controls for manual uploads, particularly for high-risk vendors like V-77x.
   - Implement automated alerts for four-eyes violations or unusually fast approvals.
   - Clarify delegated authority for emergency approvals to prevent rubber-stamping.

**Limitations**
The assessment is constrained by data-quality issues, including shared-account usage and source-system mapping errors. The absence of direct evidence of wrongdoing does not preclude misconduct, but neither does it confirm it. Further investigation is required to distinguish between control exceptions, emergency handling, and potential fraud.

**Next Steps**
The audit team will prioritize the high-priority case clusters for detailed review, with a focus on vendor documentation, approval logs, and emergency justifications. Findings will be reported to management for corrective action.
