# Process Redesign for Sustainable, High-Resilience Industrial Spare-Parts Distribution

## 1. System Boundary and Assumptions

### Operational and Lifecycle Scope
The system boundary for this process redesign is defined as an extended **gate-to-delivery** and **internal manufacturing/refurbishment** operational lifecycle for the spare-parts distribution network (12,000 annual orders). The boundary includes all activities from order intake to confirmed point-of-use customer delivery, including secondary reverse-logistics loops and internal manufacturing cycles:

*   **Inside the Boundary:**
    *   **Internal Energy & Machining (`energy_kwh`):** Machine group operations across `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, and `inspection_cell`, including idling, setup, and active processing energy, as well as handling power in packaging cells.
    *   **Material Consumption & Scrap (`material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`):** Raw metal blank scrap from primary machining, rework machining scrap, obsolete parts from forecast batch mismatches, standard packaging corrugated/cushioning materials, and reinforced heavy-duty crating.
    *   **Outbound & Reverse Transport (`transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode`):** Well-to-wheel transport emissions across all active distribution modes (`parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`), explicitly accounting for dedicated van empty return legs (deadhead emissions) and secondary legs resulting from partial shipments (`partial_shipment` $\rightarrow$ `backorder_wait`).
    *   **Process Quality & Exception Loops:** Rework loops (`Inspection` $\rightarrow$ `Rework production` $\rightarrow$ `CNC production`), repackaging cycles (`Rework packaging`), customer damage returns, and order cancellations due to availability delays.

*   **Outside the Boundary:**
    *   Deep Tier-2+ extraction and smelting of raw alloy ingots prior to supplier blank delivery.
    *   Customer-side machine operation energy during the operating life of the installed spare part.
    *   End-of-life part scrapping/recycling after decades of field operation (except immediate transit-damaged returns).

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SYSTEM BOUNDARY: Extended Gate-to-Delivery & Internal Manufacturing Operations                          |
|                                                                                                         |
|  [Order Intake] ---> [Availability Check] ---> [Pick Stock] / [MTO / Emergency CNC]                     |
|                                                      |                 |                                |
|                                             [Partial Shipments]   [Heat Treat]                          |
|                                                      |                 |                                |
|                                                      v                 v                                |
|  [Confirmed Delivery] <--- [Outbound Logistics] <--- [Packaging] <--- [Inspection] <--> [Rework CNC]   |
|   (Ground/Air/Van/Truck)   (Standard/Reinforced)                                                        |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Key Operational Assumptions and Data Uncertainties
1.  **Flattened Order Log Representation:** Cases represent whole customer orders. Where `Pick from stock` leads to `Partial shipment` (1,010 cases, 14.3% of stock orders), downstream transport legs and packaging events multiply for a single order identifier.
2.  **Transport Carbon Factors:** Carbon intensity follows modal physics: air freight carries a ~4–6x carbon penalty per order over ground parcel (18.5 vs. 3.6–4.2 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$), while dedicated vans carry an extreme carbon intensity (38.0–52.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$) largely driven by single-order capacity utilization and empty return trips.
3.  **Customer SLA & Slack Elasticity:** Service-contract customers operate under contractual uptime SLAs where unmitigated delays carry severe financial penalties. Conversely, regular customers exhibit schedule elasticity if commitments are communicated transparently up front.
4.  **Data Uncertainties:** The event log captures scrap and energy per order but does not isolate machine warm-up/idle power from cut time; empty backhaul emissions for dedicated vans are integrated into the trip emission factor rather than logged as distinct return cases.

To evaluate environmental performance alongside service, quality, and resilience, the following multidimensional indicator set is derived directly from the event attributes and discovered process paths:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Rework Energy Overhead Ratio** | $\%$ of total energy | `energy_kwh` in cases where `rework_flag == True` / Total `energy_kwh` | $< 5\%$ of total fleet energy (currently elevated on Path 83) |

| **High-Carbon Mode Share** | $\%$ of shipped cases | Cases where `route_mode` $\in$ (`parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`) / Total Shipped | Decrease from $17.3\%$ ($2,040 / 11,800$) to $< 8\%$ |

| **Route Redundancy Index (R1)** | Boolean / Count | Availability of verified backup carrier lane per customer segment | $100\%$ active backup routes during disruption |

## 3. Intervention Options

### Option 1: Green Shipping Recommender & Air-to-Ground Shift (S1 & S4)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract (16% share, 1,920 cases; 22% SP1 non-conformance) and Standard-stock Regular orders shipped via `Package standard` $\rightarrow$ `Ship air` (620 cases).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Implements a real-time dispatch engine at `Package standard`. The engine calculates temporal route slack:
    $$\text{Slack} = \text{Promised Lead Time} - \text{Warehouse Elapsed Time} - \text{Ground Transit Time}$$
    If $\text{Slack} \ge 0$ with historical on-time probability $> 85\%$, the order is locked to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck`. For Service-contract orders, this is combined with an automated **priority picking protocol** that compresses internal warehouse dwell from 18h to 4h, unlocking an additional 14h transit buffer. This eliminates unnecessary air freight (saving ~14.3 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$ per converted order).
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Service SLAs are fully preserved because ground transit easily satisfies the 41h p90 commitment when warehouse dwell is compressed. Air transport is maintained in the carrier routing table as a dynamic surge fallback, satisfying Policy R1.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Conformance rate of Policy SP1; transition frequency of `Package standard` $\rightarrow$ `Ship air` vs. `Ship ground`; warehouse dwell duration between `Receive order` and `Package standard`; OTD rate for Service-contract stock orders.

### Option 2: Dynamic Batching with SLA-Bounded Dwell-Time Caps (S2)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Made-to-order (MTO) orders (Regular and Service-contract, 34% combined share) entering `Plan production` $\rightarrow$ `Batch wait` (3,020 cases) and `CNC production` $\rightarrow$ `Heat treatment` (1,820 cases).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Replaces open-ended static batch targets (which hold items up to 38h to reach 12–20 parts for a 1.3 kWh/part rating) with a dynamic, SLA-aware queue release timer. Orders in `Batch wait` are released to `CNC production` or `Heat treatment` as soon as either: (a) optimal batch size ($N \ge 10$) is attained, or (b) the earliest due-date order in queue reaches its maximum allowable dwell threshold (12h for Service-contract, 24h for Regular). This stabilizes energy consumption at an efficient 1.4–1.7 kWh/item while preventing queue stagnation.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Eliminates the 1.7x late-delivery risk multiplier associated with heat-treatment batch starvation; compresses MTO p90 cycle times from 180h down to $< 125\text{h}$; drastically curtails downstream panic expediting to `Ship air`.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Queue dwell distribution in `Batch wait`; batch size histograms at `CNC_A` and `Heat treatment`; correlation between `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, and `late_delivery_flag`.

### Option 3: Slack-Based Machine Routing from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** MTO orders routed to `CNC_B` resulting in Path 83 (`MTO` $\rightarrow$ `Batch wait` $\rightarrow$ `CNC_B` $\rightarrow$ `Inspection` $\rightarrow$ `Rework` $\rightarrow$ `Ground`; 5% share, 600 cases).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Process mining indicates `CNC_B` generates an 18% rework rate compared to 9% on `CNC_A`, with rework loops consuming 32.0 $\text{kWh}$ and 1.8 $\text{kg}$ scrap per order. The scheduling logic is modified to prioritize `CNC_A` for all complex MTO geometries, reserving `CNC_B` strictly for simple geometries or when `CNC_A` queue depth threatens delivery slack.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Directly eliminates ~50% of rework loops on converted volume; reduces scrap by 0.9–1.1 kg per avoided rework case; reduces median cycle time for affected paths from 160h to $< 110\text{h}$; improves first-pass quality.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Routing split between `CNC_A` and `CNC_B`; transition count of `Inspection` $\rightarrow$ `Rework production`; `material_scrap_kg` and `energy_kwh` by machine group.

### Option 4: Bi-Daily Consolidated Truck Scheduling for Non-Urgent Stock (S5)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock Regular orders (39% share, 4,680 cases; median cycle time 31h, p90 62h) with delivery slack $> 36\text{h}$.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Batches loose parcel shipments into scheduled consolidated truck departures (`Consolidated truck`) operating on fixed bi-daily timetables (11:00 and 17:00). Consolidated truck freight achieves higher vehicle fill rates, reducing transport carbon emissions by 35–55% compared to individual ground parcel couriers (reducing emissions from 4.2 to ~2.3 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$/order).
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Introduces a controlled 12–18h consolidation dwell time, which is absorbed by the generous 62h p90 lead-time envelope of Regular stock orders without increasing late deliveries.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Transition frequency `Package standard` $\rightarrow$ `Consolidated truck`; dwell time at staging docks; transport carbon savings; OTD compliance in the Standard-stock Regular segment.

### Option 5: Automated Packaging-Route Compatibility & Quality Gate (S6 & Q1)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Packaging rework transitions (`Package standard` $\rightarrow$ `Rework packaging` [260 cases] and `Package reinforced` $\rightarrow$ `Rework packaging` [360 cases]), and heavy MTO parts ($> 8\text{ kg}$) vulnerable to transit damage.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Enforces an automated digital gate at packaging workstation selection. The ERP system cross-references part weight, dimensional geometry, and selected `route_mode` before generating packaging work orders. Parts $> 8\text{ kg}$ or delicate machined components are automatically routed to `Package reinforced`, while standard durable items are locked into optimized, right-sized `Package standard`.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Completely eliminates 620 annual packaging rework events (5.2% of total packaging volume), preventing packaging material waste and uncrating labor; reduces in-transit damage returns by 40% on heavy MTO parts; prevents late deliveries caused by packaging loops (saving 8–16h per case).
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Transition count to `Rework packaging`; Q1 policy conformance rate; `package_material_kg` consumption; damage-related return/warranty claim rate.

### Option 6: In-Line Inspection Quality Gates & Queue Pacing (S7)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Inspection` $\rightarrow$ `Rework production` (470 cases) and `Inspection` $\rightarrow$ `Package reinforced` (3,470 cases), targeting rushed inspections during queue surges.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Rushed inspection queues allow subtle dimensional defects to pass into downstream packaging and field shipment, causing severe material and transport waste upon return. This intervention implements standardized digital CMM (Coordinate Measuring Machine) inspection protocols with hard minimum pacing times and automated calibration alerts after every 15 MTO cycles.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Catches machining non-conformances prior to energy-intensive heat treatment or packaging crating; reduces downstream customer defect escapes by $> 60\%$; cuts rework energy waste while adding only 15–30 minutes to inspection dwell.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** `Inspection` dwell time distribution; rework loop initiation points (`Inspection` vs. customer return); scrap kg logged at inspection; downstream warranty return rates.

### Option 7: Critical-Downtime Triage, Precautionary Downgrading, and Shared Courier Pooling (S8 & S9)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract segment (11% share, 1,320 cases; 38.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$ average; Path 84 dedicated van emitting 52.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Splits the `Critical-downtime` case intake into two structured tiers:
    1.  *Tier 1 (Active Plant Breakdown):* Retains immediate `Emergency production` and dedicated rapid transport, but transitions dedicated van contracts to an industrial shared-courier network with guaranteed backhaul matching to eliminate empty return deadheading.
    2.  *Tier 2 (Precautionary / Next-Shift Critical):* Routed to priority stock pick / fast-track CNC with express ground parcel or dedicated consolidated express.
    Enforces mandatory logging of downtime telemetry to satisfy Policy SP2.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Eliminates 31% of unjustified SP2 dedicated van trips; slashes average Critical transport emissions from 38.0 to $< 18.0\text{ kgCO}_2\text{e}$; preserves 100% on-time delivery ($\le 15\text{h}$) for genuine plant-down emergencies.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Policy SP2 conformance rate (% dedicated vans with verified Tier 1 justification); ratio of Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 critical orders; transport carbon intensity in the Critical segment; plant-down SLA breach rate ($< 1\%$ target).

### Option 8: Intelligent Availability Checks to Eliminate Partial Shipments (S11)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Availability check` $\rightarrow$ `Pick from stock` $\rightarrow$ `Partial shipment` (1,010 cases, 14.3% of stock orders; Path 80 generating 24% late delivery, 7.2 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$, and 79h cycle time).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Modifies the ERP availability check algorithm. Instead of automatically splitting multi-line orders when a single low-value item is missing, the system evaluates line-item criticality and restock arrival times. If backordered items will arrive within 12–24h, the order is held in an aggregated buffer to release as a single, consolidated shipment (`Pick from stock` $\rightarrow$ `Package standard` $\rightarrow$ `Consolidated truck`/`Ground`).
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Eliminates duplicate corrugated packaging and redundant freight trips for over 700 split shipments annually; reduces late delivery on backordered orders from 24% to $< 8\%$; lowers customer handling touchpoints while improving order completeness.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Transition frequency `Pick from stock` $\rightarrow$ `Partial shipment`; backorder wait duration; secondary shipment transport $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$; customer order fulfillment completeness index.

### Option 9: Carbon-Aware Promise-Date Negotiation for Regular MTO (S10)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Made-to-order Regular segment (22% share, 2,640 cases; 94h median, 180h p90 cycle time, 18% late delivery, 0.90 kg scrap).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Introduces dynamic promise-date quoting at `Receive order`. The quoting engine provides transparency to non-urgent Regular customers: selecting a flexible delivery window (+48h) offers a "Green Partner Discount" and allows the production planner to group parts into optimal CNC_A and heat-treatment batches ($\ge 12\text{ parts}$), cutting energy per part from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh and avoiding air expediting.
*   **Service, Quality, and Resilience Effect:** Aligns customer expectations with batch production physics; slashes late deliveries from 18% to $< 7\%$; eliminates schedule volatility and emergency production disruptions on the shop floor.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Customer adoption rate of green promise dates; batch size distribution for Regular MTO; late delivery rate against negotiated promise dates; express air shipping share for Regular MTO.

## 5. Rebound and Displacement Risks

A localized sustainability intervention can easily trigger unintended adverse effects elsewhere in the supply chain if cross-functional interactions are ignored. Below are six specific rebound and displacement risks identified across the process network, along with their systemic mechanics:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| CROSS-PROCESS DISPLACEMENT DYNAMICS                                                                |
|                                                                                                    |
|  [Max Batching Size] --------(+ Waiting Time)--------> [Downstream Schedule Panic] ---> [AIR SHIP] |
|        |                                                                                           |
|        +----------------------(+ Forecast Error)-----> [Inventory Obsolescence Scrap]              |
|                                                                                                    |
|  [Lightweight Packaging] -----(+ Transit Damage)-----> [Scrapped Heavy Alloy Part]                 |
|                                                              |                                     |
|                                                              +--> [Full Re-Machining (32 kWh)]     |
|                                                              +--> [Emergency Van (52 kgCO2e)]      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Batching-Induced Transport Displacement (Batching vs. Express Freight)
*   *Mechanism:* Forcing CNC and heat-treatment operations to wait until maximum batch thresholds ($\ge 15\text{ parts}$) are met minimizes machine energy per unit (dropping to 1.3 kWh/item). However, queue dwell inflates up to 38h.
*   *Displacement Effect:* When orders finally exit inspection, their remaining SLA buffer is completely exhausted. Logistics coordinators panic and switch outbound transport from ground parcel (3.6 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$) to express air freight (23.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$) or dedicated vans (52.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$). The minor energy saving of ~1.5 kWh (~0.6 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$ equivalent) is displaced by a massive 20–50 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$ logistics emissions spike.

### 2. Packaging Dematerialization vs. Catastrophic Metal Scrap (Packaging vs. Scrap)
*   *Mechanism:* Attempting to minimize packaging footprint (`package_material_kg`) by replacing heavy reinforced wooden/foam crating with lightweight standard corrugated cartons for MTO parts.
*   *Displacement Effect:* Made-to-order industrial parts exceeding 8 kg suffer transit impact shocks, causing surface brinelling or geometric distortion. A 40% increase in transit damage requires full component scrapping (0.85 kg high-grade alloy scrap), an entire duplicate remanufacturing cycle (20.0–32.0 kWh), and hot-shot emergency courier delivery (52.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$). The material saving of 2 kg of packaging generates a 50x net environmental penalty.

### 3. Machine Routing Bottlenecking & Emergency Production Cascades (Routing vs. Energy)
*   *Mechanism:* Shifting 100% of Made-to-order volume away from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` to eliminate `CNC_B`'s 18% rework rate.
*   *Displacement Effect:* `CNC_A` becomes a severe capacity bottleneck. Queue times skyrocket, starving downstream assembly and triggering the `Availability check` $\rightarrow$ `Emergency production` path (760 cases). Emergency production bypasses standard setup efficiencies, consumes unbatched overtime power, and forces emergency dedicated courier dispatches.

### 4. Overproduction Obsolescence Rebound (Batching vs. Material Waste)
*   *Mechanism:* Running large standard batches for low-turnover or customized spare-part variants to optimize CNC spindle utilization and heat-treat thermal cycles.
*   *Displacement Effect:* The event log proves that 6% of batch-produced items remain unshipped after 14 days due to demand forecast errors. In customized industrial applications, these parts become obsolete dead stock that is eventually written off and scrapped, turning raw embodied material and processing energy into pure industrial waste.

### 5. Partial Shipment Suppression vs. Downstream Plant Outage (Consolidation vs. Resilience)
*   *Mechanism:* Strictly blocking all partial shipments to prevent secondary freight legs (saving 3.6 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$) on multi-line orders where an in-stock seal is ready but a secondary structural fastener is backordered.
*   *Displacement Effect:* Holding the critical seal prevents an industrial customer from executing a planned maintenance overhaul, leading to an unplanned catastrophic equipment shutdown. The customer responds with emergency escalation, demanding hot-shot helicopter/air courier charter, canceling contracts, or running diesel auxiliary generators at the plant—completely obliterating the environmental saving.

### 6. Carbon-Aware SLA Gaming and Customer Behavior Rebound (Negotiation vs. Expediting)
*   *Mechanism:* Providing commercial discounts for customers who accept longer "green" delivery windows.
*   *Displacement Effect:* Customers systematically accept the eco-discount to capture cost savings, but when their maintenance schedules tighten, they retroactively declare false "Critical-downtime" emergencies. This shifts volume from planned ground transport into emergency dedicated vans (52.0 $\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$), creating extreme schedule volatility for dispatchers and degrading network predictability.

## 6. Balanced Recommendation

Rather than deploying disconnected green initiatives, the organization must implement an integrated, multi-tier **Sustainable Process Operating Architecture**. This architecture balances decarbonization, quality assurance, customer service, and supply chain resilience through four mutually reinforcing pillars:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| INTEGRATED SUSTAINABLE PROCESS ARCHITECTURE                                                        |
|                                                                                                    |
|  [PILLAR 1: Upstream Demand & Promise Engineering]                                                 |
|   * Dynamic SLA Quoting (S10)        * Intelligent Anti-Splitting Availability Engine (S11)        |
|                               |                                                                    |
|                               v                                                                    |
|  [PILLAR 2: SLA-Constrained Shop-Floor Manufacturing & Quality Pacing]                             |
|   * Dynamic Dwell-Capped Batching (S2) * Slack-Aware Machine Dispatching (S3) * In-Line Gates (S7) |
|                               |                                                                    |
|                               v                                                                    |
|  [PILLAR 3: Algorithmic Packaging & Green Logistics Orchestration]                                 |
|   * Green Shipping Recommender (S1/S4)* Automated Packaging Gate (S6/Q1) * Critical Triage (S8/S9) |
|                               |                                                                    |
|                               v                                                                    |
|  [PILLAR 4: Closed-Loop Governance & Dynamic Resilience Safeguards]                                |
|   * Conformance Enforcement (SP1/SP2/Q1) * Multi-Carrier Active Standby (R1) * Exception Logs (S12) |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Pillar 1: Upstream Demand & Promise Engineering (Interventions S10, S11)
*   **Decouple and Buffer:** Implement carbon-aware promise dates for Regular MTO customers, trading commercial discounts for a 48h production buffer that enables optimal production batching.
*   **Eliminate Redundant Freight:** Deploy intelligent availability checks that hold non-critical multi-item orders for up to 24h to ship as a single consolidated package, eliminating $> 70\%$ of partial-shipment secondary freight legs without harming customer assembly schedules.

### Pillar 2: SLA-Constrained Shop-Floor Manufacturing & Quality Pacing (Interventions S2, S3, S7)
*   **Dynamic Batching:** Enforce hard dwell-time caps in `Batch wait` (12h for Contract, 24h for Regular) and heat treatment, capturing 80% of maximum energy efficiency while preventing queue-induced late deliveries.
*   **Quality-Biased Dispatching:** Automatically prioritize `CNC_A` for complex MTO parts while establishing an automated load-shedding threshold to `CNC_B` to prevent bottleneck starvation.
*   **In-Line Quality Gates:** Standardize digital inspection pacing at `Inspection_cell` to eliminate defect escapes, directly targeting the elimination of 470 rework cycles and 620 repackaging events.

### Pillar 3: Algorithmic Packaging & Green Logistics Orchestration (Interventions S1, S4, S5, S6, S8, S9)
*   **Green Shipping Default:** Mandate ground parcel or consolidated truck as the algorithmic default whenever delivery slack exceeds 85% probability, paired with priority warehouse picking for Service-contract orders.
*   **Poka-Yoke Packaging:** Integrate packaging station gating (Policy Q1) to enforce reinforced crating for heavy/MTO parts while standardizing right-sized cartons for standard items.
*   **Two-Tier Critical Triage:** Divide Critical-downtime cases into Tier 1 (Active Breakdown $\rightarrow$ pooled emergency courier with backhaul sharing) and Tier 2 (Precautionary $\rightarrow$ priority express ground), cutting dedicated van carbon emissions by $> 50\%$.

### Pillar 4: Closed-Loop Governance & Dynamic Resilience Safeguards (Interventions S12, Policy R1)
*   **Multi-Carrier Redundancy:** In strict compliance with Policy R1, maintain active, contracted secondary routing lanes across all regional corridors. If a primary consolidated carrier experiences disruption, the dispatch engine dynamically reroutes shipments to express ground or air before SLAs breach.

## 7. Validation and Governance Plan

To ensure sustained operational adherence and verify environmental improvements post-implementation, the organization will deploy an event-log-driven governance and monitoring framework.


### 1. Process-Mining Conformance Checking Framework
Continuous, automated auditing against declarative business rules executed directly on the real-time event log:

*   **Policy SP1 Conformance Monitor (Green Shipping):**
    *   *Rule:* Flag an operational non-conformance event whenever `route_mode == parcel_air` AND the calculated route slack at packaging completion satisfies $P(\text{On-Time} \mid \text{Ground}) \ge 85\%$.
    *   *Target:* Reduce SP1 violations from $22\%$ down to $< 2\%$ in Standard-stock Service-contract cases.
*   **Policy SP2 Conformance Monitor (Dedicated Van Justification):**
    *   *Rule:* Verify that every case executing `Package reinforced` $\rightarrow$ `Dedicated van` is preceded by a validated `Critical_Tier1_Justification` event containing customer telemetry.
    *   *Target:* Eliminate missing justifications ($0\%$ non-conformance, down from $31\%$).
*   **Policy Q1 Conformance Monitor (Heavy Part Packaging Protection):**
    *   *Rule:* Flag any case where part weight $> 8\text{ kg}$ and `case_type == Made-to-order` transitions to `Package standard` without an electronic `Low_Damage_Risk_Certificate`.
    *   *Target:* $\ge 99.5\%$ compliance across all manufacturing shifts.

### 2. Segment-Level Multi-Criteria Performance Auditing
Weekly automated process-mining dashboards tracking key performance indicators across all five customer-product segments:

1.  **Environmental Trajectory:** Trend analysis of average $\text{kWh}/\text{order}$, $\text{kg scrap}/\text{order}$, and $\text{transport kgCO}_2\text{e}/\text{order}$ against the baseline.
2.  **Service & Quality Protection:** Real-time tracking of median/p90 cycle times, late delivery rates against promised SLAs, and first-pass inspection yields.
3.  **Process Variant Drift:** Monitoring DFG variant frequencies to detect any emergence of unauthorized exception loops (e.g., hidden informal rework loops or bypasses of `Consolidated truck` staging).

### 3. S12 Sustainability Exception Governance & Escalation
When operational staff must override green recommendations to protect urgent customer relationships, the override is managed through a structured digital protocol:

*   **Mandatory Reason Tagging:** Overrides require selecting a root-cause code (e.g., `CARRIER_STRIKE`, `UNEXPECTED_MACHINE_BREAKDOWN`, `CUSTOMER_SLA_EMERGENCY`).
*   **Weekly Exception Review:** A cross-functional governance board (Operations Manager, Lead Process Mining Engineer, Logistics Director, Sustainability Officer) reviews the S12 Exception Log weekly.
*   **Root-Cause Mitigation:** If SP1 overrides spike on a specific regional route, logistics immediately investigates carrier transit reliability rather than blaming dispatchers, dynamically recalibrating the recommender's confidence parameters.

### 4. Dynamic Stress-Testing and Resilience Recalibration (Policy R1)
*   **Quarterly Outage Simulations:** Conduct scheduled stress-tests simulating carrier ground network shutdowns and primary `CNC_A` spindle failures to verify that alternative routing paths (Policy R1) and secondary machine allocations activate smoothly without data loss or SLA collapse.
*   **Seasonal Dynamic Recalibration:** During peak winter freight disruption windows, the SP1 confidence threshold is automatically adapted (e.g., raised from 85% to 90% slack certainty) to protect service commitments without compromising long-term environmental objectives.
