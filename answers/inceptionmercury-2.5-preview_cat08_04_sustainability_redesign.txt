

1. System boundary and assumptions
**Scope:** This assessment covers the distributor's internal operational boundary from order receipt to confirmed delivery (cradle-to-delivery). This includes processing energy (machine_group), material waste (material_scrap_kg, package_material_kg), transport emissions (transport_kgco2e), and rework loops (rework_flag).

**Inclusions:**
*   Order processing, inventory checks, and production batches.
*   Packaging and outbound logistics (route_mode).
*   Internal rework (inspection loops, production rework).

**Exclusions:**
*   Upstream supplier production and raw material sourcing.
*   Customer-side installation or product use.
*   End-of-life product disposal (unless classified as return/rework).

**Uncertainties:**
*   Carrier emission factors vary; transport_kgco2e relies on average estimates.
*   Obsolescence risk depends on future demand accuracy, which is not fully captured in historical logs.
*   Customer tolerance for lead-time increases is assumed based on current promised_lead_time_hours adherence.

2. Sustainability-related process indicators
Based on event attributes and DFG paths, we define the following indicators:

*   **Energy Intensity:** Avg energy_kwh per line item, segmented by batch_id and machine_group.
*   **Transport Emissions:** Avg transport_kgco2e per order, segmented by route_mode and segment.
*   **Material Waste:** Avg material_scrap_kg and package_material_kg per order.
*   **Rework Rate:** Ratio of cases with rework_flag = True to total cases.
*   **Batch Efficiency:** Avg batch_size vs. wait time per batch; Obsolescence rate (items >14 days not shipped).
*   **Service Performance:** Late delivery % and median/p90 cycle_time.
*   **Quality Compliance:** Rate of Q1 violations (reinforced packaging misses) correlated with rework/returns.
*   **Resilience:** Share of orders on alternative routes; Dedicated van utilization rate.

5. Rebound and displacement risks
1.  **Batching vs. Obsolescence:** Longer batch waits (S2) reduce energy per item but increase risk of obsolescence (currently 6% for batch-produced items), causing waste.
2.  **Consolidation vs. Expediting:** S5 consolidates shipments but adds wait time; if this causes missed deadlines, it may trigger more air shipments later, negating transport gains.
3.  **Packaging vs. Material Waste:** Enforcing reinforced packaging (S6) reduces damage but doubles package_material_kg; unnecessary for low-risk parts, causing material displacement.
4.  **Quality Gates vs. Throughput:** Moving production to CNC_A (S3) reduces rework but may increase queue times, potentially delaying other orders.
5.  **Emergency Sharing vs. Resilience:** S8 reduces dedicated van use but lowers capacity buffer; during a disruption, resilience could suffer if shared lanes are unavailable.
6.  **Carbon-Aware Promise vs. Customer Trust:** Negotiating dates (S10/S4) reduces emissions; if customers perceive delivery as slow, they may shift to competitors without similar constraints.

6. Balanced recommendation
We recommend a four-pillar redesign package focusing on high-volume, low-risk changes first, followed by quality-driven improvements.

1.  **Smart Routing (S1, S4, S5):** Enforce SP1 compliance strictly for Standard-stock Service-contract. Use ground consolidation for non-urgent stock (S5). This addresses the highest transport impact (air/ground ratio) without compromising Critical-downtime service.
2.  **Production & Quality (S2, S3, S6):** Cap batch waits (S2) to limit lateness, but prefer CNC_A for MTO (S3) to reduce rework waste (high energy cost per scrap kg). Implement routing checks (S6) to prevent packaging rework loops.
3.  **Resilience Guardrails (S8, R1):** Replace low-utilization dedicated vans with shared emergency lanes (S8) but mandate alternative route availability for Service-contract customers (R1).
4.  **Governance (S12):** Log all SP2/Q1 exceptions. If SP1 compliance drops >10% during implementation, rollback S4/S5 for affected segments.

This package prioritizes transport efficiency in high-volume segments where service risks are lower, while using quality interventions to reduce waste in high-energy MTO processes.

7. Validation and governance plan
**Implementation Phase:**
*   **A/B Testing:** Apply S4 and S5 to 50% of eligible Standard-stock Service-contract orders for 4 weeks.
*   **Conformance Monitoring:** Automate checks for SP1, SP2, and Q1 violations in real-time. Alert if SP2 justification is missing (currently 31% rate).

**Post-Implementation Analysis:**
*   **Event Log Comparison:** Compare new log to historical DFG. Track changes in route_mode frequencies and rework loops.
*   **Service Monitoring:** Segment service levels (late % and p90 time). If MTO late delivery exceeds baseline by >5%, tighten batch caps.
*   **Resilience Audit:** Verify alternative routes remain open for Service-contract cases during any observed carrier disruptions.

**Exception Governance:**
*   Review the sustainability exception log monthly. If overrides on S4/S5 exceed 10% of volume, retrain the green recommender model on route reliability.

*   **Final Decision:** If transport emissions decrease by >15% with no significant rise in late delivery (>2%), expand S1/S5 to 100% of stock orders.
