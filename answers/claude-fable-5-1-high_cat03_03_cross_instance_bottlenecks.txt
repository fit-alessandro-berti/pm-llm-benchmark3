## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3). High-arity events (the 12:05 `Batch Cure`, 10:20 `Quarantine Lot`, 13:50 `Release Lot`) are worth treating as nodes because they synchronize several object types at one timestamp.

**Edge types.**
- Structural O2O edges: `requires` (Job→Component), `contains` (ComponentLot→Component), `batches` (Batch→Job), `consolidates` (Shipment→Job).
- Event-mediated co-participation: objects related to the same event (B1–OVEN-1–RACK-7–J1–J2–J3–C1–C2–C3 via `Batch Cure`; J4–C4–CNC-2–FIX-A via `Machine Process`).
- Resource-hold intervals, reconstructed from acquire/release (J3–FIX-A 09:00–11:40) or state records (RACK-7 cleaning until 14:35; CAL-3 recalibration 16:35–17:20).
- Derived Job–Job coupling: shared Batch, Shipment, ComponentLot, or time-overlapping Tool/Machine hold.

**Edge list (D = directly recorded, I = inferred).**

```

J4 requires C4 (D)      J5 requires C5 (D)      J6 requires C6 (D)
```

The derived layer shows all six jobs in a single connected component: J6 has no batch and no shared lot, yet is coupled to J5 through S3 and CAL-3, J5 to J2 through L7 and to J4 through B2, J4 to J3 through FIX-A and to J1 through S1. "Independent-looking" jobs are one graph.

**Higher-order motifs (more than two object types).**
1. *Batch synchronization star*: Batch–Job×k–Machine–Tool (B1, J1/J2/J3, OVEN-1, RACK-7). The batch starts at the latest member's readiness, exporting one job's upstream delay to the others.
2. *Tool-mediated machine handoff*: Job–Tool–Machine–Job (J3 holds FIX-A; J4 needs it on CNC-2). The machine is free (10:05–10:47) but the transition is infeasible, so the wait is invisible to the machine view.
3. *Lot spanning batches*: ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch (L7 → C2, C5 → J2, J5 → B1, B2). One quality decision touches both batches and, via the override, splits into compliance exposure (J2) and schedule delay (J5).
4. *Shipment across batches*: Shipment–Job–Batch (S1 → J1 ∈ B1, J4 ∈ B2). S1 inherits the worst case of two batch timelines; S2 (both B1) and S3 (J5 ∈ B2, J6 unbatched) do not.
5. *Calibration serialization*: Tool–Machine–Job–Shipment (CAL-3, TEST-1, J5, J6, S3). Jobs sharing a shipment also share the calibration tool, so recalibration lands on S3's last-pack time.
6. *Rack turn cycle*: Tool–Batch–Batch–Machine (RACK-7, B1, B2, OVEN-1). One rack, 50 min cure plus 100 min cleaning, paces the oven path regardless of oven utilization.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are in minutes; each states the counterfactual it depends on. None is causal proof.

**C1. B1 synchronization waiting (J1, J2).** J1 ready 08:10, J2 08:25, J3 11:55, cure 12:05. Waits: J1 = 235, J2 = 220, J3 = 10; total 465 job-minutes. Counterfactual A (each job cured alone when ready, oven and rack free): avoidable waiting 235 and 220. Counterfactual B (start when the second job is ready): J1 waits 15, J2 0, saving 440, but J3 slips to a later batch and RACK-7 is consumed earlier. Caveat: J2's 08:25 readiness is only valid because of the override; under policy J2 was releasable at 13:50, so its real wait is undefined and B1 should arguably have held only J1 and J3.

**C2. FIX-A for J4.** J4 at CNC-2 09:50, `Machine Process` 11:45: 115 min. FIX-A released 11:40, J4 started five minutes later. Under the counterfactual "FIX-A available at 09:50": 09:50–10:05 CNC-2 busy (15, not tool-attributable); 10:05–10:47 CNC-2 idle with J4 present (42, tool-attributable with high plausibility); 10:47–11:40 CNC-2 busy with work that overtook J4 (53, attributable only if J4 would have held the machine); 11:40–11:45 changeover (5). Bounds: 42–100. J3's 160-min hold exceeds plausible machining time and spans the idle window, suggesting the fixture was held through inspection/rework (conjecture).

**C3. S1 consolidation for J1.** Packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25: 355 min. Counterfactual "J1 dispatched with S2/S3-like lead time" (25–35 min after last pack): attributable ≈ 320–330. J4 waited 15. About 95% of S1's holding time fell on the early job.

**C4. CAL-3 for J6.** Ready 16:05, test 17:25: 80 min. Counterfactual "no recalibration between J5 and J6": J6 starts ~16:40, wait ≈ 35, so ≈ 45 attributable to CAL-3. TEST-1's 50-min idle (16:35–17:25) coincides exactly with recalibration; CAL-3 was occupied 100 min continuously (16:10–17:50), i.e., the test *pair* ran at 100% while TEST-1 alone showed 50%.

**C5. B2 and RACK-7 for J4.** Ready 13:10, B2 start 14:50: 100 min, of which RACK-7 unavailable 85, waiting for J5 5, start latency 10. Counterfactual "J4 cured alone once the rack was clean": saves only 15. OVEN-1's 115-min idle (12:55–14:50) is 87% rack cleaning, not absent demand.

**C6. L7 quarantine for J5.** 10:20–13:50 = 210 min; J5 ready 50 min after release. Counterfactual "no quarantine": J5 ready ≈ 11:10, but B2 would still be gated by RACK-7 (14:35) and J4 (13:10). The lot constraint bound for J5 but was *masked* for B2 by the rack; removing one constraint alone yields almost nothing.

**C7. Cumulative J1 exposure.** 235 (B1) + 355 (S1) = 590 min waiting on other jobs. The six-job sample cannot flag this: the maximum sample z-score at n = 6 is (n−1)/√n ≈ 2.04, so "no job above 1.8 SD" excludes almost nothing.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job.** Visible: control-flow, rework loops, edge medians. Hidden: activity-less waits (tool, batch, shipment), lot events (absent by extraction), the override. Misleading: "Stage Component→Machine Process and Pack→Dispatch medians of 1.1 h look normal", when J4's staging wait was ≈ 2 h and J1's dispatch wait ≈ 6 h; six-case medians hide bimodality and the 1.8-SD test is vacuous at n = 6.
- **Machine.** Visible: utilization, sequence, gaps. Hidden: demand present during gaps. `Batch Cure` appears once per batch, so per-job oven throughput is understated. Misleading: "longest gap 48 min on CNC-2 means slack", when J4 stood tool-blocked in it.
- **Batch.** Visible: Load→Cure→Unload (~50 min cure). Hidden: member readiness, hence the 235/220-min waits; rack cleaning unless RACK-7 is joined. Misleading: "batch lead time is short".
- **Tool.** Visible (if acquire/release exist): holds, cleaning, calibration. Hidden: who waited; duplicates one `Batch Cure` under RACK-7 and OVEN-1. Misleading: "FIX-A utilization is moderate" although one 160-min hold blocked the only other user.
- **ComponentLot.** Visible: quarantine/release. Hidden: affected jobs (linkage inconsistent across pipelines) and the bypass by J2. Misleading: a clean 210-min quarantine with no downstream consequence.
- **Shipment.** Visible: Create→Load→Dispatch (1.1 h). Hidden: J1's 355 min before `Create Shipment`. Duplicates `Dispatch` (once per shipment, three times per job view). Misleading: "no shipment bottleneck".

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting.** Utilization measures one resource; a transition is feasible only when a conjunction (machine ∧ tool ∧ batch partners ∧ released lot ∧ shipment partners) holds. The binding member rotates (FIX-A morning, RACK-7 early afternoon, CAL-3 late afternoon), so each resource is idle often while the conjunction is rarely satisfied. Idle time is a symptom of blocking: CNC-2's 42-min, OVEN-1's 100-min and TEST-1's 50-min gaps all coincide with tool unavailability. Synchronization then multiplies one job's readiness variability into waiting for several others, so waiting scales with coupling, not machine load.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J3 → FIX-A → CNC-2 → J4 → B2 → S1 → J1** (Job, Tool, Machine, Batch, Shipment).
- Temporal precedence: J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00; J4 arrives 09:50; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; FIX-A released 11:40; J4 processes 11:45; ready for B2 13:10; B2 cures 14:50; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25 with J1 packed since 13:30.
- Resource feasibility: J4's `Machine Process` needs CNC-2 ∧ FIX-A, first jointly available 11:40; S1 needs both members packed.
- Policy constraints: single-fixture assignment; ship-complete rule for S1.
- Causal speculation: J3 held FIX-A through inspection/rework. Had FIX-A been released ~10:05, J4 would have been batch-ready ~11:30, but B2 would still have waited for RACK-7 (14:35), so the FIX-A saving for S1 depends on what happened to J4 between 15:40 and 19:10 (Narrative C). J1's 355-min wait is over-determined.

**Narrative B: L7 → C2/C5 → J2/J5 → B1/B2 → RACK-7 → S3** (ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Shipment).
- Temporal precedence: L7 quarantined 10:20; J2 enters B1 12:05 under override; L7 released 13:50; J5 ready 14:40; RACK-7 clean 14:35; B2 starts 14:50; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; S3 dispatches 35 min after J6's pack.
- Resource feasibility: J5 could not stage until release; B2 could not start until RACK-7 was clean.
- Policy constraints: quarantine blocks processing; the override let J2 bypass it, so the log says "processed normally" while policy says "not releasable".
- Causal speculation: had the quarantine ended earlier, J5 would still have waited for RACK-7; had RACK-7 been ready earlier, B2 would have waited for J5 or J4 would have cured alone. Two constraints bound within five minutes; neither is provably dominant.

**Narrative C: J5/J6 → CAL-3 → TEST-1 → S3 (and plausibly J4 → S1)** (Job, Tool, Machine, Shipment, Batch).
- Temporal precedence: J5 ready 16:00, J6 16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; J4 unloaded from B2 ~15:40 yet packed 19:10.
- Resource feasibility: `Final Test` needs TEST-1 ∧ CAL-3; CAL-3 occupied continuously 16:10–17:50.
- Policy constraints: recalibration trigger (count- or time-based, unknown); FIFO between J5 and J6.
- Causal speculation: J4 has no `Final Test` in the excerpt; 3.5 h from unload to pack is consistent with J4 queuing behind J5, recalibration and J6 (test ≈ 17:55–18:20, pack ≈ 19:10). If so, CAL-3, not FIX-A, is the proximate cause of S1's late dispatch and J1's 355-min wait. This is the single most valuable missing fact (Section 8).

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Readiness-window batching (synchronization).** Start a batch when the oldest ready member has waited ≥ 60 min or the batch is full; allow single-job cures when the rack is free. Benefit: J1/J2 waits 235/220 → ≤ 60. Helped: early-ready jobs, downstream shipments. Harmed: late members (J3) pushed to the next rack cycle; more RACK-7 cleaning cycles. Cost: energy per part, cure-quality revalidation. Perverse metric: "batch fill rate" or "cures per shift". Evaluation: per-Job (Batch Cure − ready) joined through Batch and Tool objects, RACK-7 occupancy per day; judge on the Job-type OC-DFG edge into Batch Cure, not on OVEN-1 utilization.

2. **Second RACK-7 or offline cleaning (tools).** Benefit: rack cycle 150 → ~50 min; B2 could start 13:10 (J4 alone) or 14:40. Helped: J4, J5, B2, indirectly S1/J1. Harmed: oven becomes binding and its 44% will rise; contamination must be tracked per rack. Cost: capital, cleaning labor. Perverse metric: "rack utilization". Evaluation: Batch–Tool interactions per rack; oven idle time decomposed into rack-blocked vs demand-free using joint Tool and Machine state.

3. **Fixture release discipline for FIX-A (tools).** Release at end of `Machine Process`, not after inspection/rework; add a second fixture if J3's hold is genuinely required. Benefit: J4 saves 42–100 min. Helped: J4, S1, J1. Harmed: reworked jobs needing re-fixturing; extra CNC-2 setups. Cost: setup time, dimensional risk. Perverse metric: "first-pass yield" pressure encourages keeping the fixture. Evaluation: Tool hold vs Machine busy durations per Job; count of (job at machine, machine idle, tool held) triples.

4. **Age-based split dispatch (shipment policy).** Dispatch when any member has been packed > 2 h, or assign shipment membership after packing. Benefit: J1's 355 → ≤ 120 min. Helped: early-packed jobs. Harmed: freight cost, customers wanting consolidated delivery; J4 may travel alone. Perverse metric: "consolidation ratio". Evaluation: per-Job (Dispatch − Pack) by Shipment composition, mixed-batch vs same-batch shipments; Job-type Pack→Dispatch against Shipment-type Create→Dispatch.

5. **Calibration scheduling for CAL-3 (tools).** Recalibrate in TEST-1 idle windows before a test wave, or hold a second calibrated standard. Benefit: J6 saves 45–50 min; S3 earlier; plausibly J4/S1. Harmed: none unless intervals are stretched and measurement risk rises. Cost: second standard, labor. Perverse metric: "tests between calibrations". Evaluation: CAL-3 state timeline joined to Job ready-for-test events; waiting decomposed into machine-busy, tool-busy, both-idle.

6. **Logging improvements (logging, not operations).** Record `Wait for Tool` start/end, `Ready for Batch/Test/Shipment`; relate lot events to Jobs via Component O2O in the standard pipeline; ingest overrides as OCEL events related to Job, Lot, Batch; log tool state changes as events. Benefit: inferred edges become observed. Harmed: none operationally; risk of blame culture around visible overrides. Cost: integration. Perverse metric: "number of waits logged" discourages logging. Evaluation: share of Job–Tool and Job–ComponentLot interactions that are direct; consistency of the interaction summary across pipelines.

7. **Quarantine-aware batching (lot coupling).** Block batch entry for jobs whose lot is quarantined; expose expected release time to the scheduler. Benefit: removes J2-type exposure. Harmed: quarantined jobs wait explicitly longer. Perverse metric: "zero overrides" met by not recording them. Evaluation: count of Jobs whose Batch Cure precedes Release Lot of a related lot.

Spillovers: 1, 2 and 5 move waiting onto OVEN-1 and TEST-1, which may then become real bottlenecks; 4 trades waiting for freight cost; 3 and 7 shift risk toward quality.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization as primary):**
1. Cure validation mandates a minimum load of three, making the B1 wait a quality requirement rather than a scheduling choice.
2. RACK-7 or OVEN-1 was actually unavailable between 08:25 and 12:05, so J1/J2 could not have cured earlier regardless of policy.
3. J1's and J2's "ready for batch" timestamps prove to be data-entry times, not physical readiness.
4. Over more days, early-ready batched jobs show waits comparable to unbatched jobs; the 235/220-min figures are a one-day artifact.
5. J3's late readiness was driven by an independent cause (material shortage) and batches usually start within 30 min of the second member.
6. A fixed carrier cut-off (e.g., one 19:00 truck) would have held J1 anyway, so batch waiting changed no delivery outcome.
7. J4's missing `Final Test` occurred ~15:50, before J5, so the S1 delay arose between test and pack, unrelated to CAL-3 or batching.

**Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**
1. Explicit `Ready for Batch`, `Ready for Test`, `Ready for Shipment` events related to Job and to Batch/Tool/Shipment.
2. `Wait for Tool` start/end events with Tool and Machine as related objects.
3. Tool state-change events (`Start/End Cleaning`, `Quarantine Tool`, `Calibrate`) with reason codes and trigger rule (count- vs time-based).
4. Batch policy attributes: minimum/maximum load, planned start, trigger reason for actual start.
5. Shipment attributes: carrier cut-off, ship-complete flag, planned vs actual member list at `Create Shipment`.
6. Lot events linked to Jobs and Batches in the standard pipeline, plus override events (who, when, justification) as OCEL events.
7. Machine queue snapshots so idle-with-demand can be separated from idle-without-demand.
8. J4's `Final Test` event with its CAL-3 relation, and the identity and fixture requirement of the CNC-2 job processed 10:47–11:40.
