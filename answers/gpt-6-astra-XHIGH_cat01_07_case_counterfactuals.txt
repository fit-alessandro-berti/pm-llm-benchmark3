## 1. Feasibility model

Let `X ≺ Y` mean that X completes no later than Y starts; `∥` means concurrency is permitted, not guaranteed. Index k identifies an evidence version and its assessment. A request includes both its issuance and the subsequent external collection interval.

```text
Register ≺ Triage ≺ every subsequent internal case activity
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check_0

If originals are required: Originals Receipt_0 ≺ Document Check_0
Identity Check ≺ Assessment_k                         for every k
Document Check_k ≺ Assessment_k                       for every k

If Assessment_k issues Request More Info_k:
    Assessment_k ≺ Request More Info_k
If Compliance Review_k issues Request More Info_k:
    Assessment_k ≺ Compliance Review_k ≺ Request More Info_k
For every issued request:
    Request More Info_k [including response collection]
        ≺ Document Check_(k+1) ≺ Assessment_(k+1)
    If new originals are needed:
        Originals Receipt_(k+1) ≺ Document Check_(k+1)

Assessment_latest ≺ Decision ≺ Notify
If high risk OR high value:
    Assessment_latest ≺ Compliance Review_final ≺ Decision

K-104 preparation branch:
    Triage ≺ Escalation Advice_early
    Escalation Advice_early ≺ Preliminary Compliance, when advice is its input
    Preliminary Compliance ≺ Compliance Review_final, when its work is reused
    Preparation may overlap Identity Check and Document Check.

No precedence edge between Identity Check and Document Check is required.
Originals constrain Document Check, not the start of Identity Check.
```

```text
Register is the first process activity; Triage is next.
External arrivals are not internal service and do not occupy a listed worker.

Assessment requires both verification branches to be complete, with findings available.
Adverse verification findings can be assessed; completion does not mean acceptance.
A decision has no outstanding information request or unchecked response.
After any information request, both Document Check and Assessment repeat.
For high-risk/high-value cases, final compliance reviews the latest assessment
and evidence, or explicitly reaffirms its review after that assessment.
An earlier screen never substitutes for this final review.

Resource(final Assessment) != Resource(final Compliance Review).
C1 alone performs certified compliance work; S1 provides advice only.
Assignments respect the stated resource pools and document competence.
For each worker, all actual service intervals [start, complete) are disjoint,
including preparation, final review, decision, and notification intervals.

Task release/readiness does not guarantee immediate service or a decision slot.
Queue snapshots are uncertain forecasts, not resource reservations or calendars.
Provisional reservations occupy or protect real capacity; preemption, release,
idle time, and displacement must be accounted for.
Service ranges guide scenarios; they are not hard bounds or distributions.

K-337 forbids Request More Info_2; it does not waive final compliance,
four-eyes separation, or the need to document the unresolved discrepancy.
K-411 requires genuine concurrent work with safe evidence/version handling
and contemporaneous, auditable service timestamps.
```

K-104 changes scheduling and adds a preparation branch; its decision standard remains unchanged. K-219 changes resource routing, potentially inducing an otherwise unnecessary rework branch. K-411 changes task release and locking, allowing concurrency already permitted by the process. K-337 changes the allowed evidence and decision policy by removing a rework option; it is not merely a timing change.

Cycle time below runs from Register start through Notify completion, in elapsed calendar hours. K-337's final factual Monday–Wednesday are April 13–15. Factual cycle times are 58 h 30 m, 48 h, 214 h 10 m, and 29 h 30 m, respectively. All proposed slots are conditional examples requiring roster and booking confirmation. Overnight document work is observed, but continuous availability of every pool is not assumed. Ranges are scenario envelopes, not confidence intervals or exhaustive bounds; “base” denotes a worked middle scenario, not an estimated most likely outcome.

There is a shared-calendar inconsistency: K-219 assigns D1 on Tuesday April 7, 09:30–13:30, inside K-337's D1 interval of 08:30–14:30. Those factual traces cannot both be literal uninterrupted service by one person. Each single-case analysis therefore uses its own factual baseline and approximately fixed background load, rather than asserting feasibility of the union of the logs. Joint scheduling requires reconciling that conflict; it must not be reproduced as allowable resource overlap.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The ambiguity was available at Triage, so its late discovery did not depend on new document evidence alone. Nevertheless, the eight-hour complex D1 check and the subsequent A1 queue remain prerequisites. Moving S1's two-hour advice earlier does not itself save two hours of cycle time: factual advice already finished well before C1 started Wednesday at 13:10. The causal opportunities are reusable preparation and an earlier feasible final-review slot.

**Minimal intervention:** Keep registration, triage completion, both checks, and A1's Tuesday 15:10–19:10 assessment unchanged. Send the packet at Monday 08:50 and tentatively reserve final-review capacity against that forecast assessment completion. One worked schedule moves S1 to Monday 09:00–11:00 and places preliminary C1 work Tuesday 04:00–05:30, overlapping D1's ongoing check. That early C1 slot is a conditional booking, potentially involving displacement; the queue snapshots do not establish its availability. Keep the final sanctions judgment with C1 after A1 finishes.

**Temporal propagation:** Suppose 1.5 hours of preliminary reading remains useful and reduces factual final-review service from 4.5 to 3 hours. A confirmed Wednesday 10:00–13:00 final slot advances review start by 3 h 10 m. With M1 deciding 13:20–13:50 and notifying 13:50–14:00, cycle time becomes 53 h 50 m, 4 h 40 m shorter. Review completion advances 4 h 40 m, and the available M1 slot preserves that gain while leaving K-411's factual 14:00 M1 start intact. Identity, documents, and assessment finish at their factual times. If earlier C1 completion does not obtain an earlier M1 slot, some or all of the gain disappears.

**Process-feasibility check:** C1's preliminary and final intervals are disjoint, and the final review follows Tuesday's latest assessment. A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes separation; S1 does not certify compliance. Preliminary evidence is explicitly versioned, and changed material is reread. Any new request requires another document check, assessment, and final compliance review. A provisional reservation cannot authorize reviewing an unfinished assessment or processing K-104 simultaneously with another C1 case; displaced work must move elsewhere in the calendar.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: final C1 review Tuesday 20:00–23:00, M1 Wednesday 09:10–09:40, and notification by 09:50 save 8 h 50 m, without overlapping K-219’s factual M1 work; nearby feasible slots motivate the approximate 0–12-hour favorable envelope. Base: the Wednesday 10:00 review schedule saves 4 h 40 m. Worst: urgent arrivals preempt the reservation, the early packet saves no final reading, and duplicated work or a missed signature slot adds roughly 4–24 hours or more. A newly identified evidence gap adds the external-response and repeat-service path, potentially beyond that envelope. Earlier detection may improve defensibility even when elapsed time increases.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 calendars showing no usable pre-assessment capacity, packet-version comparisons showing negligible reusable content, or unchanged M1 availability would undermine the expected time saving. Evidence that reservations delay more urgent, higher-consequence reviews would reverse a favorable system-level judgment even if K-104 finishes earlier. Conversely, repeated proof that early S1 advice prevents later information requests would strengthen the intervention substantially.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Neither earlier routing nor a free document worker can remove the originals prerequisite at Monday 17:30. Identity already finishes at 16:20. The factual document branch therefore contains sixteen hours between receipt and D1 start, followed by four hours of service. D2 can trade some of that wait for longer service; the handwriting creates a separate quality and rework pathway.

**Minimal intervention:** Preserve the prefix through Originals Receipt and route to D2 at 17:30. “As soon as originals arrive” is immediate assignment and eligibility, not a claim that an occupied D2 becomes free. For a clean-check base, assume D2 is available Monday 21:00–Tuesday 03:00, a six-hour service interval compatible with the rough standard-document range. This is an illustrative realization near the Monday afternoon forecast, not a mechanically updated queue estimate. Preserve A2's 2.5-hour assessment and M1's factual service durations.

**Temporal propagation:** Compared with D1 completion Tuesday 13:30, D2 completion at 03:00 advances readiness by 10.5 hours: 12.5 hours earlier service start minus two additional service hours. If an actual A2 slot is available Tuesday 10:00–12:30, followed by M1 at 14:00–14:20 and notification by 14:30, total cycle time is 29.5 hours, saving 18.5 hours. The saving exceeds the readiness gain because the case catches a Tuesday signature opportunity instead of waiting until Wednesday. If A2 and M1 retain their factual bookings, earlier verification produces no completion gain.

**Process-feasibility check:** D2 starts after originals arrive; assessment starts after both checks; no compliance review is mandated by the unchanged low-risk/low-value attributes. D2 may flag illegible handwriting, but the formal Request More Info is issued by Assessment, consistent with the allowed process. A response never goes directly to Decision: D2, or another qualified available document worker, rechecks it before a new assessment. If clarification requires replacement originals, their receipt adds another prerequisite rather than being assumed instantaneous.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: an earlier D2 completion and next-morning assessment/signature can save around 18–24 hours. Base: the clean Tuesday 14:30 completion saves 18.5 hours; downstream congestion can reduce this to zero. For a concrete adverse branch, A2 requests clarification Tuesday 12:30, the external party responds Wednesday 12:30, D2 rechecks Wednesday 16:30–22:30, and A2 reassesses Thursday 08:00–10:30. M1 decides Thursday 12:00–12:20 and notifies by 12:30. This 75.5-hour cycle is 27.5 hours longer than factual. Slower responses or additional queues motivate the wider 0–48+ hours of deterioration; an unnoticed misreading can create a fast but worse decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Actual D2 availability after receipt, service times stratified by handwriting difficulty, and independent error audits are decisive. If D2's start advantage is smaller than its additional service and rework burden, routing loses its benefit. A2/M1 calendars with no earlier slots also eliminate the clean-path gain. Conversely, similar clarification and error rates for D1 and D2 would make the substantial D1 wait avoidance more credible.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Keep the first response, second document check, and second assessment factual. At Friday April 10, 10:30, the remaining discrepancy triggers an external interval lasting 70.5 hours through Monday April 13, 09:00. This exceeds the rough 8–36-hour guidance because the recorded case crosses a weekend. D1 then spends 3.5 hours on a third check and A1 2.5 hours on a third assessment; final compliance follows on Tuesday April 14. The delay reflects both evidence acquisition and service calendars.

**Minimal intervention:** At Assessment 2 completion, replace the second request with an explicit recommendation based on the discrepancy and available evidence. In the base scenario, A1 recommends rejection because the unresolved supplier totals prevent adequate assurance. Preserve the completed assessment's timing, but change its recorded disposition and rationale. C1 independently evaluates that recommendation and the evidence limits before M1 decides. Approve-with-conditions is an alternative only where enforceable restrictions actually contain the unresolved risk; a promise to obtain the same missing confirmation after approval would merely hide the prohibited loop.

**Temporal propagation:** Assume C1 can review Friday April 10, 15:30–19:30. If M1's next slot is Monday April 13, 08:30–09:00, notification finishes 09:10. Cycle time is 166 h 10 m, saving 48 hours. The removed external wait and six service hours are not simply subtracted: compliance still consumes four hours, and M1's weekend unavailability absorbs much of the earlier readiness. If the original April 14 C1 slot is the earliest real opportunity, deleting the loop can leave final notification unchanged at April 15, 09:10.

**Process-feasibility check:** Assessment 2 becomes the latest assessment supporting the decision; its recorded unresolved issue is preserved. Final compliance occurs after it, with A1 and C1 distinct. No second request, third document check, or third assessment is invented, and there is no outstanding request at closure. Rejection can be justified by insufficient assurance without pretending that the evidence supports factual approval. If applicable decision authority permits neither rejection on this record nor defensible conditions, this intervention is infeasible; mandatory compliance cannot be waived to manufacture completion.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: C1 Friday 11:00–15:00, M1 15:30–16:00, and notification by 16:10 produce a 101 h 10 m cycle, saving 113 hours. Base: Friday review and Monday signature save 48 hours, with rejection replacing approval. Worst: unchanged or later C1/M1 access removes the time benefit, while uncertainty increases deliberation and could even delay closure. The more consequential worst outcome is a fast, indefensible conditional approval with loss exposure, or an unnecessary rejection followed by appeal and reopening. Such downstream consequences are not bounded by the measured Register-to-Notify saving.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The discrepancy's materiality, permitted grounds for rejection, enforceability of conditions, and C1's Friday capacity matter more than the nominal deleted duration. Evidence that the missing confirmation was decisive to safe approval would rule out assuming unchanged quality. Evidence that it was redundant and policy permits a documented judgment could support conditional approval. Appeal/reopening outcomes could reverse an apparent operational gain even where the event-log cycle time falls sharply.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The document task cannot be owned while identity holds the case lock. Yet independent digital documents need no identity-first ordering under the normative process. The factual readiness time is Tuesday 19:30, the later check completion, followed by fourteen hours until Wednesday assessment. Removing the artificial release dependency creates an opportunity to start D2 earlier; it does not add D2 or A2 capacity.

**Minimal intervention:** At Tuesday 09:35, create both tasks with controlled independent ownership and versioned evidence updates. Keep I2 at 11:30–13:20 and both factual service durations. Suppose D2 has a verified Tuesday 12:30–17:00 slot. The workers then genuinely overlap for fifty minutes, from 12:30 to 13:20. Record task creation, queue entry, ownership, actual service start/completion, evidence commit, and event-ingestion time separately so the workaround does not merely backdate the document activity.

**Temporal propagation:** Readiness advances 2.5 hours, from 19:30 to 17:00. In the base scenario, A2 still has no available slot before Wednesday 09:30–12:00 and M1 remains at 14:00–14:20 with notification by 14:30. Cycle time therefore remains 29.5 hours, while the assessment queue wait grows from fourteen to 16.5 hours. In a favorable alternative, A2 can work Tuesday 17:30–20:00, finishing before K-219’s factual A2 service begins; M1 Wednesday 09:10–09:30 and notification by 09:40 give a 24 h 40 m cycle, saving 4 h 50 m. This leaves K-219's factual Wednesday 08:30–09:00 M1 work intact. Earlier readiness benefits completion only through available downstream opportunities.

**Process-feasibility check:** Identity and document verification concern independent evidence, both finish before assessment, and A2 sees their committed results. I2 and D2 are different people; neither may overlap another assigned service interval. The digital/no-originals attributes remove the originals gate, and low risk/value requires no final compliance review. Safe task ownership must prevent lost writes or inconsistent evidence versions. If either check finds a dependency requiring identity resolution, that particular document work must wait; the workaround is not authority to ignore substantive dependencies.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best within the illustrated morning-signature pattern saves 4 h 50 m; additional actual evening signature capacity could yield more. Base saves zero while improving verification readiness. Worst: no earlier D2 capacity makes the operational change ineffective, or unsafe concurrency introduces correction work and misses later assessment/signature slots, adding roughly 6–24 hours or more. A formal information request arising from such errors still requires the repeat document-check and assessment path. Faster recorded timestamps without earlier actual work yield no real benefit at all.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Audited ownership and application events showing no actual simultaneous work would invalidate a concurrency claim. D2 calendars without earlier capacity, identity-dependent document requirements, or persistent A2 congestion would weaken the speed case. Conflicting evidence versions or higher defect rates would reverse the favorable feasibility/quality judgment. Repeated access to same-day A2 slots would strengthen the completion benefit beyond the zero-gain base scenario.

Treat each rollout as 100 eligible arrivals in a week, not 100 promised completions. Exact weekly throughput cannot be inferred without rosters, background demand, arrival patterns, and discipline. At the rough 4–8-hour standard-document service levels, 100 D2 checks require 400–800 hours, exceeding even one person's 168 calendar hours. The stated pools therefore cannot absorb that sustained stream on D2 within a week without substantial backlog or changed capacity.

For K-104, the worked preparation uses 1.5 C1 hours and saves 1.5 later: useful timing redistribution, but zero net certified-service saving. Across 100 comparable cases, 150 hours move forward, while total C1 demand remains 450 hours—already beyond one reviewer’s weekly calendar capacity before background work. Incomplete packets, abandoned reservations, and urgent preemption can turn that into extra demand. Reserving capacity cannot increase C1's supply; unreserved urgent cases bear the displacement. A released reservation helps only if another ready case can use it in time.

For K-219, copying the clean worked service durations would free 400 D1 hours while adding 600 D2 hours before rework. These are workload illustrations, not achievable weekly schedules. Corporate cases benefit on D1, but standard D2 customers lose capacity. Clarification adds another check and assessment, potentially making A2 the next constraint. If K-411 is also deployed, its earlier document releases compete in that same D2 queue.

K-337 avoids repeated D1/A1 work, but brings mandatory C1 demand forward and may create appeals later. Weekend timing makes elapsed savings discontinuous: avoiding Friday's supplier request is valuable, yet Friday-evening compliance may still wait until Monday for M1. External-response duration is neither occupied worker time nor automatically transferable capacity.

K-411 changes release timing, not intended service volume. Assessment queues can absorb all verification gains; increased simultaneous workload may also raise service times. Earlier task creation can lengthen recorded queues even when hidden waiting falls. Validate actual overlapping service and evidence commits, separating event time from batch-entry time. Evaluate all cases' waiting, quality, and unfinished work beyond the rollout week; multiplied target-case savings would conceal interference and bottleneck migration.

The ranking prioritizes valid processing and decision quality, then spillovers, achievable completion gains, and what a reversible pilot can establish. It is conditional on the stated competence and logging assumptions, not a claim of a universally optimal policy.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust because its intervention removes an artificial dependency while preserving verification, evidence standards, and the decision route. Its strongest claim is earlier readiness, not guaranteed earlier notification. The worked base gains nothing at completion because A2 remains the constraint. A credible pilot must demonstrate real simultaneous work and intact evidence before treating timestamp improvements as productivity.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential to save several days. Prohibiting another information request changes what evidence the organization accepts for closure. A defensible rejection can satisfy the process, but it differs materially from factual approval; conditional approval can retain substantial exposure. Final independent compliance remains mandatory. Appeals and reopened cases belong in the evaluation, or faster notification can conceal displaced work and harm.

K-104's favorable local counterfactual is especially difficult to transport into a weekly reservation policy. A single case can obtain earlier access by rearranging C1's calendar; one hundred cases cannot each gain priority over one another. Incomplete preparation and idle reservations can worsen the certified-review bottleneck. K-219 also scales poorly once D2's spare capacity disappears, particularly if K-411 releases competing work earlier.

The most valuable additional data are actual staffed calendars and bookings, priority/preemption records, task-ready timestamps, service intervals, evidence versions, external-request and response events, and decision-slot availability. D2 handwriting error and clarification rates distinguish useful substitution from avoidable rework. For K-337, discrepancy materiality, permitted decision grounds, and subsequent appeals matter as much as timing. First reconcile the overlapping factual D1 assignments. Then assess target and non-target waits, unfinished work, and decision quality together; none of the four single-case schedules establishes a system-wide causal effect.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** The stated process and role restrictions govern every counterfactual; high-risk/high-value final compliance and four-eyes separation remain mandatory under K-337's changed decision policy.
2. **temporal:** Dates are local calendar time, K-337's final week is April 13–15, and cycle time is Register start to Notify completion. Unlisted shifts, bookings, and queue evolution are unknown; every new slot is conditional.
3. **resource:** Each resource identifier represents one worker with disjoint service intervals. The conflicting factual D1 assignments are unresolved data limitations, so the four individual traces are not treated as one verified pooled schedule.
4. **structural:** No unreported dependency prevents the proposed independent checks. Existing originals remain available, and new originals are required before a repeat check only when the requested replacement evidence requires them.
5. **behavioral:** K-104's initial packet can support some useful advice/preparation, but reuse and slot retention are uncertain. Early S1 work alone does not guarantee less C1 work or earlier completion.
6. **quality:** D2 is qualified for K-219's document type, but equivalence to D1 on difficult handwriting is unproven. Clarification and independent error detection are required to assess the routing tradeoff.
7. **quality:** K-337 can reach a documented, authorized rejection or defensible conditional approval on existing evidence. If neither is permitted, the intervention is infeasible; unchanged factual approval quality is never assumed.
8. **interference:** Single-case examples hold unaffected arrivals, external responses, and background load approximately fixed while accounting for displaced slots. At rollout, queues, priorities, workload, rework, and later appeals respond endogenously; target-case savings are not additive.
