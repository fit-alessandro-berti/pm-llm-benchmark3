# 1. Objectives and constraints

Resource assignment in service operations is a multi-objective socio-technical problem. Optimizing purely for immediate throughput degrades long-term organizational resilience by starving trainees of learning opportunities and burning out specialists. Conversely, over-indexing on training or strict continuity can breach immediate Service Level Agreements (SLAs) or trap complex cases with under-qualified owners.

### Optimization Objectives
1. **SLA Compliance & Urgency Management:** Minimize deadline breaches on urgent and high-priority cases, accounting for both service time and handover-induced waiting delays (+2.7h median delay on complex handovers).
2. **Quality & Rework Minimization:** Match case complexity to resource competence to prevent rework loops (which increase 1.8x after >2 reassignments) and avoid the +3.3h service time penalty of keeping weak-skilled owners on complex tasks.
3. **Continuity of Ownership:** Preserve case context by keeping cases with their `current_owner` when handover costs are high and competence is sufficient.
4. **Workload Fairness & Burnout Prevention:** Balance open case counts and available hours across the team while protecting staff flagged with high overtime risk.
5. **Organizational Resilience & Skill Development:** Systematically allocate normal/low-priority cases to staff with documented training priorities to eliminate single-point-of-failure bottlenecks in **Security** and **Approval**.

### Hard Constraints (Safety, Compliance, and Capacity)
* **HC1 (Skill Floor):** Resources with skill level `0` cannot be assigned the activity unilaterally. (Exception: explicit co-assigned training pairs where the supervisor holds full sign-off authority and mentors the trainee).
* **HC2 (Batch Capacity Cap):** No resource may receive more than **4 new assignments** (including supervision duties) in the current decision batch.
* **HC3 (High-Priority Competence):** `urgent` and `high` priority cases require skill level $\ge 2$, unless paired with an explicit level `3` supervisor.
* **HC4 (Security Compliance):** **Security Review** requires skill level `3` (`R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`), or level `1–2` (or designated security trainees) working under explicit supervision by `R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`.
* **HC5 (Approval Compliance):** **Final Approval** requires skill level `2–3` to finalize. Skill level `1` may prepare the case only under co-assignment with a level `2–3` approver.
* **HC6 (Batching Eligibility):** **Quality Check** may be batched only if `priority` $\in \{\text{normal, low}\}$ and `sla_deadline` $> 24\text{h}$.

### Soft Constraints (Preference Trade-offs)
* **SC1 (Overtime Protection):** Resources with `overtime_risk == "high"` (`R2_Bruno`, `R4_David`, `R9_Iris`) receive a heavy scoring penalty and should not receive new work unless an `urgent` SLA is at risk and no safer capacity exists.
* **SC2 (Training Quota):** At least **20%** of `normal`/`low` priority cases (minimum 2 of the 9 cases in the current snapshot) must advance documented `training_priority` goals, provided `sla_deadline` $> 16\text{h}$.
* **SC3 (Continuity vs. Competence Trade-off):** When `handover_cost_band == "high"` and `case_complexity` $\ge 0.70$, retaining the `current_owner` is preferred if their skill is $\ge 2$. If their skill is `0` or `1`, the +3.3h weak-skill penalty and rework risk outweigh the +2.7h handover penalty, mandating reassignment or supervised pairing.
* **SC4 (Bottleneck Preservation):** When specialist capacity is scarce (`Security`, `Approval`), penalize assigning level `3` specialists to routine (`complexity` $< 0.50$) tasks in non-bottleneck skills if level `2` resources are available.

# 2. Assignment policy

The **Explainable Dynamic Resource Assignment (EDRA)** policy processes the queue in two phases: **Case Prioritization** (sorting the queue by urgency and handover risk) and **Candidate Scoring** (evaluating eligible single resources and supervised pairs).

### Algorithm & Pseudocode

```python
def assign_queue_edra(cases, resources):
    # PHASE 1: Sort Queue by Urgency Score U(c)
    # Urgent/short-SLA cases and high-reassignment cases get first pick of capacity
    for c in cases:
        priority_weight = {"urgent": 100, "high": 60, "normal": 30, "low": 10}[c.priority]
        sla_pressure = max(0, (48 - c.sla_hours) / 48.0) * 40
        loop_risk = min(c.reassignment_count * 10, 30)
        c.urgency_score = priority_weight + sla_pressure + (20 * c.complexity) + loop_risk
        
    sorted_cases = sort_descending(cases, key=lambda x: x.urgency_score)
    batch_counts = {r.id: 0 for r in resources}
    normal_low_count = count(c for c in cases if c.priority in ["normal", "low"])
    training_assigned = 0
    assignments = {}

    # PHASE 2: Evaluate Candidates for Each Case
    for c in sorted_cases:
        candidates = generate_valid_candidates(c, resources, batch_counts)
        best_score = - infinity
        best_candidate = None
        
        for cand in candidates: # cand can be Single(r) or Pair(trainee, supervisor)
            # 1. Skill-Match Score (S_skill): range [-20, +35]
            if cand.is_pair:
                s_skill = 25 # Supervised competence baseline
            else:
                if cand.skill == 3:
                    s_skill = 35 if c.complexity >= 0.60 else 22 # Save specialists on easy work
                elif cand.skill == 2:
                    s_skill = 30 if c.complexity < 0.75 else 18
                elif cand.skill == 1:
                    s_skill = 15 if c.complexity < 0.45 else -20

            # 2. Continuity & Handover Score (S_cont): range [-15, +35]
            if c.current_owner in cand.members:
                if cand.effective_skill >= 2:
                    s_cont = {"high": 35, "medium": 20, "low": 10, "none": 0}[c.handover_cost]
                    if c.reassignment_count >= 2: s_cont += 15 # Anti-loop bonus
                else:
                    s_cont = -15 # Historical evidence: weak owner adds +3.3h service time & rework
            else:
                s_cont = {"high": -20, "medium": -10, "low": -3, "none": 0}[c.handover_cost]

            # 3. Workload & Overtime Fairness Score (S_fair): range [-45, +25]
            r_primary = cand.primary_worker
            load_factor = (15 - r_primary.open_cases) * 1.2 + (r_primary.hours_rem * 1.5)
            ot_penalty = {"low": 0, "medium": -10, "high": -45}[r_primary.overtime_risk]
            batch_penalty = batch_counts[r_primary.id] * (-8)
            s_fair = load_factor + ot_penalty + batch_penalty

            # 4. Training & Development Score (S_train): range [-15, +30]
            if matches_training_priority(cand, c):
                if c.priority in ["normal", "low"] and c.sla_hours >= 16:
                    quota_boost = 10 if (training_assigned / normal_low_count) < 0.20 else 0
                    s_train = 25 + quota_boost
                else:
                    s_train = -15 # Too risky for training
            else:
                s_train = 0

            # 5. Resilience & Bottleneck Avoidance (S_res): range [-15, +12]
            s_res = 0
            if cand.has_specialist_in(["Security", "Approval"]) and c.required_skill not in ["Security", "Approval"]:
                s_res -= 15 # Protect scarce Security/Approval specialists from other tasks
            if is_eligible_for_batching(c, cand): # Quality Check, normal/low, SLA > 24h
                s_res += 12 # Efficiency bonus for batching Quality Checks

            total_score = s_skill + s_cont + s_fair + s_train + s_res
            if total_score > best_score:
                best_score = total_score
                best_candidate = cand

        # Record assignment and update dynamic state
        assignments[c.id] = best_candidate
        for r in best_candidate.members:
            batch_counts[r.id] += (0.5 if best_candidate.is_pair and r == best_candidate.supervisor else 1)
            r.open_cases += 1
        if best_candidate.supports_training:
            training_assigned += 1

    return assignments
```

### Tie-Breaking and Exception Handling
* **Tie-Breaking Rule:** If two candidates score within $1.0$ point of each other: (1) prefer the candidate with lower `overtime_risk`, (2) prefer the candidate with more `hours_remaining_today`, and (3) prefer the candidate who maintains `current_owner` or `team` continuity.
* **Exception Handling (Fallback Mode):** If all valid candidates for an `urgent` case have `overtime_risk == "high"` or have hit the batch cap, the policy triggers a **Manager Escalation Flag**: it allows assigning to a `medium` overtime-risk specialist up to a cap of 5 cases, or pairs a low-load level `2` worker with a brief supervisor sign-off, logging `assignment_reason = "SLA_EXCEPTION_FALLBACK"`.

# 5. Explanation to workers

> ### How Our Team's Smart Work-Routing Policy Works
> 
> **Why we updated our assignment process**  
> We want our work distribution to be fair, transparent, and sustainable. Previously, assigning tasks simply to "whoever is free first" caused too many frustrating handovers and dropped complex cases on people without support. On the other hand, sending work only to top specialists burned out our most experienced colleagues and prevented the rest of the team from learning new skills.
> 
> **How the system makes decisions**  
> Every time a batch of cases is assigned, the system balances five practical commitments to you:
> 1. **Protecting Your Well-Being & Hours (`OVERTIME_RELIEF`):** If you are carrying a heavy backlog or approaching overtime risk, the system actively shields you from new assignments and routes your incoming queue to colleagues with more breathing room. No one can receive more than 4 cases in a batch.
> 2. **Respecting Case Ownership (`CONTINUITY`):** We know how frustrating it is to lose a case you have already invested time in—or to inherit a complex case cold. Whenever you have the right skill level and capacity, the system keeps your current cases with you.
> 3. **Guaranteed Support on Growth Tasks (`TRAINING_SUPERVISED`):** You will never be left alone on a high-stakes case outside your comfort zone. However, when a normal-priority case has plenty of time on the clock, we intentionally route it toward your personal **Training Priority**—and we explicitly pair you with a specialist mentor so you have dedicated time to learn.
> 4. **Breaking Frustrating Loops (`ANTI_LOOP`):** When a case has bounced around multiple times, the system flags it for a specialist reset so one person can resolve it cleanly from start to finish.
> 5. **Smart Batching (`BATCH_EFFICIENCY`):** For routine Quality Checks with long deadlines, the system groups them together for a single reviewer so you don't have to constantly switch context.
> 
> **Your Voice & Override Rights**  
> Every assignment comes with a clear **Reason Tag** (like *Continuity*, *Overtime Relief*, or *Supervised Training*) so you always know *why* a case landed on your desk. These indicators are used to audit the **system's fairness**, never to penalize individual workers. If a case is more complex than the data suggests, you can request a peer pairing or reassignment at any time with one click.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **3** | **Unsupervised Skill Mismatch Rate** | $\frac{|\{e \in E \mid \text{skill}(\text{org:resource}, \text{required\_skill}) < 2 \land \neg \text{is\_supervised}(e)\}|}{|E|}$ | $0\%$ on `high`/`urgent` cases; $< 2\%$ overall | Audits hard compliance constraints (HC2, HC4, HC5) and prevents the +3.3h weak-skill service time penalty. |

| **7** | **Post-Assignment Rework & Appeal Rate** | Share of cases where `concept:name == "Rework Correction"` occurs after an activity, grouped by `assignment_reason` and `handover_cost_band`. | $< 8\%$ overall; no statistically significant spike on `TRAINING` cases | Tests whether supervised training or overtime-relief handovers inadvertently introduce defects. |
| **8** | **Training Assignment Share** | $\frac{|\{e \in E_{\text{normal/low}} \mid \text{assignment\_reason} \in \{\text{'TRAINING\_CONTINUITY'}, \text{'TRAINING\_SUPERVISED'}\}\}|}{|E_{\text{normal/low}}|}$ | $\ge 20\%$ of `normal`/`low` assignments | Audits Soft Constraint SC2 to guarantee long-term skill building is not sacrificed for short-term speed. |
| **9** | **Specialist Bottleneck Ratio** | Ratio of mean waiting time (`@@approx_bh_overall_wtime`) to service time on `Security Review` and `Final Approval` in the Performance DFG. | Wait-to-Service Ratio $< 1.5$ | Signals when specialist queues are forming and more cross-training pairs must be triggered. |
| **10** | **Policy Deviation / Override Rate** | $\frac{|\{e \in E \mid \text{assignment\_reason} \in \{\text{'MANUAL\_OVERRIDE'}, \text{'SLA\_EXCEPTION\_FALLBACK'}\}\}|}{|E|}$ | $< 10\%$ (if $>10\%$, recalibrate scoring weights) | High override rates indicate that the scoring model is missing real-world nuances known to workers. |

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The Python script below demonstrates how to ingest the process event log, enrich it with service and waiting times, discover organizational networks and roles, and compute the process-mining audit indicators using **PM4Py**.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

def audit_and_learn_assignment_policy(csv_path: str, skill_matrix_df: pd.DataFrame):
    # 1. Load and format Event Log for PM4Py
    raw_df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw_df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
    )
    # Ensure org:resource is set as standard resource column
    log_df["org:resource"] = log_df["org:resource"].astype(str)

    # 2. Enrich log with case service and waiting times
    # Adds columns like '@@service_time' and '@@approx_bh_overall_wtime'
    enriched_df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log_df)
    activity_service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(log_df, aggregation_measure="median")
    print("Median Service Time per Activity (sec):", activity_service_times)

    # 3. Discover Organizational Networks & Resource Similarity
    # 3a. Handover of Work Network (audits Indicator 1: Handover frequency & paths)
    hw_metric = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log_df)
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(hw_metric, "audit_handover_network.png")

    # 3b. Working Together Network (audits Indicator 8: Supervised training pairs)
    wt_metric = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log_df)
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(wt_metric, "audit_supervision_network.png")

    # 3c. Organizational Roles & Activity-Based Resource Similarity
    # Identifies natural backup candidates when specialists are overloaded
    org_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log_df)
    res_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log_df)

    # 3d. Batching Discovery (audits Rule 8: Quality Check batching compliance)
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log_df)
    print(f"Discovered {len(batches)} activity batches across resources.")

    # 4. Performance DFG & Bottleneck Analysis (Indicator 9: Specialist Bottlenecks)
    perf_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_df)
    for (act_from, act_to), metrics in perf_dfg.items():
        if act_to in ["Security Review", "Final Approval"]:
            print(f"Transition {act_from} -> {act_to} | Mean Wait (s): {metrics.get('mean', 0):.1f}")

    # 5. Filtering by Case Attributes & Variants to Audit Rework & SLA Risk
    # 5a. Filter high-priority cases and inspect SLA performance
    high_prio_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log_df, attribute_key="case_priority", values=["urgent", "high"], level="case"
    )
    # Identify slow cases taking longer than 8 hours (28,800 seconds)
    slow_high_prio_df = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
        high_prio_df, min_performance=28800, max_performance=float("inf")
    )

    # 5b. Variant Analysis to detect repeated Diagnosis / Rework loops (Indicator 7)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log_df)
    rework_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log_df, attribute_key="concept:name", values=["Rework Correction"], level="case"
    )
    rework_rate = rework_cases["case:concept:name"].nunique() / log_df["case:concept:name"].nunique()

    # 6. Compute Policy Audit Indicators Table
    merged = enriched_df.merge(
        skill_matrix_df, left_on=["org:resource", "required_skill"], right_on=["resource", "skill_name"], how="left"
    )
    
    # Indicator 2: High-loop reassignment rate (>2 reassignments)
    case_max_reassign = log_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["reassignment_count"].max()
    high_loop_share = (case_max_reassign > 2).mean()

    # Indicator 3: Unsupervised skill mismatch on high/urgent priority
    unsupervised_mismatch = merged[
        (merged["case_priority"].isin(["urgent", "high"])) &
        (merged["skill_level"] < 2) &
        (~merged["assignment_reason"].str.contains("SUPERVISED", na=False))
    ]

    # Indicator 4: Workload fairness (Coefficient of Variation of workload_snapshot)
    resource_loads = log_df.groupby("org:resource")["workload_snapshot"].mean()
    workload_cv = resource_loads.std() / resource_loads.mean()

    # Indicator 8: Training assignment share on normal/low priority cases
    norm_low_events = log_df[log_df["case_priority"].isin(["normal", "low"])]
    training_share = norm_low_events["assignment_reason"].str.contains("TRAINING", na=False).mean()

    audit_summary = {
        "total_cases_audited": log_df["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "rework_case_rate": round(rework_rate, 4),
        "high_reassignment_loop_rate": round(high_loop_share, 4),
        "unsupervised_high_prio_mismatch_count": len(unsupervised_mismatch),
        "workload_fairness_cv": round(workload_cv, 4),
        "normal_low_training_share": round(training_share, 4),
        "slow_high_priority_cases": slow_high_prio_df["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    }
    return audit_summary, res_similarity, org_roles
```
