# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed pattern—service time falling steadily from 18.8 min to 10.8 min across a shift while error rates trace a J-curve (3.4%  2.5%  7.9%) and 7-day rework climbs to 13.7%—cannot be reduced to fatigue, learning, or any single mechanism. Instead, it is the product of **three distinct operational regimes across the shift**, shaped by an evolving interaction between case-mix selection, queue-induced speed–accuracy tradeoffs, workarounds, resource experience, and shift-level demand structure.

### Phase 1: Early-shift complexity carryover and selective allocation (Positions 1–15)
At the start of a shift, workers confront inherited backlog from the prior shift—especially on Early (start queue 61; 48% high complexity in hour 1), Late (start queue 70), and Night (56% high complexity, urgent exceptions). Across all human staff, positions 1–5 have an average complexity of 0.72 and 41% high-priority share. Because high complexity adds roughly 7–10 minutes to service time and doubles baseline error risk (e.g., 22.4 min and 4.2% error vs. 12.7 min and 2.1% error under low WIP), early-shift service times are naturally long (18.8 min). Senior workers selectively absorb complex carryover cases early, keeping Senior error rates low (2.1%), whereas Juniors exposed to early complex cases show elevated initial errors (5.1%) and long service times (19.6 min), reflecting a mix of complexity mismatch and genuine early-shift warm-up friction.

### Phase 2: Mid-shift case-mix dilution masquerading as learning (Positions 16–50)
As the carryover backlog clears, mean complexity falls from 0.72 to 0.50, high-priority share drops from 41% to 25%, and queues stabilize at 31–33. Service time declines to 12.9–13.6 min and errors reach their lowest level (2.5–2.7%). Viewed naively, this looks like a classic within-shift learning curve. However, the experience-stratified data expose the illusion: for Seniors, Mid-level, and Night staff, later-shift low-WIP error rates (2.2%, 2.9%, 3.7%) are virtually identical to their first 20 events (2.1%, 2.8%, 3.6%). Only Juniors show a meaningful drop in low-WIP error rate (5.1%  4.4%), indicating that genuine warm-up plays at most a modest, junior-specific role while **case-mix dilution** drives most of the early-to-mid shift speed-up.

### Phase 3: Late-shift queue pressure, batching, and conditional quality collapse (Positions 51–76+)
The central puzzle—why service time *continues to fall* (11.4  10.8 min) while errors *triple* (2.5%  7.9%) even though case complexity is at its *lowest* (0.43)—is driven primarily by **queue pressure interacting with experience, workarounds, and end-of-shift cognitive strain**:

1. **Speed–accuracy tradeoff under high WIP:** Between positions 31–50 and 76+, average queue length nearly triples (31  88). Within *both* complexity bands, crossing into high WIP (`>70`) cuts service time by ~25% (12.7  9.4 min for low complexity; 22.4  16.8 min for high complexity) while nearly tripling errors (2.1%  5.8% and 4.2%  10.9%). Late-shift speed is therefore **queue-driven corner-cutting, not learning**.
2. **Why errors rise only in some conditions:** Pure time-on-task fatigue cannot explain the data because **late-shift error rates remain flat when WIP stays low** (e.g., Seniors 2.2%, Mids 2.9%, Juniors 4.4%). Likewise, Early shift ends with a manageable queue (42) and keeps last-two-hour errors at 4.2% (12.7 min ST), whereas Day shift ends in a severe demand surge (queue 75) and suffers 7.5% errors alongside rushed 10.9 min service times. Fatigue likely acts as a **force multiplier** rather than an independent cause: cognitively depleted workers—especially Juniors (12.4% high-WIP errors) and Mid-level staff (6.8%)—lose their capacity to preserve quality once WIP exceeds 70, whereas Seniors maintain stronger protective habits (4.9%).
3. **Batching and measurement distortion:** During late Day shift, `Junior_H` and `Junior_I` respond to mounting queues by sequentially batching 9–18 consecutive `Document Review` events at very short service times. This inflates volume in the 51–75 and 76+ position bins, mechanically pulls down late-shift mean service time, and drives downstream errors and rework. Simultaneously, deferring `Quality Check` until the final 45 minutes concentrates error *detection* and correction bursts at shift end, making late-shift error metrics appear even sharper and rushing final-hour verification.
4. **Automation feedback loop:** Under `queue_length_at_start > 70`, humans override `Bot_Risk` classifications more frequently. Rushed upstream triage and batched `Document Review` under high WIP can pass degraded or incomplete inputs to `Bot_Risk`, triggering downstream misclassifications that force human corrections and rework loops—compounding congestion during peak demand.
5. **Night-shift circadian and case-mix divergence:** Night shift breaks the "fast and error-prone" late-shift pattern: its last two hours are slower (15.2 min) with 5.1% errors despite lower queue volume (55). Here, **circadian slowing** and a heavy concentration of **urgent, high-complexity exceptions** (56%)—rather than high-volume batching—shape performance.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To separate these mechanisms rigorously, combine **process-mining discovery/filtering** (to capture control-flow paths, waiting times, batching, and rework loops) with **panel econometric / mixed-effects modeling** (to isolate within-worker causal effects).

### Analytical design

1. **Within-resource comparisons:** Include worker fixed effects (`C(org:resource)`) so all estimates compare the *same person* across different shift positions, WIP levels, and complexities. This eliminates confounding from Seniors taking hard cases early or `Night_L`/`Night_M` working exclusively on Night shift.
2. **Within-shift position effects (separating sequence from elapsed time):** Model `sequence_position_within_shift` (activity-specific repetition/warm-up) separately from `minutes_since_shift_start` (elapsed duration/fatigue), using splines or piecewise bins, while excluding automated `Bot_Risk` events from human sequence counts.
3. **WIP/queue stratification and non-linearity:** Stratify analyses into `Low (<30)`, `Medium (30–70)`, and `High (>70)` WIP bands and estimate piecewise slopes (`wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`) and the interaction `minutes_since_shift_start × wip_band`. Test whether late-shift error surges vanish in the `Low WIP` stratum. Use exogenous case arrival rates (`pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`) to separate external demand surges from worker-created backlogs.
4. **Complexity and activity adjustment:** Control for `case_complexity_score` (continuous + quadratic), `priority`, `concept:name`, and `activity_family` in every service-time and error model. Run Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition between Positions 1–15 and 16–50 to quantify how much of the early speed-up is purely case-mix change vs. genuine warm-up.
5. **Shift and time-of-day controls:** Include `shift_id` and wall-clock hour (`hour_of_day`) fixed effects. Specifically analyze **shift-overlap windows** (09:00–14:00 Early vs. Day; 14:00–17:00 Day vs. Late) to isolate circadian effects from time-on-shift.
6. **Batching detection and isolation:** Run `pm4py.discover_batches` and generate dotted charts (`pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart`) per activity and resource to tag every event with `is_batched` and `batch_size`. Re-estimate late-shift service time and error curves with and without batched `Document Review` and final-45-minute `Quality Check` bursts.
7. **Resource-experience interactions:** Model interactions `experience_tier × wip_band`, `experience_tier × case_complexity_score`, and `experience_tier × early_shift_indicator` (using `resource_experience_months` continuously and by tier) to quantify differential vulnerability to early complexity vs. late WIP pressure.
8. **Rework, error-detection timing, and automation downstream analysis:** Link `error_flag`, `rework_flag`, and `Rework Correction` activities back to the *originating* event rather than the *detecting* `Quality Check` event. Use performance DFGs and path-performance filtering to trace `Bot_Risk` overrides and test whether upstream high-WIP/batched `Document Review` predicts downstream `Bot_Risk` corrections and 7-day rework.

# 6. Within-resource statistical models separating competing mechanisms
human_log["log_st"] = np.log(human_log["event_service_min"].clip(lower=0.1))

# 4. Intervention options

### 1. Strictly cap shift length for all workers
* **Expected benefit:** Modest reduction in late-shift cognitive fatigue, particularly when fatigue compounds high-WIP stress in hours 7–8.
* **Risks:** Does not address the root driver (WIP > 70). Cutting shift length without adding overlapping headcount reduces active capacity during Day/Late demand peaks, *increasing* queue length, accelerating speed–accuracy collapse, and creating extra shift handovers and carryover backlog.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Track `wip_at_start` distribution during transition hours, shift-end queue length, handover frequency (`org:resource` transitions within a case), and risk-adjusted error rates stratified by `minutes_since_shift_start` and WIP band.

### 2. Move complex cases away from the first hour of each shift
* **Expected benefit:** Reduces early-shift friction for Juniors (who currently show 5.1% errors in the first 20 events) and smooths early-shift service-time spikes.
* **Risks:** High risk if applied indiscriminately. Delaying complex carryover cases violates SLAs on urgent/high-priority claims and **pushes complex cases into late-shift high-WIP windows**, where high-complexity error rates surge from 4.2% (low WIP) to **10.9%** (high WIP). Seniors already handle early complex cases at 2.1% errors. A far safer modification is routing early complex cases away from *Juniors* toward Seniors rather than delaying them in time.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Monitor share of high-complexity cases processed under `wip_at_start > 70`, waiting time for high-priority cases (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`), and complexity-stratified error rates by shift hour and experience tier.

### 3. Add a queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70
* **Expected benefit:** Directly targets the empirical risk inflection point where service time compresses and errors jump sharply across all worker groups and complexity bands. When linked to concrete operational responses (e.g., activating Senior relief, pausing non-urgent batching, or routing complex cases away from Juniors), it can prevent prolonged high-WIP error zones.
* **Risks:** Alert fatigue or perverse speed-up if workers interpret the alert as a mandate to work *even faster* to bring WIP back below 70, worsening corner-cutting and `Document Review` batching.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Measure minutes spent above WIP > 70 per shift, event service time and error rate immediately *after* an alert triggers (testing for perverse speed-up), and `Bot_Risk` override rates during alert episodes.

### 4. Force random assignment of cases to reduce selection bias
* **Expected benefit:** Eliminates cherry-picking and produces cleaner observational comparisons across resources.
* **Risks:** Operationally harmful. Pure random assignment sends high-complexity carryover cases to Juniors—exposing them to the highest-risk regime (10.9% high-WIP/high-complexity error zone; 12.4% Junior high-WIP error rate)—while underutilizing Senior expertise on trivial cases. If analytical fairness is desired, use **complexity-tiered routing** with controlled randomization only *within* skill-appropriate eligibility pools.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Track `case_complexity_score` distribution by experience tier, Junior error and 7-day rework rates on high-complexity cases, and `Specialist Referral` escalation rates.

### 5. Introduce mandatory micro-breaks in the final two hours
* **Expected benefit:** Helps replenish cognitive focus during the final two hours and disrupts mechanical sequential batching (such as `Junior_H` and `Junior_I`'s 9–18 event `Document Review` runs).
* **Risks:** Unstaggered breaks during Day shift's peak demand (end queue 75) temporarily cut throughput and push WIP higher, potentially negating fatigue gains. Moreover, micro-breaks alone will not solve structural under-capacity when queues exceed 70.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Compare pre/post batching incidence (`pm4py.discover_batches`), post-break vs. pre-break event service time and error rates within the same WIP stratum, and queue-length trajectory during staggered break windows.

### 6. Defer low-priority quality checks to the next shift
* **Expected benefit:** Temporarily relieves the last-45-minute congestion burst at the end of Day shift.
* **Risks:** **Not recommended as a throughput-boosting measure.** Removing or deferring quality checks to boost speed merely hides and postpones errors: defective decisions leak downstream (`Customer Callback`, `Close Case`, 7-day rework), real-time learning feedback disappears, and deferred checks dump additional workload onto the Late shift—which already inherits a queue of 70! The real fix is **continuous, paced Quality Check sampling throughout the shift** rather than batching QC into the last 45 minutes or pushing it onto the next shift.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Track `Quality Check` waiting time (`pm4py.filter_paths_performance`), share of `Quality Check` events occurring in the final 45 minutes of a shift, 7-day `Rework Correction` rates on low-priority cases, and Late-shift starting queue length.

### 7. Add an extra senior reviewer during day-shift demand peaks
* **Expected benefit:** Highest-leverage operational intervention. Directly suppresses Day-shift peak WIP (currently ending at 75), keeps more work below the WIP = 70 threshold, absorbs high-complexity cases where Seniors maintain much lower error rates under pressure (4.9% vs. Juniors' 12.4%), prevents the Late shift from inheriting a queue of 70, and creates bandwidth for continuous `Quality Check` and `Bot_Risk` override oversight.
* **Risks:** Higher staffing cost; potential bottleneck shift downstream (e.g., `Specialist Referral` or `Close Case`); risk of slowing Junior skill development if Seniors absorb all complex work without structured coaching.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Track Day-shift end queue and Late-shift start queue, share of events occurring at WIP > 70, Junior and Mid-level late-shift error rates, overall 7-day rework rate (`pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`), and case cycle time via `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`.

### 8. Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates
* **Expected benefit:** Virtually none; penalizing an outcome heavily confounded by shift timing, arrival surges, and assignment structure does not remove queue pressure or batching incentives.
* **Risks:** **Strongly recommend rejecting.** Punishing raw late-shift error rates unfairly targets Day/Late workers and Juniors for system-created WIP spikes (>70). It triggers severe gaming behaviors: cherry-picking easy cases, delaying hard cases until handover, concealing errors, rushing `Quality Check` sign-offs to avoid flagging peers, and higher attrition among developing staff.
* **Event-log monitoring:** Even if never adopted, monitor anti-gaming indicators—such as sudden drops in self-flagged errors alongside *rising* 7-day customer rework, unexplained spikes in shift-end deferrals, or widening complexity gaps between workers.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Drawing worker-level performance conclusions from raw event-log metrics in this center is both analytically invalid and ethically hazardous for five core reasons:

1. **Selection bias and unequal case-mix exposure:** Case allocation is non-random. Seniors absorb complex early-shift backlogs, Night staff (`Night_L`, `Night_M`) handle urgent exceptions (56% high complexity) under circadian strain, and Day/Late workers confront end-of-shift queue surges (queues 70–75) that Early workers largely avoid (end queue 42). Ranking individuals on unadjusted service time or error rates confuses **who received the hardest work or worst queue** with individual competence.
2. **Measurement error and error-detection timing artifacts:** Event logs record when an error is *flagged* or *corrected*, not necessarily when it was *committed*. Because `Quality Check` is frequently delayed until the final 45 minutes of a shift, errors made hours earlier cluster artificially into late-shift timestamps. Similarly, 7-day rework may be attributed to a downstream step (`Close Case` or `Eligibility Decision`) even when the root defect originated in an upstream rushed `Initial Triage` or batched `Document Review`. Including instant `Bot_Risk` events in activity aggregates further distorts baseline benchmarks.
3. **Queue pressure is a management-created system condition:** Individual caseworkers do not control claim arrival rates, staffing schedules, or Day-to-Late backlog accumulation. The data show that when WIP stays below 30, every experience group maintains stable quality throughout the shift; quality degrades sharply only when systemic under-capacity forces WIP above 70. Holding individuals accountable for speed–accuracy tradeoffs induced by chronic understaffing misattributes organizational design failures to worker negligence.
4. **Automation quality and misplaced accountability (`Bot_Risk`):** Human overrides of `Bot_Risk` increase when `queue_length_at_start > 70`. If `Bot_Risk` produces poorer classifications during peak volume—or reacts poorly to noisy upstream inputs—workers who slow down to scrutinize and correct `Bot_Risk` look less productive (longer service time, more correction events) than workers who blindly rubber-stamp automated outputs. Conversely, if automated misclassifications slip through during high WIP, downstream human workers may be unfairly blamed for automation defects.
5. **Harms of punitive error-rate management:** Punishing workers based on raw late-shift error rates violates procedural fairness and invokes Goodhart's Law. Juniors (such as `Junior_H` and `Junior_I`) may have adopted `Document Review` batching as an informal coping response to overwhelming Day-shift queues rather than carelessness. Punitive oversight drives defensive behavior—cherry-picking easy claims, dumping complex cases across shift boundaries, suppressing error flags, and burning out junior talent—while leaving actual 7-day rework untouched or worse. Event-log analytics must be governed as a **system-improvement and coaching instrument**, using risk-adjusted, WIP-stratified metrics reviewed collaboratively with frontline staff.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

To support continuous operational control without creating punitive or throughput-only blind spots, the dashboard should refresh every 15 minutes for real-time queue indicators and daily/weekly for risk-adjusted quality and fairness metrics.

| # | Indicator | Operational definition & stratification | PM4Py / Event-log source | Trigger threshold & intended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Complexity-Adjusted Service Time** | Ratio of observed to expected `event_service_min` (expected from activity × complexity × priority baseline), segmented by `wip_band` and experience tier; excludes `Bot_Risk`. | `pm4py.get_service_time`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`, event timestamps (`time:timestamp - start_timestamp`). | Ratio `< 0.80` during WIP `> 70` flags unsafe speed-up/corner-cutting; ratio `> 1.25` under Low WIP flags coaching or tooling bottleneck. |
| **2** | **Case Waiting Time & Arrival-to-Finish Ratio** | Median and P90 inter-activity waiting time (especially before `Quality Check` and `Initial Triage`) plus rolling arrival-rate vs. finish-rate ratio by shift hour. | `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, `pm4py.filter_paths_performance`. | Arrival/finish ratio `> 1.15` for 2 consecutive hours or `Quality Check` P90 wait `> 2 hours` triggers dynamic staffing reallocation. |
| **3** | **Real-Time WIP & High-Pressure Exposure** | Live `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` trajectory per shift, plus `% of human events executed at WIP > 70` (risk-zone exposure). | Event attributes `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`, `pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph`. | WIP `> 65` (early warning) / `> 70` (action threshold) activates Senior peak support and pauses non-urgent administrative tasks. |

| **6** | **Case-Mix & Complexity–Priority Balance** | Mean `case_complexity_score` and `% high-priority` share by shift hour, experience tier, and WIP band, specifically tracking `% of high-complexity cases processed at WIP > 70`. | `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, `minutes_since_shift_start`, `wip_at_start`. | `> 20%` of high-complexity cases entering WIP `> 70` or `> 30%` assigned to Juniors during peak load triggers complexity-aware routing rules. |

| **8** | **Shift Handover Backlog & Queue Ownership Changes** | Inherited vs. bequeathed queue size and mean complexity at every shift boundary (Early/Day/Late/Night), plus count of mid-case resource handovers and carryover cases aged `> 1 shift`. | `shift_id`, `shift_start_time`, `queue_length_at_start`, `org:resource` transitions in `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`. | Bequeathed queue `> 60` or inherited high-complexity share `> 45%` triggers shift-overlap staffing review rather than individual blame. |
| **9** | **Worker-Load Fairness & Exposure Equity Index** | Dispersion (Gini / IQR) across workers within each experience tier for: (a) `% of events under WIP > 70`, (b) mean `case_complexity_score`, (c) consecutive high-complexity streaks, and (d) late-shift peak exposure. | `org:resource`, `resource_experience_months`, `wip_at_start`, `case_complexity_score`, `sequence_position_within_shift`. | Persistent imbalance (`> 20%` deviation from peer-tier median in high-WIP or high-complexity exposure over 2 weeks) triggers equitable rotation and workload rebalancing. |

### Governance and anti-gaming design notes
* **Paired metrics only:** Never display service time without adjacent risk-adjusted error and 7-day rework rates, and never display worker error rates without WIP exposure and complexity mix alongside them.
* **Role-based visibility:** Operations managers see team/shift/tier aggregates and fairness indices; individual-level views are reserved for private, non-punitive coaching and workload balancing rather than public leaderboards or disciplinary triggers.
