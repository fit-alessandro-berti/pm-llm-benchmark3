# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Main finding:** routing matters, but the roster is already short of capacity under a reasonable interpretation of the service ranges. Pooling performs best on the illustrative SLA and tail-delay objective below; a restrictive hybrid improves utilization balance but can make SLA performance worse. A robust recommendation must therefore combine resource-aware dispatch with an explicit response to insufficient staffing, rather than promise that reservations alone solve every shock.

The following are modeling assumptions, not observations:

- Arrivals are independent Poisson counts within each two-hour block, with uniform arrival times. Priority and case type are independent conditional on block. The supplied percentages do not establish either independence or Poisson variability; both require sensitivity testing.
- Conditional service times are uniform within the ordinary or complex-tail range. Tail indicators and durations are independent across activities and cases. The dispatcher sees mixture means and elapsed progress, not the sampled future completion time. Correlated complexity and clustered incidents are alternatives to test.
- U1/U5 increase P1 by eight percentage points; the remaining probability is redistributed to P2/P3 while preserving their original ratio. U2 keeps the arrival count fixed and doubles the E/ES *weights* before renormalization. Section 2 also evaluates the interpretation that their absolute arrival rates double.
- An empty initial system receives one day's arrivals. Cases then drain over a 72-hour horizon measured from 08:00 on the arrival day with the supplied shifts repeating on subsequent days, no additional arrivals, and Chen returning after the absence day. SLA clocks use elapsed time, including breaks, overnight waiting, and the outage. This is a cohort experiment, not evidence of steady-state stability.
- Every resource handles at most one activity at a time. A two-branch sequential case uses one resource qualified for both branches; parallel routing uses distinct resources and a join before Validation. Branch order for sequential ME is Mechanical then Electrical; for ES it is Electrical then Software. Mode and sequential owner are committed after Intake; changing that commitment is not modeled.
- Handover is charged once for a change of resource on an activity transition. Intake-to-branch handovers are charged separately on split branches. At the join, one consolidated seven-minute handover is charged if any contributing branch uses a different resource from Validation. Mechanical-to-electrical/software transfer adds five minutes when sequential owners differ, or once at an ME split join. Intake and Validation are treated as neutral teams. These are explicit interpretations of otherwise underspecified team and join rules.
- Conservatively, the receiving resource is occupied during handover; setup also occupies the resource. Eight-minute parallel coordination occupies elapsed time but no resource. Treating handover as unstaffed elapsed delay is an important alternative and should improve capacity relative to these simulations.
- A diagnostic-family switch between different cases costs five setup minutes. A within-case sequential switch does not, following the wording “between consecutive cases.” Intake/Validation do not reset the resource's last diagnostic family.
- Only lower-priority diagnostic service can be preempted by P1. The interrupted case, across all its branches, can suffer at most one priority preemption. Its unfinished active service increases by 10%, and it resumes on the same resource. Calendar/outage pauses retain progress without restart cost. Overtime finishes already-started work for at most 30 minutes per resource; no new activities start after regular shift end.

Let (T_c) be arrival-to-Validation-completion elapsed time, (s_{p,omega}=Pr(T_c\le d_p\mid p,omega)), and (u_{r,omega}) be occupied minutes divided by regular available minutes after breaks. Overtime is in the numerator, so (u) can exceed 100%. Report utilization balance as (CV(u)=\mathrm{sd}(u)/\mathrm{mean}(u)), excluding absent resources. Low CV alone is insufficient: uniformly overloaded people are still overloaded.

Two defensible objectives are:

1. **SLA/tail minimax:** lexicographically minimize, over Base and U1–U5, the worst P1 shortfall ((0.90-s_{1,omega})_+), then worst P2 and P3 shortfalls, then worst scenario p90 of (T_c), then mean elapsed time and coordination burden. This favors pooling in the supplied experiment. It treats all supplied shocks as relevant without inventing their occurrence probabilities.
2. **Fairness-constrained robustness:** require each individual's *planned* base utilization to remain at or below 90%; then minimize worst-scenario SLA loss plus utilization CV and the maximum individual overload. If that constraint is infeasible for sustained demand, report infeasibility and determine needed additional capacity instead of quietly dropping the fairness condition. Actual utilization and confidence bounds must also be monitored because uncertain service can exceed a plan.

The illustrative “hybrid” tests an admission budget toward objective 2, with P1 overrides; it is not a proof that the fairness constraint is satisfied. The primary recommendation uses objective 1 to select a routing core and treats objective 2 as a staffing/deployment requirement.

Decision variables include activity-resource assignment, diagnostic mode and owner(s), dispatch order, temporary skill protection, overflow admission, preemption, and bounded overtime. Additional or retimed coverage is a separate management decision, outside the simulated roster.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

Under the uniform-mixture assumption,

\[
\mu_a=(1-p_a)(L_a+U_a)/2+p_a(L_a^{tail}+U_a^{tail})/2.
\]

The base means in minutes are Intake **6.94**, Mechanical **26.30**, Electrical **32.82**, Software **38.50**, Validation **15.74**. A branch count, not just a case count, determines diagnostic load:

\[
W_{M,b}=n_b(q_{M,b}+q_{ME,b})\mu_M,\quad
W_{E,b}=n_b(q_{E,b}+q_{ME,b}+q_{ES,b})\mu_E,\quad
W_{S,b}=n_b(q_{S,b}+q_{ES,b})\mu_S.
\]

Intake and Validation each have (n_b) tasks. These are **arrival-cohort workload estimates**, not claims that all these stages execute in the arrival block. Stage lag moves work into later capacity and can intensify the late-day shortage.

# 3. Candidate policies

The three coded policies share calendar compliance, one-resource occupancy, earliest priority service, and the explicit overhead interpretation above. Their operational differences are as follows.

**A. Fixed specialist queues.** Ada handles Intake/Validation; Ben Mechanical; Chen Electrical; Farah Software; Deepa Validation until 16:00 and Mechanical/Electrical/Validation thereafter; Emil Software, adding Intake/Electrical after 16:00 and Validation after 18:00. With Chen absent, Deepa becomes the designated Electrical owner before 16:00. These late-shift exceptions are a fixed roster, not opportunistic pooling. No other overflow is permitted. Tasks are selected by priority, then case deadline, then queue-entry time. P1 may preempt an eligible lower-priority diagnostic job when no eligible idle worker exists; the once-per-victim-case rule applies. Two-branch cases use separate-resource fork/join routing, paying coordination even if queues prevent actual overlap. The limited common-stage queues retain ownership only when consistent with the roster. This policy preserves practiced speeds and simple accountability but strands some capacity and has poor absence coverage.

**B. Full qualified pooling.** All on-shift resources qualified for an activity are eligible. Rank queued tasks by P1/P2/P3, earliest case deadline, then queue age. For the highest-ranked task, choose the idle resource minimizing expected occupied work: resource-adjusted mean service plus applicable handover and setup. Thus pooling already values continuity; it does not mean random assignment. At diagnostic release, compare the earliest forecast two-resource fork/join completion with the best retained sequential completion; use the faster predicted plan. Forecasts use calendar availability and mean remaining current service, but ignore other queued jobs, so they can be optimistic. Parallel branches must use distinct resources. P1 preempts an eligible lower-priority diagnostic job only if no eligible idle resource is available; interruption is limited once per victim case. There is no capacity reservation or individual utilization admission cap. The overflow trigger is simply eligible capacity being idle while a job waits. This exploits complementary skills, but can consume scarce Software workers for common work and exceed the fair planning budget.

**C. Budgeted hybrid with targeted P1 protection.** Eligibility is pooled, with these additional rules:

- P1 is always first. For other cases, calculate deadline slack from the mixture-mean diagnostic work plus Validation; promote cases with less than 60 minutes of slack. Promote P3 after 12 elapsed hours. Among equal ranks, smaller slack and older queue entry win. The coded slack estimate is deliberately simple and conservative: it does not subtract every completed diagnostic contribution.
- For P2/P3, reject a new assignment if actual used minutes that day plus expected assigned work would exceed 90% of the worker's regular daily availability. P1 may override, and actual tail service can also overrun the budget; both are exceptions to report. This is admission control, not a guarantee on actual utilization.
- When a P1 case has less than 90 minutes until deadline, protect downstream diagnostic skills while it is at Intake, and Validation while it is in diagnosis. If only one qualified person is currently on shift for a protected activity, do not assign that person a lower-priority task expected to occupy more than 15 minutes. Release protection when the threat disappears; other short work remains eligible.
- Add a transparent scarcity charge of (20\times\text{queued tasks}/\text{on-shift qualified alternatives}) for each queued diagnostic skill a proposed assignment would displace. Add (20u_{projected}^2) as a fairness penalty. These coefficients are illustrative minute-equivalents, not empirically calibrated values.
- Use parallelism only when predicted diagnostic-join completion improves by more than ten minutes over retained sequential service. Otherwise favor sequential continuity.
- Preempt only when P1 is forecast to be at risk and estimated time saved exceeds restart, setup, handover, and a ten-minute margin. The once-per-case limit still applies.
- Cross-skill overflow occurs through the scarcity/fairness score when specialists are occupied, provided the admission and protection rules permit it. If nobody qualifies, the task remains queued and an overload alarm is required.

C can avoid wasteful reservations and some interruptions, but a strict admission budget in an undersized system shifts substantial work into tomorrow. Its preemption test can also be too conservative. Those are hypotheses tested by the scenario comparison, not assumed advantages.

Uncertainty is substantial. For B/Base, the 10th–90th percentile **seed range** is approximately 368–663 minutes for mean elapsed time and 1,082–1,447 for p90; for B/U5 it is 704–1,187 and 1,443–2,542. C improves base utilization CV from .10 to .08 and maximum utilization from 104% to 98%, but does not bring actual maximum utilization below 90%. Pooling also incurs fewer handover/setup minutes here because its completion-cost score preserves continuity; pooling need not increase handovers relative to fixed queues. This evidence does not justify recommending C merely because it is called “hybrid.”

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Use resource-aware pooling, Policy B, as the provisional routing core, with explicit overload escalation and targeted reservation rules validated before activation.** Among the three tested candidates it offers the best worst-scenario SLA/tail trade-off: estimated worst P1 attainment is 64%, versus 57% for C and 16% for A, and its worst mean seed p90 is 1,778 rather than 2,155 or 2,516 minutes. This is comparative robustness across U1–U5, not achievement of the 90% targets.

For routine deployment, add a transparent workload plan that meets the base 90% requirement. If sustained daily demand resembles the modeled base, even raw work exceeds capacity including maximum overtime; backlog must grow. Allowing one cohort to drain into empty subsequent days disguises that instability. A fair, stable normal operation therefore needs added capacity, changed coverage, or a clearly acknowledged demand/service improvement. It cannot be certified by rearranging these six people alone.

Under the uniform means, ignoring speed and overhead, additional regular availability (A\) must satisfy (0.90(2,490+A)\ge2,720.6), so **at least 533 available minutes** are needed for the base fair plan. Applying the same fairness objective to U5 gives (0.90(2,040+A)\ge3,442.2), or **at least 1,785 additional available minutes**. These are optimistic capacity bounds, not exact staffing prescriptions: skill/time-window constraints, setup, and handover require more. An extra minute of slow cross-skilled capacity is not a minute of fastest-specialist capacity.

Coverage should address morning qualified Software/Electrical backup for Chen, the 14–16 Electrical peak, and qualified Mechanical plus common-stage capacity after 16:00. Extending Mechanical coverage into the final arrival block directly addresses a calendar-caused SLA failure; simply adding early Mechanical capacity does not. Any shift change or supplemental staffing is outside the fixed-roster experiment and must be evaluated separately.

Use these proposed, auditable mode triggers:

- Every 15 minutes, project demand plus backlog over the next 120 minutes using mean service and the current skill/calendar matrix. A projected resource requirement above 90% in base is a staffing alarm; a feasible skill-constrained assignment must be checked, rather than comparing only aggregate minutes.
- If any P1 has predicted completion later than arrival+100 minutes, or an eligible diagnostic queue has more than 30 minutes of oldest wait, activate targeted protection for its next diagnostic skill and Validation. Favor fast specialists and release unrelated reservations. Ten-/fifteen-minute buffers are starting values for pilot tuning, not inferred optimal thresholds.
- When Chen is absent, protect Farah for morning Software and Deepa for Electrical when available; assign Intake to the remaining suitable resources. In U5, Farah protection is necessary but insufficient: the Software workload bound itself triggers supplemental coverage immediately.
- Switch to **surge pooling** when projected workload exceeds available capacity, the morning arrival rate exceeds 1.4 times base for two reviews, or the outage occurs with growing Intake backlog. Release discretionary lower-priority idle reservations and escalate staffing. Do not implement C's automatic daily admission cut-off as the primary surge response: its simulated delays are worse. Fairness exceptions, if operationally authorized, must be visible, temporary, and paired with relief; extra overtime beyond 30 minutes is not an option.
- Preempt only a qualifying lower-priority diagnostic task when a P1 has no idle feasible resource and predicted SLA recovery exceeds restart plus switching cost. Record why. Calibrate this guard in a protected pilot: C preempted far less often and had worse P1 attainment; an ablation must determine whether its guard or other restrictions caused that loss. No repeat preemption of the same victim case is allowed.
- Return from surge mode after two consecutive reviews show projected load below 80%, no threatened P1, and diagnostic/Validation oldest waits below 15 minutes. Hysteresis avoids repeated policy switching.

These protective adjustments form a **proposed guarded pooling variant**, not a fourth policy with measured results in the table. Its dispatch and reservation changes require backtesting; the numerical evidence is for B as implemented. If extra capacity cannot be provided, report the infeasible workload and expected SLA trade-off explicitly rather than presenting a robust target-meeting plan.

Fixed queues could be preferable at demonstrably low load when cross-skill quality or setup costs are substantially worse than assumed. Budgeted C could be preferable when overtime/fairness is the dominant objective and deferring work is acceptable, especially after capacity is added. Neither condition is established by this prompt.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

**Estimate gaming:** shortest/estimated-finish rules can reward understated complexity or premature completion recording. Freeze initial estimates, retain every revision and author, score calibration by skill/resource/type, and audit deviations without penalizing workers merely for receiving difficult cases. Priority upgrades should require a reason; arbitrary reclassification undermines reservation logic.

**P3 starvation:** P1 interruptions and repeated P2 arrivals can consume all capacity. Use explicit aging, a minimum feasible share of protected P3 work, and separate reporting of maximum P3 age and 24-hour misses. C's aging rule is not sufficient when its admission budget blocks all lower-priority work. A minimum share also needs capacity; it cannot be added as a cost-free promise.

**Skill erosion and quality:** persistent specialization leaves backups unpracticed; excessive pooling routes work to slower or less practiced people. Maintain supervised cross-skill rotations outside P1 surges and monitor rework/Validation failures alongside speed. The supplied matrix does not establish equal diagnostic quality.

**Preemption and coordination:** the 10% restart penalty is only one cost. The interrupted worker can incur another family-switch setup on resumption, and documentation/context can suffer. A case-level interruption ledger must cover both branches. Count split-branch coordination and failed joins separately; parallelizing everything may finish branches early while delaying everyone else's scarce skills.

**Validation and fairness:** protecting diagnostics without Validation capacity creates a finishing queue and can waste P1 gains. Fairness should cover skill mix, interruption burden, overtime, difficult cases, and schedule constraints as well as normalized busy minutes. Low CV during U5 is not a success when everybody is overloaded. Tail overruns explain some actual utilization breaches, but they do not excuse repeatedly planning above 90% in base.

**Interpretability:** operators should see priority/deadline, eligible resources, the selected resource's expected work and penalties, scarce-skill protection, and override reasons. Keep the score components visible. A solver may check feasibility behind the scenes, but dispatch should remain explainable at the case level.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Collect an interval log of active service segments and explicit overhead/delay events, plus a decision log. Required identifiers are case, activity instance/operation, branch, event/segment, resource, and policy version. Record:

- Case arrival, type, priority and revisions; original SLA deadline and continuously elapsed SLA clock.
- Stage/branch queue-entry time, diagnostic fork/join times, ready time, assignment decision time, and eligible-resource set with exclusion reasons.
- Selected resource, skill used, planned/actual service, multiplier, complexity estimate and revisions, score components, reservation and overflow reasons.
- Segment start/end; pause/resume times and reason; victim case's prior-preemption count; remaining service estimate and restart minutes; calendar/break/outage boundaries.
- Handover sender/receiver, start/end, team crossing, setup family before/after, and parallel coordination start/end.
- Policy version, scenario/shock flag and onset, absence, forecast load, staffing override, overtime, completion and Validation/rework result.

Deployment indicators should include priority-specific attainment and p90/p95 elapsed completion, predicted P1 breaches, stage oldest wait and queue workload, branch synchronization wait, normalized resource utilization and overtime, utilization CV, family switches, handover minutes per completed case, restart minutes, and end-of-day/backlog recovery. Stratify by arrival block, case type, policy version, and shock. Include incomplete cases and avoid comparing only completions.

PM4Py can format the interval dataframe, discover directly-follows and process models, assess conformance to Intake–diagnostic fork/join–Validation, compare variants, obtain activity service-time statistics, and derive organizational handover networks. Use an explicitly concurrent model: an ordinary completion-ordered trace can falsely serialize the branches. Validate the actual branch precedences and join condition from operation/branch identifiers separately, including the one-interruption limit and qualification/calendar constraints.

Important timing distinctions:

- Pass `start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp'` explicitly to timing analyses. A completion-only default can produce zero apparent service time even though the input has an interval column.
- `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` produces **case-level aggregate logged service, sojourn, and residual waiting**, repeated on the case's rows. With overlapping intervals, summed labor service can exceed elapsed sojourn; the resulting waiting field may be negative. It is not a valid diagnostic-queue wait. Nor does its first logged event automatically equal original case arrival.
- Calculate active labor by summing segment durations, active elapsed coverage by their interval union, and arrival-to-completion elapsed time from the explicit arrival. Elapsed nonactive time is elapsed time minus active interval coverage; it includes queues, handover/setup where excluded from service, coordination, and calendar pauses. It is not pure queueing.
- Calculate each queue episode from stage-ready/requeue to assignment, and each stage sojourn from ready to completed operation. Sum service segments by operation before estimating an activity's service distribution; treating interrupted segments as separate complete activities biases means downward. Resumed assignments and calendar pauses need distinct accounting.
- `discover_handover_of_work_network` on completion-ordered service events is descriptive. A concurrent sibling finishing after another is not necessarily a handover; derive the operational handover graph from logged sender/receiver and causal activity edges. Repeated resource self-links can come from pause segments.

Drift analysis should compare reconstructed operation service and tail exceedance rates by skill/resource against prior windows, adjusting for case mix and calendars. Handover/setup drift appears in transition-specific counts and durations. Bottleneck migration appears as rising ready-to-assignment wait and queue workload for Electrical, Software, or Validation, combined with active utilization and availability—not merely a large directly-follows edge duration. Overlay shock onset to distinguish a changed process from temporary loss of capacity.

# 8. Reproducible Python

This self-contained model encodes the supplied tables, three dispatch policies, and all scenario changes. Dependencies are NumPy, pandas, and PM4Py. It is a transparent minute-resolution event-calendar simulation: allocation, phase completion, joins, interruptions, and calendar pauses are processed explicitly; the one-minute discretization is an approximation. It prints repeated-seed metrics and variability, SLA fractions, and interval-log diagnostics, and writes its own simulated logs to `/tmp`.

The experiment omits continuing arrivals during drainage, weekends/holidays, travel distances, resource collaboration during handover, within-activity migration, diagnosis quality/rework, latent correlated complexity, updated skill learning, and optimal matching of all queued jobs. Parallel forecasts ignore other queued jobs; slack and reservation lookahead are deliberately simple. Reserved protection is a conditional eligibility rule, not a reserved named worker sitting idle. These omissions are reasons for validation, not hidden claims of operational realism.

```python
import itertools
import math
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Minutes from 08:00; independent uniform mixture draws are assumptions.
SERVICE = pd.DataFrame([
    ('Intake',5,8,10,14,.08), ('Mechanical',18,28,35,55,.15),
    ('Electrical',22,35,40,65,.18), ('Software',25,40,45,80,.20),
    ('Validation',10,18,22,35,.12)],
    columns=['activity','lo','hi','tail_lo','tail_hi','tail_p']).set_index('activity')
ACT = list(SERVICE.index)
SKILL = pd.DataFrame([
    [1,1,1.35,np.nan,1.15], [1.1,1,np.nan,np.nan,1],
    [1.1,np.nan,1,1.25,1.1], [1,1.25,1,np.nan,1],
    [1.1,np.nan,1.3,1,1], [1,1.4,1.4,1.4,1.2]],
    index=['Ada','Ben','Chen','Deepa','Emil','Farah'], columns=ACT)
SHIFT = pd.DataFrame([
    ('Ada',0,480,255,285), ('Ben',0,480,240,270),
    ('Chen',0,480,270,300), ('Deepa',120,600,360,390),
    ('Emil',240,720,480,510), ('Farah',0,240,-1,-1)],
    columns=['resource','begin','end','break_begin','break_end']).set_index('resource')
TYPES = ['M','E','S','ME','ES']
BRANCH = {'M':['Mechanical'],'E':['Electrical'],'S':['Software'],
          'ME':['Mechanical','Electrical'],'ES':['Electrical','Software']}
DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    [0,7,.10,.55,.35,.30,.25,.15,.20,.10],
    [120,9,.15,.55,.30,.25,.25,.15,.25,.10],
    [240,7,.12,.58,.30,.22,.28,.18,.20,.12],
    [360,10,.18,.52,.30,.20,.30,.18,.20,.12],
    [480,6,.10,.50,.40,.20,.25,.25,.15,.15],
    [600,3,.05,.45,.50,.25,.20,.30,.10,.15]],
    columns=['begin','arrivals','P1','P2','P3']+TYPES).astype(float)
SCENARIO = pd.DataFrame([
    ('Base',1,0,1,False,1,False), ('U1',1.7,.08,1,False,1,False),
    ('U2',1,0,2,False,1,False), ('U3',1,0,1,True,1,False),
    ('U4',1,0,1,False,2,False), ('U5',1.7,.08,1,True,1,True)],
    columns=['scenario','morning_factor','p1_add','e_es_factor',
             'chen_absent','diag_tail_factor','intake_outage']).set_index('scenario')
# Explicit overhead assumptions: receiving resource performs the handover;
# parallel join coordination is elapsed time with no resource occupancy.
HANDOVER, TEAM_MOVE, SETUP, COORD = 7, 5, 5, 8
RESTART, OVERTIME, PLAN_CAP = .10, 30, .90
SLA = {1:120,2:480,3:1440}
DIAG = tuple(ACT[1:4])
POLICIES = ['fixed','pool','hybrid']

def inputs(scenario, seed):
    s = SCENARIO.loc[scenario]
    d, v = DEMAND.copy(), SERVICE.copy()
    morning = d['begin'] < 240
    d.loc[morning,'arrivals'] *= s.morning_factor
    d.loc[morning,'P1'] += s.p1_add
    # Preserve the P2:P3 ratio when P1 rises.
    old_other = DEMAND.loc[morning,['P2','P3']].sum(axis=1)
    d.loc[morning,['P2','P3']] = DEMAND.loc[morning,['P2','P3']].mul(
        (1-d.loc[morning,'P1'])/old_other,axis=0)
    midday = d['begin'].isin([240,360])
    d.loc[midday,['E','ES']] *= s.e_es_factor
    d.loc[midday,TYPES] = d.loc[midday,TYPES].div(d.loc[midday,TYPES].sum(axis=1),axis=0)
    v.loc[list(DIAG),'tail_p'] *= s.diag_tail_factor
    v['mean'] = (1-v.tail_p)*(v.lo+v.hi)/2 + v.tail_p*(v.tail_lo+v.tail_hi)/2
    rng, cases = np.random.default_rng(seed), []
    for row in d.itertuples(index=False):
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(row.arrivals)):
            typ = rng.choice(TYPES,p=[getattr(row,k) for k in TYPES])
            p = int(rng.choice([1,2,3],p=[row.P1,row.P2,row.P3]))
            arrival = int(rng.integers(int(row.begin),int(row.begin)+120))
            draws = {}
            for a in ['Intake']+BRANCH[typ]+['Validation']:
                z = v.loc[a]
                tail = rng.random() < z.tail_p
                draws[a] = rng.uniform(z.tail_lo,z.tail_hi) if tail else rng.uniform(z.lo,z.hi)
            cases.append(dict(id=len(cases),arrival=arrival,typ=typ,p=p,
                              due=arrival+SLA[p],draws=draws,preempted=False,
                              finish=None,mode=None,diag_done=[]))
    return s,d,v,cases

def run(scenario,policy,seed,horizon=3*1440):
    s,d,v,cases = inputs(scenario,seed)
    names = list(SKILL.index)
    speed = {(r,a):SKILL.at[r,a] for r in names for a in ACT}
    mu = v['mean'].to_dict()
    calendar = SHIFT.to_dict('index')
    resources = {r:dict(job=None,last_family=None,last_case=None) for r in names}
    avail = (SHIFT.end-SHIFT.begin-(SHIFT.break_end-SHIFT.break_begin).clip(lower=0)).to_dict()
    used = {(day,r):0 for day in range(3) for r in names}
    jobs, events, decisions, preemptions = [], [], [], 0
    base_time = pd.Timestamp('2026-01-05 08:00',tz='UTC')

    def present(r,t):
        return not (r=='Chen' and s.chen_absent and t//1440==0)

    def on(r,t,continuation=False):
        z, minute = calendar[r], t%1440
        return (present(r,t) and z['begin']<=minute<z['end']+(OVERTIME if continuation else 0)
                and not(z['break_begin']<=minute<z['break_end']))

    def fixed_role(r,a,t):
        if policy!='fixed': return True
        minute = t%1440
        if r=='Ada': return a in ['Intake','Validation']
        if r=='Ben': return a=='Mechanical'
        if r=='Chen': return a=='Electrical'
        if r=='Farah': return a=='Software'
        if r=='Deepa':
            if minute>=480: return a in ['Mechanical','Electrical','Validation']
            return a==('Electrical' if s.chen_absent and t//1440==0 else 'Validation')
        return (a=='Software' or (minute>=480 and a in ['Intake','Electrical'])
                or (minute>=600 and a=='Validation'))

    def eligible(j,r,t):
        if pd.isna(speed[r,j['a']]) or not fixed_role(r,j['a'],t): return False
        if j.get('pin') is not None and j['pin']!=r: return False
        c = cases[j['cid']]
        if c['mode']=='parallel' and j['a'] in DIAG:
            siblings = [k for k in jobs if k['cid']==j['cid'] and k['a'] in DIAG and k is not j]
            if any(k.get('r')==r for k in siblings): return False
        return True

    def add(c,a,t,parents=None,pin=None):
        j=dict(cid=c['id'],a=a,queue=t,ready=t,parents=parents or [],pin=pin,
               episode_queue=t,status='queued',r=None,remaining=None,done_service=0.,phase=None,phase_left=0,
               segment_start=None,ever_started=False)
        jobs.append(j)
        return j

    def estimate_free(r,a,t):
        busy = resources[r]['job']
        wait = 0 if busy is None else max(1,v.at[busy['a'],'mean']*speed[r,busy['a']]-busy['done_service'])
        q = t+int(math.ceil(wait))
        # A forecast ignores other queued jobs; its limitations are reported.
        for z in range(q,min(q+2880,horizon)):
            if on(r,z) and fixed_role(r,a,z): return z
        return horizon

    def plan(c,t,parent):
        aa = BRANCH[c['typ']]
        if len(aa)==1:
            c['mode']='single'; add(c,aa[0],t,[parent]); return
        seq = []
        for r in names:
            if all(pd.notna(speed[r,a]) for a in aa):
                start = max(estimate_free(r,a,t) for a in aa)
                cost = start-t+sum(mu[a]*speed[r,a] for a in aa)
                cost += HANDOVER*(r!=parent['r'])
                seq.append((cost,r))
        par = []
        for r1,r2 in itertools.permutations(names,2):
            if pd.notna(speed[r1,aa[0]]) and pd.notna(speed[r2,aa[1]]):
                cost = max(estimate_free(r1,aa[0],t)-t+mu[aa[0]]*speed[r1,aa[0]]+HANDOVER*(r1!=parent['r']),
                           estimate_free(r2,aa[1],t)-t+mu[aa[1]]*speed[r2,aa[1]]+HANDOVER*(r2!=parent['r']))
                cost += COORD + (TEAM_MOVE if c['typ']=='ME' else 0)
                par.append((cost,r1,r2))
        best_seq = min(seq,default=(float('inf'),None))
        best_par = min(par,default=(float('inf'),None,None))
        gain = 10 if policy=='hybrid' else 0
        if policy=='fixed' or best_par[0]+gain<best_seq[0]:
            c['mode']='parallel'
            for a in aa: add(c,a,t,[parent])
        else:
            c['mode']='sequential'; c['seq_owner']=best_seq[1]
            add(c,aa[0],t,[parent],best_seq[1])

    def overhead(j,r):
        pp = [] if j['ever_started'] else j['parents']
        h = HANDOVER if any(k['r']!=r for k in pp) else 0
        # Teams are diagnostic families; Intake/Validation are neutral.
        cross = any(k['a'] in DIAG and j['a'] in DIAG and
                    ((k['a']=='Mechanical')!=(j['a']=='Mechanical')) and k['r']!=r for k in pp)
        h += TEAM_MOVE if cross else 0
        if j['a']=='Validation' and cases[j['cid']]['typ']=='ME' and len(pp)==2:
            h += TEAM_MOVE if pp[0]['r']!=pp[1]['r'] else 0
        st = resources[r]
        setup = SETUP if (j['a'] in DIAG and st['last_family'] is not None
                          and st['last_family']!=j['a'] and st['last_case']!=j['cid']) else 0
        return h,setup

    def rank(j,t):
        c=cases[j['cid']]
        tail_left=sum(mu[a] for a in BRANCH[c['typ']])+mu['Validation']
        slack=c['due']-t-tail_left
        if policy=='hybrid':
            level=0 if c['p']==1 else (1 if slack<60 else c['p'])
            if c['p']==3 and t-c['arrival']>=720: level=1
            return (level,slack,j['queue'],j['cid'])
        return (c['p'],c['due'],j['queue'],j['cid'])

    def threatened_skills(t):
        need=set()
        for c in cases:
            if c['p']==1 and c['arrival']<=t and c['finish'] is None and c['due']-t<90:
                live=[j for j in jobs if j['cid']==c['id'] and j['status']!='done']
                if any(j['a']=='Intake' for j in live): need.update(BRANCH[c['typ']])
                elif any(j['a'] in DIAG for j in live): need.add('Validation')
        return need

    def close(j,t,reason='phase_complete'):
        if j['segment_start'] is not None and t>j['segment_start']:
            c=cases[j['cid']]
            events.append({'case:concept:name':str(c['id']),'concept:name':j['phase'],
                'start_timestamp':base_time+pd.Timedelta(minutes=j['segment_start']),
                'time:timestamp':base_time+pd.Timedelta(minutes=t),'org:resource':j['r'],
                'operation_id':jobs.index(j),'kind':'service' if j['phase']==j['a'] else 'overhead',
                'stage':j['a'],'end_reason':reason,'policy_version':'illustrative_v1','queue_entry':base_time+pd.Timedelta(minutes=j['queue']),
                'priority':c['p'],'case_type':c['typ'],'policy':policy,'scenario':scenario,
                'mode':c['mode'],'remaining_sla_min':c['due']-t})
        j['segment_start']=None

    def start(j,r,t):
        h,st=overhead(j,r)
        # Calendar/preemption resumes retain the resource and unfinished service.
        if j['ever_started']:
            z=resources[r]
            st=SETUP if j['a'] in DIAG and z['last_family']!=j['a'] and z['last_case']!=j['cid'] else 0
        else:
            j['remaining']=int(math.ceil(cases[j['cid']]['draws'][j['a']]*speed[r,j['a']]))
        j.update(r=r,pin=r,ever_started=True,status='running')
        phases=[]
        if h: phases.append(('Handover',h))
        if st: phases.append(('Setup',st))
        phases.append((j['a'],j['remaining']))
        j['phases']=phases
        j['phase'],j['phase_left']=j['phases'].pop(0)
        j['segment_start']=t
        resources[r]['job']=j

    def complete(j,t):
        c=cases[j['cid']]; j['status']='done'; resources[j['r']]['job']=None
        if j['a']=='Intake': plan(c,t,j)
        elif j['a'] in DIAG:
            c['diag_done'].append(j)
            aa=BRANCH[c['typ']]
            if c['mode']=='sequential' and len(c['diag_done'])==1 and len(aa)==2:
                add(c,aa[1],t,[j],c['seq_owner'])
            elif len(c['diag_done'])==len(aa):
                if c['mode']=='parallel':
                    events.append({'case:concept:name':str(c['id']),'concept:name':'Coordination',
                        'start_timestamp':base_time+pd.Timedelta(minutes=t),
                        'time:timestamp':base_time+pd.Timedelta(minutes=t+COORD),
                        'org:resource':None,'operation_id':-1-c['id'],'kind':'delay',
                        'stage':'Coordination','priority':c['p'],'case_type':c['typ'],
                        'policy':policy,'scenario':scenario,'mode':c['mode']})
                add(c,'Validation',t+(COORD if c['mode']=='parallel' else 0),c['diag_done'])
        else: c['finish']=t

    arrivals={}
    for c in cases: arrivals.setdefault(c['arrival'],[]).append(c)
    for t in range(horizon):
        for c in arrivals.get(t,[]): add(c,'Intake',t)
        # Breaks, off-shift periods and the outage pause service without restart cost.
        for r,z in resources.items():
            j=z['job']
            if j is not None and (not on(r,t,True) or
                    (s.intake_outage and 90<=t<120 and j['a']=='Intake')): close(j,t,'intake_outage' if s.intake_outage and 90<=t<120 and j['a']=='Intake' else 'calendar')
        waiting=[j for j in jobs if j['status']=='queued' and j['ready']<=t]
        waiting.sort(key=lambda j:rank(j,t))
        for j in waiting:
            c=cases[j['cid']]
            if c['p']!=1 or j['a'] not in DIAG: continue
            options=[]
            for r,z in resources.items():
                victim=z['job']
                if not on(r,t) or not eligible(j,r,t) or victim is None: continue
                vc=cases[victim['cid']]
                if victim['phase']!=victim['a'] or victim['a'] not in DIAG or vc['p']==1 or vc['preempted']: continue
                est=max(1,v.at[victim['a'],'mean']*speed[r,victim['a']]-victim['done_service'])
                extra=RESTART*est+SETUP+overhead(j,r)[0]
                if policy=='hybrid' and (c['due']-t>est+mu[j['a']]*speed[r,j['a']]+mu['Validation']
                                        or est<=extra+10): continue
                options.append((est,r))
            free=any(resources[r]['job'] is None and on(r,t) and eligible(j,r,t) for r in names)
            if options and not free:
                _,r=max(options); victim=resources[r]['job']; close(victim,t,'preempted')
                vc=cases[victim['cid']]; vc['preempted']=True; preemptions+=1
                victim['remaining']+=int(math.ceil(RESTART*victim['remaining']))
                victim['estimated_restart']=RESTART*max(1,mu[victim['a']]*speed[r,victim['a']]-victim['done_service'])
                victim['status']='queued'; victim['ready']=t+1; victim['episode_queue']=t
                resources[r]['job']=None
        protected=threatened_skills(t)
        for j in waiting:
            if j['status']!='queued' or j['ready']>t: continue
            if s.intake_outage and 90<=t<120 and j['a']=='Intake': continue
            c=cases[j['cid']]; candidates=[]
            for r,z in resources.items():
                if z['job'] is not None or not on(r,t) or not eligible(j,r,t): continue
                h,st=overhead(j,r)
                work=mu[j['a']]*speed[r,j['a']]
                if j['ever_started']: work=max(1,work-j['done_service'])+j.get('estimated_restart',0)
                work+=h+st
                projected=(used[t//1440,r]+work)/avail[r]
                if policy=='hybrid' and c['p']!=1 and projected>PLAN_CAP: continue
                if policy=='hybrid' and c['p']!=1 and work>15:
                    if any(pd.notna(speed[r,a]) and
                           sum(on(rr,t) and pd.notna(speed[rr,a]) for rr in names)==1
                           for a in protected): continue
                scarcity=0
                if policy=='hybrid':
                    for a in DIAG:
                        backlog=sum(k['status']=='queued' and k['ready']<=t and k['a']==a for k in jobs)
                        alternatives=sum(on(rr,t) and pd.notna(speed[rr,a]) for rr in names)
                        if backlog and pd.notna(speed[r,a]) and j['a']!=a:
                            scarcity+=20*backlog/max(1,alternatives)
                candidates.append((work+scarcity+(20*projected**2 if policy=='hybrid' else 0),r))
            if candidates:
                _,r=min(candidates)
                decisions.append(dict(case=c['id'],stage=j['a'],minute=t,chosen=r,
                    eligible=tuple(rr for rr in names if eligible(j,rr,t) and on(rr,t)),
                    score=rank(j,t),planned_service=mu[j['a']]*speed[r,j['a']],
                    policy=policy,scenario=scenario,queue_wait=t-j['episode_queue']))
                start(j,r,t)
        for r,z in resources.items():
            j=z['job']
            if j is None or not on(r,t,True): continue
            if s.intake_outage and 90<=t<120 and j['a']=='Intake': continue
            if j['segment_start'] is None: j['segment_start']=t
            used[t//1440,r]+=1
            j['phase_left']-=1
            if j['phase']==j['a']:
                j['remaining']-=1; j['done_service']+=1
                if j['a'] in DIAG: z['last_family'],z['last_case']=j['a'],j['cid']
            if j['phase_left']==0:
                close(j,t+1)
                if j['phases']:
                    j['phase'],j['phase_left']=j['phases'].pop(0)
                    j['segment_start']=t+1
                else: complete(j,t+1)
        if all(c['finish'] is not None for c in cases) and t>=720: break
    for z in resources.values():
        if z['job'] is not None: close(z['job'],horizon,'censored')
    el=pd.DataFrame(events); dl=pd.DataFrame(decisions)
    completed=[c for c in cases if c['finish'] is not None]
    elapsed=[c['finish']-c['arrival'] for c in completed]
    # If censored, quantiles are NOT represented as all-case quantiles.
    active=[r for r in names if present(r,0)]
    u=np.array([used[0,r]/avail[r] for r in active])
    out=dict(scenario=scenario,policy=policy,seed=seed,n=len(cases),
             mean_delay=np.mean(elapsed) if elapsed else np.nan,
             p90_delay=np.quantile(elapsed,.9) if elapsed else np.nan,
             max_resource_utilization=max(u,default=np.nan),
             utilization_fairness=np.std(u)/np.mean(u) if np.mean(u)>0 else np.nan,
             handover_or_setup_burden=(el.loc[el.kind=='overhead','time:timestamp']-
                 el.loc[el.kind=='overhead','start_timestamp']).dt.total_seconds().sum()/60 if not el.empty else 0,
             preemptions=preemptions,backlog_20=sum(c['finish'] is None or c['finish']>720 for c in cases),
             completed=len(completed),censored=len(cases)-len(completed))
    for p in [1,2,3]:
        cc=[c for c in cases if c['p']==p]
        out[f'P{p}_hit']=sum(c['finish'] is not None and c['finish']<=c['due'] for c in cc)
        out[f'P{p}_n']=len(cc)
    return out,el,dl

if __name__=='__main__':
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

The prompt supplies expected block arrivals and service supports, not realized traces, conditional densities, resource learning, correlation, demand seasonality, team memberships, staffing costs, or rework outcomes. It does not identify the true mean or tail delay, the joint probability that both branches are complex, or the steady-state arrival process. The simulation's numerical rankings are conditional on its explicit distribution, handover-occupancy, calendar-pause, and one-cohort assumptions. No statistical claim of real 90% SLA attainment is warranted.

A validation plan should proceed as follows:

1. **Validate the event semantics and calibrate.** Reconstruct several complete arrival cohorts including unfinished cases. Check active service separately from elapsed activity duration; verify break pauses, actual skill multipliers, handover staffing, team transitions, joins, and restart behavior. Estimate service distributions and priority/type dependence. Audit missing start/end events and clock synchronization. Check conformance to skill eligibility and diagnostic-before-Validation rules.
2. **Backtest with paired workloads.** Replay historical arrivals for all policies, with warm-up and continuing next-day arrivals so inherited backlog cannot disappear into an empty future. Use common service draws across policies, hundreds of seeds where appropriate, and bootstrap whole days or incident clusters. Include the alternate U2 volume interpretation, correlated tails, burst arrivals, handovers as unstaffed delay, and slower/faster conditional service means. Report SLA differences and their uncertainty, p95, individual utilization, overtime, oldest P3 age, and time to recover the backlog after each shock.
3. **Check feasibility before live testing.** Solve or enumerate a skill/calendar capacity assignment with the base 90% planning condition and bounded overtime. Include common-stage and transfer workload. Evaluate concrete extra-coverage rosters. A failure to find capacity is a staffing finding, not evidence that workers should be dispatched more aggressively.
4. **Run shadow dispatch first.** Produce auditable alternative assignment, parallelization, and preemption decisions without changing live work. Compare forecast error, operator feasibility, likely handovers, and predicted P1 risk. Test the software on overlapping branches and a once-preempted victim case before trusting its clocks or reports.
5. **Pilot guarded pooling in a limited staffed window.** Use a P1 rescue owner, retain operator override, prohibit unapproved fairness/overtime exceptions, and start with P2/P3 routing changes. Randomize independent incident pools or sufficiently long policy periods, accounting for queue carryover; case-by-case randomization in one shared queue causes interference. Keep P1 rescue rules common initially, acknowledging that this limits causal conclusions about P1 policy differences. Expand to P1 only after the safeguards and capacity are demonstrated.
6. **Predefine stop and expansion criteria.** Stop a pilot period for an unexplained P1 forecast breach, qualification/calendar violation, unauthorized repeated preemption, or exhaustion of the overtime/fairness budget. Review these separately from unavoidable long-tail misses. Expand only after a sufficiently sized sample supports the desired priority attainment and resource constraints; do not infer success from a few P1 cases or a smaller average delay. Retain all overrides in the analysis.
