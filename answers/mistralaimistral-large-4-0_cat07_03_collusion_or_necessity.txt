# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Pattern A — Fast emergency approvals (Buyer_CarlaApprover_Gita; Buyer_DanManager_Hugo)**

- Benign 1: Designed emergency/rush channel. Carla is specialized in emergency medical supplies; Gita is the delegated approver for urgent cases; Dan handles weekend facilities repairs with Hugo as exception manager. The 8–10 minute approvals may reflect a pre-authorized fast lane for genuine emergencies. *Support:* policy documents describing emergency procurement; incident/clinical urgency tickets linked to cases; approvals concentrated on emergency_flag=True with high priority; retrospective justifications eventually completed. *Refute:* emergency flags on low-risk categories or vendors; approvals without linked urgency evidence; justifications missing, late, or boilerplate.
- Benign 2: Data-quality / shared-account attribution. "Approver_Gita" is a shared account; entered_by shows assistants; org:resource may not reflect the actual human approver. *Support:* access logs showing distinct human credentials; delegation records; corrected mapping showing different approvers. *Refute:* access logs showing Gita's own credentials used at all hours; no delegation records; entered_by matches approved_by after correction.
- Benign 3: Informal specialization. A stable buyer-approver pairing developed for efficiency in a small team. *Support:* consistent pairing across routine and emergency cases; no rule against pairing; low amounts in the routine subset. *Refute:* pairing used to route high-risk cases around controls; amounts escalate within the pair.

**Pattern B — Same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification (Manager_Hugo, Approver_Gita); late justifications**

- Benign 1: Batch data entry after quarter close. Justifications were performed contemporaneously but entered later from a spreadsheet (quarter_close_upload); identical timestamps are a migration artifact. *Support:* source spreadsheet metadata with earlier creation dates; email trails; manager sign-off; original timestamps in the spreadsheet. *Refute:* no contemporaneous evidence; justifications are generic; same person both overrode and justified with no independent review.
- Benign 2: Role design / policy ambiguity. The policy may allow the exception owner (Manager_Hugo) to record the justification, or the "different human resource" requirement may apply only to PO approval, not to the override-justification pair. *Support:* policy text and delegation matrix showing Hugo as exception owner; documented interpretation. *Refute:* normative rule explicitly requires a different human resource for retrospective justification; no documented exception.

**Pattern C — VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim: bank-detail change + payment approval (fast, manual_upload, V-77x concentration, four-eyes violation)**

- Benign 1: Legitimate vendor banking-platform migration. V-77x recently changed banking platform; several bank-detail changes were legitimate mass-maintenance operations, but the linkage between the maintenance ticket and the procurement case is missing in the event log. *Support:* vendor migration project records; change tickets matching the bank changes; bank confirmation letters from the vendor; callback verification logs; no change to beneficiary account for most cases. *Refute:* bank details changed to accounts not matching vendor-confirmed details; payments to changed accounts; migration tickets missing or not matching.
- Benign 2: Role design / small-team coverage. In some regions, vendor-master maintenance and AP payment approval are performed by a small team; the four-eyes rule may not have been formally extended to the bank-change + payment-approval pair. *Support:* policy documents showing rule scope; compensating controls (vendor callback, dual authorization for bank changes, payment-run review). *Refute:* rule explicitly covers the pair; no callback or dual-authorization records; same person both changed bank details and approved payment.
- Benign 3: Manual upload due to system limitation. manual_upload was used because the ERP could not process urgent vendor updates; the high manual share reflects a system constraint, not intent. *Support:* system outage/change records; batch upload logs; supervisor approval of manual process; manual uploads not concentrated in high-risk vendors. *Refute:* manual uploads concentrated in V-77x and timed immediately before payment approvals.

**Pattern D — Subcontracting loops (GitaCarlaGita; HugoDanHugo; LenaKimLena)**

- Benign 1: Clarification/correction loops. The approver returns the case to the buyer for clarification or correction, then re-approves; normal in emergency procurement and after approval comments. *Support:* case notes showing changed fields between events; email threads; substantive differences between first and second approval. *Refute:* loops with no substantive change; repeated same-day cycles; approvals after minimal delay with no review evidence.
- Benign 2: Automated exception feedback (Bot_MatchAP_KimBot_Match). System-driven exception handling, not human collusion. *Support:* bot logs; exception reason codes; automated re-matching after correction. *Refute:* human overrides of bot exceptions without reason codes; overrides followed by payment release.

**Pattern E — Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by same person (VendorMaster_Lena, VendorMaster_Marc)**

- Benign 1: Role design. The vendor-master team is responsible for both onboarding checks and bank-detail maintenance; the four-eyes rule may not cover this pair. *Support:* role descriptions; policy scope; segregation between vendor-master and payment approval (the critical control). *Refute:* same person also approves payments; no compensating controls.
- Benign 2: Separation from payment approval. Even if the same person checks and changes bank details, the payment approval is performed by a different person (AP), preserving the key control. *Support:* cases where the AP approver differs from the vendor-master resource; payment-run review. *Refute:* cases where the same person also approves payment (the LenaKim subset).

**Pattern F — Shared-account / mapping problems (Approver_Gita shared account; Bot_PO/human same org:resource in legacy_erp)**

- Benign 1: Data-quality defect. Shared accounts and mapping-table errors are known IT issues; they distort attribution but do not imply misconduct. *Support:* IT tickets for shared-account remediation; corrected mapping tables; access logs showing distinct humans. *Refute:* shared account used to obscure who approved; no remediation plan; approvals at unusual hours by the shared account.
- Benign 2: Legacy system limitation. legacy_erp mapped bot and human to the same org:resource; the apparent same-person PO creation/approval is an artifact. *Support:* mapping table documentation; corrected data showing distinct resources; source-system logs. *Refute:* corrected data still shows same human; no mapping error found.

**Pattern G — Routine high co-working pairs (Buyer_AlineApprover_Elena; Buyer_BorisApprover_Felix)**

- Benign 1: Informal specialization / stable team pairing. High co-working frequency alone is not suspicious; these pairs show low emergency, low high-amount, and low manual shares. *Support:* team structure; consistent with workload table; no rule against pairing. *Refute:* pairing shifts to high-risk attributes (high amounts, emergency flags, manual source) without explanation.
- Benign 2: Workload balancing. Aline and Elena are high-volume resources; the pairing reflects queue assignment, not collusion. *Support:* case assignment logs; queue configuration. *Refute:* cases routed to the pair outside normal queue rules.

**Pattern H — Temp_Nina  Buyer_Carla (end-of-quarter backlog)**

- Benign 1: Temporary surge support. Temp_Nina was brought in for end-of-quarter data entry; the 4-minute handoff reflects queue processing, not collusion. *Support:* access provisioning records; supervisor review; limited permissions (data entry only). *Refute:* Temp_Nina had approval rights; entries concentrated in emergency cases without review; access outside provisioned period.
- Benign 2: Data-quality / system-generated assignment. The "Assign Buyer" event may be system-driven assignment to Carla's queue. *Support:* assignment rules; system logs. *Refute:* manual assignment by Temp_Nina to Carla for high-risk cases.

import pandas as pd

# Four-eyes violations: same human resource performs both activities in a case.

# Per-case activity-pair durations by resource pair
def pair_duration(log, act_a, act_b):
    a = log[log["concept:name"] == act_a][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"]
    ].rename(columns={"time:timestamp": "t_a", "org:resource": "res_a"})
    b = log[log["concept:name"] == act_b][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"]
    ].rename(columns={"time:timestamp": "t_b", "org:resource": "res_b"})
    m = a.merge(b, on="case:concept:name")
    m["hours"] = (m["t_b"] - m["t_a"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return m

po_appr = pair_duration(df, "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order")
print(po_appr.groupby(["res_a", "res_b"])["hours"]
      .agg(["median", "count"]).sort_values("median"))

bank_pay = pair_duration(df, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval")
print(bank_pay.groupby(["res_a", "res_b"])["hours"]
      .agg(["median", "count"]).sort_values("median"))

- Access logs for Approver_Gita showing assistant delegation records and distinct human approvers; entered_by fields matching approved_by after correction.
- Emergency policy documents and incident/ticket records linking emergency_flag=True to genuine clinical or facilities urgency; approvals concentrated on documented emergencies.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review of the procurement-to-payment event log for the last quarter identified a mixture of control exceptions, plausible emergency-handling patterns, informal specialization, and material data-quality limitations. We did not find, and this report does not assert, evidence of fraud or collusion.

The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim cluster involving vendor family V-77x: nine cases where the same human resource changed vendor bank details and approved payment, a median interval of 17 minutes between the bank change and payment approval (versus a global median of 7.8 hours), 83% manual_upload source-system share, and 68% high-amount cases. This combination represents a control exception on the most sensitive activity pair in the procure-to-pay cycle and warrants immediate targeted review. It may, however, reflect a legitimate vendor banking-platform migration with missing case linkage, a small-team role design, or a system-driven manual process; these explanations must be tested before any conclusion is drawn.

Other patterns are more consistent with emergency handling or informal specialization. The Buyer_CarlaApprover_Gita and Buyer_DanManager_Hugo pairs show very fast approvals concentrated in emergency-flagged cases, but also late retrospective justifications and same-person override-and-justification events, indicating a weakened compensating control rather than proven misconduct. Routine high-volume pairs (Buyer_AlineApprover_Elena, Buyer_BorisApprover_Felix) appear consistent with stable specialization and low-risk attributes.

Data-quality limitations are material: a shared approver account, legacy_erp mapping errors, batch-entered justifications after quarter close, and missing migration-ticket linkage mean that org:resource attribution cannot be relied upon without correction. We recommend targeted follow-up on the LenaKim subgroup (bank confirmation letters, callback verification logs, access logs, payment register), remediation of shared accounts and mapping defects, and re-testing of four-eyes rules on corrected data. High co-working frequency alone was not treated as suspicious.
