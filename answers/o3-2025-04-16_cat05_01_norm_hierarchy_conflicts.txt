## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method  

Assumptions (explicit)  
A-1  Legal hierarchy: Board-approved global policy (GPIP-4) outranks any local document unless the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) issues a clause-A7 written exception.  
A-2  A jointly signed Incident Directive can override “ordinary” local workflow (per B7, C4) but cannot silently waive GPIP-4 controls unless it expressly cites A7 or the CRO later ratifies it.  
A-3  The CFO may interpret or waive Finance-specific approvals, but not Compliance clearance or segregation-of-duties, because no charter confers that power.  
A-4  Specific-over-general is applied only inside a single authority level. Example: ECD-2 (specific and temporary) can defeat DE-AAC-PROC-17 timing rules, but not GPIP-4 segregation.  
A-5  Later-in-time does not automatically trump higher-level policy.  
A-6  Emergency necessity is relevant only where an authoritative text links necessity to a waiver.  
A-7  Evidence hierarchy: (1) contemporaneous third-party artefacts (phone logs, signed PDFs), (2) system logs, (3) user statements. ERP timestamps during outage ON-44 are rebuttable by stronger evidence.  
A-8  Burden of proof rests on Northbridge to demonstrate that a required control occurred before supplier commitment when GPIP-4 makes timing essential (A1–A4).  
Decision method  
Step 1 – Identify the governing rule(s) at the moment of each activity.  
Step 2 – Test whether an authorised exception defeats the rule.  
Step 3 – Match reliable time-stamped evidence to the temporal requirement.  
Step 4 – Classify: COMPLIANT (all mandatory controls in time), JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (valid higher-order override), NONCOMPLIANT (control missing or late, no valid override), INDETERMINATE (evidence gap).

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations  

1. CFO email cannot waive any pre-commit Finance control (authority strictly interpretive).  
   • Changes P-505 and P-507 to NONCOMPLIANT.  
   • Credible because GPIP-4 fixes dual-approval and CFO is not “independent controller.”  
2. Incident Directive qualifies as an A7 CRO-level exception by corporate custom.  
   • Would cure Compliance and Finance timing for P-502 and P-503 (become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION).  
   • Weak: CRO was absent; GPIP-4 explicitly reserves A7 to CRO.  
3. “Critical sterilization-line lots” limited to 24-h needs (Internal Audit view).  
   • P-503 (backup-line pumps) and P-508 (pump) fall outside ECD scope, hence fully subject to SOP  remain NONCOMPLIANT with higher severity.  
   • Credible because wording “contain current contamination” implies immediacy.  
4. ERP timestamp is determinative despite outage note.  
   • P-505 commit time 18:06 => outside ECD window; no CFO waiver  NONCOMPLIANT.  
   • Weak: phone call and supplier acknowledgment are better contemporaneous evidence.  
5. Incident Command member is de facto budget owner during emergency.  
   • Would satisfy R2 for P-503, shifting result to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.  
   • Weak: charter lacks explicit budget authority; GPIP-4 requires “independent” owner.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification  

Proposed clause to insert after GPIP-4 A7  

“A7-bis  Incident Directives (ID) may temporarily override local operating procedures for scope-limited emergency response.  
(a) IDs shall:  
   i.  be countersigned by the CRO or, if unavailable, by the CFO plus General Counsel;  
   ii. state whether Finance, Compliance, and segregation controls are waived or remain intact;  
   iii. define objective scope and an expiry date/time;  
   iv. be lodged in the controlled policy repository within four hours.  
(b) Controls listed in A1–A4 remain in force unless an ID explicitly cites and waives them.  
(c) Any commitment above EUR 150,000 during an ID still requires Finance Controller and Legal clearance.  
(d) Post-incident, all ID-based purchases must be reviewed within 5 working days; missing approvals may be mitigated but not back-dated.  
Anti-normalisation safeguard: Consecutive IDs for the same process gap within 12 months trigger mandatory CRO review and board notification.”
