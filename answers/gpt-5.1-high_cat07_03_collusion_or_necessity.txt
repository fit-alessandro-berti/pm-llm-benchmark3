# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## 2.1 Emergency purchase channel (Carla / Gita and Dan / Hugo)

- **Benign explanation 1 – formal emergency lane**: The organization may have a documented emergency process where designated buyers and approvers fast-track urgent medical and repair spend, including temporary relaxation or interpretation of four-eyes for predefined catalog or framework-contract orders.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Emergency SOPs referencing `emergency_flag`, named roles for Buy er_Carla, Buyer_Dan, Approver_Gita, Manager_Hugo; alignment between `emergency_flag=True`, purchase_category, contract_type, and incident/maintenance tickets; conformance checks showing timely `Retrospective Justification` by someone else in most cases.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Numerous fast approvals where `emergency_flag=False` or purchase_category does not fit an emergency; missing or generic retrospective documentation; approvals outside normal working patterns without associated incidents.
- **Benign explanation 2 – specialization and pre-agreement**: Carla and Dan may be specialists executing pre-negotiated arrangements (framework agreements, standing orders) where commercial risk is already mitigated and approvers mainly check budget and coding.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Contract repository showing long-term agreements tied to these cases; limited price negotiation steps; consistent, modest variance in unit prices; approvals often within policy thresholds.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Frequent one-off, high-amount suppliers without framework contracts; large unexplained price dispersion; repeated overrides of approval limits.

## 2.2 Vendor family V-77x bank-change / payment cluster (Lena / Kim)

- **Benign explanation 1 – banking-platform migration / mass maintenance**: The log already notes a banking-platform change for vendor family V-77x; concentrated `Change Vendor Bank Details` and `Payment Approval` events could reflect a controlled, time-bound remediation project executed by specialized staff.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Change-management tickets linking V-77x cases to an approved migration project; project documentation describing use of `manual_upload`; reconciliation logs showing old vs new bank accounts with sign-off; clustered timestamps aligned with migration windows.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Scattered timing unrelated to any formal project; absence of tickets or approvals; inconsistent or frequently changing destination accounts; lack of independent review of sample bank changes.
- **Benign explanation 2 – risk-based centralization of higher-risk suppliers**: AP_Kim is described as working on higher-risk suppliers; pairing with VendorMaster_Lena may be an intentional control to keep sensitive vendors with a trained team.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Policy or RACI assigning V-77x to a specialist team; evidence that other high-risk vendors are similarly centralized; documented secondary checks (e.g., call-backs) outside the core ERP.
  - **Evidence refuting**: V-77x treated differently from peers without justification; no sign of periodic independent reviews; overrides of standard controls justified only by “urgency” or business pressure.

## 2.3 Same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification

- **Benign explanation 1 – bulk back-filling from spreadsheets**: 12 of 17 same-person justifications share identical timestamps and are sourced from `quarter_close_upload`, consistent with a clerk importing existing justifications from spreadsheets under a single user ID.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Archived spreadsheets or ticketing records containing the original justifications with diverse authors; upload logs mapping each row to a different manager; evidence that the uploader is an administrative role rather than an approver.
  - **Evidence refuting**: No underlying documentation predating the upload; narrative fields obviously written at upload time rather than contemporaneously; uploads performed by the same approver who also executed the override.
- **Benign explanation 2 – proxy data entry “on behalf of” approvers**: A back-office user may enter justifications for multiple managers, but the process failed to capture the true justifier in `org:resource`.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Workflow descriptions or service-desk tickets describing proxy entry; fields such as `entered_by` showing an assistant while `approved_by` points to the real approver; access logs confirming multiple managers instructed the assistant to document their decisions.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Access logs or emails show approvers relying on the proxy to both decide and document; repeated patterns where justification fields are very generic or copy-pasted across unrelated cases.

## 2.4 Subcontracting loops and tight collaboration patterns

- **Benign explanation 1 – iterative clarification and correction**: Patterns such as Gita→Carla→Gita and Hugo→Dan→Hugo can reflect questions, document corrections, and re-submissions typical for urgent or complex cases.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Case notes or comments referring to missing documents, coding corrections, or clarifications; successive events with different activities (e.g., `Negotiate Terms`, `Change Vendor Bank Details`) rather than pure “approve–approve” loops; eventual involvement of additional resources for final sign-off.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Loops dominated by minimal activity changes (e.g., quick re-approvals) without new information; approvals consistently happening within minutes with no supporting documentation changes.
- **Benign explanation 2 – supervision and training**: Senior staff may temporarily re-check work done by juniors, generating apparent subcontracting patterns without malicious intent.
  - **Evidence supporting**: HR or organizational charts showing mentoring relationships; notes in cases labeled as “training” or “shadowing”; decreasing frequency of loops over time as juniors become autonomous.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Loops remain stable over time with no expansion of involved staff; same individuals retain exclusive control over high-value cases despite apparent training justification.

## 2.5 Manual / legacy source systems and shared accounts

- **Benign explanation 1 – technical limitations and historical design**: Shared accounts (e.g., `Approver_Gita`) and legacy mappings between Bot_PO and human approvers may be remnants of older system designs, not attempts to hide identities.
  - **Evidence supporting**: System documentation explaining shared IDs; detailed `entered_by` / `approved_by` fields and access logs that still allow attribution to natural persons; migration plans to phase out shared accounts.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Weak or missing access logs; inconsistent linking between shared IDs and actual staff; resistance to remediating shared accounts despite known control gaps.
- **Benign explanation 2 – special handling channels for exceptions**: `manual_upload`, `legacy_erp`, and `quarter_close_upload` channels may have been created to process backlogs, corrections, or migrations under time pressure.
  - **Evidence supporting**: Change requests establishing these channels for limited periods; monitoring dashboards or reconciliations specific to manual uploads; clear closure dates or decommissioning plans.
  - **Evidence refuting**: Manual or legacy channels used indefinitely for normal business; high-value or unusual vendors routed disproportionately through them without compensating controls or review.

# 3. Case prioritization

## High-priority case clusters

- **H1 – V-77x bank-detail change and payment cases (Lena / Kim, manual_upload)**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Combines four-eyes violations, sensitive activity pair (`Change Vendor Bank Details` + `Payment Approval`), high vendor concentration, high share of high-amount cases, short approval delays, and strong reliance on `manual_upload`. This pattern is compatible with collusion but also with centralized migration; either way, it represents a critical control exposure.  
  - **First checks**: Retrieve vendor master change tickets, bank confirmation evidence, and payment remittance data; compare `vendor_bank_account` before/after; review `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and access logs for VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim; reconcile each V-77x payment to underlying contracts and invoices; confirm existence of independent post-migration review.

- **H2 – Four-eyes violations on bank details and payments across all vendors**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Even outside V-77x, any case where the same human both maintains bank details and approves payments is high risk, regardless of amount_band. These cases directly violate the normative rule and are relatively few, so they are practical for full-file review.  
  - **First checks**: For each violating case, inspect change logs, payment approvals, invoice images, and supporting documentation; check whether the same natural person (not just account) performed both steps via `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and access-log identities; verify whether compensating reviews were performed after the fact.

- **H3 – Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification by same person (non-quarter_close_upload)**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Subset of cases where same-resource justification cannot easily be explained by spreadsheet migration (i.e., `source_system` ≠ `quarter_close_upload` or timestamps not perfectly aligned). These are the most plausible instances of real-time self-justification.  
  - **First checks**: Retrieve emergency override rationale, incident or ticket references, and justification texts; verify timing between override and justification (expect ≤3 working days); confirm involvement of an independent approver elsewhere in the case; examine any deviations from documented emergency procedures.

## Medium-priority case clusters

- **M1 – Emergency fast-approval POs (Carla / Gita and Dan / Hugo)**  
  - **Reason for priority**: High share of `emergency_flag=True` and critical purchase categories explains fast approvals, but the combination of speed, repeated subcontracting loops, and occasional four-eyes violations warrants assurance that the emergency lane is well-controlled rather than ad hoc.  
  - **First checks**: Sample cases by amount_band and purchase_category; inspect emergency SOPs, incident/repair tickets, and `Retrospective Justification` content; compare timing of override, approval, goods receipt, and justification; confirm that non-emergency cases in these pairs behave more like the global median.

- **M2 – Manual_upload and legacy_erp cases with high amounts outside V-77x**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Manual or legacy source_systems are more error-prone; high-amount cases routed through them could indicate process workarounds or system gaps. Even if no four-eyes violation is logged, data-quality issues could hide real violations.  
  - **First checks**: Filter to `source_system in {"manual_upload", "legacy_erp"}` and high amount_band; analyse whether Bot_PO vs human approver mappings are correct; review reconciliation or monitoring reports; inspect a sample of invoices and approvals for completeness and timeliness.

- **M3 – VendorMaster_Lena / AP_Kim subcontracting loops (Lena→Kim→Lena)**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Repeated back-and-forth around high-risk activities (bank changes, exceptional payments) may signal unclear roles or over-concentration of knowledge. While compatible with remediation work, it increases the chance that errors go undetected.  
  - **First checks**: Examine activity sequences within these loops; check whether corrections are documented; look for escalation to independent reviewers; compare loop behaviour in emergency vs non-emergency and high vs low amount_band cases.

## Low-priority or monitoring-only clusters

- **L1 – Routine Aline / Elena and Boris / Felix flows**  
  - **Reason for priority**: High event volumes but low four-eyes violation rates, low vendor concentration, and near-baseline manual source-system use. High co-working frequency alone is not suspicious and appears consistent with standard buyer–approver teams.  
  - **First checks**: Use these pairs mainly as a baseline for normal timing, vendor diversity, and control performance; perform limited sampling on high-amount or unusual-category cases only.

- **L2 – VendorMaster_Marc / AP_Iris cluster**  
  - **Reason for priority**: Medium vendor concentration and moderate manual source_system share, but without the same level of four-eyes violations or emergency patterns observed in V-77x. This looks more like ordinary onboarding and payment processing.  
  - **First checks**: Periodic sample-based review of bank changes and payments; confirm that standard verification procedures are followed; monitor for any drift towards higher concentration or increasing manual uploads.

## 4.5 Declarative / log-skeleton checks for Retrospective Justification

Conceptually, we want a rule: for any case with `Emergency Override`, there must be a `Retrospective Justification` by a different `org:resource` within 3 working days.

```python
# Restrict to cases with Emergency Override
e_cases_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "concept:name", ["Emergency Override"], level="event", retain=True
)

# Discover a DECLARE or log-skeleton model as baseline
declare_model = pm4py.discover_declare(e_cases_log)
declare_conf = pm4py.conformance_declare(e_cases_log, declare_model)

# Alternatively, use log skeleton
skeleton_model = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(e_cases_log)
skeleton_conf = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(e_cases_log, skeleton_model)
```

In practice, a custom check can combine PM4Py features with pandas:

```python
import pandas as pd

df_e = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(e_cases_log)

def check_retrospective(row_group):
    overrides = row_group[row_group["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    justifs = row_group[row_group["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
    if overrides.empty:
        return False
    if justifs.empty:
        return True  # violation: missing justification
    # Minimum time difference and different-resource condition
    min_diff = (justifs["time:timestamp"].min() - overrides["time:timestamp"].min()).days
    different_res = not set(overrides["org:resource"]).intersection(justifs["org:resource"])
    return (min_diff > 3) or (not different_res)

violations_by_case = (
    df_e.groupby("case:concept:name").apply(check_retrospective)
)
```

This flags cases where justifications are missing, late, or performed by the same resource.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- **Linkage between bank changes and problematic payments**: Cases where V-77x bank changes are followed by payments to accounts not confirmed by independent vendor communication, chargebacks, or disputes; mismatches between vendor legal names and account holders in external banking data.
- **Identity-level concentration of power**: Access logs or HR mappings showing that the same natural person (rather than just the same account) repeatedly performs both bank-detail changes and payment approvals across multiple vendors or regions, especially via `manual_upload`.
- **Intentional circumvention of four-eyes controls**: Emails, chat messages, or ticket notes instructing staff to “push through” payments or justifications via shared accounts or manual channels specifically to avoid delays or additional approvers.
- **Questionable retrospective justifications**: Boilerplate or copy-pasted justifications, created long after events without supporting tickets; evidence that the same person both requested and justified emergency overrides in their own interest.
- **Anomalous vendor patterns**: Concentration of high-margin, low-competition purchases on a small set of vendors linked to the high-risk resource pairs; repeated one-off vendors with similar bank accounts or contact details.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- **Robust documentation of emergency and migration processes**: Clear, approved procedures for emergency handling and V-77x banking-platform migration, including named roles, four-eyes exceptions, and compensating controls, with sample cases aligning to these rules.
- **Strong attribution and access controls**: Detailed `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and system access logs that unambiguously link shared accounts (e.g., `Approver_Gita`) and Bot_PO mappings to multiple independent individuals with appropriate segregation-of-duties.
- **Independent reviews and reconciliations**: Evidence that high-risk clusters (V-77x, manual_upload, emergency overrides) are subject to periodic independent review, with documented sample testing, issue tracking, and remediation.
- **Consistent vendor master governance**: For V-77x and similar vendors, presence of complete onboarding files, bank verification evidence, and periodic re-confirmation; audit trails showing that detected four-eyes “violations” were approved as controlled exceptions.
- **Improved data-quality mappings**: Demonstrable corrections to legacy_erp and shared-account mappings, with before/after analyses showing that many earlier apparent violations were artefacts of merging bot and human identities in `org:resource`.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The analysis of the procurement-to-payment event log identifies a small number of concentrated risk clusters against an otherwise broadly routine process. Most patterns observed in the Buyer_Carla / Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan / Manager_Hugo pairings appear consistent with a structured emergency-handling channel and informal specialization in urgent medical and repair-related purchases. While these channels exhibit faster-than-average approvals and occasional four-eyes exceptions, they are strongly associated with `emergency_flag=True` and do not, on their own, support a conclusion of misconduct. Instead, they indicate the need to formalize emergency procedures, clarify documentation requirements, and confirm that retrospective justifications are performed independently and within the prescribed timeframe.

The most concerning subgroup is the combination of VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim for vendor family V-77x, where sensitive bank-detail changes, payment approvals, vendor concentration, high-value cases, and manual_upload processing coincide. This pattern could be explained by a legitimate banking-platform migration and risk-based centralization, but it also represents a material control weakness that warrants targeted review. Additional concern arises from four-eyes violations and same-person retrospective justifications in a subset of emergency cases, although data-quality issues (shared accounts, spreadsheet uploads, legacy mappings) limit the reliability of resource-level signals.

Accordingly, internal audit should prioritize detailed testing of V-77x bank-change and payment cases, followed by focused sampling of emergency overrides and manual/legacy source-system transactions. The report should emphasize that the current evidence indicates control exceptions and data-quality limitations rather than proven fraud or collusion, while recommending improvements to segregation-of-duties monitoring, documentation of emergency and migration processes, and remediation of shared-account and mapping issues.
