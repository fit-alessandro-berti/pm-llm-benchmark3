# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

- A “case” is identified by `case:concept:name`; events are sorted by `time:timestamp`.
- Activity labels are exactly the visible labels given in the scenario.
- The event log has no `risk_classification` attribute, no `emergency` attribute, and no explicit joint-authorization event.
- “Accepted” cases are recognized by the presence of `Activate Vendor`; “rejected” cases are recognized by `Reject Vendor`.
- In the implemented model, **R5 is interpreted strictly**: after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor`.
- R6 is treated as an external policy override that cannot be verified from the event log because its authorization is not logged.
- Temporal deviations are warnings, not automatic control-flow violations.

## Two possible resolutions of the R5/R6 conflict

### Resolution 1: Strict R5, no unlogged emergency override

`Obtain Legal Opinion` must be before `Activate Vendor` in every case containing `Sanctions Escalation`. The event log is the only conformance source, and unlogged authorizations are ignored.

**Compliance risk:** legitimate emergency activations will be flagged as violations unless the external authorization is manually added to the case before checking. Operations will see false positives.

### Resolution 2: R6 override with external authorization

`Activate Vendor` may occur before `Obtain Legal Opinion` only when an external joint authorization exists, and the legal opinion must arrive within 24 hours. Because the event log does not contain the authorization attribute, this cannot be checked automatically from the event log alone.

**Compliance risk:** unauthorized emergency activations could pass if the external authorization is not audited; the Legal department’s “under all circumstances” rule is relaxed.

**Chosen implemented resolution:** Resolution 1, strict R5. R6 is represented in the external policy layer, but without the `emergency_authorization` attribute the trace is not accepted under the strict model.

| R7 | DECLARE | `precedence(("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"))`. | Simple precedence constraint. | Also enforced structurally in the Petri net: escalation consumes a token produced by `Run Sanctions Screening`. |
| R8 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence(("Enhanced Due Diligence","Manager Approval"))` and `noncoexistence(("Compliance Approval","Manager Approval"))`. | “Should not co-occur” is non-coexistence. | Procedural XOR routing also prevents these pairs in normal cases. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## Compact formal description

The procedural backbone is an accepting Petri net. A trace is accepted iff there is a complete firing sequence from the initial marking `{start}` to the final marking `{closed}`.

The net represents:

1. `Receive Onboarding Request` starts every case.
2. A silent fork enables `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` concurrently.
3. `Classify Risk` is enabled only after all three checks have completed.
4. An optional clarification loop is encoded with places `clarification_open` and `clarification_wait`:

   - `Request Clarification` moves a token from `clarification_open` to `clarification_wait`.
   - `Receive Clarification` moves it back.
   - Final outcomes require `clarification_open`, so a pending clarification blocks activation/rejection.

5. Sanctions escalation is optional:

   - `Run Sanctions Screening` produces a token enabling either silent `No Escalation` or `Sanctions Escalation`.
   - If escalation occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must fire before activation or closing.

6. After `Classify Risk`, the case chooses one of:

   - reject route: `Reject Vendor -> Close Onboarding`;
   - low-risk route: `Manager Approval -> Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment -> Close Onboarding`;
   - high-risk route: `Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval -> Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment -> Close Onboarding`.

7. Post-activation behavior included before first release is `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor -> Verify Bank Account -> Release First Payment`. Changes after the first release are deliberately excluded.

## Explanation of modeling choices

- **Concurrency**: the silent `t_fork` transition creates separate tokens for the three checks, so any interleaving of `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` is allowed.
- **Choices**: the `Classify Risk` place feeds three mutually exclusive paths: reject, low-risk, or high-risk.
- **Clarification loops**: the places `clarification_open` and `clarification_wait` enforce alternating `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` cycles. Final outcomes require `clarification_open`, so a pending clarification blocks activation and rejection.
- **Terminal outcomes**: `Close Onboarding` can fire only after `Release First Payment` or after `Reject Vendor`, and any pending sanctions-legal token must be resolved.
- **Post-activation behavior**: the net permits `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor -> Verify Bank Account -> Release First Payment` before the first payment. Changes after first payment are deliberately excluded; those must be handled by the external data layer if they occur.

## DECLARE rule semantics

| Rule | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `init` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | Every case starts with `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `response` | `Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification` | Every clarification request is eventually followed by a clarification response. |
| `altresponse` | `Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification` | Between two requests, a response must occur. |
| `precedence` | `Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification` | Every response is preceded by a request. |
| `response` | `Sanctions Escalation -> Obtain Legal Opinion` | Every escalation is eventually followed by a legal opinion. |
| `precedence` | `Run Sanctions Screening -> Sanctions Escalation` | Escalation can occur only after sanctions screening. |
| `precedence` | `Classify Risk -> Manager Approval`, `Classify Risk -> Compliance Approval`, `Classify Risk -> Enhanced Due Diligence`, `Classify Risk -> Reject Vendor` | All final-route activities occur after classification. |
| `precedence` | `Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval` | Compliance approval is preceded by EDD. |
| `response` | `Manager Approval -> Activate Vendor` | Manager approval eventually leads to activation. |
| `response` | `Compliance Approval -> Activate Vendor` | Compliance approval eventually leads to activation. |
| `response` | `Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval` | EDD eventually leads to compliance approval. |
| `response` / `precedence` | `Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment` | Activation is followed by first payment; every first payment is preceded by activation. |
| `response` / `precedence` | `Release First Payment -> Close Onboarding` | First payment is followed by close; close is preceded by first payment. |
| `response` / `precedence` | `Reject Vendor -> Close Onboarding` | Rejection is followed by close; close is preceded by rejection. |
| `response` | `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor` | Every bank-details change is eventually followed by suspension. This is the partial activity-level part of R11. |
| `noncoexistence` | pairs | The two activities never occur in the same case. This is used for R1 and R8. |

I use `alternate response` rather than `chain response` because the clarification response need not be the immediately next event. I use `noncoexistence` rather than `nonsuccession` because R1/R8 forbid co-occurrence in both orders.

## Orientation and overlap

- `equivalence(A, B)` means `A` and `B` occur the same number of times in every conforming case.
- `always_before(A, B)` means when both `A` and `B` occur, `A` occurs before `B`.
- `always_after(A, B)` means when `A` occurs, `B` eventually occurs after it.
- `never_together(A, B)` means `A` and `B` never occur in the same case.
- `activ_freq[A]` is the set of allowed occurrence counts for `A`.
- `directly_follows(A, B)` means `B` can be the immediate successor of `A`.

This layer overlaps with the DECLARE layer. For example, `always_before(("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"))` duplicates the DECLARE precedence rule, and `never_together` duplicates `noncoexistence`. The main disagreement is under the R6 override: the strict rule `always_before(("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor"))` would no longer be valid if emergency activation before legal opinion were allowed.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

The values are in seconds:

- `48 * 3600 = 172800` seconds, standard deviation `86400` seconds.
- `12 * 3600 = 43200` seconds, standard deviation `21600` seconds.
- `72 * 3600 = 259200` seconds, standard deviation `129600` seconds.

Temporal deviations are warnings rather than automatic control-flow violations because elapsed time is normative, not descriptive of the allowed process structure. A late legal opinion or a late first payment may be caused by workload, waiting time, or external dependencies. R12 explicitly says these are “normative enough to flag but not automatically reject.”
