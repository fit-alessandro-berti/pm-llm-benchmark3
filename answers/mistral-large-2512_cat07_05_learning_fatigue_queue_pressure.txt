# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed patterns reflect **competing and interacting mechanisms** that cannot be reduced to a single cause. Here’s how they combine:

1. **Early-shift dynamics (positions 1–30):**
   - **Case-mix dominates**: The first 5 events are 41% high-priority and 0.72 complexity, reflecting carryover from prior shifts (especially night  early). Seniors selectively take complex cases early (batching), which inflates service times (18.8 min) and error rates (3.4%).
   - **Learning/warm-up**: Service time drops to 13.6 min by positions 16–30 as workers adapt, but error rates also fall (2.5%) because complexity declines (0.54). Juniors show the largest improvement (19.6  13.7 min), suggesting experience gaps matter early.

2. **Mid-shift stabilization (positions 31–50):**
   - **Queue pressure emerges**: WIP stabilizes (~31), and complexity is lowest (0.50). Service time plateaus (12.9 min) as workers optimize throughput, but error rates remain low (2.7%) because pressure is moderate and cases are simpler.
   - **Circadian effects**: Day shift workers are at peak alertness, but late-shift workers (e.g., night) are already fatigued, though this is masked by fewer cases.

3. **Late-shift deterioration (positions 51+):**
   - **Queue pressure accelerates**: WIP spikes to 88 by position 76+, driving a **speed-quality tradeoff**. Service time falls to 10.8 min (fastest), but errors rise to 7.9% because:
     - Workers rush to clear backlogs (especially day shift, ending with 75 WIP).
     - High WIP forces multitasking, increasing cognitive load.
     - Seniors buffer pressure better (4.9% errors) than juniors (12.4%), but all groups degrade.
   - **Fatigue compounds pressure**: Night shift’s late errors (5.1%) and slow service time (15.2 min) suggest circadian misalignment, but day shift’s late errors (7.5%) are higher, implying pressure > fatigue.
   - **Batching and workarounds**: Quality Checks are deferred, creating rework bursts. Juniors batch Document Review to clear queues, but this may reduce errors (sequential processing is efficient).

4. **Why service time falls while errors rise only under pressure:**
   - **Service time declines** due to:
     - Learning (early shift).
     - Simpler cases (mid-shift).
     - Queue pressure (late shift, especially high WIP).
   - **Errors rise only under high WIP** because:
     - Pressure forces speed, sacrificing accuracy.
     - Complexity and pressure interact (10.9% errors for high WIP + high complexity).
     - Fatigue exacerbates this, but pressure is the primary driver (e.g., day shift’s late errors despite peak alertness).

**Key insight**: The system is **not in equilibrium**. Early shifts inherit complexity, mid-shifts optimize, and late shifts degrade under pressure. Fatigue matters but is secondary to queue pressure and case-mix.

# 3. Proposed analyses

### **1. Within-resource comparisons**
- **Goal**: Isolate individual performance trends.
- **Method**:
  - For each resource, regress service time/error on sequence position, controlling for complexity, WIP, and time-of-day.
  - Compare slopes across experience levels (seniors vs. juniors).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  from pm4py.filtering import filter_event_attribute_values
  filtered_log = filter_event_attribute_values(log, "org:resource", ["Senior_A", "Junior_H"])
  pm4py.get_service_time(filtered_log)  # Compare distributions
  ```

### **2. Within-shift position effects**
- **Goal**: Test if sequence position predicts performance beyond time-of-day.
- **Method**:
  - Bin sequence positions (e.g., 1–5, 6–15) and compare service time/error, stratified by WIP/complexity.
  - Use shift fixed effects to control for circadian rhythms.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
  log["sequence_bin"] = pd.cut(log["sequence_position_within_shift"], bins=[0,5,15,30,50,75,1000])
  pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, "sequence_bin", ["1-5", "76+"])  # Compare bins
  ```

### **3. WIP/queue stratification**
- **Goal**: Quantify queue pressure’s impact.
- **Method**:
  - Stratify by WIP bins (<30, 30–70, >70) and regress service time/error on WIP, controlling for complexity.
  - Test interaction: `WIP × complexity`.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log["wip_bin"] = pd.cut(log["wip_at_start"], bins=[0,30,70,1000])
  pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "wip_bin", ["(70, 1000]"])  # High WIP cases
  ```

### **4. Complexity adjustment**
- **Goal**: Separate complexity effects from other mechanisms.
- **Method**:
  - Standardize service time/error by complexity (e.g., residuals from regressing service time on complexity).
  - Compare adjusted metrics across sequence positions/WIP bins.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
  log["adjusted_service_time"] = log["service_time"] - log["case_complexity_score"] * beta  # From regression
  ```

### **5. Shift and time-of-day controls**
- **Goal**: Disentangle circadian effects from shift position.
- **Method**:
  - Regress service time/error on:
    - Time-of-day (hour).
    - Sequence position.
    - Shift fixed effects.
  - Compare same sequence positions across shifts (e.g., first 20 events of early vs. night).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python

  pm4py.filter_time_range(log, "06:00:00", "14:00:00")  # Early shift
  ```

### **6. Batching detection**
- **Goal**: Identify and quantify batching.
- **Method**:
  - Use `pm4py.discover_batches` to detect sequential events with short service times.
  - Compare error rates for batched vs. non-batched events.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python

  pm4py.view_dotted_chart(log, format="svg", attributes=["org:resource"])  # Visualize batches
  ```

### **7. Resource-experience interactions**
- **Goal**: Test if experience buffers pressure.
- **Method**:
  - Regress error rates on:
    - WIP.
    - Experience (months).
    - Interaction: `WIP × experience`.
  - Compare slopes for seniors vs. juniors.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log["experience_bin"] = pd.cut(log["resource_experience_months"], bins=[0,6,24,100])
  pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "experience_bin", ["(24, 100]"])  # Seniors
  ```

### **8. Rework/error downstream analysis**
- **Goal**: Trace errors to root causes.
- **Method**:
  - For cases with errors/rework, analyze:
    - Prior activities (e.g., Bot_Risk classifications).
    - Queue pressure at time of error.
    - Resource experience.
- **PM4Py**:

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

1. **Worker-level conclusions can be unfair**:
   - **Selection bias**: Seniors may self-select complex cases, making their error rates appear lower. Juniors may be assigned simpler cases, masking their true performance.
   - **Measurement error**: "Error" flags may reflect documentation issues (e.g., missing fields) rather than true mistakes. Rework may be due to upstream errors (e.g., Bot_Risk misclassifications).
   - **Queue pressure is management-created**: High WIP is often a result of staffing decisions, not worker effort. Penalizing workers for late-shift errors ignores systemic causes.

2. **Automation quality**:
   - Bot_Risk’s errors are invisible unless corrected by humans. Blaming workers for correcting automation masks the root cause (poor bot design).
   - Automation may reduce fatigue for simple cases but increase cognitive load when corrections are needed.

3. **Punitive use of error rates**:
   - Error rates are **lagging indicators** of systemic issues (e.g., understaffing, poor triage). Punishing workers may increase stress and turnover without addressing root causes.
   - **Goodhart’s Law**: If error rates become targets, workers may avoid complex cases or underreport errors, degrading service quality.

4. **Batching and workarounds**:
   - Batching (e.g., Junior_H/I’s Document Review) may improve efficiency but is often invisible to managers. Punishing "short service times" could discourage beneficial batching.
   - Deferring quality checks may reduce late-shift rework but increase downstream errors.

5. **Circadian and fatigue effects**:
   - Night shift workers are inherently disadvantaged by circadian misalignment. Comparing their error rates to day shift workers is unfair without controlling for time-of-day.
   - Fatigue is **non-linear**: A worker may perform well for 6 hours but degrade rapidly in the 7th hour. Capping shifts may help, but rigid rules ignore individual variability.

**Key principle**: **Error rates are a property of the system, not the worker**. Interventions should focus on **system design** (e.g., WIP caps, automation quality) rather than individual performance.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

### **1. Service Time Trends**
- **Indicator**: Mean service time by sequence position within shift, stratified by WIP/complexity.
- **Visualization**: Line chart with sequence bins on x-axis, service time on y-axis, colored by WIP.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  pm4py.view_events_distribution_graph(log, "sequence_position_within_shift", "service_time", format="svg")
  ```

### **2. Error and Rework Rates**
- **Indicator**: Error rate and rework rate by sequence position, WIP, and resource group.
- **Visualization**: Stacked bar chart (sequence bins × error/rework rates) with tooltips for WIP/complexity.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log["error_rework"] = log["error_flag"] | log["rework_flag"]
  pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "error_rework", [True])
  ```

### **3. WIP and Queue Pressure**
- **Indicator**: Mean WIP at start of event, by time-of-day and shift.
- **Visualization**: Heatmap (hour × shift) with WIP as color intensity.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log["hour"] = log["time:timestamp"].dt.hour
  wip_by_hour = log.groupby(["hour", "shift_id"])["wip_at_start"].mean().unstack()
  ```

### **4. Complexity Mix**
- **Indicator**: Share of high-complexity cases by sequence position and shift.
- **Visualization**: Area chart (sequence bins × complexity share).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  log["complexity_bin"] = pd.cut(log["case_complexity_score"], bins=[0,0.5,1])
  pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, "complexity_bin", ["(0.5, 1]"])
  ```

### **5. Batching Indicators**
- **Indicator**: % of events in batches (e.g., 5 consecutive events with service time < 5 min).
- **Visualization**: Bar chart (activity × batching rate), colored by resource group.
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)
  log["is_batched"] = log["case:concept:name"].isin([b.case_id for b in batches])
  ```

### **6. Handover and Queue Ownership**
- **Indicator**: % of cases handed over between resources/shifts.
- **Visualization**: Sankey diagram (shift  shift or resource  resource).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)  # Look for handover paths
  ```

### **7. Worker-Load Fairness**
- **Indicator**: Gini coefficient of service time/error rate across resources.
- **Visualization**: Lorenz curve (cumulative % of resources × cumulative % of errors).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  resource_errors = log.groupby("org:resource")["error_flag"].mean()
  gini = 1 - 2 * np.trapz(np.sort(resource_errors), dx=1/len(resource_errors))
  ```

### **8. Automation Interaction**
- **Indicator**: % of Bot_Risk classifications corrected by humans, by WIP.
- **Visualization**: Line chart (WIP bins × correction rate).
- **PM4Py**:
  ```python
  bot_errors = log[(log["org:resource"] == "Bot_Risk") & (log["error_flag"] == True)]
  pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "org:resource", ["Bot_Risk"])
  ```

### **Dashboard Layout**
- **Top row**: Real-time alerts (e.g., WIP > 70, error rate spike).
- **Middle row**: Trends (service time, error rate, WIP by sequence position).
- **Bottom row**: Drill-downs (batching, automation, fairness).
- **Interactivity**: Filter by shift, resource group, or time range.
