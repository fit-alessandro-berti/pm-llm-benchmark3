Shared causal mechanisms between sites include the core queuing synchronization logic: parallelizing verification branches reduces critical path duration if and only if both tasks execute concurrently without starving downstream stages (`true_overlap -> waiting_before_assessment -> cycle_time`), and rework cycles compound processing delays and subsequent appeals (`rework -> cycle_time -> appeal`).

Site-specific mechanisms heavily govern Site B's operational reality. First, statutory receipt of physical originals (`originals_required -> waiting_before_assessment`) interposes an exogenous 18-hour delay before document checking can begin. Second, legacy IT architecture enforces exclusive case locking (`IT_case_lock -> true_overlap`), obstructing simultaneous multi-user processing. Third, downstream escalation to specialized legal review (`legal_capacity -> rework -> cycle_time`) induces recursive rework loops absent at Site A.

The primary effect modifiers are `originals_required`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, and `assessment_capacity`. Specifically, `originals_required` nullifies concurrency benefits for 62% of claims; `IT_case_lock` turns parallel assignments into conflicting handoffs; `role_specialization` prevents cross-functional load smoothing across pools; and fixed `assessment_capacity` (3 FTEs) causes queuing congestion when verification tasks complete synchronously.

Consequently, identical control-flow diagrams do not guarantee transportability. Control flow defines only formal permissibility, whereas actual cycle time is governed by operational capacity, resource boundaries, IT concurrency primitives, physical arrival latencies, and feedback loops. Transporting the AND-split without addressing Site B's structural moderators creates phantom concurrency, severe assessment queues, and amplified tail latency rather than genuine acceleration.

### Scenario Reasoning & Derivations

1. **Optimistic Structural Transfer:** Site A achieved a 9h median reduction, but at least 6h was attributable to non-concurrency co-interventions (+2 FTEs, -8 pp complexity, API upgrade, huddles). True concurrency saves at most $\min(\text{Identity}, \text{Document}) = \min(3.4, 8.1) = 3.4\text{h}$ of critical path. For digital claims (38%), up to 3.4h is saved. For originals (62%), if Identity Check (3.4h) is completed during the 18h mail delay, 3.4h of critical path is also eliminated once originals arrive. However, queuing friction and absence of added FTEs limit the whole-site median reduction to 3.0–5.0h, with p90 improving 6–10h. Confidence is low (20%) because it presumes zero IT lock contention and zero downstream queuing.

2. **Segmented Digital-Claim Transfer:** Targets the 38% digital claims for immediate concurrent verification. For this cohort, critical path compresses by 6–8h (saving service and queue handover time). For the 62% physical cohort, Identity Check is launched concurrently with postal transit, saving 2.5–3.5h of post-receipt dwell without generating idle Document Check WIP. Multiplying subgroup gains by volume shares ($0.38 \times 7\text{h} + 0.62 \times 3\text{h} \approx 4.5\text{h}$ theoretical, minus CONWIP buffering) yields a realistic site-wide median cycle reduction of 2.5–4.0h, and p90 reduction of 5.0–8.0h. Confidence is high (80%).

3. **Bottleneck-Shift Scenario (Average improves/stagnates, tail explodes):** Under blanket parallelization, 3,400 cases finish verification in synchronized bursts. By Kingman's formula ($V \propto \frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2} \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$), synchronizing arrivals dramatically inflates inter-arrival variance $c_a^2$ at Assessment. With only 3 Assessors and baseline utilization already high ($\rho \approx 0.85$), waiting time before Assessment surges from 20h to 35–45h. For fast digital cases, cycle time may decrease slightly (-1h), but for complex cases and physical claims, the queuing delay compounds, blowing out the p90 tail by +18 to +32h (reaching 128–142h). Confidence is high (75%) if management implements blanket parallelization without assessment controls.

4. **IT-Lock Failure Scenario:** If management mandates parallelization without modifying the CMS, reviewers opening a case encounter an active edit lock. Reviewers either wait (re-serializing the process) or review paper printouts offline and back-enter data. In process logs, timestamps cluster at shift-end (showing false overlap), but real cycle time is stagnant (0 to -1h) and coordination overhead adds 8–16h to p90. Confidence is 70%.

5. **Legal & Rework Congestion Scenario:** Accelerating verification routes the 40% legal cohort to the 2-FTE Legal Review pool faster. The legal queue explodes. Concurrently, 360 annual supplement requests return to Document Check and Assessment. Because these reworks lack priority lanes, they compete with incoming parallel claims, causing systemic gridlock. Median cycle time slows by +2 to +5h, and p90 inflates catastrophically by +25 to +45h. Confidence is 65%.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

### Process Specification

1. **Eligibility Rules:**
   - At `Register`, the CMS automatically classifies claims into two execution tracks based on statutory criteria:
     - **Track 1 (Digital Fast-Track, ~38%):** Claims where all required documentation is submitted digitally and statutory physical originals are exempt.
     - **Track 2 (Staged Physical Intake, ~62%):** Claims legally requiring original paper documents.
     - **Cross-cutting Flag:** High-Value / Public-Sector (~40%) flagged at intake for downstream statutory `Legal Review`.

2. **Concurrent Execution Rules:**
   - **Track 1:** The CMS simultaneously instantiates `Identity Check` and `Document Check` immediately after `Register`.
   - **Track 2:** The CMS instantiates `Identity Check` and initiates `Originals Receipt` tracking concurrently after `Register`. `Document Check` task creation is strictly withheld until physical delivery is confirmed.
   - For both tracks, `Identity Check` and document verification activities proceed concurrently.

3. **Statutory Prerequisites:**
   - *Originals Gate:* Under statutory mandate, `Document Check` for Track 2 claims cannot be assigned or executed until `Originals Receipt` is logged.
   - *Assessment Gate:* `Assessment` cannot be initiated until BOTH `Identity Check` and `Document Check` are recorded as completed.
   - *Legal Gate:* For flagged high-value/public claims, `Legal Review` must take place after `Assessment` before any final `Approve` or `Reject` decision.

4. **Queue and WIP Controls (CONWIP):**
   - Enforce hard personal WIP caps matching Site A's operational discipline:
     - Identity Reviewers: Maximum 4 active open cases per FTE (pool cap = 16).
     - Document Reviewers: Maximum 5 active open cases per FTE (pool cap = 35).
     - Assessors: Maximum 6 active open cases per FTE (pool cap = 18).
   - Dynamic Assessment Throttle: If the queue before Assessment exceeds 20 unassigned cases, the system temporarily throttles the release of new Track 1 verifications, preventing downstream batch flooding.
   - Daily Queue Huddles: Department supervisors conduct daily 15-minute cross-pool huddles to balance flow and expedite cases aging past 60 hours.

5. **Resource Responsibilities & Collaboration:**
   - Specialized pools are preserved (4 ID, 7 Doc, 3 Assessor, 2 Legal).
   - For Track 1 cases, an asynchronous "Verification Notes" pane allows ID and Doc reviewers to share flags (e.g., mismatched address, suspicious employer) in real time without blocking each other.

6. **Handling of Legal-Review and Supplement Loops:**
   - Standardized Pre-Legal Checklist: Assessors must verify all statutory exhibits against a 5-point compliance rubric prior to routing to Legal Review.
   - Fast-Track Supplement Lane: When Legal Review issues `Request Supplement`, the case is marked `PRIORITY_REWORK`. Upon receipt of supplemental documents, it routes directly to the original Document Reviewer and Assessor with priority dispatching, bypassing the general intake queue.

7. **IT Re-engineering & Logging Requirements:**
   - Split monolithic case lock into component-level locks: `Lock_IdentityTab` and `Lock_DocumentTab`. Both reviewers can update their respective sub-records concurrently.
   - Transactional Telemetry: Mandate immutable, automated timestamps for `task_assigned`, `task_started` (first record open), `task_suspended`, and `task_completed`. Prohibit manual retrospective entry.

### Separating Mechanism Failure from Implementation Failure

Separating mechanism failure from implementation failure requires isolating whether an observed lack of cycle-time reduction stems from invalid theoretical assumptions (mechanism failure) or execution friction in IT, queueing, and human behavior (implementation failure).

Process-mining telemetry provides the diagnostic discriminator:
1. **Implementation failure** occurs when intended parallel tasks fail to execute concurrently due to external frictions. Signatures include: low true overlap (<20%) despite parallel assignment, high CMS lock-contention exceptions, worker offline workarounds with clustered retrospective timestamps, or queue starvation where tasks sit unassigned. Here, the causal intervention was never truly delivered; the remedy is software refactoring, CONWIP caps, and workflow automation.
2. **Mechanism failure** occurs when parallelization is faithfully executed (true overlap >65%, clean granular timestamps, zero lock conflicts), yet system performance degrades or stagnates. Signatures include: severe post-verification queue spikes before Assessment (waiting time jumping from 20h to 30h+), increased rework from lack of cross-task visibility (rework climbing beyond 20%), or downstream legal review congestion. In this case, the theoretical model of independent concurrent verification fails under Site B's specialized capacity constraints and rework dynamics.

Stratified tracking of true execution overlap, queue waiting times, and handoff latencies rigorously untangles delivery fidelity from structural efficacy.

Using PM4Py (Process Trees, Accepting Petri Nets, and POWL).

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (

def run_pipeline():

    print("Site A After is Workflow Net:", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_a_after))

    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("3 & 4. Constructing Site B Candidate Model using POWL Classes")
    print("=" * 70)
    # Define primary workflow transitions
    t_register = Transition(label="Register")
    t_identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
    t_originals = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
    t_tau_digital = SilentTransition()
    t_document = Transition(label="Document Check")
    t_assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")

    # Document intake branch: Choice between physical originals receipt vs digital exemption
    intake_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_originals, t_tau_digital])

    # Core Verification Partial Order:
    # Assessment explicitly waits for BOTH Identity Check and Document Check
    verification_po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[t_register, t_identity, intake_choice, t_document, t_assessment]
    )
    verification_po.add_edge(t_register, t_identity)
    verification_po.add_edge(t_register, intake_choice)
    verification_po.add_edge(intake_choice, t_document)
    # Explicit synchronization edges:
    verification_po.add_edge(t_identity, t_assessment)
    verification_po.add_edge(t_document, t_assessment)

    # Legal Review Branch with Supplement Loop:
    # do: Legal Review; redo: Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment
    t_legal = Transition(label="Legal Review")
    t_supplement = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
    t_doc_redo = Transition(label="Document Check")
    t_ass_redo = Transition(label="Assessment")

    redo_supplement_seq = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_supplement, t_doc_redo, t_ass_redo])
    redo_supplement_seq.add_edge(t_supplement, t_doc_redo)
    redo_supplement_seq.add_edge(t_doc_redo, t_ass_redo)

    legal_supplement_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[t_legal, redo_supplement_seq]
    )

    # Optional Legal Review: XOR with silent bypass for non-legal claims
    t_tau_no_legal = SilentTransition()
    legal_branch = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[legal_supplement_loop, t_tau_no_legal]
    )

    # Final Determination (Approve / Reject) followed by Notify
    t_approve = Transition(label="Approve")
    t_reject = Transition(label="Reject")
    decision_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_approve, t_reject])
    t_notify = Transition(label="Notify")
    decision_seq = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[decision_choice, t_notify])
    decision_seq.add_edge(decision_choice, t_notify)

    # Assemble complete Site B candidate model
    site_b_powl = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[verification_po, legal_branch, decision_seq])
    site_b_powl.add_edge(verification_po, legal_branch)
    site_b_powl.add_edge(legal_branch, decision_seq)

    print("Site B POWL candidate constructed successfully.")

    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("5 & 6. Converting Site B POWL to Petri Net and Verifying Properties")
    print("=" * 70)
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_b_powl)

    is_wf_net = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_b)
    soundness_result, _ = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b)

    print("Site B Net is Workflow Net:", is_wf_net)
    print("Site B Net is Sound:", soundness_result)

    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("7. Playout Simulation (Generating Sample Execution Traces)")
    print("=" * 70)
    simulated_traces = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    print(f"Generated {len(simulated_traces)} simulation traces. Displaying 4 diverse samples:")
    for idx, trace in enumerate(simulated_traces[:4]):
        events = [ev["concept:name"] for ev in trace if ev["concept:name"] is not None]
        print(f"  Trace {idx + 1}: {' -> '.join(events)}")

    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("8. Critical Operational Constraints Not Encodable in Control-Flow Models")
    print("=" * 70)
    limitations = [
        "1. Data-Dependent Branching: Guard predicates (e.g., Claim_Value >= 50,000 OR Sector == 'Public') "
        "governing mandatory routing to Legal Review cannot be enforced in standard Petri nets/POWL.",
        "2. Resource Capacity Limits: Work net formalisms assume infinite concurrent firing capacity, "
        "ignoring Site B's hard resource bottlenecks (4 ID, 7 Doc, 3 Assessor, 2 Legal FTEs).",

        "4. Real-Time Durations and Dwell Latency: Stochastic temporal distributions—notably the 18h median "
        "postal delay for physical originals and queue dwell before Assessment—are absent from pure partial orders."
    ]
    for lim in limitations:
        print(lim)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    run_pipeline()

## 9. Recommendation

Recommendation: **conditional go**

Site B should proceed with a strictly bounded, conditional implementation rather than copying Site A’s blanket rollout.

**What should be transferred:**
Site B should transfer the architectural principle of concurrent verification for eligible cases, paired with Site A’s managerial controls: daily queue huddles, strict personal WIP limits (maximum 4–5 open verification tasks per reviewer), and granular task-level telemetry.

**What should not be copied:**
Site B must explicitly reject blanket task instantiation immediately after registration. Spawning `Document Check` across all cases would be disastrous because 62% of claims legally require physical originals (imposing a median 18-hour external postal latency), during which unexecutable tasks would clog queues and trigger case-lock deadlocks. Furthermore, Site B must not copy Site A’s uncoordinated handoffs, as Site B lacks cross-trained generalists and operates with a severe downstream bottleneck of only 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers.

**Which subgroup should enter first:**
The intervention must commence exclusively with **Stratum 1: Fully Digital, Standard-Complexity Claims (approximately 38% of volume)**. These claims possess immediate document availability, require no legal review, and exhibit minimal rework risk, closely mirroring Site A’s operational preconditions. Physical-original claims must remain in a sequential or staged intake track where `Identity Check` overlaps with postal transit, but `Document Check` is withheld until physical receipt.

**Expected benefits and risks:**
For the pilot subgroup, expected benefits include a 6–8 hour reduction in verification duration, translating to a whole-site median cycle time reduction of 2.5–4.0 hours without capital expenditure. The dominant risks are downstream queue explosion at Assessment (due to synchronized completions), reviewer lock contention in the legacy CMS, and increased rework if reviewers miss cross-domain fraud signals.

**Triggers for expansion, modification, or abandonment:**
- *Expansion trigger:* If the digital pilot demonstrates true overlap exceeding 65%, maintains rework below 18%, and keeps Assessment queue wait under 22 hours, expand parallelization to Stratum 2 (staged mail-overlap for physical originals).
- *Modification trigger:* If Assessment waiting time increases by >25% (exceeding 25 hours), implement a CONWIP release throttle feeding Assessment before continuing.
- *Abandonment trigger:* If case-lock contention exceeds 5% of transactions, rework spikes above 21%, or 90-day appeals rise above 6.0%, immediately abort the intervention and revert to baseline sequential routing.

## 10. Assumption register

1. IT Lock Granularity / Concurrent Multi-User Access — why needed: Concurrent verification requires two different specialists to access and update case records simultaneously; without component-level locking, parallelization cannot physically execute within the software — how to test: Conduct multi-user concurrency stress tests in the CMS staging environment where simulated reviewers simultaneously edit identity and document tabs on identical claims — consequence if false: Reviewers will be blocked by exclusive case locks, forcing them to work offline and retroactively back-enter timestamps, generating phantom concurrency in logs, severe coordination delays, and data synchronization loss.

2. Originals Transit Delay Exogeneity — why needed: Staging Document Check to await physical arrival relies on the 18-hour median transit window remaining independent of internal process restructuring — how to test: Track transit elapsed time (`timestamp_originals_received - timestamp_case_registered`) across cohorts before and during the pilot to confirm distributional invariance — consequence if false: If postal handling batches or slows under modified registration notifications, the critical path expands, negating any upstream concurrency gains.

3. Downstream Assessment Capacity Absorption — why needed: Upstream parallelization compresses the critical path only if downstream stages do not form an offsetting queue bottleneck due to synchronized batch arrivals — how to test: Monitor the median and 95th percentile waiting time in the Assessment input queue before and after releasing parallelized batches — consequence if false: The 20-hour baseline waiting time before Assessment will balloon to 30+ hours, completely wiping out the 3–4 hour savings achieved during verification and increasing total cycle time.

4. Independence of Verification Findings (Quality Invariance) — why needed: Executing Identity Check and Document Check simultaneously without prior visibility into the other check's preliminary findings must not impair detection of fraud or documentation anomalies — how to test: Compare rework rates, inter-reviewer discrepancy logs, and subsequent 90-day appeal rates between parallelized and sequential cases in the pilot — consequence if false: Missed discrepancies will escape verification, causing rework to surge from 17% to >22% and 90-day appeals to rise, generating costly downstream correction loops.

5. Legal Review Demand and Supplement Stationarity — why needed: Downstream legal workload and the 26.5% supplement request rate must remain stable to isolate the true treatment effect of verification parallelization — how to test: Monitor weekly counts of cases routed to Legal Review and the proportion of `Request Supplement` decisions across treatment and control arms — consequence if false: Faster case delivery could trigger defensive review practices by legal officers, increasing supplement requests and trapping cases in repetitive re-verification loops.

6. Event Logging Fidelity and Timestamp Accuracy — why needed: Valid causal inference and process-mining conformance checking require truthful event ordering and true task interval overlap measurement — how to test: Compare client-side user interface telemetry (active window focus and click events) against backend transactional database event timestamps for an audited 10% sample — consequence if false: Process mining will measure "phantom parallelism" (retroactive bulk logging) rather than genuine concurrent human execution, invalidating empirical evaluation.

7. Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (No Capacity Borrowing) — why needed: Processing parallelized treatment cases must not siphon reviewer attention or capacity away from control cases, which would artificially degrade control performance and bias effect estimates — how to test: Measure control case queue dwell times across periods with varying treatment case volumes, or use strictly partitioned reviewer sub-pools — consequence if false: Estimated cycle-time gains will be artificially inflated by cross-arm contamination and capacity borrowing, giving a false impression of universal scalability.

8. Digital Intake Channel Integrity and Stationarity — why needed: The economic viability and whole-organization cycle-time reduction of the segmented intervention rely on a reliable volume of purely digital claims (~38%) with complete initial documentation — how to test: Monitor monthly intake proportions and audit digital registrations for post-intake physical document requests — consequence if false: If claimants submit incomplete digital packages to game the faster track, Document Reviewers will stall awaiting missing papers, converting fast-track parallel cases into stalled exceptions.
