## 1. What is observed

**Availability.** `automation=1` in 12 of 40 branch-months: Alpha and Beta from April, Gamma from July, Delta and Epsilon never. The flag is absorbing and matches the stated dates.

**Actual use.** `automated_fast_path_pct` climbs 4 to 48 (Alpha), 6 to 50 (Beta), 4 to 32 (Gamma), still rising in August, so at most half of cases actually completed through the automated fast path. It already sits at 4 to 6 before availability, a floor that is not automation use.

**Selection.** The April adopters had the highest pre-period medians (Alpha 48.9h, Beta 45.9h) and largest volumes; the non-adopters had the lowest (Delta 34.5h, Epsilon 40.3h). Gamma adopted after a readiness pilot. Adoption correlates with baseline level and size.

**Co-occurring changes.** `escalation_pct` rises exactly 2.0pp in March in all five branches; `high_complexity_pct` rises exactly 5pp in May in all five; April arrivals jump 14.4% at Alpha and Beta versus about 4% elsewhere; August arrivals fall about 7.3% everywhere. Alpha loses 2 FTE in May, Beta gains 1, Gamma loses 1, Delta gains 1. Load per FTE at Alpha moves from 32.4 to 38.6.

**Measurement limits.** Cycle time exists only as branch-month median and p90; case-level distributions, fraud-desk waiting and queue states are absent. CSAT is a monthly mean with 20 to 45% response and no response counts. Rework and escalation are shares of a changing mix.

## 2. Causal estimands

Notation: `g` is a branch's adoption month (Alpha, Beta: April; Gamma: July), `e = t - g` is relative month, `D` is availability, `S` is realised automated-fast-path share.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | population | time horizon | outcome | unit of analysis | interpretation | identifiable from supplied data? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 (ITT-availability) | making the system available in a branch-month vs. not available, use left to the branch | the three adopting branches, at their realised adoption dates | e = 0 to 4 (Alpha, Beta), e = 0 to 1 (Gamma) | log `median_cycle_h` | branch-month, aggregated to ATT(g,e) | policy-relevant effect of switching a branch on, bundled with whatever adoption pace and local practice changes managers chose; this is the quantity leadership can actually buy | Partially. ATT(g,e) is computable against not-yet-treated and never-treated branches, but with 3 treated and 2 never-treated clusters there is no usable sampling variance; parallel trends and no-interference are assumptions, not tests. |
| E2 (dose response) | raising realised `automated_fast_path_pct` by 10pp, holding availability fixed | adopter-months with `D = 1` | contemporaneous month plus one-month lag | log `median_cycle_h`, log `p90_cycle_h` | branch-month | how much of the benefit tracks actual substitution rather than mere availability; the "treatment version" that scales | No, not credibly. Within adopters, `S` rises almost monotonically with calendar time, so its slope is collinear with learning, seasonality, the August de-load and manager practice change. Only bounds are available. |
| E3 (central tendency) | E1 contrast | adopters | e = 0 to 4 | `median_cycle_h`, level and log | branch-month median (a summary, not a case) | effect on the typical case, subject to the fact that a difference of medians is not the median of case-level differences | Partially, same caveats as E1; case-mix shifts alone can move a recorded median. |
| E4 (tail) | E1 contrast | adopters | e = 0 to 4, plus `p90/median` ratio as a shape measure | `p90_cycle_h`; secondary `p90_cycle_h / median_cycle_h` | branch-month quantile summary | whether automation protects or amplifies the slow tail; must be reported separately from E3 because a fast path can shorten the middle while lengthening escalated work | Partially, and more weakly than E3: the p90 is a single order statistic per branch-month with no dispersion information. |

| E8 (dynamic effect) | E1 contrast, indexed by e | adopters | e = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 separately | log `median_cycle_h`, `rework_pct` | branch-month, ATT(e) | separates a one-off level shift from a growing learning effect; essential because the intervention itself is changing (use doubles from month 0 to month 4) | Partially. e = 0 to 2 are supported by three cohorts; e = 3 and 4 rest on Alpha and Beta only and coincide with the August de-load. |

| E10 (spillover / interference) | non-adopter exposure to a fraud desk loaded by adopters vs. an unloaded desk | Delta and Epsilon, April to August | e-independent, calendar-indexed | log `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h` of non-adopters | branch-month | violation of SUTVA: if adopters congest the shared desk, the control branches are partly treated and every DiD above is inflated | No. There is no unexposed comparison group at all after March, and no fraud-desk waiting data. | E11 (capacity vs. speed) | E1 contrast, holding arrivals per FTE fixed | adopters | e = 0 to 4 | log `median_cycle_h` at counterfactual constant load | branch-month | distinguishes genuine capacity creation from a favourable load coincidence; Alpha absorbed +19% load per FTE versus March, +28% versus January, with no median deterioration, which is the strongest operational signal in the data | No. `load_per_fte` is partly post-treatment (routing was targeted at adopters), so conditioning on it blocks part of the effect and induces collider bias. Reportable only as an unadjusted observation. |

Two cross-cutting complications. **Interference:** the March fraud desk makes every branch's outcome a function of every other branch's automation, so "the effect of automating Alpha" is not defined independently of what Beta does; the estimands above should formally be read as effects under the observed rollout profile, not as effects of isolated single-branch adoption. **Treatment version:** availability at 25% use and availability at 50% use are different interventions, so E1 aggregated over e = 0 to 4 averages over versions of the treatment in proportions that a future rollout will not reproduce.

## 3. Identification strategy

Primary strategy is S1; S2 to S4 are alternatives run as cross-checks, not as substitutes.

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 (primary): cohort-by-period DiD in the Callaway–Sant'Anna spirit, ATT(g,t) with not-yet-treated and never-treated comparisons, aggregated to ATT(e)** | two adoption cohorts, `g = April` (Alpha, Beta) and `g = July` (Gamma); each treated month compared to that cohort's own month `g-1`; never-treated used throughout, not-yet-treated used only while still untreated | for `g = April`: Gamma (until June), Delta, Epsilon; for `g = July`: Delta, Epsilon only | none inside the estimator; outcomes in logs so common multiplicative shocks (May complexity, August de-load) difference out; case mix and load examined separately as post-treatment quantities | E1, E3, E4, E5, E6, E8 | (i) parallel trends between cohort `g` and its comparison set absent automation; (ii) no anticipation before `g`; (iii) no interference (violated by the shared fraud desk); (iv) absorbing treatment | placebo ATT(g,t) for pre-adoption months (obtained: +3.4 and +3.6 log points at e = -3, -2 for the April cohort; -1.6 and +1.8 for Gamma at e = -3, -2); the exact-2.0pp March escalation jump and exact-5pp May complexity jump confirm common shocks are genuinely common; use Gamma both as control and later as treated | Alpha's and Beta's favourable pre-trend (about -1.5 log points per month in the relative gap) is extrapolated as if it were zero, so the estimate inherits any real differential trend. Nothing in the design fixes the fraud-desk spillover, which inflates the estimate in an unknown amount. |
| **S2: adopter-specific synthetic control / synthetic DiD on the pre-adoption path** | one intervention date per adopter, treated as a separate case study | a weighted average of the other four branches chosen to match each adopter's pre-adoption level and slope | implicit, through the donor weights | E1, E3, E4 per branch | the weighted donor pool reproduces the adopter's untreated path; no donor is contaminated by the treatment | pre-period fit RMSPE; leave-one-donor-out; in-space placebos (assign a fake date to each never-treated branch) | Only four donors and three pre-periods for Alpha and Beta, so the pre-fit is nearly saturated and weights are not identified; in-space placebo inference has at most five permutations. Donors are contaminated after March. |
| **S3: interrupted time series with common-shock adjustment ("adjusted ITS")** | month-of-adoption break in level, plus optional slope change, per branch | the branch's own history, with the never-treated average deviation subtracted month by month as a shock proxy | never-treated average deviation from its own January to March base; season / de-load absorbed by that proxy | E1, E3, E4, E8 | the never-treated average is a valid proxy for common shocks; no unmodelled break coincides with adoption | compare raw ITS (no control) against adjusted ITS: the two answers diverge sharply (see section 5), which is itself the diagnostic | Adoption in April coincides with the routing campaign, and Gamma's July adoption sits one month before the common August de-load, so a break at `g` is not attributable to automation alone. |
| **S4: treatment-intensity (dose response) panel with branch and calendar-month fixed effects** | continuous `automated_fast_path_pct`, zero-to-low before availability | all 40 branch-months; identification comes from within-branch variation in use | branch FE, calendar-month FE; robustness with `high_complexity_pct` and `load_per_fte` (both post-treatment) | E2, and E8 indirectly | realised use is not caused by the outcome (no reverse causality from short queues to more fast-path completions) and is not a proxy for learning that would have happened anyway | compare the between-adopter dose gradient with the within-adopter gradient; split adopter-months at 34pp use; check the sign of the `load_per_fte` coefficient as a plausibility test (it comes out negative, i.e. implausible) | Use rises monotonically with time in every adopter, so the dose coefficient absorbs any smooth time trend in the treated branches. Conditioning on load or complexity conditions on mediators and colliders. |

**Why S1 is preferable here.** The data have exactly the structure that breaks a naive two-way fixed-effects regression: staggered adoption with two cohorts, effects that grow with relative time, and a period (July and August) in which the earlier cohort is already treated. In that setting the single `treated_now` coefficient is a weighted average of comparisons that include already-treated Alpha and Beta serving as controls for Gamma, with weights that can be negative and that mix effect heterogeneity into the estimate. Fitting it here returns -10.9 log points for the median, and the corresponding event-study bins return -9.1, -10.9 and -12.7, so the single number silently averages a growing effect. S1 avoids forbidden comparisons by construction: every treated cell is differenced against its own cohort's last untreated month and against branches that are still untreated at that moment. That matters concretely because Gamma is the most informative control for April to June and the second treated cohort thereafter; S1 uses it in both roles without contradiction. S1 also exposes rather than hides the two facts leadership most needs: the pre-adoption placebo terms are not zero for Alpha and Beta, and the effect profile by relative month is not flat. Finally, because it is a set of explicit two-period, two-group contrasts, each number can be traced back to eight cells of the CSV and audited by a manager, which matters more than efficiency when there are five clusters and inference is effectively unavailable.

## 4. Process-aware causal mechanisms

| mechanism | process artifact or metric affected | expected signature if beneficial | expected signature if harmful | evidence in supplied data | unresolved ambiguity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast-path substitution: `Register -> Automated Triage -> Resolve -> Close` replaces the manual fast path | `automated_fast_path_pct`, `median_cycle_h`, share of cases never touching a specialist | fast-path share rises and the median falls roughly in proportion to the substituted share, with no rise in escalation or rework | fast-path share rises while the median is flat or worse, meaning the automated path is not faster per case, only differently labelled | fast-path share rises 4 to 48 (Alpha), 6 to 50 (Beta), 4 to 32 (Gamma); relative to never-treated branches the median gap moves about -10 log points at all three | The observed median change is far smaller than a pure "half the cases now take the fast lane" story predicts, so either the automated path saves little per case or the remaining manual cases got slower. Branch-month medians cannot separate these. |
| Specialist-queue congestion: more cases routed into `Specialist Review` and to the central fraud desk | `escalation_pct`, `p90_cycle_h`, fraud-desk waiting (not recorded) | escalation rises slightly while p90 is stable or falls, indicating the specialist queue absorbed the extra load | escalation rises and p90 rises more than in comparators, indicating a new bottleneck downstream of triage | escalation rises exactly 2.0pp in March in every branch (fraud desk), then a further +0.8 to +1.1pp only in the month each branch adopts (Alpha +1.0, Beta +0.8, Gamma +1.0), with no such step in Delta or Epsilon | The extra escalations are visible, their cost is not. Fraud-desk waiting is unrecorded and shared, so the p90 comparison may understate congestion because the comparators also queue behind the same desk. |
| Learning and implementation fidelity | `automated_fast_path_pct` trajectory, ATT by relative month | use climbs and outcomes improve monotonically with use, with the largest gains at the highest use | use climbs but outcomes plateau or degrade, indicating misuse, over-routing, or workarounds | ATT(e) for the median: -6.6, -11.2, -10.5, -9.1, -12.6 log points at e = 0 to 4; adopter-months with use above 34pp average -13.3% vs. -11.3% below 34pp | The gradient is real but shallow and non-monotone (e = 3 is weaker than e = 2). Learning, seasonality and the August de-load all rise and fall together, so fidelity gains cannot be separated from calendar effects. |
| Case-mix and routing change | `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`, registration-time risk class | outcomes improve even though the mix got harder and volume rose, i.e. gains survive an adverse mix shift | apparent gains disappear once mix and volume are accounted for, i.e. the "effect" was easier work | complexity +5pp in May in all five branches, and Alpha continues to 36% by June; April arrivals +14.4% at Alpha and Beta vs. about 4% elsewhere; Alpha's complexity ends 8pp above its baseline while Delta's ends 5pp above | Complexity is measured at registration, so automation cannot have caused it, but the routing campaign sent *more* cases to adopters and possibly different ones. A composition shift within a risk class is invisible here. |
| Staffing pressure | `fte`, `arrivals / fte` | outcomes hold or improve while load per FTE rises, which is capacity creation rather than a speed trick | outcomes only improve where staffing improved, which is a labour effect misattributed to software | Alpha: 34 to 32 FTE while load per FTE goes 30.1 to 42.7 (peak) with a roughly flat then falling median; Beta gained 1 FTE; Gamma lost 1 in June; Delta gained 1 in June yet still deteriorated | Alpha's case is the strongest evidence for capacity creation and also the most confounded: it simultaneously lost staff, gained volume, and got harder cases. The three effects cannot be separated with monthly aggregates. |
| Rework displacement: the `[optional Rework]` loop is replaced by an earlier escalation | `rework_pct` together with `escalation_pct` | rework falls and total downstream touches fall, so loops were genuinely eliminated | rework falls only because the same cases now appear as escalations, so loops moved rather than disappeared | rework drops sharply exactly at adoption (Alpha 13.2 to 11.2, Beta 12.8 to 10.4, Gamma 14.6 to 12.6) with no change in Delta or Epsilon in those months; escalation rises about 1pp in the same cells | Roughly a 2pp rework fall against a 1pp escalation rise is consistent with both genuine elimination and displacement; a displaced case is counted once in escalation but may consume more specialist time than the loop it replaced. Undecidable without event-level data. |
| Tail amplification: a bimodal process, very fast automated closures plus a slower residual, widens the distribution | `p90_cycle_h`, `p90 / median` ratio | the p90 falls at least proportionally to the median, so the ratio is stable | the median improves while the p90 lags, so the ratio rises and the worst-served customers get worse | in absolute terms Alpha's and Beta's p90 are about +9 to +10 log points *above* their own baselines across the post period, and the `p90/median` ratio rises in every branch after May but ends highest in Alpha (2.34) and Gamma (2.30), lowest in Beta (2.20) | The ratio rises in never-treated branches too (Delta 2.07 to 2.26), so the May complexity shock explains most of it. Automation's marginal contribution to tail dispersion is small, ambiguous in sign, and not separable from complexity. |
| Survey selection | `csat_1to5`, response rate 20 to 45%, counts unavailable | satisfaction rises where and when cycle time improves, with a stable respondent base | measured satisfaction rises because faster, simpler automated cases are more likely to respond, with no change for slow cases | CSAT rises about +0.09 points relative to never-treated in all three adopters and its month-to-month movement tracks cycle time closely in *every* branch, treated or not | A 0.09-point move on a 1-to-5 scale is well inside what a swing in response composition can generate. Without response counts there is no standard error and no way to reweight. |

## 5. Quantitative evidence

**Windows, fixed before looking at outcomes.** For Alpha and Beta: pre = Jan to Mar (3 months, all pre-availability), post = Apr to Aug (5 months), with Apr to Jun reported separately as the window in which Gamma is still a valid not-yet-treated control. For Gamma: pre = Jan to Jun (6 months), post = Jul to Aug (2 months). Comparison branches are always evaluated over the identical calendar windows. Logs are used for cycle times so that the common multiplicative May and August shocks difference out; "lp" means log points (100 x log difference), which for these magnitudes is within about one point of a percentage change.

*Arithmetic fact, and the single most important one in this section:* on their own histories, Alpha's median barely moved and Beta's got worse; both branches' p90 rose about 10% above baseline. Only Gamma improved on its own history, and its post window is the two months in which arrivals fell everywhere. **A customer at Alpha or Beta did not experience shorter waits after April than before April.** Any claim of improvement therefore rests entirely on a counterfactual in which those branches would have deteriorated.

### 5.3 Treatment intensity rather than the binary flag

Method: for each branch compute its outcome as a percentage deviation from its own Jan-Mar base, subtract the never-treated average deviation for the same month, and relate the residual gap to `automated_fast_path_pct`.

| use band | adopter-months | median gap | p90 gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| use <= 34pp (Alpha Apr-May, Beta Apr-May, Gamma Jul-Aug) | 6 | -11.3% | -9.8% |
| use > 34pp (Alpha Jun-Aug, Beta Jun-Aug) | 6 | -13.3% | -11.5% |
| within-treated slope | 12 | -0.12 lp per pp of use | -0.11 lp per pp of use |

A fixed-effects regression of log median on the continuous share (branch and calendar-month effects, branch-clustered) gives -3.4 lp per 10pp of use; adding `high_complexity_pct` and `load_per_fte` attenuates it to -1.6 lp per 10pp, but in that specification `load_per_fte` carries a *negative* coefficient (more load per FTE, shorter cycle time), which is operationally implausible and marks the specification as collinear rather than better identified.

*Interpretation.* The dose gradient exists but is far too shallow to be the whole story: doubling use from about 26pp to about 48pp buys perhaps 2 additional log points, whereas going from no availability to 26pp use buys about 11. That pattern is what a *step* looks like, not a dose response. Operationally this is important: most of the observed gain appears at first switch-on, which is consistent with a change in triage discipline, work-in-progress control or manager attention accompanying go-live, rather than with per-case automation savings that scale with volume. If the benefit really scaled with use, Alpha at 48% use should look roughly twice as good as Alpha at 25% use; it does not.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Alpha and Beta were chosen *because* they had the worst cycle times, which is textbook selection on the outcome. Two things argue against pure mean reversion: their pre-period series are stable rather than spiking (Alpha 50.4, 46.9, 49.4; Beta 45.4, 45.9, 46.4), and reversion cannot explain why *rework* fell by 2pp in the exact adoption month while comparators were flat. Two things argue for caution. First, the S1 placebo terms are not zero: for the April cohort the pre-adoption pseudo-ATT is +3.4 and +3.6 lp at e = -3 and e = -2, i.e. the adopters' position relative to controls was already improving by roughly -1.5 lp per month before automation existed. Extrapolating that trend linearly across the post period absorbs about 6 lp of Alpha's 14 lp estimate. Second, the effect is measured against branches whose absolute levels are 20 to 40% lower, and a proportional specification does not fully neutralise a level difference of that size; if slow branches mechanically converge toward a system-wide mean, part of the estimate is convergence, not automation. Gamma is the useful check: it had six pre-months, its pre-adoption placebo terms are small and unsystematic (-1.6 and +1.8 lp at e = -3 and e = -2), it was *not* the worst branch, and it still shows -10 lp. That the least-selected adopter shows the same magnitude is the strongest single piece of evidence against a purely mean-reversion reading. One honest qualification: Gamma's *distant* placebo terms are large (-10.0 lp at e = -6), but those months use Alpha and Beta inside the comparison set and span the whole January-to-June complexity build-up, so they are a poor trend test; the nearby placebos are the informative ones.

### 5.5 Sensitivity: bounded conclusions

**Outcome 1, `median_cycle_h`.** Four estimators of the same quantity, in log points:

| adopter | no control group (ITS) | DiD vs never-treated | DiD after extrapolating own pre-trend | DiD if 25% of control deterioration is adopter-caused spillover | DiD if 50% is spillover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | **-1.0** | -14.1 | -8.0 | -10.9 | -7.6 |
| Beta | **+3.9** | -9.3 | -11.4 | -5.9 | -2.6 |
| Gamma | **-8.3** | -10.1 | -11.6 | -9.6 | -9.2 |

Excluding the no-control column (which assumes the counterfactual was flat, contradicted by all four comparison branches), the defensible interval for the median effect is roughly **-3% to -14%, centred near -8% to -10%**. The interval's lower end is set by the spillover scenario, the upper end by the untouched DiD.

**Outcome 2, `p90_cycle_h`.** DiD gives -9.7 (Alpha), -8.9 (Beta), -8.2 (Gamma) lp; pre-trend adjustment gives -4.2, -10.8, -9.5; the no-control view gives **+9.1, +9.9, -0.3**, i.e. tails got absolutely worse at both April adopters. Defensible interval: **0% to -10%, centred near -6%**, with the explicit rider that in absolute terms the tail did not improve for Alpha or Beta. The median and tail effects must not be merged: the central estimate for the median is roughly 3 to 4 lp larger than for the tail, and the `p90/median` ratio rose in every adopter (Alpha 2.01 to 2.34).

**Outcome 3, `escalation_pct`.** Range +0.7 to +1.1pp on a base of 8 to 8.7pp, i.e. **+6 to +11% relative**, and the sign is not in doubt because the step occurs in each adopter's own switch-on month and nowhere else. This is the only outcome for which I would state a direction with high confidence.

**A load observation that no estimator above captures.** Alpha carried 30.1 arrivals per FTE in January and 38.6 in August, a 28% increase in load per productive FTE, having lost 2 FTE and gained 14% more routed volume, while its median fell 12% below its own January-to-March base by August. Under any convex queueing relationship a 28% load increase with unchanged service capacity should have raised waiting time substantially. This is the strongest operational argument that something real changed at Alpha, and it is an argument about *capacity*, not about per-case speed. It cannot be turned into an estimate here because load, staffing and complexity all moved together.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

**Next branch.** Delta only. Epsilon stays untreated at least three more months as the sole never-treated control; it is also the branch in genuine distress (rework 16.2%, CSAT 3.75), so it is the wrong place for an unevaluated change. Delta is small, stable, and the sharpest test of the congestion-relief hypothesis, which predicts near-zero benefit there; a null would be decisive.

**Assignment and phase-in.** Do not switch Delta on branch-wide: randomise inside Delta at the intake queue, assigning arriving cases to automated or manual triage by a pre-registered 50/50 rule stratified on registration risk class and arrival hour, for eight weeks, then ramping to 100% if guardrails hold. This turns five clusters into thousands of randomised cases, the only route to identification here. Keep Epsilon at zero availability so the branch-level staggered design continues in parallel.

**Minimum measurement.** Three months of case-level pre-period in Delta and Epsilon before switch-on (monthly aggregates are insufficient), then six months post; the pre-registered primary endpoint is the case-level cycle-time distribution, reported as median, p90 and survival curve separately.

**Process-mining and queue data.** Event logs with `Register`, `Manual Triage`, `Automated Triage`, `Specialist Review`, `Rework`, `Resolve`, `Close` timestamps and resource IDs; waiting versus processing time per activity; variant frequencies and conformance; rework-loop counts per case rather than a flag; specialist and fraud-desk queue length, arrival and service times sampled hourly; work-in-progress; and a case-level automation flag so intensity is measured, not inferred.

**Spillover monitoring.** Before switch-on, instrument the fraud desk: per-branch referral counts, queue length, waiting time. Report Delta's referrals weekly and watch whether other branches' desk waits rise as Delta ramps. Branch gains bought with a longer shared queue are a transfer, not a benefit.

**Guardrails.** Halt the ramp if, in two consecutive weeks, escalated-case p90 rises more than 10% above pre-period, the `p90/median` ratio exceeds 2.5, fraud-desk median wait rises more than 20%, CSAT among escalated customers falls more than 0.15 points, or automated-path reopen rates exceed manual-path rates.

**Decision rule.** Scale further only if Delta's randomised median effect is at least -5% with an interval excluding zero, the 90th-percentile effect is no worse than zero, escalation rises no more than 1.5pp against funded desk capacity, and no guardrail has fired. If the median improves but the tail worsens, scale with the automated path restricted to low-risk classes. If Delta is null, stop expanding and reposition automation as congestion relief for high-volume branches only.

def load() -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV))
    df["month_dt"] = pd.PeriodIndex(df["month"], freq="M")
    df["t"] = df["month_dt"].astype("int64")
    df["t"] = df["t"] - df["t"].min() + 1                 # t = 1..8 for 2026-01..2026-08
    df["G"] = df["branch"].map(ADOPTION)                  # adoption month, 0 = never
    df["load_per_fte"] = df["arrivals"] / df["fte"]
    df["p90_over_median"] = df["p90_cycle_h"] / df["median_cycle_h"]
    # relative (event) time; never-adopters get NaN, deliberately not 0
    df["rel_time"] = np.where(df["G"] > 0, df["t"] - df["G"], np.nan)
    df["treated_now"] = ((df["G"] > 0) & (df["t"] >= df["G"])).astype(int)
    # treatment INTENSITY: actual completed automated fast-path share, not availability
    df["intensity"] = df["automated_fast_path_pct"] / 100.0
    for c in OUTCOMES:
        df["log_" + c] = np.log(df[c])
    return df.sort_values(["branch", "t"]).reset_index(drop=True)


def validate(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    print("=" * 100)
    print("SECTION 1  PANEL VALIDATION")
    print("=" * 100)
    problems = []
    if len(df) != 40:
        problems.append(f"expected 40 branch-months, found {len(df)}")
    if df.drop(columns=["rel_time"]).isna().sum().sum() != 0:
        problems.append("unexpected missing values")
    if df.groupby("branch")["t"].count().nunique() != 1:
        problems.append("panel is unbalanced")
    for b, g in df.groupby("branch"):
        a = g.sort_values("t")["automation"].to_numpy()
        if (np.diff(a) < 0).any():                       # treatment must be absorbing
            problems.append(f"{b}: automation switches off again")
        implied = int(g.loc[g["automation"] == 1, "t"].min()) if a.max() == 1 else 0
        if implied != ADOPTION[b]:
            problems.append(f"{b}: implied adoption t={implied} != assumed {ADOPTION[b]}")
    for c, lo, hi in [("csat_1to5", 1, 5), ("high_complexity_pct", 0, 100),
                      ("automated_fast_path_pct", 0, 100), ("rework_pct", 0, 100),
                      ("escalation_pct", 0, 100)]:
        if df[c].min() < lo or df[c].max() > hi:
            problems.append(f"{c} outside [{lo},{hi}]")
    if (df["p90_cycle_h"] < df["median_cycle_h"]).any():
        problems.append("p90 below median in some cell")
    print("Structural checks:", "PASS" if not problems else problems)

    # DATA-QUALITY FLAG (not a structural error): the automated fast-path share is 4-6%
    # even where automation was unavailable, so the intensity variable has a floor that
    # is NOT automation use. Either the variant classifier mislabels manual cases or a
    # legacy auto-close rule exists. This matters for the dose-response reading.
    pre = df[(df["G"] == 0) | (df["t"] < df["G"])]
    print("\nFLAG  automated_fast_path_pct in branch-months WITHOUT availability:",
          sorted(int(v) for v in pre["automated_fast_path_pct"].unique()))

    print("\nAdoption structure (t=1 is 2026-01):")
    print(df.groupby("branch").agg(G=("G", "first"),
                                   pre_months=("automation", lambda s: int((s == 0).sum())),
                                   post_months=("automation", "sum")).to_string())

    print("\nCommon shocks: present in ALL branches, therefore not attributable to automation")
    esc = df.pivot(index="t", columns="branch", values="escalation_pct")
    print("  escalation_pct change t2->t3 (central fraud desk, March):",
          (esc.loc[3] - esc.loc[2]).round(2).to_dict())
    hc = df.pivot(index="t", columns="branch", values="high_complexity_pct")
    print("  high_complexity_pct change t4->t5 (May product change):   ",
          (hc.loc[5] - hc.loc[4]).round(2).to_dict())
    arr = df.pivot(index="t", columns="branch", values="arrivals")
    print("  arrivals % change t3->t4 (routing campaign to Alpha/Beta):",
          ((arr.loc[4] / arr.loc[3] - 1) * 100).round(1).to_dict())
    print("  arrivals % change t7->t8 (common August de-load):         ",
          ((arr.loc[8] / arr.loc[7] - 1) * 100).round(1).to_dict())

            fig = plt.figure(figsize=(8, 4.5))
            ax = fig.add_subplot(111)
            for b, g in df.groupby("branch"):
                ax.plot(g["t"], g[c], marker="o", label=b)
                if ADOPTION[b]:
                    ax.axvline(ADOPTION[b], ls=":", lw=0.8)
            ax.set_title(f"{c} by branch (dotted vertical lines = adoption months)")
            ax.set_xlabel("month index (1 = 2026-01)")
            ax.set_ylabel(c)
            ax.legend(fontsize=8)
            fig.tight_layout()
            fig.savefig(f"trend_{c}.png", dpi=110)
            plt.close(fig)
        print("\nSaved one PNG per outcome (five separate figures, no subplots).")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. TRANSPARENT ADOPTER-SPECIFIC PRE/POST CONTRASTS
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def wmean(df, branch, ts, col):
    return df.loc[(df.branch == branch) & (df.t.isin(ts)), col].mean()


def prepost(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Windows fixed a priori, identical calendar windows for the comparison branches:
         Alpha, Beta : pre = t1-t3 (Jan-Mar); early post = t4-t6; full post = t4-t8
         Gamma       : pre = t1-t6 (Jan-Jun); post = t7-t8
       In the t4-t6 window Gamma is still NOT-YET-TREATED and is therefore a valid control.
    """
    print("\n" + "=" * 100)
    print("SECTION 3  PRE/POST CONTRASTS AND DiD vs NOT-YET-TREATED / NEVER-TREATED")
    print("=" * 100)
    specs = [("Alpha", [1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6], "early t4-t6", ["Gamma", "Delta", "Epsilon"]),
             ("Beta", [1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6], "early t4-t6", ["Gamma", "Delta", "Epsilon"]),
             ("Alpha", [1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6, 7, 8], "full t4-t8", ["Delta", "Epsilon"]),
             ("Beta", [1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6, 7, 8], "full t4-t8", ["Delta", "Epsilon"]),
             ("Gamma", [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6], [7, 8], "post t7-t8", ["Delta", "Epsilon"])]
    rows = []
    for b, pre, post, label, ctrls in specs:
        for c in OUTCOMES:
            y0, y1 = wmean(df, b, pre, c), wmean(df, b, post, c)
            c_lvl = np.mean([wmean(df, k, post, c) - wmean(df, k, pre, c) for k in ctrls])
            c_log = np.mean([np.log(wmean(df, k, post, c)) - np.log(wmean(df, k, pre, c))
                             for k in ctrls])
            rows.append(dict(adopter=b, window=label, outcome=c, pre=y0, post=y1,
                             own_change=y1 - y0, control_change=c_lvl,
                             DiD_level=(y1 - y0) - c_lvl,
                             DiD_logpoints=100 * (np.log(y1) - np.log(y0) - c_log),
                             controls="+".join(ctrls)))
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    print(out.round(3).to_string(index=False))
    print("\nARITHMETIC ONLY. 'own_change' is what the branch actually experienced;")
    print("the DiD columns additionally ASSUME (a) parallel trends absent automation,")
    print("(b) no automation-induced spillover onto the control branches (the shared")
    print("fraud desk violates this by construction), and (c) no anticipation before")
    print("adoption. None of the three is testable with these variables.")
    return out


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. RELATIVE-TIME INDICATORS AND GROUP-TIME ATT(g,t)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def att_gt(df: pd.DataFrame, col: str, log: bool = True) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Callaway-Sant'Anna-style ATT(g,t), computed explicitly.

    Comparison set for cohort g at period t = never-treated PLUS not-yet-treated at t,
    excluding cohort g itself. Baseline period = g-1. This deliberately avoids the
    negative-weighting problem of a naive two-way FE regression under staggered timing
    with dynamic, heterogeneous effects.
    ASSUMPTION for a causal reading: parallel trends between cohort g and its
    comparison set, no anticipation, and no interference between branches.
    """
    y = ("log_" + col) if log else col
    piv = df.pivot(index="branch", columns="t", values=y)
    rows = []
    for g in sorted({v for v in ADOPTION.values() if v > 0}):
        treated = [b for b, gg in ADOPTION.items() if gg == g]
        periods = list(range(1, g - 1)) + list(range(g, int(df.t.max()) + 1))  # skip g-1 (base)
        for t in periods:
            comp = [b for b, gg in ADOPTION.items()
                    if (gg == 0 or gg > t) and b not in treated]
            if not comp:
                continue
            d_treat = piv.loc[treated, t].mean() - piv.loc[treated, g - 1].mean()
            d_comp = (piv.loc[comp, t] - piv.loc[comp, g - 1]).mean()
            rows.append(dict(cohort=f"g={g}", t=t, rel_time=t - g, n_comp=len(comp),
                             comparison="+".join(comp),
                             att=(d_treat - d_comp) * (100 if log else 1),
                             kind="POST" if t >= g else "PLACEBO(pre)"))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["cohort", "t"])


def event_study(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    print("\n" + "=" * 100)
    print("SECTION 4  ATT(g,t) BY RELATIVE TIME  (not-yet-treated / never-treated comparisons)")
    print("=" * 100)
    for c in OUTCOMES:
        log = c != "csat_1to5"          # csat is a bounded survey mean; keep it in points
        unit = "log points" if log else "points"
        r = att_gt(df, c, log=log)
        print(f"\n--- {c}  [{unit}] ---")
        print(r.round(3).to_string(index=False))
        post, plac = r[r.kind == "POST"], r[r.kind == "PLACEBO(pre)"]
        print(f"  simple average POST ATT {post.att.mean():+.3f} {unit}"
              f"   |  average PLACEBO pre-adoption ATT {plac.att.mean():+.3f} {unit}")
        print("  dynamic profile by relative month:",
              {int(k): round(v, 2) for k, v in post.groupby("rel_time").att.mean().items()})
        print("  A non-zero PLACEBO value is evidence AGAINST parallel trends; it is not")
        print("  corrected here, it is reported so the reader can discount the POST value.")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. PANEL MODELS: descriptive TWFE, binned event study, and INTENSITY models
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def models(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    print("\n" + "=" * 100)
    print("SECTION 5  PANEL REGRESSIONS (DESCRIPTIVE, branch-clustered SEs, only 5 clusters)")
    print("=" * 100)
    d = df.copy()
    # relative-time indicators, binned so a 3-cohort / 8-period panel stays estimable
    def bin_rel(r):
        if not np.isfinite(r):
            return "never/pre"          # never-treated branch-months
        e = int(r)
        if e <= -2:
            return "pre_m2plus"        # placebo bin
        if e == -1:
            return "never/pre"         # reference category
        if e <= 1:
            return "post_0_1"
        if e <= 3:
            return "post_2_3"
        return "post_4plus"
    d["rel_bin"] = pd.Categorical(d["rel_time"].map(bin_rel),
                                  ["never/pre", "pre_m2plus", "post_0_1",
                                   "post_2_3", "post_4plus"])

    def fit(formula, label, note):
        res = smf.ols(formula, data=d).fit(cov_type="cluster",
                                           cov_kwds={"groups": d["branch"]})
        keep = [k for k in res.params.index
                if not k.startswith(("C(branch)", "C(month)", "Intercept"))]
        print(f"\n### {label}\n    {formula}\n    {note}")
        print(pd.DataFrame({"coef": res.params[keep], "cluster_se": res.bse[keep],
                            "t": res.tvalues[keep]}).round(4).to_string())
        print(f"    n={int(res.nobs)}  clusters=5  R2={res.rsquared:.3f}"
              "   (p-values deliberately not printed: see warnings)")

    for c, unit in [("log_median_cycle_h", "log points"), ("log_p90_cycle_h", "log points"),
                    ("rework_pct", "pp"), ("escalation_pct", "pp"), ("csat_1to5", "points")]:
        print("\n" + "-" * 100)
        print(f"OUTCOME {c}  (coefficients in {unit})")

        # MODEL A: two-way FE with a single post dummy.
        # For this to be an ATT it needs parallel trends AND homogeneous, non-dynamic
        # effects. The ATT(g,t) output above shows effects that GROW with relative time,
        # so this coefficient is a weighted average with possibly negative weights in
        # which already-treated Alpha/Beta act as controls for Gamma. Reported only as a
        # summary statistic, NOT as a causal effect.
        fit(f"{c} ~ treated_now + C(branch) + C(month)",
            "MODEL A  descriptive two-way FE, single post dummy",
            "NOT a valid ATT under staggered timing with dynamic effects.")

        # MODEL B: binned relative-time (event study). pre_m2plus is the placebo term.
        fit(f"{c} ~ C(rel_bin) + C(branch) + C(month)",
            "MODEL B  descriptive event study with binned relative time",
            "pre_m2plus ~ 0 would support parallel trends; post bins trace the dynamics.")

        # MODEL C: treatment INTENSITY instead of availability.
        # ASSUMPTIONS for a causal reading: realised fast-path share is not caused by the
        # outcome (no reverse causality from short queues to more fast-path completions),
        # and intensity is not merely proxying a smooth time trend or learning that would
        # have occurred anyway. Intensity rises monotonically with calendar time in every
        # adopter, so neither assumption can be checked here.
        fit(f"{c} ~ intensity + C(branch) + C(month)",
            "MODEL C  treatment-intensity model (continuous automated fast-path share)",
            "coef = change per 1.00 (=100pp) of realised automated fast-path share.")

        # MODEL D: robustness probe only. high_complexity_pct and load_per_fte are partly
        # POST-treatment (volume was rerouted to adopters; automation may affect recorded
        # mix), so conditioning on them blocks part of the causal path and can induce
        # collider bias. Attenuation here is NOT evidence of a smaller true effect.
        fit(f"{c} ~ intensity + high_complexity_pct + load_per_fte + C(branch) + C(month)",
            "MODEL D  intensity + case mix + load per FTE (MEDIATOR-ADJUSTED, biased)",
            "check the sign on load_per_fte: if negative, the specification is collinear.")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. INTENSITY GRADIENT AND SENSITIVITY / BOUNDED CONCLUSIONS
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def intensity_and_sensitivity(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    print("\n" + "=" * 100)
    print("SECTION 6  INTENSITY GRADIENT, PRE-TREND ADJUSTMENT, SPILLOVER SCENARIOS")
    print("=" * 100)
    base_t = [1, 2, 3]
    for c in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
        # counterfactual proxy: never-treated average deviation from its own Jan-Mar base
        # ASSUMPTION: Delta+Epsilon capture the common shocks adopters would also have felt
        dev = pd.DataFrame({b: (df[df.branch == b].set_index("t")[c]
                                / df[df.branch == b].set_index("t")[c].loc[base_t].mean() - 1)
                            for b in df.branch.unique()})
        adj = dev.sub(dev[NEVER].mean(axis=1), axis=0) * 100
        print(f"\n--- {c}: % deviation from own Jan-Mar base, net of never-treated average ---")
        print(adj.round(2).to_string())
        rows = []
        for b in ["Alpha", "Beta", "Gamma"]:
            g = ADOPTION[b]
            for t in range(g, 9):
                rows.append(dict(branch=b, t=t, adjusted_gap_pct=adj.loc[t, b],
                                 use_pct=int(df[(df.branch == b) & (df.t == t)]
                                             ["automated_fast_path_pct"].iloc[0])))
            print(f"  {b}: placebo pre-period mean {adj.loc[:g - 1, b].mean():+.2f}%"
                  f" | post mean {adj.loc[g:, b].mean():+.2f}%"
                  f" | final month {adj.loc[8, b]:+.2f}%")
        r = pd.DataFrame(rows)
        lo, hi = r[r.use_pct <= 34], r[r.use_pct > 34]
        slope = np.polyfit(r.use_pct, r.adjusted_gap_pct, 1)[0]
        print(f"  INTENSITY: use<=34pp mean {lo.adjusted_gap_pct.mean():+.2f}% (n={len(lo)});"
              f" use>34pp mean {hi.adjusted_gap_pct.mean():+.2f}% (n={len(hi)});"
              f" within-treated slope {slope:+.3f}% per pp of use")

    print("\n--- Sensitivity 1: extrapolating each adopter's own pre-adoption relative trend ---")
    rows = []
    for c in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
        piv = df.pivot(index="branch", columns="t", values="log_" + c)
        ctrl = piv.loc[NEVER].mean(axis=0)
        for b in ["Alpha", "Beta", "Gamma"]:
            g = ADOPTION[b]
            gap = piv.loc[b] - ctrl                       # relative position vs never-treated
            pre_t, post_t = np.arange(1, g), np.arange(g, 9)
            slope, icept = np.polyfit(pre_t, gap.loc[pre_t].to_numpy(), 1)
            dev = gap.loc[post_t].to_numpy() - (icept + slope * post_t)
            rows.append(dict(outcome=c, branch=b, n_pre=len(pre_t),
                             pre_trend_lp_per_month=100 * slope,
                             no_control_ITS_lp=100 * (piv.loc[b, post_t].mean()
                                                      - piv.loc[b, pre_t].mean()),
                             DiD_lp=100 * (gap.loc[post_t].mean() - gap.loc[pre_t].mean()),
                             pretrend_adjusted_lp=100 * dev.mean()))
    print(pd.DataFrame(rows).round(2).to_string(index=False))
    print("  'no_control_ITS' assumes the counterfactual was FLAT (contradicted by all four")
    print("  comparison branches). 'pretrend_adjusted' assumes the pre-trend was LINEAR and")
    print("  would have continued. Neither assumption is verifiable; the spread between the")
    print("  three columns IS the honest uncertainty about the effect size.")

    print("\n--- Sensitivity 2: fraud-desk spillover discount (median_cycle_h, log points) ---")
    piv = df.pivot(index="branch", columns="t", values="log_median_cycle_h")
    ctrl = piv.loc[NEVER].mean(axis=0)
    for share in [0.0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50]:
        out = {}
        for b in ["Alpha", "Beta", "Gamma"]:
            g = ADOPTION[b]
            pre_t, post_t = np.arange(1, g), np.arange(g, 9)
            # if a share of the controls' deterioration was CAUSED by the adopters
            # (fraud-desk congestion), the true counterfactual deterioration is smaller
            c_change = (ctrl.loc[post_t].mean() - ctrl.loc[pre_t].mean()) * (1 - share)
            out[b] = round(100 * (piv.loc[b, post_t].mean()
                                  - piv.loc[b, pre_t].mean() - c_change), 2)
        print(f"  {share:.0%} of control deterioration attributed to spillover -> {out}")
    print("  Because interference can only inflate the DiD here, the undiscounted DiD is an")
    print("  UPPER BOUND on the magnitude of benefit, not a central estimate.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` In every month after each branch switched the system on, and in no other branch-month, recorded rework fell by about 2 percentage points (Alpha 13.2 to 11.2, Beta 12.8 to 10.4, Gamma 14.6 to 12.6) while specialist and fraud-desk escalation rose by about 1 percentage point on top of a 2.0-point March step that all five branches shared; over the same window the three adopters' medians and p90s held roughly flat against their own baselines while the two never-adopters' medians rose 13 to 17% and their p90s rose 17 to 23%.
- `Suggestive:` Automation relieved congestion rather than making individual cases faster, which is why Alpha absorbed a 28% rise in load per productive FTE with two fewer FTE and an 8-point harder case mix without its median deteriorating, and why almost all of the apparent gain arrives in the first month at 25% use rather than accumulating as use doubles to 50%.
- `Conditionally causal:` Conditional on the never-treated branches being a valid counterfactual, on no anticipation, and on the shared fraud desk not transmitting adopter-induced congestion to them, availability of automated triage reduced median cycle time by roughly 8 to 10% relative to counterfactual, with a defensible range of 3 to 14%, and reduced the 90th percentile by a smaller and less robust 0 to 10%; both figures fall by roughly half if each adopter's own pre-adoption trend is extrapolated or if a quarter of the comparison branches' deterioration is attributed to spillover.
- `Not supported:` The single statement leadership asked for, "automation reduces cycle time by X%", together with any claim that automation improved customer satisfaction, improved worst-case waits, or eliminated rework: customers at Alpha and Beta waited no less after April than before, the p90 at both branches sat about 10% above baseline throughout, the 0.09-point CSAT movement is indistinguishable from survey non-response composition, and roughly half the rework reduction may simply have become earlier escalations whose downstream cost is unmeasured.
