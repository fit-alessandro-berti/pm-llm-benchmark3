## 1. Target definition

**Why the requested target does not exist as stated.** "90-day default for every application" is observed only when an application is activated. Rejected and withdrawn applicants were never given credit, so their default outcome is not missing: it is *undefined under the policy that generated the data*. It only becomes defined under a hypothetical "what if we had activated them" intervention. Transferred cases leave the lender's book, and open cases have not finished yet. The warehouse label `default_90d` is therefore a conditional quantity, "default given activation". Calling it "default for every application", or filling the gaps with zeros, would mix three different quantities.

A quick check shows how misleading the convenient version is. Among labelled activated accounts the default rate is 2,700 / 21,600 = 12.5%. If the 9,100 rejected and 5,700 withdrawn cases are added as "non-default", the rate becomes 2,700 / 36,400 = 7.4%. Worse, the model would learn that the profiles that trigger rejection (high risk) look "safe". The signal is inverted exactly where the lender most needs it.

**Primary target (chosen): a factual competing-outcome ("multi-state") target for all applications that are still undecided at the prediction point, under the current (post-1 July 2025) policy.**

- **Unit of analysis:** one application × one prediction point (landmark). If several landmarks are used, rows from the same case are clustered, and all bootstrap, split and cross-validation operations work at case level.
- **Prediction time (primary):** an elapsed-time landmark, t = S_i + 48 h, where S_i is the timestamp of `Application Started`. A case is eligible only if no `Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn` or `Transferred` event has occurred by t. Secondary landmarks are 24 h and 7 days, plus fixed event-count prefixes k = 2, 3, 4, all truncated before any decision event (Section 4).
- **Horizon:** activation must occur within A = 60 days of S_i. A is confirmed from 2024 cohorts as the smallest window containing at least 95% of activations, and this is reported. Default is then counted within 90 days after `Account Activated`. The outcome feed can add up to 35 days of lag, so a fully matured label needs S_i + 60 + 90 + 35 = S_i + 185 days.
- **Outcome states Y_i at the horizon:**
  - BAD: activated within 60 days and defaulted within 90 days of activation;
  - GOOD: activated within 60 days and no default within 90 days;
  - REJ: rejected;
  - WD: withdrawn;
  - TRF: transferred;
  - NTU/UNRES: accepted but not taken up, or unresolved at S_i + 60 days. This is a real outcome state, not censoring.
  - Cases still open at the data cutoff before S_i + 60 days are *right-censored*. They are not assigned a state.
- **Population:** applications with an observed `Application Started` between 1 Jan 2024 and 31 Dec 2025 that are undecided at the landmark. The deployment population is the post-policy regime (P2). Pre-policy data (P1) is used with an explicit regime term.
- **Estimand:** the individual cumulative incidence of the BAD state given the observable history,

  F_BAD(H_i(t)) = P(Y_i = BAD | H_i(t), undecided at t, regime P2)
               = ∫ from t to S_i+60d of f_act(s | H_i(t)) · q(s, H_i(t)) ds,

  where f_act is the cause-specific sub-density of activation at time s (with rejection, withdrawal and transfer as competing events), and q(s, H) = P(default within 90 days | activated at s, H). The model also reports the component probabilities P(activate), P(reject), P(withdraw), P(transfer) and q. At population level, the estimand is the mean F_BAD in the eligible P2 cohort, reported next to its Aalen–Johansen estimate.

**Why this is defensible.**
- *Operationally:* the early warning answers "which applications in today's pipeline are on course to become defaulted accounts on our book if the process continues as it currently operates?" An application that current underwriting will reject anyway needs no warning, because the process already catches it. A risky application that is likely to be approved does need one.
- *Statistically:* every component is a factual event recorded in the log. No outcome is imputed for never-exposed applicants. Open cases and immature labels are handled as censoring, so post-policy cohorts contribute partial information instead of being discarded.
- *Honestly:* the target is explicitly policy-relative. Its value changes if the approval policy changes, and it must be re-estimated.

**Credible alternatives (different questions).**
1. **Conditional default among activated accounts,** P(default90 | activated, H(t)). This is the warehouse target and appears as the q component above. It answers "how risky is this applicant *if it ends up activated under current policy*?" It is valid for accounts that resemble historically approved ones. It is *not* the risk of rejected applicants.
2. **Time to default after activation with right censoring.** The prediction point is at or after activation, with a 0–90 day survival or hazard model. This is a post-booking monitoring or collections question and uses different, richer data. It does not predict from "the first few events".
3. **Policy-specific counterfactual risk,** P(default90 under do(activate) | H(t)), or risk under a specified alternative approval policy. This is the only formulation that assigns a default risk to every application, including rejected ones. It requires conditional exchangeability of the approval decision given H(t), positivity (applicants below the old thresholds had essentially zero approval probability), consistency and no interference. These assumptions are not credible from these data alone. The target can only be bounded or partially identified (Section 3), and it must be labelled "assumption-dependent".

## 2. Cohort construction

**As-of conventions**
- Event cutoff C_E = 31 Dec 2025 23:59:59, the end of the event window. Only events with `time:timestamp` ≤ C_E exist in the analysis.
- Label cutoff C_L = 15 Feb 2026. A label is "known" only if `default_90d` is non-null and `label_available_at` ≤ C_L.
- Case status is computed from these two cutoffs, never from warehouse flags. The same function is re-run with earlier cutoffs for backtests (Section 7).
- Timestamps are normalised to UTC. I assume events are recorded in near real time. If an ingestion timestamp exists, it replaces the event timestamp in all as-of rules.

**Inclusion rules**
- The first observed event is `Application Started`, with S_i in [1 Jan 2024, 31 Dec 2025].
- The case has a unique identifier after duplicate and migration cleaning.
- For a given landmark: S_i + landmark ≤ C_E, and the case is undecided at the landmark.

**Exclusion or flagging rules**
- Left-truncated cases (first event is not `Application Started`) are excluded and counted.
- Test or synthetic cases are excluded.
- Cases whose only events are post-migration duplicates are collapsed into one case.
- Cases that reappear on our platform through a transfer link are merged into one cluster when a link exists. The roughly 40% without a link cannot be merged and are flagged as potential duplicates (`source_system` = partner, channel = transfer-in).
- Cases decided before the landmark are not eligible at that landmark. They are counted, not deleted from the study, because a warning comes too late for them.

**Regime assignment.** P1 means S_i < 1 Jul 2025; P2 means S_i ≥ 1 Jul 2025. Cases started in P1 but decided under P2 thresholds ("straddlers") are flagged and analysed as a sensitivity group.

**Cohort-flow table (all 48,000 applications)**

| Group | n | Status as of C_E / C_L | Role in primary analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Activated, 90-day outcome matured and labelled | 21,600 (18,900 non-default, 2,700 default) | Fully observed | Stage 1: activation event. Stage 2: complete 90-day follow-up (BAD or GOOD). |
| B. Activated, outcome not matured or missing | 3,600 | Split below | Stage 1: activation event. Stage 2: depends on subgroup. |
| B1. Activation + 90 d > C_L | computed from data | Ordinary right censoring (still inside the outcome window) | Censored at verified follow-up; defaults already recorded count as events. |
| B2. 90 d < activation age ≤ 125 d, no label | computed | Delayed label (not yet mature) | Censored at the lag-adjusted verified date; the feed-delay distribution is modelled. |
| B3. Activation age > 125 d, no label | computed | Selectively unobserved: probably a permanently missing feed, concentrated in partner-originated accounts | Excluded from the Stage 2 likelihood; handled by inverse-probability-of-observation weighting plus a tipping-point analysis. |
| C. Rejected | 9,100 | Competing terminal outcome; default undefined (never exposed) | Stage 1 competing event. Never labelled non-default; absent from Stage 2. |
| D. Withdrawn | 5,700 | Competing terminal outcome; default undefined | Stage 1 competing event. |
| E. Transferred | 2,900 (about 1,740 linkable, about 1,160 not) | Competing terminal outcome for our book. For an "ecosystem" target it is missing follow-up instead. | Stage 1 competing event. Linked outcomes are used only in sensitivity analyses. |
| F. Still open | 5,100 | Administrative right censoring in the application phase | Stage 1: censored at C_E. Split F1 (age < 60 d, genuinely pending) and F2 (age ≥ 60 d, stale; NTU/UNRES state or possible unlogged abandonment). Accepted-but-not-activated cases sit here. |
| **Total** | **48,000** | | |

Totals check: 21,600 + 3,600 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 = 48,000.

**Order-of-magnitude check on B.** There are 25,200 activations over 24 months, about 1,050 per month. Activations after 13 Oct 2025 cannot be mature by 15 Feb 2026 (13 Oct + 125 days = 15 Feb). That is about 2.6 months, or roughly 2,700 accounts. So on the order of 900 of the 3,600 are likely past maturity with no label, which makes them candidate permanently missing feeds. This is a plausibility check, not a count; B1, B2 and B3 are computed exactly by the pipeline.

**Maturity implications**
- A fully mature composite label requires S_i ≤ 13 Aug 2025 (15 Feb 2026 minus 185 days). That leaves only about 6 weeks of post-policy starts.
- A fully mature activation-level label requires activation ≤ 13 Oct 2025, which is essentially the migration date.
- A complete-case, fixed-horizon design would therefore throw away almost all post-policy information. This is the main reason for a censoring-aware primary analysis.

All cohort counts are reported by status × regime × split × channel, and separately after each landmark's eligibility filter.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

**Mechanisms (kept distinct)**

1. **Ordinary right censoring.**
   - Open applications at C_E (group F) are censored in the application phase.
   - Activated accounts still inside the 90-day window (B1) are censored in the default phase at their verified follow-up time.
   - Both are administrative, calendar-driven censoring. They are plausibly independent of risk given S_i, regime and covariates, but cohort drift can violate this.
2. **Delayed labels (B2).** The event may already have happened but not yet been reported. This is the "incurred but not reported" problem. Default-free follow-up is credited only up to C_L − 35 days, the date before which reporting is assumed complete. Defaults recorded internally before then count as events. The 7–35 day delay distribution is estimated on mature accounts, by channel and period, to validate this rule.
3. **Selection through activation.**
   - Stage 2 is estimated only on activated accounts. This is not censoring: rejected and withdrawn cases have no default outcome under the observed policy.
   - The primary target avoids needing that outcome by treating rejection and withdrawal as competing states.
   - Any use of q for non-activated applicants is extrapolation under the counterfactual assumptions in Section 1.
   - The 1 July 2025 threshold change alters who is selected. The P1 → P2 shift is both a threat (the q relationship may differ) and a partial natural experiment (the marginal band approved under one regime but not the other).
4. **Transfer.**
   - *Primary:* transfer is a competing terminal event for "default on our book". It is fully observed in the log, so it needs no linkage.
   - *Secondary (ecosystem default):* transfer is missing follow-up. The 60% link rate is almost certainly not random (it depends on partner, period and platform integration).
   - Linked pairs are also a duplication risk and are clustered across splits.
5. **Permanently missing outcome feeds (B3).** The outcome exists but will never arrive. The pattern is enriched in partner-originated accounts and may be informative, for example if a partner stops reporting on accounts that went into collections. This is potentially missing not at random.

**Primary analysis strategy: landmark multi-state model with discrete-time cause-specific hazards.**
- **Stage 1 (application phase):** a person-period table in daily or 2-day intervals from the landmark to S_i + 60 d. The causes are activation, rejection, withdrawal, transfer and NTU. Open cases are censored at C_E. The model is multinomial discrete-time hazard (for example multinomial logistic or gradient boosting on person-periods) with prefix features, calendar terms and a regime indicator plus interactions.
- **Stage 2 (default phase):** discrete-time hazard for default in weekly intervals from 0 to 90 days after activation. Inputs are the landmark features, the activation delay and the regime. Censoring for B1 and B2 follows the rules above.
- **B3 handling:** B3 accounts are removed from Stage 2. The remaining activated accounts are weighted by 1 / P̂(outcome observable | channel, source_system, region, amount, activation month, regime, landmark features), estimated on all activated accounts past maturity. Weights are truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles. This assumes missing at random given these covariates.
- **Combining the stages:** F_BAD = Σ_s P̂(activation at s | H) · q̂(s, H). This is the empirical version of the estimand. Fixed-horizon evaluation metrics use inverse-probability-of-censoring weights (IPCW).
- **Survival software:** implemented with standard survival or competing-risk tooling (`lifelines`, `scikit-survival`, R `mstate`/`riskRegression`). PM4Py is used only for data engineering.

**Sensitivity analyses**
1. **Naive benchmark:** a complete-case binary model on the 21,600, and separately the "rejected = non-default" labelling. These are reported only to show how much they distort prevalence, calibration and feature direction.
2. **Delta-adjusted tipping point for B3:** assume the missing-feed default odds equal δ × the model-predicted odds, for δ ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3}. Report the δ at which calibration or decision conclusions change. Add worst-case bounds (all B3 default vs none default).
3. **Transfer as missing follow-up:** use linked outcomes for the 60%. For unlinked transfers, bound the result from both sides or impute using linked-transfer hazards with a δ adjustment.
4. **Stale open cases (F2):** censored (primary) vs treated as NTU or withdrawal.
5. **Regime:** Stage 2 trained on P2 only vs pooled with regime interactions. Stage 1 trained on P2 only, because approval behaviour is policy-specific.
6. **Default source:** Stage 2 events from the feed only vs internal `Default Recorded` events only. Concordance is checked on mature accounts.
7. **Competing-risk formulation:** a Fine–Gray sub-distribution model vs cause-specific hazards for F_BAD.
8. **Counterfactual alternative (explicitly assumption-heavy):**
   - Manski-style bounds for the rejected population (q between 0 and 1, narrowed by monotonicity in `risk_score` if that is defended);
   - a local estimate around the policy-change threshold band, assuming nothing else changed on 1 July 2025;
   - IPW-based "risk if activated" restricted to the region of common support (estimated approval probability within [0.05, 0.95]).

**Testability of assumptions**

| Assumption | Testability |
|---|---|
| Feed delay ≤ 35 days, and its distribution by channel | Testable on mature accounts |
| Internal default events agree with the feed | Testable on mature accounts |
| Administrative censoring non-informative given covariates | Partially testable: compare early vs late cohorts over overlapping follow-up; confounded by drift |
| Transfer linkage random given observables | Partially testable: compare prefix features of linked vs unlinked; outcome dependence is untestable |
| q stable across the policy change | Partially testable on the post-policy mature slice (activations 1 Jul – 13 Oct 2025) |
| Stale open cases truly pending | Partially testable by re-extracting later |
| Permanently missing feeds MAR given covariates | Untestable from these data; becomes testable if a random sample of missing feeds is chased with the partner (double sampling, strongly recommended) |
| Counterfactual default of rejected applicants | Fundamentally untestable without randomised or exploratory approvals; only bounds or local threshold evidence exist |
| No unmeasured confounding of the approval decision | Untestable; underwriters see bureau and document information outside the log |

## 4. Prefix extraction

**General as-of rule.** A prefix for case i at prediction time t contains only:
- events with timestamp ≤ t;
- events strictly before the first decision event (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`), when that decision happened before t, in which case the case is ineligible anyway;
- attribute values as recorded on those events.

Events with the same timestamp as the decision are excluded (conservative tie handling). `Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact` and `Default Recorded` can never occur in an eligible prefix; an assertion enforces this. Case-level attributes are taken from the first event (`requested_amount`, `channel`, `region`). The audit checks whether they change later, which would indicate overwriting or back-filling.

**Fixed event-count prefixes (k = 2, 3, 4)**
- Built after renaming and de-duplication. The prediction time is the timestamp of the k-th event.
- A case enters the k-prefix cohort only if it has at least k pre-decision events. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` returns shorter traces unchanged, so they are explicitly filtered out.
- Short cases are *not padded*. Padding would let the model learn "short prefix = fast decision" from information not yet available. Instead, the number and outcome mix of cases decided before k events are reported, because this is the population the warning cannot reach.
- Event-count prefixes implicitly condition on process speed: a fast and a stalled case with the same k events sit at very different clock times. Elapsed time since start is therefore always a feature, and results are stratified by it.

**Elapsed-time prefixes (24 h, 48 h primary, 7 d)**
- The prediction time is S_i + Δ. The case is eligible if it is undecided at S_i + Δ and S_i + Δ ≤ C_E.
- The prefix is every event up to S_i + Δ, so prefixes can have different lengths, and "nothing happened since KYC" is legitimate information.
- This matches how an operational warning is issued: on a clock, for the current pipeline.
- Landmarking avoids immortal-time bias: outcomes are counted only from the landmark onward, among cases still at risk.

**Repeated document loops**
- `Request Documents → Credit Review` cycles are encoded as loop counts, time spent in loops, time since the last request, and a loop-collapsed control-flow string (for example `Start > KYC > Review > (Docs>Review)^n`).
- Raw sequences are therefore not dominated by rare long variants, and the encoding stays fixed-width.
- The loop count at the prefix is available; the eventual total loop count is not.

**Activity renaming and duplicates (15 Oct 2025 migration)**
- A versioned mapping table from old to canonical names is supplied by system owners. It is validated by comparing activity sets, start/end activities and directly-follows graphs before and after the migration. Activities that appear in only one period and are not mapped are unresolved renames.
- Duplicates: same case, same canonical activity, timestamps within a tolerance (for example 60 s), usually with different `source_system`. The first occurrence is kept. Duplicate counts are reported by period, because leftover duplicates inflate loop and review counts after the migration.
- Prefixes are generated only *after* normalisation. A `post_migration` flag is kept for monitoring, not as a predictive feature.

## 5. Process-aware features

**Static case attributes (known at `Application Started`)**
- `case:channel` (direct, broker, partner / transfer-in), `case:region`, `case:requested_amount` (value at the first event), and `source_system` (for data-quality stratification only).
- Region and channel need a fair-lending review: they can act as proxies for protected characteristics.

**Prefix-derived features (computed from events ≤ t)**
- **Control flow:**
  - loop-collapsed prefix string, or one-hot encoded activities and bigrams;
  - whether KYC has completed;
  - number of KYC attempts or repeats;
  - whether Credit Review has started;
  - last activity.
- **Timing:**
  - time since start;
  - time from start to KYC completion and from KYC to Credit Review;
  - time since the last event (a proxy for inactivity);
  - inter-event time mean and maximum;
  - start hour, weekday and holiday indicators.
  - Only single completion timestamps exist, so service vs waiting time is not identifiable per activity.
- **Rework:** document requests so far, time since the last request, and repeated reviews.
- **Resource:**
  - number of distinct resources so far and handovers;
  - identity of the KYC and review resources assigned so far (high-cardinality, encoded with out-of-time smoothing);
  - resource experience recomputed as of t (count of prior cases handled before t in the log, noting left truncation at 1 Jan 2024), *not* the stored extraction-time experience.
- **Risk score:** the last `risk_score` value recorded on an event ≤ t, the number of updates so far, and the change since the first value. This is valid only if score history is preserved at event level (audited in Section 6).
- **Documents:** documents received by t, rebuilt from event-level timestamps, not from `documents_total`.

**Features that need historical reference data (computed as of t, from the past only)**
- **Queue and system load:**
  - system WIP (started but undecided applications) at t;
  - reviewer backlog at t;
  - arrivals in the previous 7 days;
  - recent median decision time.
  - All are computed from events ≤ t. Daily aggregates that include the rest of the day are not allowed.
- **Resource history:** resource-level historical approval and rework rates, computed from cases decided before t.
- **Variant novelty:** frequency of the prefix variant in a reference window before t; flag unseen variants.
- **Outcome encodings:** historical default rates by region, channel or resource (target encoding) may use only labels with `label_available_at` ≤ t. This is the maturity-lag rule; without it, target encoding leaks recent outcomes.

**Cohort and calendar features**
- regime indicator (P1/P2);
- month of start, to capture seasonality;
- days since the policy change;
- straddler flag.
- The regime is included so that pooled models can separate regime effects. In deployment all cases are P2, so regime is effectively a constant that selects the P2 calibration.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Populated only after the final decision; encodes approval (non-null implies accepted) and the limit amount. The limit is also an intervention that affects default, so it acts as a mediator. | Never available at an early landmark. | Drop | Target and decision leakage. Non-null values inside prefixes are a symptom of warehouse back-filling and are reported. |
| `decision_reason` | Populated at or after Accept/Reject; directly reveals the decision and often the risk rationale. | Available only at or after the decision. | Drop (descriptive use only, e.g. reject profiles for bounds) | Post-decision information. |

| `default_90d` | The label itself, repeated on events (including pre-activation rows). | Label construction only, with `label_available_at` ≤ label cutoff. | Drop as feature | Direct target leakage. |
| `label_available_at` / label availability indicator | Availability correlates with channel, partner and maturity; "label known" implies activation. | Label construction and censoring only. | Drop as feature | Encodes activation and missingness mechanisms. |
| `data_extract_time` | Constant or period proxy; identifies snapshot. | Never a feature. | Drop | No causal or predictive meaning at t. |
| `source_system` | Proxy for post-migration period and partner origin. | Known at start (value on the first event). | Repair: use for cleaning and stratification, not as a predictor | Would let the model learn migration artifacts. |

| Transfer linkage table | Contains future information (the case left and reappeared). | Outcome follow-up in sensitivity analyses only. | Drop as feature | Future and incomplete (60%) information. |

## 7. Validation design

**Why a random case split misleads here**
1. It mixes P1 and P2. The model learns policy-period artefacts and is evaluated on a blend that never occurs in deployment, which is P2 only.
2. It puts renamed and duplicated post-migration events on both sides, so period-specific naming leaks.
3. Cases close in time share queues, resources, same-day snapshots and score recalibrations. Random neighbours make evaluation optimistic.
4. Immature and censored cases appear in both train and test with labels that would not have been known at training time.
5. Linked transfer duplicates can fall on different sides.
6. Evaluating only on random subsets of the 21,600 measures performance *within the selected activated population*, not on the application pipeline.

`pm4py.split_train_test` may be shown as a naive baseline, and its optimism is reported.

**Primary temporal design (split by case start time)**

| Split | Case starts | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 1 Jan 2024 – 31 Mar 2025 | Model fitting (P1) |
| Validation | 1 Apr – 30 Jun 2025 | Tuning and early stopping (P1) |
| Test | 1 Jul – 14 Oct 2025 | Post-policy, pre-migration. Stage 1 fully evaluable. Composite fully mature only for starts ≤ 13 Aug 2025; the rest via IPCW with partial follow-up. |
| Monitor | 15 Oct – 31 Dec 2025 | Post-migration. Stage 1 (censored) and feature/label-shift monitoring only; default component not yet evaluable. |

- Training labels are those known at the training cutoff: the case table is rebuilt with event cutoff and label cutoff equal to the training cutoff. Immature training cases enter as censored.
- The test period is evaluated with outcomes known at 15 Feb 2026, using censoring-aware metrics.
- After evaluation, the final model is refit on all data with the regime term, then recalibrated on P2.

**Rolling-origin backtests.** Origins are 30 Sep 2024, 31 Dec 2024, 31 Mar 2025, 30 Jun 2025 and (Stage 1 only) 30 Sep 2025. At each origin O:
- rebuild case status and labels as of O;
- train on starts before O;
- score starts in (O, O + 3 months];
- evaluate with later-known outcomes.

This mimics the production cadence and exposes performance decay and label-maturity effects.

**Specific checks**
- **Policy change:**
  - fit on P1, evaluate on P2 unchanged ("transport");
  - then after intercept/slope recalibration on early P2;
  - then with P2-only Stage 1.
  - Report how much recalibration was needed. Stage 1 is expected to break (approval rates shift); q may transport better, which is tested on the mature P2 activations (1 Jul – 13 Oct 2025).
- **Migration:**
  - compare activity-frequency and prefix-variant distributions before and after, and the share of unseen variants;
  - compare Stage 1 performance on the monitor window against test;
  - remaining gaps after mapping indicate unresolved renames.
- **Label maturity:**
  - report effective sample sizes and event counts by maturity status;
  - never compute fixed-horizon metrics on immature cohorts without IPCW;
  - show metric stability as the label cutoff moves.
- **Calibration:**
  - calibration of F_BAD by risk decile against the Aalen–Johansen estimate of observed cumulative incidence;
  - calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, integrated calibration index;
  - separately for q (observed Kaplan–Meier 90-day default among activated) and for Stage 1 cause probabilities.
- **Subgroups:** channel (especially partner / transfer-in, where feeds are missing), region, amount band, regime, straddlers, pre/post migration, loop count, prefix length or speed, and `source_system`. Confidence intervals come from a case-cluster bootstrap. Calibration by subgroup is part of the fair-lending review.
- **Distribution shift:**
  - population stability index / KS by month for key features;
  - adversarial validation (a classifier that separates train from test periods);
  - competing-outcome mix over time (label shift);
  - share of unseen prefix variants;
  - drift in B3 missingness by channel.

**Metrics (consistent with the censored competing-risk target)**
- **Composite F_BAD at the horizon:**
  - IPCW time-dependent Brier score and a scaled version against a null model;
  - cause-specific time-dependent AUC (cumulative/dynamic) for BAD vs everything else;
  - competing-risk concordance (Wolbers);
  - IPCW precision/recall at operational review capacity (for example the top 5% of the pipeline);
  - decision curves / net benefit at relevant thresholds. BAD is rare, about 7% of resolved applications, so PR-type metrics are more informative than ROC alone.
- **Stage 1:** IPCW multiclass log loss and Brier score; per-cause calibration.
- **Stage 2 (q):** Uno's C, time-dependent AUC and IPCW Brier score at 90 days, and a calibration plot.
- **Baselines:** `risk_score` as of t alone; static attributes alone; the naive 21,600-case model.
- All metrics are reported with bootstrap confidence intervals and under the Section 3 sensitivity scenarios (δ-adjusted B3, transfer handling).

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**What the study can claim**
- Predictive association between early-process histories and the factual probability that an undecided application becomes a defaulted account on the lender's book within the defined horizons, under the post-July-2025 policy and process.
- Separate predictive probabilities for rejection, withdrawal, transfer and activation.
- For activated accounts, the conditional 90-day default risk q.
- Calibration and performance estimates that account for censoring and delayed labels, conditional on the stated missingness assumptions.

**What it cannot claim**
- That a low F_BAD means "safe". It may just mean "likely to be rejected or to withdraw". Components must be shown alongside F_BAD.
- That q applied to rejected applicants measures their default risk. That is counterfactual risk under an approve decision, identified only under untestable assumptions and only within common support. It is reported as bounds or clearly labelled scenarios.
- Any causal effect of process features (loops, reviewers, waiting time) on default. These are selection and proxy signals. For example, more document requests may reflect underwriter suspicion rather than cause default.
- Validity for partner-originated accounts beyond what the δ-sensitivity supports, or for ecosystem-wide default after transfer.
- Transportability to a different policy regime without re-estimating Stage 1.

**Three separate concepts**
- *Predictive association:* the observed conditional probability given H(t) under current practice.
- *Selection effects:* who is observed with outcomes is determined by approval policy, applicant withdrawal, transfer routing and feed completeness.
- *Counterfactual default risk:* what would happen under a different decision. It requires causal assumptions and ideally experimental or exploratory approvals.

**Decision-use boundary**
- Suitable for prioritising reviewer attention, requesting extra verification, or forecasting bad-book inflow in the current pipeline.
- Not suitable as an automated rejection rule or a replacement for the regulated credit scorecard without separate governance. In the EU, credit scoring is high-risk under the AI Act, and adverse-action reasons must be explainable and non-proxy.

**How deployment can change the data-generating process**
1. **Performative feedback on labels.** Flagged applications receive more document requests, lower limits or rejection. Flagged high-risk applicants then rarely activate, so future activated populations are more selected, positivity worsens, and q is estimated on ever narrower data. Warned accounts that are handled well default less, so the model appears miscalibrated because it worked.
2. **Feature shift caused by the model.** Extra verification increases loops and waiting times. The same features then mean something different ("loop because the model flagged", not "loop because documents were missing").
3. **Behavioural and strategic responses.** Brokers and partners learn which early behaviour triggers flags and steer, delay or split applications. Flagged applicants may withdraw more, and risky cases may be transferred more, moving risk into the missing-follow-up channel.
4. **Queue and resource dynamics.** Rerouting flagged cases to specialists changes backlog, resource assignment and experience patterns, so queue features drift.
5. **Further policy changes** alter Stage 1 hazards directly.

**Mitigations**
- Log model score, action and version at the prediction point.
- Keep a small randomised no-action holdout (or randomised intensity of review) so factual outcomes remain evaluable and causal effects of actions can be estimated.
- Monitor B3 missingness.
- Chase a random sample of missing partner feeds.
- Re-run the backtest after each policy or system change.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
"""Censoring-aware cohort, prefix and leakage-audit pipeline (pandas + PM4Py >= 2.7).
Survival / competing-risk fitting is NOT done in PM4Py; hooks are marked below."""
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CSV = "applications_events.csv"
CID, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
EVENT_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2025-12-31 23:59:59", tz="UTC")   # end of event window
LABEL_ASOF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15", tz="UTC")              # extraction date
POLICY_CHANGE = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01", tz="UTC")
MIGRATION = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15", tz="UTC")
OUTCOME_H, FEED_LAG_MAX = pd.Timedelta(days=90), pd.Timedelta(days=35)
K, LANDMARK = 3, pd.Timedelta(hours=48)
# ASSUMPTION: placeholder rename map from migration owners; verified by the DFG diff below
RENAME = {"KYC Verification": "Identity/KYC Check", "Doc Request": "Request Documents"}
DECISION = ["Accepted", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]
POST_DECISION = DECISION + ["Contract Signed", "Account Activated",
                            "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"]
FORBIDDEN = {"final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d",
             "label_available_at", "data_extract_time", "documents_total",
             "resource_experience", "source_system"}

# --- 1. Load, canonicalise activity names, remove migration duplicates -------------
raw = pd.read_csv(CSV, dtype={CID: str})
for c in [TS, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
    raw[c] = pd.to_datetime(raw[c], utc=True, errors="coerce")
raw[ACT] = raw[ACT].replace(RENAME)
raw = raw.sort_values([CID, TS], kind="stable")
gap = raw.groupby([CID, ACT])[TS].diff()
raw["is_dup"] = gap.notna() & (gap <= pd.Timedelta(seconds=60))   # ASSUMPTION: 60 s tolerance
print("duplicates removed (False=pre, True=post migration):\n",
      raw.groupby(raw[TS] >= MIGRATION)["is_dup"].sum())
raw = raw[~raw["is_dup"]].drop(columns="is_dup")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id=CID, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)

# --- 2. Rename validation: activities seen in only one migration period -------------
acts = {}
for name, part in [("pre", df[df[TS] < MIGRATION]), ("post", df[df[TS] >= MIGRATION])]:
    dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(part, activity_key=ACT, case_id_key=CID, timestamp_key=TS)
    acts[name] = {a for edge in dfg for a in edge} | set(sa) | set(ea)
print("unmapped? only pre:", acts["pre"] - acts["post"], "only post:", acts["post"] - acts["pre"])

# --- 3. Case table as it was knowable at (event_cutoff, label_asof) -----------------
def build_cases(d, event_cutoff, label_asof):
    d = d[d[TS] <= event_cutoff]
    g = d.groupby(CID)
    c = pd.DataFrame({"start": g[TS].min(), "first_act": g[ACT].first(),
                      "channel": g["case:channel"].first(), "region": g["case:region"].first(),
                      "amount": g["case:requested_amount"].first()})
    for a, col in {"Accepted": "t_acc", "Rejected": "t_rej", "Withdrawn": "t_wd",
                   "Transferred": "t_trf", "Account Activated": "t_act",
                   "Default Recorded": "t_def"}.items():
        c[col] = d[d[ACT] == a].groupby(CID)[TS].min()
    c["t_decision"] = c[["t_acc", "t_rej", "t_wd", "t_trf"]].min(axis=1)
    lab = d[d["default_90d"].notna() & (d["label_available_at"] <= label_asof)]
    c = c.join(lab.groupby(CID).agg(y=("default_90d", "max"), label_at=("label_available_at", "min")))
    act, age = c["t_act"].notna(), label_asof - c["t_act"]
    conds = [c["first_act"] != "Application Started", act & c["y"].notna(),
             act & (age <= OUTCOME_H), act & (age <= OUTCOME_H + FEED_LAG_MAX), act,
             c["t_rej"].notna(), c["t_wd"].notna(), c["t_trf"].notna(), c["t_acc"].notna()]
    names = ["excluded_left_truncated", "A_activated_labeled", "B1_in_window_censored",
             "B2_label_pending_delayed", "B3_feed_missing_selective", "C_rejected_competing",
             "D_withdrawn_competing", "E_transferred_competing", "F_accepted_not_activated"]
    c["status"] = np.select(conds, names, default="F_open_censored")
    # Stage-2 (default phase) follow-up; ASSUMPTION: internal default events complete to verified_to
    verified_to = label_asof - FEED_LAG_MAX
    fu_end = (c["t_act"] + OUTCOME_H).clip(upper=verified_to)
    obs_def = (c["t_def"] <= fu_end) & ((c["t_def"] - c["t_act"]) <= OUTCOME_H)
    c["s2_event"] = np.where(c["y"].notna(), c["y"], obs_def.astype(float))
    c["s2_days"] = np.where(c["s2_event"] == 1, (c["t_def"] - c["t_act"]).dt.days.fillna(90),
                            np.where(c["y"].notna(), 90, (fu_end - c["t_act"]).dt.days.clip(lower=0)))
    c.loc[~act | (c["status"] == "B3_feed_missing_selective"), ["s2_event", "s2_days"]] = np.nan
    # Stage-1 (application phase): open cases censored at event_cutoff, not labelled
    c["s1_days"] = (c["t_decision"].fillna(event_cutoff) - c["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    c["regime"] = np.where(c["start"] < POLICY_CHANGE, "P1_pre_policy", "P2_post_policy")
    c["straddler"] = (c["start"] < POLICY_CHANGE) & (c["t_decision"] >= POLICY_CHANGE)
    return c

cases = build_cases(df, EVENT_CUTOFF, LABEL_ASOF)
# For strict backtests call build_cases(df, origin, origin) so training labels are as-of origin.
bins = pd.to_datetime(["2024-01-01", "2025-04-01", "2025-07-01", "2025-10-15", "2026-01-01"], utc=True)
cases["split"] = pd.cut(cases["start"], bins=bins, right=False,
                        labels=["train_P1", "valid_P1", "test_P2", "monitor_post_migration"])
# TODO: map linked transfer IDs to one cluster and force each cluster into a single split.
print(pd.crosstab(cases["status"], cases["split"], margins=True))  # grand total must be 48,000
# Baseline only (NOT primary): pm4py.split_train_test(df, train_percentage=0.8)

# --- 4. Pre-decision events only, then prefixes ------------------------------------
pre = df.merge(cases[["start", "t_decision"]], left_on=CID, right_index=True)
pre = pre[(pre[TS] < pre["t_decision"].fillna(EVENT_CUTOFF + pd.Timedelta(seconds=1)))
          & ~pre[ACT].isin(POST_DECISION)]
pk = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(pre, length=K, case_id_key=CID)   # shorter traces returned as-is
n_ev = pk.groupby(CID).size()
print(f"k={K}: eligible={int(n_ev.eq(K).sum())}, fewer than k pre-decision events={int(n_ev.lt(K).sum())}")
pk = pk[pk[CID].isin(n_ev[n_ev == K].index)]                       # no padding of short cases
lm_ok = cases.index[(cases["t_decision"].isna() | (cases["t_decision"] > cases["start"] + LANDMARK))
                    & (cases["start"] + LANDMARK <= EVENT_CUTOFF)]
lm = pre[pre[CID].isin(lm_ok) & (pre[TS] <= pre["start"] + LANDMARK)]

# --- 5. As-of features (only prefix rows + past-only system state) ------------------
_starts = np.sort(cases["start"].values.astype("int64"))
_decs = np.sort(cases["t_decision"].dropna().values.astype("int64"))

def prefix_features(p, t_pred):
    g = p.groupby(CID)
    f = pd.DataFrame({"trace": g[ACT].agg(" > ".join), "n_events": g.size(),
                      "n_doc_requests": g[ACT].agg(lambda s: int((s == "Request Documents").sum())),
                      "n_reviews": g[ACT].agg(lambda s: int((s == "Credit Review").sum())),
                      "risk_score_asof": g["risk_score"].last(),   # last non-null value <= t_pred
                      "n_resources": g["org:resource"].nunique(),
                      "last_resource": g["org:resource"].last()})
    t = t_pred.reindex(f.index)
    f["h_since_start"] = (t - g[TS].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    f["h_since_last_event"] = (t - g[TS].max()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    tp = t.values.astype("int64")                                   # WIP from events <= t only
    f["wip_at_pred"] = np.searchsorted(_starts, tp, "right") - np.searchsorted(_decs, tp, "right")
    # Not used: extract_temporal_features_dataframe (window aggregates see later same-window events);
    # insert_case_service_waiting_time (needs start timestamps, which this log lacks).
    return f.join(cases[["channel", "region", "amount", "regime", "split"]])

t_k = pk.groupby(CID)[TS].max()
t_lm = cases.loc[lm_ok, "start"] + LANDMARK
feat_k, feat_lm = prefix_features(pk, t_k), prefix_features(lm, t_lm)

# --- 6. Leakage audit ---------------------------------------------------------------
def leakage_audit(feat, rows, t_pred):
    assert not FORBIDDEN & set(feat.columns), "forbidden column in feature table"
    assert not rows[ACT].isin(POST_DECISION).any(), "post-decision event inside prefix"
    assert (rows.groupby(CID)[TS].max() <= t_pred.reindex(feat.index)).all(), "event after t_pred"
    dec = cases.loc[feat.index, "t_decision"]
    assert (dec.isna() | (dec > t_pred.reindex(feat.index))).all(), "decision before t_pred"
    # Non-null post-decision fields inside prefixes reveal warehouse back-filling
    return rows[["final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d"]].notna().mean()

print("k-prefix back-fill shares:\n", leakage_audit(feat_k, pk, t_k))
print("48h-landmark back-fill shares:\n", leakage_audit(feat_lm, lm, t_lm))
train_ids = feat_k.index[feat_k["split"] == "train_P1"]
test_ids = feat_k.index[feat_k["split"] == "test_P2"]
v_train = pm4py.get_variants(pk[pk[CID].isin(train_ids)], activity_key=ACT,
                             case_id_key=CID, timestamp_key=TS)
print("train k-prefix variants:", len(v_train), "| test cases with unseen prefix:",
      round(float((~feat_k.loc[test_ids, "trace"].isin(feat_k.loc[train_ids, "trace"])).mean()), 3))

# --- 7. Hooks (outside PM4Py) --------------------------------------------------------
# Stage 1: expand feat_lm x (s1_days, status) into person-period rows; fit multinomial
#          discrete-time cause-specific hazards; F_open_censored rows are censored, not negatives.
# Stage 2: activated cases with (s2_days, s2_event); weekly discrete-time default hazard;
#          IPW for B3 via P(outcome observed | channel, region, amount, month, regime).
# Combine: F_BAD = sum_s P(activation at s | H) * q(s, H); evaluate with IPCW Brier/AUC per split;
#          run delta-adjusted tipping-point for B3 and transfer-as-missing sensitivity.
```
