## 2. Rule Interpretation

- RR1: For the same case, the normalized human actor of `Draft_Change_Request` must not be the normalized human actor of `Approve_Change`.
- RR2: For the same case, the normalized human actor of `Implement_Change` must not be the normalized human actor of `Verify_Implementation`.
- RR3: For `HIGH`-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by the QM or a valid QM delegate. A delegation is valid only if its effective interval covers the event, its scope covers the change, the principal is absent/formally unavailable where required, and no prohibited subdelegation occurred.
- RR4: In a `HIGH`-risk emergency, `Approve_Change` may be performed by the IC or a valid IC delegate. A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. The emergency flag and safety rationale are accepted here as credible unless contradicted by evidence.
- RR5: A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance only when session evidence reliably maps the action to an authorized human. Aliases must be normalized before segregation checks.
- RR6: Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators, not proof of collusion.

Business-day count for RR4: Day 0 is the day of the emergency implementation. The next business day is day 1; the following business day is day 2. The deadline is 23:59 UTC on day 2. Weekends and site holidays are excluded; no holidays occurred in this window. Therefore, an emergency implemented on Tuesday 2026-08-11 has a Thursday 2026-08-13 deadline. CHG-505’s Friday 2026-08-14 QM review is late under either this convention or an inclusive convention.

Principal on leave: Leave is an administrative availability fact, not an automatic revocation of a standing role. If the principal genuinely performed the action, the technical role authority is still present. The main issue becomes identity and whether leave records were misleading. For delegations, the principal’s actual availability is relevant to the delegation’s absence condition.

Later database entry: A QMS record created after the event cannot by itself prove an earlier delegation. It can corroborate a contemporaneous signed document. D-QM-01 is supported by a signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09, before its effective interval, so the later migration record is not the primary proof.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Repeated Clara–Ben pair

- Facts: In the prior 60 days, Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes. They are in the same production cell.
- Benign explanation: During Asha’s leave, Ben was the only valid QM delegate for production equipment, and Clara was a frequent drafter. Staffing concentration alone can explain a high pair frequency.
- Adverse explanation: Clara and Ben may coordinate to reduce scrutiny and process changes more quickly than independent review would allow.
- Discriminating evidence: Compare review duration, comment depth, rejection rates, QA follow-ups, and whether the pair remains concentrated when another QM delegate is available. Use handover/working-together network edge weights and compare against a baseline.

Reciprocal approval pattern

- Facts: Ben drafted four LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved. No direct financial benefit is known.
- Benign explanation: Low-risk training documents can flow naturally between two qualified colleagues; reciprocity may be a simple workload artifact.
- Adverse explanation: There may be a quid-pro-quo arrangement in which each person approves the other’s work without substantive review.
- Discriminating evidence: Inspect approval comments, version histories, time-to-approval, and whether the pair approved each other’s changes immediately. Large-volume one-line approvals would support further investigation.

Shared workstations and service accounts

- Facts: CHG-503 `svc_qms` verification came from Diego’s workstation. CHG-509 `svc_qms` verification came from a verifier station with strong Eva attribution.
- Benign explanation: Service accounts are shared by design; Diego may have entered the room for unrelated work; Eva may have been the actual operator at the verifier station.
- Adverse explanation: Diego may have verified his own implementation through the service account; Eva may have approved and then verified the same case under a service identity.
- Discriminating evidence: Correlate badge times with exact session login/logout times, require individual FIDO re-authentication, and preserve CCTV or terminal-level audit records.

Absence conflicts

- Facts: Asha’s account was active during annual leave; Ben’s account was used during certified sick leave.
- Benign explanation: The named individuals may have genuinely worked while on leave; leave records may not have been updated.
- Adverse explanation: Credentials were shared, or someone else impersonated the absent employee.
- Discriminating evidence: MFA/device identity, network location, biometric verification, phone/calendar records, and confirmation of whether leave was formally canceled or amended.

Invalid subdelegation

- Facts: Farid attempted to pass D-IC-03 authority to Clara. D-IC-03 explicitly prohibits subdelegation.
- Benign explanation: Farid may have believed an emergency required immediate delegation and acted informally.
- Adverse explanation: Clara exercised emergency approval authority without authorization, and the same person drafted and approved the change.
- Discriminating evidence: Review the email thread, training records, whether Clara sought confirmation from Diego or Asha, and whether the emergency actually required Clara’s action.

These indicators are not automatic proof of collusion. They are screening signals requiring corroboration.

## 6. Control Improvements

- Delegation creation: Require delegation to be created in the QMS before it becomes effective. Prohibit subdelegation in the workflow itself, not only in a PDF.
- Effective-time recording: Use signed, timestamped delegation records with `valid_from` and `valid_until` stored in the controlled QMS. Migration records should never be used to backdate an earlier delegation.
- Principal availability: Integrate HR leave and sick-leave data with the QMS. Flag any log-in by a principal during a recorded absence and require a leave override or reconciliation.
- Alias management: Maintain a canonical employee identity with a clear alias crosswalk. Reject processes where the same human appears under two different aliases in the same case.
- Individual authentication: Replace generic service-account sessions with human-bound, re-authenticated sessions for approval and verification activities. Require FIDO or equivalent for critical steps.
- Emergency review: Add automatic escalation for `Retrospective_QM_Review`; calculate the two-business-day deadline from the implementation timestamp; assign named QM delegates by scope, including non-production equipment such as cooling.
- Pair rotation and network monitoring: Track handover and working-together networks as screening indicators. Use pair frequency, reciprocal approvals, and concentrated handovers to trigger sampling, not automatic accusation.
- Evidential retention: Retain signed delegation PDFs, QMS migration records, VPN/badge/FIDO logs, CCTV where available, and raw session identifiers so ambiguous cases can be resolved later.
