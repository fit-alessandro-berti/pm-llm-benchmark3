# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Assumptions used (explicit):
- **A1**: A job is “eligible” when it has arrived *and* is dispatchable; we only observe arrival scans (which may lag true arrival by up to 2 minutes). Thus, eligibility sets below are **plausible**, not certain.
- **A2**: For appointment jobs, the simplest policy is “do not start before appointment; try to start at/after appointment within some tolerance window,” but tolerance is unknown.
- **A3**: `est_min` is what dispatch sees; in this trace, realized segment lengths match `est_min` exactly, so SPT predictions are unusually sharp.

- The **08:32** and **09:32** choices strongly reject priority and SPT as primary rules (both would have advanced priority-1 jobs earlier and/or the shortest job).
- The **08:20** choice is best explained by **appointment gating** (start the 08:20 appointment), with FIFO used as the default when no appointment is immediately due.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

Primary diagnosis: **FIFO** with **appointment gating** as an override.

- Supporting IDs/episodes:
  - **A02, A05, A07**: start order after 08:32 follows arrival order among non-appointment jobs (FIFO-like).
  - **A03**: served exactly at its appointment time (08:20), consistent with an appointment override layer.
  - **A07 before A08**: explicit evidence that priority is not the main sequencing rule.
- Challenging IDs/episodes:
  - **A03 vs A02/A04 at 08:20**: violates pure FIFO; also contradicts both strict priority and strict SPT. This can be explained by appointment gating, but only if (i) appointments are binding enough to override, and (ii) A04 was eligible/visible.
  - **A06 appointment started 09:20 (late)**: challenges a “strict appointment-first” interpretation; consistent with a tolerance window or FIFO fill-in that can cause small delays.

## QB

Primary diagnosis: **FIFO-like selection among waiting jobs**, plus **discretionary urgent preemptions** and **resume-in-progress bias**.

- Supporting IDs/episodes:
  - **B02**: B01 is interrupted and B02 starts immediately at 09:16 (urgent-interrupt behavior).
  - **B01**: resumes after interruptions; total service time matches its estimate (45), consistent with **preemptive-resume** rather than restart.
  - **B04 at 10:28**: chosen ahead of higher-priority B06 and shorter appointment job B07 → FIFO dominates selection among waiting jobs late in the trace.
- Challenging IDs/episodes:
  - **09:48 chooses B03 while B05 (prio-1) exists**: if B05 was eligible then, strict priority-at-dispatch is violated; suggests eligibility delay, manual exception, or that “priority” is not systematically used except for certain urgent types.
  - **B07 appointment 10:30 not honored**: contradicts appointment scheduling as a meaningful rule in QB.

## QC

Primary diagnosis: **SPT/SEPT early**, with **deadline/priority override** and possible **aging** later.

- Supporting IDs/episodes:
  - **C02 first at 10:10** and the early sequence **C02 → C03 → C04**: consistent with choosing the shortest estimated job among the backlog.
  - **C05 at 10:54**: consistent with an “SLA rescue” override keyed on priority/deadline risk.
  - **C06 at 11:34**: consistent with a return to SPT when no overriding emergency dominates.
- Challenging IDs/episodes:
  - **C05 not chosen at 10:32** (despite prio-1 and the earliest deadline): challenges a pure deadline/priority discipline; can be explained if C05 was not yet dispatchable/visible.
  - **C01 chosen at 11:40 over C07 (prio-2, earlier deadline, shorter)**: challenges both SPT and deadline-based rules; suggests anti-starvation/aging or C07 ineligibility.

## QD

Primary diagnosis: **skill-based routing** with FIFO/age behavior within eligible sets; appointments and resource calendars matter.

- Supporting IDs/episodes:
  - **D05**: only fully qualified on D-F and is executed there immediately after D-F’s meeting ends (12:20).
  - **D06**: appointment at 11:30 starts exactly at 11:30 (strong appointment compliance in QD).
  - **D07 before D10 on D-X**: supports FIFO/age over priority and SPT within the D-X-eligible pool.
  - **D10 at 12:16 on D-X**: consistent with skill-constrained fallback because D-F is unavailable until 12:20.
- Challenging IDs/episodes:
  - **D-X idle 11:20–11:30**: if D-X was truly available and eligible jobs existed, a work-conserving FIFO/priority/SPT policy would start something earlier; suggests reservation for appointments, unlogged setup/travel, or missing queue-entry information.
  - **D-F idle 11:36–12:00 despite eligible work**: suggests meeting-prep, reservation, or unlogged activities; otherwise contradicts work-conserving dispatch.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Episode 1 (QC, 10:32): C04 chosen over C05

Competing explanations:
- **E1: SPT/SEPT primary** (with deadlines not used unless “critical”): would pick **C04 (22)** over **C05 (40)**.
- **E2: Deadline/priority primary**: would pick **C05 (prio-1, earliest deadline 10:50)**.

Observed: **C04**.

One additional observable that would discriminate:
- **`queue_entry_ts` / “dispatchable_from_ts” for C05**, or a boolean **`released_to_dispatcher`** at 10:32. If C05 was not dispatchable (triage/parts/authorization), E2 is not falsified by this choice. If C05 was dispatchable, E1 gains strong support.

## Episode 2 (QB, 09:31): resume B01 vs start B03

Competing explanations:
- **E1: Resume-in-progress bias** (setup/context costs): after an urgent interruption ends, resume the paused job unless another *urgent* class exists.
- **E2: Strict priority scheduling** (non-preemptive when idle, but priority governs next selection): would start **B03 (prio-2)** before resuming **B01 (prio-3)**.

Observed: **resume B01**.

One additional observable that would discriminate:
- A policy field or event attribute **`interrupted_job_position`** ∈ {resume-immediately, requeue-front, requeue-back} plus a **remaining-time estimate** at 09:31. If the policy is “resume-immediately,” the observation is expected under E1 even when higher-priority jobs wait.

## Episode 3 (QD, 11:20): D-X idle despite backlog

Competing explanations:
- **E1: Appointment reservation**: D-X is intentionally held so that appointment D06 begins exactly at 11:30 (and/or to keep capacity for dual-skill work).
- **E2: Hidden unavailability/unlogged work**: D-X is “available” in roster terms but not actually dispatchable (setup, travel, tool calibration, ad-hoc task).

Observed: **no service until 11:30**.

One additional observable that would discriminate:
- A per-minute **resource state log** for D-X (idle/available vs busy/setup/away) and an explicit **appointment tolerance** (“can start early?”). If D-X was truly idle and D06 was not allowed to start early, E1 is favored.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked (most → least plausible), with impact on diagnosis:

1. **Queue-entry / dispatchability delay after arrival scan (triage, paperwork, parts, authorization)**  
   - Why plausible: many “violations” disappear if some jobs were not yet eligible when they appear by arrival time (e.g., **B05 at 09:48**, **C05 at 10:32**, **C07 at 11:40**).  
   - Effect: would make FIFO/SPT/priority inference substantially less certain; would particularly weaken claims that QC “ignored” priority early.

2. **Unlogged resource states (setup, micro-breaks, meeting prep, travel, secondary tasks)**  
   - Why plausible: explicit gaps exist (e.g., QA 08:30–08:32; QD D-X 11:20–11:30; QD D-F 11:36–12:20).  
   - Effect: could turn some “policy” signals into artifacts of missing work; especially important for QD where idles drive ambiguity.

3. **Policy changes by SLA risk (“rescue mode”) or dispatcher overrides**  
   - Why plausible: QC shows a clean shift at **10:54** consistent with “deadline endangered” behavior; QB interruptions have stated reasons.  
   - Effect: supports diagnosing QC as hybrid; in QB it supports treating preemptions as discretionary rather than a single consistent discipline.

4. **Different local subqueues per resource / preference routing (especially in QD)**  
   - Why plausible: multi-skill routing often uses primary assignment (mechanical→D-M, firmware→D-F) and only spills to flexible resources under rules not observed.  
   - Effect: would strengthen “skill-based routing” while making FIFO/priority within QD harder to infer without per-resource queue views.

5. **Noisy or strategically inaccurate `est_min` (gaming SPT), or dynamic estimate updates**  
   - Why plausible in general, but here realized times equal `est_min` exactly.  
   - Effect: if estimates were manipulated, QC’s apparent SPT could be illusory; however, the data provided gives little direct support for this.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized collection plan (tailored to these ambiguities):

1. **Dispatchability / eligibility timeline**
   - `queue_entry_ts` (when job becomes dispatchable) distinct from arrival scan
   - `eligibility_block_reason` (parts missing, authorization pending, diagnostics running, customer not confirmed)
   - `eligible_set_snapshot` recorded at each dispatch decision (IDs and why eligible/ineligible)

2. **Dispatch decision logging**
   - `dispatch_decision_ts` for every assignment and every deliberate “idle/hold” decision
   - `selected_job_id` plus `ranked_candidates` (or at least top-3) and a `selection_reason_code` (FIFO, appointment, priority, SLA rescue, manual override)
   - `policy_version` / configuration hash active at that time

3. **Appointments as windows**
   - `appointment_earliest_start`, `appointment_latest_start`, and tolerance policy (“can start early?”, “late threshold”)
   - `appointment_priority_override` flag (does appointment outrank prio-1? or only within same class?)

4. **Preemption/resumption semantics (critical for QB)**
   - `preempt_ts`, `preempt_reason_code`, `preempting_job_id`
   - resume policy: `resume_mode` ∈ {resume-immediately, requeue-front, requeue-by-arrival, restart}
   - `remaining_est_min` at preemption/resume and any `context_switch_cost_est`

5. **Resource state and calendar fidelity (critical for QD)**
   - per-minute `resource_state` (available/idle/busy/setup/away/meeting)
   - explicit calendar events with enforceability (hard stop vs soft)
   - location/tool constraints if they exist (e.g., certain benches for firmware)

6. **Deadline/SLA visibility and computation (critical for QC)**
   - `deadline_visible_ts` and whether dispatcher sees it
   - computed risk metrics at decision time: `slack = deadline - now - est_remaining`, plus “rescue threshold” parameters

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable queue**: **QD**, because skill requirements and resource calendars create strong, observable constraints (e.g., D05→D-F after meeting). Even with missing state logs, “skill-based routing” is hard to explain away.
- **Least identifiable queue**: **QC**, because the trace is consistent with multiple hybrids (SPT + SLA rescue + aging), and because unseen eligibility/deadline visibility could flip key interpretations (C05 and C07 episodes).
- **Most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone**: **strict priority scheduling** (especially treating “priority explains every FIFO violation”). Across queues, many apparent priority effects can be confounded by appointments, eligibility delays, resume-in-progress bias, and skill constraints; inferring “priority-first” without eligibility and decision logs would likely be wrong.
