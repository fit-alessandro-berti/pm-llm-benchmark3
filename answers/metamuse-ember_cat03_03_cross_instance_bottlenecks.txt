## 1. Interaction-graph description

The appropriate graph is not a Job-only process graph, but a typed object-interaction graph whose nodes are object instances and whose edges carry qualifiers such as *requires, contains, batches, consolidates, uses,* and *processed-on*. Important object types are **Job** (J1–J6), **Component** (C1–C6), **ComponentLot** (L7, L8), **Machine** (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), **Tool** (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), **Batch** (B1, B2), and **Shipment** (S1–S3). Coupling arises mainly through shared Batch, Tool, ComponentLot, and Shipment nodes, not through direct Job-to-Job hand-offs.

Compact edge list:

```text
J1-requires->C1; J2-requires->C2; J3-requires->C3;
J4-requires->C4; J5-requires->C5; J6-requires->C6
L7-contains->C2,C5; L8-contains->C1,C3
B1-batches->J1,J2,J3; B2-batches->J4,J5
S1-consolidates->J1,J4; S2-consolidates->J2,J3; S3-consolidates->J5,J6
B1/B2 -uses-on-> OVEN-1 with RACK-7       [event-derived]
J3/J4 -uses-> FIX-A on CNC-2             [event-derived / inferred hold]
J5/J6 -uses-> CAL-3 on TEST-1            [event-derived]
L7 -affects/inconsistently-linked-> J2,J5 [partially observed]
J1 <-> J4 via S1; J5 <-> J6 via S3        [partner coupling]
```

Higher-order motifs involving at least three object types:

1. **Batch synchronization barrier:** Job–Component–ComponentLot–Batch–Machine/Tool. J1, J2, and J3 cannot enter B1’s cure until the batch starts, although their component readiness times differ.
2. **Tool-mediated machine contention:** Job–Tool–Machine. J3’s hold on FIX-A is temporally adjacent to J4’s delayed CNC-2 start; similarly, CAL-3 serializes J5 and J6 on TEST-1.
3. **Shared-lot fork:** ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch. L7 contains C2 and C5, linking J2 and J5, but J2 receives a supervisor override into B1 while J5 follows formal release.
4. **Consolidation sink:** Job–Batch–Machine–Shipment. J1 is packed early but remains coupled to J4 through S1; J5 is coupled to J6 through S3.
5. **Calibration serialization:** Job–Tool–Machine. Every Final Test needs CAL-3, so TEST-1 can be idle while a ready Job still cannot test.

**Direct observations** are the selected O2O relations and event relations, such as the 12:05 Batch Cure event relating B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3, and C1–C3. **Inferred edges** include “J4 waited for FIX-A,” “RACK-7 prevented an earlier B2 start,” and “CAL-3 recalibration, rather than TEST-1 capacity, delayed J6.” Those inferences are plausible because timestamps align, but there is no explicit `Wait for Tool` event and no controlled counterfactual.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

The calculations below quantify *candidate* waiting, not proven causal effects.

1. **B1 synchronization for J1 and J2.** B1 starts at 12:05; J1 was ready at 08:10 and J2 at 08:25. Thus J1’s synchronization wait is **3 h 55 min (235 min)** and J2’s is **3 h 40 min (220 min)**, versus 10 min for J3. Total batch-attributable job waiting is 465 job-minutes. *Counterfactual:* J1/J2 could have been cured earlier in a partial or separate batch. *Caveat:* this assumes “ready” means oven-ready and that an earlier cure was technically and economically feasible. If chemistry, oven preheating, or a fixed campaign prevented that, the waiting is policy-induced rather than purely avoidable, but still synchronization-induced.

2. **FIX-A and J4 on CNC-2.** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40; J4’s Machine Process starts at 11:45. J4’s arrival-to-start interval is **1 h 55 min (115 min)**. If only about five minutes of setup were needed after 09:50, the plausible tool-associated delay is roughly **110 min**. *Counterfactual:* FIX-A was free at J4’s arrival and J4 was otherwise ready. *Caveat:* no waiting event is recorded; CNC-2 is explicitly idle only from 10:05 to 10:47, and J4 might not have been fully ready before 11:45.

3. **S1 consolidation and J1.** J1 is packed at 13:30, J4 at 19:10, and S1 dispatches at 19:25. J1 therefore waits **5 h 55 min (355 min)** after packing. Of this, **5 h 40 min (340 min)** is waiting for J4 to be packed and 15 minutes is post-partner loading/dispatch. *Counterfactual:* J1 could ship alone shortly after packing, say around 13:45. *Caveat:* the contract may require joint shipment; also, S1’s 15-minute tail is shorter than S2’s 25 minutes and S3’s 35 minutes, so the large delay is partner readiness, not loading.

4. **CAL-3 and J6.** J6 is ready for final test at 16:05 and tests 17:25–17:50, a **80-minute** ready-to-test interval. J5 tests 16:10–16:35, CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20 (**45 min**), and TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25 (**50 min**). *Counterfactual A:* without recalibration between J5 and J6, J6 might start near 16:35, saving about **50 min**. *Counterfactual B:* with a second CAL-3, J6 might start around 16:10, saving about **75 min**, unless TEST-1 itself is the binding server. The defensible range is therefore **45–75 min**, not the full 80 minutes.

5. **B2, J4/J5, and RACK-7.** OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50 (**115 min**). J4 is ready at 13:10 and waits **100 min** until 14:50; J5 waits only **10 min** from 14:40. RACK-7 is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. *Counterfactual:* if J5 had been ready at 14:35, B2 could perhaps have started then, saving J4 about 75 minutes relative to 14:50. But because J5 was not ready until 14:40, RACK-7 alone cannot explain the 14:50 start; synchronization to J5 explains J4’s 13:10–14:40 interval (**90 min**), while RACK-7 explains why no earlier single-job or partial cure could use the oven before 14:35.

6. **L7 hold.** The L7 quarantine at 10:20 to release at 13:50 is **3 h 30 min (210 min)**. J2 nevertheless enters B1 at 12:05 through an override, while J5 proceeds only after release and is batch-ready at 14:40. *Counterfactual:* without the lot hold, J5 might have been ready earlier. *Caveat:* the evidence does not show when J5 would otherwise have been ready, and J2’s override proves the formal hold was not an absolute physical barrier for every job.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job flattening.** *Visible:* the canonical ReleaseStageMachineInspectPackDispatch flow, two rework loops, and median stage times. *Hidden/duplicated:* Batch partners, tool holds, lot release, and shipment partners; the same Batch Cure event is duplicated across J1–J3. *Misleading conclusion:* because no job is more than 1.8 SD above the mean and median edges look short, managers may conclude individual jobs are “normal,” missing that J1’s 235-minute batch wait and 355-minute shipment wait are cross-object effects.

**Machine flattening.** *Visible:* typical activity sequences and utilization: CNC-2 54%, OVEN-1 44%, TEST-1 58%. *Hidden/duplicated:* which job is waiting, for which tool/batch/lot, and whether a gap is setup, cleaning, recalibration, or synchronization. *Misleading conclusion:* idle machines appear to prove excess capacity; in fact OVEN-1 can be idle because RACK-7 is cleaning, CNC-2 idle because FIX-A is held, and TEST-1 idle because CAL-3 is recalibrating.

**Batch flattening.** *Visible:* B1 and B2 as oven campaigns and their cure times. *Hidden/duplicated:* divergent job readiness inside each batch and the fact that one cure event serves three jobs. *Misleading conclusion:* counting two batch events makes oven work look small, while the job-level impact is 465 job-minutes in B1 alone.

**Tool flattening.** *Visible:* acquisition, use, and release of FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3 if those events are retained. *Hidden/duplicated:* unrecorded waiting for a tool because there is no `Wait for Tool` activity. *Misleading conclusion:* a tool may appear lightly used while it is the binding constraint, as with CAL-3 recalibration from 16:35 to 17:20.

**ComponentLot flattening.** *Visible:* L7/L8 containment and quarantine/release if lot objects are retained. *Hidden/duplicated:* inconsistent links to Jobs; the supplied Job flattening omits L7 events entirely. *Misleading conclusion:* J2 and J5 look independent even though both share L7 and the override creates divergent paths.

**Shipment flattening.** *Visible:* CreateLoadDispatch medians of 0.8 h and 0.3 h. *Hidden/duplicated:* time a packed job spends waiting for its partner. *Misleading conclusion:* shipments look fast, while J1 spends 355 minutes packed-but-undispatched.

Low machine utilization can coexist with long job waiting because utilization measures machine busy time, whereas job lead time includes waiting for complementary objects and policy barriers. A job may be waiting for a batch partner, a tool, a lot release, calibration, or a shipment partner while the machine is idle or processing another object. Idle time is therefore not proof of slack; it may be proof that the *wrong* complementary resource is unavailable.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J3/FIX-A  J4  B2  S1  J1.**  
- *Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40; J4 arrives at CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts at 11:45; J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10; B2 starts at 14:50; J4 packs at 19:10; S1 dispatches at 19:25, while J1 had packed at 13:30.  
- *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 is idle during part of J4’s wait, making FIX-A scarcity feasible as a cause.  
- *Policy constraints:* B2 requires J4 and J5 together; S1 requires J1 and J4 together.  
- *Causal speculation:* FIX-A contention plausibly delayed J4, but J4’s 14:50–19:10 interval is not fully explained, so FIX-A cannot be assigned the entire S1 delay.

**Narrative B: L7  C2/C5  J2/J5  B1/B2.**  
- *Temporal precedence:* L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50; J2 enters B1 at 12:05 through override; J5 is ready for B2 at 14:40.  
- *Resource feasibility:* C2 and C5 share L7, so a lot-level block is feasible for both.  
- *Policy constraints:* formal release applies to J5, while a supervisor override bypasses it for J2.  
- *Causal speculation:* the lot couples the jobs administratively, but because J2 proceeds before release and J5 is ready only after release, the realized delay attributable to L7 remains unproven.

**Narrative C: J5/J6  CAL-3  TEST-1  S3.**  
- *Temporal precedence:* J5 and J6 are ready at 16:00/16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50.  
- *Resource feasibility:* TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25 while J6 is ready, and every Final Test requires CAL-3, so tool unavailability is feasible despite machine availability.  
- *Policy constraints:* recalibration may be mandatory, but the evidence does not state its trigger.  
- *Causal speculation:* CAL-3 plausibly serializes the jobs; however, S3’s dispatch is measured from last packing, not last testing, so test delay should not be conflated with shipment dwell without pack times.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Threshold/partial batching.** *Target:* B1/B2 synchronization. *Benefit:* start B1 after a maximum wait, e.g. 30–60 min, or when two jobs are ready. *Helped:* J1, J2, J4. *Potentially harmed:* J3, J5, OVEN-1 efficiency, and cure consistency. *Cost/risk:* extra cures, energy, and possible quality variation. *Perverse metric:* batch fill rate encourages indefinite waiting. *Evaluation:* object-centric comparison of per-Job ready-to-batch-start lag, batches per job, energy per job, and downstream pack/dispatch dwell before/after.

2. **Tool duplication and reservation.** *Target:* FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7 contention. *Benefit:* second FIX-A or scheduled FIX-A priority for J4-like jobs; recalibrate CAL-3 outside ready windows or add a second CAL-3; parallel RACK-7 cleaning. *Helped:* J4, J6, B2 jobs. *Potentially harmed:* J3 or J5 if reservations deprioritize them; calibration quality if rushed. *Cost/risk:* capital, maintenance, and calibration drift. *Perverse metric:* tool utilization encourages keeping one tool busy rather than minimizing job wait. *Evaluation:* per-Job tool-wait intervals, TEST-1 idle-while-ready minutes, CNC-2 idle-while-ready minutes, and defect/rework rates.

3. **Shipment max-dwell/split policy.** *Target:* S1/S3 consolidation. *Benefit:* dispatch a packed job if its partner is not ready within a threshold, or split S1. *Helped:* J1 and potentially J5. *Potentially harmed:* J4/J6 joint-delivery expectations and freight cost. *Cost/risk:* extra shipments and customer inconvenience. *Perverse metric:* on-time dispatch percentage can encourage premature splitting or delayed packing timestamps. *Evaluation:* pack-to-dispatch dwell by Job, shipment cost per Job, split-shipment rate, and customer-complete-delivery rate.

4. **Logging integration rather than operational change.** *Target:* missing Wait-for-Tool/Batch/Lot/Shipment events, L7-to-Job links, override reasons, RACK-7 state. *Benefit:* makes causes observable and prevents false machine-capacity conclusions. *Helped:* all jobs analytically. *Potentially harmed:* none physically, but staff may face recording burden. *Cost/risk:* misclassification and privacy/surveillance concerns. *Perverse metric:* event-completeness counts can encourage low-quality “wait” events. *Evaluation:* OCEL join completeness, proportion of waiting intervals with reason codes, and discrepancy between Job-flattened and object-centric DFGs.

5. **Lot-release governance.** *Target:* L7 quarantine coupling. *Benefit:* expedite review, partial release by component risk, or pre-staging while quarantined. *Helped:* J5 formally; J2 by reducing need for override. *Potentially harmed:* quality if release is rushed. *Cost/risk:* QA labor and defect risk. *Perverse metric:* average release time can encourage rubber-stamping. *Evaluation:* lot hold duration per component/job, override rate, downstream defects, and rework loops.

6. **Critical-path sequencing.** *Target:* interaction between batch, tool, and shipment deadlines. *Benefit:* prioritize J4-like jobs that block S1, avoid recalibration between ready jobs, and sequence oven batches by shipment criticality. *Helped:* J4, J1 via S1, J6. *Potentially harmed:* J3/J5 if deprioritized. *Cost/risk:* starvation and complex scheduling. *Perverse metric:* machine utilization favors easy jobs over critical-path jobs. *Evaluation:* shipment-level completion time, per-Job waiting by cause, and starvation measures such as maximum wait per Job.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. B1 could not legally or technically start before J3 because the cure recipe requires all three components together.
2. J1/J2 “ready” timestamps are planning times, not physical oven-ready times; actual material or fixture readiness was closer to 12:05.
3. OVEN-1 preheating or RACK-7 cleaning would have prevented a start before about 12:00 anyway.
4. Earlier partial cures were tried and caused defects, rework, or higher cost exceeding the waiting benefit.
5. J1/J2 would not have packed or dispatched earlier even if cured earlier because downstream inspection, testing, or shipment partners were still late.
6. B2’s 14:50 start is explained entirely by RACK-7 cleaning until 14:35 plus a fixed 15-minute setup, not by J5 synchronization.
7. Splitting batches would require additional oven capacity that does not exist, making synchronization a capacity constraint in disguise.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. Explicit `Wait for Batch`, `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Lot Release`, and `Wait for Shipment Partner` events with start/end timestamps and reason codes.
2. Tool acquire/release events for every tool use, including FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3, with holder and next user.
3. Batch metadata: expected batch size, minimum fill, cure recipe constraints, preheat start, and whether partial batches are allowed.
4. RACK-7 state transitions: available, in clean, quarantine start/end, and cleaning required after each batch.
5. Lot quarantine reason, release criteria, partial-release flags, override author, override justification, and quality outcome.
6. Calibration due time, recalibration trigger, CAL-3 expiration, and whether recalibration can be deferred or parallelized.
7. Shipment contract terms: joint-delivery requirement, split cost, cutoff time, and maximum packed-job dwell.
8. Job readiness definition: component staged, fixture available, machine setup complete, and batch-ready as separate timestamps.
9. Machine setup/changeover times and oven load limits, to separate capacity from policy.
10. Downstream pack and dispatch timestamps per Job, so shipment dwell is not inferred from shipment creation.
