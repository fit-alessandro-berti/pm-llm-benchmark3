## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register  Triage
Triage  Identity Check
Triage  Originals Receipt, where originals are required
Originals Receipt  Document Check, where originals are required
Triage  Document Check, where originals are not required
Identity Check  Assessment
Document Check  Assessment
Assessment(i)  Request More Info(i), if issued
Request More Info(i)  Document Check(i+1)
Document Check(i+1)  Assessment(i+1)
Assessment(final)  Compliance Review(final), for high-risk or high-value cases
Compliance Review(final)  Decision, for high-risk or high-value cases
Assessment(final)  Decision, for non-high-risk/non-high-value cases
Decision  Notify
```

```text
Identity Check and Document Check may overlap only if originals are not required.
A preliminary escalation/compliance screen may overlap verification, but cannot replace final Compliance Review.
Final Compliance Review must use the latest Assessment evidence or explicitly reaffirm it.
The performer of final Assessment cannot perform final Compliance Review.
A resource cannot execute overlapping service intervals.
A Request More Info requires a subsequent Document Check and Assessment before Decision.
Suppressing a Request More Info loop changes the admissible decision policy: unresolved evidence must be conditioned, declined, escalated, or otherwise explicitly dispositioned.
Queue values are forecasts; task readiness does not guarantee immediate service.
Real-time timestamps are evidence of concurrency only if task ownership, locks, and service intervals also permit it.
```

K-104 changes timing and preparation, while also introducing provisional capacity reservation. K-219 changes document-resource routing. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by preventing further evidence collection. K-411 changes timing and task-release behavior through controlled parallel routing; it should not change verification standards.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The major delay was not verification alone. D1’s complex-document queue delayed evidence completion, and the sanctions issue was only operationalized at Assessment. C1 then received a fully formed high-risk case late and had to conduct a largely cold review while its queue remained constrained.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create an advisory packet for S1 and a provisional C1 work item containing the ambiguity, customer identifiers, and anticipated document set. This is preparation only; it is not a final compliance decision and does not bypass final Assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** Identity and D1 document work remain prerequisites for final Assessment. However, S1 can frame questions early and C1 can review available sanctions data before documents finish. Once A1 completes Assessment, C1 needs to reconcile assessment conclusions and newly complete evidence rather than start from zero. The gain is therefore mainly reduced compliance queue/service exposure after Tuesday 19:10, not removal of the D1 or A1 waits.

**Process-feasibility check:** No-originals rules permit concurrent verification and preliminary work. Final Assessment remains before final Compliance Review. A1 performs final Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance Review, satisfying four-eyes. A reservation must not be treated as completed compliance.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is a short final C1 reconciliation and same-day Tuesday or early Wednesday decision. Base case is a several-hour-to-half-day improvement. Worst case is that early material is too incomplete, C1 re-reads it, and reserved capacity displaces an urgent case without helping K-104.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The conclusion weakens if C1’s actual queue discipline prevents reservations, if sanctions evidence cannot be reviewed before final documents, or if S1’s early advice rarely changes final C1 effort.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Originals constrained document checking until Mon 17:30. D1 then did not start until Tue 09:30, but gave an accurate four-hour check. The intervention trades D1’s later specialist start for D2’s potentially earlier but less certain handwritten-document review.

**Minimal intervention:** Release a D2 task immediately upon Originals Receipt, with the same document-check standard and a defined escalation rule to D1 if handwriting is illegible or materially ambiguous.

**Temporal propagation:** The earliest possible document completion moves forward only if D2 has a real service interval after receipt. A D2 review may take longer than D1’s four hours, but can still finish Monday night or early Tuesday, making the case assessment-ready earlier. Whether final completion improves depends on A2 and M1 queue availability; earlier readiness may simply wait in Assessment.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check. Identity can occur before or overlap the receipt/check sequence but both verification checks complete before Assessment. No compliance review is required for this non-high-risk, non-high-value case.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is legible handwriting, early D2 completion, and an earlier A2 slot. Base case is modest acceleration with unchanged approval. Worst case is D2 requests clarification, creating a new document check and assessment cycle that exceeds the original timeline.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Prefer D1 if measured D2 availability after receipt is poor, D2 handwriting clarification rates are high, or D1’s next-available time is often earlier than the factual case suggests.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The second loop was costly because it began Friday, depended on an inconsistent external supplier, crossed a weekend, and triggered a third document check and assessment. It also resolved the remaining discrepancy that allowed the factual approval.

**Minimal intervention:** At Assessment 2, prohibit another information request, but require A1 to document the discrepancy, its materiality, and proposed disposition. C1 then conducts a final review of that evidence state, and M1 makes an explicit reject, conditional-approve, or risk-accepted decision.

**Temporal propagation:** Eliminating Request More Info 2 removes the external wait from Friday to Monday, the Monday D1 activity, and Tuesday A1 reassessment. The case can enter compliance after Assessment 2, subject to C1 availability. This is not a pure scheduling improvement: it substitutes a decision under residual uncertainty for evidence resolution.

**Process-feasibility check:** The standard rework rule applies only after a Request More Info is issued. Here no second request occurs. Since the case is high risk and value, a final C1 review remains mandatory after Assessment 2; C1 is distinct from A1. Decision must explicitly address the unresolved inconsistency.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is a defensible conditional approval with controls or a prompt rejection, delivered several days earlier. Base case is a faster but more restrictive decision. Worst case is an unsafe approval, later loss or compliance finding, or an avoidable rejection followed by appeal.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The intervention should not be used if policy requires confirmation for this discrepancy type, if its value cannot be bounded by conditions, or if historical second confirmations frequently reveal material adverse facts.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The case lock created artificial serialization: D2’s task was not created until I2 completed. Since documents were digital and originals were unnecessary, this was a system-control constraint rather than a normative dependency.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create independently owned I2 and D2 tasks under a controlled workaround, with immutable start/complete events, lock ownership, and reconciliation before Assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** D2 can begin before I2 completes, so the case becomes verification-ready at the later of the two real completions rather than after sequential completion. In the factual trace that can move readiness from Tue 19:30 to around the early afternoon. Yet A2’s Wednesday queue can absorb most of the gain, leaving final timing much less improved than verification timing.

**Process-feasibility check:** The no-originals rule permits concurrent Identity and Document Checks. Assessment remains blocked until both are complete. There is no compliance requirement. Real-time timestamps must reflect actual work, not delayed batch posting.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is genuine overlap plus an available earlier A2 slot. Base case is earlier readiness but a similar Wednesday assessment. Worst case is no completion benefit and a control defect requiring remediation or repeat verification.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The change is unattractive if simultaneous system access causes conflicting evidence states, if D2’s genuine capacity is absent, or if the assessment queue uses strict FIFO such that readiness does not affect service order.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation/compliance preparation | Earlier S1 work; fragmented and reserved C1 work, possibly lower final C1 effort. | K-104’s late compliance wait; potentially final C1 service time. | C1 reserved-capacity queue; possibly S1 advisory queue. | Urgent high-risk cases competing for C1. | Ambiguous: positive only if preparation materially avoids final review work. | A small number of reservations can consume the only C1 slack and sharply delay urgent arrivals. |
| K-219 D2 routing | D1 demand falls; D2 demand rises; rework probability may rise. | D1 queue, especially for complex work. | D2 queue and, if clarification occurs, document/assessment rework queues. | D2’s ordinary cases; D1 complex corporate cases may benefit. | Ambiguous to mildly negative at scale without eligibility screening. | Rework has a threshold effect: one clarification creates external delay plus repeated downstream work. |
| K-337 suppress second loop | Avoids D1, A1, and external-response demand; may increase C1/M1 exception-review effort. | D1 and A1 queues; external follow-up backlog. | C1/M1 exception or escalation workload; downstream monitoring. | Other cases waiting for D1/A1; compliance cases needing C1. | Operationally positive, risk-adjusted ambiguous or negative. | Weekend timing makes a removed request worth far more than its service time; risk losses are tail-driven. |
| K-411 parallel verification | Same I2/D2 service per case, but earlier concurrent release and higher simultaneous WIP. | Case-lock backlog and verification-ready delay. | Assessment queue; simultaneous task-management load. | Cases already waiting for A2; other I2/D2 tasks if priority changes. | Usually positive if assessment capacity is protected. | When early releases exceed A2 capacity, benefit collapses into an assessment queue. |

Applied to 100 similar cases in one week, these interventions cannot be evaluated as independent local improvements. Reserving provisional C1 capacity for K-104-like cases is especially sensitive because C1 is the sole certified reviewer. Even if preliminary work reduces final review time, widespread reservation can block urgent arrivals and create a priority-management problem. A capped reservation pool, expiry rule, and triage threshold would be necessary.

Routing K-219-like work from D1 to D2 shifts rather than removes capacity. D1 may gain useful capacity for genuinely complex corporate files, but D2 can become saturated quickly. If handwritten cases have even a moderately higher clarification rate, each failure produces external delay, another document check, and another assessment; aggregate rework can outweigh earlier starts.

Suppressing K-337’s second loop removes demand from D1 and A1 and avoids supplier waits, especially weekend-induced calendar delay. But it transfers work and risk into C1, M1, conditions management, appeals, and potential losses. It should be restricted to discrepancies with pre-approved bounded-risk disposition options, not made a blanket speed policy.

K-411-like parallelization produces true benefit only where I2 and D2 actually have available intervals and assessment can absorb earlier-ready work. Otherwise, the bottleneck migrates to A2 and waiting merely moves downstream. Real-time timestamps improve observability, but timestamps alone do not prove concurrency: task ownership, lock records, and non-overlapping resource intervals must agree. External-response and weekend effects further mean service-hour savings are not proportional to calendar-time savings.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411: digital, standard, no originals, low-risk/low-value cases with independent identity/document evidence. | K-104: high-risk/high-value cases with a triage-detectable compliance ambiguity and a defined preliminary packet template. |
| assignment rule | Randomize eligible cases by arrival block, stratified by day and expected A2 load; preserve normal priority rules. | Randomize only within a capped daily number of eligible cases; stratify by urgency and forecast C1 load. |
| sample/support logic | Start with 20–30 cases plus matched controls; require IT support, operational owner, and audit observer. | Start with 15–25 cases plus matched controls; reserve no more than a pre-set share of C1 capacity. |
| primary outcome | End-to-end Register-to-Notify elapsed time, reported with assessment-queue wait separately. | End-to-end elapsed time and final C1 review effort, net of reservation occupancy. |
| process-path outcome | Share with genuine overlapping I2/D2 intervals; verification-ready time; Assessment wait. | Share reaching final review with reusable preliminary work; time from Assessment completion to final compliance completion. |
| quality outcome | Verification defects, duplicate evidence, reopened cases, and audit exceptions. | Compliance findings, condition quality, reversals, escalations, and post-decision issue rate. |
| queue-spillover outcome | A2 wait for non-pilot cases; I2/D2 delays by class; WIP and priority inversions. | C1 waits for urgent non-pilot cases, reservation expiry/waste, and S1 workload. |
| logging validation | Reconcile real-time events to system locks, task ownership, resource calendars, and evidence versions. | Reconcile packet contents, S1 advice, preliminary C1 work, final C1 rationale, and reservation timestamps. |
| stopping rule | Stop if control defects, false timestamps, or non-pilot A2 median wait exceed pre-set tolerance. | Stop if urgent C1 waits worsen materially, reservation utilization is low, or compliance quality exceptions increase. |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411, controlled parallel verification. It removes a non-normative serialization created by a legacy lock while retaining the same evidence requirements, decision policy, and resource qualifications. Its benefit is uncertain at final completion because earlier-ready cases may wait for A2, but the intervention is reversible and produces highly informative operational evidence if timestamps, locks, and ownership are validated.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential speed. It appears to remove several calendar days, largely because it avoids an external supplier response over a weekend. But that apparent efficiency is obtained by changing the decision under unresolved evidence. A conditional approval or rejection may be defensible in selected bounded-risk situations, yet blanket suppression can create losses, compliance findings, appeals, and customer harm that are not visible in cycle-time metrics.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104. A single provisional C1 reservation can plausibly accelerate one high-risk case, especially if early work reduces cold-review effort. Applied widely, however, reservations compete for the only fully certified reviewer and can displace urgent cases. The resulting system effect depends on arrival volatility, triage accuracy, reservation expiry, and whether preliminary work genuinely reduces final-review service time.

The most valuable additional data are resource-level task calendars, queue-entry and queue-release timestamps, priority and pre-emption rules, and actual C1 reservation utilization. For K-219, document features, D2 clarification rates, and rework outcomes are essential. For K-337, discrepancy taxonomy, conditional-decision performance, supplier response distributions, weekend effects, and post-decision loss or appeal outcomes are more valuable than average service durations alone. Across all cases, evidence-version logs and task-lock data are needed to distinguish real concurrency from retrospective timestamp artifacts.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — Assessment cannot begin until both required verification checks are complete, even where those checks were performed concurrently.  
2. **structural** — A preliminary compliance screen for K-104 does not satisfy the mandatory final compliance review requirement.  
3. **resource** — C1 is the binding certified-compliance resource during the observed window and cannot review two cases simultaneously.  
4. **temporal** — Queue snapshot values are directional forecasts rather than guaranteed waiting times or strict service-order commitments.  
5. **behavioral** — D2 has a materially higher clarification propensity than D1 for handwritten documents, but not for all standard documents.  
6. **quality** — K-337’s unresolved discrepancy can be explicitly bounded through rejection, conditions, or authorized risk acceptance; it cannot be silently ignored.  
7. **interference** — Earlier task release in K-411 can increase A2 congestion even when total assessment service demand is unchanged.  
8. **behavioral** — Real-time task timestamps are reliable only when corroborated by task ownership, system-lock, and evidence-version records.
