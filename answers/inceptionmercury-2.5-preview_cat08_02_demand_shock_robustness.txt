

# 1. Stress scenarios

1.  **Winter Wave (Demand Mix Shift):** Total weekly volume rises by 40% compared to normal. The proportion of Urgent cases increases to 30% and Complex-panel cases to 22%. This shifts load heavily toward medical validation and specialist review.
2.  **Assay Capacity Failure (Equipment Outage):** One assay machine (M2) goes offline due to prolonged maintenance failure. Total assay capacity drops by ~50%, forcing all cases onto M1. Repeat assay rates increase due to M1 fatigue.
3.  **Validation Gap (Specialist Absence):** The specialist reviewer is absent for five consecutive business days (unplanned leave). Complex-panel and high-risk cases cannot proceed to medical validation without specialist clearance.
4.  **Courier Compression (Logistics Disruption):** Weather conditions compress courier arrivals into a single morning window (08:00–10:00). Pre-analytical check receives 120% of normal daily intake in the first 4 hours, overwhelming clerks.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

*   **Winter Wave:** The primary bottleneck migrates from **Pre-analytical check** (96% peak util) to **Medical validation** and **Specialist review**. Complex cases require specific validators. When Pre-analytical finishes early, technical validation throughput (101% utilization) is insufficient to clear queues for medical approval. Relieving preparation increases queues at assay run.
*   **Assay Capacity Failure:** The bottleneck becomes **Assay run**. With M2 down, M1 utilization spikes to >120%. **Technical validation** receives input intermittently. Repeat assay loops (1260 in peak) increase as M1 quality degrades under load. Relieving Pre-analytical does not help if Assay is starved.
*   **Validation Gap:** The bottleneck is **Specialist review**. **Technical validation** accumulates backlog (hold time increases). Cases pile up at Hold for specialist review (36h median queue). Relieving Assay creates longer wait for validation. Medical validators cannot proceed without specialist sign-off.
*   **Courier Compression:** The bottleneck moves immediately to **Courier intake** and **Pre-analytical check**. Clerks (85% peak util) exceed shift limits to catch up, raising error rates. **Accession correction specialists** (91% peak util) become swamped with errors. Relieving Registration does not help if Pre-analytical queue remains blocked.

# 3. Robust redesign package

We propose a core package of three interventions designed to stabilize the process under most stress scenarios without over-optimizing for average conditions.

1.  **Early Pre-analytical Defect Triage (I6):** Implement a strict 2-hour triage rule for Pre-analytical check. Reject poor samples immediately rather than holding for potential rework.
    *   **Why Robust:** Reduces recollection loops (540+ cases normal), which consume intake capacity and delay urgent cases.
    *   **Assumptions:** Clinicians accept immediate rejection; courier re-delivery logistics can be optimized.
    *   **Failure Risk:** May increase total sample count (more recollections) if initial collection quality doesn't improve.

2.  **Smooth Courier Intake (I7):** Shift pickup slots and enforce staggered delivery windows.
    *   **Why Robust:** Flattens the Pre-analytical queue spike during courier compression scenarios.
    *   **Assumptions:** Logistics partners agree to schedule changes.
    *   **Failure Risk:** External dependencies; may not solve internal capacity constraints if total volume is high.

3.  **Partial Cross-Training (I4 Modified):** Cross-train only one technical validator to perform accession corrections, not two.
    *   **Why Robust:** Provides limited buffer for Accession correction without depleting technical validation capacity.
    *   **Assumptions:** One validator can switch tasks without significant loss of validation throughput.
    *   **Failure Risk:** If technical validation queues grow beyond 16h, this person must be removed from correction duties immediately.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

These policies activate only when specific trigger conditions are met.

*   **Dynamic Priority Dispatch (Modified I1):**
    *   **Trigger:** Urgent cases > 25% of WIP OR Pre-analytical queue > 9.5h.
    *   **Policy:** Urgent cases bypass Batch wait. **Constraint:** Routine batch size capped at 12 to prevent starvation.
    *   **Switch-off:** Urgent < 15% OR Technical validation queue > 12h.
*   **Specialist Overflow Outsourcing (I10):**
    *   **Trigger:** Hold for specialist review queue > 30h OR Specialist absent > 3 days.
    *   **Policy:** Route Complex-panel overflow to partner lab.
    *   **Switch-off:** Specialist available AND queue < 10h.
*   **Assay Machine Segmentation (I3):**
    *   **Trigger:** M2 utilization > 95% OR M2 failure mode detected.
    *   **Policy:** Dedicated M1 to Urgent/Complex; Route Routine to M2.
    *   **Switch-off:** M2 stable AND M1 utilization < 90%.
*   **Evening Validation Shift (I5):**
    *   **Trigger:** Peak period (defined by 80%+ tech validator util) AND Medical validation queue > 15h.
    *   **Policy:** Activate temporary evening shift for Medical validation only.
    *   **Switch-off:** Medical queue < 5h.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

*   **Technical Validation Capacity Loss:** Cross-training (I4) risks reducing validation throughput if the technician spends too much time on corrections. In peak periods (101% util), this bottleneck could break completely if the validator is assigned correction tasks.
*   **Routine Starvation:** Dynamic priority (I1) allows Urgent bypass. If the cap on Routine batches is ignored, Routine samples may wait beyond 72 hours, leading to compliance failures.
*   **Outsourcing Quality Risk:** Relying on a partner lab (I10) reduces internal burden but introduces dependency on their quality standards and traceability systems, which are not fully controlled.
*   **Staff Fatigue:** Even with evening shifts (I5), error rates rise after 9-hour shifts. The process relies heavily on human attention in Validation, which is vulnerable to burnout during winter waves.

# 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Monitor these indicators daily. Prioritize leading metrics to prevent queues from exceeding thresholds.

**Leading Indicators (Daily/Real-time):**
*   **Pre-analytical Queue Depth:** Warning band > 6h (Median is 1.8h normal, 9.5h peak). Threshold: >5h.
*   **Assay Machine Utilization:** Warning band > 90%. Threshold: >95%.
*   **Recollection Rate:** Warning band > 10% of intake. Threshold: >8%.
*   **Medical Validator Backlog:** Warning band > 10 cases pending. Threshold: >15 cases.

**Lagging Outcome Metrics (Weekly):**
*   **Urgent SLA Compliance:** % of cases released within 18h.
*   **Case P90 Cycle Time:** By case type (Target: Routine <72h, Urgent <24h).
*   **Specialist Queue Awaiting:** Average hours in Hold for specialist review.
