## 1. Analytical assumptions

In this analysis a **case notion** is the set of events that become the trace of one concrete object when the OCEL is flattened on a chosen object type. Each instance of that type (O1, I11, P3, \ldots) is treated as a distinct case identifier; an event is placed into a case if and only if the object appears in the event’s direct E2O relation set. No silent closure under the O2O graph is performed.

**Convergence** occurs when a single event is related to multiple objects of the same type; on flattening the event is therefore copied into every corresponding case. **Divergence** occurs when a single object participates in multiple parallel or sequential sub-flows of the same activity types (most clearly O1’s two independent pack–dispatch–confirm paths). Both phenomena are first-class structural features of the given snapshot, not noise.

Copied shared events are treated as **both** evidence and duplication. They constitute positive evidence that the process deliberately consolidates work across objects; at the same time they produce statistical duplication of frequencies, DFG edge weights and performance aggregates unless an explicit normalisation step is applied. Consequently every quantitative claim derived from a flattened log must be accompanied by a statement of whether raw (duplicated) or de-duplicated counts are being reported.

The only analytical transformation permitted is an **explicit** enrichment that propagates selected O2O relations after the initial flattening (for example, attaching an invoice’s payments to every order that the invoice bills). Such enrichment is never applied silently when defining the case notion itself; the prompt’s insistence on “direct E2O” is respected.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Cross-order package P3 – Order flattening.**  
   Event e13 (Pack Package, related objects O2, O3, I21, I31, P3) is copied into both the O2 and the O3 cases. Consequently the activity “Pack Package” receives frequency 2 instead of 1 and the DFG edge Pack \to Dispatch (via the equally duplicated e16) is likewise doubled. The effect is harmful for any packing-effort or resource-capacity calculation; it is informative only when the analyst deliberately inspects the multi-object relation set to recover the consolidation.

2. **Cross-order package P4 – Order flattening.**  
   Events e14, e17 and e27 are each duplicated into O3 and O4. O3 therefore appears to execute two distinct Pack Package activities (e13 for P3 and e14 for P4). Frequency inflation of packing and of the subsequent consolidated dispatch is again harmful for effort metrics; the same pattern simultaneously reveals that O3’s items travel in two different physical packages—an informative divergence signal if recognised as such.

3. **Consolidated delivery D2 – Order flattening.**  
   Events e16 (Dispatch) and e24 (Confirm Delivery) appear in the three cases O1, O2 and O3. Raw frequency of both activities is therefore tripled. The five-hour interval 11:05–16:00 is added into the cycle-time calculation of every one of the three orders, even though the physical delivery is a single consolidated truck movement. Harmful for naïve order-level lead-time averages and capacity planning; context-dependent for studies that explicitly wish to quantify consolidation impact.

4. **Consolidated delivery D2 – Package and Delivery flattenings.**  
   In the Package log e16 and e24 are each present in both the P2 and the P3 cases (duplication \times2). In the Delivery log the same two events form the entire two-event trace of the single case D2. The former inflates dispatch counts; the latter truncates the lifecycle so severely that packing lead time disappears and the multi-order nature of D2 is visible only in the related-object column, not in the activity sequence. Both effects are harmful for end-to-end logistics performance; they remain informative for pure transit-time KPI design.

5. **Consolidated invoicing V2 – Order flattening.**  
   Events e19 (Issue Invoice), e22 and e25 are each copied into O2 and O3. The single physical invoice therefore contributes frequency 2 to “Issue Invoice” and each of the two orders appears to receive its own complete invoice-plus-two-payments sequence. Harmful for any calculation of invoicing process cost or invoice volume; residual informative value lies in the per-order accounts-receivable visibility that the duplication preserves.

6. **Partial payments on V2 – three different flattenings.**  
   - Invoice flattening correctly sequences e22 (M2, partially_settles) then e25 (M3, completes_settlement) inside the single case V2—informative.  
   - Order flattening duplicates both events into O2 and O3, so each order appears to be paid twice—harmful for payment-volume metrics.  
   - Payment flattening isolates M2 and M3 as two independent singleton cases; no DFG edge exists between them and the instalment relationship is recoverable only via the O2O qualifiers. Harmful for instalment-process discovery unless object-interaction summaries are consulted.

7. **Near-singleton lifecycle – Payment flattening.**  
   Each of M1–M4 is related solely to its own Record Payment event (e21, e22, e25, e26 respectively). Every case therefore contains exactly one event, the DFG contains no edges, variant analysis is vacuous and every performance metric is zero-duration. This is the textbook illustration of the prompt’s warning that a lifecycle becomes artificially short when the chosen object participates in only one or two event types. Harmful for any sequential or performance analysis; useful only as a negative control that demonstrates the limits of flattening.

8. **O1 split-shipment divergence – Order flattening.**  
   Inside the single case O1 the events e11/e15/e23 (P1/D1 path) and e12/e16/e24 (P2/D2 path) coexist. The resulting trace exhibits two concurrent Pack–Dispatch–Confirm sub-paths. Traditional single-path DFG algorithms may therefore invent loops, parallel gateways or multi-instance markers that do not exist in the underlying logistics. Informative of genuine split shipments; harmful if the analyst assumes every order follows a sequential template.

9. **Item-level financial truncation.**  
   No Item ever appears in the related-object sets of e18–e31. Consequently every item case ends at Confirm Delivery; invoice, payment and Close Order activities are simply absent. The artificial shortness is harmful for any claim about “item lifecycle completeness” and simultaneously hides that I11 and I12 belong to the same commercial order.

10. **Package-level upstream truncation.**  
    Create Order and Add Item events never list a Package in their E2O sets. Package cases therefore begin at Pack Package; all order-creation and item-addition lead time is invisible. Performance figures measure only the short pack-to-confirm window and systematically understate true order-to-delivery latency whenever packages wait for items from other orders (the situation of P3 and P4).

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

Flattened logs that remain useful after explicit qualification:

- **Order** (with mandatory de-duplication notes) – safe for order-cycle-time distributions, completion rates, and the commercial question “did every order reach Close Order?” provided frequencies of shared activities are reported both raw and unique-event-normalised.  
- **Package** – safe for pure logistics questions: packing duration, multi-order pack frequency, transit-time from pack to confirm.  
- **Invoice** – safe for accounts-receivable questions: invoice-to-cash intervals, incidence of partial settlements (V2).  

Flattened logs that should be created only for negative-control or specialised purposes: Item (warehouse item tracking), Delivery (last-mile KPI), Payment (payment-count audits).

Questions that cannot be answered safely from any single flattened log and therefore require object-centric artefacts:

- “Which physical consolidations link which orders?” \to OC-DFG or object-interaction summary.  
- “How do object types interact over time?” \to OTG and ET–OT graphs.  
- “Are there recurring multi-object execution patterns (e.g., the recurring O2+O3 co-occurrence in P3/V2/D2)?” \to equivalent-execution clustering or directly the object-interaction summary.  
- Any frequency or performance claim that mixes shared and non-shared events \to side-by-side comparison of the flattened DFG against the OC-DFG edge weights (the latter count each event once).

Contradictory findings are reconciled by treating the object-centric view as the semantic ground truth for event uniqueness and for multi-object cardinality, while the flattened views supply the classic case-level performance and variant statistics after explicit de-duplication. Whenever a flattened frequency exceeds the corresponding OC-DFG frequency, the difference is exactly the convergence multiplicity and must be reported as such.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position C: Reject a single lead object for this process.**

**Decision criteria.**  
(1) Coverage of the full set of activity types without severe truncation.  
(2) Simultaneous control of both convergence-induced frequency inflation and divergence-induced multi-path complexity.  
(3) Faithfulness to the explicitly listed business facts (order splits, cross-order packages, multi-order deliveries, consolidated invoices, partial payments).  
(4) Ability to answer both operational and financial end-to-end questions without mandatory heavy post-processing that effectively re-introduces object-centric reasoning.

**Strongest supporting evidence.**  
No object type satisfies the four criteria at once. Order flattening maximises activity coverage yet produces the highest convergence (e16 and e24 each appear in three cases; e13/e14/e19/e22/e25 each appear in two) and the highest divergence (O1’s two complete sub-paths). Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice and Payment each omit large contiguous segments of the process, generating the artificial short lifecycles warned about in the prompt. The business facts themselves are statements about multi-object relationships; any analysis that forces a single lead object necessarily either hides or statistically distorts those relationships.

**Strongest counter-argument.**  
Order remains the closest analogue to a traditional commercial case and yields the longest traces that include both Create Order and Close Order. Many stakeholders frame KPIs exclusively at order level. With disciplined de-duplication of shared events, Order flattening can still answer a useful subset of questions.

**Residual risk.**  
Analysts or tools may still default to the Order view, publish inflated frequencies, and treat the resulting DFG as “the” process model, thereby institutionalising the distortions.

**Conditions under which the decision would change.**  
If the sole analytic goal were order-completion rates and order-cycle times, and if a standardised, audited de-duplication procedure plus mandatory side-by-side OC-DFG reporting were enforced, a qualified Position B (Order primary, Package and Invoice secondary) would become acceptable. Pure Position A (single unqualified lead) remains indefensible for the given snapshot.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Shared-event down-weighting.**  
   Construct a filtered event log that retains only events whose E2O cardinality for the chosen object type equals 1. Re-discover the DFG and recompute all frequency and performance metrics. The difference isolates the pure contribution of convergence.

2. **Direct versus propagated relations.**  
   Produce two Order flattenings: one using only the supplied E2O relations, another after an explicit O2O enrichment that attaches every payment and invoice to the orders they ultimately affect. Compare activity presence, cycle-time distributions and variant counts. Any material change demonstrates the impact of the (normally forbidden) silent propagation.

3. **Lead-object swap.**  
   Repeat the entire matrix exercise with Package or Invoice forced as the primary case notion. Quantify how many of the eight concrete distortions reverse, attenuate or intensify. Persistent high distortion under every choice reconfirms Position C.

4. **Frequency metric triangulation.**  
   For every activity that participates in multi-object events (especially Pack Package, Dispatch, Issue Invoice, Record Payment) report three numbers side-by-side:  
   (a) raw event count in the flattened log,  
   (b) number of unique events (from the OC-DFG),  
   (c) total number of object participations (sum of E2O cardinalities).  
   Systematic divergence among the three figures quantifies the precise multiplicity of each consolidation and supplies the normalisation factors required for any quantitative claim.

Additional checks include timestamp-consistency verification (shared events must keep identical timestamps across all copies) and a controlled removal of the known consolidations (P3, P4, D2, V2) to measure how much of the observed distortion is attributable to the intentionally designed multi-object patterns.
