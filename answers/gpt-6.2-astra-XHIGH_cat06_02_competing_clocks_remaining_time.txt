## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Chemistry waiting and service.** The two active jobs free their analysts after 0.4–0.9 and 1.2–2.0 working hours. On the priority path the first available analyst takes the urgent sample. B-417 usually starts on the other analyst around Fri 16:42–17:30. Even a 1.5 h assay starting at 16:42 has 12 minutes left at closing. Under FIFO, assigning the four waiting jobs successively to the next available analyst gives a typical wait of about 5–7 *open* hours from now, so B-417 usually starts Mon around 10:30–12:30. Its own service then requires triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) working hours. There are only 2.5 chemistry hours left Friday, followed by a 62 h closure. Normal chemistry results typically arrive Mon around 09:00–11:00 under priority or 13:00–16:00 under FIFO. Friday's elapsed wait since the request is already spent.

**Microbiology and the join.** The primary incubation has 44.5–52.5 h left and becomes technically ready Sun 20 Sep, 12:00–20:00. Authorization in 25–45% of cases gives a recorded first result Sun 13:00–23:00. The 55–75% routine-staffing path gives a result Mon 08:30–09:30. Both windows fall after Saturday QA closes. The join timestamp is **max(usable chemistry timestamp, usable microbiology timestamp)**. Incubation and the staffing calendar explain the long weekend floor on a smooth release. Chemistry often supplies the actual last timestamp on Monday, particularly under FIFO. A Sunday read can therefore save little time on a normal batch while saving much more if it starts confirmation early.

**QA waiting and service.** Before Monday opening there are 4.5 QA hours Friday and 4 Saturday. Roughly three more arrivals are expected in those 8.5 h. One reviewer can process about three to four average batches’ worth of work in that time, leaving a variable backlog. Monday's two reviewers also work while B-417 is still waiting for its join. In the middle model, smooth-path queue waits range from immediate service to about 5 open hours at the 75th percentile and 10 at the 90th. The batch's own review follows that wait. For example, chemistry at Mon 14:30 and a normal microbiology result at 09:00 give a 14:30 join. A 2 h wait, 1.5 h review and 0.5 h release finish at 18:30 Monday. A 4 h wait and 3.5 h review on the same join carry 2 h of review past closing into Tuesday, giving a 10:30 Tuesday release.

## 4. Forecast summary

**Plan for late Monday through Tuesday afternoon for the median outcome.** The following ranges span favorable, middle and adverse parameter choices. I simulated 90,000 paths per choice, using low, midpoint and high rework probabilities, respectively. The favorable case pairs higher on-call coverage with more documentation-only investigations, and the adverse case pairs lower coverage with more laboratory investigations. Arrival-rate dispersion also increases across the three cases (gamma shape 3, 2 and 1). These are sensitivity ranges for quantiles. Overnight closures create gaps inside the clock-time windows.

**Electronic Release is the most plausible terminal event.** Most paths have no investigation, and a query, rerun or investigation can still end in acceptance. Batch Rejected is possible after a confirmed critical defect. The inputs support neither a numerical rejection probability nor a separate rejection-time percentile. If critical defects frequently terminate investigations early, the upper release-based time estimates would overstate time to either terminal event.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **QA disposition and investigation type — mainly path uncertainty.** A query costs working hours, while the laboratory route costs days. The estimated laboratory-route share crosses 10% in the adverse case, enough to move the overall P90 into a different part of the distribution.
2. **QA backlog at the actual join — mainly queue uncertainty.** Arrivals, variable review lengths and the change from one reviewer to two determine whether a Monday join finishes that evening or waits overnight.
3. **Temperature-excursion status — mainly dependence uncertainty.** The missing observation affects both assay risks and the investigation decision. Counting the three triggers as independent would misweight the tail.
4. **Microbiology indication and timing of the first staffed read — mainly path uncertainty, amplified by the calendar.** Dispatch has limited leverage on a clean Sunday result because QA is closed. It can bring a confirmation forward from Tuesday into Monday.
5. **Chemistry queue discipline and Friday spillover — mainly queue uncertainty.** Passing three ordinary samples can buy several working hours on Monday. A rerun late that day can also encounter the 18:00 closure.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**Engineering resolves the temperature-excursion flag.** This is the most informative early observation while both assay outcomes are pending. In the middle model, a confirmed flag puts investigation risk at about 42% even before the assays report and moves the conditional P90 to around Sat 26 Sep morning. A clear flag leaves investigation possible through either assay or routine QA, but reduces its modeled probability to about 13% and brings the conditional P90 forward to around Wed 23 Sep afternoon. If the gap remains unresolved, retain the mixture.

The secondary trigger is **QA ordering additional laboratory work**. Once that order is observed, the 3–7 calendar days belong in the central forecast for that batch, followed by the applicable disposition and recording time.
