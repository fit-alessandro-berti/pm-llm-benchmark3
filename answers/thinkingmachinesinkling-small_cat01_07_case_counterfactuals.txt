Weekend effects vanish, which is valuable because supplier responses rarely arrive on weekends; but if 30 cases now require conditional approvals, audit load and appeal exposure accumulate linearly with volume.

**K-411 at scale:** Creating concurrent tasks for 100 cases saves ~5h of artificial case-lock delay per case locally. But if assessment is the fixed bottleneck (A1/A2 queues at 7–10h in snapshots), pushing 100 verifications 5h earlier floods assessment with 100 additional arrivals. Queue growth is superlinear: a 4h fixed queue can become 12–16h under arrival-rate doubling. Timestamp behavior is critical: if the workaround uses batch entry rather than real-time intervals, process mining records false concurrency—resources appear at two tasks simultaneously—corrupting resource usage analysis and creating audit gaps. True concurrency requires distinct resource intervals; poorly logged concurrency is worse than sequential execution because it hides actual workload.

**Cross-cutting:** Weekend and external-response effects matter for K-337 (second loop crosses weekends) and K-219 (originals arrival is external). Reservation (K-104) and concurrency (K-411) are internal scheduling interventions, but both can degrade system performance if downstream queues are rigid. The most dangerous system-wide effect is bottleneck migration to assessment (K-411, K-219) and to compliance (K-104).

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-104 (earlier escalation and provisional compliance preparation)**. It respects every structural rule—RegisterTriage, concurrent identity/document where permitted, four-eyes separation (A1 vs C1), and the distinction between preliminary and final compliance—while changing only orchestration and timing. Its downside is localized to C1 capacity, and that downside is observable and reversible by stopping reservations. The decision policy is unchanged, which is critical because high-risk/high-value approvals with unresolved ambiguity must remain defensible.

The most dangerous despite potential speed is **K-337 (suppressing the second rework loop)**. It offers the largest calendar reduction—potentially 36–48 hours—because it eliminates a weekend-spanning external response and a redundant document/assessment cycle. However, it transforms the decision from evidence-complete approval to conditional or rejected approval with an explicitly acknowledged discrepancy. That is not a timing change; it is a quality and compliance policy change. At scale, 100 such cases could produce a portfolio of conditionally approved high-value contracts with material supplier discrepancies, creating audit, appellate, and loss-exposure risks that are difficult to reverse once decisions are signed.

Where a local counterfactual is least transportable is **K-411 (parallel verification despite case lock)**. The case-level path is feasible if a workaround permits concurrent I2 and D2 intervals with real-time stamps. But the local saving depends entirely on whether the assessment queue absorbs earlier arrivals or becomes congested. At 100 cases, the bottleneck migrates deterministically to A1/A2, and if the workaround produces false concurrency in logs, process integrity degrades. The intervention requires system-level timestamp architecture, not just case-level routing, making it the least portable without infrastructure change.

The most valuable additional data are: (1) **C1 interval-level workload data** showing whether preliminary review is separable from final review; (2) **D2 clarification/rework rates on handwritten versus printed standard documents**, to validate substitution; (3) **assessment arrival-rate curves** to test bottleneck migration under concurrency; (4) **supplier response-time distributions by case type**, to calibrate whether loop suppression is safe; and (5) **case-lock event logs** to distinguish true task ownership concurrency from batch-entry artifacts.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural:** The four-eyes rule applies specifically to the final Assessment and final Compliance Review resources, not to preliminary screening. (structural)
2. **Temporal:** Queue forecasts (e.g., C1 20h at Mon 08:00) are unbiased predictors of actual resource wait times, though they are not deterministic. (temporal)
3. **Resource:** C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer available in this window; no substitutable resource exists. (resource)
4. **Behavioral:** D2 has a materially higher clarification/rework rate on handwritten standard documents than D1. (behavioral)
5. **Quality:** The unresolved supplier discrepancy in K-337 is minor enough that conditional approval is defensible and not material to loss exposure. (quality)
6. **Interference:** Cases are independent arrivals; one case’s queue displacement does not alter other cases’ service-time distributions or external response times. (interference)
7. **Structural:** A controlled workaround can create true concurrency for distinct resources (I2 and D2) without violating exclusivity if task ownership is correctly logged. (structural)
8. **Temporal:** External supplier responses for rework are independent of process-stage timing and do not systematically cluster on weekends. (temporal)
