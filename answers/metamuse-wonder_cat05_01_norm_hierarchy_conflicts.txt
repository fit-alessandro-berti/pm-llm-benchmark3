## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts taken as given.** The dates, amounts, supplier status, actors, sequence of recorded events, ECD-2 window, outage windows, and stated delegations/non-delegations in the case table are treated as factual inputs. Where the table says “no separate event,” I treat that as *no evidence of that act*, not metaphysical proof that no conversation occurred. Where it says a person was requester/committer/authorizer, that identity fact is given.

**Assumptions, explicitly labelled.** 
- A1: Board-approved GPIP-4 is presumptively the integrity floor for Northbridge purchases: segregation of duties, pre-commitment Finance/budget-owner approval at EUR 50,000+, and Compliance pre-clearance above EUR 100,000 or for new suppliers are not “ordinary workflow” unless validly excepted. This is an interpretation, not a quotation: GPIP-4 A4 says emergency waives tendering only and A7 channels exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer.
- A2: ECD-2 is a real, signed, time-boxed incident directive with operational force inside its stated scope and window, especially for PO-timing and approved-supplier/register constraints. It is not automatically an A7 exception because A7 names the Chief Risk Officer and GPIP-4 expressly leaves the COO/Incident Commander question open.
- A3: A local SOP may add stricter controls; under GPIP-4 A5 it may not silently weaken GPIP-4. Conversely GPIP-4 does not automatically erase local evidence/workflow rules where they are compatible.
- A4: The CFO email is evidence of an authoritative *Finance-control interpretation* within the Finance domain, but it is lower-formality evidence than a controlled policy: ordinary email, not in the repository, expressly caveated by “not meant to remove essential integrity controls,” and not stated to waive Compliance or segregation.
- A5: Emergency necessity is a justification only when scope, imminence, authority, and proportionality line up. A real contamination crisis does not convert every site-continuity purchase into an emergency purchase.

**Decision method.** For each purchase I ask, in order: (1) What rule was triggered by amount, supplier status, actor identity, purpose and time *at the moment of supplier commitment*? (2) Was each required act performed before commitment by the right actor, or validly substituted/excepted before commitment? (3) If not, is there a scoped, temporally valid exception from a source competent for that issue? (4) Is the timing proved by the best contemporaneous evidence rather than the most convenient timestamp? (5) Does later approval merely mitigate under GPIP-4 A6 rather than cure? 

**Burden and classifications.** The buyer/company bears the burden of proving an exception, delegation, extension, or timely recording by contemporaneous evidence. An unproved approval is treated as an evidential failure; whether it is also a substantive breach can be INDETERMINATE. COMPLIANT means all controlling rules are satisfied on the stated facts. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION means an apparent deviation is covered by a competent, scoped, timely exception/interpretation on my primary reading. NONCOMPLIANT means at least one controlling rule was breached or an invoked exception fails on scope, authority, timing, or actor. INDETERMINATE means the strongest classification turns on an unresolved premise or missing decisive evidence. Severity reflects integrity risk, amount, supplier risk, self-approval, and reversibility—not merely rule count.

**Specificity, later-in-time, and global/newest heuristics rejected.** “Later,” “global,” and “strongly worded” are not self-executing trump cards. ECD-2 can defeat SOP B1/B3 *for sterilization-line lots inside its window* because it is specific, signed, incident-scoped and expressly addresses timing/onboarding. It does not thereby defeat GPIP-4 Compliance/segregation merely by silence. The CFO email is later than ECD-2 but narrower in authority: strong for Finance interpretation, weak for Compliance and segregation. Post-commitment approvals are recorded as mitigation, not retroactive satisfaction, per GPIP-4 A6 and ordinary pre-commitment logic.

**Resulting hierarchy by issue.** For *segregation and Compliance*, GPIP-4 is presumptively controlling unless a true A7 exception is proved. For *Finance mechanics during ECD-2*, GPIP-4 states the control objective while the CFO email may supply a temporary Finance interpretation up to EUR 150,000. For *PO timing and approved-supplier register inside the contamination scope/window*, ECD-2 is the specific operative overlay over SOP B1/B3. For *local evidence and emergency-form discipline*, SOP remains useful unless ECD-2 clearly displaces the step. For *time proof*, the best contemporaneous evidence outranks a lagging or fast ERP clock.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-502 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise that must change: ECD-2, jointly signed by COO and Incident Commander and following prior practice of signed incident directives as temporary overlays, counts as a GPIP-4 A7 exception or as an equivalent emergency exception despite CRO absence. If that premise holds, ECD-2’s precedence over “supplier-onboarding requirements” could be read to defer new-supplier Compliance and ordinary Finance sequencing for a same-day sterilization need. Credibility: moderate as an organizational-practice argument, weak as a text/authority argument. GPIP-4 A7 names the CRO, A4 waives tendering only, prior overlay practice never involved new-supplier Compliance, and the CRO did not ratify. The alternative is strongest for PO/register relief, weakest for Compliance.

**P-503 — alternative: INDETERMINATE rather than NONCOMPLIANT on scope only, but not COMPLIANT.** Premise: Operations’ definition of “critical” controls and backup-line pumps with three days’ stock are “critical sterilization-line lots.” That would bring ECD timing/register relief into play and make scope defensible. It still does not cure PROC-3’s triple role under segregation, the absence of Compliance at EUR 140k, or the absence of any independent approval. To reach COMPLIANT, one would additionally need the implausible premises that Emergency_Authorize by the requester/committer is not final business approval and that the CFO email waives Compliance and segregation—contrary to D3/D4. Thus scope uncertainty affects reasoning, not primary outcome.

**P-505 — alternative: NONCOMPLIANT.** Premise: the CFO email is merely exhortatory or, even if authoritative for Finance, cannot displace GPIP-4 A2’s board-level requirement of an independent budget owner plus Finance Controller *before* commitment; joint Incident Command authorization is operational, not budgetary. Under that premise, Finance_Approve at 19:00 is a post-commitment mitigation under A6 and the purchase breaches A2 despite being inside ECD-2 and Compliance-cleared. Credibility: substantial. It is the main reason confidence is low-medium. The timing sub-issue is weaker for the alternative: supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is strong contemporaneous evidence against treating ERP 18:06 as conclusive.

**P-507 — alternative: NONCOMPLIANT for late recording and/or Finance.** Two premises could flip it. First, “recorded within four hours” in C3 could mean recorded in the authoritative ERP, not merely a PDF created and queued; then ERP appearance at 14:30 breaches the 4-hour record rule. That reading is credible linguistically but weak process-wise because C3’s purpose is prompt memorialization of verbal authorization, and the PDF was created at 12:40 during an outage. Second, if CFO D1 cannot substitute GPIP A2 Finance/budget-owner approval, absence of Finance is substantive. That premise is credible for the same board-policy reason as P-505; the case is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only because Compliance—the control least capable of CFO/ECD displacement—was timely and the recording evidence is strong.

**P-508 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise: D2’s “obtain Legal clearance as well” naturally means the D1 sufficiency of joint Incident Command authorization continues above EUR 150k *plus* Legal, rather than expiring at EUR 150k. On that reading, Compliance, Legal, segregation, necessity, ECD timing/register relief, and CFO Finance interpretation all align, making the absence of a separate Finance stamp a justified emergency substitution. Credibility: moderate to strong textually (“as well” suggests addition, not replacement), but the EUR 150k cap in D1 and GPIP A2’s budget-owner language prevent treating it as settled. A one-line CFO confirmation would likely flip P-508 to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; proof that Finance Controller refused or was bypassed would flip it to NONCOMPLIANT.

**P-506 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION if verbal extension were proved.** Premise: Incident Command had authority to extend ECD-2 orally overnight and the buyer’s statement accurately reports that extension. Even then, the extension would need to cover PO sequencing and Plant timing, and GPIP A2 would still require budget-owner/Finance satisfaction; Finance was pre-commitment but Plant/budget-owner proof is missing. Credibility: weak. ECD-2 has a hard written window, A7 exceptions are written/scoped/time-limited, and the claimant offers no joint authorization record. Sympathetic recovery purpose does not substitute for exception discipline after expiry.

**P-501 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION under SOP emergency alone.** Premise: B4’s emergency finding (stoppage in four hours) itself displaces not only PO sequencing but also the ordinary identity of the committing approver, or Plant/Finance approvals at EUR 25k+ are post-commitment regularizations rather than preconditions. Credibility: weak-to-moderate. B4 carefully conditions Shift Lead substitution on Plant Manager absence plus active delegation; the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. The emergency was real, but the SOP’s own safeguard was not used.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Four different failures must not be collapsed.**
- *Substantive policy breach:* the required act did not occur before commitment or the actor lacked authority—e.g., P-502 Compliance after commitment for a new supplier; P-503 self-authorization and missing Compliance; P-504 outside scope with unapproved supplier; P-506 post-expiry PO/Plant sequence.
- *Logging defect:* the act occurred but the system of record shows it late—potentially P-507 ERP ingestion at 14:30 where a signed PDF was created at 12:40; potentially P-505 ERP 18:06 where supplier acknowledgment predates expiry. Logging defects require evidence preservation and correction notes, not automatic findings of substantive breach.
- *Late evidence of a timely act:* a document created later may still prove an earlier act if it is contemporaneously anchored—supplier acknowledgment, call recording metadata, signed PDF creation time. P-505 is the clearest example: acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is better evidence of commitment timing than a possibly fast ERP clock.
- *Inability to prove compliance:* no adverse act is proved, but the exception/approval burden is unmet—P-508 Finance substitution above EUR 150k; P-505/P-507 budget-owner status; P-506 claimed verbal extension. These should be recorded as evidential non-compliance with control design even when bad faith is not inferred.

**Immediate case-level remediation.**
- P-501: obtain retrospective Plant/Finance confirmation expressly labelled mitigation, not cure; document why SHIFT-1 committed without delegation; brief Shift Leads that reachability defeats B4 substitution; verify goods were needed and priced reasonably.
- P-502: complete a post-incident Compliance/new-supplier risk review of the filter supplier, quality/sterility documentation, beneficial ownership/sanctions checks, and whether goods were quarantined or released; Finance to record late approval as mitigation; consider supplier suspension until onboarding completes.
- P-503: independent review by someone other than PROC-3; confirm whether pumps were used, return/cancel if unused and contract permits; obtain Compliance review for >EUR 100k failure; segregation coaching and possible disciplinary/process finding for self-authorization; do not allow PROC-3 to approve remedial closure.
- P-504: regularize supplier status or terminate cafeteria arrangement; confirm food-safety risk separately through EHS; instruct that “site continuity” is not ECD scope; recover/ratify contract only through ordinary PO process.
- P-505: preserve supplier email headers, call logs, ERP clock-drift evidence, Compliance and authorization records in an incident evidence pack; obtain CFO/Finance written confirmation whether joint authorization was intended as Finance satisfaction; annotate ERP timestamp as disputed rather than silently altering it.
- P-506: no further reliance on verbal extension; obtain written determination that ECD-2 expired; Plant to confirm receipt/need; Finance to state whether post-expiry purchases require fresh emergency declaration; consider whether goods should be ratified under ordinary authority with breach notation.
- P-507: authenticate PDF metadata and document-system outage queue; file PDF as the C3 record with ERP cross-reference; obtain Finance confirmation under CFO interpretation; verify seals’ criticality and supplier quality docs despite Compliance clearance.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Draft replacement hierarchy/exception clause:**

“1. **Controlling floor.** GPIP segregation of duties, pre-commitment Finance Controller and independent budget-owner approval for purchases of EUR 50,000 or more, and pre-commitment Compliance clearance for purchases above EUR 100,000 or from any new supplier are integrity controls. They may be displaced only by a written exception under paragraph 4. No emergency, incident directive, email, operational urgency, or later approval silently waives them.

2. **Local procedures.** Site SOPs may impose stricter controls and define evidence, sequencing, forms, and delegations. A local or incident rule may relax a local workflow step only where it states the step relaxed, the scope, and the period, and only if the relaxation does not weaken paragraph 1.

3. **Incident directives.** An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander may, for defined critical lots and a stated window not exceeding 96 hours unless renewed in writing, permit commitment before purchase order and use of a supplier not yet on the approved register. It must state: covered sites, lots or lot categories by objective need horizon, maximum amount per purchase and in aggregate, authorized supplier categories, required joint authorizers, recording deadline, and expiry. It does not amend paragraph 1 unless it also satisfies paragraph 4.

4. **Integrity exceptions.** Only the Chief Risk Officer, or a named alternate if the CRO is unavailable for more than two hours, may issue a written, scoped, time-limited exception to paragraph 1. The exception must identify the rule, purchase or class, amount cap, supplier, compensating controls, and end time. If the CRO is unavailable and delay would create imminent safety or sterility risk, the COO, CFO and Head of Compliance acting jointly may issue a provisional exception for no more than 24 hours and no more than EUR 150,000 per purchase; it lapses unless the CRO ratifies or declines in writing within two business days. Segregation of duties may never be excepted to permit a requester or committer to be a final approver of the same purchase; a substitute approver must be named.

5. **Finance interpretation.** The CFO may prescribe how Finance approval is evidenced during an incident, including joint Incident Command authorization up to a stated cap, provided the authorizers are independent of the requester/committer and at least one is a designated budget owner or Finance delegate named in the directive. Above the cap, Finance Controller approval and, where stated, Legal clearance are required before commitment.

6. **Time and evidence.** The legally relevant commitment time is the time the supplier is bound, proved by the best contemporaneous evidence—supplier acknowledgment, call recording, signed authorization, access log—rather than a later ERP entry. Verbal emergency authorization must be recorded in a durable record within four hours; system ingestion delay during a declared outage does not breach the deadline if record creation is proved. Emergency forms are due within 24 hours unless the directive states a shorter period.

7. **Expiry and anti-normalization.** Incident relief expires automatically at the stated time; oral extensions are void. Any renewal must be written before expiry and re-justify scope and amounts. Emergency purchasing may not be used for convenience, stockpiling beyond the defined need horizon, or recurring needs; more than a set number or value of emergency purchases in a rolling quarter triggers automatic Compliance review, a report to the Audit Committee, and suspension of the emergency code pending review. Post-incident ratification may mitigate discipline and validate payment where goods were received in good faith, but must be recorded as mitigation and not as retroactive compliance unless paragraph 4 expressly so provides.”
