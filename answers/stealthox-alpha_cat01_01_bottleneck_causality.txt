## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 60/100.**

The strongest evidence for this verdict is the aggregate-versus-stratified reversal. Crudely, Orion is 6.72 h slower (21.96 vs 15.24 h), yet within every complexity stratum Orion is faster — Low 2.14 h, Medium 2.21 h, High 3.43 h — and a complexity-standardized contrast (pooled weights 40/33/27%) is 2.51 h. The crude gap tracks Orion's case mix (42.9% high-complexity, 92.9% night, 64.3% compliance-required versus 12.5/25/25% for Nova) and decomposes into queue wait (+4.0 h) more than service (+2.0 h); within strata, Orion's service times are equal or slightly lower. This pattern is inconsistent with a uniformly slower group.

Against a clean "confounded-only" reading: routing selects on unrecorded difficulty, so within-complexity cells still carry unobserved case risk; several cells hold one or two cases (Orion-Low n=2, Nova-High n=2), so the reversal itself is fragile; only completed cases are observed, so differential censoring of long cases can distort either group; and night shift, high arrival load, and the group label are nearly entangled, blocking separation of "Orion" from "night congestion."

The proposed 50% reassignment may therefore succeed only by accident. If the crude gap is pure composition, expected gains are near zero. It can backfire: Nova lacks Orion's certification depth and night coverage, so migrated complex or night cases could face longer waits and more appeals, while halving Orion's inflow perturbs queue dynamics nonlinearly. Any real gain would come from relieving Orion's night queue load — a capacity mechanism better targeted directly than through blanket relabeling.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 (weeks 1–4) | Instrument only: per-event timestamps, hourly queue snapshots, staffing rosters, open-case census; build propensity/overlap atlas | None (measurement) | None (observational) | Read-only; privacy review | Group-specific DFGs and variant frequencies (PM4Py); waiting-time attribution per edge once timestamps exist; overlap cells | Identifies dominant mechanism (queue vs service vs compliance) and sizes the pilot | Pause if timestamp capture <95% complete |
| P2 (weeks 5–10) | Small **randomized crossover routing pilot**: 10–15% of Medium-complexity cases (both shifts) assigned to the non-default group via stratified blocks (shift × load) | Total effect of the label in the near-overlap region | Case | Exclude High complexity and policy-mandated CC cases; daily quota caps; independent monitor; pre-registered analysis | ITT cycle contrast; wait/service decomposition; spillover on each group's queue; variant conformance drift | Unbiased local total effect + mediation shares | Halt and revert if pilot-arm appeal rate >2× baseline or p90 cycle exceeds historical p95 for 3 consecutive days |
| P3 (weeks 8–16, overlapping) | **Capacity/flow intervention with labels frozen**: pre-schedule and parallelize compliance checks; smooth night staffing to the arrival curve; stepped-wedge across weeks/queues | queue_wait and compliance-overhead mediators | Queue/week (cluster) | Routing unchanged; staggered rollout with control windows | Change in wait segments around Compliance Check; residual-overhead ladder; DFG timing deltas | Direct test of mediation: if cycle falls without rerouting, the group label was a proxy for capacity | Pause wedge if CC backlog grows or quality flags rise |
| P4 (decision gate) | Synthesize P1–P3; only if ITT shows label harm and capacity levers are exhausted, rebalance 10–15% with guardrails — never 50% | System equilibrium | Policy level | Staged ramp; automatic rollback triggers; continuous appeal/SLA monitoring | System throughput, tail latencies, appeal rate | Equilibrium/general-equilibrium effects | Automatic rollback on any quality or SLA breach |

**Final recommendation.** Do not execute the 50% reassignment on current evidence. The crude 6.7-hour gap reverses sign within every complexity stratum and is dominated by queue wait, so the expected benefit is likely illusory, while the risks are concrete: Nova lacks Orion's certification depth and night coverage, so migrated high-complexity or night cases would probably wait longer and appeal more, and halving Orion's inflow disrupts a routing policy that appears to be doing its job. Instead: (1) instrument the process — per-event timestamps, queue snapshots, staffing rosters, and an open-case census; (2) run the small randomized crossover pilot on medium-complexity cases with pre-registered stop rules on appeals and tail latency; (3) attack the mechanisms directly — compliance capacity and night staffing — without changing labels. Decide on intention-to-treat estimates plus mediation shares. If any permanent rebalancing is warranted, scale to 10–15%, never 50%, with automatic rollback triggers.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unrecorded severity driving routing within complexity strata.** Most valuable addition: log the routing engine's decision inputs/score at `Classify` (or collect a blinded severity rating), enabling genuine within-stratum risk adjustment.
2. **Completed-case selection (survivorship bias).** Most valuable addition: a full case registry with status (open/abandoned/closed) and registration timestamps — even a monthly WIP snapshot would bound the censoring direction and magnitude.
3. **Queue mechanics are invisible.** Most valuable addition: per-activity event timestamps plus hourly queue-length and staffing snapshots, enabling waiting-time attribution per DFG edge and queue-state reconstruction.
4. **Reviewer-level heterogeneity hidden inside group means.** Most valuable addition: stable reviewer identifiers with certification metadata, enabling within-reviewer and within-team comparisons that bypass the group label.
5. **Coarse, post-process-contaminated quality signal.** Most valuable design change: an internal QC audit that timestamps defect detection and classifies defect type, separating process-caused defects from downstream 30-day effects before appeals occur.
