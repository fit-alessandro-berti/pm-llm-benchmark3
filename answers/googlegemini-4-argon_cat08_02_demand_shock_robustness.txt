# 1. Stress scenarios

To avoid designing for misleading historical averages, the lab must evaluate performance against four distinct operational stress scenarios derived from the process logs and variant behaviors:

### Scenario A: Winter Infection Wave (Volume Surge + Case-Mix Shift)
* **Characteristics:** Weekly arrivals jump from 2,300 (normal) to 3,300 (peak), driven by a case-mix shift: Urgent cases rise from 18% to 29%, and Complex-panel cases rise from 13% to 20%, while Routine drops from 61% to 42%.
* **Operational Stress:** Urgent orders carry an 18-hour contractual SLA penalty, yet peak median Urgent cycle time hits 17h (p90 = 33h), meaning nearly half of Urgent orders breach SLA. The surge in Urgent and Complex-panel cases disproportionately loads Assay Machines (98% utilization), Technical Validation (101%), and Medical Validation (93% utilization, 22h median queue).

### Scenario B: Assay Machine Degradation / M2 Instability
* **Characteristics:** Total volume is moderate-to-high, but Machine M1 experiences an unplanned outage or M2 suffers heightened calibration drift (especially during night shifts).
* **Operational Stress:** M2 runs faster but has a significantly higher failure rate. In the peak DFG, `Assay run -> Repeat assay` jumps from 6.7% (1,140 / 17,030) to 14.8% (1,260 / 8,500). Because Repeat-after-failure cases consume assay capacity twice and wait a median of 26h for re-run (`Repeat assay -> Assay run`), M2 degradation triggers a self-reinforcing parasitic load loop that starves fresh orders of machine capacity.

### Scenario C: Downstream Clinical Bottleneck (Medical Validator & Specialist Absence)
* **Characteristics:** The every-second-Friday absence of the Specialist Reviewer coincides with a Complex-panel surge or unexpected sick leave in the Medical Validation pool.
* **Operational Stress:** In the peak DFG, 50.8% of cases leaving Technical Validation require Medical Validation or Specialist Review (up from 26.4% in normal periods), because Complex-panel cases are 3.1x more likely to need Medical Validation and 2.4x more likely to need Specialist Review. `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` balloons from 18h to 48h, and `Technical validation -> Medical validation` triples from 8h to 24h. Upstream stations remain clear, but completed assays pile up unreleased.

### Scenario D: Front-End Compression (Courier Delays & Pre-Analytical Defect Surge)
* **Characteristics:** Weather or traffic compresses the 10:00 and 17:00 courier arrival spikes into massive batch drops, combined with high sample defect rates from overwhelmed external clinics.
* **Operational Stress:** Courier intake median queue jumps from 1.0h to 7.5h, and `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` rises from 2h to 9h. Pre-analytical defects (`Request recollection` + `Manual accession correction`) rise from 6.9% (1,260 / 18,120) in normal periods to 12.3% (1,200 / 9,730) in peak periods. The 2 Accession Correction specialists hit 91% utilization (14h queue), and registration clerks work >9h overtime shifts, increasing data-entry errors and feeding more recollection loops that cannibalize intake capacity.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Relieving an isolated constraint in this network inevitably shifts the queue downstream or triggers feedback loops.

```
[Courier/Reg] -> [Pre-Analytical] -> [Prep/Batch] -> [Assay M1/M2] -> [Tech Val] -> [Med/Spec Val]
      ^                 |                                 |  ^
      +-- (Recollect) --+                                 +--+ (Repeat Loop: 14.8% Peak)
```

### Scenario A Migration (Mix Shift & Surge)
1. **Initial Bottleneck:** Pre-analytical check (96% util, 9.5h queue) and Preparation benches (89% util, 11h queue).
2. **First Migration (To Assay):** If managers apply **I8 (Automate registration)** and **I6 (Early pre-analytical triage)**, samples flood into Preparation and Assay faster. To protect the 18h SLA, managers apply **I1 (Strict Urgent-first dispatch)**. Urgent bypass of `Batch wait` (which fell from 74% to 58% in peak) is forced back up. However, breaking batches fragments M1/M2 schedules, reducing effective assay capacity and driving Assay utilization past 98% (queue >18h).
3. **Second Migration (To Validation):** Even if Assay throughput is maintained, Technical Validation is already at 101% utilization (16h queue). Pushing more Urgent and Complex cases through Assay immediately swamps Technical Validation, and subsequently Medical Validation (22h queue), where Urgent and Complex cases converge.

### Scenario B Migration (Assay Degradation / Rework Loop)
1. **Initial Bottleneck:** Assay Machines M1/M2 (98% util, 18h queue).
2. **First Migration (Self- Ingestion via M2):** To protect Urgent and Complex cases from M1/M2 queues, managers implement **I3 (Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex; route Routine to M2)**. While Urgent queues drop initially, shunting 100% of Routine volume onto M2 during high-load and night shifts causes `Assay run -> Repeat assay` (already 14.8% in peak) to spike further.
3. **Second Migration (Starvation & Downstream Flood):** Repeat-after-failure cases have a peak median cycle time of 145h (p90 = 240h). As Routine cases fail on M2, they become Repeat-after-failure cases—which now demand high priority to avoid extreme p90 breaches, either invading M1 or creating an insurmountable backlog on M2. Furthermore, `Repeat assay -> Medical validation` (120 cases in peak) adds direct, unbuffered load onto Medical Validators.

### Scenario C Migration (Clinical Validation Absence)
1. **Initial Bottleneck:** Specialist Review (36h peak queue) and Medical Validation (22h peak queue).
2. **First Migration (Upstream Spillover):** Because Technical Validation is at 101% utilization, managers cross-train 2 Technical Validators to help elsewhere (**I4**) or leave them unassisted. However, if upstream stations are optimized (e.g., via **I2: Larger Routine batches of 24**), Assay dumps large waves of completed runs into Technical Validation (`Assay run -> Technical validation` takes 15h in peak).
3. **Second Migration (WIP Lockup):** Relieving Technical Validation without expanding Medical/Specialist capacity simply pushes cases into the 24h `Technical validation -> Medical validation` edge and the 48h `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` edge. Urgent cases pass Assay in 10 hours but breach their 18h SLA sitting in the 22h Medical Validation queue.

### Scenario D Migration (Front-End Compression)
1. **Initial Bottleneck:** Courier Intake (7.5h queue), Registration (85% util, overtime errors), and Accession Correction (91% util, 14h queue).
2. **First Migration (Technical Validation Starvation/Overload):** To clear the 14h Accession Correction queue (`Manual accession correction -> Test preparation` is 16h in peak), managers deploy **I4 (Cross-train 2 Technical Validators for Accession Correction)**. This unblocks 420+ stuck orders at the front end, releasing a sudden wave of samples into Preparation and Assay.
3. **Second Migration (Downstream Collision):** When that wave exits Assay ~15–20 hours later, Technical Validation has *reduced* staffing because 2 validators were pulled upstream via I4. Technical Validation utilization shoots well above 101%, turning a front-end delay into a late-stage bottleneck.

# 3. Robust redesign package

To stabilize the system across scenarios without adding universal capacity, we implement a **Flow-Smoothed, Quality-Gated Redesign Package** combining modified versions of **I7, I8, I6, and a capped I2**.

### 1. Front-End Smoothing & Error Prevention (I7 + I8 + Overtime Cap)
* **Mechanism:** Implement **I7 (Shift courier pickup slots)** to flatten the 10:00 and 17:00 arrival spikes into four staggered windows, and **I8 (Automate registration for orders with complete digital metadata)**. Strictly cap Registration Clerk shifts at 9 hours.
* **Why it is robust:** Eliminates the root cause of internally generated rework. In the peak log, 1,200 cases (12.3%) fail Pre-analytical check, partly driven by clerk fatigue after 9h shifts. Automating clean digital orders reduces clerk utilization from 85%, preventing overtime errors, lowering load on the 2 Accession Correction specialists, and freeing clerk time from physician recollection calls.
* **Downstream Protection:** Because I8 + I7 would otherwise flood Pre-analytical check (96% peak util) and Preparation, automated registration releases *Routine* orders into a paced digital buffer rather than dumping them onto the floor all at once.

### 2. Early Pre-Analytical Defect Triage (I6)
* **Mechanism:** Split Pre-analytical check into a 2-hour rapid visual/barcode screen upon sample arrival (**I6**), separating clean samples from defects immediately.
* **Why it is robust:** Currently, `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` takes 9h in peak, and `Pre-analytical check -> Request recollection` takes another 7h—wasting 16h before a recollection is even requested (followed by 60h for `Request recollection -> Receive sample`). Catching defects within 2h cuts 14h off the recollection loop and prevents bad samples from consuming Preparation and Assay capacity.

### 3. Time-Capped Dynamic Batching at Preparation (Modified I2 + Controlled Bypass)
* **Mechanism:** Allow Routine batch sizes to scale up to 24 (**I2**) during high volume to preserve Preparation efficiency and minimize Assay setup losses, **but enforce a hard 4-hour time-cap on `Batch wait`** (down from 14h peak median). Urgent cases bypass batching *only* into dedicated micro-slots on Assay machines every 2 hours, rather than continuous random preemption.
* **Why it is robust:** Uncontrolled Urgent bypassing (74% normal) destroys machine schedules when Urgent volume hits 29% in peak, while pure batching of 24 starves Urgent cases. Periodic micro-slots guarantee Urgent cases wait at most 2h at Prep while protecting Assay machine run-blocks from fragmentation.

### Key Assumptions & Failure Modes
* **Assumptions:** (1) At least 40–50% of incoming orders have complete digital metadata for I8; (2) Couriers have contractual flexibility to shift pickup times (I7); (3) Early triage (I6) can be performed by existing intake staff without adding a new queue.
* **Where it may fail:** If an external clinic updates its IT system and sends corrupted metadata in bulk, I8 fails and dumps manual errors onto the 2 Accession Correction specialists. Furthermore, this baseline package does not add capacity to Technical Validation (101% peak util) or Medical Validation (93% peak util).

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

Because no static rule can optimize competing case mixes at >95% utilization, the lab will use four **Triggered Contingency Policies** governed by explicit activation and deactivation thresholds.

### Policy P1: Bi-Directional Cross-Training & Time-Sliced Deployment (Modified I4)
* **Target Scenario:** Scenario D (Front-end defect surge) vs. Scenario A (Downstream validation overload).
* **Policy:** Cross-train the 2 Accession Correction specialists and 4 Technical Validators so both pools can perform basic Technical Validation and Accession Correction. **Rule:** Technical Validators may *only* be pulled to Accession Correction during morning courier spikes (10:00–13:00) and *never* when Technical Validation queues are critical.
* **Trigger ON:** Move 1–2 Technical Validators to Accession Correction if `Accession Correction queue > 6h` (normal is 3h, peak is 14h) **AND** `Technical Validation queue < 8h`.
* **Trigger OFF:** Immediately return staff to Technical Validation when `Accession Correction queue < 3h` **OR** `Technical Validation queue >= 8h`.

### Policy P2: M2 Quality-Gated Routing & Night-Shift Lockout (Modified I3 + I1)
* **Target Scenario:** Scenario B (Assay machine degradation) & Scenario A.
* **Policy:** Instead of unconditionally routing all Routine cases to M2 (**I3**, which spikes repeats), use M1 as the primary workhorse for Urgent, Complex-panel, and Repeat-after-failure cases, plus overflow Routine. Route Routine to M2 *only* during day/evening shifts when calibration staff are present. During night shifts—where Repeat-after-failure cases cluster—down-rate M2 to low-sensitivity Routine assays only, and suspend strict Urgent preemption (**I1**) in favor of 2-hour cadence batching so M1 is not fragmented.
* **Trigger ON:** Activate when `Assay queue > 8h` OR `Urgent Case Mix > 22%`. Activate **M2 Quarantine** (halt Complex/sensitive Routine on M2) if the rolling 12-hour `Assay run -> Repeat assay` rate exceeds **9.0%** (normal is 6.7%, peak is 14.8%).
* **Trigger OFF:** Deactivate when `Assay queue < 4h` and M2 repeat rate remains `< 7.0%` for 24 consecutive hours.

### Policy P3: Clinical Validation Surge Shield (I5 + I10)
* **Target Scenario:** Scenario C (Specialist absence / Complex-panel wave) & Scenario A.
* **Policy:** Activate a temporary evening Medical Validation shift (**I5**) to clear Urgent and Complex queues. Simultaneously, outsource Complex-panel overflow to the partner lab (**I10**) *prior* to `Test preparation`. Even with a 12h transport delay, outsourcing bypasses the internal `Assay (18h) + Tech Val (16h) + Spec Review (36h) + Med Val (22h)` peak queues, saving ~80 hours of internal waiting on Complex cases (normal p90 is 118h, peak p90 is 210h) and freeing internal validators for Urgent SLAs.
* **Trigger ON:**
  * Activate **I5 (Evening Med Val shift)** when `Medical Validation queue > 6h` (normal is 2h) OR `Urgent cases > 24% of daily intake`.
  * Activate **I10 (Outsource Complex-panel)** when `Specialist Review queue > 12h` (normal is 5h, peak is 36h) OR automatically 48 hours before the Specialist’s bi-weekly Friday absence if `Complex-panel mix > 15%`.
* **Trigger OFF:** Deactivate I5 when `Medical Validation queue < 3h` for 2 consecutive days; deactivate I10 when `Specialist Review queue < 6h` and the specialist is on duty.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with the redesign and dynamic policies, four structural vulnerabilities remain:

1. **Technical Validation Saturation (101% Peak Utilization):**
   * *Nature of Fragility:* Cross-training (I4) takes 4 weeks and only shifts existing capacity between Accession Correction and Technical Validation. Because Technical Validation hits 101% utilization in peak periods, simultaneous surges in front-end defects (Scenario D) and assay throughput (Scenario A) leave no slack. If one Technical Validator calls in sick during a peak week, the 16h queue will explode regardless of routing rules.
2. **M1 Single Point of Failure:**
   * *Nature of Fragility:* Policy P2 relies on M1 as the high-reliability anchor for Urgent, Complex-panel, and Repeat-after-failure cases. If M1 suffers a hardware outage lasting >8 hours, all critical volume falls onto M2—immediately triggering high repeat rates (14.8%+) and guaranteed contractual SLA penalties on Urgent orders.
3. **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability — Automation "Flash Floods" & Metadata Blindness (From I8 + I6):**
   * *Nature of Fragility:* Automating registration (I8) and accelerating triage to 2 hours (I6) strips away the natural buffer that registration queues previously provided. If an external clinic's EHR software update introduces a systematic mapping flaw that passes automated digital validation (appears "complete" to I8) but contains clinical assay mismatches, hundreds of flawed orders will bypass Registration Clerks and hit Preparation and Assay simultaneously before human detection at Technical Validation.
4. **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability — Split-Sample Traceability Errors (From I10):**
   * *Nature of Fragility:* Outsourcing Complex-panel overflow (I10) when a single lab order contains multiple sample tubes (e.g., an Urgent tube kept in-house and a Complex-panel tube sent to the partner lab) creates split-order reconciliation risks at `Medical validation -> Release result`, potentially delaying partial urgent releases or causing mismatched patient reports.

### Lagging Outcome Metrics (Verification Only)
1. **Urgent SLA Breach Rate (p90 Cycle Time vs. 18h Contractual Limit):**
   * *Baseline:* Normal p90 = 19h (~12% breach); Peak p90 = 33h (>45% breach). *Target under redesign:* Keep Urgent p90 **< 16.5h** and median **< 11h**.
2. **Routine & Repeat-After-Failure Tail (p90 Cycle Time):**
   * *Baseline:* Routine p90 = 68h normal / 120h peak (Target: 72h); Repeat-after-failure p90 = 132h normal / 240h peak. *Target under redesign:* Keep Routine p90 **< 72h** and Repeat-after-failure p90 **< 96h** (verifying that Urgent priority is not starving non-urgent WIP).
3. **Order Cancellation Rate (`Cancel order / Register order`):**
   * *Baseline:* 3.1% normal (570 / 18,400) -> 5.5% peak (540 / 9,900), heavily driven by failed recollections (`Request recollection -> Cancel order` rises from 35% to 41.5% of recollections in peak). *Target under redesign:* Keep total cancellations **< 3.5%** via I6 (2h early triage) and I8.
