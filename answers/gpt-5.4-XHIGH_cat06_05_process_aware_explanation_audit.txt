## 2. Process-model consistency checks
1. **Prefix-time availability test.** Every feature and every explanation token should carry `event_time`, `known_at`, `source_table`, and `as_of_rule`. Recompute each case from the event log truncated immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed` (`t0`). Any feature with `known_at > t0`, missing timestamp lineage, or a mismatch between the warehouse value and the prefix-only recomputation fails the audit.

2. **Complete-trace detector.** Add static lineage checks that flag definitions using whole-case constructs such as `total_*`, `final_*`, `last_*`, `*_anywhere`, full-trace variants, or sequence mining over the entire trace. These can be valid for retrospective analytics but not for an early-warning explanation unless they are recomputed on the truncated prefix. This is why `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is different from the impossible-looking reversed pattern: it is semantically plausible later, but still inadmissible at `t0`.

3. **Activity-order feasibility and enabledness checks.** Encode the normative process with declarative rules or a Petri-net/BPMN model and run prefix-aware conformance checks. For the prefix `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`, only those two activities are observed. At `t0`, `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Documents Requested`, and conditionally `Fraud Investigation Opened` are future-possible; `Documents Received` is not yet enabled; `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, and `Claim Closed` are impossible at `t0`. Explanations may refer to observed state or current backlog, but not to impossible or merely future events as if they were already known.

4. **Same-timestamp ambiguity handling.** When multiple events share a timestamp and no reliable sequence index exists, treat their within-timestamp order as unknown. Order-dependent features and sequence explanations should then be suppressed or marked unresolved. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` therefore cannot be treated as a genuine reversed sequence; at best it is ordering noise, and because the normative order is the opposite, it should be blocked from explanation use until the source system provides a sequence index.

5. **Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` semantics.** A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` event is legitimate only if there was an earlier `Partial` or `Deny`, then an `Appeal`, then reconsideration. Validation should check both event multiplicity and the decision labels that enable appeal. This avoids misclassifying legitimate appeal-driven reconsideration as duplicate noise.

6. **Role of DFG, footprint, alignment, and declarative checks.** DFGs and sequence mining are descriptive and useful for candidate generation or drift monitoring, but a frequent edge is not a normative rule and is never proof of `t0` availability. Footprint/declarative constraints are better for hard ordering rules such as "appeal requires prior partial/deny" and "claim closed follows final decision or withdrawal." Prefix alignments help determine what is enabled after the observed prefix, but they still need timestamp lineage to catch mutable or warehouse-derived leakage.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations
A feature can be admissible for prediction yet still be unsuitable for causal explanation, intervention design, or fairness-sensitive use.

| Class | Signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`; `initial_reserve` if frozen at intake; repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` after strict `< t0` logic | These features can be described as early indicators associated with expected handling effort or claim-history burden. | Do not say they inevitably cause a breach or justify immutable-customer blame. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`; `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; repaired `document_count_at_t0`; historized `team_at_t0` if captured correctly | These can support statements about current operational load, remaining slack, or the claim’s present processing state. | Do not treat them as root causes independent of staffing policy, queue design, or operating changes. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region`; `claimant_channel`; possibly `team_at_t0` if it proxies geography or routing | At most, say the model found an association that needs governance review. | Do not claim region/channel causes delay, and do not use these as customer-facing reasons or intervention targets without fairness evidence. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week` | It may capture seasonality, holidays, staffing patterns, or policy regime. | Do not claim that certain weeks are inherently bad, or assume this effect will stay stable after process changes. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, and all five sequence-pattern features in their current form | Nothing operational should be claimed from these versions until strict `t0` lineage is proven. Some have repair paths (`team_at_t0`, `score_at_t0`, `prefix_variant_id`, `document_count_at_t0`, `document_requests_to_t0`). | Do not present them as predictive, causal, or actionable early explanations. Also distinguish the two sequence classes: `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is later-feasible but future-only, while `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is not credible even as an ordered later pattern under the stated process. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation
1. **Split retrospective and predictive marts.** Keep outcome/closure analytics in a separate store from the `t0` feature store so post-closure fields cannot accidentally enter training or explanations.

2. **Require historical availability metadata.** Every feature should store `valid_from`, `known_at`, source lineage, and mutability/version information; this is essential for team assignment, reserve amounts, queue snapshots, risk scores, and document counts.

3. **Make prefix-only recomputation the default training and explanation path.** Build train, validation, and explanation datasets by truncating each case at the declared prediction point and recomputing all features from that prefix only.

4. **Add automated process-conformance gates.** Block deployment when features or explanation patterns violate enabledness, order constraints, appeal semantics, or same-timestamp ordering requirements.

5. **Repair features, retrain, and recalibrate.** After removing leaked fields and rebuilding repairable ones, retrain the model and recalibrate probabilities; compare discrimination and calibration before and after cleanup rather than assuming the old score scale still holds.

6. **Audit explanation stability.** Check whether local explanations change materially under repeated extracts, small snapshot-lag perturbations, or minor model re-fits; unstable drivers should not be shown to users or operations staff.

7. **Run proxy and fairness analysis.** Evaluate `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and any repaired routing/team features for disparate error rates, calibration differences, and counterfactual sensitivity across groups and channels before deciding whether to retain them.

8. **Monitor schema and process drift.** Track missing sequence-index rates, snapshot lag, policy changes, holiday periods, feature-definition edits, and path-distribution drift; when drift breaks explanation validity, suppress the affected explanations until the lineage and model are refreshed.
