# 1. Fairness dimensions

**Outcome parity (raw).** Approval rates range from 78% (A) to 58% (D), and cycle-time tails differ sharply (p90: 21.4 vs 38.8 days). Raw gaps are a screening signal only: the groups differ enormously in case mix (e.g., 51% high-risk-band for D, 46% incomplete documentation for B), so raw parity is neither expected under fair administration nor diagnostic of unfairness when absent.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** After controlling for measured complexity, gaps shrink but persist: approval 5.5 pp (B), 2.2 pp (C), 3.1 pp (D); cycle time +2.6/+4.1/+0.9 days. These residuals suggest something beyond measured complexity, but their interpretation is capped by covariate quality (see §5): `documentation_completeness` and `risk_score_band` are themselves potentially contaminated variables, so the adjusted numbers are best read as a plausible range, not a point estimate of unfairness.

**Procedural burden.** Burden = extra steps, handovers, and evidence demands imposed on comparable cases. Group B carries adjusted excesses of +9.5 pp on Request Missing Documents and +8.0 pp on Supervisor Review; Group C +4.2 pp and +5.5 pp. Repeated document requests and added review layers shift work onto applicants, which matters independently of the final decision.

**Timeliness.** Not just totals but *where* time is lost. The largest group-specific waits sit exactly at rights-preserving steps: Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment (3.8 days, 7 bilingual staff) and Accommodation Review  Eligibility Assessment (4.7 days, 5 specialists). Tail inequality (p90 gaps of ~13–17 days for B/C) indicates the disadvantage is worst for the most complicated cases.

**Consistency of routing.** Like cases should travel like paths regardless of group. Variant shares diverge massively (V4 language path: 24% B vs 1% A; V5 accommodation path: 31% C vs 2% A) — largely legitimate, since these steps are need-triggered. But adjusted excesses in document requests and supervisor review for B, *after* controlling for completeness, hint that thresholds for scrutiny may be applied inconsistently across groups.

**Quality/accessibility of support.** The log shows that support steps exist and how long they take, but not whether they work: whether notices are understandable, whether language support resolves the barrier or merely adds a stop, whether accommodation reviews produce usable adjustments. A support step that is slow *and* ineffective is pure burden.

**Appeal/rework burden.** Appeal rates of 13% (B), 10% (C), 16% (D) vs 6% (A), with adjusted appeal probabilities +4.4 pp (B) and +5.7 pp (D), and rework rates roughly double for B/C/D. Appeals extend time-to-finality and impose effort on applicants; elevated rates signal either worse decisions, worse explanations, or differential willingness/ability to appeal — currently indistinguishable.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** Group A cases spread over 52 caseworkers; Group B concentrated on 7 bilingual caseworkers plus 3 overloaded regional officers; Group C on 5 specialists, two of whom also perform supervisor review. Queueing logic alone predicts systematically slower service for concentrated groups — an allocative inequity independent of any individual decision-maker's intent.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

**Goal:** separate (i) disparity (observed difference), (ii) discrimination (difference attributable to group identity through a mechanism lacking legitimate justification), (iii) legitimate routing (policy-mandated divergence), and (iv) uncertainty (sampling noise and covariate contamination).

**Matching / stratification.**
- Strata: `risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`, `channel`, `household_size_band`, `prior_case_count` (banded), `region`, `month`.
- Treat `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested` as *triggers*, not nuisance controls: stratify *within* triggered populations (e.g., among all accommodation-requesting cases, does C still wait longer than A-cases requesting accommodation?) to isolate administration quality from mandate incidence.
- Use coarse exact matching or overlap-weighted IPW; report discard rates and positivity diagnostics; if B/C are nearly absent from some strata, restrict conclusions accordingly.

**Path/variant comparison.** Within each stratum, compare variant distributions (permutation or chi-square tests), per-variant median/p90 durations, and transition-level performance-DFG differences. Classify divergences as (a) mandated (presence of support/fraud steps), (b) ordering anomalies (same activities, different sequence), or (c) unexplained skip/addition. Only (b)/(c) bear directly on consistency.

**Resource-assignment comparison.** Per stratum: caseworker-pool size, cases-per-worker-per-day, handover counts per case, working-together network density, and assignment probability to overloaded resources. Test whether group predicts assignment to the 3 overloaded officers or dual-hatted specialists conditional on region and case type.

**Waiting-time decomposition.** Split each edge into queueing vs service components (via service/waiting-time insertion). Attribute each group's cycle-time gap to specific edges. Key diagnostic: if groups differ mainly in *waiting* at support steps  capacity/allocation problem; if they differ in *service* time at shared activities (e.g., longer eligibility assessments for B)  differential effort or scrutiny.

**Sensitivity analysis for biased covariates.**
- Refit adjusted models three ways: full controls; dropping `documentation_completeness` (possible mediator/collider — it is partly *produced by* the process under study); dropping `risk_score_band` (possibly historically biased proxy).
- Compute how strong an unmeasured confounder would need to be to null each adjusted gap (E-value-style bounding).
- Report results as ranges ("the adjusted approval gap for B lies between X and Y depending on covariate assumptions"), never as a single authoritative number.

**Subgroup checks.** Region × group (are supervisor-backlog regions the same regions where B/C concentrate?), channel × group (does the in-person verification penalty explain part of B's gap?), and key intersections (B × in-person × incomplete docs; C × accommodation). Check temporal stability by month to distinguish persistent inequity from seasonal backlog.

**Uncertainty discipline.** Bootstrap CIs on every adjusted difference and edge-duration contrast; pre-specify primary endpoints (adjusted cycle time; support-step wait; adjusted appeal probability) to avoid post-hoc selection; adjust for multiplicity or interpret cautiously.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

**Likely justified (with caveats):**

| Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|
| D's high Fraud Review share (V3: 28% vs 9% A) | Consistent with 51% high-risk band and stated policy; Risk Screening  Fraud Review waits are similar across groups (2.0–2.8 d), so the mandate is not administered with group-based delay. Caveat: `risk_score_band` neutrality unverified. |
| Presence of V4/V5 for B/C | Steps are rights-preserving and need-triggered (57% language support; 62% accommodation requested). Their *existence* is legitimate. |
| Part of B/C's higher Request-Docs share | Raw incompleteness is genuinely higher (46%/36%), so some excess is real case mix. |

**Concerning:**

1. **Support steps function as time penalties.** V4 median 19.2 d and V5 median 21.6 d vs 5.8 d for the simple path; waits of 3.8 d (language) and 4.7 d (accommodation) stem from 7- and 5-person pools. Because policy sets no SLA for these steps, they absorb all slack. A right that costs two weeks is a diminished right.
2. **Spillover delay.** Completeness Check  Request Docs wait of 1.6 d for B vs 0.4 d for A, attributed to bilingual-staff dependency — the capacity shortfall propagates beyond the support step itself.
3. **Adjusted +9.5 pp Request Docs for B despite controlling completeness.** Either the control fails (completeness is degraded by language barriers *before* measurement, so conditioning is inadequate) or B faces a higher effective evidentiary bar. Both readings demand audit; neither is exonerating.
4. **Adjusted +8.0 pp Supervisor Review for B.** No stated policy trigger links language preference to mandatory supervisory review. If not need-driven, this is inconsistent routing that adds delay (Supervisor  Decision wait 1.9 d vs 1.0 d).
5. **Resource concentration and dual-hatting.** B on 7 bilingual + 3 overloaded officers; C on 5 specialists, two doing supervisor review too (workload conflict and potential self-review of escalations). Fragile, burnout-prone, and predictably slower for the assigned groups.
6. **Handover chains.** General  bilingual  general (B) and general  specialist  supervisor (C) multiply coordination cost and information loss precisely for applicants with the greatest communication needs.
7. **Appeal burden gradient.** Adjusted +4.4 pp (B) and +5.7 pp (D) appeal probability, with 20–24% rework — the process generates contested outcomes disproportionately among already-disadvantaged groups. Cause undetermined.
8. **Channel penalty.** In-person verification wait (1.4 d B vs 0.8 d A) is operational, not need-based, but lands hardest on B (38% in-person).

**Not established:** intentional differential treatment; whether residual approval gaps reflect unmeasured legitimate variation or biased covariates.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

*Legitimate / neutral:*

- **M1 — Mandated complexity.** Policy requires support and fraud steps for certain needs; groups differ in need prevalence, producing different paths and longer cycles even under flawless administration.
- **M2 — Genuine case-mix severity.** More incomplete files and higher risk legitimately generate more requests and reviews.
- **M3 — Channel operations.** Batched in-person intake explains verification waits; B's channel mix transmits this into group-level gaps.

*Structural / capacity (concerning):*

- **M4 — Specialist scarcity and queueing.** Seven bilingual and five accommodation staff create long queues exactly at rights-preserving steps; absent SLAs, these queues absorb all system slack.
- **M5 — Workload imbalance and key-person risk.** Three overloaded officers and dual-hatted specialists slow everyone routed to them; daily-granularity data hides intra-day starvation.
- **M6 — Regional backlogs.** Supervisor backlogs in two regions coincide with B/C concentrations, compounding geographic and group inequity.

*Potentially discriminatory / quality-related (concerning):*

- **M7 — Differential scrutiny.** Adjusted excess document requests and supervisor reviews for B suggest a stricter effective evidence threshold when language barriers raise staff doubt — burden silently shifted to the applicant.
- **M8 — Communication-quality deficits.** Notifications harder to understand (language, format, plain-language failures) would raise appeals and rework with no change in underlying decision correctness.
- **M9 — Proxy-bias feedback loop.** `risk_score_band` and `prior_case_count` encode past enforcement; D is routed to fraud review partly on historically contaminated signals, and today's denials become tomorrow's "prior cases," reinforcing future flags.
- **M10 — System-savvy appeal behavior.** D's high prior-case count may raise appeal propensity independent of decision quality — an alternative to M8 for D specifically.

These mechanisms are not mutually exclusive; the observed pattern is most consistent with M1+M4+M5 dominating the timeliness gaps and M7/M8/M9 being live hypotheses for the residual outcome and appeal gaps.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Endogenous `documentation_completeness`.** It is partly *produced by* the process (language barriers, channel friction, staff diligence). Conditioning on it removes some of the very disparity under study (over-control) while failing to capture its pre-process causes (under-control). Its status as mediator, collider, or confounder is unresolved, so any single adjusted estimate is fragile.
- **Historically biased `risk_score_band`.** If the score was calibrated on past decisions made under unequal conditions, adjusting for it standardizes to an unfair baseline. Neither raw nor adjusted approval comparisons can settle fairness for D without validating the score itself.
- **Missing applicant needs.** Disability severity, assistive-technology needs, literacy, digital access, transportation, work schedules, and caregiving obligations all affect the ability to respond to document requests and appeal deadlines. Unobserved heterogeneity here masquerades as a "group effect."
- **Unobserved resource workload.** Daily-granularity workload cannot distinguish a two-minute touch from a committed day; queue position, overtime, and parallel caseloads are invisible. Waiting-time attribution is therefore approximate, and "overloaded officer" labels rest on coarse evidence.
- **Appeal quality blind spot.** `appeal_flag` records occurrence only — no grounds, overturn rates, or resolution times. Elevated appeals could indicate error (process unfairness) or informed persistence; the current data cannot distinguish these, and they imply opposite remedies.
- **Narrow outcome construct.** `case_outcome` ignores explanation clarity, dignity of treatment, correct benefit amounts, and downstream churn. An "approval" with an under-calculated award is not equivalent to a fair outcome.
- **Selection and composition.** Who applies online vs in-person, who requests accommodation, and who survives into "prior cases" are all selected processes; small cells in region × group × channel intersections inflate noise; timestamp semantics (waiting vs processing) are inferred rather than recorded.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

1. **Add capacity for language support and accommodation review** (hire/train bilingual caseworkers, contract qualified interpreters, cross-train generalists for routine support tasks, pool specialists across regions).
   *Side effects:* cost; diluted specialization if cross-training is shallow; variable interpreter quality; hiring lag requires interim queue rebalancing; seasonal demand may leave new capacity idle.
2. **Service-level targets for support steps** (e.g., Language Support Contact initiated 1 business day from need identification; Accommodation Review scheduled 2 days; Completeness  Request-Docs handoff 1 day).
   *Side effects:* gaming (starting clocks early, token contacts); cannibalizing the general queue unless paired with capacity; rushed support interactions degrade quality; requires trustworthy clock definitions (start at need identification, not first contact).
3. **Routing redesign to reduce handovers** (single accountable case owner; warm handovers with structured context transfer; co-locating bilingual staff with general teams; letting qualified generalists resolve simple language/accommodation tasks without referral).
   *Side effects:* reduced deep specialization; training investment; risk of inconsistent application of specialized criteria; must monitor that de-specialization does not lower decision quality.
4. **Decision-explanation improvements** (translated, plain-language notices; accessible formats — large print, screen-reader compatible; optional in-person walkthroughs; "reasons + what would change the decision" statements).
   *Side effects:* longer drafting time initially; translation costs; badly designed templates can standardize opacity; unfunded mandate if staff time is not allocated.
5. **Continuous monitoring of adjusted disparities** (dashboard of adjusted approval/cycle-time/support-wait/appeal gaps with CIs, sliced by group × region × channel, with alert thresholds).
   *Side effects:* Goodhart's-law gaming; false reassurance if covariates are biased — monitor raw and adjusted side by side; minimum-cell-size suppression for privacy; alert fatigue.
6. **Audit `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`** (validate error rates and predictive parity by group; inter-rater reliability of completeness assessments; explicit language-barrier allowances before marking files incomplete; allow applicants to contest risk classifications; remove or transform contaminated features).
   *Side effects:* transitional inconsistency while recalibrating; possible short-term accuracy loss; stakeholder resistance; the audit itself needs ground truth that may not exist.

A complementary seventh option — root-cause review of a sample of appeals, fed back into decision-quality training — addresses the appeal signal directly, at the cost of analyst time and possible sampling bias toward vocal appellants.

for s, sdf in df.groupby("stratum"):

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The process appears **procedurally unfair in specific, identifiable, and correctable respects**, while several outcome-level questions remain genuinely indeterminate.

**Best supported findings:** Rights-preserving support steps currently operate as time penalties — 3.8- and 4.7-day waits at language-support and accommodation queues, driven by 7- and 5-person staffing pools with no service-level targets — and Groups B and C face concentrated, overloaded resource assignments and extra handovers that are consequences of system design, not applicant complexity. This is a structural timeliness and burden inequity that persists after controlling for case mix, and it is actionable now.

**Moderate-confidence concern:** Group B's adjusted excesses in document requests (+9.5 pp) and supervisor review (+8.0 pp) suggest either differential scrutiny or inadequate measurement of documentation barriers; both possibilities warrant audit, and neither can currently be distinguished from artifact.

**Indeterminate:** Whether the residual approval gaps (5.5/2.2/3.1 pp) reflect unfair decision-making, biased covariates (`risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`), or unmeasured legitimate variation; and whether elevated appeal rates signal worse decisions or worse explanations. Claims of intentional discrimination, or precise quantification of adjusted outcome unfairness, exceed what this evidence supports.

**Calibrated conclusion:** not "indeterminate overall" — the capacity-driven delays at support steps are well-evidenced and fixable — but "procedurally unfair in identified dimensions (timeliness of rights-preserving steps, resource-allocation concentration, likely differential burden), with outcome disparities provisionally indeterminate pending audits of the risk score and documentation measure."
