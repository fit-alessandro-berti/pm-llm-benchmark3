## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **not identifiable from the supplied evidence**. Confidence in that verdict: **70/100**.

The crude Orion mean is higher by 6.7 hours, but Orion also carries 42.9% high-complexity cases versus Nova’s 12.5%, 92.9% night work, mostly high arrival load, and 64.3% compliance-required cases. Stratifying by complexity reverses the association: Orion is faster by about 3.4 hours in high-complexity cases, 2.2 hours in medium, and 2.1 hours in low. This reversal shows that the aggregate comparison is compositional, not causal. It does not, by itself, prove Orion is beneficial: within complexity strata, Orion and Nova still differ on shift, load, compliance, and unobserved risk; several comparison cells are missing; only completed cases are observed; and the “Orion” label bundles staffing, shift, queueing, and routing policy.

The strongest evidence for non-identifiability is the absence of randomization, sparse overlap, completed-case selection, and lack of event-level timestamps. The strongest evidence for a possible true Orion effect is the consistency of the within-complexity reversal and lower Orion wait/service times inside those strata.

A blind 50% reassignment could succeed if Orion’s apparent speed is actually an artifact of unmeasured risk and Nova can handle those cases faster. It could fail by moving complex work to a less experienced group. It could backfire by shifting queues, changing shift coverage, and disrupting the routing system. The proposal is not justified by these data.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No-change retrospective learning phase; do not reroute cases yet | map overlap, queue behavior, and completed-case selection | retrospective case/stratum | no operational change; analyst firewall; document current routing rule | group-specific DFGs; trace variants; overlap counts; wait/service decomposition where available; queue-state reconstruction | defines feasible causal contrast; avoids acting on non-exchangeable averages | if no overlapping cells or no routing logs exist, move directly to randomized pilot |
| 2 | Small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot | after Classify, in eligible strata, randomly assign a capped share of cases to Orion or Nova | case, blocked by complexity, compliance, shift, and load | cap pilot volume at ~10–15%; preserve priority rules; monitor queue lengths; cluster-robust standard errors | timestamps from assignment to review start, compliance start/end, review start/end, request info, close; trace variants; DFG | unbiased estimate of the total resource-group effect; mediation if intermediates are measured | stop if either arm exceeds a wait threshold, backlog grows, appeals rise, or quality audit declines; revert to usual routing |
| 3 | Compliance/queue capacity intervention | add dedicated compliance handling, cross-trained reviewers, or shift overlap; do not intentionally change resource-group label | work queue/scheduling unit or rota | maintain routing rule from phase 2; monitor fairness; avoid overtime-driven quality loss | queue wait around compliance; compliance service time; handoff delays; trace variant frequencies; total cycle distribution | interrupted time-series or stepped-wedge estimate of the compliance/queue mechanism | rollback capacity change if queue times worsen or quality outcomes deteriorate |
| 4 | Conditional scale-up or revised routing rule | if the pilot shows a defensible effect, scale gradually only in strata where benefit is demonstrated | routing rule / case flow | stepwise increments; preserve ability to revert; keep event-level monitoring | same timestamps; queue-state dashboard; group-level workload and outcome surveillance | validates system-level effect under real queue interference | revert any step if operational KPIs, quality, or wait targets degrade |

Final recommendation: The committee should reject the 50% reassignment as a first move. The observed +6.7 h Orion gap is dominated by case mix, shift, load, compliance requirements, and selection into the completed-case sample; the within-complexity reversal warns that the crude comparison is not a safe effect estimate. Instead, adopt a learning-first sequence: no change plus retrospective overlap/queue analysis; a small randomized pilot in feasible strata; then a compliance/queue capacity intervention. Only after those phases should routing percentages be changed, and then gradually and only in strata where the pilot shows benefit. This protects operations from the two opposite errors: sending complex work to a less experienced team if Orion is truly faster, or keeping an inefficient assignment if Orion is truly slower. The necessary administrative investment is modest: event timestamps, queue snapshots, and an open-case registry. These are prerequisites for any defensible causal conclusion.

def manual_dfg(sub):
    from collections import Counter
    edges = Counter()
    starts = Counter()
    ends = Counter()
    for trace in sub["trace"]:
        acts = [a.strip() for a in trace.split(">")]
        starts[acts[0]] += 1
        ends[acts[-1]] += 1
        for a, b in zip(acts, acts[1:]):
            edges[(a, b)] += 1
    return dict(edges), dict(starts), dict(ends)

    if pm4py is None:
        return manual_dfg(sub)

        return pm4py.discover_dfg(log)

        warnings.append(f"PM4Py parsing failed; fallback used: {exc}.")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Unobserved within-complexity case severity is a major residual confounder. The single most valuable additional data field would be a pre-classification risk score or prior claim history.
2. The exact routing rule and assignment mechanism are not recorded. The most valuable fix would be a log of routing-engine inputs, outputs, and rule versions for every case.
3. The data lack event-level timestamps, so queueing, compliance, and repeated review cannot be separated. The most valuable addition would be timestamped activities: classify complete, compliance start/end, review start/end, request info send/return, and close.
4. Only completed cases are observed, making selection bias possible. The most valuable design change would be an open-case registry with current elapsed time and eventual status for every case.
5. Queue state and interference are unmeasured, and the effect of moving one case may depend on other cases in the queue. The most valuable addition would be a queue snapshot at the moment of assignment: depth by group, priority mix, and available staffing.
