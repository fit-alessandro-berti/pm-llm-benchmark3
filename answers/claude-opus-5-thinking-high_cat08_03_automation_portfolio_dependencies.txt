# Automation Portfolio for Small-Business Loan Origination

## Framing: why frequency is the wrong ranking key

Before ranking anything, it is worth quantifying what actually consumes end-to-end time. Multiplying each activity's frequency by its median waiting time gives a rough "waiting-time exposure" per activity (an indicator of where case time accumulates across the population, **not** an additive critical path — see the concurrency caveat below):

| Activity | Freq x median wait (h) | Share of total waiting exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Customer signing | ~321,000 | ~20% |
| Credit analysis | ~249,000 | ~16% |
| Contract preparation | ~167,000 | ~11% |
| Financial document review | ~166,000 | ~11% |
| Wait for external valuation | ~136,000 | ~9% |
| Normalize financial statements | ~104,000 | ~7% |
| Committee packaging | ~101,000 | ~6% |
| Committee decision | ~70,000 | ~4% |
| Loan activation | ~57,000 | ~4% |
| Resolve entity mismatch | ~54,000 | ~3% |
| KYC check | ~46,000 | ~3% |
| Request missing documents | ~26,000 | ~2% |
| Classify product and exposure | ~24,000 | ~1.5% |
| **Register application** | **~18,000** | **~1.1%** |

Register application — the module management wants first — is the *second-lowest* contributor of waiting exposure despite being the second-highest-frequency activity, because its service time is 8 minutes and its queue is 1.5h against an end-to-end waiting total of roughly 190h for a plain case. Two further facts kill the "automate the biggest queue" instinct:

- **The back-office queue is a scheduling artifact, not a capacity shortage.** Register + Classify + Request missing documents together consume roughly 4,800 person-hours against 12 FTE, and utilization falls below 65% after 15:00. The visible queue is caused by batching and shift coverage, so it can be attacked with a rota change at zero capital cost.
- **The credit-analyst pool is the real capacity constraint.** Financial document review (~6,900h), Normalize financial statements (~6,500h) and Credit analysis (~16,300h) sit on 9 FTE. Normalization plus document review is roughly 45% of analyst load. Any module that accelerates *arrivals* into this pool without raising its effective capacity converts a local improvement into a longer downstream queue (Little's law: same service capacity, faster arrivals, bigger queue before Credit analysis, currently 28h).

Two log-quality caveats that shape everything below: KYC check and Financial document review are **concurrent in reality but recorded sequentially**, so KYC's 4h wait is largely hidden inside the document branch and cannot simply be added or subtracted; and `Remediate control issue` (650 in / 510 out around Contract preparation) does not appear in the activity table at all, i.e. part of the rework is measured only in the DFG.

Three DFG anomalies also carry real signal:

- `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation: 380` — cases that leave mismatch resolution **without returning through KYC check**. This is the mechanism behind provisional signing (6.5%) and remediation (1.1%), and it is a control defect, not just a variant.
- `Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis: 5,000` versus Credit analysis frequency 8,900 and `KYC check -> Credit analysis: 4,200` — roughly 3,900–4,200 cases reach credit analysis with **no recorded normalization**. Either they are genuinely simple (likely Credit-line renewal) or analysts normalize inside the 110-minute Credit analysis step, which is hidden work that automation scoping must cover.
- `Committee decision -> Contract preparation: 3,650` versus Contract preparation frequency 7,600 — only about half of contracts pass committee. The committee's weekly cadence is therefore a severe constraint for a *subset* (High-exposure), not a universal bottleneck.

## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies.** A8 is only as good as A6, A4 and A7 outputs — Committee packaging waits on KYC status, normalized statements, valuation (when required) and credit notes, so auto-assembly of unreconciled artifacts industrializes the 15% packaging exception rate. A9 needs structured committee-decision fields to enforce "contract terms must match decision"; today only 3,650 of 7,600 contracts even have a committee decision, so the delegated-authority half needs its own machine-readable decision record first. A7's triage depends on A2-quality exposure classification: an 8% misclassification rate applied to a "valuation not required" rule creates uncontrolled collateral gaps. A5 depends on the document taxonomy that A6 produces, which is why the checklist should ride on A6 rather than precede it.

**Legal and compliance controls.** Three hard gates constrain sequencing. (i) KYC clearance must precede Customer signing — so A10 cannot ship as a pure convenience tool; it must implement the gate, and A4 must close the `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` bypass (380 cases) that currently allows unre-cleared cases downstream. (ii) Four-eyes on Credit analysis and Committee packaging for High-exposure SME (5.8% violations at month end) means A8 must enforce identity-based segregation of duties, and A6 must not let one person both accept extracted figures and package them. (iii) Contract terms must match the committee decision (8.5%, 14% for Credit-line renewal), which makes A12/A8 prerequisites for A9. Across all modules, the audit trail of model suggestion, human override and final decision is a build requirement, not a phase-2 feature — retrofitting it is what usually forces re-certification.

**Human escalation capacity.** This is the binding constraint on the compliance modules. Compliance specialists are 3 FTE, already absorbing ~1,600h of mismatch work with a 38% exception rate, and the handover network concentrates dependency on two seniors — a single-point-of-failure risk. A3 converts volume screening into escalations, so **A3 after A4** is the only safe order. Symmetrically, A1/A2/A5 all increase clean inflow to 9 credit analysts whose pool is the system constraint, so **A6 before any inflow accelerator**. Legal ops (4 FTE) is idle when KYC is slow and overloaded at month end; smoothing KYC/mismatch variance (A4) improves legal-ops utilization before A9 automates their work.

**External valuation dependency.** 2,350 cases wait a median 58h with 19% late, and only 1,980 flow onward to Committee packaging — roughly 370 leak into re-requests or lateness. This is outside direct control, so the levers are triage (avoid valuations that are not genuinely required, notably in the 18% Working-capital population), parallelization (issue the request at classification/credit-analysis *start* rather than after `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request`), and panel routing against measured valuer SLAs. Because packaging is an AND-join, valuation lateness caps the benefit of every other upstream improvement for Equipment finance (71% require valuation).

**Core-banking integration constraints.** Only two modules can be deeply integrated in the first six months. The genuinely deep candidates are A1 (customer/account master), A9 (product and terms data), A11 (booking) and A3 (screening stack). A6 (document store plus credit-engine feed) and A4 (KYC/customer master) are medium-depth; A10 (e-sign platform plus workflow gate), A7 (valuer portal), A5 (customer portal) and A12 (case workbench) are configuration-level. This is precisely why a bottleneck-first portfolio is *feasible* within the IT constraint while a labor-first portfolio spends both deep slots on A1 and A9 — the two modules whose prerequisites are weakest.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Portfolio A — Quick win / labor-first: A1, A2, A5, A9.**
Optimizes visible FTE savings and political credibility: roughly 2,500–3,000 back-office hours plus a large share of legal ops' 4,400 contract-preparation hours, on the highest-frequency activities with the highest feasibility ratings. A9's control-rule checks satisfy the compliance requirement in the thinnest defensible way. Sacrifices: it spends both deep-integration slots (A1, A9) and touches only ~13% of waiting exposure; it does nothing for the analyst queue, the mismatch serialization, external valuation or signing; and it accelerates arrivals into a constrained analyst pool, so end-to-end cycle time may not improve at all. It also ships A9 while its prerequisite (reliable decision data and a maintained rule library) is absent. Note that A11 — the most tempting addition — is excluded outright: automating activation on top of 6.5% provisional signing and 8.5% contract remediation is exactly the downstream-bot-on-unreliable-controls anti-pattern.

**Portfolio B — Bottleneck and synchronization: A6, A4, A10 (KYC-gated), A7.**
Optimizes end-to-end cycle time and waiting time at the four places where case time actually accumulates: the analyst constraint (A6), the 31h mismatch serialization that is on the critical path in 82% of its cases (A4), the 44h signing tail that is the single largest waiting exposure (A10), and the 58h external synchronization point feeding the packaging AND-join (A7). A4 is the compliance-strengthening module (KYC integrity, re-clearance enforcement, audit trail, reduced dependence on two seniors) and A10's hard gate is a second control win. It uses only two medium-depth integrations, keeping IT capacity in reserve. Sacrifices: modest headline FTE savings, so it is politically harder to sell; the visible back-office queue persists (addressed by a rota change, not a module); Committee packaging (26h), contract preparation (22h) and the contract-remediation loop remain untouched until year 2; and it depends on document quality holding up in A6.

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first: A4, A3, A12, A8.**
Optimizes control violation rates and audit defensibility. A4 plus A3 harden the KYC chain (including precedence before signing), A12 addresses the contract-remediation loop that is 14% for Credit-line renewal, and A8 becomes the enforcement point for four-eyes segregation of duties in High-exposure cases. Sacrifices: the majority of cases see little cycle-time relief because signing (44h), normalization (20h) and the credit-analysis queue (28h) are untouched; A3 raises escalation volume into a 3-FTE pool, which may *worsen* KYC waiting before it improves; measured rework will rise as A12 surfaces policy ambiguity; and direct labor savings are the lowest of the three, making year-two funding harder to defend.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

Directional estimates against a plain-case baseline of roughly 190h of waiting; magnitudes are ranges, not point ROI.

**Portfolio A (A1, A2, A5, A9).**
*Cycle time:* removes ~1.5h at Register and ~1–2h at Classify, plus perhaps 8–12h of the 22h Contract preparation queue for clean cases — call it 10–15h gross, under 8%. Net could be **flat or negative** for the 9,200 document-review cases as `Classify -> Financial document review` and `Classify -> KYC check` arrivals accelerate into unchanged analyst capacity.
*Waiting time:* front-of-process queues shrink; the 18h and 28h queues before Financial document review and Credit analysis grow. Median waiting simply migrates one hop downstream.
*Rework:* A2 attacks the 8% misroute rate; A5 should cut the 3,300/2,800 document loop materially; A9 could take contract remediation from 8.5% toward 4–5% **only if** the rule library is current — otherwise `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` (650) rises.
*Handovers:* fewer back-office handovers, but new model-exception handovers appear, and the RM-to-customer loop persists because A5 without an RM instruction template treats the symptom.
*Resource demand:* back office frees ~2,500–3,000h it was not short of; legal ops frees real capacity but stays month-end-peaked; **analysts get worse**.
*Control violations:* contract-terms matching improves; KYC precedence (6.5% provisional signing), the 380-case mismatch bypass and four-eyes (5.8%) are all untouched, and higher throughput at month end tends to increase four-eyes violations.

**Portfolio B (A6, A4, A10, A7).**
*Cycle time:* normalization queue 20h to roughly 4–8h for the clean-document majority; the 28h queue before Credit analysis to roughly 15–20h as ~22% of analyst load is offloaded and hidden normalization inside the 110-minute Credit analysis step shrinks; signing 44h to roughly 20–28h. That is **~35–45h, or 20–25%, for a typical case**, and considerably more for the exposed variants: mismatch cases (31h plus the `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` re-loop of 1,160) and Equipment finance cases where parallelizing the valuation request removes serialization behind Credit analysis on top of the 58h wait.
*Waiting time:* three of the four inputs to the Committee packaging AND-join arrive earlier and more consistently, so its 26h queue improves indirectly even without A8; the weekly committee cadence for High-exposure then becomes the visible constraint, which is the correct next target.
*Rework:* document loops fall because A6 detects missing and internally inconsistent statements at intake; the 38% mismatch exception rate is handled faster with better first-pass quality; valuation lateness (19%) falls with SLA-based routing; the 21% signing reminder loop falls with automated nudging.
*Handovers:* fewer analyst-to-RM-to-customer round trips, fewer specialist ping-pongs on `Resolve entity mismatch <-> KYC check`, and reduced concentration on the two senior specialists — a direct social-network improvement.
*Resource demand:* analyst work shifts from transcription to judgment (which is what the 110-minute High-exposure cases need), with a **new** exception queue for low-quality PDFs that must be explicitly staffed (plan for 15–25% of documents). Compliance specialists move to genuinely ambiguous entities. Back office is unchanged — fix it with the post-15:00 rota, not capital.
*Control violations:* provisional signing should drop from 6.5% toward well under 1% via the hard gate; the 380-case mismatch bypass goes to zero by construction. Four-eyes and contract-terms matching remain **unaddressed** — cover them with detective controls (see section 5) until A8/A12 land.

**Portfolio C (A4, A3, A12, A8).**
*Cycle time:* strong for subsets — 1,750 mismatch cases (31h), 3,900 packaging cases (26h), the 650-case remediation loop — but perhaps only 10–15h for a typical case, because the two largest exposures (signing, credit analysis) are untouched. Expect an initial *increase* in KYC waiting if A3 escalations outrun the 3-FTE pool.
*Waiting time:* packaging improves mechanically via auto-assembly, but the AND-join still waits on un-normalized statements and late valuations, so realized gains will disappoint until A6/A7 exist.
*Rework:* mismatch rework falls; measured contract remediation likely rises before it falls as A12 surfaces ambiguous policy; document loops unchanged.
*Handovers:* fewer compliance ping-pongs, but a new model-to-specialist escalation handover on every KYC hit.
*Resource demand:* the acute risk is the 3-FTE specialist pool; this portfolio requires cross-training KYC ops into first-line mismatch triage, otherwise A3 creates the very queue it was meant to remove.
*Control violations:* best of the three — KYC precedence, four-eyes via segregation-of-duties enforcement in A8, and contract-terms matching all improve, with the strongest audit position.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| **A6 extraction errors on low-quality PDFs** propagating silently into the credit model | Confidence thresholds with automatic routing to an analyst below threshold; field-level provenance links back to the source page; automated reconciliation checks (balance-sheet identities, cross-year consistency); sampled four-eyes review of extracted figures that feed the model; per-source-quality performance dashboards |
| **A3 false negatives** = missed sanctions/AML exposure | Shadow (champion–challenger) running for at least one full cycle before any decision authority; recall floor agreed with compliance; no auto-clearing above a defined risk tier; independent periodic validation; every suggestion, override and final decision logged |
| **A2 misclassification routing cases onto the wrong control path** (exposure tier drives four-eyes and committee cadence) | Conservative bias toward the higher exposure tier; mandatory human confirmation for any High-exposure prediction and for any downgrade; monitor post-hoc reclassification rate as a leading indicator |
| **A10 identity/consent defects and industrialized provisional signing** | Strong customer authentication; KYC clearance as a hard, non-overridable workflow precondition; no provisional-signing path in the tool; any exception requires dual approval plus a logged, time-boxed waiver with mandatory remediation tracking |
| **A9 stale rule library creating legal exposure at scale** | Versioned, effective-dated rule library with a named legal owner; mandatory legal sign-off per release; automated diff report of contract fields against committee-decision fields; release gate blocked if the rule set is older than an agreed review interval |
| **A11 activating loans with an incomplete control chain** | Do not build in year 1. When built: hard completeness assertions on KYC clearance, decision-to-terms match and signature validity, with fail-closed behaviour and a human queue for every blocked case |
| **Model drift** (document formats, applicant mix, valuer behaviour) | Monthly performance and input-distribution monitoring per segment (Micro / SME / High-exposure x product); retraining triggered by threshold breach rather than calendar; automated conformance checking of the event log to detect new variants and edge-count spikes on `Request missing documents`, `Resolve entity mismatch` and `Remediate control issue` |
| **Exception handling becomes the new bottleneck**; residual work is harder and less varied | Size and staff each exception queue explicitly before go-live; publish exception SLAs; rotate staff to preserve manual competence; monitor exception-queue age, not just automation rate |
| **Four-eyes erosion at month end** (5.8% in High-exposure) | Identity-based segregation-of-duties enforcement in the workflow so the same user cannot complete Credit analysis and Committee packaging; automated month-end detective control reporting violations weekly to compliance from day one, even before A8 exists; month-end staffing rota to remove the incentive |
| **Accountability dilution** ("the model decided") | Model output is advisory by policy; a named human owner is recorded for every credit, KYC and contract decision; override reasons are mandatory structured fields; compliance retains second-line challenge and can suspend any module |
| **Shadow work and workaround variants** created by rigid automation (an RM-resistance signal) | Track undocumented activities and off-system rework in the log; include RMs in design of A5/A10 customer messaging; monitor customer complaint and abandonment rates alongside cycle time |
| **Benefit mis-measurement** because KYC and document review are recorded sequentially though concurrent | Fix event-log lifecycle timestamps (start/complete) before the first module goes live; otherwise post-implementation gains will be attributed to the wrong activity and the analyst-queue guardrail cannot be evaluated |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 — Months 0–1.5: measurement and free wins (no modules consumed).**
Fix the event log so KYC check and Financial document review carry start/complete timestamps and true concurrency is visible; register `Remediate control issue` as a first-class activity. Rebalance back-office shifts to use the post-15:00 capacity that currently runs below 65% — this addresses management's visible queue without spending a module. Issue an RM instruction template for document requests (the social-network finding says RMs trigger the loops). Turn on two detective controls immediately: month-end four-eyes violation reporting and an alert on every `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` transition without re-clearance. Baseline per-variant cycle time, per-activity waiting, and DFG loop edge counts.
*Gate to Phase 1:* baseline dashboard live and agreed with compliance; concurrency correctly represented.

**Phase 1 — Months 1–6: the two deep-integration slots plus two configuration modules.**
Deep slot 1 = **A6** (document store plus credit-engine feed). Deep slot 2 = **A4** (KYC/customer master). Configuration-level, so no IT depth consumed: **A10** on the existing e-sign platform with the KYC gate, and **A7** as valuer triage and routing with the process redesign that moves the valuation request to run in parallel with Credit analysis instead of after it.
Sequence within the phase: A4 and A10's gate go live together (the gate is only credible once mismatch re-clearance is enforced); A6 starts in assist-only mode with 100% analyst review, then relaxes to threshold-based straight-through; A7 begins with triage rules and SLA measurement before routing logic is automated.
*Monitoring metrics:* median waiting before Credit analysis (guardrail — must not increase), normalization queue, mismatch queue and re-loop count, e-sign adoption and reminder-loop rate, provisional-signing rate, valuation lateness, straight-through and override rates per module, exception-queue age per pool, specialist escalation volume against 3-FTE capacity.
*Gate criteria to proceed / expand:*
- A6: at least ~85% straight-through extraction on the top three document types with material field errors under ~1% in sampled audit, and no increase in `Financial document review -> Request missing documents`. Otherwise stay in assist-only mode.
- A4: mismatch median waiting from 31h to under ~18h; **zero** mismatch-to-contract transitions without re-clearance.
- A10: e-sign adoption above ~70%; provisional signing below ~0.5%; reminder loop from 21% to under ~10%.
- A7: valuation lateness from 19% to under ~10%; at least ~60% of collateral cases requested in parallel; documented, audited basis for every "valuation not required" decision.
- Hard stop rule: if the waiting time before Credit analysis rises, pause all inflow-accelerating work until analyst capacity recovers.

**Phase 2 — Months 6–12: stabilize, harden, and prepare year 2.**
No new modules (budget is four). Push A6 coverage to the ~3,900 cases that currently reach Credit analysis with no recorded normalization; extend A10 to remaining customer segments; complete A7 panel routing; codify the exception-handling operating model and staffing for each new queue; run the first independent model validation and drift review. In parallel, do design-only work for year 2 — **A12** first (to build the rule inventory and expose policy ambiguity), then **A8** with segregation-of-duties enforcement, then **A9** using the third deep-integration slot, with **A1**, **A2** and full **A5** as cheap inflow accelerators once analyst capacity is demonstrably released. **A11** remains gated until provisional signing and contract-remediation rates are proven low for two consecutive quarters.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Adopt Portfolio B — A6, A4, A10 (KYC-gated) and A7 — as the first-year portfolio.**

It is the only portfolio that satisfies all five constraints simultaneously: four modules; a genuine control-strengthening component (A4 enforcing KYC re-clearance and closing the 380-case bypass, reinforced by A10's hard signing gate); only two medium-depth core-banking integrations in the first six months, leaving IT capacity in reserve; no increase in customer friction, since A10 and A7 make life easier for RMs and customers rather than harder; and a visible political win, because a 20–25% reduction in end-to-end time for typical cases — larger for Equipment finance and mismatch variants — is a stronger story than a headcount saving in a pool that already idles after 15:00.

**Should the highest-frequency activity be automated first? No — but it should be automated second.** Register application carries about 1.1% of total waiting exposure, has an 8-minute service time, and sits in a 12-FTE pool whose queue is a batching and shift-coverage artifact rather than a capacity shortage. A1's own risk note concedes the decisive point: it *exposes* the classification, KYC and document-review bottlenecks. In a system where 9 credit analysts are the binding constraint and roughly 45% of their load is document review plus normalization, automating intake pushes cases faster into that constraint. Local KPIs would improve, back-office headcount would fall, and end-to-end cycle time would plausibly stay flat or deteriorate as the 18h and 28h queues absorb the released volume — the classic outcome of optimizing a non-constraint.

The correct sequence is therefore to **raise the constraint's capacity before accelerating arrivals into it**: A6 first, then A1/A2/A5 in year 2 once the analyst queue has demonstrably shortened. At that point A1 becomes cheap, low-risk and genuinely additive rather than a mechanism for relocating a queue.

The trade-offs being accepted are explicit. Portfolio B leaves Committee packaging (26h), the weekly committee cadence, contract preparation (22h) and the contract-remediation loop (8.5%, 14% for Credit-line renewal) untouched in year 1; it relies on detective controls rather than preventive automation for four-eyes and contract-terms matching; and it depends on A6 performing on real document quality, which is why A6 launches in assist-only mode behind a measurable gate. Those gaps are addressed in year 2 by A12, then A8 with segregation-of-duties enforcement, then A9 — in that order, because automating contract generation and loan activation on top of a control chain that still permits provisional signing and terms mismatches would industrialize precisely the failures the bank is trying to eliminate. Automation shifts where compliance work happens; it does not transfer compliance responsibility away from the bank, and every module above ships with the suggestion / override / decision audit trail that keeps that responsibility traceable to a named human.
