# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Assumptions used throughout, labelled because they are needed to reconstruct decision sets:

- **A1 Timestamp eligibility**: I treat the recorded arrival time as the earliest provably dispatch-visible time. Because arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes, a job may have been physically present slightly earlier; boundary cases are marked uncertain. This is needed to define eligible waiting sets.
- **A2 Appointment semantics**: Appointment times are treated as target times or windows with unknown tolerance. Small late starts may be compatible with appointment scheduling; large late starts are evidence against a strict appointment rule. This is needed because QA, QB, and QD contain appointment evidence of different strength.
- **A3 Resource availability**: The listed availability exceptions are taken as true but not necessarily exhaustive. Unexplained idle time is treated as a possible unrecorded availability or readiness constraint, not as proof of idle resource. This is needed especially for QD.
- **A4 Estimates**: `est_min` is used for SPT/SEPT predictions. In the supplied log, realized total durations match the estimates, but dispatch may have had unpublished updates or private information. This is needed because SPT cannot be evaluated without an estimate definition.
- **A5 Preemption/resumption**: Interrupted jobs retain remaining work. Whether an interrupted job keeps its original queue position or is requeued is ambiguous; both interpretations are considered. This is needed for QB.

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Prediction conventions:

- **FIFO** uses recorded arrival order. For preempted jobs, FIFO can be ambiguous if the interrupted job is requeued; I note both where relevant.
- **Priority** uses priority class, with priority 1 highest. Ties are broken by arrival or shortest estimate where noted.
- **SPT/SEPT** uses `est_min`; for interrupted jobs it is interpreted as shortest remaining processing time where a remaining estimate can be inferred.
- **Appointment** gives precedence to jobs whose appointment time or window is open or imminent; exact tolerance is unknown.
- **Skill-based** restricts eligibility to qualified resources; where several jobs are eligible, another rule is needed.

# 7. Final synthesis

The most identifiable queue is **QD**. Despite the unexplained D-F availability gap, the combination of skill requirements, resource specialization, the D05 wait for the only fully qualified firmware+electrical resource, the D06 appointment, and the routing of D10 to D-X provides multiple independent traces of skill-based routing with appointment and resource-specific FIFO behavior.

The least identifiable queue is **QC**. Its log contains evidence for SPT, priority, deadline escalation, FIFO cleanup, and possible eligibility holds, but no single mechanism explains all decisions. The C04 and C01 selections are especially damaging to simple SPT, priority, or deadline rules unless unobserved holds or manual exceptions are assumed.

The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone is **preemptive priority scheduling**, especially for QB. The visible preemption of B01 by priority-1 B02 is salient, but later choices repeatedly delay priority-1 and priority-2 jobs, resume interrupted lower-priority work, and show dispatcher reassignment. Inferring a stable preemptive-priority rule would overstate the system’s urgency responsiveness, underestimate queue aging, and could lead to harmful staffing or SLA decisions. A close second danger would be inferring pure SPT in QC, because short-job behavior appears early but later deadline and priority behavior is inconsistent.
