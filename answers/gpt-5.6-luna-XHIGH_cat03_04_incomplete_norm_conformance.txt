## 1. Conformance semantics

I treat a graph comparison as evidence about observations, not as a complete legal interpretation of the process. A forbidden relation is a direct conformance concern when its scope, object identity, event ordering, and relation semantics are reliable. It is not made harmless by being rare. A missing mandatory relation is a concern only when the relevant population is in scope and the log is sufficiently complete; the denominator and the abstraction used to detect the relation matter.

An unexpected but unregulated relation is an observation outside the norm, not a violation. It may be operationally useful or worth governing later. Absence of a typical relation is a typical-pattern absence: it can indicate an exception, but it is permitted unless another rule applies. A likely data-quality error is a graph edge whose business meaning is undermined by an identifier collision, missing association, or event-capture defect. An abstraction mismatch occurs when the norm says “eventually” but the summary tests direct succession, or when an aggregate object-type edge hides per-event gaps. Insufficient evidence means that the summary cannot distinguish these explanations; it calls for tracing individual cases rather than a definitive classification.

The specification is open-world for everything not explicitly covered. Thus, an absent edge from a normative graph is not evidence of prohibition, and an observed edge is not nonconforming merely because it is not listed. It is closed-world only for the stated forbidden relations and for mandatory associations or transitions within their stated scope. Even those closed-world checks remain conditional on correct identity, complete enough data, and the specified abstraction.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG summary can establish that, for an object instance, one event type was observed directly before another, subject to the graph construction and its event window. It is useful for finding the two Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package cases, measuring the 98/100 creation-to-validation pattern, and describing typical invoice and closure paths. It cannot by itself establish eventual reachability, prove that an absent successor never happened, explain why a transition was skipped, or validate object identity. Aggregation also hides whether the same exceptional objects account for multiple observations.

N1 is especially important: “should eventually enter validation” is a reachability rule, while the summary uses direct succession as an approximation. An intervening review could make Create Order -> Validate Order absent while the Order still conforms to N1. Conversely, a direct edge in 98 cases does not prove that all 100 eventually validate unless complete traces are checked. N2 and N5 are typical, so their absent direct edges are not violations. N3 is different: it is explicitly forbidden and, if the Order identity and temporal order are correct, the two cases are genuine high-severity findings. N6 and all unlisted OC-DFG paths remain open-world.

### OTG

An OTG establishes aggregate object-type relationships and, where represented, structural relations such as object cobirth or codeath. It can show that T1, T2, and T3-type relations exist and can flag the forbidden Order–Payment cobirth. It cannot show that every event instance has the required association, that a weight equals a population denominator, or that a relationship derived by a join is semantically valid. It also cannot decide policy for T4 or T5, which are explicitly unspecified.

The 200 Package–Delivery interaction edges look conforming at the OTG level, but E6 reports only 198 of 200 Dispatch Package events with Delivery. That is a classic aggregate-versus-event inconsistency, not a reason to discard either result. Similarly, the 1450 Order–Item edge does not erase the two Add Item events with no Order association. The seven codeath edges are unregulated. A type-level relation can therefore be conforming while a per-event association is suspicious, and a suspicious edge can disappear after correcting an identifier join.

### ET–OT

ET–OT directly tests whether event types are associated with required object types. It can establish the complete E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 coverages, the two-event E2 gap, the two-event E6 gap, and the one-event E8 gap in this snapshot. It is the clearest family for mandatory association coverage. It cannot establish event ordering, eventual business outcomes, correctness of object identity, or whether an unlabeled payment is a legitimate exception. A carrier reference is evidence to investigate, not automatically an ET–OT relation.

The one Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is a forbidden ET–OT observation, but the known reused numeric identifier makes data repair the leading explanation. Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase is legitimate and unspecified; its additional Customer link is unregulated. ET–OT can thus expose a real association defect that OC-DFG and OTG miss, while it can also produce an apparent violation from a bad join. Counts must always be read against the number of applicable events, not against total log size.

- N5’s 92 payment paths are typical, while the four write-offs and possibly the fourteen no-successor invoices are permitted exceptions or unresolved cases.

Unregulated:

- Validate Order -> Issue Invoice (72), Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review (3), and Reopen Order -> Close Order (1) are not OC-DFG violations.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. N1 does not specify the permitted time window or whether direct succession is required. The risk is false alarms or missed overdue validation. Clarify an eventual-validation deadline and define direct succession separately; make eventual validation mandatory and direct succession typical or unspecified.

2. The norm does not define log completeness or right-censoring. The fourteen invoices without successors could be reported as unpaid when the observation simply ended. Declare the observation boundary and ledger reconciliation rule; treat absence as unspecified until completeness is established.

3. “Standard Order” and “externally shipped Package” are not operationally defined. The risk is applying N1 or N4 to the wrong population. Add explicit inclusion and exclusion attributes; retain the applicable transition as mandatory for included objects.

4. N3 lacks a precise definition of cancellation effectiveness, dispatch commitment, and same-Order identity. A late cancellation or cross-order join can produce opposite business decisions. Define temporal precedence and immutable identifiers; keep the prohibition mandatory/forbidden as appropriate.

5. N4 does not say whether Pack must be directly before Dispatch or merely precede it eventually, nor whether carrier evidence substitutes for a Delivery association. Clarify both transition and acceptable evidence; make the chosen shipment control mandatory.

6. E8 mentions typed unapplied cash, but the snapshot has no typed event. Unlabeled exceptions can become false violations or conceal unreconciled funds. Require an explicit unapplied-cash status and matching workflow; invoice linkage is mandatory unless that exception is recorded.

7. N2 and N5 have no frequency, aging, or tolerance bands. Operations cannot tell an acceptable exception rate from a deteriorating process. Define reporting thresholds and aging triggers; keep the relation typical rather than turning normal exceptions into mandatory behavior.

8. OTG weights and event-level cardinality are not defined. The 200 T2 edges cannot be compared safely with 198 E6 associations without knowing edge construction. Specify whether every event must produce an edge and how aggregate edges are counted; make per-event required links mandatory.

9. Cobirth and codeath lack temporal granularity and business-event identity. Coarse timestamps may falsely trigger T6, while codeath may be useful but currently ungoverned. Define business-event keys and time windows; retain true same-event Order–Payment cobirth as forbidden and explicitly leave codeath unspecified until governed.

10. The norm does not define identity provenance or join quality for sensor and financial data. A reused numeric ID can contaminate several models. Require globally scoped source identifiers and reject ambiguous joins; keep a genuine Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link forbidden.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

Immediate containment should first quarantine the two cancelled Orders that appear to have dispatched, verify whether packages physically left, and stop any remaining downstream fulfillment or settlement action if the cancellation is valid. Next, investigate the Order–Payment cobirth before relying on it for controls. Isolate the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge from financial reporting while the join is repaired. These actions target explicit forbidden behavior; the rare review and codeath observations need no containment.

Data repair should reconstruct immutable event and object identifiers, correct the suspected sensor join, and de-duplicate Add Item and Payment associations. Add the carrier-reference-to-Delivery links where they can be proven, label the one unapplied payment if finance confirms that status, and re-run all three graph families after repair. Preserve the pre-repair PM4Py diagnostics for audit comparison.

Process investigation should trace the two N1 cases for eventual validation, the two N4 package cases, the two Add Item events without Orders, the two dispatch events without Delivery, and the one unmatched payment. Separately reconcile the four write-offs and fourteen no-successor invoices to ledger state, due dates, and the export boundary. Review the 3 compliance reviews as legitimate control cases rather than escalating them because they are rare.

Norm revision should resolve the ten gaps above, especially eventual-versus-direct semantics, population scopes, exception labels, and identifier/time definitions. Monitoring should report mandatory coverage by applicable population, forbidden events as case-level alerts, typical patterns as trends with aging and thresholds, and data-quality linkage errors separately. Track OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT discrepancies together so aggregate conformance cannot mask event-level gaps.
