**Framework Choice:** We use **Interventional Indirect Effects (IIE)** rather than Natural Direct/Indirect Effects. Natural effects require cross-world assumptions that are violated when mediators (like queue wait) depend on the treatment (flag) and affect future treatments (resource allocation). IIE allows us to simulate "what if flagged cases were routed differently while holding queue dynamics constant," which is more robust for process mining.

**Confounding and Interference:**
Interference arises because `U_system_congestion` is shared; one flagged case entering `manual_queue_wait` increases wait times for subsequent unflagged cases. Exposure-induced confounding occurs because `arrival_load` influences both `priority_lane` assignment and `triage_wait`, and the flag itself alters system load. Treatment-confounder feedback exists if reviewers adjust `complexity` assessments based on seeing the flag. We cannot separate individual case effects from system-wide queue dynamics without structural modeling.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1.  **Hidden Risk:** If unflagged cases contain high-risk profiles (low `risk_score` but high `U_underlying_risk`) that currently slip through undetected, removing the flag could increase harm despite faster times.
2.  **Queue Interference:** If the "protected capacity" (P3) causes critical delays for unflagged cases that are actually high-priority emergencies, the net system utility decreases.
3.  **Decision Quality:** If the observed longer cycle time for flagged cases is primarily due to necessary rigorous checks preventing false approvals, shortening the time increases compliance risk.
4.  **Timestamp Semantics:** If `triage_start` reflects when a human *views* the case but work began earlier (backlogged), the observed triage acceleration is illusory.
5.  **Positivity/Lack of Overlap:** If all flagged cases are inherently High Complexity, we cannot estimate the effect of the flag on Low Complexity cases (no counterfactual support).
6.  **Reviewer Behavior:** If reviewers speed up flagged cases specifically because they know they are being audited (Hawthorne effect), the effect disappears once monitoring stops.

## 9. Final synthesis

We directly observe that flagged cases experience significantly shorter wait times before triage begins compared to unflagged cases, confirming the priority lane mechanism. However, this acceleration is offset by substantially longer waiting periods in the manual review queue and higher escalation rates, resulting in comparable or longer total cycle times.

The plausible mechanism is that the flag successfully routes cases to faster initial intake but forces them into a bottlenecked, high-scrutiny downstream process. This is likely compounded by confounding: flagged cases are intrinsically more complex and risky, requiring more effort regardless of the flag. The relationship remains unidentified regarding whether the flag itself causes the downstream delay or merely identifies cases that would delay anyway.

The most useful next evidence comes from Policy P3 (Protected Capacity). By testing whether dedicated resources reduce the manual queue wait for flagged cases without starving unflagged ones, we can isolate whether the delay is due to resource contention or inherent case difficulty. This distinguishes process inefficiency from necessary risk mitigation.
