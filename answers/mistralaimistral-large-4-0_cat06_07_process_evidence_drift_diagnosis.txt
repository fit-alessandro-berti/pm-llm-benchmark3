## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March** — stable baseline. Observed breach 0.18–0.19, mean predicted 0.18–0.19, AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE 0.022–0.025, unknown <0.3%, missing 1%, label delay 32–34d. Variant shares stable: V1 ~45%, V2 ~35%, V3 ~20%. Part edge 28–35h. No drift.

**April** — schema/pipeline incident. The workflow migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`; the feature pipeline recognized only the old label; duplicates and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` appeared. Unknown activity 17%, missing queue 14%, unmapped variants 18%. Observed breach stayed 0.19, but mean predicted rose to 0.28, AUROC fell to 0.67, ECE rose to 0.110. This is measurement failure, not real outcome deterioration. Label maturity 100% and stable part edge support this.

**May** — partial recovery. The mapping patch was deployed; unknown activity fell to 2%, missing queue to 3%, AUROC recovered to 0.78, ECE to 0.050. Residual pipeline effects remain: the cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature was not fully backfilled for April, and some April records were not recomputed. May is improved but not fully trustworthy.

**June** — real routing/process mix change. The remote-routing policy increased V2 from 34% to 52%, reduced V1 from 43% to 30%, and reduced V3 from 22% to 18%. Observed breach dropped from 0.18 to 0.15. Matured breach by variant stayed stable: V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35. The remote-fix edge stayed stable. Mean predicted 0.20 exceeded observed 0.15, and ECE worsened to 0.060. This is covariate/process drift, not concept drift. The model needs recalibration or routing features, not necessarily retraining.

**July** — early supplier process drift. The parts supplier began missing targets on 10 July. The `Order Part -> Part Received` edge rose from 42h in June to 65h. V3 matured breach rose from 0.35 to 0.46. Overall observed breach was 0.19, AUROC 0.76. Label maturity was 98%. This is process drift concentrated in V3.

**August** — supplier process drift becomes severe; early concept drift; some censoring. The part edge reached 130h. V3 share rose to 35%. V3 matured breach reached 0.64. Overall observed breach rose to 0.28, mean predicted was only 0.19, AUROC fell to 0.68, ECE rose to 0.120. Maturity was 93%. This is real deterioration in the V3 path; the model underpredicts. It is not just label delay.

**September** — population shift overlaps with supplier process drift and censoring. Partner Z reached 24% of cases. V3 share rose to 42%. The part edge reached 148h. V3 matured breach was 0.61. Partner Z observed breach was 0.45 versus 0.27 for others, but Partner Z labels mature more slowly. Rural travel distance and device age are higher for Partner Z, and those features were not in the model. Overall observed breach was 0.31, mean predicted 0.21, AUROC 0.66, ECE 0.140. Maturity was 82%. This month cannot be attributed to one cause.

**October** — label maturity/censoring dominates. Maturity was only 55%, median label delay 52d, and the outcome API backlog disproportionately affected longer resolution paths. Observed breach 0.16 is not comparable to mature months and is likely a severe underestimate. Mean predicted was 0.23, AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205, ECE 0.130. Process evidence continued: part edge 155h, V3 41%, Partner Z 27%. October should not be treated as improvement or as model failure alone.

These are not one homogeneous failure. April is a measurement incident; June is a mix shift; August–September are a real V3 process/concept deterioration plus population shift; October is a label-availability artifact. Treating them as one drift type would lead to the wrong remedy.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**Test 1: Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**  
Procedure: For each case-start month, plot cumulative label maturity against days since diagnosis. Define a mature cohort as cases with at least 90 days of follow-up or a known outcome. Backtest the model on fixed vintages: predict at diagnosis, evaluate only on matured labels.  
Expected under label censoring: October metrics on all labels show an artificially low breach rate and inflated calibration error; matured-only metrics show a higher breach rate and may still be poor if the model is degraded.  
Expected under true model deterioration: Matured-only metrics remain poor even after maturity.  
Expected under pipeline failure: April maturity is 100%, so fixed-vintage backtesting does not fix April; metrics remain degraded until features are recomputed.

**Test 2: Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**  
Procedure: Re-extract events from the raw source; map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` back to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`; remove duplicates; recompute prefix features; compare April/May metrics before and after.  
Expected under pipeline failure: April AUROC/Brier/ECE return near baseline, e.g. AUROC ~0.81 and ECE ~0.025; May residual improves.  
Expected under real drift: Recomputation does not restore April metrics; observed breach remains stable but model performance remains degraded.

**Test 3: Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**  
Procedure: Compute AUROC, Brier, ECE, and mean predicted versus observed by month × variant × source on matured labels only.  
Expected under covariate/process mix shift: Overall metrics degrade but within-variant metrics remain stable; the V2 share increase explains the overall breach drop.  
Expected under concept drift: Within-V3 metrics degrade while V1/V2 remain stable.  
Expected under population shift: Partner Z metrics are poor and non-Partner Z metrics are stable; adding Partner Z features or reweighting improves calibration.

**Test 4: Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**  
Procedure: Use PM4Py to discover DFG and performance DFG by month; compare edge frequencies and median times, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis  `Remote Fix`, and diagnosis  `Dispatch Technician`.  
Expected under process drift: Part edge times rise from July onward; variant shares shift in June and August–October; remote-fix edge remains stable.  
Expected under pipeline failure: April DFG shows `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` and missing edges; after normalization, the DFG resembles baseline.  
Expected under concept drift without process drift: DFG edge times remain stable but outcome rates change; here both occur, so combine with Test 5.

**Test 5: Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**  
Procedure: Fit or compare models within each variant by month on matured cases; test month × feature interactions; compare calibration slope and intercept; check whether a part-delay feature explains the month effect.  
Expected under concept drift: V3 model coefficients or risk ordering change; the same feature profile has higher breach probability in August–September than in January–June; calibration slope <1 or intercept shifts.  
Expected under process drift only: The conditional relationship P(Y|X) is stable; the V3 breach increase is explained by measured process features such as part delay; the month effect disappears after adding part delay.  
Expected under population shift: The Partner Z effect disappears after adding rural/device age features or reweighting.

**Test 6: Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z**  
Procedure: Compare Partner Z and non-Partner Z on case features; compute propensity scores or weights; reweight non-Partner Z to the Partner Z distribution or vice versa; evaluate the model on reweighted samples; check overlap.  
Expected under population shift: Model performance on Partner Z improves after reweighting or adding missing features; non-Partner Z remains stable; the observed breach difference shrinks after adjustment.  
Expected under concept drift: Reweighting does not fix within-V3 calibration; both sources worsen similarly in V3.  
Expected under label censoring: Reweighting on immature labels remains unstable; matured-only reweighting is needed.

**Test 7: Feature missingness and lineage audits**  
Procedure: Audit missing queue feature, unknown activity share, duplicate rates, and feature computation lineage; trace April records from raw events to features; compare cached versus recomputed features.  
Expected under pipeline failure: April missing queue 14%, unknown 17%, cached feature mismatches; after recomputation missingness drops to ~1%.  
Expected under real drift: Missingness remains low; feature distributions shift but no lineage errors appear.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment now**  
- Freeze training and automatic retraining.  
- Exclude October from training/evaluation until label maturity is at least 95%; use only matured subsets for August–September.  
- Do not recalibrate on April/May until pipeline features are recomputed.  
- Use model outputs as advisory only; add manual review for V3 cases with long part delays and for Partner Z cases.  
- Monitor the part edge and variant shares daily.

**Short-term recalibration after pipeline repair and label maturity**  
- Recalibrate on matured months: January–March, June–July, and matured subsets of August–September, using Platt scaling or isotonic regression.  
- Consider segment-specific intercepts for variant and source, especially V3 and Partner Z, because overall calibration is poor but within-segment calibration may be stable.  
- Add routing/variant features to address the June mix shift.  
- Add supplier-delay features such as current median part edge and days since order.  
- Add Partner Z features such as rural travel distance and device age.  
- Do not recalibrate around the April pipeline bug; use recomputed April/May only after validation.

**Feature repair**  
- Recompute April/May features after normalizing `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` to `Initial Diagnosis Completed` and removing duplicates.  
- Backfill the cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature for April.  
- Add new features: variant, source, partner flag, rural distance, device age, supplier delay indicators.  
- Validate lineage and missingness before using these months.

**Full retraining**  
- Retrain only after labels mature, e.g. September/October cases with at least 90 days of follow-up, and after pipeline repair.  
- Safe months: January–March definitely; April/May only after recomputation; June–July with caution; August–September only matured subsets; October excluded until mature.  
- A segment-specific model is justified for V3 because V3 breach risk is much higher and rising, from 0.30–0.35 baseline to 0.61–0.64 in August–September, and supplier drift affects V3 specifically. V1/V2 are stable and lower; a single model with strong variant interactions may suffice initially.  
- Partner Z may need separate calibration or features initially; a fully separate Partner Z model should wait until enough matured Partner Z data exist.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Predictive monitor**  
Track AUROC, Brier, and mean predicted risk versus observed breach on matured labels only. Alert if AUROC < 0.75 or Brier > 0.18 for two consecutive months, or if the mean predicted/observed ratio leaves [0.9, 1.1].

**Calibration monitor**  
Track ECE, calibration slope/intercept, and reliability curves by month. Alert if ECE > 0.05 or slope leaves [0.8, 1.2].

**Label-maturity monitor**  
Track percent mature labels by case-start month, median label delay, and backlog age. Alert if maturity < 95% or median delay > 45d. This alarm should suppress training/evaluation, not trigger retraining.

**Data-quality monitor**  
Track unknown activity share, missing queue feature, duplicate rate, unmapped variants, and schema changes. Alert if unknown > 2%, missing > 5%, duplicates > 0.5%, or new activity labels appear.

**Process-variant monitor**  
Track V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares by month. Alert if any variant share changes more than 10 percentage points month-over-month or unmapped exceeds 1%.

**Performance-DFG monitor**  
Track median edge times, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis  `Remote Fix`, and diagnosis  `Dispatch Technician`. Alert if the part edge exceeds 72h or rises more than 50% versus baseline.

**Population monitor**  
Track source/partner share, rural/device age distributions, and PSI. Alert if a new source exceeds 5% or PSI exceeds 0.2.

**Intervention/deployment monitor**  
Track model version, recalibration/retraining events, policy changes, and alert actions. Require human approval for retraining.

**Alert dependencies**  
- Label-delay alarm  blocks training/evaluation; does not trigger retraining.  
- Data-quality alarm  blocks training; triggers pipeline repair; suppresses calibration alerts until resolved.  
- Process-variant/performance-DFG alarm  triggers segment review; may trigger feature addition or segment-specific recalibration, not automatic full retraining.  
- Population alarm  triggers stratified analysis and reweighting; not automatic retraining.  
- Only the combination of mature labels, clean data quality, and degraded predictive/calibration on matured cohorts triggers retraining.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Load raw events
raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp"])

# Format for PM4Py
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

# Normalize renamed diagnosis label BEFORE feature extraction
df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})

# Keep unknown activities visible for audit rather than silently dropping
df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({"UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY": "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY_AUDIT"})

# Duplicate handling: sort and drop exact duplicate event rows.
# If an event_id column exists, prefer dropping duplicates on event_id.
df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "activity"])
if "event_id" in df.columns:
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["event_id"], keep="first")
else:
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first")

# Cohort definitions:
# - event-calendar month: month in which events occurred (activity volume view)
# - case-start month: month in which the case started (outcome/label maturity view)
df["event_month"] = df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
case_start = df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")
df = df.merge(case_start, on="case_id")
df["case_start_month"] = df["case_start"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)

def monthly_process_summary(df, month_col, month):
    """Return auditable monthly process evidence for one cohort type."""
    cohort = df[df[month_col] == month].copy()
    if cohort.empty:
        return None

    # Event-calendar cohort: restrict to events inside the calendar month.
    # Case-start cohort: keep all events for cases that started in that month.
    if month_col == "event_month":
        start = pd.Timestamp(month + "-01")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(0)
        cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(cohort, start, end)

    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        cohort,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        log,
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        case_id_key="case_id"
    )
    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        log,
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        case_id_key="case_id"
    )
    perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        log,
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        case_id_key="case_id"
    )
    temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        log,
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        case_id_key="case_id"
    )

    return {
        "month": month,
        "cohort_type": month_col,
        "n_cases": cohort["case_id"].nunique(),
        "n_events": len(cohort),
        "unknown_activity_share": (cohort["activity"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY_AUDIT").mean(),
        "variants": variants,
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "temporal_features": temporal_features,
    }

months = sorted(df["event_month"].unique())
summaries = []

for month in months:
    # Event-calendar cohort: what happened in that month
    summaries.append(monthly_process_summary(df, "event_month", month))
    # Case-start cohort: cases that started in that month (better for label maturity)
    summaries.append(monthly_process_summary(df, "case_start_month", month))

# summaries is an auditable monthly list; no model is fitted here.
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**1. Retraining on immature labels, especially October**  
Risk: The model learns that the breach rate is 0.16 when the true matured rate is likely higher; underprediction worsens and future high-risk cases are missed.  
Mitigation: Enforce a label-maturity threshold, e.g. at least 95% maturity or 90 days of follow-up; use fixed-vintage backtesting; exclude October until labels mature.

**2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug, especially April/May**  
Risk: Recalibrating on April’s inflated mean predicted risk or missing features distorts the model; when the pipeline is fixed, the model is miscalibrated on clean data.  
Mitigation: Repair the pipeline first; recompute features; validate; only recalibrate on clean, matured data.

**3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept/process drift in August–September**  
Risk: Attributing August–September deterioration to population shift or label delay misses the V3 supplier delay; high-risk V3 cases are not flagged and breaches increase.  
Mitigation: Monitor performance-DFG edges; segment by variant; add supplier-delay features; retrain the V3 segment on matured data.

**4. Attributing Partner Z’s population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence**  
Risk: The observed breach difference of 0.45 versus 0.27 may be driven by slower label maturity, missing rural/device age features, and higher V3 share; overcorrecting with fairness constraints or ignoring real risk differences is possible.  
Mitigation: Stratify by source and maturity; add missing features; use reweighting or transport-style checks; evaluate on matured labels before fairness interventions.

**5. Treating June’s routing mix shift as concept drift and retraining unnecessarily**  
Risk: Retraining on June data without variant features may overfit to the new mix; ignoring the mix shift causes overprediction.  
Mitigation: Include variant/routing features; recalibrate the intercept; monitor variant distribution; avoid full retraining until labels mature.

**6. Using October’s observed breach rate as comparable to mature months**  
Risk: A false conclusion of improvement is drawn; the model appears better but is actually evaluated on censored labels.  
Mitigation: Gate all metrics on label maturity; report matured-only metrics; do not compare raw monthly breach rates across different maturity levels.
