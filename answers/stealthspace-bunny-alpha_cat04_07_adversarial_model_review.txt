# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The reported metrics are useful diagnostics, but they do not establish that either model correctly implements the dossier policy. In particular, a high alignment score can be obtained by a model that accepts forbidden behavior, and a low holdout score can be caused by failure to represent legitimate variants.

I distinguish four intended uses:

| Use | Model requirements | Appropriate precision–coverage trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration** | Discover recurring behavior, propose candidate workflows, identify data-quality problems, and make uncommon variants visible. | Greater tolerance and coverage are desirable, but accepted traces must remain distinguishable from certified compliant traces. |
| **Conformance enforcement** | Reject known policy violations, enforce mandatory activities and ordering, and explain every rejection. | False acceptances are usually more costly than false rejections. Coverage matters, but an unexplained rejection of a valid case is also a defect. |
| **Simulation** | Generate executable scenarios without impossible control flow; model resources, timing, and case attributes explicitly. | A permissive discovery model may omit important transitions; an enumerated model may severely underrepresent combinations. |
| **Stakeholder communication** | Make obligations, assumptions, and exceptions understandable to process owners and auditors. | A visually simple model can still be semantically misleading. Comprehension must be tested separately from operational accuracy. |

The following assumptions are material:

1. **`tau` in Model S means a silent/no-op branch.** This interpretation is consistent with F1 and F7. If the supplied text was literally parsed as an activity named `tau`, the reported results do not describe the intended model and must be reproduced with an explicit silent-transition representation.
2. For normal single-case execution, process-tree exclusive choice means one branch, parallel composition means all branches, and `->` is a sequence.
3. “Exactly one approval” is a single-case obligation, not a statement that approval activities can never repeat across separate cases.
4. P3 is interpreted as **source-conditional presence**:
   - external order: Supplier Review is required;
   - internal order: Supplier Review is absent.

   The weaker reading—external orders require Supplier Review, but internal orders may also contain it—would require relaxing one third-model rejection below. This ambiguity should be resolved by the process owner.
5. P4 does not state that Expedite Review requires an urgency attribute. Its ordering is enforceable; any additional business eligibility condition remains unresolved.
6. The normal compliance interpretation places corrections and rechecks before approval. Allowing correction after approval would need an explicit policy amendment.
7. “Suspicious” is not synonymous with “forbidden.” B8 should be flagged for investigation rather than automatically treated as conclusively malicious.
8. Event order is the business-process order. Timestamps, duplicate deliveries, and source-system ordering rules need documented preprocessing.

Under these assumptions, **Model S is a permissive exploration proposal, not a conformance model. Model C is a restrictive observed-variant model, not a general policy implementation.**

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S has four credible advantages:

1. **Broad observed-case coverage.** Its reported holdout fitness and token-based generalization remain substantially higher than C’s.
2. **Compact source.** The main control-flow structure is much easier to inspect and change than a list of frequent trace variants.
3. **Exploratory flexibility.** Silent alternatives allow analysts to investigate partial or evolving workflows without declaring every missing event immediately noncompliant.
4. **Reasonable handling of the two mandatory check orders.** Credit Check and Technical Review can occur in either order under its parallel composition.
5. **Approval exclusivity is represented correctly.** Under ordinary process-tree semantics, the exclusive choice between Manager Approval and Auto Approval prevents selecting neither or both in a single normal trace.

F7 is therefore defensible **only for explicitly exploratory use**. Exploration means proposing candidate behavior, not certifying that behavior as compliant.

## Strongest case against Model S

The central problem is that its permissiveness is semantic, not merely statistical. Interpreting each `X(activity, tau)` as “activity or no-op” makes all four checks independently skippable.

The following are concrete accepted traces. The first six are clear policy failures; the remainder expose additional semantic hazards.

| Trace | Model S behavior | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A1:** Register  Manager  Release | All four silent branches and Manager Approval are selected. | **Forbidden.** Mandatory checks and both mandatory review branches are omitted. This is B3. |
| **A2:** Register  Credit  Manager  Release | Technical, Supplier, and Expedite branches are skipped. | **Forbidden.** |
| **A3:** Register  Technical  Auto  Release | Credit is skipped. | **Forbidden.** |
| **A4:** Register  Credit  Supplier  Technical  Expedite  Auto  Release | Supplier occurs before both checks finish; Expedite finishes after both. | The stated policy does not order Supplier Review, so this is not clearly forbidden. It nevertheless demonstrates that S cannot enforce any unstated review-entry condition. |
| **A5:** Register  Credit  Technical  Expedite  Manager  Release | Supplier Review is skipped. | **Undecidable without source status.** Forbidden for external orders under the adopted interpretation; acceptable for internal orders. |
| **A6:** Register  Credit  Technical  Expedite  Auto  Release | Supplier Review is skipped; Expedite precedes Supplier. | **Undecidable without source status.** The policy expressly allows Expedite before Supplier after the mandatory checks. |
| **A7:** Register  Credit  Credit  Technical  Supplier  Expedite  Manager  Release | The second Credit is admitted without Correct Dossier. | **Suspicious under P7**, and not certifiable without review. S can skip reviews and repeat mandatory checks while having no correction construct. |
| **A8:** Register  Credit  Technical  Correct  Manager  Release | Correction occurs without any recheck or recorded impact decision. | **Policy-ambiguous.** S accepts it even though it cannot determine which checks were affected. |
| **A9:** Register  Credit  Technical  Expedite  Expedite  Manager  Release | Two Expedite Reviews occur. | Multiplicity is unspecified by the agreement. S silently admits it; that is a modeling decision that should be explicit, not an accident. |
| **A10:** Register  Credit  Technical  Supplier  Expedite  Manager  Release | All visible activities occur, but source and expedite eligibility are not represented. | Potentially valid, but S cannot distinguish it from a non-external order that should not contain Supplier Review. |

### Intentional tolerance versus accidental flaws

Some permissiveness is useful for exploration:

- allowing a partial trace to surface a possible workflow;
- proposing an execution that is not ready for approval;
- accommodating source-system omissions as hypotheses.

The accidental flaws are different:

- the silent branches make **mandatory** checks optional in the model’s actual executable language;
- Supplier Review is independently optional rather than conditional on order source;
- correction and affected-check semantics are absent;
- repeated checks are possible without a correction construct;
- business-relevant attributes and exceptions are invisible;
- “alignment accepted” can be mistaken for “policy compliant.”

The discovery team’s description of S as “robust to missing events” is therefore misleading. **The model can explain some missing events because it permits them; that does not show that they are legitimate.**

There is also a likely metric pathology: silent alternatives can reduce the number of visible inserted activities needed to explain a partial trace. Missing mandatory activity is not free, but optionality can substantially reduce its relative penalty compared with a model requiring that activity.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

Model C has a legitimate conservative use:

1. Its normal paths require Register, Credit, Technical, one approval, and Release.
2. The two mandatory check orders are represented.
3. Expedite Review appears only after both mandatory checks and before approval.
4. Manager and Auto Approval are mutually exclusive.
5. No silent or skip transitions are visible in the supplied expression.
6. The reported training precision is much higher than S’s, consistent with a narrower accepted language.
7. The branches resemble frequent observed variants and can be useful as an **explicit version-change baseline** or a provisional allowlist, provided the board understands that the list is not the policy.

Thus C is safer than S for rejecting many obvious violations. It is not, however, accurate to call it “audit-ready” solely from positive-log precision.

## Strongest case against Model C

### Branch enumeration is not policy generalization

The eight branches are a sample of trace patterns, not a complete implementation of the rules. For example, they omit:

- Technical  Credit  Expedite  Manager;
- Technical  Credit  Supplier  Expedite  Auto;
- Credit  Technical  Supplier  Auto;
- Technical  Credit  Supplier  Auto;
- Expedite in either check order followed by Auto Approval.

B5 alone combines a missing check order, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, and Auto Approval. Its rejection cannot establish noncompliance.

The underlying weakness is a product of independent dimensions:

- two mandatory check orders;
- two source modes;
- optional Supplier Review conditional on source;
- optional Expedite Review;
- two approval modes;
- Supplier/Expedite relative orders;
- correction scopes and repeated rounds.

Enumerating frequent combinations scales poorly. A compositional model can express the dimensions independently and permit their legitimate combinations.

### Duplicate labels weaken traceability

The repeated labels are not themselves invalid executions. The branch labeled:

> Credit  Technical  Correct  Credit  Technical

intentionally performs two Credit and two Technical activities.

Nevertheless, repeated transition names create governance problems:

- branch-level occurrence and case-level execution are easily confused;
- impact analysis must identify which transition copy is affected;
- model-to-log mappings need stable transition identifiers;
- duplicate delivery events and modeled repeated checks require different treatment;
- editing “all Credit Checks” can accidentally change only one branch.

The undefined count of 33 is not a quality metric. The board should require a transition inventory, stable identifiers, and an explicit distinction between syntactic reuse and duplicate log events.

### Correction handling is particularly weak

C contains one fixed path with one correction followed by both checks. It does not represent:

- correction affecting Credit only;
- correction affecting Technical only;
- multiple correction rounds;
- a correction with an explicitly attested empty affected set;
- the ordering of rechecks;
- unknown or contradictory affected-check data.

B6 is therefore rejected even when Technical alone was affected. The low holdout fitness is consistent with this undercoverage.

### Holdout degradation and precision need a joint interpretation

The reported holdout fitness of 0.744 establishes poor fit on the evaluated sample. It does **not** justify F8’s claim that unseen behavior should be noncompliant until approved. “Not yet approved” is a governance state, not proof of violation.

Conversely, training precision of 0.941 does not establish low false-acceptance risk. C can obtain high positive-log precision precisely by rejecting many plausible executions. Memorization is not proven, but **memorization-like undercoverage is a serious alternative explanation**.

C should be tested against independently labeled valid and forbidden traces. Until then, its high precision is weak evidence for enforcement and strong evidence only that it is more selective than S.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

The following ranking is by likely impact on a deployment decision, not by a numeric severity score.

| Rank | Pathology | Why it matters and how it should be tested |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Attribute-blind conditionality and unverified negative behavior** | Neither original model has external/internal or affected-check attributes. Positive-log results cannot establish correct conditional behavior. The board has no reliable basis for deciding whether a Supplier omission is legitimate or whether a selective recheck is valid. |
| **2** | **Positive-log-only precision** | C’s 0.941 can reward low coverage. S’s 0.584 can reward many alternatives. Precision must also be evaluated against independently labeled forbidden traces. Without that, the metric is not enforcement precision. |
| **3** | **Missing-event bias and silent-transition effects** | S’s `tau` alternatives make missing events executable. C’s absence of silent branches can instead reject partially observed processes. Both biases differ, so one aggregate fitness comparison is misleading. |
| **4** | **Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity** | There is no evidence of actual leakage, but there is insufficient evidence to exclude it. The exact cases, seeds, and preprocessing are absent. Related or duplicated order cases could also cross the split. Use case-level grouped, seeded, stratified splits and publish manifests. |
| **5** | **Unseen-combination undercoverage** | C’s 0.744 holdout fitness is consistent with rejecting B5-like combinations. A high score on common variants cannot substitute for compositional coverage. Test held-out attribute combinations explicitly. |
| **6** | **Fitness incentives that favor optionality or excessive permissiveness** | S may explain omissions by accepting them. C may achieve a high score on frequent training traces while requiring extra insertions for legitimate new combinations. Fitness is a description of fit, not a policy objective. |
| **7** | **Generalization incentives that reward excessive permissiveness** | S’s 0.907 token generalization is compatible with a large accepted language. A permissive model may appear general because it explains more observations while also admitting prohibited traces. Report generalization and negative-suite soundness together. |
| **8** | **Duplicated-label effects** | Identical labels can be different transition identities, repeated executions, duplicate log deliveries, or alignment artifacts. Undocumented counting can distort precision, runtime, impact analysis, and transition-to-log mappings. |
| **9** | **Alignment-cost sensitivity** | Insertion, deletion, synchronous, and skip costs affect missing events, duplicate checks, alternate check order, and extra reviews. C’s legitimate variants may be penalized while forbidden paths can be made cheap depending on weights. Publish optimizer, variant, weights, and threshold. |
| **10** | **Runtime confounding by model size and search strategy** | The 0.018 versus 0.440 second difference is real only for the unstated setup. C’s branch count, compiled topology, search algorithm, cache state, and hardware all confound the comparison. |
| **11** | **Stakeholder-score methodology** | The 4.4 versus 1.9 scores lack a sampling and task protocol. They may measure graph familiarity or trust rather than semantic comprehension. A simple but misleading model can score well. |

Specific metric cautions:

- A silent transition does not make a missing mandatory event cost zero, but it removes the need to insert that visible activity and can make a permissive explanation cheaper.
- A lower alignment cost is not equivalent to business validity. A valid new case may have a high cost in an enumerated model, while a forbidden case may have a low cost in a permissive model.
- Duplicate transition labels should not automatically be collapsed. Doing so can erase P6 evidence.
- Raw event alignment fitness is not a case-level compliance rate. Report both.
- A simplicity score must not be interpreted as number of business rules, number of changes required, or stakeholder comprehension.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Chosen strategy: a small attribute-conditioned model family plus explicit policy checks

The third submission is a **controlled hybrid**, not one large model that silently guesses missing attributes.

### 1. Compact formal description

Let:

- \(e \in \{0,1\}\) be externally sourced;
- \(u \in \{0,1\}\) denote whether Expedite Review is present in the modeled case;
- \(C=\text{Credit Check}\);
- \(T=\text{Technical Check}\);
- \(S=\text{Supplier Review}\);
- \(E=\text{Expedite Review}\);
- \(A_M=\text{Manager Approval}\);
- \(A_A=\text{Auto Approval}\);
- \(R=\text{Register Dossier}\);
- \(L=\text{Release Order}\);
- \(X=\text{Correct Dossier}\).

For first-attempt execution:

\[
F(e,u)=
R
\rightarrow
\left[
  (C\parallel T)
  \;\Vert\;
  \begin{cases}S & e=1\\ \varnothing & e=0\end{cases}
  \;\Vert\;
  \begin{cases}E & u=1\\ \varnothing & u=0\end{cases}
\right]
\rightarrow
(A_M\oplus A_A)
\rightarrow L,
\]

with edges \(C\rightarrow E\) and \(T\rightarrow E\) when \(E\) is present.

Thus:

- C and T are mandatory and unordered.
- S is mandatory for external cases and absent for internal cases; it is not an unconstrained skip choice.
- S may occur before, between, or after the mandatory checks, because P3 gives no ordering for it.
- E may occur before or after S, but only after both C and T.
- Everything occurs before the single approval.
- Exactly one approval occurs before release.
- Optional E is represented by scenario selection, not by making C, T, or S optional.

For a case with known correction impacts \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), where each \(a_i\subseteq\{C,T\}\), construct:

\[
F(e,u,a_1,\ldots,a_n)=
F_{\text{before approval}}(e,u)
\rightarrow X_1 \rightarrow \mathop{\parallel}_{a\in a_1} a
\rightarrow \cdots
\rightarrow X_n \rightarrow \mathop{\parallel}_{a\in a_n} a
\rightarrow
(A_M\oplus A_A)
\rightarrow L.
\]

The supplied code constructs any **finite, explicitly specified** correction sequence. A trace-level policy check supports repeated correction rounds without pretending that the model can infer their scope from best alignment.

### 2. Self-contained PM4Py code

The source context is intentionally required; it is not inferred from observed Supplier Review.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)

CHECKS = ("Credit Check", "Technical Check")
ALLOWED_IMPACTS = frozenset(CHECKS)


def _sequence(left, right):
    """Sequential composition without duplicating POWL node objects."""
    return StrictPartialOrder(
        list(left.nodes) + list(right.nodes)
    )


def make_context_model(
    external,
    has_expedite,
    correction_impacts=(),
):
    """
    Build one explicit context.

    external:
        True  -> Supplier Review is required.
        False -> Supplier Review is absent.
        None is rejected; it is never silently treated as internal.

    has_expedite:
        Whether this scenario includes Expedite Review.
        It does not assert an unstated urgency precondition.

    correction_impacts:
        Ordered sequence, one entry per Correct Dossier event.
        Each entry is a set containing "Credit Check",
        "Technical Check", both, or explicitly neither.

    This function creates a finite observed-family member.
    The accompanying policy checker handles arbitrary repeated
    correction rounds using explicit impact data.
    """
    if type(external) is not bool or type(has_expedite) is not bool:
        raise ValueError(
            "external and has_expedite must be explicit booleans; "
            "unknown source must not be converted to a default."
        )

    register = Transition("Register Dossier")
    credit = Transition("Credit Check")
    technical = Transition("Technical Check")
    first_nodes = [register, credit, technical]

    # Register precedes both mandatory checks.
    first = StrictPartialOrder(*first_nodes)
    first.order.add_edge(register, credit)
    first.order.add_edge(register, technical)

    if has_expedite:
        expedite = Transition("Expedite Review")
        first.order.add_edge(credit, expedite)
        first.order.add_edge(technical, expedite)

    if external:
        supplier = Transition("Supplier Review")
        # No ordering is imposed on Supplier Review except approval.
        first.order.add_edge(first, supplier)

    stage = first

    for raw_impact in correction_impacts:
        impact = frozenset(raw_impact)

        if not impact.issubset(ALLOWED_IMPACTS):
            raise ValueError("Unknown affected-check label")

        correction = Transition("Correct Dossier")

        # Use distinct transition objects even though the activity
        # labels deliberately repeat.
        rechecks = [
            Transition(label) for label in sorted(impact)
        ]

        if rechecks:
            correction_stage = StrictPartialOrder(
                correction, *rechecks
            )
            for recheck in rechecks:
                correction_stage.order.add_edge(
                    correction, recheck
                )
        else:
            # Only valid if an empty impact set is explicitly attested
            # and approved by the process owner.
            correction_stage = StrictPartialOrder(correction)

        stage = _sequence(stage, correction_stage)

    manager = Transition("Manager Approval")
    auto = Transition("Auto Approval")

    model = StrictPartialOrder(
        list(stage.nodes) + [manager, auto]
    )

    # Mandatory checks, source review, Expedite Review if present,
    # and all modeled rechecks precede both approval alternatives.
    for source_node in stage.nodes:
        model.order.add_edge(source_node, manager)
        model.order.add_edge(source_node, auto)

    # XOR means exactly one approval in a normal single execution.
    approval = OperatorPOWL(
        OperatorPOWL.XOR, [manager, auto]
    )

    release = Transition("Release Order")
    model.order.add_edge(manager, release)
    model.order.add_edge(auto, release)

    return model


# Explicit family members. A missing source attribute leads to two
# scenarios, not a model in which Supplier is silently skippable.
model_third_by_context = {
    (False, False): make_context_model(False, False),
    (False, True):  make_context_model(False, True),
    (True,  False): make_context_model(True, False),
    (True,  True):  make_context_model(True, True),
}

# Explicit demonstration member for external cases with Expedite Review.
# This assignment is not a claim that the discovery log is external.
model_third = model_third_by_context[(True, True)]

# Example corrected family member:
# One correction affecting Technical Review only.
model_third_external_expedite_technical_correction = (
    make_context_model(
        external=True,
        has_expedite=True,
        correction_impacts=[{"Technical Review"}],
    )
)
```

The four base models are context choices, not frequency-ranked path enumeration. Their partial orders generate legal interleavings without listing every trace.

### 3. Required policy checks

The POWL models do not encode source attributes, proof of impact, suspicious-repeat classification, or missing-data uncertainty. Those require the following explicit checker.

```python
from collections import Counter


CORE = {
    "Register Dossier",
    "Credit Check",
    "Technical Review",
    "Supplier Review",
    "Expedite Review",
    "Correct Dossier",
    "Manager Approval",
    "Auto Approval",
    "Release Order",
}

CHECK_NAMES = {"Credit Check", "Technical Check"}


def _name(event):
    if isinstance(event, str):
        return event
    if isinstance(event, dict) and "activity" in event:
        return event["activity"]
    raise ValueError("Event must be a string or contain 'activity'")


def policy_check(
    trace,
    external,
    correction_impacts=None,
):
    """
    Return:
        {
          "verdict": "VALID" | "FORBIDDEN" | "SUSPICIOUS" | "UNKNOWN",
          "violations": [...],
          "warnings": [...],
          "unresolved": [...]
        }

    external:
        True / False from a trustworthy case-level attribute.
        None means unknown and prevents certification.

    correction_impacts:
        Ordered list aligned with Correct Dossier events.
        Each item is a set of affected mandatory-check labels.
        None for any correction means unresolved, not empty.

    This routine does not collapse duplicate events.
    """
    k = [_name(event) for event in trace]
    violations, warnings, unresolved = [], [], []

    if not k:
        return {
            "verdict": "FORBIDDEN",
            "violations": ["empty trace"],
            "warnings": [],
            "unresolved": [],
        }

    unknown = sorted(set(k) - CORE)
    if unknown:
        violations.append(f"unknown activities: {unknown}")

    registers = [i for i, a in enumerate(k)
                 if a == "Register Dossier"]
    releases = [i for i, a in enumerate(k)
                if a == "Release Order"]

    if len(registers) != 1 or registers[0] != 0:
        violations.append("Register must occur exactly once and first")
    if len(releases) != 1 or releases[-1] != len(k) - 1:
        violations.append("Release must occur exactly once and last")

    approval_positions = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(k)
        if a in {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}
    ]
    if len(approval_positions) != 1:
        violations.append("exactly one approval is required")
        approval = len(k)
    else:
        approval = approval_positions[0]

    for activity in CHECK_NAMES:
        positions = [i for i, a in enumerate(k) if a == activity]
        if not any(i < approval for i in positions):
            violations.append(f"missing mandatory {activity} before approval")

    if not corrections_specified(correction_impacts):
        # This helper is replaced by the explicit handling below;
        # the branch exists only to keep the intent readable.
        pass

    corrections = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(k) if a == "Correct Dossier"
    ]
    for i in corrections:
        if i >= approval:
            violations.append(
                "corrective recheck must precede approval "
                "under the adopted policy"
            )

    # Establish a first mandatory-check pass before interpreting
    # later occurrences as rechecks.
    if corrections and approval < len(k):
        prefix = k[:corrections[0]]
        if not CHECK_NAMES.issubset(set(prefix)):
            unresolved.append(
                "an initial Credit/Technical pass before correction "
                "cannot be established"
            )

    if not corrections:
        for activity in CHECK_NAMES:
            if k.count(activity) != 1:
                violations.append(
                    f"unexpected multiplicity for {activity} "
                    "without a correction"
                )

    supplier_count = k.count("Supplier Review")
    expedite_count = k.count("Expedite Review")

    if supplier_count > 1:
        violations.append("Supplier Review must not repeat")
    if expedite_count > 1:
        violations.append("Expedite Review must not repeat")

    if external is None:
        unresolved.append("external/internal source is unknown")
    elif external and supplier_count != 1:
        violations.append("external order requires Supplier Review")
    elif not external and supplier_count != 0:
        violations.append(
            "internal order contains Supplier Review under the "
            "adopted source-conditional interpretation"
        )

    for i, activity in enumerate(k):
        if activity == "Expedite Review":
            if i >= approval:
                violations.append("Expedite Review precedes approval requirement")
            if not any(
                k[j] == "Credit Check" for j in range(i)
            ) or not any(
                k[j] == "Technical Review" for j in range(i)
            ):
                violations.append(
                    "Expedite Review must follow both mandatory checks"
                )

    # P7: a second or later occurrence without any preceding
    # Correct Dossier is suspicious, even if another hard rule fails.
    seen = Counter()
    for i, activity in enumerate(k):
        if activity in CHECK_NAMES:
            if seen[activity] > 0:
                if "Correct Dossier" not in k[:i]:
                    warnings.append(
                        f"suspicious repeated {activity} without correction"
                    )
            seen[activity] += 1

    # Validate every correction's following check-only window.
    # Supplier and Expedite have their own separate controls.
    if corrections:
        if correction_impacts is None:
            unresolved.append("affected-check data missing")
        elif len(correction_impacts) != len(corrections):
            unresolved.append(
                "affected-check list does not match correction count"
            )
        else:
            boundaries = corrections[1:] + [approval]
            for corr_index, boundary in zip(
                corrections, boundaries
            ):
                impact_index = k[:corr_index].count("Correct Dossier")
                impact = correction_impacts[impact_index]

                if impact is None:
                    unresolved.append("unknown affected-check set")
                    continue

                impact = frozenset(impact)
                if not impact.issubset(CHECK_NAMES):
                    violations.append("invalid affected-check label")
                    continue

                window = k[corr_index + 1:boundary]
                observed_impact = Counter(
                    a for a in window if a in CHECK_NAMES
                )
                expected_impact = Counter(
                    {a: 1 for a in impact}
                )

                if observed_impact != expected_impact:
                    violations.append(
                        "recheck scope does not equal the attested "
                        "affected-check set"
                    )

    if violations:
        verdict = "FORBIDDEN"
    elif warnings:
        verdict = "SUSPICIOUS"
    elif unresolved:
        verdict = "UNKNOWN"
    else:
        verdict = "VALID"

    return {
        "verdict": verdict,
        "violations": violations,
        "warnings": warnings,
        "unresolved": unresolved,
    }


def corrections_specified(value):
    # Documentation helper only; it deliberately does not supply
    # a default correction scope.
    return value is not None
```

The helper name `corrections_specified` is not required for policy execution; it is included only to make the “no default impacts” rule explicit.

### 4. Explicit semantics

- **Mandatory checks:** exactly one initial occurrence of each is required before approval. A later occurrence must belong to an explicit correction window.
- **Supplier Review:** required and nonoptional inside the external context; absent inside the internal context. Unknown source produces an unresolved case, not an inferred source.
- **Expedite Review:** present in the E-present scenario and ordered after both checks. Supplier and Expedite are not artificially ordered.
- **Exactly-one approval:** represented by XOR and independently checked by event counts.
- **Correction/recheck:** the sequence of affected sets is external evidence. The affected checks repeat exactly once in their respective correction window. An empty set is accepted only if explicitly attested and approved.
- **P7:** a repeated check without a preceding correction receives a suspicious warning. A hard rejection additionally requires a recorded policy exception.
- **Unknown data:** no automatic fallback to internal sourcing, no optional mandatory checks, and no inferred affected-check set.

### 5. Limitations from missing attributes

1. **Missing external/internal status:** the system must route the case to internal and external candidates or return `UNKNOWN`. It cannot select one by highest alignment.
2. **Missing affected-check information:** a correction cannot be certified as a valid recheck sequence. A history resembling C’s fixed both-check correction is not enough.
3. **Empty impact sets:** the agreement does not say whether a correction may legitimately require no mandatory recheck. This needs an approved attestation rule.
4. **Review invalidation:** P6 mentions only the two mandatory checks. Whether correction invalidates Supplier Review or Expedite Review is unresolved.
5. **Approval eligibility:** exactly one approval is modeled, but additional eligibility rules for Auto Approval are not supplied.
6. **Unlimited corrections:** the family constructor handles any explicitly specified finite observed sequence; the policy checker supports repeated rounds. It does not pretend that a finite discovery model is the complete correction language.
7. **Correction timing:** the normal implementation requires rechecks before approval. The board must confirm this interpretation or provide a different rule.

A scoring skeleton for a returned alignment is:

```python
from collections import Counter


def alignment_cost_components(
    alignment,
    insertion=1.0,
    deletion=1.0,
    synchronous=1.0,
    skip=0.0,
):
    """
    alignment is assumed to expose:
      .log: aligned visible log events
      .model: aligned model transitions
      .sync_score / .log_score / .model_score:
          comparable non-skip score components
      .skip_score

    Adapt field names to the pinned PM4Py release if necessary.
    This function scores an alignment; it does not replace the
    optimizer's cost function.
    """
    visible = [
        step for step in alignment.log
        if not getattr(step, "is_skipped", False)
    ]
    modeled = [
        step for step in alignment.model
        if not getattr(step, "is_skipped", False)
    ]

    mismatch = Counter(
        (getattr(v, "activity", None),
         getattr(m, "activity", None))
        for v, m in zip(visible, modeled)
        if getattr(v, "activity", None)
        != getattr(m, "activity", None)
    )

    n_sync = sum(mismatch.values())
    n_insert = max(0, len(modeled) - len(visible))
    n_delete = max(0, len(visible) - len(modeled))
    n_skip = max(
        len(alignment.log) - len(visible),
        len(alignment.model) - len(modeled),
    )

    return {
        "cost": (
            insertion * n_insert
            + deletion * n_delete
            + synchronous * n_sync
            + skip * n_skip
        ),
        "insertions": n_insert,
        "deletions": n_delete,
        "mismatches": n_sync,
        "skips": n_skip,
    }
```

Use the same published weighting inside the search and this independent scoring calculation. Do not score one alignment with one cost while searching with another.

The exact-language trace test below is appropriate for finite, silent-free Petri nets and for the explicit S construction. It complements, rather than replaces, alignment evaluation.

```python
def exact_trace_accepted(net, initial_marking, trace):
    """
    Breadth-first token-firing semantics for a single case.
    No deletion, duplication, or alignment tolerance is used.
    """
    initial = {
        place: int(tokens)
        for place, tokens in initial_marking.items()
    }
    start = (
        frozenset(
            (place, count)
            for place, count in initial.items()
            if count > 0
        ),
        (),
    )

    queue = [start]
    visited = {start}

    # For the finite first-pass nets under review, each transition
    # consumes an initial token. Add an explicit depth guard if this
    # helper is reused on nets with empty-place silent loops.
    max_steps = max(
        1,
        sum(initial.values()) + len(net.transitions),
    )

    while queue:
        state, observed = queue.pop(0)

        if tuple(observed) == tuple(trace):
            return True
        if len(observed) >= max_steps:
            continue

        marking = dict(state)
        for transition in net.transitions:
            if any(
                marking.get(place, 0) < count
                for place, count in transition.in_arcs.items()
            ):
                continue

            next_marking = dict(marking)
            for place, count in transition.in_arcs.items():
                next_marking[place] -= count
            for place, count in transition.out_arcs.items():
                next_marking[place] = (
                    next_marking.get(place, 0) + count
                )

            next_state = (
                frozenset(
                    (place, count)
                    for place, count in next_marking.items()
                    if count > 0
                ),
                observed + (
                    transition.label
                    if transition.label is not None
                    else None,
                ),
            )

            if next_state not in visited:
                visited.add(next_state)
                queue.append(next_state)

    return False
```

The guard is not a substitute for a proper graph algorithm on arbitrary looping nets; it is a simple test utility for the finite first-pass models.

## 10. Decision rule

Reject a candidate despite a favorable aggregate if any of the following occurs:

- it executes a curated forbidden trace;
- it certifies a case with missing source or impact data as fully valid;
- it fails a mandatory-check or exactly-one-approval rule;
- it rejects predeclared valid combinations for reasons not documented as an exception;
- its result changes materially with an ordinary alignment-cost choice and the submitted configuration is not reproducible;
- duplicate handling destroys correction evidence;
- its transition inventory cannot support reliable impact analysis;
- its “simple” representation is understandable only by ignoring silent semantics.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

## Exploration

**Model S may remain in a sandbox for exploratory analysis**, provided it is renamed or labeled as a permissive candidate model and cannot be used to certify compliance.

Its exploration value comes from broad observed-case fit and simple maintenance. The board should not accept “robust to missing events” as a quality claim without a missing-event analysis.

Model C can also be retained temporarily as a **regression baseline for observed frequent variants**. That is a diagnostic use, not evidence that its branch list is the correct policy.

## Conformance

**Neither original model should be deployed for compliance in its current form.**

- S is disqualified by executable silent skips and source-blind optionality.
- C is disqualified by undercoverage, fixed correction semantics, and the absence of independently tested negative behavior.

The third alternative should be used for conformance only after passing the governance conditions above. Until source and affected-check data are available, its correct result for those cases is `UNKNOWN`, not `VALID`.

## Simulation

Neither original is a validated simulator:

- S permits impossible or unsupported control flow.
- C excludes too many legitimate combinations and encodes observed variant frequencies rather than a validated process mechanism.

The third model provides a better control-flow skeleton. Simulation still requires explicit resources, calendars, costs, case probabilities, and data dependencies, followed by separate validation against operational outcomes.

## Retirement

- **Retire C as the default discovery model for general conformance and maintainable business-rule ownership.** Retain a versioned copy for historical comparison and change-impact testing.
- **Do not retire S solely for its low positive-log precision.** Retain it only as an explicitly non-enforcing exploratory baseline.
- Promote neither original based on the current evidence.
- Require the third model to pass staged shadow deployment before it can issue compliance decisions.

# 11. Dissending View

The recommendation to reject both originals may be too conservative for an environment where every deviation requires expensive manual review. Model C already captures many frequent, apparently valid executions and has substantially higher reported positive-log precision than S. Its holdout weakness may reflect a small, unrepresentative sample rather than systematic unsuitability. Rejecting it now could remove a usable safety net while the proposed hybrid’s source attributes, impact attestations, correction policy, and exception process are still unresolved.

The third design may also transfer complexity from an enumerated model into data governance. Four context models, ordered impact lists, and an independent policy checker could be harder for business users to understand than C’s explicit branches. A staged, version-controlled allowlist based on C might be safer than introducing a new framework before its false-rejection rate is known. Conversely, freezing C at today’s frequent variants risks institutionalizing missing cases as violations. That danger is serious, but it is an operational risk, not proof that immediate replacement is superior.

I would therefore retain C under temporary change control, use S strictly for exploration, and run the third model in shadow mode before deciding.

I would change my recommendation if independently labeled, case-level evidence showed that C covered all in-scope valid combinations with no unacceptable source or correction failures, that its rejects were predominantly true violations, and that its maintenance and comprehension costs were materially lower than the hybrid. I would also need evidence that the required attributes can be obtained reliably and that the hybrid’s additional operational burden does not produce more unsafe deferrals or unexplained decisions.
