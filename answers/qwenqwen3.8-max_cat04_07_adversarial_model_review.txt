# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The reported metrics are treated as **evidence about model-log alignment**, not as ground truth about policy compliance. The board should not choose a model by maximizing an aggregate score; the metrics measure different, sometimes conflicting, governance goals.

I distinguish four intended use cases:

1. **Exploration / business discovery**  
   Goal: understand frequent behavior, find variants, communicate broadly.  
   Acceptable trade-off: higher recall / fitness and simplicity are valuable; some overpermissiveness is tolerable if the model is explicitly marked “not for enforcement.”

2. **Conformance enforcement / compliance gating**  
   Goal: detect forbidden cases and prevent inappropriate release.  
   Acceptable trade-off: precision against forbidden behavior, negative-trace rejection, and auditability matter more than raw fitness or generalization. A model that accepts B3 or B4 is not acceptable for enforcement, even if its fitness is high.

3. **Simulation / what-if analysis**  
   Goal: generate plausible future cases, estimate throughput, test policy changes.  
   Acceptable trade-off: the model must not systematically invent forbidden paths; it should represent optionalality and rework realistically. Overly enumerated models may simulate only memorized variants.

4. **Stakeholder communication and maintainability**  
   Goal: business and IT can understand, challenge, and update the model.  
   Acceptable trade-off: simplicity and clear semantics matter; duplicate labels and branch explosion reduce ownership and increase change risk.

Assumptions used in this review:

- The process-tree operators are interpreted in the usual PM4Py sense: `->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` parallel, `*` loop, `tau` silent skip.
- “Accepts” in the probe tables means **exact syntactic language acceptance**, not alignment fitness. With alignments, a rejected trace may still obtain partial fitness through log moves or model moves.
- The probes B1–B8 are policy tests, not a complete oracle.
- The missing `external/internal` and `affected check` attributes are real governance gaps, not nuisances to be silently ignored.
- The reported metrics are not fully comparable because seeds, preprocessing, alignment costs, and held-out cases were not supplied.

I do **not** average the metrics into one number. Any weighting would be use-case dependent; for compliance, negative-trace rejection should dominate simplicity, while for exploration, simplicity and recall may dominate.

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S is simple, fast, robust to missing events, and easy for business users to read. For **exploration**, its permissiveness can be useful: it does not break when Supplier Review is missing, it tolerates different check orders, and it avoids overfitting to rare branch combinations. Its high holdout fitness and low runtime are attractive if the goal is only to visualize or cluster behavior.

## Strongest case against Model S

Model S is unsafe for compliance because it treats mandatory checks as optional and destroys required ordering. Its `+(X(activity, tau), ...)` block makes each of Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, and Expedite Review independently optional and parallel. This is not merely noise tolerance; it changes policy semantics.

At least five concrete admitted behaviors are undesirable or policy-ambiguous:

| Trace admitted by Model S | Problem | Intentional noise tolerance or accidental semantic flaw? |
|---|---|---|
| `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Skips both mandatory checks. Forbidden by P2 and probe B3. | Accidental semantic flaw: mandatory checks are optionalized by `tau`. |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Skips Technical Review. Forbidden by P2. | Accidental semantic flaw. |
| `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Expedite Review occurs before mandatory checks. Forbidden by P4 and probe B4. | Accidental semantic flaw caused by parallel block. |
| `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | No mandatory checks; Supplier and Expedite occur without Credit/Technical. Forbidden or at least highly suspicious. | Accidental semantic flaw. |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` when the case is internal | Supplier Review occurs even though P3 says it is required only for external orders. Because the external/internal attribute is absent, Model S silently allows Supplier for all cases. | Partly intentional optionalization, but policy-ambiguous because the condition is missing. |
| `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Skips Credit Check and allows Supplier without mandatory Credit Check. | Accidental semantic flaw. |

Model S also **cannot represent legitimate correction behavior** because `Correct Dossier` is absent. Thus it may reject valid B6-style cases if used for conformance, while simultaneously accepting dangerous cases such as B3.

The key distinction:

- **Intentional noise tolerance**: skipping Supplier or Expedite when events are missing may be acceptable in exploration.
- **Accidental semantic flaw**: skipping Credit Check or Technical Review, or allowing Expedite before mandatory checks, violates agreed policy.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

Model C is audit-friendly for known, frequent variants. It explicitly enumerates acceptable paths and rejects obvious forbidden traces such as:

- `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`
- `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`
- `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order`

For a strict allow-list regime, this is attractive. Its high training precision and low permissiveness reduce the chance of silently accepting unobserved behavior.

## Strongest case against Model C

Model C is overfit, brittle, and hard to maintain. It mistakes “frequent observed variants” for “complete policy.”

Major problems:

1. **Branch enumeration**  
   Model C enumerates eight full traces. This does not scale. Adding Supplier, Expedite, Auto Approval, correction patterns, and alternate check orders creates combinatorial explosion.

2. **Duplicate labels**  
   The reported duplicate-label count of 33 reflects repeated activities across branches. This complicates impact analysis, alignment interpretation, and ownership. If “Credit Check” appears in many branches, changing Credit Check semantics requires touching many places.

3. **Unseen combinations are rejected by default**  
   The model rejects several plausible valid cases:
   - B2: `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`
   - B5: `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
   - Supplier Review with Auto Approval in many orders.
   - Expedite Review after Technical Review/Credit Check order not enumerated.
   - Correction followed by only one mandatory check.

   F4 explicitly says legitimate holdout cases combine Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate check order. Model C treats these as noncompliant unless a new branch is added.

4. **Correction handling is inadequate**  
   Model C contains only one correction branch:
   `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`.

   It does not represent:
   - correction affecting only Credit Check;
   - correction affecting only Technical Review;
   - correction followed by Auto Approval;
   - correction with Supplier Review or Expedite Review;
   - repeated corrections.

   This violates the spirit of P6 and F6.

5. **Holdout degradation suggests memorization**  
   The drop from training fitness 0.973 to holdout fitness 0.744 is large. Given F4, this is likely caused by legitimate unseen combinations, not merely invalid behavior.

6. **High precision may be an artifact of memorization**  
   Precision was computed only against positive training traces. Model C can score highly by reproducing the training allow-list while still being wrong as a policy model. It may have high positive precision and high false-negative rejection of valid new behavior.

7. **Maintainability is poor**  
   F3 is credible: duplicate labels and branch enumeration make impact analysis difficult. A governance board should not adopt a model that requires manual branch addition for every legitimate new combination.

# 7. Third Alternative

I propose a **block-structured procedural backbone plus explicit data/policy constraints**. It is safer than Model S because Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory and Expedite Review is ordered after them. It is more generalizable than Model C because it uses compact control-flow blocks instead of enumerating every full trace. It also explicitly represents correction/recheck behavior, while acknowledging that the missing `affected check` attribute prevents full enforcement.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

Let:

- `R` = Register Dossier
- `C` = Credit Check
- `T` = Technical Review
- `D` = Correct Dossier
- `S` = Supplier Review
- `E` = Expedite Review
- `M` = Manager Approval
- `A` = Auto Approval
- `L` = Release Order

The third model has the form:

```text
Third = R · MAND · CORR* · POST · APPR · L

MAND = (C · T) | (T · C)

CORR = D · ( C | T | (C · T) | (T · C) )

POST = (S? || E?)

APPR = M | A
```

Meaning:

- `R` is first and `L` is last.
- Credit Check and Technical Review are both mandatory before approval and may occur in either order.
- Zero or more correction cycles may occur after the mandatory checks.
- Each correction cycle must be followed by one of:
  - Credit Check only;
  - Technical Review only;
  - Credit Check then Technical Review;
  - Technical Review then Credit Check.
- Supplier Review and Expedite Review are optional in the control-flow backbone, but Supplier Review is governed by an external `external/internal` data check.
- Expedite Review may occur before or after Supplier Review, but only after the mandatory checks and before approval.
- Exactly one of Manager Approval and Auto Approval occurs.
- Repeated Credit Check or Technical Review without a preceding Correct Dossier is structurally prevented by the backbone.

This is not a silent “anything goes” model. Mandatory checks are not optional. Supplier Review is optional only because the external/internal attribute is missing; the governance layer must mark such cases as uncertain rather than automatically compliant.

## 7.3 Declarative / external policy dictionary and checks

The process-tree backbone should not be used alone for compliance. It should be paired with explicit post-hoc checks.

```python
from typing import Sequence, Mapping, Any, List

MANDATORY_CHECKS = ("Credit Check", "Technical Review")
APPROVALS = ("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval")
SUPPLIER = "Supplier Review"
EXPEDITE = "Expedite Review"
CORRECT = "Correct Dossier"
RELEASE = "Release Order"

POLICY_CONSTRAINTS = {
    "mandatory_before_approval": list(MANDATORY_CHECKS),
    "exactly_one_approval": list(APPROVALS),
    "expedite_after_mandatory": list(MANDATORY_CHECKS),
    "expedite_before_approval": list(APPROVALS),
    "repeat_requires_correction": {
        "activities": list(MANDATORY_CHECKS),
        "requires_predecessor": CORRECT,
    },
    "supplier_condition": {
        "activity": SUPPLIER,
        "case_attribute": "external_internal",
        "external_value": "external",
        "internal_value": "internal",
        "required_if": "external",
        "forbidden_if": "internal",
        "missing_attribute_status": "uncertain_do_not_auto_approve",
    },
    "correction_affected_check": {
        "case_attribute": "affected_check",
        "allowed_values": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review", "both", "none"],
        "missing_attribute_status": "uncertain_do_not_auto_approve",
    },
}


def activity_names(trace: Sequence) -> List[str]:
    """
    Accepts either a list of activity names or a list of event dicts.
    """
    names = []
    for e in trace:
        if isinstance(e, Mapping):
            names.append(e.get("concept:name"))
        else:
            names.append(str(e))
    return names


def posthoc_policy_checks(
    trace: Sequence,
    case_attributes: Mapping[str, Any] | None = None
) -> dict:
    """
    Skeleton for external policy checks.
    Returns violations and uncertainties.
    """
    case_attributes = case_attributes or {}
    acts = activity_names(trace)

    violations = []
    uncertainties = []

    # Exactly one approval
    approvals = [a for a in acts if a in APPROVALS]
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        violations.append("exactly_one_approval_violation")

    # Mandatory checks before first approval
    if approvals:
        first_approval = acts.index(approvals[0])
        for mandatory in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
            if mandatory not in acts[:first_approval]:
                violations.append(f"missing_{mandatory}_before_approval")

    # Expedite ordering
    if EXPEDITE in acts:
        expedite_idx = acts.index(EXPEDITE)
        seen_before_expedite = set(acts[:expedite_idx])
        if not set(MANDATORY_CHECKS).issubset(seen_before_expedite):
            violations.append("expedite_before_mandatory_checks")

        if approvals:
            first_approval_idx = acts.index(approvals[0])
            if expedite_idx > first_approval_idx:
                violations.append("expedite_after_approval")

    # Supplier condition depends on missing attribute
    supplier_present = SUPPLIER in acts
    external_internal = case_attributes.get("external_internal")

    if external_internal == "external" and not supplier_present:
        violations.append("missing_supplier_review_for_external_case")
    elif external_internal == "internal" and supplier_present:
        violations.append("supplier_review_present_for_internal_case")
    elif external_internal is None:
        uncertainties.append("external_internal_attribute_missing")

    # Correction affected-check condition
    if CORRECT in acts:
        affected = case_attributes.get("affected_check")
        if affected is None:
            uncertainties.append("affected_check_attribute_missing")
        else:
            # Full implementation should inspect each correction cycle:
            # after each Correct Dossier, collect the next repeated mandatory
            # checks until the next correction/approval/release and compare
            # with the declared affected_check value.
            pass

    # Simple repeated mandatory check detector.
    # This is intentionally conservative; the backbone already prevents
    # repeats without correction in the modeled language, but raw logs
    # still need checking.
    seen_mandatory = set()
    both_mandatory_done = False
    last_correct_idx = -1
    last_mandatory_idx = -1

    for i, a in enumerate(acts):
        if a == CORRECT:
            last_correct_idx = i

        if a in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
            if both_mandatory_done:
                if last_correct_idx <= last_mandatory_idx:
                    violations.append("repeated_mandatory_check_without_correction")
                    break

            seen_mandatory.add(a)
            last_mandatory_idx = i

            if not both_mandatory_done and set(MANDATORY_CHECKS).issubset(seen_mandatory):
                both_mandatory_done = True
                last_mandatory_idx = i

    return {
        "violations": violations,
        "uncertainties": uncertainties,
    }
```

## 7.4 Explicit semantics

- **Mandatory checks**: Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory. They can occur in either order, but both must occur before approval.
- **Optional Supplier Review**: Supplier Review is optional in the control-flow model only because the external/internal attribute is missing. The external check treats missing attribute cases as uncertain, not automatically valid.
- **Expedite Review ordering**: Expedite Review is placed in the `POST` block after the mandatory-check block and correction loop, and before approval. It may occur before or after Supplier Review because `POST` is a parallel optional block.
- **Exactly-one approval**: The final exclusive choice permits exactly one of Manager Approval or Auto Approval.
- **Correction/recheck behavior**: Each `Correct Dossier` must be followed by one of the allowed recheck patterns. Multiple corrections are allowed by the loop. Repeats of Credit Check or Technical Review outside a correction cycle are not generated by the backbone.
- **Suspicious repeats**: The backbone prevents B8-style repeats. The external checker can also flag raw logs that contain repeated mandatory checks without a preceding Correct Dossier.

## 7.5 Limitations caused by missing attributes

1. **Missing external/internal attribute**  
   The model cannot enforce P3 by itself. Supplier Review is structurally optional, but the policy layer must not auto-approve cases where the attribute is missing.

2. **Missing affected-check attribute**  
   The model allows any of Credit-only, Technical-only, or both after correction. This is more general than Model C but less precise than the desired P6 semantics. If the attribute says “Technical Review only,” but the trace repeats Credit Check, the external checker should flag the case.

3. **Correction position**  
   The proposed backbone places correction before Supplier/Expedite. If the business allows correction after Supplier Review or Expedite Review, the model will reject some valid cases. This is a conservative choice. A POWL or partial-order variant could relax this if stakeholders confirm the rule.

4. **Zero-recheck corrections**  
   The current backbone requires at least one mandatory recheck after Correct Dossier. If business policy allows corrections that affect no mandatory check, an attribute-gated `tau` branch should be added. It should not be added silently.

5. **Duplicate labels**  
   The process-tree implementation repeats Credit Check and Technical Review labels in correction branches. This is less extreme than Model C’s full branch enumeration, but it still creates alignment ambiguity. Future logs should include a `check_instance_id` or `rework_indicator`.

## 9.4 Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation tests

```python
def drop_first_activity(df, activity, case_id_col="case:concept:name", activity_col="concept:name"):
    out = []
    for case_id, group in df.groupby(case_id_col):
        idx_to_drop = group.index[group[activity_col] == activity]
        if len(idx_to_drop) > 0:
            out.append(group.drop(index=idx_to_drop[0]))
        else:
            out.append(group)
    return pd.concat(out)

def duplicate_first_activity(df, activity, case_id_col="case:concept:name", activity_col="concept:name"):
    out = []
    for case_id, group in df.groupby(case_id_col):
        idx_to_dup = group.index[group[activity_col] == activity]
        if len(idx_to_dup) > 0:
            duplicated_row = group.loc[[idx_to_dup[0]]].copy()
            out.append(pd.concat([group, duplicated_row]))
        else:
            out.append(group)
```

## 9.9 Stakeholder comprehension protocol

Replace the informal 1–5 score with a documented protocol:

1. Select participants from compliance, operations, IT architecture, data analysis, and audit.
2. Give each participant the same five tasks:
   - identify whether B3 is allowed;
   - identify whether B4 is allowed;
   - explain what happens after Correct Dossier;
   - add or describe the change needed for a new external urgent path;
   - determine whether Supplier Review is mandatory for an internal case.
3. Measure:
   - correctness;
   - time to answer;
   - confidence;
   - System Usability Scale or equivalent;
   - qualitative misunderstanding notes.
4. Require that compliance-relevant questions are answered correctly by a predefined majority.

## 9.10 Decision rule for rejecting a model despite good aggregate metrics

A model should be rejected for compliance if any of the following holds:

- it accepts any curated forbidden trace as fully compliant;
- it fails soundness or has unreachable mandatory activities;
- mandatory-check recall on curated valid holdout cases is below the agreed threshold, e.g. 0.99;
- negative-suite false acceptance rate is greater than zero for forbidden traces;
- holdout performance collapses on a policy-relevant stratum, such as correction cases or external cases;
- the model’s high fitness depends on cheap silent skips of mandatory activities;
- missing attributes cause automatic approval rather than uncertainty routing;
- maintainability metrics or stakeholder task performance show that the model cannot be safely updated.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

## Model S

Model S may be used **only for exploration and business communication**, with a clear watermark that it is not a compliance model. It should not be connected to any automated approval, release, or exception-suppression workflow.

It should not be retired entirely if analysts need a simple exploratory view, but it must be accompanied by warnings:

- Credit Check and Technical Review are not enforced.
- Expedite Review ordering is not enforced.
- Correct Dossier behavior is absent.
- Supplier Review is not conditioned on external sourcing.

## Model C

Model C should **not be used as the production compliance gate** in its current form. It is too brittle, overfits known variants, handles correction poorly, and creates maintainability problems through branch enumeration and duplicate labels.

It may be retained temporarily as an **audit reference allow-list** if the organization explicitly wants to treat all unseen behavior as noncompliant and route exceptions to humans. However, given F2, F3, F4, and F6, it should be retired as the primary operational model.

## Third alternative

The third alternative should be piloted as the candidate governance model, but only under these conditions:

1. **Soundness verified**  
   The converted Petri net must pass soundness and reachability checks.

2. **Negative suite passes**  
   It must reject all curated forbidden traces, including B3, B4, and B8-style repeats.

3. **Valid unseen combinations pass**  
   It must accept curated valid cases such as B2, B5, B6-with-attribute, and external urgent Auto Approval cases, subject to policy confirmation.

4. **Missing attributes are handled as uncertainty**  
   Cases missing `external/internal` or `affected_check` must not be auto-approved. They should be routed to review.

5. **Alignment costs are published**  
   Fitness and precision must be reported under at least two cost regimes: default and strict missing-event costs.

6. **Duplicate-label ambiguity is mitigated**  
   Either the log should add rework identifiers, or the implementation should use a Petri-net/labeling strategy that reduces alignment ambiguity.

7. **Stakeholder comprehension is retested**  
   The model must be understandable enough for compliance officers to explain why a case is accepted or rejected.

8. **Exception workflow exists**  
   Cases that are structurally valid but data-incomplete should not be silently blocked or silently approved.

Until those conditions are met, no model should be used for fully automated compliance enforcement.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation should not replace Model C with a more permissive hybrid for compliance. The board's primary duty is to prevent unauthorized releases, not to maximize replay fitness on messy logs. Model C's low holdout fitness is not a defect if the holdout contains unapproved or unreviewed behavior; in a regulated setting, unseen combinations should be exceptions routed to humans. Model C's enumerated branches provide an auditable allow-list, and its high training precision shows it does not invent behavior. The proposed third model reintroduces risk: it makes Supplier Review optional in the control-flow, allows any recheck after Correct Dossier because the affected-check attribute is missing, and relies on post-hoc data checks that are currently unavailable. It also still duplicates labels and may create alignment ambiguity. A safer path is to keep Model C as the compliance gate, add a controlled exception workflow, and only use Model S for exploration. The third model should remain a research prototype until the missing attributes are captured.

I would change my decision if the following evidence appeared:

- Business stakeholders formally confirm that the currently unseen combinations, such as Technical/Credit order with Auto Approval and Supplier/Expedite with Auto Approval, are valid policy behavior.
- A curated negative suite shows that Model C rejects an operationally unacceptable number of valid cases, creating exception overload.
- The third alternative demonstrates zero acceptance of forbidden traces under strict alignment costs.
- The organization captures `external/internal` and `affected_check` attributes, allowing the third alternative to enforce P3 and P6 rather than merely flag uncertainty.
- Maintainability tests show that the third alternative can be updated with fewer change steps and fewer duplicate-label ambiguities than Model C.
