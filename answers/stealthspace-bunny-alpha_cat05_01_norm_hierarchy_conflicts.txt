## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts and assumptions

**Established facts:** GPIP-4 was effective throughout these purchases; DE-AAC-PROC-17 was effective but limited to maintenance and production-support purchases at Aachen; ECD-2 was limited by time and purpose; the CRO issued no written exception or ratification; and the CFO’s email did not enter the controlled policy repository. All stated times are treated as CEST.

I also make the following **interpretive assumptions**, which are not established facts:

1. **The Incident Command authorization is authentic but not automatically a global-policy exception.** Its competence to modify Aachen’s ordinary workflow is supported by B7, the incident circumstances, and prior practice. Its ability to displace GPIP-4 controls must instead be demonstrated through the issuing authority’s competence and the directive’s wording.

2. **Two distinct Incident Command members are not automatically an “independent budget owner” and “Finance Controller.”** The supplied facts expressly leave their budget authority uncertain. C3 can supply purchase authorization and, if the actors possess the required roles and the applicable source permits equivalence, other approvals. It cannot create those roles by terminology alone.

3. **The CFO may interpret or implement Finance controls within the CFO’s actual authority.** D1 is relevant to the sufficiency of operational approvals, including an operational interpretation of joint Incident Command authorization. It is not treated as authority to waive Compliance clearance, segregation of duties, or a mandatory independent-budget-owner requirement.

4. **B2 is not silently converted into an express pre-commitment rule.** B1 expressly sequences the PO before commitment; B4 expressly permits commitment before the PO in an emergency. B2 requires Plant and Finance approval but does not expressly say “before commitment.” I therefore distinguish:
   - a missing approval entirely;
   - a later approval;
   - and an approval whose required timing is not stated.

   A functional, pre-commitment reading of B2 is reasonable, but it remains an interpretation rather than an established textual requirement.

5. **“Critical sterilization-line lots” is not resolved merely by an Incident Command label.** I assume that directly affected-line parts, and reasonably necessary backup-line parts, could be covered. P-503’s three-day stock forecast makes that assumption contestable. This uncertainty affects C-specific permissions, but does not remove GPIP-4 requirements that are otherwise applicable.

6. **The relevant control time is the time of supplier commitment**, not PO creation, invoice payment, case closure, or the latest approval. Where commitment is evidenced by a call or acknowledgment, the analysis distinguishes negotiations, acceptance, and an enforceable commitment.

### Decision method

I apply the following sequence:

1. **Identify authority and competence.** Separate global-policy authority, local operating authority, incident-operational authority, and Finance interpretation authority.
2. **Check source scope:** entity, site, purchase type, supplier status, amount, purpose, and effective time.
3. **Select the rule relevant to the particular control purpose.** Supplier eligibility, Compliance clearance, Finance authorization, segregation, PO sequencing, and emergency records are distinct controls.
4. **Apply dimension-specific precedence.** A specific permission may displace a local workflow rule without displacing a global control on a different subject.
5. **Test the act as it occurred.** Neither a later rule nor a later approval is assumed to operate retroactively.
6. **Distinguish an exemption, an authorized equivalent, and a breach.** A valid authorized equivalent satisfies a control; an express exception permits departure; a breach is a departure without such a basis.
7. **Apply an evidential burden of proof.** Mandatory pre-commitment controls must be affirmatively demonstrated. A missing event is not automatically proof that no compliant alternative authorization occurred, particularly where C3 or D1 is invoked. Conversely, an organization cannot rely on an exception, role, scope extension, or authority it has not established.

A finding of `NONCOMPLIANT` below means that a required control was affirmatively absent, late where pre-commitment was required, or outside an established authorization on the supplied facts. `INDETERMINATE` means a material proposition—often commitment time, actor authority, or an unstated timing rule—cannot responsibly be resolved from the supplied record.

### Defeasible pseudo-logic

- `applies(G0, purchase, t) ==> normally apply(applicable_global_controls, purchase, t)`.
- `amount >= 50000 and commitment(c,t) ==> normally require(independent_budget_approval(before=c), Finance_Controller_approval(before=c)) unless supported_by(valid_authorized_equivalent_or_G7_exception)`.
- `amount > 100000 OR new_supplier(supplier) ==> normally require(Compliance_Clearance(before=c)) unless overrides_for(Compliance, explicit_scope, time)`; permission to use a supplier alone is insufficient.
- `requester(x)=purchase OR committer(x)=purchase ==> prohibit(x as final_business_approver)`; two IC members are not a cure if one is the prohibited final approver.
- `applies(C0,purchase,c) ==> overrides_for(PO_timing, purchase, C0_window)` and `C2 ==> allows(nonregister_supplier, purchase, C0_window)`.
- `applies(C0,purchase,c) ==> normally require(two_distinct_IC_members authorize before=c, record(authorization within=4h))`.
- `applies(C3,purchase,c) ==> normally add_incident_authorization(purchase) unless clear_competent_override(additional_control)`; it does not silently establish G2 role qualifications.
- `applies(D1,purchase,c) and amount<=150000 and within_D1_period ==> Finance_interpretation(joint_IC_approval may satisfy operational_approval)` only to the extent supported by actual authority and role qualifications.
- `applies(B4,emergency) ==> normally allow(PO_after_commitment) and allow(Shift substitution only if PlantManager_absent AND delegation_active)`; no general waiver follows.
- `approval after commitment ==> normally record(late_approval); does_not_automatically_cure(missing_precommitment_approval)`.
- `asserted_override ==> require(authority, express_target_control, scope, time, written_form_where_required); otherwise treat_as(INDETERMINATE_or_NONCOMPLIANT depending proof)`.
- `timestamp_disputed ==> evaluate(actual_event supported_by best_evidence); separately test(recording_deadline); never infer(normative_exception from outage)`.

- **All cases:** Make any corrective approvals prospectively and label them as such. Backdating, deleting original entries, or adding an “emergency” label to a form after the event would compound the control problem.

### Monitoring and control improvements

1. **Role-bound approvals:** The ERP should prevent a requester or committer from completing the final business-approval role and should verify budget-owner and Finance Controller mappings before enabling commitment.
2. **Control-separated supplier onboarding:** Supplier-register permission and Compliance clearance should be distinct workflow gates. A temporary supplier permission must not automatically populate the Compliance field.
3. **Clock-bound emergency tokens:** A C3 authorization should generate a four-hour recording timer and an automatic expiry at the directive deadline.
4. **Append-only offline records:** Offline emergency approvals should use signed PDFs or immutable logs with trustworthy event time. System entry time must remain separately visible.
5. **Scope and threshold checks:** The system should capture the affected asset, operational need, site, purpose, and directive window, with warnings for ambiguous “critical” classifications.
6. **Exception register:** Only the authorized exception issuer should be able to select a global exception code. The CRO’s absence should prevent a system from representing an A7 exception as valid.
7. **Emergency-normalization monitoring:** Report emergency percentages by site, supplier, value, and reason; sample expired forms; require root-cause coding; and trigger management review when emergency use persists after the incident. Repeated “site continuity” labels without the defined harm should be escalated for control redesign.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

A concise replacement provision could read:

> **Precedence and Temporary Incident Overlays.** GPIP-4 remains the default entity-wide control framework. A controlled local procedure may add controls but may not reduce a GPIP-4 requirement. A duly issued incident directive may modify only the enumerated local workflow, site, asset/lot, incident purpose, purchase type, and time expressly identified in that directive. A later email or ordinary practice does not amend a controlled policy.
>
> **Emergency authorization.** An emergency overlay may permit commitment before a purchase order and may permit a specifically delegated substitute where the designated approver is unavailable. It does not, by itself, waive segregation of duties, mandatory Finance approval, Compliance clearance, or supplier-risk controls. Joint Incident Command authorization constitutes a purchase authorization and may evidence an operational approval only where each required role is documented in advance. Two Incident Command members are presumed neither to be an independent budget owner nor a Finance Controller merely by membership in Incident Command.
>
> **Amounts and suppliers.** Purchases of at least EUR 50,000 require an independent budget owner and Finance Controller before commitment. Purchases above EUR 100,000, and purchases from a new supplier, require Compliance clearance before commitment. Permission to purchase from a non-register supplier is a separate onboarding permission and does not satisfy Compliance clearance.
>
> **Segregation.** Neither the requester nor the person making the supplier commitment may be the final business approver. A valid emergency authorization does not cure an improper final approval. Any change to this prohibition requires an express board-policy amendment; it cannot be created by an incident label or Finance email.
>
> **Authority and exceptions.** The CFO may issue written instructions concerning the implementation and evidence of Finance controls but may not replace required approver roles without an authorized control mapping. The Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, named-control, site-specific, time-limited exception. An unsigned, oral, or inferred exception is invalid. Silence concerning an incident directive is not approval to override GPIP-4.
>
> **Records and expiry.** Emergency authorization must be recorded within four hours, and the emergency form within 24 hours. Corrected or retrospective records must preserve the original entry and be labeled as late evidence; they cannot be backdated. An authorization is effective only if the supplier commitment is proven to occur before expiry. No directive rolls over automatically. A renewal must be written, independently authorized, and time-bounded before expiration.
>
> **Safeguard against emergency normalization.** Every emergency purchase must identify the expected safety harm or stoppage, affected asset, duration, and business-continuity alternative. Emergency use and expired-record compliance must be reported to Audit and senior management. Repeated emergency classifications without the defined harm trigger a formal review and corrective-control plan rather than continued informal exceptions.
