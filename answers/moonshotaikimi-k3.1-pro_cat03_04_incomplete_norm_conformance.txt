## 1. Conformance semantics

Conformance must be stated relative to an artifact—source process, recorded event log, or derived graph—and to the norm’s scope and abstraction.

- **Forbidden behavior:** An in-scope observation matches an explicitly forbidden relation, such as N3, T6, or E10. One occurrence is sufficient for a diagnostic deviation. Establishing forbidden *real-world behavior*, rather than a forbidden derived edge, may still require validation of identities, timestamps, and ingestion.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** A required relation is absent for an in-scope instance at the abstraction intended by the norm. Aggregate edge existence or 98% coverage does not prove instance coverage. Physical process omission and missing event/object linkage remain different remedial problems.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** A relation is neither listed nor explicitly forbidden. Under the open-world assumption, this is not a violation under this norm, although another policy may govern it.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** A typical relation is absent, or an alternative path occurs. This is not noncompliance by itself; N2, N5, and T3 expressly admit exceptions, and no frequency threshold exists.
- **Likely data-quality error:** Positive evidence, such as the reused identifier behind the Sensor Heartbeat edge, suggests that the graph misrepresents reality. This changes confidence and remediation, but does not erase the diagnostic finding.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** The observed test does not implement the business rule’s semantics—for example, testing N1’s eventual validation using only direct succession.
- **Insufficient evidence:** Graph summaries cannot decide scope, per-instance coverage, timing, future events, or whether recording reflects physical execution.

The norm is closed-world for explicit prohibitions and mandatory requirements within scope. Otherwise, absence from an edge set is not prohibition. Conversely, calling something “unspecified” does not make it mandatory, desirable, or approved by every other policy.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

OC-DFG establishes observed, object-projected directly-follows patterns. It does not by itself establish eventual occurrence, physical execution, causality, or completeness beyond the snapshot.

The central danger is converting N1’s **“eventually enter validation”** rule into “Validate must directly follow Create.” That would falsely reject a legitimate `Create -> Review -> Validate` path. Conversely, an eventual rule without any deadline can under-enforce control. The two missing direct edges therefore require full-lifecycle queries; the three review paths cannot simply be assumed to explain them.

N4 is different: it is not described as an eventual-rule approximation. If its two Packages are in scope and the supplied abstraction implements N4 as intended, they are mandatory deviations, although physical versus recording noncompliance remains open. They need not be the same two cases as the Delivery gaps.

N3 is an explicit prohibition, not a typical-pattern statistic. N2’s 72 invoice edges are expressly plausible intermediates, while its 83 closure edges need not form a partition with them. For N5, neither 92/110 nor the absence of a successor creates a payment obligation; future events and credit terms matter.

### OTG

OTG establishes type-level interactions and lifecycle co-occurrence, but weights do not prove per-instance or per-event coverage. Thus, Order–Item weight 1,450 does not repair the indicated Add Item–Order deficit, and Package–Delivery weight 200 does not prove that every Dispatch interaction satisfied T2.

T6 remains forbidden despite being a single edge. T4 and T5 are unspecified, while Package–Delivery codeath is simply outside this norm. Codeath neither substitutes for Dispatch–Delivery interaction nor constitutes a violation.

A model can consequently look conforming at OTG type level while remaining suspicious at event level.

### ET–OT

ET–OT can test required and forbidden event/object associations when event totals and scopes are known. Create Order–Order 100, Add Item–Item 1,450, Pack Package–Package 200, Dispatch Package–Package 200, and Record Payment–Payment 93 show no stated shortfall. They do not validate other mandatory associations.

Using the supplied denominators, two Add Item events indicate an E2 gap, two Dispatch events indicate E6 gaps, and one Record Payment event indicates an E8 gap. Carrier references do not satisfy E6’s object relation. Likewise, T3’s acceptance of unapplied cash does not override E8: this event is not explicitly typed for the exception.

E10 is definitely a forbidden edge in the derived model, but probably not evidence of real sensor/financial behavior. Conversely, a Delivery may exist somewhere in OTG while a particular Dispatch event still fails E6.
