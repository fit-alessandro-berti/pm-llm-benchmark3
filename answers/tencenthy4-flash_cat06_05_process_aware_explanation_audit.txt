## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should operate at four levels: data/lineage, prefix extraction, process conformance, and explanation generation.

1. **Canonical event-time foundation**
   - Require every event to have `case_id`, `activity`, `event_time`, `recorded_time`, and a reliable `sequence_index`.
   - If timestamps tie, the `sequence_index` is authoritative; if missing, mark ordering as ambiguous rather than inferring from ingestion order.
   - Define `t0` as the first `Coverage Check Completed` event, not the first event with that label under ambiguous ordering.

2. **Prefix-time availability checks**
   - For each training case, materialize the admissible prefix: all events with `event_time <= t0` and sequence position at or before `t0`.
   - Every feature must have an event-time lineage proving `valid_from <= t0` and no dependency on rows with `event_time > t0`.
   - Add automated “future oracle” tests: if a feature perfectly or strongly predicts final decision, appeal, closure, or total duration, inspect it for leakage.

3. **Lineage and dependency checks**
   - Maintain a feature dependency graph from raw events/attributes to model features and explanation features.
   - Tag each node as `prefix-safe`, `future-derived`, `mutable-unknown`, `complete-trace`, or `proxy/fairness-review`.
   - Fail model training if any feature used at `t0` has a `future-derived` or `complete-trace` ancestor.

4. **Prefix-state legality**
   - At `t0`, the observed prefix is `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`.
   - **Legally future-possible after `t0`**: `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Documents Requested`, fraud opening if a trigger exists.
   - **Not observed and not usable at `t0`**: document receipt cycles, settlement decisions, appeals, closure, final decision, final handler, complete variants.
   - **Process-impossible at `t0`**: `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`; closure cannot precede damage assessment under the normative process.

5. **Conformance and alignment checks**
   - **Alignment checks** compare each trace against the normative model and identify where observed events deviate. They are useful for locating misplaced or missing events but may struggle with parallelism such as fraud investigation.
   - **Footprint checks** verify allowed successor/predecessor relations. They should flag `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as violating the normative footprint.
   - **Prefix checks** verify that the prefix state is one the model can actually be in at prediction time. They should reject explanations referencing future-only activities.
   - **DFG checks** identify frequent direct follows. They are diagnostic, not normative: a frequent `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` edge indicates bad ordering, timestamps, or extraction logic; it does not make the edge valid.
   - **Declarative checks** encode rules such as: document receipt requires prior request; damage assessment precedes first settlement decision; appeal requires a prior Partial or Deny decision; closure requires final decision or withdrawal; fraud investigation requires a trigger and may overlap other work.

6. **Same-timestamp ambiguity tests**
   - Detect all tied timestamps, especially `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed` in Case Z.
   - If `sequence_index` is missing, do not choose an order based on ingestion. Mark the pattern as ambiguous and exclude directional explanation claims.
   - Run sensitivity checks under both possible orderings; if the explanation changes sign or magnitude, quarantine it.

7. **Repeated settlement decision semantics**
   - Treat `Settlement Decision Recorded` as a coarse label that may legitimately occur twice.
   - Validate that the first decision follows damage assessment, that appeal occurs only after Partial or Deny, and that a second decision follows the appeal.
   - Therefore `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is process-consistent for later-stage analysis, unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`.
   - It remains inadmissible at `t0` because neither event has occurred.

8. **Explanation-layer conformance**
   - Sequence explainers must mine patterns only from prefixes ending at `t0`.
   - Each local explanation item must carry an “available at `t0`” flag.
   - Explanation text templates should avoid causal verbs such as “causes,” “determines,” or “drives” unless a separate causal identification study supports them.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Standards:

- **Predictive validity** means the feature is available at `t0`, has correct event-time lineage, and improves out-of-time predictive performance.
- **Causal interpretation** requires an identified causal question, confounder control or experiment, and temporal ordering. SHAP importance alone is not causal.
- **Operational actionability** requires a controllable lever and evidence that changing it changes the outcome. Queue length may be actionable through staffing; claimant region or final decision is not an intervention lever.
- **Fairness review** is required when a feature is associated with protected or socioeconomic characteristics, even if it is predictive and temporally valid.

| Class | Signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`; `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`; verified as-of-`t0` `initial_reserve`; prefix-only document request counts if actually observed by `t0` | These are associated with breach risk conditional on information available at `t0` and may support risk stratification if validated out of time. | Do not claim they cause breach, are inherently unfair, or are actionable merely because they are predictive. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`; repaired as-of-`t0` document backlog; fraud opener/trigger if observed before `t0` | These describe the process state at prediction time. They may be operationally meaningful if the organization can alter staffing, backlog handling, or triage. | Do not assume congestion causally increases breach without intervention evidence. Do not use snapshots that include post-`t0` observations. |

They should be audited with removal and subgroup tests. | Do not say region/channel “drives delay.” Do not use them as justification for differential service quality. Predictive association is not fairness clearance. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`; holiday indicators; policy-change indicators | These may represent temporal regime shifts, seasonality, or known operating conditions. Useful for monitoring and out-of-time validation. | Do not claim week 52 or any holiday week intrinsically causes breach. Separate calendar effects from policy changes and holidays. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Build a point-in-time feature store**
   - Store event time, ingestion time, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, update history, and source row identifiers for every feature.
   - Support as-of joins at arbitrary event timestamps, not extraction date.
   - Tag fields as `t0-admissible`, `complete-trace`, `mutable-unknown`, `sensitive/proxy`, or `post-outcome`.

2. **Version mutable features and scores**
   - Any risk score, reserve, handler, employee experience, or resource-role feature must have historical versions.
   - Store model version and input snapshot for derived scores.
   - Do not use `current`, `final`, `last`, `anywhere`, or complete-variant features at prediction time unless proven to be event-time snapshots.

3. **Recompute training and explanation data from prefixes**
   - For every case, freeze the prefix ending at `t0`.
   - Recompute all features from that prefix and compare with warehouse values; mismatches become leakage incidents.
   - Sequence explainers must mine patterns from prefixes only, never from full traces.

4. **Implement process-conformance gates**
   - Add footprint, DFG, alignment, prefix, and declarative checks to the training pipeline.
   - Block features and explanations that reference impossible states, future-only activities, or unordered same-timestamp edges.
   - Treat repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` as legitimate only when preceded by appeal after Partial or Deny; do not treat that legitimate repetition as license for other future-label use.

5. **Recalibrate after feature removal**
   - Retrain or at least recalibrate the model after removing leaky features; SHAP values from the old model are not valid explanations for the repaired model.
   - Evaluate calibration with reliability curves, Brier score, log loss, and subgroup calibration using out-of-time validation.
   - Reassess decision thresholds and alert volumes after leakage removal, because predicted probabilities will likely move.

6. **Validate explanation stability**
   - Test whether feature ranks and signs remain stable across bootstrap samples, time windows, and small timestamp perturbations.
   - Check local fidelity after repair: rerun explainers on the prefix-only model and verify that removed features no longer appear.
   - Require explanation generators to distinguish “associated with risk,” “process state,” and “caused by” language.

7. **Run proxy and fairness analysis**
   - Audit region, channel, prior-history counts, reserve, and any language or repair-network variables for subgroup calibration differences, false-positive/false-negative gaps, and resource-allocation impacts.
   - Compare models with and without proxies; document whether performance gains justify fairness risks.
   - Use sensitive attributes for auditing even if they are not used for prediction, subject to governance.

8. **Monitor schema and process drift**
   - Monitor new activity labels, changed field semantics, extraction-date joins, snapshot lag, sequence-index completeness, DFG changes, holiday/policy regimes, and conformance violations.
   - Trigger revalidation when a new post-coverage path appears or when `queue_length_at_t0` snapshot behavior changes.
   - Keep an explanation incident log for any feature or sequence pattern that passes offline checks but fails production time/lineage checks.
