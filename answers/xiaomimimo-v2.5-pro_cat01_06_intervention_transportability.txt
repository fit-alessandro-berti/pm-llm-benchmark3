**Effect modifiers.** `originals_required` is the dominant effect modifier: it reduces the eligible share from ~100 % to ~38 %, directly attenuating the population-average treatment effect. `IT_case_lock` is a second modifier that may reduce `true_overlap` to near zero even for eligible cases. `assessment_capacity` and `legal_capacity` are downstream moderators: if upstream parallelization succeeds, these bottlenecks may absorb the time savings, shifting rather than reducing cycle time. `case_complexity` moderates both `cycle_time` and `rework`, meaning the treatment effect may differ between high- and low-complexity strata.

**Why similar control-flow diagrams do not guarantee transportability.** A process tree or POWL model encodes *ordering constraints among activities* but is silent on eligibility conditions, resource capacities, queue disciplines, task durations, IT system behavior, and data-dependent branching. Site A's before/after trees differ only by a `+` operator replacing a `` between two tasks, yet the observed outcome change is confounded by at least four co-interventions whose effects are encoded nowhere in the control-flow diagram. At Site B, the same control-flow edit would apply to only 38 % of cases, would be obstructed by case-locking IT, and would feed into a downstream legal-review loop that Site A never had. Transportability therefore requires matching the *full causal graph*—including selection nodes, capacity constraints, and co-interventions—not merely the control-flow skeleton.

**Reasoning chain for scenario construction:**

*S1* takes the most optimistic reading: Site A's 9 h median reduction was partly due to parallelization (estimated 3 h after subtracting API upgrade, additional reviewers, and mix-shift effects). Applying 3 h to the eligible 38 % of Site B cases and 0.5 h spillover to ineligible cases yields a weighted reduction of ~1.6 h. However, queue dynamics may amplify or dampen this, so the range is 2–4 h for eligible cases and a blended 2–4 h overall if spillover is larger. The p90 reduction is proportionally smaller because tail cases are driven by factors other than verification handoff (originals delay variance, legal loops).

*S2* narrows eligibility to the intersection of "no originals" and "digital documents," estimating this at 25–30 % of volume based on Site B's higher paper share. The per-case benefit is similar to S1 but applies to fewer cases, yielding a smaller aggregate effect.

*S3* models the bottleneck-shift mechanism: 3 assessors handle 3,400 cases over 6 months (18 cases/assessor/day). If verification throughput increases, cases arrive at Assessment faster, but the assessor pool cannot absorb the surge. The assessment queue grows, offsetting verification savings for the median but dramatically worsening the p90 as a small fraction of cases experiences long assessment waits. This is the canonical "average improves, tail worsens" scenario.

*S4* assumes the IT constraint is binding: case locks prevent true concurrent task execution. The intervention becomes cosmetic—tasks are created simultaneously but executed sequentially with added coordination overhead. Median effect is near zero; tail may worsen slightly from workarounds.

*S5* models the interaction between parallelization and the legal-review loop. With 40 % of cases requiring Legal Review and 2 FTE legal reviewers, the legal pool is already constrained. If parallelization increases throughput to Assessment (and hence to Legal Review), the legal queue grows. Cases that enter the supplement loop experience additional Document Check + Assessment cycles, each now queuing longer. The p90 effect is severe because the tail is dominated by cases cycling through the supplement loop.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

### Eligibility rules

| Rule | Criterion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Original documents NOT legally required (38 % of cases) | Full parallelization: create Identity Check and Document Check tasks simultaneously at registration |
| R2 | Original documents legally required (62 % of cases) | Partial parallelization: create Identity Check and Originals Receipt tasks simultaneously at registration; create Document Check task in "pending originals" state; activate Document Check when Originals Receipt completes |
| R3 | Public-sector or high-value case (40 %) | After Assessment, route to Legal Review queue (statutory requirement—cannot be waived) |
| R4 | All cases | Registration must complete before any verification task is created |

### Concurrent task creation

- **R1 cases:** At registration, the system creates two active tasks: `Identity_Check` and `Document_Check`. Both enter their respective reviewer queues immediately. Assessment is blocked until both complete.
- **R2 cases:** At registration, the system creates two active tasks: `Identity_Check` and `Originals_Receipt`. A `Document_Check` task is created in a "pending" state and is activated automatically when `Originals_Receipt` is logged as complete. Assessment is blocked until both `Identity_Check` and `Document_Check` complete.

### Statutory prerequisites (mandatory, not relaxable)

1. `Document_Check` may not begin until original documents are received (R2 cases).
2. `Assessment` requires both `Identity_Check` and `Document_Check` to be complete.
3. `Legal_Review` is mandatory for public-sector and high-value cases.
4. `Request_Supplement` triggers mandatory re-execution of `Document_Check` and `Assessment`.
5. Final `Approve` or `Reject` decision requires all preceding mandatory tasks to be complete.

### Queue and WIP controls

| Control | Limit | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Identity reviewer WIP cap | 6 open tasks per reviewer (24 total across 4 FTE) | Prevents queue explosion; mirrors Site A's limit |
| Document reviewer WIP cap | 8 open tasks per reviewer (56 total across 7 FTE) | Larger pool allows higher cap |
| Assessment queue depth alert | 15 cases | Early warning of assessment bottleneck |
| Legal review queue depth alert | 10 cases | Early warning of legal bottleneck |
| Pending-originals queue | No cap, but weekly age report | Monitor for stale cases waiting for originals |

### Resource responsibilities

| Role | Pool | Tasks | Cross-training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity reviewer | 4 FTE | Identity Check | No (role-specific) |
| Document reviewer | 7 FTE | Document Check (initial + supplement) | No (role-specific) |
| Assessor | 3 FTE | Assessment (initial + reassessment) | No (role-specific) |
| Legal reviewer | 2 FTE | Legal Review; issue Request Supplement | No (role-specific) |

### Handling of legal-review and supplement loops

1. After `Assessment`, cases flagged for legal review are routed to the Legal Review queue.
2. Legal Review may `Approve`, `Reject`, or issue `Request_Supplement`.
3. `Request_Supplement` creates a new `Document_Check` task (priority: high) linked to the original case.
4. After the new `Document_Check` completes, the case enters the `Assessment` queue for reassessment.
5. After reassessment, the case returns to the Legal Review queue.
6. The loop terminates when Legal Review `Approve`s or `Reject`s.
7. Maximum supplement rounds: 3 (after which the case is escalated to a senior legal reviewer).

### Logging requirements

- System must record **task creation**, **task start**, and **task completion** timestamps for every task.
- All timestamps must be **system-generated** (no manual entry).
- Back-entry is prohibited: system rejects entries with timestamps > 5 minutes in the past.
- Each task must record the **reviewer ID**.
- The system must calculate and log the **overlap indicator**: whether `Identity_Check` and `Document_Check` (or `Originals_Receipt`) have overlapping active intervals for each case.

### POWL-style process specification

```
Site_B_Parallelized = StrictPartialOrder {
    nodes: [Register, Verification, Assessment, Decision, Notify]
    edges: Register  Verification
           Verification  Assessment
           Assessment  Decision
           Decision  Notify
}

Verification = StrictPartialOrder {
    nodes: [Identity_Check, DocPath]
    edges: (none — concurrent execution)
}

DocPath = XOR {
    children: [
        StrictPartialOrder {                        # R2: originals required
            nodes: [Originals_Receipt, Document_Check]
            edges: Originals_Receipt  Document_Check
        },
        Document_Check                               # R1: no originals required
    ]
}

Decision = XOR {
    children: [
        XOR { children: [Approve, Reject] },         # direct decision (no legal review)
        StrictPartialOrder {                          # legal review path
            nodes: [LegalLoop, LegalDecision]
            edges: LegalLoop  LegalDecision
        }
    ]
}

LegalLoop = LOOP {
    body: Legal_Review
    redo: StrictPartialOrder {
        nodes: [Request_Supplement, Document_Check_R, Assessment_R]
        edges: Request_Supplement  Document_Check_R
               Document_Check_R   Assessment_R
    }
}

LegalDecision = XOR { children: [Approve_R, Reject_R] }
```

### Partial-order relation set

```

Verification < Assessment
Assessment   < Decision
Decision     < Notify

# Inside DocPath (choice):

# Document_Check standalone           (branch 2)

# Inside Decision:
LegalLoop < LegalDecision            (legal branch)
# Inside LOOP redo:
Request_Supplement < Document_Check_R < Assessment_R

# Statutory: Assessment waits for BOTH Identity_Check AND Document_Check
```

### How the pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure ( 220 words)

The pilot design separates mechanism failure from implementation failure through three features. **First**, stratified randomization by originals requirement and legal-review eligibility isolates the causal effect within each subgroup. If the treatment shows no benefit in Stratum S1 (no originals, no legal review)—the stratum most structurally analogous to Site A—the parallelization mechanism itself likely fails, possibly because the handoff wait is not a binding constraint at Site B or because capacity constraints absorb the benefit. If the treatment works in S1 but fails in S3/S4, the failure is structural (the originals prerequisite blocks parallelization), not a mechanism problem.

**Second**, process-mining measurement of actual task overlap verifies implementation fidelity. If the overlap rate in the treatment arm remains near the baseline 6 % despite the parallel-task-creation instruction, the implementation failed—likely due to case locking or workflow confusion. A high overlap rate with no cycle-time improvement indicates mechanism failure (parallelization does not reduce the binding bottleneck).

**Third**, the monitoring of queue depths at assessment and legal review distinguishes between "parallelization works but shifts the bottleneck" and "parallelization does not work at all." If assessment queue depth increases in tandem with a flat or improving verification cycle time, the bottleneck shifted—a mechanism success with an operational side-effect requiring capacity intervention, not an abandonment signal. The combination of stratified effects, overlap verification, and queue-depth monitoring enables precise diagnosis.

```python
# Legal-review loop
legal_review        = Transition(label="Legal Review")
request_supplement  = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
# Decision transitions (direct path)
approve_d           = Transition(label="Approve")
reject_d            = Transition(label="Reject")
# Decision transitions (legal path)
approve_l           = Transition(label="Approve")
reject_l            = Transition(label="Reject")
# Notify
notify              = Transition(label="Notify")

# ---------- sub-models ----------

# Branch 1 of DocPath: Originals_Receipt  Document_Check
doc_with_originals = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[originals_receipt, doc_check_post_orig]
)
doc_with_originals.order.add_edge(originals_receipt, doc_check_post_orig)

# DocPath: XOR (originals-required path, no-originals path)
doc_path = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[doc_with_originals, doc_check_no_orig],
)

# Verification: Identity_Check and DocPath run CONCURRENTLY
#   — deliberately NO edge between identity_check and doc_path
verification = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[identity_check, doc_path])
# (no .order.add_edge calls  fully concurrent)

# Supplement redo body: Request_Supplement  Document_Check  Assessment
supplement_redo = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[request_supplement, doc_check_supp, assessment_r]
)
supplement_redo.order.add_edge(request_supplement, doc_check_supp)
supplement_redo.order.add_edge(doc_check_supp, assessment_r)

# Legal-review loop: body = Legal_Review, redo = supplement_redo
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.LOOP,
    children=[legal_review, supplement_redo],
)

# Direct decision (no legal review)
direct_decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[approve_d, reject_d],
)

# Legal decision (after loop exits)
legal_decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[approve_l, reject_l],
)

# Legal path: legal_loop  legal_decision
legal_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[legal_loop, legal_decision])
legal_path.order.add_edge(legal_loop, legal_decision)

# Post-assessment decision: XOR (direct, legal)
post_assessment = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[direct_decision, legal_path],
)

# ---------- main process order ----------
site_b_model = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, verification, assessment, post_assessment, notify]
)
site_b_model.order.add_edge(register, verification)
site_b_model.order.add_edge(verification, assessment)       # Assessment waits for full Verification
site_b_model.order.add_edge(assessment, post_assessment)
site_b_model.order.add_edge(post_assessment, notify)

# ---------- convert to Petri net ----------
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_b_model)

print("\n" + "=" * 60)
print("Site B CANDIDATE (POWL  Petri net)")
print(f"  Places      : {len(net_b.places)}")
print(f"  Transitions : {len(net_b.transitions)}")
print(f"  Arcs        : {len(net_b.arcs)}")
print(f"  Workflow net: {pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_b)}")
try:
    sound_result = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    if isinstance(sound_result, tuple):
        print(f"  Sound       : {sound_result[0]}")
    else:
        print(f"  Sound       : {sound_result}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"  Soundness check raised: {e}")

# ================================================================
# 4.  PLAYOUT — sample traces
# ================================================================
print("\n" + "=" * 60)
print("Site B PLAYOUT (20 sample traces)")
try:
    event_log = pm4py.play_out(
        net_b, im_b, fm_b,
        no_traces=20,
    )
    for i, trace in enumerate(event_log[:20]):
        acts = [ev["concept:name"] for ev in trace]
        print(f"  Trace {i + 1:>2}: {'  '.join(acts)}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"  Playout raised: {e}")

# 5.  MODEL LIMITATIONS
# ================================================================
limitations = """
LIMITATIONS OF THE POWL / PETRI-NET MODEL
==========================================

1. DATA-DEPENDENT LEGAL REQUIREMENT
   The XOR between the direct-decision branch and the legal-review branch
   is a static probabilistic choice.  In reality, the legal-review
   requirement is determined by case attributes (public-sector flag,
   monetary value threshold) known after registration.  The model cannot
   encode conditional routing based on data values.

2. ORIGINALS REQUIREMENT AS DATA-DEPENDENT GATE
   The XOR inside DocPath ("originals required" vs. "no originals") is
   likewise a simplification.  Whether originals are required depends on
   case type and jurisdiction — a data predicate, not a random choice.

3. RESOURCE CAPACITY AND WIP LIMITS
   The model has no concept of resource pools (4 identity reviewers,
   7 document reviewers, etc.) or per-reviewer WIP caps.  Queueing
   delays, resource contention, and utilization are invisible.  The
   playout treats every transition as instantaneous and always available.

4. CASE-LOCKING BEHAVIOUR
   Site B's legacy IT system locks a case to one active task owner.
   This constraint — which may prevent truly concurrent Identity Check
   and Document Check execution — is not represented in the model.

5. DURATIONS AND ARRIVAL RATES
   All transitions are untimed.  The model cannot predict cycle times,
   queue depths, waiting distributions, or utilization.  A simulation
   model (e.g., with BPMN-based or queuing-network simulation) would be
   needed for quantitative forecasting.

6. SUPPLEMENT LOOP DEPTH
   The LOOP operator allows unbounded re-execution.  In practice there
   may be a cap (e.g., 3 supplement rounds) or escalation rules that
   terminate the loop early.  These are not encoded.

7. TIMESTAMP RELIABILITY / BACK-ENTRY
   The model assumes perfect, real-time timestamp recording.  Site B's
   back-entry problem (timestamps entered after the fact due to case-lock
   workarounds) means that real event-log data may not match the model's
   causal ordering.

8. REVIEWER SPECIALISATION
   The model does not enforce that only qualified reviewers fire specific
   transitions.  In reality, an identity reviewer cannot perform a
   document check and vice versa.

9. TEMPORAL ARRIVAL PATTERNS
   The playout draws traces from the structural model without modelling
   the arrival process.  Time-of-day, day-of-week, and seasonal patterns
   in case arrivals — which affect queue dynamics — are absent.

10. INTERVENTION ASSIGNMENT
    The model represents the treatment process only.  It does not encode
    the randomisation mechanism or the control-condition process that
    would be needed to simulate the pilot experiment.
"""
print(limitations)
```

## 9. Recommendation

**`conditional go`**

**What should be transferred.** The core mechanism—parallelizing Identity Check and Document Check for cases where both tasks can legally begin after registration—is theoretically sound and structurally applicable to approximately 38 % of Site B's case volume (those not requiring original documents). For the remaining 62 %, a partial version should be transferred: parallelizing Identity Check with Originals Receipt, and pre-creating the Document Check task in a "pending originals" state so it can activate immediately when originals arrive. The concept of daily queue coordination (adapted from Site A's huddles) should also be considered as a separate, low-cost intervention.

**What should not be copied.** The blanket instruction to "create both tasks for every case" must be rejected. Site A's observed nine-hour median reduction should not be used as a planning target; it is confounded by at least four co-interventions (API upgrade, two additional reviewers, simpler incoming mix, and queue huddles) in an uncontrolled before/after design. The assumption that the case-management system supports simultaneous task ownership must not be taken on faith—it requires a technical feasibility test against Site B's legacy platform. Finally, Site A's cross-trained reviewer model does not transfer; Site B's role-specialized pools mean that parallelization changes task timing but not resource flexibility.

**Which subgroup should enter first.** Cases that require neither original documents nor legal review—approximately 20–25 % of volume. This subgroup maximizes mechanistic similarity to Site A (both verification tasks can genuinely start concurrently, no downstream legal bottleneck absorbs upstream gains, no originals prerequisite blocks Document Check). Success in this subgroup provides the cleanest test of the parallelization mechanism.

**Expected benefits and risks.** For the initial subgroup, a median cycle-time reduction of 2–4 hours is a plausible central estimate, derived from a conservative decomposition of Site A's evidence that attributes roughly one-third of the observed improvement to parallelization. The p90 reduction is likely smaller (0–5 hours) because Site B's tail is driven by originals-delivery variance and legal-review loops that parallelization cannot address. Risks include: increased rework if parallel checks are less thorough (Site A saw a +0.6 pp increase; Site B's already-elevated 17 % baseline may be more sensitive); assessment queue congestion if verification throughput increases faster than the 3-assessor pool can absorb; IT system failures if the case-lock prevents concurrent task states; and staff confusion during the transition.

**Evidence triggers.** *Expand* to the full non-originals stratum if the pilot shows a statistically significant median reduction of at least 2 hours with no rework increase exceeding 1 percentage point and no assessment queue depth exceeding 20 cases for more than one week. *Modify* the intervention (add WIP controls, adjust queue priorities, or add assessor capacity) if the assessment queue depth increases by more than 30 % or if the p90 cycle time increases by more than 10 hours. *Abandon* the parallelization component if the pilot shows no median benefit after 500 treatment cases, if rework increases by more than 3 percentage points, or if IT system failures affect more than 5 % of treatment cases. The partial parallelization for the originals stratum should be tested in a separate pilot phase, initiated only after the non-originals subgroup demonstrates a positive and stable effect.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Parallelization of Identity Check and Document Check reduces the inter-task handoff waiting time.**
   *Why needed:* This is the core causal mechanism; without a reduction in handoff wait, there is no cycle-time benefit.
   *How to test:* In the pilot, measure the time between Identity Check completion and Document Check start (or between Document Check completion and Assessment start) in treatment vs. control arms, using system-generated timestamps.
   *Consequence if false:* The intervention has no cycle-time benefit. Abandon parallelization; investigate whether the binding bottleneck is elsewhere (e.g., originals delay, assessment queue).

2. **Cases not requiring original documents can be reliably identified at registration.**
   *Why needed:* The eligibility segmentation (R1 vs. R2) depends on correctly flagging which cases are eligible for full parallelization.
   *How to test:* Audit a random sample of 200 registered cases; verify that the originals-required flag correctly predicts whether originals are subsequently requested. Measure sensitivity and specificity.
   *Consequence if false:* Eligible cases are misclassified, reducing the intervention's effective share and potentially creating compliance violations (Document Check started without required originals). Tighten the flagging rule or move to a conservative default (all cases treated as originals-required).

3. **The legacy case-management system can support simultaneous creation of Identity Check and Document Check tasks for the same case.**
   *Why needed:* If the system locks cases to one active task owner, true parallelization is impossible without IT changes.
   *How to test:* Conduct a technical feasibility test: create two concurrent tasks on a sandbox case and verify that both can be owned by different reviewers simultaneously with correct timestamps.
   *Consequence if false:* The intervention requires a system patch or workaround protocol before deployment. Cost and timeline increase significantly; consider a phased approach where the system change is a prerequisite for the pilot.

4. **The assessment pool (3 FTE) has sufficient capacity to absorb increased throughput from faster verification.**
   *Why needed:* If parallelization speeds up verification but assessment is the binding constraint, the benefit is shifted rather than realized, and the assessment queue may grow.
   *How to test:* Monitor assessment queue depth and assessor utilization during the pilot. Compare to baseline (median 20 h waiting before assessment).
   *Consequence if false:* Assessment becomes the binding constraint. Options: add assessor capacity; implement assessment prioritization rules; or accept that the intervention's benefit is limited to reducing verification-phase wait (not total cycle time).

5. **Parallel verification does not increase the rework rate or supplement request rate.**
   *Why needed:* If parallel checks are less thorough (no cross-referencing between identity and document evidence), increased rework could offset cycle-time gains and harm quality.
   *How to test:* Compare rework and supplement rates between treatment and control arms. Conduct a quality audit of a sample of parallel-verified cases.
   *Consequence if false:* Add a coordination checkpoint (e.g., a brief "cross-reference review" after both checks complete) before Assessment. This partially re-sequentializes the process but may be necessary for quality. If the rework increase exceeds 3 pp, abandon full parallelization.

6. **The 18-hour median originals-receipt delay is the binding constraint for the 62 % of cases requiring originals, and parallelizing Identity Check with Originals Receipt provides meaningful benefit.**
   *Why needed:* The partial parallelization (T2) for originals cases assumes that Identity Check can productively run during the originals wait.
   *How to test:* In the T2 pilot arm, measure whether Identity Check typically completes before Originals Receipt. Calculate the fraction of cases where Identity Check finishes first and the time saved.
   *Consequence if false:* Partial parallelization provides negligible benefit for originals cases (e.g., if originals arrive before Identity Check finishes). Focus intervention only on non-originals cases; for originals cases, pursue a separate intervention to reduce originals-processing time.

7. **Staff can adapt to the new parallel workflow without significant training costs or productivity loss during the transition.**
   *Why needed:* The intervention requires reviewers to manage concurrent tasks and a new task-creation protocol, which may be unfamiliar.
   *How to test:* Track error rates, cycle times, and rework during the first two weeks (ramp-up period). Survey reviewers on workflow clarity and system usability.
   *Consequence if false:* Extend the training period; pilot duration increases. If errors persist beyond the ramp-up, consider a simplified version (e.g., parallelization only for cases flagged as "simple" by the registration system).

8. **The treatment effect is sufficiently homogeneous within the non-originals, non-legal-review stratum (S1) to warrant a single intervention design.**
   *Why needed:* If the effect varies dramatically by case complexity, document type, or reviewer experience within S1, a single design may be suboptimal and the pilot may miss subgroup-specific harms.
   *How to test:* Estimate treatment-effect heterogeneity within S1 using regression with interaction terms (treatment × complexity, treatment × document type). Examine forest plots of stratum-specific effects.
   *Consequence if false:* Further segment the intervention by complexity or document type. For example, apply full parallelization only to low/medium-complexity cases initially, and test a modified version (with a cross-reference checkpoint) for high-complexity cases. This increases pilot complexity but reduces the risk of averaging over harmful and beneficial sub-effects.
