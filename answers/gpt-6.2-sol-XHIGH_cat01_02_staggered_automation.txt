## 1. What is observed

Alpha and Beta gained branch-level system availability in April, Gamma in July after a readiness pilot, and Delta and Epsilon remained unavailable through August. Alpha and Beta were selected for high volume and historically slow cycle times. Gamma’s later start reflected readiness. These are operationally selected cohorts with different starting conditions.

The recorded automated fast-path share rises from 4% to 25% at launch for Alpha and Gamma and from 6% to 27% for Beta. It continues rising after launch. This is the share of all cases recorded as completing the automated fast path. Automated cases escalated to review fall outside that numerator. The 4–6% readings during months coded unavailable make the provenance of the path flag and the scope of the availability indicator important to verify.

The fraud desk opened for all branches in March. Routing shifted toward each adopting cohort at launch, productive FTEs changed at several branches, and registration risk mix shifted around the May product change. Local practices and implementation fidelity may also have varied.

The panel contains 40 branch-month summaries. Monthly medians and p90s describe different parts of the distribution and cannot recover pooled case-level quantiles. Escalation and rework rates have limited detail about destinations and loops. Survey means describe respondents, with approximate response rates of 20–45% and unavailable response counts. Queue waits, outstanding cases, and case-level timing and routing histories are absent.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary — cohort-by-calendar-month difference-in-differences | April and July adoption cohorts. Subtract each cohort’s last pre-launch month, then the same calendar-month change in the comparison branches. Summarize post months with explicit equal weights. | Delta and Epsilon in the main series. Add Gamma in April–June as a sensitivity check if pilot activity had yet to affect it. | Branch differencing and aligned calendar months. Use pre-rollout characteristics for comparability checks. Treat concurrent load, staffing and risk changes as sensitivity inputs until their roles are established. | M, T, R, E and D for the observed rollout regime, plus descriptive respondent contrasts | Comparable untreated changes at each cohort/date, no consequential anticipation, stable outcome definition and follow-up, and no differential spillovers beyond the exposure specified in the estimand | Pre-period changes and placebo dates, one-donor-at-a-time comparisons, alternate baseline windows, pilot audit, and external records of routing, staffing and desk exposure | Differential changes at launch or shared-desk effects on donors can produce the same pattern as an availability effect. |
| Comparative interrupted time series | Cohort-specific level and slope breaks at rollout in calendar time, with additional recorded intervention dates | Delta and Epsilon on the same calendar, plus Gamma up to any pilot exposure for the April cohort | Pretrend and seasonal terms with longer histories, dated routing and FTE plans, product mix and desk changes where independently measured | Immediate and gradual changes in M and T relative to continuation of the counterfactual series | Stable untreated relative trend and sufficient independent timing variation to distinguish breaks | Inspect residual autocorrelation, pre-period prediction, placebo dates and alternative break forms. A coincident routing break is inseparable from an automation break by timing alone. |
| Baseline-centered synthetic comparison | Fit donor weights using pre-rollout centered trajectories, fix weights before launch, then follow each cohort separately | Delta and Epsilon, with pre-pilot Gamma eligible only for a window in which it remains untreated | Lagged outcomes and predetermined workload, staffing and risk characteristics | M and T for an adopter whose untreated trajectory can be reproduced by the donor pool | Good out-of-sample pre-fit, stable donor relationship, common support for changes, and limited donor exposure to rollout | Hold out pre-periods, inspect raw level and change overlap, vary weights and donors, and report fit error | Few donors and short histories make good pre-fit easy to overstate. The early adopters’ high cycle-time levels already exceed donor levels. |

The primary comparison keeps each cohort on the same calendar as its donors. This matters because the March desk launch and May product change coincide with large movements in several series. Delta and Epsilon provide a fixed comparison through August. Including Gamma only before its own launch tests donor choice, subject to checking pilot exposure. Separate cohort-month contrasts preserve differences between launch and later exposure and avoid using Alpha or Beta as untreated controls for Gamma. Equal branch weights target a typical branch in each cohort. Case-volume weights would change that target and still could not reconstruct pooled quantiles.

With three early pre months and two donors, this is chiefly a transparent benchmark for what the untreated path would have to look like. Similar short-run pre-movements are limited evidence for parallel untreated trends through the product and routing changes. Historical selection may induce rebound, and the desk may transmit the treatment to controls. The panel cannot distinguish a routing campaign synchronized with go-live from the launch package by calendar adjustment alone. Conditioning mechanically on realized fast-path use, arrivals, FTE or mix could also remove policy channels or introduce selection. Verify which changes were independently planned, report donor and trend sensitivities, and interpret the resulting contrasts conditional on those assumptions.

The April cohort’s p90 rose in absolute terms while increasing less than in the donors. Its adjusted median and p90 differences were −2.25 h and −4.10 h in April and −5.45 h and −7.50 h in August, with reversals between those dates. Gamma and both donors improved in the summer. Adding Gamma as a not-yet-treated donor for March → April–June gives gaps of −3.46 h at median and −4.72 h at p90, compared with −3.92 h and −5.57 h using Delta/Epsilon alone for those same dates.

**Intensity.** From April to August, Alpha/Beta’s mean share rose from 26% to 49%, their mean median fell 3.65 h, and their mean p90 rose 3.75 h after a June peak. Gamma’s July → August share rose 7 pp, alongside a −2.40 h median and −4.40 h p90 change. The comparison branches, whose shares stood still, improved by 2.25 h and 4.20 h respectively. In a descriptive regression with branch and calendar-month indicators, an additional 10 pp of observed fast-path share is associated with −1.30 h at the median and −1.84 h at p90. Including availability as a separate regressor changes these slopes to −0.70 h and −0.22 h. The share moves with go-live, tenure, eligibility and case completion. A team can also record a higher share of fast-path completions when work is easier to clear. These are associations between branch-month summaries.

**Pre-trends and rebound.** Relative to Delta/Epsilon, Alpha/Beta’s Jan → Feb median and p90 changes were +0.35 h and +0.65 h, then −0.65 h and −1.25 h from Feb → Mar. Gamma’s Jan → Jun relative movements were +1.40 h and +2.60 h, ending at its worst observed pre-launch month. Selecting slow branches or using a temporarily poor launch baseline can exaggerate apparent improvement through regression to the mean. Gamma’s raw post p90 change is +9.45 h from a Jan–Feb baseline and −16.10 h from June. Its donor-adjusted gap with the earlier baseline is −6.03 h. Three early pre months and a shifting calendar give little basis for ruling out differential shocks or rebound.

**Sensitivity.** Using one donor at a time with the last-month baselines yields median gaps of −4.31 to −4.03 h for Alpha/Beta and −5.35 to −2.10 h for Gamma. The respective p90 ranges are −5.95 to −5.45 h and −9.85 to −3.75 h. Donor agreement can coexist with a common bias. Let δ be the treated-minus-donor difference in *untreated* outcome change. With consistent measurement and a common or negligible desk exposure effect, the implied policy change is the observed gap minus δ. Illustratively allow δ of ±5 h at the median and ±8 h at p90, given the multi-hour swings around the product and load changes. The median scenarios are −9.17 to +0.83 h for Alpha/Beta and −8.73 to +1.28 h for Gamma. The p90 scenarios are −13.70 to +2.30 h and −14.80 to +1.20 h. Relative untreated improvement of only 3.73–4.17 h at the median or 5.70–6.80 h at p90 would explain the corresponding gaps fully. Analogous stress tests of ±2.5 pp for rework and ±1 pp for escalation yield envelopes across the two cohorts of −4.50 to +0.57 pp and −0.10 to +2.00 pp. For scale, the donors’ mean rework rose 2.45 pp between March and June, and the March escalation shift was 2 pp everywhere. The widths are analyst-chosen assumptions about bias.

For survey selection, assume illustratively that exactly 30% respond in both of Gamma’s June and July months. On the 1–5 scale, allowing missing ratings anywhere on the scale gives all-customer means of 1.873–4.673 and 1.921–4.721, respectively. The raw all-customer change could then be −2.75 to +2.85 points even with the observed +0.16 among respondents. This calculation addresses missing outcomes only. The untreated July counterfactual adds further uncertainty.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

expand with an embedded evaluation

Start with a randomized subset of existing intake pods in Delta and Epsilon. Block by branch and baseline load and risk, then randomly assign comparable pods to early availability or delayed availability. Keep delayed pods concurrent through the primary assessment. Retain cases under the assignment at registration, including manual cases, overrides and escalations. Keep routing and staffing rules stable during this comparison and record deviations. If there are too few separable pods, randomize offers among pre-eligible arrivals at a fixed low initial saturation. Interpret that comparison as an offer effect at the tested saturation and reserve branchwide activation for a later capacity test.

Collect at least 12 weeks of standardized baseline measurements and 12 weeks of concurrent post-launch intake, with a fixed follow-up long enough to assess p90 and reopenings. Size the evaluation from baseline variability and the number of independent assignment units. Log linked registration, triage offer and receipt, rule version, override, queue entry, service start, resolution, closure and reopening, plus risk, routing origin, productive hours and survey denominators. Measure variant frequencies, stage waiting versus service time, workload, median, p90, deadline misses, rework and audited routing errors. Track open-case age and incomplete follow-up by intake cohort. Use time-to-resolution analysis when cases remain open.

Track arrivals, staffed capacity and queue age at specialist queues and the fraud desk by referral origin. Include untreated branches and manual cases in spillover monitoring. Review outcomes weekly for high-risk arrivals and escalated cases, and keep a manual fallback. Pause the affected workflow for a verified severe missed referral or a sustained breach of agreed queue or tail limits.

Before launch, ratify thresholds against service commitments. For example, use prespecified randomization-based intervals to advance one saturation step only if the comparison supports at least 2 h of median improvement and its uncertainty excludes more than 5 h of p90 harm, 1 pp of extra rework and 0.10 points of CSAT harm under consistent survey follow-up. Also require acceptable audited routing quality, high-risk performance and desk SLAs. Inconclusive results call for extending the holdout, adding assignment units or adjusting capacity before another scale decision.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Recorded automated fast-path completion increased at every launch. Alpha and Beta’s average monthly p90 increased over their full post-launch windows, while Gamma’s median and p90 improved from June into July and August.
- Suggestive: Same-calendar comparisons favor adopters on median cycle time, p90 and recorded rework, with increased specialist referral. The comparison pattern is compatible with fast-path substitution and some relief in the center of the distribution.
- Conditionally causal: If comparison branches track the adopters’ untreated changes and outcomes share comparable measurement and desk exposure, the historical rollout regime improved branch-month medians by roughly 4 hours relative to deferral over the observed windows. Those assumptions determine whether that interpretation holds.
- Not supported: A universal percentage reduction attributable to automation alone, a causal return to higher fast-path use, or a guarantee that expanding to both branches will improve tail times or all-customer satisfaction.
