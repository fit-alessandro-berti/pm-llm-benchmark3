## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**The distinction in this case.** Resilience is the *deliberate substitution* of a control with an equivalent-strength alternative so that the *protected outcome* (patient supply, product integrity, chain of custody) is preserved under degraded conditions. Concealed noncompliance is the *omission* of a control under time pressure, where the operational metric (cycle time, fill rate) looks preserved but the protected outcome is silently degraded and the gap is pushed into a future audit queue.

**True resilience (keep).**
- **B (manual authorization)** substitutes an offline list + phone for an electronic check; the *control objective* (eligibility verification) is still met, just with weaker throughput. The compliance staff are doing the same work the system would.
- **F (temporary release control)** restores pharmacist dual-control for controlled substances; the protected outcome (controlled-substance integrity) is largely preserved (dual-control gap 2.9% vs 0.2% — elevated but bounded by real-time human control).
- **I (defer noncritical)** and **J (backlog prioritization)** are capacity-rationing choices, not control omissions; they trade service level for mission protection, which is the legitimate logic of resilience.

**Concealed noncompliance (retire).**
- **C (skip authorization)** omits eligibility verification for ambient products on the assumption that "history = standing authorization." This is the clearest case: throughput preserved, control gone, evidence only reconstructable (and possibly unreconstructable).
- **G (dispatch without scan)** breaks chain of custody at the most safety-critical boundary (warehouse  courier). The 9.5% traceability exception rate and the persistent `Invoice close  Audit remediation` tail are its direct fingerprints. Recall-sensitive and controlled-substance products shipped this way are exposure that no amount of after-the-fact reconstruction fully closes.

**Ambiguous — resilience or shortcut depending on risk appetite and time-bounding.**
- **A (hospital fast lane)** is resilience *if* the fast lane still enforces product-specific minimum controls; it becomes noncompliance if "fast" means "skip authorization for critical products."
- **D (snapshot inventory)** is resilience in the first hours and concealed risk after ~1 shift, because stale snapshots cause over-promising and recall-blind shipments.
- **E (manual pick list)** is resilience for physical flow but a shortcut for traceability unless batch/lot/temperature are captured contemporaneously.
- **H (phone POD)** is resilience for ambient; it is concealed noncompliance for controlled substances.
- **K and L** are necessary reconciliation/repair but are *not* resilience in the strict sense — they are the *cost* of earlier shortcuts. Treating them as resilience would be a category error: they restore evidence, not control. A recall that lands during the gap is not protected by later remediation.

**Diagnostic rule.** Ask two questions of each adaptation: (1) Did the *control objective* survive, or only the *operational movement of goods*? (2) Is the evidence *contemporaneous*, or only reconstructable? If the answer is "goods moved, evidence deferred," it is concealed noncompliance regardless of how well the cycle-time metric looked.

## 4. Recovery process model

I use a structured process-tree notation. Each block is a control stage with a guard condition; `||` denotes parallel branches; `` denotes sequence; `[MIN-CONTROL]` tags a non-negotiable control; `(recoverable)` tags work that may be deferred to the reconciliation tail.

```
INCIDENT-RECOVERY-TREE

1. TRIGGER
   condition: WMS OR scanning OR release-system unavailable for > 30 min
              OR temperature-logging unavailable for cold-chain SKU
   action: declare Disruption Mode; freeze noncritical intake; open offline control pack

2. INTAKE & TRIAGE  (parallel)
    Receive order
    Classify segment: Hospital-critical | Pharmacy-standard | Wholesale-bulk
    Classify product risk: Cold-chain | Controlled-substance | Recall-sensitive | Ambient
   guard: every case gets a paper disruption-case-ID stamped at receipt

3. AUTHORIZATION  [MIN-CONTROL: eligibility must be verified before dispatch]
    If system available  Validate authorization (normal)
    If system unavailable  Manual authorization check (B)
         Offline customer/product authorization list (refreshed each shift)
         Phone confirmation logged with timestamp + caller ID
         NO standing-history authorization for controlled-substance or recall-sensitive
   forbidden: C (skip authorization) for any non-ambient; for ambient only with documented
              fallback rule and customer's written standing order on file

4. INVENTORY RESERVATION
    If system available  Inventory reservation (normal)
    If unavailable  Manual snapshot reservation (D)
        guard: snapshot age stamped on every pick list; refresh every shift
        guard: snapshot age > 1 shift  re-verify by physical count before pick
        (recoverable): Reconcile manual reservation (K) at recovery, time-boxed  3 days

5. PICK & PACK  (parallel)
    Manual pick list (E)
        [MIN-CONTROL: contemporaneous capture sheet] — batch, lot, expiry, quantity,
           picker initials, pick time. NOT reconstructed later.
    Product-specific parallel controls:
         Cold-chain  temperature reading at pick + at pack  [F extended]
         Controlled-substance  dual pharmacist sign-off on paper  [F]
         Recall-sensitive  lot check against recall bulletin at pack

6. QUALITY / RELEASE  [MIN-CONTROL by product]
    Cold-chain  pharmacist release + temperature log present  (forbidden: release without temp)
    Controlled-substance  dual-control signature present  (forbidden: sample-only release)
    Recall-sensitive  lot cleared against recall bulletin  (forbidden: release if lot in recall)
    Ambient  sample-based pharmacist release acceptable

7. DISPATCH  [MIN-CONTROL: custody handoff evidence]
    If scanner available  Dispatch with scan (normal)
    If unavailable  Manual handoff manifest (replaces G)
        guard: courier signs manifest with case-ID, batch, lot, quantity, timestamp
        guard: cold-chain  temperature at handoff recorded
        guard: controlled-substance  sealed tamper-evident container + courier ID
   forbidden: Dispatch without scan AND without manifest (i.e., the T2 G pattern)

8. PROOF OF DELIVERY  [MIN-CONTROL by product]
    Ambient  Phone POD acceptable (H) with timestamp + recipient name
    Cold-chain  signed paper POD including arrival temperature
    Controlled-substance  signed paper POD + pharmacist recipient confirmation
        forbidden: phone-only POD for controlled-substance

9. BACKLOG MANAGEMENT  (parallel, throughout)
    Defer noncritical (I): time-bounded, communicated, contractual-notice given
    Backlog prioritization (J): explicit rule =
        1. Hospital-critical life-saving  same-day
        2. Hospital-critical routine  24h
        3. Pharmacy-standard  FIFO with fairness cap (no customer > 2× median wait)
        4. Wholesale-bulk  capacity-slack only
    Backlog age published daily by segment & product-risk

10. RECONCILIATION & AUDIT REMEDIATION  (post-recovery)
     Reconcile manual reservation (K): close within 3 days of system return
     Audit remediation queue (L): every disruption-case-ID reviewed;
         close only if missing control can be evidenced by contemporaneous record
         (paper manifest, signed POD, temp log). If not evidenced  escalate to
         regulatory/QA, do NOT auto-close.
     Residual gap report to management before declaring full recovery

11. EXIT
    condition: all four hold —
       (a) system fitness  0.93,
       (b) traceability exceptions  1.5%,
       (c) cold-chain & controlled-substance gaps  0.5%,
       (d) audit-remediation queue closed or formally accepted by QA/regulatory
```

**Key design principles embedded.**
- Minimum controls are *product-gated*, not segment-gated: a hospital fast lane does not excuse a cold-chain temperature gap.
- Evidence capture is *contemporaneous*, not reconstructable; this is the single biggest fix versus T2.
- The audit-remediation tail (L) is *not* an exit criterion substitute; full recovery requires the queue to close or be formally accepted, not merely to shrink.
- Backlog fairness is explicit (no customer > 2× median wait) to prevent the Wholesale p90 = 180h phenomenon recurring.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** deliberate, time-bounded service degradation for non-critical segments (I, J) and the use of slower manual controls that preserve the *control objective* (B, F, paper manifests replacing scans). It should **not accept** the omission of a control whose only justification is throughput — specifically C (skip authorization), G (dispatch without scan or manifest), and phone-only POD for controlled-substance/cold-chain (H misapplied). These are not resilience; they are compliance debt booked against a future audit queue that, in the event of a recall or diversion, may be unrecoverable. The evidence that would change this recommendation is narrow: a contemporaneous, signed, product-gated manual evidence trail for each adaptation. If, in a future incident, the audit-remediation queue is empty at the moment cycle-time metrics return to baseline, the recovery is real; if the queue is non-empty, "recovered" is a metric illusion and the disruption is not over.
