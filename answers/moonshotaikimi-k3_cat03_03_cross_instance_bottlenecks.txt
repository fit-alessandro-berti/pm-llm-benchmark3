## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job viewpoint:** Shows each job’s activity sequence, rework, and median edge times. It hides shared batch, tool, lot, and shipment membership. If a shared `Batch Cure` is copied into J1, J2, and J3, it can appear as three independent cures. The omission of L7 events from J2/J5 is especially misleading. A median `PackDispatch` of 1.1 h can coexist with J1’s 5 h 55 min tail; “no job beyond 1.8 SD” does not rule out coupling.

- **Machine viewpoint:** Shows utilization, activity order, and event gaps. It hides why a machine is starved and which jobs were ready. The conclusion “OVEN-1 has 56% idle capacity” is misleading when the rack, batch members, or lot release are missing. Moreover, the reported longest OVEN gap of 73 min conflicts with the stated 115-minute idle interval; TEST-1’s 39-minute summary also conflicts with a 50-minute idle interval. Calendar or boundary definitions may explain this, but machine gaps are not wait evidence.

- **Batch viewpoint:** Shows membership, last-ready time, launch lag, and shared machine/tool use. It hides member-specific upstream histories and downstream consequences. A batch DFG may make B1 look efficient because curing starts 10 min after J3 is ready, while hiding J1’s 225-minute membership wait.

- **Tool viewpoint:** Shows FIX-A handoff, RACK-7 cleaning, and CAL-3 recalibration. It hides unrecorded requests and whether a waiting job had alternatives. Some holds are inferred from acquire/release, so a tool-utilization DFG could label cleaning or reservation as productive work and still miss queueing.

- **ComponentLot viewpoint:** Shows that one L7 hold broadcasts to C2/C5 and potentially J2/J5 across B1/B2. It hides the separate supervisor override and job-specific quality disposition. Expanding the lot event into both job traces would duplicate it and falsely imply that J2 and J5 were equally blocked.

- **Shipment viewpoint:** Shows `Create ShipmentLoadDispatch`, but not how long a packed job waits for partners. Thus S1 can have a normal 0.8 h creation-to-load median while J1 waits 340 min for J4.

Low machine utilization can therefore coexist with long job waiting because production requires an **AND-combination** of machine, tool, complete batch, released lot, and sometimes shipment partner. A machine may be idle precisely while jobs queue for a complementary object. Aggregation over six jobs and medians conceals these narrow synchronization windows.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable until 12:05 for an independent reason.
2. J1/J2 “ready” timestamps mean kitting requested, not quality- and resource-ready.
3. The three jobs have a mandatory recipe or minimum batch size, making separation infeasible.
4. An operator, inspection certificate, or L8 release—not J3—was the actual gate.
5. Historical matched cases show early-ready members do not wait longer than singleton jobs.
6. Partial-batch trials do not improve end-to-end completion because downstream inspection or packing absorbs the saved time.
7. Timestamp timezone, clock drift, or event-order corrections materially change the readiness intervals.
8. J1/J2 would have been held by another lot or quality event even if B1 had started earlier.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

- `Request Tool`, `Grant Tool`, `Release Tool`, queue position, and preemption reason.
- Explicit `Wait for Batch`, `Wait for Lot`, `Wait for Shipment`, and queue-exit events.
- Precise readiness semantics and identifiers of all unsatisfied prerequisites.
- Batch minimum/maximum size, recipe compatibility, load start/end, and rack slot assignment.
- RACK-7 cleaning start, end, reason, approver, and qualification status.
- Lot hold reason, sample result, partial-release eligibility, and supervisor-override event ID.
- Machine calendars, planned downtime, operator assignments, and setup start/end.
- CAL-3 due time, certificate validity, calibration reason, and whether recalibration is mandatory after every test.
- Shipment customer, destination, cutoff, carrier appointment, loading start/end, and split authorization.
- Event source system, extraction pipeline, relation qualifier, confidence, and schema version.
