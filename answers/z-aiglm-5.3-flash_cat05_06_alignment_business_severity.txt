## 1. Materiality Framework

This framework scores **business severity independently of raw alignment cost**, then assigns **confidence** as a separate epistemic judgment.

**Severity axes (scored 1–5 per case, incident level)**

| Dimension | Question asked |
|---|---|
| Control objective | Which control was defeated or degraded (payment authorization, fraud screening, notification duty, record integrity)? |
| Amount/exposure | What value was placed at risk, regardless of eventual loss realization? |
| Reversibility | Is the outcome recoverable (internal record) or irreversible (funds disbursed, customer informed)? |
| Timing | Did the control precede the consequential action, or only appear after the fact? |
| Customer/regulatory harm | Was a claimant, counterparty, or supervisory obligation actually or potentially harmed? |
| Recurrence | Is this an isolated instance or part of a pattern (pattern leverage is reflected in remediation priority, not by inflating per-incident severity)? |
| Evidence reliability | Would the surviving evidence withstand audit, forensics, or dispute (immutable linkage vs. verbal assurance vs. mutable fields)? |
| Model scope/attribution | Is the "deviation" actually the model failing to represent a legitimate outcome (deny/withdraw), or the log failing to capture real behavior? |

**Severity anchors**

- **1 – Negligible:** Compliant or purely cosmetic deviation (logging noise, fitting silent moves).
- **2 – Measurement/records defect:** Conduct appears sound; reporting, metrics, or record completeness impaired; no money or customer impact.
- **3 – Moderate control degradation:** A control outcome was likely achieved but not provably, or evidence sits outside the system of record; audit/regulatory exposure without demonstrated loss.
- **4 – Serious:** A prohibited action demonstrably executed (payment without a required control, or strong fraud indicators), bounded exposure.
- **5 – Critical:** Irreversible major disbursement before the applicable control set; systemic breakdown of a P2-class safeguard.

**Confidence (separate axis: Low / Medium / High)** expresses how firmly the *finding itself* is established given: (a) the supplied alignments being authoritative diagnostics, (b) independent corroboration (timestamps, admissions, external documents), and (c) availability of plausible competing explanations. **A missing log event is never proof the activity did not occur** — it establishes at most an evidentiary gap; confirming or excluding performance requires external evidence.

**Interaction rule:** High severity × High confidence  contain/remediate immediately. High severity × Medium/Low confidence  investigate fast (verification is cheaper than the exposure). Low severity × High confidence  batch or automate the fix. Zero-cost alignments and perfect fits are treated as *neutral*, never as assurance that data-dependent policy (P1/P2) held.

**Pattern E — Data-blind auto-approval (zero-cost policy breaches)** *(cases: CL-609, CL-610; evidence: four cases with fully synchronous Auto Approve paths violating P1/P2)*
Business risk: the most dangerous class — structurally perfect traces executing prohibited payments; classic vehicle for diversion fraud (CL-610) and unreviewed mid-size exposures (CL-609); invisible to any pure conformance program. Likely cause: router validates topology, not attributes; or policy predicates absent; or risk/bank-change attributes arrive asynchronously after routing. Confidence: High that breaches occurred; Medium on root cause pending tests. Recommended owner: Workflow/Product Engineering + Fraud Policy.

**Pattern F — Pre-payment authorization breach on high-value claims** *(cases: CL-602, CL-604)*
Business risk: irreversible disbursement ahead of the mandated Fraud Screen / Manual Approve chain — precisely what P3 declares severe irrespective of alignment economics. Likely cause: undetermined between human workaround and automation defect; both credible. Confidence: High that breaches occurred; investigation required for mechanism. Recommended owner: Compliance Investigation now; Payments Operations redesign subsequently.

**Pattern G — Telemetry admixture** *(case: CL-601; recurrent estate-wide)*
Business risk: cosmetic only — cost inflation that trains stakeholders to ignore conformance signals. Likely cause: UI event streams entering business logs without an abstraction layer. Confidence: High. Recommended owner: Platform/Logging Standards.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

For each: competing hypotheses + discriminating evidence. Method note throughout: **an absent core-log event is evidence of an evidentiary boundary, not proof of non-performance**; several pairs of hypotheses below turn exactly on that distinction.

1. **CL-602 — fraud screen skipped vs. screened off-system.** H1: screen genuinely not performed (urgency/pressure). H2: performed in a standalone fraud tool or by phone, never propagated to core. Discriminators: fraud-tool query logs keyed to the claim ID; IP/session audit for the fraud application that day; approver's mail/chat/calendar; screen-share or telephony records around the approval timestamp; absence of *any* candidate artifact strengthens H1, one isolated unlinked artifact suggests H2 (still non-compliant for evidence purposes).

2. **CL-604 — human urgency bypass vs. payment-state-machine defect vs. clock skew.** H1: an operator deliberately released payment pre-approval. H2: payment instruction queued from an earlier lifecycle state fired automatically upon a spurious status transition. H3: bank and core clocks disagree and the 10:03/10:20 ordering is an artifact. Discriminators: initiating user ID on the payment order; scheduler/job logs and status-transition event history; NTP synchronization records for both systems; frozen instruction copies in the bank interface gateway. Independent bank confirmation of acceptance essentially excludes benign reinterpretation regardless.

3. **CL-609/CL-610 — static data-blindness vs. late-arriving attributes vs. event-latency.** H1: the router never evaluates amount/risk/bank-change (predicates unencoded). H2: risk score was computed asynchronously and defaulted LOW-blank at decision time. H3 (CL-610-specific): the bank-change event existed upstream but hadn't propagated to the workflow's view. Discriminators: persist and inspect the router's input snapshot at the decision instant; compare risk-engine request timestamps with routing timestamps; measure median propagation lag of bank-change events; replay the month's auto-approvals against snapshots.

4. **CL-606 (and cohort of 9, especially the 2 unstable-ID cases) — systemic omission vs. sporadic outage vs. linkage defects.** H1: external assessments *never* enter core (design gap). H2: a temporary integration failure hit specific days/vendors. Discriminators: time-series correlating adjuster-report dates with missing log events; per-vendor breakdown; contract/invoice references and portal metadata to recover a stable key for the two suspect cases; interview a sample of adjusters about submission workflow. Note the framing: we are testing *where the record lives*, not whether assessment occurred (a signed report at 10:05 already supports occurrence).

5. **CL-608 — genuine timely portal send vs. post-hoc manual entry.** H1: notification truly went out at +3 h. H2: someone entered the CRM reference hours/days later to "close the loop". H3: correct message, later-retouched link (undetectable if field is mutable — hence the control gap is independent of H1/H2). Discriminators: CRM item created-by/created-at vs. modified-by/modified-at version history; portal/gateway server-side delivery logs; message-ID cross-check with dispatch infrastructure; wording/style forensics of the field content.

6. **CL-607 / CL-612 — real intake diversity vs. misrouted cases.** H1: the process legitimately produces denials and withdrawals (business reality the model ignores). H2: these cases landed in the wrong workflow and should never have been claim cases. Discriminators: distribution of denial reasons; entry-channel analysis of affected cohorts; volumes vs. product expectations; the owners' agreed case taxonomy; check whether withdrawn cases leaked further processing after the exit (downstream task creation would indicate exit handling itself is broken).

7. **CL-603 retry cluster — connector configuration vs. broker redelivery semantics.** H1: aggressive client timeout resends after successful delivery. H2: broker at-least-once redelivery without consumer idempotency keys. Discriminators: connector timeout/retry configuration diff history; dead-letter and redelivery counters on the broker; presence/absence of idempotency keys in payloads; correlation of duplicate bursts with latency spikes.

8. **CL-611 — legacy vocabulary split vs. ad-hoc developer renaming.** H1: the dual name predates a migration and the mapping change was overdue housekeeping. H2: an unbudgeted in-code rename drifted from the signed procedure and was papered over afterwards. Discriminators: version history of the mapping table and deployment notes; effective-date coverage of historic cases; procedure-document revision history versus first appearance of `Assess Damage` in logs.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw cost measures how far a log wanders from a model **syntactically**, under a cost function someone configured. It knows nothing about money, sequencing in the real world, attributes, or consequences. The case set proves the point repeatedly:

1. **Uniform cost-function design flattens categories.** Every visible move costs 1, deliberately unweighted. Consequence: CL-602 — skipping the mandatory Fraud Screen before a EUR 480k disbursement on a freshly changed bank account — scores exactly 1, the same price as one `Autosave` line in CL-601. A one-unit metric cannot separate a nuisance from a catastrophe, so it detects nothing except disagreement.

2. **Data conditions are invisible; zero cost can accompany zero compliance.** CL-609 and CL-610 are perfectly synchronous: cost 0, "exemplary" fitness. Yet CL-609 auto-paid EUR 18,000 at MEDIUM risk — breaching the P1 amount and risk bars — and CL-610 paid into a bank account created one hour earlier, breaching the seven-day rule and displaying a textbook diversion-fraud fingerprint. Sequence-shape conformance cannot even *see* the predicate that made these acts illegal. Conversely, CL-605's cost-0 fit is genuine compliance only because someone checked P1 *outside* the alignment; the alignment contributed nothing to that assurance.

3. **Hidden moves always fit — and fitting is not testimony.** CL-605's `T(tau, c=0)` consumes no cost and, correctly interpreted, witnesses no activity at all; it is conversion plumbing. Any analysis that reads "the tau explains the flow" as substantive verification would be laundering silence into evidence. Silent moves are semantically inert by construction; their only role here is to warn that zero-cost segments carry no factual payload whatsoever.

4. **Scope mismatches invert the ranking.** CL-607 (denied, nothing paid) and CL-612 (withdrawn, nothing paid) earn costs of 4 each — the highest cohort in the dataset — while CL-604, an actual pay-before-approval event on EUR 90k, earns 3. Ranking by raw cost would send investigators chasing paperworkFabricationsT while the irreversible transfers sort *lower*. Disputed model scope (P6) thus manufactures fake risk inflow precisely where business risk is nil.

5. **Granularity, labels, and duplicated telemetry tax noise.** CL-611 costs 2 — double CL-602 — for renaming one activity. CL-601 costs 3 for workbench UI events, equal to CL-604's score for a payment that beat its own approval to the bank. CL-603 contributes cost for a transport retry of a successfully delivered message. In every instance the cost ledger punishes representational friction identically to substantive failure — and occasionally harder than the worst case present.

6. **Missing-but-external evidence is booked as absence.** CL-606 (assessment performed by a licensed external adjuster at 10:05, documented outside core) and CL-608 (portal notification at +3 h, linked via a mutable field) both appear as model moves: "the model demanded it, the log lacks it." Reality: the activities very likely occurred where the model wasn't looking; the genuine defect is provenance and immutability of linkage. The alignment's contribution is a *pointer* to an evidentiary seam, not a finding of non-performance — the reverse error (treating absence as proof) is equally forbidden, notably for CL-602 where skip-versus-unlogged-sidechannel remains formally open.

7. **Irreversibility makes cost functionally monotone in the wrong variable.** Alignment cost is a symmetric distance: model repairs "consume" deviations at a posted price, so CL-604's trace *looks* repairable after three moves. Real-world consequence is not symmetric: the bank accepted EUR 90k at 10:03 and no subsequent model move recalls it. Likewise CL-602 and CL-610 — money out is money out, however elegantly the replay absorbs it. Business risk accumulates monotonically along time; edit-distance penalties do not.

8. **Repetition amplifies what cost cannot weight.** One unit of CL-603 noise, multiplied by 74 identical occurrences from one connector, becomes a systemic monitoring signal — something a per-trace scalar cannot express without a second, deliberate analytical pass.

Conclusion: cost ranks computational disagreement; risk is a function of control objectives, exposure, real-time ordering, data predicates, evidence law, and reversibility. The two scales must be maintained in separate columns — literally so in the code below — and never substituted for one another.

"""
PM4Py reproduction + business-risk aggregation for the insurance-claim alignment review.

SCOPE AND AUTHORITY NOTE (requirement 5):
- The SUPPLIED case alignments in the assignment are treated as AUTHORITATIVE diagnostics.
- PM4Py's optimizer may return a DIFFERENT (equally optimal) alignment, different tuple
  notation, different naming/labels, or a different silent-transition structure than the
  supplied table (including the T(tau) artifact in CL-605, which depends on the converter).
  Any such difference is an alternative optimum or a formatting variance — NEVER a reason
  to overwrite the supplied analysis.
- Technical alignment costs and the analyst-supplied BUSINESS SEVERITY ratings are kept
  in SEPARATE dataframe columns throughout (requirement: keep them separated).

LOG_TRACES = {

try:
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(event_log, net, im, fm)

    def _classify(pair):
        """Coarse S/L/M/T counting over an alignment step tuple (log, model)."""
        log_part, model_part = pair
        log_lab = log_part if isinstance(log_part, str) else None
        mod_lab = model_part if isinstance(model_part, str) else None
        if log_lab is not None and mod_lab is not None:
            return "S"
        if log_lab is not None and mod_lab is None:
            return "L"
        if log_lab is None and mod_lab is None:
            return "T(silent, fitting)"          # tau consumes nothing, proves nothing
        if isinstance(mod_lab, str) and ("tau" in mod_lab.lower() or ">" in mod_lab):
            return "T(silent, fitting)"
        return "M"

    def _summarize(steps):
        counts = {}
        for p in steps:
            k = _classify(p)
            counts[k] = counts.get(k, 0) + 1
        return ", ".join(f"{k}x{v}" for k, v in counts.items())

    print("\n[reproduction] PM4Py alignments (may differ from supplied authoritative alignments):")
    if isinstance(diag, dict):                      # newer pm4py: variant-keyed mapping
        iterator = diag.values()
    else:                                           # classic: one result per trace, in order
        iterator = diag
    for idx, res in enumerate(iterator):
        steps = res.get("alignment", []) if isinstance(res, dict) else []
        print(f"  case#{idx+1:02d}: {_summarize(steps)} | raw alignment dict available for inspection")
except Exception as exc:
    print(f"\n[warn] PM4Py conformance step skipped ({exc}). "

    ("CL-608", 6, 0, 1, 0, 1, "crm_notify_link_mutable",    "P4 met if CRM trusted",      "Missing evidence (weak link)",  3, "Medium"),
    ("CL-609", 6, 0, 0, 0, 0, "data_blind_auto_approve",    "FAIL P1 (amt+risk)",         "Data-aware policy failure",     4, "High"),
    ("CL-610", 6, 0, 0, 0, 0, "data_blind_auto_approve",    "FAIL P1 (bank chg <7d)",     "Data-aware policy failure",     4, "High"),
    ("CL-611", 6, 1, 1, 0, 2, "label_alias_mismatch",       "Substantively PASS",         "Event-label mismatch",          2, "High"),
    ("CL-612", 3, 1, 3, 0, 4, "out_of_scope_withdrawal",    "OUT OF SCOPE (P6)",          "Process-model mismatch",        2, "High"),
], columns=["case_id", "sync_moves_S", "log_moves_L", "model_moves_M", "silent_T_fitting",

supplied = supplied.merge(df_log.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
    amount_eur=("amount_eur", "first"),
    risk_level=("risk_level", "first")).reset_index().rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case_id"}),

print("\n=== Supplied-alignment case table (technical cost SEPARATE from business severity) ===")
print(supplied.to_string(index=False))

agg_class = (supplied.groupby("deviation_class")
