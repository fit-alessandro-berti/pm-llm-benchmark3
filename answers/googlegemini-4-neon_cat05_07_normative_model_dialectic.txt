### D. Burden-of-Proof Framework
Conformance review requires asymmetric evidentiary standards aligned to governance risk:
- **Declaring Operational Noncompliance:** The reviewer/prosecution bears the burden of **clear and convincing evidence** that (a) an authoritative, in-scope rule applied at the time of execution, (b) the deviation reflects actual operational behavior rather than an ETL/logging artifact, and (c) no valid, active exception overlay covered the trace. - **Establishing a Justified Exception:** The defense/operations bears the **affirmative burden of production and proof** that (a) a formally authorized overlay existed and was active at execution time, (b) every prerequisite eligibility, cap, monitoring, and SLA condition of that overlay was satisfied, and (c) evidence is traceable via immutable identifiers. Informal expectations of “imminent approval” or undocumented self-extensions fail this burden as a matter of governance.
- **Identifying Model/Scope Defects:** Either party may establish a model or scope defect by showing that CS-9’s text, stakeholder consensus, or semantic equivalence contradicts M0’s rigid encoding without introducing unmitigated risk.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### Core Thesis
The supplier-onboarding operation exhibits **material, systemic noncompliance** with Compliance Standard CS-9. While M0 undeniably contains control-flow omissions and the onboarding log suffers from CRM mirroring loss, those defects cannot explain away—and must not be used to launder—severe control bypasses, unauthorized post-expiry continuation of temporary powers, manual overrides of eligibility controls, and hidden identity/independence violations.

### 1. Formal Authority and the Integrity of Controlled Standards
CS-9 (N1) is the sole formally approved and controlled compliance standard. Neither steering-committee minutes (PD-3, N2) nor operational convenience (N5) amends a controlled policy. Even if PD-3 is charitably credited as a bounded “monitored exception” during June–July, Compliance explicitly clarified that it never authorized weakening CS-9 permanently. Treating informal consensus or anticipated policy changes as live authorization destroys the boundary between controlled governance and ad hoc operational discretion.

### 2. Expiry Means Expiry: Normalization of Deviance
Both temporary overlays had explicit expiration dates—and operations breached both:
- **Post-Pilot Drift (SUP-714 and the August Cluster):** PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. Yet **6 cases** used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August, including **SUP-714** (2026-08-02), solely because operations normalized the pilot and assumed permanent approval was “imminent.” Anticipating a policy amendment is not compliance; leaving an unapproved automation path open in production is a textbook failure of change and access control.
- **Self-Extended Emergency Powers (SUP-705):** FD-7 expired automatically at 2026-07-12 23:59. Operations invoked the emergency bypass again on 2026-07-13 without a written extension. Emergency powers are strictly time-boxed precisely because front-line teams always experience ongoing operational pressure after a crisis. Allowing operations to unilaterally declare when an emergency continues renders automatic sunset clauses meaningless.

### 3. Severe High-Risk Breaches and Failed Preventive Controls
The aggregate and case-level record demonstrates that weak system guards enabled high-severity risk exposures:
- **SUP-703 (Eligibility Override on High-Risk Spend):** An operator manually selected “pilot” for an ineligible HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, bypassing `Manual_Procurement_Approval` and pre-activation `Quality_Review` before a **EUR 180,000** purchase. This was not an isolated error: **14 of 312** June–July `Auto_Approve` cases (4.5%) were clearly ineligible, and **9 more** (2.9%) lacked eligibility attributes entirely—meaning the system permitted automated activation without even collecting mandatory eligibility data.
- **SUP-708 (Sanctions Gate Failure):** A HIGH-risk supplier was activated **40 minutes before** `Sanctions_Check` and prior to `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, with no pilot or emergency overlay. Even under PD-3 and FD-7, pre-activation `Sanctions_Check` is an absolute hard floor. The absence of transactions during that 40-minute window is fortuitous luck, not a control; a single payment to a sanctioned entity creates strict-liability regulatory exposure.

### 4. Compliance Failures Invisible to M0
Focusing solely on M0’s false positives conceals dangerous **false negatives** where traces fit M0 perfectly:
- **SUP-712 (SoD Evasion via Aliases):** The trace is 100% control-flow conformant with M0, yet requester and manual approver resolve to the same individual through two aliases. This directly violates CS-9’s human SoD requirement and exposes a severe identity-resolution blind spot.
- **SUP-715 (Impaired Control Independence):** While M0 passes and CS-9 only names requester/approver SoD, the quality reviewer and manual approver were spouses within the same reporting line who failed to file mandatory Conflict-of-Interest disclosures. Concentrating two consecutive gatekeeping roles inside a single household and supervisory hierarchy undermines independent assurance.

### 5. Desirable Outcomes Do Not Retroactively Amend Policy
Operations points to benign outcomes—zero transactions during **SUP-708**’s 40-minute gap, low spend in **SUP-705** (EUR 12,000), valid eligibility in **SUP-714**, and retrospective completion of `Quality_Review`. In compliance engineering, **ex post absence of loss does not validate ex ante control bypass**. Preventive controls exist to bound tail risk before harm materializes.

### 6. Explicit Acknowledgment of Model and Data Defects
A credible prosecution does not overreach. The record genuinely shows that M0 and the event pipeline generate substantial false-positive deviations: **SUP-707** (and 47 `Request_More_Info` cases) strengthened due diligence; **SUP-709** reflects an ambiguous pre-screen scope; **SUP-711** is a deterministic ETL duplicate; **SUP-713** is a benign label rename (`QA_Clearance`); and the post-July-1 spike in missing notifications from **2% to 29%** (affecting **SUP-706**) stems from the CRM migration (N4). Stripping away these artifacts does not exonerate operations—rather, it isolates a stark core of confirmed, high-risk noncompliance (**SUP-703**, **SUP-708**, **SUP-712**, **SUP-714**, **SUP-705**, **SUP-715**, and the 14 ineligible + 6 post-pilot + 9 unverified `Auto_Approve` cases) that demands immediate enforcement and hard preventive controls.

### 1. Structural Obsolescence and Missing Control-Flow Expressiveness
M0 fails to model legitimate, consensus-endorsed control-flow behaviors:
- **Missing Clarification Loop (SUP-707 / N5):** Operations used `Request_More_Info` in **47 cases** to obtain missing certificates before approval. Compliance agrees this is desirable, and **0 of 47 cases** activated before evidence arrived. M0 flags all 47 cases as noncompliant solely because its POWL omits a loop construct.
- **Label Drift Without Control Drift (SUP-713):** UI and procedure renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance`; audit records prove identical substantive verification, and the mapping was formally approved one week later. Flagging **SUP-713** is pure syntactic brittleness.
- **Partial-Order Concurrency vs. Static Ordering:** M0 already recognizes concurrency between `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`, yet rigidly prohibits controlled provisional activation overlays even when formally authorized and bounded by compensating controls.

### 2. Authorized Policy Overlays Excluded from M0
M0 was never updated to reflect formal organizational decisions:
- **PD-3 Pilot Governance (SUP-702 and 289 Eligible Cases):** Steering-committee governance explicitly authorized PD-3 for June–July, and Compliance explicitly consented in writing that the “pilot may proceed as a monitored exception.” In **SUP-702**, every substantive requirement was satisfied (LOW-risk DE corporate-group supplier, pre-activation `Sanctions_Check`, next-business-day `Quality_Review`, complete eligibility fields, and CRM-verified notification at +2 hours via stable `notification_id`). Branding **289 eligible pilot cases** as “noncompliant” because model owners never updated M0 punishes operations for executing an executive mandate.
- **FD-7 Flood Emergency (SUP-704 and Context of SUP-705):** **SUP-704** satisfied every single FD-7 safeguard: active window (2026-07-11), critical pump supplier, pre-activation `Sanctions_Check`, EUR 18,000 spend (under the EUR 20,000 cap), daily transaction monitoring, and next-day quality and manual approval. Even in **SUP-705** (2026-07-13), flood recovery was factually ongoing and operations adhered to FD-7’s substantive risk caps (EUR 12,000 spend, pre-activation sanctions, next-day quality and manual approval); the failure lies largely in leadership’s lack of a rapid emergency-extension mechanism rather than reckless operational behavior.

### 3. Ambiguous Scope Boundaries
M0 is improperly applied to process populations it was never designed to govern:
- **Abbreviated Pre-Screen Rejections (SUP-709):** CS-9 explicitly states that rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen process and leaves M0’s scope ambiguous. Requiring a full `Quality_Review` on a lead that already failed `Sanctions_Check` is operationally absurd and wastes compliance capacity.
- **Supplier Reactivations (SUP-710):** Reactivating a supplier closed six months earlier with a still-valid quality certification is a distinct lifecycle process on which CS-9 is completely silent. Operations still ran `Sanctions_Check` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` prior to activation. Penalizing operations for missing `Register_Supplier` and redundant `Quality_Review` reflects a policy/model gap, not misconduct.

### 4. Telemetry Breakdown Inflates Apparent Nonconformance
A massive share of M0’s reported deviations originates in IT/data engineering rather than procurement operations:
- **CRM Migration Blind Spot (N4 / SUP-706):** Moving `Notify_Requester` to CRM caused log missingness to jump **14.5× (from 2% to 29%)**. Nearly a third of compliant post-July-1 cases now fail M0’s `Notify_Requester` requirement solely due to broken event mirroring. In **SUP-706**, every risk-bearing control (`Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`) executed in strict sequence, and CRM contains a +3-hour message; mutable linkage is an IT design flaw, not an operational failure.
- **Migration Event Duplication (SUP-711):** Duplicate `Register_Supplier` rows share an identical source transaction ID. Failing to deduplicate source logs before running conformance checking is a basic data-pipeline error.

### 5. Distinguishing Trace Deviation from Business Misconduct
Conflating **SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713** with genuine violations buries real risk in alert fatigue. Replacing M0 with a multi-layered, data-aware normative architecture—and fixing ETL/CRM telemetry—is a prerequisite to credible compliance measurement.

### 6. Explicit Acknowledgment of Genuine Risk and Limits on Informal Practice
The defense cannot—and should not—excuse substantive control failures. **SUP-703** (and the **14 ineligible** plus **9 unverified** `Auto_Approve` cases) proves that manual operator selection of pilot eligibility is unacceptable and created real EUR 180,000 high-risk exposure. **SUP-708**’s pre-sanctions activation of a HIGH-risk supplier is a serious preventive-control failure regardless of zero interim spend. **SUP-712**’s alias-based self-approval violates CS-9 unambiguously. Likewise, continuing PD-3 after expiry (**SUP-714** and 5 other August cases) and operating post-expiry under FD-7 (**SUP-705**) demonstrate that informal operational norms cannot substitute for formal feature-flag cutoffs and documented extensions. Finally, **SUP-715** highlights a legitimate CoI disclosure failure that requires tighter organizational controls.

### 2. System-Level Judgment
Both operational compliance and normative model validity are **materially deficient**, but for distinct, separable reasons:

- **Operational Compliance Verdict — Materially Noncompliant (Targeted but Severe):** Even after stripping out every model, scope, and logging false positive, operations exhibits **unacceptable control failures**: (a) hard preventive gates are absent, allowing pre-sanctions activation (**SUP-708**) and operator self-selection of pilot eligibility on high-risk spend (**SUP-703**, plus **14 ineligible** and **9 unverified** June–July `Auto_Approve` cases); (b) temporal governance is broken, as operations continued both PD-3 (**SUP-714** and 5 peers) and FD-7 (**SUP-705**) past expiry; and (c) identity/independence controls fail outside control-flow logs (**SUP-712**, **SUP-715**).
- **Model & Data Validity Verdict — M0 Is Unfit for Standalone Enforcement:** M0 cannot serve as an authoritative compliance oracle in its current form. It generates systematic **false positives** on beneficial clarifications (47 `Request_More_Info` cases), authorized overlays (289 eligible PD-3 cases, FD-7), abbreviated pre-screen rejections (**SUP-709**), reactivations (**SUP-710**), label renames (**SUP-713**), ETL duplicates (**SUP-711**), and broken CRM telemetry (29% notification loss). Worse, it suffers from dangerous **false negatives** on SoD (**SUP-712**), CoI (**SUP-715**), eligibility/spend caps (**SUP-703**), and policy expiry (**SUP-705**, **SUP-714**).

### 3. Where Each Side’s Argument Is Rejected
- **Rejections of the Prosecution:**
  1. *Rejecting categorical illegitimacy of PD-3 during its active window:* Compliance’s contemporaneous written statement (“pilot may proceed as a monitored exception”) created valid operational reliance for compliant June–July cases (**SUP-702**). Retroactively declaring 289 eligible pilot cases noncompliant because governance failed to update CS-9/M0 violates basic fairness and burden-of-proof principles.
  2. *Rejecting presumption of noncompliance on degraded telemetry (**SUP-706**):* With a documented 29% CRM mirroring drop, absent onboarding-log notifications cannot be equated with operational failure; mutable linkage yields `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`, not `NONCOMPLIANT`.
  3. *Rejecting rigid M0 enforcement on unmodeled/unscoped variants (**SUP-707**, **SUP-709**, **SUP-710**, **SUP-713**):* Enforcing M0 literally would penalize risk-reducing certificate requests and demand wasteful QA on sanctions-rejected leads.
- **Rejections of the Defense:**
  1. *Rejecting factual-necessity self-extension (**SUP-705**) and anticipated-amendment drift (**SUP-714**):* Automatic sunset clauses exist specifically to force executive/compliance re-authorization. Operational beliefs that floods persist or permanent approval is “imminent” cannot substitute for written authority.
  2. *Rejecting “no harm, no foul” mitigation as exculpation (**SUP-708**, **SUP-705**, **SUP-714**):* Zero spend during a 40-minute pre-sanctions activation window is luck, not compliance; preventive gates must be evaluated ex ante.
  3. *Rejecting the framing that high deviation rates are “mostly obsolescence”:* While obsolescence inflates volume, the confirmed cluster of **14 ineligible** and **9 unverified** `Auto_Approve` cases, **6 post-pilot** cases, **SUP-703**, **SUP-708**, and **SUP-712** proves systemic preventive-control failure that model revisions alone will not fix.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A single control-flow model cannot govern multi-dimensional compliance. Compliance verification must be decomposed into **five coordinated layers**, each assigned to the technical or organizational mechanism suited to its formal expressive power:

### Layer 1: Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL + Canonical Event Mapping)
- **Belongs in:** **PM4Py POWL** (plus deterministic ETL label normalization and transaction-ID deduplication).
- **Design:**
  - Standardize canonical event labels via a versioned ontology map (`QA_Clearance` -> `Quality_Review`) and deduplicate on `(case_id, source_transaction_id)` prior to conformance evaluation.
  - Retain concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` on the standard onboarding route, alongside a repeatable `Request_More_Info` loop (`0..*`) permitted prior to decision.
  - Model distinct, explicitly bounded control-flow routes: (1) Standard Manual-Approval Route, (2) Low-Risk Auto-Approval Route (with mandatory pre-activation `Sanctions_Check` and `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`), (3) Emergency Provisional-Activation Route (mandatory pre-activation `Sanctions_Check`, post-activation retrospective `Quality_Review` and decision), and (4) Abbreviated Pre-Screen Rejection / Withdrawal Route (eliminating mandatory `Quality_Review` after sanctions failure).
  - Enforce `Notify_Requester` strictly after the activation/rejection milestone and strictly before `Close_Case` on every route.
  - **Architectural Rule:** Because POWL `XOR` choices are unguarded, production conformance checking must **instantiate/select the authorized sub-POWL per case** using Layer 4’s active policy overlay rather than blindly accepting any branch of the union POWL.

### Layer 2: Temporal, Behavioral, and Cross-Event Constraints
- **Belongs in:** **DECLARE / Log-Skeleton / LTL-over-traces checks** (and ERP hard workflow blocks).
- **Design:**
  - **Universal Hard Precedence (Invariant across all routes):** `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` — never waivable, even in emergencies.
  - **Clarification Integrity:** `Precedence(Request_More_Info_Resolved, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` and no activation while an open info request exists.
  - **SLA Deadlines (Metric Temporal / Data-Enhanced DECLARE):** On provisional routes (Auto-Approve and Emergency), `Quality_Review` and retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval` (emergency) must occur within **2 business days** of `Activate_Supplier` (using business-calendar timestamps); automated suspension triggers on SLA breach.
  - **Emergency Monitoring:** Daily `Transaction_Monitoring_Check` events required for every calendar day between provisional `Activate_Supplier` and retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.

### Layer 3: Data-Aware Eligibility, Amount, and Scope Rules
- **Belongs in:** **Pandas / SQL / Rule-Engine Data Checks** (evaluated against immutable case-start eligibility snapshots) and **ERP/API Preventive Guards**.
- **Design:**
  - **Auto-Approve Eligibility Guard (system-computed, never operator-selected):** `risk_tier == 'LOW'` AND `country == 'DE'` AND `approved_corporate_group == True` AND `automated_master_data_valid == True` AND `active_overlay == 'PD-3'` (or future permanent policy ID). Missing attributes default to `False` (fail-closed, eliminating the 9 unverified cases).
  - **Emergency Spend Cap Guard:** Cumulative PO/invoice spend between provisional `Activate_Supplier` and retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval` must satisfy `cumulative_spend_eur <= 20000`, enforced via ERP hard payment/PO block.
  - **Explicit Scope Rules (Separate Case Types / Process Variants):**
    - *Rejected Pre-Screen Leads:* Tagged `case_type = 'INTAKE_PRESCREEN'`; governed by the abbreviated pre-screen route (`Register -> Sanctions -> [RMI*] -> Reject -> Notify -> Close`) so they do not pollute full onboarding KPIs while remaining fully auditable.
    - *Supplier Reactivations:* Tagged `case_type = 'REACTIVATION'`; governed by a dedicated reactivation SOP and sub-model requiring `Initiate_Reactivation`, mandatory `Sanctions_Check`, automated master-data/bank-change diff, conditional `Quality_Review` (mandatory if prior certification > 12 months old or scope/ownership changed), `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, `Activate_Supplier`, `Notify_Requester`, and `Close_Case`.
    - *Applicant Withdrawals:* Tagged `outcome = 'WITHDRAWN'`; permitted prior to activation via `Withdraw_Application -> Notify_Requester -> Close_Case`, terminating open check obligations cleanly.

### Layer 4: Resource, Identity, and Conflict-of-Interest Controls
- **Belongs in:** **Pandas/SQL Graph & Identity Checks** backed by **HR/IAM Master Data** and **Organizational Controls**.
- **Design:**
  - **Canonical Identity Resolution:** Map all system usernames/aliases (`actor_alias`) to an immutable `canonical_person_id` sourced from HR/IAM at event emission time.
  - **Expanded Segregation of Duties (CS-9 Amendment):** Enforce `canonical_person_id(Register_Supplier/Requester) != canonical_person_id(Manual_Procurement_Approval)` AND prohibit `canonical_person_id(Quality_Review) == canonical_person_id(Manual_Procurement_Approval)` (closing **SUP-712**).
  - **Relationship & Hierarchy Conflict Checks:** Cross-check `canonical_person_id` pairs across `{Requester, Quality_Review, Manual_Procurement_Approval}` against HR reporting-line graphs (`manager_chain` overlap) and disclosed CoI registries (`household/spousal/financial_tie`). Missing CoI attestation or undisclosed conflict blocks approval (**SUP-715**).
  - **Delegation Verification:** Any acting approver must link to a time-bounded, signed `delegation_id` in IAM.

### Layer 5: Policy-Overlay Governance and Organizational Controls
- **Belongs in:** **Organizational Governance + Feature-Flagged Policy Registry (Code-as-Policy)**.
- **Design:**
  - Every pilot or emergency exception must be registered in a versioned **Policy-Overlay Registry** with signed Compliance + Executive approval IDs, explicit scope predicates, mandatory compensating controls, and **hard automated expiration timestamps**.
  - **Automated Feature-Flag Sunset:** Production workflows (`Auto_Approve`, provisional activation) read live validity from the Policy-Overlay Registry and **automatically disable** at `effective_to_utc` unless a cryptographically logged extension record exists (preventing **SUP-705** and **SUP-714**).
  - **CRM Telemetry Reconciliation:** Replace mutable text links with mandatory immutable `notification_id` foreign keys generated synchronously at case creation/decision, coupled with nightly dead-letter reconciliation to drive notification missingness below 0.5%.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### A. Structural Limitations of a Single POWL (Explicit Disclosure)
A single POWL `M1` is inherently **pure control-flow**:
1. **Unguarded `XOR` Branching:** Combining standard, auto-approve, emergency, and pre-screen-rejection routes inside one `Operator.XOR` means pure POWL replay cannot verify whether a case *qualified* for the branch it took (e.g., **SUP-703**, **SUP-705**, or **SUP-714** would appear control-flow fitting if their required branch events were logged). **Mitigation:** In the code below, each route is constructed as a modular POWL sub-model (`build_standard_route`, `build_auto_approve_route`, `build_emergency_route`, `build_prescreen_exit_route`) AND composed into `M1`. Production conformance checking must invoke `select_authorized_powl(case_context, overlay_registry)` to check each case strictly against the **authorized route sub-POWL**, while `M1` serves as the unified architectural reference model.
2. **Loops (`Request_More_Info`):** In PM4Py POWL, `Operator.LOOP(do=SilentTransition(), redo=Transition("Request_More_Info"))` accurately models `0..*` repetitions (`Request_More_Info*`) concurrently with checks, but cannot natively bound the maximum cycle count or verify that requested certificates actually arrived before approval; those checks belong in the DECLARE/data layer.
3. **Unenforceable Non-Control-Flow Predicates:** Risk tiers, country/group eligibility, EUR 20,000 spend caps, 2-business-day SLAs, policy effective windows, canonical human SoD, and spousal/reporting-line CoI rules cannot be expressed in POWL and are enforced via the accompanying Policy-Overlay Table and Data/Event Schema.
4. **Reactivation Lifecycle:** Reactivation (**SUP-710**) is intentionally excluded from `M1` (which starts at `Register_Supplier`) and handled as a separate lifecycle variant (`case_type = 'REACTIVATION'`) to avoid weakening greenfield registration invariants.

### B. Executable PM4Py POWL and Guard-Dispatcher Code

```python
from datetime import datetime, timezone
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def t(label: str) -> Transition:
    """Return a fresh Transition node to preserve POWL tree node uniqueness."""
    return Transition(label=label)


def rmi_loop() -> OperatorPOWL:
    """
    Model 0 or more Request_More_Info cycles using PM4Py LOOP(do=tau, redo=RMI).
    Allows arbitrary interleaving when placed concurrently inside a StrictPartialOrder.
    """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), t("Request_More_Info")],
    )


def build_standard_route() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Standard CS-9 route:
    - Concurrent Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review, and 0..* Request_More_Info cycles.
    - Followed by XOR(Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier).
    - Followed by Notify_Requester (prior to Close_Case in parent model).
    """
    sanctions = t("Sanctions_Check")
    quality = t("Quality_Review")
    clarifications = rmi_loop()
    checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality, clarifications])
    # No edges inside `checks`: Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review, and RMI* are concurrent.

    manual_approval = t("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate = t("Activate_Supplier")
    approved_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
    approved_branch.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

    reject = t("Reject_Supplier")
    decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approved_branch, reject])

    notify = t("Notify_Requester")

    route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks, decision, notify])
    route.order.add_edge(checks, decision)
    route.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
    return route


def build_auto_approve_route() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Governed low-risk auto-approval route (PD-3 / future permanent amendment):
    - Concurrent Sanctions_Check and Automated_Master_Data_Validation strictly precede
      Auto_Approve -> Activate_Supplier.
    - Quality_Review is mandatory before Close_Case and may occur either before or
      (within 2 business days, enforced by data/SLA layer) after Activate_Supplier.
    - Notify_Requester strictly follows Activate_Supplier and may occur before or
      after retrospective Quality_Review (supporting SUP-702 +2h CRM notification).
    """
    sanctions = t("Sanctions_Check")
    amdv = t("Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
    pre_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, amdv])

    auto_approve = t("Auto_Approve")
    activate = t("Activate_Supplier")
    quality = t("Quality_Review")
    notify = t("Notify_Requester")

    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[pre_checks, auto_approve, activate, quality, notify]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(pre_checks, auto_approve)
    route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
    route.order.add_edge(activate, notify)
    # `quality` has no edge relative to `auto_approve`/`activate`/`notify`, so it may
    # execute pre- or post-activation, but is guaranteed to complete before Close_Case.
    return route


def build_emergency_route() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Governed emergency/provisional activation route (FD-7 pattern):
    - Sanctions_Check MUST precede provisional Activate_Supplier (non-waivable).
    - Retrospective Quality_Review followed by retrospective decision
      XOR(Manual_Procurement_Approval, Reject_Supplier) occurs after Activate_Supplier.
    - Notify_Requester follows Activate_Supplier and precedes Close_Case (allowing
      either immediate provisional notification or post-retrospective notification).
    """
    sanctions = t("Sanctions_Check")
    activate = t("Activate_Supplier")
    quality = t("Quality_Review")
    retro_decision = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[t("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), t("Reject_Supplier")],
    )
    notify = t("Notify_Requester")

    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, activate, quality, retro_decision, notify]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
    route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)
    route.order.add_edge(quality, retro_decision)
    route.order.add_edge(activate, notify)
    return route


def build_prescreen_exit_route() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Abbreviated pre-screen rejection (resolving SUP-709) or applicant withdrawal:
    - Branch A (Pre-screen Reject): Sanctions_Check (concurrent with 0..* RMI) -> Reject_Supplier.
    - Branch B (Withdrawal): Optional Sanctions_Check -> Withdraw_Application.
    - Followed by Notify_Requester before Close_Case.
    """
    sanctions_rej = t("Sanctions_Check")
    clarifications = rmi_loop()
    prescreen = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_rej, clarifications])
    reject = t("Reject_Supplier")
    prescreen_reject = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[prescreen, reject])
    prescreen_reject.order.add_edge(prescreen, reject)

    opt_sanctions = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[t("Sanctions_Check"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    withdraw = t("Withdraw_Application")
    withdrawal = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[opt_sanctions, withdraw])
    withdrawal.order.add_edge(opt_sanctions, withdraw)

    exit_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR, children=[prescreen_reject, withdrawal]
    )
    notify = t("Notify_Requester")

    route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[exit_choice, notify])
    route.order.add_edge(exit_choice, notify)
    return route


def build_revised_powl_M1() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Unified revised normative POWL M1:
    Register_Supplier -> XOR(Standard, Auto_Approve, Emergency, PreScreen_Exit) -> Close_Case.
    Every branch guarantees Notify_Requester precedes Close_Case and Sanctions_Check
    precedes Activate_Supplier.
    """
    register = t("Register_Supplier")
    close = t("Close_Case")

    route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            build_standard_route(),
            build_auto_approve_route(),
            build_emergency_route(),
            build_prescreen_exit_route(),
        ],
    )

    M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, close])
    M1.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
    M1.order.add_edge(route_choice, close)
    return M1


def wrap_route_for_case_check(route_powl: StrictPartialOrder) -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """Wrap a single authorized route between Register_Supplier and Close_Case."""
    register = t("Register_Supplier")
    close = t("Close_Case")
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_powl, close])
    model.order.add_edge(register, route_powl)
    model.order.add_edge(route_powl, close)
    return model


def select_authorized_powl(case_ctx: dict) -> tuple[str, StrictPartialOrder]:
    """
    Data-aware policy-overlay guard preventing unguarded XOR abuse (e.g., SUP-703/705/714).
    Returns (authorized_rule_id, authorized_sub_POWL) without reading external files.
    """
    ts = case_ctx["effective_timestamp_utc"]

    # Abbreviated pre-screen / withdrawal scope
    if case_ctx.get("case_type") == "INTAKE_PRESCREEN":
        return ("CS-9-PRESCREEN", wrap_route_for_case_check(build_prescreen_exit_route()))

    # FD-7 Emergency overlay (strict UTC window + critical relief + valid authority)
    fd7_start = datetime(2026, 7, 10, 0, 0, tzinfo=timezone.utc)
    fd7_end = datetime(2026, 7, 12, 23, 59, 59, tzinfo=timezone.utc)
    if (
        case_ctx.get("emergency_authority_id") == "FD-7"
        and case_ctx.get("is_critical_relief") is True
        and fd7_start <= ts <= fd7_end
    ):
        return ("FD-7", wrap_route_for_case_check(build_emergency_route()))

    # PD-3 Pilot overlay (strict UTC window + fail-closed eligibility snapshot)
    pd3_start = datetime(2026, 6, 1, 0, 0, tzinfo=timezone.utc)
    pd3_end = datetime(2026, 7, 31, 23, 59, 59, tzinfo=timezone.utc)
    if (
        case_ctx.get("pilot_authority_id") == "PD-3"
        and case_ctx.get("risk_tier") == "LOW"
        and case_ctx.get("country") == "DE"
        and case_ctx.get("approved_corporate_group") is True
        and pd3_start <= ts <= pd3_end
    ):
        return ("PD-3", wrap_route_for_case_check(build_auto_approve_route()))

    # Default fail-closed baseline: CS-9 Standard Route
    return ("CS-9-STD", wrap_route_for_case_check(build_standard_route()))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    M1 = build_revised_powl_M1()
    print("Constructed M1:", type(M1).__name__, "| Top-level nodes:", len(M1.nodes))
```

### D. Event and Data-Collection Proposal

To eliminate telemetry blind spots, mutable linkages, alias evasion, and unverified eligibility, the onboarding event log and case-header store must enforce an **append-only, schema-validated event contract**:

| Schema Area | Required Fields | Integrity & Governance Control | Problem Resolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Event Identity & Deduplication** | `event_id` (UUIDv7), `case_id`, `case_type` (`ONBOARDING`, `INTAKE_PRESCREEN`, `REACTIVATION`), `source_system`, `source_transaction_id`, `raw_activity_label`, `canonical_activity_label`, `ontology_version` | Unique composite constraint on `(source_system, source_transaction_id, canonical_activity_label)` enforces idempotent ingestion; versioned ontology maps UI renames (`QA_Clearance` -> `Quality_Review`) deterministically. | **SUP-711** (migration duplicates), **SUP-713** (label drift), **SUP-709/710** (scope confusion). |
| **Bi-Temporal Immutable Timestamps** | `effective_timestamp_utc` (when business action occurred), `recorded_timestamp_utc` (when written to immutable WORM log), `clock_sync_source` | Append-only WORM storage; backfills are logged as separate correction events with original `recorded_timestamp_utc` preserved (no silent overwrites). | **SUP-708** (ordering proof), **SUP-706** (post-hoc backfill detection), **SUP-705/714** (expiry enforcement). |
| **Policy & Rule Versioning** | `applied_policy_id`, `policy_version`, `powl_model_version`, `pilot_authority_id`, `emergency_authority_id`, `authority_approval_doc_hash` | Populated automatically by the Policy-Overlay Registry at decision time; hard-blocks execution if `effective_timestamp_utc > effective_to_utc` or `authority_approval_doc_hash` is absent. | **SUP-702**, **SUP-704**, **SUP-705**, **SUP-714**, and the 6 post-pilot August cases. |
| **Immutable Eligibility Snapshot** | `eligibility_snapshot_id`, `risk_tier`, `country_iso2`, `corporate_group_id`, `approved_corporate_group_flag`, `amdv_result_code`, `amdv_timestamp_utc`, `snapshot_hash` | Computed server-side from master-data/risk engines at `Register_Supplier` and re-verified before `Auto_Approve`; operator UI cannot override eligibility flags; null fields fail closed. | **SUP-703**, **SUP-702** (missing AMDV proof), 14 ineligible + 9 missing-attribute pilot cases. |
| **Notification Telemetry (CRM Integration)** | `notification_id` (immutable UUID foreign key generated by onboarding service), `crm_message_id`, `delivery_status`, `delivery_timestamp_utc`, `link_origin` (`SYSTEM_SYNC` vs. `MANUAL_RECON`) | Onboarding service issues `notification_id` prior to CRM dispatch and passes it as an immutable correlation key; CRM emits webhook/CDC event back to WORM log; mutable free-text case links are deprecated and flagged non-authoritative. | **SUP-706** and the 2% -> 29% post-CRM-migration missingness spike (N4). |
| **Actor Identity, Aliases, Delegation & CoI** | `actor_alias` (raw login), `canonical_person_id` (HR immutable ID), `actor_org_unit`, `manager_person_id`, `delegation_id`, `delegation_valid_from_to`, `coi_attestation_id`, `coi_conflict_flag` | Real-time IAM lookup resolves all aliases to `canonical_person_id`; hard ERP gate blocks `Manual_Procurement_Approval` if requester or QA reviewer shares `canonical_person_id`, direct reporting-line/household CoI flag without signed waiver, or invalid delegation. | **SUP-712** (alias SoD breach), **SUP-715** (undisclosed spousal/reporting-line CoI). |
| **Financial & Monitoring Telemetry** | `provisional_activation_flag`, `cumulative_spend_eur_pre_retro`, `daily_monitoring_run_id`, `retro_qa_due_utc`, `retro_approval_due_utc` | ERP links PO commitments to `provisional_activation_flag` and hard-caps spend at `spend_cap_eur` (EUR 20,000) until retrospective approval posts; automated scheduler alerts/suspends supplier if `retro_qa_due_utc` breaches. | **SUP-704**, **SUP-705** (unverified daily monitoring), and emergency tail-risk exposure. |
