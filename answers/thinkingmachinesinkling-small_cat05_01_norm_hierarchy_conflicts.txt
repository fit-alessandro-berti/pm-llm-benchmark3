1. **Authority / specificity**: More specific clauses govern within their scope (ECD-2 governs timing/supplier-onboarding for defined critical lots; CFO D1 governs Finance interpretation up to EUR 150k). Global integrity rules (A1, A3) prevail over local or incident overlays unless an A7 CRO exception exists.
2. **Time-bound application**: Rules are applied at the moment of the act (commitment). ECD-2 expired at 18:00 on 07-18; a commitment at 18:06 without contemporaneous evidence of pre-expiry action falls outside it.
3. **Emergency necessity**: Emergency excuses competitive tendering (A4) and, under SOP B4, PO sequence if actively delegated. It does not excuse Compliance clearance for new suppliers, segregation, or budget-owner approval.
4. **Evidence discipline**: During the ERP outage (07-14 08:30–13:00), timestamps may be delayed or accelerated by up to ~12 minutes. Contemporaneous telephone recordings, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, and access logs carry higher weight than a recovered ERP entry.
5. **Burden of proof**: The party claiming an exception (ECD-2, emergency, CFO interpretation) bears the burden to prove scope, timing, and that the waived control is explicitly covered.
6. **Post-hoc approval**: Per A6, later approvals mitigate but do not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 — Alternative PRIMARY: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**
- **Changed premise**: Interpret SOP B4 as self-executing: the 4-hour stoppage prediction satisfies the emergency definition, and because the Plant Manager was “reachable” but did not *act*, the Shift Lead’s commitment is implicitly authorized by the urgency of the incident, with delegation being a procedural formality that does not invalidate the emergency.
- **Why credible**: The stoppage was real; the amount is moderate (38k); A2 is not triggered; the emergency form was filed promptly.
- **Why weak**: SOP B4 explicitly requires *active delegation* for substitution; “reachable” undermines the substitution rationale; B5 protects Finance but is silent on Plant, yet the absence of pre-approval still deviates from B2.

**P-502 — Alternative PRIMARY: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**
- **Changed premise**: Accept that the organization’s prior practice (directives as temporary policy overlays) extends to Compliance for new suppliers during an active incident, and that CFO D1 implicitly covers both budget and Finance controls because Finance oversight encompasses vendor onboarding risk.
- **Why credible**: Joint IC authorization was robust; supplier evidence is contemporaneous; ECD-2 explicitly allows outside-register suppliers.
- **Why weak**: C5 explicitly excludes Compliance; D3 explicitly denies CFO authority over Compliance; A3 is triggered by “any new supplier,” not amount, and has no emergency carve-out.

**P-503 — Alternative PRIMARY: INDETERMINATE (or JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION under broad directive overlay)**
- **Changed premise**: Accept Incident Command’s labeling as dispositive for “critical sterilization-line lots” (C6); treat the two-authorizer requirement (C3) as satisfied even though one authorizer (PROC-3) is also the requester/committer, because the authorization is a collective incident decision, not a personal approval; treat missing Compliance and Finance approvals as deferred by the directive’s “precedence over ordinary steps.”
- **Why credible**: The contamination event was active; the pumps were needed for contingency; no CRO was available.
- **Why weak**: C5 is explicit silence; A1 is structural and violated by the same actor; A3 applies to >100k regardless; “critical contingency” is not “needed within next 24 hours” per Internal Audit; no A7 exception.

**P-505 — Alternative PRIMARY: NONCOMPLIANT**
- **Changed premise**: Reject the CFO email as operative for Finance approval; treat the ERP timestamp 18:06 (not corrected by outage note because direction is unknown) as conclusive that commitment occurred after ECD-2 expiry; conclude ECD-2 does not apply, so B1, B2, and A2 all require pre-commitment approvals, none of which occurred.
- **Why credible**: Outage note says “may have an ERP timestamp up to 12 minutes later”; it does not confirm the direction; supplier acknowledgment does not prove contractual commitment.
- **Why weak**: Call log at 17:57 and acknowledgment at 17:59:31 are strong contemporaneous evidence of supplier engagement near 18:00; treating 9 min as “fast” is reasonable; requiring perfect temporal certainty would over-weight a single database entry.

- **P-502**: Quarantine goods until Compliance_Clearance completed retroactively; document why new-supplier review was delayed; require 48-hour post-incident compliance review for all ECD-2 new suppliers.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Clause for immediate adoption (replacement / supplement)**

> **Emergency Continuity Directives and Structural Procurement Controls — Hierarchy, Scope, and Safeguards**
>
> (a) **Authority and form**: Only a written, scoped, time-limited exception issued by the Chief Risk Officer under GPIP-4 A7 may waive A1 (segregation of duties), A2 (pre-commitment budget-owner and Finance Controller approval), or A3 (Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers or purchases above EUR 100,000). An incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander (e.g., ECD-2) may override SOP B1 (PO sequence) and SOP B3 (approved-supplier register) for purchases within its defined scope and effective window, provided the directive explicitly states the functional line, the maximum time horizon (e.g., “required within 6 hours” or “needed within 24 hours”), and the monetary ceiling, if any. The directive expires automatically at the stated time and may be extended only by a CRO-signed, scoped, time-limited written extension.
>
> (b) **Scope definition**: “Critical sterilization-line lots” or similar language is defined by the combination of (i) the affected production line, (ii) a time-bound need (e.g., required within 8 hours to prevent stoppage), and (iii) confirmation by Operations and Quality that existing stock is insufficient to cover the defined horizon. Backup-line or contingency components not needed to prevent imminent stoppage within the defined horizon fall outside the directive unless specifically listed.
>
> (c) **Thresholds and controls — never waived by directive alone**:
> - **A1 (Segregation)**: No requester, supplier-committer, or Incident Command authorization member may serve as the final business approver for the same purchase. Incident Command authorization under ECD-2 C3 satisfies operational authorization for timing; it does not replace the final business approval required by GPIP-4 A1.
> - **A2 (Budget and Finance)**: For purchases  EUR 50,000, an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller must approve before commitment. The CFO may interpret Finance controls (D1/D2) during the directive window, but the CFO interpretation does not eliminate the budget-owner requirement or the need for contemporaneous documentation. If pre-commitment Finance/budget approval is genuinely impossible due to unavailability, it must be documented within 4 hours and ratified by the CRO within 24 hours; absent such ratification, the purchase is non-compliant.
> - **A3 (Compliance / new supplier)**: For any new supplier or any purchase > EUR 100,000, Compliance_Clearance must be completed before commitment. This control is **not** waived by ECD-2 (C5) and is **not** within CFO authority (D3). During an active directive, Compliance review must proceed in parallel; goods from new suppliers must be quarantined until clearance is documented.
>
> (d) **Evidence and retrospective recording**: During any ERP or document-system outage, the time of commitment is established by the earliest contemporaneous non-system evidence (supplier acknowledgment, telephone recording, signed authorization PDF, access log). A recovered database entry made after recovery is not conclusive proof of the true commitment time. All Incident Command authorizations must be recorded in a non-system or backup record within 4 hours and reconciled to the ERP within 24 hours.
>
> (e) **Emergency normalization safeguard**: Any purchase made under emergency or incident-directive authority that (i) exceeds the directive’s defined scope or monetary ceiling, (ii) occurs after expiry without a CRO-signed extension, (iii) uses a new supplier without Compliance_Clearance, or (iv) lacks segregation of duties, must be reported to Internal Audit within 24 hours of discovery. Deliverables must be held in quarantine until full compliance is documented, and the authorization chain must be reviewed by the CRO within 5 business days. Repeated use of emergency authority for the same supplier, same category, or same line within 30 days triggers automatic escalation to the board audit committee.
>
> (f) **Hierarchy statement for future directives**: Where this clause and a directive conflict, this clause governs for structural integrity controls (A1, A2, A3, A7); the directive governs for timing (PO sequence) and supplier onboarding only, and only within its explicit scope and time window.

This clause resolves authority (CRO for exceptions; COO/IC for timing/supplier only), scope (defined by function + time + need), thresholds (50k/100k preserved), segregation (always required), new suppliers (always cleared), retrospective records (contemporaneous > ERP), expiry (automatic; CRO extension only), and normalization safeguard (audit + quarantine + escalation).
