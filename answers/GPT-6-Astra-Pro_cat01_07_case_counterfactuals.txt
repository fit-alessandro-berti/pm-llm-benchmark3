## 1. Feasibility model

**Observation boundary and time metric.** I measure cycle time from the start of `Register` to completion of `Notify`. The factual durations are **58 h 30 min for K-104, 48 h for K-219, 214 h 10 min for K-337, and 29 h 30 min for K-411**. For K-337, the second Monday is April 13, and notification occurs on Wednesday, April 15, 2026. Define \(\Delta T=T_{\mathrm{counterfactual}}-T_{\mathrm{factual}}\), so negative values indicate faster completion. These are elapsed hours, not staffed working hours.    

In the relations below, \(x\prec y\) means that \(x\) must complete before \(y\) starts; equal boundary timestamps are permitted. Parallel branches need not start simultaneously. The supplied rules require an evidence-sensitive join before assessment and renewed review after relevant rework—not one universal sequence. 

```text
Symbols:
R = Register; T = Triage; I = Identity Check
O_j = Originals Receipt relevant to document version j
D_j = Document Check j; A_j = Assessment j
C_j = Compliance Review, or explicit reaffirmation, for assessment version j
Q_j = Request More Info j, including its external-response interval
E = Escalation Advice; P = Preliminary Compliance Work
Dec = Decision; N = Notify

Partial-order relations:
R ≺ T
T ≺ I
T ≺ D_1
O_1 ≺ D_1                                      [originals required]

I ≺ A_j and D_j ≺ A_j                          [every assessment version j]

A_j ≺ Q_j                                     [assessment requests information]
or A_j ≺ C_j ≺ Q_j                            [compliance requests information]
Q_j ≺ D_(j+1) ≺ A_(j+1)
O_(j+1) ≺ D_(j+1)                             [replacement originals required]

A_final ≺ C_final ≺ Dec                       [high risk OR high value]
A_final ≺ Dec                                 [otherwise]
Dec ≺ N

K-104 preparation branch:
T ≺ E ≺ P
P ≺ C_final                                  [when final review reuses preparation]
No required ordering of E/P against I or D.
A_final ≺ C_final remains mandatory.

K-411 verification branches:
T ≺ I and T ≺ D_1
No I ≺ D_1 edge; A_1 waits for BOTH completions.
```

```text
Conditional and declarative constraints:
- Register is first; Triage precedes internal verification work.
- Assessments consume identified document/evidence versions.
- A formal information request invalidates the old path to Decision:
  a new Document Check and Assessment are required.
- High-risk/high-value decisions require compliance after the latest
  relevant Assessment. Earlier screening alone cannot satisfy this.
- Final assessor != final compliance reviewer.
- C1 performs final certified compliance; S1 remains advisory.
- Resource service intervals [start, finish) are mutually exclusive,
  including preliminary C1 work, Decision, and Notify.
- A reservation is a scheduling commitment, not completed service.
  It cannot authorize final compliance before assessment readiness.
- External-response waiting is not continuous assessor/reviewer service.
- Queue snapshots are uncertain forecasts, not guaranteed appointment times.
- K-337 forbids a second information-request loop after Assessment 2,
  but does not waive compliance, four-eyes, or evidence accountability.
- K-411's workaround must preserve evidence consistency, task ownership,
  and the assessment join; timestamps must represent actual execution.
```

**Scheduling model.** For each activity, readiness is the latest prerequisite completion. Its actual start is the next available resource slot under the applicable calendar, bookings, and dispatch policy. In particular,

$$
r(A_j)=\max\{f(I),f(D_j)\}.
$$

Earlier readiness can therefore produce an earlier start, longer queue waiting, or no change in final notification. The service ranges and queue snapshots constrain plausible scenarios but do not identify a unique scheduling function. 

**A material data inconsistency.** K-219 occupies D1 on Tuesday 09:30–13:30, while K-337 occupies D1 on Tuesday 08:30–14:30. These cannot both be literal service intervals for one non-overlapping resource.   Accordingly, the constructions below are conditional focal schedules, not certification of the entire factual resource ledger. One *hypothetical* joint repair for the other three interventions would move K-219’s D1 work to Tuesday 14:30–18:30, still before its 20:00 assessment. That is an additional background reconciliation under A3—not an observation or an intervention benefit. K-219’s D2 intervention itself removes the documented D1 collision.

**Intervention classification.** K-104 primarily changes timing and reservation priority, but also introduces preparatory work; it is not literally effort-free rescheduling. K-219 changes resource routing, with a possible consequential rework branch. K-337 changes both the allowed decision policy and the available loop structure. K-411 removes an implementation-level serialization edge while preserving the normative activity set and decision policy.

**Uncertainty convention.** The numerical ranges below are envelopes of explicitly constructed scenarios, not confidence intervals, probability-weighted expectations, or hard bounds. “Base” means a plausible illustrative schedule. “Worst” means the illustrated adverse scenario; longer delays remain possible. Assumptions A1–A8 are audited in Section 8.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage notices sanctions ambiguity on Monday, but S1 is not engaged until Tuesday 19:20, after assessment. The final assessment finishes Tuesday 19:10; C1 starts Wednesday 13:10, an 18-hour gap, and spends 4.5 hours reviewing. Notification follows at Wednesday 18:40. These observations support investigating earlier preparation, but they do not establish that earlier escalation would resolve the ambiguity or guarantee a C1 slot. 

**Minimal intervention:** Keep identity, D1 document verification, and A1 assessment unchanged. Move the observed two-hour S1 advice interval to Monday 09:00–11:00. At triage, reserve against *forecast* assessment readiness, not knowledge of the future factual completion time. In the base scenario, C1 performs one hour of preliminary work on Tuesday 10:30–11:30; assume that this preparation and earlier advice make two hours of later reading reusable. Sending a packet does not constitute certified clearance.

**Temporal propagation:** Documents still finish Tuesday 05:40 and assessment still finishes Tuesday 19:10. The base provisional C1 slot is Wednesday 08:00–10:30: 2.5 hours of remaining final work after preparation, rather than another full 4.5-hour review. C1 therefore finishes **7 h 10 min earlier** than factually. An available M1 slot Wednesday 12:00–12:30, followed by notification until 12:40, produces a **52.5-hour cycle: six hours faster**. The gain is smaller than the earlier compliance completion because the new gap to M1 is longer. All proposed slots are conditional on exclusive availability.

**Process-feasibility check:** S1 remains advisory. C1’s preliminary work is real resource demand, separate from final certification, and the final review begins after A1’s latest assessment. A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes. Moving S1 advice avoids counting both an early and the factual late advice interval unless circumstances actually require repetition. In this schedule, S1 advice overlaps verification; C1 preparation occurs later, while the case awaits assessment. The intervention permits preliminary concurrency but does not require C1 to be available during the document check itself.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best illustrated: final C1 work Tuesday 19:30–22:00 and M1 signing Wednesday 09:00–09:30, with notification at 09:40, yield **49.5 hours, ΔT = −9 h**. This M1 slot avoids K-219’s observed Wednesday 08:30–09:00 service. Base is **52.5 hours, ΔT = −6 h**. A neutral branch retains Wednesday’s original signing slot despite earlier review completion. Adverse: an incomplete packet provides no saving and the provisional booking is lost; final C1 work Thursday 08:00–12:30, signing 13:00–13:30, and notification at 13:40 yield **77.5 hours, ΔT = +19 h**. That adverse slot is a conditional scenario, not a forecast inferred from the snapshots.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Reservation histories could show that earlier bookings never survive urgent overrides, or manager calendars could show that no earlier signature is possible. Evidence-version records could reveal that preliminary reading is unusable after assessment, making total C1 effort increase. Conversely, demonstrated reuse, reliable readiness forecasts, and backfilling of released slots would strengthen the intervention. Harm to urgent non-target cases could overturn a favorable local result.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Identity finishes Monday 16:20, but originals do not arrive until 17:30. D1 starts Tuesday 09:30, completes at 13:30, and produces an accurate result without rework. Assessment begins 6.5 hours later and finishes Tuesday 22:30; notification occurs Wednesday 09:00. The document-resource intervention cannot move the original arrival or infer that difficult handwriting is harmless for D2. 

**Minimal intervention:** Assign the document task to D2 at Monday 17:30, preserving the receipt and identity timestamps. “As soon as originals arrive” means immediate release/assignment, not permission to start while D2 is busy. Use a base D2 interval Monday 22:30–Tuesday 04:30: five hours of waiting and six hours of service. Do not also improve supplier responsiveness, assessor speed, or document quality.

**Temporal propagation:** The six-hour D2 service is two hours longer than factual D1 service, but its earlier start makes document completion **nine hours earlier**. With identity already complete, assessment readiness also advances nine hours. An illustrative A2 slot Tuesday 11:00–13:30 preserves the factual 6.5-hour assessment wait. An M1 opportunity Tuesday 16:00–16:20 and notification until 16:30 produce a **31.5-hour cycle, ΔT = −16.5 h**. Notification improves more than document readiness because the case catches an earlier signing opportunity instead of waiting overnight. Without that opportunity, much of the advantage disappears.

**Process-feasibility check:** D2 starts after receipt; assessment follows both checks. The case is neither high-risk nor high-value, so the supplied rules do not require compliance. If D2 flags handwriting concerns that require formal information collection, model an assessment-issued `Request More Info`, followed by a new document check and assessment—not a direct jump from clarification to decision. If replacement originals are required, add their receipt before the new check; the illustrated rework scenario assumes clarification can be accepted without another physical-original dependency.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best illustrated: D2 Monday 19:30–23:30; A2 Tuesday 08:00–10:30; M1 11:00–11:20; notification at 11:30. This gives **26.5 hours, ΔT = −21.5 h**. Base is **31.5 hours, ΔT = −16.5 h**. Adverse: D2 runs Monday 22:30–Tuesday 06:30; assessment Tuesday 13:00–15:30 requests clarification; a 36-hour response arrives Thursday 03:30; D2 rechecks Thursday 08:00–14:00; A2 reassesses 18:00–20:30; M1 signs Friday 08:30–08:50 and notifies by 09:00. This gives **96 hours, ΔT = +48 h**. Longer external delays are not excluded.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The decisive evidence is D2’s handwriting-specific clarification and error rates, actual availability after receipt, and whether earlier readiness changes M1 access. As a deliberately simplified sensitivity calculation, mixing only the base branch and the illustrated rework branch gives \(E[\Delta T]=-16.5+64.5p\), where \(p\) is the rework-branch probability. The timing benefit changes sign at approximately **25.6%**. No such probability is supplied, and quality losses could make reassignment unattractive even below that threshold.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Assessment 2 finishes Friday, April 10, at 10:30 with an unresolved inconsistency. The second supplier response arrives Monday, April 13, at 09:00: **70.5 hours later**, already demonstrating why the rough 8–36-hour response guidance is not a hard upper bound. Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 add six service hours plus queue delays. Final assessment readiness is Tuesday, April 14, at 10:30; notification is Wednesday, April 15, at 09:10. 

**Minimal intervention:** Preserve the complete prefix through Assessment 2, including the first information request and second document check. Remove only the second information request and its consequential Document Check 3 and Assessment 3. Replace “obtain another confirmation before deciding” with “make an accountable decision using existing evidence.” This changes decision policy; it does not establish that the discrepancy has disappeared. The intervention expressly permits conditional approval or rejection. 

**Temporal propagation:** Compliance eligibility moves from Tuesday, April 14, at 10:30 to Friday, April 10, at 10:30—**96 hours earlier**. In the base schedule, C1 reviews Friday 13:00–17:00, but no earlier M1 opportunity is assumed before Monday 08:30–09:00; notification finishes Monday at 09:10. The resulting cycle is **166 h 10 min, ΔT = −48 h**. Compliance completion advances 98.5 hours, but the new Friday-to-Monday signing wait is 50.5 hours longer than the factual overnight signing wait. That absorbs much of the potential gain. Simply subtracting the external wait and removed service durations would miss this propagation.

**Process-feasibility check:** C1 reviews after Assessment 2, using its identified evidence version, and a different person performs assessment and compliance. An adverse compliance conclusion can support rejection; “review completed” does not mean “approval recommended.” Conditional approval is feasible only where the unresolved matter can legitimately remain conditional under the stated decision policy. It must not defer a mandatory approval prerequisite or conceal the prohibited loop after notification. No new assessment is needed merely because another loop is disallowed; none of its triggering new evidence has arrived.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best illustrated: C1 Friday 11:00–15:00; M1 15:30–16:00; notification at 16:10. This yields **101 h 10 min, ΔT = −113 h**. Base is Monday 09:10 notification, **ΔT = −48 h**. Worst illustrated for timing: no earlier C1 or M1 slot is available, so the original Tuesday review and Wednesday notification remain, **ΔT = 0**. A difficult adverse review could take longer still. Across all branches, rejection or conditional approval may replace the factual approval; the decision-quality downside is not bounded by the cycle-time range.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Evidence that the discrepancy concerns an indispensable approval requirement would rule out conditional approval, though not necessarily rejection. Appeal, reversal, financial-loss, and erroneous-rejection data could show that shorter closure is a poor operational trade. Calendar evidence could eliminate the assumed Friday review or Monday signature. Conversely, documented materiality criteria and comparable cases defensibly decided on existing evidence would support a narrowly governed version of the policy.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** Triage creates only the identity task. Identity finishes Tuesday 13:20, after which the document task becomes ownable; D2 works 15:00–19:30. Assessment then waits until Wednesday 09:30, a 14-hour gap after verification readiness. Thus the observed serialization is an implementation constraint, while a substantial downstream queue remains even after verification. 

**Minimal intervention:** At Tuesday 09:35, create both tasks and allow controlled independent ownership. Keep I2’s factual 11:30–13:20 interval and D2’s 4.5-hour service requirement. In the base scenario, D2 obtains a 12:00–16:30 slot. The workaround changes eligibility, not resource capacity or required work. Capture actual service timestamps and evidence versions rather than reconstructing apparent overlap from later data entry.

**Temporal propagation:** I2 and D2 genuinely overlap from 12:00 to 13:20: **80 minutes**. The verification join moves from Tuesday 19:30 to 16:30, three hours earlier. Suppose A2 can start Wednesday 08:00 rather than 09:30 and completes at 10:30. The assessment wait actually increases from 14 to 15.5 hours, absorbing half the readiness gain. With the same two-hour wait to M1, signing occurs 12:30–12:50 and notification completes at 13:00. Cycle time is **28 hours, ΔT = −1.5 h**.

**Process-feasibility check:** No originals are required, so concurrent verification is normatively allowed. Both checks finish before assessment, and no mandatory compliance step is omitted. Independent ownership must not permit inconsistent evidence snapshots or conflicting writes to shared decision-relevant data. A2’s proposed Wednesday 08:00–10:30 interval does not overlap the other supplied A2 intervals. Resource exclusivity still requires checking unobserved bookings. Earlier task creation alone is not proof that services overlapped.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best illustrated: D2 Tuesday 11:30–16:00; A2 17:00–19:30; M1 Wednesday 08:00–08:20; notification at 08:30. This fits before K-219’s observed A2 Tuesday 20:00 service and M1 Wednesday 08:30 service, giving **23.5 hours, ΔT = −6 h**. Base gives **ΔT = −1.5 h**. Worst illustrated: keep the base earlier checks but retain factual assessment and signing slots; notification remains Wednesday 14:30, **ΔT = 0**. Queue reordering or operational failures can produce additional delay beyond these illustrative schedules.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Evidence that the lock protects essential consistency would make the proposed workaround infeasible without redesign. Actual service records could show D2 was unavailable until 15:00 anyway. Assessment and signing calendars could show all earlier readiness is absorbed downstream. Also distinguish measurement effects: base document waiting is 2 h 25 min from earlier eligibility, versus factual 1 h 40 min. That larger measured wait is compatible with a faster case and is not, by itself, evidence of worsened capacity.

Treat 100 similar cases as a separate weekly scenario for each policy, not as four simultaneously implementable schedules. Arrivals are not completions. At the illustrative service times, 100 K-219 cases remove 400 D1-hours but require 600 D2-hours before rework; 100 K-104 cases require 350 C1-hours, assuming one preparation hour and 2.5 final-review hours each. A single person has at most 168 elapsed hours per week, even before other work or time off. Thus these particular workload scenarios cannot clear within one week using the named resources. Actual staffed hours and starting backlog are needed for any sustainable-volume claim.

Compliance reservations allocate scarce capacity; they do not create it. With preparation effort \(p\) and reusable saving \(s\), net C1 effort changes by \(p-s\) per case. Incomplete packets can make \(s\) zero, while held-but-unusable slots waste capacity unless backfilled. Provisional reservations should expire or release on readiness failure, permit urgent override, and be evaluated against delays to all C1 customers. S1 advice must not become unauthorized final certification.

D1-to-D2 substitution can release specialist capacity for complex documents while delaying standard digital cases, including K-411-like work. Clarification creates additional document and assessment demand. Conversely, suppressing K-337’s final loop removes 350 D1-hours and 250 A1-hours per 100 analogous histories, but retains final compliance and may create appeals or losses outside the measured process.

Parallel verification advances assessment arrivals without reducing assessment service. Bottlenecks can therefore migrate from case locks or documents to A2, C1, or M1; earlier work may simply wait downstream. Simultaneous adoption makes D2 substitution and early digital checks direct competitors.

Weekend effects are discontinuous: avoiding Friday-to-Monday supplier waiting can still leave Friday approvals awaiting Monday signatures. A weekly cohort also carries unfinished work into later weeks. Measure creation, eligibility, assignment, actual start/finish, evidence version, and recording time separately. Earlier task creation can lengthen measured queue waiting even when cases improve; batch-entered timestamps cannot demonstrate genuine overlap. Evaluate whole-system completions, open work, service hours, decision quality, and non-target waiting—not 100 times one case’s time saving.

## 7. Final synthesis

Controlled parallel verification in K-411 appears most robust, provided the case lock is merely an avoidable ownership restriction rather than an essential data-integrity safeguard. It retains both checks, their resources, the assessment join, and the decision policy.
