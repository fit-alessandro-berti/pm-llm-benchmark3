    time = np.asarray(time, float); event = np.asarray(event, int)
    et = np.unique(time[event==1])

    d = np.array([((time==t)&(event==1)).sum() for t in et])

def km(time, event, horizon=H):
    time=np.asarray(time,float); event=np.asarray(event,int)
    et=np.unique(time[event==1]); et=et[et<=horizon]
    S=1.0; G=0.0; grid=[]; vals=[]

        S*= (1-d/n); grid.append(t); vals.append(S)
        if n>d: G += d/(n*(n-d))

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**[OBS] The naive numbers.** Resolved cases only: n = 13 (A 6, B 7). Breach rate = 4/13 = **30.8%**; A 2/6 = 33.3%, B 2/7 = 28.6%; mean duration A **54.7 h**, B **45.6 h**  "B is 9.1 h faster and breaches less".

Mechanisms, with case IDs:

1. **Survivor / length-biased selection.** The resolved-only view discards 19/32 = 59% of cases, and specifically discards the worst tails: **S26** (open 104 h, idle 72 h), **S11** (P1, open 81 h, idle 55 h), **S25** (P1, open 79 h, idle 48 h), **S09** (open 92 h, idle 31 h). 2. **Competing risks treated as censoring.** S06 (withdraw 27), S12 (withdraw 15), S05/S23 (reject 9/11), S22/S30 (withdraw 44/70), S08/S21 (transfer 68/26) are *absorbing states*, not censoring. **[EST]** A resolve-only KM that censors them gives on-time resolution **0.361** vs the correct Aalen–Johansen **0.304** — a **+5.6 pp (18% relative)** overstatement, because censoring at withdrawal implicitly assumes those cases would have resolved at the same rate as live cases.
3. **Conditioning on a collider.** "Resolved" is itself an outcome of the competing-event process. Restricting to it conditions on a post-baseline variable affected by both cohort and severity; any cohort contrast inside that stratum is not a causal or even a clean descriptive contrast.
4. **Informative censoring.** Censoring here is *stall-correlated*: all four open cases above 72 h have gaps of 31–72 h, and among the pre-72 open cases **S10** (escalated at 28 h, idle 19 h at age 47) looks stalled while **S31** (screened at 20 h, idle 3 h at age 23) looks actively progressing. Independent censoring treats them as exchangeable with their risk-set peers. **[HYP]** This makes KM/AJ *optimistic* about resolution and *pessimistic-free* about breach; §6 quantifies.
5. **Cohort imbalance and confounded rollout.** **[OBS]** Priority mix A = 3/7/5 (P1/P2/P3), B = 5/7/5. B carries more P1, and P1 has the worst E2. Worse: **all four B cases without `AutoCheck` (S20, S23, S26, S30) are P3**, while all 12 B P1/P2 cases have it.
