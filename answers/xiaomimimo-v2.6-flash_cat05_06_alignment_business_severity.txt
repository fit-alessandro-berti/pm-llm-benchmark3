## 1. Materiality Framework

**What the raw number is.** `raw_cost` is symmetric unit-cost edit distance between the trace and the model. It answers only: *"how many log/model moves must be inserted/deleted to make this trace fit the tree?"* It contains no amount, no risk rating, no bank-change condition, no timing, no irreversibility, no evidence-quality term, and it inherits whatever the model's scope happens to be. Everything below is therefore a separate judgment layered on top of the alignment semantics.

**Severity dimensions (each case is scored on all eight):**

| Dimension | Question | Weighting logic |
|---|---|---|
| Control objective | Which policy objective is touched — authorization (P1/P2), fraud prevention, payment integrity (P3), customer/regulatory communication (P4)? | Breach of a *preventive* control that guards an irreversible act outranks a breach of a *documentary* control. |
| Amount / exposure | EUR at risk or already disbursed? | Scales exposure but does not dominate: a EUR 4,500 bank-change auto-approval can outrank a EUR 62,000 label mismatch. |
| Reversibility | Can the money/decision be recovered? | Settled payment  irreversible  severity amplifier; a corrected label is fully reversible  severity damper. |
| Timing | How close to the irreversible act was the failure? | Bank change 1h before payment, payment 17 min before approval  acute; assessment logged late but before approval  mitigated. |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Would an ombudsman, regulator, or claimant be harmed? | Missing/ unprovable notification (P4) and wrongful payment to a changed beneficiary are harm-bearing; telemetry is not. |
| Recurrence | One-off or systematic? | A pattern's population severity can exceed any single instance (e.g., 74 retries/month vs. one 480k fraud-screen gap). |
| Evidence reliability | Immutable vs. mutable, in-core vs. external, stable identifiers? | Weak evidence raises severity of *demonstrability* (we may be unable to prove compliance) and lowers confidence in the narrative. |
| Model scope | Is the model authoritative for this path? | Under a disputed scope (P6), distance from the model carries weak evidential weight — the model may simply be wrong. |

**Severity anchors**

- **5 – Critical:** control breached and payment irreversible on high exposure/high risk (P2/P3 breach): CL-602, CL-604.
- **4 – Major:** policy objective defeated with meaningful or acute-fraud exposure, including *silent* violations: CL-609, CL-610.
- **3 – Significant:** substantive control apparently met but evidence incomplete, mutable, or off-system: CL-606, CL-608.
- **2 – Moderate:** representational defect (label, missing branch, scope dispute) with no substantive control failure: CL-607, CL-611, CL-612.
- **1 – Minor:** technical noise with no control relevance: CL-601, CL-603, CL-605.

**Confidence  severity.** Severity is the *consequence if our reading is right*; confidence is *the probability that our reading is right*, driven by corroboration, immutability, and scope agreement. They are orthogonal and are used differently: **severity drives priority; confidence drives the action type** (high confidence  remediate/contain; low confidence  investigate before acting). Confident-but-low-severity items (CL-601, CL-603) are pipeline fixes; high-severity-but-medium-confidence items (CL-602, CL-608) require evidence reconstruction *before* conclusions are drawn. Note also that confidence about a *deviation's presence in the evidence* is not confidence that the *activity did or did not occur*: `M(Assess Loss)` proves the core log lacks the event, not that the assessment never happened.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

*A missing event proves only that the event is absent from this log; it is not proof that the activity did not occur. Each block below therefore states competing hypotheses and the evidence that separates them.*

**H1 — CL-602: no Fraud Screen on a EUR 480k bank-change claim**
- **H1a** Fraud screening was genuinely skipped (control bypass). **H1b** It was performed verbally/off-system and never recorded (evidence failure only). **H1c** It was performed in a separate fraud system whose events were never merged into this log.
- **Discriminating evidence:** (i) query the fraud case-management system for a reference tied to this claim/beneficiary; (ii) check whether fraud-system events are merged into the conformance log for *other* cases on the same day — presence elsewhere favours H1c, never-merged favours H1b/H1a; (iii) contemporaneous approver notes/CRM entries timestamped *before* 10:03-equivalent payment time; (iv) beneficiary bank-change record cross-checked against any auto-generated fraud alert; (v) structured interview. Under P2 the burden of proof sits with the control owner: absent an artifact, severity remains 5 regardless of which hypothesis wins.

**H2 — CL-604: payment 10:03 vs approval 10:20**
- **H2a** Genuine sequence breach (released before authorization). **H2b** Clock skew between bank timestamp source and workflow timestamp source. **H2c** Approval was performed before payment but recorded later (logging latency), or the approval record was backdated after payment.
- **Discriminating evidence:** NTP/clock-sync audit across both systems; approval audit trail showing record *creation*, *edit*, and maker-checker completion times; raw bank file/statement timestamps; payment-initiation authority log. Approval-record creation time post-dating the bank acceptance with no earlier completion entry  H2a/H2c; a consistent same-direction skew across all cases that day  H2b. H2c (backdating) is the highest-consequence branch and must be excluded explicitly.

**H3 — CL-606: no core Assess Loss, adjuster report signed 10:05**
- **H3a** Assessment performed externally, core never updated (capture gap). **H3b** Report exists but the assessment was not performed to procedure (paper-first/retrospective signature). **H3c** The activity was logged under another label in that period.
- **Discriminating evidence:** immutable signature metadata (timestamp before Fraud Screen/Manual Approve), document-to-case linkage integrity; search the same window for `Assess Damage` or other assessment labels (H3c overlaps Pattern C); adjuster upload/interface logs; manual reconciliation of the **two cohort cases lacking a stable identifier** — these are where H3b is most plausible. A contemporaneous, immutably-signed report preceding fraud/approval strongly favours H3a.

**H4 — CL-608: CRM portal message with a mutable link**
- **H4a** Portal message genuinely delivered; only the cross-reference field is weak. **H4b** Message record created but delivery to the claimant unproven (draft/placeholder). **H4c** Notification was sent from core but core logging failed.
- **Discriminating evidence:** portal outbox/delivery receipts with immutable timestamps; CRM field-edit history (was the link mutated after creation?); gateway/SMS/email logs; claimant read receipt or secure-message acknowledgment; cross-check with cases where core and CRM both log (to quantify how often core logging alone fails, H4c). A delivery receipt inside 24h closes P4 substantively; no receipt escalates severity to 4.

**H5 — CL-609 / CL-610 and the 4-case cohort: cost-0 policy violations**
- **H5a** P1/P2 data conditions were never encoded in the workflow (policy exists only on paper). **H5b** Conditions encoded but bypassed via override or parameter error. **H5c** Conditions encoded and evaluated, but the risk score or bank-change flag was stale/NULL at approval time (data latency) or written afterwards.
- **Discriminating evidence:** workflow rule configuration with effective-dating; replay of the four cases against rule versions *as they stood at the approval timestamp*, using flag values as-of that timestamp; flag-write timestamps versus approval timestamp; override/authorization records; change log of the rules repository. No rule version ever contained the amount condition  H5a; rule present but flag NULL/stale  H5c; rule present and flag correct yet auto path taken  H5b (deliberate bypass, escalate to audit). H5a is the most likely given that P1/P2 live outside the tree; H5c is the most operationally dangerous because it makes *future* enforcement fail-open.

**H6 — CL-611: `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`**
- **H6a** Pure label drift, semantically identical activity. **H6b** Two genuinely distinct activities (damage assessment vs loss assessment) that were conflated by the mapping table.
- **Discriminating evidence:** core activity definitions versus the signed procedure and UI screenshots; field-level content comparison (inputs, actor, output, duration); whether both labels ever co-occur within one case; mapping-table change record (who/when/why). Never co-occurring + identical attributes  H6a (remediate by mapping); co-occurring or differing attributes  H6b (remediate by model/procedure change, not mapping).

**H7 — CL-607 / CL-612: cost-4 scope cases**
- **H7a** Model intentionally scoped to payable claims; logs should be filtered before conformance. **H7b** The model should include Deny/Withdraw branches (model debt). **H7c** Deny/Withdraw events are captured inconsistently across channels, so even a scoped model would misfire.
- **Discriminating evidence:** signed scope statement/RACI for M0 versus intake; governance decision records on P6; consistency of Deny/Withdraw logging across core, portal, and call-center channels; proportion of non-payable cases over a full month. The decision right determines the fix: H7a  scope filter; H7b  model repair; H7c  event-capture harmonization first.

**H8 — CL-603 / the 74-case retry cluster**
- **H8a** One logical notification, transport retry re-emitted the event (as stated). **H8b** Two distinct send authorizations that happened to reuse a message ID.
- **Discriminating evidence:** connector retry metrics and attempt counters for those message IDs; identity of payload, recipient, and channel; claimant-side delivery logs showing one vs two received messages. Same ID + retry counter >1 + single delivery  H8a confirmed, remediate by de-duplication. Any H8b instance would be a customer-communication defect, not noise.

**H9 — CL-601 / telemetry**
- **H9a** UI lifecycle intentionally logged to the same stream. **H9b** Logger scope accidentally broadened by a configuration change. **H9c** Telemetry is a proxy for real manual work the model ignores (granularity decision).
- **Discriminating evidence:** logging configuration history and diff; whether downstream consumers already filter these events; sampling to confirm no control-relevant activity hides behind them (P5 explicitly allows telemetry to matter *if it reveals a meaningful control issue* — e.g., abnormally long workbench sessions on high-value claims). If such correlation exists, filtering must be conditional, not blanket.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

`raw_cost` is a *distance metric between a trace and a model*, not a risk measure. Formally, `cost =  unit_cost(move)` over an optimal edit script. Every term that business risk depends on — amount, risk rating, bank-change recency, timestamp order relative to authority, reversibility, evidence mutability, customer harm — is absent from the objective function. The supplied configuration makes this concrete: **uniform cost 1 for every visible log/model move, no business-specific weighting**, so a UI click, a transport retry, a skipped fraud screen, and an unlogged EUR 75k assessment are all priced identically. Below, each failure mode is tied to supplied cases.

1. **Cost-function design (uniform, symmetric, context-free).** CL-601 scores 3 for three UI events; CL-602 scores 1 for a missing fraud screen on EUR 480,000 with HIGH risk and a bank change one day before payment. Under the supplied cost function these are ranked 3 : 1 against the far more consequential case. Symmetry compounds this: the function charges the same unit whether the missing move concerns telemetry or a mandated preventive control. Cost measures *how much editing*, never *how much harm*.

2. **Data conditions are invisible (zero-cost policy failures).** CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous — `raw_cost = 0` — yet CL-609 breaches the P1 amount cap (EUR 18,000 > EUR 5,000) and CL-610 breaches P1 *and* P2 (bank changed one hour before an auto-approval). The process tree grants Auto Approve unconditionally; P1/P2 live only in the overlay. Because the tree is the yardstick, a model weaker than policy produces *perfect* scores for *imperfect* controls. A conformance system that cannot see the amount field cannot see this class of risk at all — and the additional evidence shows this is not hypothetical: **four cases in the month are fully synchronous despite violating P1 or P2**.

3. **Scope determines what counts as a deviation.** CL-607 (denied at pre-screen) and CL-612 (claimant withdrew) both score 4 — joint maximum in the sample — although neither involved an approval, a payment, or a policy breach. The cost is paid because the model expects a happy path that *should not* continue: `M(Pay Claim)` literally penalizes the log for *not paying a denied claim*. The sign of the deviation is inverted relative to business meaning, and P6 leaves the scope itself disputed. Cost here measures the model owner's opinion, not exposure.

4. **Hidden (silent) moves are free by construction.** CL-605 shows `T(tau, c=0)`: the net consumes a routing step with no log counterpart and no cost. This is why zero cost is *necessary but not sufficient* evidence of compliance —  can absorb genuine anomalies elsewhere at zero price. Conversely,  carries no exculpatory business meaning: CL-605's compliance comes from the *context* (EUR 800, LOW, no bank change), not from the alignment.

5. **Activity granularity and duplicated telemetry price noise as deviation.** CL-601's three telemetry moves and CL-603's one duplicate notification are sub-business-granular artifacts: the same logical fact (a claim processed; a claimant notified once) is represented two or three times in the stream. Across the month, **74 cases** show duplicate `Notify Claimant` events from a single retrying connector — one engineering defect, 74 "deviations." Cost counts events, not incidents, so population-level cost statistics are systematically biased toward logging chattiness.

6. **Missing-but-external evidence is priced identically to a missing control.** CL-606's `M(Assess Loss)` (cost 1) coexists with a signed external adjuster report; CL-602's `M(Fraud Screen)` (cost 1) coexists with no artifact anywhere. The metric is identical; the evidentiary situations are opposite — one is largely remediated by external proof, the other is an open control breach. Cost cannot read a document, assess its mutability, or check whether an identifier is stable (2 of the 9 external-adjuster cases lack one).

7. **Irreversibility and timing are absent from the objective.** CL-604's deviation is *ordering*: payment at 10:03, approval at 10:20. Edit distance prices the reordering at 1–3 units regardless of whether the sum is EUR 90,000 and regardless of the fact that a settled payment cannot be recalled. P3 designates this a "severe control failure" *even if the model alignment can repair it cheaply* — the policy statement itself concedes that cheap alignment  safe control. Alignment cost is symmetric in a world where business consequences are profoundly asymmetric.

8. **Empirical disconfirmation within this single set.** Reading the supplied table as a ranking exercise fails on its own data: the `raw_cost  4` group (CL-607, CL-612) is the *least* risky group; the `raw_cost = 0` group (CL-605 clean, but CL-609 and CL-610 violating policy) contains two live violations; `raw_cost = 1` spans a harmless retry (CL-603), a largely remediated evidence gap (CL-606), a fragile notification proof on EUR 250k (CL-608), and a EUR 480k fraud-screen breach (CL-602). There is no monotone mapping from cost to severity anywhere in this sample.

**What cost *is* good for.** Cost is a legitimate *triage input after decomposition*: it tells you where the trace and the model disagree, and the *move types* (L / M / S / ) tell you whether that disagreement is noise, missing evidence, model debt, or real deviation. The failure mode is treating the scalar as the verdict. The correct workflow is: decompose the alignment  attach business context (amount, risk, flags, timestamps, evidence quality)  apply the policy overlay  assign severity and confidence separately  keep both numbers side by side, never blended.
