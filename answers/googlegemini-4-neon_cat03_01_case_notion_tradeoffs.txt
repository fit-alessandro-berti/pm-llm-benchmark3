## 1. Analytical assumptions

In object-centric process mining, a **case notion** is a projection choice: selecting one object type $T$ as the case identifier maps each object $o \in T$ to the chronologically ordered sequence of events directly related to $o$ in the event-to-object (E2O) table. Flattening is therefore not neutral data extraction; it imposes a single-entity worldview on a multi-entity process.

Two structural pathologies govern how flattening behaves:

- **Convergence** occurs when a single event is directly linked to multiple objects of the chosen case type (an $N:1$ relationship from case objects to events). Flattening copies that event into every participating object’s trace—for example, `e16` (`Dispatch` of `D2`) is copied into `O1`, `O2`, and `O3` when flattening on `Order`.
- **Divergence** occurs when multiple events of the same activity type attach to one case object without a true sequential dependency between them, typically because the object fans out into parallel child objects or repeated sub-events (a $1:N$ relationship from case objects to events). Flattening forces concurrent sub-lifecycles onto one timeline, producing apparent loops or interleaved transitions—for example, `O1` containing `I11/P1/D1` and `I12/P2/D2`, or `V2` receiving two payment instalments (`M2`, `M3`).

I treat **copied shared events as both evidence and duplication**, depending on the analytical question. They are valid *relational evidence* that an object’s lifecycle genuinely depended on a shared milestone (e.g., `O2` cannot be fulfilled until `e16` and `e24` occur on `D2`). Simultaneously, they are *statistical duplication* for operational volume accounting, resource utilization, and unweighted edge-duration averages: copying `e16` across three orders does not mean three trucks left the warehouse.

For the baseline analysis, I strictly use **direct E2O relations only** and never silently propagate links across the object-to-object (O2O) graph. Three explicit, controlled analytical transformations are permitted only as secondary variants (tested in Section 7): (1) directional O2O relation propagation (e.g., attaching `Create Order` to `Invoice` via `bills` to measure order-to-invoice lag); (2) fractional event weighting ($1/k$ where $k$ objects of type $T$ share an event) to correct additive KPI inflation; and (3) event-label enrichment using O2O qualifiers (splitting `Record Payment` via `partially_settles` vs. `completes_settlement`).

### 2. Cross-order packages (`P3`, `P4`) in `Item` vs. `Package` flattening: hidden consolidation and workload double-counting
- **Affected flattening**: `Item` (`I21, I31, I32, I41`) and `Package` (`P3, P4`), involving `e13` and `e14`.
- **Distortion**: Flattening on `Item` copies `e13` to `I21` and `I31`, and `e14` to `I32` and `I41`, inflating `Pack Package` from 4 to 6 (+50%) while erasing the O2O fact that `P3` and `P4` cross order boundaries (`O2+O3`, `O3+O4`). Conversely, flattening on `Package` preserves the true count of 4 packing events and exact pack-to-dispatch waits, yet equally hides that `P3` and `P4` are cross-order consolidations rather than single-order parcels.
- **Assessment**: **Context-dependent**. In `Item`, frequency inflation is harmful for warehouse capacity planning but acceptable if measuring "items packed"; hiding cross-order bundling in both views is harmful whenever picking/packing errors or customer-specific SLAs depend on multi-order batching.

### 3. Consolidated delivery (`D2`) across `Order`, `Item`, and `Package` flattenings: asymmetric transit-time bias and 100% volume inflation
- **Affected flattening**: `Order`, `Item`, and `Package`, involving `D2` (`e16`, `e24`) and `D3` (`e17`, `e27`).
- **Distortion**: `D2` consolidates `P2` (`O1/I12`) and `P3` (`O2/I21`, `O3/I31`). `e16` and `e24` are triplicated in `Order` and `Item` and duplicated in `Package`. Combined with `D3`’s duplication across `O3/O4` and `I32/I41`, `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` counts double from 3 to 6 (+100%) in `Order` and `Item`, and rise from 3 to 4 (+33%) in `Package`. Mean `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` duration shifts depending solely on case notion: true `Delivery` mean is **613.3 min** (`D1`: 180m, `D2`: 295m, `D3`: 1,365m), whereas `Package` pulls it **down to 533.8 min** (double-weighting faster `D2`), and `Item` pulls it **up to 632.5 min** (weighting `D2` 3× and `D3` 2×).
- **Assessment**: **Context-dependent**. Weighting by `Item` or `Order` is informative for *customer-experienced* delivery latency, but **harmful** if misinterpreted as carrier fleet performance or shipment volume.

### 4. Consolidated invoicing (`V2`) in `Order` flattening: false 1:1 billing equivalence and deflated payment latency
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O2`, `O3`), involving `V2` (`e19`, `e22`, `e25`).
- **Distortion**: Copying `e19` (`Issue Invoice`) into both `O2` and `O3` inflates invoice count from 3 to 4 (+33%), so every order trace contains exactly one `Create Order`, one `Issue Invoice`, and one `Close Order`. A log skeleton (`discover_log_skeleton`) will report strict 1:1 cardinality `equivalence` across those three activities, concealing consolidated billing. Moreover, `V2`’s quick first payment (`e19` 12:05  `e22` 12:40, 35 min) is counted twice, deflating mean `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` latency from **448.3 min** (7h 28m on `Invoice`) to **345.0 min** (5h 45m on `Order`).
- **Assessment**: **Harmful**. It hides consolidated billing policy, exaggerates AR invoicing workload, and makes cash collection appear 23% faster than invoice reality.

### 5. Partial payments (`M2`, `M3`) on `V2` in `Order` vs. `Invoice` flattening: spurious rework cycle vs. masked settlement duration
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O2`, `O3`) and `Invoice` (`V2`), involving `e22` (`M2`), `e24` (`D2`), `e25` (`M3`), and `e27` (`D3`).
- **Distortion**: In `Order`, `D2`’s `Confirm Delivery` (`e24`, 16:00) lands chronologically between `M2` (`e22`, 12:40) and `M3` (`e25`, 09:00 next day). Because `e22`, `e24`, and `e25` are all shared by `O2` and `O3`, flattening creates a duplicated 2-cycle `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment` (backward edge frequency = 2, duration = 17h 00m), and in `O3` an alternating chain `RP -> CD -> RP -> CD` (`e22 -> e24 -> e25 -> e27`). This looks like delivery-triggered billing rework. In `Invoice`, the trace `e19 -> e22 -> e25` cleanly surfaces `Record Payment -> Record Payment` (20h 20m), avoiding the spurious cycle—but the `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` edge still measures only time to the *first* instalment (35 min), hiding `V2`’s 20h 55m full settlement unless end-to-end case duration is checked, and dropping the O2O qualifiers `partially_settles` vs. `completes_settlement`.
- **Assessment**: **Harmful** in `Order` (fabricates cross-domain rework loops); **informative with caveats** in `Invoice` (correctly exposes instalments, provided analysts distinguish first-payment lag from full settlement).

### 6. Degenerate singleton lifecycle in `Payment` flattening (and stub truncation in `Delivery`)
- **Affected flattening**: `Payment` (`M1`–`M4`: `e21`, `e22`, `e25`, `e26`); secondarily `Delivery` (`D1`–`D3`).
- **Distortion**: Flattening on `Payment` discards 27 of 31 events (87.1%) and produces four length-1 traces (`<Record Payment>`). The resulting DFG has **zero edges**, zero throughput time, and a single trivial variant; `M2` and `M3` appear completely unrelated. `Delivery` exhibits a milder lifecycle truncation (80.6% event loss, length-2 stub traces `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`), retaining valid transit times but discarding all upstream packing delays and load sizes.
- **Assessment**: **Harmful** for `Payment` if fed into control-flow or performance discovery (produces empty/zero-duration models); **informative** for `Delivery` strictly as a scoped transit KPI, but harmful if used to diagnose end-to-end order delays.

### 7. False sequentialization of parallel fulfilment and billing streams in `Order` flattening
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O1`–`O4`), involving `e15`–`e27`.
- **Distortion**: In all four orders, invoicing (`e18`–`e20`) and initial payment (`e21`, `e22`, `e26`) occur chronologically after `Dispatch` (`e15`–`e17`) and before `Confirm Delivery` (`e23`, `e24`, `e27`). Consequently, the direct physical edge `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` **disappears completely (0 occurrences)** from the `Order` DFG, replaced by `Dispatch -> Issue Invoice -> Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery`. A log skeleton also infers `always_before(Confirm Delivery, Record Payment)` and `always_after(Dispatch, Issue Invoice)`.
- **Assessment**: **Harmful** for causal interpretation—it implies finance gates carrier delivery—though **mildly informative** as a descriptive calendar observation that customers in this snapshot tended to pay before final delivery confirmation.

### 8. Divergence self-loops and coincidental log-skeleton cardinality equivalence in `Order` flattening
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O1`, `O3`), involving `e05`–`e17`, `e23`, `e24`, `e27`.
- **Distortion**: Because `O1` splits across `P1/D1` and `P2/D2` and `O3` splits across `P3/D2` and `P4/D3`, `Order` flattening generates sequential self-loops (`Add Item -> Add Item`, `Pack Package -> Pack Package`, `Dispatch -> Dispatch`, and in `O1`, `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery`), resembling repeated rework rather than parallel sub-objects. Remarkably, convergence duplication makes per-order activity counts coincide exactly: in `O1` and `O3`, `Add Item = Pack Package = Dispatch = Confirm Delivery = 2`; in `O2` and `O4`, all four equal `1`. Thus `discover_log_skeleton` on `Order` will assert **exact 1:1 count equivalence** among `Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery`—completely disguising the actual 6-item : 4-package : 3-delivery physical consolidation!
- **Assessment**: **Harmful**. Both the apparent rework loops and the coincidental 1:1 cardinality invariants directly contradict the underlying logistics topology.

### 9. Deceptive 100% conformance and commercial blindness in `Item` flattening
- **Affected flattening**: `Item` (`I11`–`I41`), involving `e05`–`e17`, `e23`, `e24`, `e27`.
- **Distortion**: All 6 items yield the exact same 4-step trace (`Add Item -> Pack Package -> Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`), creating a single-variant, loop-free DFG with zero structural deviations. Every order-splitting complexity (`O1` across `D1` and `D2`), multi-order dependency (`D2`, `V2`), and instalment delay (`V2/M3`) vanishes alongside the 15 omitted commercial/financial events.
- **Assessment**: **Harmful** if used as an executive health check (creates an illusion of a perfectly deterministic process), though **informative** as a narrow sanity check verifying that no line item bypassed packing or dispatch before delivery confirmation.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

A defensible analytics architecture separates **safe flattened views** from **native object-centric representations** rather than forcing one flat log to answer every question.

**Flattened logs to retain (and their safe scope):**
- **`Order` log**: Create *only* for customer-facing order-to-close SLA (`Create Order` to `Close Order`), checking whether all 4 orders closed after final delivery and final payment, and inspecting customer-experienced milestone interleaving. Never use for logistics counts, carrier durations, or AR volume.
- **`Delivery` log**: Create for exact carrier shipment counts (3) and unbiased transit durations (`D1`: 3h00m, `D2`: 4h55m, `D3`: 22h45m).
- **`Invoice` log**: Create for exact billing counts (3), instalment detection (`V2`’s 2-payment variant), and time-to-full-settlement (`Issue Invoice` to last `Record Payment`).
- **`Package` log (optional)**: Create for warehouse packing volume (4) and true `Pack Package -> Dispatch` staging waits (`P1`–`P4`: 53–60 min), while prohibiting `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` aggregation on `Package` because of `D2` duplication.
- **Do not create** a `Payment` flattened process log (analyze `M1`–`M4` in tabular AR reports joined to `Invoice`), and restrict `Item` flattening strictly to item-weighted customer-experience metrics.

**Questions requiring native object-centric artefacts:**
- **OC-DFG (`discover_ocdfg`)**: Required to display true concurrency—showing `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` on `Delivery`/`Package`/`Item` executing in parallel with `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` on `Invoice`, rather than falsely serialized behind `Order`—and to compare **event frequency**, **unique object frequency**, and **total object frequency** side-by-side on the same node/edge (e.g., `Dispatch` = 3 events, 3 deliveries, 4 packages, 6 items, 6 order-event links across 4 unique orders).
- **Object Type Graph (`discover_otg`)**: Required to expose the structural root causes of convergence and divergence across `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment` before running any discovery algorithm.
- **Event Type–Object Type graph (`discover_etot`)**: Required to audit which activities bind multiple object types and at what multiplicity (e.g., verifying that `Pack Package` bridges `Order`, `Item`, and `Package`, while `Record Payment` bridges `Order`, `Invoice`, and `Payment`).
- **Object interaction summary (`ocel_objects_interactions_summary`)**: Required to quantify cross-order entanglement—immediately revealing that `O2` and `O3` co-occur in 6 events (`e13, e16, e19, e22, e24, e25`), `O1/O2/O3` intersect in `e16/e24` via `D2`, and `O3/O4` intersect in `e14/e17/e27` via `P4/D3`.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering (`cluster_equivalent_ocel`)**: Useful *only* when anchored on a justified lead object to test whether multi-object execution topologies repeat across cases. Crucially, standard connected-component extraction fails here because cross-order consolidation (`O1`–`P2`–`D2`–`P3`–`O2/O3`–`P4`–`O4`) fuses **all 24 objects and 31 events into a single connected component**.

**Reconciling contradictory findings:** Whenever a flattened view and an OCEL view disagree (e.g., `Order` reports 6 dispatches and no `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` edge, while `Delivery` and OC-DFG report 3 dispatches and an active transit edge), resolve the conflict by a three-step protocol: (1) check the **unit of observation** (event vs. unique object vs. object-event pair), (2) inspect **convergence multiplicity** via `etot` and interaction summaries to see whether the excess count is shared-event copying, and (3) inspect **per-type OC-DFG projections** to distinguish true causal dependencies from calendar interleaving across concurrent sub-objects.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Selected position: B. Use a primary lead object (`Order`) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (`Delivery`, `Package`, and `Invoice`, with `Item` as a scoped physical sub-lifecycle).**

### Decision criteria
1. **End-to-end commercial boundary coverage**: Ability to anchor the full process window from customer commitment (`Create Order`) to obligation completion (`Close Order`).
2. **Metric fidelity by operational domain**: Zero unflagged convergence distortion in warehouse, carrier, and accounts-receivable KPIs.
3. **Visibility of multi-object dependencies**: Explicit preservation of split orders (`O1`), cross-order consolidation (`P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `V2`), and partial settlement (`M2`, `M3`).
4. **Governance practicality**: Alignment with real business accountability (order management, logistics, and finance).

### Strongest supporting evidence
`Order` is the sole object type covering all 31 events and all 8 activity types (`Item`, the runner-up, drops 48.4% of events), and it is the only object with a complete commercial lifecycle (`e01`–`e04` through `e28`–`e31`). Retaining `Order` as the *primary* lead object anchors customer SLA reporting and order-closure conformance, while explicitly elevating `Delivery` (0% duplication; true carrier transit), `Package` (exact packing volume and staging waits), and `Invoice` (0% duplication; exact billing volume and `V2` instalment tracking) as *mandatory secondary lifecycles* neutralizes every operational distortion documented in Section 3. Because the primary lead is explicitly paired with an OC-DFG and secondary views, `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` on `Order` is also justified as a structural diagnostic to compare ego-centric order execution topologies (`O1`–`O4`).

### Strongest counterargument
Position **C** (rejecting any lead object) has strong theoretical force here: all four orders belong to **one weakly connected component** via `D2`, `P3`, `P4`, and `V2`, meaning `Order` does not even partition the event log into independent cases. Designating `Order` as "primary" risks giving commercial metrics implicit primacy over equally fundamental logistics and financial cycles.

### Residual risk
Even with explicit qualifications, downstream stakeholders or BI dashboards may lazily query the `Order`-flattened table for dispatch counts, invoice counts, or DFG rework loops, re-introducing +35.5% to +100% inflation and false `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` cycles if semantic guardrails are not enforced in code and reporting schemas.

### Conditions under which I would change this decision
- **Switch to C (reject any lead object)** if cross-order consolidation scales into a dominant batch/account-based model (e.g., consolidated freight runs and periodic master invoices spanning dozens of orders, where individual order closure becomes decoupled from shipment and payment cycles).
- **Switch to A (single `Order` lead)** only if business rules prohibit cross-order packing, delivery, and invoicing *and* split-order shipments are rare, reducing convergence and divergence on `Order` to negligible levels.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

The script below implements Position B programmatically, pairing flattened views (`Order` primary; `Delivery`, `Package`, `Invoice`, `Item` secondary) with native OCEL 2.0 discovery and `Order`-anchored equivalent-execution clustering. Storing and cross-checking quantitative metrics—not merely inspecting rendered graphs—is essential because clean-looking DFGs (`Item`, `Package`) hide massive omission and weighting biases while dense DFGs (`Order`) hide convergence artifacts.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Assumes `ocel` is an existing pm4py.OCEL object representing the snapshot.
print(ocel)

# 1. Baseline inventory: unique event-activity and object-type frequencies
activity_col = ocel.event_activity
otype_col = ocel.object_type_column
eid_col = ocel.event_id_column
oid_col = ocel.object_id_column

unique_activity_counts = ocel.events[activity_col].value_counts()
object_type_counts = ocel.objects[otype_col].value_counts()
total_unique_events = ocel.events[eid_col].nunique()

# Audit E2O multiplicity per (event_type, object_type) to flag convergence risk
e2o_multiplicity = (
    ocel.relations.groupby([eid_col, activity_col, otype_col])[oid_col]
    .nunique()
    .reset_index(name="objects_per_event")
)
convergence_summary = (
    e2o_multiplicity.groupby([activity_col, otype_col])["objects_per_event"]
    .agg(["count", "mean", "max"])
)

# 2. Controlled flattening across all six object types (direct E2O only)
object_types = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
flattened_logs = {}
flattened_dfgs = {}
flattened_skeletons = {}
flattening_audit_rows = []

for ot in object_types:
    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flattened_logs[ot] = flat_log

    # Discover DFG and Log Skeleton (note: Payment yields 0 DFG edges)
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    flattened_dfgs[ot] = {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": start_acts,
        "end_activities": end_acts,
    }
    flattened_skeletons[ot] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_log)

    # Quantify retention vs. duplication explicitly (visual inspection cannot show this)
    retained_unique = flat_log[eid_col].nunique()
    flattened_rows = len(flat_log)
    flattening_audit_rows.append({
        "object_type": ot,
        "cases": flat_log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "unique_events_retained": retained_unique,
        "unique_events_lost": total_unique_events - retained_unique,
        "flattened_rows": flattened_rows,
        "duplicated_copies": flattened_rows - retained_unique,
        "dfg_edge_count": len(dfg),
    })

flattening_audit_df = pd.DataFrame(flattening_audit_rows)

# 3. Native object-centric discovery (reconciling concurrency & multiplicity)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
interactions_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

# 4. Compare event, unique-object, and total-object metrics inside OC-DFG
ocdfg_edge_comparison = []
for ot, edge_dict in ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].items():
    for edge, ev_couples in edge_dict.items():
        ocdfg_edge_comparison.append({
            "object_type": ot,
            "edge": edge,
            "event_couples": len(ev_couples),
            "unique_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][ot].get(edge, set())),
            "total_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][ot].get(edge, [])),
        })
ocdfg_edge_metrics_df = pd.DataFrame(ocdfg_edge_comparison)

# 5. Lead-object equivalent-execution clustering (justified under Position B)
# Anchored on `Order` to inspect structural heterogeneity across O1..O4;
# does NOT assume connected-component independence (all 4 orders share 1 component).
order_equivalent_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")
order_cluster_sizes = {k: len(v) for k, v in order_equivalent_clusters.items()}
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

To verify that analytical conclusions reflect operational reality rather than projection artifacts, execute four structured sensitivity checks:

1. **Removing or down-weighting copied shared events**:
   Compare unweighted `Order`, `Item`, and `Package` metrics against two corrected variants: (a) **$1/k$ fractional attribution**, where each shared event contributes $1/k$ to frequency and weighted averages (e.g., `e16`/`e24` receive weight $1/3$ per order in `O1`–`O3`, restoring total `Dispatch` count from 6 to exactly 3), and (b) **strict exclusion** of multi-object events (`e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27` on `Order`). The exclusion test isolates which transitions depend solely on consolidated operations (`O2` and `O3` lose nearly all mid-process events, proving how structurally dependent they are on cross-order sharing).

2. **Direct E2O relations vs. explicitly propagated O2O relations**:
   Construct a controlled O2O-propagated OCEL variant—for instance, propagating `Create Order` (`e01`–`e04`) onto `Invoice` via `bills`, and `Issue Invoice`/`Record Payment` onto `Item` via `bills` + `contains`. Compare against direct E2O: propagating `Order` to `Invoice` usefully exposes order-to-invoice lead time, yet introduces a `Create Order -> Create Order` divergence self-loop on `V2` (`e02 -> e03`); propagating `Invoice` to `Item` triplicates `e19` across `I21, I31, I32`, demonstrating that indiscriminate O2O propagation amplifies convergence rather than curing lifecycle truncation.

3. **Lead-object perturbation across all viable anchors**:
   Re-run control-flow, log-skeleton, and throughput pipelines replacing `Order` with `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice`, and track KPI drift across a fixed benchmark table: `Pack` count (4 vs. 6), `Dispatch` count (3 vs. 4 vs. 6), mean transit time (533.8m vs. 613.3m vs. 632.5m vs. absent on `Order`), and mean invoice-to-first-payment time (345.0m vs. 448.3m). Any KPI exhibiting >10% variance across lead objects must be locked to its authoritative domain lifecycle rather than reported on the lead object.

4. **OC-DFG metric triangulation (events vs. unique objects vs. total objects)**:
   For every activity and edge in `ocdfg`, compare all three count annotations:
   - *Events* (operational executions: e.g., `Dispatch` = 3, `Pack Package` = 4, `Issue Invoice` = 3);
   - *Unique objects* (distinct entities touched per type: e.g., `Dispatch` touches 3 `Delivery`, 4 `Package`, 6 `Item`, and 4 unique `Order` objects);
   - *Total objects* (object-event pairs: e.g., `Dispatch` accumulates 6 `Order`-event links across `e15`–`e17` because `O1` and `O3` appear in two dispatches each).
   Divergence between *unique objects* and *total objects* diagnoses $1:N$ split lifecycles (`O1`, `O3`, `V2`), while divergence between *events* and *total objects* diagnoses $N:1$ consolidation (`P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `D3`, `V2`), providing a formal mathematical audit of every distortion before executive reporting.
