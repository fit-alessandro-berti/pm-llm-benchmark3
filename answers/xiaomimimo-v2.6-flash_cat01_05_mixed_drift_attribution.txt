def tv_distance(v, ref, subset=None):
    v = np.asarray(v, float); ref = np.asarray(ref, float)
    if subset is not None:
        v = v[subset]; ref = ref[subset]
    sv, sr = v.sum(), ref.sum()
    if sv == 0 or sr == 0: return np.nan
    return 0.5 * float(np.abs(v/sv - ref/sr).sum())

def cosine_distance(v, ref):
    v = np.asarray(v,float); ref=np.asarray(ref,float)
    nv, nr = np.linalg.norm(v), np.linalg.norm(ref)
    if nv==0 or nr==0: return np.nan
    return 1 - float(v@ref)/(nv*nr)

For **p90 (+53 h)** the queueing share is higher (~65–75%) because queueing variance, not mean processing, dominates the tail.

### 3.4 Candidate change points by drift type

**Logging drift**
- **CP-L1 = 2026-03-01 (sudden):** labels 911, raw fitness 0.375 in one month, duplicates 04%, missing resource 1.04.8%, new `Open CaseRegister` edge — *absent* from mapped fitness and normalized DFG.
- **CP-L2 = 2026-04-01 (gradual):** missing resource continues 4.85.25.5…6.5% monotonically through July; duplicates creep 46%. Detected by a 3-point monotone-increase rule, invisible to the step rule.

**Behavioral drift**
- **CP-B1 = 2026-05-05 (sudden):** `AssessRequestInfo` 1224/100 in one month; rework variants 1324%; fast variants 5545%; normalized TV 0.0050.063.
- **CP-B2 = 2026-07-01 (step):** decision rate falls to 72/100 (10.5 vs baseline); TV peaks at 0.089/0.116; mapped fitness reaches its minimum 0.86.
- (Candidate **CP-B3 = 2026-10-01**: senior/complexity ratio steps 0.270.54; could equally be a case-mix or a `Senior Assessment` **definition** change.)

**Performance drift**
- **CP-P1 = 2026-05-05 (sudden):** median 32.036.5 h (+17%), p90 7184 h (+24%).
- **CP-P2 = 2026-06-15  2026-07 (ramp):** median 40.844.2 h, p90/median ratio warn-level breach (+6%) at July; tail inflation continues to August.
- **CP-P3 = 2026-10-01 (step):** median 41.547.8 h (+53% alert-level vs baseline), p90 96116 h (+71%).
- **CP-P4 = 2026-12-05 (improvement):** median 6.7 h, p90 22 h (confounded with seasonal load).

### Global vs. multiple change points

**The data do not support one global change point.** March breaks raw fitness, label count and missing-resource but leaves mapped fitness and the normalized DFG essentially intact; May/June breaks rework, supplier share and cycle time but leaves the label inventory flat at 11; October breaks case mix, senior-assessment rate and p90 while leaving labels, duplicates and supplier share stable. **A single common cause would have to move all artifacts together** — the observed *disagreement between artifacts is itself the strongest evidence for mechanism-specific change points*. Twelve monthly points also give very low power for formal methods (PELT/binseg on monthly residuals would be unreliable); daily/weekly event-level series should be used instead, and **no formal change-point method has yet been applied** — everything above is a threshold heuristic.

**Primary monitoring population:** **L0 — all cases registered in the month, on mapped labels with technical duplicates removed, with Supplier Z and complexity retained as *covariates/strata* rather than filters.** Rationale: filtering to non-Z or standard-complexity cases would delete exactly the population drift we must detect (supplier share +28 pp; complexity +24 pp) and would silently redefine "the process". Every L0 alarm should be *diagnosed* by automatic re-computation at L2/L3 (does the signal survive conditioning?) — the primary population is for **detection**, the strata are for **attribution**.

**Seasonality.** No monitor uses a single universal threshold. Each monitor carries its own reference: (a) **same-calendar-month prior year** once a second year exists; (b) until then, a **Jan–Feb pre-drift baseline plus an explicit seasonal index** derived from the historical narrative (renewal season raises volume and complexity), applied as a *residual* — i.e. alert on `observed  E[observed | volume, complexity, supplier share]`, not on the raw value. M5/M6/M8/M11/M12 are all re-evaluated on the seasonally adjusted residual before an alert is raised. Recurrence is therefore reported as "expected seasonal recurrence" rather than "new drift", and only a **residual** breach is escalated.

**Multiple testing.** Thirteen monitors × 12 months  156 tests/year. Controls: (i) **hierarchical gating** — schema monitors (M1–M3) are checked first; if any is red, statistical monitors (M5–M13) are suppressed and reported as "measurement unreliable" rather than as drift; (ii) **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.10** applied across the statistical monitors each month, separately from the schema tier which uses deterministic tolerances; (iii) **persistence rules differ by onset type** — sudden mechanisms (label change, supplier outage) alarm on 1 month, gradual mechanisms (queueing, identifier creep) require 2 consecutive months, and seasonal mechanisms require a residual breach; (iv) **per-monitor control limits** (warn/alert two-tier) rather than one threshold; (v) the same alert is only re-raised after a documented **recovery-to-baseline** event, preventing alert storms.

# ---------- A1. validation ---------------------------------------------------------------
def validate(monthly: pd.DataFrame, dfg: pd.DataFrame):
    issues, fatal = [], []
    if monthly["month"].duplicated().any():
        fatal.append("duplicate months in monthly summary")
    if not pd.PeriodIndex(monthly["month"], freq="M").is_monotonic_increasing:
        fatal.append("months not chronologically sorted")
    for c in PCT_COLS:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 100).all():
            fatal.append(f"{c} outside [0,100]")
    for c in FITNESS_COLS:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 1).all():
            fatal.append(f"{c} outside [0,1]")
    if (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] < monthly["raw_model_fitness"] - 1e-9).any():
        issues.append("mapped fitness below raw fitness somewhere (unexpected)")
    if ((monthly["fast_variant_pct"] + monthly["rework_variant_pct"]) > 100).any():
        issues.append("variant percentages exceed 100 (variants are not exhaustive)")
    pre = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE_MONTHS)]
    if not (pre["distinct_activity_labels"] == 9).all():
        issues.append("baseline label count is not 9")
    if not (dfg["Register->Eligibility"] == 100).all():
        issues.append("Register->Eligibility is not pinned at 100 (normalisation assumption broken)")
    missing_months = sorted(set(monthly["month"]) - set(dfg["month"]))
    if missing_months:
        issues.append(f"DFG fragment missing months: {missing_months} -> DFG metrics will be NaN there")
    for m in fatal:
        raise ValueError("FATAL: " + m)
    for i in issues:
        print("[validate] NOTE:", i)
    print(f"[validate] OK: {len(monthly)} months, {len(dfg)} DFG rows, "
          f"{len(PCT_COLS)} percentage metrics checked.")
    return True


# ---------- A2. baseline-relative changes ------------------------------------------------
def baseline_relative(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE_MONTHS)].drop(columns="month").mean()
    rows = []
    for _, r in monthly[~monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE_MONTHS)].iterrows():
        for m in base.index:
            b, v = base[m], r[m]
            rows.append({
                "month": r["month"], "metric": m,
                "baseline": b, "value": v,
                "delta_abs": v - b,
                "delta_rel": (v / b - 1) if b not in (0, np.nan) and b != 0 else np.nan,
            })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


# ---------- A3. transparent DFG-fragment distance ----------------------------------------
def tv_distance(vec, ref, subset=None):
    """Total variation (0.5 * L1) between L1-normalised rate vectors."""
    v, r = np.asarray(vec, float), np.asarray(ref, float)
    if subset is not None:
        v, r = v[subset], r[subset]
    if v.sum() == 0 or r.sum() == 0:
        return np.nan
    return 0.5 * float(np.abs(v / v.sum() - r / r.sum()).sum())


def cosine_distance(vec, ref):
    v, r = np.asarray(vec, float), np.asarray(ref, float)
    nv, nr = np.linalg.norm(v), np.linalg.norm(r)
    if nv == 0 or nr == 0:
        return np.nan
    return 1.0 - float(v @ r) / (nv * nr)


def dfg_distances(dfg: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    cols = [c for c in dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    ref = dfg.loc[dfg["month"] == BASELINE_MONTHS[0], cols].to_numpy(float).ravel()
    beh = [i for i, c in enumerate(cols) if c != "Register->Eligibility"]   # drop the pinned edge
    out = []
    for _, r in dfg.iterrows():
        v = r[cols].to_numpy(float)
        out.append({"month": r["month"],
                    "tv_full": tv_distance(v, ref),
                    "tv_behavioral": tv_distance(v, ref, subset=beh),
                    "cosine": cosine_distance(v, ref)})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)


# ---------- A4. raw -> mapped fitness recovery -------------------------------------------
def fitness_recovery(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE_MONTHS)].mean()
    raw_loss = base["raw_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    denom = base["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "month": monthly["month"],
        "raw_fitness_loss": raw_loss,
        "artifact_explained_share": np.where(denom > 0, (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"]
                                                         - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]) / denom, np.nan),
        "gap_mapped_minus_raw": monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"],
    })

THRESHOLDS = {
    "distinct_activity_labels":    ("logging",    "abs", 1.0,  2.0,  1, np.nan),

    "mapped_model_fitness":        ("behavioral", "abs", 0.03, 0.06, 2, np.nan),
    "median_cycle_h":              ("performance","rel", 0.10, 0.40, 2, 2.0),
    "p90_cycle_h":                 ("performance","rel", 0.15, 0.50, 2, np.nan),
    "p90_median_ratio":            ("performance","rel", 0.06, 0.12, 1, np.nan),
    "dfg_tv_full":                 ("behavioral", "abs", 0.05, 0.08, 1, np.nan),
    "decision_rate_per_100":       ("behavioral", "abs", 5.0,  9.0,  1, np.nan),
}


def _first_crossing(vals, months, base, mode, thr, persistence):
    dev = np.abs(vals - base) if mode == "abs" else np.abs(vals / base - 1)
    hit = np.nan_to_num(dev, nan=-1) >= thr
    for i in range(len(hit) - persistence + 1):
        if hit[i:i + persistence].all():
            return months[i]
    return None


def _first_trend(vals, months, trend_min):
    if np.isnan(trend_min):
        return None
    for i in range(2, len(vals)):
        if vals[i - 2] < vals[i - 1] < vals[i] and (vals[i] - vals[i - 2]) >= trend_min:
            return months[i]
    return None


def change_points(monthly, dfg_dist, dfg):
    df = monthly.copy()
    df["p90_median_ratio"] = df["p90_cycle_h"] / df["median_cycle_h"]
    df = df.merge(dfg_dist[["month", "tv_full"]].rename(columns={"tv_full": "dfg_tv_full"}), on="month", how="left")
    df = df.merge(dfg[["month", "Assess_or_Senior->Decision"]]
                  .rename(columns={"Assess_or_Senior->Decision": "decision_rate_per_100"}), on="month", how="left")

    base_df = df[df["month"].isin(BASELINE_MONTHS)].mean(numeric_only=True)
    rows = []
    for metric, (family, mode, warn, alert, pers, trend) in THRESHOLDS.items():
        if metric not in df.columns:
            continue
        sub = df[["month", metric]].dropna()
        months, vals = sub["month"].tolist(), sub[metric].to_numpy(float)
        b = base_df.get(metric, np.nan)
        if np.isnan(b) or b == 0:
            b = vals[0]
        rows.append({"metric": metric, "family": family,
                     "cp_warn": _first_crossing(vals, months, b, mode, warn, pers),
                     "cp_alert": _first_crossing(vals, months, b, mode, alert, pers),
                     "cp_trend": _first_trend(vals, months, trend)})
    cp = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    print("\n=== candidate change points (rule: |dev| >= warn/alert, persisted N months; "
          "plus 3-point monotone trend) ===")
    print(cp.to_string(index=False))
    print("\n=== distinct candidate change points per drift family ===")
    for fam, g in cp.groupby("family"):
        dates = sorted({d for d in pd.concat([g["cp_warn"], g["cp_alert"], g["cp_trend"]]).dropna()})
        print(f"  {fam:<11}: {dates}")
    return cp

import copy
import pandas as pd

try:
    import pm4py
    PM4PY_OK = True
except Exception as e:                      # pragma: no cover
    PM4PY_OK, _ = False, e

CASE, ACT, TS = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp"


def prepare_event_log(df, activity_mapping=None, dedupe_subset=None, keep_original=True):
    """Format an event dataframe WITHOUT mutating the caller's data.

    Returns {'raw': original-labelled log, 'normalized': mapped/deduped log}.
    The original dataframe and the 'raw' log are never overwritten.
    """
    if not PM4PY_OK:
        raise ImportError("pm4py is required")
    raw_df = df.copy()                                   # defensive copy
    log_raw = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw_df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)

    norm_df = log_raw.copy()                             # second, independent copy
    if activity_mapping:                                 # label mapping on the COPY only
        norm_df["concept:name"] = norm_df["concept:name"].replace(activity_mapping)
    if dedupe_subset:                                    # drop duplicated technical events
        norm_df = norm_df.drop_duplicates(subset=dedupe_subset)

    out = {"raw": log_raw, "normalized": norm_df}
    if not keep_original:
        out.pop("raw")
    return out


def slice_window(log, start, end, mode="both"):
    """pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start_time, end_time, mode in {'events','cases','both'}).

    mode='both' keeps only cases fully inside the window (avoids month-boundary
    censoring when computing cycle time / decision rates).
    """
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start_time=start, end_time=end, mode=mode)


def monthly_slices(log, freq="ME"):
    """Yield (period_label, sub_log) for each calendar month, case-complete."""
    lo, hi = log["time:timestamp"].min(), log["time:timestamp"].max()
    for p in pd.period_range(lo, hi, freq=freq):
        s, e = p.start_time, p.end_time + pd.Timedelta(seconds=1)
        yield str(p), slice_window(log, s, e, mode="both")


def monthly_dfg(log, freq="ME"):
    """{month: (dfg, start_activities, end_activities)} via pm4py.discover_dfg."""
    out = {}
    for label, sub in monthly_slices(log, freq=freq):
        if len(sub) == 0:
            continue
        dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
        out[label] = (dfg, start_act, end_act)
    return out


def dfg_rate_table(dfg_by_month, cases_by_month, per=100):
    """Edge counts per `per` cases -> the normalised DFG-rate table used in Part A."""
    rows = []
    for m, (dfg, _, _) in dfg_by_month.items():
        n = cases_by_month.get(m, np.nan)
        row = {"month": m}
        for (a, b), c in dfg.items():
            row[f"{a}->{b}"] = per * c / n if n else np.nan
        rows.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def variant_table(log, top_k=20):
    """Top-k variant share + spectrum; uses pm4py.get_variants(log)."""
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)                    # {variant_tuple: count}
    s = pd.Series(variants).sort_values(ascending=False)
    share = s / s.sum()
    return pd.DataFrame({"variant": s.index, "count": s.values,
                         "share": share.values}).head(top_k), s


def temporal_profile(log):
    """pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(log) -> time-of-day/day-of-week envelope."""
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(log)


def skeleton_and_declare(log, activities=None):
    """Optional log skeleton and DECLARE discovery (API varies slightly by version)."""
    skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)           # {'violations','allowed','required'}
    declare = None
    try:
        declare = pm4py.discover_declare(
            log, considered_activities=activities)        # version-dependent signature
    except TypeError:
        declare = pm4py.discover_declare(log)             # fallback: all activities
    return skeleton, declare


def conformance_pair(df, activity_mapping, net=None, im=None, fm=None, method="alignments"):
    """Compare RAW vs NORMALIZED labels without overwriting either.

    Returns a dict of fitness per labelling; the model is re-discovered per labelling
    unless a model is supplied, which isolates label semantics from model choice.
    """
    logs = prepare_event_log(df, activity_mapping=activity_mapping,
                             dedupe_subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    results = {}
    for tag, log in logs.items():
        if net is None:
            net_i, im_i, fm_i = pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive(log)
        else:
            net_i, im_i, fm_i = net, im, fm
        if method == "alignments":
            fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net_i, im_i, fm_i)
            diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net_i, im_i, fm_i)
        else:
            fit = pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(log, net_i, im_i, fm_i)
            diag = None
        results[tag] = {"fitness": fit, "diagnostics": diag,
                        "start_activities": pm4py.get_start_activities(log),
                        "end_activities": pm4py.get_end_activities(log)}
    return results


def segment_comparison(df, by, activity_mapping, window_a, window_b):
    """Core discrimination routine: same artifact, two segments, two windows.

    by          : event attribute used for segmentation (e.g. 'supplier', 'complexity')
    window_a/b  : (start, end) strings
    """
    logs = prepare_event_log(df, activity_mapping=activity_mapping)
    out = {}
    for label, log in logs.items():
        for seg in sorted(log[by].dropna().unique()):
            seg_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, by, [seg], level="event")
            for wname, (s, e) in {"A": window_a, "B": window_b}.items():
                wlog = slice_window(seg_log, s, e, mode="both")
                dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(wlog)
                variants, _ = variant_table(wlog, top_k=10)
                out[(label, seg, wname)] = {
                    "cases": wlog["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
                    "dfg": dfg,
                    "top_variant_share": float(variants["share"].iloc[0]),
                }
    return out

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusion (high confidence): the March raw-fitness collapse is logging/semantic drift, not behavior.** Three independent artifacts break on the same date — the label split (`Validate Eligibility`  `Eligibility Screen`), duplicated `Open Case`, and missing resource identifiers 1.04.8% — while mapped fitness (0.90) and the normalized DFG (TV 0.004–0.005) are essentially unchanged, and the population is unchanged (complexity 2022, supplier 1516). Mapping recovers 93% of the raw fitness loss. Only a simultaneous, perfectly masked business change would overturn this.

**Strong (high–moderate): supplier-driven rework from 2026-05-05.** The supplier share (1843%), `AssessRequestInfo` (1224/100) and rework variants (1324%) step in the *same* month, and the implied rework rate among Supplier Z cases (~40% May, ~51% Jul) is plausible. Timing rules out staffing (06-15) and seasonality (10-01). But the implied rate becomes implausible from October (66%, 75%) — my main reason to posit a second, non-supplier rework driver.

**Strong (moderate–high): capacity-driven queueing from 2026-06-15.** Service time moves only 4.85.4 h while reconstructed waiting rises much more; p90/median widens 2.182.47; cycle time keeps rising while supplier share *falls*. I attribute ~54% of the median-cycle increase to queueing — but that share is a **residual**, so it absorbs anything unmodelled.

**Strong (moderate): the October–November step is population drift** (volume +29%, complexity +45%), with the senior-assessment rate rising faster than the complexity flag (ratio 0.270.54) — jointly mediated by the July rule and possibly a definition change.

**Plausible only:** (a) the priority rule's redistribution of delay — invisible in aggregates, supported only by indirect ratio evidence; (b) persistent behavioral adaptation, since rework never returns to baseline (21 vs 11.5 in December); (c) the December improvement being caused by the interventions — overtime, campaign and seasonal load decline coincide.

Attribution is cleanest where onsets are abrupt and mechanisms are external; weakest where mechanisms interact (supplier × staffing × season; rule × season). I would revise this account if prior-year data showed the same October profile without these mechanisms; if event-level alignments showed no within-supplier rework difference; if waiting-time reconstruction showed no June step; or if the senior-assessment ratio failed to break at 2026-07-01 within complexity strata.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** the activity-label inventory changes (a label appears or retires, or any label's frequency share moves >2 pp) while mapped conformance and the normalized DFG stay within their control limits, **THEN** open a logging/schema ticket, suppress conformance alarms for one cycle, and re-run all metrics on the mapped log, **BECAUSE** semantic drift can imitate control-flow drift, and the March episode shows a label split can destroy raw fitness (0.930.55) while the actual process is unchanged (mapped 0.90, TV 0.004).

2. **IF** raw fitness drops 0.10 while mapped fitness drops 0.03 in the same month, **THEN** attribute the loss to label/duplication artifacts, repair the mapping and the duplicate filter, and do not escalate to the process owner, **BECAUSE** the artifact share of raw-fitness loss was 85–96% post-migration, and the residual is small enough that acting on raw fitness alone would misdirect remediation.

3. **IF** mapped fitness or normalized DFG TV breaches its seasonally adjusted limit **while** M1–M3 (labels, duplicates, resource completeness) are green, **THEN** run case-level alignment diagnostics and compare variant spectra by supplier and complexity, **BECAUSE** conformance drift with stable measurement indicates genuine control-flow/behavioral drift rather than logging drift — exactly the pattern seen from May onward (mapped fitness 0.900.86, labels flat at 11).

4. **IF** `AssessRequestInfo` rises 8 per 100 cases above the seasonally adjusted baseline for two consecutive months and the rise concentrates in one supplier, **THEN** notify supplier management, trigger a document-quality audit of that supplier's bundles, and forecast the rework-driven cycle-time impact, **BECAUSE** incomplete document bundles mechanically generate re-`Collect Documents`/`Assess` loops, and the supplier-attributable calculation (implied Z rework 40–51% during the disruption) shows this channel alone can account for the early rework increase.

5. **IF** median cycle time rises <10% while p90 rises >20%, the p90/median ratio widens, and `Assess` service time is stable within the stratum, **THEN** classify the event as capacity/queueing drift and escalate staffing, overtime or queue-priority review rather than process redesign, **BECAUSE** growing dispersion with flat service time is a waiting-time signature — the observed +17.9 h median deterioration was ~54% queueing while service time moved only +0.6 h.

6. **IF** cycle time, volume and complexity all rise in the same calendar window in which they rose historically (renewal season), **THEN** compare against the seasonally adjusted forecast and alert only if the residual breaches the limit, labelling any confirmed breach "seasonal recurrence — residual" rather than "new drift", **BECAUSE** recurring drift must be distinguishable from novel drift, and with only one observed season every October signal would otherwise be misreported as an emerging problem.

7. **IF** missing-resource or duplicate-event rates breach their schema limits, **THEN** quarantine resource-centric and multiplicity-based analyses for the affected periods, rerun control-flow metrics on the deduplicated/mapped log, and report complete-case sensitivity beside every team-level number, **BECAUSE** this missingness is non-random (concentrated in migrated activities), so it biases team throughput, bottleneck attribution and DFG edge weights while leaving sequence-based conformance fitness largely intact.
