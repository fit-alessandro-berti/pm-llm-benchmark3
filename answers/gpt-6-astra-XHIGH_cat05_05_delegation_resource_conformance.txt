## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let H(e) be the human who actually performed event e, t(e) its timestamp, and C(e) its case. An unresolved H(e) yields an unknown result where identity is necessary; it must not yield an automatic pass merely because two account strings differ. Use PASS, FAIL, and UNKNOWN separately from investigative severity and intent.

1. **RR1:** For every draft d and approval a in the same case, H(d) must differ from H(a). Compare all relevant occurrences, not only the first pair. A known common human establishes failure; incomplete attribution prevents a demonstrated pass. The rule applies to LOW-risk cases and emergencies as well as ordinary HIGH-risk cases.

2. **RR2:** For every implementation i and verification v in the same case, H(i) must differ from H(v). Apply the same identity and repeated-event treatment. A service account cannot establish separation from its human operator. RR2 does not state that only a person currently titled Independent Verifier may verify.

3. **RR3:** An ordinary HIGH-risk approval is authorized only if its actual human holds the QM role at t(e), or a valid delegation grants that human the relevant QM approval authority. For a delegation D, require all of: identified authorized principal and delegate; event within the effective interval; activity and change scope covered; incident coverage where specified; required principal absence or formal unavailability at the event; an admissible delegation chain; and adequate evidence that the grant already existed. Delegation never overrides RR1 or RR2. No generic LOW-risk approver-role restriction is supplied, so one must not be invented.

4. **RR4:** The emergency route permits HIGH-risk implementation after an approval by a standing IC or valid IC delegate, provided the emergency flag and contemporaneous safety rationale are credible. The route also requires a QM or valid QM delegate to perform retrospective review within two business days. The initial approval must precede implementation. A later review cannot erase an invalid initial approval or a same-human RR1 failure, and a timely review by an unauthorized actor does not meet the review requirement.

   For this assessment, count Monday–Friday using the supplied site calendar, with no holidays. Exclude the implementation date, count the next business day as day one, and allow through the end of business day two. Use UTC because that is the supplied timestamp basis and no different site timezone or closing hour is given. Both emergency implementations occurred Tuesday, August 11; day one is Wednesday, August 12, and day two is Thursday, August 13. CHG-505's Friday, August 14 review is late; CHG-510's Wednesday, August 12 review is timely. A stricter same-clock-time convention, or measuring from the approval a few minutes earlier, gives the same classifications here. The August 15–16 weekend does not extend either deadline. The operational policy should specify timezone, trigger, and cutoff explicitly.

5. **RR5:** Check authority and separation against attributed humans, preserving confidence and provenance. A valid grant to Ben does not authorize anyone who can use `u_bk`. Conversely, different aliases for Ben do not create two independent people. Session origin, badge presence, strong authentication, and action-bound signatures have different evidential weight.

6. **RR6:** Network concentration, reciprocal activity, account/absence conflicts, and shared infrastructure create investigation leads. They do not establish an agreement to evade controls. Assess assignment opportunities, staffing, workload, timing, content, and independent identity evidence before attributing collusion or intent.

**Principal activity during leave.** Asha retains her standing QM role while on annual leave. If she personally approved CHG-504, it is an authorized QM approval; formal leave is not a prohibition on her exercising that role under the supplied rules. Her action could make an absence-conditioned delegation unavailable during the time she was actually available, depending on the controlled definition of absence. It does not retroactively invalidate D-QM-01 on August 11–12 or automatically cancel the entire leave period. Her VPN ended at 09:40 on August 13; Ben's disputed approval was at 13:00. Establish whether she resumed unavailability rather than assuming all-day availability or permanent delegation revocation. If someone else used her credentials, the VPN is not proof that she was available at all. The code treats the VPN window as disputed availability and otherwise retains the recorded leave as supporting evidence, subject to this manual qualification.

Ben's sick leave likewise does not, under these rules, automatically extinguish an otherwise valid delegation. It creates a substantial identity concern, particularly when coupled with his explicit denial and an office-origin session. Any separate fitness-for-duty or leave policy would need its own evidence and rule basis.

**Effective time versus recording time.** D-QM-01 was signed August 9 at 18:00 and became effective August 10. Its database creation on August 12 at 09:15 does not invalidate CHG-501's August 11 approval. The signed instrument supplies contemporaneous evidence of the earlier grant; verify the signature, document integrity, and migration linkage. A later database entry alone cannot establish that authorization existed earlier or retroactively cure an unauthorized act. Preserve both effective time and recording time. The grant's stated scope is QM approval of production-equipment changes; neither retrospective review authority nor inclusion of all cooling changes should be inferred silently.

Technical conformance is a minimum control test. Distinct humans may still rubber-stamp one another. Conversely, a current-role mismatch can be harmless if historical authority was valid. Eva's verification after August 12, Grace's verification, an approver also verifying, and a drafter also implementing are not additional prohibitions in RR1–RR6. Qualification and broader independence concerns can warrant improvements without being mislabeled as existing rule failures.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Create delegations with explicit, enforceable permissions.** Use controlled, signed grants naming immutable principal/delegate identities, precise UTC start/end times, permitted activities, change categories, incident IDs, absence conditions, and any permission to subdelegate. Separate approval authority from retrospective-review authority. Validate the complete chain and block D-IC-04-type grants where a parent prohibits onward delegation. Provide an approved backup for an unavailable delegate.

2. **Record effective and transaction times separately.** Preserve when a grant was signed, when it takes effect, when it expires or is revoked, and when each system recorded it. Retain the original signed artifact and migration provenance. Permit reconstruction of event-time authorization without treating delayed ingestion as either automatic invalidity or permission to backdate a grant.

3. **Make principal availability explicit.** Define whether absence means leave status, actual unavailability, or a controlled duty state. Record temporary returns and subsequent unavailability with effective timestamps. Reconcile exceptional principal activity with active absence-conditioned grants; do not revoke an entire historical interval simply because an account logged in once. Escalate contested leave-time actions and arrange accountable cover during Ben's absence.

4. **Enforce SoD against stable human identities.** Maintain versioned account-to-human mappings, including legacy aliases. Apply RR1 and RR2 to all relevant case events before approval or verification is accepted. An alias change must not reset SoD history. Track corrections without overwriting the original evidence.

5. **Bind each regulated human action to individual authentication.** Keep technical service identities separate from human signers. Require individual re-authentication or an action-bound electronic signature for approvals, verification, and QM review, including at shared terminals. Retain session, device, token, action, and transaction identifiers together. Prevent unattended-session reuse and investigate compromised credentials without attributing every action to the account owner automatically.

6. **Control the full emergency lifecycle.** Require an incident link, contemporaneous safety rationale, authorized pre-implementation approval, and a named QM reviewer or valid review delegate. Calculate due dates with a controlled site calendar and explicit timezone/cutoff; alert and escalate before expiry. Prevent administrative closure from presenting a case as compliant while a qualifying review is overdue or unauthorized. Document any immediate operational disposition separately so patient-safety needs do not depend on concealing a compliance gap.

7. **Strengthen independence through staffing and review design.** Introduce risk-based reviewer rotation or independent sampling with exceptions for scarce expertise and emergency coverage. Make this a prospective policy, not a retrospective accusation against Clara and Ben. Review whether high-risk cases should additionally separate approval from verification and specify verifier competence. Until adopted, those additional requirements are recommendations rather than existing RR violations.

8. **Use networks as contextual monitoring.** Maintain activity-specific handover and case-co-participation measures, with clear denominators and identity confidence. Compare within compatible cells, shifts, risk groups, workloads, and valid delegation periods. Investigate unusual changes alongside review content and outcomes. Require human assessment before assigning a collusion finding.

9. **Retain evidence sufficient to reconstruct the decision.** Preserve original event times and ingestion times, signed documents, document hashes, role/delegation history, availability records, alias versions, incident rationale, review artifacts, and session/authentication evidence under controlled access and retention. Record collection provenance and corrections. Preserve the relevant disputed sessions promptly because later interviews cannot replace missing action-bound evidence.
