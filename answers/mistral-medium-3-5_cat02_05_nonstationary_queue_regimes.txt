# 1. Data audit and estimands

### Queueing Quantities and Units
- **Arrival intensity ()**: Daily counts of new cases during opening hours (07:00–19:00, 12 hours = 720 minutes). Unit: cases/day or cases/hour.
- **Completion intensity ()**: Daily counts of completed cases during opening hours. Unit: cases/day or cases/hour.
- **Service time**: Active handling time (excluding waiting). Reported as median and p90 in minutes. Not mean flow time (which includes waiting).
- **WIP (Work in Progress)**: Mean number of cases in the system, measured via 15-minute snapshots (`mean_wip`). Unit: cases.
- **Capacity**: `staff_fte` (full-time equivalent concurrent staff). Unit: FTE.
- **Complexity mix**: `complex_pct` (% of arrivals following multi-step variant). Unit: %.
- **Peak arrivals**: `peak_08_10_pct` (% of daily arrivals between 08:00–09:59). Unit: %.

### Opening-Hours Convention
- Service center operates **07:00–19:00** (12 hours/day).
- No service occurs outside these hours, but cases can carry over overnight (backlog at 07:00 is unobserved for most days).

### Limitations of Aggregates
1. **No mean service time**: Only median and p90 are provided; mean is unknown, preventing direct Little’s Law application without assumptions.
2. **No backlog at 07:00**: Cannot compute exact WIP at start of day or carry-over effects.
3. **No interarrival times**: Daily counts mask intraday burstiness (e.g., `peak_08_10_pct` hints at non-uniform arrivals).
4. **No flow time distribution**: Median/p90 service time  flow time (which includes waiting). Flow time is unobserved.
5. **Non-independent days**: Serial dependence likely due to backlog carry-over (e.g., low completions on 2026-04-15  high WIP on 2026-04-16).
6. **Outage and intervention timing**: Outage on 2026-04-15 (180 minutes) and staffing intervention on 2026-04-29 have unclear start/end times.
7. **Case mix shifts**: `complex_pct` increases from ~20% to ~45% after 2026-04-19, possibly altering service time distributions.
8. **Pooled averages obscure regimes**: E.g., weekend vs. weekday, pre/post-intervention, outage/recovery.

### Threats to Naive Inference
1. **Non-stationarity**: Demand (`arrivals`), service (`median_service_min`), and capacity (`staff_fte`) vary across days.
2. **Outliers**: 2026-04-15 (outage) and 2026-04-16 (recovery) are atypical.
3. **Censoring**: Overnight backlog is unobserved; completions may include cases from prior days.
4. **Simpson’s Paradox**: Pooled averages (e.g., mean WIP) may reverse regime-specific trends (e.g., high WIP during outage recovery vs. normal days).
5. **Burstiness**: Hourly arrival vectors show spikes (e.g., 2026-04-21: 34 arrivals in 08:00–09:00), violating Poisson assumptions.
6. **Serial dependence**: Low completions on one day (e.g., 2026-04-15) inflate WIP the next day (2026-04-16).
7. **Unobserved confounders**: `complex_pct` may correlate with unmeasured factors (e.g., staff experience).

def fano_factor(counts):

# --- Change-point heuristic (CUSUM for arrivals) ---
from scipy.stats import norm

def cusum_change_point(daily_series, threshold=3.0):
    series = daily_series.values
    n = len(series)
    mean_all = np.mean(series)
    std_all = np.std(series, ddof=1)
    cusum = np.zeros(n)
    change_points = []
    for i in range(1, n):
        cusum[i] = cusum[i-1] + (series[i] - mean_all) / std_all
        if abs(cusum[i]) > threshold:
            change_points.append(i)
            cusum[i] = 0  # Reset
    return change_points

arrivals = daily_data["arrivals"].values

# --- Little's Law (approximate) for stable regimes ---
def littles_law(df, regime, throughput_col="completed", wip_col="mean_wip"):
    regime_df = df[df["regime"] == regime]
    lambda_ = regime_df[throughput_col].mean()
    L = regime_df[wip_col].mean()
    if lambda_ > 0:
        flow_time_days = L / lambda_
        flow_time_hours = flow_time_days * 12  # 12-hour day
        return flow_time_hours
    return np.nan
