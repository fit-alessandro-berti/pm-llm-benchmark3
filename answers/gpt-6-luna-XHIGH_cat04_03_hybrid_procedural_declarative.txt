# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

Assumptions used below:

- The stable procedural backbone is mandatory, while clarification, sanctions escalation, legal opinion, post-activation bank changes, temporal expectations, and resource separation are cross-cutting constraints.
- Conformance to the procedural tree is checked on the projection containing only its backbone activities. The omitted cross-cutting activities are checked on the unprojected trace by the layers assigned below.
- `Reject Vendor`, `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, and `Close Onboarding` are treated as outcome/finalization events for the clarification monitor. This conservative reading ensures no pending clarification crosses an outcome boundary.
- The first `Obtain Legal Opinion` after an escalation satisfies that escalation. If there are repeated escalations, each must have a later opinion. Timestamps are comparable, timezone-aware instants.
- An accepted case is one that activates. The applicable approval is Manager Approval on the low-risk route and Compliance Approval on the high-risk route.
- Missing evidence is unknown, not proof that a requirement was met. For activation-critical checks, unknown evidence is held for review rather than silently passed.

There are two defensible resolutions of R5/R6:

1. **Legal-priority, strict gate (selected):** R5 governs. After an escalation, an opinion must precede activation. The emergency statement in R6 is not implemented as an exception because R5 says “under all circumstances” and the event log cannot prove the joint authorization. An emergency claim requires policy clarification before it can override the gate. This reduces the risk of unauthorized activation but can delay legitimate urgent operations and can flag an activation that Operations considers permitted.
2. **Explicit emergency carve-out:** R5 is the ordinary rule; R6 is a documented exception. Activation may precede the opinion only if a trusted external authorization record proves joint authorization before activation, and the opinion arrives within 24 hours after activation. This supports operations but carries the compliance risk of accepting an activation if the sidecar evidence is stale, incomplete, or not truly joint.

The implemented model uses resolution 1. The external validator below also shows how resolution 2 would be evaluated, so the policy effect is explicit. No activity-only model can infer the emergency authorization from these traces.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Forbids both activities in one case regardless of their order. | Also present in the procedural choice and Log Skeleton. `nonsuccession` is insufficient because it only forbids one order. |
| R2 | Procedural ProcessTree, with DECLARE guards | The post-classification XOR has a reject branch with no approval, a low branch with Manager Approval, and a high branch with Compliance Approval. Add noncoexistence for Manager/Compliance and each approval/Reject. | The tree can express exactly one route-dependent approval and no approval on the reject branch. | DECLARE alone cannot condition “exactly one” on accepted versus rejected. Its rules express at-most-one and approval/reject exclusion; the tree supplies the route obligation. |
| R3 | Stateful event monitor; DECLARE approximation | `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`; the authoritative monitor tracks a pending request and rejects a second request or outcome before its response. | The monitor can tie each request to a later response and enforce the outcome boundary. | `altresponse` requires a response before another request, but by itself does not guarantee response before an outcome if a response is logged later. The monitor is authoritative. No adjacency is required, so `chainresponse` would be too strong. |
| R4 | DECLARE | `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | A response is required sometime after each escalation. | Does not require the opinion before activation; R5 is a separate ordering condition. |
| R5 | Separate ordered-event policy check (strict selected policy) | If escalation precedes activation, require a later opinion after escalation and strictly before activation. | This check can condition opinion-before-activation on a prior escalation without requiring opinions on cases that were never escalated. | Ordinary activity-only `precedence` cannot express that condition exactly; `precedence(Opinion, Activate)` would incorrectly require an opinion for every activation. |
| R6 | Separate authorization/data-aware policy check | Under the alternative carve-out only: if activation precedes the post-escalation opinion, require verified joint authorization at or before activation and opinion within 24 hours. | Authorization is external to the event log and cannot be inferred from activities or timestamps. | Conflicts with R5. It is not enabled by the selected strict policy; missing sidecar evidence yields policy-dependent/held, never an assumed exception. |
| R7 | DECLARE, redundantly Log Skeleton | `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)`; Log Skeleton `always_before={(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)}`. | Precedence means each escalation must have a screening somewhere earlier. | Both are activity-order constraints and do not prove screening results or validity. |
| R8 | DECLARE, redundantly procedural | `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` and `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)`; the route tree also keeps EDD off the low-risk route. | Noncoexistence forbids both activities in either order. | The second rule duplicates the at-most-one part of R2. |
| R9 | Resource-aware validation | Compare the resource on the applicable Verify Bank Account event with the applicable approval; compare Capture Master Data resource with Activate Vendor resource. | Actors are event attributes, not activity labels. | Activity-only DECLARE, trees, and skeletons cannot distinguish actors. Missing or ambiguous resource identity is unknown and fails closed for activation. |
| R10 | Log Skeleton, redundantly procedural | `activ_freq` allows `{0,1}` for Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor, and Release First Payment. Each route in the tree has each at most once. | Log Skeleton directly expresses allowed occurrence-count sets. | The backbone tree also enforces one occurrence, but activity projection must retain duplicates of backbone activities to detect them. |
| R11 | Stateful, timestamp-aware monitor | For each Change Bank Details after activation, require a later Suspend Vendor; for any later Release First Payment, require a Verify Bank Account after the relevant change and before that payment. | This is conditional on event order and requires tracking the latest change and later payment. | Generic `response(Change, Suspend)` would also constrain changes before activation and would not link re-verification to a later payment. |
| R12 | Temporal profile | Three supplied pairs with means and standard deviations in seconds; deviation threshold `zeta=2.0`. | Keeps expected duration separate from hard control flow. | Pairwise timing cannot establish authorization, causation, or which of multiple repeated events belongs together without an event-pairing rule. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

1. **Compact formal description.** Let `||` denote parallel composition, `→` sequence, and `×` exclusive choice:

   `Receive Onboarding Request → (Capture Master Data || Run Sanctions Screening || Verify Bank Account) → Classify Risk → (Reject Vendor → Close Onboarding × Manager Approval → Activate Vendor → Release First Payment → Close Onboarding × Enhanced Due Diligence → Compliance Approval → Activate Vendor → Release First Payment → Close Onboarding)`

2. **Self-contained PM4Py ProcessTree code:**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def activity(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)


def operator(op, *children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=op)
    node.children.extend(children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
    return node


procedural_model = operator(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    activity("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    operator(
        Operator.PARALLEL,
        activity("Capture Master Data"),
        activity("Run Sanctions Screening"),
        activity("Verify Bank Account"),
    ),
    activity("Classify Risk"),
    operator(
        Operator.XOR,
        operator(
            Operator.SEQUENCE,
            activity("Reject Vendor"),
            activity("Close Onboarding"),
        ),
        operator(
            Operator.SEQUENCE,
            activity("Manager Approval"),
            activity("Activate Vendor"),
            activity("Release First Payment"),
            activity("Close Onboarding"),
        ),
        operator(
            Operator.SEQUENCE,
            activity("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
            activity("Compliance Approval"),
            activity("Activate Vendor"),
            activity("Release First Payment"),
            activity("Close Onboarding"),
        ),
    ),
)
```

3. **Behavior and scope.** The parallel operator allows any interleaving of the three checks after receipt and before classification. The XOR makes rejection, low-risk acceptance, and high-risk acceptance mutually exclusive. Each accepted route has exactly its applicable approval, and activation, first payment, and close follow in order. The reject route has no approval, activation, or payment.

   Clarification cycles and sanctions/legal events are deliberately excluded from this backbone tree because their placement is cross-cutting: clarification can recur without being adjacent to other work, and an opinion may be after escalation but must be before activation under the selected strict policy. They are evaluated on the full trace by DECLARE and stateful checks. For procedural conformance, project the input trace onto the tree's activity labels; validate the removed events separately. This is a deliberate hybrid boundary, not an assertion that those omitted events are optional in policy.

   Change Bank Details and Suspend Vendor are also excluded from the onboarding backbone. R11 applies only to post-activation changes and conditionally affects later payments, so the dedicated stateful monitor is authoritative. The tree models the ordinary first-payment path and does not try to encode this post-activation sub-process.


| Encoded rule | Direction and semantics |
|---|---|
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | For each request, a response must occur later and before another request. It is not a chain rule: unrelated activities may intervene. The external monitor additionally requires the response before an outcome. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every escalation requires a later opinion. It does not say that the opinion must immediately follow the escalation or precede activation. |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Every escalation must have a screening earlier in the trace. This is the reverse-direction idea from response: precedence constrains when the target may occur, while response constrains what must eventually follow the source. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Reject and activate may not both occur, regardless of order. `nonsuccession` would only prohibit Activate after Reject, allowing the opposite order, so it is not strong enough for R1. |
| `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | The two approval activities cannot co-occur. Combined with the process tree's route choice this supports exactly one on acceptance. |
| `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` | The high-risk due diligence and manager approval cannot co-occur. |
| `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Reject Vendor)` and `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Reject Vendor)` | Neither approval may appear in a rejected case, whichever event comes first. |

There is intentionally no `chainresponse`: it would require Receive Clarification to be the very next event after Request Clarification, which R3 does not require. The DECLARE metadata values are explicit and syntactically shaped as requested; `support` and `confidence` do not make a rule true by themselves and must not be treated as policy evidence.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": set(),
    "always_before": {
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"),
    },
    "always_after": set(),
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Reject Vendor"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Reject Vendor"),
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

The orientation of `always_before` is `(required earlier activity, constrained later activity)`: Run Sanctions Screening must have occurred before Sanctions Escalation. `never_together` pairs are unordered in meaning; each listed pair forbids co-occurrence in either order. The frequency sets express at most one occurrence, including zero for routes where the activity is not used.

No equivalence or direct-follow pair is asserted. In particular, the three checks can be concurrent, and “follows” in the backbone does not always mean “directly follows” in a full trace with cross-cutting work. The screening/escalation pair duplicates DECLARE R7. The never-together pairs duplicate R1/R2/R8 guards and do not encode the conditional approval obligation by themselves. This skeleton does not express actor identity, R5's conditional precedence, R6 authorization, or R11's change/reverification state.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200, 129600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

The conversion is hours multiplied by 3,600 seconds. A pair is flagged when its absolute deviation from the mean exceeds `zeta * standard_deviation`; equality at two standard deviations is not over the threshold. Pairing should use the next valid response/opinion/payment for the triggering event, while respecting the clarification and escalation event matching rules.

These are normative monitoring expectations, not hard gates. A late opinion can still satisfy the strict ordering rule if it precedes activation, while attracting a temporal warning. Conversely, an opinion arriving quickly after activation does not cure an R5 ordering failure under the selected strict policy. Missing an endpoint means the temporal pair cannot be scored; the independent response/order check determines whether that absence is a violation.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

The checks below use the full event log, not the procedural projection. Each event needs a case identifier, activity name, timestamp, and stable resource identifier. `org:resource` can stand in for a stable identifier in the supplied test cases. In production, use immutable person IDs rather than display names.

```python
from collections import defaultdict

APPROVALS = {"Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"}
OUTCOMES = {
    "Reject Vendor",
    "Activate Vendor",
    "Release First Payment",
    "Close Onboarding",
}


def check_clarification_cycles(events):
    """Return (status, reason); status is pass, fail, or not evaluated."""
    pending = False
    for e in sorted(events, key=lambda x: x["time:timestamp"]):
        a = e["concept:name"]
        if a == "Request Clarification":
            if pending:
                return "fail", "A second clarification request occurred before the response."
            pending = True
        elif a == "Receive Clarification":
            pending = False
        elif a in OUTCOMES and pending:
            return "fail", "An outcome occurred while clarification was pending."
    if pending:
        return "fail", "The trace ended with an unanswered clarification request."
    return "pass", "No pending clarification crossed a request or outcome boundary."


def check_segregation_of_duties(events):
    """R9 checks. Missing identity, time, or required pairing evidence is unknown/fail-closed."""
    unknown = []
    violations = []
    ordered = sorted(events, key=lambda x: x.get("time:timestamp"))
    verifications = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account"]
    captures = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data"]
    approvals = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] in APPROVALS]
    activations = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]

    for approval in approvals:
        prior = [v for v in verifications
                 if v.get("time:timestamp") is not None
                 and approval.get("time:timestamp") is not None
                 and v["time:timestamp"] < approval["time:timestamp"]]
        if not prior:
            unknown.append("No uniquely applicable earlier bank verification for approval.")
            continue
        verification = prior[-1]
        vr, ar = verification.get("org:resource"), approval.get("org:resource")
        if not vr or not ar:
            unknown.append("Missing resource identity on verification or approval.")
        elif vr == ar:
            violations.append("Verify Bank Account and applicable approval share a resource.")

    for activation in activations:
        if not captures:
            unknown.append("No Capture Master Data event to compare with activation.")
            continue
        for capture in captures:
            cr, ar = capture.get("org:resource"), activation.get("org:resource")
            if not cr or not ar:
                unknown.append("Missing resource identity on capture or activation.")
            elif cr == ar:
                violations.append("Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor share a resource.")

    if violations:
        return "fail", violations
    if unknown:
        return "not evaluated", unknown  # Hold activation/payment for evidence; do not count as pass.
    return "pass", "All applicable resource comparisons are separated."


def check_legal_gate(events, case_id, policy="R5_strict", auth_records=None):
    """R4/R5 and optional R6 exception. auth_records are trusted external evidence."""
    auth_records = auth_records or {}
    ordered = sorted(events, key=lambda x: x.get("time:timestamp"))
    escalations = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Sanctions Escalation"]
    opinions = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"]
    activations = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]

    for escalation in escalations:
        et = escalation.get("time:timestamp")
        if et is None:
            return "not evaluated", "Missing escalation timestamp."
        later_opinions = [o for o in opinions
                          if o.get("time:timestamp") is not None
                          and o["time:timestamp"] > et]
        if not later_opinions:
            return "fail", "Escalation has no later legal opinion (R4)."
        opinion = later_opinions[0]
        for activation in activations:
            at = activation.get("time:timestamp")
            if at is None:
                return "not evaluated", "Missing activation timestamp."
            if at > et and opinion["time:timestamp"] > at:
                if policy == "R5_strict":
                    return "fail", "Opinion followed activation after escalation (R5)."
                if policy == "R6_exception":
                    evidence = auth_records.get(case_id)
                    if not evidence or evidence.get("verified") is not True:
                        return "not evaluated", "Emergency joint authorization is not evidenced."
                    approvers = set(evidence.get("approvers", []))
                    authorized_at = evidence.get("authorized_at")
                    if len(approvers) < 2 or authorized_at is None or authorized_at > at:
                        return "fail", "Joint authorization was absent, insufficient, or late."
                    if opinion["time:timestamp"] - at > hours(24):
                        return "fail", "Post-activation opinion exceeded 24 hours."
                else:
                    raise ValueError("Unknown policy")
    return "pass", "Legal response and selected ordering policy are satisfied."


def hours(n):
    from datetime import timedelta
    return timedelta(hours=n)


def check_post_activation_bank_changes(events):
    ordered = sorted(events, key=lambda x: x.get("time:timestamp"))
    activations = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]
    activation_time = min((e.get("time:timestamp") for e in activations), default=None)
    changes = [e for e in ordered
               if e["concept:name"] == "Change Bank Details"
               and activation_time is not None
               and e.get("time:timestamp") is not None
               and e["time:timestamp"] > activation_time]
    suspensions = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Suspend Vendor"]
    verifications = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account"]
    payments = [e for e in ordered if e["concept:name"] == "Release First Payment"]

    for change in changes:
        ct = change.get("time:timestamp")
        if ct is None:
            return "not evaluated", "Missing bank-change timestamp."
        if not any(s.get("time:timestamp") is not None and s["time:timestamp"] > ct
                   for s in suspensions):
            return "fail", "Post-activation bank change has no later suspension."
        later_payments = [p for p in payments
                          if p.get("time:timestamp") is not None
                          and p["time:timestamp"] > ct]
        for payment in later_payments:
            pt = payment["time:timestamp"]
            if not any(v.get("time:timestamp") is not None
                       and ct < v["time:timestamp"] < pt for v in verifications):
                return "fail", "A later first payment lacks a post-change verification."
    return "pass", "No triggered post-activation bank-change obligation is open."
```

`check_segregation_of_duties` assumes the latest verification before each approval is the applicable verification. Where multiple checks can be associated ambiguously, production data should include a check/case sub-process identifier and explicit approval-to-check linkage; missing linkage is unknown and must be reviewed. Capture-to-activation compares the relevant capture in the case to each activation. Missing case IDs prevent safe grouping and must also yield unknown/fail-closed.

The emergency sidecar should be keyed by case and contain verifiable approver identities, the authorization instant, and a verification/audit reference. The code requires two distinct approvers and authorization no later than activation. Under the selected R5 policy, the exception branch is not used. Under R6, absent authorization evidence returns `not evaluated` and the operational gate must hold the case rather than assume permission.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated enforcement:** R1 is guarded by the ProcessTree choice, DECLARE noncoexistence, and Log Skeleton never-together. R7 is in DECLARE and `always_before`. R8 and approval/reject exclusions are repeated in DECLARE, the tree, and the skeleton. This is useful defense in depth, but deployment should report one policy finding with the enforcing layers listed, not three unrelated incidents.
- **Loops and post-activation work:** A single approval/activation/payment route is coherent for the stable onboarding backbone. Repeated clarification is not naturally represented by that tree; projecting cross-cutting activities and separately monitoring each request avoids forcing a misplaced loop. R11 may create later Suspend, Verify, and Release events. A strict one-shot tree for the full log would reject legitimate post-activation behavior, so those events belong to the stateful extension.
- **Vacuity:** Response and precedence constraints pass when their source/target activities do not occur. A trace with no sanctions escalation says nothing about R4/R5. A rejected case has no applicable approval-resource comparison. Record “not triggered” separately from evidence of positive compliance when reporting results.
- **Conditionality blind spots:** Activity-only precedence cannot express “opinion before activation only if escalation occurred” without either under-enforcing or rejecting ordinary activations. Likewise, DECLARE response alone does not tie an unanswered request to the outcome boundary. The external monitors are authoritative for these conditional rules.
- **Missing case and resource attributes:** Identical activity sequences can satisfy or violate R9 depending on actors. Case IDs, stable resource IDs, event timestamps, and (for ambiguous repeated checks) linkage IDs are necessary. Missing values must not be imputed from activity order. Emergency authorization is explicitly absent from the test log and requires a trusted sidecar.
- **Timestamp-only limits:** A timestamp establishes observed order and elapsed duration, not that an authorization was valid, that an opinion addressed the relevant escalation, or that an actor had the required role. Coarse or tied timestamps also make strict “before” ambiguous; tied opinion and activation times should be treated as unresolved unless a reliable sequence number disambiguates them.
- **Temporal ambiguity:** Repeated events need a defined matching policy. Matching a request to a later response after a second request would conceal an R3 defect; pair each request with its next response before the next request. Temporal profile deviations are warnings and do not override hard ordering constraints.
- **Metadata risk:** DECLARE `support` and `confidence` describe model/statistical metadata in some workflows. Values of 1.0 here are explicit placeholders requested for the dictionary; they do not establish stakeholder approval, prove a policy, or justify relaxing a violation.
- **Model/version conflict:** R5 and R6 cannot both be unconditional hard rules. Store the chosen policy version with each conformance result and surface any emergency claim separately. Do not merge them into a single ambiguous “legal opinion required” status.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Use the ProcessTree above as the canonical representation of the stable control-flow backbone. Use the strict R5 legal gate as the authoritative policy for the current implementation, with R6 available only after an explicit policy decision and verified sidecar authorization. Treat resource/data checks as hard activation controls; treat R12 deviations as warnings.

Run checks in this order:

1. Validate event completeness, case grouping, stable resource identifiers, and timestamps; mark missing evidence as unknown.
2. Check the projected trace against the procedural tree, retaining duplicate backbone events so at-most-once violations remain visible.
3. Run DECLARE and Log Skeleton checks on the full activity trace, then deduplicate overlapping findings by underlying requirement.
4. Run the clarification, legal-gate, resource segregation, and post-activation bank-change state monitors using full event attributes and authorization sidecar data.
5. Calculate temporal-profile deviations after event pairing; report them as warnings without converting them into control-flow failures.

Show findings by requirement and source layer, including whether the rule was triggered, passed, failed, or could not be evaluated. For T6-like cases, state the policy version and separate “R5 ordering failure” from “R6 exception evidence missing.” A missing attribute is not a pass, and a timing warning does not erase or create a hard policy violation.
