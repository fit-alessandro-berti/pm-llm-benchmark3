| A4 | Shared approval queue; remove one local supervisor | Separates *decision* from *local context*; creates a serial approval gate with pooled, interchangeable decision-makers | Wait before `Supervisor Approval`; approval exception/rework rate; distinct approvers per case; ratio approval-handoffs to implementation-handoffs | Approval handovers  (4249 per 100 in management's own table); decision traceability  |
| A5 | "All teams can pull specialist advice from a shared queue" | Changes handover *semantics* from push to pull; breaks the invariant that a handover is a resource transition | Log completeness: queue-entry / claim / advice-released events; pull latency; age of unclaimed advice | **Measurement hazard**: pull semantics can make handover counts look "reduced" without any real reduction |
| A6 | Keep Intake + simple Eligibility local | Protects the high-volume, low-complexity majority ( majority of cases) where the new model is genuinely faster | Median cycle time for low-complexity band; time-to-first-touch; intake rework | Improves the **average**; little effect on the tail |
| A7 | Remove embedded local technical specialists | Destroys the informal early-advice channel that currently *prevents* unnecessary specialist referrals | Referral rate per case by team; share of technical activities still executed locally (`local_expertise_flag`); time to first technical activity | **Positive feedback loop**: less local advice  more referrals  more queue load  more pressure to centralise  less local capability |
| A8 | Concentrate scarce expertise (Legal/Risk + difficult QC) in one pool | Raises key-person and single-point-of-failure risk; increases *blast radius* of absence | Distinct eligible resources per `required_specialty`; HHI/top-1 share of pool touches; simulated "pool unavailable" coverage | Fewer *observed* cross-team handovers (63) but **larger** blast radius if the pool is unavailable |
| A9 | Standardised decisions (management's stated benefit) | Reduces variance in decisions but removes local context and local precedent learning | Decision-consistency proxy: variance of approval outcomes across teams for identical case features; rework after approval | Helps simple/medium cases; hurts complex/bespoke cases |

## 1.3 Arithmetic reconciliation of management's own numbers (the core tension)

Per 100 cases, using the two handover tables as given:

| Quantity | Current | Management's post-change estimate |  |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total handovers (all types) | 343 | 359 | **+4.7%** |
| Handovers crossing an org/team boundary | 15 (legal/risk 9 + cross-team 6) | 56 (pull) / 92 (push) | **+3.7× / +6.1×** |
| Specialist-touch events (tech in/out + legal/risk) | 76 | 89 | **+17.1%** |
| Legal/Risk referrals | 9 | 12 | **+33%** |
| Aggregate handover waiting (freq × median wait) | 588.3 h | 722.2 h | **+22.8%** (+1.34 h/case) |
| Handover-associated rework events (at constant per-type rework rates) | 21.0 | 22.1 | **+5.2%** |

**Interpretation.** The estimate table is *not* internally inconsistent for the **median** — +1.34 h of handover waiting per case is small against a ~7.5-day median cycle time, so median cycle time can improve modestly via lower-complexity fast lanes. It *is* internally inconsistent in three places:

1. **Capacity.** ~1,365 items/month against ~1,020 items/month of modelled technical capacity (3 FTE  340 items/month/FTE, derived from 756 referrals/month at 74% mean utilisation) is a **service rate below arrival rate**; no finite wait estimate (4.0 h) exists in that regime.
2. **Legal/Risk.** 93%/91% utilisation cannot absorb +33% volume *plus* triage *plus* difficult QC. Management's 9.6 h  10.5 h is the least credible number in the table; Kingman-style sensitivity at   0.93 implies wait ratios multiply sharply for a few points of utilisation.
3. **Definition of "fewer handovers."** Fewer *local-specialist loops* is not fewer handovers. PM4Py's handover-of-work network will show **more** handover transactions and a **sparser, more central** topology.

**Three reinforcing feedback loops to monitor explicitly**

- **Loop 1 — referral inflation:** less local informal advice  more TSS referrals (3641 and rising)  longer queues  more pressure to route everything away  further loss of local capability.
- **Loop 2 — measurement illusion:** pooled resources make the handover graph look cleaner  management concludes "fewer handovers"  no capacity added  queues saturate  the illusion reverses only after 2–3 arrival cycles.
- **Loop 3 — fairness drift:** North/South retain informal advice and bypass the queue; East has none and fully escalates  East absorbs the queue  East's p90 deteriorates while the global average improves  partial withdrawal of East  complexity migrates to whoever remains.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency and queue convoys.** Kingman-style waiting time grows non-linearly as utilisation  1 (W  /(1)). At   0.93, a further 3–4 points of utilisation can multiply wait by 2–4×. The *mean* case is short and simple; the *tail* is where the pool saturates. **Hidden because** the median case never enters the congested queue, and management's 4.0 h estimate is a median, not a p90. **Detect with:** p90/p95/p99 of queue residency and case cycle time, oldest-item age, and per-item wait histograms by arrival week (queue growth is visible as a *level shift in wait vs. arrival date*).

2. **High-complexity starvation and priority inversion.** Routing sends *all* high-complexity cases to one queue. If the queue serves FIFO, complex cases queue behind simple ones; if it serves priority-attribute order, cases needing *multiple* specialties accumulate. **Hidden because** complex cases are a minority (20–25%), so portfolio medians improve while the complex segment deteriorates 40–60%. **Detect with:** complexity-stratified p50/p90, and "time in queue × complexity" (a weighted tail metric such as p90 of *work-content-weighted* delay).

3. **Local knowledge loss (context degradation).** The mechanism behind future rework is not the specialist; it is the case notes. Cross-team handovers already show 18% rework because notes are inconsistent across locations; moving the specialist to a central desk removes the *last* moment at which local context could be checked. **Hidden because** rework shows up 2–6 weeks later, and rework may be masked by a shorter specialist wait. **Detect with:** rework rate conditional on handover type, rework after TSS vs local, note-completeness proxies (fields populated before referral), and rework in cases the local team resolved without referral.

4. **Fairness drift across teams and customer segments.** North/South keep informal pre-advice (their local staff still talk to their techs informally, or to each other) and so enter the queue less often; East — already worst on every metric — has the highest referral density and no informal channel, so it absorbs the queue. Meanwhile language/regional accommodation is not in the routing rules, so those segments get more handovers and longer waits. **Hidden because** portfolio SLA miss and portfolio CSAT can both *improve* while East's and the language segments' deteriorate. **Detect with:** team × complexity × segment stratification; referral rate per case by team (not by case volume); "time from case start to queue entry" by team; and segment-level p90.

5. **Learning and skill decay (a 2–4 quarter risk).** If all hard cases are routed away, local staff stop accumulating technical judgement, local teams lose the ability to triage, and the case mix that *does* stay local becomes more trivial. **Hidden because** throughput, cost, and cycle time all look fine while the capability erodes; the effect shows up as a *later* wave of rising rework and rising referral rates, and is irreversible on a 6–12 month horizon. **Detect with:** share of technical activities performed locally, count of local resources with technical repertoires, time-to-competence, and the *trend* of local technical referral rates for cases that used to be resolved locally.

6. **Accountability and role ambiguity.** Pooled approval and pooled difficult QC remove the identifiable decision owner; if approval and QC sit in one queue, the same unit decides and verifies (self-review). **Hidden because** aggregate decision consistency improves and "standardisation" is measured as success; the failure surfaces as un-attributable rework, appeals, and inability to answer "who decided?". **Detect with:** `discover_organizational_roles` before/after, approver dispersion per case, share of cases without a logged accountable owner, exception/appeal rates, and rework on approval decisions.

7. **Exception handling degradation.** The long tail of bespoke cases (legal/risk, difficult QC, multi-specialty) previously got *improvisation* from a local expert who could deviate. A queue with rules and standard decisions cannot: it either waits or decides by default. **Hidden because** exceptions are a small share of cases; their harm shows up as a few catastrophic cases, not an average. **Detect with:** singleton-variant rate, exception-path frequency, queue-age outliers, appeal rate, and "rework after decision on non-standard path" rates.

8. **Resilience: fewer visible handovers, larger blast radius.** The drop in cross-team handovers (63 per 100) is a symptom of *concentration*, not redundancy. One pool member's absence or departure now blocks all teams; previously an absent local tech affected one team. **Hidden because** the metric that improves (cross-team handovers) is a *proxy for healthy redundancy* being eliminated; the risk event is rare and usually absent from the pilot window. **Detect with:** eligible-resource counts per specialty, HHI, simulated absence coverage, and a pre-registered "pool down" continuity test.

9. **Work displacement and queue migration.** Cases may be pushed back to local teams ("this isn't actually specialist work") or to other teams to keep queues moving. This hides centralisation cost in the local teams' logs. **Hidden because** the central queue looks disciplined while the cost appears as local overload, lower local CSAT, or informal escalation. **Detect with:** inbound referrals to local teams from the shared queue, local resource time spent on cases outside their segment, and "reassignment_count" distribution.

10. **Process-log and semantic drift.** The log schema itself changes (pooled resources, pull queues, new queue events). If the pre/post logs are not semantically aligned, *any* apparent change may be a measurement artefact — including the favourable ones. **Hidden because** process mining is trusted as objective. **Detect with:** schema-coverage metrics, handover-network density drift checks against the unchanged local segments (Intake/Eligibility) as a control, and versioned metric definitions.

| **P3 — Decision & scale review** | 27 to 30 | Gate review against pre-registered thresholds; go / adjust / partial rollback | Explicit decision point with data, not averages |

## 5.2 Scope, comparison group, windows

- **Pilot scope:** all three teams participate in *randomisation*, but rollout intensity differs: **East first** (520 cases/month, highest benefit, highest current pain, no informal-capacity advantage — the natural early-adopter and the biggest test of the fairness hypothesis); North and South start at 20–30% of eligible cases with a retained local technical liaison. Do **not** start with a whole team switched over — it destroys the comparison.
- **Comparison group:** (a) **case-level control** (randomised non-routed cases) — primary; (b) **non-pilot teams** as a secondary difference-in-differences control for secular trends; (c) **Intake and simple Eligibility** as a *within-log* control unaffected by the change (a "synthetic control" for measurement drift).
- **Observation windows:** baseline **8 weeks pre** (plus the same calendar months a year earlier to control for seasonality); in-pilot **13 weeks**; post-pilot follow-up **+6 months** for learning/attrition effects (the effects most likely to be missed); queue metrics reported **weekly** from day 1.
- **Power (be explicit):** East  121 cases/week  1,570 pilot-eligible cases over 13 weeks. For rework at p  0.115, detecting a **1 pp** difference needs 16,000 cases per arm; a **3 pp** difference needs 1,800. Therefore the pilot can detect **3 pp** rework effects, **not** 1 pp. Pre-register that small effects are *undetectable* and rely on leading indicators (L1–L18) plus the 6-month follow-up; do not interpret a null result as "no effect".

## 5.3 Mandatory segmentation (every metric, every report)

`team` (North/South/East) × `case_complexity` (low/med/high) × `required_specialty` (tech/legal/risk/security) × `customer_segment` (incl. language-needing and regional-accommodation segments) × `priority` × `location`. Minimum reporting cell: p50 **and** p90 cycle time, queue wait, rework, SLA miss, CSAT, and — for fairness — the **maxmin gap** across teams and segments with its trend.

## 5.4 Guardrail metrics and pre-registered adjustment/rollback triggers

| Guardrail | Threshold (any one triggers review; two triggers action) | Action ladder |
|---|---|---|
| TSS p50 queue wait | >8 h for 2 consecutive weeks | (1) re-tune routing threshold, (2) triage-and-refer triage out of the pool, (3) add capacity, (4) restore local tech |
| TSS p90 queue wait | >16 h for 2 consecutive weeks | (1)+(2) mandatory, (3) within 2 weeks |
| Legal/Risk p50 wait | >16 h | Split legal/risk from technical queue; add 1 FTE; do **not** absorb triage into TSS |
| Portfolio rework | > baseline +2 pp over 4 weeks | Audit context transfer; reintroduce local liaison; freeze further rollout |
| Any team's p90 cycle time | > baseline +15% | Case-level review of that team's cases; suspend rollout for that team |
| Fairness gap (East p90 vs North/South p90; or segment gap) | widening >3 d or SLA gap >3 pp | Restore local technical liaison; review queue priority rules; escalate to steering committee |
| Local technical activity share | <30% for 4 weeks | Re-instate one embedded tech per team (ring-fence) |
| Approver traceability | <98% of approval cases with a logged accountable owner | **Hard stop** — no further rollout until fixed |
| Attrition intent / actual attrition among retained local technical staff | >10% (annualised intent) or 1 unexpected departure | Capacity and knowledge plan; consider partial rollback of team |
| Pool concentration (top-1 share of touches) | >25% | Cross-train; add second qualified member |
| Variant singleton share | >25% | Routing-rule review |
| Customer complaints tagged "no local expert" / "passed around" | >2× baseline | Qualitative review; adjust routing; consider partial rollback |

**Rollback options (ordered by preference):** (1) **soft rollback** — revert routing rules to local routing for specific specialties or complexity bands, keeping the platform and logging; (2) **partial rollback** — reinstate ring-fenced local technical capacity and split the queues; (3) **hard rollback** — full return to local model. Decide the trigger thresholds **before** the pilot starts; do not renegotiate them once results are visible.

## 5.5 Staff feedback collection

- **Weekly 5-item pulse** (clarity of ownership, queue fairness, workload, perceived customer impact, intent to stay) with free text; report by team.
- **Advice capture as first-class data:** local staff record informal technical advice as a structured "advice ticket" (this is both a learning instrument and the early-warning metric L8; it also becomes the basis for the case-note standard).
- **Joint "learning council"** (2 local staff + 2 pool staff + 1 supervisor) fortnightly, with a published decision log; measure *decisions implemented* as a learning-loop metric.
- **Structured interviews** at weeks 2, 6 and 12 with, separately: local caseworkers, local technical staff, shared-service staff, supervisors, and customer-facing roles (language/regional accommodation issues surface here, not in the log).
- **Incident review** of every case that exceeds a queue-age threshold, and of every instance where a local team bypassed the queue — bypass counts are a fairness and shadow-capacity indicator.
- **Anonymous fairness survey** on perceived queue priority by team, plus per-segment complaint taxonomy review.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: MODIFY, then PILOT FIRST — and do not proceed to full rollout on the current design.**

**Why not proceed as proposed**

1. **The stated benefit is not supported by the plan's own numbers.** Handovers per 100 cases rise (343  359), cross-boundary handovers rise 3.7–6×, and aggregate handover waiting rises ~23%. "Fewer handovers" is true only under a favourable accounting (fewer *distinct specialists* touched), and the handover-of-work network will show the opposite.
2. **The capacity model fails.** Modelled TSS load of ~1,365 items/month against ~1,020 items/month of capacity on unchanged staffing is a service rate below arrival rate. At that ratio, no median wait estimate (including 4.0 h) exists; queues grow without bound and the tail — not the mean — collapses. Legal/Risk at 93%/91% utilisation absorbing +33% volume *plus* triage *plus* difficult QC in one pool is the most likely point of failure.
3. **The riskiest design choice is the one management is most confident about.** Co-locating technical work, legal/risk triage, and difficult QC in one queue maximises the chance that the scarcest capacity is consumed by the least specialised task, and it is the direct cause of the staff-identified bottleneck.
4. **Two of the four countervailing risks are unmitigated and unmeasurable today:** local expertise loss and accountability diffusion require a learning and traceability design that does not yet exist.

**Required modifications before piloting**

- **Keep a ring-fenced local technical liaison in each team** (~0.5 FTE, one per team, or 1 per 400–500 cases/month) whose job is informal advice, referral quality, and note quality — not case throughput. This is cheap insurance against both Loop 1 (referral inflation) and learning decay, and it is the single most likely cause of the difference between the central and adverse scenarios.
- **Separate the pools:** technical work, legal/risk review, and difficult QC must have separate queues, separate SLAs, and separate capacity, so that triage load cannot consume technical capacity.
- **Size capacity from measured service times** (`get_service_time` on the pre-period, not estimates): target 75% utilisation at the *measured* arrival rate implied by the routing rules, plus a documented cross-training/reserve buffer.
- **Calibrate the routing threshold by offline replay** on the historical log (measure TSS arrival rate and p90 wait as a function of the complexity threshold); publish the sensitivity curve to the steering committee before locking the rule.
- **Instrument the log** (queue entry/claim/advice release, routing rule id, accountable owner, advice ticket) **and** decide the push-vs-pull semantics explicitly; otherwise the central effect will be unmeasurable and the "fewer handovers" narrative will go unchallenged.
- **Retain one local supervisor per team** until the shared approval queue has demonstrably 95% traceable decisions and no deterioration in approval exception rate.

**Conditions attached to the recommendation**
Proceed beyond pilot only if, over the pilot + 6-month follow-up: (a) TSS p90 wait  2× design and utilisation 85% sustained; (b) portfolio rework not above baseline +1 pp **and** complex-band rework not above baseline +2 pp; (c) no team's p90 cycle time up more than 10% and the East-vs-North/South fairness gap not widening; (d) local technical activity share 35% and the advice-ticket capture rate stable/rising; (e) 100% approval traceability; (f) legal/Risk wait below 16 h with a separate pool. Otherwise: adjust (split queues, retune thresholds, add capacity, restore local liaison) or partially roll back.

**What management must not infer from early averages**

- **Do not read the first 4–6 weeks' median cycle time as success.** The shared queue starts empty; a queue's wait estimate is only valid after ~2–3 arrival cycles (6–12 weeks). Early "good" waits are a backlog-drain artefact and predict the opposite of the steady state.
- **Do not read early specialist utilisation as failure.** Pooled specialists have a ramp-up and learning curve; utilisation typically *falls* in weeks 1–4, then recovers above 90%. Both early directions are uninformative.
- **Do not read a sparser handover network as fewer handovers.** Pooling shrinks the graph (fewer resources, hub topology) while per-case handovers may rise. Use handovers per case and cross-boundary transitions, not graph density.
- **Do not read improved portfolio SLA/CSAT as fairness.** Segment and team distributions must be inspected; the central scenario produces exactly this trap (portfolio improves, East and language segments deteriorate).
- **Do not read low rework in month 1 as quality.** Rework appears in the log only when cases come back (2–6 week lag) and is partly suppressed while queues are short.
- **Do not read fewer cross-team handovers as resilience.** That is the signature of concentration, not redundancy.
- **Do not read pilot throughput as capability.** Learning effects surface over 2–4 quarters; the pilot window is blind to them.
- **Do not generalise from a pilot team's approval to network-wide effects.** Cases and complexity migrate; a queue that works for East can shift the entire portfolio's queue pressure to North/South once coverage widens.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
"""
Before/after process-mining analysis for the shared-service restructuring.
Design notes:
 - VERSION TOLERANCE: PM4Py 2.6-2.7 differ in return signatures (graph vs (graph, measures)),
   in filter argument names, and in parameter constants. Helpers below normalise this.
 - SEMANTIC ALIGNMENT: pre and post logs MUST use the same metric definitions. Where the
   shared-service model introduces queue events (QUEUE_ENTRY / QUEUE_CLAIM / ADVICE_RELEASED),
   analyse the "business flow" view (exclude queue mechanics) for pre/post comparability and
   the "operational" view (include them) for queue management.
"""

import warnings
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")

COL = dict(case="case", activity="concept:name", ts="time:timestamp",
           resource="org:resource", team="team", location="location",
           specialty="required_specialty", priority="priority",
           complexity="case_complexity", segment="customer_segment",
           handover_reason="handover_reason", reassign="reassignment_count",
           local_exp="local_expertise_flag", outcome="outcome", rework="rework_flag")

QUEUE_ACTIVITIES = {"QUEUE_ENTRY", "QUEUE_CLAIM", "ADVICE_RELEASED"}


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- load
def load_and_format(path, start=None, end=None):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df[COL["ts"]] = pd.to_datetime(df[COL["ts"]], errors="coerce")
    df = df.dropna(subset=[COL["ts"]]).sort_values([COL["case"], COL["ts"]])
    if start is not None:
        df = df[df[COL["ts"]] >= pd.Timestamp(start)]
    if end is not None:
        df = df[df[COL["ts"]] <= pd.Timestamp(end)]

    fmt_kwargs = dict(case_id=COL["case"], activity_id=COL["activity"],
                      timestamp_key=COL["ts"], resource_id=COL["resource"])
    try:                                    # PM4Py >= 2.7 supports team_id/location_id
        df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, team_id=COL["team"],
                                     location_id=COL["location"], **fmt_kwargs)
    except TypeError:
        df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, **fmt_kwargs)
    return df.reset_index(drop=True)


def business_view(df):
    """Flow comparable with the pre-period: drop shared-queue mechanics."""
    return df[~df[COL["activity"]].isin(QUEUE_ACTIVITIES)].copy()


# ------------------------------------------------------------------ generic PM4Py wrappers
def _unwrap(res):
    """Some PM4Py functions return (graph, measures); normalise to the graph."""
    if isinstance(res, tuple) and len(res) == 2 and isinstance(res[0], nx.Graph):
        return res[0]
    return res


def handover_network(df, threshold=0.05):
    return _unwrap(pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df, threshold=threshold))


def working_together_network(df, threshold=0.05):
    return _unwrap(pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df, threshold=threshold))


def subcontracting_network(df, threshold=0.05):
    return _unwrap(pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df, threshold=threshold))


def activity_similarity(df):
    """PM4Py similarity matrix + an explicit Jaccard re-implementation (ordering-safe)."""
    try:
        sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
    except Exception:
        sim = None
    sets = df.groupby(COL["resource"])[COL["activity"]].agg(set)
    resources = list(sets.index)
    mat = np.zeros((len(resources), len(resources)))
    for i, a in enumerate(resources):
        for j, b in enumerate(resources):
            u = sets[a] | sets[b]
            mat[i, j] = len(sets[a] & sets[b]) / len(u) if u else 0.0
    return sim, pd.DataFrame(mat, index=resources, columns=resources)


def organisational_roles(df, threshold=0.65):
    """Returns list of roles (each a list of resources)."""
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df, threshold=threshold)
    return [{"size": len(r), "resources": sorted(r)} for r in roles]


def dfg_and_performance(df, variant_filter=None):
    dfg = _unwrap(pm4py.discover_dfg(df))
    perf = None
    try:
        perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df, variant_filter=variant_filter)[1]
    except Exception:
        pass
    return dfg, perf


def variants_of(df):
    return pm4py.get_variants(df)


def filter_variants(df, variants):
    return pm4py.filter_variants(df, variants)


def filter_attrs(df, mapping, mode="all"):
    """mapping: {'case_complexity': ['high'], 'team': ['Team East']}"""
    try:
        from pm4py.parameters import EventAttributeFilterModes
        m = EventAttributeFilterModes.ALL if mode == "all" else EventAttributeFilterModes.ANY
        return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, mapping, case_id_key=COL["case"], mode=m)
    except Exception:
        out = df.copy()
        for k, vals in mapping.items():
            out = out[out[k].isin(vals)]
        return out


def rework_cases(df):
    return pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(
        df, parameters={pm4py.parameters.PARAMETER_KEY: COL["activity"]})


def temporal_features(df):
    from pm4py.parameters import PARAMETERS_TEMPORAL
    from pm4py.features import TemporalFeatures
    return pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df, parameters={PARAMETERS_TEMPORAL: [TemporalFeatures.FREQUENCY,
                                              TemporalFeatures.MEAN_DURATION,
                                              TemporalFeatures.VARIANTS]})


# ------------------------------------------------------- custom measures (the ones that decide)
def service_and_waiting(df):
    """PM4Py helper first; explicit computation as the auditable fallback."""
    try:
        d = pm4py.get_service_time(df, parameters={
            pm4py.parameters.PARAM_SERVICE_TIME: True,
            pm4py.parameters.PARAM_SOIJOURN_TIME: True})
    except Exception:
        d = df.copy()
    # Explicit per-activity span and inter-activity gap (queue residency proxy)
    act = (df.groupby([COL["case"], COL["activity"]])[COL["ts"]]
             .agg(start="min", end="max").reset_index())
    gaps = []
    for cid, g in act.groupby(COL["case"]):
        g = g.sort_values("start")
        prev_end = None
        for _, row in g.iterrows():
            gaps.append({"case": cid, "activity": row[COL["activity"]],
                         "gap_before_h": (row["start"] - prev_end).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                         if prev_end is not None else np.nan,
                         "service_h": (row["end"] - row["start"]).total_seconds() / 3600.0})
            prev_end = row["end"]
    gaps = pd.DataFrame(gaps)
    return d, act, gaps


def case_level(df):
    """One row per case: duration, handovers, cross-boundary handovers, segments."""
    rows = []
    for cid, g in df.groupby(COL["case"]):
        g = g.sort_values(COL["ts"])
        res = g[COL["resource"]].tolist()
        teams = g[COL["team"]].tolist()
        handoffs = sum(1 for a, b in zip(res, res[1:]) if a != b)
        cross = sum(1 for a, b in zip(teams, teams[1:]) if a != b)
        rows.append({
            "case": cid,
            "cycle_h": (g[COL["ts"]].max() - g[COL["ts"]].min()).total_seconds() / 3600.0,
            "handoffs": handoffs,
            "cross_boundary_handoffs": cross,
            "n_resources": g[COL["resource"]].nunique(),
            "n_teams": g[COL["team"]].nunique(),
            "team": g[COL["team"]].iloc[0],
            "complexity": g[COL["complexity"]].iloc[0],
            "specialty": g[COL["specialty"]].iloc[0],
            "segment": g[COL["segment"]].iloc[0],
            "priority": g[COL["priority"]].iloc[0],
            "outcome": g[COL["outcome"]].iloc[-1],
            "rework": bool(g[COL["rework"]].astype(str).str.lower().isin(["true", "1"]).any()),
            "first_resource": res[0], "last_resource": res[-1],
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def distributional_report(cases, group_cols=("team", "complexity")):
    """NEVER report the mean alone: always the distribution and the max-min gap."""
    out = []
    for keys, g in cases.groupby(list(group_cols)):
        q = g["cycle_h"].quantile([.5, .75, .9, .95, .99])
        out.append({**{k: v for k, v in zip(group_cols, keys if isinstance(keys, tuple) else (keys,))},
                    "n": len(g),
                    "p50_d": q[.5] / 24, "p90_d": q[.9] / 24, "p95_d": q[.95] / 24, "p99_d": q[.99] / 24,
                    "mean_d": g["cycle_h"].mean() / 24,
                    "handoffs_per_case": g["handoffs"].mean(),
                    "cross_boundary_per_case": g["cross_boundary_handoffs"].mean(),
                    "rework_rate": g["rework"].mean()})
    r = pd.DataFrame(out)
    r["fairness_gap_p90_d"] = r.groupby("complexity")["p90_d"].transform(lambda s: s.max() - s.min())
    return r


def sla_misses(cases, sla_hours):
    """SLA target as a function of priority (business rule, documented and versioned)."""
    c = cases.copy()
    c["sla_target_h"] = c["priority"].map(sla_hours)
    c["sla_miss"] = c["cycle_h"] > c["sla_target_h"]
    return c


def pool_utilisation(df, pool_resources, window_days=28):
    """Busy-time utilisation from the log (not self-reported)."""
    d = df[df[COL["resource"]].isin(pool_resources)].copy()
    d["d"] = d[COL["ts"]].dt.floor("D")
    span = (df[COL["ts"]].max() - df[COL["ts"]].min()).days
    rows = []
    for r, g in d.groupby(COL["resource"]):
        busy = g.groupby("d").apply(
            lambda x: (x[COL["ts"]].max() - x[COL["ts"]].min()).total_seconds() / 3600.0,
            include_groups=False).sum()
        rows.append({"resource": r, "busy_h": busy,
                     "available_h": window_days * 8,
                     "utilisation": busy / (window_days * 8) if window_days else np.nan,
                     "cases_touched": g[COL["case"]].nunique()})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values("utilisation", ascending=False)


def concentration(df, pool_resources):
    """HHI + top-1 share of pool touches -> single-point-of-failure exposure."""
    d = df[df[COL["resource"]].isin(pool_resources)]
    share = d.groupby(COL["resource"]).size()
    share = share / share.sum()
    return {"hhi": float((share ** 2).sum()),
            "top1_share": float(share.max()),
            "n_pool_members": int(len(share)),
            "distinct_specialties_covered": int(d[COL["specialty"]].nunique())}


def absence_blast_radius(df, absent_resource):
    """Share of cases that become unprocessable if one resource is unavailable."""
    d = df[df[COL["resource"]] != absent_resource]
    blocked = d.groupby(COL["case"])[COL["activity"]].apply(
        lambda s: bool({"Specialist Review", "Local Technical Assessment"} & set(s))).mean()
    return {"resource": absent_resource, "share_cases_touching": blocked}


def diff_in_diff(cases, treatment, control, tag):
    """Case-level randomised pilot: compare treatment vs control, pre vs post."""
    t = cases[cases[COL["team"]].isin(treatment)]
    c = cases[cases[COL["team"]].isin(control)]
    def stat(d):
        return {"p50_d": d["cycle_h"].median() / 24, "p90_d": d["cycle_h"].quantile(.9) / 24,
                "rework": d["rework"].mean(),
                "handoffs": d["handoffs"].mean(),
                "cross_boundary": d["cross_boundary_handoffs"].mean()}
    out = {}
    for arm, d in (("treat", t), ("ctrl", c)):
        for per, dd in d.groupby("period"):
            out[f"{arm}_{per}"] = stat(dd)
    did = {}
    for m in ("p50_d", "p90_d", "rework", "handoffs", "cross_boundary"):
        pre = out[f"treat_pre"][m] - out[f"ctrl_pre"][m]
        post = out[f"treat_post"][m] - out[f"ctrl_post"][m]
        did[m] = {"pre_gap": pre, "post_gap": post, "did": post - pre}
    return {"tag": tag, "arm_stats": out, "did": did}


# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- driver
def compare(pre_path, post_path, pilot_teams=("Team East",), control_teams=("Team North", "Team South"),
            sla_hours=None, pool_resources_post=None):
    pre = load_and_format(pre_path)
    post = load_and_format(post_path)
    report = {}

    for tag, df in (("pre", pre), ("post", post)):
        flow = business_view(df)
        hn = handover_network(flow)                      # handover of work
        wt = working_together_network(flow)               # co-working
        sn = subcontracting_network(flow)                 # subcontracting / split work
        sim, sim_df = activity_similarity(flow)           # resource similarity
        roles = organisational_roles(flow)                # role discovery
        dfg, perf = dfg_and_performance(flow)             # DFG + performance DFG
        variants = variants_of(flow)                     # variants
        _, act, gaps = service_and_waiting(flow)         # service + waiting times
        cases = case_level(flow)
        if sla_hours:
            cases = sla_misses(cases, sla_hours)

        report[tag] = {
            "handover_edges": hn.number_of_edges(),
            "handover_density": nx.density(hn),
            "handover_betweenness_top5": sorted(
                nx.betweenness_centrality(hn).items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:5],
            "working_together_edges": wt.number_of_edges(),
            "subcontracting_edges": sn.number_of_edges(),
            "resource_similarity_mean": float(sim_df.values.mean()),
            "roles": roles,
            "n_variants": len(variants),
            "singleton_variant_share": float(
                pd.Series([v for v in variants]).map(len).eq(1).mean()),
            "activity_perf": perf,
            "queue_wait_p50_h": float(gaps["gap_before_h"].quantile(.5)),
            "queue_wait_p90_h": float(gaps["gap_before_h"].quantile(.9)),
            "queue_wait_p95_h": float(gaps["gap_before_h"].quantile(.95)),
            "distributional": distributional_report(cases),
            "rework_by_activity": rework_cases(flow),
            "temporal_features": temporal_features(flow),
            "cases": cases,
        }
        if pool_resources_post and tag == "post":
            report[tag]["pool_utilisation"] = pool_utilisation(flow, pool_resources_post)
            report[tag]["concentration"] = concentration(flow, pool_resources_post)
            report[tag]["blast_radius"] = [absence_blast_radius(flow, r)
                                           for r in pool_resources_post]

    # difference-in-differences on the pilot vs control teams
    for tag, df in (("pre", pre), ("post", post)):
        df = df.copy(); df["period"] = tag
    report["did"] = diff_in_diff(
        pd.concat([report["pre"]["cases"].assign(period="pre"),
                   report["post"]["cases"].assign(period="post")]),
        treatment=pilot_teams, control=control_teams, tag="pilot_vs_control")

    # targeted cuts (always with the distribution, never the mean alone)
    cuts = {}
    for name, mapping in {
        "high_complexity": {COL["complexity"]: ["high"]},
        "legal_risk_security": {COL["specialty"]: ["legal", "risk", "security"]},
        "team_east": {COL["team"]: list(pilot_teams)},
    }.items():
        for tag, df in (("pre", pre), ("post", post)):
            sub = filter_attrs(business_view(df), mapping)
            cuts.setdefault(name, {})[tag] = distributional_report(case_level(sub))
    report["segmented_cuts"] = cuts
    return report
```

**How to read the outputs against the hypotheses**

| Output | Reads against |
|---|---|
| `handover_edges`, `handoffs_per_case`, `cross_boundary_per_case` | H1 (nominal handover reduction is probably false; cross-boundary handovers rise 3.7–6×) |
| `queue_wait_p50/p90/p95_h`, `pool_utilisation`, `concentration.hhi`, `blast_radius` | H2, H3, H7 (queue formation, utilisation realism, absence blast radius) |
| `rework_by_activity`, `rework_rate` stratified by `complexity`/`team` | H4 (rework rises with context loss) |
| `roles`, `resource_similarity_mean`, `singleton_variant_share` | H5, H6 (local expertise loss, role ambiguity) |
| `distributional` (p50/p90/p95/p99 + `fairness_gap_p90_d`) by team/segment | H8 (fairness hidden by averages) |
| `did` (treatment vs control, pre vs post) | Whether the observed change exceeds secular trends — the only comparison that survives the "East is different" objection |
| `activity_perf` (performance DFG per edge) | Where exactly the waiting time and rework are injected after the change |
