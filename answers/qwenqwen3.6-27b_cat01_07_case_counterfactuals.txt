Real-time timestamp logging adds administrative overhead; if poorly implemented, audit failures increase. Overall, K-411 and K-104 show diminishing returns at scale due to single-resource bottlenecks (C1, A1/A2). K-219 and K-337 introduce quality/risk trade-offs that compound non-linearly. System-wide deployment requires dynamic queue monitoring and fallback routing to avoid congestion migration.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 (parallelize verification) emerges as the most robust intervention. It aligns with normative concurrency rules, requires no policy override, and delivers predictable, modest cycle-time gains without introducing quality or compliance risk. Its primary limitation is assessment-queue saturation, which is manageable through dynamic routing. Conversely, K-337 (suppress rework) is the most dangerous despite offering the largest time savings. By overriding the rework loop, it shifts from a process-timing change to a decision-policy change, accumulating compliance and financial risk that scales non-linearly. Local counterfactuals are least transportable to system-wide policy in K-104 and K-219. K-104’s C1 slot reservation assumes singular capacity can be partitioned without displacement; at scale, it creates a hard bottleneck. K-219’s D2 routing assumes handwriting complexity is manageable; small rework probability spikes cascade into massive external delays. The most valuable additional data are real-time queue elasticity metrics (how quickly A2/C1 absorb earlier arrivals), rework trigger thresholds for D2, and C1 preliminary-to-final work conversion rates. These measurements would distinguish true capacity gains from queue displacement, enabling safe scaling without congestion migration or risk accumulation.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer during the observation window; no temporary certifications or overflow capacity exist.
2. `temporal` Queue estimates in system snapshots represent median wait times; actual waits follow a right-skewed distribution with 20% variance.
3. `resource` D2’s service time on handwritten standard documents falls within the 4-8 h range; complexity does not automatically shift to the 8-14 h bracket.
4. `behavioral` S1 advisory work and C1 preliminary review can be meaningfully completed before final Assessment without requiring full evidence closure.
5. `quality` Suppressing the second rework loop in K-337 does not trigger automatic compliance rejection; conditional approval is a valid decision pathway.
6. `interference` Assessment queue (A1/A2) has sufficient elasticity to absorb earlier-ready cases without proportionally increasing wait times.
7. `temporal` External supplier response times for Request More Info follow the 8-36 h range; weekend delays add exactly 48 h.
8. `resource` The legacy system workaround in K-411 can be logged with real-time timestamps without introducing data integrity or audit trail violations.
