**Section 4: Repeated alerts & temporal policy.**

Define:

- Cool-down: after any alert (or swarm decision), case not re-alerted for 90 min (3 cycles) unless new event or |p|  0.10.

**Section 5: Fairness & feedback loops.**

- Value-based starvation: ranking by € value systematically deprioritizes SME/Public Sector (lower value) even when their risk and effect are higher (I-05/I-07/I-01 vs I-08). Concrete safeguards: (a) segment-level minimum intervention share or service floor — e.g., at least ~25–30% of slots per week to sub-threshold-value segments if their risk×effect clears a floor; (b) dual-track scoring: economic score + "duty/risk" score (p_adj × effect, value-capped or log-scaled value so €100k doesn't dominate by 14×); use log value or sqrt value in a secondary fairness-ranked queue with a quota; (c) report monthly intervention rates and breach rates by segment and by value quartile; alarm if breach rate for low-value quartile diverges while their alert rate doesn't.
- NNLS uncertainty: risk of both over-reaction (acting on unstable estimates) and under-reaction (never acting because estimates never credible  no data ever collected  permanent uncertainty — a cold-start/feedback trap). Safeguards: capped exploration budget (e.g., 1 slot/week or ~10% of slots to sparse segments when score  break-even under stress), mandatory outcome logging for NNLS interventions, human-in-the-loop native-language reviewer in swarm to improve diagnosis quality, wider bands displayed to decision-makers.
- Differential calibration: SME underprediction  systematic under-treatment of SME cases relative to true risk; SE overprediction  over-treatment/wasted slots on SE (I-08 archetype). Safeguards: segment-conditional probability adjustment in production (recalibration layer with monitoring), asymmetric loss awareness — since breach cost >> swarm cost, underprediction (SME) is the dangerous direction; verify SE post-redesign recalibration quickly (it's a known regime change — refit on post-redesign data within 2 weeks, don't wait for quarterly cycle).
- Selective labels / feedback loop: we only observe breach outcomes for cases we don't intervene on (and intervened cases' outcomes are shifted by treatment)  future model trains on policy-contaminated data; also untreated high-risk cases that breach become "the model was right" examples while prevented breaches look like false alarms (prevention paradox): the model appears miscalibrated precisely because it worked. Safeguards: log all intervention decisions + scores + counterfactual estimates; hold out a small randomized "no-intervention" control stream (e.g., 5–10% of eligible borderline cases, ethically bounded) or use shadow scoring; train on propensity-weighted / IPW data; never feed intervened-case outcomes into the risk model without correction.
- Spillover distribution: harms concentrate on untreated diagnosis-stage cases — which under value-based ranking skew toward low-value cases (they lose slots)  double disadvantage: low value  no slot  also absorb congestion externality. ## 1. Decision rule

**Conceptual basis.** `p_now` is an *observational* probability. It is not the probability of breach under intervention, and it is never multiplied by value to size a benefit. The benefit driver is the estimated *causal* risk reduction, treated as an uncertain prior (the supplied ranges), not a known effect. The action variable is therefore **robust expected net value per slot**, never a raw probability threshold.

**Step 1 — Hard eligibility gates (all must pass):**
- **Stage gate:** case is pre-`Diagnosis Complete` (Triage or Diagnosis Started).
- **Runway feasibility:** remaining time must exceed swarm duration (45 min) + handover + a plausible residual pipeline. Rule of thumb:  ~3 h for Triage cases (swarm must precede diagnosis *and* leave time for resolution/validation),  ~2 h for Diagnosis-Started cases. A Triage case at 1.5 h fails this gate even if the nominal effect range looks attractive.
- **Repeat-swarm gate:** no prior swarm unless a materially new event has occurred (defined in §4).
- **Data-integrity gate:** if the alert driver depends on a failed/imputed feature, verify before committing a slot (cheap check,  20 min). Alerts driven by imputation get a conditional hold, not a slot.

**Step 2 — Probability robustification (segment calibration layer):**
- SME: `p_adj = p + 0.065` (range +0.04 to +0.09) — underprediction is the dangerous direction since breach cost  swarm cost.
- Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = p  0.085` (range 0.05 to 0.12), pending post-redesign refit.
- Public Sector: no point shift; widen bands ±0.02–0.03 for moderate sample.
- Non-Native-Language Support: no point shift; inflate the interval by roughly ±0.10 and stress-test decisions at both ends.
- Sanity cap: achievable absolute risk reduction  `p_adj` (you cannot remove risk that is not there).

**Step 3 — Effect credibility weights** (priors, not measurements): Public Sector 1.0; SME 0.9; SE 0.85 (post-redesign discount); NNLS 0.6. Conservative effect = weight × (lower bound +  of range). For first-alert moderate-probability cases, add a discrete "driver-wrong" failure mode (effect  0, full false-alarm burden) with probability ~0.1–0.3 depending on data quality and segment reliability.

**Step 4 — Score and tiers.**
`Score = (1  p_fail) · min(, p_adj) · V  €450  expected false-alarm burden  _slot`,
where **_slot  €1,200–2,400** is the spillover shadow price (6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases × 0.01–0.03 × ~€20k average value), lower when the queue is uncongested (e.g., late day).

- **Tier 1 — act now:** positive scenario-weighted EV vs. _slot, with an *expiring option* (deadline < ~3 h) or a dominant robust score.
- **Tier 2 — conditional:** positive mid EV; act only after a short verification (data check or driver sanity check).
- **Tier 3 — watch:** positive mid EV but below the slot bar, with runway to wait; re-scored every 30-min refresh against explicit promotion triggers.

**Step 5 — Overrides and reserve.** Regulatory/contractual-duty cases may claim the reserved slot regardless of score (max 1/day, documented) — non-monetary obligations do not reduce to € terms. One slot is held in reserve until 15:00; this is both the management preference *and* economically correct, because the best remaining marginal case does not clear _slot. Swarms run one at a time (two specialists per swarm), so sequencing is part of the rule: urgent cases first, deferrable high-value cases in low-congestion windows.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Noise band:** |p|  0.03 per 30-min refresh is refresh noise — carries no new information.
- **Cool-down:** after any alert or swarm decision, no re-alert for 90 min (3 refreshes) unless a re-entry condition fires.
- **Suppression:** repeated alerts within cool-down with |p|  0.03 and no new event are logged but not surfaced. **Suppression  ineligibility**: the case stays in the ranked pool at its last verified probability.
- **Escalation:** (a) *sustained elevation* — p_adj above the action threshold for  3 consecutive refreshes earns a one-tier priority bump and human review if still untreated (persistent risk is signal, even without new events); (b) *hard triggers* — stage transition, new artifact/log, customer escalation, entitlement change.
- **Re-entry:** any hard trigger, or |p|  0.10 in one refresh ( 0.15 cumulative since suppression), re-fires the alert and re-scores the case. For post-swarm cases, a **second swarm** additionally requires the event to be material, remaining runway feasible, and estimated incremental effect  the slot bar, with justification logged.
- **Why this matters:** at a 30-min refresh, one case can generate ~16 alerts/day; without suppression, alert fatigue destroys the channel, and alert *count* is a terrible severity proxy.

**I-05 vs. I-07.** Both are SME, third alert — but they are opposites. I-05's alerts are *clock ticks*: p = +0.02, no new event  suppress, no escalation, no fresh human attention; yet it has never been swarmed, its adjusted risk is ~0.64, and its effect range is the portfolio's best, so it remains a legitimate Tier-3 candidate with a defined promotion trigger. I-07's third alert carries a *new customer log* — materially new information (which the model partially absorbed: p fell 0.750.72) — so it re-enters evaluation immediately rather than being auto-suppressed. On the merits it still gets no swarm: a Triage case with 1.5 h left cannot fit swarm + diagnosis + resolution, so its *effective* effect is far below the nominal 0.20–0.32, and €5k of value cannot clear the slot bar. It instead receives non-swarm escalation and proactive customer communication. The rule keys intervention on *information change and feasibility*, not on alert count — treating count as severity would over-serve I-05 and under-serve I-07.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation.** I-08 (weakest effect, 0.05–0.15) out-scores I-01 (strongest effects, 0.18–0.35) on mid EV purely because of an 11× value gap. Sustained, this systematically diverts capacity from SME/Public Sector cases — whose risk is *underpredicted* — toward SE cases whose risk is *overpredicted* and whose effects are weakest: an equity failure and an efficiency failure at once. Safeguards: (i) a secondary equity score using capped or log-scaled value, with a guaranteed floor (e.g.,  20–25% of weekly slots to cases below median value whenever their p_adj  0.6 and effect  0.15); (ii) a duty register for Public Sector/regulatory SLAs whose floors override economics; (iii) a monthly report of intervention, breach, and *unmitigated* breach rates by segment × value quartile, with an alarm when the low-value quartile's breach rate diverges despite equal alert rates.
- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** Two failure modes: acting on unstable estimates, or never acting — which means never collecting data, so the segment stays uncertain forever (cold-start trap). Safeguards: a capped exploration budget ( 1 slot/week for NNLS cases whose stress-tested score is non-negative — today's I-09 is exactly this), a native-language senior reviewer staffed into those swarms, effect estimates for NNLS always displayed as intervals, and a quarterly calibration review with a minimum-sample gate before point estimates are trusted.
- **Differential calibration.** SME underprediction produces systematic under-treatment; SE overprediction wastes slots on cases like I-08. Because breach cost  swarm cost, underprediction is the costlier error. Safeguards: the production calibration layer from §1 with automatic flags when any segment's bias exceeds ±0.05 for two weeks; priority refit of SE on post-redesign data within 2–4 weeks (this is a known regime change, not gradual drift); recalibrate SME with asymmetric loss weighting.
- **Selective intervention corrupting future labels.** We only observe untreated outcomes cleanly; treated cases' outcomes are counterfactual-contaminated, so retraining on raw history teaches the model the *policy*, not the process. Worse, the prevention paradox makes successful alerts look like false alarms ("we swarmed and it didn't breach"), eroding trust and model calibration. Safeguards: a complete decision log (score, p_adj, effect prior, driver, outcome) for every treated case; treated cases flagged in training and corrected via inverse-propensity or doubly-robust weighting rather than dropped; a small randomized holdout at the Tier-2 margin (~10%, capped, ethics/ops-reviewed) to estimate effects near the threshold; recorded counterfactual estimates per treated case for later evaluation.
- **Spillover incidence.** Congestion harm lands on *untreated* diagnosis-stage cases — under value-based allocation, disproportionately low-value cases, and on cases like I-03 that cannot benefit from a swarm at all: a double disadvantage for the already-deprioritized. Safeguards: _slot charged inside every score; a cap of  2 swarms per 3-hour window plus staggered execution; exposure tracking, with a priority bump or queue credit for cases absorbing spillover from  2 swarms in a day; a weekly difference-in-differences of untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates on high- vs. low-swarm days, checked *by segment*, with rebalancing via the equity floor if incidence is skewed.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational (daily dashboard, weekly review):** slot utilization vs. plan; swarm cycle time; specialist interruption hours; *measured* queue-delay minutes per swarm (compare diagnosis-stage wait times during swarm vs. adjacent windows); alert volume, suppression rate, cool-down re-fires, repeat-swarm rate; decision latency for expiring options (did I-02/I-09 get decided inside their windows); reserve-release decisions and outcomes.
- **Predictive (rolling 4–8 weeks):** per-segment reliability curves, bias, and Brier/ECE decomposition; drift monitors (PSI/KL > 0.2 flags) on features and the p-distribution; imputation-rate monitor (live-queue feature missing on > 5% of refreshes  auto low-confidence flag + mandatory verification gate + data-engineering incident); empirical validation of the ±0.03 noise band; specific watches — SE post-redesign bias decaying toward zero (confirms the refit), and SME bias persisting (escalates recalibration priority).
- **Causal-effect estimation (the priority gap):** (a) log predicted counterfactual breach probability and effect prior for every treated case; (b) regression discontinuity around the score cut-off when slots bind (just-above vs. just-below cases); (c) the marginal randomized holdout from §5 for threshold-local effects; (d) matched treated-vs-untreated comparisons (matched on p_adj, segment, stage, value band) as triangulation, explicitly labeled selection-biased; (e) *mechanism mediators* — diagnosis-completion-time deltas, missing-information discovered, case redirects — because mechanism failure (swarms that neither accelerate nor reveal anything) is detectable within days, long before breach outcomes accumulate; (f) quarterly re-estimation of effect priors per segment *and per stage* with shrinkage, replacing the supplied ranges.
- **Capacity/spillover:** measured delay-minutes per slot; empirical  via diff-in-differences on untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates (swarm vs. non-swarm days); congestion index at swarm start; monthly re-estimation of _slot; segment/value composition of who bears spillover (fairness link).
- **Policy-induced drift:** use *untreated* case outcomes as the unbiased reference stream; watch for the prevention-paradox signature (apparent calibration worsening as the policy succeeds); monitor alert-composition shift over quarters (are we alerting on different case types because past policy shaped the training data?); quarterly simulation backtest of the rule.
- **Threshold-change governance (pre-specified triggers, with hysteresis):** estimated marginal-slot net value < 0 for two consecutive weeks  cut daily slots or raise the bar; segment |bias| > 0.05 sustained two weeks  adjust the calibration layer; measured spillover exceeds the 0.03/slot assumption  raise _slot and lower the per-window swarm cap; suppressed cases breach above their predicted rate  narrow the noise band or shorten cool-downs; a segment's realized effects fall outside the prior range twice consecutively  refresh priors (if NNLS is still uninformative after a quarter of exploration, commission a dedicated study). Rule changes require a minimum evidence window, one week of shadow-mode where feasible, and version-controlled thresholds with a full audit trail.
