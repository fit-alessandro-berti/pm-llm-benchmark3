# 1. Executive Memo

### Subject: Strategic Decision on the Fiber-Installation Process Pilot Redesign  

Following a 6-week pilot of Redesign R, management faces a critical decision on the fiber-installation process. The redesign, focused on coordinator ownership, task centralization, survey skipping, and post-billing activation checks, has yielded conflicting outcomes. Clear benefits include faster median case completion and reduced handovers, but there are alarming trade-offs in long-tail service durations, rework, complaint escalation, and cost including downstream impacts.  

Given the tension between increased efficiency for simple and low-risk cases and deteriorating outcomes for complex cases, **we recommend a hybrid approach (Alternative E)**:  
1. **Deploy core elements of Redesign R selectively** for low-risk cases to leverage gains in speed and efficiency.  
2. **Suspend the survey-skipping and post-billing activation practices** for medium/high-risk cases until evidence proves their reliability.  
3. Conduct targeted **measurements (M1, M4, M6, M7)** to resolve key uncertainties in building-risk stratification, downstream costs, and specific conformance issues.  

This recommendation allows management to balance immediate scaling where beneficial while mitigating the risks for vulnerable segments.  

### Trade-offs and Rationale:  
- The **median cycle time reduction (14.2 to 10.8 days)** demonstrates success in simple low-risk categories (e.g., single-family homes), aligning with strategic goals for quicker installations and reduced handovers.  
- However, the **reduced p90 cycle time for complex cases (31 to 38.5 days)** exposes implementation flaws, particularly for apartments, old buildings, and permit-sensitive cases prone to skipped steps and upstream errors.  
- **Customer complaints, SLA breaches, and rework rates** expose limitations in the building-risk predictive rule and process control safeguards. Activating billing prior to a working service worsens downstream correction burdens (complaints up from 6.1% to 9.8%; second visits up from 11% to 18%).  
- Segment analysis confirms stark disparities: robust gains for simple houses, but major penalties for high-risk cases.  

By scaling only the redesign elements proven effective and rigorously measuring the unresolved risks, this hybrid approach minimizes negative impacts while maintaining momentum in process improvement.

| **High fitness but low precision**               | High fitness reflects accurate representation of simplified cases. Low precision reflects unconstrained variation in skipped or exception routes. |

| **Fewer handovers, lower resilience**            | Coordinator ownership lowered handovers but created bottlenecks when coordinators were over-utilized or absent.                   |
| **Lower visible cost, higher downstream cost**   | Skipping steps reduced front-end costs while causing downstream complaints, rework, and SLA breach penalties.                    |

# 6. Final Recommendation  

### Immediate Action:  
1. **Deploy Hybrid Redesign R (Alternative E):**  
    - Scale **coordinator ownership and task simplification** for simple houses and low-risk cases.  
    - Suspend survey-skipping and post-billing activation for medium/high-risk cases pending evidence.  

2. **Invest in Priority Measurements**:  
    - Validate risk-stratification accuracy (M1, M6).  
    - Measure full downstream cost impacts (M4).  
    - Monitor conformance to process controls (M7).  

### Pause:  
- Broader deployment to medium/high-risk segments.  

### Scale:  
- Simplified process for low-risk segments where gains are demonstrable.  

### Measure and Validate:  
If M1, M6, and M4 confirm controllable risk and cost, scale gradually to more segments. Conversely, failure in these experiments would justify segment-specific modifications or partial rollbacks.  

This approach leverages validated gains while minimizing negative externalities, ensuring decisions are data-driven and flexible for future adjustments.
