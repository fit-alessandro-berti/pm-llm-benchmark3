# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Each social-network-analysis (SNA) view over an event log measures a different relation between resources. Mira_Coordinator looks contradictory only if we forget that these networks measure different things. Below, for each view, what it implies and what it does **not** imply.

## Handover-of-work network

**What it measures.** A directed edge A→B is drawn when, within a case, an event by A is directly (or closely) followed by an event by B. It captures *flow of work* and *routing*, not shared effort.

**What it implies about Mira.** Mira has very high out-degree centrality (0.82), high in-degree (0.51), and the highest betweenness (0.77) among humans. She sits *on the path* between Bot_Rules and the specialists/adjusters: Bot_Rules→Mira (920), then Mira→Omar_Medical (210), Mira→Priya_Legal (180), Mira→each adjuster (280–350). She is a **routing hub**: work arrives at her and is dispatched onward in many directions. High betweenness means many shortest paths in the handover graph pass through her — she is structurally a broker/gateway.

**What it does NOT imply.** Handover does not prove Mira *does* the downstream work, that she is a bottleneck, or that she holds authority. A→B handovers also arise from pure automated or clerical routing. Directed adjacency is also sensitive to event ordering and timestamp granularity, so a "handover" can be an artifact of how lifecycle transitions are logged rather than a real transfer of responsibility. High centrality here is a claim about *position in the flow*, not about value or control.

## Working-together network

**What it measures.** An undirected edge whose weight is the share of cases in which the pair *co-occurs* (both appear on the same case, regardless of order). It captures *sustained collaboration on the same case*.

**What it implies about Mira.** Her co-case rates are strikingly low: 8% with Carla, 7% Diego, 6% Elise/Farid, 5% Alice, 4% Bruno — despite hundreds of handovers to exactly those people. This is the core paradox. The resolution: **handover ≠ collaboration.** Mira appears on a case briefly (route/assign/reopen/clarify), then leaves; the downstream resource then does the substantive work over many events. So she *touches* many cases but *co-labors* on few. Her weighted working-together degree (0.16) is far below the adjusters (0.33–0.49) and even the team leads (0.43–0.46).

**What it does NOT imply.** Low co-case weight does **not** mean Mira is unimportant or peripheral to outcomes. It means her contribution is *transactional and time-sliced*, not *co-productive*. Working-together also cannot see direction, so it cannot tell us whether she initiates or receives; and because it is normalized by case count, a resource who makes one short touch on many cases will always look "peripheral" here by construction.

## Subcontracting network

**What it measures.** With n=2: A does something, B does something, then A returns within 2 events (A…B…A). It captures a *delegate-and-return* pattern — work handed out and taken back, i.e. A retains ownership while B contributes a sub-task.

**What it implies about Mira.** She has the highest subcontracting betweenness (0.69) and is the A in the two largest patterns: Mira→Omar→Mira (126, "Request Missing Information → Medical Review → Assign/Reopen") and Mira→Priya→Mira (98, "Escalate Complaint → Legal Review → Reopen/Assign"). Combined with the handover round-trips (Omar→Mira 160, Priya→Mira 140), this strongly suggests Mira **delegates specialist questions and re-integrates the answer**, keeping control of the case around the specialist step. That is the signature of an exception-desk / case-brokering function, not of a doer.

**What it does NOT imply.** Subcontracting-in-the-SNA-sense is a *structural pattern*, not evidence of a contractual or hierarchical subcontract. The A…B…A shape can also be produced by ordinary rework loops, by clarification cycles, or by logging order — so it does not prove Mira *supervises* Omar/Priya or that she has decision authority over them. It shows a recurring return-of-control motif, which is consistent with several role hypotheses.

## Performance / time statistics

**What it measures.** Service time (active handling per event), waiting time, and each resource's contribution to case cycle time.

**What it implies about Mira.** Her per-event service time is short (4.2 min) and her cycle-time contribution is "high variance." She is *fast per touch* but appears on the *slow, exceptional* cases. The decisive evidence is the natural experiment: during her 6-day absence, ordinary low-value cycle time was unchanged, but exception_flag=True cases piled up and their 90th-percentile cycle time rose 8.4→13.9 days. This localizes her value to the **tail** of hard/exception cases, not the mainstream. Note also where the *real* time sinks are: Vendor_Verify (2.1 h), Vendor_Pay (1.3 h), Priya (51 min), Omar (37 min) — these dominate absolute duration and must not be ignored.

**What it does NOT imply.** Short service time does not mean low importance (a queue-router can be brief yet pivotal). Conversely, the absence effect shows *impact under her current design*, not that she is irreplaceable in principle — the same routing could, in theory, be done by a rule, a rota, or a backup. Time stats also cannot separate "she is the bottleneck" from "she stands next to the bottleneck (specialists/vendors)."

## Role-discovery / activity-profile similarity

**What it measures.** `discover_organizational_roles` groups resources by similar *activity profiles*; activity-based resource similarity measures overlap in *what activities* resources perform.

**What it implies about Mira.** Her activity set — Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist — is **unique**. Adjusters cluster around Assess Damage/Negotiate; team leads around Triage/Approve; temps around Validate/Close; bots around ingest/rules; vendors around single steps. Mira likely forms a **singleton role** ("exception/coordination desk") that similarity-based clustering would isolate. This corroborates that she is not a redundant adjuster or a second team lead but a distinct organizational function.

**What it does NOT imply.** A distinct activity profile does not tell us whether the role is *designed* (a chartered exception desk) or *emergent* (a person who accreted these tasks informally). Role discovery is descriptive of behavior, silent on authority and intent.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

The evidence is intentionally ambiguous; below are four hypotheses. None is proven; each makes falsifiable predictions.

## H1 — Formal coordinator / exception desk (designed router)

**Mechanism.** Mira is the chartered exception-handling desk: Bot_Rules routes flagged/complex cases to her; she triages, requests missing info, assigns the right specialist/adjuster, and re-integrates answers, then hands ownership back to a team lead for approval.

**Supporting evidence.** Her activity set (Request Missing Info, Assign Specialist, Reopen, Escalate) reads like a desk charter; highest handover and subcontracting betweenness; she touches 22% of all claims but 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints; short service time (transactional); delegate-and-return subcontracting motifs with both specialists.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Low working-together weight and low co-case rates with team leads (4–5%) sit oddly with a *formal* coordinator, who might be expected to collaborate more visibly. A process owner explicitly denies she is a team lead. So the "formal" label is partly asserted, not confirmed.

**Predictions.** With a documented desk, we would expect (a) stable, rule-consistent routing (given case_type/priority/region, the chosen specialist is predictable), (b) a written escalation policy, (c) a named backup, and (d) her Assign Specialist events preceding almost all specialist involvement.

## H2 — Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism.** Exception-case throughput implicitly depends on Mira's individual knowledge of "where hard cases go." She is a single point of failure for the exception path even though the mainstream flows around her.

**Supporting evidence.** The absence experiment: exception queues accumulated and P90 exception cycle time rose 8.4→13.9 days while ordinary cases were unaffected. Adjusters describe her as "the person who knows where hard cases should go" — tacit, person-bound knowledge. Highest human betweenness = structural chokepoint on the exception path.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Ordinary cases were *unaffected*, so she is not a global bottleneck — only for the exception tail (~a fifth of volume). Her short service time argues she is not saturating on effort; the delay on absence is a *routing gap*, not her being overloaded. Bottleneck-in-time is more plausibly Vendors/specialists.

**Predictions.** If key-person dependency is real: removing her (without replacement) reliably degrades exception P90; her routing decisions are hard to predict from logged attributes alone (high residual entropy), indicating tacit knowledge; a trained backup would restore performance.

## H3 — Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

**Mechanism.** Mira connects otherwise-separated clusters — the two team-based clusters (North: Alice/Carla/Elise; South: Bruno/Diego/Farid), the specialist pair (Omar/Priya), the automation layer (Bot_Rules), and the temp/clerical layer. She is the bridge that lets a case cross organizational boundaries.

**Supporting evidence.** Highest betweenness in both handover and subcontracting networks (the classic broker signature). She handovers to *both* teams' adjusters and to both specialists. Low co-case rate is *expected* of a broker: brokers connect groups without belonging to any (structural holes). Cross-team coordinator is her stated remit.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Broker-ness alone doesn't establish whether the brokerage is efficient or a symptom of missing routing rules; a good rules engine could span the same boundaries. Betweenness can be inflated by log ordering, so some "bridging" may be artifactual.

**Predictions.** Removing her should *increase* direct cross-cluster handovers (teams contacting specialists directly) and formal escalations (the operations manager's claim). Network-community detection should place her *between* communities, not inside one.

## H4 — Compliance/quality gatekeeper vs. uncontrolled-discretion risk (dual-control concern)

**Mechanism.** Mira both *detects* problems (screens exceptions, fraud-update loops with Bot_Rules) and *selects* the next handler (Assign Specialist). Combining detection and selection concentrates discretion in one person.

**Supporting evidence.** Compliance reviewer's explicit worry; Bot_Rules→Mira→Bot_Rules "Fraud Screen → manual exception → Fraud Screen update" loop shows her overriding/adjusting automated screening; she owns Reopen and Escalate, both control-sensitive actions; her 48% exception share is the highest of any human.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** There is **no evidence of wrongdoing** — only a control-design concern. High discretion is not misuse; it may be exactly what an exception desk needs. Whether her choices deviate from policy is untested by the data given.

**Predictions.** An audit sampling her Assign Specialist and Reopen decisions would reveal whether routing is consistent with policy; segregation-of-duties analysis would show whether the same person both flags and routes the same case without a second check.

*(Also considered and partly rejected:* **data artifact** — timestamp/lifecycle ordering could inflate handover/subcontracting counts and depress co-case rates; plausible as a *contributor* but cannot explain the real absence effect. **Informal team lead** — contradicted by the process owner and by her non-approval activity set. **Automation monitor** — supported only by the Bot_Rules fraud loop, too narrow to be her whole role.)*

# 3. Risk assessment

*(Calibrated: I flag concerns, not misconduct. No evidence supports any wrongdoing claim.)*

- **Bottleneck risk — moderate, localized.** She is not a mainstream bottleneck (ordinary cycle time flat during absence), but she is a throughput chokepoint for the *exception tail* (P90 8.4→13.9 days). The larger *time* bottlenecks are Vendor_Verify, Vendor_Pay, and the specialists; her risk is *routing latency*, not handling effort.

- **Coordination risk — real.** Much routing intelligence appears to be tacit ("knows where hard cases should go"). If routing logic is undocumented, coordination is fragile to her availability and hard to audit or improve.

- **Key-person risk — high for exceptions, low overall.** The absence experiment is direct evidence of dependency on the exception path. No named backup is evident. This is the most actionable, evidence-backed risk.

- **Compliance / control risk — potential, unproven.** Detection + selection in one person (plus Reopen/Escalate ownership and Fraud-Screen overrides) concentrates discretion. This is a *segregation-of-duties design* question, not an accusation. It merits an audit, not a sanction.

- **Workload-fairness risk — moderate.** She carries 48% exception share and 64%/71% of reopens/complaints — the "hardest" mix. This is a burnout/single-point-of-load concern and also means her queue is the first to overflow under stress.

- **Data-quality risk — non-trivial.** Handover/subcontracting metrics are sensitive to event ordering, lifecycle:transition handling, and timestamp granularity (bot events are seconds apart). Some centrality may be inflated and some co-case suppression exaggerated. Conclusions should be validated after cleaning lifecycle/timestamps before any structural change.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

- **Handover-of-work.** Recompute `discover_handover_of_work_network` split by `case_type`, `priority`, and `exception_flag` (via `filter_event_attribute_values`). Test whether Mira's out-degree/betweenness collapses on non-exception cases (expected if H1/H3) and dominates on exceptions. Compare edge stability week-over-week to check robustness.

- **Working-together.** Run `discover_working_together_network` and apply community detection (e.g. networkx Louvain) on the resulting graph. Confirm two team communities + specialist pair, and verify Mira sits *between* communities (broker) rather than inside one — the structural-hole test for H3.

- **Subcontracting.** Run `discover_subcontracting_network` at n=1,2,3 and inspect stability of the Mira→Omar→Mira and Mira→Priya→Mira motifs. Quantify how often the "A returns" step is a control action (Reopen/Assign/Approve) vs. rework, to distinguish supervision-like return from mere loops.

- **Activity similarity / role discovery.** `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` + `discover_organizational_roles` to confirm Mira is a singleton role. Then measure routing *predictability*: train a simple classifier (specialist/adjuster chosen ~ case_type, priority, amount_band, region, exception_flag). Low accuracy ⇒ tacit knowledge (key-person risk, H2); high accuracy ⇒ rules-encodable routing.

- **Filtering (exception/activity/variants/time).** Use `filter_event_attribute_values` on exception_flag, `filter_variants`, and time windows including the 6-day absence. Compare exception-case variants with vs. without Mira, and pre/post absence, to isolate her causal footprint on cycle time and queue buildup.

- **Service / waiting time.** Use `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` to decompose cycle time into service vs. waiting per resource. Expected: waiting dominates at Vendor_Verify/Vendor_Pay and at specialist queues; Mira's own service is negligible but the *wait before* her (queue for the exception desk) is what grows in her absence. This separates "Mira is slow" (false) from "the exception queue behind Mira is fragile" (likely).

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately (low-regret, reversible).**
- Document the routing logic: capture, as explicit rules, how Mira maps (case_type, priority, amount_band, region, exception_flag) to specialist/adjuster. This de-risks key-person dependency without removing her.
- Name and train a backup for the exception desk so a future absence does not reopen the P90 gap.
- Add a lightweight second check where the *same person* both flags an exception and selects the handler on control-sensitive actions (Reopen, Fraud-Screen override), addressing the segregation-of-duties concern without alleging misuse.

**Monitor (instrument before deciding).**
- Track exception-tail P90/P99 cycle time, her queue depth, and her exception/complaint share weekly. Watch vendor and specialist waiting times, which are the true duration drivers.
- Clean lifecycle/timestamp data and re-run the SNA to confirm the centrality picture is not partly artifactual.
- Measure how much of her routing is rules-encodable vs. tacit.

**Do NOT change until more evidence (avoid harm).**
- **Do not remove or demote Mira.** The absence experiment and the operations manager's warning indicate removal would raise formal escalations and specialist overload. Removal is only defensible *after* the routing rules are documented, a backup is trained, and a rules-engine or coordinator can absorb the brokering function — i.e., after the lost coordination is demonstrably replaced.
- Do not rewire team-lead approval flows or vendor steps based on SNA centrality alone.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is best understood as the exception desk and routing broker for hard claims, not a team lead and not a mainstream worker. She appears on many cases only briefly — to route, request information, assign a specialist, reopen, or escalate — then hands the case on. That is why she looks extremely central in "who passes work to whom" and "delegate-and-return" networks, yet peripheral in "who works together on a case": she connects people rather than co-working with them. Her value concentrates on the difficult tail of cases: when she was away for six days, ordinary claims were unaffected but complex ones backed up and their slowest cycle times nearly doubled. The main risks are dependence on one person's know-how and the fact that she both spots problems and picks who handles them. Recommended: document her routing rules, train a backup, and add a light control check — but do not remove her until that coordination is safely replaced. Note that vendors and specialists, not Mira, drive most actual processing time.
