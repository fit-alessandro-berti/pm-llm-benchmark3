# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

**Conventions used below.** *Observed* means a value in the tables in the prompt. *Derived* means calculated from those values. *Eligible* is reconstructed as: recorded arrival $\le$ decision time, not yet completed, skill-qualified for the resource in question, and resource available at that moment. A service-start time is used as a proxy for the dispatch-decision time, but the true decision may have been made earlier.

**[Assumption 1]** Appointment values are treated as point times because no window widths are supplied; an unknown tolerance could move appointment eligibility. **[Assumption 2]** A dual-skill job (e.g. firmware+electrical) needs both skills on the same resource; this is directly supported for D05, whose note says D-F is “the only fully qualified resource,” and derived for D07 from the skill table. **[Assumption 3]** Resource windows/meetings/calibration are hard and non-preemptive unless the data show a segment crossing them.

**Derived check:** for all 32 jobs, summed `service_segments` equals `est_min`. Realised-duration noise is therefore zero in the supplied data. That does **not** prove dispatch used `est_min` itself — an internal estimate could differ — but it lowers the plausibility of “noisy realised durations” as an explanation.

Confidence is a subjective rating that the stated primary + secondary combination captures dispatch, not certainty that a pure textbook rule holds.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA — FIFO + appointment

**Support.** A02 over A04 at 08:32; A05 over A08+A07 at 09:05; A07 over A08 at 09:32 despite A08 being P1 with est25. A06 at 09:20 is also FIFO-first. The 08:52–08:58 calibration exactly fills the gap between A02’s end and A04’s start — a known constraint, not a dispatch choice.

**Challenge.** A03 jumps A02 at 08:20 — a violation of pure FIFO, but A03’s appointment is exactly 08:20, so it is explained by the appointment component. It is, however, also the SPT choice (10 vs 20), so the episode alone cannot separate appointment from SPT. The 2-min idle 08:30–08:32 before A02 has no recorded cause (setup, scan, or batching) — a minor undocumented gap. A06 starts 5 min after its appointment point while already FIFO-first, so “appointment late” does not prove a missed appointment rule; A08’s P1 status never produces a jump, which strongly challenges any priority claim for QA.

## QB — FIFO-dominant hybrid with P1 override

**Support.** FIFO/resume: B01 resumes at 09:31 and 10:14 as the oldest job; B04 at 10:28 over P2 B06; B06 at 10:36 over B07. P1 override: B02 preempts at 09:16 with the recorded reason “urgent arrival,” and B05 jumps B04 at 10:00. The explicit interruption reasons are evidence that preemption and reassignment are real mechanisms here, not artefacts.

**Challenge.** B03 at 09:48 over P1 B05 contradicts the P1 override unless B05 was not yet in the dispatcher’s known set or B01’s reassignment reordered the queue. B01’s resume at 09:31 over P2 B03 contradicts priority and SPT but fits resume-first — an undocumented precedence rule. B07’s appointment is ignored (starts 10:56, 26 min after 10:30; note says “not honored on time”), which challenges any appointment component in QB. If resumption had a service-time cost, B01’s segments would sum to more than 45; they sum to exactly 45, so the cost is elapsed delay only.

## QC — SPT with overrides

**Support.** C02 at 10:10 defeats both FIFO (C01) and priority/EDF (C03). C03 at 10:18 fits SPT, priority and EDF simultaneously. C06 at 11:34 defeats FIFO, priority and EDF. Three of six decisions are clean SPT wins in contested sets.

**Challenge.** C04 at 10:32 defeats SPT (C06), priority and EDF (C05) — this requires an undocumented snapshot/commitment exception or a hidden eligibility filter; the 2-min scan delay cannot explain it because C06 was recorded at 10:25, at least 7 min before the decision. C05 at 10:54 fits priority/EDF but is the longest job and starts after its own deadline — deadline handling is at best ineffective. C01 at 11:40 defeats SPT, priority and EDF and causes C07 to miss; only FIFO/aging (C01 waiting 100 min) explains it, and any waiting-time cap is undocumented.

## QD — skill-based routing, FIFO within skill

**Support.** Every assignment respects skill eligibility, and several are sole-eligible regardless of order rule: D01D-M, D02D-F, D05D-F (“only fully qualified”), D07D-X (only mech+elec resource, derived), D10D-X while D-F is in meeting. Within-pool FIFO is shown by D04 over P1 D08 (11:25) and D07 over P1 D10 (11:48). D03 at 11:21 is both FIFO-first and SPT among D-F-eligible jobs.

**Challenge.** D06’s appointment jump over FIFO D07 and P1 D10 at 11:30 contradicts pure skill+FIFO and needs the appointment component. D-X is idle 11:20–11:30 although D07 is eligible from 11:14 — that fits appointment reservation but is otherwise unexplained. D-F is idle 11:36–12:20 although D05 (30 min) is eligible on D-F from 11:07; starting at 11:36 would cross the 12:00 meeting, so a meeting-protection assumption is needed. D03 is assigned to D-F at 11:21 although D-X became available at 11:20 — a specialist-first preference or a pre-assignment 1 min earlier would be needed; scan/rounding could cover 1 min but not the longer idles.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## 4.1 QA, 08:20 — appointment vs SPT (both predict A03)

Two strongest explanations: **(A) Appointment:** jobs become due at their appointment point and jump FIFO. **(B) SPT:** the shortest `est` goes next. In the observed episode A03 is both due and shortest, so they agree. **Counterfactual:** if a due appointment job had `est` 20 and the FIFO job had `est` 10, appointment predicts the appointment job, SPT predicts the FIFO job. **Discriminating observable:** a logged `release_time` / `eligible_from` for appointment jobs — if A03-type jobs are marked ineligible before the appointment point even when the resource is free, appointment-as-release is supported; if they are eligible earlier and chosen only when shortest, SPT is supported. The dispatch decision snapshot’s `appointment_due` flag would serve the same purpose.

## 4.2 QB, 10:28 — FIFO vs priority-with-aging (both can be made to predict B04)

**(A) FIFO:** B04 (arr 09:35) before B06 (arr 10:05). **(B) Priority + aging:** B06 is P2 vs B04 P3, but B04’s 53-min wait promotes it above B06. Pure priority predicts B06 and is already contradicted; the live contest is FIFO vs priority-with-aging. **Counterfactual:** a P3 job waiting 53 min vs a P2 job waiting 10 min — FIFO still picks P3, aging picks P3 only if a promotion threshold has been crossed. **Discriminating observable:** the **effective priority of B04 at the decision moment** (and any `promoted_at` timestamp). If B04’s effective priority is still 3 when chosen, FIFO is supported; if it was promoted to 2 before 10:28, aging is supported.

## 4.3 QC, 11:40 — SPT/EDF vs FIFO anti-starvation (predict C07 vs C01)

**(A) SPT/EDF:** C07 (est18, ddl 12:00) before C01 (est36, ddl 13:00); derived result would meet both deadlines. **(B) FIFO/anti-starvation cap:** C01 has waited 100 min and is promoted regardless of `est`/deadline. Observed C01 already eliminates pure SPT/EDF for this episode, but not a threshold version. **Counterfactual:** an old long job waiting 60 min vs a short urgent job — SPT/EDF picks the short job, a 100-min cap does not yet promote the old job. **Discriminating observable:** a `starvation_promoted_at` / max-wait cap field for C01 in the dispatch snapshot, or the policy version’s stated threshold. A promotion timestamp just before 11:40 would support the cap; absence of any promotion record with FIFO rank 1 would support plain FIFO for the tail.

## 4.4 QD, 11:30 — appointment precedence vs skill-pool FIFO (predict D06 vs D07)

**(A) Appointment precedence/reservation:** D-X was held for D06’s 11:30 appointment. **(B) Skill-pool FIFO:** D07 (arr 11:14) should go first on D-X. Observed D06 eliminates pure FIFO on D-X and is consistent with appointment, but the 11:20–11:30 idle could also be an availability-logging artefact. **Counterfactual:** if the appointment job had arrived after D07 had already started, reservation could not hold the resource. **Discriminating observable:** a **resource reservation record** linking D-X to D06 created at D06’s arrival (11:10) or booking time. A pre-existing D-XD06 reservation explains the idle; its absence plus a D-X availability start of 11:30 (rather than 11:20) would shift the explanation to availability data.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable: QD.** Skill eligibility alone forces or nearly forces most resource assignments (D01, D02, D05, D07, D10), independently of any ordering rule, and within-pool FIFO is then shown by two P1 jobs (D08, D10) losing to earlier arrivals. The remaining uncertainty is about the idle periods, not about the primary mechanism.

**Least identifiable: QB.** Preemption, resumption and reassignment make B01’s eligibility at each decision depend on unrecorded requeue state; the best-fitting account (FIFO for P2/P3 with a P1-only override) and the main rival (priority-with-aging plus resume-first) agree on most observed choices and differ only in effective-priority records that were not supplied. QC is the next least identifiable, because its key decisions are triple-confounded by priority/SPT/EDF and two decisions need extra hypotheses.

**Most dangerous claim to infer: that QB follows full preemptive priority.** The evidence for preemption rests on one labelled urgent arrival (B02 at 09:16), while the majority of decisions — B01’s resumes, B04 over B06, B06 over B07 — are FIFO/resume behaviour, and P2 demonstrably does **not** jump P3. Generalising “preemptive priority” from B02 would license preempting for every higher-priority arrival, multiplying the fragmentation already visible in B01 (45 min of work spread over 86 min elapsed) and producing priority inversions the queue does not actually exhibit. The same caution applies in QC to an EDF claim: deadlines are present as attributes, but both deadline jobs miss, so treating deadlines as the dispatch rule would overstate the protection urgent jobs actually receive.

Cross-queue lesson: the presence of an attribute — priority in QA, appointments in QB, deadlines in QC — does not establish that the attribute drives dispatch. In each queue the workable diagnosis is a hybrid in which a documented secondary mechanism (appointment in QA, P1 override in QB, deadline/P1 override in QC, appointments within skill pools in QD) explains the main violations of the primary rule, and the exceptions in §5 are not decorative: without availability, snapshot, and requeue state, the primary rules cannot be tested at all.
