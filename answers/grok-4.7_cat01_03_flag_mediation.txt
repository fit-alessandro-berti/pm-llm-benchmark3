## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is a bundle label. In this log it coincides with a hard threshold on `risk_score` (flagged scores 0.64–0.93, unflagged 0.28–0.61). Current policy then does further things: priority triage, a strong push into manual review, and, from review, occasional escalation into a shared senior queue. “Set the flag to zero” is well-defined only once we say which downstream equations still read the badge, and which still read the score, the file, and complexity. Hiding the badge while staff still see the score (P1) is a different regime from keeping the lane and dropping the mandate (P2), from adding review capacity (P3), or from changing escalation (P4).

Natural direct and indirect effects are not used. They need a cross-world mediator — the queue this case would have joined under the other flag — placed inside a world that still contains the flag’s other consequences and other cases’ routes. Shared queues, exposure-induced confounding, and feedback from triage findings into later decisions make that pair of states unrealizable. Controlled direct effects are used only for a protocol switch that can actually be fixed: whether review is mandatory. Waiting times are interventional mediators. Under a stated capacity and routing regime, waits are drawn from the queue distribution that regime induces. The contrasts below are separable regime contrasts.

```text
                    U_underlying_risk, U_case_narrative, triage findings)

if manual_review:
    manual_queue_wait <- shared WIP, peers' flags, arrival_load, U_shift_capacity
    manual_service    <- complexity, review_resource, U_underlying_risk
    escalation := senior_rule(findings, complexity, U_staff_perception, capacity)
    if escalation:
        escalation_queue_wait <- senior WIP, peers
        senior_service        <- U_underlying_risk, findings
        decision_outcome      <- senior_service, U_underlying_risk
    else:
        decision_outcome      <- manual_service, U_underlying_risk
else:
    decision_outcome          <- triage findings, U_underlying_risk

residual close clock starts at the last stage that actually occurred
cycle_time := close - arrival

Feedback across cases:
  each review entry/exit and each escalation entry/exit updates U_queue_state
  seen by later arrivals. U_queue_state then affects their waits and
  their chance of escalation.
```

Interference is built into the shared review and senior queues. Sending a case into review or escalation occupies capacity that other cases on the same shift would have used, so one case’s cycle time depends on other cases’ flags. A contrast that flips one flag and holds every peer route fixed is not an available policy. Exposure-induced confounding follows because the score, complexity, and unobserved risk influence the flag and also influence review, escalation, effort, and the decision. Conditioning on review or escalation, which the flag shifts, opens a path through those factors. Treatment-confounder feedback runs along the case: triage findings change the review decision, review findings change escalation, and the queue left by earlier flagged cases confounds later waits and later escalation choices. A natural indirect effect additionally requires a cross-world wait, the queue time under no flag, inserted into a world that still contains the flag’s other consequences, including peers’ routing. A shared queue cannot be in both of those states. The estimands above are contrasts of fully specified regimes.

The parenthesis is (cases with that stage / subgroup size). `NA` means nobody in the row has the stage. Flagged cases are faster through triage and slower to finish. Median arrival-to-triage-start is 0.20 h versus 2.50 h, and median triage service is 0.60 h versus 0.95 h, yet median cycle time is 16.45 h versus 5.75 h. Every flagged case enters manual review and waits a median 6.75 h to start; 4 of 10 unflagged cases are reviewed, with conditional median wait 1.75 h. Escalation is 6/10 versus 2/10, and when it occurs the median wait is near 7 h with about 2 h of senior service. Close residuals sit near 1 h. The same triage acceleration appears inside High complexity (wait 0.20 h versus 2.20 h) and the manual-wait gap appears there too (8.00 h versus 2.00 h). On the review-without-escalation path, median cycles almost meet (8.80 h versus 8.30 h), so the aggregate gap is largely which path is taken. That description is still not a flag effect: scores do not overlap, T1/T2 are used only for flagged cases, escalation is mostly High complexity, and each entry changes the shared queue that other cases face.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the bundle change cycle time at the score where the flag actually switches? | Local threshold (regression-discontinuity) design at the operational cut, using a pre-declared bandwidth. This log’s cut lies in (0.61, 0.64] and the bandwidth is empty, so the design needs a period in which cases land on both sides | Complexity, arrival hour, arrival_load, and the running score. Do not adjust for review, escalation, or queue time; those are post-flag | The threshold is deterministic and other case features are continuous at the cut. It can eat some of the 5–6 h within-path manual-wait gap and leaves the senior queue in place | Low miss risk, because the mandate stays. Two-tier speed can make protected reviews shallower | Harmful if capacity is reallocated; small if FTE is actually added | Long waits are observed. Headcount versus a parking rule is not identified | Consider only after an audit shows the mandate catches defects the judgment path misses, and only with new headcount |
| P4 same review routing; change escalation criteria (4a) or senior capacity (4b) | Unchanged | 4a removes senior-queue entries. 4b shortens the ~7 h wait and leaves occurrence alone. High complexity is already escalated in 7/8 cases | 4a can be large and is aimed at the longest path (escalated median 21.90 h vs 8.80 h). 4b is a smaller, safer cut | 4a is high risk: every reject in this log was escalated. 4b has less quality risk | The senior pool is shared, so both variants affect unflagged High cases | Escalation tracks High complexity at least as strongly as the flag | Do not tighten criteria in the first pilot. Revisit senior capacity if escalated cases remain slow under P2 |

Run guarded P2. Keep priority triage. Suspend automatic manual review only for arrivals with flag 1, Medium complexity, and risk_score below 0.85. High complexity and any score at or above 0.85 stay on today’s mandatory review path. In this log the eligible slice is the five Medium flagged cases, all approved.

Assign whole 4-hour arrival blocks, 1:1, stratified by arrival_load at the block start. Treatment blocks use the suspension; control blocks keep P0. Block assignment is the rule because a shared queue makes case-level coin flips a blend of the mandate effect and spillovers.

Guardrail: stop the treatment if High-complexity manual-queue median wait in treatment blocks exceeds the control median by more than 2 h, or if review WIP at any manual start exceeds the pre-pilot peak.

Primary outcomes: median cycle time of eligible cases, and median cycle time of all arrivals in the block. Secondary: arrival-to-triage-done, review occurrence, manual-queue wait given review, escalation occurrence, the recorded decision, and a blinded re-review of skipped and reviewed eligible cases.

Collect start and completion times for every stage that occurs, WIP at each transition, resource ids, score, complexity, whether the flag was displayed, overrides, and the audit result. Leave absent stages missing.

Stop if the blinded defect rate on skipped cases exceeds that on reviewed controls in the same score band; if eligible review occurrence does not fall, showing the written mandate is not binding; or if the High-complexity wait guardrail trips.

```python
TIME_COLS = [
    "arrival",
    "triage_start",
    "triage_done",
    "manual_start",
    "manual_done",
    "escalation_start",
    "escalation_done",
    "close",
]


def hours(start, end):
    """Elapsed hours, or NaN if either endpoint is missing. Never returns 0 for a skipped stage."""
    if pd.isna(start) or pd.isna(end):
        return np.nan
    return (end - start).total_seconds() / 3600.0


def add_event(rows, case, activity, start, end, resource, attrs):
    if pd.isna(start) or pd.isna(end):
        return
    rows.append(
        {
            "case:concept:name": case["case"],
            "concept:name": activity,
            "time:timestamp": end,
            "start_timestamp": start,
            "org:resource": resource if pd.notna(resource) and resource != "-" else pd.NA,
            "flag": int(case["flag"]),
            "risk_score": float(case["risk_score"]),
            "complexity": case["complexity"],
            "arrival_load": case["arrival_load"],
            "outcome": case["outcome"],
            **attrs,
        }
    )


def stage_table(cases):
    out = cases.copy()
    out["arrival_to_triage_start"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["arrival"], out["triage_start"])
    ]
    out["triage_service"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["triage_start"], out["triage_done"])
    ]
    out["triage_done_to_manual_start"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["triage_done"], out["manual_start"])
    ]
    out["manual_service"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["manual_start"], out["manual_done"])
    ]
    out["manual_done_to_escalation_start"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["manual_done"], out["escalation_start"])
    ]
    out["escalation_service"] = [
        hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["escalation_start"], out["escalation_done"])
    ]
    residuals = []
    for row in out.itertuples(index=False):
        if pd.notna(row.escalation_done):
            residuals.append(hours(row.escalation_done, row.close))
        elif pd.notna(row.manual_done):
            residuals.append(hours(row.manual_done, row.close))
        else:
            residuals.append(hours(row.triage_done, row.close))
    out["residual_to_close"] = residuals
    out["total_cycle"] = [hours(a, b) for a, b in zip(out["arrival"], out["close"])]
    out["manual_review"] = out["manual_start"].notna()
    out["escalation"] = out["escalation_start"].notna()
    return out


def summarize(frame, stage_cols, label):
    n = len(frame)
    print(f"\n=== {label}  n={n} ===")
    if n < 8:
        warnings.warn(
            f"Small subgroup '{label}' (n={n}). Medians are descriptive only.",
            stacklevel=2,
        )
    for col in stage_cols:
        observed = frame[col].dropna()
        support = len(observed)
        if support == 0:
            print(f"{col}: NA (0/{n}); stage absent for every case in this row")
            continue
        if support < 5:
            warnings.warn(
                f"'{label}' / {col}: only {support}/{n} cases have this stage.",
                stacklevel=2,
            )
        print(
            f"{col}: median={observed.median():.2f}h  "
            f"mean={observed.mean():.2f}h  support={support}/{n}"
        )


def _as_hours(value):
    if value is None or (isinstance(value, float) and np.isnan(value)):
        return value
    if isinstance(value, pd.Timedelta):
        return value.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    if isinstance(value, np.timedelta64):
        return float(value / np.timedelta64(1, "s")) / 3600.0
    # PM4Py sometimes returns mean seconds.
    try:
        number = float(value)
    except (TypeError, ValueError):
        return value
    if number > 48:  # larger than any plausible mean in hours in this extract
        return number / 3600.0
    return number


def print_dfg(label, frequency, performance):
    print(f"\n--- frequency DFG: {label} ---")
    for edge, count in sorted(frequency.items(), key=lambda item: (-item[1], item[0])):
        print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {count}")
    print(f"--- performance DFG (mean completion-to-completion, hours): {label} ---")
    print(
        "  These edge times equal waiting time of the target stage plus its service "
        "time. They are computed only on cases that traverse the edge. "
        "They are not causal effects."
    )
    for edge, value in sorted(performance.items()):
        print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {_as_hours(value):.2f}h")
```

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk. If a risk measure staff can see, and `risk_score` omits, accounts for the within-High manual-wait gap (about 8 h versus 2 h) and for later defects among Medium approvals, the cycle gap is case substance. P2 would then be the wrong cut even in the Medium band.
2. Queue interference. If, under block randomization, High-complexity waits in treatment blocks rise by more than eligible Medium cases gain, keeping priority triage while adding judgmental reviews is harmful at the shift level. P2 should stop even if eligible cases look faster.
3. Decision quality. If the blinded audit finds a higher defect rate among skipped Medium flagged cases than among reviewed controls in the same score band, the cycle-time gain is a miss. The mandate should stay, and the question moves toward P3 with real extra headcount or toward P4b.
4. Timestamp semantics. If start stamps are assignment or queue-entry times rather than the start of work, or if local clocks differ by resource, then the measured “service” intervals contain hidden waiting. The apparent triage-service advantage and the similarity of manual service would not survive a corrected clock, and the wait-versus-service split would be redrawn.
5. Positivity and overlap. If a period with cases on both sides of the cut, or a randomized badge suppression at the same score, shows no remaining cycle-time gap, the observational flag contrast is case mix. The bundle story would be restricted to scores far above the cut, where this sample has no unflagged cases at all.
6. Reviewer behavior. If, once automatic review is suspended, reviewers still open every Medium flagged file because they can see the score, the mandate was not the binding constraint. P2 then changes the written rule and not the path, and the useful intervention becomes capacity (P3 or P4b) or a real change to the information set.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed, flagged scores sit in 0.64–0.93 and unflagged scores in 0.28–0.61. Median triage wait and service are 0.20 h and 0.60 h when flagged, versus 2.50 h and 0.95 h otherwise, while median cycle time is 16.45 h versus 5.75 h. All flagged cases enter manual review (queue median 6.75 h); 4 of 10 unflagged cases do (1.75 h). Escalation occurs for 6 flagged and 2 unflagged cases and adds about 7 h of waiting plus 2 h of senior work. Triage resources split by flag. All four rejects are High-complexity M2 escalations, two per group.

A priority lane explains the fast triage. A review mandate explains the path mix that creates the aggregate cycle-time gap. Where both groups are reviewed and not escalated, median cycles almost meet, 8.80 h and 8.30 h, because triage savings and extra manual waiting offset. Escalation follows High complexity on both sides of the flag and holds those cycles near 19–22 h. Unflagged Low-complexity cases, median 4.45 h with no review, pull the unflagged side down.

The mandate, the lane rule, and T1/T2 team identity remain entangled. So do staff-visible risk missing from the score, spillovers in the shared queues, and decision quality beyond Approve/Reject. Natural direct and indirect effects would need cross-world queue states under interference, which a shared queue cannot host.

Next evidence is a block-randomized pause of automatic review for Medium flagged cases scored below 0.85, leaving priority triage and High-complexity review unchanged, with cycle time, peer waits, and a blinded audit as endpoints.
