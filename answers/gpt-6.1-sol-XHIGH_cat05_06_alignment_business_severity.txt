## 1. Materiality Framework

The supplied alignments describe how the recorded trace differs from the payable-claim model. They are authoritative diagnostic inputs. Their model moves are hypothetical repairs, not proof that the corresponding activities were omitted in reality or should have occurred for every claim.

Use the following business severity scale, with explicit reassessment when evidence changes:

| Severity | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 — Negligible | Fitting behavior or well-supported technical noise, with no identified substantive control failure. |
| 2 — Low | Model, mapping, correlation, or evidence-quality weakness; substantive performance is plausible or supported, but assurance needs repair. |
| 3 — Material | A meaningful service or control deficiency with moderate exposure, such as a confirmed notification breach or an unresolved evidence gap that materially impairs oversight. |
| 4 — Major | A confirmed bypass of approval eligibility or another important preventive control, with meaningful exposure or fraud opportunity. |
| 5 — Critical | Payment demonstrably precedes required approval, or a highly exposed, fraud-sensitive payment lacks substantiated mandatory screening. Immediate investigation and containment are warranted. |

Assess these dimensions together, rather than adding them mechanically:

- **Control objective:** distinguish prevention of unauthorized payment, coverage and assessment validity, and timely claimant communication from interface housekeeping.
- **Amount and exposure:** claim principal indicates possible exposure, not proven loss. A EUR 250,000 claim with a notification-link weakness is not automatically riskier than a EUR 4,500 payment to a recently changed bank account.
- **Reversibility and timing:** stopping an unpaid claim is easier than recovering accepted funds. Screening and approval must precede payment; a later approval cannot restore the preventive control retrospectively. Bank acceptance does not itself establish final settlement or impossibility of recovery.
- **Customer and regulatory harm:** consider diverted funds, incorrect payment or denial, delayed communication, and inability to demonstrate required controls. The question establishes business policies, not specific statutory violations or penalties.
- **Recurrence:** a connector producing 74 duplicate events suggests a systematic data issue; four ineligible automatic approvals suggest a systematic preventive-control weakness. Frequency and per-case materiality remain separate.
- **Evidence reliability:** weigh independently recorded bank times, authenticated assessments, stable identifiers, retained audit histories, and verbal assertions differently. A mutable link weakens attribution even when a message exists.
- **Model scope:** a model for payable claims can penalize legitimate denials and withdrawals. Decide the population and legitimate termination states before interpreting those penalties as missing operational duties.

Confidence measures the strength of the evidence for an interpretation; severity measures the consequence or urgency of the issue being assessed. They are independent. CL-602 has a high-confidence absence of retained fraud evidence, but only medium confidence that screening itself did not occur. Its severity remains critical because the mandatory control is unsubstantiated on a EUR 480,000, HIGH-risk payment following a bank change. Conversely, the retry explanation for CL-603 has high confidence and negligible severity.

The scores below are triage judgments, not calculated risk estimates. Most synchronous notifications lack real timing evidence in the question: they establish a matched activity, not compliance with the 24-hour deadline. Only CL-608 supplies affirmative relative timing for its external message.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

For the supplied alignment, raw cost is the sum of move costs: each visible log or model move costs 1, synchronous moves cost 0, and hidden routing costs 0. This uniform edit-distance objective does not contain principal amount, fraud risk, bank history, timing, evidence reliability, or harm. It ranks representational repairs under a chosen model and cost function.

1. **Uniform cost discards materiality.** CL-601 has cost 3 for three UI events; CL-602 has cost 1 for an unsubstantiated mandatory fraud screen on EUR 480,000 with HIGH risk and a recent bank change. Equal visible-move weights cannot express the difference. Business-weighted costs could help triage, but their assumptions would need governance and would still not measure realized loss or confidence.
2. **Structural fit is not data-aware compliance.** CL-609 and CL-610 cost 0 because M0 admits the automatic branch without guards. Their attributes violate P1, and CL-610 independently triggers P2. No reweighting of their existing zero-cost synchronous moves can reveal the missing eligibility predicates; evaluate policy data separately or enrich the model.
3. **Ordering and reversibility matter beyond edits.** CL-604's early payment is a log move; the later model payment is a hypothetical repair. The two payment moves do not establish duplicate disbursement. Real bank acceptance before recorded approval creates exposure that a cheap sequence repair cannot undo. Recovery feasibility and actual approval time require evidence.
4. **Model scope changes the interpretation of omissions.** CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4 largely because a payable-only lifecycle cannot end at denial or withdrawal. Model moves for approval and payment do not establish an operational failure to approve/pay those cases. CL-612's notification gap remains separately relevant, rather than being dismissed along with the inappropriate payment expectation.
5. **Activity labels and granularity alter distance.** CL-611's likely single renaming costs 2 because substitution is represented by a log move and a model move. CL-601 includes UI granularity outside the business model; CL-603 represents a transport retry as another notification. Choosing logical message versus attempt-level events changes distance without necessarily changing business behavior.
6. **Evidence location is different from activity performance.** CL-606 has external assessment evidence despite its model move. CL-608 has permitted portal/CRM evidence at +3 hours despite its missing core notification. Both need attribution checks; neither model move proves nonperformance. The mutable link in CL-608 makes assurance weaker than a stable retained record, a distinction the cost cannot express.
7. **Hidden routing is fitting.** CL-605's tau is an internal transition, not an unlogged human activity. It adds no supplied cost or business violation. A silent transition cannot stand in for fraud screening or another required visible control.

Costs are useful for finding recurring move signatures, detecting changes in recording behavior, and measuring fit within a stable, appropriate scope. Comparisons require the same cost function, model version, event mapping, and trace population. They do not constitute compliance certification. Alternative optimal alignments can distribute moves differently, but the supplied diagnostic assignments remain the basis of this analysis. Severity, confidence, and evidence gaps must be reported alongside technical cost, with no conversion of edit counts or claim amounts into invented expected losses.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

Its calculated cost is therefore retained separately from the supplied authoritative raw cost. def auto_eligibility(row):
    if "Auto Approve" not in traces[row["case"]]:
        return "not used"
    amount, risk, bank = row["amount_eur"], row["risk"], row["recent_bank_change"]
    bank_known = not pd.isna(bank)
    fails = (
        (not pd.isna(amount) and amount > 5000)
        or risk not in {"LOW", "UNKNOWN"}
        or (bank_known and bool(bank))
    )
    if fails:
        return "P1 violation"
    if pd.isna(amount) or risk == "UNKNOWN" or not bank_known:
        return "unknown eligibility"
    return "P1 eligible"


def p2_triggers(row):
    if "Pay Claim" not in traces[row["case"]]:
        return "not applicable: no observed payment"
    reasons = []
    if not pd.isna(row["amount_eur"]) and row["amount_eur"] > 50000:
        reasons.append("amount > EUR 50000")
    if row["risk"] == "HIGH":
        reasons.append("HIGH risk")
    bank = row["recent_bank_change"]
    if not pd.isna(bank) and bool(bank):
        reasons.append("recent bank change")
    return "; ".join(reasons) if reasons else "no known trigger"


def core_route_before_payment(case):
    activities = traces[case]
    if "Pay Claim" not in activities:
        return "not applicable"
    payment_position = activities.index("Pay Claim")
    observed = all(
        activity in activities and activities.index(activity) < payment_position
        for activity in ("Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve")
    )
    # 'Not evidenced' is deliberately not called 'did not occur'.
    return "core route evidenced" if observed else "core route not evidenced"
