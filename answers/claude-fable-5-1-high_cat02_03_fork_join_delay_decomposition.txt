# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Notation: case index c ∈ {F01, …, F12}; branch index i ∈ {A, B, C}. All quantities are in minutes, computed from complete timestamps accurate to one minute (so every figure carries ±1 min of rounding uncertainty; I do not propagate that further because it is far below the effect sizes discussed).

Logged events per case: `arr_c` (arrival), `fr_c` (fork_release), `s_{c,i}`, `e_{c,i}` (branch start/end), `fs_c`, `fe_c` (final start/end).

Derived quantities

- Upstream elapsed: `U_c = fr_c − arr_c`
- Branch queue: `Q_{c,i} = s_{c,i} − fr_c` (waiting after the fork permits the branch to begin; label "queue" is a modelling assumption, see §7)
- Branch service: `S_{c,i} = e_{c,i} − s_{c,i}`
- Branch elapsed: `E_{c,i} = Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} = e_{c,i} − fr_c`
- Join ready (not logged, derived from the AND-join rule): `JR_c = max_i e_{c,i}`
- Critical branch: `crit_c = argmax_i e_{c,i}` (ties broken A < B < C and flagged; none occur in this data)
- Branch synchronization wait: `W_{c,i} = JR_c − e_{c,i}` (zero for the critical branch by construction)
- Fork-join stage elapsed: `G_c = JR_c − fr_c = max_i E_{c,i}`
- Downstream waiting before F: `D_c = fs_c − JR_c`
- Final service: `V_c = fe_c − fs_c`
- Total flow time: `T_c = fe_c − arr_c`
- Slack of the critical branch: `slack_c = E_{c,(1)} − E_{c,(2)}`, the gap between the largest and second-largest branch elapsed; the critical branch can be accelerated by at most this amount before another branch becomes critical.

Non-overlapping identity (the decomposition without double counting):

`T_c = U_c + G_c + D_c + V_c`, with `G_c = max_i (Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i})`.

The four segments partition the interval [arr_c, fe_c]; the residual is exactly 0 for all 12 cases (checked in the code).

Why summing branch synchronization waits would double count. For every branch, `Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W_{c,i} = G_c`: each branch's own row already accounts for the entire stage, whether by working, queueing, or waiting at the join. The three branch rows therefore describe the same interval [fr_c, JR_c] three times, so `Σ_i W_{c,i} = 3·G_c − Σ_i E_{c,i}` is not a duration of the case but a triple-counted overlap. Example F07: `W_A = 25`, `W_B = 0`, `W_C = 52`, sum 77 > `G_c = 75`; adding 77 to the queue and service figures would produce a "flow time" of 20 + (50+75+23) + 77 + 15 + 20 = 280 min against a real 130 min. The correct elapsed-time roles are: `G_c` enters the identity once; `W_{c,i}` are diagnostic measures of how far each branch is from being critical (they equal `E_{c,crit} − E_{c,i}`), and they are the per-branch "cushion" that any acceleration of branch i must exceed before the case gains anything.

Consistency of the individual branch view: `Σ_i E_{c,i}` is total branch work-plus-queue content and is also not elapsed time; for F07 it is 148 min inside a 75-min stage, which is simply the statement that three things ran in parallel.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

Branch comparison (n = 12 cases, minutes)

| branch | service mean | service range | queue mean | queue range | elapsed mean | elapsed p90 | elapsed max | times critical | times runner-up | mean sync wait | mean sync wait when not critical |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 50.0 | 50–50 | 0.0 | 0–0 | 50.0 | 50 | 50 | 2 | 10 | 11.0 | 13.2 |
| B | 37.5 | 30–45 | 19.2 | 5–30 | 56.7 | 74 | 75 | 8 | 2 | 4.3 | 13.0 |
| C | 20.2 | 18–30 | 10.4 | 5–35 | 30.6 | 53 | 57 | 2 | 0 | 30.4 | 36.5 |

Flow-time budget (means): upstream 19.6 + stage 61.0 + downstream 14.7 + final 20.0 = 115.3. The fork-join stage is 53% of flow time; the stage is on average only 4.3 min above B's mean elapsed and 11 min above A's constant.

Ranking by influence on completion time

1. B — Machine Stress Test (most influential). It is the last branch in 8 of 12 cases, it produces both 75-min stages (F07, F12), its elapsed p90 (74) and max (75) are the tail of `JR`, and its mean sync wait is the smallest (4.3). Crucially its influence is not service-driven: mean service (37.5) is below A's (50); B is critical because queue (mean 19.2, up to 30) sits on top of service (up to 45). Queue and service in B are positively associated (correlation ≈ 0.57 across the 12 cases), so the long-service tests are also the ones that wait longest, which compounds the tail. In cases where B is critical its slack over the runner-up (always A) is 5–25 min, so B improvements are capped by A's 50-min floor.
2. A — Laboratory Assay (second, as the binding floor rather than the frequent last-finisher). A has the largest mean service but is critical only twice (F01, F11), each time by a small margin (5 and 10 min). Its real influence is structural: it is the runner-up in 10 of 12 cases, so it defines the minimum stage of 50 min and it caps every gain from improving B or C. No case can have a stage below 50 min while A stays at 50. Its zero queue and constant duration, together with at least four cases running A simultaneously (08:55–09:00: F01–F04), point to an automated or fixed-duration protocol rather than a staffed queue (hypothesis).
3. C — Documentation Review (third). Critical in 2 of 12 cases (tied with A in frequency; this is an honest tie on that criterion alone), never the runner-up, mean sync wait 30 min, i.e. C usually finishes with half an hour to spare. Its two critical cases are driven by sporadic holds (queue 35 and 25 min with slightly longer service), not by typical behaviour. In both cases another case that forked later was served first, and C had idle time while F04/F08 were waiting, which is inconsistent with a simple capacity queue and points to case-specific blocking (missing or incomplete documentation is one hypothesis). C's influence is a tail-risk problem, not a throughput problem.

Why A ≻ C despite equal critical frequency: A's slack when critical and A's runner-up role limit gains everywhere; C's excess is fully removable case by case without affecting others (removing C's holds saves at most 7 + 5 = 12 min in total because A is right behind).

Near-ties worth stating: F01 (A by 5 over B) and F10 (B by 5 over A) and F08 (C by 5 over A) are within 5 min, i.e. within a few minutes of timestamp rounding of flipping; F04 (C by 7) is similar. Conclusions about which branch is critical in those four cases are fragile; the aggregate conclusion (B dominates, A is the floor) is not.

Additional observation on B's queue: B is not served FIFO across cases (F08 forked 09:55 and started B at 10:00, while F07 forked 09:40 waited until 10:10), and at least three B tests overlap at several times (09:05–09:25, 10:10–10:30, 10:50–11:15). So B's queue is not a single-server line; it may reflect heterogeneous rigs, test-type routing, or setup time that is logged as waiting. This matters for CF2 (§4): "halving the queue" is a target, not yet an identified mechanism.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

Method. For branch counterfactuals I recompute `JR'_c = max_i e'_{c,i}` with the modified branch and report the reduction `JR_c − JR'_c`. Realised flow-time savings depend on what the final station does with an earlier `JR`, so I report three variants: (i) pass-through, `D_c` unchanged (upper bound); (ii) final start frozen at the observed time (lower bound, zero); (iii) middle estimates from a pooled FIFO simulation of the final station (2 or 3 servers, 20-min service, 8-min minimum handover lag equal to the smallest observed `D_c`), applied identically to baseline and scenario so that knock-on effects between cases are included. Assumption label: the 8-min lag as a fixed handover component is an assumption chosen because it is the minimum observed wait with everything idle; it is not identified from the data.

CF1 — reduce A service by 20% (50 → 40 min in all cases)

- Direct benefit: only where A is critical. F01 saves 5 (A drops to 40, B at 45 becomes critical, so only the 5-min slack is realised), F11 saves 10 (A and B tie at 40). Total 15 min, 1.25 min per case, about 1% of mean flow time, despite A having the largest mean service and 120 min of service removed in total.
- Zero-benefit cases: 10 of 12, because B (8) or C (2) remains the last branch.
- Secondary effects: the important one is indirect. Lowering A's floor from 50 to 40 raises the slack available to B improvements in every B-critical case; CF5 shows the interaction: 117.5 > 92.5 + 15 = 107.5, a 10-min superadditive gain (F01 +2.5, F05 +2.5, F10 +5). Downstream masking is severe for CF1 in the 2-server simulation: F11's earlier `JR` (11:20) collides with F09 (11:15) and F10 (11:20) already waiting, so only 5 of 15 min survive; with a third server the full 15 survive. A-side resource utilisation falls (if A is a staffed resource), but there is no evidence A is capacity-constrained at all.

CF2 — reduce B queue delay by 50%

- Direct benefit: in the 8 B-critical cases, saving = min(0.5·Q_B, slack). Realised: F02 10, F03 10, F05 10, F06 15, F07 15, F09 12.5, F10 5, F12 15 = 92.5 min, mean 7.7 min per case (6.7% of mean flow time), tail stage 75 → 60. In F02, F03, F05, F09 the saving is truncated by A's 50-min floor (A becomes critical or tied); in F05 and F10 B ceases to be critical altogether.
- Zero-benefit cases: F01, F04, F08, F11 (A or C critical).
- Secondary effects: after CF2 the critical branch shifts to A in about half the cases; further B work yields nothing without CF1. Because B's queue mechanism is not identified (non-FIFO, multi-rig overlap), halving the queue may require rig capacity, scheduling, or setup reduction; each has a different cost. Downstream: in the 2-server simulation the saving is not masked but slightly amplified (97.5), because earlier B completions also de-conflict the final station for later cases (F11 gains 5 min through knock-on). This holds only if the final station really dispatches on `JR`; if its 8–20 min lag is a fixed schedule rather than a queue, some of the 92.5 min would be absorbed (lower bound zero).

CF3 — final-station capacity / downstream waiting

Counterfactual variants (observed `JR`, FIFO by `JR`, 20-min service):

| variant | total saving | mean per case |
|---|---:|---:|
| CF3a pooled 2 servers, 8-min minimum lag (pure queue + handover) | 75 | 6.25 |
| CF3b add a third server, 8-min lag | 80 | 6.67 |

| CF3d 3 servers, no lag | 176 | 14.67 |

- Direct benefit of adding capacity (CF3b vs CF3a): 5 min in total, all in F11. Two servers are already sufficient for this arrival pattern (utilisation of the final station over 09:08–12:40 is 240 min of work in roughly 424 station-minutes, about 57%).
- Direct benefit of pooling and dispatching on readiness (CF3a): 75 min, i.e. 43% of downstream waiting, with no new resources.
- Direct benefit of removing the handover lag (CF3c): 171 min, essentially all downstream waiting; whether that lag is removable depends on what it is, which the log cannot tell (§7).
- Zero-benefit cases: F01 under CF3a/b (already at the 8-min floor); none under CF3c/d.
- Secondary effects and masking: the downstream stage currently masks small branch improvements when the final station is locally busy (CF1's F11 saving shrinks from 10 to 0 under the 2-server model) but does not mask CF2, and dispatching on `JR` actually converts branch savings into extra downstream de-conflicting. Conversely, if branch redesigns compress `JR` clustering (several cases becoming ready within minutes of each other), a 2-server station with a 20-min service will start to create genuine queueing; CF5 combined with CF3a should be re-simulated before committing.

CF4 (additional) — cap C's queue at 5 min (remove the F04/F08 holds): saves 7 + 5 = 12 min, then A is critical in both cases. Zero benefit in 10 cases. Its value is tail protection and predictability, not mean flow time.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

Redesign 1 — Pool the final station and dispatch on `join_ready`, then attack the handover lag (CF3a → CF3c), before adding any resource.

- Throughput: unchanged (F is not saturated, about 57% busy); the gain is flow time, not capacity.
- Flow-time tail: removes 7–12 min from most cases immediately (mean 6.25), cutting the 130-min maximum to about 123; with the handover lag eliminated the mean falls by about 14 min (12%).
- Resource utilisation: F1/F2 utilisation unchanged in total but more even; the idle windows created by the odd/even rule disappear.
- New bottlenecks: none expected at F with current arrivals; if upstream redesigns (Redesign 2) compress `JR` clustering, genuine F queueing appears and a third resource or shorter F service becomes relevant. Check `both busy` minutes after the change.
- Synchronization effects: none on the branches; but a faster F exposes the fork-join stage as an even larger share of flow time (from 53% toward 60%), so it should be sequenced with Redesign 2.
- Pilot/measure first: log the reason for each minute between `JR` and `final_start` for a week (assembly, waiting for reviewer, waiting for slot). Measure the wait when a resource is idle; if it does not fall below 8 min, the lag is a process step and should be redesigned as such (e.g., auto-assembly of the dossier when the last branch completes). Also confirm that the AND-join rule really holds (no F starting before all branches end; true in all 12 cases here).

Redesign 2 — Reduce B's queue and shorten the A protocol together (CF5), not separately.

- Throughput: a shorter A raises A capacity if A is a staffed or shared instrument (not identified); B queue reduction likely means scheduling B tests in fork order with rigs reserved on `fork_release`, or reducing setup logged as waiting.
- Flow-time tail: CF2 alone brings the worst stage from 75 to 60 and saves 92.5 min in total; adding CF1 lifts the total to 117.5 and lowers the minimum possible stage from 50 to 40. Mean flow time falls by about 10 min (8.5%), the maximum from 130 to about 115.
- Resource utilisation: B rigs work the same hours but with less idle-while-waiting; A utilisation drops 20%.
- New bottlenecks: after CF2, A becomes critical or tied in roughly half of the cases (F02, F05, F06, F10 and near-ties elsewhere), so B improvement beyond about 50% of queue is worthless without the A change; after both, C's sporadic holds (F04/F08) become the largest remaining tail risk and CF4 (a document-completeness check at or before `fork_release`) should follow.
- Synchronization effects: sync waits equalise across branches; total elapsed variance in `JR` falls, which makes F arrivals more regular (good) but potentially bunched right after each fork (watch F).
- Pilot/measure first: (a) B's dispatch discipline: record rig identity and reason for wait, and test whether FIFO on `fork_release` reduces the 20–30 min waits; (b) whether A's 50 min is a fixed incubation (not compressible by staffing) or a manual protocol; (c) re-run this decomposition weekly and track `times critical` per branch as the KPI, not mean service.

Risks common to both: with 12 cases from a single morning the estimates are fragile; the odd/even resource pattern and the 8–20 min lag may be artefacts of one shift's practice; all counterfactuals assume no behavioural response (e.g., reviewers filling freed time with other work).

    tcols = [c for c in df.columns if c not in ("case", "final_resource")]
    for c in tcols:
        df[c] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[c])
    return df


def minutes(a: pd.Series, b: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    return (b - a).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0


def decompose(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Every fork-join quantity below is a CUSTOM pandas calculation.
    Standard directly-follows performance (e.g. discover_performance_dfg) only
    measures the lag between consecutive events of a case; it does not know
    which branch was last, so it cannot recover join_ready, per-branch
    synchronization waiting, or the critical branch. Those are derived here
    with max-based fork-join logic."""
    out = pd.DataFrame({"case": df["case"]})
    out["upstream_elapsed"] = minutes(df["arrival"], df["fork_release"])
    for i in BRANCHES:
        out[f"{i}_queue"] = minutes(df["fork_release"], df[f"{i}_start"])
        out[f"{i}_service"] = minutes(df[f"{i}_start"], df[f"{i}_end"])
        out[f"{i}_elapsed"] = out[f"{i}_queue"] + out[f"{i}_service"]
    ends = df[[f"{i}_end" for i in BRANCHES]]
    join_ready = ends.max(axis=1)                      # JR_c = max_i end_{c,i}
    out["join_ready"] = join_ready
    for i in BRANCHES:                                 # sync_{c,i} = JR_c - end_{c,i}
        out[f"{i}_sync"] = minutes(df[f"{i}_end"], join_ready)
    out["stage_elapsed"] = minutes(df["fork_release"], join_ready)   # = max_i elapsed_{c,i}

    def crit(row):
        el = {i: row[f"{i}_elapsed"] for i in BRANCHES}
        best = max(el.values())
        winners = sorted([i for i, v in el.items() if v == best], key=TIE_ORDER.get)
        return pd.Series({"critical_branch": winners[0],
                          "critical_tie": "/".join(winners) if len(winners) > 1 else ""})
    out = pd.concat([out, out.apply(crit, axis=1)], axis=1)
    # slack of the critical branch over the runner-up: how much it can be
    # accelerated before another branch becomes critical
    out["slack_to_2nd"] = out[[f"{i}_elapsed" for i in BRANCHES]].apply(
        lambda r: sorted(r)[-1] - sorted(r)[-2], axis=1)
    out["downstream_queue"] = minutes(join_ready, df["final_start"])
    out["final_service"] = minutes(df["final_start"], df["final_end"])
    out["total_flow_time"] = minutes(df["arrival"], df["final_end"])
    # Non-overlapping identity check (must be 0 for every case):
    out["identity_residual"] = (out["upstream_elapsed"] + out["stage_elapsed"]
                                + out["downstream_queue"] + out["final_service"]
                                - out["total_flow_time"])
    return out


def final_station_diagnostics(df: pd.DataFrame, dec: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Split downstream waiting into (a) minutes during which BOTH F1 and F2
    were busy with other cases (capacity-attributable) and (b) the remainder,
    during which at least one final resource was idle (policy/handover lag)."""
    rows = []
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        jr = dec.loc[dec["case"] == r["case"], "join_ready"].iloc[0]
        busy_until = {}
        for res in ["F1", "F2"]:
            others = df[(df["final_resource"] == res) & (df["case"] != r["case"])
                        & (df["final_start"] <= jr) & (df["final_end"] > jr)]
            busy_until[res] = others["final_end"].max() if len(others) else jr
        first_free = min(busy_until.values())
        cap_wait = max(0.0, (first_free - jr).total_seconds() / 60.0)
        total_wait = (r["final_start"] - jr).total_seconds() / 60.0
        rows.append({"case": r["case"], "join_ready": jr.strftime("%H:%M"),
                     "final_start": r["final_start"].strftime("%H:%M"),
                     "downstream_queue": total_wait, "both_busy_wait": cap_wait,
                     "idle_resource_wait": total_wait - cap_wait})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def simulate_final(join_ready: pd.Series, n_servers: int, min_lag: float,
                   service: float = 20.0) -> pd.Series:
    """FIFO-by-join_ready pooled final station with a fixed handover lag.
    Returns the simulated final_end per case (index aligned to join_ready)."""
    order = join_ready.sort_values().index
    free = [pd.Timestamp("2026-05-19 00:00")] * n_servers
    end = pd.Series(index=join_ready.index, dtype="datetime64[ns]")
    for idx in order:
        k = min(range(n_servers), key=lambda j: free[j])
        start = max(join_ready[idx] + pd.Timedelta(minutes=min_lag), free[k])
        free[k] = start + pd.Timedelta(minutes=service)
        end[idx] = free[k]
    return end


def counterfactuals(df: pd.DataFrame, dec: pd.DataFrame) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    base_jr = dec["join_ready"]
    base_total = dec["total_flow_time"]

    # Baseline final station re-simulated with the same model used for the
    # scenarios, so that masking by downstream congestion is measured
    # like-for-like (pooled FIFO, 20-min service, 8-min minimum handover lag).
    base_sim2 = simulate_final(base_jr, 2, 8.0)
    base_sim3 = simulate_final(base_jr, 3, 8.0)

    def branch_scenario(name, a_end, b_end, c_end):
        jr = pd.concat([a_end, b_end, c_end], axis=1).max(axis=1)
        saved_jr = (base_jr - jr).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        # Upper bound: downstream lag unchanged -> saving passes straight through.
        # Lower bound: final_start cannot move earlier than observed -> zero.
        # Middle estimates: saving that survives a pooled FIFO final station
        # with 2 or 3 servers (knock-on effects between cases included).
        s2 = (base_sim2 - simulate_final(jr, 2, 8.0)).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        s3 = (base_sim3 - simulate_final(jr, 3, 8.0)).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        return pd.DataFrame({"case": dec["case"], "scenario": name,
                             "saved_pass_through": saved_jr,
                             "saved_through_fifo2": s2,
                             "saved_through_fifo3": s3,
                             "saved_if_final_fixed": 0.0,
                             "new_join_ready": jr})

    res = []
    # CF1: A service -20%
    a_end = df["A_start"] + (df["A_end"] - df["A_start"]) * 0.8
    res.append(branch_scenario("CF1 A service -20%", a_end, df["B_end"], df["C_end"]))
    # CF2: B queue -50%
    b_shift = (df["B_start"] - df["fork_release"]) * 0.5
    res.append(branch_scenario("CF2 B queue -50%", df["A_end"], df["B_end"] - b_shift, df["C_end"]))
    # CF4 (extra): C queue capped at 5 min (remove the F04/F08 documentation holds)
    c_q = df["C_start"] - df["fork_release"]
    c_shift = (c_q - pd.Timedelta(minutes=5)).clip(lower=pd.Timedelta(0))
    res.append(branch_scenario("CF4 C queue capped at 5", df["A_end"], df["B_end"], df["C_end"] - c_shift))
    # CF5 (extra): CF1 + CF2 combined
    res.append(branch_scenario("CF5 A-20% and B queue-50%", a_end, df["B_end"] - b_shift, df["C_end"]))
    branch_cf = pd.concat(res, ignore_index=True)

    # CF3: downstream. Variants of the final station, all with the observed
    # join_ready, FIFO by join_ready, 20-min service:
    #   3a  two servers, min handover lag 8 (= observed minimum)  -> "pure queue" model
    #   3b  three servers, min lag 8                              -> add capacity
    #   3c  two servers, min lag 0                                -> remove policy lag
    #   3d  three servers, min lag 0
    variants = {"CF3a 2 servers, lag 8": (2, 8.0), "CF3b 3 servers, lag 8": (3, 8.0),
                "CF3c 2 servers, lag 0": (2, 0.0), "CF3d 3 servers, lag 0": (3, 0.0)}
    rows = []
    for name, (n, lag) in variants.items():
        sim_end = simulate_final(base_jr, n, lag)
        total = (sim_end - df["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        rows.append(pd.DataFrame({"case": dec["case"], "scenario": name,
                                  "sim_total": total, "saved": base_total - total}))
    down_cf = pd.concat(rows, ignore_index=True)
    return branch_cf, down_cf


def build_pm4py_intervals(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """One row per branch/final activity with start_timestamp and completion
    time:timestamp (interval log)."""
    parts = []
    for act, s, e, res in [("Laboratory Assay", "A_start", "A_end", None),
                           ("Machine Stress Test", "B_start", "B_end", None),
                           ("Documentation Review", "C_start", "C_end", None),
                           ("Release Decision", "final_start", "final_end", "final_resource")]:
        p = pd.DataFrame({"case:concept:name": df["case"], "concept:name": act,
                          "start_timestamp": df[s], "time:timestamp": df[e],
                          "org:resource": df[res] if res else act})
        parts.append(p)
    ev = pd.concat(parts, ignore_index=True).sort_values(["case:concept:name", "start_timestamp"])
    return ev

# 7. Limits of inference

- Causality. The decomposition is arithmetic on timestamps; "critical branch" means last to complete, not "cause of delay". B being last in 8 cases is consistent with B being slow, with B being started late by a scheduler that knows A takes 50 min anyway (deliberate de-prioritisation), or with B tests being dispatched after C for staffing reasons. The counterfactuals assume that shortening one branch leaves the others' timestamps unchanged; if branches share staff or a scheduler reacts to slack, that assumption fails.
- Branch resource capacity. No resource is logged for A, B or C. Overlap counts give only lower bounds: at least 4 concurrent A assays, at least 3 concurrent B tests, at least 2 concurrent C reviews (F04 and F05 overlap 09:30–09:35). Whether A's zero queue reflects unlimited capacity, an automated incubation, or reservation at fork time cannot be told apart; whether B's 5–30 min "queue" is capacity waiting, setup, transport, or rig heterogeneity is unidentified (the non-FIFO order F08-before-F07 rules out a single FIFO line but not much else).
- Work preemption and interruptions. Start/end pairs give elapsed service, not net working time. B services of 30–45 min and C services of 18–30 min may include interruptions; the two long C services (F04 22, F08 30) could be rework, waiting for a missing document mid-review, or genuinely more material. The 12-min C idle windows before F04 and F08 were served are consistent with a hold (missing inputs) but also with a reviewer break.
- Join semantics. `join_ready` is derived from the stated AND rule. The data are consistent with it (no `final_start` precedes any branch end, the minimum gap is 8 min) but they cannot prove it; if F in reality also needs e.g. a supervisor sign-off or a batch release slot, some of the "downstream queue" is actually a further activity and the decomposition is mislabelled, not wrong in total.
- Downstream waiting: queue or policy? 151 of 176 min of waiting occurred while a final resource was idle; the resources alternate strictly by case parity; even fully idle moments (F01, F12) show 8–10 min of lag. That is strong evidence against pure capacity queueing and for a dispatch rule or a fixed handover step, but the log cannot distinguish (i) a round-robin assignment policy, (ii) an unlogged dossier-assembly task, (iii) reviewers working on other processes not in this log, or (iv) periodic batch pick-up. Only the first would be a "free" fix; the others move or need work rather than eliminating it. The CF3 savings should therefore be read as an upper bound on what a dispatch change can deliver.
- Timestamp resolution and sample size. One-minute rounding makes the four critical-branch calls decided by ≤5–7 min (F01, F04, F08, F10) fragile; 12 cases from a single morning with a strictly regular 15-min arrival pattern and a 20-min upstream step cannot support estimates of tail percentiles, day-of-week effects, or load-dependence (the 15-min inter-arrival interval means the system was never stressed; behaviour at higher load is extrapolation).
- Counterfactual modelling. The final-station simulation assumes FIFO by `join_ready`, a 20-min deterministic service and an 8-min fixed lag; each is an assumption chosen to match the observed minimum, not identified structure. Reported savings are bounds under stated assumptions, and the ranking of interventions (dispatch policy > B queue > A service > C holds > extra F capacity) is more robust than any single number.
