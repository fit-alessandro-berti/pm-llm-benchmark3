**Causal mechanisms that appear shared**  
The core mechanism is the reduction of idle time between steps: when two independent checks can be performed concurrently, the overall time from registration to the point where both are complete shortens. This reduces the readytoassess timestamp and, if assessment capacity is not saturated, translates into a shorter overall cycle.

**Sitespecific mechanisms**  
Site A’s improvement was amplified by several factors absent at Site B: a shift toward simpler digital claims, a registry API upgrade that reduced identitycheck latency, and the addition of two document reviewers. Site B has a legalreview stage with a supplement loop, a high proportion of cases requiring physical originals, and a caselocking IT system that prevents true concurrency. These mechanisms are not present in Site A’s data.

**Effect modifiers**  
The proportion of digitalonly cases, the originalsrequired flag, the IT caselock behaviour, the degree of role specialisation, and the capacity of the assessment and legal pools are all effect modifiers. They determine whether the same intervention produces a net benefit, no effect, or even harm.

**Why a similar controlflow diagram does not guarantee transportability**  
A controlflow diagram captures only the authorised sequencing of tasks, not the datadependent prerequisites (originals), resource constraints, or the capacitydriven queuing dynamics. Site A’s process diagram after the change is isomorphic to the intended diagram for Site B, but the underlying operational realities differ so much that the same diagram can produce an entirely different performance outcome.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Modified intervention**  
Apply parallelisation only to **cases where original documents are not legally required** (digitalonly claims). For these cases, create both an Identity Check task and a Document Check task immediately after Registration. The tasks may be executed concurrently by the separate identity and document reviewer pools. For cases that require original documents, keep the current process (Identity Check and Originals Receipt may start in parallel; Document Check waits for originals). The legalreview stage and its supplement loop are not altered.

**Eligibility rules**  
- Eligible: a claim flagged as “no originals required” at Registration.  
- Ineligible: all claims legally requiring physical originals, or claims already in a rework loop.

**Concurrency and prerequisites**  
- `Register` must complete before any verification task.  
- For eligible cases, `Identity Check` and `Document Check` start immediately after `Register` and may proceed in parallel.  
- `Assessment` requires both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to be completed.  
- The statutory prerequisite that `Document Check` must follow originals receipt is preserved; for eligible cases originals are not required, so the check can start immediately.  
- Legal review, supplement requests, and rework follow the standard sequential path and are not parallelised.

**Queue and WIP controls**  
- Each identity reviewer is limited to at most 5 open identity tasks.  
- Each document reviewer is limited to at most 6 open document tasks.  
- A daily 10minute huddle reviews queue lengths and rebalances work if one pool is falling behind.

**Resource responsibilities**  
- Identity reviewers perform only identity checks.  
- Document reviewers perform only document checks.  
- Assessors and legal reviewers remain in their current roles.

**Handling of legalreview and supplement loops**  
- After `Assessment`, the case may proceed to `Legal Review` or directly to `Approve`/`Reject`.  
- If `Legal Review` issues a `Request Supplement`, the case reenters the `Document Check` and `Assessment` sequence (sequential, not parallelised).  
- The identity check is not repeated in the supplement loop.

**Logging requirements**  
- Systemgenerated timestamps must record the creation, assignment, start, and completion of each task.  
- The casemanagement system must be modified to allow multiple active tasks for eligible cases; otherwise the pilot cannot proceed.

**Partialorder relation set (POWLlike description)**  
For eligible cases:  
- `Register` < `Identity Check`  
- `Register` < `Document Check`  
- (`Identity Check` + `Document Check`) < `Assessment`  
- `Assessment` < XOR( `Approve`, `Reject`, `Legal Review` )  
- `Legal Review` < XOR( `Approve`, `Reject`, `Request Supplement` )  
- `Request Supplement` < `Document Check`  
- `Request Supplement` < `Assessment`  (the loop back)  

For ineligible (originalsrequired) cases the current partial order remains:  
- `Register` < `Identity Check`  
- `Register` < `Originals Receipt`  
- `Identity Check` < `Document Check`  
- `Originals Receipt` < `Document Check`  
- `Document Check` < `Assessment`  
- … same subsequent steps.

## 7. Pilot for transport learning

| design element | specification | causal purpose | operational safeguard | processmining measurement |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **unit of assignment** | claim (case) | directly links the intervention to the outcome | no interference between cases if the system allows multiple active tasks | caselevel trace with all task start/end timestamps |
| **randomisation / phased assignment** | clusterrandomised by week and team: each week, eligible claims arriving on MondayWednesday are assigned to the parallelisation (treatment) arm; ThursdayFriday claims are controls (sequential) | avoids contamination by controlling for time trends and team effects | the same staff work on both arms; potential carryover is measured by analysing the transition periods | compare overlap %, cycle time, and waitingbeforeassessment between arms using the event log |
| **eligible strata** | digitalonly claims (no originals required) | isolates the subgroup where the mechanism can truly operate | prevents dilution of the effect by originalsrequired cases | separate process models for treatment and control within the stratum |
| **treatment versions** | T1: parallel Identity and Document Check (candidate intervention); T2: current sequential process | directly tests the added value of parallelisation | both versions follow the same eligibility rules; only the task creation logic differs | compare the frequency of the “parallel” pattern (both tasks active simultaneously) between arms |
| **primary outcomes** | median cycle time, p90 cycle time, waitingbeforeassessment | captures the intended benefit and the risk of tail worsening | tracked in real time; a stop rule is activated if p90 increases by >10% | use process mining to recompute these metrics from the event log, not from manual reports |
| **secondary outcomes** | rework rate, supplement request rate, appeal rate, identity and document queue lengths, % of time cases spend with multiple active tasks | monitors quality and capacity sideeffects | reviewed weekly by the pilot steering group | discover conformance of the actual process to the intended partial order |
| **interference controls** | the evaluation uses cases from the same weeks but different days; mixedarm periods are short; analysis adjusts for calendar time | prevents contamination between arms | if evidence of carryover appears (e.g., treatment speeds up control cases by clearing queues), the design will be switched to a full crossover of twoweek blocks | measure queue lengths and resource utilisation by arm and day |
| **power / minimumsupport logic** | minimum 400 eligible cases per arm ( 8 weeks of volume) to detect a 10% reduction in median cycle time with 80% power, assuming a coefficient of variation of 0.6 | ensures the pilot can detect a meaningful effect | the pilot will run for at least 8 weeks unless a stop rule is triggered earlier | the event log is continuously checked for data quality; incomplete traces are flagged |
| **duration** | 8–12 weeks | balances the need for statistical power with the risk of a prolonged underperforming intervention | a formal review after 4 weeks decides whether to continue, expand, or stop | process mining dashboards updated daily |
| **stop rules** | 1) p90 cycle time increases by >10% relative to the control arm; 2) rework rate increases by >2 percentage points; 3) more than 20% of eligible cases cannot be processed because of IT lock failures | protects against harm and implementation failure | rules are preregistered and automatically flagged by the monitoring system | the event log is the sole source for computing stoprule triggers |
| **transportability analysis** | at the end of the pilot, compare the effect size (reduction in median cycle time) and the increase in overlap between Site B’s digitalonly cases and Site A’s overall beforeafter results, after adjusting for casemix and cointerventions | provides a datadriven estimate of the transportable effect | the analysis will be prespecified before unblinding | use the event log to build a structural causal model with the measured overlap as a mediator |

**Separating mechanism failure from implementation failure**  
The pilot explicitly measures both the *process change* (true overlap, derived from systemgenerated timestamps) and the *outcome* (cycle time). If the overlap does not increase, the implementation has failed—most likely because the IT lock was not effectively lifted or staff reverted to sequential work. If the overlap increases substantially but cycle time does not improve, the mechanism has failed—for example, because assessment became the bottleneck and absorbed the time saving. The event log allows us to construct a mediation analysis: overlap  waitingbeforeassessment  cycle time. This decomposition tells us whether the causal chain is broken and, if so, at which link, preventing a mistaken conclusion that the intervention “does not work” when it was never properly implemented.

## 9. Recommendation

**Conditional go**

**What should be transferred**  
The principle of starting independent verification tasks (Identity Check and Document Check) concurrently after Registration should be transferred, but only for the subgroup of cases where no physical originals are legally required. The daily queue huddles and a WIP limit per reviewer (5–6 tasks) are lowcost, lowrisk practices that can be transferred immediately.

**What should not be copied**  
Do not attempt to parallelise Document Check for cases that require originals – the statutory constraint cannot be changed, and the intervention would add complexity without benefit. Do not assume that the full 9hour median reduction observed at Site A is achievable; the confounded evidence means the true effect of parallelisation alone is much smaller. Do not copy the crosstraining of reviewers; Site B’s role separation is compatible with parallelisation as long as the system supports multiple active tasks.

**Which subgroup should enter first**  
The pilot should be restricted to **digitalonly claims (no originals required)**, which constitute approximately 38% of volume. This subgroup mirrors the conditions under which Site A’s parallelisation worked and avoids the confounding effect of originals receipt delay.

**Expected benefits and risks**  
- *Benefit*: a 2–4 hour reduction in median cycle time for eligible cases, translating to an overall median reduction of 1–2 hours.  
- *Risks*: assessment bottleneck, legalreview queue congestion, and a possible increase in the p90 tail. If the IT caselock is not lifted, the intervention will produce no true overlap and the pilot will fail.

**Evidence that would trigger expansion, modification, or abandonment**  
- *Expand*: if the pilot shows a statistically significant reduction in median cycle time for eligible cases without a deterioration in p90, rework, or appeal rates, and the overlap metric exceeds 50%, the intervention can be rolled out to all digitalonly claims.  
- *Modify*: if median cycle time improves but p90 worsens, add assessment or legal review capacity before expanding.  
- *Abandon*: if the IT lock cannot be removed, or if after 12 weeks there is no improvement in cycle time, or if rework/appeal rates increase meaningfully, the intervention should be stopped.

## 10. Assumption register
