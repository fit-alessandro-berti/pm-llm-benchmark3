## 1. Conformance semantics

Each observation gets two judgments: a normative one (which norm entry applies, with what status and scope) and an evidential one (is it credible execution behaviour, or an artefact of extraction or abstraction?). It is a violation only when both point the same way.

- **Forbidden behaviour**: an observed relation matching a forbidden entry (N3, T6, E10) that is credible as real execution. Closed-world: one occurrence suffices, with no tolerance.
- **Missing mandatory behaviour**: an in-scope instance (N1: newly created standard Orders; N4: externally shipped Packages; T1/T2: per interaction; E1–E8: per event) lacking the required relation at the abstraction the rule actually means. Closed-world only inside the scope, so denominator and scope membership must be established first.
- **Unexpected but unregulated**: relations marked unspecified (N6, T4, T5, E9) or not mentioned (codeath, MCR–Customer, Create→MCR). Open-world: at most a candidate for norm review or referral to another policy owner.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: instances not following a typical relation (N2, N5, T3). Not a violation unless the exceptions are not of the legitimate kinds. With no tolerance bands, an implausible share is a judgment call.
- **Likely data-quality error**: better explained by extraction faults (reused-ID joins, unmaterialised objects, timestamp ties, misattributed event-to-object links) than by execution. The normative label stays ("forbidden relation present in the log"), but the remediation owner is the data pipeline.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the rule's meaning (eventually-follows, per-event obligation) is checked with a representation that cannot express it (directly-follows, type-level edges, aggregate weights).
- **Insufficient evidence**: the summary cannot separate the hypotheses (missing denominators, censoring, overlapping counts).

**Open vs. closed world.** PM4Py's graph comparison is symmetric and closed-world: every unmodelled observed edge is "additional" and every unobserved model edge is "missing". This norm is closed-world only for forbidden entries (any presence counts) and in-scope mandatory entries (any absence counts). Typical, unspecified and unmentioned relations are open-world: presence and absence are both permitted. An "additional edge" therefore means nothing normatively unless it matches a forbidden entry, and a "missing edge" matters only if mandatory, in scope, and evaluated at the right abstraction.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**Establishes**: per object type, which activity pairs occur adjacently in lifecycles, and for how many objects. For forbidden "after" rules that is sufficient: Cancel→Dispatch directly-follows in 2 Order lifecycles proves precedence (N3), if timestamps and links are right.

**Cannot establish**: eventual precedence, gaps, deadlines, or which instances. The asymmetry cuts both ways. For mandatory N1, an observed edge proves satisfaction but its absence proves nothing, so 98/100 is a lower bound on conformance. For forbidden N3, Cancel → X → Dispatch never appears as an edge, so 2 is a lower bound on violations.

**Eventual-as-directly danger**: reading "should eventually enter validation" as "Validate directly follows Create" makes every legitimate intermediate (the unrecorded review, MCR in 3 Orders) a false violation. False positives then scale with process richness, not noncompliance. N4 has the same risk if a labelling step sits between Pack and Dispatch.

**Counts and scopes**: N1 covers only newly created standard Orders; non-standard Orders among the 2 may be out of scope. Counts do not partition: 98 + 3 > 100 and 72 + 83 > 98 imply repeats, ties or overlap, so subtraction is invalid.

**Cross-abstraction**: Validate→Close (83) conforms to N2, yet joined with Invoices it might reveal Orders closed without invoicing. The missing Create→Validate is suspicious as directly-follows but may conform as eventually-follows. Dispatch appears in the Order graph only if Dispatch events link directly to the Order, not just via the Package.

### OTG

**Establishes**: type-level existence and weight of interaction, cobirth and codeath relations. Presence-type forbidden rules are checkable directly (T6, weight 1).

**Cannot establish**: per-event obligations. T1/T2 are scoped per interaction, but the Package–Delivery weight of 200 can come from other events, such as the confirmation event producing codeath, while ET–OT shows 2 Dispatch events without Delivery. So T2 conforms at type level and fails at instance level.

**Counts**: cobirth and codeath derive from lifecycle boundaries. A missing earlier Payment event or a bulk-loaded creation can manufacture T6's single edge, so it needs event-level confirmation; codeath (7) is equally derived and benign. Weight semantics (pairs or events) are unstated: T1's 1450 may be distinct Order–Item pairs.

### ET–OT

**Establishes**: which event types touch which object types, and how often. Suited to "every event of type X involves type Y" (E1–E8) and forbidden presence (E10).

**Cannot establish**: instances, ordering, or attributes. The E8 unapplied-cash label and E6 carrier reference are invisible. There are no event-type totals, so "every event" is inferred from maximum weights. The table is partial (Validate, Close, Issue Invoice and Cancel are absent), so a missing row such as MCR–Order is not evidence of absence, which weakens the apparent Create→MCR/Order–RiskCase inconsistency.

**Cross-abstraction**: E7 (93) is satisfied while E8 (92) exposes a gap. Add Item–Item and OTG T1 (both 1450) look complete while Add Item–Order (1448) suggests 2 events without Order, unless weights count pairs.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**
- Satisfied per counts: E1, E3, E4, E5, E7, T1, T2 at type level.
- N1 (2/100): a violation only if Validate never follows and the Orders are new and standard; otherwise an abstraction mismatch.
- N4 (2/200): a violation if Pack is absent or after Dispatch; conforming if an intermediate step exists.
- E6/T2 (2): data quality if the carrier references resolve to real deliveries; otherwise a violation.
- E8 (1): a violation as recorded; exempt only if explicitly typed unapplied cash, since finance's plausibility claim does not meet the labelling condition.
- E2 (2): a violation if the per-event check confirms; nothing if weights count pairs.

**Forbidden**
- N3 (2): stands unless timestamps or links prove wrong; the current text gives no Reopen carve-out.
- T6 (1): becomes data quality if the link or birth derivation is wrong.
- E10 (1): a forbidden relation in data; process behaviour only if genuine, which is implausible.

**Typical**
- N2: 83 direct closes; the rest pass through legitimate intermediates (Issue Invoice 72).
- N5: 92 paid, 4 written off (a named exception), 14 open; a finding only if past due without credit terms.
- T3: 92 links; unapplied cash is legitimate.

**Unregulated**
- N6 Reopen→Close (1), T4 (100), T5 (3), E9 (3), MCR–Customer (3), Create→MCR (3), Validate→Issue Invoice (72), codeath (7).
- These become relevant only when they interact with a regulated relation: Reopen between Cancel and Dispatch (N3), MCR between Create and Validate (N1), or a breach of the separate customer policy.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import copy
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# A. NORMATIVE METADATA — maintained OUTSIDE the bare PM4Py structures.
#    norm_ocdfg (dict), norm_otg (tuple) and norm_etot (tuple) only say
#    "node/edge exists (with a weight)". Status, scope, temporal semantics
#    (directly vs eventually) and exemptions cannot be expressed there.
#    norm_* must contain only the EXPECTED skeleton (mandatory + typical):
#    a forbidden edge placed inside them would be read by PM4Py as expected.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
NORM_META = {
    "ocdfg": {  # keyed (object_type, source, target)
        ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): dict(id="N1", status="mandatory", temporal="eventually", scope="new standard Order"),
        ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"): dict(id="N2", status="typical", temporal="eventually"),
        ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): dict(id="N3", status="forbidden", temporal="eventually"),
        ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): dict(id="N4", status="mandatory", temporal="precedes", scope="external shipment"),
        ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): dict(id="N5", status="typical", exceptions={"Write Off", "Issue Credit Note"}),
        ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"): dict(id="N6", status="unspecified"),
    },
    "otg": {
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"): dict(id="T1", status="mandatory", per_event="Add Item"),
        ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"): dict(id="T2", status="mandatory", per_event="Dispatch Package"),
        ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"): dict(id="T3", status="typical"),
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer"): dict(id="T4", status="unspecified"),
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): dict(id="T5", status="unspecified"),
        ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"): dict(id="T6", status="forbidden"),
    },
    "etot": {
        ("Create Order", "Order"): dict(id="E1", status="mandatory"),
        ("Add Item", "Order"): dict(id="E2", status="mandatory"),
        ("Add Item", "Item"): dict(id="E3", status="mandatory"),
        ("Pack Package", "Package"): dict(id="E4", status="mandatory"),
        ("Dispatch Package", "Package"): dict(id="E5", status="mandatory"),
        ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): dict(id="E6", status="mandatory"),
        ("Record Payment", "Payment"): dict(id="E7", status="mandatory"),
        ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): dict(id="E8", status="mandatory", exempt_attr=("payment_kind", "unapplied_cash")),
        ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): dict(id="E9", status="unspecified"),
        ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): dict(id="E10", status="forbidden"),
    },
}
# Data-quality knowledge is also external to the graphs.
DQ_SUSPECTS = {("etot", ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice")): "join on reused numeric identifier",
               ("etot", ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery")): "carrier reference only in event attribute"}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# B. DISCOVERY + RAW PM4PY DIAGNOSTICS (never modified afterwards)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
observed = {"ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
            "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
            "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)}
raw = {"ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg),
       "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg),
       "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)}
RAW_DIAGNOSTICS = copy.deepcopy(raw)  # audit copy; the semantic layer only reads it
# Caveats: the OC-DFG comparison pools (a, b) flows across object types and
# its measure deltas compare absolute counts, meaningless for a norm without
# frequencies; ET-OT 'delta_rel' has the same problem.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# C. TYPE-LEVEL OBSERVED RELATIONS (object type kept explicitly)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
obs_flows = {(ot, a, b): len(pairs)
             for ot, flows in observed["ocdfg"]["edges"]["event_couples"].items()
             for (a, b), pairs in flows.items()}
obs_otg = observed["otg"][1]    # {(t1, rel, t2): weight}
obs_etot = observed["etot"][3]  # {(activity, object_type): weight}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# D. INSTANCE-LEVEL CHECKS — mandatory scopes are per event / per object,
#    which aggregate graphs cannot express.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
EID, ACT, TS = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.event_activity, ocel.event_timestamp
OID, OT = ocel.object_id_column, ocel.object_type_column
rel = ocel.relations

def events_lacking(activity, required_type):
    ev = rel[rel[ACT] == activity]
    return sorted(set(ev[EID]) - set(ev.loc[ev[OT] == required_type, EID]))

def eventually_pairs(ot, a, b):
    """Objects of type ot with some b strictly after some a (any gap)."""
    r = rel[rel[OT] == ot]
    first_a = r[r[ACT] == a].groupby(OID)[TS].min()
    last_b = r[r[ACT] == b].groupby(OID)[TS].max()
    j = pd.concat([first_a.rename("a"), last_b.rename("b")], axis=1)
    return set(j.index[(j["b"] > j["a"])]), set(j.index[j["a"].notna() & ~(j["b"] > j["a"])])

instance = {
    "N1_unvalidated": eventually_pairs("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order")[1],  # still filter to 'standard' scope
    "N3_dispatch_after_cancel": eventually_pairs("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package")[0],  # superset of DFG hits
    "N4_no_prior_pack": eventually_pairs("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package")[1],
    "E2_add_item_no_order": events_lacking("Add Item", "Order"),
    "E6_dispatch_no_delivery": events_lacking("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"),
    "E8_payment_no_invoice": events_lacking("Record Payment", "Invoice"),  # then apply exempt_attr
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# E. SEMANTIC CLASSIFICATION LAYER (separate records, linked to raw output)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def classify(family, key, present, violating_instances=None):
    meta = NORM_META[family].get(key)
    dq = DQ_SUSPECTS.get((family, key))
    if meta is None or meta["status"] == "unspecified":
        cls = "unregulated (open world)"
    elif meta["status"] == "forbidden":
        cls = "forbidden present" if present else "ok"
    elif meta["status"] == "mandatory":
        cls = "missing mandatory (in scope)" if violating_instances else "ok"
        if meta.get("temporal") == "eventually" and not violating_instances and not present:
            cls = "abstraction mismatch (DF absent, eventually satisfied)"
    else:  # typical
        cls = "typical-pattern absence (not a violation)" if not present else "ok"
    if dq and cls not in ("ok", "unregulated (open world)"):
        cls += " | likely data-quality error: " + dq
    return dict(family=family, key=key, norm=(meta or {}).get("id"), semantic_class=cls,
                instances=violating_instances, raw_ref=family)  # pointer into RAW_DIAGNOSTICS

semantic = []
for key in set(obs_flows) | set(NORM_META["ocdfg"]):
    inst = {("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): instance["N1_unvalidated"],
            ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): instance["N3_dispatch_after_cancel"],
            ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): instance["N4_no_prior_pack"]}.get(key)
    semantic.append(classify("ocdfg", key, key in obs_flows, inst))
for key in set(obs_otg) | set(NORM_META["otg"]):
    semantic.append(classify("otg", key, obs_otg.get(key, 0) > 0))
for key in set(obs_etot) | set(NORM_META["etot"]):
    inst = {("Add Item", "Order"): instance["E2_add_item_no_order"],
            ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): instance["E6_dispatch_no_delivery"],
            ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): instance["E8_payment_no_invoice"]}.get(key)
    semantic.append(classify("etot", key, obs_etot.get(key, 0) > 0, inst))

report = {"raw_pm4py": RAW_DIAGNOSTICS,            # unchanged tool output
          "semantic": pd.DataFrame(semantic),       # interpretation layer
          "note": "Semantic classes are triage labels, not legal or business verdicts."}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1: eventually vs. directly-follows; no deadline; "standard" undefined.** Risk: false violations from reviews in between, or unvalidated Orders missed. Clarify: "Every standard Order is validated within X days and before fulfilment", with review events named as permitted intermediates. Status: mandatory; intermediates explicitly unspecified.
2. **N3 vs. Reopen (N6) and partial cancellation.** Risk: disputes over whether reopened Orders may ship. Clarify: "Dispatch after Cancel without an intervening authorised Reopen." Status: forbidden, with a specified carve-out.
3. **N4: precedence vs. adjacency; "external shipment" not observable.** Risk: scope cannot be computed; intermediates cause false positives. Clarify: Pack precedes Dispatch, and a shipment-type attribute is recorded. Status: mandatory (rule and attribute).
4. **T2/E6: does a carrier attribute satisfy Delivery?** Risk: endless data-vs-process disputes. Clarify: a Delivery object is required; an attribute-only reference is a data defect with a repair deadline. Status: mandatory.
5. **E8 labelling: who labels unapplied cash, and when.** Risk: unlabelled unapplied cash looks like misapplication. Clarify: payment type is recorded at booking; unlinked, unlabelled payments are reviewed within N days. Status: the label is mandatory; unapplied cash stays typical (T3).
6. **E10 scope is too narrow.** Risk: the same ID collision on Payments passes silently. Clarify: any telemetry event type linked to any financial object type. Status: forbidden.
7. **Lifecycle relations: only T6 is covered.** Risk: benign codeath (7) keeps being flagged. Clarify: declare Package–Delivery codeath permitted. Status: explicitly unspecified.
8. **No tolerances or censoring rules.** Risk: arbitrary escalation, or serious rare cases dismissed. Clarify: zero tolerance for forbidden and in-scope mandatory rules; review bands for typical rules, computed on cases complete at the snapshot. Status: bands typical, zero tolerance mandatory.
9. **T4/MCR–Customer defer to an unnamed policy.** Risk: nobody checks them. Clarify: cite the policy. Status: explicitly unspecified, with a pointer.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**
- N3 (rank 1): trace both Orders and Packages, recall or contact customers if goods are in transit, and block Dispatch for cancelled Orders.
- T6 (rank 2): quarantine the Order and Payment from reporting and identify the creating event and source system.
- E10: exclude the link from analytic views, keeping raw data.

**Data repair**: type-qualified join keys plus a collision scan (E10); Delivery objects for the 2 carrier references (E6/T2); resolve ties and duplicates behind the overlapping counts; verify the birth derivation before trusting T6.

**Process investigation**: N3 root cause (integration latency or manual override); whether a prepayment path creates Payments at order creation (T6); reconcile the E8 payment; eventually-follows checks for the 2 N1 Orders and 2 N4 Packages, with only unresolved cases becoming violations; aging on the 14 invoices.

**Norm revision**: adopt the section 6 clarifications, starting with N1/N4 semantics, N3/Reopen, E8 labelling and E10 generalisation.

**Monitoring**: instance-level eventually-follows checks (N1, N3, N4) and per-event checks (E2, E6, E8) alongside PM4Py graph conformance; alert on any forbidden relation. Keep Reopen, MCR, codeath, RiskCase and Customer relations visible without escalation; rarity (1, 3, 7) is not a finding.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust**: N3 occurs in 2 Order lifecycles and, because directly-follows implies precedence, is forbidden behaviour unless timestamps or links are wrong. Validate→Issue Invoice, MCR–Customer, codeath and Reopen→Close are benign because the norm is open-world for them, and the write-offs are a named N5 exception.

**Dependent on norm incompleteness**: whether the N1 and N4 gaps are violations (eventually semantics), E8's exemption (labelling), N3 under reopening, and the meaning of "standard" and "external".

**Dependent on suspected data quality**: E10 (reused ID), E6/T2 (carrier attribute), T6 (birth derivation or misattributed links), and the E2 gap and count overlaps (weight semantics, ties).

**Undecidable from graph summaries**: which instances are involved; eventual ordering; whether the 2 N1 Orders are among the 3 MCR Orders; whether the N4 and E6 Packages overlap; payment attributes; invoice due dates; and event totals per type.
