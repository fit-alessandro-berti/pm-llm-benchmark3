* **Secondary & tertiary bottlenecks:** **Pre-analytical check** (96% utilization, 9.5h queue) and **Accession correction** (91% utilization, 14.0h queue across only 2 specialists) degrade quality upstream: `Pre-analytical check -> Request recollection` nearly doubles in rate (4.2%  8.0%), and 26.2% of cases waiting in Manual accession correction end up sent to Request recollection anyway (110 of 420 cases, up from 14.0% in normal periods), wasting specialist capacity before triggering a 60h recollection loop. Relieving Accession correction by pulling Technical validators (I4) pushes Technical validation well past 101%, creating runaway WIP upstream of Medical validation. * **Migration trap:** Applying strict Urgent-first dispatch (I1) or routing all Routine to M2 (I3) during M2 degradation worsens total repeat volume and starves Complex-panel orders (which need multiple successful assays). Once M1 returns online, releasing the accumulated Assay backlog in a single wave immediately overwhelms **Technical validation** (already fragile at 76–101%) and **Medical validation**, causing a post-recovery SLA crash 12–24 hours after the machine is fixed.

#### S3: Specialist-Review & Medical Validator Absence

Flushing the evening wave rapidly through Prep into Assay without night-shift QC controls converts a logistics delay into an S2-style repeat-assay bottleneck the following day.

### 3. Robust redesign package

Rather than adding headcount everywhere or chasing historical average cycle time, the baseline redesign targets **rework elimination, arrival variance reduction, bounded priority protection, and targeted cross-training**—interventions that remain valuable across S1–S4.

1. **Automated Digital Registration + Hard 9-Hour Clerk Shift Cap (Modified I8)**
   * *Design:* Deploy I8 for all orders with complete electronic metadata and prohibit clerk shifts exceeding 9 hours (redeploying saved clerk hours to physician-callback resolution and pre-analytical support).
   * *Why robust:* Directly suppresses error generation in S1 and S4, shrinking the flow into the 2-person Accession correction bottleneck (`Pre-analytical -> Manual accession correction`: 4.3% in peak) and curbing recollection cancellations.
   * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes referring clinics can supply structured digital metadata; fails if digital orders contain silent clinical coding errors that bypass human scrutiny.
   * *Downstream overload risk:* Faster registration pushes a steeper wave into **Pre-analytical check** (96% peak utilization) and **Assay machines** (98%). Must be paired with I6 and I7 below.

2. **Early 2-Hour Pre-Analytical Defect Triage + Automated Physician Notification (Modified I6)**
   * *Design:* Screen incoming tubes within 2 hours for labeling, volume, and hemolysis defects; route clean rejections through templated electronic physician notices rather than manual clerk phone calls.
   * *Why robust:* Stops defective samples from wasting 9.5h in Pre-analytical queues, 14.0h in Accession correction (where 26.2% in peak still end in recollection), and scarce Prep/Assay capacity—effective across S1, S2, and S4. Starts the 42–60h recollection clock immediately.
   * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes clear, standardized rejection criteria; fails if rushed triage creates false rejections, inflating recollection loops and order cancellations (already 41.6% of recollections in peak).
   * *Downstream overload risk:* Can temporarily overload **Pre-analytical check** intake personnel during courier spikes (S4) and increase follow-up calls to **Registration clerks** unless automated physician notifications work reliably.

3. **Smoothed Courier Pickup Slots + Night-Shift M2 Preventive QC Protocol (I7 + Operational Guardrail)**
   * *Design:* Stagger courier pickups to break the 10:00 and 17:00 arrival spikes (I7), paired with mandatory calibration/QC checks before night-shift M2 runs.
   * *Why robust:* Lowers instantaneous queue surges across Courier intake, Pre-analytical check, and Prep benches in S1 and S4 without capital investment, while preventing S4 bursts from degenerating into S2 night-shift repeat cascades.
   * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes clinics/hospitals accept staggered pickup windows; fails during severe regional weather (S4) when road delays override schedules.
   * *Downstream overload risk:* Moving daytime volume into later afternoon/evening slots shifts more workload onto evening/night **Assay runs (M2)** and **Technical validation**—hence the mandatory night-shift QC guardrail and evening handoff alignment.

4. **SLA-Slack Priority Dispatch with Starvation Guardrails (Modified I1, Rejecting Unbounded I1 & Permanent I2)**
   * *Design:* Replace strict Urgent-first preemption (I1) with a two-lane, age-weighted dispatch rule at Prep and Assay: maintain a reserved fast-lane for Urgent orders (restoring >75% batch-wait bypass without fragmenting entire machine schedules) while automatically promoting Routine orders after 36h of age and Complex-panel orders after 48h. Keep Routine batch sizes at 12 by default (rejecting permanent I2) with a 4-hour maximum batch-flush timer.
   * *Why robust:* Protects the 18h Urgent penalty SLA across S1–S4 while preventing the WIP explosion and Routine/Complex-panel starvation caused by strict I1 or oversized batches (I2). Note: Routine and Complex-panel cannot be prioritized simultaneously with Urgent; this rule explicitly sacrifices modest Routine median speed (still well inside 72h) to protect Urgent p90 and prevent Complex-panel tail runaway.
   * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes LIS/bench software can display order age/SLA slack; fails under extreme capacity loss (S2 M1 outage) where reserved Urgent capacity cannot clear all Urgent orders without external relief.
   * *Downstream overload risk:* Pushing Urgent orders faster through Prep and Assay concentrates Urgent pressure directly on **Technical validation** (101% peak) and **Medical validation** (93% peak, 22h queue).

5. **Pre-Season Cross-Training *Into* Technical Validation (Inverting I4)**
   * *Design:* Reject permanent I4 (pulling Technical validators into Accession correction destroys the single station already at 101% peak utilization). Instead, use the 4-week cross-training window ahead of winter to cross-train 2–3 Prep bench / Pre-analytical staff as backup Technical validators (and use I8+I6 to shrink Accession correction demand).
   * *Why robust:* Directly addresses the 101% peak instability in Technical validation across S1 and post-recovery surges in S2 without permanent staffing increases.
   * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes regulatory certification permits 4-week cross-trained staff to validate standard Routine cases (`Technical validation -> Release result`); fails if simultaneous intake and validation spikes occur (S1+S4), leaving Prep benches short-handed.
   * *Downstream overload risk:* Accelerating Technical validation throughput shifts the bottleneck directly into **Medical validation** (93%) and **Specialist review** (87%) during Complex-panel/Urgent waves (S1, S3), requiring the scenario-specific valves below.

### 4. Scenario-specific policies

These interventions are too costly, risky, or counterproductive to run permanently and activate only via explicit hysteresis triggers (separate **ON** and **OFF** thresholds to prevent thrashing):

* **Policy P1 — Temporary Evening Medical-Validation Shift (I5)**
  * *Target scenarios:* **S1 (Mix Shift)** and **S3 (Validator Absence)**.
  * *Trigger ON:* Medical validation median queue >6h for 2 consecutive shifts, OR Urgent p90 cycle time >15h, OR confirmed validator absence reducing Medical validation capacity by 20%.
  * *Trigger OFF:* Medical validation queue <3h for 3 consecutive days AND Urgent/Complex-panel intake share returns below 38% combined.
  * *Downstream risk:* Minimal downstream friction at `Medical validation -> Release result` (2–5h), though rapid evening releases of anomalous results can generate next-morning physician inquiries at **Registration/Intake**.

* **Policy P2 — Conditional Complex-Panel Overflow Outsourcing (I10)**
  * *Target scenarios:* **S1 (Mix Shift)**, **S2 (M1 Outage)**, and **S3 (Specialist Absence)**.
  * *Trigger ON:* Specialist review queue >16h, OR scheduled Friday specialist absence coincides with >18% Complex-panel mix, OR M1 unplanned outage projected >12h. Route non-Urgent Complex-panel orders (which tolerate a 12h transport delay far better than 36h Specialist queue + 48h review edge + 22h Med Val queue = 106h internal wait) to the partner lab.
  * *Trigger OFF:* Specialist review queue <8h for 48h AND M1 operational with Assay queue <6h.
  * *Downstream risk:* Adds outbound/inbound load to **Courier intake** and creates result-reconciliation/traceability load on **Accession correction** and **Medical validation** if partner IT interfaces are not fully automated.

* **Policy P3 — Dynamic Machine Routing & Repeat Quarantine (Conditional Modified I3)**
  * *Target scenarios:* **S1 (Healthy-Fleet Mix Shift)** vs **S2 (M1 Outage / M2 Degradation)**.
  * *Trigger ON (S1 Split Mode):* Both M1 and M2 healthy (`Assay run -> Repeat assay` <8% on M2) AND Assay utilization >85% with Urgent/Complex share >40%. Route Urgent, Complex-panel, and any Repeat-after-failure cases to M1; route stable Routine assays to M2.
  * *Trigger OFF / S2 Quarantine Mode:* Immediately suspend I3 split if M2 24h rolling repeat rate exceeds **10%** OR M1 goes offline. Under S2, halt low-margin Routine runs on degraded M2 for rapid maintenance/calibration, activate P2 (outsource Complex-panel), and reserve healthy capacity strictly for Urgent and aging (>48h) Routine orders.
  * *Downstream risk:* Under S1 Split Mode, overloading M2 with Routine increases repeat risk, which can rebound onto **Assay** and **Technical validation**; under S2 Quarantine Mode, post-repair batch flushing can overwhelm **Technical validation** unless throttled.

* **Policy P4 — Emergency Prep Batching (Bounded I2) & Reverse-Flex Accession Support (Bounded I4)**
  * *Target scenarios:* **S4 (Courier Compression)** for Prep; isolated **Accession spikes** only when Technical validation has slack.
  * *Trigger ON (Bounded I2):* Preparation bench queue >6h AND Courier arrival burst >1.5× normal wave; temporarily expand Routine batch size from 12 to 18–20 (not 24) while keeping the Urgent bypass lane open and enforcing a 4h max-wait flush. *Trigger OFF:* Prep queue <3h for 2 shifts.
  * *Trigger ON (Bounded I4):* Accession correction queue >8h **AND** Technical validation utilization <80% (queue <3h); allow 1 cross-trained Technical validator to clear Accession backlog for half-shifts only. *Trigger OFF:* Technical validation queue >4h OR Accession queue <4h.
  * *Downstream risk:* Bounded I2 pushes lumpy Routine waves into **Assay machines** (increasing `Batch wait -> Assay run`), while Bounded I4 risks sudden **Technical validation** backlogs if triggered when an Assay wave is about to finish.
  * *Explicitly rejected policy (I9 — Hold Routine releases for batch reports):* Rejected during peaks because Routine p90 already reaches 120h (vs 72h target) and holding Routine does not relieve Technical validation's core cognitive bottleneck—only automated release formatting (zero added wait) is acceptable.

### 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even after deploying the robust base and scenario-specific policies, several structural fragilities remain—including risks introduced by the redesign itself:

1. **Multi-Tube Order Synchronization Blind Spot (Data/Process Limitation):** The process log is flattened to the lab-order level, hiding individual sample tubes. A Complex-panel order with 3 tubes can have 2 tubes pass while 1 tube enters a 60h recollection loop or M2 repeat, stalling the entire order in invisible partial-WIP limbo between Assay and Medical validation.
2. **Compound Regional Shock (Simultaneous S1 + S2 + Regional Partner Saturation):** During a severe regional winter wave, the partner lab used for P2 (I10) faces the same infection surge, degrading its turnaround time beyond the assumed 12h transport overhead just as local M1/M2 capacity nears 98%.
3. **Biweekly Friday Specialist Structural Gap:** Because one specialist is unavailable every second Friday, any Thursday evening Complex-panel spike creates a recurring 60–72h weekend hold cliff that P1 (Medical validation shift) cannot resolve alone without specialist cross-coverage or pre-Friday P2 diversion.
4. **Redesign-Induced Risk — Automation Pass-Through & Pre-Analytical Surge (from I8):** Removing human registration checks for "digitally complete" orders accelerates arrivals into Pre-analytical check (96% peak utilization) and allows subtle clinical-context mismatches (e.g., wrong test code for tube type) to slip downstream, surfacing only at Technical or Medical validation where rework is far more expensive.
5. **Redesign-Induced Risk — Triage Over-Rejection & Cancellation Inflation (from I6):** Under peak stress, aggressive 2-hour defect triage can push borderline-usable samples into `Request recollection`. Because 41.6% of peak recollection loops end in `Cancel order`, an overly strict triage rule can silently degrade clinical care quality while making internal cycle-time metrics look artificially clean.
6. **Redesign-Induced Risk — Night-Shift Repeat Amplification & Cross-Training Starvation (from I7 and Inverted I4):** Smoothing courier pickups (I7) shifts more assay workload into evening/night shifts where M2 failure rates cluster; if night-shift QC lapses, I7 amplifies S2. Likewise, pulling cross-trained Prep staff into Technical validation during a combined intake + validation wave can unexpectedly starve Preparation benches.

### 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Because cycle times range from 11h to 145h (and recollection/specialist loops take 42h–60h), **lagging outcome metrics** (Urgent >18h breach %, Routine >72h breach %, Complex-panel p90, overall rework %, cancellation %) only confirm a crisis *after* queues have already diverged. Daily peak operations should be steered by a compact set of **leading indicators** measured per shift, kept strictly separate from lagging outcomes:

#### Lagging Outcome Metrics (Tracked Daily/Weekly for Calibration — Not Real-Time Control)
* **Contractual & SLA Compliance:** Urgent % >18h (target <5%; normal p90 is already 19h), Routine % >72h (target <10%), Complex-panel p90 cycle time.
* **End-to-End Quality & Attrition:** Total order rework % (normal 7.5% vs peak 11.8%), % of recollection orders ending in `Cancel order` (watch for I6 over-rejection if >40%), and Repeat-after-failure p90 cycle time.
* **System Flow Health:** % of cases experiencing at least one queue >12h (normal 22% vs peak 54%) and net unfinished WIP delta (inflow minus releases/cancellations, flagging unstable >100% utilization).
