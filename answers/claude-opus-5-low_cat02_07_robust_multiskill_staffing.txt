# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 What is observation vs. what I am adding

**Observations (given):** control flow `Intake -> diagnostic -> Validation`; five case types; service-time ranges with a complex-tail branch and its probability; a 6-resource skill/speed/shift matrix; six two-hour demand blocks with priority and type mixes; handover/cross-team/setup/coordination penalties; 30-minute overtime cap; 90% utilization fairness ceiling; three SLA targets; five uncertainty scenarios.

**Everything else below is assumption, estimate, hypothesis or recommendation, labelled as such.**

## 1.2 Demand assumptions

- **A1 (arrival process).** "Expected arrivals" per two-hour block is read as the mean of a Poisson count, with arrival instants uniform inside the block. *Needed because* the prompt gives only an expectation; a deterministic reading would understate congestion and materially inflate SLA attainment (a deterministic day hides the queue-building tail). In the code the count is deterministic per block (`round(n)`) and randomness enters through mix, service times and tail draws — this is a **deliberate variance-reduction choice**, and it means my SLA figures are *optimistic* relative to a Poisson day. Flagged as a known bias, not hidden.
- **A2 (mix independence).** Priority and case type are drawn independently from the block percentages. *Needed because* no joint distribution is given. Hypothesis worth testing in real data: P1 skews toward Mechanical/safety work, which would change the reservation design in §3.
- **A3 (no abandonment, no rework).** Cases wait until served; Validation never fails back to diagnostics. *Needed because* neither is specified. This is optimistic; a rework loop of even 10% at Validation would push the system further past capacity.
- **A4 (day boundary).** Work not started before a resource's last available minute (shift end + 30 min overtime) spills to the next day and is reported as `unserved_pct` — an explicit carry-over metric rather than an artificially inflated flow time.

## 1.3 Service-time assumptions

- **A5 (ranges -> distributions).** Each range is treated as **Uniform(lo,hi)**, selected by a Bernoulli(complex-tail probability) mixture. *Needed because* the prompt gives support but no shape. Uniform is the maximum-entropy choice on a bounded interval. Consequence: the mixture's variance is dominated by the tail branch, not by within-branch spread.
- **Resulting unconditional means (base multipliers = 1.00):**

| activity | E[ordinary] | E[tail] | p_tail | **E[service]** | E[service] under U4 (2p) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intake | 6.5 | 12.0 | 0.08 | **6.94** | 7.38 |
| Mechanical | 23.0 | 45.0 | 0.15 | **26.30** | 29.60 |
| Electrical | 28.5 | 52.5 | 0.18 | **32.82** | 37.14 |
| Software | 32.5 | 62.5 | 0.20 | **38.50** | 44.50 |
| Validation | 14.0 | 28.5 | 0.12 | **15.74** | 17.48 |

- **A6 (multipliers are multiplicative on the drawn time),** so a 1.25 resource on a 40-min draw takes 50 min. Stated in the prompt; I apply it to the *drawn* value, not the mean.
- **A7 (breaks pause work, they do not restart it).** A task straddling a break is paused and resumed with no penalty; only *preemption by a P1 case* triggers the 10% restart penalty. *Needed because* the prompt attaches the restart penalty to preemption specifically. This is why the simulator records work as segments (a natural pause/resume event pair for §7).

## 1.4 How ME/ES parallelism is decided (a decision variable, not a constant)

Let the two branches have expected resource-adjusted durations `d1 <= d2`, and let `H` be the handover penalty a second resource would incur.

- **Parallel path length** ≈ `max(d1,d2) + H_second_branch + 8` (coordination), and it burns **two** resources.
- **Sequential-on-one-cross-skilled-resource path length** ≈ `d1 + d2 + 0` (no handover, no 5-min cross-team, no 8-min coordination), and it burns **one** resource.

So parallel wins on *this case's* flow time only when `min(d1,d2) > H + 8 ≈ 15` minutes **and** a second resource is genuinely idle. Since every diagnostic branch has a mean of 26–39 minutes, parallel almost always wins the *local* race — but it wins by consuming ~`d1` extra resource-minutes of a system that (see §2) is already over-subscribed. **This is the central tension of the task: parallelism is a flow-time purchase paid for in capacity, and in an overloaded day it is a bad trade for anything but P1.**

Decision rule I adopt in the hybrid policy: **parallelize only when (a) the case is P1, or (b) the shorter branch exceeds 20 minutes *and* the qualifying pool has a free resource with no other queued work at that skill.** Otherwise run sequentially on a cross-skilled resource (Ada or Farah for ME; Chen, Emil or Farah for ES).

Note a structural fact that constrains this: the only ME cross-skilled resources are **Ada (M 1.00 / E 1.35)** and **Farah (1.40/1.40, and only until 12:00)**; the only ES cross-skilled resources are **Chen (E 1.00 / S 1.25)**, **Emil (E 1.30 / S 1.00)** and **Farah**. Deepa and Ben cannot do Software at all; Ben cannot do Electrical.

## 1.5 Objective formulations

I evaluate three explicit objectives rather than one, because the recommendation flips depending on which is chosen.

**O1 — Mean-delay objective (the naive one, included as a foil):**
`min E[flow − service]` averaged over all completed cases in the base scenario.

**O2 — Tail/SLA-risk objective (primary):**
`min  w1·(90 − P1_SLA)+ + w2·(90 − P2_SLA)+ + w3·(90 − P3_SLA)+ + w4·CVaR_10%(flow)`
with `w1 >> w2 > w3` (I use 10 / 3 / 1) and `CVaR_10%` = the mean flow time of the worst decile. This objective is *scenario-weighted*: I score it as the **worst value across {base, U1..U5}**, not the mean, so a policy cannot buy its score with base-case performance.

**O3 — Fairness/robustness objective:**
`min  max_scenario [ mean_delay ] + λ·(max_util − 0.90)+ + μ·(1 − Jain(util)) + ν·handover_minutes_per_case`
where Jain's index over resource utilizations measures load-sharing equity, the `(max_util − 0.90)+` term prices the prompt's explicit fairness ceiling, and the handover term prices the coordination burden the operators actually feel.

**Definition of "robust" used throughout:** a policy is robust if (i) its *worst-case* score across base and U1–U5 is good, (ii) its *degradation slope* from base to U5 is shallow, and (iii) its failure mode under shock is *graceful and chosen* (P3 delayed) rather than *arbitrary* (whichever queue happens to be blocked).

## 1.6 Decision variables

1. Eligibility sets `E(activity, priority, time)` — who may take what.
2. Priority score / dispatch rule for queue selection.
3. Reservation: which resource-minutes are held for P1.
4. Preemption permission (which priorities may be preempted, and a "protect the near-done" rule).
5. Parallel-vs-sequential trigger for ME/ES.
6. Handover minimization weight (a bonus for case continuity).
7. Overflow triggers (queue-length or predicted-SLA-breach thresholds that open a wider pool).

## 2.3 The headline finding: the base scenario is structurally over-subscribed

Offered load is **2718 minutes against 2490 usable minutes = 109%** *before* any speed multiplier above 1.00, *before* handovers, and *before* parallel-branch coordination. Adding a realistic effective multiplier of ~1.05–1.10 (routing is not always to the fastest resource) and ~11–14 minutes of handover/setup/coordination per case (~470–590 minutes/day) puts the real requirement near **3200–3400 minutes, i.e. 128–137% of capacity**. Overtime adds at most 6 × 30 = 180 minutes and cannot close a ~800-minute gap.

**Therefore: no routing policy can make this day feasible.** This is an estimate, not an observation, but it is robust to the assumption choices — even at every multiplier = 1.00 and zero handovers the day is at 109%. Policies here do not differ in *whether* work spills; they differ in **who waits, how predictably, and whether the P1 clock is protected**. Any answer claiming ~90% SLA attainment for all three priorities under these tables is arithmetically inconsistent with the capacity table above. My simulation confirms this: even full pooling leaves ~20% of cases uncompleted within the day.

**Recommendation R0 (independent of routing):** the honest fix is capacity/shift redesign — extend Farah beyond 12:00 or move her to 12:00–16:00, and shift one 08:00–16:00 resource to 12:00–20:00. Routing policy is a second-order lever here and should be presented to management as such.

## 2.4 Bottleneck identification, base

- **Electrical is the binding skill** (796 min/day, 29% of all work) but it has five qualified resources — so it is a *volume* bottleneck, not a *skill* bottleneck, in the morning.
- **The real base bottleneck is the 16:00–20:00 window.** Capacity collapses from 5 resources to 2 to 1 while arrivals only fall from 10 to 6 to 3. Load/capacity is 1.86 then 1.60. Everything that queues in the 14:00–16:00 peak lands here.
- **A hard structural gap: after 18:00 there is no Mechanical capability at all.** Emil is the only resource on shift and has no Mechanical skill. Expected M+ME arrivals in 18:00–20:00 ≈ 1.05 cases/day; these *cannot* be completed and must either wait for Deepa/Ben the next morning or be scheduled before 18:00. **Any 18:00–20:00 mechanical case is a guaranteed P1 SLA breach.** This is the single most actionable finding in the whole scenario and is invisible to a mean-delay analysis.
- **Validation is a latent second bottleneck** (660 min/day, 24%). Everyone can do it, so it rarely blocks — but it sits *after* the coordination delay, so it absorbs all upstream variance and is where SLA clocks actually expire.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three are specified as executable dispatch rules. Common notation: at a dispatch instant `t`, for a ready task `(case c, activity a)`, define
`slack(c,t) = deadline(c) − t − E[remaining work]`, where `deadline(c) = arrival + {120, 480, 1440}` by priority.

## P_spec — Strict specialization with fixed queues (baseline)

- **Eligibility.** Each activity has one named primary queue and one named backup, fixed for the day:
  - Mechanical → Ben, then Ada. Electrical → Chen, then Deepa. Software → Emil, then Chen.
  - Intake → Ada, then Deepa. Validation → Ben, then Deepa.
  - No other resource may take the task, ever, regardless of idleness.
- **Priority score.** Within a queue, strict priority class (P1 > P2 > P3), FIFO within class.
- **Reservation.** None.
- **Preemption.** A P1 arrival preempts a running P2/P3 *on that queue only*; restart penalty = 10% of remaining active service; at most once per case; the preempted case returns to head-of-class.
- **Parallelization.** ME/ES always run in parallel (the two branches are simply routed to their two owning queues) → always pays the 8-minute coordination.
- **Handover minimization.** None — handovers are structural: the case moves Intake(Ada) → diagnostic(Ben/Chen) → Validation(Ben), so a 7-min handover is incurred at almost every step, plus the 5-min cross-team charge on ME.
- **Overflow.** None. This is the point of including it.

## P_pool — Full pooling with a transparent dispatch rule

- **Eligibility.** Any resource with a non-`-` multiplier for the activity, currently on shift and idle.
- **Dispatch rule (auditable, one line).** *"Give the task to the qualified resource that would finish it earliest."* Formally: assign to `argmin_r [ max(t, ready_r) + setup_r + handover_r + E[service_a] × mult(r,a) ]` — **earliest-finish-time (EFT)**, which naturally accounts for speed multipliers, current busy-until time, and setup/handover penalties without operators needing to reason about them.
- **Priority score for queue selection.** When several tasks compete for one freed resource, pick the smallest `slack`, i.e. a **least-slack-first / EDD** rule, breaking ties by priority class. This is the classic tail-minimizing rule.
- **Reservation.** None — every minute is available to whoever needs it soonest.
- **Preemption.** P1 preempts any running P2/P3 whose remaining service exceeds 15 minutes (protects near-done work from a pointless 10% restart charge), once per case.
- **Parallelization.** Always parallel for ME/ES.
- **Overflow.** Not needed; the pool is already global.

## P_hybrid — Reserved P1 capacity + controlled cross-skill overflow (recommended)

This is P_pool with four disciplines added. Each addresses a specific failure discovered in §2.

1. **Dynamic P1 skill reservation.** During the 10:00–16:00 peak, the fastest resource per skill is **held out of the eligible set for P2/P3 work**: Ben for Mechanical, Chen for Electrical, Emil for Software. They still take P1 work instantly, and they still take P2/P3 *Intake and Validation* (so the reservation costs less than it looks). Reservation lifts automatically if the reserved resource has been idle ≥ 20 minutes, or if any P2 case has `slack < 30 min`.
2. **Case-continuity bonus (handover minimization).** The EFT score gets a **6-minute credit** if the candidate resource already handled the previous activity of this case. Rationale: keeping the case saves 7 (handover) + possibly 5 (cross-team) minutes, so a 6-minute credit is a conservative half-share and prevents the system from chasing a marginally faster server at a net loss. This is the single cheapest improvement available — see the handover column in §4.
3. **Threshold-based parallelization.** ME/ES run **sequentially on one cross-skilled resource** (Ada or Farah for ME; Chen, Emil or Farah for ES) unless the case is P1, or `min(d1,d2) > 20 min` *and* the second skill's queue is empty. Saves 7 + 5 + 8 = up to 20 minutes of pure overhead per multi-branch case and, more importantly, frees a whole resource.
4. **Explicit overflow triggers (the audit-friendly part).** Widen the eligible set to *all* qualified resources including slow ones (Farah at 1.40, Ada at 1.35 on Electrical) when **any** of:
   - queue length for a skill ≥ 3, or
   - the oldest waiting P2 has `slack < 45 min`, or
   - any P1 is waiting at all, or
   - a declared shock flag is set (absence, outage, surge).
   - **Reverse trigger:** revert to the narrow pool when the skill queue is empty for 15 consecutive minutes. The hysteresis prevents oscillation.
- **Preemption.** As P_pool, plus a hard cap of **one preemption per resource per hour** to stop preemption cascades in U1/U5 where P1 share is 18–23%.
- **Failure-mode choice.** When capacity binds, P3 is deliberately the class that waits — but with a **starvation guard**: any P3 waiting > 6 hours has its `slack` score floored so it outranks fresh P2 work. Deliberate degradation, not accidental.

**A fourth policy worth naming (not fully simulated):** *P_shift* — P_hybrid plus the shift change from R0 (move Farah to 12:00–16:00, move one 08:00–16:00 resource to 12:00–20:00). Per §2.3 this is worth more than the difference between any two routing policies, and it is the only thing that closes the 18:00–20:00 mechanical gap.

# 4. Scenario comparison

Estimates come from the simulator in §8 (6 seeds per cell, deterministic block counts, uniform service draws). **Scale and uncertainty must be read explicitly:**

- `mean_delay` and `p90_flow` are **minutes**; delay = flow time − own service time (i.e. waiting + handover + coordination).
- `unserved_pct` = % of cases not completed before their servers' last available minute; these are **excluded** from delay/p90 statistics and **counted as SLA failures**. Ignoring this column makes the delay columns look far better than reality — with ~20–57% spillover, the delay figures describe only the cases that got through.
- **Seed dispersion (printed by the code as `mean_delay_sd` / `P1_SLA_sd`) is large and must not be glossed over.** Across 6 seeds the standard deviation is **4–22 min on `mean_delay`** and **10–31 percentage points on `P1_SLA`**. The P1 spread is huge because only ~5–8 cases per day are P1, so each case is worth 12–20 points. The standard error of a cell mean is roughly `sd/√6`, i.e. **±2–9 min** on `mean_delay` and **±4–13 points** on `P1_SLA`. Consequently: **`mean_delay`, `p90_flow`, `unserved_pct` and the utilization/handover columns are well resolved and support firm conclusions; individual `P1_SLA` cell differences below ~15 points are not significant at 6 seeds.** The P1 claims in §5 therefore rest on the *consistent sign of the P_hybrid−P_pool gap across U1, U2 and U5* and on the mechanism (a reserved lane), not on any single cell. A production study would need 50+ seeds to resolve P1 directly — this is a genuine limitation of the evidence, not a formality.
- `max_util` is the highest single-resource utilization (fairness ceiling = 0.90 — **every policy in every scenario breaches it**, another symptom of §2.3). `jain` is Jain's fairness index over the six utilizations (1.0 = perfectly equal).
- `handover` = mean handover + cross-team + coordination minutes per completed case.
- `resilience` and `main_failure_mode` are qualitative judgements, derived from the degradation slope base→U5.

## Reading the table

1. **No policy meets any SLA target anywhere.** P1 attainment peaks at 60% against a 90% target. This is the §2.3 capacity gap, not a routing failure, and it should be stated to stakeholders before any policy is chosen.
2. **P_spec is dominated everywhere** — worse on every column in every scenario, and its Jain index (0.69–0.86) exposes the mechanism: it strands capacity. It is the correct control, not a candidate.
3. **P_pool wins the mean-delay objective (O1) decisively and in every scenario** — 28.5 vs 51.9 in base, and it also wins `unserved_pct` everywhere. If the objective were "minimize average delay", the analysis would stop here.
4. **But P_hybrid appears to win the tail/SLA objective (O2) where it matters most.** It beats P_pool on **P1_SLA in U1 (38.6 vs 24.7), U2 (60.6 vs 53.6) and U5 (23.8 vs 15.8)** — the three highest-P1-pressure scenarios. Each gap individually is within or near the noise band above; what makes the pattern credible is that the sign is consistent across all three high-P1-pressure scenarios and absent in the low-pressure ones (base, U4), which is exactly what a reserved-capacity mechanism predicts. It also has the shallowest degradation slope: P1_SLA falls 53.6 → 23.8 from base to U5 (−29.8 pts) versus P_pool's 59.7 → 15.8 (−43.9 pts). It also carries the lowest handover burden in every single scenario (10.3–11.3 vs 11.1–12.3), directly attributable to the continuity bonus and sequential branching.
5. **P_hybrid's cost is explicit and it is P2/P3.** It sacrifices 6–15 points of P2_SLA and 20–25 minutes of mean delay to buy P1 protection. Under O2's weights (10/3/1) that trade is strongly positive; under O1 it is negative. **This is the honest core of the recommendation: the two policies optimize different things and both answers are defensible.**
6. **U3 is P_hybrid's weak scenario** (P1 42.5 vs P_pool 44.7, unserved 38.9 vs 27.0) — reserving Emil for Software on a day when Chen is gone over-tightens an already two-deep pool. **This is a real defect and it drives the trigger design in §5.**

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary: **P_hybrid**, under objective O2 (tail/SLA risk, scored on the worst scenario)

**Why it is the robust choice, stated precisely:**

- **Worst-case P1 performance is what fails first and P_hybrid is best at it.** Its minimum P1_SLA across all six scenarios is 23.8 (U5); P_pool's is 15.8 and P_spec's is 7.5. Robustness defined as worst-case, not average-case, selects P_hybrid.
- **Its degradation slope is the shallowest** (−29.8 P1 points base→U5 vs −43.9), meaning its behaviour under shock is more predictable — which is what an operations manager actually needs to promise a customer.
- **Its failure mode is chosen rather than arbitrary.** Under stress it starves P3 (with a 6-hour guard), which is a decision someone signed off on. P_pool under stress fails wherever the queue happens to be longest, and P_spec fails wherever a fixed queue happens to be blocked — neither is defensible in an incident review.
- **It has the lowest coordination overhead in every scenario** (~10.5 min/case vs ~11.3–12.3), from the continuity bonus and sequential ME/ES. In a system at 109%+ of capacity, ~1–2 minutes/case × 42 cases = 40–80 recovered resource-minutes/day, which is real.
- Its fairness index (0.93–1.00) is essentially tied with P_pool's and far above P_spec's.

**I explicitly do not recommend it on base-case mean delay, where it loses to P_pool by 23 minutes.** That is the trade being bought.

## Fallback mode: **P_pool_wide** (P_hybrid with all reservations suspended)

Not a different policy — the *same* policy with rule 1 switched off and rule 4's overflow permanently on. Operators see one system with a documented "surge mode" switch.

## Trigger thresholds (auditable, all computable from the live event log)

**Switch P_hybrid → P_pool_wide when any of:**
- **T1 (thin pool):** the qualified, on-shift pool for any skill drops to ≤ 2 resources. *This fires automatically in U3* (Software 08:00–12:00 has only Farah) and would have fixed P_hybrid's U3 defect identified in §4 note 6. **This is the most important trigger in the design.**
- **T2 (throughput):** skill queue length ≥ 5, or total WIP ≥ 12 cases.
- **T3 (deadline pressure):** ≥ 2 P2 cases with `slack < 30 min`, or any P3 waiting > 6 h.
- **T4 (declared shock):** an absence, an IT outage, or observed arrivals in a block ≥ 1.4 × plan (this fires ~30–40 min into U1 and immediately in U5).

**Switch back** when all triggers have been clear for 20 consecutive minutes (hysteresis; prevents mode flapping, which would generate spurious setup penalties).

**Escalate to P_hybrid_strict** (reservation extended to *two* resources per skill, ME/ES forced sequential for all non-P1, overtime pre-authorized) when the P1 arrival rate exceeds 2/hour — i.e. U1 and U5.

## When another policy is preferable

- **Choose P_pool if the contracted objective is average or median resolution time**, or if P1 volume is genuinely low (< 8% of arrivals) so a reserved lane sits idle. Under objective O1, P_pool dominates in all six scenarios and I would recommend it without hesitation.
- **Choose P_spec only if** cross-skill quality risk is real and unmodelled here — the multipliers capture *speed*, not *accuracy*. If a 1.40 resource also has a materially higher Validation-failure rate, the whole pooling case weakens, and that data is not in the prompt.
- **Above all, choose R0/P_shift first.** Moving Farah's 4 hours from 08:00–12:00 to 12:00–16:00 adds ~240 minutes exactly where load/capacity is 1.14–1.86, and staffing one Mechanical-capable resource past 18:00 closes a *guaranteed* SLA breach. Both are worth more than the P_pool/P_hybrid gap. Recommending a routing policy without saying this would be misleading.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of estimates.** `slack` depends on `E[remaining work]`, which in practice comes from resource-entered estimates. A resource who inflates estimates gets easier work and looks efficient; one who deflates them gets a lower priority score and is punished. **Mitigation:** compute expected service from the *historical distribution by (activity, resource)* mined from the log, never from self-report, and monitor per-resource `actual/planned` ratio as a drift indicator (§7). Also watch for **priority inflation** — a 90-minute P1 target is a strong incentive to classify P2 as P1, and P1 share is already 18% in the 14:00–16:00 block. Audit the P1 classification rate per intaker.
- **Starvation of P3.** Structural, not incidental: with load at ~109–137%, *something* must wait, and P_hybrid names P3. The 6-hour slack-floor guard bounds it, but at U5 volumes even that guard will be violated. **Be explicit with stakeholders**: P3 attainment of 46–65% under stress is the design, not a bug. Report a daily P3 aging histogram, not just a mean.
- **Skill erosion.** Reservation and EFT dispatch both concentrate work on the fastest resource for each skill (Ben/Mechanical, Chen/Electrical, Emil/Software). Over months, Ada's Electrical (1.35) and Deepa's Mechanical (1.25) will drift *worse* from disuse — precisely the cross-skills the overflow triggers depend on in U2/U3. **Mitigation:** a deliberate practice quota (e.g. each secondary skill must be exercised ≥ 2×/week, dispatched during observed slack), and monitor each resource's multiplier estimated from the log over a rolling 30-day window. An unused backup is not a backup.
- **Excessive preemption.** At P1 shares of 18–23% (U1/U5), naive preemption produces cascades: each preemption costs 10% of remaining service plus a fresh handover, and it lengthens *two* cases. My rules cap it (only if remaining > 15 min; once per case; once per resource-hour). Monitor preemptions per P1 case — if it exceeds ~0.5, the reservation lane is too small and should be widened instead of preempting more.
- **Coordination overhead as an invisible tax.** At 10–14 min/case × 42 cases, handover/setup/coordination consumes 440–590 min/day — **17–24% of total usable capacity, comparable to an entire extra resource.** The continuity bonus and sequential ME/ES exist for this reason. Risk in the other direction: the continuity bonus can hold a case on a slow resource (Farah at 1.40 on a 50-min task loses far more than the 12 min saved), so the 6-minute credit must stay small and must be re-tuned from log data, not left as a constant.
- **New bottleneck at Validation.** Validation is 24% of all work and sits after the 8-minute coordination gate, so it absorbs all upstream variance and is where SLA clocks expire. Everyone is qualified, so it never *shows* as a blocked queue — it shows as a uniform lateness. Watch Validation waiting time as a separate stage metric (§7); if pooling pushes Validation waiting above ~20 min, the reserved-P1 lane must extend to cover Validation, not only diagnostics.
- **Fairness.** Every policy breaches the stated 0.90 utilization ceiling in every scenario (0.96–1.00). P_spec breaches it *unfairly* (Jain 0.69–0.86: some at 0.98, others idle); P_pool and P_hybrid breach it *evenly* (Jain 0.93–1.00). Even breach is better, but it is still a breach, and sustained ~97% utilization is not survivable for humans over weeks — it produces sick days, which is exactly scenario U3. **The fairness ceiling is evidence for R0, not a metric to optimize around.**
- **Human interpretability.** EFT ("who finishes it soonest") and least-slack ("whose deadline is closest") are both explainable in one sentence and defensible to a resource who asks "why did I get this?". The parts that are *not* self-explaining are the reservation and the 6-minute continuity credit — these need a visible reason code on every assignment ("Chen skipped: P1 reserve, 10:00–16:00"). **A dispatch decision an operator cannot explain will be overridden manually, and manual overrides are the most common cause of a policy pilot returning a null result.**

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## 7.1 Event attributes to capture (per activity instance)

| attribute | why it is needed |
|---|---|
| `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `org:resource` | minimum viable log |
| `queue_entry_timestamp` | the moment the task became *ready* (predecessor finished), distinct from `start_timestamp`. Without it, waiting time cannot be separated from handover and coordination. |
| `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp` | interval log → service time is measurable directly |
| `assignment_decision_timestamp` | dispatcher decision time; the gap to `start_timestamp` is pure dispatch latency |
| `eligible_resource_set` (list) | **the key counterfactual attribute** — enables "was a faster resource available and not chosen?" and validates that overflow triggers actually fired |
| `skill_used` (Mechanical/Electrical/Software/Intake/Validation) | setup penalties depend on skill-family switches between consecutive tasks per resource |
| `planned_service`, `actual_service` | ratio = estimate drift and gaming detection |
| `pause_start`/`pause_end`, `resume_reason` (break / preemption / shift-end) | separates break-pauses (free) from preemption-restarts (10% penalty) |
| `handover_flag`, `handover_minutes`, `crossteam_flag`, `setup_minutes`, `coordination_minutes` | makes the 17–24% overhead tax measurable rather than inferred |
| `preemption_flag`, `preempted_case_id`, `restart_penalty_minutes` | preemption cascade detection |
| `branch_mode` (parallel / sequential), `branch_id` | validates the §1.4 parallelization decision against realized flow times |
| `priority`, `priority_changed_at`, `sla_deadline`, `sla_clock_remaining`, `sla_breach_flag` | SLA clock is an attribute, not something recomputed downstream |
| `policy_version`, `mode` (hybrid / pool_wide / strict), `mode_switch_reason` | **essential** — without it, a mid-day mode switch looks like unexplained concept drift |
| `scenario_flag` / `shock_flag` (absence, outage, surge, tail-day) | lets you condition every analysis on the shock rather than averaging across it |

## 7.2 PM4Py analyses and what each would reveal

- **`pm4py.format_dataframe(..., start_timestamp_key=...)`** — establishes the interval log. Everything below depends on start timestamps being registered; with only completion timestamps, "service time" degenerates into inter-event time and the whole handover analysis is wrong.
- **`pm4py.get_service_time(log, aggregation_measure="mean"/"median"/"all")`** — per-activity service time. Track it **grouped by `org:resource`** to estimate the speed multipliers empirically and detect skill erosion (§6). A rising median for Ada on Electrical is the erosion signal.
- **`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)`** — adds `@@service_time`, `@@waiting_time`, `@@sojourn_time` **at case level**. **Correct labelling matters here:** `@@service_time` is the *union of busy intervals* across the case (so for parallel ME it is *not* the sum of both branches — overlapping work is counted once), and `@@waiting_time` is `sojourn − service`, which therefore lumps together true queueing, handover delay, coordination and cross-team time. Do **not** report `@@waiting_time` as "queue time"; report it as *non-service time* and decompose it separately with the explicit `handover_minutes` / `coordination_minutes` attributes. Stage-specific queueing must be computed independently as `start_timestamp − queue_entry_timestamp` per activity — PM4Py's case-level aggregates cannot give you that.
- **`pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)`** — quantifies the handover graph. Under P_spec expect dense Ada→Ben→Ben edges; under P_hybrid the continuity bonus should visibly thicken *self-loops* (same resource retaining the case) and thin the cross-team edges. **This is the direct empirical test of whether the continuity bonus is working.** Compare the network before/after rollout on the same weekday mix.
- **`pm4py.discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg`** with the standard variant filter — shows where waiting concentrates by edge. Bottleneck migration under shocks is visible as the heaviest edge moving from `Intake→Electrical` (U1) to `Electrical→Validation` (U2) to `Intake→Software` (U3/U5).
- **Concept-drift detection:** slice the log by `policy_version` and by two-hour block, and compare per-activity service-time distributions and DFG edge performance. A shift that coincides with `policy_version` is a policy effect; one that does not is genuine drift (estimate gaming, erosion, or a changed case mix).
- **Resource-level:** `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` and a per-resource utilization series to police the 0.90 ceiling and the Jain index daily.
- **Conformance:** build a small Petri net / declarative model of the *intended dispatch policy* (e.g. "no P2 assignment to a reserved resource during 10:00–16:00 unless an overflow trigger is set") and run alignment-based conformance against the real log. **Deviations are manual overrides** — the most important thing to measure in a pilot, per §6.

# 8. Reproducible Python

```python
# Robust multi-skill staffing: scenario-capacity simulation for three policies.
# All penalty mechanisms are named constants or explicit rules -- nothing hidden.
# Omitted mechanisms are listed at the bottom of this block.
import itertools, math, random
import pandas as pd

SERVICE = {
 "Intake":      dict(ord=(5,8),   tail=(10,14), p=0.08),
 "Mechanical":  dict(ord=(18,28), tail=(35,55), p=0.15),
 "Electrical":  dict(ord=(22,35), tail=(40,65), p=0.18),
 "Software":    dict(ord=(25,40), tail=(45,80), p=0.20),
 "Validation":  dict(ord=(10,18), tail=(22,35), p=0.12),
}
def hm(h,m=0): return h*60+m
RES = {
 "Ada":   dict(sk={"Intake":1.00,"Mechanical":1.00,"Electrical":1.35,"Validation":1.15},
               shift=(hm(8),hm(16)),  brk=(hm(12,15),hm(12,45))),
 "Ben":   dict(sk={"Intake":1.10,"Mechanical":1.00,"Validation":1.00},
               shift=(hm(8),hm(16)),  brk=(hm(12),hm(12,30))),
 "Chen":  dict(sk={"Intake":1.10,"Electrical":1.00,"Software":1.25,"Validation":1.10},
               shift=(hm(8),hm(16)),  brk=(hm(12,30),hm(13))),
 "Deepa": dict(sk={"Intake":1.00,"Mechanical":1.25,"Electrical":1.00,"Validation":1.00},
               shift=(hm(10),hm(18)), brk=(hm(14),hm(14,30))),
 "Emil":  dict(sk={"Intake":1.10,"Electrical":1.30,"Software":1.00,"Validation":1.00},
               shift=(hm(12),hm(20)), brk=(hm(16),hm(16,30))),
 "Farah": dict(sk={"Intake":1.00,"Mechanical":1.40,"Electrical":1.40,"Software":1.40,"Validation":1.20},
               shift=(hm(8),hm(12)),  brk=None),
}
OVERTIME = 30                      # minutes past shift end, per resource (given)
BLOCKS=[
 (hm(8), hm(10), 7,  dict(P1=10,P2=55,P3=35), dict(M=30,E=25,S=15,ME=20,ES=10)),
 (hm(10),hm(12), 9,  dict(P1=15,P2=55,P3=30), dict(M=25,E=25,S=15,ME=25,ES=10)),
 (hm(12),hm(14), 7,  dict(P1=12,P2=58,P3=30), dict(M=22,E=28,S=18,ME=20,ES=12)),
 (hm(14),hm(16),10,  dict(P1=18,P2=52,P3=30), dict(M=20,E=30,S=18,ME=20,ES=12)),
 (hm(16),hm(18), 6,  dict(P1=10,P2=50,P3=40), dict(M=20,E=25,S=25,ME=15,ES=15)),
 (hm(18),hm(20), 3,  dict(P1=5, P2=45,P3=50), dict(M=25,E=20,S=30,ME=10,ES=15)),
]
BRANCH={"M":["Mechanical"],"E":["Electrical"],"S":["Software"],
        "ME":["Mechanical","Electrical"],"ES":["Electrical","Software"]}
FAMILY={"Mechanical":"mech","Electrical":"es","Software":"es"}   # team families
SLA={"P1":120,"P2":480,"P3":1440}
# ---- EXPLICIT penalty constants (never buried in the scheduler) ----
HANDOVER = 7   # resource change between consecutive activities of a case
CROSSTEAM= 5   # mech <-> electrical/software team move, waived if same resource keeps case
SETUP    = 5   # a resource switching diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases
COORD    = 8   # parallel ME/ES coordination before Validation; zero if sequential

# ---------------- scenarios ----------------
def scenario_config(name):
    c=dict(blocks=[list(b) for b in BLOCKS], tail_mult=1.0, absent=set(), outage=None)
    if name in ("U1","U5"):                     # morning surge x1.7, P1 +8pp (taken from P3 then P2)
        for b in c["blocks"]:
            if b[0]<hm(12):
                b[2]=b[2]*1.7
                p=dict(b[3]); shift=8.0
                take=min(shift,p["P3"]); p["P3"]-=take; rem=shift-take; p["P2"]-=rem; p["P1"]+=shift
                b[3]=p
    if name=="U2":                              # E and ES doubled 12:00-16:00, mix renormalized
        for b in c["blocks"]:
            if hm(12)<=b[0]<hm(16):
                m=dict(b[4]); m["E"]*=2; m["ES"]*=2
                tot=sum(m.values()); b[4]={k:v*100/tot for k,v in m.items()}
    if name in ("U3","U5"): c["absent"]={"Chen"}
    if name=="U4": c["tail_mult"]=2.0           # complex-tail probabilities doubled
    if name=="U5": c["outage"]=(hm(9,30),hm(10))# Intake blocked
    return c

def pick(rng,d):
    tot=sum(d.values()); r=rng.random()*tot; a=0
    for k,v in d.items():
        a+=v
        if r<=a: return k
    return k

def gen_cases(cfg,rng):
    # Deterministic block counts (variance reduction); randomness enters via mix and service draws.
    cases=[];i=0
    for s,e,n,pri,mix in cfg["blocks"]:
        for _ in range(int(round(n))):
            i+=1
            cases.append(dict(cid=f"C{i:03d}", arr=s+rng.random()*(e-s),
                              pri=pick(rng,pri), typ=pick(rng,mix)))
    cases.sort(key=lambda c:c["arr"])
    return cases

def draw(act,rng,tail_mult):
    d=SERVICE[act]; p=min(1.0,d["p"]*tail_mult)          # A5: uniform-mixture service model
    lo,hi=(d["tail"] if rng.random()<p else d["ord"])
    return lo+rng.random()*(hi-lo)

# ---------------- calendar (shifts, breaks, overtime) ----------------
def base_windows(r,cfg):
    if r in cfg["absent"]: return []
    s,e=RES[r]["shift"]; e=e+OVERTIME; b=RES[r]["brk"]
    if not b: return [[s,e]]
    return [[s,b[0]],[b[1],e]]

def merge(ws):
    ws=sorted([list(w) for w in ws]); out=[]
    for w in ws:
        if out and w[0]<=out[-1][1]+1e-9: out[-1][1]=max(out[-1][1],w[1])
        else: out.append([w[0],w[1]])
    return out

class Cal:
    """Free-segment calendar. A7: breaks PAUSE work (segments), they do not restart it."""
    def __init__(self,cfg):
        self.free={r:base_windows(r,cfg) for r in RES}
    def place(self,r,earliest,dur):
        segs=[];rem=dur
        for w in self.free[r]:
            if rem<=1e-9: break
            s=max(w[0],earliest)
            if s>=w[1]-1e-9: continue
            take=min(rem,w[1]-s); segs.append((s,s+take)); rem-=take; earliest=s+take
        if rem>1e-6: return None            # does not fit in the day -> case spills
        nf=[]
        for w in self.free[r]:
            cur=[w[0],w[1]]
            for (a,b) in segs:
                if a>=cur[0] and b<=cur[1]+1e-9:
                    if a-cur[0]>1e-9: nf.append([cur[0],a])
                    cur=[b,cur[1]]
            if cur[1]-cur[0]>1e-9: nf.append(cur)
        self.free[r]=[w for w in nf if w[1]-w[0]>1e-9]
        return segs

# ---------------- policies ----------------
POLICIES={
 "P_spec":   dict(pool=False, reserve=False, parallel="always",    pref="specialist"),
 "P_pool":   dict(pool=True,  reserve=False, parallel="always",    pref="eft"),
 "P_hybrid": dict(pool=True,  reserve=True,  parallel="threshold", pref="cont_eft"),
}
SPECIALIST={"Mechanical":["Ben","Ada","Deepa","Farah"],
            "Electrical":["Chen","Deepa","Emil","Ada","Farah"],
            "Software":["Emil","Chen","Farah"],
            "Intake":["Ada","Deepa","Farah","Ben","Chen","Emil"],
            "Validation":["Ben","Deepa","Emil","Chen","Ada","Farah"]}

def eligible(act,cfg,pol,pri,t):
    out=[r for r in RES if act in RES[r]["sk"] and r not in cfg["absent"]]
    if not pol["pool"]:
        out=[r for r in SPECIALIST[act] if r in out][:2]      # primary + one backup only
    if pol["reserve"] and pri!="P1" and act in ("Mechanical","Electrical","Software"):
        # dynamic P1 reservation: hold the fastest server per skill during the 10:00-16:00 peak
        keep={"Mechanical":"Ben","Electrical":"Chen","Software":"Emil"}[act]
        if hm(10)<=t<hm(16) and len(out)>1 and keep in out: out=[r for r in out if r!=keep]
    return out

def simulate(pol_name,scn,seed):
    pol=POLICIES[pol_name]; cfg=scenario_config(scn); rng=random.Random(seed)
    cases=gen_cases(cfg,rng); cal=Cal(cfg); ev=[]; last={r:None for r in RES}
    recs=[]
    for c in cases:
        t=c["arr"]; svc=0.0; hand=0.0; prev_res=None; prev_fam=None
        if cfg["outage"] and cfg["outage"][0]<=t<cfg["outage"][1]: t=cfg["outage"][1]  # U5 IT outage
        def run(act,earliest,prev_res,prev_fam):
            """Earliest-finish-time dispatch with explicit handover / cross-team / setup charges."""
            nonlocal svc,hand
            elig=eligible(act,cfg,pol,c["pri"],earliest)
            best=None
            for r in elig:
                d=draw(act,rng,cfg["tail_mult"])*RES[r]["sk"][act]      # speed multiplier
                extra=0.0
                if prev_res is not None and r!=prev_res: extra+=HANDOVER
                fam=FAMILY.get(act)
                if fam and prev_fam and fam!=prev_fam and r!=prev_res: extra+=CROSSTEAM
                if fam and last[r] and last[r]!=fam: extra+=SETUP
                segs=cal.place(r,earliest+extra,d)
                if segs is None: continue
                fin=segs[-1][1]
                # continuity bonus (P_hybrid only): 6-min credit for keeping the case
                key=fin if pol["pref"]!="cont_eft" else fin-(6 if r==prev_res else 0)
                if best is None or key<best[0]: best=(key,r,segs,d,extra)
                for (a,b) in segs: cal.free[r].append([a,b])            # undo tentative placement
                cal.free[r]=merge(cal.free[r])
            if best is None: return None
            _,r,_,d,extra=best
            segs=cal.place(r,earliest+extra,d)
            if FAMILY.get(act): last[r]=FAMILY[act]
            svc+=d; hand+=extra
            ev.append(dict(case=c["cid"],act=act,res=r,start=segs[0][0],end=segs[-1][1],
                           work=sum(b-a for a,b in segs)))
            return (r,segs[-1][1],d)
        def spill():
            recs.append(dict(cid=c["cid"],pri=c["pri"],typ=c["typ"],flow=None,svc=None,hand=None))
        r0=run("Intake",t,None,None)
        if r0 is None: spill(); continue
        prev_res,t,_=r0
        branches=BRANCH[c["typ"]]
        if len(branches)==1:
            rr=run(branches[0],t,prev_res,None)
            if rr is None: spill(); continue
            prev_res,t,_=rr; prev_fam=FAMILY[branches[0]]
        else:
            xs=[r for r in RES if all(b in RES[r]["sk"] for b in branches) and r not in cfg["absent"]]
            # threshold parallelization: sequential on one cross-skilled resource unless P1
            go_seq = bool(xs) and (pol["parallel"]!="always") and c["pri"]!="P1"
            if go_seq:
                cur=t; pr=prev_res
                for b in branches:
                    rr=run(b,cur,pr,prev_fam)
                    if rr is None: cur=None; break
                    pr,cur,_=rr; prev_fam=FAMILY[b]
                if cur is None: spill(); continue
                t=cur; prev_res=pr                    # no COORD charge on the sequential path
            else:
                ends=[]
                for b in branches:
                    rr=run(b,t,prev_res,None)
                    if rr is None: ends=None; break
                    ends.append(rr)
                if ends is None: spill(); continue
                t=max(e[1] for e in ends); prev_res=max(ends,key=lambda e:e[1])[0]
                t+=COORD; hand+=COORD; prev_fam="mixed"
        rv=run("Validation",t,prev_res,prev_fam)
        if rv is None: spill(); continue
        _,tend,_=rv
        recs.append(dict(cid=c["cid"],pri=c["pri"],typ=c["typ"],flow=tend-c["arr"],svc=svc,hand=hand))
    df=pd.DataFrame(recs); evd=pd.DataFrame(ev)
    util={}
    for r in RES:
        avail=sum(w[1]-w[0] for w in base_windows(r,cfg))
        busy=evd[evd.res==r].work.sum() if len(evd) else 0     # 'work' excludes paused break time
        util[r]=busy/avail if avail>0 else float('nan')
    return df,evd,util

def metrics(df,util):
    done=df.dropna(subset=["flow"]); out={}
    out["unserved_pct"]=round(100*df.flow.isna().mean(),1)     # spilled to next day = SLA failure
    out["mean_delay"]=round((done.flow-done.svc).mean(),1) if len(done) else None
    out["p90_flow"]=round(done.flow.quantile(0.9),1) if len(done) else None
    for p in ["P1","P2","P3"]:
        s=df[df.pri==p]
        ok=((s.flow<=SLA[p]) & s.flow.notna()).sum()
        out[p+"_SLA"]=round(100*ok/len(s),1) if len(s) else None
    u=[v for v in util.values() if v==v]
    out["max_util"]=round(max(u),2)
    out["jain"]=round(sum(u)**2/(len(u)*sum(x*x for x in u)),3)
    out["handover_min_per_case"]=round(done.hand.mean(),1) if len(done) else None
    return out
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and service ranges alone

- **Nothing about queueing variance, which is what actually drives SLA attainment.** Expected arrivals fix the *mean* of the arrival process; SLA attainment is a *tail* property, and the tail depends on the arrival process's variability (Poisson? batched after an outage? correlated with the shock?) and on the service-time *shape* inside each range. Two systems with identical means and identical ranges can differ by a factor of two in P90 delay. **All P90 and SLA numbers in §4 are conditional on A1 and A5 and should be treated as ordinal, not cardinal.**
- **Nothing about correlation.** Are complex-tail cases more likely to be P1? Do ME cases cluster in the morning? Is the U3 absence correlated with the U1 surge (burnout)? Independence (A2) is assumed, and every dependence I can imagine makes the system *worse*, not better.
- **Nothing about whether the multipliers are stable.** A 1.40 multiplier measured historically may itself be an artifact of that resource receiving only the hard cases. Speed multipliers must be re-estimated from the log *conditioning on case complexity*, or pooling will be mis-priced.
- **Nothing about quality.** Speed is modelled; accuracy is not. If cross-skilled work has a higher Validation-failure rate, the pooling advantage shrinks or reverses. This is the largest unmodelled risk to the recommendation.
- **The absolute SLA numbers are not usable as forecasts.** They are only usable to *rank* policies, and even that ranking is objective-dependent (§4 note 6, §5).
- **The 109% capacity result (§2.3) is the one conclusion that IS robust** to all of the above, because it uses only means and the given shift table.

## 9.2 Pilot design — distinguish the policies without risking P1 cases

**Principle: never randomize a P1 case.** P1 has a 120-minute clock and a safety-adjacent flavour; an unlucky assignment is a real harm. Randomize only P2/P3, and let P1 always run under the *best-known* rule.

1. **Phase 0 — instrumentation only (2–3 weeks, zero policy change).** Deploy the §7 attribute set on the current process. Deliverables: empirical service-time distributions per (activity, resource) — this replaces assumption A5 with data and re-estimates the multipliers; the true arrival process (test Poisson-ness per block); a measured handover/setup/coordination burden to compare against my 10–14 min/case estimate; and a baseline handover-of-work network. **If Phase 0 confirms the ~109% load, escalate the staffing conversation (R0) before proceeding — piloting routing on an infeasible system risks concluding "policies don't matter".**
2. **Phase 1 — shadow mode (2 weeks).** Run all three dispatchers in software on live queue state; log what each *would* have assigned alongside what actually happened. No behaviour change, no risk. This is enough to detect gross implementation bugs and to check that the overflow triggers fire when expected. It cannot measure delay (the counterfactual queue never materializes), so it is a safety gate, not an evaluation.
3. **Phase 2 — restricted randomization (4–6 weeks, the actual experiment).**
   - **Unit of randomization: the two-hour block**, not the case. Case-level randomization is contaminated because policies act through *shared queues* — a pooled assignment changes the resource state seen by the next specialized case. Block-level assignment keeps the interference within the unit.
   - **Design: a balanced Latin square over (weekday × block-of-day × policy)** so that the 14:00–16:00 peak and the 16:00–18:00 collapse are each observed under all three policies an equal number of times. Without this, the block effects in §2.2 will swamp the policy effect.
   - **Carve-outs (safety):** P1 cases always dispatched under P_hybrid regardless of the block's assigned policy; the T1 thin-pool trigger and the P3 6-hour starvation guard are **always active in every arm** — they are safety rails, not treatment.
   - **Sample size:** ~42 cases/day × 5 days × 6 weeks ≈ 1250 cases, ~400 per arm. Given the observed dispersion (§4), that is adequate to detect a ≥10-point P2_SLA difference but **marginal for P1** (P1 is ~13% of volume, ~55 cases/arm) — so P1 conclusions should come from the *simulation calibrated on Phase 0 data*, not from the pilot alone. State this limitation up front rather than over-claiming from an underpowered arm.
   - **Primary endpoint:** P2_SLA attainment (adequately powered, aligned with objective O2's second term). **Secondary:** P90 flow time, handover minutes/case, max utilization, Jain index, P3 aging tail.
4. **Phase 3 — stress rehearsal, not a live shock.** U3 and U5 must never be created deliberately. Instead: (a) replay the Phase-0 log through the calibrated simulator with Chen removed and with the outage injected, to validate the mode-switch triggers offline; (b) use *naturally occurring* absences and demand surges as observational events, flagged by `shock_flag`, and compare policy behaviour on those days with the matched non-shock days.
5. **Stopping rules (pre-registered before the pilot starts).** Halt an arm immediately if: any P1 breach attributable to the dispatcher; P3 aging exceeds 12 hours for any case; any resource exceeds 0.95 utilization for two consecutive days; or manual override rate exceeds 15% (per §6, overrides mean the policy is not really being tested — investigate interpretability before continuing).
6. **Analysis.** Conformance-check the realized log against the intended policy model (§7) *before* comparing outcomes, and report per-arm override rates alongside the endpoints. A policy that looks good only because operators quietly ignored it has not been validated.
