# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

“Core” here means recurring work within an expected responsibility and authority boundary. It is not simply the activity with the highest count.

## Automation intake and entitlement-precheck boundary

- **Core:** Nora’s A1 and A2 events are clearly core because they are the only observed activities and occur at very high volume.
- **Peripheral:** There is no evidence that Nora owns downstream diagnosis, customer communication, implementation, closure, or approval.
- **Complexity and authority:** The low median complexity of 0.11 should not be interpreted as an organizational judgment that low-complexity cases are unimportant. It more likely reflects that automation is applied to predictable intake and precheck scenarios. Nora performs a rule-based precheck, not human commercial, risk, or supervisory judgment.
- **Important semantic distinction:** A2 performed by Nora should be distinguished from human A2 validation. The same activity label may cover materially different work.

## Permanent front-office request resolution

- **Core:** A1, human A2 validation, A4, and A12 are the recurring service-resolution activities for R01 and R02. R01 has 220/390/310/260 events in those activities; R02 has 210/415/335/280.
- **Peripheral:** R01’s A3, A5, A7, A8, and A10 counts indicate experienced generalist support, but not automatic membership in technical or commercial authority roles. R02’s smaller counts in these activities are consistent with routine frontline support.
- **Complexity:** R01’s higher high/critical exposure than R02’s suggests a capability or assignment tier within the same broad role. The data supports a distinction between a routine frontline role and an experienced generalist tier, but not necessarily a separate formal department.
- **Authority:** Front-office staff should have authority to resolve standard cases and gather information, not to silently substitute for final pricing, security, or supervisory decisions.

## Temporary frontline surge support

- **Core:** Kai and Lina’s core work is restricted simple-work intake, entitlement-note completion, standard response, and closure.
- **Peripheral:** The few A3 and A8 events should be investigated as escalated assistance, training exposure, or logging artifacts rather than treated as full diagnosis or negotiation authority.
- **Complexity and substitution:** Their near-identical profiles and very low complexity indicate a temporary surge-support pool. The weeks 8–10 reopen pattern is significant: 17 reopened cases tied to incomplete A2 notes show that high activity volume did not equal equivalent quality or independent readiness.
- **Authority:** Temporary status is not itself evidence of lower ability, but it is evidence that the organization should verify training, delegation, and supervision before assigning complex or high-risk work.

## Technical solution engineering

- **Core:** A3, A5, and A10 are strongly core for both Chen and Dina: Chen has 260/240/215 events; Dina has 255/235/220.
- **Peripheral:** Quality involvement, knowledge updates, and reopens are plausible technical feedback work. Chen’s A6 work is security-adjacent support, but A6 should remain formally owned by the security specialist unless delegation records state otherwise.
- **Complexity:** Similar activity profiles hide a meaningful workload gradient. Chen’s median complexity is 0.71, versus 0.48 for Dina, and Chen handles substantially more high and critical work.
- **Authority:** Technical engineers should own solution design and implementation within their competence boundary. They should not be inferred to have security sign-off, pricing authority, or final supervisory approval merely because they touch cases that require those decisions.

## Security risk-review and critical-incident specialist

- **Core:** A6 is Erik’s core specialist activity. The 96 security reviews are not numerous relative to all frontline events, but they are highly concentrated and risk-significant.
- **Peripheral:** A3/A5/A10 interactions are best understood as specialist consultation or control implementation support. A11/A13/A14 are likely incident-quality, learning, or remediation contributions.
- **Complexity:** Erik’s median complexity of 0.86 and 55 critical cases make case severity essential to the role definition.
- **Authority:** Erik may have a security review or veto function, but final managerial approval can still belong to Hana. The organization should explicitly distinguish risk sign-off, implementation advice, and final business authorization.

## Commercial exception and negotiation specialist

- **Core:** Fatima’s A7 and A8 work is clearly core: 122 pricing reviews and 90 negotiations, concentrated in medium/high-complexity cases.
- **Peripheral:** A9 events should normally be interpreted as commercial recommendation, preparation, or workflow participation unless the audit trail explicitly says Fatima made a final delegated approval. A2/A3 work supplies context for commercial decisions.
- **Substitution:** Giorgio’s temporary cover during Fatima’s absence is valuable resilience evidence, but the pricing-duration increase from 38 to 74 minutes shows that the cover arrangement was not operationally equivalent.
- **Authority:** Pricing review and customer negotiation may be commercially specialized, while final exception approval remains supervisory. These need separate audit states.

## Supervisory approval and escalation authority

- **Core:** A9 is Hana’s dominant, authority-defining activity: 138 events. High/critical case exposure supports the interpretation that this is not routine clerical approval.
- **Peripheral:** A8, A13, and occasional validation, diagnostic, pricing, quality, or closure interactions are escalation interventions rather than evidence that Hana owns frontline, technical, commercial, or quality work.
- **Substitution:** Mateo’s 21 preliminary approvals while Hana was unavailable demonstrate operational cover, but the 13 later rechecks demonstrate that preliminary review and final approval are not equivalent.
- **Authority:** This role should be modeled as a final decision point unless explicit delegated-authority records show otherwise.

## Quality assurance and knowledge governance

- **Core:** A11 and A14 dominate for Ivan and Julia. Together, their quality checks and knowledge updates are much more concentrated than their technical-support actions.
- **Peripheral:** A3/A5/A10 may represent defect diagnosis, verification, or rework validation. A12 and A13 may represent closure and reopen-control checks.
- **Complexity:** Their median complexity values are moderate because quality work can span routine and non-routine cases. This does not mean QA personnel own technical implementation.
- **Authority:** QA has gatekeeping authority over conformance or readiness, not necessarily authority to design the solution or approve commercial and security risk decisions.

## Incident coordination and cross-functional escalation brokerage

- **Core:** Mateo’s core is not a single high-frequency coded activity. It is the recurring coordination pattern: handling reopens, routing escalations, collecting specialist input, and performing preliminary review. The process fragments explicitly label a Mateo “Coordinate” step even though the enumerated activity list does not contain a dedicated coordination activity.
- **Peripheral:** Mateo’s broad touch pattern across intake, diagnosis, security, pricing, closure, and knowledge updates should be treated as triage, chasing, and coordination evidence rather than proof that he owns all those domains.
- **Complexity and substitution:** His median complexity of 0.77 and 76 critical cases make him a high-risk routing role. Security referral filtering reduced false escalations, but the two critical waits over two days show the risk of a broker becoming a queue or decision bottleneck.
- **Authority:** Preliminary A9 work is not final supervisory authority. The activity taxonomy should ideally distinguish “request approval,” “recommend approval,” and “final approval.”

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

## R07_Giorgio: commercial shadow role and senior frontline bridge

Giorgio is the strongest observed commercial shadow role.

- **Benefits:** He provides resilience during Fatima’s absence, can communicate with customers, understands frontline context, and has entitlement and pricing certifications. His A7/A8 activity volume is substantial, not incidental.
- **Risks:** The absence-period duration increase from 38 to 74 minutes indicates slower execution, possible lower familiarity, lower capacity, or more complex cases. It would be unsafe to conclude that he is a fully interchangeable commercial specialist based only on event counts.
- **Recommended interpretation:** Keep R07’s primary role as senior frontline service resolution, with a documented commercial-cover capability. Define which pricing thresholds he may handle, when Fatima or Hana must review, and what coverage metrics trigger escalation.

## R13_Mateo: cross-functional escalation broker

Mateo is a bridging role rather than a generic all-purpose worker.

- **Benefits:** He connects reopens to the correct technical, security, commercial, and supervisory path. His filtering of low-risk Enterprise Security referrals reduced false-escalation complaints and may protect Erik from inappropriate demand.
- **Risks:** A coordinator can become a hidden bottleneck or unofficial decision maker. The two critical cases waiting more than two days show that reducing false positives must not suppress or delay true critical incidents.
- **Authority boundary:** The 13 Hana rechecks after Mateo’s preliminary approval work are direct evidence that coordination and preliminary assessment should not be logged or governed as final approval.

## R05_Erik: rare but critical specialist

Erik is a specialist overlay on high-risk cases, not merely a low-volume technical resource.

- **Benefits:** Centralized security review supports consistency, auditability, and specialized risk control. His critical-case concentration justifies preserving specialist review even when it appears inefficient by throughput measures.
- **Risks:** A single specialist creates capacity and continuity risk. Mateo’s filtering is useful, but it should be combined with explicit critical-case bypass rules, on-call coverage, and service-level targets.
- **Interpretation:** A small cluster, low similarity, or low total event count must not cause the organization to eliminate the role or merge it blindly into technical engineering.

## R14_Nora: automation boundary role

Nora is a useful operational bridge from customer intake to human work.

- **Benefits:** High-volume automated registration and precheck can reduce routine workload and provide consistent initial data.
- **Risks:** Treating automated A2 as equivalent to a human entitlement decision can create automation overreach, hidden exceptions, and misleading role-discovery results.
- **Control implication:** Record whether each A2 is an automated precheck, a human validation, or a human override. This makes handovers and accountability auditable.

## R11_Kai and R12_Lina: temporary-workforce bridge

Kai and Lina form a legitimate seasonal capacity mechanism.

- **Benefits:** They absorb simple request volume and preserve experienced capacity for complex work.
- **Risks:** The reopen pattern tied to incomplete entitlement notes shows that closure volume alone is a poor measure of effectiveness.
- **Control implication:** Use scoped work queues, validation checklists, sampled quality review, and feedback before expanding their authority. Their temporary assignment should not be treated as evidence of permanent specialist competence or incompetence.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_parquet("order_to_resolution.parquet")
# Or: df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution.csv")

df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True, errors="raise")

required = [
    CASE, ACT, TS, RES,
    "case_complexity",
    "temporary_substitution",
    "escalation_level"
]
missing = set(required) - set(df.columns)
if missing:
    raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {missing}")

# PM4Py formatting sorts/prepares the dataframe while retaining
# other event and case attributes.
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id=CASE,
    activity_key=ACT,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover behavioral role candidates and activity similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
discovered_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log_df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log_df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

# These normally return dictionaries keyed by (resource_1, resource_2).
handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log_df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

working_together_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log_df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

# Optional: inspect the critical-event handover network separately.
# This works when escalation_level is repeated on the relevant events.
critical_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log_df,
    "escalation_level",
    {"critical"},
    level="event",
    retain=True
)

critical_handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    critical_log,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Convert network dictionaries to inspectable dataframes
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def edge_frame(network):
    """
    PM4Py high-level organizational-network functions normally return:
    {(resource_a, resource_b): weight, ...}
    """
    rows = []
    for edge, weight in network.items():
        if isinstance(edge, tuple) and len(edge) == 2:
            source, target = edge
            rows.append(
                {"source": source, "target": target, "weight": float(weight)}
            )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["source", "target", "weight"])

similarity_edges = edge_frame(resource_similarity)
handover_edges = edge_frame(handover_network)
working_together_edges = edge_frame(working_together_network)
critical_handover_edges = edge_frame(critical_handover_network)

print("Highest activity-profile similarities")
print(similarity_edges.sort_values("weight", ascending=False).head(20))

print("Highest handover edges")
print(handover_edges.sort_values("weight", ascending=False).head(30))

print("Highest working-together edges")
print(working_together_edges.sort_values("weight", ascending=False).head(30))

# In particular, inspect Mateo's incoming/outgoing cross-functional handovers.
mateo_handoffs = handover_edges[
    (handover_edges["source"] == "R13_Mateo") |
    (handover_edges["target"] == "R13_Mateo")
].sort_values("weight", ascending=False)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Build case-context evidence by resource
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# case_complexity is assumed numeric in this example. If it is categorical,
# replace the map with the organization's approved ordinal/severity mapping.
complexity_map = {
    "low": 0.25,
    "medium": 0.50,
    "high": 0.75,
    "critical": 1.00
}

log_df["_complexity_num"] = pd.to_numeric(
    log_df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce"
)

needs_mapping = log_df["_complexity_num"].isna()
log_df.loc[needs_mapping, "_complexity_num"] = (
    log_df.loc[needs_mapping, "case_complexity"]
    .astype(str)
    .str.lower()
    .map(complexity_map)
)

log_df["_is_temporary_substitution"] = (
    log_df["temporary_substitution"]
    .fillna(False)
    .astype(str)
    .str.lower()
    .isin({"true", "1", "yes", "y"})
)

log_df["_is_critical"] = (
    log_df["escalation_level"]
    .fillna("")
    .astype(str)
    .str.lower()
    .str.contains("critical")
)

# Deduplicate at case-resource level before deriving median case complexity.
# This avoids counting every event in a long case as a separate handled case.
case_resource = (
    log_df.groupby([CASE, RES], as_index=False)
    .agg(
        case_complexity=("_complexity_num", "first"),
        had_temporary_substitution=("_is_temporary_substitution", "max"),
        handled_critical_case=("_is_critical", "max")
    )
)

resource_context = (
    case_resource.groupby(RES, as_index=False)
    .agg(
        handled_cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
        median_case_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        temporary_substitution_case_share=(
            "had_temporary_substitution", "mean"
        ),
        critical_case_share=("handled_critical_case", "mean")
    )
)

# Activity counts remain useful, but only as one input.
activity_profile = (
    pd.crosstab(log_df[RES], log_df[ACT])
    .add_prefix("n_")
    .reset_index()
)

substitution_activity_counts = (
    log_df.loc[log_df["_is_temporary_substitution"]]
    .groupby([RES, ACT])
    .size()
    .rename("substitution_events")
    .reset_index()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Flatten the PM4Py role result for a review table
# PM4Py versions may expose role members as role.originator_importance,
# role.resources, or dictionary keys. This helper handles common forms.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def get_role_field(role, field, default=None):
    if isinstance(role, dict):
        return role.get(field, default)
    return getattr(role, field, default)

def get_resource_weights(role):
    candidate = (
        get_role_field(role, "originator_importance")
        or get_role_field(role, "resources")
        or get_role_field(role, "originators")
        or {}
    )

    if isinstance(candidate, dict):
        return candidate

    if isinstance(candidate, (list, tuple, set)):
        return {resource: np.nan for resource in candidate}

    return {}

role_rows = []
for role_id, role in enumerate(discovered_roles):
    activities = (
        get_role_field(role, "activities")
        or get_role_field(role, "activity_set")
        or []
    )

    for resource, importance in get_resource_weights(role).items():
        role_rows.append({
            "discovered_role_id": role_id,
            RES: resource,
            "role_member_importance": importance,
            "discovered_activities": list(activities)
        })

role_membership = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)

role_evidence = (
    role_membership
    .merge(resource_context, on=RES, how="left")
    .merge(activity_profile, on=RES, how="left")
    .sort_values(
        ["discovered_role_id", "median_case_complexity"],
        ascending=[True, False]
    )
)

print(role_evidence[
    [
        "discovered_role_id", RES, "role_member_importance",
        "median_case_complexity",
        "temporary_substitution_case_share",
        "critical_case_share",
        "discovered_activities"
    ]
])

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Review rule: do not convert a discovered behavioral cluster directly
# into a formal role or authority assignment.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# For every candidate cluster, review:
# - case complexity and critical-case share;
# - temporary-substitution periods separately from normal periods;
# - handover direction and broker position;
# - whether A9 is preliminary, delegated, or final approval;
# - titles, certifications, delegation limits, and audit outcomes;
# - reopens, service time, waiting time, and quality outcomes.
#
# Example: R06 and R07 may be behaviorally similar in A7/A8 work, but
# pricing-duration deterioration during absence cover means the mined
# similarity is not sufficient evidence of interchangeable formal authority.
```

The expected interpretation of the networks is:

- **Handover network:** Nora to frontline; frontline to technical; technical to QA; commercial to supervisor; security and reopen cases through Mateo; reopens to specialists or Hana.
- **Working-together network:** identifies resources appearing in the same cases, but does not establish delegation or final authority.
- **Similarity network:** validates obvious same-role candidates such as Chen/Dina and Ivan/Julia, while also revealing potentially misleading pairs such as Fatima/Giorgio and Hana/Mateo.
- **Critical-only handover network:** is especially useful for testing whether Mateo’s filtering protects Erik appropriately or introduces delay for true critical cases.

# 6. Operational interpretation

## Staffing and capacity

Management should plan capacity by role boundary and complexity, not by total event volume.

- Maintain separate staffing views for routine frontline work, complex frontline work, technical engineering, security review, commercial exceptions, supervisory approvals, QA, and coordination.
- Use R01 and R07 as experienced frontline capacity, but do not assume they can replace technical, security, or commercial specialists without defined delegation.
- Treat R07 as a planned commercial backup candidate. The observed increase from 38 to 74 minutes during Fatima’s absence indicates that backup capacity, playbooks, and approval thresholds need strengthening before relying on him for longer absences.
- Ensure critical security escalation has a bypass mechanism. Mateo’s filtering reduces false positives, but critical cases should not wait behind low-risk triage.
- Consider shift alignment: Chen and Ivan provide late-shift technical/quality presence, while Dina, Julia, Fatima, Hana, and Erik are day-shift. Nora’s 24/7 intake may create waiting queues outside specialist coverage periods.

## Training and resilience

- Train temporary agents on entitlement-note completeness and require sampled QA before expanding their scope. The 17 reopen cases are a concrete feedback signal.
- Cross-train R07 in pricing policy and escalation criteria, but define the commercial limits he can handle independently.
- Create a documented backup plan for Hana’s approval role. Mateo can perform preliminary review, but final authority, recheck timing, and emergency delegation must be explicit.
- Build an on-call or secondary-review arrangement for Erik’s security role. Critical security coverage should not depend on one person plus informal filtering.
- Use Chen’s higher-complexity work as a mentoring and knowledge-transfer opportunity for Dina, without assuming that similar activity counts imply equal critical-case readiness.
- Preserve Ivan and Julia as quality and knowledge-governance capacity. Their work reduces recurrence risk and should not be treated as expendable overhead.

## Risk control and performance monitoring

Monitor outcomes by role, complexity, and substitution status:

- Reopen rate and entitlement-note completeness for frontline and temporary work.
- Pricing review duration, approval rework, and customer outcomes for Fatima and Giorgio.
- Critical-case waiting time, security-review backlog, false-escalation rate, and missed-escalation rate for Erik and Mateo.
- A9 recheck rate and approval cycle time for Hana and Mateo.
- Defect escape rate, QA turnaround, and knowledge-base update coverage for Ivan and Julia.
- Service and waiting time by complexity rather than aggregated across all cases.

The process model should also distinguish automated precheck, human validation, preliminary approval, and final approval. Without those distinctions, mining will repeatedly overstate authority for bots, coordinators, and shadow workers.

## Cautions when acting on mined roles

1. **Do not reorganize, promote, or reduce staff based only on discovered clusters.** Activity patterns reflect current assignments, shift coverage, automation, temporary absences, and logging conventions. They do not independently prove competence, authorization, or managerial accountability.

2. **Do not treat substitution as proof of permanent interchangeability.** Giorgio’s commercial cover and Mateo’s preliminary approvals were useful, but both generated evidence of non-equivalence: longer pricing duration and substantial supervisory rechecking.

3. **Do not use low volume to remove specialist roles.** Erik’s security work is low-volume relative to routine service, but it is disproportionately critical and risk-bearing.

4. **Do not interpret network centrality automatically as excess staffing or a bottleneck to eliminate.** Mateo may be a necessary escalation broker. Before changing the role, verify whether centrality reflects value-adding coordination, missing workflow automation, poor routing rules, or an unsafe concentration of decision power.
