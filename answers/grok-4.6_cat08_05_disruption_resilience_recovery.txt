# 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 (Day −30 to −1) — Baseline, controlled high-volume flow.** The warehouse ran the normative path at scale (42,000 cases): Receive → Validate authorization → Inventory reservation → Pick → Pack → Quality/release → Dispatch → Proof of delivery → Invoice close. Fitness was 0.96 (91% fitting traces). Holds and backorders existed but were small (700 compliance holds, 900 backorder waits, 450 repack loops). Hospital-critical median 8h / p90 15h; pharmacy 23h / 49h; wholesale 36h / 72h. Control defects were rare: traceability 0.8%, missing cold-chain temperature records 0.4%, missing controlled-substance dual control 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellation 2.1%. Risk posture: low residual operational risk, high conformance.

**T1 (Day 0 morning) — Sudden WMS loss.** The warehouse management system and scanning backbone failed. The live reservation–pick–scan–release chain stopped. This is the trigger point: no live inventory truth, no scan events, no system authorization, no electronic chain of custody. Risk posture flipped from process-controlled to improvised within hours.

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2) — Manual workaround, mission-first / control-last.** 5,600 cases entered a new variant family. Almost all orders hit Emergency triage (5,100/5,600). Three forks then appeared: Manual authorization check (2,500), Skip system authorization check (1,600), and Hospital-critical fast lane (1,000). Inventory was decided from a printed morning snapshot (3,920 to Manual pick list; 580 deferred). Physical flow continued via paper/spreadsheet pick lists (3,600 packs), then split: Temporary release control (1,650) versus Dispatch without scan (1,800 from pack, plus 1,450 after temporary release). Delivery evidence moved to Phone proof of delivery (2,600) and Spreadsheet invoice hold (2,200). Noncritical work was parked (960 backlog; 320 cancelled from that queue).

Metrics show **asymmetric service protection, not general resilience**. Hospital-critical median rose only 8h→10h and fill stayed 91%. Pharmacy-standard median more than doubled (23h→54h, p90 49h→118h) and fill fell to 61%. Wholesale median 36h→96h, p90 72h→180h. Control collapse was simultaneous: traceability exceptions 0.8%→9.5%, missing cold-chain docs 0.4%→4.8%, missing dual control 0.2%→2.9%, rework 3.5%→12.5%, cancellations 2.1%→7.8%. Fitness 0.71; only 44% of traces fit the normative model. Move-on-log: skip authorization, dispatch without scan, phone PoD, spreadsheet invoice hold. Move-on-model: missing quality/release, missing scan, missing PoD, missing temperature record. Risk posture: **clinical supply partially protected; compliance and traceability debt accumulating faster than operations can see it.**

**T3 (Day 3 to Day 6) — Partial recovery, hybrid and still leaky.** Order intake returned; inventory sync and scanning remained unreliable. 11,400 cases. Cycle times improved but did not normalize (hospital 9h / 18h; pharmacy 38h / 90h; wholesale 84h / 160h). Defect rates halved from T2 but remained multiples of baseline: traceability 5.4%, cold-chain 2.3%, dual-control 1.4%, rework 9.0%, cancel 5.2%. Fitness 0.82; 59% fitting traces. Dominant reality: electronic path plus leftover manual residues. Risk posture: operational optimism; residual stale-inventory and reconstruction error.

**T4 (Day 7 to Day 21) — Stabilization with a hidden compliance tail.** 35,000 cases. Hospital median returned to 8h (p90 16h, still slightly worse than 15h). Pharmacy 28h / 74h and wholesale 58h / 120h remained structurally slower than T0. Control rates improved but did not close: traceability 2.2% (still ~3× baseline), cold-chain 0.9%, dual-control 0.5%, rework 5.8%, cancel 3.0%. Fitness 0.90; 76% fitting. The DFG reveals the real recovery work: Backlog prioritization (1,500), Reconcile manual reservation (1,400; 300 then cancelled), Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200), Invoice close → Audit remediation (2,300). Cycle-time dashboards looked “almost recovered” while 2,300 cases still sat in after-the-fact evidence repair. Temporal profile: Invoice close → Audit remediation created a long compliance tail. Risk posture: **operations declared stable; regulated evidence was not.**

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

Resilience, in this process, is **preserving the mission under degraded conditions while keeping a minimum, contemporaneous control set for the risk that the product actually carries**. Concealed noncompliance is **keeping the physical and commercial flow looking intact while the evidence that would prove legality, identity, temperature, or authorization is missing, assumed, or invented later**.

The T2 metrics make the distinction empirical, not moral. Hospital-critical median cycle time barely moved (8h→10h) and fill stayed 91%. That is real adaptive capacity: Emergency triage + Hospital-critical fast lane + Defer noncritical order reallocated scarce pick, pack, and compliance time to the customer class whose failure is clinically intolerable. Manual authorization check and Temporary release control are the same idea applied to control quality rather than speed: they spend scarce expert time where the statute and the patient risk are highest. Backlog prioritization, reservation reconciliation, and a bounded audit factory are resilience in recovery time — they re-impose order after improvisation.

They are not resilience if the protected outcome is only a dashboard. Dispatch without scan (1,800 + 1,450) and Skip system authorization (1,600) kept cases moving and therefore protected throughput statistics. They also produced the T2 fitness collapse (0.96→0.71; 91%→44% fitting traces) and the 9.5% traceability exception rate. Phone proof of delivery recovered *a* signal of delivery, but the temporal profile of Dispatch without scan → Phone PoD is highly variable, and a phone call does not identify a controlled-substance recipient. Invoice close → Audit remediation on 2,300 T4 cases is the accounting identity of concealed noncompliance: the operational case is already “done,” cash is in motion, and the missing scan, temperature record, or authorization is being reconstructed when memories and paper have decayed. After-the-fact remediation can reduce inspection exposure. It cannot convert a shipment that left without a temperature trace into a shipment that was temperature-controlled.

**True resilience (keep and formalize):** B Manual authorization check; I Defer noncritical order (if rules are explicit); J Backlog prioritization (if rules are explicit); K Reconcile manual reservation; A Hospital-critical fast lane *as priority*, not as an authorization waiver; F Temporary release control *for controlled substances*.

**Risky shortcuts (retire or cage):** C Skip system authorization check; G Dispatch without scan as an unlogged handoff; D Manual inventory snapshot after the snapshot is stale; H Phone PoD used as sufficient evidence for controlled or cold-chain product; L treated as equivalent to contemporaneous quality/release.

**Ambiguous, depends on risk appetite:** E Manual pick lists (necessary for physical continuity, noncompliant if identity is not captured the same shift); A when the fast lane precedes *any* eligibility evidence; F when cold-chain is only sampled; H for ambient hospital shipments where a named clinician confirms receipt; I if pharmacy starvation is accepted for 48–72 hours with notice, but not if it becomes silent cancellation (T2 cancel 7.8%, 320 from backlog).

The governing rule from the log is simple: **a deviation that protects hospital fill while substituting a weaker but contemporaneous control (offline list, pharmacist release, paper chain of custody) is resilience. A deviation that protects cycle time by deleting the control and promising to write it back later is concealed noncompliance.** T4’s persistent audit queue after “stabilization” is the proof that the second pattern was used at scale.

# 4. Recovery process model

The redesigned recovery process is a **declared operating mode**, not an unofficial variant soup. Use a mode machine with hard triggers, then a risk-stratified happy path. Notation below is a process tree: `→` sequence, `×` exclusive choice, `∧` and-split (all children required), `↺` loop, `*` optional.

## Trigger conditions (enter / stay / exit)

**Enter Incident Mode (IM)** when any of these is true:
- WMS, scan backbone, or inventory reservation service unavailable > 30 minutes; or
- scan-to-dispatch success rate < 95% for 1 hour; or
- authorization service unavailable and electronic hold/release cannot be written; or
- confirmed or suspected integrity compromise of inventory or customer-master data.

**Stay in IM** until *all* exit conditions hold:
- live reservation and scan path restored for ≥ 4 consecutive hours; and
- snapshot age = 0 (live system is source of truth); and
- open “missing contemporaneous control” tickets for controlled and cold-chain are either closed or owned with a dated plan; and
- reservation reconciliation backlog age p90 < 24h.

**Do not exit** merely because intake is up (that was T3) or median hospital cycle time is back to 8h (that was T4).

## Top-level process tree

```
IM =
  Receive order
  → Emergency triage
  → ( × Hospital-critical fast lane
      × Standard incident path
      × Defer / backlog )
  → Risk-minimum control pack      // depends on customer × product
  → Inventory truth step           // snapshot only if fresh
  → Manual pick with identity capture
  → Pack
  → Release gate                   // not optional for high-risk
  → Dispatch with chain of custody
  → Delivery evidence
  → Financial close with evidence status
  → ( * Reconciliation & audit case )
```

## Triage rules (Emergency triage)

Score every order on two axes. Customer: Hospital-critical > Pharmacy-standard > Wholesale-bulk. Product: Controlled-substance = Cold-chain = Recall-sensitive > Ambient. Combine:

|  | Hospital-critical | Pharmacy-standard | Wholesale-bulk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled / cold-chain / recall-sensitive | **P0** — fast lane, full minimum controls, never skip | **P1** — process today, full minimum controls | **P2** — process if capacity remains after P0/P1 |
| Ambient | **P0/P1** — fast lane allowed; authorization may be offline but *must exist* | **P2** — process after P0/P1 | **P3** — defer, time-boxed |

Rules that must be written, not tribal:
- P0 never waits behind P2/P3. This is A, formalized.
- P3 deferral expires at 48h (wholesale) / 24h (pharmacy ambient). At expiry: ship, obtain a documented customer delay agreement, or cancel with notice. This stops T2’s silent 7.8% cancellation pattern.
- No skip-authorization path for new customers, credit holds, or any non-ambient product. C is deleted as a default variant.
- Capacity that B (manual authorization) and F (pharmacist release) cannot cover is absorbed by deferral (I), not by skipping the control (C).

## Minimum controls by product / customer risk

These replace “Temporary release control samples some orders.” They are the only legal IM happy path.

**All orders**
- Written or photographed order identity (customer, SKU, qty, lot/serial if present).
- Named worker IDs on pick, pack, release, dispatch.
- Inventory truth: live system if up; else snapshot **plus snapshot timestamp**. Snapshot older than 8 hours cannot authorize a pick without a bin-level physical confirm.
- Evidence ticket opened *now* if any required event cannot be written to the system. Do not wait for invoice close.

**Hospital-critical, any product**
- Eligibility: system check if up; else Manual authorization check (B) *before* dispatch. Fast lane may start pick in parallel but may not dispatch without an eligibility result.
- Delivery: named receiving clinician or pharmacy; phone PoD allowed for ambient only, with call log, name, role, time.

**Controlled-substance, any customer**
- Dual control contemporaneous (two people or pharmacist + second witness).
- No skip-authorization. No phone-only PoD. No dispatch without a custody pack (seal number, recipient ID, photo or signed paper).
- Temporary release control is **100%**, not sampled.

**Cold-chain, any customer**
- Temperature evidence at pack and at handoff (logger reading, go/no-go, or validated pack-out photo + pack-out time).
- F must include this; sampling is not allowed.
- Phone PoD may confirm arrival but does not replace the temperature record.

**Recall-sensitive**
- Recall-status check against the last known list, timestamped. Stale snapshot alone is insufficient.

**Ambient + known customer + Pharmacy/Wholesale**
- Offline standing-authorization list permitted if the list is dated, versioned, and the checker is a trained authorizer (B-lite).
- Dispatch without scan permitted only with photo of carton labels + courier manifest line.
- This is the *only* place a degraded authorization path survives, and it is logged as an exception, not as a normal event.

## Manual evidence capture (replace reconstruct-later)

At each physical step, capture a **minimum evidence object** onto a preprinted or offline-app form:

1. **Pick:** SKU, lot/serial, qty, bin, picker ID, photo of product label, snapshot version used.
2. **Pack:** carton ID, packer ID, temperature pack-out if cold-chain, seal number if controlled.
3. **Release:** releaser ID, checklist result, holds.
4. **Dispatch:** courier, manifest line, photo of loaded carton IDs, dispatcher ID. This retires naked G.
5. **Delivery:** recipient name/role, time, method (signed paper / photo / phone). Method constrained by product risk as above.

Same-shift (not T4) reconstruction job: scan or photograph the evidence object into the case by end of shift. That turns E from a traceability hole into a delayed-write.

## Backlog prioritization

When exiting the darkest part of IM (T3-like state):

```
Backlog prioritization →
  rank by (customer class, product risk, age, remaining expiry)
  → × Validate authorization → Inventory reservation → ...
    × Customer-notified cancel (only if expiry, recall, or customer agrees)
    × Hold for evidence completion (if product already shipped)
```

Age-without-priority is how wholesale p90 hit 180h. Priority-without-age is how pharmacy fill fell to 61% and stayed damaged into T4 (28h / 74h vs 23h / 49h). Use a published matrix plus an age cap: any P2 older than 72h escalates to a named owner.

## Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

Split what T4 conflated.

**K — Reservation reconciliation (operational, before promising more stock)**
```
Reconcile manual reservation =
  match paper picks and dispatches to system stock
  → × confirm and write reservation
    × adjust stock and continue
    × cannot prove stock → cancel or repurchase with QA
  → only then Pick on the live system
```
Exit criterion: p90 age of unreconciled T2/T3 reservations < 24h. T4’s 1,400 reconcile / 300 cancel volume should be planned staffing, not surprise.

**L — Regulated-evidence case (compliance, not a post-invoice junk drawer)**
Open at the moment of deviation (missing scan, missing temperature, missing dual control, missing authorization, phone-only PoD on a restricted product). Status travels *with* the case:

```
Financial close =
  × Evidence complete → Invoice close → END
  × Evidence incomplete → Invoice on “compliance hold”
      → Evidence completion
      → × close
        × disclose / notify / recall decision
```

Audit remediation may still exist for residual defects, but it is no longer the default home of 2,300 already-invoiced cases. After-the-fact reconstruction is allowed only to *attach contemporaneous artifacts* (the photo taken at pick, the call log taken at delivery). Creating a temperature record that was never observed is forbidden.

# 5. Resilience indicators

Measure two clocks: **service clock** (did the patient/customer get product?) and **evidence clock** (was the required control true at the time of the act?). T4 failed by watching only the first.

## Leading (hours to 1–2 days; use to stay in or tighten IM)

- **P0 fill rate, rolling 6h** — hospital-critical lines fully shipped. Target ≥ 90% during IM (T2 achieved 91%; that is the floor, not a stretch).
- **Control-complete dispatch share, rolling 6h** — dispatches that have the minimum control pack for their product class. Target ≥ 95% for controlled and cold-chain; ≥ 85% for ambient. A rising G/H share is a leading failure.
- **Snapshot age at pick** — median and p90. If p90 > 8h, freeze non-P0 picks until a count refresh.
- **Authorizer and pharmacist utilization** — queue time for B and F. If wait > 30 minutes, defer P2/P3 (I); do not open C.
- **Same-shift evidence capture rate** — share of manual picks with lot/serial photo uploaded before shift end. Leading indicator of whether E will become T4 archaeology.
- **Backlog age by class** — p50/p90 of deferred pharmacy vs wholesale vs hospital. Detects unfair I/J in real time.
- **Scan / reservation service health** — the T1 trigger metrics themselves.

## Lagging (days to weeks; use to refuse a false “we’re recovered”)

- **Throughput and cycle time by segment** — median and p90 vs T0: hospital 8h/15h, pharmacy 23h/49h, wholesale 36h/72h. T4 pharmacy 28h/74h and wholesale 58h/120h mean recovery was incomplete.
- **Service protection** — fill rate by customer class; hospital vs pharmacy gap. A 91% / 61% split is a fairness event, not a success.
- **Traceability exception rate** — T0 0.8%; T2 9.5%; T4 still 2.2%. Recovery declaration requires ≤ 1.2% or a dated close plan.
- **Control completeness** — missing cold-chain temperature (T0 0.4% / T2 4.8% / T4 0.9%) and missing dual control (0.2% / 2.9% / 0.5%). These are not interchangeable with traceability.
- **Rework / correction** — T0 3.5%; T2 12.5%; T4 5.8%. Persistent rework means the happy path is still dirty.
- **Cancellation rate and cancel reason mix** — T0 2.1%; T2 7.8%. Separate customer-requested, expiry/recall, and “we ran out of capacity.”
- **Backlog age and fairness** — distribution of wait by customer group; 450 T4 cancels from backlog prioritization must be explainable by the published matrix.
- **Hidden workload** — count and age of open reservation-reconcile cases (K) and regulated-evidence cases (L). **Operational recovery is false while Invoice close → Audit remediation is still a fat edge.** Target: evidence-case p90 age < 5 days; no invoicing of incomplete high-risk cases.
- **Conformance** — fitness and percent fitting traces vs the *incident-mode* model, not only the peacetime model. Peacetime fitness 0.96/91% is the exit target; IM should be measured against the designed IM tree so that B/E/F are conforming, while C/G are still deviations.
- **Fairness index** — pharmacy fill / hospital fill, and pharmacy p90 / hospital p90. T2 ≈ 61/91 and 118/21 are the warning signature.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

Prioritized. Effort is relative (S/M/L). Benefit and residual risk assume the T0–T4 pattern repeats.

**P1. Pre-authorize an Incident Mode playbook with the control packs in Section 4.**  
Effort: M (policy, SOP, training, offline forms/app).  
Benefit: Converts A/B/F/I from improvisation into a conforming variant; stops C and naked G from becoming the default DFG. Expected to cut T2-like traceability exceptions well below 9.5% without giving up hospital fill near 91%.  
Residual risk: Playbook not executed under stress; needs drills.

**P2. Pre-build the offline evidence kit (pick/pack/release/dispatch forms, photo workflow, versioned authorization and inventory lists).**  
Effort: M.  
Benefit: Makes E and D safe for a defined window; enables same-shift reconstruction instead of 2,300-case T4 tails.  
Residual risk: Paper still lost; mitigate with phone photos to a hardened inbox.

**P3. Staff a surge bench for manual authorization and pharmacist release; never staff throughput by deleting that bench.**  
Effort: M–L (cross-train, on-call roster, decision rights).  
Benefit: B and F remain available so C is unnecessary. Directly attacks the 2.9% dual-control and 4.8% temperature holes.  
Residual risk: Surge staff still saturate; then I must shed P3, not skip controls.

**P4. Time-box inventory snapshots and require bin confirmation after expiry.**  
Effort: S–M.  
Benefit: Prevents D from rotting into wrong-stock shipments and phantom reservations that later cancel (300 in K).  
Residual risk: Physical counts are slow; accept that P3 volume dies before inventory truth does.

**P5. Forbid unlogged dispatch and phone-only PoD for controlled and cold-chain.**  
Effort: S (rule) + M (custody pack, courier clause).  
Benefit: Removes the largest move-on-log deviation and the variable Dispatch → Phone PoD delay as a control substitute.  
Residual risk: Some hospital ambient delays of minutes to hours; acceptable against the T2 hospital p90 of 21h.

**P6. Publish deferral and backlog rules; notify pharmacies when they are P2/P3.**  
Effort: S.  
Benefit: Keeps I/J as resilience rather than informal rationing; should reduce franchise damage from the 61% pharmacy fill and 7.8% cancels.  
Residual risk: Transparent bad news still loses orders; better than silent cancellation.

**P7. Split K and L from “cycle time recovered.” Add evidence status to the case, and do not invoice high-risk incompletes.**  
Effort: M (case states, finance policy, staffing a reconcile/evidence cell).  
Benefit: Stops the T4 illusion: hospital 8h median while 2,300 audit-remediation cases and 1,400 reconciles are still open. Forces contemporaneous artifacts.  
Residual risk: Cash delay; smaller than recall, license, or hospital-harm risk.

**P8. Drill the mode twice a year with a WMS-dark tabletop and a 4-hour live paper run on a non-controlled subset.**  
Effort: M.  
Benefit: Fitness in the next T2 should be measured against the IM model and stay high because the variant is known.  
Residual risk: Drill ≠ cyber chaos; still the only way to keep B/F from being invented on Day 0.

**P9. Instrument the leading indicators in Section 5 on a 6-hour board visible to operations and compliance together.**  
Effort: S–M.  
Benefit: Detects the T2 signature (hospital OK, pharmacy collapsing, G/H rising, snapshot aging) while it is still reversible.  
Residual risk: Gaming if only hospital fill is rewarded; pair with control-complete dispatch.

**P10. After-action legal/QA review of T2 skip-authorization and missing temperature/dual-control cases; do not rely on L as if it were contemporaneous compliance.**  
Effort: M.  
Benefit: Quantifies residual patient-safety and license exposure already sitting in the 5,600 T2 cases.  
Residual risk: Some gaps cannot be repaired; disclosure may be required. That is a feature.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** a designed degradation: hospital-critical fill near 90%+, slower pharmacy and wholesale service, explicit time-boxed deferrals, paper-and-photo evidence, and a visible reconciliation cell that continues after trucks are rolling. That is the T2 lesson that actually worked (hospital 8h→10h, fill 91%) and the T4 lesson that was under-funded (audit and reservation tails). Management should **not accept** throughput or “median cycle time recovered” as proof of resilience when authorization is assumed (1,600 skips), product leaves without a custody event (1,800+1,450 un-scanned dispatches), cold-chain temperature or dual control is missing at multiples of baseline (4.8% and 2.9% in T2; still 0.9% and 0.5% in T4), or invoice close precedes evidence completion (2,300 post-close remediations). Those are concealed noncompliance with a delayed labor bill, not recovery. Change the recommendation only if new evidence shows that (1) a live degraded system can write contemporaneous authorization, lot/serial, temperature, and dual-control events at ≥ 95% for high-risk SKUs without the paper kit, or (2) the next incident’s leading board shows control-complete dispatch and same-shift evidence capture staying above target while hospital fill holds — in which case IM can be thinner. Until then, prefer a slower, fully evidenced hospital-first mode over a fast, reconstruct-later warehouse.
