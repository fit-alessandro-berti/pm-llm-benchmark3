## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

No repair below is presented as uniquely determined. Confidence values express relative support, not calibrated probabilities established from frequencies.

**Hard invariants**

- **Referential integrity:** An E2O addition must reference an existing event and object; an O2O addition must use existing objects, the stated direction, and a qualifier already meaningful in the log. No new event, object, timestamp, or attribute may be invented.
- **Uniqueness in the selected bundle:** The same event–object–qualifier or source–qualifier–target tuple should not be duplicated.
- **`Record Payment` cardinality:** Rule 7 makes exactly one Payment mandatory. Both `ev13` and `ev14` already satisfy this. This does **not** make an Invoice mandatory, because unapplied cash is explicitly possible.
- **Temporal possibility:** Packing should not follow dispatch, dispatch should not follow delivery confirmation, and payment should not precede the payment object’s recording context. All candidate repairs considered remain temporally possible, so chronology rarely distinguishes them decisively here.

**Strong defaults**

- `Pack Item`, `Dispatch Package`, and `Record Payment` ordinarily have their customary Item/Package, Package/Delivery, or Payment/Invoice participants. A missing customary participant is evidence of incompleteness, although rule 3 and rule 7 allow exceptions.
- An existing, otherwise unused object whose stable identifier almost exactly matches an event identifier is strong evidence: `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421` against `ev10.external_ref=N-421?`.
- Lifecycle continuity is strong evidence. For example, `PK-8` is packed and dispatched but, without `DEL-Y`, has no carrier; `DEL-Y` conversely has no lifecycle event.
- The existing O2O graph is strong evidence about intended object groupings: `ORD-A contains IT-A1/IT-A2`, `ORD-B contains IT-B1`, and `PK-8 packs IT-A2`.

**Weak heuristics**

- Exact amount decomposition—here `130 + 100 = 230`—supports, but does not prove, that `INV-44` bills both orders or that both payments settle it.
- Equal customer, route, timestamp, identifier prefix, or partially matching references are corroborating heuristics. `N-42*` intentionally matches both carrier references and therefore has little discriminating force.
- Identifier substrings in scanner references are weaker than stable object attributes. `PK8-2` resembles `PK-8`, but could be a scanner, sequence, or erroneous prefix.
- Parallel timing, such as both dispatches occurring at 10:00, supports consolidation only weakly; distinct concurrent deliveries are equally plausible.

**Evidence against a repair**

- A repair that mixes previously separate object groups needs additional independent support. Adding `IT-B1` to `PK-8` would mix items from `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`.
- An edge inferred only from another proposed edge receives no independent confirmation from that edge. This matters especially for proposed O2O edges derived from proposed E2O edges.
- Leaving an object orphaned is evidence against a repair but is not itself proof that the missing relation must take a particular target. `DEL-Y`, `PAY-2`, and `ORD-B`’s incomplete downstream lifecycle may reflect genuine partial capture.

Evidence is combined by requiring convergence across kinds: identifier, time, lifecycle, cardinality, customer/route, amount, and existing O2O structure. Identifier plus lifecycle generally outweighs amount alone; amount plus uniqueness of the available Invoice is stronger than amount alone but still defeasible.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A accepts P03 and P05, thereby connecting the otherwise orphaned `DEL-Y` to the dispatched `PK-8`. It provisionally accepts P09–P12 because customer, uniqueness of the invoice, timing, lifecycle defaults, and amount decomposition converge, while explicitly retaining uncertainty. It leaves P01, P02, and all `ev11` delivery/order expansions unresolved.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** No addition. `IT-B1` remains packed by an event but has no accepted package. This preserves the observed order separation at the cost of an incomplete Item lifecycle.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Add `ev10–DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. Do not add DEL-X. DEL-X and DEL-Y remain distinct simultaneous deliveries.
- **ev11 and deliveries:** Keep only the observed `ORD-A` relation. Neither delivery is uniquely or jointly confirmed in the repaired graph.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Provisionally add both the E2O and O2O billing relations.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Provisionally add both relations, while retaining unapplied cash as an alternative explanation.

This yields mostly coherent lifecycles: orders are created and populated; PK-7 and PK-8 are dispatched through separate deliveries; one invoice plausibly covers both C17 orders; and both payments plausibly settle it. The unresolved confirmation and IT-B1 packing are intentional gaps, not defects to be hidden.

### Bundle B: consolidated-fulfilment completion

Bundle B interprets the case as a consolidated C17 shipment and billing episode. It accepts P01–P03, P05, and P09–P13.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Add `ev08–PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. PK-8 becomes a mixed-order package containing IT-A2 and IT-B1.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** As in Bundle A, assign PK-8 to DEL-Y rather than collapsing both packages into DEL-X.
- **ev11 and deliveries:** Treat the wildcard portal acknowledgement as jointly confirming DEL-X and DEL-Y, and add ORD-B as an acknowledged order.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Accept billing for both orders.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Accept PAY-2 as the second settlement payment.

This is internally coherent: both same-customer orders are packed, dispatched along NORTH routes, jointly acknowledged, jointly invoiced, and fully paid. Its weakness is cumulative inference. Several individually plausible completions reinforce one another narratively, but they are not independent observations. In particular, the apparent elegance of “everything belongs to one consolidated episode” may be manufactured by the repairs.

### Bundle C: split-process sceptical interpretation

Bundle C accepts only the DEL-Y assignment and rejects the completion narrative elsewhere.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Leave unresolved; `PK8-2` may be scanner metadata or an unknown package that cannot be invented.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Assign to DEL-Y, as in A and B.
- **ev11:** Treat it as an order-level portal confirmation for ORD-A only; no delivery attribution.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Do not add. The 230 total could include charges associated primarily with ORD-A or another unrecorded component.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave PAY-2 unapplied or intended for a missing/external invoice.

Bundle C produces more orphans but avoids manufacturing convergence. It is useful as a sensitivity baseline, especially for financial conclusions.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A has creation, item additions, invoice, payment context indirectly through INV-44, and an order-level delivery confirmation. ORD-B has creation and item addition; Bundle A provisionally adds billing, but no accepted packing, dispatch, or confirmation completion. This asymmetry is plausible if capture is incomplete. Bundle B closes ORD-B much more aggressively through the mixed package and joint confirmation.

**Item.** IT-A1 follows Order  Package PK-7  Delivery DEL-X. IT-A2 follows Order  PK-8  DEL-Y under Bundle A. IT-B1 is added to ORD-B and appears in a Pack Item event, but Bundle A gives it no package. That is an orphan lifecycle stage, expressly allowed as a scanner-outage consequence. Bundle B completes it by making PK-8 contain items from two orders—a suspicious cross-order one-to-many package unless mixed packing is operationally normal.

**Package.** PK-7 is complete through dispatch and delivery-carrier assignment, although its delivery confirmation is unresolved. PK-8 becomes similarly dispatched under Bundle A. Bundle B additionally increases PK-8’s contents from one item to two. No package has a many-delivery relation in Bundle A; Bundle B also preserves one delivery per package.

**Delivery.** DEL-X carries PK-7. DEL-Y changes from a total orphan in the unrepaired data to carrying PK-8. Neither has an accepted Confirm Delivery event relation. That absence is preferable to arbitrarily assigning the wildcard event. Bundle B gives both deliveries the same confirmation event, creating a many-deliveries-to-one-event pattern that rule 5 permits but does not establish.

**Invoice.** INV-44 already bills ORD-A. Bundle A provisionally makes it bill two orders and receive two payment settlements. Both one-to-many patterns are semantically plausible for a shared-customer consolidated invoice, but neither should be treated as independently verified merely because the amounts partition neatly.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is complete. PAY-2 has a Record Payment event and bank reference; Bundle A provisionally attaches it to INV-44. If that is wrong, PAY-2 is an example of unapplied cash rather than a data error requiring repair. Payment objects each retain exactly one recording event.

**Event lifecycle.** No event is invented and no event count changes. `ev08` remains a plausible item-only exception in Bundle A; `ev11` remains order-only, also explicitly possible under rule 5. Orphan events in the strict sense do not arise because every event retains at least one observed object.

**Independent support and circularity.** `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is mostly a structural restatement of the proposed `ev10–DEL-Y` relation plus the existing pattern `DEL-X carries PK-7`; it must not then be cited as independent proof of the E2O edge. Likewise, `INV-44 bills ORD-B` follows from the proposed invoice interpretation and does not independently corroborate `ev12–ORD-B`. The amount evidence, customer evidence, and lifecycle evidence support the bundle as a whole, but E2O/O2O pairs derived together count as one evidentiary chain, not two.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log.** DEL-Y is isolated, PK-8 is dispatched without a Delivery, IT-B1’s packing lacks a Package, ORD-B disappears after item addition, and PAY-2 is financially unattached. Delivery-flattened behavior shows DEL-X progressing from Dispatch Package onward only insofar as other DEL-X events exist; in fact, no Confirm Delivery event is attached to either delivery. Package flattening shows PK-8 reaching Dispatch Package but no delivery context.

**OC-DFG.** Bundle A adds Delivery participation to `ev10`, so the Delivery object-type path gains a Dispatch Package occurrence for DEL-Y and strengthens Package–Delivery co-occurrence around dispatch. It does not create a Delivery Confirm Delivery edge because `ev11` remains unattributed. Bundle B additionally attaches `ev08` to PK-8 and `ev11` to both deliveries. This creates or strengthens Item/Package paths through the mixed PK-8 and a shared Confirm Delivery node in both delivery lifecycles. Bundle B may therefore suggest synchronization between DEL-X and DEL-Y where the data only contain a wildcard.

**OTG.** Bundle A adds a `DEL-Y –carries PK-8` interaction and provisional `INV-44 –bills ORD-B` and `PAY-2 –settles INV-44` interactions. Bundle B adds `PK-8 –packs IT-B1`, directly connecting the two order subgraphs through a package. That can transform apparently separate order processes into one interacting fulfilment network.

**ET–OT graph.** Bundle A increases incidence of Dispatch Package–Delivery, Issue Invoice–Order, and Record Payment–Invoice. Bundle B further increases Pack Item–Package and Confirm Delivery–Delivery/Order incidence. These are frequency changes in event-type/object-type involvement, not discoveries of new activities.

**Flattenings.**  
- **Order:** Bundle A’s provisional invoice edge makes `ev12` appear in ORD-B’s flattened trace; `ev14` may also be propagated if flattening follows only direct E2O relations, depending on implementation semantics, but invoice-mediated payment should not automatically be treated as a direct order event. Bundle B’s mixed package and joint confirmation can place packing, dispatch, and confirmation behavior into both order traces, duplicating shared events.  
- **Package:** Bundle B gives PK-8 two Pack Item events and potentially a confirmation path through either delivery; Bundle A retains one pack event.  
- **Delivery:** Bundle A gives DEL-Y a dispatch lifecycle; Bundle B gives both deliveries the same confirmation event, duplicating `ev11` across flattened traces.  
- **Invoice:** Bundle A and B make INV-44 span both orders and both payments. Invoice flattening may consequently merge two order histories.  
- **Payment:** Bundle A/B attach PAY-2 to an invoice event context; Bundle C leaves its trace limited to its own recording event.

**Frequencies.** Event-based activity frequencies do not change because no events are added: there remain, for example, three Pack Item and two Record Payment events. Unique-object and total-object counts do change. Dispatch Package involves two unique Deliveries under Bundles A/B rather than one; Record Payment involves one unique Invoice under A/B if P11 is accepted; Pack Item involves two unique Packages under Bundle B rather than one observed package plus an unresolved case. Analysts must state which frequency semantics a tool displays.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Bundle A makes ORD-B look more similar to ORD-A at billing/payment stages while retaining distinct fulfilment evidence. Bundle B can make the orders appear to share package, dispatch, and confirmation executions, potentially clustering them as equivalent consolidated executions. That equivalence may be entirely repair-induced.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Wildcard forced to one carrier:** Assigning `ev11` only to DEL-X because ORD-A is already present would manufacture a complete DEL-X lifecycle and leave DEL-Y artificially incomplete, despite `N-42*` matching both.

2. **Scanner-prefix overinterpretation:** Treating `PK8-2` as conclusive would create a mixed-order PK-8. Subsequent analysis could falsely report consolidated packing, shared delays, and cross-order dependencies.

3. **Arithmetic treated as identity:** Using `130 + 100 = 230` as proof would merge ORD-B into INV-44 and both payments into one settlement story, hiding fees, discounts, partial billing, or coincidence.

4. **Circular graph completion:** Inferring `DEL-Y carries PK-8` from `ev10–DEL-Y`, then citing that O2O edge as confirmation of the dispatch assignment, double-counts one inference and exaggerates confidence.

5. **Lifecycle-completion bias:** Giving every Item a package, every Package a delivery, every Delivery a confirmation, and every Payment an invoice removes genuine exceptions such as scanner outages, portal identifier loss, and unapplied cash.

6. **Bulk-event duplication:** Attaching `ev11` to both deliveries and both orders duplicates one real-world acknowledgement across several flattened traces. Case counts and directly-follows edges may then suggest repeated confirmations.

7. **Bulk-dispatch collapse:** Assigning PK-8 to DEL-X merely because dispatches are simultaneous would erase a plausible second delivery, orphan DEL-Y, and create a false one-delivery/two-package routing pattern.

8. **False process equivalence:** Bundle B’s shared package, invoice, payments, and confirmation could make ORD-A and ORD-B appear to be executions of one consolidated process. Clustering, conformance, and performance results would then measure the analyst’s completion assumptions as much as the observed process.
