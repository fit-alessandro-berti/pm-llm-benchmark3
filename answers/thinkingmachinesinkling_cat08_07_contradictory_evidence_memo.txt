5. Value-of-information analysis

Ranked by decision value—i.e., which uncertainty, if resolved, most changes whether and how we scale, suspend, or redesign.

**1. M4 — Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after billing start.**  
*Uncertainty:* Is the redesign truly cheaper, or are hidden complaint/revisit costs erasing the EUR 23 visible saving?  
*Decision impact:* If full-loaded cost exceeds baseline significantly, we must suspend or restructure the redesign regardless of median speed. If it is at or below baseline for safe segments, it supports selective scaling.

**2. M6 — Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes.**  
*Uncertainty:* Is the survey-skip rule salvageable with threshold tuning, or is it fundamentally unreliable for medium-risk and older buildings?  
*Decision impact:* If false-positive skip rates are high across risk strata, kill the rule permanently. If it works only for a narrow, well-defined low-risk group, restrict it there.

**3. M1 — Randomized pilot of survey skipping by verified building-risk stratum.**  
*Uncertainty:* What is the causal effect of skipping surveys on FTR, second visit, and complaint within each segment?  
*Decision impact:* Provides the causal evidence needed to either reintroduce a guarded skip rule or confirm it should never be used outside the simplest cases.

**4. M7 — Run conformance monitoring for billing-start-before-activation and missing permit checks.**  
*Uncertainty:* Are the alignment violations predictive of complaints and tail delays within days, or are they harmless?  
*Decision impact:* If violations strongly predict failure, enforce hard stops in the process platform; justifies the immediate suspension of these rules and sets a rollout gate (<1% violations).

**5. M2 — Instrument start timestamps for installation visit, activation test, and manual activation.**  
*Uncertainty:* Where exactly does time accumulate, and is the coordinator bottleneck real?  
*Decision impact:* Corrects temporal analysis (currently based on sparse start times), improves process-model precision, and identifies whether delays occur in scheduling, travel, or recovery loops.

**6. M3 — Add structured reason codes for failed visit, manual activation, complaint escalation, and second visit.**  
*Uncertainty:* Are failures driven by the redesign rules (skipped survey, premature billing) or by external factors (customer absence, building access)?  
*Decision impact:* Enables targeted fixes—e.g., if “failed visit” is dominated by “no survey done,” it confirms the skip rule is the root cause; if dominated by “customer absent,” the redesign is not at fault.

**7. M5 — Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence.**  
*Uncertainty:* Is the resilience problem structural to single ownership, or is it solvable with backup protocols?  
*Decision impact:* Determines whether the organizational model needs a shared-context mechanism, a deputy coordinator pool, or a return to partial team ownership.

**8. M8 — Collect customer experience separately for “speed to appointment” vs. “working service after installation.”**  
*Uncertainty:* Did NPS fall (+21  +17) because customers value correct installation more than speed?  
*Decision impact:* Informs messaging and trade-off framing; less urgent for the operational decision, but useful for marketing and retention strategy.

6. Final recommendation

**Decision:** Adopt **Option E — Hybrid “Safe R” with immediate suspension of high-risk rules, selective scaling, and an 8-week instrumented experiment.**

**Immediate action (this week):**
- **Pause:** The predictive survey-skip rule and the post-billing-start activation check in all pilot regions. Do not include them in any rollout package.
- **Scale:** Deploy the “Safe R” package—coordinator ownership, merged address validation/appointment scheduling, and standard activation-before-billing—to **verified simple, low-risk residential houses only**. Use complete segment/building-risk data; if classification is missing, default to baseline controls.
- **Protect:** Enforce coordinator utilization caps (<85%) and implement a shared-context backup (e.g., case notes accessible to second-visit and activation teams) to address the resilience gap revealed by the centralized working-together network.

**What to scale conditionally:**
- For apartments, old/permitted buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance installs: retain baseline rules (mandatory survey/permit verification, activation before billing) and do not apply predictive skips. Pilot coordinator ownership only if load monitoring confirms it does not exceed 85%.

**What to measure next (8-week experiment in two regions with full instrumentation):**
- Run **M4** (60-day full cost), **M6** (predictive-rule validation), **M1** (randomized skip by stratum), and **M7** (real-time conformance monitoring) as the core decision experiments.
- Add **M2** (start timestamps) and **M3** (reason codes) as operational instrumentation.
- Include **M5** (resilience/absence test) and **M8** (split NPS) as organizational and customer-experience diagnostics.

**Thresholds / evidence required to justify broader rollout beyond safe simple-house segments:**
1. FTR  80% in **all tested segments** (not just simple houses).
2. p95 cycle time  45 days for apartments and old buildings.
3. Full-loaded cost (including 60-day downstream handling)  baseline EUR 198 for each segment.
4. Overall complaint rate  6.5% and SLA breach  13%.
5. Conformance violations (billing before activation; missing permit checks) < 1%.
6. Predictive survey-skip rule, if ever reintroduced, demonstrates <10% false skip rate and no significant FTR degradation in M1/M6.
7. Coordinator utilization sustained <85% with near-zero stall rate under absence (M5).

**Why this is defensible despite incomplete evidence.** The redesign is not uniformly good or bad; it is **segment-contingent and rule-contingent.** The process-mining artifacts—high fitness/low precision, alignment violations, centralized resource networks, and sharp DFG rerouting—show exactly where the design broke: ungoverned exceptions replaced governed ones. By suspending the two rules tied directly to violations (survey skip, post-billing activation), retaining the organizational improvement for the segment that benefits, and requiring full cost and conformance validation before widening, management captures value where it is proven safe and protects customers and margins where it is not.
