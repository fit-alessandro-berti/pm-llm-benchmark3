## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 ("Prepare_Payment"  "Release_Payment") can be broken down into multiple control objectives and corresponding threat scenarios:

1. **Single-User Authorization Control (Preventive)**
   - **Objective**: Ensure that no single person can both initiate and authorize a payment.
   - **Related Controls**: MG-4 (independent manager approval), ED-1 (emergency single-operator permission).

2. **Duplicate and Sanctions Detection (Detective)**
   - **Objective**: Detect attempts to process duplicate or sanctioned payments.
   - **Related Controls**: AV-2 (automated validation before release).

3. **Financial Statement Accuracy and Transaction Integrity (Detective/Preventive)**
   - **Objective**: Ensure that recorded payments match underlying invoices and that the payment process is not compromised by fraud or error.
   - **Related Controls**: RA-7 (retrospective audit and recall), MG-4 (independent manager review).

4. **Emergency Payment Authorization (Preventive/Detective)**
   - **Objective**: Allow for urgent payments while maintaining some level of oversight and control.
   - **Related Controls**: ED-1 (emergency single-operator permission).

5. **Audit and Recall Capability (Detective/Corrective)**
   - **Objective**: Enable retrospective review and recovery of payments if defects are identified.
   - **Related Controls**: RA-7 (retrospective audit and recall).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

To evaluate whether a compensating control package is fully equivalent, partially equivalent, merely mitigating, or ineffective, consider the following structured test:

1. **Timing**:
   - **Preventive Controls**: Must be enacted before the high-risk event (e.g., ED-1 must be active before release).
   - **Detective Controls**: Must operate within the acceptable time window (e.g., RA-7 audit must occur within one business day of release).
   - **Corrective Controls**: Must be capable of acting promptly after detection (e.g., RA-7 must be initiated quickly).

2. **Independence**:
   - Controls must be performed by different human resources or automated systems to prevent collusion or a single point of failure.

3. **Coverage**:
   - Controls must address all relevant scenarios related to the control objective (e.g., AV-2 must cover duplicate detection, bank-account changes, and amount limits).

4. **Overrideability**:
   - Preventive controls should limit or prevent overrides by higher-level managers or automated overrides that bypass the intended checks (e.g., AV-2 warnings should not override the need for manager approval).

5. **Recoverability**:
   - Detective and corrective controls must allow for recovery of losses or remediation of defects (e.g., RA-7 audit and recall must be effective).

6. **Evidence Quality**:
   - Controls must generate high-quality evidence that can be independently verified (e.g., manager approval logs, audit trails).

7. **Collusion Resistance**:
   - Controls must resist attempts by insiders to circumvent the controls (e.g., AV-2 should block duplicate invoices regardless of who performs the validation).

**Why “Same Expected Loss” is Not Sufficient**:
- **Loss Magnitude vs. Control Effectiveness**: The expected monetary loss does not account for the effectiveness of controls in preventing or detecting the loss. A high-risk control failure might result in a low monetary loss if the loss is mitigated by other controls, while a low-risk control failure could lead to a significant loss if other controls fail.
- **Control Coverage**: A control may be technically sound but not fully cover all scenarios (e.g., AV-2 may pass but not detect fraud in a complex transaction).
- **Operational Context**: Controls must consider the operational context, such as the frequency of payments, vendor relationships, and the potential for collusion or insider threats.

### Explanation of Control Types

- **Preventive Controls**: Actions taken before a high-risk event to ensure compliance (e.g., MG-4 manager approvals, AV-2 automated validation). They aim to stop the payment from being initiated in the first place.
- **Detective Controls**: Actions that occur after an event to detect any issues (e.g., RA-7 audit, AV-2 warnings). They are crucial for identifying problems that have already occurred.
- **Corrective Capacity**: The ability to remediate or recover from a detected issue (e.g., RA-7 audit and recall can correct or mitigate financial losses).

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

To monitor and improve compliance with FEP-1, consider the following evidence collection and monitoring rules:

1. **Same-User Payment Rate**:
   - **Metric**: Track the frequency of same-user payments.
   - **Alert Threshold**: >10% of total payments in a month may warrant review.

2. **Override Rate**:
   - **Metric**: Track the frequency of AV-2 overrides.
   - **Alert Threshold**: More than 5% of validations overridden may indicate a need for control adjustment.

3. **Late Manager Approval**:
   - **Metric**: Time between Release_Payment and Manager_Ratify.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Delays greater than 24 hours may indicate control failure.

4. **Audit Timing**:
   - **Metric**: Time from Release_Payment to Audit_Review.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Audits completed after the recall window (typically 1 business day) may indicate insufficient detective controls.

5. **Bank Change Proximity**:
   - **Metric**: Time between bank account change and payment release.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Changes within 24 hours of release should trigger additional review.

6. **Repeated Resource Pairs**:
   - **Metric**: Frequency of the same two users performing both steps.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Repeated pairings should be reviewed to ensure independence.

7. **Shared Workstations**:
   - **Metric**: Track workstation usage for payment-related activities.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Shared workstations without proper segregation may indicate collusion risk.

8. **Control Outages**:
   - **Metric**: Frequency of AV-2 and other control outages.
   - **Alert Threshold**: Outages resulting in payment releases should be investigated.

**Distinguishing Alert from Proof of Misconduct**:
- **Alert**: Indications of potential control failure (e.g., same-user rate, override rate, audit delays).
- **Proof of Misconduct**: Concrete evidence of intentional wrongdoing (e.g., fabricated invoices, unauthorized overrides).
