# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let \(c\) index cases and \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\) index branches. Write \(a_c,r_c,f_c\) for arrival, fork release, and final start; \(s_{ci},e_{ci}\) for branch start and completion; and \(f_c^+\) for final completion.

All durations below are elapsed minutes.

**Structural interpretation:** the supplied data do not log a synchronization activity. Consequently, synchronization waiting must be reconstructed from branch completions rather than obtained from a directly-follows relationship.

Define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_c &= f_c-a_c
&&\text{upstream elapsed time},\\
Q_{ci} &= s_{ci}-f_c
&&\text{branch queue waiting},\\
H_{ci} &= e_{ci}-s_{ci}
&&\text{branch service},\\
J_c &= \max_i e_{ci}
&&\text{join-ready / latest branch completion},\\
W_{ci} &= \max(0,J_c-e_{ci})
&&\text{waiting after completion for the other branches},\\
K_c &= J_c-f_c
&&\text{elapsed fork–join stage time},\\
D_c &= \max(0,f_c-J_c)
&&\text{elapsed time between readiness and final start},\\
R_c &= f_c^+-f_c
&&\text{final service},\\
T_c &= f_c^+-a_c
&&\text{total case flow time}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the model in which final work becomes executable at readiness,

\[
\boxed{
T_c=U_c+K_c+D_c+R_c
}
\]

Thus \(K_c\), rather than the sum of branch durations, is an additive component of case flow time.

### Why branch waiting and durations cannot simply be added

The three branches run in parallel. Their service and queue intervals overlap, and their post-completion waits often refer to overlapping portions of the same elapsed stage.

For bookkeeping, also define a **completion overrun**, which is not waiting:

\[
L_{ci}=\max(0,e_{ci}-J_c).
\]

Because all three branch starts are at or after fork release, and the minimum branch start is the fork in these data,

\[
\boxed{
K_c=
\frac{
\sum_i(Q_{ci}+H_{ci})
-\sum_i L_{ci}
+\sum_i W_{ci}
}{3}
}
\]

for this three-branch structure. More generally, the denominator is the number of branches \(m\).

The subtraction of overruns is necessary: a branch finishing after join-ready has no positive synchronization wait. F01 illustrates this—A finishes five minutes after join-ready.

The waiting term still cannot be interpreted as a separate addition to \(T_c\). Its members can describe the same elapsed period, while \(K_c\) already includes the parallel work and waiting. In F02, the three branch queue-plus-service durations total 135 minutes, while synchronization waits total 45 minutes:

\[
K_{\mathrm{F02}}=\frac{135-0+45}{3}=60.
\]

Adding the three 60-minute stage copies would count concurrent elapsed time three times. Conversely, adding the 45 minutes of waiting directly to 135 would count the same coordination periods again.

**Interpretation assumption for \(D_c\):** it is operational downstream delay. Calling it a resource queue additionally assumes that final work is released to start as soon as the join condition and resource availability permit. Section 7 explains why the timestamps do not establish that assumption.

The branch **offset** below is queue plus service, measured from fork release to branch completion:

\[
O_{ci}=Q_{ci}+H_{ci}=e_{ci}-f_c.
\]

It is more relevant to branch influence than service alone, but even \(O_{ci}\) does not control the join unless it exceeds the other branch offsets. Percentiles use the empirical nearest-rank convention.

### Influence ranking

**1. B — strongest observed control.**

- It is last in eight of twelve cases, substantially more often than C or A.
- It has the largest mean queue-plus-service offset: 56.67 minutes.
- Its mean active service is only 37.50 minutes, but its mean queue is 19.17 minutes. Ignoring that queue would materially misidentify its role.
- It owns two of the three latest join-ready observations: F10 and F12; A owns F11.
- Its mean synchronization wait is small because it usually finishes the parallel work, not because its elapsed path is short.

**2. C — meaningful second-place influence.**

- C is last in four cases, all of which are driven by relatively favorable completion offsets.
- Its mean synchronization wait of 30 minutes says that C often finishes while A or B continues—not that C is the most common last branch.
- C is particularly influential in F04 and F08. In both, A is a close second: A finishes seven minutes before C in F04 and five minutes before C in F08.

**3. A — weakest observed control despite the largest mean service.**

- A has the largest mean service, 50 minutes, but no observed branch queue.
- It is last only in F11.
- It is nevertheless a plausible secondary constraint: A is just behind C in F04 and F08, and A finishes five minutes after join-ready in F01.

Thus the observed ranking is **B > C > A**. There are no current last-completion ties, and the \(8\)-versus-\(4\) last-branch frequencies should not be described as a statistical tie. Nevertheless, this is a descriptive ranking of only twelve cases, not a causal estimate of population-wide branch importance.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

### Counterfactual assumptions

**CF1 — fixed-response assumption:** unless stated otherwise, only the selected branch completion changes. Other branch times remain fixed.

**CF2 — fixed-final-schedule assumption for branch acceleration:** final start and completion remain at their logged times. If a branch acceleration moves join-ready beyond that fixed final start, the maximum possible saving is capped accordingly.

**CF3 — ideal-readiness assumption for the final-station counterfactual:** final work starts exactly at join-ready, preserving its logged 20-minute service. This calculates an upper bound on removing \(D\); it does not prove that current release policy or capacity permits it.

For an accelerated branch, let \(J'_c\) be the maximum of its new completion and the unchanged competing completions. With fixed final timestamps,

\[
\text{direct saving}_c
=
\min\!\left(\text{original }T_c,\,
\max(0,f_c-J'_c)\right).
\]

The third counterfactual instead calculates

\[
T'_c=a_c+J'_c+R_c,\qquad
\text{saving}_c=D_c.
\]

| F05 | 0 | 0 | 10 | 5 |

### A service reduced by 20%

Reducing every A service duration by ten minutes benefits only F11.

In F11, A would finish at 11:20, tying B. The saving is therefore **10 minutes, not 20**: a further A reduction would require reducing B as well. In the other eleven cases, A is already earlier than the controlling completion, so ten minutes of released A capacity creates zero case-level flow-time benefit under the fixed-response assumption.

This is the clearest demonstration that the largest mean service is not necessarily the strongest completion control.

### B queue reduced by 50%

This benefits F01, F03, F07, and F12, producing 52.5 total minutes of savings.

In the other eight cases:

- F02, F05, F06, F09, F10, and F11 retain an A-controlled join.
- F04 and F08 retain a C-controlled join.
- F02 and F06 become **A–B ties** after the B change.

The counterfactual therefore shifts the last-branch distribution to B in four cases, A in six, and C in two, counting the two A–B ties under A. B remains jointly critical in F02 and F06.

Likely secondary effects include increased synchronization waiting for A or C while B is accelerated. That waiting is redistributed elapsed time, not an additional cost to total flow time.

### Final-station capacity or immediate readiness

The ideal calculation removes 181 minutes, averaging 15.08 minutes per case. Every case benefits, but the result is an upper bound:

- If \(D\) represents avoidable queueing, extra capacity or earlier dispatch might recover part of this amount.
- If \(D\) represents scheduled or policy-based release, adding a server may recover little or nothing.
- Starting at readiness may require resource reassignment and interaction with later cases; the calculation does not solve those scheduling constraints.

Branch identities do not change under this isolated counterfactual. However, a real final-station redesign changes downstream event times and could change future resource contention, dispatch behavior, and effective branch completion patterns.

### Diagnostic: C five minutes earlier

A uniform five-minute reduction in C completion benefits all twelve cases because C’s slack to the controlling branch is at least five minutes in every case.

After the change:

- B controls nine cases.
- A controls F04 and F08.
- F08 becomes an A–C tie.
- C no longer controls an observed case.

This supports C as the second most influential branch and motivates a targeted C pilot. It does not mean that every five-minute service reduction is operationally feasible.

### Does downstream congestion mask branch improvements?

In the fixed-final-schedule counterfactuals, downstream waiting does not mask these branch savings: the final completion clock remains fixed, and an earlier join simply creates more observed readiness-to-start delay.

That is not a general guarantee. If accelerated branches release final work earlier and the final station remains occupied, queue interactions could absorb part of the benefit. No such response is modeled here. The observed downstream delays are only 10–20 minutes, so they are modest relative to the branch-stage time, but the logged evidence does not identify their cause.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### 1. Pilot a faster C path, with a five-minute per-case target

Use a C fast lane, parallel administrative review where feasible, or a targeted reduction in C queue-plus-service time. The five-minute counterfactual is a useful **pilot target**, not a guaranteed redesign specification.

If achieved for every case under the fixed-final assumption:

- Mean total flow falls from 115.25 to 110.25 minutes.
- The empirical p90 falls from 125 to 120 minutes.
- B becomes the primary constraint in nine cases; A becomes the constraint in two, with one A–C tie.
- A five-minute C reduction is operationally substantial relative to C’s mean service of 20.17 minutes, so the pilot must establish whether it represents genuine work reduction, earlier availability, or parallelization.

**Throughput:** shorter parallel work can increase completion frequency only if cases arrive continuously and other resources permit it. The twelve arrivals are regularly spaced, so these data do not establish a steady-state throughput rate.

**Flow-time tail:** the modeled p90 improves by five minutes, but C’s four critical cases may expose A or B as the next constraint.

**Resource utilization:** A and B service loads remain unchanged. The design reduces administrative queue/service exposure. Moving work to another administrative resource must be measured; resource identities for A–C are not supplied.

**Synchronization effects:** C’s early completion can transfer waiting to B or A without increasing total fork–join time. F04 and F08 are especially useful pilot cases because A is close behind C.

**Measure first:** C’s first-work-available and first-work-start timestamps, actual active versus interrupted time, routing and reassignment, B/A handoff behavior, and whether administrative parallelism creates a new shared-resource queue.

### 2. Diagnose and remove unnecessary final readiness delay, adding flexible capacity only if justified

Instrument a final-ready event distinct from final start. Test whether cases can be dispatched immediately after synchronization rather than held for a batch, scheduled release, or administrative approval.

If a case is genuinely resource-blocked at readiness, pilot flexible cross-trained final resources or an additional server. Do not add permanent capacity solely because the elapsed \(D\) field is nonzero.

The supplied history is not strong evidence of a final-station capacity shortage:

- \(D\) averages only 15.08 minutes and never exceeds 20 minutes.
- Each final resource handles six 20-minute jobs, or 120 minutes of work.
- Over the common 07:55–12:40 observation window, that is 42.1% of each resource’s 285 available minutes, before considering its actual availability calendar.
- Within the listed assignments, no earlier same-resource case blocks a case for the entire observed \(D\); the residual intervals after earlier same-resource completion are generally shorter than \(D\).

These observations favor investigating release policy before permanent capacity expansion.

**Throughput:** immediate dispatch can improve completion timing if the 15-minute average delay is avoidable, but adding a server alone will not help if the delay is policy-based.

**Flow-time tail:** ideal removal would reduce mean flow to 100.17 minutes and p90 to 115. The actual gain depends on whether the delay is dispatchable or resource-induced.

**Resource utilization:** cross-training can smooth peaks; permanent capacity may remain underused. A new bottleneck could arise if dispatch shifts earlier to already-busy technical resources, although these counterfactuals do not change branch times directly.

**Synchronization effects:** earlier final starts do not change the logical join, but they can change downstream overlap and subsequent resource availability.

**Measure first:** join-ready timestamp, release/approval timestamp, resource-ready timestamp, final queue, dispatch batch size, routing choice, and actual resource utilization. A pilot should distinguish “ready but held” from “ready and resource-blocked.”

def minutes(delta):
    return float(delta / pd.Timedelta(minutes=1))


def p90(series):
    """Empirical nearest-rank p90, matching the report."""
    values = np.sort(series.dropna().to_numpy())
    return float(values[int(np.ceil(0.90 * len(values))) - 1])


def critical_label(end_row):
    """
    Deterministic tie handling:
    - one winner: return that branch;
    - tied winners: return all winners in fixed A,B,C order.

    A tie therefore never depends on arbitrary row order.
    """
    latest = end_row.max()
    winners = [
        b for b in branches
        if np.isclose(
            minutes(end_row[b] - latest),
            0.0,
            atol=1e-9,
            rtol=0.0,
        )
    ]
    return winners[0] if len(winners) == 1 else "|".join(winners)

# 2. Custom pandas fork-join decomposition.
#
# PM4Py directly-follows performance does not reconstruct join-ready,
# post-completion waiting, or a parallel-branch max operation from these
# events. All quantities involving fork release, max completion,
# synchronization waiting, and final readiness are custom calculations.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
case_rows = []

for _, r in raw.iterrows():
    row = {
        "case": r["case"],
        "arrival": r["arrival"],
        "fork_release": r["fork_release"],
        "upstream_elapsed": minutes(r["fork_release"] - r["arrival"]),
    }

    ends = pd.Series({b: r[f"{b}_end"] for b in branches})
    join_ready = ends.max()
    row["join_ready"] = join_ready
    row["fork_join_elapsed"] = minutes(join_ready - r["fork_release"])

    total_queue_service = 0.0
    total_sync = 0.0
    total_overrun = 0.0

    for b in branches:
        start = r[f"{b}_start"]
        end = r[f"{b}_end"]

        queue = minutes(start - r["fork_release"])
        service = minutes(end - start)
        sync = max(0.0, minutes(join_ready - end))
        overrun = max(0.0, minutes(end - join_ready))
        offset = minutes(end - r["fork_release"])

        row[f"{b}_queue"] = queue
        row[f"{b}_service"] = service
        row[f"{b}_sync"] = sync
        row[f"{b}_overrun"] = overrun
        row[f"{b}_offset"] = offset

        total_queue_service += queue + service
        total_sync += sync
        total_overrun += overrun

    row["critical_branch"] = critical_label(ends)
    row["downstream_queue"] = max(
        0.0, minutes(r["final_start"] - join_ready)
    )
    row["final_service"] = minutes(r["final_end"] - r["final_start"])
    row["total_flow_time"] = minutes(r["final_end"] - r["arrival"])

    # For m parallel branches whose minimum start is fork_release:
    # K = [sum(queue + service) - sum(overrun) + sum(sync)] / m.
    reconstructed_stage = (
        total_queue_service - total_overrun + total_sync

    assert np.isclose(reconstructed_stage, row["fork_join_elapsed"])

    assert np.isclose(
        row["total_flow_time"],
        row["upstream_elapsed"]
        + row["fork_join_elapsed"]
        + row["downstream_queue"]
        + row["final_service"],
    )

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Counterfactuals.
#
# Branch interventions are independent. Other branch timestamps remain
# fixed, and the logged final start/end remain fixed.
#
# The final-station intervention is an ideal-readiness calculation:
# final start = join_ready, final service unchanged. It is not a claim
# that current release policy or capacity makes that schedule feasible.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
base_ends = raw[[f"{b}_end" for b in branches]].copy()
base_flow = decomposition.set_index("case")["total_flow_time"]


def evaluate_counterfactual(label, new_ends, accelerated_branch=None,
                            ideal_final_readiness=False):
    result_rows = []

    for case_id in raw["case"]:
        r = raw.loc[raw["case"] == case_id].iloc[0]
        ends_c = new_ends.loc[case_id]

        if accelerated_branch is None:
            new_join = ends_c.max()
        else:
            competing = [
                ends_c[b]
                for b in branches
                if b != accelerated_branch
            ]
            new_join = max(
                [ends_c[accelerated_branch]] + competing
            )

        old_final_start = r["final_start"]
        old_final_end = r["final_end"]

        if ideal_final_readiness:
            new_final_start = new_join
            new_final_end = (
                new_final_start
                + pd.Timedelta(minutes=float(r["final_end"] - r["final_start"]))
            )
            new_downstream = 0.0
        else:
            new_final_start = old_final_start
            new_final_end = old_final_end
            new_downstream = max(
                0.0, minutes(old_final_start - new_join)
            )

        new_flow = minutes(new_final_end - r["arrival"]) - new_downstream
        old_flow = float(base_flow.loc[case_id])
        saving = old_flow - new_flow

        assert saving >= -1e-9
        assert np.isclose(
            new_flow,
            max(0.0, minutes(new_final_start - r["arrival"]))
            + minutes(new_final_end - new_final_start),
        )

        result_rows.append({
            "case": case_id,
            "new_join_ready": new_join,
            "new_critical_branch": critical_label(ends_c),
            "new_downstream_delay": new_downstream,
            "new_total_flow_time": new_flow,
            "saving_min": saving,
        })

    result = pd.DataFrame(result_rows)
    summary = pd.DataFrame([{
        "counterfactual": label,
        "total_saving_min": result["saving_min"].sum(),
        "mean_saving_min": result["saving_min"].mean(),
        "median_saving_min": result["saving_min"].median(),
        "p90_saving_min": p90(result["saving_min"]),
        "cases_improved": int((result["saving_min"] > 1e-9).sum()),
        "mean_new_flow": result["new_total_flow_time"].mean(),
    }])
    return result, summary


# A service -20%, keeping A_start fixed.
a20_ends = base_ends.copy()
a20_ends["A_end"] = raw["A_start"] + 0.8 * (
    raw["A_end"] - raw["A_start"]

# B queue -50%, equivalently B service duration unchanged.
b50_ends = base_ends.copy()
b50_ends["B_end"] = raw["B_start"] + 0.5 * (
    raw["B_end"] - raw["B_start"]

# Diagnostic C acceleration: completion five minutes earlier.
c5_ends = base_ends.copy()
c5_ends["C_end"] = raw["C_end"] - pd.Timedelta(minutes=5)

cf_results = {}
cf_summaries = []

cf_results["A_service_minus_20pct"], s = evaluate_counterfactual(
    "A_service_minus_20pct", a20_ends, accelerated_branch="A"
)
cf_summaries.append(s)

cf_results["B_queue_minus_50pct"], s = evaluate_counterfactual(
    "B_queue_minus_50pct", b50_ends, accelerated_branch="B"
)
cf_summaries.append(s)

cf_results["ideal_final_D_zero"], s = evaluate_counterfactual(
    "ideal_final_D_zero",
    base_ends,
    ideal_final_readiness=True,
)
cf_summaries.append(s)

cf_results["C_5min_earlier"], s = evaluate_counterfactual(
    "C_5min_earlier", c5_ends, accelerated_branch="C"
)
cf_summaries.append(s)

counterfactual_summary = pd.concat(cf_summaries, ignore_index=True)

savings_output = pd.DataFrame({
    "case": raw["case"],
    "baseline_flow": base_flow.to_numpy(),
    "A20_saving": cf_results["A_service_minus_20pct"]["saving_min"],
    "B50_saving": cf_results["B_queue_minus_50pct"]["saving_min"],
    "ideal_D0_saving": cf_results["ideal_final_D_zero"]["saving_min"],
    "C5_saving": cf_results["C_5min_earlier"]["saving_min"],
    "A20_new_critical": cf_results["A_service_minus_20pct"][
        "new_critical_branch"
    ],
    "B50_new_critical": cf_results["B_queue_minus_50pct"][
        "new_critical_branch"
    ],
    "C5_new_critical": cf_results["C_5min_earlier"][
        "new_critical_branch"
    ],
})

print("\nCOUNTERFACTUAL PER-CASE SAVINGS (minutes)")
print(savings_output.to_string(index=False, float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.3f}"))

print("\nCOUNTERFACTUAL AGGREGATE SUMMARY")
print(
    counterfactual_summary.to_string(
        index=False, float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.3f}"
    )
)

# 7. Limits of inference

1. **Causality is not identified.** The timestamps show which branch finishes last, not why. B’s influence could reflect its work content, queue policy, resource assignment, or interactions not logged. The counterfactuals change durations by assumption; they do not estimate causal production functions.

2. **Branch resources and capacity are unknown.** No resource identifiers, calendars, utilization, or assignment rules are supplied for A, B, or C. The reconstructed A queue of zero means A starts at fork release; it does not mean the branch has unlimited capacity.

3. **Active service is an interval definition, not proof of uninterrupted work.** Complete events at start and completion support elapsed service duration, but do not reveal interruptions, pauses, rework, or preemption. The counterfactual duration reductions assume that the required acceleration can be obtained within the logged branch intervals.

4. **Final waiting is not necessarily a resource queue.** The join condition is necessary for final start, but it need not trigger immediate execution. Batch releases, scheduled decisions, approvals, priority rules, or other policies could explain \(D\). Adding resources cannot remove a policy-mandated delay.

5. **Resource availability is only partially observed.** The listed final assignments show historical same-resource service intervals, but not complete availability calendars, other work, or dispatch rules. Ideal \(D=0\) is therefore an upper bound rather than a demonstrated feasible schedule with the existing two resources.

6. **Parallelism creates non-additive effects.** Reductions can change the critical branch, create ties, and redistribute synchronization waiting. A branch improvement’s value depends on the competing completion offsets and potentially on downstream response.

7. **The sample is small and regularly structured.** Twelve cases on one day, with arrivals fifteen minutes apart, do not support a reliable steady-state throughput forecast, seasonal tail estimate, or population-wide branch ranking. The reported means, percentiles, and frequencies are descriptive for this event table.

8. **The upstream interval is not further decomposed.** Arrival-to-fork time is 15 minutes in F01 and 20 minutes in the other cases, but no upstream preparation events are supplied. Its internal waiting and service composition cannot be identified.

9. **PM4Py does not supply the missing fork-join semantics automatically.** The custom max-based pandas calculations establish join-ready, branch synchronization, and readiness-to-final-start delay. PM4Py validates the logged activity intervals and observed event relationships; it should not be interpreted as independently discovering those unlogged coordination delays.
