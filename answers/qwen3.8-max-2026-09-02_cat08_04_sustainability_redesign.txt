# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental boundary used:** a practical order-to-close lifecycle boundary for spare-part fulfillment. The assessment includes environmental impacts that are visible in, or directly caused by, the logged process:

- **Inside the boundary**
  - Production energy for CNC, heat treatment, inspection, emergency production, and rework loops (`energy_kwh`, `machine_group`, `batch_id`, `batch_size`).
  - Material waste from production scrap and rework (`material_scrap_kg`, `rework_flag`).
  - Packaging material use and packaging rework (`package_material_kg`, `Rework packaging` loop).
  - Transport emissions and mode choices (`transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode`: parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck).
  - Hidden environmental costs of waiting and batching: longer batch waits, late deliveries, expedited corrections, and obsolescence of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days.
  - Extra transport and packaging caused by partial shipments, backorders, returns, and re-shipment.
  - Energy and material consequences of emergency production and dedicated-van use for Critical-downtime cases.

- **Outside the boundary**
  - Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier-side production impacts, except where reflected in scrap or rework waste.
  - Customer-side downtime impacts, machine downtime at customer sites, and broader economic cost of delayed repairs.
  - End-of-life recycling or disposal impacts unless they appear as returns/rework in the log.
  - Facility-level heating, lighting, and warehouse building energy not captured in `energy_kwh`.
  - Capital-equipment embodied carbon and employee commuting.

- **Key assumptions and remaining uncertainty**
  - `transport_kgco2e` is treated as an operational transport indicator; it may not include full well-to-wheel or packaging lifecycle effects.
  - The log is flattened by order, so batch-level energy and scrap allocation to individual orders may be approximate.
  - Obsolescence is only partially visible: the prompt notes that 6% of batch-produced items may not ship within 14 days when forecasts are wrong, but the full material and energy cost of those items is not fully quantified.
  - Service quality is proxied by `promised_lead_time_hours`, late delivery, cancellation due to delay, rework, and returns; some customer dissatisfaction may not appear in the log.
  - Resilience is inferred from route alternatives, emergency production, partial shipments, backorders, dedicated-van justification, and carrier-disruption policies.
  - The discovered process model has low precision because emergency paths and partial shipments combine in many ways during disruptions; therefore, validation should use event-level checks, not only model-level conformance.

# 3. Intervention options

### S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment

- **Target path or segment:** All shipment decisions, especially Standard-stock Service-contract orders currently shipped by air and Made-to-order orders using air despite sufficient slack.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Recommends parcel_ground or consolidated_truck when the predicted probability of meeting the promised lead time is above the SP1 threshold of 85%. This avoids high-emission air shipments and shifts eligible orders to lower-carbon modes.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service can be preserved if route reliability and slack are used rather than applying a blanket air ban. Quality is mostly unaffected. Resilience is protected if the recommender retains at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers, consistent with R1.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, `expedite_flag`, late delivery by route, SP1 violation rate, carrier reliability, transport kgCOe per segment, and exception overrides.

### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order cases following `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production -> Heat treatment -> Inspection`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Increases batch size where due-date slack exists, reducing energy per item. CNC_A energy per item falls from 2.8 kWh at batch size 1–3 to 1.3 kWh at batch size 12–20; heat treatment is also most efficient above batch size 10.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Without caps, batch wait increases and late-delivery risk rises, especially for heat-treatment batches. With segment-specific caps, service can remain acceptable. Obsolescence risk must be controlled by limiting large batches when forecast confidence is low.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `batch_id`, `batch_size`, batch wait duration, `energy_kwh`, late delivery by batch size, heat-treatment wait, obsolescence rate, and items not shipped within 14 days.

### S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order cases, especially the high-rework path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** CNC_B has a higher rework rate than CNC_A (18% vs 9%). Avoiding rework reduces repeated CNC energy, scrap, inspection loops, and replacement shipments.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** CNC_A may be slower or more queued, so the shift should only occur when slack exists. CNC_B should remain available for urgent jobs and overflow to avoid creating a new bottleneck.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `machine_group`, `rework_flag`, `material_scrap_kg`, `energy_kwh`, queue time, due-date slack, late delivery, and emergency-production triggers.

### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground unless slack is insufficient

- **Target path or segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders, particularly the 22% SP1 violation cases where air is used although promised lead time may still allow ground.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Parcel air emits approximately 4–6 times more kgCOe per order than ground. Priority picking can recover handling time, allowing ground transport without service loss.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service can remain acceptable if priority picking is reliable and promised lead time has enough slack. A fallback air option should remain for true exceptions and disruptions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `case_type`, `customer_type`, `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, pick lead time, late delivery, SP1 conformance, and transport kgCOe.

### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders

- **Target path or segment:** Non-urgent Standard-stock Regular orders and eligible Made-to-order orders currently shipped as individual ground parcels.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCOe by about 35–55% compared with individual ground parcels. Twice-daily departures create predictable consolidation windows.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Consolidation adds a 12–24 hour wait, so it should only be used when promised lead-time probability remains high. Resilience improves if multiple carriers or departure slots are available, but worsens if all volume depends on one consolidation hub.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, consolidation wait time, missed cutoffs, late delivery, transport kgCOe, carrier diversity, and service-contract alternative-route availability.

### S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment

- **Target path or segment:** Orders entering `Package standard` or `Package reinforced` that later go to `Rework packaging`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Prevents packaging rework caused by incompatible packaging choice, route mode, or part geometry. This reduces extra packaging material, rehandling energy, and possible reshipment.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service improves because fewer orders are delayed by packaging rework. Quality improves if correct packaging reduces damage. Resilience effect is neutral.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Rework packaging` frequency, `package_material_kg`, `route_mode`, part geometry attributes if available, damage-related returns, and repeat packaging events.

### S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order cases with high rework loops, especially `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`, and cases processed on CNC_B.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Rushed inspections miss defects, causing later rework, returns, and packaging problems. A targeted quality gate reduces repeated production energy, scrap, and replacement transport.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Inspection time may increase initially, but end-to-end cycle time can fall if rework loops and returns decrease. Quality improves. Resilience is positive if inspection capacity is not a single point of failure.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `inspection_cell` queue time, `rework_flag`, `machine_group`, scrap kg, return/rework loops, Q1 certification events, and late delivery after inspection changes.

### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes

- **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders using dedicated_van, especially where justification is missing or the urgency is precautionary.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Shared emergency lanes can reduce empty return trips and lower transport kgCOe while still providing urgent delivery.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** This is beneficial only for true critical cases where the shared lane meets the required response time. A dedicated-van fallback must remain for severe disruptions and true downtime events.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, SP2 justification events, `case_type`, criticality reason, transport kgCOe, empty-return evidence, cycle time, late delivery, and carrier-disruption performance.

### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract cases, which have high transport emissions (average 38.0 kgCOe/order).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** True downtime cases keep dedicated vans or emergency production, while precautionary urgent cases can use priority ground, stock availability, or consolidated emergency lanes.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service risk rises if classification is wrong. Clear rules, customer confirmation, and exception approval are needed. Resilience improves if critical capacity is protected from non-true emergencies.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** downtime reason, `expedite_flag`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, `route_mode`, late delivery, dedicated-van justification, and post-implementation reclassification rates.

### S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order Regular customers, where late delivery is high (18%) and production energy is high.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Customers can accept slightly longer or more flexible promise dates in exchange for lower-carbon production and transport: larger batches, CNC_A routing, ground transport, and consolidated trucking.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service can improve if promises become more realistic. However, customer acceptance is uncertain, and some customers may request overrides, creating rebound risk.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** changes in `promised_lead_time_hours`, accepted promise dates, `expedite_flag`, batch size, route mode, late delivery, cancellation rate, and customer override frequency.

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path or segment:** Stock orders following `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Package standard` or `Backorder wait`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Fewer partial shipments reduce second shipments, extra transport, additional packaging, and repeated customer contacts.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Complete-order shipment can increase first-delivery waiting time and backorder risk. Exceptions are needed for critical items, customer-approved splits, and service-contract obligations.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** partial-shipment rate, `Backorder wait` duration, second-shipment frequency, transport kgCOe, packaging material, late delivery, cancellations, and customer segment.

### S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides

- **Target path or segment:** All cases where SP1, SP2, or Q1 is intentionally overridden.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Makes trade-offs visible. For example, air shipments, dedicated vans, and packaging exceptions must be justified, approved, and reviewed.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Improves governance. It prevents silent normalization of high-emission exceptions while still allowing justified service or resilience overrides.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** exception counts, reason codes, approver, affected segment, route mode, packaging choice, subsequent late delivery, rework, returns, and repeat offenders.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

I recommend a **segmented green-to-promise operating model**, not a single isolated sustainability initiative. The redesign should combine routing rules, production batching controls, quality gates, critical-case triage, and exception governance.

| Redesign pillar | Included interventions | Main environmental target | Service / quality / resilience guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise-led green routing | S1, S4, S5, SP1 enforcement | Reduce avoidable air and individual ground transport; increase consolidated trucking | Use ground/consolidated only when predicted on-time probability is above 85%; retain alternative routes for Service-contract customers. |
| Quality-first Made-to-order production | S2, S3, S7 | Reduce energy, scrap, and hidden rework waste in MTO paths | Batch only with wait caps; shift CNC_B to CNC_A only when slack exists; keep CNC_B for urgent overflow. |
| Packaging and shipment integrity | S6, Q1 enforcement | Reduce packaging rework, damage, returns, and excess material | Reinforced packaging remains required for heavy Made-to-order parts unless inspection certifies low risk. |
| Critical-downtime triage and resilient emergency logistics | S8, S9, SP2 enforcement | Reduce unjustified dedicated-van emissions while protecting true downtime response | Dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification; shared courier lanes need strict SLA and fallback capacity. |
| Demand shaping and order completeness | S10, S11 | Reduce expedited production, partial shipments, and second shipments | Customer consent and critical-item exceptions are required; service-contract obligations must not be violated. |
| Governance and exception visibility | S12 | Prevent silent rebound and normalization of high-emission exceptions | All SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides are logged, approved, and reviewed. |

**Why this package is balanced:**

1. **It targets the largest lifecycle hotspots.**  
   Made-to-order cases dominate energy and scrap, Critical-downtime dominates transport emissions, and Standard-stock Service-contract contains avoidable air shipments. The package addresses all three rather than optimizing one narrow metric.

2. **It does not force the lowest-emission option where service or resilience would break.**  
   Critical-downtime cases are not forced onto ground transport. Dedicated vans remain available for true downtime, but unjustified use is reduced through SP2 justification and better triage.

3. **It treats rework and obsolescence as environmental outcomes, not just quality issues.**  
   CNC_B rework, inspection loops, packaging rework, and large-batch obsolescence all create hidden lifecycle waste. S2, S3, S6, and S7 directly address these sources.

4. **It uses caps and exceptions to prevent rebound.**  
   Batching is allowed only with waiting-time caps. Consolidation is allowed only with sufficient slack. Air and dedicated vans are not banned but require evidence-based justification.

5. **It preserves resilience.**  
   Alternative routes remain for Service-contract customers. CNC_B remains available as overflow. Shared emergency courier lanes are introduced only with fallback capacity. Exception logs make disruption-related overrides visible.

**Suggested sequencing:**

- **Phase 1 – Quick wins and guardrails:** S12 exception log, S4 priority pick plus ground for eligible Standard-stock Service-contract orders, S6 packaging compatibility checks, SP2 justification enforcement.
- **Phase 2 – Production and routing optimization:** S1 green shipping recommender, S2 dynamic batching with caps, S3 CNC_B-to-CNC_A shift, S7 inspection quality gate.
- **Phase 3 – Demand and critical-case refinement:** S9 critical triage, S8 shared emergency courier pilot, S10 carbon-aware promise negotiation, S11 partial-shipment rule changes.

- Check whether CNC_A routing changes create bottlenecks that increase emergency production or expedited transport.

### 7.6 Exception governance

The sustainability exception log should record:

- Policy overridden: SP1, SP2, or Q1.
- Case segment and customer type.
- Reason for override.
- Approver.
- Expected service or resilience benefit.
- Expected environmental cost.
- Actual outcome: late delivery, rework, return, transport emissions, scrap, and obsolescence.

Governance cadence:

- Weekly operational review of high-risk exceptions.
- Monthly sustainability and service review by segment.
- Quarterly reassessment of routing rules, batching caps, inspection gates, and critical-triage criteria.
- Root-cause analysis when exceptions rise, especially if caused by carrier disruption, inspection congestion, or forecast error.

### 7.7 Evaluation method

Because the discovered model has low precision during disruptions, validation should not rely only on model conformance. Use:

- Event-level conformance checks for SP1, SP2, and Q1.
- Variant mining to detect new inefficient or risky paths.
- Before/after comparison by segment and path.
- Phased rollout or matched comparison groups where feasible.
- Control charts for late delivery, rework, energy, transport kgCOe, scrap, and obsolescence.
- Review of unintended consequences, such as increased expedite flags after consolidation or increased emergency production after batching caps.
