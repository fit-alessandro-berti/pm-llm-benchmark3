# 2. Expected temporal signatures

To systematically differentiate between physical mechanisms and logging artifacts, we define a multi-dimensional signature space.

### Key Diagnostic Discriminators

1. **Causality Inversion Test**: If $s_i < t_{\text{queue\_arrival}, i}$, physical execution cannot have started at $s_i$. This is conclusive evidence of timestamp quantization/rounding (as in G7) or corrupted clocks.
2. **Step Duration Uniformity**: If $e_i - e_{i-1} \approx \text{constant}$ while all $s_i$ are identical (G2), the process is physically sequential with a grouped start trigger. Conversely, if $s_{i+1} - s_i \approx \text{constant}$ while all $e_i$ are identical (G3), the process is physically sequential with a grouped completion trigger.
3. **Recording Lag Profile**: True physical events recorded by PLCs or scanners exhibit sub-second to few-second recording lag ($t_{\text{rec}} - e < 5\text{s}$). Retrospective data entry exhibits either uniform bulk insertion (G3, G6) or stepped manual entry lags (G7).
4. **Tolerance Sensitivity ($\tau$)**: True simultaneous events (G1) maintain zero timestamp difference at millisecond resolution. Quantized events (G7) disperse or create boundary collisions when shifted by non-grid tolerances.

### Detailed Cluster-by-Cluster Analysis

- **G1 (Apply label, RBT-7)**: 
  - *Mechanism*: 4 items are labeled simultaneously in 3.0 min. 
  - *Bias*: If an analyst naively sums service times ($4 \times 3.0 = 12.0\text{ min}$) over a 3-minute elapsed window, apparent resource utilization is $400\%$, triggering a false bottleneck alert. When modeled with batch capacity $K=4$, service time is 3.0 min per batch (0.75 min/item), utilization is $100\%$ during the cycle, and waiting time (2–6 min) correctly captures batch accumulation.

- **G2 (Surface inspection, T-B)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Single operator inspects sequentially (step times: 8m, 6m, 7m, 6m; total 27m), but the tablet logs $s = 10:00$ for all items.
  - *Bias*: Service times are recorded as 8, 14, 21, and 27 min (mean 17.5 min vs. true 6.75 min). Station utilization is calculated as $(8+14+21+27)/27 = 259\%$. In-station queue waiting time for S02–S04 is disguised as active processing, masking shop-floor work-in-progress (WIP) and artificially inflating labor duration.

- **G3 (Release review, T-C)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Single reviewer works sequentially from 11:02 to 11:30 (starts at 11:02, 11:10, 11:17, 11:24), but submits the form in bulk at 11:30.
  - *Bias*: Service times are recorded as 28, 20, 13, and 6 min (mean 16.8 min vs. true 7.0 min). E01 appears to take 28 min. Total recorded duration is 67 min in a 28-min window ($239\%$ utilization). Downstream processes experience an artificial batch-release shock at 11:30 rather than a smooth piece-by-piece handoff.

- **G4 (Pack item, T-D)**:
  - *Mechanism*: High-precision, single-piece serial flow (4.0 min per item, total 20 min).
  - *Bias*: No data corruption. However, if PM4Py's "Sequential batching" label is misinterpreted by analysts as a batch setup or batch policy, it can lead to inappropriate lean interventions (e.g., attempting to eliminate batching where true single-piece flow already operates).

- **G5 (Root-cause analysis, analyst_pool)**:
  - *Mechanism*: Up to 3–4 analysts work concurrently under one shared account. Total logged duration is $25 + 15 + 20 + 10 = 70\text{ min}$ over a 30-min window (13:00–13:30).
  - *Bias*: If analyzed assuming single-resource capacity ($K=1$), utilization is $233\%$, suggesting severe multitasking inefficiencies or impossibly high labor output. If recognized as a multi-server queue ($M/M/c$ with $c=3\text{ or }4$), service times (10–25 min) and queue waits (15–20 min) are unbiased and accurate.

- **G6 (Close ticket, T-F) — The Bulk Closure Paradox**:
  - *Mechanism*: Administrative end-of-day closure of 7 tickets in one bulk transaction at 17:00 (duration = 0 min, recorded at 17:01).
  - *The Paradox (Inflated Waiting + Near-Zero Service)*:
    1. *Inflated Waiting Time*: Tickets arriving between 14:05 and 16:25 sit in the log with waiting times of 35 to 175 min (mean 104.4 min). This waiting time is purely administrative holding latency, NOT physical processing backlog or operational starvation.
    2. *Near-Zero Service Time*: Recorded start and complete are both 17:00:00 (duration = 0.0 min). The actual investigation/resolution work performed earlier in the day is completely omitted from the log.
  - *Performance Impact*: SLA tracking reports massive false waiting-time violations; capacity planning models conclude ticket closure requires zero labor resource; process discovery generates artificial end-of-day transaction spikes.

- **G7 (Manual measure, T-G)**:
  - *Mechanism*: 15-minute rounding by `legacy_MES` forces all starts to 14:00 and completes to 14:15.
  - *Bias*: Total logged duration is $5 \times 15 = 75\text{ min}$ in a 15-min window ($500\%$ apparent load). Causality inversions ($s < t_{\text{queue}}$) produce negative queue waiting times ($-1$ to $-11$ min), breaking standard queuing models (Little's Law, Kingman's formula) and corrupting conformance checking.

- **G8 (Repair triage, T-H)**:
  - *Mechanism*: 38-minute pre-execution delay caused by an un-synchronized 35-minute safety inspection (15:00–15:35), followed by perfect 7-minute serial triage.
  - *Bias*: The 38-minute queue wait is attributed to the repair triage resource. An analyst unaware of equipment downtime would diagnose triage as an under-staffed bottleneck, inappropriately recommending additional triage labor rather than optimizing safety maintenance scheduling.

# 5. Independent pandas diagnostics per cluster
def compute_cluster_diagnostics(c_df):
    n = len(c_df)
    u_start = c_df['start_timestamp'].nunique()
    u_end = c_df['time:timestamp'].nunique()
    u_rec = c_df['recorded_timestamp'].nunique()
    
    # Pairwise overlap rate
    intervals = list(zip(c_df['start_timestamp'], c_df['time:timestamp']))
    overlaps = 0
    total_pairs = n * (n - 1) // 2 if n > 1 else 1
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i + 1, n):
            s1, e1 = intervals[i]
            s2, e2 = intervals[j]
            if s1 == e1 and s2 == e2:
                if s1 == s2:
                    overlaps += 1
            else:
                if max(s1, s2) < min(e1, e2):
                    overlaps += 1
    overlap_rate = overlaps / total_pairs if total_pairs > 0 else 0.0
    
    # Peak concurrency
    time_pts = sorted(list(set(c_df['start_timestamp'].tolist() + c_df['time:timestamp'].tolist())))
    max_conc = 0
    if (c_df['start_timestamp'] == c_df['time:timestamp']).all():
        max_conc = n
    else:
        for tp in time_pts:
            active = sum((c_df['start_timestamp'] <= tp) & (c_df['time:timestamp'] > tp))
            if active > max_conc:
                max_conc = active
        if max_conc == 0:
            max_conc = 1
            
    # Durations, lags, and queue build-up metrics
    serv_durations = (c_df['time:timestamp'] - c_df['start_timestamp']).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    rec_lags = (c_df['recorded_timestamp'] - c_df['time:timestamp']).dt.total_seconds()
    causality_violations = (c_df['start_timestamp'] < c_df['queue_timestamp']).sum()
    queue_buildup_delay = (c_df['start_timestamp'].min() - c_df['queue_timestamp'].min()).total_seconds() / 60.0
    
    act = c_df['activity'].iloc[0]
    res = c_df['resource'].iloc[0]
    pm4py_cat = batch_results['Default (dist=900s, min=2)'].get((act, res), 'Not detected')
    
    return pd.Series({
        'Activity': act,
        'Resource': res,
        'N': n,
        'Unique Starts': u_start,
        'Unique Ends': u_end,
        'Overlap Rate': f'{overlap_rate:.2f}',
        'Max Concurrency': max_conc,
        'Mean Service (m)': f'{serv_durations.mean():.1f}',
        'Mean Rec Lag (s)': f'{rec_lags.mean():.1f}',
        'Causality Inversions': causality_violations,
        'Queue Buildup Delay (m)': f'{queue_buildup_delay:.1f}',
        'PM4Py Category': pm4py_cat
    })

# 6 & 7. Output tables
diag_table = df.groupby('cluster', group_keys=False).apply(compute_cluster_diagnostics)
print("=== CLUSTER-LEVEL INDEPENDENT DIAGNOSTICS & PM4PY COMPARISON ===")
print(diag_table.to_string())

print("\n=== PM4PY PARAMETER SENSITIVITY SWEEP ===")

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### 1. Sensitivity to Resource Identity Assumptions (Trusted vs. Shared Pool)

- **Cluster G5 (`analyst_pool`)**:
  - *If Resource ID is Trusted (Single Worker)*: Concurrency reaching 4 implies an individual human performing massive parallel multitasking across 4 complex root-cause analyses simultaneously. This leads to the conclusion of severe cognitive task-switching overhead and an apparent utilization of $233\%$.
  - *If Treated as Shared Pool ($M/M/c$ with $c \ge 3$)*: The 4 overlapping cases represent 3 to 4 distinct analysts working independently on single cases. Utilization per worker is a healthy $\approx 60-80\%$, individual service times (10–25 min) are accurate, and no multitasking penalty exists.
- **Cluster G6 (`T-F`)**:
  - *If Resource ID is Trusted*: T-F is a single worker performing administrative bulk ticket cleanup at shift end.
  - *If Shared Account*: The tickets could have been handled by multiple field engineers throughout the day, with an administrative clerk logging them under a single terminal account.

### 2. Sensitivity to Timestamp Precision & Interval Censoring

- **Cluster G7 (`legacy_MES`, 15-min precision)**:
  - *If Timestamps are Treated as Exact*: All 5 measurements occurred simultaneously between 14:00:00 and 14:15:00. This produces a direct logical contradiction: 4 cases arrived after their recorded start time ($s < t_{\text{queue}}$).
  - *If Treated as Interval-Censored ($[t - 7.5\text{m}, t + 7.5\text{m}]$)*: The true execution intervals $[\underline{s}_i, \overline{e}_i]$ fall within $[13:52:30, 14:22:30]$. Reconstructing the sequence using `recorded_at` (14:16, 14:17, 14:18, 14:19, 14:20) reveals 5 sequential measurements of $\approx 2-3\text{ minutes}$ each, resolving the causality paradox.
- **Clusters G2 and G3 (1-min precision)**:
  - The $\pm 30\text{s}$ boundary censoring does not mask the macroscopic step pattern ($6-8\text{ min}$ increments), proving that UI affordances ("begin group" and bulk folder submit), not rounding noise, generated the batch patterns.
