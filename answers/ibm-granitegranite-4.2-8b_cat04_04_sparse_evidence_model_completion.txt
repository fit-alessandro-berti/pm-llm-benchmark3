> **Why DFG bidirectional edges do not prove concurrency**  
> The DFG shows both `("Credit Check", "Engineering Review"): 17` and `("Engineering Review", "Credit Check"): 11`. This reflects that in some traces Credit Check precedes Engineering Review and in others the opposite order occurs. It does **not** indicate that the two activities often finish at the same time; it merely records the observed precedence pattern across the 30 traces. Without timestamps that differentiate simultaneity from ordering due to ingestion index, we cannot claim that the two checks are truly concurrent (i.e., overlapping in time). Hence models must avoid statements like “Credit Check and Engineering Review always run in parallel”; instead they may allow either order or a partial order that permits concurrency but does not assert it definitively.

# 2. Shared Assumptions  

All three models share the following explicit assumptions (used consistently across candidates):

1. **Event semantics** – Each activity corresponds to a completion event; no intermediate states are logged. The log entry order reflects the chronological order of completions, with ties broken by ingestion index (arbitrary but consistent). No hidden parallelism is assumed beyond what can be inferred from order.

2. **Missing Supplier Confirmation** – About 5–10% of Supplier Confirmation events are absent from the log due to an integration issue (per S5). The log therefore never contains a Supplier Confirmation event for some external orders that truly require it; the omission is systematic but not linked to component type in the current data. Models treat Supplier Confirmation as *optional* and do **not** attempt to recover the missing attribute at the modeling level.

3. **Optional nature of Supplier Confirmation** – Per S3 & S4, Supplier Confirmation is mandatory for external components and can be legitimately skipped for stocked components. Since the external/stocked attribute is unavailable, the model must allow Supplier Confirmation to be present or absent without requiring additional conditions other than “after Register Order” and “before Approve Configuration”. No model will require Supplier Confirmation to wait for both checks (D5 is respected as a lower bound only when Supplier Confirmation is present).

4. **Optionality of Expedite Review** – Expedite Review occurs at most once and must be preceded by completion of both Credit Check and Engineering Review (D6). It may appear before or after Supplier Confirmation when both are present (per S6). Models will enforce the “both checks  Expedite Review” constraint; the relative order with Supplier Confirmation is left flexible (i.e., not fixed unless the candidate chooses to impose an order).

5. **Optionality representation** –  
   - In ProcessTree models, optional nodes are represented using `Xor` branches where one branch is “skip” (silent) and the other is the activity. Silent branches emit no event and can be omitted in a trace.  
   - In POWL models, optional activities are included as regular nodes but without mandatory precedence links that would force their appearance; the partial order only contains mandatory precedence edges. A trace that omits an optional node simply does not contain that node; the model’s language therefore includes both versions (with and without the optional node).  
   - No model will generate traces that contain Supplier Confirmation or Expedite Review when the corresponding node is omitted; omission is modelled explicitly.

6. **Start/End constraints** – Every trace must begin with `Register Order` and end with `Archive Case`. This follows D1 and is enforced in all candidates.

7. **Fitnessaware uncertainty handling** – Balanced and Permissive models include explicit “mayormaynot” annotations (in comments) for uncertain constraints (e.g., “Supplier Confirmation may appear after only one of the two checks”). Conservative models explicitly exclude any such conditional behaviour unless directly observed.

8. **Trace consistency with observed variants** – All three models are constructed solely from the 30 observed variant strings; no external domain knowledge beyond the listed rules is used to invent new activities or reorder existing ones.

# 3. Conservative Candidate  

- **Inductivebias statement** – The conservative candidate admits only behaviour that is directly observed in the 30 traces or that follows highconfidence mandatory rules (D1–D2, D3, D4, D6). It does **not** assume concurrency between Credit Check and Engineering Review beyond the observed two possible orders, does **not** allow Supplier Confirmation to appear before both checks (i.e., it requires at least one check to precede Supplier Confirmation when Supplier Confirmation is present), and does **not** allow Expedite Review to appear before both checks are completed. All optional nodes are placed in explicit `Xor` branches with a “skip” option, ensuring that any trace produced by the model is either exactly one of the observed variant patterns (including the omission of Supplier Confirmation or Expedite Review) or a trace that respects the conditional lower bound “Supplier Confirmation after at least one of Credit Check/Engineering Review”. No unobserved interleaving (e.g., Supplier Confirmation immediately after Register Order, or Expedite Review before the first check) is admitted.

- **Compact formal expression** (PM4Py ProcessTree string, using `Xor` for optional nodes and silent skip):  

  ```text
  Register Order
      Xor(
          Branch: Credit Check -> 
              Xor(
                  Branch: Supplier Confirmation -> Engineering Review -> Approve Configuration
                  Branch: Engineering Review -> Approve Configuration
              )
          ,
          Branch: Engineering Review -> 
              Xor(
                  Branch: Supplier Confirmation -> Credit Check -> Approve Configuration
                  Branch: Credit Check -> Approve Configuration
              )
      )
      After the second check (i.e., after the node that completes the pair), insert an optional Expedite Review branch that can appear before or after Supplier Confirmation (if Supplier Confirmation was taken) but always before Approve Configuration. Implemented as a local Xor after the second check:
          Xor(
              Branch: Expedite Review -> Approve Configuration
              Branch: Approve Configuration
          )
  ```

  In code this tree is built with pm4py’s `Operator` objects; silent “skip” branches are represented by an empty `Xor` that emits no event.

- **Selfcontained PM4Py code assigning the model to `model_conservative`**:

- **Behavior deliberately excluded**  
  1. Supplier Confirmation appearing immediately after Register Order (i.e., before either Credit Check or Engineering Review).  
  2. Expedite Review appearing before both Credit Check and Engineering Review are completed.  
  3. Simultaneous appearance of both Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review where Supplier Confirmation precedes both checks (i.e., Supplier Confirmation before the first check).  
  4. Any trace that contains Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review in the same branch without the required “after second check” XOR placement (the model forces Expedite Review to appear after the second check).  
  5. Traces that contain both Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review in an order that is neither observed (e.g., Supplier Confirmation  Expedite Review  Credit Check) – only orders consistent with at least one check before Supplier Confirmation and both checks before Expedite Review are admitted.

- **Known underfitting risks**  
  1. The model assumes that Supplier Confirmation never appears before the first check; if external orders sometimes start Supplier Confirmation in parallel with the first check (e.g., concurrently with Credit Check), those traces would be rejected even though they could be reasonable.  
  2. By fixing Expedite Review after the second check, the model cannot generate traces where Expedite Review starts as soon as the second check finishes (which is already enforced) but also cannot allow Expedite Review to be interleaved between the two checks (e.g., Credit Check  Expedite Review  Engineering Review). Such interleavings are not observed but could be plausible under urgent conditions; they are excluded to stay conservative.  
  3. The XOR branching for Credit Check vs Engineering Review forces a strict choice of order; while observed variants include both orders, the model does not allow a trace where both checks are partially overlapping in a way that would produce the same linear log with Credit Check and Engineering Review adjacent but indistinguishable (e.g., simultaneous completions logged in arbitrary order). This is acceptable because the log cannot resolve simultaneity; the model simply picks one order per execution, which matches observed behaviour.

# 4. Balanced Candidate  

- **Inductivebias statement** – The balanced candidate seeks a compact, generalizable description that respects all highconfidence mandatory constraints (D1–D2, D3, D4, D6) while explicitly managing uncertainty about the relative timing of Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review with respect to Credit Check and Engineering Review. It allows Credit Check and Engineering Review to be concurrent (no mandatory precedence between them), permits Supplier Confirmation to appear after at least one of the two checks (reflecting D5) but does not require it to wait for both, and permits Expedite Review to appear after both checks (mandatory) but in either order relative to Supplier Confirmation when both are present. Uncertainty is encoded through a strict partial order (POWL) that contains only mandatory precedence edges; optional nodes are included without extra edges, meaning the partial order language includes both versions (with and without the optional node). This avoids overcommitting to a particular interleaving while still providing a structured model that can be checked for soundness and fitness.

- **Compact formal expression** (POWL strict partial order description):  

  Nodes: `Register Order`, `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`, `Expedite Review`, `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production`, `Archive Case`.  

  Mandatory edges (strict):  

  ```
  Register Order  Credit Check
  Register Order  Engineering Review
  Register Order  Supplier Confirmation      (Supplier Confirmation always after order registration)
  Register Order  Expedite Review           (Expedite Review always after order registration)

  Credit Check  Approve Configuration
  Engineering Review  Approve Configuration
  Supplier Confirmation  Approve Configuration   (when present)
  Expedite Review  Approve Configuration        (when present)

  Approve Configuration  Release Production
  Release Production  Archive Case

  Credit Check  Expedite Review
  Engineering Review  Expedite Review        (both checks must finish before Expedite Review starts)
  ```

  *No edge between Credit Check  Engineering Review, nor between Supplier Confirmation  any check (except Register Order).*

  This partial order respects D3 (both checks before Approve), D4 (Supplier Confirmation before Approve), D6 (both checks before Expedite Review), and D2 (Approve  Release  Archive). It does **not** impose `Supplier Confirmation  Credit Check` or `Supplier Confirmation  Engineering Review`, thereby allowing Supplier Confirmation to appear after the first check and before the second (observed) as well as after both checks. It also does **not** impose an edge between Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review, allowing either order when both are present.

- **How to decide between sequence, concurrency, and partial order for Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, and Expedite Review**  

  1. **Credit Check  Engineering Review** – Both highfrequency opposite orders are observed (V1 vs V2). No highconfidence rule forces one before the other; D3 only requires *both* before Approve. Therefore the balanced model treats them as **concurrent** (no precedence edge) and allows either order in a trace. The partial order contains only `Register Order  Credit Check` and `Register Order  Engineering Review`; thus traces with Credit Check before Engineering Review or viceversa are both possible.

  2. **Supplier Confirmation** – D5 says it may begin after *either* Credit Check or Engineering Review has produced enough information; it need not wait for both. The conservative rule (Supplier Confirmation after at least one check) is respected. The balanced model does **not** add an edge from Supplier Confirmation to either check, allowing Supplier Confirmation to appear after Register Order, after the first check (and possibly before the second), or after both checks. Thus the partial order permits three kinds of relative placement: (i) Supplier Confirmation immediately after Register Order (if the first check is concurrent and Supplier Confirmation starts early), (ii) Supplier Confirmation after the first check and before the second, and (iii) Supplier Confirmation after both checks. All are consistent with observed variants and D5.

  3. **Expedite Review** – D6 mandates completion of both Credit Check and Engineering Review before Expedite Review starts. Therefore the balanced model adds mandatory edges `Credit Check  Expedite Review` and `Engineering Review  Expedite Review`. No edge between Expedite Review and Supplier Confirmation is added, so when both Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review are present they can appear in either order relative to each other (consistent with S6).

  4. **Partial order decision** – Because the only certain constraints are those derived from highconfidence rules (D1–D2, D3, D4, D6) and the “at least one check precedes Supplier Confirmation” lower bound (which is already satisfied by the lack of a mandatory edge from Supplier Confirmation to the checks), the balanced model uses a **strict partial order** that encodes only mandatory edges. This yields a language that includes all observed traces, admits plausible unobserved interleavings (e.g., Supplier Confirmation right after Register Order), and excludes clearly violated behaviours (e.g., Expedite Review before either check). The decision avoids overspecifying concurrency (which would require picking a linear order) while still providing a structured representation amenable to soundness and precision checks.

- **List of behavior deliberately excluded** (balanced model excludes only the clearly contradictory behaviours):  

  1. Expedite Review appearing before **either** Credit Check or Engineering Review is completed (violates D6).  
  2. Supplier Confirmation appearing before Register Order (violates D1 start).  
  3. Release Production appearing before Approve Configuration (violates D2).  
  4. Archive Case appearing before Release Production (violates end order).  
  All other combinations of Credit Check  Engineering Review order, placement of Supplier Confirmation relative to checks, and order between Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review are allowed when consistent with the mandatory edges.

- **Known underfitting risks**  

  1. The balanced model does **not** encode the conditional “Supplier Confirmation after at least one check” as a formal constraint; it only omits edges from Supplier Confirmation to the checks. Consequently it could theoretically generate a trace where Supplier Confirmation appears before **both** checks (i.e., immediately after Register Order) even for external components where a check is genuinely required before confirmation. While such a trace might be plausible in rare urgent cases, it is less likely than the observed pattern (Supplier Confirmation usually after a check). This slight overgenerality is acceptable for the balanced candidate because it respects uncertainty and aims for reasonable generalization.  
  2. By using a strict partial order without explicit “optional” branches, the model’s language includes traces that contain both Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review *and* also contain both checks in an order that respects the mandatory edges but could produce a trace where Supplier Confirmation appears after Expedite Review even though D5 suggests Supplier Confirmation may need check information. This is allowed (consistent with S6) and thus not a risk.  
  3. The model does not capture the possible 5–10% missing Supplier Confirmation due to integration issues at the trace level; it assumes Supplier Confirmation is always present if the node is emitted. In validation we will test omission traces explicitly, and the model will accept traces without Supplier Confirmation because the partial order does not require it (no incoming edge from checks to Supplier Confirmation). Thus underfitting in terms of missingness is not an issue; however the model cannot explain *why* a Supplier Confirmation is missing (which is fine).

# 5. Permissive Candidate  

- **Inductivebias statement** – The permissive candidate admits all behaviours that are consistent with the broad domain rules (highconfidence mandatory constraints D1–D2, D3, D4, D6) **and** with the most generous interpretation of optional timing constraints: Supplier Confirmation may appear as soon as Register Order (i.e., before either Credit Check or Engineering Review) if the component type were external and the check information is deemed sufficient; Expedite Review still requires completion of both checks but may appear before or after Supplier Confirmation without additional restrictions. The model intentionally relaxes the “at least one check precedes Supplier Confirmation” lower bound, allowing Supplier Confirmation to precede both checks in a trace (i.e., Register Order  Supplier Confirmation  Credit Check/Engineering Review). This reflects a reasonable generalization for urgent or parallel procurement actions that might start confirmation concurrently with credit/engineering checks. All other mandatory constraints remain enforced. The permissive model is expressed as a small ProcessTree that enumerates both possible orders of Credit Check/Engineering Review (XOR) and places Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review in optional branches that can appear anywhere after Register Order and before Approve Configuration, respecting the Expeditebothchecks rule.

- **Compact formal expression** (PM4Py ProcessTree string with optional nodes placed after Register Order and before Approve Configuration):  

  ```text
  Register Order
      Xor(
          Credit Check -> 
              Xor(Supplier Confirmation -> Engineering Review -> Approve Configuration,
                  Engineering Review -> Approve Configuration)
          ,
          Engineering Review -> 
              Xor(Supplier Confirmation -> Credit Check -> Approve Configuration,
                  Credit Check -> Approve Configuration)
      )
      After Register Order (i.e., at the root level after Register Order), insert an optional Supplier Confirmation branch that can fire before the XOR of checks or after the second check. Implemented as an Xor sibling of the XORchecks branch:
          Xor(
              Supplier Confirmation -> (followed by the existing XORchecks subtree),
              (the existing XORchecks subtree)
          )
      Similarly, after the second check (i.e., after the leaf that completes the pair), insert an optional Expedite Review branch that can appear before or after Supplier Confirmation (if Supplier Confirmation was taken) but always before Approve Configuration.
  ```

All traces that respect mandatory edges (Register Order first, Approve before Release, both checks before Approve, both checks before Expedite Review) are admitted.

- **Boundary between “reasonable generalization” and forbidden behavior**  

  - **Reasonable (admitted)**: Any trace that (i) starts with Register Order, (ii) ends with Archive Case after Release Production, (iii) contains both Credit Check and Engineering Review (in either order) before Approve Configuration, (iv) contains Supplier Confirmation only if it appears after Register Order (may appear before, between, or after the checks), (v) contains Expedite Review only if both checks are completed before it appears, and (vi) respects Approve  Release  Archive. No trace where Expedite Review appears before either check is admitted. No trace where Release Production appears before Approve is admitted.  

  - **Clearly forbidden (excluded)**: Expedite Review before either Credit Check or Engineering Review; Supplier Confirmation before Register Order; Release Production before Approve; Archive Case before Release. All other interleavings involving Supplier Confirmation and checks are allowed (including Supplier Confirmation immediately after Register Order).

- **List of behavior deliberately excluded**  

  1. Expedite Review before the completion of both Credit Check and Engineering Review (violates D6).  
  2. Release Production before Approve Configuration (violates D2).  
  3. Archive Case before Release Production (violates D1 end).  
  4. Supplier Confirmation before Register Order (log always starts with Register Order).  

  All other interleavings (e.g., Supplier Confirmation before both checks, Supplier Confirmation between checks, Expedite Review before or after Supplier Confirmation) are admitted if they satisfy the mandatory precedence constraints.

- **Known underfitting risks**  

  1. The permissive model admits traces where Supplier Confirmation appears immediately after Register Order even for external components where a check might be required before confirmation; this could overgenerate businessinfeasible traces (e.g., confirming a supplier without verifying credit). However, because the model respects the “optional” nature (Supplier Confirmation may be skipped), such traces are not mandatory and can be filtered later if compliance requires a check first.  
  2. By allowing Supplier Confirmation before both checks, the model does not encode D5’s “after either check has produced enough information” nuance; a trace where Supplier Confirmation appears before any check could be less realistic for some external components but remains reasonable for stocked components where confirmation may be already known. The permissive candidate intentionally leans toward maximal plausibility, accepting this modest risk.  
  3. The treebased representation may generate duplicate traces (e.g., Supplier Confirmation both present and absent in the same execution) if not careful with silent branches; the validation step will check that each generated trace contains at most one occurrence of each activity and respects optionality.

def evaluate_model(model_obj, log):
    """
    model_obj can be a ProcessTree (pm4py.process_tree.ProcessTree) or a StrictPartialOrder.
    Returns dict with fitness (alignments), precision (alignments), simplicity (value), and soundness flag.
    """
    # Convert ProcessTree or POWL to Petri net
    if isinstance(model_obj, pm4py.process_tree.ProcessTree):
        net = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_obj)
    else:  # StrictPartialOrder
        net = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_obj)  # pm4py supports conversion from strict partial order
    # Initialise markings
    im = pm4py.get_initial_marking(net)
    fm = pm4py.get_final_marking(net)
    # Soundness check
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, log)
    # Fitness: number of perfect alignments / number of traces (approximate)
    alignments = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    fitness = len([a for a in alignments if a['is_perfect']]) / len(log) if log else 0.0
    # Precision: number of imperfect alignments / number of traces (simple)
    imperfect = len([a for a in alignments if not a['is_perfect']]) / len(log) if log else 0.0
    # Simplicity (PM4Py provides a heuristic)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    return {
        "sound": sound,
        "fitness": fitness,
        "precision": imperfect,   # empirical precision approximation
        "simplicity": simplicity
    }

# 9. Recommendation and DataCollection Plan  

- **Immediate use:** `model_balanced` is recommended for routine workflow monitoring and compliance reporting. It satisfies all highconfidence mandatory constraints, admits all observed traces, and generates only plausible unobserved traces (e.g., Supplier Confirmation right after Register Order). Its partialorder representation is simple enough for domain stakeholders to understand and audit, while the explicit omission of unnecessary edges keeps precision risk moderate. Soundness checks will confirm that no trace violates D6 or D2, providing a safety net for compliance.

- **Sensitivity analysis:** `model_permissive` should be used for exploratory scenario analysis (e.g., “what if we allow Supplier Confirmation to start before either check?”) and for stresstesting the workflow under urgency conditions. By comparing fitness, precision, and trace language size between `balanced` and `permissive`, analysts can quantify how sensitive the model is to the relaxation of the “at least one check precedes Supplier Confirmation” rule.

- **Datacollection plan to resolve main ambiguities:**  

  1. **Capture component type** (external vs. stocked) at order registration. This will allow distinguishing when Supplier Confirmation is mandatory (external) vs. optional (stocked), resolving S4 vs. S3.  
  2. **Record a lightweight “checkstatus timestamp”** for Credit Check and Engineering Review indicating when each activity *started* (or at least when enough information was produced). This will enable observing whether Supplier Confirmation truly began after one check completed (supporting D5) or possibly concurrent.  
  3. **Log Expedite Review eligibility** (a flag indicating whether a request for expedited review was raised and when). This will clarify whether Expedite Review occurs before or after Supplier Confirmation in urgent cases.  
  4. **Include a “Supplier Confirmation present/absent” flag** in the log (if integration issue persists, also capture “Supplier Confirmation missing due to integration” vs. “Supplier Confirmation skipped due to stocked component”). This helps quantify the 5–10% missing rate and differentiate omission from intentional skip.  
  5. **Collect up to 20–30 new traces** that include at least one of the following unobserved patterns:  
     - Supplier Confirmation immediately after Register Order (no check yet).  
     - Expedite Review before Supplier Confirmation while both checks are already done.  
     - Supplier Confirmation after only the first check (already observed) plus a new pattern where Supplier Confirmation appears before the second check in the opposite branch (e.g., Register Order  Supplier Confirmation  Engineering Review  Credit Check).  
     These traces will directly test the boundary between “reasonable” and “clearly undesirable” for each model.  

  6. **Validation loop:** After collecting the new traces, recompute fitness, precision, and simplicity for all three candidates. Compare the number of unobserved but admitted traces (permissive) versus rejected traces (conservative). Use the manual positive/negative suite expanded with the newly collected traces. If the balanced model’s precision (fraction of imperfect alignments) remains low (<0.1) while still admitting all observed and plausible traces, it continues to be suitable for compliance. If precision rises substantially, consider tightening the optional Supplier Confirmation placement (e.g., require Supplier Confirmation after at least one check) in the balanced model.

Confidence in these directional claims is moderate because only singledirection stakeholder statements exist.

- **Plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation:**  

  If future logs consistently show Supplier Confirmation occurring **before** the first of Credit Check/Engineering Review (i.e., Register Order  Supplier Confirmation  Credit Check/Engineering Review), then the “at least one check precedes Supplier Confirmation” rule (D5) would be violated in practice. In that case the conservative model (which requires Supplier Confirmation after at least one check) would reject many real traces, reducing its fitness. The balanced model would still be acceptable (it allows Supplier Confirmation after Register Order), while the permissive model would remain valid. Conversely, if logs almost never show Supplier Confirmation before both checks, the permissive model’s relaxation would lead to higher precision risk (generating many spurious traces that never occur), making the conservative or balanced model preferable.  

  Therefore the recommendation of `model_balanced` as the default is reasonable under current evidence, but should be reevaluated after collecting a few traces of the “Supplier Confirmation early” pattern.
