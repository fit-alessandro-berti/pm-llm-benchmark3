## 1. Feasibility model

```text
PO (≺ precedence, || allowed concurrency):

Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check                    (if originals_required = no)
Triage ≺ Originals Receipt                 (if originals_required = yes)
Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check         (if originals_required = yes)
Identity Check || Document Check            (allowed only when originals_required = no)

{Identity Check, Document Check} ≺ Assessment[k]            (k = 1..n)
Assessment[k] ≺ Request More Info[k]                         (optional)
Request More Info[k] ≺ Document Check[k+1] ≺ Assessment[k+1]

Assessment[k_final] ≺ Compliance Review[k_final]             (if high-risk OR high-value)
Compliance Review[k_final] ≺ Decision ≺ Notify

Triage ≺ Escalation Advice                                    (optional; can be early or post-assessment)
Triage ≺ Preliminary Compliance Screen                         (optional; prep only)
```

```text
- Rule 1/8: Register is first; Decision precedes Notify.
- Rule 2: Identity Check and the latest Document Check must both complete before each Assessment.
- Rule 3/4: If originals_required=no, Identity Check and Document Check may overlap; if yes, Originals Receipt must precede every Document Check (Identity Check may still occur earlier).
- Rule 5/10: If high-risk or high-value, a final Compliance Review must occur after the final Assessment and before Decision; any preliminary compliance work does not replace it.
- Rule 6/7: If Request More Info happens, then a new Document Check and a new Assessment are required before any Decision; if high-risk/high-value, the final Compliance Review must be redone or explicitly reaffirmed after that last Assessment.
- Rule 9 (four-eyes): resource(final Assessment) ≠ resource(final Compliance Review).
- Rule 11: Each resource is single-tasking (no overlapping service intervals for the same person).
- Logging constraint (K-411): timestamps are entered in real time; no batch backfill that would fabricate concurrency.
```

- K-104 alters **timing/scheduling** (adds early prep and a reservation/priority policy), not routing or decision policy.
- K-219 alters **routing/resource assignment** (D1 -> D2) and therefore timing and rework risk.
- K-337 alters the **allowed decision policy/control-flow** (forbids a rework loop and forces an explicit decision under residual discrepancy).
- K-411 alters **timing/control-flow** (enables concurrency) and **logging**, not routing or decision policy.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The triage signal (sanctions ambiguity) is present at Mon 08:20–08:50 but is not converted into early specialist work; the ambiguity is rediscovered at Assessment (Tue 15:10–19:10), then escalated (Tue 19:20–21:20), and only then does C1 enter, when C1’s backlog is still material. Because C1 starts late and without pre-reading, the compliance service time is closer to the “read from scratch” end of the range and is also exposed to queue timing (start Wed 13:10).

Minimal intervention: Immediately after Triage, send S1 a structured escalation packet (suspected sanctions match, corporate structure summary, key identifiers, doc list and gaps) and tentatively reserve a C1 slot after the expected assessment completion; use that reserved capacity for a short preliminary compliance screen (not the final review) while Identity/Document work is still queued/processing.

Temporal propagation: The intervention does not materially change the long Document Check wait on D1, so the critical path up to Assessment stays similar. What changes is readiness and duration at compliance: (i) C1 can pre-read stable evidence (registries, prior decisions, sanctions guidance) during upstream waiting, reducing the final Compliance Review service time by ~1–3h; (ii) a reservation/priority policy can shift the start of the final review earlier than Wed 13:10 once Assessment completes, but only to the extent C1 is not preempted by other urgent cases. Because final compliance must still follow the final Assessment, gains come from queue-position and reduced re-reading, not from “starting compliance before assessment.”

Process-feasibility check: Register -> Triage remains first; Identity and Document checks complete before Assessment; final Compliance Review still occurs after the final Assessment and before Decision; the four-eyes rule holds (A1 vs C1); any preliminary compliance screen is explicitly non-substitutive; no resource is scheduled to overlap with itself.

Best/base/worst case: Best: S1 responds early enough to focus the packet and C1 spends ~2h pre-reading; the reserved slot enables C1 to start the final review in the morning rather than midday, and the final review is shorter, yielding ~8–18h cycle-time reduction. Base: C1 prep saves ~1h and the reservation only modestly advances the start (~2–5h), yielding ~3–6h reduction. Worst: packet incompleteness makes prep unusable and urgent compliance arrivals break the reservation; net gain is ~0 and may even be slightly negative (~+0–4h) while other cases are displaced.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If C1 cannot reserve/priority-schedule work (strict FIFO, no preemption) or if preliminary packets rarely reduce final compliance time, the reservation becomes pure displacement; similarly, if sanctions ambiguity routinely triggers an external info request, the intervention could add rework rather than shorten the path.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Originals arrive Mon 17:30, but the Document Check waits for D1 until Tue 09:30, consistent with D1’s long queue early week. This idle period delays the moment the case can join the Assessment queue; once document work completes Tue 13:30, the case still waits behind A2’s queue until Tue 20:00, and manager decision happens the next morning.

Minimal intervention: As soon as Originals Receipt occurs, route the case to D2 rather than waiting for D1, keeping all other routing and compliance logic unchanged.

Temporal propagation: If D2’s queue at Mon evening is materially shorter than D1’s, D2 can start earlier, moving “documents complete” earlier even if D2’s service time is longer (handwritten work tends toward the upper end of D2’s range). That earlier readiness can translate into earlier Assessment only if A2’s queueing discipline allows earlier-ready cases to capture earlier service (rough FIFO) and if A2 has slack at that earlier time; otherwise the case simply waits longer in the Assessment queue and the end-to-end gain is capped. The main downside channel is rework: if D2 requests clarification on handwriting, Rule 7 forces a repeat Document Check and repeat Assessment, so a small increase in rework probability can dominate any earlier-start benefit.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check; Identity Check and the (latest) Document Check complete before Assessment; no compliance step is required for this low-risk/low-value case; if Request More Info occurs, the required repeat Document Check and Assessment are included before Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best: D2 starts within a few hours of Mon 17:30, completes overnight, Assessment pulls into an earlier Tue slot, and M1 signs the same day, yielding ~14–24h reduction. Base: D2 starts late evening, completes early Tue, but Assessment still occurs Tue evening and M1 still signs Wed morning, yielding ~4–10h reduction. Worst: D2 triggers Request More Info and external response takes 8–36h, forcing another Document Check and Assessment and yielding +1–2 days with increased error/clarification risk.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If D2’s realized backlog is worse than D1’s on Mon evening, or if M1 decisions are effectively batched to fixed windows (making earlier readiness irrelevant), the cycle-time improvement disappears; conversely, if handwriting difficulty is overstated and D2 performs comparably to D1, routing to D2 becomes much more robust.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The case’s duration is dominated by two rework loops driven by supplier inconsistencies; the second Request More Info spans Fri-to-Mon, creating a weekend delay that then cascades into an additional Document Check and Assessment iteration before compliance and decision. The loop structure is compliant but converts external latency into long internal idle time and repeated D1/A1 utilization.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 (Fri 08:00–10:30), disallow a second Request More Info and require the organization to decide explicitly under the residual discrepancy, either rejecting or approving with recorded conditions/controls; then perform the required final Compliance Review (since the case is high-risk/high-value) before Decision.

Temporal propagation: Removing the second external loop eliminates (i) the Fri-to-Mon external wait and (ii) the subsequent internal work on Document Check 3 and Assessment 3, so the case becomes “decision-eligible” earlier (immediately after Assessment 2 plus compliance). However, some of that saved time can reappear as waiting in different queues: the case now enters the Compliance queue earlier (Fri) when C1 availability is uncertain, and ambiguous evidence may lengthen the Compliance Review and manager deliberation. The net improvement therefore depends on whether the system is constrained by external response time (highly improved) or by C1/M1 capacity (less improved).

Process-feasibility check: The final Compliance Review still follows the final Assessment (now Assessment 2) and precedes Decision; four-eyes is preserved (A1 vs C1). The intervention explicitly changes decision policy (it forbids a control-flow loop) but does not waive mandatory compliance; the decision must document how the unresolved discrepancy is handled (conditions or rejection), satisfying the “no silent ignore” requirement.

Best/base/worst case: Best: C1 can start Friday afternoon and M1 can decide before the weekend, yielding ~4–5 days reduction but a higher probability of conditional approval or rejection. Base: compliance occurs Friday or Monday with decision Monday/Tuesday, yielding ~1–3 days reduction with conditional approval being common. Worst: C1 refuses to sign without confirmation (forcing rejection or escalation outside the modeled activities) or C1/M1 backlog is dominant, so time gain is small while the quality/compliance risk is high.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If regulatory or contractual policy requires complete supplier confirmation (making a forced decision noncompliant), or if empirical data show second requests nearly always resolve cheaply and prevent major losses, then suppressing the loop is value-destructive; likewise, if C1’s queue is structurally the binding constraint, the cycle-time benefit may be much smaller than the “removed weekend wait” suggests.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The case lock prevents creation/ownership of the document task until identity completes, forcing sequential verification even though originals are not required and concurrency is allowed. This delays Document Check start to 15:00 and shifts the completion of both checks later, after which the case waits for an already grown assessment queue.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can accept and work their tasks concurrently; require real-time timestamping to ensure measured overlap corresponds to true overlap.

Temporal propagation: By allowing D2 to start while I2 is working (or waiting) on identity, the completion time of “both checks done” can move earlier by a few hours (bounded by D2’s queue and service time). Whether that converts into earlier end-to-end completion depends on A2: if A2 is the binding bottleneck, earlier readiness mostly increases time spent waiting in the Assessment queue and the net cycle-time gain is small. The logging requirement does not change physical flow, but it materially improves the ability to detect bottleneck migration (e.g., more cases becoming assessment-ready earlier without an increase in assessor throughput).

Process-feasibility check: Because originals are not required, concurrent Identity Check and Document Check is permitted; both still complete before Assessment; Decision precedes Notify; no new compliance step is introduced; resource single-tasking is preserved and timestamps reflect actual work times (not later batch entry).

Best/base/worst case: Best: D2 starts near the same window as I2 and completes early enough that the case enters the assessment queue earlier and captures an earlier A2 slot, yielding ~2–8h reduction. Base: D2 is still queue-limited and Assessment timing is unchanged, yielding ~0–3h reduction but better timestamp fidelity. Worst: workaround adds coordination overhead or induces logging errors; earlier readiness increases assessment backlog for other cases, and net cycle time is ~0 or slightly worse while analytics degrade.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If internal policy actually requires identity to complete before any document work (not stated), or if A2 scheduling is effectively batched/fixed so earlier readiness cannot advance service, the benefit becomes purely data-quality; if timestamp backfill persists, the intervention can appear successful in logs while not improving throughput.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation + provisional C1 reservation | +S1 early advisory time; +C1 preliminary screen; potentially -C1 final review time (prep saves re-reading) but +scheduling overhead | (For treated cases) compliance waiting time if reservation works | (For others) compliance queue if reservations/priority displace them; possibly S1 queue | Other high-risk/high-value cases needing C1; urgent compliance arrivals | Higher variance in C1 wait times; displacement shows as longer tails for non-target cases | Once C1 utilization approaches saturation, small reservation increases can cause disproportionate waiting-time growth (priority/displacement feedback) |
| K-219 route documents to D2 after originals | Shift workload D1 -> D2; per-case doc service time may increase; rework risk can add extra Document+Assessment iterations | D1 queue (and D1 availability for complex corporate cases) | D2 queue; potentially Assessment queue if rework rises | Cases that rely on D2 for standard digital work; cases previously benefiting from D2’s shorter queue | Bottleneck can migrate from D1 to D2; rework loops create bursty downstream demand | Rework probability is multiplicative: small increases can explode total work because each loop forces both Document Check and Assessment again |

At 100 similar cases/week, the interventions stop being “local” because they change who waits behind whom. For K-104, reserving C1 time behaves like preemption: every reserved hour is an hour someone else cannot use. Unless reservations are capped and released early when packets are incomplete, the compliance queue for non-target cases grows and can even feed back to the target cases (missed slots, re-planning). The only way it scales is if the preliminary packet reliably reduces final-review service time (often 1–3h) enough to offset the blocked time; otherwise total C1 workload rises and waiting-time tails worsen.

K-219’s systematic substitution from D1 to D2 moves work to a slower server for handwriting-heavy cases. The initial benefit—starting earlier right after Originals Receipt—vanishes once D2 becomes the bottleneck, and any extra “Request More Info” probability is multiplicative: each loop forces another Document Check and Assessment, consuming scarce assessor hours and pushing other cases out. In that regime, congestion migrates from Documents to Assessment even if average document-start time improves. A rollout must therefore segment by document type and continuously compare D2’s queue advantage against its rework rate; otherwise you can worsen both cycle time and workload.

K-337 avoids rework and weekend external delays, freeing D1/A1 capacity, so Document and Assessment queues may shrink; but it pushes ambiguity downstream to C1 and M1. Reviews become harder and longer, conditional decisions rise, and post-decision exception handling can emerge as a hidden queue not represented in the event log. K-411 increases true concurrency in Identity/Documents and mainly pulls “ready-for-assessment” earlier; at scale this typically increases work-in-process arriving at Assessment, so the bottleneck migrates to A1/A2 unless assessor capacity or prioritization changes too. Finally, timestamp behavior matters: if the workaround leads to batch entry, apparent concurrency rises without real throughput gains, and queue forecasts or policy evaluations become biased.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-411**: it exploits already-permitted concurrency for non-originals cases and adds real-time timestamping. Its cycle-time gains are bounded by the assessment bottleneck, but its downside is mainly observable bottleneck migration (more cases becoming assessment-ready earlier) and data-quality risk if logging discipline fails; both are reversible and measurable. The most dangerous despite potential speed is **K-337**: it appears fast by deleting an external rework loop, but it changes the decision policy under residual discrepancy, raising compliance defensibility, loss exposure, and appeal/re-opening risk. Those downstream consequences can create hidden queues (audits, exceptions) that are not captured as simple event-log duration.

The local counterfactual that is least transportable to a system-wide policy is **K-104’s reservation of scarce C1 capacity**: a single-case reservation can advance one case with modest displacement, but at scale reservations become capacity subtraction on a single-server bottleneck and can increase queue tails for everyone, including the reserved cases when preemption and replanning occur. K-411 also becomes less “transportable” in the sense that its per-case benefit saturates once Assessment becomes the binding constraint, so single-case gains can vanish under rollout unless assessor capacity or prioritization changes.

Most valuable additional data are: per-resource calendars and shift boundaries; time-stamped queue length/work-in-process by pool; explicit “task created” timestamps (to separate readiness from logging lag); reservation/preemption logs for compliance; distributions of external response times with weekend effects; and rework triggers labeled by cause (handwriting ambiguity vs missing totals vs sanctions ambiguity). These enable separating true concurrency from apparent concurrency and quantifying where bottlenecks migrate under policy change.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The listed normative rules fully describe mandatory precedence/iteration constraints; “Escalation Advice” and “Preliminary Compliance Screen” are optional activities that do not replace required steps.
2. `temporal` — The provided service-time ranges are approximately applicable during this week and can be used as uncertainty bounds (not point estimates).
3. `resource` — Queue snapshots approximate start-of-service delays for each pool under roughly FIFO behavior, except where an intervention explicitly introduces priority/reservation.
4. `resource` — C1 can allocate limited preliminary work (order of 1–2h per treated case) without violating single-tasking, but only by displacing other work.
5. `behavioral` — D2 has a higher chance than D1 of requesting clarification on difficult handwriting, which can trigger a Rule-7 rework loop.
6. `quality` — Conditional approvals are permissible and can be issued without introducing additional unmodeled activities beyond Decision/Notify (conditions are recorded in the decision artifact).
7. `interference` — For single-case counterfactuals, other cases’ arrivals/priorities are held approximately fixed; displacement effects are treated qualitatively and then revisited in the 100-case rollout analysis.
8. `interference` — Under rollout, queueing is nonlinear near capacity: pulling work forward (parallelization/reservations) can migrate the bottleneck to Assessment or Compliance and can increase waiting-time variance even if mean service times do not change.
