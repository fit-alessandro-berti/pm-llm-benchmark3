# 1. Executive memo

**Decision: choose E, with a controlled experiment before any wider expansion.** Extend the simplified validate-and-schedule path to clearly identified simple, low-risk houses in a limited, monitored rollout. Retain coordinator ownership where there is adequate capacity and a named backup. Immediately suspend survey skipping for medium-risk addresses and activation after billing start; restore survey and permit checks for relevant apartments and older buildings. Do not scale the full Redesign R nationally.

The pilot demonstrates a real operational opportunity, but not a general improvement. Median duration fell from 14.2 to 10.8 days and cases with at least three handovers fell from 34% to 12%. Simple houses, 34% of pilot cases, improved on speed, first-time-right installation, and complaints. Meanwhile p90 and p95 rose to 38.5 and 62 days, first-time-right fell from 82% to 76%, second visits rose from 11% to 18%, and complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%. The visible cost reduction (€184 to €161) is insufficient evidence of savings: the incomplete estimate including complaint and revisit handling moves in the opposite direction (€198 to €207). Billing before successful activation and absent permit checks require immediate control, irrespective of average speed.

The pattern is consistent with a fast path that helps straightforward orders while moving work and delay into exceptions. Lower survey-team utilization and 92% second-visit-team utilization support that interpretation. Coordinators reduced handovers, yet 93% utilization in two regions and stalls during absence suggest fragile ownership. The pilot model's high fitness (0.94) does not validate those paths: its precision (0.51) and generalization (0.56) are weak, and alignments expose missing or out-of-order controls. These are associations and diagnostic signals, not proof that any single redesign component caused the harm. Pilot regions had a different building mix; risk attributes, start timestamps, and downstream costs are incomplete.

Use an eligible, stratified randomized test of survey skipping, with permit-sensitive cases excluded until requirements and risk classification are verified. Separately compare coordinator ownership with backed-up shared coverage. Track 30-day complaints and 60-day all-in costs by region and building-risk stratum; monitor activation-before-billing and required permit checks case by case. This preserves the measured simple-house benefit while limiting exposure where the pilot performed poorly. Set prospective stop rules for control breaches, complaints, repeat visits, and tail delays. Broader rollout should depend on matched or randomized segment results, complete downstream economics, and demonstrated capacity resilience—not the aggregate median alone.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

- **High fitness, low precision:** A permissive discovered model can replay 94% of observed pilot traces while allowing many combinations of skipped survey, manual activation, repeat visit, and early billing that rarely or should never occur. Precision of 0.51 and generalization of 0.56 weaken the claim that the fitted model describes a stable, disciplined process. The alignment deviations identify concrete sequencing concerns, not just an abstract score.
- **Better median, worse tail:** The merged validation/scheduling step speeds common simple-house cases. Medium-risk apartments and old buildings have much worse p90 outcomes, and failed-visit→second-visit delays grew where coordinators are heavily loaded. Their exceptions can worsen the upper tail while the larger fast group pulls down the median. Sparse start timestamps prevent clean separation of queue time from task time.
- **Fewer handovers, lower resilience:** One coordinator keeps context and reduces transfers; the working-together network also becomes centralized around coordinators and activation specialists. At 93% utilization, little spare capacity remains for exceptions or absence, so cases can stall despite fewer formal handovers.
- **Lower visible cost, higher downstream cost:** Skipping surveys and moving activation checks can remove upfront effort, while manual activation, second visits, complaints, and delayed resolution add later cost. The 60-day cost picture is incomplete, so €207 versus €198 is a warning estimate rather than a proven net loss.
- **Simple-house gains, apartment/old-building harm:** Low-risk houses have fewer dependencies and benefited across speed and quality measures. Apartments and older buildings are more likely to need surveys, permits, and activation troubleshooting. Their segment results and the DFG/alignments are consistent with an overbroad skip rule; differing pilot case mix and incomplete risk data prevent a firm causal attribution.

# 4. Decision alternatives

| Alternative | Advantage | Principal risk | Reasonable if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Scale all of R nationally** | Captures median-speed and handover gains quickly. | Exposes more customers to tail delays, rework, complaints, early billing, and possible missing required checks. | Controlled evidence shows acceptable outcomes in each risk segment, activation/permit gates work, full costs are favorable, and capacity is adequate. These conditions are not met now. |
| **B. Roll back to baseline** | Restores familiar controls and may reduce exception harm quickly. | Gives up demonstrated simple-house gains and improved case ownership. | Corrected controls and targeted deployment still fail, or severe harm cannot be contained while learning. |
| **C. Scale ownership only; suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation** | Preserves fewer handovers while removing the most concerning shortcuts. | Ownership may still centralize bottlenecks; blanket use may miss the simple-house scheduling benefit. | Backup coverage and capacity tests show ownership works independently of the other changes. |
| **D. Controlled regional experiment** | Separates component and segment effects under better measurement. | Delays wider benefits and requires enough follow-up for complaints and cost. | Confounding is too large for a rollout decision; use safety gates and continue the low-risk path only within controlled limits. |
| **E. Targeted hybrid plus measurement** | Retains the strongest segment benefit while restoring checks for higher-risk cases and generating evidence for expansion. | Requires reliable risk identification and more complex routing and monitoring. | Risk classification, control gates, staffing, and prospective stop rules can be enforced. **Preferred now.** |

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

Ranked by likely effect on the next rollout decision, speed, and ability to act. Run fast controls and data collection concurrently; rank does not imply waiting for one study to finish before starting another.

| Rank | Measure/experiment | Uncertainty resolved and possible decision change |
|---|---|
| **1 — M7** | Case-level conformance monitoring quantifies billing before successful activation and absent required permit checks by segment. Any confirmed control breach triggers correction or a pause of the affected path immediately; sustained compliance is a prerequisite for expansion. |
| **2 — M1** | Randomize survey skipping within eligible building-risk strata and regions, excluding permit-sensitive cases until requirements are clear. The causal effect on first-time-right, p90/p95, second visits, and complaints could justify narrow use or permanent suspension of skipping. |
| **3 — M4** | Capture complete 60-day costs after billing, including revisits and complaints. A favorable all-in result supports expansion; a persistent increase could erase the apparent €23 upfront saving and favor C or narrower E. |
| **4 — M6** | Validate the predictive building-risk rule against field-survey findings, including false-low-risk rates. Poor calibration would narrow eligibility or end skipping even if some average test results look good; strong calibration helps define safe strata for M1 and rollout. |
| **5 — M5** | Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team or backed-up ownership when coordinators are absent. If backup retains speed and removes stalls, extend ownership; if not, limit it or redesign coverage. |
| **6 — M3** | Structured reasons for failed visits, manual activation, complaints, and second visits identify which exception mechanisms dominate. This directs fixes and can distinguish survey, capacity, customer, and activation problems, though reason codes alone do not establish causality. |
| **7 — M2** | Start timestamps for installation, activation test, and manual activation separate active work from waiting. This would determine whether capacity or task redesign best addresses the tail, but has less immediate stop/go value than outcomes and control checks. |
| **8 — M8** | Separate appointment-speed and working-service experience measures test whether improved access offsets post-installation frustration. This helps tune customer priorities and communication; it is unlikely to justify unsafe sequencing or high rework by itself. |

# 6. Final recommendation

**Adopt E now, with C's control safeguards and D's experimental discipline.**

- **Immediate action:** Enforce successful activation before billing and required permit checks; audit and correct affected pilot cases. Set case-level alerts and stop rules. Give coordinators a backup and cap intake where utilization leaves no exception capacity.
- **Pause:** Survey skipping for medium-risk and permit-sensitive addresses, post-billing activation, and national rollout of the full redesign. Route apartments and older buildings through surveys/permit checks according to verified risk and requirements.
- **Scale:** Expand the merged validate/schedule path only for verified simple, low-risk houses in a limited phased rollout. Extend coordinator ownership only with coverage, capacity, and measured outcomes.
- **Measure next:** Start M7 and M3 immediately; launch M1 and M6 for eligible risk strata, M5 for ownership resilience, and M4 with complete 60-day follow-up. Add M2 and M8 to explain delays and customer experience. Compare contemporaneous, risk-stratified cohorts; report intention-to-treat results and 30-day complaints, not just completed-case medians.
- **Broader-rollout gate:** Predefine segment-specific success against matched or randomized controls: no billing before successful activation or missing required permit checks; no material worsening of p90/p95, first-time-right, 30-day complaints, SLA breaches, or 60-day all-in cost; sustained simple-house speed gains; and reliable backup coverage without 93% coordinator or 92% second-visit bottlenecks. Require enough follow-up and precision to rule out operationally meaningful harm, especially in apartments and old buildings. Expand only into strata that pass those gates, with the same monitoring and stop rules.
