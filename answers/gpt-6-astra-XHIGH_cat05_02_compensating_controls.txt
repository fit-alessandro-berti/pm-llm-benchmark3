## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 separates initiation from authorization so that one person cannot independently cause an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. Different user names establish recorded separation, not necessarily independent judgment or authentic human identity. Conversely, a same-user payment can face an effective independent decision before funds become irrevocable.

| Objective or threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent unilateral initiation and authorization | Prevents specified invalid transactions, but is not independent human authorization and can be overridden. | Prevents unilateral commitment when an independent manager substantively reviews and can reject the payment before irrevocability. | Detects unauthorized handling afterward; corrects only if stopping, recall, or recovery remains feasible. | Conditionally permits one operator while retaining independent managerial approval and audit. It is not an additional independent actor. |
| Prevent duplicate, excessive, or basic PO-inconsistent payments | Direct preventive coverage for duplicates, operator limits, and basic PO matching, subject to configuration and override controls. | Can independently challenge invoice, amount, and supporting documentation. | Detects these defects; correction depends on timing and bank action. | Requires a full, unoverridden AV-2 pass and an amount below EUR 100,000, plus manager approval and audit. |
| Prevent unauthorized payees, recent bank substitution, and sanctions exposure | Checks sanctions, ownership, and recent changes. Does not establish who actually controls a compromised account. | Can independently verify payment instructions and challenge changes; consulting the same compromised source is weaker. | Can detect discrepancies, but potentially after irreversible loss. | Restricts recent bank changes and requires an established vendor and functioning validation; it does not eliminate account compromise. |
| Prevent payment for nonexistent goods/services or fabricated commercial support | Does not establish receipt or commercial reasonableness. A basic PO match cannot fill this gap. | Substantive invoice, bank, and receipt review can prevent unsupported payment. Doubtful receipts need independent operational corroboration. | Can uncover fabricated receipts or absent delivery. Late discovery does not reverse settlement. | Inherits MG-4 and RA-7 coverage. Urgency and a clean automated pass do not themselves prove delivery or reasonable pricing. |
| Ensure authentic, accountable authorization despite credential misuse | Does not identify the human controlling a compromised account. | Strong only when the actual manager authenticates and decides independently. An operator using a manager credential adds no independent approval. | Can detect sharing or impersonation if identity, session, and access records are reliable. Recovery remains conditional. | Requires an independent manager. A token or shared service account cannot supply a second human actor. |
| Resist collusion, coercion, conflicts, and rubber-stamping | Does not detect collusion, although it can still stop covered defects. | Independence and substantive challenge help; a colluding, conflicted, or passive manager defeats the safeguard. | Independent audit and cross-case analysis can detect coordinated behavior, subject to audit independence and evidence quality. | Inherits the components' weaknesses. Several events within the same compromised relationship do not create independent protections. |
| Prevent emergency privileges becoming routine bypasses | Does not establish necessity or correct token timing. | Can challenge the emergency and withhold approval while intervention remains possible. | Detects convenience use, late tokens, recurring exceptions, and unmet conditions. | Its conjunctive preconditions form a preventive permission boundary when enforced. Retroactive documentation does not prove prior activation. |
| Limit irreversible exposure and enable correction | Blocks covered defects before release. | Prevents final loss where approval or rejection controls a genuine stop point. | Detective first; partly corrective only where investigation and action finish while stopping or recovery remains feasible. | Requires pre-settlement manager approval and next-business-day audit, but does not guarantee recall after settlement. |

ED-1 is a permission composed of conditions, not a separate control whose benefit should be counted again alongside AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7. A correct outcome does not prove a sufficient package, and a suspicious pattern does not prove fraud.

The audit and recovery clocks also differ. Completion within one business day is not synonymous with action within 24 elapsed hours. Settlement is normally the internal-control point of irrevocability. The generic statement about usual recall within 24 hours does not demonstrate that a particular settled payment remained recoverable.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Use the following structured test:

1. **Identify the exposure and final decision point.** Establish whether payment completed, which error or fraud could occur, and when execution became irrevocable. A blocked attempt is outside the completed-payment scope of FEP-1.
2. **Separate formal eligibility from substantive protection.** Record nominal user separation and test all seven ED-1 conditions. Distinguish known failures from missing evidence. A real emergency does not waive a failed precondition.
3. **Establish authentic independence and authority.** Identify the humans behind accounts; verify L3+ manager authority, conflicts, and the ability to refuse payment. A reporting relationship merits examination but is not automatically a disqualifying conflict.
4. **Map coverage to material threats.** Examine invoice, receipt, amount, payee, bank details, account control, and commercial basis. A full AV-2 pass supports only its stated checks. Two reviewers relying uncritically on the same fabricated receipt share a failure mode.
5. **Test timing and enforceability.** Prefer independent review before release. Post-release approval can protect final commitment only if rejection can stop settlement. Approval must cover the final instruction; subsequent material changes should invalidate it.
6. **Test overrideability and common control.** Determine who can disable validation, override warnings, create tokens, change instructions, or use another person's credentials. Several recorded approvals are weak if one operator can manufacture them.
7. **Evaluate detection and correction separately.** Verify audit completion, full coverage, depth, independence, escalation latency, and actual banking cutoffs. An audit started near a deadline may leave insufficient time to act.
8. **Judge residual risk against an explicit acceptance standard.** Consider potential loss, systemic scope, recurrence, uncertainty, and collusion resistance, using transaction-linked evidence rather than activity counts.

| Package assessment | Sufficiency standard |
|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent** | Comparable coverage of material threats, authentic independent authorization, enforceable timing before irrevocability, protected overrides, and reliable evidence. Applicable exception conditions are demonstrated. Equivalence does not mean immunity to all fraud; ordinary four-eyes review also has limits. |
| **Partially equivalent** | Substantive independent protection exists, but a material coverage, timing, authority, or assurance gap remains. Bounded risk acceptance may be defensible; unrestricted substitution is not established. |
| **Merely mitigating** | Some likelihood or consequence is reduced without supplying the missing independent decision or equivalent protection. Examples include AV-2 alone and an audit after practical recovery has expired. |
| **Ineffective for the material exposure** | Relevant safeguards were unavailable, bypassed, compromised, superficial, or too late to interrupt the main loss mechanism. Unrelated checks may still have worked. |

“Same expected loss” is not the only possible standard. Equal averages can conceal different maximum losses, irreversible outcomes, concentrated fraud opportunities, correlated failures, and estimation uncertainty. Independence, accountability, exception discipline, and limits on catastrophic exposure may be required even when estimated average loss is small. Repeated small exceptions can also create substantial aggregate exposure.

The classifications below assess substantive protection separately from formal FEP-1 and ED-1 status. OBJECTIVE_MET means the described package gives convincing substantive protection, with explicitly stated verification qualifications; it is not certification of missing facts. No manager's level is supplied, so formal MG-4/ED-1 authorization cannot be conclusively certified even in the strongest cases. A finding that a deviation is plausibly tolerable does not itself authorize recurring convenience exceptions.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive role | Detective role | Corrective role | Conditions and limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 hard block | Stops a covered defect before release, as in AP-205. | Identifies the validation exception. | May lead to amendment or cancellation; the block itself prevents loss rather than recovering funds. | Requires availability, correct rules and bypass protection. Does not establish receipt or commercial reasonableness. |
| AV-2 warning with override | Protective only if override entails an effective independent decision before execution. | Reports a risk indicator. | Can prompt correction or cancellation while intervention is possible. | An operator controlling the override credential defeats independence, as in AP-202. |
| MG-4 before release | Can directly prevent unilateral authorization. | Detects errors and unsupported claims in the prepared instruction. | Can require correction or cancellation before release. | Requires L3+ authority, authentic independence, substantive review, and binding to final payment details. |
| MG-4 after release, before settlement | Can prevent irrevocable loss if rejection can stop execution. | Detects defects in an already released instruction. | Can trigger withdrawal or amendment while still possible. | Hybrid. A timestamp alone does not prove stop power or an enforced approval gate. |
| MG-4 after settlement | Cannot prevent the completed irrevocable transfer. | Can discover error, abuse, or missing support. | May initiate exceptional recovery or remediation, without assured success. | Cannot retrospectively become independent pre-commitment authorization. |
| RA-7 | Generally no prevention of the original release; remediation can prevent recurrence. | Independently audits emergency payments with 100% coverage and timely completion. | Partly corrective only if detection, decision, and bank action occur while stopping, recall, or recovery is feasible. | Audit completion, findings, escalation and bank status matter more than an audit event count. |
| ED-1 | Restricts access to the exception and mandates protective components. | Its audit requirement examines use and payment validity. | Inherits its components' intervention capacity. | Permission is conjunctive; ED-1 does not independently validate an invoice or supply another reviewer. |

I use “preventive” relative to the harm being assessed. Approval after Release_Payment cannot prevent the already recorded nominal FEP-1 violation. It can prevent an irreversible erroneous transfer before settlement, while detecting defects in the released instruction. “Hybrid” therefore best describes effective pre-settlement ratification in AP-201 and AP-207. If the bank would execute regardless of the reviewer's decision, the action is only detective for that transfer.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Collect transaction-linked evidence: actual human identity and credential ownership; manager level, conflicts and review content; approval bound to the precise final instruction; underlying receipt and vendor evidence; AV-2 coverage, configuration, health, warnings and overrides; token activation, expiry and ingestion records; bank acceptance, holds, settlement and recall cutoffs; and audit start, completion, scope, independence, findings, escalation and recovery outcomes. Preserve original chronology when correcting logging errors.

Prioritize AP-202's override access and related-party investigation, AP-208's outage and bank-verification failure, AP-204's fabricated receipt and related payment population, and AP-206's token provenance. Examine AP-209 proportionately because of its linkage, without presuming a defect. Document any bounded tolerance of AP-201 and prevent convenience-based recurrence. Complete the authority and enforcement evidence for AP-203 and AP-207.

An alert is a request for investigation, not proof of misconduct. Fabricated support and verified credential misuse are stronger evidence than pair frequency or shared equipment. Neither the pair's denial nor its repeated association settles collusion; organizational design and transaction-level evidence are needed.

PM4Py behavior can vary across versions, including convenience-function availability, default violation selection, return representation, and treatment of missing or repeated activities. The code checks the returned completed-case set against a direct resource comparison for this fixture, retries explicit violation selection if needed, and refuses to silently report a disagreeing set. It supports DataFrame and conventional EventLog results. A version without the named API requires a compatible installation; this block does not install packages or contact a service.

The resource comparison assumes the supplied u-identifiers represent the stated humans. Production checking needs an authoritative human-to-account mapping; shared service accounts and aliases cannot establish independence. Multiple lifecycle occurrences require explicit matching, which this block refuses to guess.

The audit-completion field represents the supplied audit descriptions for preliminary screening; AP-201 is explicitly only a start. Full audit scope and independence still need evidence. Business-day and 24-hour flags are disclosed screening conventions, not certification of RA-7 compliance or bank recoverability. The 37-payment pair history comes from the supplied facts and is distinct from the two paired completed cases present in this CSV.
