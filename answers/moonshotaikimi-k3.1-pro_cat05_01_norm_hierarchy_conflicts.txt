## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts and interpretations are distinct.** The facts are the source texts, event records, organizational facts, and outage information supplied above. In particular, there is no Chief Risk Officer exception or ratification; Incident Command members are not shown, as a group, to be budget owners; and the CFO’s authority over Finance controls is not stated to include Compliance or segregation-of-duties waivers.

I use the following **interpretive assumptions**, rather than treating them as additional facts:

1. **Authority before wording or recency.** GPIP-4 is the global baseline. A later or more specific document prevails only on an issue within its issuer’s authority and stated scope. B remains effective as a stricter local procedure under A5. The prior practice of treating signed incident directives as overlays supports ECD-2’s effect on local workflow, but does not establish that ECD-2 is an A7 exception.
2. **No unnecessary conflict.** If two controls can operate together, both apply. For example, ECD-2 can permit use outside the supplier register while GPIP-4 still requires Compliance clearance for a new supplier. Likewise, commitment before a PO does not prevent pre-commitment Finance or Compliance review.
3. **Primary treatment of ECD-2 and the CFO email.** Within its scope and time window, ECD-2 overrides ordinary local PO sequencing and supplier-register restrictions, subject to C3. It is not presumed to waive A1–A3. The CFO email is not disregarded merely because it is an email: it can interpret Finance evidence and, when read with ECD-2, displace some separate local operational approval steps. It cannot turn an Incident Command member into an independent budget owner or Finance Controller, eliminate A1 or A3, extend ECD-2, or operate retroactively. D4 reinforces this narrow reading.
4. **Emergency necessity is not independent authority.** Necessity can support B4 emergency status or a narrow reading of “critical” lots. It cannot supply missing approval authority. For C6, Operations’ label is not conclusive, but Internal Audit’s 24-hour test is not adopted as an express rule either. Direct containment nexus, urgency, and available alternatives all matter.
5. **Controls are assessed at the relevant act.** A2 and A3 are assessed at supplier commitment. B2 contains no express timing phrase, but I interpret Plant and Finance “approval” as pre-commitment controls: B4 expressly relaxes PO timing, and B5 confirms that emergency status does not remove Finance approval. Later review may mitigate consequences but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control.
6. **Commitment and expiry.** Supplier commitment means the external act by which Northbridge becomes committed, not merely ERP entry. ECD-2 must be effective at that act. The CFO email applies only from 2026-07-15 07:20 and only during ECD-2’s operative window. A verbal extension does not amend ECD-2’s written window.
7. **Evidence and burden.** The proponent of an exception must establish its authority, scope, timing, and conditions. Authentic later-produced evidence can prove that an earlier act was timely; a later approval is not evidence that the approval itself occurred earlier. ERP timestamps are not conclusive where better contemporaneous evidence exists. ON-44 applies only to affected recovery entries, not indiscriminately to every later case.
8. **Classification.** NONCOMPLIANT requires an affirmative breach under the stated interpretation or a clearly failed invoked exception. INDETERMINATE is used where unresolved actor, authority, scope, or commitment-time evidence is outcome-dispositive. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION requires every material deviation to be covered and all non-waived controls to be satisfied. Severity reflects control significance, not proven financial loss.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement hierarchy/exception clause:**

> **Incident procurement overlays.** A COO/Incident Command directive may supersede only expressly identified local operational steps, such as PO sequencing and supplier-register use. A CFO instruction may prescribe Finance evidence but may not waive Compliance, segregation of duties, or any actor-specific GPIP approval. Suspension of a GPIP control requires a written, rule-specific, scoped, time-limited exception signed by the CRO or a pre-named deputy CRO; silence preserves the control. Local stricter controls remain applicable unless expressly and lawfully displaced.
>
> **Scope and thresholds.** “Critical lot” means an item with a documented direct nexus to containing the incident and required for the affected sterilization operation within 24 hours. Backup or contingency stock outside that test requires explicit line-item inclusion in the CRO exception. Joint Incident Command authorization is additional to, and does not by itself satisfy, GPIP budget-owner, Finance Controller, or Compliance requirements. Up to EUR 150,000 it may replace only the expressly listed local operational approvals; above EUR 150,000 Legal clearance is additionally required. Thresholds apply to the total related commitment, and splitting is prohibited.
>
> **Independence and suppliers.** Neither the requester nor the person committing Northbridge may serve as either emergency authorizer or final business approver. An outside-register supplier may be used only within the directive’s scope, but every new supplier and every purchase above EUR 100,000 still requires pre-commitment Compliance clearance unless that precise control is covered by a valid CRO exception.
>
> **Records and expiry.** Verbal emergency authorization must be recorded within four hours, and the emergency form within 24 hours of commitment. An authenticated signed PDF or equivalent durable record counts when created; later ERP entry is a logging issue. Authentic retrospective evidence may prove a timely act, but later approval cannot be backdated or treated as automatic cure. Extensions must be written and signed before expiry; oral extensions are ineffective, and commitments after expiry follow ordinary rules.
>
> **Anti-normalization safeguard.** An initial directive may not exceed 72 hours and may be extended only once through the same written authority process. Every incident purchase must undergo independent review within five business days, with recurring emergency directives, cumulative spend, and repeated use by the same requester or supplier reported to the executive risk committee.
