Alpha was selected on *historically poor* cycle times, so mean reversion is a live alternative: Alpha's untreated JanFeb swing alone is 3.5 h, the same order as the entire claimed effect (3.2 to 5.6 h). Beta was selected on the same criterion yet shows raw deterioration (+2.06 h), which is inconsistent with a uniform strong effect and consistent with either genuine heterogeneity or DiD artefacts. Gamma is the cleanest timing case (deterioration in May–Jun reverses exactly at adoption), but July also brings demand relief, and because queueing is convex in utilisation, Gamma (38.2 cases/FTE) should benefit more from relief than Delta (33.4) — a parallel-trends violation in levels that logs only partly fix.

**5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions (four outcomes).**
- *Median:* both-control DiD 3.2 to 5.6 h; mix-matched 2.4 to 4.8 h; log specification 8.5% to 13.1%; if 1–2 h of the controls' deterioration is itself fraud-desk spillover from adopters, DiD shrinks to 1.2 to 4.6 h; allowing ±3 h for mean reversion/noise gives a **bounded range of 0% to 12%, most plausible 3% to 9%; zero is not excluded.**
- *p90:* raw +7% to +11% versus DiD 6.5% to 10.3%; the p90/median ratio rises in adopters as much as in controls (Alpha more), so there is **no tail-specific gain**: range **10% to +11%, sign unresolved.**
- *Rework:* 2.1 pp (DiD) to +1.3 pp (Alpha raw LATE); sign flips with window. Branch-visible only; desk-side loops unknown  **system rework not identified.**
- *CSAT:* with response rate r0.30 and scale [1,5], Manski bounds for Alpha Aug are [1.89, 4.69] (width 2.8). If fast-path customers average 4.2 and escalated 3.4, a **12.5pp** shift in respondent fast-path share manufactures +0.10 — trivially achievable when the fast-path share rose 25–45pp. Combined with CSAT  intercept  0.017×median (residuals 0.04), the survey provides **no independent confirmation**; range 0.10 to +0.20 under strong assumptions, otherwise unidentified.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

**Next branch.** Epsilon first: it has the most headroom (rework 16.2%, complexity 38%, p90 91.4 and rising) and is the closest mix match to Alpha, so it is both the best expected-benefit candidate and the most informative replication. Delta enters ~8 weeks later as a concurrent comparison and then crosses over; its fast, low-complexity profile makes it the branch where benefit is least likely and where a null result is most informative.

**Assignment mechanism.** Two levels. (i) Branch level: randomize the start *week* within a pre-agreed 4-week window (and randomize order if leadership permits), giving design-based variation in timing. (ii) Case level: within available branches, a seeded 50/50 routing rule assigns *eligible* arrivals to automated vs manual triage, stratified by registration risk class and arrival week; the highest-risk classes stay manual and are logged separately. This identifies the fast-path version effect (E2) and the tail effect (E4) with case-level randomization, while week clusters support policy-level (ITT) inference. Freeze routing-campaign shares and FTE for the evaluation window; log every deviation and override.

**Minimum measurements.** 6 months of retrospectively extracted case-level pre-data plus 12 weeks post at stable dose; weekly (not monthly) aggregates. Collect a true event log (case id, activity, resource, start/end timestamps), queue length and WIP at triage/specialist/fraud desk, referral reason codes, desk-side rework loops, hours worked and overtime, and within-class mix descriptors.

**Spillover monitoring.** Fraud-desk arrivals, waiting time and SLA breaches by referring branch *and* channel; if desk wait rises >20% from baseline, throttle borderline referrals and re-estimate using end-to-end customer time including desk wait.

**Customer guardrails.** p90 and share of cases >72h; escalation and rework; CSAT with response counts and rates by channel plus a random non-respondent follow-up sample. Automatic pause if the automated arm's p90 worsens >10% or CSAT falls >0.15 for two consecutive weeks.

**Scale-up rule.** Expand only if the randomized case-level estimate shows 5% median reduction, tail non-inferiority (upper 95% bound of p90 change 0), and CSAT non-inferiority at a 0.10 margin after non-response adjustment. Otherwise iterate the routing rules — do not enlarge the footprint.

        rows.append(dict(branch=a, outcome=v, pre=pre, post=post, late=late,
                         d_raw=d_raw, d_raw_pct=100 * d_raw / pre,
                         d_ctrl_Delta=ctrl_d["Delta"], d_ctrl_Epsilon=ctrl_d["Epsilon"],
                         DiD_both=did_both, DiD_pct=100 * did_both / pre,
                         DiD_matched=did_raw_match := d_raw - ctrl_d[matched],
                         DiD_leaveout_other=d_raw - ctrl_d["Delta" if matched == "Epsilon" else "Epsilon"]))
pp = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print("\n=== ARITHMETIC pre/post contrasts + DiD (NOT causal effects; see A1-A7) ===")
print(pp.round(3).to_string(index=False))
print("\nGamma vs its own May-Jun (same mix/FTE) baseline:")
for v in OUTCOMES:
    print(f"  {v:16s} {wmean('Gamma',(5,6),v):8.3f} -> {wmean('Gamma',(7,8),v):8.3f}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4. relative-time indicators
adopt = df.loc[df.automation == 1].groupby("branch")["t"].min()
df["g"] = df["branch"].map(adopt)
df["rel"] = df["t"] - df["g"]                     # NaN for never-treated
print("\n=== cohort / event-time support (never-treated have rel = NaN) ===")
print(df.dropna(subset=["rel"]).groupby(["g", "rel"]).size().rename("n").to_string())
for e in [-3, -2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4]:                # rel = -1 is the omitted baseline
    df[f"e_{e}".replace("-", "m")] = (df["rel"] == e).astype(int)
print("Only 2 pre-months are free of the March fraud-desk shock; July cohort has 1 branch.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 5. descriptive panel models
G = df["branch"].nunique()
tcrit = stats.t.ppf(0.975, G - 1)

def fit_cluster(formula, data):
    return smf.ols(formula, data=data).fit(cov_type="cluster",
                                          cov_kwds={"groups": data["branch"]})

print("\n=== M1: DESCRIPTIVE staggered TWFE (binary availability) ===")
print("Labelled DESCRIPTIVE: with ramping, cohort-specific effects, TWFE uses already-treated")
print("units as controls and can place NEGATIVE weights on ATTs (Goodman-Bacon). Not an ATT.")
m1 = fit_cluster("median_cycle_h ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)", df)
print(f"automation coef = {m1.params['automation']:+.3f} h, cluster SE = {m1.bse['automation']:.3f}")

print("\n=== M2/M3: TREATMENT-INTENSITY models (dose per 10pp of automated fast path) ===")
print("Causal reading needs A5 (dose exogenous given FE+covariates) -- implausible: dose co-moves")
print("with learning, the June load peak and the May mix shock; no valid instrument exists")
print("(availability also changes referral routing, breaking the exclusion restriction).")
specs = {
    "M2 dose + branch FE + month FE": "dose10 + C(branch) + C(month)",
    "M3 + load + complexity (may be POST-treatment mediators -> total effect no longer targeted)":
        "dose10 + load + high_complexity_pct + C(branch) + C(month)",
}
for v in OUTCOMES:
    for name, rhs in specs.items():
        m = fit_cluster(f"{v} ~ {rhs}", df)
        b, se = m.params["dose10"], m.bse["dose10"]
        print(f"{v:16s} | {name[:28]:28s} | {b:+8.4f} /10pp  SE={se:6.4f} "
              f"t={b/se:+6.2f}  t(4) 95% CI=[{b-tcrit*se:+.3f},{b+tcrit*se:+.3f}]")

print("\n-- branch jackknife for the median dose slope (leave-one-branch-out) --")
for b in sorted(df.branch.unique()):
    m = fit_cluster("median_cycle_h ~ dose10 + C(branch) + C(month)", df[df.branch != b])
    print(f"  drop {b:8s}: {m.params['dose10']:+.4f} h/10pp")

print("\n-- within-adopter dose contrast, first -> last treated month (median vs p90) --")
for a in ["Alpha", "Beta", "Gamma"]:
    g = df[(df.branch == a) & (df.automation == 1)]
    f, l = g.iloc[0], g.iloc[-1]
    dd = l.automated_fast_path_pct - f.automated_fast_path_pct
    print(f"  {a:8s} dose +{dd:.0f}pp | median {l.median_cycle_h-f.median_cycle_h:+.1f}h "
          f"({(l.median_cycle_h-f.median_cycle_h)/dd*10:+.2f}/10pp) | "
          f"p90 {l.p90_cycle_h-f.p90_cycle_h:+.1f}h "
          f"({(l.p90_cycle_h-f.p90_cycle_h)/dd*10:+.2f}/10pp)")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6. pre-treatment placebo
# Gamma is untreated through June; give it a FAKE April adoption and compare to Delta/Epsilon.
# A significant placebo => differential (mix/load) trends, i.e. A1 fails. 3 clusters only.
sub = df[(df.branch.isin(["Delta", "Epsilon"])) | ((df.branch == "Gamma") & (df.t <= 6))].copy()
sub["placebo"] = ((sub.branch == "Gamma") & (sub.t >= 4)).astype(int)
mp_ = smf.ols("median_cycle_h ~ placebo + C(branch) + C(month)", data=sub).fit(
    cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": sub["branch"]})
print("\n=== PLACEBO (fake April adoption for Gamma, pre-treatment window only) ===")
print(f"placebo coef = {mp_.params['placebo']:+.3f} h (SE {mp_.bse['placebo']:.3f}); "
      "non-zero => non-parallel pre-trends from the May mix/demand shock, NOT automation.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 7. explicit warnings
print("\n=== WARNINGS ===")
print(f"* ONLY {G} CLUSTERS (2 never-treated). Cluster-robust asymptotics are unreliable;")
print("  t(G-1) intervals, wild cluster bootstrap or randomization inference over the few")
print("  feasible adoption dates are required. Do NOT report naive OLS p-values.")
print("* Outcomes are AGGREGATED medians/p90s. Mean-of-monthly-medians != pooled median;")
print("  quantile treatment effects and P(cycle>72h) are NOT identified without case records.")
print("  Ecological aggregation can reverse signs and hides within-risk-class mix.")
print("* Interference: a shared fraud desk serves all branches, so controls are partially")

print("  harms controls. Desk waiting times are unrecorded -> system effect not identified.")
print("* CSAT: 20-45% response, counts unknown, and CSAT ~ affine in the branch median;")
print("  it is not independent confirmation of customer benefit (A6 fails).")
print("* Repeated identical values and 4-6% 'automated' fast path under automation==0 suggest")
print("  imputation/definition problems: audit lineage before any policy-grade estimate.")
print("* No coefficient above is a causal effect. The defensible next step is a randomized")
print("  case-level routing rule plus randomized phase-in timing (see rollout recommendation).")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Availability was staggered and targeted, use ramped from ~25% to ~50% of cases, and every branch — adopter or not — absorbed a March fraud-desk step (+~2pp escalation), a May complexity increase and a June demand peak; in raw levels Alpha's and Beta's median changed by 0.8% and +4.5% while their p90 rose 9.8% and 10.6%, escalation rose ~2.5–2.9pp, and all five branches' p90/median ratios widened.
- **Suggestive:** Relative to never-treated branches on identical windows, adopter medians improved by ~3.2–5.6 h (7–12%) and p90 by ~6.2–8.8 h (6.5–10%), with a dose association of 0.13 h per percentage point of fast-path share; mix-matched pairs, log specifications and Gamma's reversal of its own May–June deterioration point the same way, and escalation is consistently ~1pp higher than the common shock.
- **Conditionally causal:** *If* pre-trends were parallel absent the routing/FTE/mix changes, *if* mean reversion from selection on poor history is small, and *if* the shared fraud desk did not slow controls, then availability plausibly reduced typical cycle time by roughly 3–9% in high-load branches over 2–5 months while adding ~1pp of specialist/fraud referrals — with no demonstrated tail benefit and possibly a worse tail at high dose.
- **Not supported:** "Automation reduces cycle time by X%" as a single portable number, any claim of tail/SLA improvement, any claim of rework elimination (rather than displacement to the unmeasured fraud desk), any customer-satisfaction gain (20–45% response, unknown counts, CSAT nearly a deterministic function of the median), and any transfer of Alpha/Beta magnitudes to the smaller, faster, lower-complexity Delta.
