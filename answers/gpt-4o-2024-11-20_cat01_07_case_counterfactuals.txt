## 1. Feasibility model

### Partial-order relation set:
```text
1. Register < Triage
2. Triage < Identity Check
3. Triage < Document Check
4. Identity Check < Assessment
5. Document Check < Assessment
6. Assessment < Compliance Review (if high-risk or high-value)
7. Compliance Review < Decision
8. Decision < Notify
9. Originals Receipt < Document Check (when originals required)
10. Request More Info < (Revised Document Check AND Revised Assessment)
11. Preliminary compliance < Final Compliance Review
```

### Conditional and declarative constraints:
```text
1. No final compliance review can happen before the final assessment.
2. A single resource cannot handle two tasks simultaneously.
3. High-risk/high-value cases require full compliance review.
4. A four-eyes rule must separate the roles of the assessor and the final compliance reviewer.
5. Early completion of tasks does not guarantee immediate downstream processing due to queue predictions, not guarantees.
6. Rework loops require new evidence, assessments, and compliance reviews (for high-risk/high-value cases).
7. Escalation advice does not replace compliance or assessment but supports decision points.
8. When originals are required, document verification must be deferred until originals are received.
```

### Explanation:
- **Interventions altering timing only:** K-104 (preliminary compliance work and resource reservation), K-411 (parallelizing tasks).
- **Interventions altering routing:** K-219 (reassigning resources from D1 to D2).
- **Interventions altering decision policy:** K-337 (skipping the second request for more info).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

- **Factual mechanism:** Sanctions ambiguity noted at Triage was not escalated, delaying preparation for compliance review. Most compliance work occurred after Assessment.
- **Minimal intervention:** Send a preliminary packet to S1 and reserve C1 capacity at Triage. Preliminary compliance review begins concurrently with verification tasks.
- **Temporal propagation:** Early compliance work shortens C1 effort but may push the C1 queue delay upstream for other cases.
- **Process-feasibility check:** The preliminary review is compatible since it leaves final compliance dependent on Assessment completion.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: cycle time reduction by 10 hrs; Base: 6 hrs saved; Worst: neutral or minor delay due to displaced urgent cases in the compliance queue.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Any evidence showing preliminary compliance is unhelpful (e.g., ambiguity not resolvable before verification tasks).

### K-219

- **Factual mechanism:** Handwritten documents sent to D1 caused a delay due to long queues. D2 was not utilized despite availability.
- **Minimal intervention:** Assign D2 once originals are received.
- **Temporal propagation:** D2 begins earlier but slower task completion may result in rework; delays cascade into Assessment if errors occur.
- **Process-feasibility check:** As D2 is slower, assessment readiness may be deferred and rework risk increased.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: queue savings of 6 hrs; Base: 3 hrs delay due to rework; Worst: added 10-20 hrs if rework propagates.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Empirical error rates for D2 with handwritten documents exceed tolerance levels.

### K-337

- **Factual mechanism:** Two rework loops were completed to resolve supplier discrepancies, consuming over 36 hrs in total.
- **Minimal intervention:** Prohibit the second rework loop and force decision based on available evidence.
- **Temporal propagation:** Eliminates second loop and accelerates final decision but risks non-compliance exposure.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Decision without full resolution narrows decision latitude and may violate risk-management standards.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: 24 hrs saved, decision upheld; Base: 12 hrs saved, mild appeal risk; Worst: major compliance/legal failure.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Supplier response reliability negates the need for prohibiting second rework.

### K-411

- **Factual mechanism:** Sequential task locking delayed document verification until Identity Check was fully processed.
- **Minimal intervention:** Override lock at Triage, allowing concurrent tasks with proper timestamping.
- **Temporal propagation:** Faster task execution advances readiness at Assessment but bottlenecks there may still delay finalization.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Logging timestamp overrides prevents concurrency violations.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: 8 hrs saved; Base: 4 hrs saved but assessment queue unchanged; Worst: unintended conflicts in task dependencies.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Audit reveals poor timestamp practices or unresolved task interlocks.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention         | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases       | sign of system-wide effect         | key nonlinearity                         |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Early compliance prep | Increased early C1 demand  | Compliance             | Identity, Assessment | Capacity for later compliance   | Mixed: reduces delays but shifts burden | Compliance queue congestion            |
| Reassign to D2        | Increased D2, spare D1     | Document               | Document (if reworked) | Downstream rework cases         | Mixed: reduces document bottleneck but risks rework | Amplification of D2's slower pace    |
| Rework prohibition    | Reduced loops in D1, A1   | Assessment             | Compliance            | Compliance/legal appeals         | Risky: shifts compliance expectations | Compliance-appeal reduction unclear    |
| Parallelization       | Increased demand on D2, I2 | Identity, Document     | Assessment            | All concurrent cases            | Favorable if concurrency is feasible | Assessment queue delay amplification   |

### 100-case weekly rollout

A 100-case rollout introduces significant pressures from resource shifts. Early compliance preparation (K-104) could lead to substantial delays for urgent high-risk cases that lack early escalation. D2 substitution (K-219) risks amplifying rework loops due to increased error rates, overwhelming D1 for corrections. Prohibiting rework loops (K-337) reduces cycle time but inherently alters decision quality thresholds, introducing appeal/legal bottlenecks. Parallel tasking (K-411) is promising but risks concentrated assessment bottlenecks due to simultaneous task readiness.

Key nonlinearity arises in compliance capacity reservations (K-104), which displaces urgent high-risk cases even as it accelerates low-priority ones. D2 substitution (K-219) creates a nonlinear error-rework interaction. Assessment queues cascade delays when parallel readiness (K-411) saturates downstream resources.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-411 (parallelizing verification tasks)** due to its broad applicability, modest resource shifts, and minimal risk to compliance quality. It allows concurrent work streams without altering the ultimate decision logic. However, the **K-337 rework prohibition** appears the most dangerous, as it risks compliance defensibility and appeal exposure for the sake of speed.

Transporting **K-104's early compliance preparation** to 100 cases creates a ripple effect through compliance queues, displacing urgent cases and compounding delays. Additional queue and timing data are valuable to refine escalation thresholds.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural**: Document and identity tasks are logically independent but affect downstream readiness.
2. **Temporal**: Queue durations follow predictable patterns but are not deterministic.
3. **Resource**: Resource specialization impacts speed and quality trade-offs across pools.
4. **Behavioral**: Escalation behavior (early vs. late) varies by triage judgment.
5. **Quality**: D2 has higher error rates with handwritten inputs compared to D1.
6. **Interference**: Reservation of resources affects other pending compliance cases.
7. **Temporal**: Earlier parallel task readiness does not guarantee downstream acceleration.
8. **Interference**: Eliminating rework loops changes decision accuracy and error recourse.
