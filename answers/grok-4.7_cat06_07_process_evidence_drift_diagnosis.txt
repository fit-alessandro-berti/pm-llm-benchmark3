## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March are a stable reference. About 3,200–3,400 cases start each month, labels are 100% mature with a 32–34 day median delay, breach is 0.18–0.19, and mean predicted risk matches it. AUROC is 0.81–0.82, Brier is about 0.131, and ECE is about 0.02. Variant mix is about 45/35/20 for V1/V2/V3, unmapped share is 0, unknown-activity share is ≤0.3%, and the queue feature is missing in about 1% of rows. Matured breach is about 0.16 / 0.11 / 0.31. The parts edge drifts only from 28 h to 35 h. Nothing in this window calls for a model change.

April is a schema incident with mature labels. Cases started (3,500), breach (0.19), maturity (100%), and label delay (34 d) all sit on the Q1 line, and the parts median is 34 h. The score side breaks: predicted risk 0.28, AUROC 0.67, ECE 0.110, unknown activities 17%, missing queue 14%, unmapped variants 18%. The unmapped mass matches the unknown-activity mass, which is what a rename to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` plus `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` and duplicated events produces when prefixes are cut at the wrong event. Variant-conditional breach barely moves, and there is no documented outcome-definition change. May’s patch is a partial repair, not evidence that April’s business risk changed. The small April uptick in path-conditional breach is second-order next to the ECE jump.

May is residual pipeline damage plus a cleaned process, not a new regime. Breach is back to 0.18 with full maturity. AUROC 0.78 and ECE 0.050 are better than April and short of Q1. Unknown share 2%, missing queue 3%, and unmapped 1% are the leftover footprint; the cached event-count feature and pre-20-Apr duplicates are the documented mechanism. Parts median 38 h and V3 breach 0.33 are still in the baseline band. Rescoring after a full feature rebuild is what decides whether any real drift is left.

June should be read as a routing-mix change. The remote policy lifts V2 to 52% and V1 falls to 30%. Headline breach falls to 0.15, and the matured path rates (0.16 / 0.09 / 0.35) reproduce that headline. Remote cycle time does not move. AUROC holds at 0.79 while ECE rises to 0.060 because the frozen scorer still emits mean risk 0.20. That is a calibration consequence of a process-mix change at a prediction point that sits before the route is taken. It is concept drift only if the same diagnosis-time features now have a different breach probability under the new policy. The pack does not contain that conditional test, and the mixture arithmetic already explains the rate drop without it. Parts median 42 h is a gentle lead-in, not the June story.

July is the supplier onset, still masked in the aggregate. The parts edge jumps to 65 h and matured V3 breach jumps to 0.46, while V1 (0.17) and V2 (0.10) stay put. V2 is still half of volume, so the mixture stays near 0.19 and the mean score (0.18) and ECE (0.042) look acceptable. AUROC slipping to 0.76 is the early ranking cost. Maturity at 98% and delay at 36 d are too mild to create this V3 gap. Aggregate calibration in July is a mixture artifact.

August is supplier-driven process and concept drift, with censoring starting to bite. The parts edge doubles to 130 h, V3 share rises to 35%, and matured V3 breach reaches 0.64. V1 and V2 remain about 0.18 and 0.11. Headline breach 0.28 and predicted risk 0.19 diverge; AUROC 0.68 and ECE 0.120 follow. Data quality is quiet (unknown 0.7%, missing queue 2%). Partner Z is still absent. Maturity is 93% and the matured-variant mixture (~0.31) already sits above the headline 0.28, so the published rate understates a real deterioration. This is not an April-style encoding failure and not a June-style benign mix shift.

September stacks three mechanisms. The parts edge lengthens further to 148 h and V3 matured breach stays extreme at 0.61, so the August concept/process regime continues. Partner Z arrives (24% of volume, 58% of its cases on V3 versus 36% elsewhere) with higher rural distance and device age outside the feature set, and volume jumps to 5,100. Maturity falls to 82% and delay to 42 d; Partner Z matures more slowly, and its 0.45 versus 0.27 cutoff gap is not yet a clean risk gap. The matured mixture (~0.34) again exceeds the headline 0.31. Predicted risk only reaches 0.21. September is a real performance failure plus a population shift plus an incomplete label set, in that order of what is already identified.

October is a label-maturity failure laid on top of an unresolved September process. Maturity 55% and delay 52 d, with the backlog worse on long paths, make the headline breach of 0.16 non-comparable. The parts edge is at its worst (155 h), V3 share is still 41%, Partner Z is 27%, and variant-conditional rates are explicitly too immature to estimate. Predicted risk 0.23 against an observed 0.16 is the signature of fast, non-breaching cases landing in the label file first. AUROC 0.64 and ECE 0.130 describe the immature extract. Unknown-activity share 0.9% shows this is not a repeat of April.

These four windows fail differently. April changes the features and the score while the outcome stays still. June changes the route mix and the base rate while path-conditional risk and discrimination stay essentially intact. August–September change parts delay and \(P(\text{breach}\mid V3)\), then add a new population, while the scorer’s mean stays near 0.20 and labels start to thin. October’s observed rate moves back toward 0.16 because the labels are censored, while the process evidence keeps deteriorating. A single drift label, or a single retrain, cannot cover that sequence.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each start month, plot the fraction of cases with a final breach label by day 30, 45, 60, and 90, stratified by variant and by Partner Z. Rebuild AUROC, Brier, ECE, and breach rate only on cases that have had a fixed follow-up (use a horizon past the October delay, on the order of 75–90 days, pre-register it). Under label censoring, October’s breach rises from 0.16 toward the August–September matured mixture as follow-up completes, and long V3 cases account for most of the late positives. Under a true October recovery, the vintage breach stays near 0.16 and the parts-edge median on those same matured cases is back near the June level. Under an April pipeline story, April’s vintage metrics stay bad at every horizon until features are rebuilt, because April is already 100% mature.

**Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning.** Map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` to `Initial Diagnosis Completed` before any prefix or feature logic. Drop exact `(case, activity, timestamp)` duplicates and consecutive same-activity events within a short epsilon; leave genuine later repeats. Recompute events-before-diagnosis, queue features, and the diagnosis prefix from this log, then rescore. Under a pipeline failure, April unknown-activity share and unmapped share collapse, predicted risk falls from 0.28 toward 0.19, and AUROC/ECE move toward 0.81/0.02, with May’s residual gap closing as well. Under real April concept drift, cleaning moves data-quality counters but path-conditional breach and calibration stay broken on the cleaned subset. Under a pure supplier-drift story, April barely changes and August–September V3 breach stays high after the same cleaning.

**Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.** Report AUROC, ECE, Brier, and mean predicted versus observed breach for V1, V2, and V3, and for Partner Z versus others, on maturity-matched cohorts. Score the intended route only if it is known at diagnosis time. Under June process mix with stable concept, within-variant ECE and AUROC stay near Q1 and the headline ECE gap disappears inside variant strata. Under supplier concept drift, V3 ECE and AUROC degrade from July onward while V2 stays calibrated. Under population shift with omitted features, Partner Z remains miscalibrated after variant stratification until rural distance and device age are added. Under October censoring, October strata look falsely good on observed breach and then converge toward September once vintages mature.

**Variant shares and DFG / performance-DFG edges.** On case-start cohorts, track V1/V2/V3/unmapped and the median, p90, and count of `Order Part → Part Received` and `Initial Diagnosis Completed → Remote Fix`. Also bucket completed edges by the month the target event occurred. Under June routing drift, V2 frequency jumps and the remote edge median stays inside 3.8–4.6 h. Under supplier drift, the parts-edge median and tail jump in July and keep rising while the remote edge does not. Under an April encoding artifact, unmapped mass appears and disappears with the unknown-activity share, and a cleaned DFG restores the 45/35/20 shape. Under label censoring alone, these process graphs, which do not use the outcome API, still show October parts delay near 155 h.

**Did the outcome relationship change on the same path?** On mature cases, estimate \(P(\text{breach}\mid \text{variant}, \text{month})\) and, inside V3, \(P(\text{breach}\mid \text{parts wait bin}, \text{month})\). Fit the same model of pre-diagnosis \(X\) in Q1 and in June, and compare coefficients or run a classifier to separate eras (concept-drift residual test). Under pure process mix, path-conditional breach is stable across May–June and a model with an explicit route feature keeps a constant \(P(Y\mid X, \text{route})\). Under policy-induced concept drift, \(P(Y\mid X)\) changes from May to June even though \(P(Y\mid \text{route})\) does not. Under supplier concept drift, wait-binned V3 breach in August exceeds wait-binned V3 breach in Q1; if it does not, the apparent concept drift was just a shift along a stable wait–breach curve (process/covariate change, recoverable with a delay feature). Under censoring, October path-conditional estimates are unstable at the cutoff and settle only after the fixed horizon.

**Population reweighting and transport checks for Partner Z.** Reweight September non-Z cases to Partner Z’s variant mix, and transport a model fit on January–August (pre-Z) onto Partner Z with and without rural distance and device age. Match on label follow-up so Z’s slower maturity is not read as risk. Under pure mix shift, reweighting non-Z cases to 58% V3 reproduces Z’s breach. Under omitted-variable population shift, a residual remains until distance and device age are included, and then transport calibration holds. Under a distinct Z concept or data-quality problem, a residual remains after variant, wait, distance, device age, and equal follow-up. Under “the whole second-half failure is Partner Z,” the same tests on August fail immediately, because Z’s share is still 0 while V3 breach is already 0.64.

**Feature missingness and lineage audits.** By month, audit unknown-activity rate, duplicate rate, fraction of cases with a prefix anchored on the canonical diagnosis, distribution of recomputed events-before-diagnosis versus the cached feature, queue-feature null rate, and score-extract timestamp versus feature-build timestamp (so a pre-backfill April scorecard is visible as lineage, not as drift). Under the April pipeline account, cache-versus-recompute disagreements spike in April, prefix-miss rate tracks the AUROC drop, and both normalize after the rebuild while outcomes stay put. Under organic covariate drift, missingness stays at the Q1 floor and PSI moves on real covariates. Under label delay, lineage and missingness stay flat in October while maturity and delay alarms fire. Gate any later test: a month that fails the lineage audit does not enter a drift detector.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Contain first. Disable automated retraining and automated recalibration. Quarantine April scores from threshold decisions and from any training extract. For open cases already on the parts path, apply an operational override driven by current supplier delay (the performance-DFG fact), because the live score is still centered near the old 0.19 base rate while matured V3 breach is above 0.60. Keep June’s remote-routing policy in place; the path-conditional evidence says that policy lowered breach. Suppress October model-quality gates until the vintage matures.

Repair features before touching the model. Normalize the diagnosis alias, apply one duplicate policy to the whole history including pre-20-Apr, recompute the cached event-count and any other derived fields, backfill the queue feature, and rebuild prefixes only at canonical diagnosis. Rescore April and May. Use the rescored metrics, not the current table, to decide whether May still contains drift.

Recalibrate only where discrimination held and labels are mature. June, and July only on the non-V3 slice, are the candidates: AUROC stayed near 0.79 while the error was mostly a base-rate miss. A temporary intercept or isotonic map fit on mature June cases can support thresholds through a mix shift. Do not fit that map on April, August, September, or October. April would teach the calibrator the bug. August–September have AUROC in the mid-0.60s, so a calibrator can move the mean and still rank the wrong cases. October’s labels are the wrong target.

Retrain after new features exist and only on safe labels. Add intended route if it is known at diagnosis, a live or lagged parts-delay signal, partner, rural distance, and device age. Drop the unrepaired cached count.

Safe training months, after the pipeline rebuild: January–March as the clean anchor; June as the post-policy, pre-crisis mature regime; May once rescoring restores Q1-like data quality. July is usable only with an explicit delay feature, otherwise it mixes two supplier regimes. Unsafe until both labels and features are repaired: April (features), August (93% mature and a different concept), September (82% mature, Partner Z under-followed, missing features), October (55% mature). A month becomes eligible when its fixed-horizon maturity crosses the pre-registered bar, not when the calendar closes.

A separate model per variant is not justified at this prediction point. The variant is the future path; splitting V1/V2/V3 models either leaks post-diagnosis routing or refuses to score the decision the business actually has. A single model with intended route and supplier delay matches the mechanism. A fully separate Partner Z model is premature: Z’s volume is large enough to matter and too thinly matured to support its own scorecard, and the fair test is whether shared features transport. Segment-specific calibration for Z can be added after that test shows a residual. It is not the next step.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Run eight monitors on a case-start cohort, with a parallel event-calendar view for workload. Each alert carries the active deployment markers (1 Apr migration, 6 May patch, 1 Jun routing policy, 10 Jul supplier, 1 Sep Partner Z, October API backlog).

Predictive monitor: AUROC and Brier versus the Q1 band, on maturity-eligible cases only. Calibration monitor: ECE and mean predicted minus observed breach, overall and by variant and source. Label-maturity monitor: maturity fraction, median and p90 label delay, maturity by variant and by Partner Z, outcome-API lag. Data-quality monitor: unknown-activity share, duplicate rate, prefix-at-diagnosis rate, cache-versus-recompute gap, queue missingness, unmapped-variant share. Process-variant monitor: V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares. Performance-DFG monitor: median and p90 of Order Part → Part Received and of diagnosis → Remote Fix, plus edge counts. Population monitor: source mix, Partner Z share, PSI on rural distance, device age, and the rest of the model matrix. Intervention monitor: policy, mapping, and model-version changelog, required as a join key on every other alert.

Dependencies, so a late label does not retrain the model: the label-maturity monitor, when maturity is below the bar or delay exceeds the Q1 p90, marks the month `non-comparable` and suppresses predictive, calibration, and retrain actions. Those months may still raise a data-quality or performance-DFG alert, because those do not read the outcome API. The data-quality monitor, when unknown activity, prefix miss, duplicate rate, or cache drift breaches its threshold, suppresses recalibration and retraining and opens a pipeline repair; it also suppresses concept-drift language on that month. Only a month that passes both gates can escalate a predictive or calibration alarm. If it does, the variant and performance-DFG monitors split the playbook: mix shift with stable path-conditional breach and a stable parts edge selects the June path (recalibration or an intended-route feature); a degraded parts edge with a higher \(P(\text{breach}\mid V3)\) selects the August path (supplier feature, then retrain on mature data). The population monitor can request the transport test; it cannot by itself declare unfairness or launch a Z-only model. The intervention monitor is context, not a mute button: a deployment marker attached to an alert changes the runbook, and the absence of a marker on a large mix shift is itself an alert.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAGNOSIS = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAGNOSIS,
    "Initial Diagnosis Completed": DIAGNOSIS,
}


def load_and_clean(path: str) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """Normalize the renamed diagnosis BEFORE features, prefixes, or DFGs."""
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"])
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS])
    df = df.drop_duplicates([CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS, ACT])
    gap_s = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].diff().dt.total_seconds()
    same = df[ACT].eq(df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift())
    near = same & gap_s.notna() & (gap_s.abs() <= 1)
    removed = df.loc[near].copy()
    removed["removal_reason"] = "consecutive_same_activity_within_1s"
    # UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is audited, not remapped. Later genuine repeats are kept.
    return df.loc[~near].copy(), removed


def _family(activities: tuple) -> str:
    acts = set(activities)
    if "Order Part" in acts:
        return "V3"
    if "Remote Fix" in acts:
        return "V2"
    if "Dispatch Technician" in acts:
        return "V1"
    return "unmapped_other"


def _edge_median_hours(log: pd.DataFrame, src: str, tgt: str) -> float | None:
    """Median hours from first src to first later tgt. Independent of DFG aggregation."""
    sub = log.loc[log[ACT].isin([src, tgt]), [CASE, ACT, TS]]
    src_t = sub.loc[sub[ACT] == src].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    tgt_t = sub.loc[sub[ACT] == tgt].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    hours = ((tgt_t - src_t).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0).dropna()
    hours = hours[hours >= 0]
    return float(hours.median()) if len(hours) else None


def _summarize(log: pd.DataFrame, cohort: str, month: str) -> dict:
    """Process evidence only. This function does not fit or score a model."""
    if log.empty:
        return {"cohort": cohort, "month": month, "cases": 0, "events": 0}
    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    n = int(sum(variants.values())) or 1
    shares = {"V1": 0, "V2": 0, "V3": 0, "unmapped_other": 0}
    for variant, count in variants.items():
        shares[_family(tuple(variant))] += int(count)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        log, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
    )
    first_diag = (
        log.loc[log[ACT] == DIAGNOSIS]
        .groupby(CASE)[TS]
        .min()
        .rename("diag_ts")
    )
    before = log.join(first_diag, on=CASE)
    n_before = before.loc[before[TS] < before["diag_ts"]].groupby(CASE).size()
    parts_key = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
    # Library performance DFG is an auditable cross-check (mean seconds in
    # current pm4py). Decisions use the explicit median computed above.
    lib_mean_h = perf.get(parts_key)
    lib_mean_h = None if lib_mean_h is None else float(lib_mean_h) / 3600.0
    return {
        "cohort": cohort,
        "month": month,
        "cases": int(log[CASE].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(log)),
        "share_v1": shares["V1"] / n,
        "share_v2": shares["V2"] / n,
        "share_v3": shares["V3"] / n,
        "share_unmapped": shares["unmapped_other"] / n,
        "dfg_edge_count": int(len(dfg)),
        "median_h_order_part_to_part_received": _edge_median_hours(
            log, "Order Part", "Part Received"
        ),
        "library_mean_h_order_part_to_part_received": lib_mean_h,
        "median_h_diagnosis_to_remote_fix": _edge_median_hours(
            log, DIAGNOSIS, "Remote Fix"
        ),
        "share_cases_with_diagnosis": float(first_diag.shape[0] / log[CASE].nunique()),
        "median_events_before_diagnosis": (
            float(n_before.median()) if len(n_before) else None
        ),
        "unknown_activity_share": float((log[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
        "missing_queue_share": (
            float(log["queue_id"].isna().mean()) if "queue_id" in log.columns else None
        ),
        "temporal_feature_rows": int(len(temporal)),
        "temporal_feature_cols": int(temporal.shape[1]),
    }


def monthly_process_evidence(path: str = "field_service_events.csv") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Two cohorts, two questions:
    - case_start: cases whose first event falls in the month. Full traces.
      Aligns to the scorecard population and to label vintage. Edges may
      finish in a later month and open cases are right-censored.
    - event_calendar: events timestamped in the month (pm4py.filter_time_range
      mode='events'). Describes that month's workload. Cross-month edges lose
      an endpoint, so parts-wait medians from this cohort are biased.
    """
    df, removed = load_and_clean(path)
    case_start = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].transform("min")
    df = df.assign(case_start=case_start)
    removed = removed.join(df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("case_start"), on=CASE)
    rows = []
    for period in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M"):
        start = period.to_timestamp(how="start")
        end = (period + 1).to_timestamp(how="start")
        started = df.loc[(df["case_start"] >= start) & (df["case_start"] < end)]
        # Library cross-check of the case-start definition.
        traces_starting = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start, end, mode="traces_starting",
            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        )
        events_in_month = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start, end, mode="events",
            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        )
        row = _summarize(started, "case_start", str(period))
        row["traces_starting_cases_crosscheck"] = (
            int(traces_starting[CASE].nunique()) if len(traces_starting) else 0
        )
        row["near_duplicate_rows_removed"] = int(
            ((removed["case_start"] >= start) & (removed["case_start"] < end)).sum()
        )
        rows.append(row)
        rows.append(_summarize(events_in_month, "event_calendar", str(period)))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["month", "cohort"]).reset_index(drop=True)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

Retraining on immature October labels teaches the wrong outcome. Long V3 and Partner Z cases are exactly the ones the backlog holds back, and those are the high-breach paths. A model fit on the 55% labeled slice learns that recent cases rarely breach. The next mature month then looks like a sudden failure, and thresholds get looser while parts delay is at 155 h. Mitigation: train only on vintages that have passed the fixed follow-up; keep October in a shadow set that is rescored after maturity; let the label-maturity monitor suppress the retrain action.

Calibrating around the April pipeline bug hides a broken prefix. Isotonic or Platt scaling fit on April’s corrupted features will pull the 0.28 mean toward the true 0.19 rate and make ECE look repaired while AUROC is still 0.67 for a reason that leaves the system as soon as the mapping is fixed. The calibrator then becomes another cache that is wrong the day the feature rebuild ships. Mitigation: data-quality gates (unknown activity, prefix anchor, cache-versus-recompute, queue nulls) must pass before any calibrator is fit; April is a regression fixture for the rebuild, not a calibration sample.

Ignoring the supplier-driven concept drift treats August as more April or more October. The parts edge and the matured V3 rate are outcome facts with quiet data quality and, by August, still-high maturity. Leaving the 0.19 score in production under-calls cases that now breach about 60% of the time once they need a part, and a June-style recalibration cannot restore an AUROC of 0.68. Mitigation: the performance-DFG alarm escalates on its own, without a predictive alarm and without a mature label; the runbook adds the delay feature and an operational override before it is allowed to close the incident as “label noise.”

Calling Partner Z an unfairness finding, or the sole cause of autumn drift, acts on a confounded gap. The 0.45 versus 0.27 cutoff contrast arrives together with a heavier V3 mix, slower labels, missing rural and device-age features, and a supplier regime that was already severe in August, when Z’s share was zero. Removing partner identity does not fix omitted risk. Training a Z-only penalty, or a Z-only model, on immature labels can either over-flag rural cases or memorize the backlog. Mitigation: the transport and reweight tests in section 3 run before any fairness intervention; legitimate operational features are added first; partner-stratified calibration waits on equal follow-up and a residual that survives those features.

Two neighboring mistakes are worth blocking with the same gates. Reading June’s ECE rise as concept drift and retraining through a policy change that actually cut breach will chase a base-rate shift and can tempt a rollback of remote routing; the variant-conditional check selects recalibration instead. Reading October’s 0.16 breach as the end of the supplier incident will stand down the override while the parts edge is still at its worst; the performance-DFG monitor stays armed when the outcome API is late.
