## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Normative hierarchy and POWL semantics.**  
CS-9 is the controlled, formally approved standard effective from 2025-01-01. M0 is a POWL implementation of part of CS-9, not a complete expression of all CS-9 obligations. In M0:

- `checks` is a `StrictPartialOrder` containing `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`. Semantically, the two checks are concurrent: either order in the log can conform, but both must occur before the decision because of the edge `checks -> decision`.
- The approved branch requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
- `decision` is an XOR between the approved branch and `Reject_Supplier`; a case should take one branch, not both.
- `Notify_Requester` follows the decision and precedes `Close_Case`.
- M0 cannot express resource identity, segregation of duties, conflict-of-interest disclosure, amounts, risk class, effective dates, business-day deadlines, loops, or incomplete/mirrored logging.

**Stipulated facts.**

1. M0 does not contain `Auto_Approve`, `Request_More_Info`, emergency provisional activation, reactivation, withdrawal, duplicate-event handling, label renaming, or resource/COI rules.
2. CS-9 requires sanctions and quality before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans for requester and manual approver. The resource rule is not represented in M0.
3. PD-3 existed as a documented steering-committee pilot for 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, but was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. Its authority is contested by the later compliance-observer statement.
4. FD-7 was effective 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59 and allowed provisional activation for critical flood-relief suppliers, subject to sanctions before activation, spend cap, monitoring, and retrospective approval/quality review.
5. From 2026-07-01, notification logging moved to CRM; only about 70% of notifications are mirrored, and older links can be mutable text fields.
6. Operations uses `Request_More_Info` loops; compliance regards the behavior as desirable, but M0 has no such activity or loop.
7. Aggregate evidence shows 312 Auto_Approve cases in June/July: 289 apparently PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility attributes; six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in early August.
8. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after the CRM migration.
9. Internal Audit identified SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

**Proposition status.**

Supported by the evidence:

- M0 is under-inclusive: it omits RMI, pilot/emergency routes, reactivation, withdrawal, resource rules, and label-mapping mechanics.
- SUP-703 used an ineligible pilot path for a HIGH-risk supplier.
- SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier before sanctions clearance.
- SUP-704 falls within FD-7’s temporal window and stated conditions.
- SUP-705 and SUP-714 occurred after written authority expired.
- SUP-712 violates CS-9’s requester/approver separation rule, even though it conforms to M0’s control flow.
- SUP-715 conforms to M0 but lacks a required COI disclosure.
- The CRM migration plausibly explains many missing `Notify_Requester` events.
- SUP-711’s duplicate `Register_Supplier` is supported by the same source transaction ID.

Disputed:

- Whether PD-3 is a valid temporary exception capable of justifying otherwise nonconforming low-risk auto-approvals, given the compliance observer’s later statement.
- Whether rejected leads and reactivations are in scope for M0/CS-9.
- Whether CRM evidence with mutable links is sufficient to prove notification.
- Whether retroactive label mapping for `QA_Clearance` is sufficient for conformance.
- Whether factual emergency conditions after FD-7 expiry can justify SUP-705.
- Whether `Auto_Approve` includes or evidences the required automated master-data validation.

Unproved:

- Whether SUP-702 and the 289 apparently eligible Auto_Approve cases all have complete automated master-data validation evidence.
- Whether SUP-706’s CRM message is reliably linked to the case and occurred before closure.
- Whether SUP-710’s prior quality certification remained valid and whether a waiver was authorized.
- Whether SUP-715’s relationship created a material conflict beyond the disclosure failure.
- Whether daily monitoring for emergency cases was complete in all cases beyond SUP-704.

**Burden-of-proof approach.**

1. **For declaring noncompliance:** the prosecution must identify an applicable rule, its effective date, the case’s inclusion in scope, and reliable evidence of breach. Where the event log is known to be incomplete because of CRM migration or migration duplication, log absence alone is not conclusive if credible external evidence exists. However, if a mandatory control has no reliable evidence and no valid exception applies, the case should be treated as noncompliant or, if evidence is genuinely ambiguous, data-quality indeterminate.
2. **For accepting an exception:** the party invoking the exception must prove valid authority, temporal applicability, case eligibility, satisfaction of all material conditions, and monitoring/evidence requirements. Informal practice, operator belief, or “imminent amendment” is insufficient. A documented pilot may justify low-risk, time-boxed exceptions if the case-specific evidence is complete; an expired directive does not continue by factual inertia.
3. **For model/scope defects:** if the deviation is caused by M0’s omission, ambiguous scope, label change, or logging architecture, and the substantive control is evidenced, the dominant classification should be model/scope defect rather than misconduct.
4. **For data-quality indeterminacy:** if the evidence is neither sufficient to prove compliance nor sufficient to prove violation, the case should be classified as data-quality indeterminate and remediated, not silently treated as compliant or noncompliant.
5. **Materiality:** breaches of sanctions-before-activation, unauthorized high-risk auto-approval, segregation of duties, and COI nondisclosure are material even absent proven loss. Low-risk, time-boxed, monitored exceptions with complete evidence are less material but still require formal governance.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is not that every deviation is bad faith; it is that the controlled standard was repeatedly bypassed without durable authority, and that some bypasses defeated high-risk preventive controls.

**1. Formal authority favors CS-9 and M0 until formally amended.**  
CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer’s initial “monitored exception” note is weakened by her later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9. FD-7 was explicit but expired automatically. Under a formal governance model, temporary convenience cannot amend a controlled standard. Therefore, cases that omit manual approval, omit pre-activation quality, or activate before sanctions are noncompliant unless a clear, written, time-boxed exception applies.

**2. High-risk unauthorized deviations are material.**  
SUP-703 is the strongest prosecution case: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier was put through the pilot despite ineligibility, activated via `Auto_Approve`, and then incurred EUR 180,000 in spend. This is not a harmless low-risk pilot; it is misuse of an exception path for a supplier category that CS-9 treats as requiring manual approval and quality before activation. SUP-708 is equally serious: a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance. CS-9 makes sanctions a precondition to activation. The absence of transactions before clearance may reduce loss, but it does not cure the control breach; sanctions screening is precisely a preventive control.

**3. Expiry matters: SUP-705 and SUP-714 show normalization of exceptions.**  
FD-7 expired on 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-705 occurred on 2026-07-13 with no written extension. Factual flood recovery does not automatically extend a written emergency directive. SUP-714 occurred on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 expired, with no permanent amendment. Operations’ belief that permanent approval was imminent is not authority. The aggregate evidence reinforces this concern: six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in early August, and 14 June/July Auto_Approve cases were clearly ineligible. This is classic normalization of deviance: a temporary exception becomes routine practice without policy change.

**4. M0 conformance can hide substantive noncompliance.**  
SUP-712 conforms to M0’s control flow but violates CS-9’s resource rule because requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through aliases. SUP-715 also conforms to M0 but lacks a required COI disclosure where the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line. If conformance were judged only by M0, these material control failures would be invisible. This supports enforcing CS-9, not merely M0, and adding resource/COI checks outside POWL.

**5. Data reliability problems weaken the defense.**  
Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after CRM migration. SUP-706 has no `Notify_Requester` in the onboarding log; the CRM message is linked only by a mutable text field entered two days later. If notification cannot be reliably evidenced, closure cannot be treated as fully compliant. SUP-702’s CRM notification is stronger because it has a stable `notification_id`, but the trace still lacks an explicit `Master_Data_Validation` event required by PD-3. The burden of proving an exception should include proving all required controls.

**6. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.**  
`Request_More_Info` may be desirable, and SUP-707 shows no activation before requested evidence arrived. But M0 has no such activity or loop, and CS-9 was not amended to include it. Likewise, successful flood recovery does not retroactively validate SUP-705 after FD-7 expired. Successful low-risk auto-approvals do not retroactively create a permanent auto-approval branch. Policy change must be controlled.

**Prosecution acknowledgment of model/data defects.**  
The prosecution must concede that M0 is not fit as the sole compliance oracle. M0 omits RMI, pilot/emergency routes, reactivation, withdrawal, label mapping, and resource rules. The CRM migration creates real notification-evidence problems. SUP-711 appears to be a duplicate caused by migration. SUP-713 involves a renamed but substantively equivalent quality activity. SUP-709 and SUP-710 expose scope ambiguity. These defects mean M0 should not be enforced literally in every case. But they do not excuse ineligible high-risk auto-approval, activation before sanctions, expired-authority auto-approval, or resource/COI violations.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is that M0 is an obsolete, incomplete, and partially mis-scoped model applied to inadequate data, and that many apparent deviations are either authorized temporary exceptions, harmless model gaps, or logging artifacts.

**1. M0’s semantics make it over-restrictive and operationally unrealistic.**  
M0 treats sanctions and quality as concurrent pre-decision checks, but it has no mechanism for iterative information gathering. SUP-707 contains two `Request_More_Info` events before approval. Compliance agrees the behavior is desirable, and no activation occurred before the requested evidence arrived. M0 cannot express this loop; treating SUP-707 as misconduct would punish a risk-reducing practice. Similarly, M0 has no auto-approval, emergency, reactivation, or withdrawal route, so it inevitably classifies governed real-world behavior as deviant.

**2. PD-3 provides a documented, time-boxed overlay for SUP-702.**  
SUP-702 was a LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group on 2026-06-15, squarely within PD-3’s window and eligibility criteria. Sanctions occurred before activation; quality occurred the next business day; CRM shows notification two hours after activation with a stable `notification_id`; pilot eligibility fields are complete. PD-3 was recorded in steering-committee minutes and a follow-up email, and the compliance observer initially wrote that the pilot could proceed as a monitored exception. The later statement that she did not approve “weakening CS-9” can be read as insisting that the pilot was an exception, not a permanent amendment. For an eligible low-risk case with monitoring evidence, SUP-702 is better characterized as a justified exception than as material noncompliance.

**3. FD-7 authorizes SUP-704.**  
SUP-704 occurred on 2026-07-11, within FD-7’s window, for a critical pump supplier. Sanctions occurred before activation; initial spend was EUR 18,000, below the EUR 20,000 cap; daily monitoring exists; quality and retrospective manual approval occurred the next business day. This is exactly the kind of controlled emergency flexibility FD-7 contemplated. Prosecuting SUP-704 under M0 would ignore a superior, temporally specific emergency directive.

**4. CRM migration and data defects explain several apparent deviations.**  
From 2026-07-01, notifications moved to CRM and only about 70% were mirrored. SUP-706’s missing log notification is therefore not automatically a process failure; it may be a logging gap. The CRM message exists, although the link is weak. SUP-711 has duplicate `Register_Supplier` events, but both rows share the same source transaction ID, indicating a migration duplicate rather than double registration. SUP-713 uses `QA_Clearance` because the procedure and UI renamed `Quality_Review`; audit evidence shows equivalent content. These are model/data mapping defects, not business misconduct.

**5. Scope ambiguity undermines rejected-lead and reactivation findings.**  
SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen, so no quality review was performed. CS-9 explicitly allows rejected leads to use an abbreviated pre-screen process but does not say whether those leads are M0 cases. The process owner says they are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance. The scope document is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation with no `Register_Supplier` and no new `Quality_Review`; prior quality certification remained valid, and CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. These are scope/model defects, not clear violations.

**6. Trace deviation is not the same as business misconduct.**  
SUP-705 and SUP-714 are governance lapses because authority expired, but both involved low spend or low-risk eligible suppliers and no alleged loss. They are better treated as exception-management failures than as severe operational fraud. SUP-707’s RMI loop improved evidence quality. SUP-713’s label rename did not remove the quality control. SUP-711’s duplicate event is a data-quality artifact. A model that cannot distinguish these from SUP-703 or SUP-708 is not a reliable compliance oracle.

**Defense acknowledgment of genuine risk and limits.**  
The defense cannot excuse SUP-703: a HIGH-risk ineligible supplier should not have been auto-approved. It cannot excuse SUP-708: activation before sanctions clearance is a direct breach of a preventive control, even if no transaction occurred before clearance. It cannot treat expired directives as open-ended authority; SUP-705 and SUP-714 require formal remediation. It also cannot ignore CS-9’s resource rule in SUP-712 or COI disclosure failure in SUP-715 merely because M0 is silent. Informal practice may reveal needed model change, but it cannot permanently displace controlled policy.

**System-level judgment.**

The operation is not uniformly noncompliant, but it is not clean either. There is material noncompliance in specific cases and patterns:

- SUP-703 and SUP-708 are severe control failures.
- SUP-705 and SUP-714, plus the six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases, show failure to stop using an expired exception.
- The 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases are noncompliant if the facts mirror SUP-703.
- The 9 Auto_Approve cases with missing eligibility attributes are not proven exceptions; they should be treated as indeterminate until evidence is produced.
- SUP-712 and SUP-715 show that M0 conformance can coexist with substantive resource/COI violations.
- Notification missingness after CRM migration is a systemic data-integrity problem.

At the same time, M0 is not valid as the sole normative conformance model. It is obsolete, incomplete, and partially mis-scoped. It fails to represent RMI, pilots, emergencies, reactivations, withdrawals, label changes, duplicate suppression, and resource rules. Therefore, the organization should not “enforce M0” literally. It should enforce CS-9 through a revised architecture: a revised POWL for control flow, declarative constraints for resource/time/response rules, pandas/data checks for eligibility/amount/identity/timestamps, and organizational governance for exceptions.

**Where I reject the prosecution.**

- I reject the prosecution’s implicit claim that every M0 deviation equals noncompliance. SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 show that authorized exceptions, scope ambiguity, data duplication, or model omission can be the dominant explanation.
- I reject treating M0 as complete when it omits CS-9’s own resource rule and known operational routes.
- I reject treating log absence of notification as conclusive after the CRM migration where credible stable CRM evidence exists, as in SUP-702. For SUP-706, however, the evidence is too weak, so the result is indeterminate rather than noncompliant.
- I reject punishing SUP-707 as misconduct when compliance itself regards RMI as desirable and no control was defeated.

**Where I reject the defense.**

- I reject the claim that model obsolescence excuses SUP-703 or SUP-708. Ineligible high-risk auto-approval and activation before sanctions are substantive breaches regardless of model fit.
- I reject informal practice or “imminent amendment” as authority for SUP-705 and SUP-714. Expired exceptions require written extension or formal amendment.
- I reject the idea that no transactions before sanctions clearance cures SUP-708. Sanctions screening is preventive; late screening defeats the control.
- I reject the idea that M0 fit is sufficient for SUP-712 and SUP-715. CS-9 and COI policy impose resource obligations outside POWL.
- I reject treating all 312 Auto_Approve cases as justified merely because many were eligible. Eligibility, validation, notification, and authority must be evidenced case by case.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should be layered so that POWL does only what POWL can do well: represent control-flow ordering and route structure. Data-aware, identity-aware, temporal, monetary, and governance conditions belong outside POWL.

**Layer 1: Controlled policy layer.**

- CS-9 should be amended through controlled change management.
- The amendment should define:
  - standard onboarding;
  - low-risk auto-approval, if made permanent;
  - emergency provisional activation;
  - rejected-lead pre-screen scope;
  - reactivation scope;
  - withdrawal scope;
  - resource and COI rules;
  - notification evidence requirements.
- Pilots and emergencies must have:
  - rule/version ID;
  - authority document;
  - effective start/end timestamps;
  - eligibility conditions;
  - spend caps;
  - monitoring requirements;
  - expiry and extension rules;
  - retrospective ratification requirements.

**Layer 2: POWL control-flow layer.**

POWL should represent:

- concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` for the standard route;
- manual approval before activation for the standard route;
- a low-risk auto-approval route with sanctions and master-data validation before activation and quality after activation;
- an emergency provisional route with sanctions before activation and quality/manual approval after activation;
- a repeatable `Request_More_Info` mechanism, or a defensible approximation;
- rejection after sanctions, with an optional full-check rejection path;
- notification before closure;
- reactivation and withdrawal routes as separate scope branches.

POWL should not attempt to enforce risk class, country, corporate-group eligibility, spend caps, business-day deadlines, actor identity, or COI disclosure.

**Layer 3: DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints.**

Declarative constraints should express:

- `Activate_Supplier` must not occur before `Sanctions_Check`, except on an emergency route.
- For auto and emergency routes, `Quality_Review` must occur within two business days after activation.
- `Request_More_Info` must not occur after activation.
- `Notify_Requester` must occur before `Close_Case`.
- Requester and manual approver must be different canonical persons.
- If a COI relationship exists, a disclosure event/record must exist.
- `Auto_Approve` may occur only when an active pilot rule and eligibility snapshot exist.
- Emergency activation may occur only within an active emergency directive window.

**Layer 4: pandas/data checks.**

Data checks should enforce:

- effective-date validation for PD-3, FD-7, and any future overlay;
- eligibility snapshots: risk class, country, corporate group, approved-group flag;
- spend caps and cumulative spend until retrospective approval;
- business-day calculations for post-activation quality;
- canonicalization of actor aliases to one person ID;
- segregation-of-duties checks;
- COI relationship and disclosure checks;
- duplicate suppression using `source_transaction_id` and idempotency keys;
- label mapping using effective dates and mapping version IDs;
- notification evidence using immutable `notification_id` and stable case linkage.

**Layer 5: Organizational controls.**

Organizational controls should include:

- exception registration and compliance monitoring;
- emergency extension approval;
- COI disclosure workflow;
- delegation approval workflow;
- CRM-to-process-log integration with immutable identifiers;
- audit sampling of auto-approvals and emergency activations;
- periodic review of model drift between POWL and operational practice.

**Explicit scope rules.**

- **Rejected leads:** If a lead fails sanctions pre-screen, it may follow an abbreviated rejection path outside the full onboarding model. If it passes pre-screen, it becomes a full onboarding case requiring standard controls unless a controlled exception applies.
- **Reactivations:** Reactivation is a separate case type. It requires sanctions check and manual approval. Quality review may be replaced by a documented `Quality_Waiver` only if prior certification is valid and waiver authority exists.
- **Withdrawals:** Withdrawal is a separate case type requiring withdrawal event, notification, and closure, without full onboarding controls.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following executable Python block constructs a revised POWL, `M1`, and includes normalized policy-overlay, scope, label-mapping, event-schema, declarative-constraint, and data-check proposals. It does not read external files. It avoids using silent transitions to hide mandatory controls; the only silent move represents the optional absence of `Request_More_Info`. If the PM4Py POWL implementation does not fully support `Operator.LOOP`, the code degrades to an approximation; in production, use a finite unrolling or a declarative log-skeleton constraint for RMI repetition.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Silent transition fallback: used only to represent "no Request_More_Info".
try:
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import SilentTransition
    def make_silent():
        return SilentTransition()
except Exception:
    def make_silent():
        return Transition(label=None)

# If a PM4Py version lacks LOOP, fall back to a unary XOR approximation.
# This is a defensible approximation only; production should use a finite
# RMI unrolling or DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints for repeatable RMI.
LOOP_OPERATOR = getattr(Operator, "LOOP", Operator.XOR)

def make_optional_rmi():
    rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=LOOP_OPERATOR,
        children=[Transition(label="Request_More_Info")]
    )
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[make_silent(), rmi_loop]
    )

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard route:
# Register -> [Sanctions, Quality, optional RMI concurrent] ->
# Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier -> Notify_Requester
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_rmi = make_optional_rmi()
std_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality, std_rmi])

std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
std_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[std_pre, std_manual, std_activate, std_notify]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_pre, std_manual)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_manual, std_activate)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_activate, std_notify)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route:
# Sanctions + Master_Data_Validation + optional RMI before Auto_Approve.
# Auto_Approve -> Activate_Supplier.
# Quality_Review and Notify_Requester are concurrent after activation,
# allowing notification before or after post-activation quality,
# while both remain before Close_Case.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_mdm = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_rmi = make_optional_rmi()
auto_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_mdm, auto_rmi])

auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
auto_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

auto_post = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_quality, auto_notify])

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_pre, auto_approve, auto_activate, auto_post]
)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_pre, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_post)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency provisional route:
# Sanctions before activation.
# Quality, retrospective manual approval, and notification are concurrent
# after activation; all must complete before Close_Case.
# Amount caps, daily monitoring, and emergency-window checks are data rules.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
em_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

em_post = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[em_quality, em_manual, em_notify])

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[em_sanctions, em_activate, em_post]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_sanctions, em_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_post)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Rejection routes:
# 1. Abbreviated pre-screen rejection after sanctions only.
# 2. Full rejection after sanctions/quality/optional RMI.
# Both notify before closure.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
rej_abbr_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_abbr = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
rej_abbr_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

reject_abbr_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rej_abbr_sanctions, rej_abbr, rej_abbr_notify]
)
reject_abbr_route.order.add_edge(rej_abbr_sanctions, rej_abbr)
reject_abbr_route.order.add_edge(rej_abbr, rej_abbr_notify)

rej_full_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_full_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
rej_full_rmi = make_optional_rmi()
rej_full_pre = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rej_full_sanctions, rej_full_quality, rej_full_rmi]
)

rej_full = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
rej_full_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

reject_full_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rej_full_pre, rej_full, rej_full_notify]
)
reject_full_route.order.add_edge(rej_full_pre, rej_full)
reject_full_route.order.add_edge(rej_full, rej_full_notify)

reject_route = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[reject_abbr_route, reject_full_route]
)

# New onboarding route choice after Register_Supplier.
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route]
)

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
new_onboarding = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice])
new_onboarding.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Reactivation with full quality review.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
react_q_req = Transition(label="Reactivate_Supplier")
react_q_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
react_q_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
react_q_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[react_q_sanctions, react_q_quality])

react_q_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
react_q_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
react_q_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

reactivation_quality_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[react_q_req, react_q_pre, react_q_manual, react_q_activate, react_q_notify]
)
reactivation_quality_route.order.add_edge(react_q_req, react_q_pre)
reactivation_quality_route.order.add_edge(react_q_pre, react_q_manual)
reactivation_quality_route.order.add_edge(react_q_manual, react_q_activate)
reactivation_quality_route.order.add_edge(react_q_activate, react_q_notify)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Reactivation with documented quality waiver.
# The waiver is an explicit event; data checks must prove certification
# validity and waiver authority. POWL alone cannot conditionally choose it.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
react_w_req = Transition(label="Reactivate_Supplier")
react_w_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
react_w_waiver = Transition(label="Quality_Waiver")
react_w_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[react_w_sanctions, react_w_waiver])

react_w_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
react_w_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
react_w_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

reactivation_waiver_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[react_w_req, react_w_pre, react_w_manual, react_w_activate, react_w_notify]
)
reactivation_waiver_route.order.add_edge(react_w_req, react_w_pre)
reactivation_waiver_route.order.add_edge(react_w_pre, react_w_manual)
reactivation_waiver_route.order.add_edge(react_w_manual, react_w_activate)
reactivation_waiver_route.order.add_edge(react_w_activate, react_w_notify)

reactivation_route = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[reactivation_quality_route, reactivation_waiver_route]
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Withdrawal route.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
withdraw = Transition(label="Withdraw_Application")
withdraw_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

withdrawal_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[withdraw, withdraw_notify])
withdrawal_route.order.add_edge(withdraw, withdraw_notify)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Top-level model.
# Entry choice selects new onboarding, reactivation, or withdrawal.
# Close_Case follows the selected entry/route; every route contains
# Notify_Requester before route completion, so notification precedes close.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
entry_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[new_onboarding, reactivation_route, withdrawal_route]
```
