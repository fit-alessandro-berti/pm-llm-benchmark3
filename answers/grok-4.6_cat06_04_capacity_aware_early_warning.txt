## 1. Decision rule

Do not rank on `p_now`. Allocate a scarce 45-minute Senior Diagnostic Swarm only when four filters pass, then score the remainder.

**Eligibility (hard gates)**  
1. Stage is `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. After `Diagnosis Complete` the swarm is treated as having essentially zero effect (do not spend a slot on high `p` alone).  
2. No prior swarm on the case unless a *material new event* has occurred (stage change, new diagnostic artifact/log, customer escalation, newly observed missing information, or a calibration-adjusted probability move of at least 0.08 that is *not* explained by a 30-minute model refresh or a failed/imputed feature).  
3. Repeated alerts with no new event are suppressed, not re-queued.  
4. Lower-bound direct value must be positive, except for a narrow urgency override defined below:  
   \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}} = \mathrm{ARR}_{\text{low}} \times V - 450 - B_{\mathrm{FA}} > 0\).

**Calibration-robust probabilities (`p*`)**  
The score uses an adjusted level, not the raw observational `p_now`. These are bias corrections for ranking and thresholding, not causal effects.  
- SME: \(p^* = p_{\text{now}} + 0.06\) (midpoint of the 0.04–0.09 underprediction).  
- Strategic Enterprise: \(p^* = p_{\text{now}} - 0.08\) (midpoint of the 0.05–0.12 overprediction after the redesign).  
- Public Sector: \(p^* = p_{\text{now}}\) (no mean shift; keep a wider interval because the sample is only moderate).  
- Non-Native-Language Support: no mean shift; widen the interval to about \(p_{\text{now}} \pm 0.15\) and rank on the *lower half* of the ARR range.  
Treat every ARR band as an *uncertain, observationally suggested* effect, not a known CATE. Never set expected benefit equal to `p_now` or to `p_now − p_intervened`.

**Act now versus wait**  
Fire now only if the case is already in `Diagnosis Started`, or remaining SLA ≤ 2.5 h (the window will close before a later review), or `p*` is high *and* still rising after a real event. Otherwise watch until `Diagnosis Started` or the next material change. High-value Strategic Enterprise cases with long clocks and large false-alarm burden are waited, not “bought” on first alert.

**Prioritization among act-now cases**  
Use a robust index, then apply portfolio constraints:

\[
S = \underbrace{\mathrm{ARR}_{\text{rob}} \times V}_{\text{use lower half of ARR; NNLS uses the lower bound}} - 450 - B_{\mathrm{FA}} + U - C - \lambda Q
\]

- \(\mathrm{ARR}_{\text{rob}}\): lower half of the supplied range (lower bound for NNLS).  
- \(U\): urgency bonus if SLA ≤ 3 h or already in diagnosis.  
- \(C\): uncertainty penalty (imputation, sparse segment, known miscalibration).  
- \(Q\): per-slot spillover penalty. Base model: about \(6 \times 0.02 = 0.12\) extra expected breaches among untreated diagnosis-stage work, plus 8–20 minutes of shared-queue delay. A slot is presumptively unjustified if even the midpoint ARR is below ~0.12 *and* \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}}\) is weak.

**Portfolio constraints (not a single threshold)**  
- At most four slots; default is **three used, one reserved** until 15:00 for a possible regulatory arrival. The reserve is a preference, not a lock: consume it if a live eligible case clears the robust hurdle after a data check, or if a regulatory case arrives and is stage-eligible.  
- Do not spend two specialists’ diagnosis capacity on more than three planned swarms before the reserve decision.  
- Fairness floor: if an SME or NNLS case is time-critical, stage-eligible, and \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}} > 0\), it cannot be ranked below a *wait-eligible* high-value case that still has a long clock.  
- One case, one swarm, unless a material new event occurs.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down.** After an alert that is not acted on, suppress re-alerts for 90 minutes *or* until a material new event, whichever comes first. A 30-minute scoring refresh is not an event.

**Suppression.** Block the case if (a) it already received a swarm and no material new event has occurred, or (b) `p*` moved by less than 0.08 with no new process signal. Log suppressed alerts for audit; do not show them to the duty manager as fresh work.

**Escalation.** A suppressed or watched case escalates to act-now when any of these fire: transition into `Diagnosis Started`; remaining SLA crosses 2.5 h with \(p^* \ge 0.50\); new diagnostic artifact; customer or regulator escalation; restored features that confirm a previously imputed jump.

**Re-entry after a swarm.** Second swarm only if a *different* failure mode is evidenced (new log, missing CI, wrong assignment) *and* the case is still pre-`Diagnosis Complete` *and* a slot remains after first-time claims. Same-reason repeats are forbidden.

**I-05 versus I-07.**  
I-05 is a textbook suppress: third alert, 0.55→0.57, no new event, refresh artifact. Its attractive ARR (0.25–0.40) and SME underprediction are reasons to *keep it on a watch list*, not to spend a slot at 12:00. Re-enter only on stage change, a true `p*` jump, or SLA ≤ 2.5 h with still-high `p*`.  
I-07 is a textbook re-entry: also a third alert, and `p` even fell slightly (0.75→0.72), but a new customer diagnostic log is exactly the input a senior swarm can use, SME calibration pushes `p*` to ~0.78, and 1.5 h remains. The slightly lower `p_now` does not justify another cool-down.

**Intraday cadence.** Review the watch list (I-01, I-04, I-06, I-08, I-10) at the next stage change and at 13:30 / 15:00. I-06’s data check is a 30–45 minute exception, not a standing poll.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based starvation.** A pure \(V \times \mathrm{ARR}\) ranking would take I-04 and I-08 and skip I-07 (€5k) and possibly I-01/I-10. That systematically withholds help from SME cases that are actually underpredicted. Safeguards: (i) the act-now vs wait split so long-clock high-\(V\) cases cannot pre-empt closing-window low-\(V\) cases; (ii) a daily floor — at least one slot (or the first reserved release) is available to below-median-\(V\) cases with \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}}>0\) and SLA ≤ 3 h; (iii) report intervention rate by value quartile, not only portfolio EV.

**NNLS uncertainty.** Sparse calibration makes I-09 look “optional.” Dropping uncertain language-support cases by default is a disparate-impact rule. Safeguard: rank NNLS on the lower ARR half rather than excluding them; require a human NNLS-capable senior in the swarm; tag the case for post-hoc effect review; do not use missing language-segment performance as evidence of low risk.

**Differential calibration.** SME risk is understated; Strategic Enterprise risk is overstated. Uncorrected, the policy over-swarms SE and under-swarms SME — the opposite of the raw-value bias, and both are unfair. Safeguard: apply the `p*` offsets above; publish weekly reliability diagrams by segment; freeze SE-only expansion of the rule until post-redesign calibration shrinks below ~0.05 ECE.

**Selective intervention labels.** Swarmed cases that then meet SLA will look “easy” in the next training extract if the intervention flag is ignored. The model will learn to copy the policy, not the untreated risk. Safeguard: persist `intervened`, `slot_used`, `stage_at_swarm`, `suppressed`, and `spillover_exposed` on every case prefix; train with action-conditioned outcomes (T-learner / IPW, not naive supervised `p`); never use post-swarm prefixes as untreated labels.

**Spillover incidence.** Delay and +0.01–0.03 risk land on the six *untreated* diagnosis-stage cases — typically lower-`p`, lower-\(V\), or less “loud” segments, including SME and unlisted work. Value-based selection can dump congestion onto the cases already deprioritized. Safeguard: cap planned swarms at three until spillover is measured; monitor diagnosis wait and breach by *non-intervened* segment; if untreated SME/public diagnosis cases take a disproportionate delay share for two consecutive days, cut the cap to two or shorten swarm duration.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational.** Daily: slots used vs reserved, stage at swarm, minutes of specialist interrupt, time from alert to swarm start, I-05-style suppress rate, I-07-style legitimate re-entry rate, and whether any swarm was launched after `Diagnosis Complete` (target: zero). Flag if reserve is burned before 13:00 without a confirmed eligible case.

**Predictive.** Weekly ECE / Brier / reliability slopes for SME, SE, Public Sector, NNLS, and imputed-feature prefixes. Track `p_now −` realized untreated breach. Recalibrate intercepts when a segment’s bias leaves the stated band for two weeks (SME 0.04–0.09 under, SE 0.05–0.12 over). I-06-type imputation prefixes get a separate slice.

**Causal-effect.** The supplied ARR bands are not identified effects. Estimate them: (i) log every near-tie (two eligible cases, one slot) as a quasi-experiment; (ii) compare breach and time-to-`Diagnosis Complete` for swarmed vs eligible-but-waited cases, adjusted for `p*`, stage, and SLA clock; (iii) when volume allows, run a two-week stepped-wedge or 20% holdout on *marginal* Watch-tier cases only — never on I-03-class ineligible cases and never by starving the fairness floor. Update ARR bands quarterly; shrink a segment’s band toward zero if the estimated effect’s CI covers 0.

**Capacity and spillover.** After each slot, measure added diagnosis-queue minutes and the 24-hour breach increment among the six designated untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Compare to 8–20 minutes and 0.01–0.03. If realized spillover exceeds 0.03 or delay exceeds 30 minutes for two days, drop the default cap from three to two and raise the ARR hurdle.

**Fairness.** Dashboard intervention rate, suppress rate, wait time, and breach rate by segment, language support, and value quartile, for both treated and untreated cases. Alert if NNLS or SME intervention rate at similar `p*` and stage falls below half the SE rate, or if spillover delay is concentrated in one segment.

**Drift and rule change.** Recalculate `p*` offsets every Monday from the last four weeks of *untreated* prefixes. Change the allocation rule when any of these hold: (1) segment ECE > 0.05 for two weeks; (2) estimated ARR for a selected profile is below the spillover hurdle; (3) net portfolio breaches (direct avoided − spillover) are negative on a two-week rolling basis; (4) fairness alerts fire twice in a month. Promote a Watch case type to Act-now only after both calibration and a non-zero effect estimate are in hand. Demote an Act-now profile (as I-02 would be if SE ARR realizes near 0.08 and spillover stays high) without waiting for a full model rebuild.

Until those measurements exist, the deployable stance is conservative: three targeted pre-diagnosis swarms (I-07, I-02, I-09), one reserve, no spend on I-03, and no spend on I-05’s unchanged repeat.
