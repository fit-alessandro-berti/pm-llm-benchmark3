# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource ID trust vs. shared accounts**: 
- If `analyst_pool` (G5) is trusted as a single resource, utilization calculations exceed 100%, and service-time distributions become bi-modal or multi-peaked due to overlapping windows. Treating it as a shared account reveals true concurrency and aligns utilization with expected capacity (100%). 
- For G3 (`T-C`), trusting the ID implies a single reviewer; the staggered starts and identical end suggest sequential work with delayed logging. If `T-C` were shared, the identical end could indicate multiple reviewers finishing simultaneously, but the context explicitly notes folder-based submission, making shared-account less likely.

**Exact vs. interval-censored timestamps**:
- G7 (`legacy_MES`) with 15-min precision: Treating timestamps as exact fixes service time at 15 min and erases variability. Interval-censoring `[14:00, 14:15]` restores realistic bounds and prevents false bottleneck signals.
- G6 (`manual_bulk_form`): Exact timestamps imply 0 service time and massive waiting. Interval-censoring `[queue_arrival, 17:00]` for waiting and `[17:00, 17:00]` for service (or exclusion) corrects the artificial zero-duration bottleneck.
- G1 (`PLC`): 1-sec precision allows exact matching, but if coarsened to 1-min, the pattern persists. Robust to tolerance changes.

**Three cases where the same temporal pattern supports multiple physical explanations**:
1. **G1 (Identical start/end)**: Could be genuine fixture-based batch processing (4 items on one fixture) OR PLC timestamp rounding/coarsening. Without fixture logs, both are defensible.
2. **G3 (Staggered start, identical end)**: Could be bulk form submission after sequential review OR multiple reviewers finishing at the same clock time due to shift handover. The form context favors bulk submission, but identical ends are structurally identical to synchronized completions.
3. **G7 (Identical 15-min windows)**: Could be legacy MES rounding to quarter-hours OR actual batch measurement where all samples are processed together in a 15-min cycle. The documented rounding rule strongly favors coarsening, but operational batching cannot be ruled out without source validation.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

1. **Execution vs. Recording timestamps**: Decouple `execution_timestamp` (when work actually starts/finishes) from `recorded_at` (DB insertion). Bulk forms and legacy systems must log execution moments, not submission moments.
2. **Source clocks**: Synchronize all workstations to a central NTP server. Log `clock_offset` metadata to correct safety-system vs. repair-queue discrepancies (G8).
3. **Resource/Badge identity**: Replace shared logins with badge-level or session-level identity mapping. If shared accounts are unavoidable, log `user_session_id` or `badge_id` alongside `resource`.
4. **Group/Batch identifiers**: Add `batch_id` or `fixture_id` to events. This explicitly distinguishes operational batches (G1) from logging batches (G6) and enables correct trace reconstruction.
5. **Workstation capacity metadata**: Document fixture limits, inspector concurrency limits, and scanner throughput. This allows process mining tools to validate whether observed concurrency exceeds physical capacity.
6. **Pause/Resume events**: Capture explicit `pause` and `resume` lifecycle events. This separates waiting, downtime, and active service time, critical for G2 (group setup) and G8 (safety check).
7. **Timestamp precision metadata**: Store `precision` as a numeric field (seconds) rather than text. Enable automatic interval-censoring in downstream analytics. Log `rounding_rule` (e.g., “down to 15-min boundary”) for legacy systems.

# 7. Final adjudication

- **High confidence interpretation**: G4 and G8. G4 shows a clear sequential pipeline with constant service and waiting, accurately captured by 1-sec timestamps. G8 shows sequential processing after a documented capacity loss (safety check), with pipeline structure preserved post-recovery. Both require no major data correction, only contextual annotation of downtime.
- **Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis**: G6 and G7. G6’s identical start/complete across a 2.5-hour queue arrival window is a logging artifact (bulk submission). Including it in waiting/service analysis creates false bottlenecks and inflated waits. G7’s timestamps are explicitly rounded to 15-minute boundaries; treating them as exact erases variability. Both should be interval-censored or excluded from service-time calculations.
- **Require operational validation**: G1, G3, G5. G1’s identical times could be fixture batching or PLC rounding; validation requires fixture cycle logs. G3’s bulk end submission masks true completion; validation requires form submission timestamps vs. actual review completion. G5’s shared account overlaps require badge/session mapping to confirm concurrency vs. sequential handoff. Until validated, these clusters should be analyzed with interval bounds and explicit concurrency flags. G2 and G4 are operationally sound but G2’s group-start bias should be noted in waiting-time reports.
