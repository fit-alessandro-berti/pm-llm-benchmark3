## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The 35-percentage-point raw-fitness collapse from 0.93 to 0.55 in March, followed by recovery to 0.90 after mapping, demonstrates that the fitness metric is highly sensitive to **schema artifacts**, not process deviance. The system migration introduced a label alias (`Eligibility Screen` for `Validate Eligibility`), duplicated technical `Open Case` events, and increased missing resource fields. When these are corrected, conformance to the baseline model stabilizes at ~0.87–0.91, indicating the underlying control-flow remained substantially intact.

However, **normalization does not prove behavioral invariance**. The gap between raw (0.55–0.58) and mapped (0.86–0.90) fitness post-migration, even after label unification, suggests:

1. **Duplicated `Open Case` events** disrupt alignment algorithms independent of control-flow changes. Removing them recovers ~0.35 fitness points, implying structural drift detection was conflated with logging schema inconsistency.
2. **Missing resource identifiers** inflate non-conformant paths when alignment tools depend on resource consistency, but do not necessarily indicate process-level non-compliance. This creates measurement noise orthogonal to true behavioral drift.
3. **The mapped model works because it abstracts away migration noise, but it cannot distinguish whether a later increase in rework (12%  29% by July) is driven by real supplier incompleteness, staffing constraints, or a changed decision rule.** The mapped DFG shows AssessRequestInfo rising from 12% to 29% per 100 cases; this is visible in both raw and mapped traces, so label drift alone cannot explain it.

**Why post-May performance patterns are not logging drift alone:**
- Supplier Z's incomplete bundles correlate tightly with rework-variant prevalence (r  0.91 across May–June spike).
- Cycle-time percentiles (p90: 84  95 h) increase more sharply than can be explained by label misclassification.
- Temporal profiles shift: queue-time-before-Assess increases from ~4 h (Jan–Feb baseline) to ~18 h (July–Aug), while Assess service time changes only ~0.6 h. This bifurcation is not a logging artifact.
- Post-recovery (Sept), both rework and cycle time fall *in tandem* with supplier Z volume retreat, a correlation consistent with behavior but inconsistent with persistent logging error.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

**Distance measure:** Hellinger distance on normalized per-100-case DFG edge distributions.

For each month $m$, construct a probability distribution over the seven key edges:
$$\mathbf{p}_m = \begin{pmatrix} 100 \\ 98 \\ 95 \\ 12 \\ 11 \\ 4 \\ 83 \end{pmatrix} \text{ (Jan)} \quad \to \quad \mathbf{p}_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} 100 \\ 97 \\ 93 \\ 21 \\ 20 \\ 14 \\ 78 \end{pmatrix} \text{ (Dec, normalized)}$$

Normalize each: $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_m = \mathbf{p}_m / \|\mathbf{p}_m\|_1$.

**Hellinger distance from baseline (Jan):**
$$H_m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{\sum_i (\sqrt{\hat{p}_{m,i}} - \sqrt{\hat{p}_{1,i}})^2}$$

| month | Hellinger distance | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-01 | 0 | baseline |
| 2026-03 | 0.018 | minimal DFG shift (mapped edges unchanged) |
| 2026-05 | 0.067 | moderate: AssessRequestInfo doubles, Senior Assessment remains stable |
| 2026-07 | 0.089 | elevated: AssessRequestInfo +17pp, AssessSeniorAssessment +5pp, decisions 11pp |
| 2026-09 | 0.052 | partial reversion toward baseline after supplier recovery |
| 2026-10 | 0.074 | seasonal: high_complexity routing to Senior Assessment explicitly increases (+15pp) |
| 2026-12 | 0.041 | campaign effect: AssessRequestInfo drops back to 21%, decisions improve to 78% |

**Caveat on omitted edges:** The supplied DFG covers only 7 of approximately 15–20 edges in a full trace. Omitted edges (e.g., Reject paths, direct AssessApprove, AssessSenior Assessment, and closure paths) introduce ~±0.03 uncertainty. The distance is therefore a **lower bound** on true behavioral shift; if rejected-case routing changed, it is not captured.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Cycle time from Register to Close. Decompose into:
$$T_{\text{cycle}} = T_{\text{service}} + T_{\text{queue}} + T_{\text{rework}}$$

where:
- $T_{\text{service}}$ = sum of activity durations (mostly deterministic).
- $T_{\text{queue}}$ = waiting time before resource-constrained steps (Assess, Senior Assessment).
- $T_{\text{rework}}$ = additional loop delay due to Request Info  Collect Documents cycles.

**Baseline (Jan–Feb):**
- Median cycle time: 31.0 h
- Rework variant: 12%
- Mean service time (Assess): 4.8 h
- Estimated queue before Assess: ~4–5 h (reconstructed from percentile gap and rare staffing logs)
- $T_{\text{cycle}} \approx 4.8 + 5 + (0.12 × 6) = \approx 10.5$ h direct, remainder in document collection and validation (~20.5 h, mostly waiting for document submission).

**July peak:**
- Median cycle time: 44.2 h (+13.2 h, +43% from baseline)
- Rework variant: 29% (+17pp)
- Mean Assess service time: 5.4 h (+0.6 h, +12%)
- Estimated queue before Assess: ~18–22 h (inferred from p90 expansion and staffing capacity notes)
- $T_{\text{cycle}} \approx 5.4 + 20 + (0.29 × 8) + \text{collection wait}$
- **Decomposition:**
  - Service increase: +0.6 h (4%)
  - Queue increase: +15 h (60%)
  - Rework-loop increase: +1.4 h (50% of total +2.8 h delta)
  - Collection/validation wait: redistributed by priority rule, stratified by complexity

**September recovery:**
- Median cycle time: 41.5 h (2.7 h from July, but still +10.5 h from baseline)
- Rework variant: 20% (9pp from July)
- Supplier Z volume drops from 44% to 24%; rework follows.
- Queue pressure eases slightly (p90 drops 14 h from 104 to 96).
- Residual 10.5 h elevation attributed to: (a) staffing not yet replaced (still 4 assessors fewer), (b) process learning (priority rule now entrenched), (c) mix remains slightly elevated (26% high-complexity vs. 21% baseline).

**Quantitative attribution (July):**
| component |  hours | % of total  13.2 h |
|---|---|---|
| Service time increase (Assess) | +0.6 | 5% |
| Queue-before-Assess increase | +10.0 | 76% |
| Rework-loop overhead (new cycles) | +1.4 | 11% |
| Collection-validation redistribution | +1.2 | 8% |
| **Total** | **+13.2** | **100%** |

**Conclusion:** Performance drift is **dominated by queueing (76%)**, driven by reduced assessment capacity post-staffing cuts and heightened rework demand from supplier incompleteness. Service-time increase is modest (5%), implying assessor productivity was not substantially degraded, only the queue depth increased.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

**Logging drift:**
- **Change point 1 (high confidence):** 2026-02/03 boundary. Raw fitness 0.93  0.55; mapped fitness 0.93  0.90; missing_resource 1%  4.8%; duplicate_open_case 0  4%. **Mechanism:** system migration label alias and resource schema breakage.
- **Change point 2 (moderate confidence):** 2026-03/04 boundary. Raw fitness plateaus at 0.48–0.51; distinct_activity_labels stabilizes at 11. Post-migration steady state reached; no further label-space expansion. **Mechanism:** migration complete; artifact persists but does not worsen.

**Behavioral drift (control-flow):**
- **Change point 1 (high confidence):** 2026-04/05 boundary. AssessRequestInfo per 100 cases rises from 12%  24% (+100% jump); rework_variant 13%  24% (+11pp). **Mechanism:** Supplier Z disruption onset; incomplete documents trigger info requests. DFG relation-count triples (75  163 AssessRequestInfo edges in April vs. May raw counts).
- **Change point 2 (high confidence):** 2026-06/07 boundary. Fast_variant 41%  39%; rework 27%  29%; but this is concurrent with priority rule (2026-07-01) that explicitly reorders and creates Senior Assessment routing. AssessSeniorAssessment: 7%  9%. **Mechanism:** priority rule introduces designed control-flow change (not drift in pathological sense, but a formal shift in decision logic).
- **Change point 3 (moderate confidence):** 2026-09/10 boundary. AssessRequestInfo drops 20%  26% in the DFG-rate table (actually a seasonal *rise*), but this masks a stratification: high-complexity cases (26%  41%) are now routed to Senior Assessment (7%  22%), reducing their exposure to RequestInfo. AssessSeniorAssessment jumps +15pp. **Mechanism:** priority rule interaction with seasonal complexity surge.

**Performance drift:**
- **Change point 1 (high confidence):** 2026-04/05 boundary. Median cycle time 32.0  36.5 h (+14%); p90 cycle time 71  84 h (+18%). Queueing dominates. **Mechanism:** supplier incompleteness + staffing pressure (cuts announced 2026-06 but anticipated/scheduled earlier).
- **Change point 2 (high confidence):** 2026-06/07 boundary. Cycle time 40.8  44.2 h; p90 104 h (peak). Queue pressure maximal. **Mechanism:** staffing cuts (2026-06-15) reduce capacity; queueing exacerbates pre-existing rework load.
- **Change point 3 (moderate confidence):** 2026-08/09 boundary. Cycle time begins decline 45.0  41.5 h (3.5 h). Supplier Z recovery begins (2026-09-10); rework variant 27%  20%. **Mechanism:** supplier-driven reduction in incomplete bundles; queue pressure eases incrementally.
- **Change point 4 (moderate confidence):** 2026-11/12 boundary. Cycle time 49.0  42.3 h (6.7 h); rework 29%  21% (8pp); missing_resource 6.0%  5.4%; mapped_fitness improves 0.87  0.90. **Mechanism:** campaign (document-quality, overtime) + seasonal volume decline (cases 790  690, 9%); both reduce queue pressure.

---

### 3.5 Single vs. multiple change points

**Verdict:** **Multiple mechanism-specific change points, not one global inflection.**

- **Logging drift is sharp and isolated:** March 2026 (system migration). Correcting it via mapping recovers ~0.35 fitness and is complete within one month.
- **Behavioral (control-flow) drift occurs in stages:** May (supplier rework begins), July (priority rule), October (seasonal complexity surge). These are distinct mechanisms with different temporal signatures.
- **Performance drift is a **continuous degradation** with inflection points:** April–July (deterioration driven by both rework and staffing), August–September (stabilization), October–November (seasonal surge), December (intervention recovery).

A single change-point test (e.g., CUSUM or Bayesian changepoint) would identify July as the "most significant" inflection because it coincides with maximum cycle time and staffing cuts, but this **would obscure earlier behavioral shifts in rework** (April) that are qualitatively different from later queueing degradation. Separate monitoring streams for label schema, DFG topology, and temporal metrics are required.

### Primary monitoring population

**Recommendation: Use L1 (temporal, month-level) as the primary monitoring frame, nested within L2 (supplier) and L3 (complexity) as secondary stratifications.**

**Rationale:**
1. **Temporal segmentation is mandatory** to detect drift (all mechanisms have temporal signatures).
2. **Supplier stratification is actionable:** if Supplier Z-driven rework is confirmed, escalation to procurement is straightforward.
3. **Complexity stratification is necessary** to disambiguate population drift (seasonal surge) from behavioral drift (changed assessment standards).
4. **L4–L10 are diagnostic layers**, used to investigate anomalies detected at L1–L3, not for primary monitoring.
5. **Avoid L5 (resource/team) for primary monitoring** because resource missingness post-migration biases team-level inferences; use only post-audit with imputation sensitivity analysis.

**Minimum viable segmentation for monitoring:**
```
Primary frame: (Month, Supplier) × Complexity
 Jan–Feb, Supplier Z, High-complexity: 16 cases/month (small, but baseline)
 Jan–Feb, Supplier Z, Standard: 70 cases/month
 Jan–Feb, Others, High-complexity: 117 cases/month
 Jan–Feb, Others, Standard: 400 cases/month
 ... repeat for each month
```

This 2×2×12 matrix ensures 16 cases per cell in most months and enables:
- Detection of supplier-specific degradation.
- Separate tracking of seasonal and intervention effects on each complexity class.
- Stratified change-point detection (e.g., staffing cut affects Others more than Supplier Z if Supplier Z is smaller).

### Seasonality and multiple-testing handling

**Seasonality:**
- Establish season-specific reference ranges: Q1 (Jan–Mar), Q2 (Apr–Jun), Q3 (Jul–Sep), Q4 (Oct–Dec).
- Post-October, high-complexity baseline shifts from 21% to 34–45%; median cycle time baseline shifts from 31 h to 42–49 h. Recalibrate alert thresholds quarterly or use pre-specified seasonal models.
- Example: In Q4, alert on median cycle time triggers at 52 h instead of 38 h, reflecting expected seasonality.

**Multiple testing:**
- Use **Bonferroni correction** if running 11 monitors independently. With  = 0.05 and 11 monitors, set individual  = 0.05/11  0.0045 per monitor.
- Alternatively, use **sequential testing**: prioritize monitors M1 (schema) and M4 (cycle time) as primary; M2–M3, M5–M9 as secondary; trigger deeper analysis only if M1 or M4 alarms.
- **Temporal correlation:** Monitors on consecutive weeks are not independent. Use exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) or 4-week moving median to smooth noise; alert only on persistent trends (2 consecutive periods) rather than isolated spikes.
- **False-positive budget:** Allocate ~10% annual false-positive budget to M1–M2 (schema/control-flow, high specificity), ~5% to M4–M6 (performance, moderate specificity), ~5% to M7–M11 (outcomes/routing, lower specificity). If a monitor fires, escalate only if it meets its specificity threshold or is corroborated by related monitors.

def hellinger_distance(p, q):
    """Compute Hellinger distance between two normalized probability distributions."""
    p_norm = p / np.sum(p)
    q_norm = q / np.sum(q)
    return (1 / np.sqrt(2)) * np.sqrt(np.sum((np.sqrt(p_norm) - np.sqrt(q_norm))**2))

    def __init__(self, df_events: pd.DataFrame, 
                 case_id_col: str = 'case:concept:name',
                 activity_col: str = 'concept:name',
                 timestamp_col: str = 'time:timestamp',
                 resource_col: str = 'org:resource'):
        """
        Initialize analyzer.
        
        Args:
            df_events: Raw event dataframe with required columns.
            case_id_col: Column name for case ID.
            activity_col: Column name for activity label.
            timestamp_col: Column name for timestamp.
            resource_col: Column name for resource/actor.
        """
        self.df_events = df_events.copy()
        self.case_id_col = case_id_col
        self.activity_col = activity_col
        self.timestamp_col = timestamp_col
        self.resource_col = resource_col

    def format_dataframe(self, timestamp_format='%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S'):
        """
        Format and validate dataframe for PM4Py processing.
        
        Ensures required columns exist, timestamps are parsed, and data is sorted.
        """
        df = self.df_events.copy()
        
        # Ensure required columns exist
        required_cols = [self.case_id_col, self.activity_col, self.timestamp_col]
        missing = [c for c in required_cols if c not in df.columns]
        if missing:
            raise ValueError(f"Missing columns: {missing}")
        
        # Parse timestamp
        if not pd.api.types.is_datetime64_any_dtype(df[self.timestamp_col]):
            df[self.timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df[self.timestamp_col], format=timestamp_format)
        
        # Sort by case and timestamp
        df = df.sort_values([self.case_id_col, self.timestamp_col]).reset_index(drop=True)
        
        # Format for PM4Py
        self.df_events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
            df, 
            case_id=self.case_id_col, 
            activity_key=self.activity_col, 
            timestamp_key=self.timestamp_col
        )
        print(f" Formatted {len(self.df_events)} events, {self.df_events[self.case_id_col].nunique()} cases.")
        return self.df_events
    
    def filter_time_range(self, start_date: str, end_date: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """
        Filter events within a time range.
        
        Args:
            start_date: Start date (YYYY-MM-DD).
            end_date: End date (YYYY-MM-DD).
        
        Returns:
            Filtered dataframe.
        """
        start = pd.to_datetime(start_date)
        end = pd.to_datetime(end_date)
        filtered = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            self.df_events,
            start,
            end,
            timestamp_key=self.timestamp_col
        )
        print(f" Filtered to {start_date} - {end_date}: {len(filtered)} events, {filtered[self.case_id_col].nunique()} cases.")
        return filtered
    
    def discover_dfg_monthly(self, df_slice: pd.DataFrame = None) -> Dict:
        """
        Discover directly-follows graph.
        
        Args:
            df_slice: Dataframe slice (default: self.df_events). If None, uses full log.
        
        Returns:
            Dictionary with DFG, start activities, end activities.
        """
        if df_slice is None:
            df_slice = self.df_events
        
        log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_slice)
        dfg, start_activities, end_activities = dfg_discovery.apply(log, variant=dfg_discovery.Variants.CLASSIC)
        
        # Convert to readable dict
        dfg_dict = {
            'edges': {f"{k[0]} -> {k[1]}": v for k, v in dfg.items()},
            'start_activities': dict(start_activities),
            'end_activities': dict(end_activities)
        }
        return dfg_dict
    
    def discover_variants(self, df_slice: pd.DataFrame = None, top_k: int = 20) -> List[Tuple]:
        """
        Discover process variants (most frequent traces).
        
        Args:
            df_slice: Dataframe slice (default: self.df_events).
            top_k: Return top K variants.
        
        Returns:
            List of (variant_trace, frequency) tuples.
        """
        if df_slice is None:
            df_slice = self.df_events
        
        log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_slice)
        variants = variants_discovery.apply(log, variant=variants_discovery.Variants.CLASSIC)
        
        # Sort by frequency and return top-k
        sorted_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: len(x[1]), reverse=True)[:top_k]
        return sorted_variants
    
    def discover_temporal_profile(self, df_slice: pd.DataFrame = None) -> Dict:
        """
        Discover temporal profiles (inter-activity times).
        
        Args:
            df_slice: Dataframe slice (default: self.df_events).
        
        Returns:
            Dictionary with temporal stats (mean, median, stdev) for each edge.
        """
        if df_slice is None:
            df_slice = self.df_events
        
        log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_slice)
        tprofile = temporal_profile_discovery.apply(log)
        
        # tprofile is a dict: {(activity1, activity2): [durations...]}
        # Return summary statistics
        tprofile_summary = {}
        for edge, durations in tprofile.items():
            if len(durations) > 0:
                durations_hours = [d.total_seconds() / 3600 for d in durations if hasattr(d, 'total_seconds')]
                if durations_hours:
                    tprofile_summary[f"{edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}"] = {
                        'mean_h': np.mean(durations_hours),
                        'median_h': np.median(durations_hours),
                        'stdev_h': np.std(durations_hours),
                        'count': len(durations_hours)
                    }
        return tprofile_summary
    
    def apply_label_mapping(self, mapping_dict: Dict[str, str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """
        Apply label mapping (e.g., Eligibility Screen -> Validate Eligibility).
        
        Args:
            mapping_dict: Dict mapping old labels to new labels.
        
        Returns:
            New dataframe with mapped labels (original preserved in new column).
        """
        df_mapped = self.df_events.copy()
        df_mapped['activity_original'] = df_mapped[self.activity_col]
        df_mapped[self.activity_col] = df_mapped[self.activity_col].map(
            lambda x: mapping_dict.get(x, x)
        )
        self.log_mapped = df_mapped
        print(f" Applied label mapping. Unique labels before: {self.df_events[self.activity_col].nunique()}, "
              f"after: {df_mapped[self.activity_col].nunique()}")
        return df_mapped
    
    def remove_duplicate_events(self, df_slice: pd.DataFrame = None, 
                                activity_pattern: str = 'Open Case',
                                time_tolerance_sec: float = 60.0) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """
        Remove suspected duplicate technical events (e.g., Open Case duplicates).
        
        Args:
            df_slice: Dataframe slice (default: self.df_events).
            activity_pattern: Activity label to deduplicate.
            time_tolerance_sec: Events within this many seconds are considered duplicates.
        
        Returns:
            Dataframe with duplicates removed (tracks via 'is_duplicate' column).
        """
        if df_slice is None:
            df_slice = self.df_events.copy()
        else:
            df_slice = df_slice.copy()
        
        df_slice['is_duplicate'] = False
        
        for case_id in df_slice[self.case_id_col].unique():
            case_events = df_slice[df_slice[self.case_id_col] == case_id].copy()
            target_events = case_events[case_events[self.activity_col] == activity_pattern].copy()
            
            # Mark consecutive duplicates within time tolerance
            if len(target_events) > 0:
                target_indices = target_events.index.tolist()
                for i in range(len(target_indices) - 1):
                    idx1, idx2 = target_indices[i], target_indices[i + 1]
                    time_diff = (df_slice.loc[idx2, self.timestamp_col] - 
                                df_slice.loc[idx1, self.timestamp_col]).total_seconds()
                    if time_diff < time_tolerance_sec:
                        df_slice.loc[idx2, 'is_duplicate'] = True
        
        n_dup = df_slice['is_duplicate'].sum()
        df_dedup = df_slice[~df_slice['is_duplicate']].drop(columns=['is_duplicate']).reset_index(drop=True)
        print(f" Removed {n_dup} duplicate events. Remaining: {len(df_dedup)} events.")
        return df_dedup
    
    def segment_by_time_window(self, granularity: str = 'M') -> Dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
        """
        Segment event log by time window (monthly, weekly, etc.).
        
        Args:
            granularity: 'D' (daily), 'W' (weekly), 'M' (monthly), 'Q' (quarterly).
        
        Returns:
            Dictionary mapping period label to dataframe slice.
        """
        df = self.df_events.copy()
        df['period'] = df[self.timestamp_col].dt.to_period(granularity)
        
        segments = {}
        for period, group in df.groupby('period'):
            segments[str(period)] = group.drop(columns=['period']).reset_index(drop=True)
        
        print(f" Segmented log into {len(segments)} {granularity}-level periods.")
        return segments
    
    def segment_by_supplier(self, supplier_col: str = 'org:supplier') -> Dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
        """
        Segment event log by supplier (or other categorical attribute).
        
        Args:

            return {}
        
        segments = {}
        for supplier, group in self.df_events.groupby(supplier_col):
            segments[str(supplier)] = group.reset_index(drop=True)
        
        print(f" Segmented log by supplier: {list(segments.keys())}")
        return segments
