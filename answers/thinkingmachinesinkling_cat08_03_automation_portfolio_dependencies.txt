A4 improves entity accuracy, reducing provisional signing (6.5 %) by ensuring clearance before contract/signing stages. Four-eyes compliance is not directly automated, but faster upstream flow reduces month-end rush pressure that drives the 5.8 % violation rate.

**Compliance-first portfolio (A3, A4, A9, A12)**

- *Cycle time:* KYC and contract stages improve, but document loops, normalization waits, and valuation waits remain untouched. End-to-end cycle time improves mainly for cases that reach contract quickly; Equipment finance and complex SME cases still face 58 h valuation and 20 h normalization delays.
- *Waiting time:* KYC wait and contract wait fall. Remediation loops (650  510) are tracked but not eliminated.
- *Rework:* Contract-decision mismatch (8.5 %, 14 % renewal) falls sharply. KYC-related rework falls. Financial-statement and document loops are unchanged.
- *Handovers:* A12 introduces structured handover for control issues, improving visibility but not reducing volume. Analysts and back office experience no workload change.
- *Resource demand:* Specialists and legal ops benefit; analysts and back office do not. Risk of “control theater” if A12 logs issues faster than the organization resolves them.
- *Control violations:* Significant improvement in auditability and contract alignment. Four-eyes violations remain unaddressed unless paired with manual enforcement.

5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk domain | Automation risk | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| **Auditability** | Model suggestions replace visible human reasoning; regulators require evidence of decision logic | Every module (A3, A4, A6, A9) must log: case ID, timestamp, model version / rule version, human user ID, override reason, final decision. No “black box” approvals for High-exposure SME. |
| **False positives / negatives (A3)** | False negative = missed sanctions/PEP hit; false positive = unnecessary escalation that overloads 3 specialists | Mandatory human escalation for all “uncertain” scores; quarterly model validation against ground-truth lists; escalation-rate monitoring with alert if >25 %. |
| **Model drift / extraction failure (A6)** | PDF quality degrades over time; extraction accuracy drifts | Confidence-score gate: outputs below threshold routed to manual normalization; monthly sampling of extracted vs. audited statements; retraining pipeline triggered if accuracy drops >5 %. |
| **Exception handling (A4, A5)** | Assistant misreads complex entity structures; portal checklist is too rigid for atypical Working-capital or Credit-line renewal cases | Human specialist review for all high-complexity entities; adaptive checklists by product (Working-capital vs. Equipment vs. Renewal); RM override option with mandatory reason code. |
| **Four-eyes compliance** | Automation of analysis (A6) and packaging (A8, if added later) can allow same user to influence both stages, especially during month-end rush (5.8 % violations) | System-enforced role check: for High-exposure SME, the user ID performing credit-analysis verification (post-A6) cannot be the same as the user assembling the committee package. Block packaging if role conflict detected. |
| **Downstream bot without upstream controls (A11)** | Activation bot runs if KYC, contract-match, or decision artifacts are incomplete | **Do not deploy A11 in Year 1.** Pre-flight checklist must verify: KYC status = cleared (A3/A4), contract terms = decision record (A9), decision present, collateral resolved if required. Human approval required for all High-exposure activations in first 6 months. |
| **Provisional signing / KYC gate failure (A10)** | Faster e-signature without hard KYC gate accelerates the 6.5 % provisional-signing violations | Hard system gate: signing event is blocked unless KYC clearance timestamp exists and is before signing timestamp. | **Resource overload shift (A6  analysts)** | Normalization automation increases clean inflow to analysts without reducing verification burden | Monitor analyst queue length and overtime. If verification load exceeds 30 % of prior extraction load, add verification support or reduce A6 automation scope for poor-quality PDFs. |
| **Customer friction (A5)** | Rigid document requests increase RM resistance and customer abandonment | Co-design checklist with RMs; include “explain why” fields; allow RM-initiated exception with audit trail, not just customer portal lockout. |

6. Recommended phased rollout

**Chosen portfolio:** **Bottleneck / Synchronization (A3, A4, A5, A6)**  
**Rationale for sequencing:** Dependencies flow from document completeness (A5)  normalization (A6)  KYC/entity clearance (A3, A4)  downstream contract and decision stages. Deep core-banking integrations are reserved for A3 (Month 4) and A6 (Month 6).

**Phase 1 – Foundation and loop-breaking (Months 1–3)**  
*Modules: A4, A5* (lighter integration; no deep core slots used)

- **A4 (Month 1):** Deploy entity-mismatch assistant in compliance ops. Assist the 3 FTE specialists with pattern matching for the 1,750 mismatch cases; specialists retain final resolution.
- **A5 (Month 2–3):** Launch adaptive missing-document portal checklist for the 3,300 request events, with RM-facing templates by product (Working-capital, Equipment finance, Credit-line renewal).

- *Monitoring metrics:* Median wait for entity mismatch (target <20 h from 31 h); missing-document loop repetition rate (target <35 % from 52 %); specialist utilization; customer-portal adoption rate; RM-reported friction index.
- *Gate criteria (Phase 1 exit):* A4 accuracy on sample >90 % with 0 missed critical mismatches; specialist queue stable; A5 loop rate reduced; no increase in customer complaints.

**Phase 2 – Deep integration and control strengthening (Months 4–6)**  
*Modules: A3 (deep integration #1), A6 pilot (deep integration #2)*

- **A3 (Month 4–5):** Deep-integrate KYC hit pre-classifier with sanctions/PEP databases and case-management system. Include mandatory escalation path and complete audit trail. Protect the specialist pool.
- **A6 (Month 5–6):** Pilot financial-statement extraction/normalization with analyst workbench integration. Deploy quality-confidence gate: high-confidence outputs feed analysts; low-confidence routed to manual normalization.

- *Monitoring metrics:* False-negative rate = 0 on audit samples; false-positive escalation rate <20 %; extraction accuracy >80 % on good PDFs, <70 % routed correctly to manual; normalization service time reduction; analyst verification queue length.
- *Gate criteria (Phase 2 exit):* Audit trail completeness 100 % for A3; model drift check passed; A6 quality gate correctly routing >95 % of poor-quality PDFs; analyst overtime not increasing.

**Phase 3 – Scale and flow integration (Months 7–9)**  
*Module: Scale A6; stabilize portfolio interaction*

- Scale A6 to full case volume (5,200 normalization events).
- Ensure A5  A6 flow works: documents requested via portal feed extraction pipeline.
- Monitor concurrent KYC and financial review (system records sequential but practice is concurrent) so automation does not force artificial serial delays.

- *Monitoring metrics:* End-to-end median cycle time by variant (Micro, SME, High-exposure; by product); waiting time at normalization (20 h target reduction) and committee packaging (26 h); rework rate in financial document review; analyst workload balance.
- *Gate criteria:* Normalization median service time reduced by 40 %; credit-analysis wait (28 h) shows downward trend due to faster artifact delivery; no overload in analyst pool.

**Phase 4 – Control verification and portfolio audit (Months 10–12)**

- Conduct four-eyes audit: verify that High-exposure SME cases do not show same user performing analysis-related verification and packaging (system logs).
- Audit A3 and A9-readiness (even though A9 is not in this portfolio, upstream data cleanliness improves contract accuracy for future waves).
- Review A12-style remediation tracking manually: count remaining contract-remediation loops (target downward trend due to cleaner upstream data).
- Monitor provisional-signing attempts: should fall below 1 % as A3/A4 clear KYC earlier and more reliably.

- *Monitoring metrics:* Four-eyes violation rate (target <2 %, down from 5.8 %); control remediation loop rate; provisional signing rate; automation override/escalation rates by module.
- *Gate criteria for Year 2:* End-to-end cycle time reduced for synchronization-heavy paths; resource demand shifted (specialists to oversight, analysts to verification) without overload; control audit clean.

**Integration constraint compliance:** Only A3 and A6 use deep core-banking integration, both within the first six months (Months 4–6). A4 and A5 rely on workflow and portal layers.

7. Final recommendation

**Recommend the Bottleneck / Synchronization portfolio (A3, A4, A5, A6).**

**Why not the highest-frequency activity first?**  
A1 (auto-registration, 11,880 cases) is the politically attractive “high-frequency win.” However, process-mining evidence shows frequency is a poor proxy for end-to-end impact. The back-office pool (12 FTE) is already underutilized below 65 % after 15:00; automating an 8-minute, 3-minute task removes visible queue length but does not reduce the median waits that dominate cycle time: 4 h at KYC, 18 h at financial review, 20 h at normalization, 26 h at packaging, 58 h at external valuation, and 31 h at entity mismatch. Faster registration without fixing these bottlenecks merely increases arrival rate at the true constraints—the 3 FTE compliance specialist pool and the 9 FTE analyst hub—potentially worsening queues and rework.

The selected portfolio targets the synchronization and rework structures revealed in the DFG:
- **A4** attacks the 1,750-case, 31-hour entity-mismatch blocker that is on the critical path in 82 % of occurrences.
- **A5** breaks the 52 % repeated missing-document loop that feeds back into the 9,200-case financial-document review stream.
- **A6** removes the 75-minute normalization burden and 20-hour wait, delivering clean artifacts to analysts and committee packaging.
- **A3** satisfies the compliance mandate for a control-strengthening module, provides audit trails, and protects the small specialist pool from being overwhelmed by false positives.

**Trade-off defense:** This portfolio sacrifices the quick labor-saving optics of A1 and the customer-facing speed of A10. In return, it reduces end-to-end synchronization delays, prevents downstream overload, and respects prerequisites: A5 improves document completeness before A6 processes them; A3/A4 clear KYC/entity status needed for contract and signing; A6 outputs feed credit analysis and packaging without requiring risky downstream bots like A11. The changed resource demand is explicit: specialists move toward oversight, analysts shift from extraction to verification, and back-office missing-document work declines—all monitored through Phase gates.

Year 2 can then safely add A1 (registration) because upstream bottlenecks will have been relieved, allowing faster inflow to actually reduce total cycle time rather than flooding queues. A9 and A10 can also be added once KYC and document reliability are mature, ensuring signing automation enforces rather than bypasses the legal KYC gate.
