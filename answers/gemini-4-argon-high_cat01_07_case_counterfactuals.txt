## 1. Feasibility model

```text
ACTIVITY UNIVERSE
  Core lifecycle:    Register (Reg), Triage (Tri), Identity Check (IdC),
                     Document Check (DocC_k, k >= 1), Assessment (Ass_k, k >= 1),
                     Decision (Dec), Notify (Not)
  Conditional/Other: Originals Receipt (Orig), Escalation Advice (Esc),
                     Preliminary Compliance Screen (PrelimC),
                     Compliance Review (CompR_m, m >= 1),
                     Request More Info (RMI_k, k >= 1)

PARTIAL-ORDER RELATIONS (Strict Precedence: A < B; Concurrency Allowed: A || B)
  1. Entry sequence:
     Reg < Tri
     Tri < IdC
     Tri < DocC_1
     Tri < Orig          (when Orig is present)
     Tri < Esc           (when triggered at Triage)
     Tri < PrelimC       (when triggered at Triage)

  2. Verification synchronization before initial Assessment:
     IdC < Ass_1
     DocC_1 < Ass_1
     IdC || DocC_1       (if OriginalsRequired = false)
     Orig < DocC_1       (if OriginalsRequired = true; IdC || Orig and IdC || DocC_1 allowed)

  3. Rework iteration order (for each rework cycle k >= 1 after RMI_k):
     Ass_k < RMI_k       OR   CompR_m < RMI_k
     RMI_k < DocC_{k+1} < Ass_{k+1}

  4. Advisory and preliminary compliance concurrency:
     Esc || IdC, Esc || DocC_k, Esc || Ass_k
     PrelimC || IdC, PrelimC || DocC_k, PrelimC < CompR_final

  5. Final gatekeeping and exit sequence (let K = final assessment index):
     Ass_K < Dec         (if HighRisk = false and HighValue = false)
     Ass_K < CompR_final < Dec   (if HighRisk = true or HighValue = true)
     Dec < Not
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS
  C1 (Originals Gate):
      (OriginalsRequired = true) ==> (Orig < DocC_1).
      (OriginalsRequired = false) ==> IdC and DocC_1 may execute concurrently after Tri.

  C2 (Mandatory Final Compliance Gate):
      (HighRisk = true OR HighValue = true) ==>
      exists CompR_final such that (Ass_K < CompR_final < Dec),
      where Ass_K is the latest relevant Assessment prior to Decision.

  C3 (Non-Substitution of Preliminary Compliance):
      PrelimC < Ass_K does NOT satisfy C2; a post-Ass_K CompR_final (or explicit
      post-Ass_K reaffirmation by a certified compliance reviewer) remains mandatory.

  C4 (Rework Loop Completeness):
      For every RMI_k, there must exist a fresh verification-assessment pair
      (DocC_{k+1}, Ass_{k+1}) with RMI_k < DocC_{k+1} < Ass_{k+1} < Dec.
      If (HighRisk = true OR HighValue = true), a new or reaffirmed CompR_final
      must follow Ass_{k+1} before Dec.

  C5 (Four-Eyes Separation of Duties):
      Resource(Ass_K) != Resource(CompR_final).
      (In this system window, CompR_final must be assigned to C1, while Ass_K in {A1, A2}.)

  C6 (Unary Resource Capacity / Non-Overlap):
      For any human resource r in {T1, T2, I1, I2, D1, D2, A1, A2, C1, S1, M1}
      and any two distinct activity instances x != y assigned to r:
      [Start(x), Complete(x)) intersect [Start(y), Complete(y)) = empty.
      (Note: S1's Escalation Advice may overlap in time with checks performed by
      other resources, but S1 cannot serve two cases simultaneously.)

  C7 (Stochastic Queueing and Shift Gating):
      Start(x) >= max(ReadyTime(x), ResourceAvailableTime(Resource(x), ReadyTime(x))).
      Queue snapshots are noisy point-in-time forecasts subject to priority preemption
      (e.g., urgent compliance arrivals) and business-hour sign-off windows (e.g., M1).
```

**Classification of the four interventions by structural mechanism:**
- **Timing and task-release / capacity-reservation interventions (`K-411` and `K-104`):** `K-411` alters **task-release timing only** by removing an artificial IT case lock at `Triage` so `Identity Check` (`I2`) and `Document Check` (`D2`) are queued concurrently; resource pools, normative prerequisites, and decision criteria remain invariant. `K-104` alters **advisory/preliminary timing and queue reservation** by releasing `Escalation Advice` (`S1`) and a `Preliminary Compliance Screen` (`C1`) at `Triage` while reserving a downstream `C1` slot; because Rule 5 and Rule 10 still require final `Compliance Review` after `Assessment`, the core control-flow gate (`Assessment -> Compliance Review -> Decision`) and decision policy remain intact.
- **Resource-routing intervention (`K-219`):** `K-219` alters **resource assignment** by routing `Document Check` from specialist `D1` to generalist `D2` upon `Originals Receipt`. Directly, this trades queue wait for slower service time; indirectly, because `D2` is less adept at difficult handwriting, it raises the transition probability into a conditional rework branch (`Request More Info -> Document Check 2 -> Assessment 2`).
- **Control-flow truncation and decision-policy intervention (`K-337`):** `K-337` alters **allowed control-flow iteration and substantive decision policy** by prohibiting `Request More Info 2` after `Assessment 2` and forcing `Compliance Review` (`C1`) and `Decision` (`M1`) to adjudicate an unresolved supplier discrepancy (converting an unconditional `Approve` on verified figures into `Approve with conditions` or `Reject`).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
`Factual mechanism:` `K-104` is a high-risk, high-value corporate case with complex documents (`originals required: no`). Although `T1` spotted a sanctions ambiguity during `Triage` (Mon 08:20-08:50), no escalation was initiated. Consequently, while `Identity Check` (`I1`, Mon 10:40-12:50) and `Document Check` (`D1`, Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40 after a 12.83 h queue wait) ran in parallel, no sanctions analysis occurred until `Assessment` (`A1`, Tue 15:10-19:10 after a 9.5 h wait) rediscovered the issue. That triggered a serial `Escalation Advice` step (`S1`, Tue 19:20-21:20) before `K-104` could even enter `C1`'s compliance queue at Tue 21:20. It then waited 15.83 h until Wed 13:10 and required a full 4.5 h `Compliance Review` (Wed 13:10-17:40) before `Decision` and `Notify` (`M1`, Wed 18:00-18:40; total cycle time 58.5 h).

`Minimal intervention:` At `Triage` completion (Mon 08:50-09:00), `T1` dispatches a preliminary escalation packet to `S1` and books a provisional post-`Assessment` slot on `C1`. `S1` performs `Escalation Advice` and `C1` performs a `Preliminary Compliance Screen` concurrently with `I1` and `D1`, while leaving `I1`, `D1`, and `A1` assignments unchanged.

`Temporal propagation:` Critically, earlier escalation does **not** accelerate `Assessment` (`A1`) readiness: `A1` is gated by `Document Check` (`D1`), which still waits behind `D1`'s 13 h Monday morning backlog and finishes at Tue 05:40, and by `A1`'s ~9.5 h Tuesday queue, so `A1` still starts around Tue 15:10. However, three downstream shifts occur after Tue 15:10: (1) `S1` completes `Escalation Advice` on Monday (e.g., Mon 09:30-11:30) and `C1` completes a 1.5-2.0 h `Preliminary Compliance Screen` by Tuesday morning, so `A1` receives pre-structured sanctions guidance at Tue 15:10, trimming `A1` service from 4.0 h to ~3.0-3.5 h (completing Tue 18:10-18:40); (2) the 2.0 h post-`Assessment` `S1` step (Tue 19:20-21:20) is removed from the critical path, making `K-104` ready for final `Compliance Review` ~2.7-3.2 h earlier (at Tue 18:10-18:40 instead of Tue 21:20); and (3) because preliminary work saves 1-3 h of final `C1` review (reducing final `C1` service from 4.5 h to ~2.0-3.0 h) and a provisional `C1` slot was reserved on Monday morning, `K-104` avoids most of the 15.83 h `C1` queue wait.

`Process-feasibility check:` Rules 1-3 and 8 are preserved (`Register -> Triage -> [I1 || D1] -> A1` and `Decision -> Notify`). Rule 10 is respected because `Preliminary Compliance Screen` is treated strictly as preparatory work and does not replace final `Compliance Review`. Rule 5 and Rule 9 (four-eyes) are satisfied because final `Compliance Review` is executed by `C1` after `A1` completes `Assessment` and before `M1` executes `Decision`. Rule 11 is respected because `S1` and `C1` perform preliminary tasks in non-overlapping intervals.

`Best/base/worst case:` **Best case (39.0-48.5 h, saving 10.0-19.5 h):** `A1` finishes Tue 18:10; the reserved `C1` slot starts Tue 20:00-21:00 for 2.0 h (completing Tue 22:00-23:00), and `M1` signs either late Tuesday night (if on call, finishing Tue 23:40) or first thing Wednesday (Wed 08:00-08:40). **Base case (49.0-52.5 h, saving 6.0-9.5 h):** The two urgent compliance cases added Mon 16:00 and `A1` timing uncertainty push the reserved `C1` slot to Wed 06:30-09:00; final `C1` review takes 2.5 h (completing Wed 09:00-11:30), with `M1` completing `Decision` and `Notify` by Wed 09:40-12:10. **Worst case (54.5-57.5 h, saving 1.0-4.0 h):** Because `D1` has not yet examined the complex corporate documents at Mon 08:50, the early packet to `S1`/`C1` misses the key ownership layers; `C1` must still read the full dossier after `A1` finishes, and urgent Monday arrivals preempt the provisional reservation, saving only the 2.0 h post-`Assessment` `S1` delay.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If historical logs show that sanctions ambiguities spotted at `Triage` are resolved or redefined in >70% of cases once `D1` parses complex corporate documents, early `S1`/`C1` work would have high rework waste and net-negative system capacity impact.

### K-219
`Factual mechanism:` `K-219` is a standard-risk/value case requiring physical originals (`originals required: yes`) with handwritten standard documents. After `Register` and `Triage` (`T2`, Mon 09:00-09:40), `Identity Check` (`I2`, Mon 14:20-16:20) proceeded before `Originals Receipt` (Mon 17:30), as allowed by Rule 4. Because `T2` routed the handwritten documents to specialist `D1`, the case waited 16.0 h behind `D1`'s heavy Monday backlog until Tue 09:30, completing `Document Check` at Tue 13:30 (4.0 h service, zero rework). After a 6.5 h wait in `A2`'s queue, `Assessment` (`A2`) ran Tue 20:00-22:30. Finishing at 22:30 missed `M1`'s daytime sign-off window, creating a 10.0 h overnight wait until `Decision` and `Notify` on Wed 08:30-09:00 (total cycle time 48.0 h).

`Minimal intervention:` Keep `Triage`, `Identity Check` (`I2`), and `Originals Receipt` (Mon 17:30) identical, but route `Document Check` at Mon 17:30 to generalist `D2` rather than `D1`.

`Temporal propagation:` At Mon 16:00, `D2`'s queue is ~5 h (versus 11 h on `D1` and 16 h actual wait to Tue 09:30). Assigning `K-219` to `D2` at Mon 17:30 allows `Document Check` to start around Mon 21:30-23:30 (saving ~10-12 h of queue wait). However, under the service-time guidance, `D2` requires 4-8 h for standard documents (and longer when handwriting is difficult), so `D2` takes ~5.5-7.5 h instead of `D1`'s 4.0 h, completing between Tue 03:30 and Tue 07:00—still **6.5 to 10.0 h earlier** than `D1`'s Tue 13:30 completion. In the **no-rework branch**, `K-219` enters `A2`'s queue by Tue 03:30-07:00 (where the Tue 08:00 snapshot shows a 6 h backlog). `A2` starts around Tue 09:30-13:00 and finishes its 2.5-3.0 h `Assessment` around Tue 12:00-16:00. Crucially, finishing `A2` during Tuesday business hours allows `M1` to execute `Decision` and `Notify` on Tuesday afternoon, eliminating the 10.0 h overnight wait (Tue 22:30 to Wed 08:30) and amplifying a 6.5-10.0 h upstream gain into a 15.5-20.5 h total cycle-time reduction. Conversely, in the **rework branch**, `D2` misinterprets or flags illegible handwriting, causing `A2` (at Tue 12:00-15:00) to issue `Request More Info`, which triggers an 8-24 h external customer response plus a mandatory second `Document Check` and second `Assessment`.

`Process-feasibility check:` Rule 4 is satisfied (`Originals Receipt` at Mon 17:30 precedes `D2` start at Mon 21:30+, while `I2` completed earlier at Mon 16:20). Rule 2 is satisfied (`I2` and `D2` both finish before `A2`). In the rework branch, Rule 6 and Rule 7 are enforced by inserting `Request More Info -> Document Check 2 -> Assessment 2` before `Decision`. Rules 5 and 9 do not apply because `K-219` is neither high-risk nor high-value.

`Best/base/worst case:` **Best case (27.5-29.5 h, saving 18.5-20.5 h):** `D2` starts Mon 21:30, takes 5.5 h (completing Tue 03:00), `A2` starts Tue 09:00 and finishes Tue 11:30, and `M1` completes `Decision` and `Notify` by Tue 12:30-14:30. **Base case without rework (31.5-32.5 h, saving 15.5-16.5 h):** `D2` finishes Tue 06:30 (7.0 h service), `A2` finishes Tue 15:30 after a 6.0 h queue wait, and `M1` signs and notifies by Tue 16:30-17:30 (note: if `D2` + `A2` delays push `A2` completion past Tue 18:30, `M1` waits until Wed 08:30 and net savings collapse to ~0 h). **Worst case with rework (72.0-80.0 h, adding +24.0 to +32.0 h):** `A2` issues `Request More Info` at Tue 14:30; customer clarifies by Wed 10:30 (20 h); `Document Check 2` (Wed 13:30-16:30) and `Assessment 2` (Thu 08:30-11:00) push `M1` `Decision`/`Notify` to Thu 09:00-17:00.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` An empirical rework rate for `D2` on handwritten standard documents exceeding ~25-30% (compared to <5% on `D1`) would make expected cycle time and resource consumption worse under `D2` routing despite the shorter initial queue.

### K-337
`Factual mechanism:` `K-337` is a high-risk, high-value case requiring originals and involving a slow, inconsistent external supplier. After `Document Check 1` (`D1`, Tue 08:30-14:30) and `Assessment 1` (`A1`, Wed 08:00-11:30) identified inconsistent supplier totals, `Request More Info 1` ran from Wed 11:30 to Thu 08:00 (20.5 h). `Document Check 2` (`D1`, Thu 12:00-17:00) and `Assessment 2` (`A1`, Fri 08:00-10:30) found that one schedule inconsistency remained. Issuing `Request More Info 2` at Fri 10:30 trapped the case across the weekend (70.5 h until Mon 09:00), followed by `Document Check 3` (`D1`, Mon 13:00-16:30), `Assessment 3` (`A1`, Tue 08:00-10:30), `Compliance Review` (`C1`, Tue 15:30-19:30), and `Decision`/`Notify` (`M1`, Wed 08:30-09:10), totaling 214.17 h.

`Minimal intervention:` Leave the entire execution up to `Assessment 2` (`A1`, Fri 08:00-10:30) unchanged, prohibit `Request More Info 2`, and route the case directly from `Assessment 2` to `Compliance Review` (`C1`) and `Decision` (`M1`) with the remaining supplier discrepancy explicitly documented for conditional approval or rejection.

`Temporal propagation:` Suppressing `Request More Info 2` eliminates three activities from the path: the 70.5 h external weekend wait (Fri 10:30-Mon 09:00), `Document Check 3` (`D1`, 4.0 h wait + 3.5 h service), and `Assessment 3` (`A1`, 15.5 h overnight/queue wait + 2.5 h service)—removing 96.0 h of elapsed time before compliance entry. However, downstream steps after Fri 10:30 do **not** simply shift by a constant offset: (1) `A1` needs ~30 min extra at the end of `Assessment 2` (completing Fri 11:00) to write a formal discrepancy report; (2) at Thu 08:00 `C1` had a 5 h queue (normal load), so on Friday morning `K-337` faces a ~2.5-5.0 h wait for `C1`; (3) because the evidence contains an unresolved financial schedule discrepancy that cannot be silently ignored, `C1`'s `Compliance Review` is substantially harder than the factual 4.0 h review of clean figures—requiring 4.5-6.5 h to draft protective conditions (e.g., retention holdback/audit covenant) or legal rejection reasons, and potentially a 1.5-2.0 h consult with `S1`; and (4) if `C1` finishes after `M1`'s Friday departure (~18:00), `Decision` and `Notify` spill over the weekend to Monday morning.

`Process-feasibility check:` Crucially, suppressing `Request More Info 2` does **not** bypass `Compliance Review`: under Rule 5 and Rule 7 (since `Request More Info 1` occurred), a high-risk/high-value case must still undergo `Compliance Review` (`C1`) after its latest `Assessment` (`Assessment 2` by `A1`) and before `Decision` (`M1`). Rule 9 (four-eyes) is satisfied because `A1` performs `Assessment 2` and `C1` performs `Compliance Review`. Rule 8 (`Decision < Notify`) holds.

`Best/base/worst case:` **Best case (102.5-103.8 h, saving 110.4-111.7 h):** `C1` waits 2.0 h, starts Fri 13:00, and completes a 4.5 h review at Fri 17:30; `M1` signs `Decision` (`Approve with conditions` or `Reject`) and `Notify` by Fri 18:10-18:50, beating the weekend cutoff. **Base case (165.5-169.0 h, saving 45.2-48.7 h):** `C1` waits 4.0 h on Friday (starting Fri 15:00) and requires 5.5 h of complex review, finishing either late Friday evening after `M1` has left or on Monday morning (Mon 10:30); `M1` completes `Decision` and `Notify` on Mon 09:30-12:00 (Week 2). **Worst case (173.5-190.0 h nominal, saving 24.2-40.7 h, or net-negative with post-decision appeal):** `C1` requires `Escalation Advice` (`S1`) on Friday afternoon to assess regulatory defensibility, pushing `C1` completion to Mon 15:30 and `M1` `Decision`/`Notify` to Mon 16:30-Tue 09:00; if `M1` rejects the case (whereas the supplier factually provided clean confirmation on Monday morning), the customer appeals or re-applies, creating a full new case cycle.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If historical data on high-value cases show that unresolved second-pass supplier discrepancies represent benign clerical timing differences >80% of the time and that rejections/harsh holdbacks trigger costly appeals or lost client relationships, forcing an early decision destroys net business value despite saving 2-4.5 days.

### K-411
`Factual mechanism:` `K-411` is a standard-risk/value case with digital standard documents (`originals required: no`). At `Triage` (`T2`, Tue 09:10-09:35), a legacy system lock allowed only `Identity Check` (`I2`, Tue 11:30-13:20 after a 1.92 h wait) to be instantiated. `Document Check` (`D2`) was not created until `I2` finished at Tue 13:20; it then waited 1.67 h and ran from Tue 15:00 to Tue 19:30 (4.5 h service). Becoming ready for `Assessment` at Tue 19:30 meant waiting 14.0 h overnight and behind `A2`'s congested queue until Wed 09:30-12:00 (2.5 h service), followed by `Decision` and `Notify` (`M1`, Wed 14:00-14:30; total cycle time 29.5 h).

`Minimal intervention:` At `Triage` completion (Tue 09:35-09:40), use a controlled task-splitting workaround to enqueue both `Identity Check` (`I2`) and `Document Check` (`D2`) simultaneously with mandatory real-time start/complete timestamp capture.

`Temporal propagation:` Because `I2` already started at Tue 11:30 and finished at Tue 13:20, `Identity Check` timing is unchanged. However, queuing `Document Check` on `D2` at Tue 09:35 (3.75 h earlier than Tue 13:20) does **not** shift `D2` completion 3.75 h earlier! Look at the Tue 08:00 system snapshot: `D2` had a **4 h queue** at Tue 08:00 (which is why `D2` was busy throughout Tuesday morning and only had 1.67 h of residual queue left when `K-411` was factually created at Tue 13:20). Joining `D2`'s queue at Tue 09:35 places `K-411` behind `D2`'s existing ~2.5-4.0 h morning backlog (though ahead of any arrivals between 09:35 and 13:20). Thus, `D2` starts between Tue 12:15 and Tue 13:35 (only 1.4-2.75 h earlier than Tue 15:00) and finishes its 4.5 h check between **Tue 16:45 and Tue 18:05** (vs. Tue 19:30 factually). This makes `K-411` ready for `Assessment` (`A2`) 1.4 to 2.75 h earlier on Tuesday late afternoon. However, at Tue 16:45-18:05, `A2`'s queue is heavily congested (6 h backlog at Tue 08:00 with one assessor in training; for example, `K-219` was already waiting for `A2` since Tue 13:30 and wasn't served until Tue 20:00-22:30). Consequently, `A2` absorbs nearly all of `K-411`'s earlier verification readiness!

`Process-feasibility check:` Rule 3 explicitly permits `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to run concurrently because `originals required = no`. Rule 2 holds because `A2` still waits for `max(Complete(I2), Complete(D2)) = Complete(D2)`. Rule 11 holds because `I2` and `D2` are distinct resources in separate pools. Requiring real-time timestamps ensures event-log provenance complies with audit rules.

`Best/base/worst case:` **Best case (26.0-27.5 h, saving 2.0-3.5 h):** Queuing on `D2` at Tue 09:35 lets `D2` finish at Tue 16:45, allowing `K-411` to jump ahead of one or two verification completions that arrived at `A2` between 16:45 and 19:30; `A2` runs Wed 07:30-10:00 (instead of Wed 09:30-12:00), and `M1` finishes `Decision`/`Notify` at Wed 11:00-12:30. **Base case (28.0-29.25 h, saving 0.25-1.5 h):** `D2` finishes at Tue 17:30, but `A2`'s Tuesday evening queue is already full (with earlier arrivals like `K-219`); `K-411` still waits overnight and starts on `A2` only slightly earlier on Wednesday morning (Wed 08:30-09:15), finishing `Notify` around Wed 13:00-14:15. **Worst case (29.5-31.0 h, adding 0.0 to +1.5 h):** Concurrent editing under the workaround triggers a document-attachment sync conflict when `I2` and `D2` close their tasks, requiring 1.0-1.5 h of IT/triage reconciliation before `A2` can open the file on Wednesday morning.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` Queue-arrival logs showing that `A2` had idle capacity between Tue 17:00 and Tue 20:00 would increase the single-case gain, whereas logs showing zero intermediate arrivals to `A2` between Tue 17:00 and Tue 19:30 would confirm that `K-411`'s queue rank at `A2` is completely unchanged (0 h gain).

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

When these four policies are rolled out simultaneously across **100 similar cases in one week**, single-case partial-equilibrium savings break down due to cross-pool interference, queue displacement, and bottleneck migration:

1. **Scarce compliance capacity (`C1` saturation):** `C1` is a single-server bottleneck starting Monday with a 20 h backlog. Applying `K-104`'s intervention across dozens of high-risk cases adds 1.5-2.5 h of preliminary screening per case while locking provisional post-`Assessment` slots. Because upstream `D1` and `A1` completion times have high variance, reserved `C1` windows either sit partially idle waiting for delayed assessments or displace unreserved urgent arrivals (like Monday afternoon's urgent additions). Meanwhile, `K-337`'s policy pushes unresolved discrepancy cases into `C1` on Thursday/Friday with 0.5-2.5 h of extra legal-structuring effort per case, turning `C1` into a gridlocked system-wide bottleneck.
2. **Substitution between `D1` and `D2` and rework feedback:** Shifting 100 handwritten cases from `D1` to `D2` (`K-219`) relieves `D1` (helping complex corporate cases like `K-104`), but because `D2` takes 1.5-4.0 h longer per case than `D1`, transferring even 25-30 cases adds 150-220 h of direct work to `D2`, wiping out `D2`'s initial 2-4 h queue advantage. Worse, if `D2` triggers `Request More Info` on 20-30% of handwritten cases, those cases loop back for a second `Document Check` and `Assessment`, compounding `D2` congestion and directly delaying `K-411`-type digital cases that rely on `D2` for parallel verification. Conversely, `K-337` removes third-pass rework from `D1`/`A1`, partially offsetting upstream document load but shifting work to post-decision appeals.
3. **Assessment-queue bottlenecks (`A1`, `A2`):** With `A2` already constrained (one assessor in training Tuesday) and `K-411` releasing `D2` tasks ~4 h earlier across 100 cases, verification completions bunch together in Tuesday/Wednesday afternoon waves. The bottleneck migrates squarely to `A2`: earlier verification readiness merely inflates `A2` waiting time without increasing `A2` throughput.
4. **Weekend/external-response cliffs and timestamp fidelity:** `K-219` and `K-337` exhibit sharp step-function nonlinearities around `M1`'s daily sign-off cutoff and the Friday weekend boundary, where a 1-2 h queue delay on `A2` or `C1` adds 10-63 h of elapsed time. Finally, if `K-411`'s workaround allows batch timestamp entry under high load, logs will show illusory concurrency (`Start(I2) == Start(D2)`) while staff actually process files sequentially, masking where queues truly form.

## 7. Final synthesis

**Most robust intervention:** Parallelizing `Identity Check` and `Document Check` via a controlled workaround with real-time timestamping (`K-411`) is the most robust intervention. It is explicitly permitted by Rule 3 when originals are not required, adds zero net service hours to any resource pool, and leaves risk and compliance standards untouched. Even when downstream `A2` congestion absorbs most of the earlier verification readiness, its worst-case outcome (assuming proper lock control) is simply zero net cycle-time gain rather than quality degradation or compliance exposure. Pairing `K-104`'s early `S1` advisory packet with non-blocking preparation is a strong second, provided hard `C1` slot reservations are avoided.

**Most dangerous intervention despite speed:** Suppressing the second `Request More Info` loop (`K-337`) is by far the most dangerous intervention. While cutting out a 70.5-hour weekend external wait and third-pass `D1`/`A1` checks yields the largest nominal time reduction (45-112 hours), it achieves speed by mutating the decision policy on a high-risk, high-value case—forcing `C1` and `M1` either to reject a client whose supplier would have confirmed the figures on Monday (triggering appeals and lost revenue) or to approve with unresolved financial discrepancies.

**Where local counterfactuals fail at system scale:** Local counterfactuals are least transportable in `K-219` (routing handwritten files from `D1` to `D2`) and `K-104` (reserving `C1` slots). For a single case on Monday evening, jumping from `D1`'s 16-hour wait to `D2`'s 5-hour queue looks transformative; across 100 cases, `D2`'s slower service (5.5-8 hours vs. 4 hours) and higher handwriting rework rate rapidly saturate both `D2` and `A2`, inverting the queue advantage. Similarly, reserving scarce `C1` slots (`K-104`) is a zero-sum queue-jumping mechanism that delays urgent compliance arrivals at scale.

**Most valuable additional data:** Fine-grained **`Ready`/`Queued` timestamps** (separating external arrival, queue wait, and active service), **resource-by-complexity rework probabilities** (`D2` vs. `D1` on handwriting), **shift/sign-off availability windows** for `M1` and `C1`, and **post-decision appeal/loss logs** are essential for reliable policy design.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **`structural` — Invariance of upstream prerequisites under early escalation (`K-104`):** Assumes that initiating `Escalation Advice` (`S1`) and `Preliminary Compliance Screen` (`C1`) at `Triage` does not alter the requirement that `Document Check` (`D1`) and `Identity Check` (`I1`) must both fully complete before `Assessment` (`A1`) can start, nor allow `Preliminary Compliance Screen` to substitute for post-`Assessment` `Compliance Review`.
2. **`temporal` — Daytime sign-off gating by `M1` (`K-104`, `K-219`, `K-337`):** Assumes from the factual logs (where cases completing `Assessment` or `Compliance Review` at Tue 22:30, Tue 19:30, and Fri late afternoon wait until 08:00-08:30 the next business day for `Decision`) that `M1` operates on a daytime business schedule (~08:00-18:30), creating step-function overnight and weekend waiting cliffs.
3. **`resource` — Single-server bottleneck rigidity of `C1` (`K-104`, `K-337`):** Assumes `C1` is the sole certified compliance reviewer throughout the observation window with no overtime elasticity or cross-trained backup, so any additional preliminary screening hours or harder discrepancy reviews directly displace other high-risk cases in `C1`'s queue.
4. **`quality` — Higher handwriting clarification/rework hazard on `D2` (`K-219`):** Assumes that generalist `D2` not only takes longer than specialist `D1` on difficult handwritten standard documents (5.5-8.0 h vs. 4.0 h) but also faces a non-trivial probability of transcription ambiguity that triggers `Request More Info` during `Document Check` or `Assessment`.
5. **`behavioral` — Compliance with real-time timestamp logging under the lock workaround (`K-411`):** Assumes that `I2` and `D2` record true start and completion timestamps in real time when using the legacy-lock workaround, rather than batch-closing tasks at the end of a shift (which would fabricate apparent concurrency or mask queue delays).
6. **`interference` — FIFO queue discipline within priority classes (`K-411`, `K-219`):** Assumes that within standard-priority work on `D2` and `A2`, cases are served in approximate first-ready, first-served order, so releasing `K-411` to `D2` at Tue 09:35 still places it behind `D2`'s existing 4-hour morning backlog and behind earlier-ready cases (such as `K-219`) in `A2`'s Tuesday evening queue.
7. **`quality` — Non-trivial legal/structuring overhead for unresolved discrepancies (`K-337`):** Assumes that because policy prohibits silently ignoring the remaining supplier discrepancy after `Assessment 2`, `C1` requires 4.5-6.5 hours (and potentially `S1` consultation) to formulate defensible conditional covenants or rejection grounds, rather than rubber-stamping the file in the factual 4.0 hours.
8. **`interference` — Ceteris paribus background arrivals in single-case counterfactuals (`All cases`):** Assumes for the single-case derivations that exogenous arrivals (such as the two urgent compliance cases arriving Mon 16:00) and other cases' service durations remain approximately fixed, before relaxing this assumption in the 100-case weekly rollout analysis.
