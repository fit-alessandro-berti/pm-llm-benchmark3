## 1. Feasibility model

The model below treats the supplied traces, queue snapshots, service-time guidance, and normative rules as the evidence base. `≺` means “must complete before,” while `∥` means “may overlap, but overlap is not mandatory.” 

```text
R  = Register
T  = Triage
I  = Identity Check
O  = Originals Receipt
D_j = j-th Document Check
A_j = j-th Assessment
Q_j = j-th Request More Info
S_pre = preliminary Escalation Advice
C_pre = preliminary compliance work
C_f = final Compliance Review
Dec = Decision
N = Notify

R ≺ T
T ≺ I

No originals required:
    T ≺ D_1
    I ∥ D_1 is permitted
    {I, D_1} ≺ A_1

Originals required:
    T ≺ O ≺ D_1
    T ≺ I
    I ∥ O is permitted
    I ∥ D_1 is permitted after O
    {I, D_1} ≺ A_1

Assessment-driven rework:
    A_j ≺ Q_j ≺ D_(j+1) ≺ A_(j+1)

Compliance-driven rework:
    C_j ≺ Q_j ≺ D_(j+1) ≺ A_(j+1) ≺ C_(j+1)

High-risk or high-value terminal path:
    A_f ≺ C_f ≺ Dec ≺ N

Other terminal path:
    A_f ≺ Dec ≺ N

Permitted preliminary path:
    T ≺ S_pre
    T ≺ C_pre
    {S_pre, C_pre} may overlap I, O, and D
    A_f ≺ C_f regardless of C_pre
```

```text
CONDITIONAL CONSTRAINTS
- originals_required => every initial Document Check starts after Originals Receipt.
- high_risk OR high_value => a final Compliance Review is mandatory.
- Request More Info => a new Document Check and a new Assessment are mandatory.
- Request More Info on a high-risk/high-value case =>
  a new or explicitly reaffirmed final Compliance Review is mandatory.
- K-337 intervention => Q_2 is forbidden after A_2; the unresolved discrepancy
  must be recorded and Decision must be either approve-with-conditions or reject.
- K-411 intervention => both verification tasks are created after Triage, but
  Assessment remains disabled until both have genuinely completed.

DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS
- resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
- A named resource cannot have overlapping service intervals.
- Only C1 can satisfy the final certified Compliance Review requirement.
- A preliminary screen is a distinct, nonfinal event and cannot be relabeled as C_f.
- K-104 final C1 work cannot begin before the final Assessment, even if a slot
  was reserved and evidence was screened earlier.
- K-219 assignment to D2 means immediate enqueue after Originals Receipt, not
  guaranteed immediate service.
- K-411 concurrency must be supported by real task-create, claim, start,
  complete, and lock events; later batch entry is not evidence of concurrency.
- Queue snapshots are uncertain workload forecasts, not deterministic schedules.
```

K-104 and K-411 primarily alter **timing and task release**. K-104 also changes priority allocation by reserving scarce C1 capacity, but it does not remove final compliance. K-411 removes an artificial system serialization without changing the normative prerequisites. K-219 alters **routing**, substituting D2 for D1. K-337 alters the **allowed decision policy**: it removes a rework branch and forces a decision under unresolved uncertainty, so it is not merely a faster version of the factual execution.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The observed delay is not solely a slow Compliance Review. The ambiguity was already visible at Mon 08:50, but the case continued through Identity, the long D1 queue, and A1 without compliance preparation. When A1 completed at Tue 19:10, the case faced approximately 18 hours of post-Assessment waiting before C1 began, followed by 4.5 hours of largely fresh reading. The early intervention cannot shorten the factual D1 or A1 stages under a minimal-change counterfactual.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage completion, create a versioned escalation packet for S1 and a provisional C1 reservation. S1’s advisory work and any nonfinal C1 evidence organization may occur while I1 and D1 work. Identity, D1 routing, Assessment ownership, and the final decision rule remain unchanged.

**Temporal propagation:** Tue 19:10 remains the hard earliest time for final compliance. A usable reservation reduces some of the subsequent queue, while reusable preliminary work can reduce final service by roughly the supplied 1–3-hour guidance. An earlier C1 completion advances notification only when M1 has a corresponding signing window; otherwise the gain is absorbed in a new Decision wait. Conversely, a final Assessment that changes the evidence version invalidates part of the preliminary work.

**Process-feasibility check:** Identity and Document Check still finish before Assessment. The final C1 event starts only after A1’s final Assessment. A1 and C1 are different resources, and C1 cannot use a reserved interval simultaneously for another case. The preliminary screen must remain explicitly labeled preliminary and must be reaffirmed against final evidence.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case: the slot begins Tuesday evening, most preparation remains valid, and notification moves forward approximately 10–16 hours. Base case: C1 obtains a Wednesday-morning rather than Wednesday-afternoon slot, producing approximately 4–10 hours of gain. Worst case: the packet is incomplete or the slot is missed, yielding no target benefit or several hours of duplicated work while still delaying another urgent case.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The conclusion would weaken or reverse if reservation logs show high no-show rates, final evidence versions frequently invalidate preliminary analysis, C1’s actual calendar had no earlier feasible slot, M1 signs only at the factual time, or urgent non-target cases experience larger delays than the target’s gain.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Identity was already complete before originals arrived, so Mon 17:30 was the earliest legal Document Check start. The factual route then imposed a 16-hour wait for D1, followed by a relatively efficient and accurate four-hour service. The remaining delay arose from the A2 queue and the timing of M1’s decision.

**Minimal intervention:** Change only the document-resource assignment: enqueue the case to D2 at Mon 17:30. Do not move Document Check before Originals Receipt, alter Identity, or bypass Assessment. If D2 flags uncertain handwriting, A2—not D2—formally decides whether to issue Request More Info.

**Temporal propagation:** The Mon 16:00 D2 forecast of five hours suggests, but does not guarantee, a late-Monday start. With 4–8 hours of D2 service, clean completion could occur roughly early Tuesday, approximately 8–12 hours before factual D1 completion. The Tue 08:00 A2 queue of six hours can convert much of that readiness gain into waiting, and M1 may still sign Wed morning. In the clarification branch, 8–36 external hours plus another D2 check, another A2 assessment, and their queues can more than erase the initial gain.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals still precede every initial Document Check, and both Identity and Document Check precede Assessment. The clean path needs no compliance because the case is neither high-risk nor high-value. The clarification branch includes the mandatory new Document Check and new Assessment before Decision.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case: D2 completes early Tuesday, the case obtains an earlier A2 position, and M1 signs Tuesday, saving approximately 8–16 hours. Base case: verification becomes ready earlier but the same M1 batch is used, saving approximately 0–8 hours. Worst case: D2 induces an avoidable clarification, causing an approximately 8–50-hour net delay and possibly changing the outcome.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The clean-route conclusion would reverse if D2’s handwriting clarification or error rate is materially above D1’s, D2’s true queue does not drain as forecast, earlier A2 readiness does not improve queue position, or M1 has no earlier signing opportunity. Conversely, strong D2 accuracy data would make the intervention more attractive.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** At Fri 10:30, the case had already completed a second Document Check and Assessment, but one discrepancy remained. The second external request lasted until Mon 09:00 because it crossed a weekend. It then induced a third D1 check, a third A1 assessment, final C1 review, and an overnight wait for M1. Thus the delay was a compound of external calendar time, repeated service, and repeated queues.

**Minimal intervention:** Preserve the entire trace through Assessment 2, record the unresolved discrepancy, and prohibit any second Request More Info from either A1 or C1. Route the available evidence to final C1 review, after which M1 must select an explicit conditional approval or rejection.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing Request More Info 2 eliminates the Friday-to-Monday external delay and prevents the subsequent D1 and A1 queue visits. The case therefore reaches C1 several days earlier. The exact notification gain depends on whether C1 can review Friday or only Monday and whether M1 signs before or after the weekend. A fast internal terminal event does not eliminate possible later appeal or reopening work.

**Process-feasibility check:** Assessment 2 becomes the final Assessment. Because the case is high-risk and high-value, C1 must still perform the final review, and A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes separation. The unresolved issue cannot be omitted from the review record. An unconditional approval that treats the discrepancy as resolved would violate the intervention.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case: C1 accepts enforceable conditions, reviews Friday, and M1 notifies Friday or early Monday, saving roughly 72–110 hours. Base case: C1/M1 complete Monday or Tuesday, saving roughly 48–96 hours but with a changed risk posture. Worst case: an unsafe conditional approval creates loss or a conservative rejection causes appeal and reopening; measured internal time falls while true end-to-end time or cost rises.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The intervention becomes infeasible if regulation or internal policy requires the discrepancy to be resolved before any decision. It also becomes unattractive if historical unresolved-discrepancy cases show high loss, appeal, reversal, or compliance-defect rates, or if proposed approval conditions are not enforceable.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The factual sequence was created by a task-release restriction rather than a business prerequisite. D2 was not allowed to claim the case until I2 completed at Tue 13:20, so Document Check started at 15:00 and ended at 19:30. The case then waited until Wed 09:30 for A2. The two delay mechanisms are therefore late entry into D2’s queue and downstream assessment congestion.

**Minimal intervention:** At Tue 09:35, create both tasks and allow I2 and D2 to claim them independently through a controlled workaround. Keep the same verification content and resources. Require server-side task lifecycle and lock events rather than reconstructing earlier timestamps later.

**Temporal propagation:** With a four-hour D2 queue forecast at Tue 08:00, D2 might start around midday rather than 15:00. At a factual-like 4.5-hour service duration, the synchronization join could move from 19:30 to approximately 16:30. That does not guarantee a three-hour final gain: A2 may still start at Wed 09:30. A larger gain is possible only if earlier readiness crosses an assessment or manager cutoff.

**Process-feasibility check:** Because no originals are required, Identity and Document Check may overlap. Both still complete before Assessment. I2 and D2 are distinct resources, and neither may overlap another assignment of its own. Decision remains after Assessment and Notify remains after Decision.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case: true overlap advances the join sufficiently to obtain a same-day A2 or M1 window, saving approximately 8–14 hours. Base case: the join moves earlier but A2 remains the bottleneck, giving approximately 0–4 hours of improvement. Worst case: lock conflicts, duplicate edits, or workaround administration add up to several hours and create an audit defect.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The conclusion would reverse if task-claim logs show no earlier D2 access, D2 was already continuously occupied, A2 strictly preserves the same eventual start regardless of arrival time, concurrent edits are not separable, or timestamp audits reveal that apparent overlap was produced by batch entry.

**100-case weekly rollout:** At 100 cases, the sign of each intervention is governed by shared queues rather than the target case’s saved waiting time. A K-104-style reservation policy creates claims on the one-server C1 pool. If preliminary work is reusable, final service may fall by 1–3 hours per case, but provisional holds, no-shows, and incomplete packets can consume or strand capacity. Urgent non-target cases then wait longer; near full utilization, a small rise in reserved load can cause disproportionate queue growth.

Moving 100 handwritten cases from D1 to D2 is substitution, not free capacity. D1’s queue should shrink, benefiting complex corporate cases for which D1 is faster, while D2’s queue rises. If D2 triggers more ambiguity flags, A2 must issue more Requests More Info, creating repeated D2 and assessment work. Thus a routing rule that is beneficial at Monday’s snapshot can reverse once D2 utilization or rework probability increases.

Suppressing second rework loops removes D1/A1 work and avoids external response delays, especially the Friday-to-Monday calendar jump. It also advances many unresolved cases toward C1 and M1 at once, producing a compliance/decision burst. Rejections, conditional approvals, appeals, and reopened cases may move work outside the measured path, so apparent throughput can improve while total demand or loss exposure worsens.

Parallel task creation for K-411-like cases keeps nominal service demand constant but shifts D2 arrivals forward and bunches cases at the identity/document join. The assessment queue may become the new bottleneck; earlier verification completion then becomes waiting rather than shorter notification time. Weekend effects remain important for any new rework. Finally, real-time start/complete events, task-claim logs, and lock records are needed: earlier or back-entered timestamps can mimic concurrency without simultaneous service. A rollout should therefore monitor non-target waiting-time distributions, not only treated-case cycle time.

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the four, K-411 is the most robust intervention. It restores concurrency already permitted by the normative model, leaves service content and decision rights unchanged, and is readily reversible. Its benefit is not guaranteed: earlier completion of verification may simply become additional waiting before A2. Even so, the main risks—data conflicts, false concurrency, and assessment-queue spillover—are observable in a controlled pilot.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite offering the largest apparent cycle-time reduction. Deleting the second information loop changes the decision policy. The unresolved discrepancy must therefore produce either a conditional approval or a rejection after final C1 review. A shorter internal trace can conceal greater loss exposure, weaker defensibility, avoidable rejection, appeal, or reopening. These downstream effects can exceed the removed weekend and rework delay.

The K-104 result is least transportable from one case to a weekly policy. A single target case can benefit from a provisional C1 reservation, but 100 such reservations do not create C1 capacity; they reprioritize it. Because C1 is the only fully certified reviewer, incomplete packets, unused slots, and urgent arrivals can make the system-wide effect negative even when the treated case improves. K-219 is also load-sensitive, because its gain depends on D2 remaining lightly loaded and not inducing clarification.

The most valuable additional data are lifecycle events for task creation, queue entry, claim, start, completion, cancellation, and reassignment; actual resource calendars and priority changes; reservation creation/use/cancellation; evidence-version identifiers for preliminary versus final review; Request More Info reason codes and external-response timestamps; and decision conditions, appeals, reopenings, and realized loss or compliance defects. These data would separate service reduction from queue displacement, true concurrency from timestamp artifacts, and faster internal closure from better outcomes.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** Within a priority class, queue order is approximately first-come-first-served unless an urgent classification or explicit reservation changes it.

2. **temporal:** Snapshot queue hours can be used to construct approximate start windows, but they are neither exact commitments nor guaranteed to drain one-for-one with elapsed time.

3. **resource:** S1’s advisory work can overlap Identity and Document checks, while every named operational resource remains a single server with non-overlapping service intervals.

4. **temporal:** Evening or overnight service is feasible because it appears in the factual traces, but M1’s overnight and weekend signing availability is unknown and is therefore scenario-dependent.

5. **quality:** K-104 preliminary work saves final C1 time only when the final evidence version remains materially consistent with the packet reviewed earlier.

6. **behavioral:** D2 has a higher probability than D1 of flagging difficult handwriting, but the magnitude is unknown; K-219 therefore requires clean and clarification branches rather than one expected duration.

7. **structural:** K-337’s intervention is feasible only if governing policy permits an explicit conditional approval or rejection without resolving the final discrepancy; otherwise the counterfactual path is inadmissible.

8. **interference:** Single-case estimates hold background operations approximately fixed except for immediate resource displacement; appeals, reopenings, and losses that occur after Notify are treated as downstream system effects rather than observed factual events.
