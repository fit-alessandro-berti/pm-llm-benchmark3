## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped gap (0.55 vs 0.90 in March; roughly 0.3-0.4 throughout) implies that most nonconformance against the old normative model is an artifact of vocabulary: relabeling `Eligibility Screen` as `Validate Eligibility` and deleting the technical `Open Case` duplicates recovers about 90-95% of the lost fitness. It does not imply the process is unchanged. Fitness is one lens; a mapping that restores replayability says nothing about frequencies of paths, waiting times, workload, or outcomes, and the mapping itself is an analyst's hypothesis about semantic equivalence — if `Eligibility Screen` actually performs shallower checks than `Validate Eligibility`, mapped fitness would still look healthy while the business control weakened.

Duplicated technical events and missing resources damage different analyses differently. Duplicate `Open Case` events corrupt control-flow artifacts (variants, DFG edges, alignments) and inflate event counts, but are removable deterministically. Missing resource identifiers do not affect control-flow at all, yet they invalidate resource-level analyses — workload, handover networks, and any attempt to verify the staffing mechanism — and because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, resource statistics are biased, not merely noisier.

Finally, the post-May patterns cannot be logging drift alone: the mapped series itself degrades (fitness 0.91 -> 0.86-0.88), the normalized DFG rates — computed after the mapping — show `Assess -> Request Info` doubling, and cycle times come from operational timestamps unaffected by the label rename. A semantic mapping cannot manufacture extra rework loops or 13 additional median hours; something real changed in behavior and performance. (~240 words)

**3.3 Cycle-time decomposition** (July vs baseline, median +13.1 h; p90 +36 h). Ranges, not point estimates:

- **Queueing: ~50-60% of the median deterioration (~7-8 h).** `Assess` service time rose only ~0.6 h while reconstructed pre-`Assess` waiting rose "much more" (stated observation); the June staffing cut and rising arrivals are consistent with nonlinear queue growth, and the p90 growing faster than the median is a classic congestion signature.
- **Control-flow/rework: ~20-30% (~3-4 h).** Extra `Request Info` loops rose from 12 to 29 per 100 cases (+17pp). Each loop adds at least one `Collect Documents` + `Assess` pass plus queueing for the repeat pass; at ~5 h service plus wait per extra pass, +0.17 loops/case plausibly adds 2-4 h to the average and drags the median upward.
- **Case mix: ~10-15% (~1-2 h).** High-complexity share rose only 22 -> 28% by July; assuming complex cases run somewhat longer, the mix shift alone moves the median modestly. (In October-November, by contrast, case mix becomes the dominant component: +20pp complexity share plus a tripled senior-assessment rate.)
- **Service: ~5% (~0.6 h).** Directly observed: 4.8 -> 5.4 h at `Assess`, itself possibly a mix effect (more complex cases per assessment) rather than slower assessors.

These shares interact (rework passes also queue; priority reshuffles who waits), so they should be read as an ordering — queueing > rework > mix ≈ service in mid-year — not an additive identity.

**3.4 Candidate change points** (at least two each):

- Logging drift: **2026-03** (label rename, `Open Case` duplication, resource missingness step from 1.0% to 4.8%); **2026-10** (possible recording change or regularization of `Senior Assessment`, historically logged inconsistently — its jump from 7 to 22 per 100 exceeds what the complexity-share increase alone would predict proportionally).
- Behavioral/control-flow drift: **2026-05** (rework-loop rate doubles; fast variants collapse); **2026-09** (partial reversal of the same loop); optionally **2026-07** (priority-rule reordering — a routing/behavioral change even though it mainly shows up in performance).
- Performance drift: **2026-06** (median/p90 inflection as staffing drops); **2026-10** (second inflection under seasonal load); **2026-12** (downward change point during recovery/intervention).

**3.5 One global change point or several?** The data clearly reject a single global change point. The fitness/label artifacts break at March and never fully recover on the raw series; rework breaks at May and partially reverts in September; cycle time breaks at June and again at October; senior routing breaks at October. Different artifacts have different onset dates, shapes (step vs ramp vs seasonal), and reversals. A mechanism-specific, multi-change-point account is the only one consistent with the aggregates; a formal test (e.g., PELT/binary segmentation per series, once event-level data allow weekly resolution) should be run per artifact, not on a pooled score.

**Primary monitoring population:** L2, the complexity-segmented monthly view (with L1 supplier overlay as the first drill-down). Complexity is the variable that mediates the most mechanisms in this log — seasonality, senior routing, the priority rule, and (partly) Supplier Z — so unsegmented aggregates are guaranteed to conflate population drift with behavioral drift, while deeper cuts (L3-L5) run into support and conditioning problems as *default* views. L2 keeps every cell above ~150 cases in normal months, which supports stable weekly statistics, and every alert raised at L2 can be escalated into L3/L4/L5 as diagnostic drill-downs rather than always-on monitors.

**Thresholds, seasonality, multiple testing.** No universal threshold: each monitor uses statistic-appropriate references — robust z-scores for label/frequency series, bootstrap percentiles for distances, class-segmented multiplicative bands for heavy-tailed waiting times. Seasonality is handled by reference-window design (M8, M6, M11 compare to the same calendar period of prior years, and M8 acts as a gate that annotates other alerts as "seasonally expected" when mix is in-band), so recurrence is distinguished from new drift by construction. With ~12 monitors x segments evaluated weekly, false discoveries are controlled by (i) requiring persistence (2-3 consecutive periods) before alerting, (ii) Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control across the weekly alert battery at q=0.1, and (iii) severity tiers: single-monitor alerts open a diagnostic ticket, while correlated alerts across mechanism-linked monitors (e.g., M4+M6+M10) escalate to the process owner.

def load_and_validate(csv_text: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(csv_text))
    required = {
        "month", "cases", "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct",
        "fast_variant_pct", "rework_variant_pct", "median_cycle_h",
        "p90_cycle_h", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness",
        "distinct_activity_labels", "missing_resource_pct",
        "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    }
    missing = required - set(df.columns)
    assert not missing, f"missing columns: {missing}"

    months = pd.PeriodIndex(df["month"], freq="M")
    assert months.is_monotonic_increasing, "months out of order"
    ords = months.astype("int64")
    assert (ords[1:] - ords[:-1] == 1).all(), "months not contiguous"

    for col in [c for c in df.columns if c.endswith("_pct")]:
        assert df[col].between(0, 100).all(), f"{col} outside [0,100]"
    for col in ["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]:
        assert df[col].between(0, 1).all(), f"{col} outside [0,1]"
    assert (df["p90_cycle_h"] >= df["median_cycle_h"]).all(), \
        "p90 below median"
    return df.set_index("month")


def baseline_relative(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = df.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS].mean(numeric_only=True)
    return df.subtract(base, axis=1)


def dfg_l1_distance(rates: pd.DataFrame,
                    reference: str = "2026-01") -> pd.Series:
    """L1 distance between monthly relation-rate vectors and the reference.

    Assumption: edges omitted from the supplied fragment are equal across
    months, so these distances are LOWER BOUNDS on true DFG distance.
    """
    ref = rates.loc[reference]
    return rates.sub(ref, axis=1).abs().sum(axis=1)


def fitness_recovery(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    """Share of the raw-fitness loss (vs baseline) recovered by mapping."""
    base = df.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS, "raw_model_fitness"].mean()
    lost = (base - df["raw_model_fitness"]).clip(lower=1e-9)
    recovered = df["mapped_model_fitness"] - df["raw_model_fitness"]
    return (recovered / lost).clip(0, 1).round(3)


def flag_change_points(df: pd.DataFrame, cols, z_thresh: float = 3.0):
    """Simple explainable rule: flag month m for metric c if the
    month-over-month delta exceeds z_thresh robust z-scores, where scale
    is the within-baseline delta (Jan->Feb only, so no drift months
    contaminate the reference), floored at 5% of the baseline level."""
    flags = []
    for c in cols:
        deltas = df[c].diff()
        within_baseline = abs(deltas.iloc[1])  # Jan->Feb delta
        scale = max(within_baseline,
                    0.05 * abs(df[c].iloc[:2].mean()), 1e-9)
        for month, d in deltas.dropna().items():
            z = d / scale
            if abs(z) >= z_thresh:
                flags.append({"month": month, "metric": c,
                              "delta": round(d, 3), "robust_z": round(z, 1)})
    return pd.DataFrame(flags).sort_values(["month", "metric"])

def slice_window(log_df: pd.DataFrame, start: str, end: str,

def normalized_view(log_df: pd.DataFrame,
                    label_map=None, drop_technical=None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Return a normalized COPY (raw log is never overwritten):
    maps migrated labels and removes technical events, so every artifact
    can be computed twice -- raw and normalized -- and diffed."""
    label_map = LABEL_MAP if label_map is None else label_map
    drop_technical = (TECHNICAL_EVENTS if drop_technical is None
                      else drop_technical)
    view = log_df.copy()
    view["concept:name"] = view["concept:name"].replace(label_map)
    view = view[~view["concept:name"].isin(drop_technical)]
    return view


def raw_vs_normalized_report(log_df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Compute DFG, variants, and temporal profile on both views.
    Divergence between views localizes logging drift; divergence of the
    normalized view over time indicates behavioral/performance drift."""
    norm = normalized_view(log_df)
    return {
        "raw": {"dfg": monthly_dfg(log_df),
                "variants": monthly_variants(log_df),
                "temporal": temporal_profile(log_df)},
        "normalized": {"dfg": monthly_dfg(norm),
                       "variants": monthly_variants(norm),
                       "temporal": temporal_profile(norm)},
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions (high confidence, ~85-90%).** The March fitness collapse is a semantic/provenance event caused by the migration, not a process change: a two-step normalization (label map plus `Open Case` dedup) recovers roughly 93% of the lost fitness, the normalized DFG rates move by only ~3 points per 100 cases, and cycle times are flat through April. Equally strong: the elevated resource missingness (~5-6%) is migration-caused measurement drift — it starts exactly in March, pre-dates the June departures, and concentrates in migrated activities. Third: the October-November shift is dominated by recurring seasonal population drift — volume and high-complexity share move together on the historically expected date, and mix-sensitive metrics move with them.

**Plausible but not proven (~60-75%).** The May-August rework wave is most plausibly supplier-driven: rework and Supplier Z share rise and fall together, bracketed by the documented disruption and recovery dates. But the aggregates never show that the extra `Request Info` loops belong to Z's cases, and Z's pre-existing complexity skew means complexity partially mediates the effect. Similarly, the June-August cycle-time deterioration is most plausibly staffing-driven queueing — near-flat service time with sharply rising reconstructed waiting is the right signature — yet the rework wave independently inflates load, so staffing and supplier mechanisms jointly, not separately, explain the queue.

**Where interaction blocks clean attribution.** Three places. First, rework x staffing: extra loops raise demand exactly when capacity fell, so their queueing contributions are multiplicative, not additive; my 50-60%/20-30% queueing/rework split is an ordering, not a measurement. Second, the priority rule: its redistribution of waiting is invisible in monthly aggregates by construction, so any of the July-onward tail growth attributed to staffing could partly be redistribution. Third, the October `Senior Assessment` tripling mixes real mix-driven routing with a possible recording regularization of a historically inconsistent label; I deliberately leave this split open. December permits no attribution at all — season, overtime, and the quality campaign are fully confounded.

**What would revise the account.** Segment-level event data showing rework concentrated in non-Z cases (would shift me toward assessor-side behavioral adaptation); waiting-time decomposition showing high-priority waiting also rising post-July (would weaken the redistribution story); evidence that `Eligibility Screen` performs different checks than `Validate Eligibility` (would upgrade March from logging to behavioral drift); prior-year logs showing no comparable renewal-season complexity surge (would reframe October as new population drift rather than recurrence); and persistence of >15/100 rework in non-Z standard cases into early 2027 (would confirm persistent behavioral adaptation). (~370 words)

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF the distinct-activity-label set changes or raw fitness drops while mapped/normalized fitness and normalized DFG rates stay within their reference bands, THEN route to data engineering to update the label-mapping table and freeze all conformance-based alerts until the mapping is verified, BECAUSE this signature indicates semantic/logging drift from a system change, and acting on raw conformance would misdiagnose a vocabulary change as a process breakdown (the March pattern).

2. IF `Assess -> Request Info` per 100 cases rises by more than 5 points and the rise is concentrated in one supplier's segment, THEN trigger a supplier bundle-completeness audit and open a document-quality corrective action with that supplier, BECAUSE segment-concentrated rework growth indicates upstream input-quality failure rather than assessor behavior change (the May Supplier Z pattern).

3. IF reconstructed waiting before `Assess` grows across all supplier and complexity segments while within-class `Assess` service time stays within 20% of baseline, THEN escalate a capacity review comparing assessor roster against arrivals times the current rework multiplier, BECAUSE uniform waiting growth with flat service time is a queueing signature of a demand-capacity gap, not of case content or individual performance (the June-August pattern).

4. IF the aggregate p90 cycle time rises while high-priority-class waiting is flat or improving and standard-class waiting deteriorates, THEN report delay redistribution to the process owner with class-level fairness metrics rather than raising a global performance alarm, BECAUSE a priority rule shifts delay between classes without changing total capacity, and an aggregate alarm would prescribe the wrong remedy (the post-July risk).

5. IF high-complexity share and volume rise but remain within the prior-year seasonal band for the same calendar weeks, and within-class DFG rates and cycle times stay in-band, THEN annotate downstream alerts as seasonally expected and suppress escalation while activating the pre-agreed seasonal staffing plan, BECAUSE recurring population drift requires capacity planning, not drift investigation (the October pattern).

6. IF `missing_resource_pct` exceeds 2% or concentrates in specific activities or teams, THEN quarantine resource-level analytics (workload, handover, per-assessor service time) and open an ETL provenance ticket before any staffing conclusions are drawn, BECAUSE informative missingness biases resource statistics and can both manufacture and mask organizational drift (the persistent post-March condition).

7. IF a segment-level rework or variant shift persists for more than four weeks after its candidate external cause is confirmed resolved (e.g., supplier recovery) and after seasonal load normalizes, THEN reclassify the change from transient disruption to persistent behavioral adaptation and review whether the normative model and targets should be updated, BECAUSE behavior that survives the removal of its trigger has become the new process, and continuing to alert against the old baseline generates permanent false positives (the December-to-2027 question).
