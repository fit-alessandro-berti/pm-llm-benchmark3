# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

The networks are different projections of the log. Their centrality values are not directly comparable, and none of them independently establishes formal authority, intentional communication, or causation.

**Handover-of-work network**

In the usual PM4Py construction, this network summarizes resource changes along ordered events within cases. Mira’s high out-degree, in-degree, and betweenness, together with `Bot_Rules  Mira` and her subsequent links to adjusters and specialists, are consistent with an **exception-intake and referral hub**. She connects the automated routing stage to several otherwise separate human groups.

This does **not** mean that 77% of claims pass through her: betweenness of 0.77 is a graph statistic, while her observed case coverage is 22%. Nor does it prove that she personally assigns every downstream task, has line-management authority, or delays work. Some edges may represent status updates or resource-field changes rather than actual delegation. `Bot_Rules` also has substantial betweenness, so the automated router remains an important structural hub.

**Working-together network**

This undirected network measures same-case resource co-occurrence, not simultaneous work, communication, or personal closeness. It reveals concentrated routine-team structures—Alice/Carla/Elise and Bruno/Diego/Farid—whereas Mira’s ties are weaker and more dispersed.

A plausible reconciliation is that routine teams repeatedly share cases with a stable small set of colleagues, concentrating edge weight, while Mira makes brief, varied interventions across a broader exception population. Handover frequency is transition-based; working-together weight is case-based and may use a different denominator. High directed brokerage and comparatively low co-case degree are therefore not inherently contradictory.

Importantly, under standard PM4Py same-case co-occurrence, **short events do not by themselves reduce an edge**: one routing event still counts as co-occurring with the other resources in that case. Repeated handovers, filters, normalization, and different case denominators could explain the apparent discrepancy. These calculations should consequently be checked before treating “peripheral” as an organizational finding. It does not mean Mira is unimportant or socially isolated.

**Subcontracting network**

With `n=2`, the network detects local patterns in which resource A appears, resource B intervenes, and A reappears within the specified event window. Mira–Omar and Mira–Priya patterns are consistent with **referral, specialist intervention, and resumption of coordination**. Her subsequent `Assign Specialist` or `Reopen Claim` events support that interpretation more than a simple one-way handoff would.

However, “subcontracting” is an algorithmic label, not proof of outsourcing, accountability, or authorization. The `Bot_Rules  Mira  Bot_Rules` pattern demonstrates why: an automated status-update loop can produce the same shape. Likewise, `Temp_1–Vendor_Verify–Temp_1` may represent ordinary verification sequencing. The results are sensitive to event ordering, lifecycle handling, and the chosen window.

**Performance/time statistics**

Mira’s 4.2-minute median service time and approximately one recorded event per touched case suggest a high-breadth, relatively shallow-touch function rather than continuous case processing. Her exception share and concentration among reopened and complaint cases indicate exception-heavy case selection; they do not show that she causes exceptions, reopens, or complaints. Event order must establish whether she detects problems or is called in afterward.

The absence evidence is the strongest indication of vulnerability: the exception-case 90th percentile rose by 5.5 days, from 8.4 to 13.9 days, while ordinary low-value claims were stable. That supports an **exception-specific coverage or capacity dependency**, not a process-wide bottleneck. It remains a six-day observational interruption, potentially confounded by volumes, case mix, other absences, queue carryover, or rule changes.

Her short service time also does not exclude long queues before her events. Conversely, high cycle-time contribution may reflect difficult cases rather than delay caused by her. Omar and Priya have long specialist service times and very complex case mixes, while `Vendor_Verify` and `Vendor_Pay` have explicit waiting-time components. These remain material alternative or additional sources of delay.

**Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity**

Activity-based resource similarity and organizational-role discovery use activity profiles, not network position. They might place Alice and Bruno together, separate senior from junior adjusters, and leave Mira as a distinct or mixed coordination profile because she combines missing-information work, reopening, complaints, and specialist assignment. That would be a result to test, not one implied by her resource name.

Such methods cannot establish seniority, formal decision rights, or tacit expertise. Rare but consequential activities may receive little weight. Automated and vendor resources should be examined both in the complete model and in a human-only view: `Vendor_Verify`, for example, may look similar to staff performing `Validate Coverage` despite having a fundamentally different organizational status.

Overall, the aggregate evidence may mix **two Mira modes**: frequent one-touch routing and less frequent, closed-loop orchestration of complex cases.

- Record a minimal referral audit trail: triggering reason, destination, manual-versus-rule decision, reason code, and accountable decision-maker. For high-value, fraud, legal, and complaint cases, verify permitted routing and use targeted sample review rather than blanket additional approvals.
- Validate resource-account usage, lifecycle/timestamp handling, activity labels, and network denominators. Monitor automated routing and vendor service levels in the same operational view so delays are not automatically attributed to Mira.

**Monitor**

Track weekly exception arrivals, backlog per available coordinator hour, time to first exception action, referral and return waits, repeated handovers, specialist acceptance and re-referral rates, specialist workload, reopening and complaint outcomes, and p90/p95 cycle time. Stratify by case mix and compare backup performance with Mira’s. Monitor active effort and complexity-adjusted workload rather than using centrality, event count, or working-together degree as personal-performance targets.

**Do not change until more evidence is available**

Do not remove Mira, declare her redundant, force her into a routine team, automate all referrals, or impose blanket separation of detection and referral solely from these networks. Those actions could destroy a useful filtering function and increase specialist load.

If a later redesign removes or distributes her duties, the replacement must include staffed queue capacity and backup, documented triage and escalation rules, explicit case ownership, adequate specialist capacity, and equivalent audit controls. It should be piloted on comparable exception cases and retained only if service, quality, workload, and compliance are no worse.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to perform an exception-facing coordination and referral function, although her formal authority and the quality of the resource data still need verification. The working-together view measures shared case participation differently, so its lower score does not make her unimportant and does not contradict the other networks. Her six-day absence is the strongest warning: ordinary claims were stable, but the exception-case tail worsened sharply. This suggests an exception-specific capacity or key-person dependency, not a proven organization-wide bottleneck. Specialist and vendor delays also remain material. There is no evidence here of misconduct or unnecessary work. Management should now provide tested backup coverage, document referral decisions and case ownership, and monitor exception queues, rework, and specialist load.
