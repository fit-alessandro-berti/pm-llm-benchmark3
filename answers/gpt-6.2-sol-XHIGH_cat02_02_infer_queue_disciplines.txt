# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

I use service boundaries as proxies for selection times because the decisions themselves are unlogged. A job is a baseline candidate if its arrival scan has occurred, it has unfinished work, and it can be served by the resource in question. Actual release, reservation, or assignment may narrow that set. A scan at time *s* permits physical arrival from approximately *s* minus two minutes through *s*, subject to minute resolution. A question mark marks a candidate near the scan boundary. A job arriving before its scan could affect a choice if dispatch had another way to see it.

FIFO uses original queue age, proxied by scan order, and gives an in-service job continuity. Priority favors the lowest priority number and breaks ties by older scan. SPT means non-preemptive shortest **expected** processing time at a free resource. For QB, the comparison uses estimated remaining work for B01, approximated by subtracting recorded work from its arrival estimate. For busy-resource decisions, the table also gives the preemptive-priority prediction. An em dash in the appointment column means the appointment rule gives no selection at the nominal time. A feature such as skill compatibility or appointment preference may leave a choice unresolved, so no tie-breaking rule is inferred from that feature alone.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

- **QA.** A02 over A04 at 08:32 and A05 and A07 over A08 at 09:05 and 09:32 support routine FIFO and weigh against persistent priority. A05 over shorter A07 also weighs against SPT. A04's 08:58 start is consistent with three rankings and has little discriminatory value. A03 ahead of A02 is the clear FIFO exception, with the due appointment as a supplied explanation. A06 starts five minutes late and would be next under FIFO anyway. Calibration explains the 08:52–08:58 gap. The 08:30 gap and the decision to let A05 occupy A06's nominal time are unexplained by the recorded calendar.
- **QB.** The B02 interruption rules out an entirely non-preemptive account of the recorded service. The B03 reassignment and B01's resumptions ahead of B03 and B06 rule against universal priority across the apparent waiting set. An urgent-only override or protected resumption is plausible, though original-age FIFO also predicts those resumptions. B05 at 10:00 is an exception to FIFO even among jobs that have never started. Its priority supplies a plausible rationale, but it was also present for the 09:48 choice of B03. Reconciling those decisions with one priority rule requires a change in effective eligibility, dispatch timing, or the type of decision. B07's late start supplies no evidence of exact appointment protection.
- **QC.** C02, C03, and C06 are positive evidence for SPT at their respective starts. At 10:32, C04 would be shortest among the remaining jobs from the 10:10 backlog if later arrivals were excluded, whereas C06 is shortest in the visible pool. C05 at 10:54 agrees with both priority and EDD, after two earlier occasions when both would have selected it. C01 over C07 at 11:40 agrees with FIFO, and could fit aging or cohort completion. The supplied context explains why none of the initial jobs starts before 10:10. It supplies no later hold, aging threshold, or SLA trigger to explain the conflicting choices.
- **QD.** D05 and D07 demonstrate the importance of skill bundles, and D04 over D08 and D07 over D10 support an age preference when the same resource can do either job. D03's earlier start than D04 cannot establish that one passed the other because they start on different resources and can run concurrently. D06's appointment also agrees with FIFO on D-X at 11:30, so the preceding idle period is more informative about possible reservation. D05 was a fit on D-F at 11:21 and lost to D03, a choice consistent with FIFO and SPT. After D03 completes, the meeting can explain holding D05 if the job requires a continuous slot. Holding D10 at 11:36 still requires an unlogged constraint, assignment, or a departure from immediate dispatch.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

1. **Availability and uninterrupted fit.** The calibration, prior QC job, D-X opening time, and D-F meeting are recorded constraints. Checking for an uninterrupted slot makes D05's wait after 11:36 plausible. D10 fits in the same interval on its estimate, so a fit check by itself leaves an unresolved QD decision.
2. **Appointment grace, reservation, and backfill.** A03 and D06 start at their nominal times. A06 starts five minutes late and B07 starts 26 minutes late. A booking can reserve capacity, temporarily improve rank, expire into ordinary work, or allow backfill up to a buffer. These few timestamps do not determine early or late tolerances. A reservation component is plausible in QA and QD, while strict on-time protection is inconsistent with the A05 and B04 placements under the recorded estimates.
3. **Dispatcher intervention, resumption preference, and advance assignment.** Both B01 stops have recorded reasons, and the D-F idle interval with D10 waiting is consistent with assignment to D-X. A pre-assigned or held case changes the effective menu, while resumption preference changes the ranking within it. If switching has a setup or continuity cost, an urgency threshold can make the selective B01 interruption pattern rational. The records establish neither the cost nor the threshold.
4. **Cohort release, aging, or an SLA-triggered policy change.** A release cohort can explain why C04 runs before C06, and a preference for clearing an old job can explain C01. A deadline review could promote C05 once it is sufficiently late or its deadline has passed. A general rule for protecting still-feasible SLAs would also have reason to promote C07 at 11:40, so this explanation needs a specific trigger or eligibility restriction.
5. **Scan, decision, or logging lag.** Scan latency can affect whether C05 was visible at 10:10 or D03 at 11:01. Because the scan may be late, it cannot by itself move a job with an earlier recorded scan to a physical arrival after the decision proxy. An advance dispatch decision or delayed eligibility update could explain an apparent pass over B05 at 09:48. These timing issues are less persuasive for jobs that had already been waiting for many minutes.
6. **Estimate revisions, noise, or strategic estimates.** A revised estimate, skill-specific setup, or a strategically low quote could change an apparent SPT order or the jobs believed to fit before a slot. The supplied arrival estimates happen to match the recorded active durations, including B01's summed segments. That weakens an explanation based on large errors in the supplied estimates for these cases. It leaves open revisions or setup costs seen by dispatch but absent from the records.

# 6. Additional event data needed

1. **Time-stamped decision and eligibility records.** Capture physical check-in, arrival scan, queue-entry and release times, dispatch-decision time, resource, chosen job, and the full eligible set with exclusion reasons. Include decisions that leave a resource idle and any reservation or assignment made before service starts. Prioritize B05 at 09:48, C06 at 10:32, and D10 at 11:36. These records distinguish a ranking choice from a job that could not be picked.
2. **Resource and appointment constraints at decision time.** Record skill bundles, effective calendars, setup and handoff allowances, uninterrupted-slot requirements, appointment early and late tolerance, capacity reservation, backfill rules, and whether a missed booking converts to ordinary work. Focus on D-F before the meeting, D-X before D06, and the A06 and B07 bookings.
3. **Interruption and resumption events.** Record the triggering job or event, preemption reason, whether dispatch or the worker initiated the stop, expected remaining time, the job's requeue timestamp or retained rank, any reservation for resumption, and the time and cost of switching. Start with both B01 stops and the decisions at 09:31 and 10:14.
4. **Decision-time ranking inputs and policy version.** Capture arrival and current estimated processing or remaining times, priority changes, due dates, forecast slack, active SLA or aging threshold, override reason, and the policy version or operating mode in effect. Look for QC decisions with conflicting priority and deadline rankings and compare decisions before and after any recorded SLA trigger.

# 7. Final synthesis

QD is most identifiable. The skills determine several feasible assignments, and the choices at 11:25 and 11:48 support FIFO within the local eligible pool. Its idle periods leave the assignment and reservation details open. QA is consistent with routine FIFO plus a soft appointment preference. QB clearly permits interruptions, with selective urgency and resumption shaping the order. QC is least identifiable because several starts agree with shortest estimates, while other starts require an additional ranking or release mechanism whose trigger is absent from the log. The most dangerous claim to operationalize is that QB guarantees immediate preemption for every higher-priority job. B02's interruption provides a single positive instance, while the B03 reassignment ahead of B05 and the B01 resumptions ahead of higher-priority work make that guarantee unsafe for SLA forecasts.
