# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network
**What it implies:** Mira is the dominant routing hub after automated triage. Out-degree centrality 0.82 and betweenness 0.77 place her on the majority of exception paths from `Bot_Rules` to specialists and adjusters. High-frequency directed edges to Omar, Priya, Carla, Diego, Elise, and Farid, plus return edges from specialists and temps, show she both dispatches and receives work. Substantively, this means many cases pass *through* her for reassignment, missing-info handling, reopen, or escalation—not that she performs the bulk of claim work.

**What it does not imply:** Handover centrality is not ownership of case outcomes, not continuous case involvement, and not proof she is a formal team lead. Short mean elapsed times on many outbound edges (16–19 min to adjusters; returns from specialists in ~11–12 min) are consistent with brief routing/clarification touches rather than deep co-processing. High betweenness can reflect a designed exception desk as easily as an unhealthy bottleneck.

## Working-together network
**What it implies:** Mira is peripheral (weighted degree 0.16; co-case rates with adjusters/team leads only 4–8%). She co-occurs on relatively few full case histories despite many handovers. This is consistent with short “touch-and-route” events: assignment, reopen, escalate, request missing information—after which the case continues with others.

**What it does not imply:** Low working-together does **not** mean she is unimportant. It specifically fails to support “core team member who stays on the case.” The contradiction with handover centrality is expected when a resource is a broker rather than a co-worker. It also does not prove isolation in the org chart; it proves limited joint case membership.

## Subcontracting network
**What it implies:** Strong ABA patterns (MiraOmarMira 126; MiraPriyaMira 98) indicate she initiates specialist work and often resumes control afterward—classic subcontracting / exception-broker behavior. The `Bot_Rules`Mira(Fraud Screen update) pattern suggests she also closes the loop on automated exception flags. Alice/Brunoadjuster subcontracting is more approval-negotiation cycling; Mira’s pattern is specialist dispatch and return.

**What it does not imply:** Subcontracting frequency is not proof of uncontrolled discretion or of optimal routing quality. It does not show whether she *selects* specialists by skill/load/rules or by informal preference. It does not measure whether the return hop adds value or delay.

## Performance/time statistics
**What it implies:** Median service time ~4.2 min per event is short relative to adjusters (17–24 min) and specialists (37–51 min), reinforcing a coordination/routing profile. She touches 22% of claims but 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints—concentrated on hard/exception work. High variance in cycle-time contribution and the 6-day absence effect (p90 exception cycle time 8.413.9 days; ordinary low-value claims stable) show exception queues depend on her availability. Vendors and bots still dominate volume and waiting-time mass (`Vendor_Verify` 2.1 h, `Vendor_Pay` 1.3 h).

**What it does not imply:** Short service time does not mean low organizational impact. High exception share is partly selection (she is assigned exceptions), not necessarily that she *creates* exceptions. Absence evidence shows dependency for exception flow, not that she is the root cause of exceptions. Cycle-time contribution “high variance” is not a verdict of inefficiency without comparing matched cases with/without her.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity
**What it implies:** Activity mix—Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist—clusters her with an exception-desk / cross-team coordinator profile, distinct from:
- Team leads (Triage, Approve Settlement),
- Adjusters (Assess Damage, Negotiate Settlement),
- Specialists (Medical/Legal Review),
- Temps (Validate Coverage, Close Claim, simpler missing info),
- Bots/vendors (ingest, rules, verify, pay).

Organizational role discovery would likely yield a separate “exception broker” role rather than merging her with Alice/Bruno.

**What it does not imply:** Activity labels alone do not establish formal authority, compliance boundaries, or whether “Assign Specialist” embeds uncontrolled discretion. Similarity to a coordinator role is a descriptive cluster, not a job-description validation.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis A — Formal coordinator / exception desk
**Mechanism:** Designed second-line queue after `Bot_Rules` for exception_flag, missing info, complaints, and specialist assignment; she routes and briefly clarifies, then exits the case.

**Evidence supporting:** Top handovers from `Bot_Rules` (920); activity profile matches exception work; short median service time; high reopen/complaint coverage; absence hurts exception p90 only; process owner’s “queue cleaner” label; temps escalate missing-info to her.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting:** Adjusters describe her as knowing “where hard cases should go” (more than pure queue cleaning); subcontracting loops with specialists suggest active case framing; compliance worry about dual detect-and-assign; low working-together with team leads (if formal, one might expect tighter lead coordination).

**Observable predictions:** Exception-tagged variants should show Mira early after Bot_Rules then long gaps; role discovery stable as its own role; SLA timers on exception queues would correlate with her capacity; replacing her with a staffed exception queue + rules should preserve routing volumes without her identity.

## Hypothesis B — Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency
**Mechanism:** Exception capacity is funneled through one person; her finite attention adds queueing delay and single-point failure, even if individual touches are short.

**Evidence supporting:** Handover betweenness 0.77; absence  exception backlog and p90 spike; high variance cycle-time contribution; touches most reopens/complaints; operations manager fears overload of specialists if removed without replacement.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting:** Ordinary low-value claims unaffected by absence; bots/vendors carry volume and waiting time; many handovers may be *by design*; short service times mean the bottleneck is decision/routing capacity, not long processing; centrality can be healthy load-bearing structure.

**Observable predictions:** Exception waiting time before Mira’s events should dominate her service time; queue length metrics rise with her utilization; parallel exception staff should reduce p90 without changing activity mix much; handover betweenness would redistribute if a second exception desk is added.

## Hypothesis C — Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker
**Mechanism:** She bridges automated intake, North/South teams, medical/legal specialists, temps, and complaint paths—translating exception signals into the right next resource when bots and team structure are insufficient.

**Evidence supporting:** Cross-edges to both teams’ adjusters and both specialists; returns from Omar/Priya; temps  Mira escalations; low working-together *with* high handover (classic broker signature); “knows where hard cases should go”; Mira–Alice/Bruno co-case only 4–5% (spans rather than sits inside one team).

**Evidence contradicting or limiting:** May still be mostly rule-following queue work; Vendor_Verify/Pay paths largely bypass her; team leads still own approval and payment release; broker narrative can romanticize what is procedural reassignment.

**Observable predictions:** Cases with multi-domain needs (medical+legal, complaint+reopen) enriched for Mira; activity-based similarity distinct from both leads and adjusters; network analysis with case_type/priority attributes should show her on cross-attribute bridges; removing informal discretion (if any) while keeping the desk would change *whom* she targets more than *whether* she appears.

## Hypothesis D — Quality gatekeeper / reopen–complaint controller (secondary)
**Mechanism:** She is the human control point that validates whether missing info, fraud residuals, or complaints justify reopen/escalate before re-entering the main path.

**Evidence supporting:** 64% reopens, 71% complaints; Bot_RulesMira fraud-screen subcontracting; specialist return then Mira again.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting:** Compliance fear that combining detection and specialist selection weakens segregation of duties; no direct evidence of quality metrics she optimizes; could be reactive queue ownership rather than proactive gatekeeping.

**Observable predictions:** Higher re-work rates on paths that skip her (if any); complaint variants with vs without Mira differ in legal involvement timing; filtering `Escalate Complaint`/`Reopen Claim` should concentrate her betweenness further.

## Hypothesis E — Data/process artifact of the assignment function (partial, not exclusive)
**Mechanism:** Logging “Assign Specialist” / short routing events on one resource ID inflates handover/subcontracting centrality without reflecting unique human judgment.

**Evidence supporting:** Very short service times; disappears for several events after routing; low working-together; bot-first architecture.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting:** Absence real operational effect; specialists and temps specifically return to her; human judgments described by adjusters and compliance; not all routing is automated—Alice/Bruno also assign routinely on non-exception paths.

**Observable predictions:** If assignment were split across multiple IDs or automated, Mira’s betweenness would collapse faster than exception volumes; comparing lifecycle transitions (start/complete) may show many near-instant completes.

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk — Moderate to high for exception segment only.**  
Absence evidence is the strongest calibration point: ordinary claims stable; exception p90 severely degraded. Risk is queueing at the exception desk, not global throughput. Bots and vendors remain larger waiting-time reservoirs for standard paths.

**Coordination risk — Material.**  
High subcontracting with specialists and reassignment to four adjusters means coordination quality (right specialist, right timing, complete context) depends on her routing. Low working-together with team leads raises risk of weak feedback into North/South capacity planning.

**Key-person risk — High for exception/reopen/complaint flows.**  
22% of claims but majority of difficult tails; no peer with comparable betweenness. Loss or overload redistributes work unclearly—operations manager’s warning of formal escalations and specialist overload is plausible. This is dependency risk, not a judgment of personal performance.

**Compliance/control risk — Plausible, not proven.**  
The dual role of handling problem detection signals and choosing the next specialist can weaken segregation of duties *if* selection is discretionary and unlogged. There is no direct evidence of bias, favoritism, or misconduct. Risk is structural: uncontrolled discretion *possibility*, auditability of assignment rationale, and concentration of reopen/escalate authority. Vendor and payment paths (Alice/Bruno  Vendor_Pay) appear more segregated.

**Workload fairness risk — Moderate, asymmetric.**  
Carla/Diego receive large Mira reassignment volumes; juniors also get rework/missing-info. If hard cases preferentially flow through Mira to certain adjusters, fairness and burnout risk exist—but current data show both seniors and juniors receive Mira edges. Temps have low exception share (0.13–0.14) and escalate upward—possible under-powered temps or appropriate shielding.

**Data-quality risk — Non-trivial.**  
Short routing events, possible missing “Assign” as a first-class activity elsewhere, and lifecycle granularity can inflate SNA centrality. Working-together understates brokers. Interpreting centrality as “importance rank” without filtering `exception_flag` and activity type risks false management conclusions. Resource naming collapse (one desk, multiple people sharing “Mira_Coordinator”) is not evidenced but should be checked.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately**
- Staff backup coverage for the exception desk (named deputies on North and South, or a shared rota) so absence cannot again inflate exception p90.
- Log assignment rationale at specialist/adjuster dispatch (structured reason codes: medical, legal, missing info, complaint, fraud residual)—reduces compliance ambiguity without removing the function.
- Publish simple queue SLAs for exception_flag cases waiting on the coordinator role (not on Mira as a person).

**Monitor**
- Exception waiting time into the coordinator role vs service time; p90 cycle time for exception_flag; reopen and complaint rates; specialist utilization after Mira hops; handover betweenness concentration (Mira vs deputies).
- Balance of Mira (or desk) outbound work across adjusters and specialists by case attributes.
- Vendor_Verify/Pay waiting times in parallel—do not let coordinator focus obscure larger automated/vendor delays on standard paths.

**Do not change until more evidence**
- Do **not** remove Mira_Coordinator or collapse the exception desk. The coordination function is load-bearing for reopens, complaints, and specialist loops; removal without a designed replacement predicts formal escalation spikes and specialist overload.
- Do not automatically “promote her to team lead” or merge her with Alice/Bruno—activity profiles and working-together networks differ; forced merge could blur approval authority.
- Do not hard-automate all specialist selection from current data alone; first measure how often routing follows stable rules vs residual discretion (role discovery + filtered handover + reason codes).
- Avoid incentive changes based solely on centrality scores; centrality here mixes designed brokerage with possible bottleneck effects.

Replacement principle if redesign proceeds later: keep an explicit exception-broker *role* (possibly multi-person), with rules-assisted routing, auditable assignment, and clear handback to team leads for approval/payment—preserving segregation on settlement release (already largely Alice/Bruno  Vendor_Pay).

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator functions primarily as the claims process’s exception broker: she handles a minority of all claims but most reopens and complaints, routing hard work from the rules engine to adjusters and medical/legal specialists. Network metrics look contradictory only at first glance—she is central in handovers and specialist “send-and-return” patterns because cases pass through her for assignment, yet peripheral in working-together metrics because her touches are short and she rarely stays on the case. That pattern fits a queue-and-triage desk more than a classic team lead. The main risks are key-person dependency on exception paths (confirmed by a sharp deterioration in exception cycle times during her absence), coordination quality, and limited auditability of specialist selection—not proven misconduct. Bots and vendors still dominate volume and much waiting time on standard work. Management should add backup coverage and assignment reason codes now, monitor exception queues and workload balance, and avoid removing or absorbing her role until a deliberate, multi-person exception function is designed to replace it.
