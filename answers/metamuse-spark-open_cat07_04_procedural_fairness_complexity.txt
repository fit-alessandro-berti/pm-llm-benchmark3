# 4. Plausible mechanisms
At least four, mixing legitimate and concerning. Each could co-exist; logs alone cannot identify which dominates.

**1. Legitimate risk-based routing (legitimate but amplifiable):**
- Higher observed high-risk share D 51% vs A 18% legitimately triggers Risk ScreeningFraud ReviewSupervisor Review per policy. This mechanically increases cycle and scrutiny for D, explaining most of V3 concentration. If risk_scores were unbiased predictors of error/fraud, this would be justified differentiation. Concern: risk_score may embed prior denied cases/prior-case-count feedback loop.

**2. Rights-preserving support creates capacity-induced bottleneck (concerning structural):**
- Language Support and Accommodation Review are required rights, but staffed at 7 bilingual and 5 accommodation specialists vs demand (B 1250 cases, 57% need language support; C 820 cases, 62% need accommodation). No service-level targets for these steps mean they are deprioritized when workloads rise. Queues (3.8d, 4.7d) plus handover loops (generalspecialistgeneral/supervisor) multiply waits and rework. Applicants exercising rights therefore experience slower, more fragmented process even when documentation and risk are equal.

**3. Language barrier inflates and biases documentation_completeness measure (measurement-bias mechanism):**
- Incomplete documentation 46% B vs 21% A may reflect forms not understood, missing translated materials, or caseworker coding of foreign-language documents as incomplete, not lower applicant diligence. Bilingual staff delay in CompletenessRequest Docs (1.6d vs 0.4d) suggests process is not equipped to assess B documentation promptly. This creates circularity: language need  coded as incomplete  triggers V2 loop  extra wait  higher observed cycle and rework  reinforced perception of complexity.

**4. Regional overload and handover design (concerning allocation):**
- Two regions with backlog disproportionately handle B, causing Supervisor ReviewDecision wait 1.9d B vs 1.0d A and extra handovers generalbilingualgeneral. Similarly, C handovers generalaccommodation specialistsupervisor with 2 staff holding dual roles concentrate bottlenecks and reduce independence of review. This is not a case-mix effect but an assignment policy: clustering minority/accommodation cases onto few specialized resources without balancing load produces inequity in timeliness and consistency.

**5. Differential scrutiny threshold / supervisor discretion (potentially concerning):**
- Adjusted +8.0pp B and +5.5pp C for Supervisor Review after controls suggests supervisors may be pulled in for language/accommodation cases out of caution, uncertainty, or bias, not documented risk. This adds 0.6-0.9d extra wait and may lower approval (-5.5pp B) via more conservative decisions. Could also reflect legitimate quality assurance if prior errors were higher, but without documented rule it is discretionary.

**6. Historical feedback loop in risk scoring (concerning data-generating mechanism):**
- risk_score_band may be trained/predicted on prior approvals, prior-case-count (D 49% high vs 12% A), and documentation history. If prior decisions were themselves disparate, risk scores reproduce disparity: D repeatedly flagged high-risk  more Fraud Review  higher denial/appeal  higher future prior-case-count  higher risk score. Logs at daily granularity cannot observe this loop, but D's large raw approval gap (58% vs 78%) shrinking to -3.1pp after adjustment suggests much is mediated through risk_score - which should not be treated as neutral ground truth.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes
Available attributes are insufficient to determine fairness conclusively; they affect both adjustment and interpretation.

**Measurement bias in key covariates:**
- **risk_score_band:** Likely composite of prior decisions, prior-case-count, channel, region. If historical process was biased, risk_score encodes that bias. Treating it as control "explains away" discrimination as legitimate routing. Cannot audit without knowing score inputs, model training data, validation by group, and whether it predicts true eligibility or past approval.
- **documentation_completeness:** Coded by caseworker after interaction. Language barriers, lack of translated forms, or caseworker unfamiliarity may cause valid foreign documents to be marked incomplete. Therefore B's 46% incomplete vs A 21% may measure process accessibility, not applicant effort. Controlling for it may mask procedural failure.
- **channel:** In-person 38% B vs 9% A could reflect lack of translated online portal, not preference. Controlling for channel without understanding choice confounds access.

**Missing applicant needs and process quality:**
- **Unobserved needs:** literacy, digital access, interpreter quality, disability severity/type, caregiving constraints, transportation. Two B applicants with same language_preference may have very different support needs; logs collapse this to binary flag.
- **Quality of explanation/dignity:** case_outcome does not capture whether decision was well-explained, whether accommodation was appropriate, interpreter present, time pressure, or respectful treatment. Longer cycle may understate burden if interactions were low-quality.
- **Correctness of outcome:** No ground truth for eligibility. Cannot distinguish accurate denial from erroneous denial. Approval parity is not accuracy parity.
- **Missing case complexity:** medical complexity, household composition nuances, income volatility not captured in household_size_band alone. Adjusted model may under-control for legitimate complexity, over-attributing gap to disparity - or over-control via biased proxies.

**Unobserved resource workload and organizational factors:**
- Resource workload observed only daily, not hourly. Intraday queue dynamics, staff breaks, concurrent tasks, supervisor availability, interpreter scheduling not visible. Cannot distinguish understaffing from inefficient scheduling.
- org:resource shows who acted, not who was available, skill level, training, or caseload complexity. Discover_organizational_roles may infer clusters but not experience.
- Handover *reason* not logged: is handover due to specialization need, load balancing, or escalation? Handover network shows frequency but not appropriateness.
- Regional backlog qualitative note not quantified: no staffing ratios, vacancy data, or arrival rates per region.

**Appeal and rework limitations:**
- appeal_flag and case_outcome capture filing, not merit, grounds, success rate, or time to decision quality. Higher appeal rate for B/D (13%/16% vs 6%) could reflect lower initial quality, greater perseverance due to need, or differential access to appeal assistance - logs cannot separate.
- Reopen/rework rate undefined in terms of who initiates (applicant vs system) and why (error correction vs new evidence).
- No data on Appeal Review independence: are same resources reviewing initial decision? Resource overlap hidden.

**Historical and selection biases:**
- Selection into log: only submitted applications observed. Those deterred by language/access barriers never appear, understating access inequity.
- Time truncation: appeal and reactivation beyond log window not captured; 90th percentile differences may be underestimated.
- Prior-case-count as control may be collider: affected by past fairness. Controlling for it blocks causal path from past disparity to current outcome.

Consequence: adjusted gaps (-5.5pp, +2.6d etc.) are sensitive to which covariates are included and cannot be interpreted as unbiased causal effects. Procedural fairness assessment must treat them as screening signals requiring qualitative audit, not definitive evidence of discrimination or its absence.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects
All mitigations must preserve support steps; removing language/accommodation review to improve cycle time would violate rights and is prohibited.

**1. Add capacity for language support and accommodation review**
- *Action:* Increase bilingual caseworkers beyond 7 and accommodation specialists beyond 5; create dedicated pools per region; decouple accommodation specialist from supervisor role; hire interpreters/on-demand video; cross-train generalists.
- *Side effects/trade-offs:* Higher labor cost; hiring lag; if demand forecasting inaccurate, idle capacity; risk of segregating B/C cases into separate queues that ghettoize expertise rather than mainstream accessibility. Mitigate by hybrid: specialists as consultants, not sole owners, to reduce handovers.

**2. Service-level targets (SLAs) for support steps**
- *Action:* Define separate timeliness thresholds for Language Support ContactEligibility and Accommodation ReviewEligibility (e.g., 1d, not 3.8/4.7d), include in dashboard, alert on breach; set max handover count.
- *Side effects:* Gaming: staff may rushed-close support contacts to meet SLA, reducing quality; may prioritize SLA cases over other urgent high-risk cases, shifting wait elsewhere; requires accurate timestamping (language arrival vs contact). Needs quality counter-metric: post-contact completeness and appeal rates.

**3. Routing redesign to reduce handovers**
- *Action:* Assign B cases end-to-end to bilingual caseworker where possible, instead of generalbilingualgeneral loops; assign C to team with accommodation expertise embedded, not sequential specialist then separate supervisor; enable single-point contact; automate documentation translation triage.
- *Side effects:* Specialized caseloads may become overloaded again if not load-balanced; loss of generalist oversight; may increase service time per worker (broader task) unless workflows streamlined. Requires workload balancing algorithm and monitoring via handover networks.

**4. Decision-explanation improvements**
- *Action:* Standardized, translated decision letters; checklist of evidence considered; plain-language denial reasons; offer pre-decision clarification call for incomplete cases; record explanation quality flag.
- *Side effects:* Increases Decision Draft time modestly; translation quality must be audited or creates new errors; may increase appeals initially as applicants better understand grounds - not necessarily negative but must be resourced. Requires training.

**5. Monitoring adjusted disparities (procedural fairness dashboard)**
- *Action:* Monthly reporting of stratified/matched gaps: approval, P(Supervisor), P(Request Docs), median/90th waits, handover counts, appeal/rework, by region/channel, with confidence intervals; decompose raw gaps into case-mix-explained vs residual.
- *Side effects:* Metric fixation; if risk_score remains in adjustment, dashboard may understate bias - must show multiple specifications (with/without risk_score). Risk of privacy re-identification in small region/group cells; requires aggregation rules. Needs governance to act on signals.

**6. Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness for bias**
- *Action:* Audit risk model inputs, weights, training labels, performance (AUC, calibration) by group; test for disparate impact at same true eligibility proxy; review documentation completeness coding rubric inter-rater reliability by language; evaluate translation availability of forms.
- *Side effects:* Audit may find score needs retraining, causing temporary routing uncertainty and increased human review load; revising documentation criteria may increase approval rates but require fraud safeguards - must balance accuracy vs burden, monitor via post-audit error rates. Political sensitivity if audit reveals historical bias.

**Additional complementary mitigations:**
- Regional load leveling: redistribute cases from two backlog regions, add supervisor capacity there. Trade-off: longer travel/remote handling may reduce local knowledge.
- Appeal quality loop: audit Appeal Review independence and success by initial resource; provide appeal navigation support. Trade-off: more appeals supported increases workload.
- Data granularity improvement: capture handover reason, interpreter presence, wait reason codes. Trade-off: additional logging burden.

Each mitigation should be piloted in one region with A/B staggered rollout, measuring both timeliness and quality (appeal sustain rate, rework) to detect shifting of burden rather than reduction.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment
Raw gaps are large but not interpretable alone: much reflects legitimate case-mix (higher risk, incomplete documentation, language/accommodation needs) and policy-required routing. Adjusted gaps narrow substantially (e.g., D approval gap from -20pp raw to -3.1pp adjusted; D cycle from +4.0d to +0.9d), indicating case-mix explains a major portion.

Nevertheless, evidence suggests **procedurally unfair burden for Groups B and C on timeliness and procedural load that persists after controlling for observed complexity**, and residual outcome/ appeal disparities for B and D that cannot be explained by documented complexity alone:

- **Group B:** Moderate concern. +2.6d adjusted cycle, +8pp Supervisor Review, +9.5pp Request Missing Documents, 4x wait for document request, 3.8d bilingual queue, concentrated resources, amplified in two regions. Part of extra steps is expected due to language need, but waiting-time penalty for exercising language rights and elevated supervisor scrutiny are not justified by documented risk.
- **Group C:** Concern for timeliness / resource fairness. Largest adjusted cycle gap +4.1d driven largely by 4.7d Accommodation Review queue and 31% V5 concentration on 5 specialists. Approval gap is smallest (-2.2pp), indicating process is relatively accurate but imposes longest wait as cost of accommodation.
- **Group D:** Indeterminate/mixed. High Fraud Review concentration appears consistent with higher measured risk, with minimal extra waiting, but adjusted approval -3.1pp and appeal +5.7pp plus 13% V6 appeal path remain. If risk_score is itself biased, legitimate routing explanation is circular. Cannot determine fairness without auditing risk_score.
- **Group A:** Reference group experiences most favorable resourcing (52 caseworkers, fewest handovers, straight-through V1 42% vs 18-22% others).

Overall, the process does not show equal raw outcomes - nor should that be expected - but it **does show uneven procedural burden tied to the use of rights-preserving steps and regional resource allocation**, which is a procedural fairness failure even when final approval is eventually granted. The evidence **does not prove intentional discrimination**; it is consistent with structural understaffing, handover design, measurement bias in risk/completeness, and backlog effects generating disparate impact.

Conclusion is **indeterminate for overall discrimination but indicates probable procedural unfairness in timeliness, burden, and support accessibility for B and C, and unresolved risk-score dependence for D**, warranting capacity, routing, SLA, and audit mitigations with monitoring of adjusted disparities rather than raw rates. Stronger conclusions would require independent eligibility ground truth, risk-score audit, and qualitative review of decision quality.
