## 1. What is observed

Automation availability (`automation=1`) begins in Alpha and Beta in April 2026 and in Gamma in July 2026; Delta and Epsilon never receive it. Availability is not treatment intensity: `automated_fast_path_pct` is only 4–6% before rollout and rises to 25–50% in adopters, so the binary flag is an intent-to-treat-like exposure, not case-level automation. Rollout was endogenous: Alpha and Beta were selected for high volume and historically poor cycle times, Gamma after a pilot, so adopters are not exchangeable with controls. Contemporaneous changes include a March centralized fraud desk, an April routing campaign adding about 10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta and about 5% to Gamma after July, staffing changes (Alpha 2 FTE in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma 1 in June, Delta +1 in June), and a May product change raising high-complexity share. Outcomes are branch-month aggregates: median and p90 cycle hours, rework, escalation, and CSAT. CSAT response rates vary 20–45% with no response counts, so survey composition may change. Branch-month medians/p90 are not case-level event logs and cannot show queue states, path variants, or within-month dynamics. These features mean observed changes mix automation, demand, staffing, routing, case mix, and measurement.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: staggered-adoption event-study / generalized DiD with never-treated and not-yet-treated controls | Relative-time indicators around adoption; calendar-month fixed effects; cohorts Apr-2026 (Alpha/Beta), Jul-2026 (Gamma), never-treated (Delta/Epsilon) | Delta/Epsilon never-treated; not-yet-treated periods of Gamma for Alpha/Beta; Delta/Epsilon for Gamma | `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`; branch FE; month FE; optionally branch-specific pre-trends | Dynamic ITT effect of availability (E1, E3, E4, E5, E6) | Conditional parallel trends; no anticipation; no spillovers; stable treatment version; no unmeasured time-varying confounders; no severe treatment-effect heterogeneity that biases two-way FE | Event-study pre-trend coefficients; placebo adoption dates; leave-one-branch-out; wild cluster bootstrap; Sun-Abraham/Callaway-Sant'Anna sensitivity | Only 5 branches / 3 treated cohorts; small-cluster inference; time-varying confounding from fraud desk, routing, product change, staffing |
| Alternative 1: synthetic control / synthetic DiD per adopter | Calendar time with adoption date | Weighted combination of Delta/Epsilon and pre-adoption periods | Pre-period outcomes, `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | Branch-specific ITT effect | Donor pool approximates counterfactual; no spillovers; stable weights | Placebo permutations; pre-period MSPE; weight inspection | Only two never-treated donors; limited covariates; cannot capture fraud-desk spillovers |
| Alternative 2: interrupted time series with control series | Segmented regression at adoption month | Each branch’s own pre-period plus Delta/Epsilon control series | Autocorrelation, seasonality, `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | Immediate level change and slope change | No other abrupt changes at adoption; no autocorrelation; stable case mix | Residual autocorrelation; placebo adoption dates; control-series comparison | Multiple events at same time (fraud desk March, routing April, product May); short series |

The staggered event-study design is preferable because it uses all available pre/post periods and both never-treated and not-yet-treated controls, rather than collapsing to a single pre/post contrast. It can represent adoption timing explicitly, estimate dynamic effects, and reveal whether effects appear immediately or after ramp-up. Calendar-month fixed effects absorb common shocks such as the May product change, while branch fixed effects absorb time-invariant branch differences. Adjusting for arrivals, FTE, and high-complexity share addresses some observed time-varying confounders. The design also makes pre-trend violations visible through relative-time coefficients. However, with only five branches and three treated cohorts, cluster-robust inference is fragile; I would use wild cluster bootstrap or randomization inference and pre-specify sensitivity analyses. The main threat is not the estimator but the absence of data on the fraud desk, routing campaign, and local practice changes. Therefore the primary strategy should be paired with process-level data collection and a staggered rollout that preserves a not-yet-treated control group.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Alpha/Beta have only three pre-adoption months. Their pre-period median/p90 are much higher than controls (47.4 vs 37.4 median; 95.7 vs 77.2 p90), consistent with selection for poor historical performance. Any improvement could partly be regression to the mean. Gamma has six pre months and shows a rising pre-trend (median 36.445.9; p90 75.4101.0), but controls also rose over the same months, so the simple Gamma pre/post improvement is partly a common May–June spike and July–August partial reversal. The May product change increased high-complexity share in all branches, and the routing campaign increased arrivals to Alpha/Beta from April. These patterns mean simple pre/post changes overstate any automation effect; the DiD comparisons reduce but do not eliminate this concern.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Do not make an immediate unconditional expansion to both Delta and Epsilon, and do not pause entirely, because there is suggestive operational benefit but material uncertainty about tail performance, escalation, and causal attribution.

Next subset: randomize or matched-pair stagger Delta and Epsilon. I would start Delta first (2026-09-01) and hold Epsilon as a not-yet-treated control until 2026-11-01, or randomize the order if operational constraints allow. Delta has lower high-complexity share and more stable baseline, making it a cleaner initial test; Epsilon’s higher complexity and worse tail make it a useful later test of generalizability. This preserves a control group and creates a second staggered cohort.

Minimum measurements: at least 6 months of pre-period case-level event logs before each branch’s start (ideally 12) and at least 6 months post. Collect case-level timestamps for Register, Automated Triage, Manual Triage, Specialist Review, Rework loops, Resolve, Close; queue lengths and waiting times for specialist review and the centralized fraud desk; automated fast-path completion, escalation, rework, cycle-time distributions (median, p90, p95), CSAT response counts and respondent characteristics, arrivals by channel, FTE by function, high-complexity share, and routing/fraud-desk referral logs.

Spillover monitoring: track fraud-desk wait times, cross-branch referrals, routing campaign volumes, and any changes in Delta/Epsilon demand while they serve as controls. Guardrails: pause or roll back a branch if p90 cycle time worsens by more than 5% relative to control, escalation rises by more than 2 percentage points without fraud-desk capacity relief, rework rises by more than 1 percentage point, CSAT declines by more than 0.1 points, or fraud-desk waiting times exceed a pre-specified threshold.

Decision rule: scale up beyond the pilot if, under a pre-registered staggered DiD/event-study analysis with wild-cluster inference, the median cycle time improves by at least 5% relative to the not-yet-treated control, p90 does not worsen by more than 5%, rework does not increase, and escalation increases are explained by fraud-desk capacity. If median improves but p90 or escalation harms emerge, expand only with queue-management changes and a second evaluation cycle.

    vals = np.asarray(vals, dtype=float)

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation availability and actual automated-fast-path use rose sharply in Alpha, Beta, and Gamma, while Delta and Epsilon remained untreated; branch-month outcomes changed in mixed directions.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to not-yet-treated branches, adopters experienced lower median cycle time and lower rework, but higher escalation; tail cycle time improved relative to controls but worsened in absolute terms for Alpha and Beta.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends, no spillovers, and no time-varying confounding hold, automation availability is consistent with a median cycle-time reduction of roughly 5–10% and a rework reduction of about 2 percentage points, but this is not established.
- `Not supported:` A single statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” for all branches, including tail performance and customer satisfaction, is not defensible.
