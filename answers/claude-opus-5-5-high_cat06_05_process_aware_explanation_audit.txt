## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Working definitions used in the table:
- **Unavailable at t0 (leakage)**: the value depends on events, fields, or snapshots that are created after the first `Coverage Check Completed`.
- **Process-inconsistent**: the item refers to an ordering the normative model forbids, or it is an artefact of broken ordering. This is a separate problem from timing.
- **Repairable lineage defect**: the concept can be known at t0, but the current computation lets post-t0 information in.
- **Proxy / drift**: the value is available at t0 but stands in for something else, such as a policy regime, a holiday, or socio-economic or channel differences.
- **Legitimate prefix / inter-case signal**: the value is derived only from the prefix, or from the state of the system at or before t0.

Structural observation: at t0 the control-flow prefix is `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed` for every case. Any control-flow feature that varies across cases (variants, document-cycle counts, fraud, decision, or appeal patterns) must therefore have been computed from post-t0 events. At this checkpoint, legitimate process signals can only come from timing, resources, attributes, and inter-case state such as queues and workload. They cannot come from the path.

Severity scale: **critical** means future information that nearly determines the label or encodes the post-t0 path. **High** means partial or unquantified leakage, or an unusable lineage. **Medium** means available, but a proxy, drift, or interpretation problem. **Low** means valid, needing only routine checks.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The suite runs in CI on the feature pipeline and on every explanation artefact, such as global rankings, local attributions, and sequence patterns. It uses the normative model in two encodings:
- a **Petri net or BPMN model**, for replay and alignment;
- a set of **declarative constraints** (Declare / LTL on finite traces), for rules that are easier to state locally.

It uses two copies of the log:
- **L_full**: complete traces;
- **L_t0**: every trace cut at the first `Coverage Check Completed`, using the reliable sequence index.

### Check A: Prefix-time availability
- **A1. Metadata contract.** Every feature has `event_time`, `knowledge_time` (when the value became queryable in the source system), and `checkpoint_scope`. Assert `knowledge_time <= t0` for every value used in training and serving. Fields without a knowledge timestamp are rejected by default. Today that covers `final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, and the reserve if it has no history.
- **A2. Truncation invariance.** Recompute every feature from `L_t0` plus point-in-time snapshots and compare with the model input. Any mismatch means leakage. This catches `num_document_requests_total`, the variant ID, and `appeal_seen_anywhere` automatically.
- **A3. Suffix perturbation.** Randomly delete, shuffle, or replace post-t0 events and post-t0 warehouse updates, then recompute features and predictions. The result must be bit-identical. Any change proves dependence on the future. This catches the nightly 23:00 document snapshot and the `<= extraction_date` claim count, which A2 misses if snapshots are reused.
- **A4. Point-in-time join audit.** Parse the SQL or dataflow for `extraction_date`, `current_`, `final_`, `last_`, `total_`, `_anywhere`, `closed_at`, and non-strict comparisons (`<=` against t0 where `<` is required). Require backward as-of joins for snapshots such as the queue.
- **A5. Statistical canaries.** Compare feature distributions between historically open cases (as they looked at a past snapshot) and closed cases. Complete-trace features differ systematically or are null for open cases. Also plot feature importance against checkpoint: a t0 feature whose importance matches an end-of-case model is suspect.

### Check B: Activity-order feasibility
- **B1. Declarative constraints.** Encode the normative rules:
  - `init(Claim Registered)`;
  - `precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Documents Requested)` and `precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`;
  - chain rules for the evidence cycle (`Documents Received` preceded by `Documents Requested`; `Documents Validated` preceded by `Documents Received`);
  - `precedence(Settlement Decision[Partial or Deny], Appeal)`;
  - `precedence(Damage Assessment Completed, Settlement Decision Recorded)`, except for withdrawal, as the model allows;
  - `Claim Closed` is the last event (no event may follow it);
  - `Claim Closed` occurs at most once.
- **B2. Checking prefixes.** Use 3-valued finite-trace semantics on prefixes: satisfied, violated, or pending. On L_full, compute the violation rate per constraint. On explanation patterns, check each `A -> B` pattern against the constraints and the normative footprint. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` violates "nothing after Claim Closed", so it is flagged as process-impossible and blocked from any explanation.
- **B3. Alignment-based conformance.** Compute optimal alignments on L_full and prefix alignments on L_t0. Online prefix alignment should report zero cost for `REG -> COV`. Log-move clusters (for example, a DAC after closure) point to logging defects rather than behaviour.
- **B4. Role and limits of footprints and DFGs.** A footprint or directly-follows graph (DFG) mined from the log describes what was recorded, not what is allowed. A frequent edge such as `Claim Closed -> DAC` produced by bulk logging is not a rule, and a rare edge is not automatically a violation. The fraud branch runs in parallel, so directly-follows edges between fraud events and evidence or assessment events are interleaving artefacts; patterns spanning concurrent branches should be expressed as eventually-follows or as concurrency relations. Alignments depend on the cost function and on the simplified model: a real "reopen" would be flagged as a deviation until it is modelled. Declarative checks do not capture resource or data semantics unless extended with data-aware constraints.

### Check C: Same-timestamp ambiguity
- **C1. Detect ties.** Find groups of events with the same case and timestamp. Report the tie rate per activity pair and source system, and the timestamp granularity (second, minute, or date-only).
- **C2. Order sensitivity.** For each tie group, enumerate the permutations (or the feasible subset) and recompute every order-dependent feature or pattern. If the value changes across permutations, mark it order-sensitive. Such values are either excluded or carried with their permutation range.
- **C3. Resolution hierarchy.**
  1. A reliable source sequence index.
  2. Lifecycle or semantic constraints (closure is last, a decision follows assessment), choosing the permutation with minimum alignment cost and recording the choice with a `resolved_by_model` flag.
  3. Otherwise leave it unresolved, and exclude order-dependent derived features.
- **C4. Ties at the t0 boundary.** Events that share the timestamp of the first `Coverage Check Completed` may lie before or after t0. Without a sequence index they are treated as after t0 (conservative exclusion), and a sensitivity check reports how often this changes features.

Case Z's `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed` tie has a missing sequence index, so it fails C2. Both permutations must be considered, and one of them is process-infeasible.

### Check D: Complete-trace dependence
- **D1. Lineage tags.** Each feature is tagged at registration in the feature store as `prefix(checkpoint)`, `point_in_time_snapshot`, `static_at_registration`, `inter_case_as_of`, or `complete_trace`. Only `complete_trace` features are banned from checkpoint models. Tags are verified by checks A2 and A3, not self-declared.
- **D2. Name-versus-logic tests.** Unit tests confirm that `_before_t0` and `_at_t0` fields really use a strict t0 bound. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` currently fails this test.
- **D3. Ex-post snapshot fields.** Warehouse fields populated at closure (`final_*`, `last_*`) are detected by comparing their update timestamps with the case closure time.

### Check E: Activity status of explanation references at t0
- **E1. Classify each referenced activity.** Replay the t0 prefix on the Petri net to obtain the marking. Then classify every activity referenced in a global or local explanation:
  - **Observed**: present in the prefix (Claim Registered, Coverage Check Completed).
  - **Enabled**: can occur next (Documents Requested, Damage Assessment Completed on the fast track, Fraud Investigation Opened if the trigger exists, possibly a withdrawal decision).
  - **Future-possible, not enabled**: reachable only through intermediate activities (Documents Received, Documents Validated, Settlement Decision Recorded, Appeal, which also requires a Partial or Deny decision first, and Claim Closed).
  - **Impossible**: unreachable or order-infeasible (anything after Claim Closed; an Appeal before any decision; a second decision without an intervening Appeal).
- **E2. Allowed wording.**
  - An explanation may assert as fact only the values of observed activities and of attributes known at t0.
  - Enabled or future-possible activities may appear only as the predicted target of a separate forecasting component ("likely to need evidence cycles"), never as observed input.
  - Any reference to an impossible activity invalidates the explanation and triggers a data-quality ticket.
- **E3. Automation.** Automatic reclassification of the sequence patterns from Section 1:
  - `Appeal -> SDR`: future-possible, not enabled, so inadmissible at t0 but valid behaviour.
  - `COV -> Fraud Investigation Opened`: enabled but not observed, so inadmissible as input.
  - `Claim Closed -> DAC`: impossible, so invalid.
  - `DocReq -> DocRec` and `DAC -> SDR`: future-possible, so inadmissible at t0.

### Check F: Legitimacy of repeated Settlement Decision events
- **F1. Label refinement.** Refine `Settlement Decision Recorded` into instances: SDR#1 (initial) and SDR#k (reconsideration).
- **F2. Assertions on every SDR#k with k ≥ 2:**
  - an Appeal lies between SDR#(k−1) and SDR#k;
  - SDR#(k−1) has decision ∈ {Partial, Deny};
  - no Appeal follows Approve or Withdrawn;
  - `Claim Closed` follows the last SDR;
  - an Appeal is not followed by closure without either a new SDR or an explicit appeal outcome.
- **F3. Classification.** Repeated decisions that fail these checks are classified as duplicate logging, system correction, or an unmodelled reopen. They are excluded from features and flagged.
- **F4. Consequences for features and explanations.** Any feature referring to "the decision" must name the instance. `final_decision` is ambiguous as well as leaked. This check is also what separates the legitimate `Appeal -> SDR` pattern from the illegitimate `Claim Closed -> DAC` pattern: the first is valid behaviour that happens to be future information, and the second is invalid ordering.

### Check G: Release gate
An explanation artefact is released only if it passes all of the following:
- A1 through A4 and D1 through D3 for every referenced feature;
- E1 and E2 for every referenced activity;
- C2 order-insensitivity, or a declared uncertainty range;
- B1 constraint compliance for every sequence pattern.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Four standards that should not be conflated
- **Predictive validity.** The feature is available at t0 with verified lineage. It improves out-of-time discrimination and calibration, is stable across regimes, and does not leak. This is the only standard needed to use a feature for scoring.
- **Causal interpretation.** A claim such as "changing X would change breach risk" requires an identification strategy: randomised or quasi-experimental variation, confounder adjustment with a defended causal graph, or natural experiments such as policy changes and staffing shocks. Shapley values measure the model's reliance on a feature, not its effect in the world, especially with correlated features.
- **Operational actionability.** The organisation can change the factor at or after t0, and changing it plausibly helps. Examples are routing, staffing, requesting documents early, and assigning a specialist. Many valid predictors, such as complexity, elapsed time, and region, are not levers. They justify triage, not intervention on the feature itself.
- **Fairness review.** Features available at t0 that proxy protected or socio-economic characteristics require analysis of disparate impact and group calibration. They require a business-necessity case and a search for less discriminatory mechanism variables. The review must also consider how predictions are used. Extra help for high-risk claims is very different from extra scrutiny or delay.

### Class 1: Defensible predictive signals
- `initial_damage_complexity`
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`
- `initial_reserve`, if it is verified as the value at t0
- `claimant_channel`, if captured at registration (it also sits in Class 3)
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, after the SQL repair (also Class 3)
- `fraud_trigger_at_t0`, if it exists with a timestamp
- documents received at or before t0

**May claim:** "Given information available at t0, claims with these characteristics have historically breached more (or less) often, and the model relies on this." They may be used to prioritise attention.

**May not claim:** that the attribute causes delay, or that changing it (for example, lowering a reserve) would reduce risk. For elapsed time, the relationship is nearly mechanical (less remaining budget), which may be stated as such.

### Class 2: Process-state or congestion signals
- `queue_length_at_t0` (backward as-of)
- workload and backlog of the team assigned at t0 (after lineage repair)
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (the slack in the 10-day budget)
- business days remaining in the window
- fast-track eligibility implied by initial complexity
- inter-case counts such as open fraud investigations in the team at t0

**May claim:** "At t0 the claim is entering a congested queue or team, and historically such claims more often miss the target." This is plausibly mechanistic through waiting time, and it is actionable at the system level, since capacity, re-routing, and prioritisation are levers.

**May not claim:** a quantified causal effect of reducing the queue without intervention evidence. Queue length is confounded by calendar, policy regime, and case mix. The claim should also not be read as blaming the claimant or the handler.

### Class 3: Proxies requiring fairness/robustness review
- `claimant_region`
- `claimant_channel`
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (repaired)
- possibly `initial_reserve` (it correlates with asset value and wealth)
- `fraud_trigger_at_t0`

**May claim:** that the feature is statistically associated with breach risk in the historical data, pending review, with the likely mechanism named as a hypothesis (for example, "region may stand in for repair-network coverage or language-support availability").

**May not claim:** "Region R4 is a delay driver" or "broker claims are problematic." It must not be used in ways that disadvantage claimants, such as deprioritising or adding scrutiny, without review.

**Remediation path:** replace the proxy with mechanism variables (network density, language-support need), then test whether the residual effect vanishes. Check group-wise calibration and error rates, and keep the feature only if it adds validity and the usage is benign. Deleting a proxy blindly can worsen calibration for the very groups it affects, and it does not remove the correlated information from the other features.

### Class 4: Drift/calendar proxies
- `calendar_week` and the hidden policy regime
- employee experience, to the extent it tracks tenure cohorts over time

**May claim:** that holidays reduce available working days inside the 10-day window (mechanistic), and that the regime after the policy change is associated with different risk, as an observed regime difference.

**May not claim:** "Week 52 is operationally worse" as a general law, or that a particular week number carries meaning next year.

**Required:** re-encode as a holiday business-day count and a `policy_regime` indicator, and validate across years and regimes with time-based splits.

### Class 5: Unusable until lineage is repaired
| Current field | Repaired feature |
|---|---|
| `final_handler_team` | `assigned_team_at_t0` |
| `risk_score_current` | risk score as of t0, from score history |
| `same_day_document_count_snapshot` | documents received at or before t0 (event-level timestamps) |
| `prior_claim_count_before_t0` | strict `< t0` bound, excluding the current claim |
| employee experience | event-date as-of join |
| `initial_reserve` | reserve history as of t0 |
| `claimant_channel` | capture-time verification |
| `last_resource_role` | role at t0 |
| `queue_length_at_t0` | backward-only join (minor fix) |

**May claim:** nothing. These fields may not appear in explanations until the repaired version passes Check A.

### Permanently inadmissible at t0 (valid at later checkpoints or retrospectively)
- `total_case_duration_hours`
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`
- `final_decision`
- `num_document_requests_total`
- `full_trace_variant_id`
- `last_resource_role`
- all five sequence patterns

The patterns that follow normative behaviour (Appeal -> SDR, DocReq -> DocRec, DAC -> SDR, COV -> Fraud) become legitimate prefix features in checkpoint models where they are observed. `Claim Closed -> DAC` is a data-quality defect at every checkpoint.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Priorities run from immediate containment (Priority 0) to ongoing monitoring (Priority 8).

**Priority 0. Contain now**
1. Withdraw the current global ranking and local explanations from operational use. Label them "trained with future information; not valid at t0".
2. Communicate that the reported model performance is inflated. `total_case_duration_hours` alone nearly encodes the label.
3. Freeze any process decisions based on the team's causal interpretations, such as "appeals cause breaches" or "the final team determines delay".

**Priority 1. Feature-store design and historical feature timestamps**
1. **Bitemporal storage.** Store every feature with event time and knowledge (ingestion or availability) time. Mutable business fields become SCD type 2 or append-only histories: team assignment, risk score, reserve, channel, employee experience. Include valid-from and valid-to plus the version of the computing model.
2. **Point-in-time joins.** Serve features only through backward as-of joins at the checkpoint time. Forbid forward tolerance windows (the ±5-minute queue join becomes backward-only with a staleness bound).
3. **Feature contracts.** Each feature declares a checkpoint scope (for example, `t0_prefix`, `at_first_decision`, or `retrospective_only`). Registration fails without a lineage tag and knowledge timestamp. Closure-populated warehouse fields are `retrospective_only` by construction.
4. **Replace snapshots with events.** Replace nightly snapshots (the 23:00 document snapshot) with event-level timestamps, such as document uploads and queue entry and exit.
5. **Logging fixes.** Persist a reliable sequence index at the source, and log reopen as an explicit activity.

**Priority 2. Prefix-only recomputation**
1. **Rebuild the training set.** Generate the training set by truncating each case at the first `Coverage Check Completed` using the sequence index. Apply the same feature code in training and serving to avoid training–serving skew.
2. **Fix defective SQL.** Correct `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (strict `< t0`, exclude the current claim).
3. **Handle the label and censoring.** Define the breach label consistently (calendar versus business days). Cases still open at extraction with less than 10 days elapsed are censored, not negatives. Exclude them or use survival or discrete-time hazard formulations, so that recent cases do not bias the model.
4. **Add later checkpoints.** Build explicit models at later checkpoints (after the first evidence request, after damage assessment, after the first decision). In those models, features such as document-cycle counts, fraud opening, and appeal status become legitimate prefix features. This keeps their predictive value where it belongs.
5. **Backtest out of time.** Use training windows strictly before test windows and cross the policy-change boundary.

**Priority 3. Process-conformance tests in CI**
1. Implement the Section 2 suite: truncation invariance, suffix perturbation, SQL lineage scan, declarative constraints with 3-valued prefix semantics, prefix alignment, tie-order sensitivity, activity-status classification, and SDR repeat legitimacy.
2. Make the explanation release gate mandatory. No explanation may reference a feature failing lineage tests, an activity that is not observed at t0, or a process-impossible pattern.
3. Maintain the normative model as a versioned artefact. Review repeated log-move clusters to decide between a data defect and an unmodelled behaviour.

**Priority 4. Calibration after feature removal**
1. Expect discrimination to fall substantially once leakage is removed. Report AUC or PR-AUC, Brier score, and log loss on out-of-time data, alongside the old numbers, so stakeholders understand the true signal level at t0.
2. Recalibrate using isotonic or Platt scaling on a held-out recent period. Check reliability curves overall, per policy regime, and per segment (region, channel, complexity).
3. Re-derive alert thresholds from capacity and cost trade-offs, for example the number of claims the team can proactively handle per day.
4. Report prediction intervals or uncertainty, especially near holidays and regime changes.

**Priority 5. Explanation stability**
1. **Correlated features.** Choose and document the attribution method. Interventional and conditional Shapley values differ when features are correlated, as with region, channel, network, and queue with calendar. Use grouped attributions for correlated clusters.
2. **Bootstrap stability.** Retrain on bootstrap samples and time windows. Report rank stability of global importances (for example, Kendall's tau) and the variance of local attributions. Do not present unstable features as drivers.
3. **Sanity checks.** Run model-parameter randomisation and label-shuffle tests; attributions must degrade. Also check local consistency, where similar prefixes should receive similar explanations.
4. **Process-feasible counterfactuals.** Counterfactual explanations may change only attributes that are available and actionable at t0 (routing, early document request), and must respect process constraints.
5. **Standard explanation template.** Every explanation states its prediction time, lists admissible drivers by class (Section 3), and includes an explicit causal-claim boundary.

**Priority 6. Proxy and fairness analysis**
1. For region, channel, prior-claim count, reserve, language, and the fraud trigger: compute group-wise calibration, false-positive and false-negative rates, and alert rates.
2. Run mechanism-substitution tests: replace region with repair-network coverage and language-support variables, and check whether predictive validity is preserved while the group disparity shrinks.
3. Document how predictions are used. If a flag triggers extra support, that is lower risk; if it triggers scrutiny, delay, or fraud suspicion, that is higher risk and needs stricter review.
4. Record business-necessity justifications and less discriminatory alternatives. Involve legal and compliance where protected-attribute proxies are plausible.
5. Do not delete proxies blindly. Evaluate whether removal harms calibration for affected groups, and whether the information persists in correlated features anyway.

**Priority 7. Causal questions, handled separately**
Questions such as "does reducing queue length or assigning a specialist team at t0 reduce breaches?" need designed studies, kept separate from the predictive model. Options are randomised routing pilots, difference-in-differences around the policy change, or instrumental-variable designs using staffing shocks. Only results from such studies may be communicated as effects.

**Priority 8. Monitoring for schema and process drift**
1. **Schema and lineage drift.**
   - Alert on new fields, changes in field semantics, null-rate shifts, snapshot schedule changes (for example, the 23:00 refresh moving), and SQL changes affecting time bounds.
   - Re-run truncation-invariance tests on every pipeline release.
2. **Process drift.**
   - Monitor conformance fitness and violation rates per constraint over time.
   - Monitor the tie rate and the rate of missing sequence indices.
   - Watch for new activities (reopen), changes in the directly-follows and concurrency structure, fast-track share, appeal rate, and evidence-cycle counts.
   - Treat policy-regime changes as triggers for recalibration or retraining.
3. **Feature, prediction, and label drift.**
   - Track the population stability index (PSI) and similar statistics for inputs, the prediction distribution, the realised breach rate, and calibration drift, lagged by 10 days for label maturity.
4. **Explanation drift.**
   - Track the distribution of attributions over time. A sudden rise in a feature's importance, especially a newly added or recently re-engineered one, is a leakage alarm that triggers the Section 2 suite before release.
