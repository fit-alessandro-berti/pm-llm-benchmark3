## 1. Executive causal assessment
**Provisional verdict:** *Partly causal through mechanisms* (confidence: **65%**)

The data shows Orion’s overall mean cycle time (21.96h) exceeds Nova’s (15.24h), but this crude comparison is misleading. Within complexity strata, Orion’s *high*-complexity cases (31.57h) are faster than Nova’s (35.00h), while Orion’s *low*-complexity cases (7.50h) are slower than Nova’s (9.64h). This reversal suggests Orion’s assignment is **not purely confounded**—it plausibly reflects routing policies that target Orion for difficult cases (high complexity, night shifts, compliance checks) where its specialized reviewers (certified, experienced) compensate for longer queue waits (e.g., 19.47h vs. 22.75h for high-complexity cases). However, Orion’s *low*-complexity cases also exhibit higher rework rates (0% vs. 10%) and longer review service times (3.45h vs. 5.06h), hinting at **selection bias** or **operational trade-offs** (e.g., night-shift inefficiencies).

The proposed 50% reassignment **may succeed** for high-complexity cases (where Orion’s expertise likely offsets queueing) but **may backfire** for low-complexity cases (where Orion’s overhead could worsen cycle times). The intervention risks **disrupting routing logic** without addressing root causes like shift-based capacity or compliance bottlenecks.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan
| phase               | intervention                                                                 | target mechanism                          | assignment unit               | safeguards                                                                 | process-mining measurements                                                                 | causal learning value                                                                 | stop/rollback rule                                                                 |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| **Capacity test**   | Reduce Orion’s night-shift workload by 30% via cross-training with Nova.     | Test shift-based capacity                  | Reviewer-level (night shift)   | Ensure compliance checks are still performed; monitor quality outcomes.      | Compare `queue_wait` and `review_service_h` for night vs. day cases across groups.       | Assess if shift is the primary bottleneck.                                               | Rollback if appeals increase by >20%.                                                     |
| **Mechanism study** | Assign all low-complexity cases to Nova; keep Orion for high-complexity.     | Isolate Orion’s value proposition          | Case-level (low vs. high)      | Track rework and quality outcomes.                                               | Compare `rework_count` and `cycle_time_h` for low-complexity cases between groups.    | Determine if Orion’s overhead is justified by high-complexity performance.            | Rollback if low-complexity cycle time increases by >15%.                                |

**Final recommendation:**
The proposal to reassign 50% of Orion’s cases to Nova is **premature** without first isolating the mechanisms driving Orion’s performance. Instead, adopt a **phased learning approach**:
1. **Pilot high-complexity cases** to test if Orion’s expertise can be replicated by Nova with targeted training.
2. **Test shift-based capacity** by reducing Orion’s night workload to isolate queueing bottlenecks.
3. **Stratify interventions** by complexity and shift to avoid confounding.

**Key insight:** Orion’s advantage is **not uniform**—it likely stems from routing high-complexity cases to its certified reviewers, but this comes at a cost for low-complexity cases. The intervention should **preserve routing logic** while addressing operational inefficiencies (e.g., night-shift capacity). Blind reassignment risks **disrupting the very mechanisms that make Orion effective** for its assigned cases.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Unobserved case risk**: *Missing data field needed:* `fraud_flag` or `case_abandonment_rate` to adjust for selection into completed cases.
2. **Reviewer fatigue**: *Missing data field needed:* `reviewer_id` and `shift_history` to model time-varying reviewer performance.
3. **Compliance performance variability**: *Missing data field needed:* `compliance_check_duration` to separate compliance wait from routing delays.
4. **Queueing dynamics**: *Design change needed:* Real-time queue monitoring to reconstruct abandonment signals (e.g., `wait_time_before_review` thresholds).
5. **Operational trade-offs**: *Missing data field needed:* `cost_per_case` or `resource_utilization` to evaluate Orion’s efficiency vs. quality trade-offs.
