# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Intended use cases distinguished.** A "better model" is not a property of a model alone; it is a property of a (model, use case) pair. I separate five:

- **U1 Exploration / discovery reporting.** Analysts want a container for observed behaviour, frequency overlays, and bottleneck views. Cost of a *false accept* (model admits something the business forbids) is low; cost of a *false reject* (model cannot replay real behaviour) is high, because unreplayable events disappear from the analysis.
- **U2 Conformance enforcement / audit gating.** The model is the compliance boundary: cases that do not fit are escalated or blocked. Cost of a false accept is high and asymmetric (a released order that skipped Credit Check is a control failure). Cost of a false reject is real but bounded (rework, investigation queue), *provided* rejections are triaged rather than auto-blocked.
- **U3 Simulation / capacity analysis.** Requires a language *plus* a probability distribution over branches and durations. Neither submission carries probabilities, so neither is fit for U3 as delivered; I will not discuss U3 further except to note that a memorised-variant model (C) is the worst possible simulation prior because it assigns zero mass to legitimate unseen behaviour.
- **U4 Stakeholder communication / training.** The model is a picture people reason from. The failure mode is *confident misunderstanding*: a comprehensible but wrong model is more dangerous than an incomprehensible one, because it is believed.
- **U5 Runtime monitoring / prediction features.** Needs low-latency replay and stable semantics under partial traces.

**How use case moves the fitness/precision trade-off.**

| Use case | What must be high | What may be sacrificed | Governing failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 Exploration | Fitness (incl. alphabet coverage) | Precision | Behaviour invisible because unreplayable |
| U2 Enforcement | Specificity against *forbidden* behaviour; fitness on *legitimate* behaviour | Precision against the observed sample | False accept of a control violation; unauditable rejection boundary |
| U4 Communication | Faithfulness of the *rules* shown | Structural completeness | Misplaced confidence |
| U5 Monitoring | Replay latency, determinism | Expressiveness | Ambiguous diagnostics |

The key asymmetry the two submissions both miss: **for enforcement, the relevant "precision" is not precision against the training log; it is the rejection rate on a curated suite of forbidden traces.** Precision-against-a-positive-log and specificity-against-negatives are different quantities, and only the second one is a control.

**Assumptions.**

- **A1 (semantics).** I read the expressions with PM4Py process-tree semantics: `->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` interleaved concurrency, `*(A,B)` = do `A`, then zero or more of (`B` then `A`), `tau` = silent. `X(a, tau)` is therefore "a is skippable".
- **A2 (evidence status).** P1–P7 are the normative reference. B1–B8 are policy test cases, not a complete oracle. The reported metric table is testimony, not measurement, because none of it is reproducible.
- **A3 (what I computed myself).** All structural facts and probe verdicts below I derived from the two given expressions with PM4Py 2.7.23.4 (parse → `convert_to_petri_net` → `conformance_diagnostics_alignments`). These are deterministic properties of the submitted artefacts, **not** new measurements on the unavailable event log; I report no new fitness/precision numbers on that log anywhere in this review.
- **A4 (parsing dependency).** I verified that `parse_process_tree` reads `tau` as a silent transition. If any downstream toolchain parsed `tau` as an ordinary activity label, Model S's optionality would collapse and its whole behaviour would change. That a headline result depends on a parser convention is itself a governance finding.
- **A5 (open scope).** The external/internal attribute (P3) and the affected-check attribute (P6) do not exist in the data. No control-flow model can decide P3 or P6 from control flow alone. Any model claiming to do so is asserting, not measuring.

# 2. Metric Interpretation

I do **not** aggregate these into a single score. The measures have different scales (three are bounded proxies with no linear meaning, one is ordinal, one is seconds, one has an undefined denominator), they are mutually confounded (runtime is partly a function of fitness; generalization is largely a function of transition count), and at least two of them (fitness for S, precision for C) are *increased by the very defects that disqualify each model*. Averaging would launder those defects into a number. A defensible weighted score would first require a published normalization and a stated loss function for false accepts vs false rejects; the board has not supplied one.

| Measure | What it suggests | What it does not establish | Concern about comparability | Decision relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Training alignment fitness** (S 0.992 / C 0.973) | Both replay most training behaviour; C's shortfall proves the *training* log already contains behaviour outside its 8 branches, which undercuts the "mirrors frequent variants" claim on its own terms. | Correctness of any kind. Fitness is monotonically increased by permissiveness: S's number is manufactured by making mandatory checks skippable. It also says nothing about whether the fitting traces are *compliant*. | PM4Py reports `log_fitness` and `average_trace_fitness` and they differ; which one is shown is unstated. Case-weighted vs variant-weighted differs sharply in a heavy-tailed log. Alignment cost function unpublished. | Low as a gate. Useful only to locate unreplayable behaviour. |
| **Holdout alignment fitness** (S 0.981 / C 0.744) | C generalises badly; S is stable across the split. | How many holdout **cases are outright rejected**. Alignment fitness gives partial credit per event: on my own probe traces, C scores 0.8 on B2 and 0.75 on B5 while accepting neither. An average of 0.744 is consistent with C rejecting the large majority of holdout cases. Nor does it establish that C's rejections are true violations (F4 says they are legitimate novel combinations). | One split, no seed, no case list, no repetition, no confidence interval. With rare variants concentrated in the tail, this number is a lottery over the unpublished seed. S's small 0.992→0.981 drop is also informative: S has *no* `Correct Dossier` transition at all, so every correction event is a log move; a fitness this high implies corrections are rare in the sample, which makes any correction-related conclusion low-powered. | High **after** conversion to `percentage_of_fitting_traces` plus triage of each rejection into "policy violation" vs "model gap". |
| **Alignment precision on training log** (S 0.584 / C 0.941) | S admits a great deal of behaviour never observed; C admits almost nothing extra. | Policy correctness, in either direction. Precision against a positive log measures proximity to *the sample*, not to *the approved language* (F5). A model that enumerates training variants scores high by construction — this is close to a tautology, not evidence. Symmetrically, part of S's escaping behaviour is legitimate-but-unseen. | Escaping-edge precision is sensitive to duplicate labels (C: alternative same-label transitions at shared prefixes) and to silent routing (S), i.e. each model is pathological for exactly this estimator. C's 0.941 rather than 1.0 is itself largely a prefix-sharing artefact of the eight branches. | High as a *warning flag*, ~zero as evidence of compliance. Must be paired with negative-suite specificity. |
| **Token-based generalization** (S 0.907 / C 0.412) | S's constructs are each exercised many times; C's are not. | Generalisation to future behaviour in any business sense. `generalization_tbr` is essentially a function of how often each transition is visited; C's converted net has 50 transitions (I counted), so most are visited rarely and the score falls almost mechanically. The metric here is largely reporting **model size and label duplication**, not behavioural generality. S's 0.907 does not mean S will accept future legitimate behaviour correctly — it accepts future *illegitimate* behaviour just as readily. | Token replay on a duplicate-label net produces its own missing/remaining-token artefacts; no settings published. | Low. Treat as a size/duplication diagnostic, not as generalisation evidence. |
| **Petri-net simplicity, arc-degree** (S 0.918 / C 0.281) | S's structure is far leaner. Verified: S = 12 places / 13 transitions / 32 arcs; C = 44 / 50 / 100. | Comprehensibility or maintainability directly, and crucially **S buys part of its simplicity by omitting `Correct Dossier` from its alphabet entirely**. You cannot earn a simplicity score by dropping a required activity from the process. | Structural statistics depend on the tree→net conversion (silent routing transitions) and on whether reduction rules were applied; neither is documented. | Tie-breaker only, and only after alphabet completeness is checked. |
| **Median alignment runtime per case** (S 0.018 s / C 0.440 s) | C is roughly 25× slower. | Production cost. Alignments are normally computed per *variant* and cached, so "per case" depends on variant multiplicity. Runtime is confounded with fitness: misfitting traces expand the A* search, so C's runtime partly measures C's own misfit. | Hardware, PM4Py version, parallelism, caching all unpublished. | Low for batch conformance (0.44 s/case is affordable); material only for U5 online monitoring. |
| **Duplicate-label count** (S 0 / C 33, definition undocumented) | C has heavy label duplication; S has none. | Any specific figure. From the given expression I count 48 labelled transitions over 9 distinct labels (RD 8, CC 9, TR 9, SR 3, ER 2, CD 1, MA 7, AA 1, RO 8) plus 2 silent = 50 transitions; that gives **39** excess labelled transitions, or **7** labels with more than one transition, or **48** total occurrences. None of the three natural definitions yields 33. The reported number is therefore **not reproducible from the artefact**. S's "0" also hides its analogue: 4 silent skip transitions. | An undefined metric is not comparable at all. | High once defined — it drives alignment ambiguity, impact analysis (F3), and net size. As reported, it is unauditable. |
| **Stakeholder comprehension, informal 1–5** (S 4.4 / C 1.9) | Business users find S far easier to read. | That they understand what S *permits*. Preference is not comprehension. # 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S is sound (verified), tiny (12 places / 13 transitions / 32 arcs), has exactly one transition per label so alignment diagnostics are unambiguous and impact analysis is trivial (F3's complaint about C does not apply), and it replays essentially everything. Three of its choices are genuinely defensible:

1. `X('Supplier Review', tau)` is **correct modelling of a real unknown**. P3 makes Supplier Review conditional on an attribute that is not in the log. Making it optional at the control-flow layer, and pushing the conditional requirement to a data-level check, is exactly right.
2. `X('Expedite Review', tau)` is correct: P4 says it is optional.
3. Robustness to F4's missing Supplier Review events is real: a model that hard-requires SR would convert a data-quality problem into a flood of false violations.

For **U1 exploration**, S is usable today with one repair (see below), and its low precision is not by itself an objection — F7 is right that an exploratory container should over-approximate.

## Strongest case against Model S

F7 is right about precision and wrong about the conclusion, because S's problem is not low precision, it is **that it does not encode a single one of the mandatory-behaviour rules**. Two independent facts settle this:

- I enumerated S's complete language: **130 distinct complete traces**. Checking them against P1–P5 (ignoring corrections, which S cannot express at all), only **36 are policy-conformant**. **94 of 130 admitted traces — 72% — violate P2 and/or P4.** A model whose language is 72% violations is not a permissive model, it is a wrong model.
- S is simultaneously *too restrictive*: it contains no `Correct Dossier` transition and no loop, so the entire correction/recheck subprocess (P6, P7) — the one genuinely repetitive part of the process — is outside its alphabet. Its "robustness to missing events" does not extend to the behaviour that actually repeats.

### Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous behaviours admitted (all verified accepted by S)

| # | Trace | Why it is unacceptable |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B3) | Both mandatory checks skipped. Direct P2 violation, and the most basic control in the process. |
| S2 | `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B4) | Expedite Review before the mandatory checks. Direct P4 violation. |
| S3 | `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | The worst compliance case in the whole language: an order fast-tracked and **auto-approved with zero checks and no human**. S accepts it. |
| S4 | `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | An externally sourced order released with no credit and no technical review. |
| S5 | `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order` | Half the mandatory control set, auto-approved. |
| S6 | `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Both *optional* reviews performed and both *mandatory* ones skipped — the exact inversion of policy. |
| S7 | `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Supplier Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | Expedite before everything; illustrates that P4's ordering rule has no representation whatsoever inside the `+` block. |

### Behaviours it wrongly *rejects*

| # | Trace | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| S8 | `... Correct Dossier, Technical Review, ...` (= B6), and any correction trace | Log move on `Correct Dossier`; legitimate correction behaviour is invisible to S. |
| S9 | `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B8) | S rejects this, which happens to match policy — **but for the wrong reason**. S rejects it because it cannot repeat *anything*, not because it knows a repeat needs a preceding correction. The same mechanism rejects the *legitimate* post-correction rechecks. A coincidentally correct verdict must not be scored as policy awareness. |

### Intentional noise tolerance vs accidental semantic flaw

- **Defensible tolerance:** optional `Supplier Review` (missing attribute, missing events); optional `Expedite Review` (genuinely optional by P4).
- **Accidental semantic flaw, not tolerance:** `X('Credit Check', tau)` and `X('Technical Review', tau)`. P2 states these are mandatory. Optionalising a *stated* mandatory activity is a modelling error that happens to raise fitness; there is no uncertainty being represented. Likewise, placing `Expedite Review` in a free parallel block contradicts an *explicit* ordering rule — nobody is uncertain about P4.
- **Alphabet error, neither of the above:** the omission of `Correct Dossier`.
- **Aggravating factor:** because the skips are silent transitions, a skipped mandatory check produces **zero alignment deviations**. S cannot even be used as a detector with post-processed alignment output; you would have to bypass the model and inspect the log directly, at which point the model is contributing nothing to the control.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

C is the only submission that (a) contains `Correct Dossier`, (b) makes both mandatory checks non-skippable, (c) enforces "approval then release", and (d) has a language that a human auditor can read exhaustively in one sitting. It is sound (verified). Every rejection it emits is explainable by pointing at the eight branches. In a control environment where the approved behaviour set is small and change-controlled, an explicit whitelist is a legitimate and even preferred design — this is the strongest form of F8, and I return to it in §11.

## Strongest case against Model C

**Branch enumeration.** C's language is exactly **8 traces** (verified). The eight branches are the frequent training variants. This is not a specification; it is a lookup table.

**High precision is a memorisation artefact.** If the model is (approximately) the training log, precision against the training log approaches 1 by construction. The test that would distinguish memorisation from knowledge — precision or specificity against *held-out* and *curated negative* behaviour — was never run (F5). The 0.941 therefore carries almost no information about C's quality as a control.

**The language is not closed under recombination of its own features.** This is F2 made concrete, and it is the decisive technical finding:

- C contains TR-before-CC (branches 2, 4) and it contains Auto Approval (branch 8), but it rejects **B2** = `RD, TR, CC, Auto Approval, RO`, a combination of two behaviours it already knows.
- C contains Supplier Review + Expedite Review (branch 6) and Auto Approval (branch 8) and alternate check order (branches 2, 4), but rejects **B5**, which combines them.

Counting only the *acyclic* policy-legal space — check order (2) × Supplier Review present/absent (2) × Expedite Review present/absent with its legal positions × approval type (2) — the policy admits **36 correction-free traces** (I enumerated them). C covers **8**. It under-covers legitimate behaviour by roughly 78% **before** corrections are considered, and correction behaviour is unbounded, so C's coverage of the full legal language is not merely incomplete, it is a set of measure zero.

**Correction handling is not just opaque, it is wrong.** C hard-codes exactly one correction pattern: `Correct Dossier` then `Credit Check` then `Technical Review`, once, with Manager Approval. It therefore *asserts* that a correction always requires re-running both checks in that fixed order — a policy claim nobody made and which F6 explicitly contradicts. Consequences: B6 (correction affecting Technical Review only) is flagged as a violation; a correction affecting only Credit Check is flagged; two corrections are flagged; a correction followed by Auto Approval is flagged; a correction on an external order is flagged. Every one of those is a *false* violation manufactured by the model.

**Holdout degradation, and why 0.744 flatters it.** The 0.973 → 0.744 gap is the textbook memorisation signature. Worse, alignment fitness gives partial credit: on my own probe traces C scores 0.8 on B2, 0.75 on B5, 0.917 on B6 while accepting none of them. The aggregate hides the fact that the *binary* holdout acceptance rate — the only statistic that matters for an enforcement gate — is far below 0.744 and was never reported.

**Duplicate labels and impact analysis.** Converted: 44 places, 50 transitions (48 labelled + 2 silent), 100 arcs, 9 distinct labels. `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` each occur 9 times; `Register Dossier` and `Release Order` 8 times each; `Manager Approval` 7. Effects: (i) alignments become ambiguous — several optimal alignments differ only in which duplicate was used, so diagnostics identify a *branch*, not a *cause*; (ii) F3 is quantified — changing the credit-check rule means editing nine transitions consistently; (iii) the duplication mechanically depresses the generalization and simplicity scores, so two of C's four bad numbers are partly reporting its encoding style rather than its behaviour; (iv) the reported "33" matches none of the natural definitions (39 excess / 7 duplicated labels / 48 occurrences), so the metric report cannot be audited.

**Maintainability.** Adding one legitimate combination is O(branches), not O(1), and the space grows multiplicatively (check order × SR × ER × approval × correction rounds × recheck scopes). With unbounded corrections the enumeration is infinite in principle: **C can never be completed**, only extended until the maintainers give up.

**Why F8 fails as a governance argument.** "Unseen behaviour should be treated as noncompliant until approved" is coherent *only if the model's language equals the **approved** language*. C's language equals the **observed** language on one unpublished 80% sample. B2 was never rejected by a compliance officer; it merely failed to appear in a random draw. Under F8, the compliance boundary becomes a function of an unpublished random seed — a control whose decisions cannot be reproduced or justified to an auditor. That, not the 0.744, is the fatal objection to C-as-delivered.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

**1. Positive-log-only precision (F5).** Precision was computed against observed positive traces only. This measures proximity to the sample, not to policy. It is maximised by memorisation (C: 0.941) and it is blind to the only question that matters for a control: does the model accept forbidden behaviour? The decisive experiment — replay B3, B4, B8 and a negative suite — takes minutes and was not run. Every headline conclusion in the submission ("C is audit-ready", "S is exploratory only") is built on this hole.

**2. Fitness rewarded by optionalising mandatory behaviour.** S's 0.992/0.981 is produced by the exact construct that makes it non-compliant. This is not a subtle bias: `X(a, tau)` converts every log-move deviation for `a` into a free silent move. Fitness under such a construct is close to a measurement of the model's own permissiveness. Any governance process that ranks models by fitness is running a competition to be maximally vague.

**3. Missing-event bias, entangled with the missing attributes.** F4 says some Supplier Review events may be missing. With no external/internal attribute, an SR-absent trace is ambiguous between "internal order, correctly no SR" and "external order, SR event lost" and "external order, control skipped". A control-flow metric cannot separate these three, so *both* directions of conclusion are unsupported: S's fitness credit for SR-absent traces is unearned, and C's rejections of SR-present novel combinations are uninterpretable. Data quality is being scored as model quality.

**4. Generalization rewarded by permissiveness, and `generalization_tbr` as a size proxy.** The token-based generalization estimator rewards transitions that are visited often. C's converted net has 50 transitions, most visited rarely, so 0.412 largely reports duplication; S's 13 transitions, all heavily visited, produce 0.907. Neither number speaks to whether the model will handle *future* behaviour correctly. As a headline "generalization" figure in a governance pack, it is actively misleading.

**5. Train/holdout split sensitivity and leakage.** No seed, no case list, no preprocessing code. With a heavy-tailed variant distribution, whether a rare variant lands in the 20% dominates C's holdout number; a single split gives no variance estimate. Leakage is also plausible in the other direction: if the split was by *event* or if duplicate cases exist, C's training fitness is inflated. Under F8, the compliance boundary literally depends on this unpublished seed.

**6. Duplicated-label effects.** Ambiguous optimal alignments (diagnostics point at a branch, not a cause), inflated state space (feeding pathology 10), depressed simplicity/generalization, and an unreproducible reported count of 33. Duplicate labels also interact with precision: at a shared prefix, several same-label transitions are enabled, which perturbs escaping-edge counts.

**7. Silent-transition effects.** S's four τ transitions make deviations *invisible*: a skipped Credit Check yields no alignment move at all. Silent transitions also enlarge the reachable-marking set of the parallel block (2⁴ skip combinations × interleavings), which is a large part of why precision is 0.584 — so S is being penalised on precision and rewarded on fitness by one and the same construct, and neither number is telling the board what it thinks.

**8. Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Default unit costs (log move 1, model move 1, silent move 0) are one arbitrary choice among many. Raising the cost of model moves changes C's numbers; making silent moves non-free changes S's numbers substantially, because S's fitness depends almost entirely on free τ moves. Two teams that chose different cost functions produced a table presented as comparable. Until the cost function is published, the S-vs-C fitness comparison has no defined meaning.

**9. Simplicity as a size proxy that rewards omission.** S scores 0.918 partly because it dropped a required activity from the alphabet. Simplicity metrics have no notion of *completeness*, so they systematically reward under-modelling. Simplicity must never be read before an alphabet-coverage check.

**10. Stakeholder-score methodology.** An informal 1–5 preference score, no protocol, no rater count, no blinding, no task battery, no inter-rater agreement. It almost certainly measures diagram size. Given that the winner permits releasing an unchecked order, this metric currently measures *how confidently people will misread the model*.

**11. Runtime confounded by model size and by misfit.** 0.018 s vs 0.440 s reflects state-space size, duplicate labels, and the fact that unfittable traces expand the A* search — so runtime and fitness are not independent measurements. Per-case medians also depend on variant caching. Low stakes, but it demonstrates that the table's entries are not eight independent observations.

### Ranking by likely decision impact

| Rank | Pathology | Whose claim it inflates | Impact if uncorrected | Cheapest test to settle it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positive-log-only precision / no negative suite | C ("audit-ready") | A memorised lookup table is deployed as a control; S's true failure never surfaces | Replay a 20-trace curated negative suite; report acceptance rate |

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy chosen: a POWL procedural backbone (partial order + explicit correction loop) + a declarative layer + post-hoc data-dependent policy checks, producing a three-valued verdict (ACCEPT / FLAG / REJECT).**

Rationale for this choice over the alternatives: a pure process tree cannot express "Supplier Review is unordered with respect to the checks while Expedite Review must follow them" without either over-sequencing or dropping the ordering rule (this is exactly the trap S fell into), so a partial order is the minimal formalism that fits P3 + P4 simultaneously. A family of attribute-conditioned models is the *right* long-term answer for P3, but it is unbuildable today because the conditioning attribute does not exist — proposing it now would be proposing a model that cannot be estimated. The declarative layer and post-hoc checks carry precisely the obligations that control flow cannot decide, and the third verdict value makes the missing attributes *visible* instead of silently resolving them in either direction.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

Backbone nodes (POWL):

- `register = Register Dossier`, `release = Release Order`
- `mandatory = PO{Credit Check, Technical Review}` with **no** edges (concurrent, both required) — P2
- `recheck = XOR( Credit Check, Technical Review, PO{Credit Check, Technical Review} )` — the three admissible recheck scopes of F6
- `redo = PO{Correct Dossier, recheck}` with edge `Correct Dossier → recheck` — P6/P7
- `corrections = LOOP(τ, redo)` = zero or more correction rounds
- `check_block = PO{mandatory, corrections}` with edge `mandatory → corrections`
- `supplier = XOR(Supplier Review, τ)` — P3, structurally optional *only* because the conditioning attribute is missing
- `expedite = XOR(Expedite Review, τ)` — P4
- `approval = XOR(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` — P5

Root partial order over `{register, check_block, supplier, expedite, approval, release}` with edges:

```
register → check_block      (P1)
register → supplier
check_block → expedite      (P4: Expedite Review after both mandatory checks)
check_block → approval      (P2: approval only after the checks)
supplier  → approval
expedite  → approval        (P4: before approval)
approval  → release         (P5, P1)
```

`supplier` is deliberately unordered with respect to `check_block` and `expedite`: policy constrains the *position* of Expedite Review but says nothing about Supplier Review beyond "required for external orders", so over-ordering it would repeat C's mistake of inventing constraints.

Measured properties of the converted net: **20 places, 22 transitions (13 labelled + 9 silent), 50 arcs, easy-sound = True** (workflow net, S-component covered, no dead tasks, all tasks live). Label duplication is 4 excess transitions (`Credit Check` ×3, `Technical Review` ×3), all confined to the recheck subtree — compared with 39 excess in Model C, and unlike C the duplicates are *semantically distinct roles* (first pass vs recheck), which is the only defensible reason to duplicate a label.

## 7.2 Self-contained PM4Py code

```python
"""model_third: POWL backbone + declarative layer + post-hoc data checks."""
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder,
)

# ---- documented semantic switches (never silent defaults) ------------------
REQUIRE_RECHECK_AFTER_CORRECTION = True   # a Correct Dossier must be followed by
                                          # at least one affected recheck
ALLOW_CORRECTION_AFTER_EXPEDITE = False   # corrections precede Expedite Review
                                          # (no log evidence either way yet)

A = lambda name: Transition(label=name)
TAU = SilentTransition


def _optional(name):
    """X(name, tau): used ONLY where conditionality is genuinely undetermined."""
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[A(name), TAU()])


def _concurrent(*nodes):
    """POWL has no PARALLEL operator: concurrency = partial order with no edges."""
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def build_model_third(require_recheck=REQUIRE_RECHECK_AFTER_CORRECTION):
    # (a) round 0: both mandatory checks, unordered, NOT skippable          (P2)
    mandatory = _concurrent(A("Credit Check"), A("Technical Review"))

    # (b) recheck scope after a correction: CC only | TR only | both     (P6/F6)
    recheck_alternatives = [
        A("Credit Check"),
        A("Technical Review"),
        _concurrent(A("Credit Check"), A("Technical Review")),
    ]
    if not require_recheck:
        recheck_alternatives.append(TAU())      # correction with no recheck
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=recheck_alternatives)

    # (c) one correction round: Correct Dossier THEN its recheck scope      (P7)
    redo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[A("Correct Dossier"), recheck])
    redo.order.add_edge(redo.children[0], redo.children[1])

    # (d) zero or more correction rounds: *(tau, redo)                      (P6)
    corrections = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[TAU(), redo])

    # (e) check block = round 0 followed by any correction rounds
    check_block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[mandatory, corrections])
    check_block.order.add_edge(mandatory, corrections)

    supplier = _optional("Supplier Review")     # P3: attribute missing
    expedite = _optional("Expedite Review")     # P4: genuinely optional
    approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                            children=[A("Manager Approval"), A("Auto Approval")])
    register, release = A("Register Dossier"), A("Release Order")

    root = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[register, check_block, supplier, expedite, approval, release])
    root.order.add_edge(register, check_block)      # P1
    root.order.add_edge(register, supplier)
    root.order.add_edge(check_block, expedite)      # P4: ER after both checks
    root.order.add_edge(check_block, approval)      # P2
    root.order.add_edge(supplier, approval)
    root.order.add_edge(expedite, approval)         # P4: ER before approval
    root.order.add_edge(approval, release)          # P5, P1
    return root


model_third = build_model_third()

net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
# pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=True)
```

## 7.3 Declarative dictionary and external checks

The declarative layer is redundant defence-in-depth for the rules the backbone already enforces (so that a future backbone edit cannot silently drop a control), plus the home for rules with no structural encoding.

```python
DECLARE_THIRD = {
    "init":        {"Register Dossier": {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "existence":   {"Credit Check":     {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    "Technical Review": {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    "Release Order":    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "exactly_one": {"Register Dossier": {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    "Release Order":    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    # precedence (A, B): B may occur only if A occurred earlier
    "precedence":  {("Credit Check",     "Expedite Review"):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Expedite Review"):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    ("Credit Check",     "Manager Approval"): {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Manager Approval"): {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    ("Credit Check",     "Auto Approval"):    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Auto Approval"):    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
}
# Checked with: pm4py.conformance_declare(log, DECLARE_THIRD)
# NOTE: "exactly one of Manager Approval / Auto Approval" has no DECLARE template in
# pm4py (coexistence exists, non-coexistence does not), so it is a custom check below.
# NOTE: do NOT add precedence(Manager Approval, Release Order): Release Order occurs in
# every case while each approval occurs in only half, so that constraint would fire on
# every Auto Approval case. P5 belongs in the custom check, not in DECLARE.

MANDATORY = ("Credit Check", "Technical Review")
APPROVALS = ("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval")


def check_exactly_one_approval(trace):                                    # P5
    n = sum(1 for e in trace if e in APPROVALS)
    return ("REJECT", f"P5: {n} approval events, expected exactly 1") if n != 1 \
        else ("ACCEPT", "")


def check_no_unexplained_repeat(trace):                                   # P7
    seen, corrections = {}, 0
    for e in trace:
        if e == "Correct Dossier":
            corrections += 1
        if e in MANDATORY:
            seen[e] = seen.get(e, 0) + 1
            if seen[e] > 1 and corrections == 0:
                return ("REJECT", f"P7: repeated {e} with no preceding Correct Dossier")
    return ("ACCEPT", "")


def check_recheck_scope(trace, affected=None):                            # P6
    """`affected` is the MISSING attribute: {round_index: {checks affected}}."""
    rounds, cur = [], None
    for e in trace:
        if e == "Correct Dossier":
            cur = set(); rounds.append(cur)
        elif cur is not None and e in MANDATORY:
            cur.add(e)
    if not rounds:
        return ("ACCEPT", "")
    if affected is None:
        return ("FLAG", f"P6 unverifiable: {len(rounds)} correction round(s), observed "
                        f"recheck scopes {[sorted(r) for r in rounds]}, "
                        f"affected-check attribute absent")
    for i, r in enumerate(rounds):
        if r != set(affected.get(i, set())):
            return ("REJECT", f"P6: round {i} rechecked {sorted(r)} but the affected "
                              f"set is {sorted(affected.get(i, set()))}")
    return ("ACCEPT", "")


def check_supplier_review(trace, external=None):                          # P3
    """`external` is the MISSING order-source attribute."""
    has_sr = "Supplier Review" in trace
    if external is None:
        return ("UNVERIFIED", "P3 undecidable: order-source attribute absent "
                              f"(Supplier Review {'present' if has_sr else 'absent'})")
    if external and not has_sr:
        return ("REJECT", "P3: external order without Supplier Review")
    if not external and has_sr:
        return ("FLAG", "P3: internal order with Supplier Review")
    return ("ACCEPT", "")


def policy_verdict(trace, structural_fit, external=None, affected=None):
    """Three-valued verdict plus the list of obligations that remain undecidable."""
    results = [check_exactly_one_approval(trace),
               check_no_unexplained_repeat(trace),
               check_recheck_scope(trace, affected),
               check_supplier_review(trace, external)]
    if not structural_fit:
        results.append(("REJECT", "backbone: no perfectly fitting alignment"))
    rejects = [m for v, m in results if v == "REJECT"]
    flags   = [m for v, m in results if v == "FLAG"]
    open_   = [m for v, m in results if v == "UNVERIFIED"]
    verdict = "REJECT" if rejects else ("FLAG" if flags else "ACCEPT")
    return verdict, rejects + flags, open_


def assess_log(log, external_by_case=None, affected_by_case=None):
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net_third, im_third, fm_third)
    out = {}
    for trace, d in zip(log, diag):
        cid = trace.attributes["concept:name"]
        acts = [e["concept:name"] for e in trace]
        out[cid] = policy_verdict(
            acts, d["fitness"] == 1.0,
            external=(external_by_case or {}).get(cid),
            affected=(affected_by_case or {}).get(cid))
    return out
```

## 7.4 Explicit semantics

| Requirement | Where enforced | Exact semantics |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory checks (P2)** | Backbone, structurally | `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` are non-skippable and unordered with respect to each other; both must precede `expedite` and `approval`. There is **no** τ alternative for either. Redundantly asserted by DECLARE `existence` + `precedence`. |
| **Optional Supplier Review (P3)** | Backbone (structural optionality) + `check_supplier_review` (obligation) | Structurally `XOR(Supplier Review, τ)`, unordered w.r.t. the checks and Expedite Review, constrained only to lie between `Register Dossier` and the approval. The *requirement* for external orders is a data-level obligation that currently returns `UNVERIFIED` for every case; it is recorded as an open obligation on the case, not silently discharged. |
| **Expedite Review ordering (P4)** | Backbone, structurally | Optional; edges `check_block → expedite → approval` force it after **all** mandatory-check activity (including any correction rounds) and before approval. Unordered w.r.t. `Supplier Review`, so both relative orders are accepted, as P4 requires. |
| **Exactly one approval (P5)** | Backbone (`XOR`) + `check_exactly_one_approval` | Exactly one of `Manager Approval` / `Auto Approval`, after everything else, immediately before `Release Order`. The custom check catches zero-approval and double-approval traces even if the backbone is later edited. |
| **Correction and recheck (P6)** | Backbone (loop + XOR of scopes) + `check_recheck_scope` | Zero or more rounds. Each round is `Correct Dossier` followed by exactly one of {`Credit Check`}, {`Technical Review`}, {both, unordered}. Which scope is *correct* depends on the missing attribute, so the control-flow layer accepts all three and the data layer returns `FLAG` (not ACCEPT, not REJECT) until the attribute exists; once it exists, `check_recheck_scope` turns `FLAG` into `ACCEPT`/`REJECT` with no model change. |
| **No unexplained repeat (P7)** | Backbone, structurally + `check_no_unexplained_repeat` | A mandatory check can only occur a second time *inside* a correction round, i.e. after a `Correct Dossier`. This is the rule S and C both got right by accident; here it is encoded. |
| **First/last (P1)** | Backbone | `Register Dossier` has no predecessors; `Release Order` has no successors; DECLARE `init` + `exactly_one` repeat the assertion. |

**Verdict semantics.** `REJECT` = a decidable policy violation (escalate/block). `FLAG` = the trace is control-flow legal but a rule that depends on a missing attribute cannot be evaluated (route to human review, count separately, never auto-block). `ACCEPT` = all decidable rules pass; the open-obligation list travels with the case so nobody mistakes "accepted" for "fully verified". This is the mechanism that avoids solving missing data by optionalising everything: **the obligation stays on the books and is visible in every report.**

## 7.5 Limitations caused by the missing attributes (and other honest limits)

1. **P3 is undecidable today.** Without external/internal, the model cannot distinguish a compliant internal order from an external order whose Supplier Review was skipped or lost (F4). Every case currently carries an `UNVERIFIED` P3 obligation. This is a *reported gap*, not a modelling decision. Required remediation: capture the attribute at registration and back-fill from the purchasing system; then replace `_optional("Supplier Review")` with an attribute-conditioned pair of models (`external` variant with mandatory SR, `internal` variant without) and validate them on the stratified holdout of §9. Until then, no report may claim P3 conformance.
2. **P6 is only partially decidable.** The model can verify that *some* recheck happened after each correction and that its scope is one of the three admissible ones; it cannot verify that the scope *matches what the correction affected*. B6-type cases are therefore `FLAG`, never `ACCEPT`. If the FLAG volume is high, that is the business case for capturing the attribute.
3. **Deliberate semantic decisions that need compliance sign-off, all switchable and none silent:**
   - `REQUIRE_RECHECK_AFTER_CORRECTION = True` — a correction with no recheck at all (`RD, CC, TR, CD, MA, RO`) is rejected. If purely administrative corrections exist, flip the switch; the τ alternative is already implemented.
   - `ALLOW_CORRECTION_AFTER_EXPEDITE = False` — corrections found *during* Supplier or Expedite Review (e.g. `RD, CC, TR, SR, ER, CD, TR, MA, RO`) are rejected, because `expedite` sits after `check_block`. This is the model's sharpest known restriction; resolving it requires counting such traces in the log, and the fix is to move `expedite` inside the correction scope or to add a second correction loop after it.
   - No correction round before the first pass of the checks.
   - `Supplier Review` may occur at most once and is unordered w.r.t. the checks.
4. **Label duplication.** `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` each map to 3 transitions (first pass, solo recheck, joint recheck). Alignment diagnostics must therefore be read at the *role* level; annotate the three transitions with a `role` attribute so impact analysis stays O(1) per rule.
5. **No probabilities.** Not a simulation model until branch probabilities and durations are fitted.
6. **The loop makes acceptance a search, not an inspection.** Unlike S and C, the third model's language is infinite, so "does it accept X" must be answered by alignment, not by reading. Correction rounds should be capped in monitoring (e.g. flag ≥3 rounds) as an operational, not structural, control.

def deterministic_split(df, seed=SEED, train_frac=0.8):
    """Hash-based: reproducible from the seed alone, no RNG state, order-independent."""
    def bucket(cid):
        h = hashlib.sha256(f"{seed}:{cid}".encode()).hexdigest()
        return int(h[:8], 16) / 0xFFFFFFFF
    cases = df["case:concept:name"].unique()
    train_ids = {c for c in cases if bucket(c) < train_frac}
    return (df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(train_ids)],
            df[~df["case:concept:name"].isin(train_ids)], sorted(train_ids))

## 9.4 Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation

```python
import random

def drop_activity(log, activity, p, seed=SEED):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    out = []
    for tr in log:
        out.append([e for e in tr if not (e == activity and rng.random() < p)])
    return out

def duplicate_activity(log, activity, p, seed=SEED):
    rng, out = random.Random(seed), []
    for tr in log:
        new = []
        for e in tr:
            new.append(e)
            if e == activity and rng.random() < p:
                new.append(e)
        out.append(new)
    return out
```

Two indices, both reported per model:

- **Missing-event robustness** (should be *high* for `Supplier Review`): drop SR with p ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5}; a model that collapses is over-fitted to data quality.
- **Mandatory-check sensitivity** (should be *high* for `Credit Check` / `Technical Review`): drop a mandatory check and measure the fitness drop and the acceptance drop. **A model whose acceptance rate does not fall to ~0 when a mandatory check is deleted is not a control.** By construction this index is ~0 for Model S; publishing it makes S's defect a number rather than an argument.
- **Duplicate-event tolerance**: duplicate `Credit Check` with no preceding correction — acceptance must drop to 0 (P7).

## 9.5 Unseen-combination tests

Build a policy oracle that enumerates the legal correction-free combinations (check order × SR × ER position × approval), subtract the training variants, and replay the remainder. Report acceptance rate on **legal-but-unseen** behaviour separately from everything else; this is the number that operationalises F2 and it is the one on which C will fail and S will pass for the wrong reason — which is why it must be read jointly with §9.3 specificity. Never use `pm4py.play_out` of a candidate model to generate this set: a model cannot be tested against its own language. Use the oracle, or `play_out` of the *third* model only to generate candidates that are then **manually adjudicated** by compliance before entering the suite.

## 9.9 Stakeholder comprehension protocol

Replace the informal 1–5 rating with a **task battery measuring correctness, not preference**: ≥10 participants per role (compliance, operations, IT, analyst), randomised model order, participants blind to model authorship and to the metric table. Each participant answers 12 forced-choice items of the form "can this trace occur under this model?" (6 legal, 6 illegal, including B3, B4, B8) plus 4 items of the form "what must happen before Expedite Review?". Record accuracy, time per item, and self-reported confidence; report accuracy with a confidence interval, Krippendorff's α across raters, and — most important — the **overconfidence gap** (confidence minus accuracy). A model with high confidence and low accuracy is a communication hazard and must be labelled as such. Publish the instrument.

## 9.10 Decision rule: rejection despite good aggregate metrics

A candidate is **rejected for enforcement use**, regardless of fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity, or comprehension scores, if **any** of the following holds:

1. It accepts **any** trace in the compliance-signed negative suite (§9.3 specificity < 1.0 on hard clauses P1, P2, P4, P5, P7).
2. It is not sound, or fails the alphabet-coverage gate (§9.7).
3. Its mandatory-check sensitivity index (§9.4) is below 0.95 — i.e. deleting a mandatory check does not reliably cause rejection.
4. `pct_fitting_traces` on the stratified holdout is below the agreed threshold in **any** stratum with non-trivial volume, and the rejected cases are not individually triaged into "true violation" vs "model gap".
5. The reported results cannot be reproduced from the published seed, case-id list, preprocessing code, and cost function by an independent reviewer.
6. Any metric moves outside its agreed tolerance under the cost sweep (§9.6) or across the 10 seeds (§9.1).
7. The overconfidence gap (§9.9) exceeds an agreed bound for the compliance role.

Symmetrically, a candidate is **rejected for exploratory use** if it fails alphabet coverage or if its holdout `pct_fitting_traces` is low enough that material behaviour would be invisible.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Compliance / enforcement: neither Model S nor Model C. Do not deploy either.**
Model S accepts B3, B4 and 94 of its 130 admitted traces violate stated policy; it fails decision rule 1 and rule 3 outright, and it fails the alphabet gate because `Correct Dossier` is absent. Model C fails on legitimacy grounds rather than permissiveness: it manufactures false violations on B2, B5, B6 and on any recombination of behaviour it already contains, and — decisively — its compliance boundary is an unpublished random sample, which fails decision rule 5. F1 is upheld against S. F8 is rejected: "unseen is noncompliant" is defensible only for an *approved* language, not an *observed* one.

**Exploration: Model S may continue, conditionally and clearly labelled.** Conditions: (a) `Correct Dossier` must be added to its alphabet — as delivered it makes correction behaviour invisible, which is a defect for exploration too; (b) every artefact derived from it carries the label "exploratory container; permits behaviour that violates P2 and P4; must not be cited in compliance reporting"; (c) it must not be shown to business audiences without that label, given the 4.4 comprehension score. Once the third model is validated, the cleanest outcome is to retire S entirely and use the third model's backbone with the FLAG/REJECT layers disabled as the exploration view — one artefact, two profiles, no divergence between what compliance and analysts believe the process is.

**Retirement.** Retire Model C **as a model** and preserve its eight branches **as a regression suite of positive traces** — that is where their real value lies, and it costs nothing. Retire Model S as delivered; it may be regenerated only with the alphabet fixed and the label attached.

**Conditions the third alternative must meet before deployment.**

1. Negative-suite specificity = 1.0 on the hard clauses (P1, P2, P4, P5, P7), with the suite signed off by compliance beforehand.
2. Soundness (already verified) and alphabet coverage (already verified) re-run in CI on every change.
3. Stratified holdout `pct_fitting_traces` published per stratum across 10 seeds, with **every** rejected case triaged into "true violation" or "model gap", and every model gap either fixed or documented as a restriction.
4. Compliance sign-off on the four explicit semantic decisions in §7.5 item 3 — in particular `REQUIRE_RECHECK_AFTER_CORRECTION` and the no-correction-after-Expedite restriction, the latter tested against the log's actual count of such traces.
5. A dated plan to capture the external/internal attribute and the affected-check attribute, with an interim commitment that no report claims P3 or P6 conformance until they exist.
6. The FLAG queue must have a named owner, an SLA, and a volume forecast **before** go-live. A flag nobody triages is worse than no flag: it converts a known unknown into a false sense of coverage.
7. A shadow period (proposed: one full quarter) in which REJECT and FLAG verdicts are produced and reviewed but do not block releases, with weekly false-positive review.
8. Change control: any edit to the backbone, the DECLARE dictionary, or the switches re-runs §9.3–§9.7 and requires re-sign-off. Language changes are policy changes.
9. Operational caps as monitoring rules, not structure: flag cases with ≥3 correction rounds and cases whose FLAG reason repeats across releases.

**One governance action independent of model choice:** the metric report itself must be rejected and re-issued. Seeds, case ids, preprocessing, cost functions, the duplicate-label definition (which currently matches no reproducible count), and the comprehension instrument are all missing. The board should not choose between two models on the basis of a table that no one can regenerate.

# 11. Dissenting View

**The strongest case against my own recommendation.**

My recommendation quietly changes what a compliance model is for. In regulated procurement, the approved-behaviour whitelist *is* the control: if a case does not match an approved pattern, a human looks at it. Model C is that whitelist. Calling its eight branches "memorisation" begs the question — the board can simply **adopt** them as the approved set, at which point its holdout failures become true positives and its 0.744 becomes a feature. My objection that "the boundary depends on a random seed" dissolves the moment compliance signs the branches; nothing forces C to remain descriptive. Maintenance cost is not a defect; O(branches) editing behind an approval step is change control.

Against that, my third model admits an **infinite** language nobody has ever approved: unbounded correction chains, every interleaving of Supplier Review with the checks, every recheck scope. I justified those from policy text that the stakeholders themselves call unresolved (P3, P6), and I made four semantic decisions unilaterally. I replaced a model whose errors are enumerable with one whose errors are latent in a partial order only alignment can probe. I also added machinery — POWL, a DECLARE layer, custom Python checks, a three-valued verdict, a FLAG queue — that a small team will not maintain; the flags will be ignored within two quarters, and an ignored FLAG is a silent ACCEPT. Finally, my validation is circular: the probes come from the same stakeholders whose policy is admittedly incomplete.

**What would change my decision.** (1) A compliance sign-off that the eight branches are *normative* rather than descriptive, plus evidence that legal behaviour outside them is genuinely rare — I would then prefer C with a formal exception process. (2) The distribution of correction rounds and recheck scopes in the log: if corrections are overwhelmingly "both checks repeat", my flexible recheck subtree is unearned complexity. (3) A measured FLAG volume and triage cost; if FLAG exceeds what the owning team can process, the three-valued design is a fiction and I should choose a two-valued boundary. (4) Negative-suite specificity and binary holdout acceptance for all three models — if the third model's specificity is below 1.0 on any hard clause, my central argument against S applies to my own model and it must be rejected on the same rule. (5) Evidence on how often novel-but-legal combinations actually arrive: if the process is genuinely stable, enumeration is cheaper than partial orders. (6) Log evidence on corrections raised during Supplier or Expedite Review; a non-trivial count would falsify my backbone's sharpest restriction and force a redesign before deployment.
