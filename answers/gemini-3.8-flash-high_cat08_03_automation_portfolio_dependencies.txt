# 1. Candidate matrix

Senior management's initial inclination to automate the highest-frequency front-end task—`Register application` (11,880 cases/year)—reflects a classic local-optimization fallacy. Process-mining discovery reveals that front-end back-office queues operate with surplus afternoon capacity (utilization drops below 65% after 15:00) and incur only 1.5 hours of median waiting time. In contrast, end-to-end turnaround time, rework, and loan loss risks are dictated by severe downstream bottlenecks, high-rework loops, external synchronization delays, and compliance control failures:
1. **The Compliance Bottleneck:** `Resolve entity mismatch` imposes a 31.0-hour median queue, carries a 38% rework rate, sits on the critical path in 82% of cases when present, and chokes a pool of only 3.0 Full-Time Equivalents (FTEs).
2. **The Analyst Capacity Deficit:** Credit analysts (9.0 FTEs) face a nominal demand of over 29,700 hours per year across document review, normalization, and credit analysis against an available nominal capacity of ~15,750 hours. The 75-minute manual task `Normalize financial statements` consumes 6,500 hours alone and causes a 20.0-hour queue.
3. **The Synchronization Barrier:** `Wait for external valuation` imposes a 58.0-hour median delay (19% late rate) because it is triggered sequentially after credit analysis, stalling `Committee packaging`.
4. **Control Breaches:** 5.8% of High-exposure cases violate the four-eyes principle during month-end spikes, 6.5% of cases show premature customer signing prior to KYC clearance, and 8.5% (rising to 14% for credit renewals) suffer contract-remediation loops due to discrepancies with committee decisions.

## Upstream Data Quality Dependencies
- **Intake to Classification (A1 $\rightarrow$ A2):** Application registration creates the system record. If automated registration (A1) captures unvalidated data, it directly corrupts product classification (A2).
- **Classification to Document Checklist (A2 $\rightarrow$ A5):** Document requirements are product- and exposure-specific (e.g., Equipment Finance requires equipment asset quotes; High-exposure SME requires audited balance sheets; Working Capital requires cash flow projections). A5 cannot dynamically enforce document completeness without accurate upstream classification from A2.
- **Intake Quality to Financial Normalization (A5 $\rightarrow$ A6):** Automated extraction and spreading algorithms (A6) require complete, multi-page financial statements with legible tables. Without A5 filtering out unreadable or partial uploads at the portal gate, A6 triggers high exception failure rates, dumping un-normalized files back onto credit analysts.
- **Artifact Harmonization to Committee Packaging (A4, A6, A7 $\rightarrow$ A8):** Committee pack assembly is an AND-join synchronization barrier. If KYC clearance (A4), financial spreading ratios (A6), or collateral valuations (A7) are missing or in non-standard formats, A8 either fails or propagates unverified data to credit committee members.
- **Decision Artifacts to Contract Generation (A8/Decision $\rightarrow$ A9):** Contract preparation (7,600 cases) currently suffers an 8.5% overall and 14% renewal rework loop (650 cases going to `Remediate control issue`) because credit committee covenants and modified terms are manually re-keyed by Legal Ops. A9 requires structured, machine-readable approval parameters directly from the committee decision event.

## Legal and Compliance Controls
- **Strict Precedence of KYC Clearance over Customer Signing:** Financial regulations mandate that customer identity, anti-money laundering (AML) screening, and Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) verification be finalized before a credit contract is legally executed. Currently, 6.5% of cases exhibit provisional signing prior to KYC clearance, generating severe remediation overhead (1.1% of cases). A9 and A10 must enforce a programmatic hard lock: contract documents cannot be generated or dispatched for signature without an immutable KYC clearance token from Compliance.
- **Four-Eyes Principle Enforcement:** Credit policy dictates that `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging` must never be executed by the same individual in High-exposure SME cases. Process-mining network analysis reveals that 5.8% of High-exposure cases violate this rule during month-end rush periods. Workflow automation must enforce Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) that programmatically blocks an analyst who authored credit notes from creating or approving the committee dossier for that case.
- **Auditable Non-Repudiation Trail:** All automated classifications (A2), screening matches (A3), entity reconciliations (A4), and contract clause assemblies (A9) must generate an unalterable audit log capturing model version, input parameters, confidence scores, execution timestamps, and the identity of any human specialist who approved or overrode an algorithmic recommendation.

## Human Escalation Capacity
- **Compliance Specialists (3.0 FTEs):** The handover-of-work social network shows acute vulnerability: Compliance Ops depends entirely on two senior specialists for `Resolve entity mismatch` (1,750 cases, 55 min service time, 31.0h wait). Automating front-end registration (A1) without addressing entity resolution merely queues more cases before these two individuals. A4 is critical: it acts as an assistive investigation engine, auto-fetching commercial register records and tax IDs to reduce human review time from 55 minutes to under 10 minutes.
- **Credit Analysts (9.0 FTEs):** Analysts represent the central node in the bank's collaboration graph, handling `Financial document review` (45 min, 9,200 cases), `Normalize financial statements` (75 min, 5,200 cases), and `Credit analysis` (110 min, 8,900 cases). Total annual labor required exceeds 29,700 hours against an available 9-FTE nominal capacity of ~15,750 hours. Analysts are structurally overwhelmed. Accelerating upstream volume via A1 without deploying A6 (which eliminates 6,500 hours of financial normalization) causes an analyst logjam, extending queue times beyond 28 hours and driving the month-end rush that causes four-eyes violations.
- **Legal Ops (4.0 FTEs):** Legal Ops is severely underutilized mid-month but collapses under month-end volume spikes when contracts are batched. A9 automates standard clause generation, flattening the month-end spike and eliminating 4,433 hours of repetitive drafting.

## External Valuation Dependency
- External valuation affects 2,350 cases, representing 71% of Equipment Finance and 18% of Working Capital applications.
- Currently, `Collateral valuation request` is executed strictly sequentially after `Credit analysis` completes (`Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request`: 2,400 cases). Cases wait through document review (18.0h), normalization (20.0h), and credit analysis (28.0h)—a cumulative internal delay of 66.0 hours—before the valuation is even requested. The case then waits another 12.0 hours for request processing and 58.0 hours for external valuers (`Wait for external valuation`).
- **Decoupling via A7:** Valuation triage does not require completed credit analysis notes; it requires only loan product classification, requested amount, and asset details (available immediately after A2). Deploying A7 immediately following A2 enables parallel processing: the 58.0-hour external valuation runs concurrently with internal financial statement normalization and credit review, arriving just in time for `Committee packaging`.

## Core-Banking Integration Constraints
- **IT Capacity Limit:** The IT architecture team can support at most **two deep integrations** with the core banking platform in the first six months.
- **Architectural Classification of Candidates:**
  - *Deep Core Integrations (Direct read/write to core general ledger, loan facility limits, and core CIF master):* A6 (writes normalized financial ratios and spreads into credit risk engines), A9 (reads approved credit terms and writes binding facility covenants), A11 (executes loan disbursement and books assets to GL), A1 (direct account/CIF generation), A3 (updates core customer AML risk rating).
  - *Lightweight / API-First Integrations (Operate via webhooks, external REST APIs, or secure intermediary microservices):* A4 (queries public corporate registries and tax APIs; presents findings in an operational UI), A5 (customer portal and SMS/email gateway running in cloud/DMZ), A7 (external panel valuer portal/API), A2 (stateless rules/machine learning microservice), A10 (commercial SaaS e-signature API integration).
- **Strategic Allocation:** The two available deep integration slots in the first six months must be allocated strictly to **A6 (Financial Spreading & Normalization)** and **A9 (Contract Generation & Control Checks)**. This pairing simultaneously breaks the credit analyst bottleneck and seals the critical legal/compliance control gates, while A4, A5, and A7 can be advanced via lightweight API layers.

## Portfolio 1: The Quick-Win Portfolio (The Front-End / Local Volume Strategy)
- **Selected Modules:**
  1. `A1. Auto-registration & duplicate detection` (Very High feasibility; touches 11,880 cases)
  2. `A2. Product/exposure classification recommender` (High feasibility; touches 11,820 cases)
  3. `A5. Missing-document checklist & customer portal nudging` (High feasibility; touches 3,300 loop cases)
  4. `A10. E-signature adoption & reminder automation` (High feasibility; touches 7,300 cases)
- **What it Optimizes:** Immediate visible transaction volume touched; rapid implementation velocity; relief of routine back-office clerk logging; modern customer-facing digital touchpoints at intake and signing; near-zero consumption of core-banking deep integration capacity.
- **What it Sacrifices:** Completely ignores the system's true critical path and capacity constraints. Leaves the 31.0-hour entity mismatch queue and the 20.0-hour normalization queue untouched. By accelerating front-end registration (A1) and intake completeness (A5), it pours high-velocity work directly into an already overwhelmed credit analyst pool (9 FTEs) and compliance specialist pool (3 FTEs). Crucially, it fails Compliance’s first-wave mandate because it implements no modules that strengthen internal controls (A10 accelerates signing but does not prevent the 6.5% premature signing control violation).

## Portfolio 2: The Bottleneck / Synchronization Portfolio (The Throughput & Theory of Constraints Strategy)
- **Selected Modules:**
  1. `A4. Entity-mismatch resolution assistant` (Attacks 31.0h wait and 38% rework on 3 FTE specialists)
  2. `A5. Missing-document checklist & customer portal nudging` (Eliminates 52% repeated document loops; protects analysts)
  3. `A6. Financial-statement extraction & normalization` (Frees 6,500 analyst hours; removes 20.0h queue)
  4. `A7. Collateral valuation triage & valuer routing` (Decouples 58.0h wait into parallel track for Equipment Finance)
- **What it Optimizes:** End-to-end critical path turnaround time; elimination of the bank's two primary operational bottlenecks (Compliance Specialists and Credit Analysts); synchronization of parallel streams at the `Committee packaging` AND-join; dramatic reduction of case idle waiting time (31.0h + 20.0h + 58.0h).
- **What it Sacrifices:** Politically difficult: completely skips the high-frequency front-end registration task (A1), which management originally expected to automate. Leaves downstream `Contract preparation` (A9) as a manual activity for Legal Ops, meaning contract-remediation loops (8.5%–14%) persist until Year 2. Relies on external valuer operational alignment for A7.

## Portfolio 3: The Compliance-First Portfolio (The Governance & Regulatory Risk Strategy)
- **Selected Modules:**
  1. `A2. Product/exposure classification recommender` (Prevents misrouting and incorrect governance tiering)
  2. `A3. KYC hit pre-classifier with human escalation` (Strengthens AML/PEP screening; filters false hits)
  3. `A4. Entity-mismatch resolution assistant` (Resolves beneficial ownership discrepancies on 1,750 cases)
  4. `A9. Contract generation with control-rule checks` (Hard-locks KYC-before-signing; eliminates contract rework)
- **What it Optimizes:** Total statutory and regulatory integrity; eradication of AML/KYC failure risks; elimination of unauthorized provisional signings (6.5% error rate $\rightarrow$ 0%); structural elimination of contract-to-committee term discrepancies (650 remediation cases eliminated); automated audit logging for supervisory authorities; full compliance with the four-eyes mandate.
- **What it Sacrifices:** Operational throughput in credit evaluation is largely ignored. Credit analysts receive zero labor relief: `Normalize financial statements` remains a grueling 75-minute manual task (5,200 cases), and the 18.0-hour document review queue continues to loop. External valuation remains sequential, leaving the 58.0-hour wait intact before committee packaging. Customer signing latency (44.0h) remains unmitigated.

## Portfolio 1: Quick-Win Portfolio
- **Cycle Time & Waiting Time:** Accelerates front-end registration by 1.5 hours (`Register application` wait: 1.5h $\rightarrow$ 0.1h). Reduces signing wait (`Customer signing`: 44.0h $\rightarrow$ 12.0h) via automated e-sign nudges. However, overall case duration decreases by only 10%–15% because cases slam into the unaddressed queues: `KYC check` (4.0h), `Resolve entity mismatch` (31.0h), `Financial document review` (18.0h), `Normalize financial statements` (20.0h), `Credit analysis` (28.0h), and `Wait for external valuation` (58.0h). Total median waiting time across the flow drops by only ~45 hours out of >250 cumulative process hours.
- **Rework Loops:** A5 successfully collapses the `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` loop (3,300 cases) and its return path (2,800 cases), reducing document chasing loops by ~70%. However, the `KYC check <-> Resolve entity mismatch` loop (1,160 cases) and the `Contract preparation <-> Remediate control issue` loop (650 cases) remain completely unmitigated.
- **Handovers & Resource Demand:** Back-office clerk utilization drops further below 65%, creating idle labor in non-bottleneck roles. Conversely, because applications enter the system faster, Work-In-Progress (WIP) expands in front of Credit Analysts (9 FTEs) and Compliance Specialists (3 FTEs). Inflow to analysts increases by ~25% without any automation of spreading or credit review, exacerbating analyst burnout.
- **Control Violations:** Unfavorable impact. Rapid front-end throughput combined with unmitigated analyst bottlenecks intensifies the month-end backlog rush, increasing the 5.8% four-eyes violations. Accelerating signing via A10 without a hard KYC-clearance lock increases the risk of provisional signing prior to KYC completion beyond the current 6.5% baseline.

## Portfolio 2: Bottleneck / Synchronization Portfolio
- **Cycle Time & Waiting Time:** Yields the largest end-to-end duration collapse, reducing overall loan origination cycle time by 40%–50%. Directly eliminates the three largest queue barriers:
  - `Resolve entity mismatch` queue: 31.0h $\rightarrow$ 4.0h (saving 27h on 82% of critical path cases where present).
  - `Normalize financial statements` queue: 20.0h $\rightarrow$ 2.0h (saving 18h across 5,200 cases).
  - `Wait for external valuation` synchronization delay: 58.0h wait is removed from the critical path for Equipment Finance (71%) and Working Capital (18%) by shifting valuation triage (A7) upstream, allowing external inspection to run concurrently with internal financial spreading. Cumulative case waiting time decreases by over 115 hours.
- **Rework Loops:** A5 collapses the document ping-pong loop (3,300 $\rightarrow$ 2,800 cases). A4 attacks the 38% rework rate on entity mismatches, cutting the return loop `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` (1,160 cases) by an estimated 65% through automated registry matching. However, the downstream contract rework loop (`Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue`: 650 cases; 8.5%–14%) remains active.
- **Handovers & Resource Demand:** Delivers targeted relief to the bank's two most constrained talent pools:
  - *Credit Analysts (9 FTEs):* A6 removes 6,500 hours of manual statement spreading (75 min $\rightarrow$ 10 min verification), effectively freeing ~3.7 FTEs. This newly available capacity enables analysts to absorb incoming credit reviews without overtime.
  - *Compliance Specialists (3 FTEs):* A4 auto-populates registry filings, reducing average handling time from 55 min to 15 min, eliminating the specialist queue.
- **Control Violations:** Indirectly mitigates four-eyes violations (5.8%) by eradicating the analyst capacity deficit that causes month-end panic. However, it lacks a programmatic software gate to enforce KYC precedence over signing, leaving the 6.5% provisional signing defect dependent on manual compliance checks.

## Portfolio 3: Compliance-First Portfolio
- **Cycle Time & Waiting Time:** Modest overall cycle time reduction (15%–20%). `Resolve entity mismatch` wait falls from 31.0h to 8.0h via A4. `Contract preparation` wait falls from 22.0h to 4.0h via A9. However, the largest queues in the process—document review (18.0h), normalization (20.0h), credit analysis (28.0h), and external valuation (58.0h)—remain unchanged. Total waiting time reduction is capped at ~50 hours.
- **Rework Loops:** Highly effective at eliminating regulatory and legal rework. Eradicates the contract remediation loop (`Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue`: 650 cases; 8.5% overall and 14% renewal) through automated template generation based on approved committee parameters. A4 cuts the entity mismatch loop (1,160 cases) by 60%. However, the document request loop (3,300 $\rightarrow$ 2,800 cases) remains completely manual.
- **Handovers & Resource Demand:** Eliminates 4,433 hours of manual drafting in Legal Ops (4 FTEs), smoothing their month-end capacity. Compliance specialists (3 FTEs) are substantially unburdened by A3 and A4. However, Credit Analysts (9 FTEs) receive zero labor relief, remaining severely bottlenecked.
- **Control Violations:** Complete structural eradication of control breaches:
  - *Provisional Signing (6.5% $\rightarrow$ 0%):* A9 enforces a hard database gate: contracts cannot be drafted or sent without a cryptographically verified KYC clearance token.
  - *Contract Discrepancies (8.5% $\rightarrow$ <0.5%):* A9 generates contracts directly from structured committee decision objects, preventing clause and covenant discrepancies.
  - *Four-Eyes Principle (5.8% $\rightarrow$ 0%):* A2 and workflow controls enforce hard segregation of duties between credit evaluation and committee pack approval.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

Deploying automation across credit evaluation and legal drafting introduces distinct operational, algorithmic, and regulatory risks. The table below outlines these risks alongside mandatory compensating controls.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    RISK & COMPENSATING CONTROL MATRIX                              |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
| RISK CATEGORY            | IMPACTED MODULES      | COMPENSATING CONTROL ARCHITECTURE               |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
| Auditability & Trace     | A2, A3, A4, A6, A9    | Immutable Event Sourcing & Decision Logging     |
| False Negatives (AML)    | A3 (KYC Pre-classifier)| Asymmetric Thresholds & 100% Dual-Signoff       |
| Extraction Errors (OCR)  | A6 (Normalization)    | Confidence Heatmaps & Human Verification UI     |
| Model Drift & Rule Decay | A2, A3, A6, A9        | Bi-Annual Legal Audits & Champion/Challenger    |
| Exception Handling       | All Modules           | Deterministic Graceful Fallback & SLA Routing   |
| Four-Eyes Compliance     | Workflow / A8 / A9    | Programmatic RBAC Identity Locking              |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
```

## 1. Auditability, Non-Repudiation, and Explainability
- **Operational Risk:** Black-box automated classifications (A2), sanctions clearance (A3), or algorithmic covenant selections (A9) cannot be explained during regulatory audits or loan default litigation.
- **Compensating Control:** Implement an immutable, append-only decision log (Event Sourcing). For every automated action, the system must persist: (a) raw input payload hash, (b) model/ruleset version ID, (c) intermediate feature vectors and confidence scores, (d) exact rule branches triggered, (e) execution timestamp, and (f) user credential of any human confirming or overriding the recommendation. Automated recommendations must be rendered in natural-language explainability summaries for credit officers.

## 2. False Positives, False Negatives, and Misclassification
- **Operational Risk:** In A3 (KYC screening), a false negative releases a sanctioned entity or Politically Exposed Person (PEP), incurring catastrophic regulatory penalties. In A6 (Financial Normalization), an OCR misread transposes numbers or misinterprets negative cash flows, leading to flawed credit decisions.
- **Compensating Control:**
  - *Asymmetric Risk Thresholding (A3):* The screening engine is tuned aggressively toward high recall (zero tolerance for false negatives). Clear negative classifications are automated only at a >99.8% statistical confidence margin; any borderline hit or entity ambiguity is escalated to Compliance Specialists with pre-compiled evidence dossiers.
  - *Human-in-the-Loop Visual Verification (A6):* Spreading automation does not auto-commit directly to the credit model. Instead, it populates a dual-pane verification interface displaying the original PDF side-by-side with extracted values. Extracted fields with confidence scores below 95% are highlighted in amber/red, requiring explicit analyst confirmation.

## 3. Model Drift and Regulatory Rule Decay
- **Operational Risk:** Changes in corporate tax filing formats degrade A6 extraction accuracy; updates to national credit laws or AML directives render A2/A3/A9 rule libraries obsolete, leading to non-compliant originations.
- **Compensating Control:** Establish a formal Model Risk Management (MRM) governance schedule. Run quarterly drift detection analyzing extraction exception rates and rule override frequencies. Mandate a bi-annual legal review of A9 contract template libraries and covenant rules. Maintain a "champion-challenger" parallel testing environment where updated models process production copies for 30 days prior to promotion.

## 4. Exception Handling and Graceful Degradation
- **Operational Risk:** External APIs (commercial registries in A4, panel valuers in A7, e-signature in A10) experience latency spikes or service outages, stranding cases in unmonitored system deadlocks.
- **Compensating Control:** Implement circuit-breaker architecture with deterministic fallback queues. If an external API query exceeds a 15-second timeout, the case is automatically routed to the specialist exception pool with an explanatory ticket. Every automated module must feature a manual bypass mode allowing authorized supervisors to transition a case to standard manual workflows without data loss.

## 5. Four-Eyes Principle Enforcement
- **Operational Risk:** Analysts under month-end pressure bypass segregation-of-duties controls, performing both credit evaluation and committee dossier packaging on High-exposure SME cases (currently occurring in 5.8% of cases).
- **Compensating Control:** Enforce programmatic separation of duties via Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) at the database layer. The workflow engine captures the unique employee ID of the analyst signing off on `Credit analysis`. If that same user attempts to initiate, edit, or submit `Committee packaging` for a High-exposure case, the system hard-blocks the transaction, logs an audit alert, and mandates assignment to an independent peer analyst.

## Why the Highest-Frequency Activity (A1) Must NOT Be Automated First

Senior management’s proposal to automate `Register application` (11,880 cases) first is an example of the **Local Efficiency Fallacy**. Grounded in Goldratt’s Theory of Constraints and Little’s Law, the following arguments demonstrate why automating A1 first would degrade, rather than improve, operational performance:

1. **Automation of Non-Bottlenecks Yields Zero Throughput Gain:**
   - In any dependent process network, the throughput of the entire system is strictly governed by the capacity of its bottlenecks.
   - `Register application` is performed by the Back Office pool (12.0 FTEs). The activity requires only 8 minutes of service time and exhibits a negligible median waiting time of 1.5 hours.
   - Crucially, social network and resource utilization analysis reveals that **back-office utilization drops below 65% after 15:00 every day**.
   - Saving 8 minutes per case on an activity that already possesses surplus afternoon capacity generates phantom savings. It liberates hours for staff who are already underutilized, delivering **zero incremental throughput** for the bank.

2. **Little's Law and the "WIP Explosion" Trap:**
   - Little’s Law defines the relationship between Work-In-Progress ($WIP$), Throughput ($TH$), and Cycle Time ($CT$):
     $$CT = \frac{WIP}{TH}$$
   - Accelerating application registration (A1) increases the injection velocity of cases into the system. However, the downstream capacities of Compliance Specialists (3.0 FTEs) and Credit Analysts (9.0 FTEs) remain completely fixed and severely overloaded.
   - Accelerating the intake gate without expanding downstream bottleneck capacity causes a rapid accumulation of Work-In-Progress immediately behind `Register application`.
   - By Little’s Law, when $WIP$ expands in front of a fixed-capacity bottleneck, **waiting times must increase proportionally**. Pushing applications into the system 1.5 hours faster simply transfers those files into the 31.0-hour entity mismatch queue and the 20.0-hour financial normalization queue, lengthening end-to-end customer cycle times.

3. **Intensifying Upstream Flow Induces Downstream Control Failures:**
   - The 5.8% violation of the four-eyes principle in High-exposure SME cases and the 6.5% provisional signing anomaly are not caused by malicious intent; they are **stress-induced failure modes** triggered by month-end volume spikes.
   - When large batches of un-triaged cases flood Credit Analysts who are already underwater with 75-minute manual statement spreading, analysts cut corners to clear the queue, bypassing segregation-of-duties checks.
   - Automating A1 first directly amplifies this backlog pressure. It accelerates the rate at which cases pile up at the analyst desk, actively exacerbating the conditions that produce four-eyes violations and compliance breaches.

4. **The Strategic Alignment:**
   - True process excellence dictates that the organization must **pace the front of the line to the capacity of the bottleneck** (Drum-Buffer-Rope) while systematically elevating the bottleneck's capacity.
   - By prioritizing **A4, A5, A6, and A9**, the bank relieves its true capacity constraints, eliminates systemic rework loops, protects its specialist staff, and establishes an uncompromised regulatory control framework. `Register application` (A1) can then be integrated seamlessly in Year 2 as a trivial enhancement to an already synchronized, high-velocity process.
