## 1. Feasibility model

```text

Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check only if no originals are required
Triage < Originals Receipt if originals are required
Originals Receipt < Document Check if originals are required
Identity Check may proceed before Originals Receipt when originals are required

Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment

Assessment < Decision for non-high-risk / non-high-value cases
Assessment < Compliance Review < Decision for high-risk or high-value cases

Request More Info may follow Assessment or Compliance Review
Request More Info < new Document Check < new Assessment
For high-risk / high-value cases:
    new Assessment < new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review < Decision

Preliminary Escalation / Preliminary Compliance Screen may occur after Triage and may overlap with Identity Check, Document Check, or Assessment
Preliminary Compliance Screen < final Compliance Review only as preparation; it does not replace final Compliance Review

Decision < Notify
```

```text
Declarative and conditional constraints:

1. Four-eyes rule: the final Assessment and final Compliance Review must be performed by different persons.
2. A resource cannot perform two service intervals at the same time.
3. Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees; actual start times depend on dynamic queue state, priority, and resource availability.
4. Identity Check and Document Check may be concurrent only when no original document is required.
5. When originals are required, Document Check cannot begin before Originals Receipt, even if Identity Check is complete.
6. A preliminary compliance screen may reduce later Compliance Review duration, but cannot substitute for the final Compliance Review.
7. After any Request More Info, a new Document Check and new Assessment are required before Decision; high-risk / high-value cases also require a new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review.
8. K-104 intervention changes preparation and scheduling, not the mandatory final compliance sequence.
9. K-219 intervention changes document-resource routing and may change rework probability.
10. K-337 intervention changes the decision policy by forbidding a second Request More Info loop after Assessment 2.
11. K-411 intervention changes task creation and logging discipline; it does not change substantive precedence rules.
```

The K-104 intervention is primarily a timing and preparation change: it adds early escalation and preliminary compliance work while preserving the final sequence. The K-219 intervention is a routing change: it substitutes D2 for D1 and therefore changes both timing and quality risk. The K-337 intervention is a decision-policy change: it removes an information-request loop and forces a decision under residual uncertainty. The K-411 intervention is a timing, concurrency, and logging change: it allows legally permissible verification work to overlap and requires real-time timestamps.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The case is high-risk and high-value, so final Compliance Review is mandatory. The factual path delays escalation until after Assessment. Triage notices sanctions ambiguity but does not escalate; Assessment later rediscovers the same issue. S1 advice then occurs after Assessment, and C1 starts late because the compliance queue is congested. The main delay is not the intrinsic compliance work alone, but the late formation of a compliance-ready package.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create a preliminary escalation packet for S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot. S1 performs preliminary issue framing and evidence indexing while Identity Check and Document Check proceed. The final Compliance Review still occurs after the final Assessment and is performed by C1, not by A1 or S1.

Temporal propagation: The intervention does not materially accelerate Identity or Document Check, because those queues are already constrained. Its main effect is to remove the post-Assessment serial escalation step and to reduce C1’s later reading burden. If Assessment still completes around Tuesday 19:10, a reserved C1 slot on Wednesday morning could start earlier than the factual 13:10 start. Preliminary work may also reduce C1 service time from the factual 4.5 h toward 3–4 h. Decision and Notify can then follow shortly after Compliance Review.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible. Preliminary compliance work is allowed, provided it does not replace the final Compliance Review. Final Compliance Review occurs after the latest Assessment. A1 performs final Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance Review, satisfying the four-eyes rule. S1 advisory work may overlap with other checks.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: C1 starts Wednesday 08:00, preliminary work saves 2–3 h, and Decision/Notify occur around midday, saving roughly 8–10 h. Base case: C1 starts Wednesday morning but preliminary evidence is partially incomplete, saving 4–7 h. Worst case: the reserved slot cannot be used because Assessment is delayed or the packet is too incomplete; C1 starts near the factual time or later, saving 0–2 h while delaying other cases.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that C1 reservations substantially delay higher-priority compliance cases, that preliminary packets are frequently incomplete, that S1 cannot produce useful framing before Assessment, or that early sanctions ambiguity requires external clarification not available before Assessment would weaken or reverse the expected benefit.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The case is not high-risk or high-value, but originals are required and the documents are handwritten. Triage routes the Document Check to D1 because handwriting may be difficult. Originals arrive Monday 17:30, but D1 does not start until Tuesday 09:30. The factual delay is mainly a D1 queue and routing delay, not a long intrinsic document-check duration.

Minimal intervention: Once Originals Receipt occurs, assign the Document Check to D2 instead of waiting for D1. This changes routing but not the mandatory precedence: Originals Receipt must still precede Document Check, and both Identity Check and Document Check must still complete before Assessment.

Temporal propagation: D2 may start earlier than D1, potentially Monday evening or Tuesday morning depending on D2’s queue. If D2 takes 5–7 h on standard but handwritten documents, Document Check could complete earlier than the factual Tuesday 13:30 completion. However, earlier document completion only helps if A2 and M1 can absorb the earlier readiness. If A2’s queue remains congested, the case may still wait until Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning. If D2 requests clarification, the case re-enters Document Check and Assessment, adding external or internal waiting time.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible. Originals arrive before Document Check. Identity Check completes before Assessment. No Compliance Review is required. If a clarification request is treated as Request More Info, a new Document Check and new Assessment are required before Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: D2 starts soon after originals, completes overnight or early Tuesday, A2 assesses Tuesday afternoon, and Decision/Notify occur Tuesday evening, saving 10–16 h. Base case: D2 completes earlier than D1 but A2/M1 schedules do not fully absorb the gain, saving 0–8 h. Worst case: D2 cannot read the handwriting, requests clarification, and rework adds 1–3 business days.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that D2’s actual queue is longer than forecast, that D2’s clarification rate on handwritten documents is high, that A2/M1 operate in fixed batches, or that D1’s freed capacity cannot be used on higher-value complex work would reduce or reverse the expected benefit.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The case is high-risk and high-value, with originals and a slow, inconsistent external supplier. Assessment 1 triggers Request More Info 1. Document Check 2 still leaves one inconsistency, and Assessment 2 requests another confirmation. The second external response crosses a weekend, causing a large delay and a third Document Check/Assessment cycle before Compliance Review and Decision.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop. The case must proceed to Compliance Review and Decision using the evidence already available. The decision may approve with conditions or reject, but it must explicitly record the unresolved discrepancy rather than silently ignore it.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 completes Friday 10:30. In the counterfactual, the case can move directly to C1 instead of entering a weekend-delayed external request. If C1 can review Friday afternoon, Decision could occur Friday evening. If C1 capacity is unavailable until Monday, Decision may occur Monday afternoon. Either way, the intervention avoids the external wait, the third Document Check, and the third Assessment. The largest saving is not the internal processing time alone, but the avoided weekend and external-response dead time.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible because Request More Info 2 is not issued. Assessment 2 is therefore the latest relevant Assessment. A high-risk/high-value case still receives Compliance Review after Assessment 2 and before Decision. A1 performs Assessment and C1 performs Compliance Review, satisfying the four-eyes rule. The intervention changes decision policy, not the mandatory compliance sequence.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: C1 reviews Friday afternoon, M1 decides Friday evening, and the case finishes 3–4 business days earlier. Base case: C1 reviews Monday, Decision occurs Monday afternoon, saving about 1.5–2 business days. Worst case: the conditional decision or rejection creates appeal, loss exposure, or post-decision monitoring that offsets the cycle-time gain.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that the unresolved discrepancy is legally material, that compliance policy requires external confirmation, that conditional approvals create high monitoring costs, or that C1/M1 cannot act quickly after Assessment 2 would reverse or reduce the expected benefit.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The case is low-risk, digital, and standard, with no originals required. The legacy system creates only the identity task, and a case lock prevents document task ownership. Identity Check therefore completes before Document Check begins. The factual path is artificially serial even though the process rules allow concurrent verification when originals are not required.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create both Identity Check and Document Check tasks. Use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can own and execute their tasks concurrently. Require real-time timestamps so the concurrency is auditable and not reconstructed later.

Temporal propagation: If both tasks are created at Tuesday 09:35, Identity Check can enter the I2 queue and Document Check can enter the D2 queue immediately. Identity Check may complete around midday, while Document Check can proceed independently. The case becomes assessment-ready when both checks complete, likely earlier than the factual Tuesday 19:30 document completion. However, the final benefit depends on A2’s assessment queue. If A2 is congested, earlier readiness may not produce earlier Decision. If A2 and M1 can absorb the earlier readiness, the case may finish several hours earlier.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible because no originals are required, so concurrent Identity and Document work is allowed. Assessment still occurs after both checks. No Compliance Review is required. Real-time timestamps preserve auditability and prevent false concurrency.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: both checks complete by late Tuesday afternoon, A2 assesses early Wednesday or Tuesday evening, and Decision/Notify move forward 4–6 h. Base case: Document Check completes modestly earlier but A2 queue absorbs little, saving 0–3 h. Worst case: queues do not move, logging overhead adds time, or poor timestamping creates audit rework.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that A2 is the binding bottleneck, that D2’s queue is longer than forecast, that the workaround creates ownership conflicts, or that timestamps are batch-entered despite the rule would weaken or reverse the expected benefit.

If applied to 100 similar cases in one week, the interventions stop being local. Early escalation/reservation for K-104 creates a protected claim on C1, the single certified compliance reviewer. For a few high-risk cases this may reduce waiting and improve preparation, but at scale reservation fragments C1’s calendar, increases context switching, and displaces urgent unplanned compliance work. The apparent target-case saving can become system-wide delay if reserved slots are held while evidence is incomplete. A better policy is a dynamic priority rule with a short-lived option, not a hard booking.

D2 substitution for K-219 shifts demand from D1 to D2. Because D1 is faster on complex work, keeping D1 free can help complex corporate cases, but if many handwritten standard cases are rerouted to D2, D2’s queue and clarification requests can grow. The net effect depends on the mix: if most handwriting is legible, cycle time improves; if not, rework loops erase the gain and create repeated assessment demand.

Suppressing the second RMI in K-337 avoids external and weekend delays, but it converts an information problem into a decision-risk problem. Across 100 slow-supplier cases, fewer document checks and assessments are performed, yet conditional approvals or rejections may increase appeals, monitoring, and loss exposure. The weekend effect is large: avoiding Friday requests prevents weekend dead time, but the policy may be unsafe where confirmation is material.

Parallel verification in K-411 increases simultaneous demand for I2 and D2 and moves readiness earlier. The bottleneck may migrate to A2 and M1. If 100 digital standard cases become parallel, assessment receives a larger, earlier wave; without added assessment capacity, queue time rises and the local gain disappears. Real-time timestamps are essential: batch entry would make concurrency look false, trigger rework, and undermine auditability. Overall, only interventions that respect the scarce bottleneck and avoid quality rework scale safely.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411’s parallel verification with real-time timestamps. It respects the existing control-flow, does not change the decision policy, and mainly removes an artificial serialization caused by the legacy lock. Its expected gain is modest and contingent on assessment capacity, but its downside is limited and reversible if logs are clean. K-104 is attractive for high-risk cases because preliminary escalation can reduce compliance preparation time and improve issue framing, but it is less transportable because it relies on reserving C1, a single bottleneck resource.

The most dangerous intervention is K-337. It produces the largest apparent cycle-time reduction by deleting a weekend-delayed rework loop, but it does so by changing the decision policy under unresolved evidence. The speed gain can be real, yet the tail risk—conditional approval, rejection, appeals, loss exposure, and weaker compliance defensibility—may dominate the operational benefit.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104’s reserved C1 slot. In a single case, holding capacity can look beneficial because the target case jumps a queue. System-wide, the same reservation can delay other urgent compliance cases and create idle slots when evidence is incomplete. K-337 is also poorly transportable because one case’s acceptable risk may not define a safe policy for all slow-supplier cases.

The most valuable additional data are event logs with resource-level busy intervals, queue-entry and queue-exit timestamps, reason codes for rework, external request/response times, C1 reservation/no-show data, and assessment-queue length by hour. These data distinguish earlier readiness from earlier completion and reveal bottleneck migration.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. The mandatory precedence rules—Register before Triage, Identity and Document completion before Assessment, and Decision before Notify—apply unchanged except where an intervention explicitly modifies task creation or decision policy. `structural`

2. High-risk and high-value cases require a final Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment, and the four-eyes rule cannot be waived by preliminary work. `structural`

3. The snapshot queue estimates are forecasts, not deterministic waiting times; actual starts depend on arrivals, priorities, interruptions, and shift patterns. `temporal`

4. C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer in the window, so any reservation or acceleration for one case necessarily consumes capacity that could have served another case. `resource`

5. D2 can perform standard document checks but is slower than D1 on complex or difficult handwritten documents and may have a higher clarification probability. `resource`

6. S1 preliminary escalation packets may be incomplete, and assessors may vary in how much they rely on preliminary compliance framing. `behavioral`

7. Suppressing a Request More Info loop changes the evidentiary basis of the decision and may increase appeal risk, loss exposure, or compliance defensibility problems. `quality`

8. Local acceleration can move congestion elsewhere: parallel verification can load Assessment earlier, and C1 reservations can displace other urgent compliance work. `interference`
