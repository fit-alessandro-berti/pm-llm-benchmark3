# 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case is not a discovered truth but a *projection rule*: pick an object type, and every event directly related to an object of that type becomes an event of that object's trace. The case notion therefore determines which questions are even expressible. I treat "end-to-end" as a claim that must be qualified by its anchor, not a property of the log.

**Convergence.** One event related to 2 objects of the *same* type. Under flattening it is copied into each case. Here: `e16`/`e24` relate O1, O2, O3 (fan-out 3); `e13`, `e14`, `e17`, `e19`, `e22`, `e25`, `e27` fan out 2 on Order.

**Divergence.** One object related to 2 events of the *same* activity, producing repetition inside a single trace. Here: O1 packs twice (`e11`, `e12`) and dispatches twice (`e15`, `e16`); O3 packs, dispatches, confirms and pays twice; V2 records payment twice (`e22`, `e25`).

**Copied events are both evidence and duplication.** `e13` in O2's trace is genuine evidence that O2 was packed at 10:12; it is duplication when counted as a second packing operation. I never delete copies silently — I carry `ocel:eid` multiplicity as an annotation.

**Permitted transformations.** (a) *Scoped upward propagation* of order-exclusive activities (Create Order, Issue Invoice, Record Payment, Close Order) from Order to its Items via `contains`. (b) O2O enrichment for load composition (D2P2,P3I12,I21,I31O1,O2,O3). (c) **Forbidden by default:** naive transitive closure. The O2O graph here is a *single connected component* containing all 24 objects, so closure would give every object all 31 events and make all six flattenings identical. (d) De-duplication keys must be `ocel:eid`: `e29` and `e30` share activity *and* timestamp (11:05), so (activity, timestamp) de-duplication would wrongly merge two distinct Close Order events.

# 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**D1 — Cross-order package P3 manufactures a packing loop (Order flattening).** `e13` (10:12) is copied into O2 and O3. In O3 it is immediately followed by `e14` (10:20), producing a `Pack Package  Pack Package` self-loop (also in O1 via `e11`,`e12`). Read as rework this is false: two distinct parcels. Worse, O2's trace shows packing at 10:12 with no indication that it waited for O3's I31 (`e08`, 09:22). **Harmful** for rework metrics, **informative** for cross-order blocking once annotated.

**D2 — Cross-order package P4 contaminates O4's delivery performance (Order flattening).** `e14`, `e17`, `e27` are shared by O3 and O4. O4's Confirm Delivery is `e27` (Feb-4 10:00), inherited from a parcel it shares with O3. Yet O4's total cycle time is 26h04 — essentially identical to O1's 26h00 — because Close Order is batched at 11:00–11:10 on Feb-4. The Order log therefore reports near-zero variance while physical parcel durations range 4h00 (P1) to 23h40 (P4). **Harmful**: a 6× operational spread is erased by an administrative batching artifact.

**D3 — Cross-order packages inflate handling counts (Package and Item flattening).** In the Package log, `e16` and `e24` appear under both P2 and P3: Dispatch count 4 vs 3 actual (+33%). In the Item log, `e16` appears under I12, I21 and I31: Dispatch count 6 vs 3 (+100%). A "shipments per day" KPI is anchor-dependent by a factor of two. **Context-dependent** — correct as "parcel dispatch participations", wrong as "trucks sent".

**D4 — Consolidated delivery D2 destroys transit measurement (Order flattening).** `e16` (11:05) and `e24` (16:00) each carry O1, O2, O3. Because Issue Invoice (`e18`/`e19`) and Record Payment (`e21`/`e22`) interleave, there is **no** DispatchConfirm Delivery edge in the Order DFG at all; transit time is fragmented across DSIIRPCD. A naive first-dispatch/last-confirm calculation gives O1 5h00 (true I11 transit 3h00) and O3 22h55 (true I31 transit 4h55): mean 13.9h vs the Delivery log's 10.2h (+36%). **Harmful.**

**D5 — Consolidated delivery D2 is invisible as consolidation (Delivery flattening).** D1, D2, D3 all produce the identical two-event trace DispatchConfirm Delivery. D2 carries P2+P3, three items and three orders; D1 carries one of each. Cost-per-delivery or utilisation analysis treats them as equal. **Harmful unless** enriched by D2P2,P3O1,O2,O3 traversal.

**D6 — Consolidated invoicing V2 fabricates duplicate payments (Order flattening).** `e22` and `e25` are copied into both O2 and O3, so each order individually shows two Record Payment events; log-wide payment count is 6 vs 4 actual (+50%). A duplicate-payment detector flags O2 and O3 — 100% false positives. O2 also appears to pay on Feb-4 09:00 although all its goods were confirmed at `e24` (16:00 Feb-3); the delay belongs to O3's invoice share, not to O2. **Harmful.**

**D7 — Partial payments: the same fact is three different artifacts.** Invoice flattening renders V2 as `Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Record Payment`, a legitimate second variant (V1/V3 have the two-event variant). Order flattening renders it as a *cycle*: O3 gives RP(`e22`)CD(`e24`)RP(`e25`)CD(`e27`), yielding 5 RPCD edges against 2 CDRP edges — a "delivery then payment then delivery" loop with no causal reality, since finance and logistics are parallel branches. Payment flattening renders it as two unrelated singletons M2, M3. Choosing last vs first payment as case end moves mean DSO from 7.47h to 14.25h (+91%). **Informative** in the Invoice view, **harmful** in the Order view.

**D8 — Near-singleton lifecycles (Payment and Delivery flattening).** M1–M4 each relate to exactly one event (`e21`,`e22`,`e25`,`e26`): the flattened DFG has zero edges, the log skeleton says only "Record Payment exactly 1", and mean case duration is 0. Delivery is barely better (2 events, 1 variant, n=3), where D3's 22h45 against D1's 3h00 invites an SLA-breach conclusion from three observations with heterogeneous loads. **Harmful** if reported as process performance; Payment is legitimately a ledger.

**D9 — Item flattening hides both convergence and divergence.** Six identical traces `Add Item  Pack Package  Dispatch  Confirm Delivery`, one variant, no loops. It cannot express that I11 and I12 are one order split across P1/P2 (divergence) or that I21 and I31 share P3 (convergence). It also omits 15/31 events including every financial control. Concluding "the process is a single straight-through variant with no rework" is **harmful**.

**D10 — Item flow-time bimodality misattributed to the SKU.** I31 = 6h38 and I32 = 24h37, added one minute apart (`e08` 09:22, `e09` 09:23) for the *same* order O3. Mean item flow time 12h20 vs median 6h41 (+85%), driven entirely by P4/D3. **Context-dependent**: informative for parcel routing, harmful as an item-level SLA.

**D11 — Variant and "complexity" illusion (Order flattening).** Four cases, four distinct variants; no variant has frequency >1. O3's 13-event trace is the longest, but O3 is merely the hub: it touches P3, P4, D2, D3 and V2, and shares 6 events with O2, 3 with O4, 2 with O1. Length here measures connectivity, not complexity. **Harmful** if used to justify the lead object.

**D12 — Invoice-before-delivery is unanswerable in the Invoice view.** `e19` (12:05 Feb-3) precedes `e27` (Feb-4 10:00) for O3's I32, but the Invoice log contains no delivery events. The compliance question requires V2O3I32P4D3 propagation or an OC-DFG. **Context-dependent** (a genuine control gap, invisible by construction).

# 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Create four flattened logs, each with a declared scope and a copy-multiplicity column.**

- **Order log (42 events):** safe for milestone existence and completeness (all 31 events covered), for "was every order created, invoiced, paid, closed", and for sequence auditing *with copies labelled*. Unsafe for any duration, rework count, or duplicate-payment claim (D2, D4, D6, D7).
- **Package log (12 events):** safe for packdispatchconfirm handling times (P1 4h00 … P4 23h40) and for identifying P4 as the outlier. Unsafe for per-order service levels.
- **Delivery log (6 events):** safe for transit duration per shipment, reported with n=3 and load composition attached. Unsafe as a process model.
- **Invoice log (7 events):** safe for AR ageing, instalment behaviour, first-vs-final settlement. Unsafe for fulfilment compliance.
- **Item log:** create only as a labelled four-activity sub-process for item flow-time distribution; never as "the process".
- **Payment:** do not create a process log. Keep M1–M4 as a transaction table joined to V1–V3.

**Questions requiring object-centric artifacts.**
- *Where do orders interfere?*  **OC-DFG** plus **objects interactions summary**: O2–O3 co-occur in 6 events (`e13,e16,e19,e22,e24,e25`), O3–O4 in 3, O1–O2 and O1–O3 in 2 each; O3 is the hub. No flattened log produces this ranking.
- *Which consolidation structures exist?*  **OTG**: P3–{I21,I31}, P4–{I32,I41}, D2–{P2,P3}, V2–{O2,O3}, V2–{M2,M3}.
- *Why do five of six flattenings truncate?*  **ET–OT graph**: Create Order and Close Order attach only to Order; Pack Package attaches to Order/Item/Package; Record Payment to Order/Invoice/Payment. Truncation is a structural property, not a data-quality defect.
- *Can objects be pooled?*  **equivalent-execution clustering**, used as a check rather than a case definition (see §5/§6).

**Reconciling contradictions.** Rule: a flattened finding is accepted only if it survives re-expression as an object-centric statement. "O3 has a payment–delivery loop" fails (OC-DFG shows two parallel branches). "P4 is slow" survives (Package and Delivery views agree). "Duplicate payments on O2/O3" fails (OTG shows one V2 with two settlements). Where both survive but differ numerically (13.9h vs 10.2h transit), the object-centric figure wins and the flattened figure is retained as an explicitly labelled anchor-specific metric.

# 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B — Order as primary lead, with explicitly secondary Package/Delivery and Invoice/Payment lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria.** (1) Event and activity coverage; (2) semantic ownership of the customer promise; (3) duplication cost; (4) KPI validity; (5) stability under propagation; (6) whether the type can index every event for audit.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Order is the only *covering* type: all 31 events relate to at least one Order, and Order participates in all 8 activities. Every alternative discards 48% of events (Item 16/31, Package 10/31, Invoice 7/31, Delivery 6/31, Payment 4/31). Create Order and Close Order exist only on Order, so no other type can express start and end. This is a coverage argument, **not** a trace-length argument — O3's 13 events are hub artifacts (D11).

**Strongest counterargument.** Order is simultaneously the worst type on every distortion axis: 11 phantom copies (1.35×), 9 convergent events, divergence in O1/O2/O3, 4 variants for 4 cases, a fabricated RPCD cycle, and a cycle-time metric frozen at ~26h by Close Order batching. Removing Close Order reveals a real 7h00–24h56 spread. And O2/O3 are not independently fulfilable (6 shared events), so "one order = one case" is a fiction.

**Residual risk.** Silent promotion: an analyst treats the Order log as *the* process and reports 26h lead time, two duplicate payments, and packing rework. Mitigation is governance, not modelling: (i) no end-to-end duration KPI may be sourced from the Order log; (ii) every Order-log DFG edge carries copy multiplicity; (iii) physical KPIs are owned by Package/Delivery, financial KPIs by Invoice; (iv) cross-object causality only from OC-DFG/OTG.

**Conditions to change the decision.** If the question becomes transport SLA or cash collection, the lead shifts to Delivery or Invoice and Order becomes secondary. If the share of orders touching cross-order packages rises materially beyond the current 3-of-4, I would replace the anchor with a *derived* Fulfilment Group object (connected component over Order–Package–Delivery) — a new object type, not a flattening. If Close Order stops being batched, Order-level cycle time becomes informative and the demotion can be relaxed.
