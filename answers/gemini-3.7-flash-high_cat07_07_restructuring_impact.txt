### Detailed Mechanism Breakdown

1. **Nominal Handover Reduction vs. Effective Ping-Pong Handovers**:
   - *Intended Mechanism*: Merging specialists eliminates informal, untracked handoffs and reduces ad-hoc cross-team transfers (historically 6 per 100 cases, with 8.9h wait and 18% rework).
   - *Process-Mining Reality*: While formal routing standardizes nominal transitions, the loss of direct communication between local caseworkers and embedded specialists creates an information gap. Incomplete initial documentation forces specialists to reassign cases back to caseworkers for clarification (`handover_reason = 'information_request'`), increasing the mean `reassignment_count` per case and creating cyclic loops in the subcontracting network (`pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network`).

2. **Central Queue Formation & Kingman's Congestion Law**:
   - *Queueing Theory Mechanism*: By Kingman's approximation formula for waiting time in a queue:
     \[
     W_q \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{C_a^2 + C_s^2}{2}\right) \cdot \tau
     \]
     where \(\rho\) is utilization, \(C_a^2\) and \(C_s^2\) are arrival and service time coefficients of variation, and \(\tau\) is mean service time.
   - *Process-Mining Reality*: Management projects a 15% increase in specialist utilization. However, Legal and Risk specialists are already operating near saturation (93% and 91% utilization). Pushing an expanded volume of high-complexity cases, combined with higher arrival and service time variance \(C_a^2, C_s^2\) from heterogeneous regional cases, pushes \(\rho \to 1\), resulting in hyperbolic increases in waiting times entering `Specialist Review`.

3. **Specialist Utilization vs. Value-Add Time**:
   - *Intended Mechanism*: Pooling specialists (N_Tech at 71%, S_Tech at 68%, E_Tech at 82%) smooths demand peaks, equalizes capacity across regions, and raises baseline utilization toward ~80-85%.
   - *Process-Mining Reality*: In the decentralized model, embedded specialists provided rapid, unlogged "desk-side" guidance that resolved minor questions in minutes. In the centralized model, every inquiry must enter a formal ticket queue. Specialist utilization increases, but a significant portion of this utilization is administrative overhead (reading case files, context-switching) rather than net-new analytical throughput.

4. **Rework Dynamics and Quality Degradation**:
   - *Process-Mining Manifestation*: Detected via `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` and `rework_flag`. In the baseline, cross-team handovers suffered an 18% rework rate due to inconsistent case notes. Centralization exposes all cases to this inconsistency. When central specialists lack local contextual knowledge, they issue recommendations that conflict with regional customer constraints, driving rework at `Quality Check` and post-`Implementation`.

5. **Local Expertise Loss & Tacit Knowledge Depletion**:
   - *Mechanism*: In the embedded model, caseworkers learned technical nuances through daily collaboration with N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech. Centralizing hard cases severs this informal apprenticeship. Local caseworkers become purely administrative triage agents, degrading `local_expertise_flag` over time and lowering initial triage accuracy.

6. **Role Clarity and Accountability**:
   - *Process-Mining Manifestation*: Evaluated via `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` and `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`. Centralization clarifies nominal job descriptions but diffuses end-to-end case ownership. Removing a local supervisor and centralizing approvals creates a detached approval bottleneck where approvers lack holistic case familiarity, leading to rubber-stamping or excessive conservative delays.

7. **Resilience to Absence and Operational Shocks**:
   - *Mechanism*: The embedded model had severe single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities (notably Team East, where E_Tech at 82% utilization drove cycle times to 8.6d and SLA misses to 9.8%). A pooled shared service provides systemic buffering against regional sickness, leave, and volume spikes.

8. **Fairness Across Teams and Customer Segments**:
   - *Mechanism*: Regional equity improves (Team East gains access to North/South specialist capacity). However, customer-level fairness may degrade: standard cases move smoothly, while complex regional cases requiring regional accommodations face compounded queue delays.

### 1. Optimistic Scenario: "High Standardization & Effective Resource Pooling"
* **Assumptions**: Case documentation is rapidly standardized; intake routing heuristics accurately classify complexity; triage rules prevent inappropriate escalations; specialists operate with minimal context friction.
* **Handover Pattern**: The handover-of-work network shows clean, structured flows from local intake/caseworkers to shared queues. Cross-team ad-hoc handovers drop from 6% to <2%. Reassignment count remains low (mean 1.10 per case).
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern**: The pooling principle successfully absorbs demand fluctuations. E_Tech is relieved of overload; median waiting time for Technical Shared Service stabilizes at 3.5h (close to the 4.0h management target). Legal/Risk queues are stabilized by cross-skilling technical specialists to perform initial compliance triage.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution**:
  * Team East median cycle time drops from 8.6d to 6.9d; 90th percentile drops from 22.8d to 15.2d.
  * Team North and South maintain stable median cycle times (~6.7d and ~7.2d).
  * System-wide SLA miss rate drops from 7.5% baseline average to <5.0%.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern**: Standardized decision frameworks reduce technical assessment variance. System-wide rework drops to 6.5%.
* **Role Clarity and Accountability**: Roles partition cleanly between customer-facing local caseworkers, analytical shared specialists, and governance-focused approvers.
* **Fairness Implications**: High equity across geographic regions; East achieves operational parity with North.
* **Early Warning Signs**: Immediate decline in East case backlog; shared queue depth remains below 1.5 days of incoming volume; specialist utilization stabilizes at 78–82%.

### 2. Central / Mixed Scenario: "Regional Divergence & Selective Congestion"
* **Assumptions**: Technical pooling works well for standard cases, but high-complexity and regionally unique cases suffer from lack of local context. Legal/Risk shared queues experience severe congestion due to pre-existing near-capacity utilization.
* **Handover Pattern**: Standard cases follow the intended two-step handover. However, 12–15% of high-complexity cases exhibit ping-pong handovers between caseworkers and shared specialists due to incomplete case documentation (`reassignment_count` averages 1.45).
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern**: Technical Shared Service maintains acceptable median wait times (~4.5h), but queue tails stretch during volume spikes. Legal/Risk queue waiting times increase from 9.6h to 14.5h median (90th percentile > 36h) due to the influx of newly routed risk cases.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution**:
  * Aggregate mean cycle time appears slightly improved or flat (~7.3d across all teams).
  * Regional divergence: East improves (median cycle time 7.5d, 90th percentile 18.0d), but North degrades (median 7.4d, 90th percentile 19.5d) and South degrades (median 7.8d).
  * 90th percentile cycle time for high-complexity cases increases by 25% across all regions.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern**: Rework rate drops slightly for standard cases (to 5.5%) but rises sharply for complex regional cases (to 14.0%), keeping overall rework flat at ~8.5%.
* **Role Clarity and Accountability**: Caseworkers begin treating shared services as an "over-the-fence" repository, reducing effort on initial investigations. Shared approvers face batching delays.
* **Fairness Implications**: Geographic disparity narrows, but intra-segment fairness worsens: high-complexity and minority-segment customers absorb the burden of queue delays.
* **Early Warning Signs**: Persistent queue growth in Legal/Risk; rise in ticket re-openings with `handover_reason = 'clarification'`; North/South caseworker survey scores declining.

### 3. Adverse Scenario: "Central Bottleneck, Deskilling & Tail Latency Explosion"
* **Assumptions**: Case notes are highly non-standard across regions; local caseworkers dump ambiguous cases into central queues to offload work; central specialists lack regional context and misdiagnose cases; Legal/Risk and Tech queues enter saturation (\(\rho > 0.95\)).
* **Handover Pattern**: Fragmented, high-churn handover network. Severe ping-pong loops (`Local Caseworker` \(\leftrightarrow\) `TSS` \(\leftrightarrow\) `Legal/Risk` \(\leftrightarrow\) `Shared Approver`). Mean `reassignment_count` exceeds 2.30 per case.
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern**: Kingman queue explosion. Central Technical queue median waiting time exceeds 14.0h (90th percentile > 48h); Legal/Risk median waiting time exceeds 32.0h (90th percentile > 96h). Shared approval queue becomes a major bottleneck (waiting time > 12h) due to the removed supervisor capacity.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution**:
  * Cycle-time distribution becomes bimodal: low-complexity cases complete in 3–5 days, while medium/high-complexity cases stall in multiple queues.
  * System-wide median cycle time increases to 9.2d; 90th percentile cycle time explodes to >30.0d across all teams (North 90th percentile reaches 28.5d, East reaches 34.0d).
  * SLA miss rate doubles to 16.5%.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern**: Central specialists misinterpret regional nuances, leading to incorrect assessments. Downstream rework spikes to 18.0%. Customer satisfaction drops drastically (North/South CSAT falls to 3.2/5).
* **Role Clarity and Accountability**: Severe diffusion of responsibility. Local caseworkers disclaim errors ("we just followed the specialist ticket"), while central specialists blame incomplete intake notes.
* **Fairness Implications**: Severe structural unfairness. Regional customer segments requiring specialized accommodation experience catastrophic delays and high rejection/rework rates.
* **Early Warning Signs**: Rapid, linear growth in unassigned shared-queue backlogs; caseworkers attaching high-priority flags to bypass queues; formal specialist grievance filings regarding poor data quality.

# 3. Leading and lagging indicators

To monitor the restructuring effectively, process-mining metrics from the event log must be separated into **leading indicators** (observable within days/weeks to catch emerging failure modes) and **lagging indicators** (observable over months as cumulative outcomes materialize).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Median & p90 Queue Waiting Time** | Leading | Weekly / 3–7 days | `insert_case_service_waiting_time` between routing handover and activity start: `time:timestamp(start) - time:timestamp(handover)` | TSS p90 wait > 8.0h; Legal/Risk p90 wait > 20.0h | Direct measurement of Kingman queue congestion in centralized specialist pools. |

| **Local Expertise Application Rate** | Leading | Monthly / 30 days | Frequency of `local_expertise_flag == True` and activity execution counts of technical steps by local caseworkers | Decline of `local_expertise_flag` > 25% from baseline | Measures deskilling of regional teams as complex tasks are completely offloaded to central queues. |
| **Cycle Time Distribution (p50, p90, p99)** | Lagging | Monthly / 30–60 days | End-to-end duration: `time:timestamp(Close) - time:timestamp(Intake)` evaluated across percentiles | p90 cycle time increases > 15% (even if median is flat/down) | Captures tail latency explosion masked by stable median performance. |

| **Role Partitioning Modularity** | Lagging | Quarterly / 90 days | Modularity score of resource clusters discovered via `discover_organizational_roles` | Role overlap index drops below 0.30 (excessive silo) or exceeds 0.75 (total diffusion) | Reflects organizational role clarity versus chaotic responsibility blurring. |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Management teams frequently evaluate reorganizations using aggregate average (mean) cycle time. This is a severe methodological flaw. In process mining and queueing systems, mean cycle time can remain perfectly stable—or even improve—while organizational health, fairness, and tail resilience collapse.

```
       AGGREGATE AVERAGE CYCLE TIME (STABLE AT ~7.2 DAYS) -- MASKS UNDERLYING RISKS
  =======================================================================================
   Risk 1: Tail Latency Explosion   ==> Mean is flat, but p95 explodes from 18d to 35d
   Risk 2: High-Complexity Starvation ==> Standard cases accelerate; complex cases stall
   Risk 3: Loss of Tacit Knowledge  ==> Informal desk-side triage vanishes; tickets formalize
   Risk 4: Cross-Segment Inequity   ==> East gains at expense of North/South & minority segments
   Risk 5: Caseworker Deskilling    ==> Frontline loses technical competence over 6-12 months
   Risk 6: Diffused Accountability  ==> "Over-the-fence" ticket dumping; no end-to-end owner
   Risk 7: Multi-Hop Exception Loops==> Bimodal distribution; complex cases ping-pong endlessly
```

### 1. Tail Latency and Extreme Delays (p90/p99 Divergence)
* **The Masking Effect**: Simple, highly automatable cases (which represent ~60-70% of total volume) pass rapidly through local intake and standardized bots, pulling down the arithmetic mean.
* **The Hidden Risk**: Cases encountering queue bottlenecks (especially in Legal/Risk or complex technical assessments) experience compounding delays. The cycle time distribution shifts from an exponential/log-normal curve to a heavily skewed, bimodal distribution where the 90th and 99th percentiles double, creating severe customer distress and regulatory breach exposure that is invisible in the overall mean.

### 2. High-Complexity Case Penalization (Simpson's Paradox in Process Flow)
* **The Masking Effect**: Aggregating all cases obscures performance inversions within complexity tiers.
* **The Hidden Risk**: If low-complexity cycle times drop by 20% (due to minor bot/routing efficiency) while high-complexity cycle times increase by 40% (due to central queue congestion), the overall weighted average cycle time improves. High-value, complex customers—who generate the highest business risk and revenue—receive significantly worse service.

### 3. Loss of Tacit Local Knowledge and Informal Gatekeeping
* **The Masking Effect**: Transition counts in the event log may show fewer "informal" interactions.
* **The Hidden Risk**: In the embedded model, a 5-minute unrecorded desk-side conversation between `N_Case1` and `N_Tech` often resolved ambiguous regional customer nuances, preventing unnecessary escalations. In the centralized model, these informal interactions are eliminated. The resulting formal tickets consume specialist queue slots, introduce handoff friction, and fail to capture unwritten regional business conventions.

### 4. Inter-Team and Customer Segment Inequity (Geographic & Demographic Fairness)
* **The Masking Effect**: A corporate-wide average aggregates Team North (820 cases), Team South (760 cases), and Team East (520 cases).
* **The Hidden Risk**: Team East was baseline-overloaded (E_Tech 82% util, 8.6d cycle time, 9.8% SLA miss). Pooling relieves East, dropping its cycle time by ~1.5 days. However, this gain may be achieved by siphoning specialist capacity away from North and South, whose cycle times and rework rates degrade. Furthermore, regional customer segments requiring specialized language or local regulatory accommodations face longer queues, producing disparate impact.

### 5. Frontline Deskilling and Long-Term Learning Erosion
* **The Masking Effect**: Short-term operational throughput can look stable or elevated during the first 1-3 months.
* **The Hidden Risk**: By stripping local caseworkers of technical assessment responsibilities, their diagnostic skills atrophy. After 6–12 months, local caseworkers lose the ability to perform accurate initial triage, resulting in higher rates of misrouted cases, lower quality case notes, and total dependency on central shared services.

### 6. Diffused Accountability and Moral Hazard ("Over-the-Fence" Dumping)
* **The Masking Effect**: Activity completion counts per resource remain steady.
* **The Hidden Risk**: When case ownership is fragmented across multiple specialized pools, psychological ownership evaporates. Local caseworkers offload half-investigated files into the shared queue to clear their local dashboards ("ticket dumping"). Central specialists, having no relationship with the end customer, issue rigid, literal recommendations without ensuring actionable resolution. When failures occur, each unit blames the other.

### 7. Exception Handling Failure and Multi-Hop Ping-Ponging
* **The Masking Effect**: The performance DFG shows standard median path durations.
* **The Hidden Risk**: Cases with non-standard exceptions do not fit clean routing rules. They bounce between `TSS`, `Legal Review`, `Local Caseworker`, and `Shared Approvers`. In an event log, these appear as low-frequency process variants (the "long tail" of variants). While each individual variant represents <1% of volume, collectively they account for 15–20% of all cases and consume a disproportionate share of organizational effort.

# 5. Pilot design

To mitigate the catastrophic risks of a "big-bang" restructuring, a controlled, phased pilot must be deployed. The pilot tests the shared-service hypothesis under realistic operational conditions while establishing safety guardrails.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    PILOT ARCHITECTURE & TIMELINE                                   |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                                    |
|  [ Pre-Pilot Baseline ]     [ Ramp-Up / Burn-In ]     [ Active Evaluation ]     [ Lag Evaluation ] |
|      Weeks -12 to 0              Weeks 1 to 4              Weeks 5 to 16          Weeks 17 to 24   |
|  * Historical log audit     * System & queue config   * Monitored execution    * Track late rework |
|  * Stratified baselining    * Staff training          * Weekly guardrails      * Customer churn    |
|  * Balance verification     * Exclude from steady     * Formal mid-point check * Final synthesis   |
|                             * state metrics           * Trigger review                             |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Pilot Scope
* **Intervention Group (Shared Service Pilot)**:
  * Merge **Team East** (historically constrained) and **Team South** into the Technical Shared Service pool (`S_Tech` + `E_Tech` pooled into a single queue).
  * Centralize Medium/High-Value Approvals and Specialist/Legal/Risk routing for Team East and Team South.
* **Control Group (Decentralized Baseline)**:
  * Keep **Team North** operating under the current embedded specialist structure (`N_Tech` remains dedicated; local supervisor retains approval authority).
* **Rationale**: Team East provides the high-utilization test case (where pooling should provide maximum relief), Team South provides the balanced regional test case, and Team North serves as an unperturbed, high-volume control baseline to control for macroeconomic or seasonal shocks.

### 2. Observation Windows & Timeline
* **Pre-Pilot Baseline Window**: 12 weeks of historical event log data prior to intervention to establish distribution baselines (p50, p90, p99) for all metrics.
* **Ramp-Up / Burn-In Phase**: Weeks 1–4. Tooling integration, queue configuration, and workflow adjustment. Data during this period is monitored for technical glitches but excluded from steady-state statistical comparison.
* **Active Pilot Evaluation Window**: Weeks 5–16 (12 weeks of steady-state execution).
* **Post-Pilot Lag Evaluation Window**: Weeks 17–24 (8 weeks of downstream tracking to measure late-maturing rework, customer appeals, and post-close disputes).

### 3. Stratification and Segmentation Scheme
All process-mining telemetry must be segmented across four explicit dimensions:
1. **Team / Location**: Team North (Control) vs. Team South (Intervention) vs. Team East (Intervention).
2. **Case Complexity**: Low vs. Medium vs. High (`case_complexity`).
3. **Required Specialty**: General Casework vs. Technical vs. Legal/Risk/Security (`required_specialty`).
4. **Customer Segment**: Standard Enterprise vs. Regional/SME vs. Specialized/Regulated (`customer_segment`).

### 4. Guardrail Metrics (Hard Stop and Throttle Limits)

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                     PILOT GUARDRAIL THRESHOLDS                                     |
+---------------------------------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+
| Metric                                | Monitoring Frequency  | Maximum Permissible Breach Limit   |
+---------------------------------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+
| Shared Queue p90 Waiting Time         | Daily / Weekly        | TSS > 8.0 hours; Legal > 20.0 hours|
| Mean Reassignment Count               | Weekly                | > 1.35 reassignments / case        |
| Rework Rate by Team                   | Weekly / Bi-weekly    | Increase > 1.5 percentage points   |
| High-Complexity p90 Cycle Time        | Bi-weekly             | Increase > 15% over baseline       |
| Segment CSAT                          | Monthly               | Drop > 0.3 points on 5-point scale |
| SLA Breach Rate                       | Weekly                | > 10.0% in any individual segment  |
+---------------------------------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+
```

### 5. Staff Feedback and Qualitative Telemetry
* **Weekly Operational Retrospectives**: 30-minute structured reviews between local caseworkers and shared specialists to examine cases with `reassignment_count >= 2`.
* **Bi-Weekly Role Clarity Pulse Surveys**: 5-question psychometric survey measuring: (a) clarity of case ownership, (b) ease of getting technical guidance, and (c) confidence in decision accuracy.
* **Specialist Cognitive Load Index**: Tracking context-switching frequency (number of distinct customer segments handled per specialist per day).

### 6. Rollback and Adjustment Triggers

```
                                  TRIGGER EVALUATION LOGIC
                                             |
             +-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
             |                                                               |
             v                                                               v
  [ CONDITIONAL ADJUSTMENT ]                                      [ HARD ROLLBACK ]
  * If TSS p90 wait > 8h OR                                       * If Legal queue wait > 30h for 5 days OR
  * Reassignment count > 1.35                                     * SLA miss rate > 15% in any team OR
             |                                                    * Overall rework rate > 14%
```

1. **Hard Rollback Trigger (Emergency Reversion)**:
   * *Conditions*: Central Legal/Risk queue waiting time p90 exceeds 30.0 hours for 5 consecutive business days; OR team-level SLA miss rate exceeds 15% in South or East; OR overall rework rate exceeds 14.0%.
   * *Action*: Halt central routing within 48 hours. Re-assign `S_Tech` and `E_Tech` back to dedicated local teams. Re-instate local supervisory approval authority.
2. **Dynamic Adjustment Trigger (Throttle & Modify)**:
   * *Conditions*: Technical queue p90 wait exceeds 8.0 hours, or bounce-back rate exceeds 12%, but SLA miss rate remains under 10%.
   * *Action*: Implement a "Hybrid Triage" rule: High-complexity technical cases remain eligible for shared pooling, but medium-complexity cases revert to local caseworker resolution with weekly specialist office hours.

# 6. Recommendation

### Core Recommendation: **MODIFY AND PILOT FIRST (HYBRID TIERED MODEL)**

A direct, full-scale implementation of the proposed restructuring must be **REJECTED**. However, an outright rejection of any restructuring is also sub-optimal because Team East is suffering from severe capacity constraints (E_Tech at 82% utilization, 8.6d cycle time, 9.8% SLA miss rate) and scarce Legal/Risk specialists are unbuffered.

Management should approve a **Modified Hybrid Architecture** and evaluate it strictly through the **16-Week Phased Pilot** described in Section 5.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                RECOMMENDED HYBRID TARGET ARCHITECTURE                              |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                                    |
|  [ TIER 1: EMBEDDED LOCAL LIAISON ]        [ TIER 2: TECHNICAL SHARED SERVICE ]                    |
|  * Caseworkers keep primary ownership      * Pooled N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech handle                  |
|  * Designated local "Technical Champion"     deep analysis for high-complexity cases               |
|    conducts 10-min pre-triage & coaching   * Standardized templates & structured handoffs          |
|                                                                                                    |
|  [ CONDITIONAL LEGAL/RISK CAPACITY ]       [ REGIONAL SUPERVISORY GOVERNANCE ]                     |
|  * Add 0.5 FTE compliance triage before    * Retain local supervisor for case sign-off             |
|    expanding Legal/Risk queue mandate      * Shared approval queue restricted to >$100k value      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Essential Architectural Modifications to Management's Proposal

1. **Do Not Centralize All Technical Touchpoints (Create Tiered Triage)**:
   * Instead of merging all tech specialists into an isolated central silo, create a **Two-Tier Model**:
     * *Tier 1 (Local Technical Champions)*: Rotate technical specialists through local teams part-time (e.g., 20% time) to provide informal pre-intake guidance, maintain local customer context, and upskill local caseworkers.
     * *Tier 2 (Pooled Shared Service)*: The remaining 80% capacity is pooled to process deep, asynchronous technical assessments for truly high-complexity cases.
2. **Cap Legal/Risk Routing Mandate Pending Capacity Expansion**:
   * Management proposed routing all cases with `required_specialty in {legal, risk, security}` to the central queue. Because Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) are already near saturation, adding case volume will guarantee queue collapse.
   * *Modification*: Deploy an automated rules engine at intake to resolve low-risk compliance flags; only route high-risk, non-standard compliance cases to central specialists. Add 0.5 FTE flex-capacity to Legal/Risk before expanding their scope.
3. **Preserve Named Local Supervisory Ownership**:
   * Do not remove the local supervisor in favor of an anonymous approval queue. Retain local supervisor sign-off for standard and medium cases to maintain accountability, reserving the central shared approval queue strictly for exceptional, high-value, cross-regional cases.

### What Management Must NOT Infer from Early Averages

Management must be explicitly cautioned against common analytical fallacies during the pilot review:

1. **Do NOT infer success from an early drop in average cycle time**:
   * If average cycle time drops from 7.4d to 6.9d in the first 6 weeks, management must verify whether this is driven entirely by simple cases bypassing queues. If the 90th percentile of high-complexity cases has increased from 18d to 25d, the organization has become more fragile, not more efficient.
2. **Do NOT equate 85–90% specialist utilization with operational efficiency**:
   * In queueing systems, operating knowledge workers at >85% steady-state utilization removes all surge capacity. Any arrival variance causes waiting times to explode non-linearly. Utilization of 78–82% is the optimal operational sweet spot.
3. **Do NOT assume stable rework rates in weeks 1–4 mean quality is unaffected**:
   * Rework and quality defects lag operational changes by 4 to 12 weeks. Cases processed in week 2 may only reveal defects during customer implementation or audit review in week 8.
4. **Do NOT interpret reduced cross-team handovers as improved collaboration**:
   * A drop in nominal handovers may simply reflect that caseworkers have stopped seeking informal advice because the barrier to entry (logging a formal ticket) is too high, leading to silent quality degradation.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The following Python script demonstrates how to use PM4Py and pandas to conduct a rigorous pre- vs. post-restructuring process-mining analysis across all dimensions: handover networks, subcontracting, performance DFGs, waiting/service times, organizational roles, and stratified subgroup metrics.

```python
"""
Organizational Restructuring Process Mining Evaluation Script
Using PM4Py to analyze handovers, queues, roles, rework, and fairness.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.sna import algorithm as sna

# ==============================================================================
# 1. DATA PREPARATION & ENRICHMENT
# ==============================================================================

def load_and_prepare_log(file_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Loads event log, formats timestamp, and standardizes pm4py column names.
    """
    df = pd.read_csv(file_path)
    
    # Standardize types and sort
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    df = df.sort_values(by=['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])
    
    # Ensure mandatory PM4Py formatting
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
        timest_format=None
    )
    return df

def split_pre_post(df: pd.DataFrame, cutoff_date: str):
    """Splits event log into Pre-Restructuring and Post-Restructuring subsets."""
    case_start_times = df.groupby('case:concept:name')['time:timestamp'].min()
    pre_cases = case_start_times[case_start_times < cutoff_date].index
    post_cases = case_start_times[case_start_times >= cutoff_date].index
    
    df_pre = df[df['case:concept:name'].isin(pre_cases)].copy()
    df_post = df[df['case:concept:name'].isin(post_cases)].copy()
    return df_pre, df_post

# ==============================================================================
# 2. SOCIAL NETWORK & HANDOVER ANALYSIS
# ==============================================================================

def analyze_organizational_networks(df: pd.DataFrame, label: str):
    """
    Discovers Handover of Work, Working Together, Subcontracting networks,
    and calculates ping-pong reciprocity metrics.
    """
    print(f"\n=================== SNA METRICS: {label} ===================")
    
    # 1. Handover of Work Network
    hw_values = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df, resource_key='org:resource')
    print(f"[{label}] Handover-of-Work Matrix discovered. Matrix shape: {hw_values.matrix.shape}")
    
    # 2. Working Together Network
    wt_values = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df, resource_key='org:resource')
    print(f"[{label}] Working-Together Matrix discovered. Density: {np.count_nonzero(wt_values.matrix)}")
    
    # 3. Subcontracting Network (Ping-Pong / Delegation Analysis)
    sub_values = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df, resource_key='org:resource')
    print(f"[{label}] Subcontracting Matrix discovered. Non-zero links: {np.count_nonzero(sub_values.matrix)}")
    
    # 4. Activity-Based Resource Similarity & Role Discovery
    sim_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df, resource_key='org:resource')
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df, resource_key='org:resource')
    print(f"[{label}] Discovered Organizational Roles:")
    for idx, role in enumerate(roles):
        print(f"   Role {idx+1}: Activities={role.activities}, Resources={role.resources}")
        
    return {
        'handover': hw_values,
        'working_together': wt_values,
        'subcontracting': sub_values,
        'roles': roles
    }

# ==============================================================================
# 3. PERFORMANCE DFG, WAITING & SERVICE TIMES
# ==============================================================================

def analyze_performance_and_queues(df: pd.DataFrame, label: str):
    """
    Calculates Performance DFG, waiting times between handovers,
    and specialist service durations.
    """
    print(f"\n============= PERFORMANCE & QUEUES: {label} =============")
    
    # 1. Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) & Performance DFG
    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)
    perf_dfg, p_start, p_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)
    
    # 2. Case Service and Waiting Times
    df_enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        df,
        resource_column='org:resource'
    )
    
    # Extract waiting times entering Specialist Review & Approval
    transitions = [
        ('Local Technical Assessment', 'Specialist Review'),
        ('Specialist Review', 'Supervisor Approval'),
        ('Local Caseworker', 'Shared Approver')
    ]
    
    print(f"[{label}] Median Transition Durations (Performance DFG sample):")
    for (act1, act2), stat in perf_dfg.items():
        if act1 in ['Local Technical Assessment', 'Specialist Review', 'Local Eligibility Review']:
            print(f"   {act1} -> {act2} | Mean Duration: {stat['mean']:.2f}s | Median: {stat['median']:.2f}s")
            
    return df_enriched, perf_dfg

# ==============================================================================
# 4. PROCESS VARIANTS & REWORK ANALYSIS
# ==============================================================================

def analyze_variants_and_rework(df: pd.DataFrame, label: str):
    """
    Analyzes variant proliferation, loopbacks, and rework rates.
    """
    print(f"\n============== VARIANTS & REWORK: {label} ==============")
    
    # 1. Variants
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
    print(f"[{label}] Total Unique Variants: {len(variants)}")
    
    # Top 5 variants coverage
    total_cases = df['case:concept:name'].nunique()
    sorted_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: len(x[1]), reverse=True)
    top_5_cases = sum(len(cases) for _, cases in sorted_variants[:5])
    print(f"[{label}] Top 5 Variants Coverage: {top_5_cases / total_cases * 100:.2f}%")
    
    # 2. Activity Rework Count
    rework_cases = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
    print(f"[{label}] Cases with Activity Rework:")
    for act, count in rework_cases.items():
        if count > 0:
            print(f"   Activity '{act}': {count} cases ({count / total_cases * 100:.2f}%)")
            
    # 3. Custom Rework Flag Rate
    if 'rework_flag' in df.columns:
        rework_rate = df.groupby('case:concept:name')['rework_flag'].max().mean() * 100
        print(f"[{label}] Log-level Rework Rate: {rework_rate:.2f}%")
        
    return variants, rework_cases

# ==============================================================================
# 5. STRATIFIED SUBGROUP ANALYSIS & FAIRNESS AUDIT
# ==============================================================================

def evaluate_fairness_and_subgroups(df: pd.DataFrame, sla_hours: float = 240.0):
    """
    Evaluates cycle times (p50, p90, p99), SLA miss rate, and reassignment count
    stratified by Team, Complexity, Specialty, and Customer Segment.
    """
    print(f"\n================= STRATIFIED FAIRNESS AUDIT =================")
    
    # Calculate case-level metrics
    case_metrics = df.groupby('case:concept:name').agg({
        'time:timestamp': lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 3600.0, # cycle time hours
        'team': 'first',
        'case_complexity': 'first',
        'required_specialty': 'first',
        'customer_segment': 'first',
        'reassignment_count': 'max',
        'rework_flag': 'max'
    }).rename(columns={'time:timestamp': 'cycle_time_hours'})
    
    case_metrics['sla_miss'] = case_metrics['cycle_time_hours'] > sla_hours
    
    dimensions = ['team', 'case_complexity', 'required_specialty', 'customer_segment']
    
    for dim in dimensions:
        if dim in case_metrics.columns:
            print(f"\n--- Subgroup Breakdown by: {dim} ---")
            summary = case_metrics.groupby(dim).agg(
                cases=('cycle_time_hours', 'count'),
                p50_cycle_days=('cycle_time_hours', lambda x: np.percentile(x, 50) / 24.0),
                p90_cycle_days=('cycle_time_hours', lambda x: np.percentile(x, 90) / 24.0),
                sla_miss_rate=('sla_miss', lambda x: np.mean(x) * 100.0),
                rework_rate=('rework_flag', lambda x: np.mean(x) * 100.0),
                mean_reassignments=('reassignment_count', 'mean')
            )
            print(summary.to_string())

# ==============================================================================
# 6. MAIN EXECUTION PIPELINE
# ==============================================================================

def run_comparative_restructuring_analysis(log_file_path: str, intervention_cutoff: str):
    """
    Main comparative execution pipeline running all analyses before vs. after.
    """
    print("Loading Event Log for Comparative Evaluation...")
    df = load_and_prepare_log(log_file_path)
    df_pre, df_post = split_pre_post(df, cutoff_date=intervention_cutoff)
    
    print(f"Pre-Restructuring Events: {len(df_pre)} | Cases: {df_pre['case:concept:name'].nunique()}")
    print(f"Post-Restructuring Events: {len(df_post)} | Cases: {df_post['case:concept:name'].nunique()}")
    
    # Run comparative modules
    for dataset, label in [(df_pre, "PRE-RESTRUCTURING"), (df_post, "POST-RESTRUCTURING")]:
        analyze_organizational_networks(dataset, label)
        analyze_performance_and_queues(dataset, label)
        analyze_variants_and_rework(dataset, label)
        evaluate_fairness_and_subgroups(dataset)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Example invocation:
    # run_comparative_restructuring_analysis('organizational_event_log.csv', '2026-06-01')
    pass
```
