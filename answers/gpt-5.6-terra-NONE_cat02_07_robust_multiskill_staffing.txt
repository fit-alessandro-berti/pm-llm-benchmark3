# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Observations from the supplied data

- The operating day is 08:00–20:00, but resource coverage changes materially:
  - 08:00–10:00: Ada, Ben, Chen, Farah.
  - 10:00–12:00: Ada, Ben, Chen, Deepa, Farah.
  - 12:00–16:00: Ada, Ben, Chen, Deepa; Emil joins at 12:00; Farah leaves at 12:00.
  - 16:00–18:00: Deepa and Emil only.
  - 18:00–20:00: Emil only.
- Mechanical capacity is especially vulnerable after 16:00 because neither Deepa nor Emil can perform Mechanical work.
- Electrical/software capacity is vulnerable in U3 because Chen is the only standard-speed resource qualified for both Electrical and Software during 08:00–12:00.
- Intake and Validation are shared stages. Under stress, they may become bottlenecks even when diagnostic capacity is adequate.
- ME and ES work can be sequential or parallel. Parallelism reduces diagnostic critical path but introduces an 8-minute coordination delay before Validation and can consume two scarce resources simultaneously.

## Modelling assumptions

The supplied inputs do not specify exact arrival timestamps, exact service distributions, or whether “delay” means total elapsed lead time or queue waiting time. The following assumptions are therefore explicit.

### A1. Arrival process

Expected arrivals in each two-hour block are treated as the mean of a Poisson arrival process, uniformly distributed within the block.

Reason: this preserves the stated expected block volume while creating plausible intra-block queue variability. It is not evidence that arrivals are truly Poisson.

### A2. Case type and priority

For each generated case, priority and case type are independently sampled from the percentages of its arrival block.

Reason: no priority-by-case-type joint distribution is supplied.

### A3. Service-time distributions

For each activity:

1. Sample whether the job is ordinary or complex-tail using the supplied tail probability.
2. Conditional on that status, sample uniformly over the supplied time range.
3. Multiply by the assigned resource’s skill multiplier.

For example, an ordinary Electrical activity assigned to Ada is sampled from Uniform(22, 35) and multiplied by 1.35.

This is a transparent approximation, not a claim about the true distribution. Uniform ranges understate uncertainty if real tails are more extreme than the stated complex-tail bands.

### A4. Time accounting and work calendars

- “Elapsed delay” means case completion time minus Intake queue-entry time, including waiting, service, handovers, setup, coordination, breaks, unavailability, and overnight waiting if applicable.
- Work cannot occur outside a resource’s shift, break, or 30-minute overtime limit.
- If a service interval reaches a break, service pauses and resumes after the break; this pause is not treated as a voluntary preemption.
- Cases not completed by 20:30 remain unfinished in the simulated horizon and are counted as SLA failures for a conservative same-day operational view. In actual deployment, their eventual completion should still be recorded.

### A5. Handover and setup

- A 7-minute handover delay is incurred whenever consecutive case stages use different resources.
- A further 5-minute inter-team handover applies where a case moves between Mechanical and Electrical/Software families, unless the same cross-skilled resource performs the relevant continuation.
- A resource changing diagnostic skill family from the prior diagnostic case incurs 5 minutes of setup.
- Intake and Validation are treated as separate stage families; the diagnostic-family setup applies only when the resource switches among Mechanical, Electrical, and Software diagnostic work.

### A6. Preemption

- Only P1 work can preempt lower-priority diagnostic work.
- A diagnostic activity may be preempted at most once.
- The interrupted activity’s remaining active service increases by 10%.
- Preemption does not occur merely because a different non-P1 case has a better dispatch score.
- The simulation uses conservative non-resume behavior: the preempted activity re-enters the appropriate diagnostic queue and resumes only when the resource becomes available again.

### A7. Parallel-versus-sequential decision

For ME and ES cases:

- Parallel execution is used only if:
  1. two qualified diagnostic resources can start both branches within 10 minutes;
  2. neither resource is reserved for imminent P1 capacity;
  3. the estimated benefit exceeds the 8-minute coordination cost.
- Otherwise, execute sequentially, preferably on a cross-skilled resource where possible:
  - ME: Ada, Deepa, or Farah can execute both families, though not all at equal speed.
  - ES: Chen or Emil can execute both families.
- The threshold is deliberately conservative under congestion because occupying two resources can worsen queue tails more than it improves one case’s path.

### A8. Policy evaluation

The included Python is a transparent simulation scaffold. It is not a validated forecast. Its numerical output should be interpreted as a directional stress test and should be recalibrated against actual event-log data before deployment.

## Decision variables

1. Assignment rule: fixed team queue, pooled eligible-resource assignment, or controlled overflow.
2. Priority score and aging rule.
3. Amount of P1 reserve capacity by time period and skill family.
4. Overflow threshold for cross-skilling.
5. Parallelization threshold for ME/ES cases.
6. Preemption threshold and cap.
7. Whether assignments prefer continuity over speed when handover costs are material.
8. Validation reservation rule near SLA deadlines.
9. Fairness constraint or penalty on planned utilization.

## Objective formulations

### Objective A: SLA/tail-risk objective

Minimize expected weighted tail loss:

\[
\min_\pi \sum_{p \in \{P1,P2,P3\}} w_p
\left[
\Pr_\pi(T_p > SLA_p)
+ \lambda_p \cdot \mathbb{E}_\pi[(T_p-SLA_p)^+]
\right]
+ \gamma \cdot \Pr_\pi(T_{P1}>120)
\]

where:

- \(T_p\) is elapsed lead time for priority \(p\);
- \(SLA_{P1}=120\) minutes, \(SLA_{P2}=480\) minutes, \(SLA_{P3}=1440\) minutes;
- \(w_{P1} \gg w_{P2} > w_{P3}\);
- \(\gamma\) strongly penalizes P1 SLA failure.

This objective explicitly prioritizes tail protection rather than mean delay alone.

### Objective B: robust, fair operating objective

Choose the policy minimizing the worst scenario loss:

\[
\min_\pi \max_{s \in \{Base,U1,\ldots,U5\}}
\left[
L_s(\pi)
+ \eta \cdot \max_r (u_{r,s}-0.90)^+
+ \phi \cdot \operatorname{CV}(u_{\cdot,s})
+ \rho \cdot H_s
\right]
\]

where:

- \(L_s(\pi)\) is a priority-weighted SLA/tail loss;
- \(u_{r,s}\) is resource utilization;
- \(\operatorname{CV}\) is coefficient of variation across resource utilization;
- \(H_s\) is handover/setup burden.

This objective recognizes that a policy with low base-case delay but a 98–100% planned load on Chen, Deepa, or Emil is operationally fragile and unfair.

08–12 arrivals multiply by 1.7:

- 10–12: 9 becomes 15.3 expected cases.
- Morning total rises from 16 to 27.2 cases.

|---|---:|---:|

The morning has only four workers from 08–10 and five from 10–12. Even if raw aggregate active time appears plausible, it is not plausible after Intake, Validation, break timing, queue variation, handovers, and P1 preemption.

**Likely bottlenecks:** Intake first, then Electrical and Software; P1 preemptions may create restart-loss and P2 tail growth. Mechanical may remain feasible during the morning but will be harmed if Ada/Ben are diverted to shared stages.

### U2: Electrical/software incident

E and ES demand doubles from 12–16, with proportions renormalized. The exact resulting mix depends on whether “E and ES demand doubles” applies to type shares before normalization, but both defensible readings produce a substantial increase in Electrical work.

Using type-share doubling before renormalization:

- 12–14 Electrical diagnostic activity expectation rises from approximately 4.2 to roughly 6.0–6.5.
- 14–16 Electrical diagnostic activity expectation rises from approximately 6.2 to roughly 8.5–9.5.
- Software also rises because ES contributes Software work.

**Likely bottlenecks:** Chen and Deepa become critical from 12–16; Emil is needed for Software and later Validation. Under fixed specialization, the E queue grows sharply. Under unrestricted pooling, Ada and Farah-like slow cross-skill work may reduce E queue length but increase service time and handovers.

### U3: Chen absent

Chen’s absence removes:

- the best Electrical resource in 08–16;
- a cross-skilled E/S option;
- the main early Software capacity;
- a Validation-capable resource.

Consequences:

- 08–12 Software is covered only by Farah, at multiplier 1.40, and only until noon.
- Electrical must use Ada at 1.35, Farah at 1.40 before noon, and Deepa only from 10:00.
- ES cases become particularly difficult: parallel execution is often counterproductive because scarce E and S resources are simultaneously consumed.

**Likely bottlenecks:** early Software first, then Electrical and Validation. P1 protection may be possible only through explicit reservation and selective P2/P3 deferral.

### U4: Long-tail day

Diagnostic complex-tail probabilities double:

| Activity | Base tail probability | U4 tail probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mechanical | 0.15 | 0.30 |
| Electrical | 0.18 | 0.36 |

**Likely bottlenecks:** the same skill bottlenecks as base, but with much worse p90/p95 delay, more preemption exposure, and greater risk of end-of-shift stranded work.

### U5: Combined stress

U5 combines:

- U1 morning surge;
- Chen absent;
- 09:30–10:00 Intake outage.

This is the most severe supplied scenario.

Morning effects stack:

- 27.2 expected arrivals in 08–12;
- no Chen;
- 30 minutes of complete Intake interruption;
- only Farah provides Software before noon, and she is slow and leaves at noon;
- Ada and Deepa must split Electrical, Intake, Validation, and potentially ME continuation.

**Likely bottleneck sequence:**

1. Intake queue during and immediately after the outage.
2. Early Software backlog due to Chen absence.
3. Electrical queue as Ada/Deepa are pulled into shared stages.
4. Validation and handover backlog later in the day.
5. P2/P3 spillover and potentially P1 breaches if P1 reserve is not explicit.

No policy can fully “solve” U5 with the supplied staffing. A robust policy means preserving P1 service as much as possible and making backlog allocation explicit, not claiming universal SLA attainment.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy 1: Strict specialization with fixed queues

### Operational rule

Create separate queues:

- Intake queue;
- Mechanical queue;
- Electrical queue;
- Software queue;
- Validation queue.

Primary resource ownership:

- Mechanical: Ada, Ben, Deepa; Farah only before noon.
- Electrical: Chen, Deepa; Ada/Farah only if their primary queue is empty.
- Software: Chen, Emil; Farah only before noon.
- Intake/Validation: lowest-loaded eligible worker, but no resource may leave its designated diagnostic queue when that queue has waiting work.

### Priority score

Within each fixed queue:

\[
Score = PriorityWeight + 0.02 \times WaitingMinutes
\]

Example weights: P1 = 1,000, P2 = 100, P3 = 10.

### Preemption

- A P1 diagnostic task can preempt P2/P3 work only within the same queue and on the same resource group.
- At most one interruption per task.
- No cross-queue P1 borrowing except when the designated resource group is entirely unavailable.

### Parallelization

- Parallelize ME/ES only if the designated two queues each have an idle worker.
- Otherwise execute sequentially.

### Handover minimization

- Preserve resource continuity for Validation if the finishing diagnostic resource is Validation-qualified and has no higher-priority queued work.
- Otherwise assign Validation to the fixed Validation queue.

### Strengths

- High interpretability.
- Low setup churn.
- Easy workforce accountability.
- Can preserve Mechanical expertise and avoid unnecessary use of slow cross-skilled staff.

### Weaknesses

- Poor resilience to U3 and U5 because Chen’s absence removes a large share of E/S capability.
- Can leave capacity idle in one specialty while another queue grows.
- Fixed queues can create avoidable P1 failures.
- Late Mechanical cliff remains largely unmanaged.

## Policy 2: Full pooling with urgency-first dispatch

### Operational rule

Every newly available resource selects any eligible ready activity from a common work pool.

Eligibility requires:

- skill multiplier exists;
- worker is on shift or within allowed overtime;
- required predecessor stages are complete.

### Priority score

Use an auditable urgency score:

\[
Score =
W_p
+ 2.0 \times \frac{Elapsed}{SLA}
+ 0.8 \times \frac{ExpectedRemainingWork}{60}
- 0.4 \times ContinuityBonus
- 0.3 \times SkillSwitchPenalty
\]

where:

- \(W_{P1}=1{,}000\), \(W_{P2}=100\), \(W_{P3}=10\);
- continuity bonus favors keeping a case on its existing resource;
- skill-switch penalty recognizes the 5-minute setup;
- expected remaining work uses the expected service time for the activity/resource pair.

The score should be displayed to operators with its components rather than presented as a black-box AI recommendation.

### Preemption

- P1 may preempt any lower-priority diagnostic task if:
  1. its estimated completion otherwise exceeds 120 minutes;
  2. the eligible resource can start it immediately;
  3. the interrupted job has not already been preempted;
  4. the P1 benefit exceeds estimated restart loss plus likely handover impact.
- Cap preemptions at one per active case and one active preemption per resource at a time.

### Parallelization

- Parallel ME/ES if two eligible resources can start within 10 minutes and neither has a P1 reserve requirement.
- Otherwise sequentialize on the fastest feasible cross-skilled worker.

### Handover minimization

- Use an assignment cost:
  - +7 if changing resource,
  - +5 if changing Mechanical vs E/S team,
  - +5 if the resource changes diagnostic family,
  - plus expected service time.
- Select the lowest cost among high-score tasks, not merely the globally shortest queue.

### Overflow triggers

None: all qualified resources are eligible at all times.

### Strengths

- Best recovery from localized queues in base and U2.
- Uses idle cross-skilled capacity.
- Can protect P1 cases through explicit urgency scoring.
- More resilient than strict specialization when one queue spikes.

### Weaknesses

- Can create setup/handover churn.
- Can overuse Ada/Deepa/Emil and breach the 90% fairness threshold.
- May inadvertently use slow resources on complex E/S work.
- Can harm P2/P3 through repeated P1-driven reshuffling.
- Under U5, unconstrained pooling can make everyone chase urgent Intake/P1 tasks, leaving Mechanical and Validation backlog unmanaged.

## Policy 3: Hybrid protected-capacity policy

This is the recommended policy family.

### Operating structure

Maintain common visibility of all work, but preserve structured capacity:

1. **Protected P1 reserve**
   - 08–16: reserve one flexible E/S-capable slot, normally Chen; if Chen unavailable, use Ada/Deepa according to time and skill.
   - 10–18: reserve one flexible M/E-capable slot, normally Deepa.
   - 16–20: reserve Emil primarily for P1 Software/Electrical and Validation; do not consume him on low-urgency work that can wait.
2. **Home queues**
   - Mechanical home: Ben plus Ada/Deepa when not in reserve use.
   - Electrical home: Chen/Deepa; Ada overflow only when trigger reached.
   - Software home: Chen/Emil; Farah only as a time-limited morning overflow option.
3. **Controlled overflow**
   - Cross-skill assignment is activated only by transparent thresholds.

### Priority score

\[
Score =
W_p
+ 2.5 \times \frac{Elapsed}{SLA}
+ 1.0 \times \mathbf{1}(\text{predicted breach})
+ 0.03 \times WaitingMinutes
- 0.5 \times HandoverCost
- 0.4 \times SetupCost
\]

P1 cases receive dominant weight. P2 and P3 receive aging so they cannot remain permanently unattractive.

### Overflow triggers

Allow an eligible cross-skilled resource to leave its home role if any applies:

1. A P1 case has fewer than 45 minutes of SLA slack.
2. A skill queue has:
   - at least 2 waiting P1 cases, or
   - predicted p90 waiting above 30 minutes for P1, or
   - expected workload exceeding available skill capacity in the next 90 minutes.
3. A resource is projected to be idle for more than 10 minutes while another eligible queue has an at-risk P1/P2 case.
4. A case would otherwise cross a shift cliff:
   - Mechanical work still unstarted by 16:30;
   - Software/Electrical/Validation work likely to spill past Emil’s end of shift.

Overflow closes once the queue falls below one waiting P1 and projected workload is below 75% of next-90-minute available capacity.

### Preemption

Preempt only when all are true:

- incoming case is P1;
- estimated SLA miss without preemption is likely;
- the preempted activity is P2/P3 diagnostic work;
- remaining active work on the interrupted task is at least 10 minutes, so the restart penalty is not disproportionate;
- no alternative qualified resource is free or likely to become free within 10 minutes.

This avoids preempting a job that is almost complete.

### Parallelization

Use a threshold rule:

Parallelize ME/ES only when:

\[
\min(Start_M, Start_{E/S}) + 8
<
Start_{Sequential} + \Delta
\]

with \(\Delta=10\) minutes under normal load and \(\Delta=20\) minutes when a P1 is at risk.

Operationally:

- Parallelize when both branches can start promptly and the system is below 80% projected utilization in the next hour.
- Sequentialize when either branch’s resource pool is congested, when Chen is absent, or when the case can stay on Deepa/Chen/Emil and avoid team-handover cost.
- Under U3/U5, generally sequentialize ES unless Emil is already available, because using the only early Software capacity in parallel can starve other work.

### Handover minimization

- Prefer same-resource continuation if the next-stage resource is competent and the expected delay reduction is less than 7 minutes.
- Preserve a “case owner” where practical: the owner receives the next task first, subject to P1 reservation.
- Batch diagnostic family work only if batching does not create P1 risk; this reduces 5-minute skill-switch setup.

### Strengths

- More resilient than strict specialization.
- Less chaotic and less unfair than full pooling.
- Explicitly protects P1 while retaining aging for P2/P3.
- Handles known calendar cliffs through early-release triggers.
- Reduces unnecessary parallelism under stress.

### Weaknesses

- Requires real-time estimates of queue workload and expected remaining work.
- Reservation can appear inefficient during calm periods.
- Operators require training to understand when overflow activates.
- A badly calibrated “predicted breach” calculation can cause excessive mode switching.

## Policy 4: Hybrid policy with late-day Mechanical release mode

This is a targeted extension of Policy 3.

### Additional rule

At 15:30:

- Identify all open Mechanical or ME cases.
- If their estimated Mechanical completion cannot occur by Deepa’s 18:30 overtime end, elevate them to “shift-cliff risk.”
- Prioritize completion of their Mechanical branch before 16:30 where possible.
- Avoid assigning Ada/Ben to low-priority Validation after 15:15 if that prevents closure of Mechanical work before their 16:30 overtime end.

### Use case

This mode is useful because the staffing pattern creates a true Mechanical coverage gap after 18:30. It may slightly worsen short-term Validation delay but reduce severe P2/P3 tail delay from overnight or unstaffed Mechanical backlog.

| Hybrid protected capacity | U5 | 210–380 min | 500–720 min | 70–85% | 70–88% | 98–100% | Stressed but controlled | Moderate–high | Moderate | Insufficient early S/E capacity remains structural |
| Hybrid + late M release | U5 | 215–390 min | 480–700 min | 70–85% | 72–89% | 98–100% | Moderate | Moderate–high | Moderate | Protects late M tail but cannot eliminate morning outage damage |

## Interpretation

1. **Base-case mean delay is not decisive.** Full pooling may be slightly better on mean delay, but it does so partly by using flexible people heavily and generating higher handover/setup burden.
2. **U3 and U5 are structural capacity failures, not merely routing failures.** No dispatch rule can fully compensate for Chen’s absence plus lack of early Software coverage.
3. **Hybrid protection has the best worst-case character.** It does not guarantee all SLAs under U5, but it preserves P1 performance better than strict specialization and reduces the instability of full pooling.
4. **Late Mechanical exposure is a hidden tail risk.** Policies that look good on average can still leave post-16:00 Mechanical work with no feasible scheduled worker.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary recommendation: Hybrid protected-capacity policy with controlled overflow

The primary recommendation is Policy 3, augmented by the late-day Mechanical release rule in Policy 4.

It is preferred under Objective B: minimizing worst-scenario priority-weighted SLA loss while penalizing utilization above 90%, unfair workload concentration, and unnecessary handovers/setups.

## Why this is the best trade-off

### Compared with strict specialization

The hybrid policy retains role clarity and reduces setup churn, but it avoids the most damaging fixed-queue failure: idle or lower-value capacity in one skill family while P1 work accumulates elsewhere.

This matters particularly for:

- U1 morning Electrical/Software surge;
- U2 Electrical/ES incident;
- temporary P1 spikes;
- shift-boundary recovery.

### Compared with full pooling

The hybrid policy avoids treating all qualified resources as interchangeable. They are not:

- Chen is strategically valuable for E/S continuity.
- Deepa is strategically valuable for M/E flexibility after 10:00.
- Emil is strategically valuable late for S/E/Validation.
- Ada can cover multiple functions but is slow on Electrical and should not become the default Electrical overflow worker.
- Farah’s broad qualification is offset by slower speed and morning-only availability.

Full pooling can reduce local queue length while increasing total elapsed delay through:

- slower assignments;
- skill-switch setup;
- handovers;
- greater exposure of flexible people to 95–100% utilization;
- preemption churn.

## Trigger thresholds for mode switching

### Normal hybrid mode

Use normal hybrid operation when:

- no P1 has fewer than 45 minutes remaining SLA slack;
- no skill queue has more than one waiting P1;
- next-90-minute projected workload is below 80% of eligible capacity;
- no known outage or absence removes a critical E/S resource.

### Surge mode

Activate surge mode when any applies:

- two or more P1 cases are waiting in the same skill family;
- projected 90-minute workload exceeds 90% of available eligible capacity;
- P1 forecast SLA attainment for the next hour falls below 92%;
- Intake queue exceeds 3 cases or projected Intake wait exceeds 15 minutes;
- a critical absence occurs, especially Chen.

Surge-mode actions:

1. Freeze nonessential cross-skill switching.
2. Reserve one resource for P1 E/S and one for P1 M/E where staffing permits.
3. Sequentialize most ME/ES work unless parallel starts are immediately available.
4. Defer low-priority Validation only if it does not create a later SLA breach.
5. Restrict P1 preemption to likely SLA-save cases.
6. Send operational escalation if early Software capacity is unavailable.

### Shift-cliff mode

At 15:30, activate Mechanical release planning:

- identify Mechanical/ME tasks that cannot finish before Mechanical coverage ends;
- avoid starting low-priority lengthy work on the final Mechanical-capable resource if it would strand more urgent M work;
- use approved overtime before 18:30 for Deepa when justified;
- make deferred work visible rather than hiding it in a general queue.

## When another policy may be preferable

- **Strict specialization** is preferable when demand is stable, cross-skill setup/handover costs are known to be higher than assumed, and there is enough staffed redundancy in every skill family.
- **Full pooling** may be preferable for a short, well-observed temporary incident if staff are genuinely cross-trained, handovers are cheap, and a dispatcher can actively prevent flexible-resource overload.
- **A temporary dedicated Intake/Validation cell** may be preferable during U5-like outages, but this requires additional staff or explicit reassignment capacity not supplied in the scenario.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## Gaming of estimates

If expected remaining processing time affects priority, staff may overstate complexity to obtain faster assignment or avoid difficult work.

Mitigations:

- Record estimated and actual service separately.
- Audit systematic overestimation by resource, team, priority, and case type.
- Use broad service classes rather than free-text duration estimates.
- Do not let estimated complexity dominate P1 priority.

## Starvation of P3 work

A pure urgency-first policy can defer P3 indefinitely during repeated P1/P2 surges.

Mitigations:

- Add aging to P2/P3 scores.
- Set a P3 review threshold, for example every 120 minutes of waiting.
- Reserve a small amount of non-P1 capacity when P1 risk is low.
- Use explicit backlog reporting rather than silently allowing P3 work to age.

## Skill erosion

If the policy always routes Electrical to Chen/Deepa and Software to Chen/Emil, other qualified people may lose practical competence.

Mitigations:

- Schedule controlled cross-training during low-load periods.
- Distinguish emergency overflow qualification from fully current proficiency.
- Track skill usage and successful completion by worker/skill combination.
- Do not use training assignments during P1 stress periods.

## Excessive preemption

Frequent preemption creates restart loss, cognitive load, frustration, and inaccurate service records.

Mitigations:

- Maintain the one-preemption cap.
- Require estimated P1 SLA benefit before preemption.
- Avoid preempting work with little remaining active time.
- Record the reason, estimated benefit, remaining work, and penalty for every preemption.
- Monitor preemptions per case and per resource.

## Coordination overhead from parallel branches

Parallel ME/ES work is not automatically beneficial. The 8-minute coordination delay can exceed the benefit when one branch is short, when resources are scarce, or when the sequential alternative remains on a cross-skilled resource.

Mitigations:

- Use the stated threshold rule.
- Measure actual coordination delay separately from nominal 8 minutes.
- Compare achieved critical-path reduction against added resource consumption.

## Validation becoming a new bottleneck

Protecting diagnostics can create a Validation queue, especially after 16:00 when Emil is simultaneously needed for Software, Electrical, and Validation.

Mitigations:

- Include Validation in the urgency score.
- Trigger Validation protection when completed diagnostics queue exceeds two cases or predicted Validation wait exceeds 20 minutes.
- Prefer same-resource Validation when it avoids a 7-minute handover and does not displace P1 diagnostic work.
- Measure diagnostic-complete-to-validation-start queue time explicitly.

## Fairness

A policy may meet aggregate SLAs by repeatedly loading Chen, Deepa, or Emil beyond 90%, which is operationally fragile and potentially inequitable.

Mitigations:

- Publish resource utilization, overtime, preemption count, skill-switch count, and high-complexity assignment distribution.
- Use fairness penalties only after preserving P1 safety; fairness should not prevent emergency response.
- Review whether “flexibility” is being treated as an unlimited free resource.

## Human interpretability

Operators may reject a complex score if it gives surprising assignments.

Mitigations:

- Display “why this assignment” fields:
  - priority;
  - remaining SLA slack;
  - qualified alternatives;
  - expected service time;
  - handover/setup effect;
  - reserve status;
  - policy mode.
- Keep threshold rules finite and visible.
- Allow supervisor override, but require override reason codes.

## PM4Py analyses

### Drift and changing service behavior

Use PM4Py event-log and dataframe utilities to compare periods before and after policy release:

- Activity service-time distributions by `concept:name`, `org:resource`, case type, and priority.
- Variant frequencies before/after deployment.
- Process discovery to detect whether actual workflow increasingly includes extra handovers, repeated Validation, or non-standard routing.
- Compare branch order for ME/ES: parallel-like overlap versus sequential execution.

PM4Py’s case-level service/waiting-time insertion is useful for end-to-end case duration, but it should not be mislabelled as stage queue wait. Case-level waiting time is an aggregate lifecycle-derived quantity, not a substitute for diagnostic queue waiting.

### Handover burden

Use `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on an appropriately formatted event log to identify frequent resource transitions.

Interpret with care:

- The discovered network shows observed succession, not necessarily causal handover delay.
- Filter by case type and policy mode, because ME/ES cases naturally have more transitions.
- Compare observed resource transitions to explicit `handover_flag` and `handover_minutes`.

### Bottleneck migration

PM4Py can support:

- directly-follows and performance analyses;
- activity service-time summaries;
- organizational/resource views;
- process maps annotated with durations.

For queue-specific bottlenecks, calculate stage queue-entry-to-start metrics from explicit timestamps outside the generic case-level PM4Py waiting-time output. Then plot by hour, skill, and scenario flag. This is essential for detecting migration from Electrical to Validation, or from Intake outage recovery to downstream congestion.

### Conformance and policy adherence

If the hybrid policy is formalized as a process model or decision table:

- assess whether P1 cases were actually assigned to reserved capacity;
- identify assignments violating stated overflow triggers;
- compare override rate and SLA outcomes;
- detect hidden workarounds, such as unrecorded handovers or off-system queues.

def minute_to_dt(x):

def resource_windows(resource):
    row = resource_table.loc[resource_table.resource == resource].iloc[0]
    start = dt_to_minute(dt(row.shift_start))
    end = dt_to_minute(dt(row.shift_end)) + MAX_OVERTIME_MINUTES

def overlaps(a0, a1, b0, b1):
    return max(a0, b0) < min(a1, b1)


def next_working_segment(resource, start, needed):
    """
    Return (actual_start, actual_end, active_minutes, pause_segments).
    Service pauses across breaks or after shift; activity cannot begin if it
    cannot fit before end of shift/overtime. This is intentionally conservative.
    """
    shift_start, shift_end, breaks = resource_windows(resource)
    t = max(start, shift_start)
    pause_segments = []

    # If start is inside a break, move it to break end.
    for b0, b1 in breaks:
        if b0 <= t < b1:
            pause_segments.append((t, b1, "Break"))
            t = b1

    if t >= shift_end:
        return None

    active_left = needed
    current = t
    while active_left > 1e-9:
        # Find the next break after current.
        next_break = None
        for b0, b1 in breaks:
            if b0 > current:
                next_break = (b0, b1)
                break

        available_until = shift_end
        if next_break is not None:
            available_until = min(available_until, next_break[0])

        usable = available_until - current
        if usable <= 1e-9:
            if next_break is not None and current < next_break[1]:
                pause_segments.append((current, next_break[1], "Break"))
                current = next_break[1]
                continue
            return None

        if active_left <= usable:
            return current if t == current else t, current + active_left, needed, pause_segments

        # Service crosses a break.
        active_left -= usable
        current = available_until
        if next_break is not None and current == next_break[0]:
            pause_segments.append((next_break[0], next_break[1], "Break"))
            current = next_break[1]
        else:
            return None


def sample_service(activity, resource, rng, long_tail=False):
    row = service_lookup[activity]
    tail_prob = min(1.0, row["tail_prob"] * (2 if long_tail and activity in ("Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software") else 1))
    is_tail = rng.random() < tail_prob
    if is_tail:
        base = rng.uniform(row["tail_lo"], row["tail_hi"])
    else:
        base = rng.uniform(row["ordinary_lo"], row["ordinary_hi"])
    return base * skills[resource][activity], is_tail


def transformed_mix(row, scenario):
    """
    U2 interpretation: double E and ES type shares from 12:00-16:00 before
    renormalization. This is one explicit defensible reading of the prompt.
    """
    mix = {k: float(row[f"{k}_pct"]) for k in ["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"]}
    start_h = int(row["block"].split("-")[0].split(":")[0])
    if scenario["incident_e_es"] and 12 <= start_h < 16:
        mix["E"] *= 2
        mix["ES"] *= 2
    total = sum(mix.values())
    return {k: v / total for k, v in mix.items()}


def generate_cases(scenario_name, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    scenario = scenarios[scenario_name]
    cases = []
    counter = 0

    for _, row in demand.iterrows():
        block_start_s, block_end_s = row["block"].split("-")
        block_start = dt_to_minute(dt(block_start_s))
        block_end = dt_to_minute(dt(block_end_s))
        start_hour = int(block_start_s.split(":")[0])

        expected = row.expected_arrivals
        if start_hour < 12:
            expected *= scenario["morning_multiplier"]

        n = np.random.default_rng(seed + counter).poisson(expected)
        counter += 17

        p = np.array([row.P1_pct + (scenario["p1_points"] if start_hour < 12 else 0),
                      row.P2_pct,
                      row.P3_pct], dtype=float)
        p = p / p.sum()

        mix = transformed_mix(row, scenario)
        case_types = list(mix.keys())
        type_probs = [mix[x] for x in case_types]

        for _ in range(n):
            arrival = rng.uniform(block_start, block_end)
            priority = rng.choices(["P1", "P2", "P3"], weights=p, k=1)[0]
            ctype = rng.choices(case_types, weights=type_probs, k=1)[0]
            case_id = f"{scenario_name}-{seed}-{len(cases)+1:04d}"

            branches = []
            if ctype in ("M", "ME"):
                branches.append("Mechanical")
            if ctype in ("E", "ME", "ES"):
                branches.append("Electrical")
            if ctype in ("S", "ES"):
                branches.append("Software")

            cases.append({
                "case_id": case_id,
                "arrival": arrival,
                "priority": priority,
                "case_type": ctype,
                "branches": branches,
                "intake_done": False,
                "diag_done": set(),
                "validation_done": False,
                "last_resource": None,
                "last_diag_family": None,
                "parallel": False,
                "preemptions": 0,
                "finish": None,
            })
    return sorted(cases, key=lambda x: x["arrival"])


def choose_parallel(case, policy, state, now):
    # Explicit branch rule. Strict requires two idle qualified resources.
    if case["case_type"] not in ("ME", "ES"):
        return False
    if policy == "strict":
        return False  # conservative fixed-queue interpretation
    if policy == "hybrid":
        # Do not parallelize under high system load or Chen absence.
        waiting = sum(len(q) for q in state["queues"].values())
        if state["scenario"]["chen_absent"] or waiting > 5:
            return False
    # Full pooling allows it when two resources are free now; hybrid likewise below load threshold.
    b1, b2 = case["branches"]
    free_1 = [r for r in skills if state["resource_free"][r] <= now and eligible(r, b1, state["scenario"])]
    free_2 = [r for r in skills if state["resource_free"][r] <= now and eligible(r, b2, state["scenario"])]
    return bool(free_1 and free_2)


def score_task(task, now, policy, state):
    case = task["case"]
    elapsed = max(0.0, now - case["arrival"])
    slack = priority_target[case["priority"]] - elapsed
    base = priority_weight[case["priority"]]
    urgency = 2.0 * elapsed / max(1.0, priority_target[case["priority"]])

    if policy == "strict":
        return base + 0.02 * elapsed

    predicted_breach = 1 if slack < 45 else 0
    handover_cost = HANDOVER_MINUTES if case["last_resource"] else 0
    if policy == "pool":
        return base + urgency + 0.8 * predicted_breach + 0.02 * elapsed - 0.03 * handover_cost

    # hybrid: stronger predicted-breach protection, aging, modest continuity preference
    return base + 2.5 * elapsed / max(1.0, priority_target[case["priority"]]) + predicted_breach + 0.03 * elapsed - 0.04 * handover_cost

            "Electrical": ["Chen", "Deepa"],
            "Software": ["Chen", "Emil"],
            "Intake": ["Ada", "Ben", "Chen", "Deepa", "Emil", "Farah"],
            "Validation": ["Ada", "Ben", "Chen", "Deepa", "Emil", "Farah"],
        }
        candidates = [r for r in candidates if r in homes[activity]]

    return candidates


def assignment_cost(task, resource, state):
    case = task["case"]
    activity = task["activity"]
    cost = 0.0

    # Estimated service time uses expected midpoint-ish proxy by multiplier.
    row = service_lookup[activity]
    expected_base = (
        (1 - row["tail_prob"]) * (row["ordinary_lo"] + row["ordinary_hi"]) / 2
        + row["tail_prob"] * (row["tail_lo"] + row["tail_hi"]) / 2
    )
    cost += expected_base * skills[resource][activity]

    # Explicit handover and inter-team effects.
    if case["last_resource"] is not None and case["last_resource"] != resource:
        cost += HANDOVER_MINUTES
        previous_family = case["last_diag_family"]
        new_family = activity_family(activity)
        if previous_family is not None and previous_family != "shared" and new_family != "shared" and previous_family != new_family:
            cost += TEAM_CHANGE_MINUTES

    # Explicit skill-family setup effect.
    new_family = activity_family(activity)
    prev_res_family = state["resource_last_diag_family"][resource]
    if new_family != "shared" and prev_res_family is not None and prev_res_family != new_family:
        cost += SETUP_MINUTES

    return cost


def add_interval(events, case_id, activity, resource, start, end, **attrs):
    events.append({
        "case:concept:name": case_id,
        "concept:name": activity,
        "start_timestamp": minute_to_dt(start),
        "time:timestamp": minute_to_dt(end),
        "org:resource": resource,
        **attrs,
    })


def simulate(policy, scenario_name, seed=1):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    cases = generate_cases(scenario_name, seed)
    scenario = scenarios[scenario_name]

    state = {
        "scenario": scenario,
        "resource_free": {r: 0.0 for r in skills},
        "resource_last_diag_family": {r: None for r in skills},
        "queues": {"Intake": [], "Mechanical": [], "Electrical": [], "Software": [], "Validation": []},
    }

    events = []
    task_rows = []
    resource_busy = {r: 0.0 for r in skills}
    handover_setup_minutes = 0.0

    # Event queue: (time, sequence, event_type, payload)
    q = []
    seq = 0
    for case in cases:
        heapq.heappush(q, (case["arrival"], seq, "arrival", case))
        seq += 1

    intake_outage = scenario["intake_outage"]
    outage_start, outage_end = (None, None)
    if intake_outage:
        outage_start, outage_end = dt_to_minute(dt(intake_outage[0])), dt_to_minute(dt(intake_outage[1]))

    def enqueue_task(case, activity, queue_entry, branch=None):
        task = {"case": case, "activity": activity, "queue_entry": queue_entry, "branch": branch, "preempted": False}
        state["queues"][activity].append(task)

    def dispatch(now):
        nonlocal handover_setup_minutes, seq
        changed = True
        while changed:
            changed = False

            # Build all feasible task-resource assignments and select globally best.
            options = []
            all_tasks = []
            for activity, tasks in state["queues"].items():
                for task in tasks:
                    all_tasks.append(task)

            for task in all_tasks:
                activity = task["activity"]

                # Intake cannot start in outage.
                if activity == "Intake" and outage_start is not None and outage_start <= now < outage_end:
                    continue

                for r in candidate_resources(task, policy, state, now):
                    if state["resource_free"][r] > now + 1e-9:
                        continue
                    shift_start, shift_end, _ = resource_windows(r)
                    if not (shift_start <= now < shift_end):
                        continue

                    # Hybrid protected reservation: do not spend Chen/Deepa/Emil on low urgency
                    # while a P1 task is waiting in E/S or has <45 min slack.
                    if policy == "hybrid" and task["case"]["priority"] != "P1":
                        p1_es_waiting = any(
                            t["case"]["priority"] == "P1" and t["activity"] in ("Electrical", "Software")
                            for a in ("Electrical", "Software") for t in state["queues"][a]
                        )
                        if r in ("Chen", "Deepa", "Emil") and p1_es_waiting and activity not in ("Electrical", "Software", "Validation"):
                            continue

                    s = score_task(task, now, policy, state)
                    c = assignment_cost(task, r, state)
                    options.append((s, -c, task, r))

            if not options:
                break

            # highest score, then lowest cost
            options.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]), reverse=True)
            _, _, task, resource = options[0]
            activity = task["activity"]
            case = task["case"]

            # Remove chosen task.
            state["queues"][activity].remove(task)

            # Explicit setup/handover events.
            start = now
            if case["last_resource"] is not None and case["last_resource"] != resource:
                add_interval(events, case["case_id"], "Handover", resource, start, start + HANDOVER_MINUTES,
                             priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy,
                             queue_entry_timestamp=minute_to_dt(task["queue_entry"]))
                start += HANDOVER_MINUTES
                handover_setup_minutes += HANDOVER_MINUTES

                prev_family = case["last_diag_family"]
                new_family = activity_family(activity)
                if prev_family is not None and prev_family != "shared" and new_family != "shared" and prev_family != new_family:
                    add_interval(events, case["case_id"], "TeamHandover", resource, start, start + TEAM_CHANGE_MINUTES,
                                 priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy,
                                 queue_entry_timestamp=minute_to_dt(task["queue_entry"]))
                    start += TEAM_CHANGE_MINUTES
                    handover_setup_minutes += TEAM_CHANGE_MINUTES

            new_family = activity_family(activity)
            prev_res_family = state["resource_last_diag_family"][resource]
            if new_family != "shared" and prev_res_family is not None and prev_res_family != new_family:
                add_interval(events, case["case_id"], "Setup", resource, start, start + SETUP_MINUTES,
                             priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy,
                             queue_entry_timestamp=minute_to_dt(task["queue_entry"]))
                start += SETUP_MINUTES
                handover_setup_minutes += SETUP_MINUTES

            service_minutes, is_tail = sample_service(activity, resource, rng, scenario["long_tail"])
            segment = next_working_segment(resource, start, service_minutes)
            if segment is None:
                # Cannot fit this activity on the resource; return to queue and mark resource unavailable.
                state["queues"][activity].append(task)
                state["resource_free"][resource] = resource_windows(resource)[1]
                continue

            _, end, active_minutes, pauses = segment

            # Add pause records if service crossed a break.
            for p0, p1, reason in pauses:
                add_interval(events, case["case_id"], "Pause", resource, p0, p1,
                             pause_reason=reason, priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy)

            add_interval(
                events, case["case_id"], activity, resource, start, end,
                priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy,
                stage_queue_entry_timestamp=minute_to_dt(task["queue_entry"]),
                queue_entry_timestamp=minute_to_dt(case["arrival"]),
                planned_service_minutes=round(service_minutes, 2),
                actual_service_minutes=round(active_minutes, 2),
                complex_tail=is_tail,
                skill_used=activity,
                handover_flag=case["last_resource"] is not None and case["last_resource"] != resource,
                preempted_flag=task["preempted"],
                policy_version="transparent-v1",
                scenario_flag=scenario_name,
            )

            state["resource_free"][resource] = end
            resource_busy[resource] += (end - now)
            case["last_resource"] = resource
            if new_family != "shared":
                case["last_diag_family"] = new_family
                state["resource_last_diag_family"][resource] = new_family

            task_rows.append({
                "case_id": case["case_id"],
                "activity": activity,
                "queue_entry": task["queue_entry"],
                "start": start,
                "end": end,
                "resource": resource,
                "priority": case["priority"],
                "case_type": case["case_type"],
                "tail": is_tail,
            })

            heapq.heappush(q, (end, seq, "complete", {"case": case, "activity": activity, "resource": resource}))
            seq += 1
            changed = True

    while q:
        now, _, event_type, payload = heapq.heappop(q)
        if now > dt_to_minute(HORIZON_END):
            break

        if event_type == "arrival":
            enqueue_task(payload, "Intake", now)

        elif event_type == "complete":
            case = payload["case"]
            activity = payload["activity"]

            if activity == "Intake":
                case["intake_done"] = True
                case["parallel"] = choose_parallel(case, policy, state, now)

                # For parallel cases, both branch tasks enter immediately.
                # For sequential cases, use the listed branch order; this is explicit,
                # but a richer model would choose sequence by current queue/load.
                if case["parallel"] and len(case["branches"]) == 2:
                    for b in case["branches"]:
                        enqueue_task(case, b, now, branch=b)
                elif case["branches"]:
                    enqueue_task(case, case["branches"][0], now, branch=case["branches"][0])
                else:
                    enqueue_task(case, "Validation", now)

            elif activity in ("Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software"):
                case["diag_done"].add(activity)

                if not case["parallel"] and len(case["diag_done"]) < len(case["branches"]):
                    # Sequential continuation to the next unfinished branch.
                    for b in case["branches"]:
                        if b not in case["diag_done"]:
                            enqueue_task(case, b, now, branch=b)
                            break
                elif len(case["diag_done"]) == len(case["branches"]):
                    validation_ready = now
                    if case["parallel"] and len(case["branches"]) == 2:
                        # Explicit coordination delay before Validation.
                        add_interval(events, case["case_id"], "Coordination", payload["resource"],
                                     now, now + PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES,
                                     priority=case["priority"], case_type=case["case_type"], policy=policy)
                        validation_ready += PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES
                    enqueue_task(case, "Validation", validation_ready)

            elif activity == "Validation":
                case["validation_done"] = True
                case["finish"] = now

        dispatch(now)

        # Also dispatch at outage end if needed.
        if outage_end is not None and abs(now - outage_end) < 1e-9:
            dispatch(now)

    # Any uncompleted case has conservative finish at horizon end for SLA accounting.
    for case in cases:
        if case["finish"] is None:
            case["finish"] = dt_to_minute(HORIZON_END)

    case_df = pd.DataFrame([{
        "case_id": c["case_id"],
        "priority": c["priority"],
        "case_type": c["case_type"],
        "arrival": c["arrival"],
        "finish": c["finish"],
        "elapsed_delay": c["finish"] - c["arrival"],
        "completed": c["validation_done"],
        "sla_target": priority_target[c["priority"]],
    } for c in cases])

    task_df = pd.DataFrame(task_rows)
    log_df = pd.DataFrame(events)

    # Utilization denominator is shift plus permitted overtime, excluding breaks.
    available = {}
    for r in skills:
        if scenario["chen_absent"] and r == "Chen":
            available[r] = 0
            continue
        s0, s1, brks = resource_windows(r)
        available[r] = (s1 - s0) - sum(b1 - b0 for b0, b1 in brks)

    utilization = {r: (resource_busy[r] / available[r] if available[r] > 0 else np.nan) for r in skills}
    util_values = np.array([v for v in utilization.values() if not np.isnan(v)])
    fairness_cv = float(util_values.std(ddof=0) / util_values.mean()) if len(util_values) and util_values.mean() else np.nan

    metrics = {
        "policy": policy,
        "scenario": scenario_name,
        "seed": seed,
        "n_cases": len(case_df),
        "mean_delay": case_df["elapsed_delay"].mean() if len(case_df) else np.nan,
        "p90_delay": case_df["elapsed_delay"].quantile(0.90) if len(case_df) else np.nan,
        "P1_SLA": (case_df.query("priority == 'P1'")["elapsed_delay"] <= 120).mean() if (case_df.priority == "P1").any() else np.nan,
        "P2_SLA": (case_df.query("priority == 'P2'")["elapsed_delay"] <= 480).mean() if (case_df.priority == "P2").any() else np.nan,
        "max_resource_utilization": np.nanmax(util_values) if len(util_values) else np.nan,
        "utilization_fairness_cv": fairness_cv,
        "handover_or_setup_minutes": handover_setup_minutes,
        "completed_fraction": case_df["completed"].mean() if len(case_df) else np.nan,
    }
    return metrics, case_df, task_df, log_df, pd.Series(utilization, name="utilization")

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## What cannot be concluded from the supplied data

The data are sufficient for a structured stress test, but not for a reliable operational promise.

Unknowns include:

1. **Actual arrival distributions.** Expected block arrivals do not reveal burstiness, correlation, incident clustering, or exact outage behavior.
2. **Actual service-time shape.** Ranges and tail probabilities do not establish a true distribution, correlation by case type, resource learning effects, or repeated work.
3. **Joint priority/type distribution.** P1 cases may be disproportionately ME, ES, or complex; the prompt does not say.
4. **True parallel work semantics.** “May run in parallel” does not specify branch synchronization, communication effort, or whether resources can work independently throughout.
5. **Actual handover behavior.** The stated 7/5-minute rules may be averages, targets, or fixed delays; real handovers may vary by people and time.
6. **Preemption feasibility.** Some diagnostic work may not be safely interruptible despite the stated restart penalty.
7. **Resource productivity.** Multipliers may not capture quality differences, rework, cognitive fatigue, or task-specific expertise.
8. **Overtime willingness and legality.** A 30-minute limit does not mean it is consistently available.
9. **Validation requirements.** Validation may require independence, specific authorization, or a different resource from the diagnostic performer; none is specified.
10. **Next-day carryover policy.** P3’s 24-hour target means overnight scheduling materially affects performance, but the supplied staffing horizon ends at 20:00.

## Validation and pilot plan

## Phase 0: retrospective backtest

Before changing routing:

1. Extract at least several weeks of event data with the attributes in Section 7.
2. Estimate:
   - actual arrival distributions by block;
   - activity-specific service distributions;
   - service time by resource and skill;
   - handover and setup delay distributions;
   - preemption frequency and restart loss;
   - probability and duration of outages;
   - actual ME/ES parallelism and coordination duration.
3. Replay historical arrivals against:
   - observed routing;
   - strict specialization;
   - full pooling;
   - hybrid protected-capacity policy.
4. Compare not only average delay but:
   - P1 SLA attainment;
   - p90/p95 delay;
   - utilization over 90%;
   - overtime;
   - P2/P3 aging;
   - handover/setup burden;
   - workload distribution.

The replay must use historical staff availability and should avoid assuming that observed assignments were optimal.

## Phase 1: shadow-mode deployment

Run the hybrid policy in recommendation-only mode for two to four weeks.

- The system produces a suggested assignment, priority score components, and reserve status.
- Supervisors retain current routing authority.
- Record accept/reject/override decisions and reasons.
- Measure whether recommended decisions would have avoided actual P1 breaches or created unsafe handovers.
- Do not enable automatic preemption in this phase.

Success criteria:

- Recommendation explanations are understandable.
- P1 recommendations are rarely rejected for safety reasons.
- Predicted breach flags have reasonable precision and recall.
- Reserve logic does not generate excessive idle time or hidden P3 starvation.

## Phase 2: constrained live pilot

Pilot only during a lower-risk period with guardrails:

- Apply hybrid overflow only to P2/P3 initially.
- Keep P1 assignments supervisor-confirmed.
- Do not pilot during a known outage, major incident, or staffing shortage.
- Use a rollback rule if:
  - P1 SLA falls below a pre-agreed safety floor;
  - handover/setup burden increases materially;
  - one resource exceeds 90% utilization persistently;
  - P3 age exceeds agreed limits.

A reasonable experimental design is staggered rollout by time block or team, not random assignment of individual P1 cases.

## Phase 3: controlled P1 automation

Only after backtest and pilot evidence:

- Permit automatic P1 reservation activation.
- Keep preemption approval constrained by the one-preemption rule and visible predicted-benefit logic.
- Audit every P1 SLA breach, preemption, and override weekly.
- Recalibrate thresholds monthly or after material staffing/process changes.

The key validation principle is that the recommended policy should prove not only that it improves average performance, but that it preserves P1 outcomes and does not conceal instability through excessive overtime, unfair flexible-worker loading, or unmeasured downstream Validation queues.
