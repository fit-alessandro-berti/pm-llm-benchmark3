## 1. Decision rule
**Eligibility Criteria (before spillover adjustment):**

1. **Stage eligibility:** The case must be *before* `Diagnosis Complete`; otherwise the swarm has negligible effect.  
   - Eligible stages: **Triage** and **Diagnosis Started**.  
   - I03 is excluded immediately.

2. **Probabilistic trigger:** Use the *adjusted* predicted probability `p_adj` rather than raw `p_now`.  
   - Calibration adjustments:  
     - **SME & NonNativeLanguage (NNL) segment:** add **+0.04** to `p_now` (underprediction).  
     - **Strategic Enterprise (SE) segment:** subtract **0.05** from `p_now` (overprediction).  
     - **Public Sector (PS) segment:** no adjustment (broadly acceptable).  

3. **Alert persistence & evidence change:**  
   - If the case has generated **multiple alerts** (2) **without a material new event**, downgrade its priority by **0.1** on the adjusted scale.  
   - I05 (third alert, no new event) gets this downgrade; I07 (new log) retains full value.  
   - If the adjusted probability exceeds a **threshold ` = 0.55`**, the case is *initially* marked **Eligible**.

4. **Quantitative decision function:**  
   Compute a **Priority Score** `S` for each eligible case:  

   \[
   S = \bigl(p_{\text{adj}} - \tau \bigr) \times V \times W
   \]

   where  
   - `V` = **Value of avoiding breach** (converted to a normalized utility, e.g., € per risk unit).  
   - `W` = **Weight for safety** = 1 if the case involves a regulatory incident; otherwise 1.  

   Cases with `S` above the **capacityadjusted cutoff** (i.e., highest scores until four slots are filled) are selected for intervention.

**Cooldown for repeated alerts:**  
   - Any case that has received **2 alerts in the last 30min without new data** will be **temporarily suppressed** (see §4) and cannot be selected in the immediate cycle.  

**Robustness:**  
   - The rule intentionally uses a **buffer** ( = 0.55) rather than an exact breachprobability equality to accommodate calibration error and model uncertainty.  
   - The multiplier `W` for regulatory incidents ensures that a strategic, highimpact case (if present) receives priority even if its raw probability is modest.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy  

**Cooldown / Suppression Logic**

1. **Immediate suppression** for any case with **2 alerts in the last 30min** **and no new event**.  
   - **I05**: Third alert with no new data  **suppress** for the current cycle; will be reevaluated at the next alert interval (30min).  

2. **Evidencebased reactivation**:  
   - If a suppressed case receives a **material new event** (e.g., new diagnostic log, ticket change), the suppression is lifted automatically, and the case is rescored using the updated `p_now`.  
   - **I07** qualifies (new customer log)  remains eligible despite repeat alerts.  

3. **Cooldown period**: After an intervention is applied, a **30minute nonintervention window** is enforced for that case to avoid immediate reswarm, but new alerts may reopen eligibility.  

4. **Escalation**:  
   - If after the cooldown the case’s adjusted probability remains **0.60** *and* the value > €30k, an **exception override** may be invoked, subject to manager approval, to reclaim a reserved slot.  

**Comparison of I05 vs I07**

- **I05**: Repeated alerts lack new information  high risk of false alarm; therefore suppressed.  
- **I07**: New log provides fresh evidence and pushes `p_adj` above  even after the alert penalty, making it a **highpriority candidate**.

## 5. Fairness and feedbackloop risks  

### Potential Biases  

1. **Valuebased prioritization** could starve lowervalue cases (e.g., I08, I04) that have higher raw probabilities but smaller breachavoidance payouts.  
2. **Segmentlevel calibration**:  
   - SME and NNL segments are *upshifted* (+0.04)  may overintervene on those with borderline probabilities, potentially depleting capacity from bettercalibrated segments (PS).  
   - Strategic Enterprise segment is *downshifted* (0.05)  could underintervene highvalue SE cases (I02, I08) that truly need help.  

3. **NonNativeLanguage Support (NNL)**: Sparse data  intervention effect estimate (0.18–0.30) is wide; intervening on I09 may bias future NNL predictions, creating a **positive feedback loop** where the model learns to overpredict for NNL cases because they’re swarmed more frequently.  

4. **Spillover externalities**:  
   - Using a slot consumes six other diagnosisstage cases (0.02 additional breach probability each under base). If those untreated cases belong disproportionately to a single segment (e.g., SE), the **fairness impact** could be uneven.  

5. **Feedback on training data**: Once a swarm results in a faster resolution, the *actual* breach probability for that case is observed as *lower*; the model’s training set is therefore altered, potentially **overfitting** to interventionenabled cases.  

### Concrete Safeguards  

1. **Quota caps per segment** for the current cycle:  
   - SME 1 slot, NNL 1 slot, SE 1 slot, PS 1 slot. This prevents overallocation to a biased segment.  
   - In the current portfolio, we observe **SME (I01, I07, I05)** – we cap at **2 slots** (I01 and I07) and reserve one for SE or PS as needed.  

2. **Randomized holdout for calibration**: Periodically **randomly hold out** a fixed proportion (e.g., 10%) of eligible cases from the swarm pool, using a MonteCarlo simulation to monitor whether the bias toward highvalue cases persists.  

3. **Spillover monitoring dashboard**: Track the **postintervention breach rates** of the six untreated diagnosisstage cases for each slot applied. If the average rise exceeds **0.015**, automatically **reduce** the assumed spillover impact in future calculations and consider reallocating a slot to mitigate the externalities.  

4. **Feedbackloop mitigation**:  
   - After each swarm, **flag** the case for **posthoc audit** of resolution time and breach outcome.  
   - Feed the **observed effect** (actual breach vs predicted) back into the model *only after a cooling period* (e.g., 48h) to allow the stochastic nature of the intervention to settle.  
   - For NNL cases, apply a **learningrate dampening factor** (multiply observed effect by 0.7) to curb aggressive model updates.  

5. **Equity review** monthly: Conduct a **fairness audit** comparing the proportion of interventions across segments versus their market share of cases. Adjust the `` threshold or the segmentspecific adjustments if disparities >15% are detected.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan  

### Operational Monitoring  

1. **Realtime dashboard** (updated every 5min):  
   - Shows current `p_ now`, stage, alert history, eligibility status, and allocated slot.  
   - Highlights any **alert superseded** (e.g., I05 suppressed) and remaining reserve slots.  

2. **Capacity utilization log**: Records each swarm slot’s start/end timestamps, queuedelay impact (estimated via observed queue length after intervention).  

### Predictive Monitoring  

1. **A/B test**: Randomly assign eligible cases to *swarmed* vs *wait* groups (controlled by a random seed). Compare observed breach rates and resolution times.  
   - Primary metric: ** breach probability** and ** average resolution time**.  

2. **Calibration drift detection**: Use **pinball loss** on a rolling 1month window; flag if segmentwise calibration error exceeds **±0.02** relative to historical error.  

### CausalEffect Estimation  

1. **Instrumental variable approach**: Treat the **“alert persistence”** (i.e., whether a case has multiple alerts without new data) as an instrument for receiving a swarm, helping isolate the **Swarm Effect** from confounders.  

2. **Differenceindifferences** comparing treated vs untreated cases **before and after** each swarm slot usage, controlling for stage and segment.  

### Capacity Monitoring  

1. **Slot exhaustion alert**: If the projected remaining slots <2 by the next decision point, trigger **preemptive reevaluation** of lowerpriority cases.  

2. **Queue health index**: Composite metric (average waiting time, backlog) to ensure spillover estimates (delay per slot ~5–8min) remain accurate under congestion levels.  

### Fairness Monitoring  

1. **Proportionate treatment audit**: Weekly report of **intervention rate per segment** vs **segment share of total cases**.  

2. **Impact parity check**: For each segment, compute **expected net benefit** under base scenario; flag if disparity >€1k.  

### Recalibration Trigger  

- **If** any of the following occurs **within a 30day window**, initiate a **policy recalibration** meeting:  
  - Observed spillover incidence >0.02 on untreated cases (2% absolute rise).  
  - Calibration drift >0.03 for a segment.  
  - Randomized holdout shows biased selection (e.g., overintervention of lowvalue cases).  

**Recalibration process**:  

1. **Update segmentspecific calibration adjustments** using the latest holdout performance.  
2. **Reestimate the riskreduction range** via the latest A/B test results.  
3. **Adjust the decision threshold ``** to reflect new evidence on overall capacity utilization and fairness metrics.  
4. Document changes in a **versioned policy log** for auditability.  

---

**Overall, the policy balances expected monetary gains against scarce capacity, accounts for heterogeneous calibration, incorporates spillover externalities, and embeds safeguards to mitigate fairness and feedbackloop risks while providing a clear monitoring and adaptation framework.**
