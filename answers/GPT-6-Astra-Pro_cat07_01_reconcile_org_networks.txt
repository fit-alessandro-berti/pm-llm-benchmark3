# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

**The most defensible interpretation is that Mira performs an exception-focused coordination and triage function that may be both valuable and fragile.** The evidence supports operational dependence on that function more clearly than it supports either formal managerial authority or a capacity bottleneck while she is present. Her disproportionate involvement in reopened claims and complaints, and the reported accumulation of exception cases during her absence, are particularly relevant. 

There is also a measurement issue: several supplied summaries cannot be combined literally under standard, common-cohort counting assumptions. Consequently, the organizational interpretation should remain provisional rather than treating every reported number as mutually reconciled.

## Handover-of-work network

In an immediate-successor handover network, an edge records one resource’s event followed by another resource’s event in the same case. It measures observed sequencing, not necessarily an explicit assignment, communication, or managerial instruction. PM4Py’s default handover interface describes this as a resource-based directly-follows graph. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

Mira has the highest reported handover out-degree centrality, in-degree centrality, and betweenness: **0.82, 0.51, and 0.77**, respectively. The listed edges connect automated exception routing to her, and connect her onward to medical and legal specialists and all four adjusters. This is consistent with a resource positioned between several operational domains rather than embedded exclusively within North or South.  

What this **does imply**, subject to validating the graph, is broad routing involvement and a potentially important intermediary position. What it **does not imply** is that Mira controls all those resources, performs most claim work, causes the elapsed time on those edges, or is necessarily overloaded. A betweenness of 0.77 is not “77% of claims depend on Mira.”

The automated resources matter: the large Bot_Ingest → Bot_Rules edge represents infrastructure-mediated routing, not human teamwork. Similarly, the team leads’ edges to Vendor_Pay represent a process interface with an external provider, not evidence that the leads perform payment processing themselves.  

## Working-together network

The reported picture is different here. Mira’s working-together weighted degree is 0.16, compared with 0.46 and 0.43 for the team leads. Her listed pairwise co-case rates are also lower than those of the North and South core pairs. These summaries suggest weaker association with a stable set of recurring case partners—not necessarily organizational marginality.  

For a literal, globally normalized co-case measure,

$$
W_{ij}=
\frac{\text{number of distinct cases containing both }i\text{ and }j}
{\text{number of cases in the selected cohort}}.
$$

A single brief event is sufficient for a resource to count as present. **Short service duration therefore does not, by itself, reduce binary co-case occurrence.** The attachment’s explanation that Mira’s low co-occurrence arises because her events are short is insufficient for the stated measure. Short interventions may explain low recorded effort or limited sustained involvement, but those are different properties. The documented PM4Py implementation likewise constructs pairs from distinct resources present in a case; its normalization must still be checked for the deployed implementation.  ([PM4Py Documentation][2])

A defensible reconciliation is **selective participation across changing counterparts**: Mira touches an exception subset, while the team cores repeatedly participate together in their routine portfolios. She can occupy important routing positions without having especially strong ties to any one team. However, dispersion alone does not guarantee low total weighted degree; case participation, numbers of partners per case, and normalization also matter.

Working-together edges do not establish simultaneous collaboration, personal relationships, trust, or supervisory authority. Conversely, a low edge weight does not show that someone’s contribution is dispensable.

## Subcontracting network

The supplied Mira → Omar → Mira and Mira → Priya → Mira patterns, with frequencies 126 and 98, are consistent with **retaining coordination responsibility while obtaining specialist input**. Her reported subcontracting betweenness of 0.69 reinforces that interpretation, subject to the counting caveats below.  

They do not prove a supervisory or contractual relationship. A return pattern could represent consultation, clarification, a callback, routine review, rework, or lifecycle logging. The activity fragments do not establish whether the return resolved the problem or initiated another unnecessary cycle.

The other patterns help calibrate interpretation. Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules could represent human handling of automated exceptions; Temp_1 → Vendor_Verify → Temp_1 could represent an external verification request and subsequent follow-up. The same structural motif can therefore reflect quite different organizational mechanisms. 

The short `n=2` definition also limits coverage. Longer specialist consultations can disappear from this network even when delegation is genuine. Before reproducing it, distinguish an exact event-position lag from a cumulative “return within a window”; documented PM4Py variants use event-position comparisons. ([PM4Py Documentation][3])

## Performance/time statistics

Mira’s reported median service time is **4.2 minutes**, across 960 events and 905 cases; 48% of her cases have the exception flag. This is compatible with short coordination interventions, but neither proves spare capacity nor establishes overload. Available working hours, arrival bursts, interruptions, unlogged coordination, and the service-time distribution are missing. Multiplying event count by a median would not recover total labor effort or utilization. 

The absence observation is stronger evidence of continuity risk than centrality alone:

$$
13.9-8.4=5.5\text{ days},
$$

a **roughly 65.5% increase in the reported exception-case 90th percentile**. Ordinary low-value claims’ median was unchanged. This supports an exception-specific dependency, but does not establish a causal effect of precisely 5.5 days. Demand, case complexity, other staffing, automation incidents, vendor conditions, and cohort selection remain possible explanations. An unchanged ordinary-claim median also says nothing definitive about its tail. 

The 42- and 51-minute mean gaps from Mira to specialists are **inter-event elapsed times**, not demonstrated waits for Mira or for the specialists. If the events are completions, those gaps can include downstream service. Subtracting a specialist’s median service time from an edge’s mean elapsed time would mix populations and summary statistics.  

Vendor_Verify’s 2.1-hour and Vendor_Pay’s 1.3-hour reported service intervals should likewise not be treated as employee hands-on work. The source specifically attributes substantial waiting-time components to these providers. Nor is the qualitative “cycle-time contribution” column a demonstrated causal allocation of delay.  

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity

Mira’s activity profile overlaps with junior adjusters and Temp_1 through Request Missing Information, and with Alice through Reopen Claim. Her combination of complaint handling, reopening, clarification, and specialist assignment nevertheless differs from both specialist execution and the team leads’ approval-oriented profiles. 

This supports a **cross-cutting functional profile**, not a proven position in the reporting hierarchy. PM4Py role discovery groups activities executed by similar sets or multisets of resources; it does not infer formal authority or professional qualifications. Similar recorded activities also do not establish interchangeability. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

Only “main activities,” rather than a complete resource-by-activity frequency matrix, are supplied. Exact similarity scores or discovered clusters therefore cannot be calculated from these summaries.

## Comparability checks that materially limit the interpretation

The following checks are conditional on a shared case cohort and compatible event definitions. They identify questions for reconciliation, not evidence of misconduct.

| Check                                                                                                                           | Consequence under the stated assumptions                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Alice–Carla is reported on 27% of cases, but Alice appears on only 665 cases. Bot_Ingest alone appears on 4,100 distinct cases. | With a common global denominator of at least 4,100, that pair’s co-case rate cannot exceed \(665/4100 \approx 16.2\%\). The 27% must use another denominator, scope, or weighting—or require correction.                                                     |
| Mira’s six listed outgoing handover frequencies sum to 1,640, versus 960 recorded events.                                       | In a standard immediate-successor event sequence, each event supplies at most one outgoing transition. Indirect handovers, multiple assignment links, lifecycle expansion, or different scopes would be needed to reconcile this.                            |
| Mira has \(960-905=55\) occurrences beyond one per case, but 224 specialist return motifs are listed.                           | Under contiguous \(A\rightarrow B\rightarrow A\) counting on those same event rows, at most 55 such returns are possible. The motif and activity tables therefore cannot share that interpretation without additional counting rules or scope differences.   |

Thus, the contradiction is partly reconcilable through different network meanings, but **not fully reconcilable numerically from the supplied definitions**. Moreover, centralities normalized on different graphs are not directly comparable magnitudes.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive: Mira could be an effective exception coordinator, a boundary-spanning broker, and a key-person dependency simultaneously.

## Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

**Mechanism.** Mira receives exceptional cases, determines the next operational step, and routes work while leaving substantive assessment and approval to others.

**Supporting evidence.** The resource description explicitly identifies cross-team coordination and an exception desk. Bot_Rules routes to Mira, and her listed activities include missing-information requests, reopening, complaints, and specialist assignment.   

**Limits.** A resource description does not establish a documented mandate, consistent routing criteria, or clear accountability. The process owner’s characterization suggests that the function may be undervalued or differently understood across the organization.

**Observable predictions.** Future logs should show recurring, explainable exception-entry points, documented destination choices, and clear transfer of responsibility. An authorized, trained substitute should reproduce much of this behavior without Mira personally handling every case.

## Hypothesis B: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

**Mechanism.** Mira supplies knowledge about where difficult cases belong, connecting teams, automation, specialists, and temporary staff rather than supervising a stable team.

**Supporting evidence.** Her listed handovers reach both North- and South-associated adjusters, medical and legal specialists, and temporary staff. Adjusters describe her as knowing where hard cases should go.  

**Limits.** Broad routing might merely execute fixed rules, rather than reflect distinctive expertise. Staff testimony is relevant but does not establish routing quality. The network inconsistencies also limit confidence in the magnitude of her brokerage.

**Observable predictions.** Validated logs should show participation across several resource communities. Among comparable exception cases, her routes should lead to fewer inappropriate referrals, fewer avoidable reassignment cycles, or faster access to the appropriate specialist—not merely more recorded transitions.

## Hypothesis C: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism.** Exception progression depends on Mira’s availability, permissions, or tacit knowledge. Separately, her capacity may sometimes constrain flow when exception demand peaks.

**Supporting evidence.** Exception queues accumulated during her absence, while ordinary low-value claims’ median cycle time did not change. Her concentration in reopened claims and complaints makes this a potentially consequential dependency. 

**Limits.** Dependence during absence is not proof that she creates a queue while present. A normally effective coordinator can be a continuity vulnerability without being an everyday bottleneck. The absence period also lacks demand, staffing, and cohort controls.

**Observable predictions.** A routine capacity bottleneck would produce rising waits before Mira’s work as demand increases while she is present. A predominantly key-person dependency would appear during unavailable periods and diminish with competent, authorized backup coverage. Persistent waits after routing would instead implicate downstream capacity or other dependencies.

## Hypothesis D: Automation monitor / quality gatekeeper

**Mechanism.** Mira interprets exceptions produced by Bot_Rules, corrects incomplete routing information, and returns cases to automated or specialist processing.

**Supporting evidence.** The Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules pattern and the large reported bot-to-Mira routing edge support a human–automation interface. Her complaint and reopening activities are also compatible with detecting unresolved problems.   

**Limits.** These patterns do not demonstrate that automated rules are defective or that her interventions improve decisions. Some exceptions may be appropriate consequences of case complexity; some updates may be administrative.

**Observable predictions.** Routing reasons and rule versions should explain concentrations of Mira interventions. Useful intervention should be followed by appropriate resolution or validated correction. Repeated unchanged bot–human cycles would instead raise questions about unresolved rules, unclear ownership, or logging duplication.

## Hypothesis E: Apparent brokerage amplified by recording practices

**Mechanism.** Some events attributed to Mira may record case ownership, system-generated assignments, shared-account activity, or duplicate lifecycle transitions rather than distinct personal actions.

**Supporting evidence.** The counting contradictions make this a material possibility. Also, Assign Specialist appears in Mira’s activity profile and fragments but is absent from the initial activity catalogue, indicating that the activity dictionary needs reconciliation.   

**Limits.** Neither shared-account use nor incorrect attribution is observed directly. A pure artifact explanation also does not, by itself, explain the reported absence-related queue accumulation and consistent descriptions of a routing function.

**Observable predictions.** Separating actual actor, case owner, assignment initiator, and automated recorder should materially change Mira’s event counts or centrality if attribution is driving the result. Genuine brokerage should remain visible after that reconciliation.

**Overall assessment:** the strongest composite explanation is an exception-coordination and boundary-spanning function with a continuity vulnerability. Normal-operation capacity saturation, beneficial quality control, and recording inflation remain additional hypotheses to test.

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk — plausible, but its location is unresolved.** The exception-tail deterioration warrants attention, yet it does not locate delay at Mira’s own queue. Cases could wait for routing, specialist acceptance, specialist execution, missing information, or a returned decision. Accelerating one stage without understanding the others could simply shift accumulation downstream.

**Coordination risk — material if the function is weakened without replacement.** The cross-resource patterns are compatible with someone resolving ambiguous ownership and directing difficult work. Removing that function could leave cases unowned, create duplicate referrals, or increase formal escalation. Conversely, unclear responsibility between Mira, the team leads, and specialists could itself generate coordination failures. The operations manager’s warning is a hypothesis to test, not an established outcome of removal.  

**Key-person risk — the clearest operational concern.** The absence observation suggests that continuity arrangements may be inadequate. The missing capability could be knowledge, decision authority, available coverage, or a combination. Merely naming a backup would not address the risk unless that person has the relevant skills, permissions, and protected capacity. 

**Compliance/control risk — credible concern, not demonstrated wrongdoing.** Detecting a problem and selecting a specialist may concentrate discretion, but the attachment supplies no rule making that combination impermissible. The appropriate questions concern documented routing criteria, qualifications, overrides, independent substantive review, and auditability. Neither a network motif nor a job title establishes a control breach. 

**Workload fairness risk — possible misvaluation and unequal burden.** Event counts and recorded minutes could undervalue coordination and interruption-heavy exception work. Raw comparisons could also unfairly rank specialists, juniors, or temporaries whose activities and case mix differ. Mira touching 64% of reopened claims is not the same as 64% of her claims reopening; it does not show that she causes reopening. Human workloads must not be ranked against bot event throughput or vendor response intervals as though they represented equivalent effort.  

**Data-quality risk — material to personnel and restructuring decisions.** Cohort definitions, lifecycle handling, resource attribution, co-case denominators, and graph normalization require reconciliation. Missing off-system coordination could understate Mira’s work, while owner-based attribution could overstate it. These possibilities can coexist. Quantitative performance rankings should not be built on the unreconciled summaries.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

The following are proposed analyses on the underlying event log, not results already established by the attachment.

## Establish a consistent event and cohort basis

Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` after validating resource identities, timestamps, lifecycle values, and activity labels. Retain start and completion information for duration analysis, while constructing a separately documented activity-instance view for sequencing.

Reconcile event counts and distinct-case counts across every network. Determine whether `exception_flag` is fixed per case or changes over time, and preserve Assign Specialist as a distinct label until its meaning is verified.

Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` for appropriately defined exception and activity cohorts, `pm4py.filter_variants` for descriptive route comparisons, and explicit time-range filtering for weekly and absence-period analyses. **Selecting cases should normally retain their full relevant traces:** deleting individual activities changes adjacency and can manufacture handovers or short return motifs. Document incomplete cases at observation boundaries.

## Handover-of-work: identify actual routing dependencies

Run `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on the validated sequence view. Alongside normalized weights, calculate raw transition counts, distinct-case counts per edge, and source-conditional destination proportions.

Compare routine versus exception cases and stable time windows. Examine whether Mira’s brokerage persists across case types and periods, rather than arising from one concentrated episode. Recompute centrality under clearly specified thresholds and weighting conventions; do not treat frequency directly as a shortest-path distance.

Retain the full human–bot–vendor graph. For a human-only view, distinguish an **induced subgraph** from a **sequence projection**: deleting bot events before discovery creates new human adjacencies that were not immediate handovers in the original log.

Where explicit assignment identifiers become available, use `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` to distinguish documented assignment relationships from mere temporal succession. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

## Working-together: separate participation from repeated interaction

Use `pm4py.discover_working_together_network`, supplemented by a pandas case–resource incidence matrix. Explicitly report:

$$
\frac{N_{ij}}{N},\qquad
\frac{N_{ij}}{N_i},\qquad
\frac{N_{ij}}{N_i+N_j-N_{ij}},
$$

representing global co-case frequency, conditional participation with resource \(i\), and Jaccard overlap.

These measures answer different questions and must not share an ambiguous “co-case rate” label. Recalculate them within exception cohorts and relevant case types. Compare recurring team pairs with Mira’s range of counterparts.

Under binary co-case counting, collapsing repeated appearances of a resource within a case should not change pair presence. This provides a direct check on whether the observed contrast reflects true participation structure or a frequency-sensitive implementation. ([PM4Py Documentation][2])

## Subcontracting: distinguish consultation from avoidable cycling

Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with a documented `n=2` interpretation, then test additional event lags. Separately construct return episodes in pandas to inspect elapsed return times, intervening activities, and repeated consultations. Exact-lag outputs should not silently be presented as cumulative window counts. ([PM4Py Documentation][3])

Classify sampled episodes using their activity context: specialist consultation followed by progression, additional clarification, renewed exception handling, or apparent duplication. Compare lifecycle-collapsed and uncollapsed views.

Report both episode counts and distinct-case counts. Account for consultations still open at extraction; otherwise, long unresolved episodes may disappear from return-based measures. Do not derive a specialist “return rate” by dividing the supplied motif frequencies by the unreconciled handover counts.

## Activity similarity and role discovery: test functional coverage

Apply `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` to complete resource–activity data. Compare raw activity volumes with within-resource activity proportions so that sheer event volume does not determine the interpretation.

Assess whether Mira’s profile remains distinctive after stratifying by case mix and whether other resources already perform parts of the same function. A practical output is a **coverage matrix** linking exception activities to observed performers.

This matrix can identify backup-training candidates, but observed activity similarity must be checked against qualifications, permissions, and routing quality before assigning substitutes. A unique discovered profile is evidence of distinctive recorded behavior—not proof that only Mira can perform the function.

## Service and waiting time: locate accumulation precisely

After reliable lifecycle pairing, use `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` as descriptive tools, validating their timestamp, overlap, and calendar treatment.

The essential operational decomposition is:

$$
\text{routing eligibility}
\rightarrow \text{Mira starts}
\rightarrow \text{assignment sent}
\rightarrow \text{specialist accepts/starts}
\rightarrow \text{specialist completes}
\rightarrow \text{next decision}.
$$

Some of these timestamps are not established by the supplied schema and may require additional instrumentation.

Measure medians and tail waits at each boundary. Separate human active work, customer-information delay, vendor turnaround, and off-hours elapsed time. With concurrent activities, case elapsed time minus the union of observed work intervals is an **uncovered-time residual**, not automatically attributable queue waiting. Completion-only gaps cannot supply this decomposition.

## Absence, backup coverage, and controlled comparisons

Reconstruct which cases were eligible for exception coordination during the absence, including cases already open and cases unfinished when the period ended. Compare consistent outcomes across cohorts; an exception-case 90th percentile and an ordinary-case median are not a valid like-for-like contrast.

Adjust or stratify using information available before routing—case type, priority, amount band, region, channel, and contemporaneous exception status—along with arrivals, staffing, and specialist/vendor availability. Do not use eventual reopening, complaints, or full future variants as baseline controls when they could result from routing decisions.

Inspect pre-period trends and uncertainty, but treat one six-day absence as limited evidence. A stronger operational test is staged, trained backup coverage that preserves coordination. Evaluate queue age, routing appropriateness, specialist load, and control outcomes together. Shared specialists create spillovers, so improved routing speed alone is insufficient evidence of system-wide benefit.

# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately

**Preserve the coordination function while making it explicit and resilient.** Document responsibility for exception triage, specialist selection, returned specialist advice, and subsequent claim ownership. Clarify the boundaries between coordination, substantive review, and approval.

Assign and train an authorized backup with protected availability. Capture Mira’s routing knowledge through worked examples and decision criteria, rather than assuming that an activity list is enough to transfer the function.

Record specialist-selection reasons and material overrides, and review applicable control requirements. Use proportionate independent checks for higher-risk decisions; do not add blanket duplicate approval to every routing action without evidence that it is necessary.

Reconcile the network calculations before using them for individual performance ratings, disciplinary conclusions, or structural elimination of the role.

## What should be monitored

Use a balanced exception-management scorecard: queue age before routing, specialist acceptance and execution delays, returned-work queues, matched case-duration tails, inappropriate referrals, reassignment cycles, reopened claims, complaints, and audit findings.

Monitor these alongside human availability, specialist workload, temporary-staff coverage, Bot_Rules exception volumes, and vendor turnaround. Distinguish case mix and cohort maturity. Centrality should remain a diagnostic indicator, not an optimization target.

Pilot shared coverage on comparable exception work. Expand it only when continuity or waiting improves without increased referral errors, specialist overload, or weakened controls. If accumulation is primarily downstream of Mira, address that downstream constraint rather than simply making her route faster.

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available

Do not remove Mira or bypass the exception desk wholesale without replacing its knowledge, authority, accountability, and capacity. Do not force all routine claims through her either: the supplied evidence does not establish a need for broader centralization.

Do not infer a promotion to formal team lead, remove discretion indiscriminately, or fully automate specialist selection from network position alone. The evidence supports clarifying the function and testing resilience—not selecting a definitive organizational title.

Finally, do not judge success by fewer handovers alone. A documented consultation can be necessary and beneficial; a superficially shorter route can leave specialists overloaded or difficult claims unresolved.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to provide cross-team exception coordination rather than conventional team leadership. She connects automated routing, adjusters, and specialists through brief interventions. That can produce high routing centrality without strong recurring ties to one team, although short event duration alone cannot explain low co-case rates.  

During her absence, the reported exception-case 90th percentile rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days, while ordinary low-value claims’ median was unchanged. This supports a continuity concern, but does not prove that Mira normally causes delays. 

Management should preserve the function, train an authorized backup, document specialist-selection reasons, and clarify decision ownership. Monitor exception queues, specialist workload, routing quality, and control compliance. Several supplied network totals require reconciliation before personnel decisions. Do not remove Mira, bypass the desk wholesale, or judge performance from event counts alone. First test whether shared coverage improves resilience without shifting delays downstream or weakening controls.
