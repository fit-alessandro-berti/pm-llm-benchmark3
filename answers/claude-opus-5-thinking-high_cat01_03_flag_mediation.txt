## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is not a free-standing manipulable cause. In these data it is a deterministic threshold on `risk_score` (every flagged case has `risk_score` >= 0.64, every unflagged case <= 0.61) and it is perfectly collinear with the triage lane and even the triage resource pool (T1/T2 serve only flagged cases, T3/T4 only unflagged). So "set `flag` = 0" is not one intervention: it is a bundle of at least four separable components that the organisation can operate independently.

Component decomposition of the flag (this is what makes the estimands below well defined):

- `A_D` display/visibility component: whether the flag value is shown to humans;
- `A_L` lane component: whether the case is routed into the priority triage lane / fast triage pool;
- `A_M` review-mandate component: whether flagging forces manual review;
- `A_E` escalation-criteria component: how the flag enters the escalation decision;
- `K` capacity parameters (review servers, senior servers, protected slots) — a system-level, not case-level, knob.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Framework and why.** I use **separable effects** (Robins-Richardson-Didelez style) as the primary framework, supplemented by **interventional (in)direct effects** (VanderWeele-Vansteelandt-Tchetgen Tchetgen random-draw versions) and, where capacity is the manipulated object, **controlled direct effects**. I deliberately do **not** use natural direct/indirect effects. Three reasons. (i) Cross-world quantities such as `cycle_time(flag = 1, manual_review(flag = 0))` require a cross-world independence assumption that is untestable and, here, substantively false: flagging changes the queue state and reviewer beliefs that jointly determine the mediator and the outcome, i.e. there is exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding, which invalidates NDE/NIE even with unlimited data. (ii) The mediators are multiple, ordered and time-varying (`manual_review` occurrence -> `manual_queue_wait` -> `manual_service` -> `escalation` -> `escalation_queue_wait`), and one of them (waiting) is a functional of other cases' exposures, so the mediator is not a case-level random variable under SUTVA. (iii) Positivity fails for the crude contrast: P(`flag` = 1 | `risk_score` = 0.35) = 0 and P(`flag` = 0 | `risk_score` = 0.86) = 0, so any "set the flag" estimand is supported only locally around the threshold (roughly 0.55-0.70, which contains 4 cases here) or must be replaced by a component intervention that is executable at any score. Separable effects map one-to-one onto P1-P4, which is exactly what a process owner can actually do.

**Where interference, exposure-induced confounding and feedback arise (< 180 words).** Interference enters at three shared servers. `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` are functionals of the concurrent set of cases and their flags, so a case's mediator depends on other units' exposures; the observed 6.50 h flagged review wait is partly self-inflicted congestion from the mandate. If triage lanes draw on one staff pool, priority service also displaces unflagged triage.

Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding: the flag changes queue state and reviewer beliefs, and both then affect `manual_queue_wait`, `escalation` and `decision_outcome`. These confounders are caused by the exposure, so adjusting for them blocks part of the effect while not adjusting leaves the mediator-outcome contrast confounded — this is precisely why natural effects are unavailable.

Treatment-confounder feedback: cases waiting long are escalated defensively, escalation loads the senior queue, longer waits then change reviewer referral thresholds and possibly future flag thresholds. Within a case, `manual_queue_wait` is simultaneously a mediator of the flag and a confounder of the escalation-outcome relation.

**Handling of stages that do not occur.** Absent stages are never imputed as zero-length observed stages and never turned into synthetic events; they are `NA`, and stage means are conditional on occurrence. Because conditional-on-occurrence means cannot be summed across arms with different routing rates, I also report an exact path-weighted identity that keeps occurrence and duration separate:

mean cycle = triage wait + triage service + P(review) x (review wait + review service) + P(escalation) x (escalation wait + senior service) + residual.

- flag 1: 0.24 + 0.60 + 1.00 x 9.20 + 0.60 x 8.92 + 1.12 = **16.51** (matches the observed mean exactly).
- flag 0: 2.48 + 0.95 + 0.40 x 4.63 + 0.20 x 8.50 + 1.19 = **8.17** (matches exactly).

Kitagawa-style split of the 8.34 h gap: triage **-2.59** h (flagged are faster); review-occurrence **+2.78** h; review-waiting **+4.62** h; review-service **-0.05** h; escalation-occurrence **+3.40** h; escalation waiting/service **+0.25** h; residual **-0.07** h. Of the +0.40 absolute increase in P(escalation), 0.30 is attributable to the higher review rate and only 0.10 to a changed escalation propensity given review (P(escalation | reviewed) is 0.60 flagged vs 0.50 unflagged). These are arithmetic decompositions of observed means, not causal effects.

**Which contrasts look like "accelerate then stall", and why they are not causal (100-180 words).** Acceleration is real and monotone in the record: flagged cases start triage 0.24 h after arrival versus 2.48 h, finish triage 0.60 h versus 0.95 h later, and are insulated from congestion (0.22 h at High load versus 0.28 h at Normal, whereas unflagged waits rise from 1.86 h to 3.10 h). The stall appears immediately afterwards: flagged review waits are 6.50 h versus 1.88 h, and 7.80 h versus 2.17 h within High complexity. Flagged cases finish triage 2.59 h earlier but enter review 1.79 h later (7.34 h versus 5.55 h). None of this is automatically causal. The flag is a deterministic function of `risk_score`, so there is no overlap; `complexity` and unmeasured risk drive service times directly; unflagged reviewed cases are a human-selected, plausibly "warm-handoff" subset; the waits are shared-queue outcomes partly produced by the mandate itself; and manual service is essentially fixed by complexity in both arms.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |

## 6. Policy comparison

Directions are relative to P0. Magnitudes are order-of-magnitude expectations anchored on the observed decomposition (triage −2.59 h, review occurrence +2.78 h, review waiting +4.62 h, escalation occurrence +3.40 h) and should be read as hypotheses to be tested, not estimates.

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0: keep current system | Flagged 0.24 h wait / 0.60 h service; unflagged 2.48 h / 0.95 h, worsening to 3.10 h under High load | Review wait 6.50 h for flagged, 1.88 h for discretionary referrals; senior queue 7 h with only ~2 concurrent servers and non-FIFO order | Flagged 16.5 h mean (median 16.45), unflagged 8.2 h (median 5.75) | Baseline. Note the flag currently yields no extra Rejects (2/10 in both arms) and 5 reviews per Reject versus 2 for discretionary referrals | Flagged mandated work occupies 10 of 14 reviews and 6 of 8 escalations, so it sets the queue state everyone else experiences | Descriptive only, n=20, no overlap in `risk_score`, one 2-day window | Do not keep as-is, but do not change routing before instrumenting the queue |
| P1: suppress display, keep score and human information | Loses the 2.24 h triage-wait advantage and the 0.35 h service advantage for high-score cases; unflagged triage may improve if lanes share staff | Review and senior queues fall roughly in proportion to the drop in mandated referrals — but only if the mandate actually disappears with the display | Flagged-eligible cases plausibly −4 to −7 h; unflagged 0 to −1.5 h; total-system mean likely down | Highest epistemic risk: removes the audit trail and the detection intent while leaving reviewers to reconstruct risk from a score they can still see. | P4: keep review, change escalation criteria or capacity | Unchanged | Attacks the longest single queue (7.0 h) and the highest-variance one (4-10 h, non-FIFO); criteria keyed on `complexity` would cut Medium-complexity escalations (R06 escalated and was approved) | Criteria change: −2 to −5 h for cases no longer escalated, and shorter senior queues for those that remain. **Pilot (P3, protected review capacity) — 250 words max.**

*Eligibility.* All cases arriving in participating teams during the 8-week pilot, `complexity` Medium or High, `risk_score` within [0.50, 1.00] so that near-threshold unflagged cases are also observed.

*Assignment.* Two-stage, cluster-first. Randomise reviewer-shifts (unit = team-shift) in a stepped wedge to one of three intensities: 0 protected slots (control), 2 protected early-window slots, 4 protected slots. Within treated shifts, allocate protected slots by a pre-registered rule (earliest `triage_done` first) so that queue discipline, not discretion, decides. Protected capacity is *added* hours in half the treated shifts and *reserved* existing hours in the other half — this is the arm that separates real capacity from redistribution.

*Guardrail.* Unflagged discretionary referrals must not exceed a 4 h 90th-percentile review wait; senior-queue length is capped and reviewer overtime is monitored. Any breach for two consecutive days triggers automatic reversion of that shift to control.

*Outcomes.* Primary: flagged `triage_done`-to-`manual_start` (mean and 90th percentile). Secondary: total `cycle_time` for flagged and for unflagged cases separately; escalation rate and senior queue wait; audited misclassification in a stratified sample; reviewer overtime.

*Minimum data.* Event-level start and completion timestamps for triage, review and escalation; an explicit "review requested" / "assigned to reviewer" timestamp; reviewer identity and roster with real capacity; queue length at each transition; referral reason; `complexity`, `risk_score`, flag; audit adjudications.

*Stopping.* Guardrail breach, senior-queue wait rising above baseline, any audited increase in missed detections, or a pre-planned interim showing a wait reduction below 1 h (mechanism refuted — pivot to window scheduling).

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If a blinded audit shows that `underlying_risk` is materially higher among flagged cases in ways `risk_score` and `complexity` do not capture, then the long downstream durations are appropriate work on genuinely harder cases rather than avoidable queueing. The 4.62 h review-wait gap would then be partly justified service and information gathering, and I would stop treating cycle time as the primary outcome. The two unflagged Rejects at scores 0.58 and 0.61 already show hidden risk exists *below* the threshold, which points to re-targeting rather than relaxing.
2. **Queue interference.** If the two-stage intensity design (Q7) shows that protected slots for flagged cases simply transfer waiting to unflagged referrals with no change in total throughput, P3 is a redistribution, not an improvement. I would then drop it in favour of P2's narrowed mandate, which reduces demand instead of reallocating supply — and if spillovers turn out to be negligible, the reverse holds and per-case estimates can be taken at face value.
3. **Decision quality.** If the audit shows that mandated review of flagged Medium-complexity cases prevents errors that discretionary review misses — for example if flagged approvals that were reviewed have lower audited error than comparable unreviewed cases — then the +2.78 h occurrence cost is buying something and P2 becomes harmful. Conversely, if the currently observed pattern holds (5 reviews per Reject flagged versus 2 discretionary, all Rejects High complexity), narrowing the mandate is the right move.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If `manual_start` records assignment or batch admission rather than the start of work, then the 6.50 h "wait" is partly service or administrative bookkeeping, and the overtaking evidence in P-E collapses. Two facts already warn about this: intervals for the same `review_resource` overlap (R01, R09 and R03 all in progress with M2 at 18:18-18:42) and the same holds for T1, so resource labels are pools, not servers. If instead the waits are off-shift calendar gaps (no review starts before 14:06 on day 1), the lever is window scheduling, not capacity, and P3's expected benefit goes to roughly zero.
5. **Positivity / overlap.** The whole flag-versus-no-flag comparison rests on a threshold with no overlap: flagged scores are 0.64-0.93, unflagged 0.28-0.61, and there is not one Low-complexity flagged case. If a wider sample still shows no cases near the cut and no `complexity`-matched overlap, then no "set the flag" estimand is estimable at all, and I would restrict every claim to component interventions (P2-P4) evaluated prospectively, abandoning the observational contrast entirely.
6. **Behaviour change by reviewers.** If reviewers respond to a policy change by re-creating the old behaviour — e.g. under P2 they refer nearly all high-score cases anyway "to be safe", or under P3 they fill protected slots with the easiest cases to hit the new metric, or under P1 they infer the flag from the visible score — then the measured mechanism is reviewer response, not routing, and all four policies converge on P0. The first-stage and monotonicity diagnostics in Q3 are designed to catch exactly this, and a weak first stage would force a redesign around incentives and queue discipline rather than routing rules.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** Flagged cases are triaged sooner (0.24 h versus 2.48 h of waiting) and faster (0.60 h versus 0.95 h), and are insulated from congestion, while unflagged waits rise from 1.86 h to 3.10 h under High load. Flagged cases nevertheless close later: 16.51 h versus 8.17 h on average. An exact path-weighted identity attributes the 8.34 h gap to −2.59 h at triage, +2.78 h from review occurring at all, +4.62 h from review queueing, +3.40 h from escalation occurring, and roughly zero from service times, which are set by `complexity` in both arms. Flagged cases finish triage 2.59 h earlier yet enter review 1.79 h later.

**Plausible mechanism.** The flag accelerates a stage that was never the bottleneck and then converts that gain into idle waiting at a shared, specialist-constrained review step whose mandated volume the flag itself created — hurry up and wait. Three of four discretionary referrals overtake older flagged cases, which suggests batching, warm handoffs, or de-prioritisation of low-yield compliance work.

**Unidentified.** Everything causal. There is zero overlap in `risk_score`, flag and triage pool are collinear, waiting times depend on other cases' treatment, and the recorded `outcome` is mechanically downstream of routing, so no natural direct or indirect effect is available.

**Most useful next evidence.** The P3 pilot with an added-versus-reserved capacity contrast, run alongside the blinded audit: it separates capacity from window scheduling from reviewer behaviour, and it does so without touching detection.
