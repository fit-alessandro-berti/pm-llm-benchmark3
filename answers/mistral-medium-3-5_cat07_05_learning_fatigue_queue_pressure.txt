# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed patterns arise from **interacting mechanisms**, not a single cause. Early in shifts, **case-mix/selection** dominates: backlog from prior shifts contains complex, high-priority cases (complexity 0.72, 41% high-priority), inflating service time (18.8 min) and errors (3.4%). As the shift progresses, **learning/warm-up** reduces service time (to 11.4 min by sequence 51–75), but this is offset by **queue pressure** in the latter half. When WIP exceeds 70 (sequence 51+), workers speed up (service time drops to 10.8 min at 76+), but **fatigue** and **time-of-day effects** (e.g., Day shift’s late demand peak) interact with pressure to spike errors (7.9% at 76+).

**Circadian effects** are nuanced: Night shifts handle more urgent exceptions (56% high complexity), but their slower service time (15.2 min) may reflect case difficulty rather than inherent inefficiency. **Batching** (e.g., deferred Quality Checks) and **automation interactions** (Bot_Risk corrections under high WIP) further distort metrics. Critically, **service time falls while errors rise only under high WIP or late in shifts**, suggesting a **speed-quality tradeoff** driven by queue pressure, not learning or fatigue alone. Juniors’ high early error rates (5.1%) imply **selection bias** (they may be assigned complex cases early), while their late-shift error surge (12.4% under high WIP) reflects **vulnerability to pressure**.

# 3. Proposed analyses

### Statistical and Process-Mining Approaches
1. **Within-resource comparisons**:
   - For each `org:resource`, model `service_time` and `error_flag` as functions of `sequence_position_within_shift`, controlling for `case_complexity_score` and `queue_length_at_start`.
   - *PM4Py pseudocode*:
     ```python
     from pm4py.statistics import sojourn_time as st
     df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
     df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
     for resource in df['org:resource'].unique():
         resource_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, 'org:resource', [resource])
         model = sm.OLS.from_formula('service_time ~ sequence_position_within_shift + case_complexity_score + queue_length_at_start', data=resource_df).fit()
         print(model.summary())
     ```

2. **Within-shift position effects**:
   - Use `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` to visualize performance by `sequence_position_within_shift` bins, stratified by `shift_id`.

3. **WIP/queue stratification**:
   - Bin `queue_length_at_start` into tertiles and compare `service_time`/`error_rate` across bins, adjusting for complexity.
   - *Code*:
     ```python
     df['wip_bin'] = pd.qcut(df['queue_length_at_start'], q=3, labels=['low', 'medium', 'high'])
     wip_stats = df.groupby(['wip_bin', 'case_complexity_score'])['service_time', 'error_flag'].mean()
     ```

4. **Complexity adjustment**:
   - Residualize `service_time` and `error_flag` on `case_complexity_score` and `priority` before analyzing temporal trends.

5. **Shift/time-of-day controls**:
   - Include `shift_id` and `minutes_since_shift_start` as fixed effects in regression models.

6. **Batching detection**:
   - Use `pm4py.discover_batches(df, activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')` to identify sequential batches (e.g., Junior_H/I’s Document Review).

7. **Resource-experience interactions**:
   - Test if `resource_experience_months` moderates the effect of `sequence_position_within_shift` on errors.
   - *Code*:
     ```python
     df['experience_bin'] = pd.cut(df['resource_experience_months'], bins=[0, 12, 36, 100], labels=['junior', 'mid', 'senior'])
     model = sm.Logit.from_formula('error_flag ~ sequence_position_within_shift * experience_bin + case_complexity_score', data=df).fit()
     ```

8. **Rework/error downstream analysis**:
   - Track `rework_flag` for cases where `error_flag == 1` using `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)`.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

- **Selection bias**: Early-shift complex cases may be assigned to seniors, while juniors get simpler cases later. Raw error rates by resource are **misleading** without adjusting for `case_complexity_score` and `queue_length_at_start`.
- **Measurement error**: `error_flag` may not capture all errors (e.g., undetected mistakes). `rework_flag` is a better proxy but lags.
- **Queue pressure as management failure**: High WIP is often a **systemic issue** (understaffing, poor scheduling), not worker failure. Penalizing workers for late-shift errors (Intervention 8) is **unfair** without controlling for WIP.
- **Automation quality**: Bot_Risk may introduce errors that humans must fix, especially under high WIP. Blaming humans for corrections is **inappropriate** without auditing bot performance.
- **Punitive metrics**: Using raw error rates for evaluations **discourages** taking complex cases. Always use **risk-adjusted metrics** (e.g., errors residualized on complexity/WIP).
