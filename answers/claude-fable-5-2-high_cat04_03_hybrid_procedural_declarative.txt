# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

**Design principle.** Each requirement gets exactly one *authoritative* layer; other layers may repeat it only as a declared redundancy. Control-flow rules go to the procedural model, DECLARE or the Log Skeleton; time expectations go to the temporal profile; anything that needs a resource, a case attribute or a three-activity ("between") condition goes to an explicit external check.

**Material assumptions**

| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| A1 | Only closed cases are judged (all eight traces end with Close Onboarding). Liveness rules (response, always-after, equivalence) are decided at case end; a running case would need a "pending" state instead of `fail`. |
| A2 | The log has no outcome attribute. "Accepted" means the trace contains Activate Vendor, "rejected" means it contains Reject Vendor. These two are the "final outcomes" of R3; Close Onboarding is only the administrative end. |
| A3 | "Should" in R2 and R8 is read as binding, because both rules protect the separation of the low-risk and high-risk routes. |
| A4 | Clarifications alternate strictly 1:1 (one request, one response). A response without an open request is a data or process error. No clarification event may occur after a final outcome. |
| A5 | Sanctions Escalation and Obtain Legal Opinion are free-floating: an escalation may happen any time after screening (before or after classification, even after activation). They are therefore not part of the procedural backbone. |
| A6 | R5 applies only to escalations that precede an activation. An opinion obtained before the escalation does not count. One later opinion clears all escalations pending at that moment. An escalation after activation is governed by R4 only. |
| A7 | R6's 24 hours are measured from the emergency Activate Vendor to the next Obtain Legal Opinion. (T6 also meets the stricter reading from the escalation: 10 h and 17 h respectively.) |
| A8 | R9: the "applicable approval" is whichever of Manager Approval / Compliance Approval occurs. Every Verify Bank Account in the case counts, including a re-verification after the approval. Resources are compared as normalized identifiers; identity resolution (one person, two accounts) is outside the model. |
| A9 | R10 means "at most once, optional". R11 is triggered only by a Change Bank Details after activation; a change before activation is not regulated by R1–R12 and is treated as unmodeled behavior by the backbone. Verify Bank Account can therefore occur more than once. |
| A10 | Timestamps define the event order. Tied timestamps on events whose order matters make the affected check `not evaluated`. |
| A11 | The alphabet has no "lift suspension" or "risk class" information. Whether a vendor is still suspended when paid, and whether the chosen route matches the risk class, cannot be checked. |
| A12 | `support = 1.0` and `confidence = 1.0` mean "normative by decree", not "observed in data". |

**The R5/R6 conflict.** R5 says the legal opinion must precede activation "under all circumstances". R6 says activation may precede the opinion in an emergency. For a trace such as T6 the two cannot both be the rule: R5 forbids it, R6 may permit it. R6 is additionally unverifiable, because neither the emergency flag nor the joint authorization is in the event log.

| Resolution | Rule applied | Effect on T6 | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A – Legal-strict (implemented)** | R5 is the rule. R6 is not recognized by the automated check. | `fail` | False positives: a genuinely authorized emergency is reported as a violation; pressure on Operations to work around the system. |
| **B – Operations exception** | R5 is the default; R6 overrides it only if (i) an emergency flag and two distinct authorizers are supplied from an external register and (ii) the opinion arrives within 24 h of activation. | `policy-dependent` while the evidence is missing; `pass` with evidence; `fail` if the 24 h are missed | False negatives if the register is weak; the exception can become a loophole filled in after the fact. |
| C – Timing-only exception (rejected) | Treat any early activation as an emergency if the opinion arrives within 24 h. | `pass` | Every R5 breach followed by a quick opinion would legalize itself. This silently merges the two policies and is not acceptable. |

**Choice: Resolution A.** It fails closed and needs no data the log does not have.

**Compliance risk of this choice.** The model encodes the Legal department's position as system truth although the organization has not settled the dispute. Legitimate emergency activations will be flagged as violations, which costs review effort and may encourage late or reordered logging of escalations. To contain this, the R5 finding is tagged with the policy name (`R5_STRICT`) and with the information whether R6's timing condition was met, so a reviewer can attach authorization evidence and record a waiver. The verdict is never silently turned green. Resolution B is implemented as a switchable alternative in Section 7 and evaluated for T6 in Section 8.

| R8 | **DECLARE** | `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)`, `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Symmetric exclusions. | Second rule duplicates R2. Backbone XOR and `never_together` repeat both. |

With R10, a "later payment" exists only if the change lies between activation and first payment. |
| R12 | **Temporal profile** | Three pairs with `(mean, std)` in seconds, `zeta = 2.0` | Statistical expectation, stated as "flag, do not reject". | Result is `warning` only. Cannot detect a missing event; lower bounds are 0 h, so "too fast" is invisible. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

**1. Compact formal description** (`→` sequence, `×` exclusive choice, `PO` partial order, `loop(τ, B)` = B zero or more times)

```
BACKBONE = Receive Onboarding Request → INTAKE → OUTCOME → Close Onboarding
INTAKE   = PO( { Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account, Classify Risk, CLAR } ;
               Capture Master Data < Classify Risk,
               Run Sanctions Screening < Classify Risk,
               Verify Bank Account < Classify Risk )
CLAR     = loop(τ, Request Clarification → Receive Clarification)
OUTCOME  = Reject Vendor × ACCEPT
ACCEPT   = APPROVAL → Activate Vendor → AMEND1 → Release First Payment → AMEND2
APPROVAL = Manager Approval × (Enhanced Due Diligence → Compliance Approval)
AMEND1   = loop(τ, PO({Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor, Verify Bank Account} ;
                      Change Bank Details < Suspend Vendor, Change Bank Details < Verify Bank Account))
AMEND2   = loop(τ, Change Bank Details → Suspend Vendor)
```

Sanctions Escalation and Obtain Legal Opinion are deliberately absent. The procedural check runs on the log projected onto the 16 backbone labels.

**2. PM4Py code (POWL)**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def partial_order(nodes, edges):
    """Strict partial order; every precedence edge is passed explicitly (transitive ones included)."""
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return po


receive = Transition(label="Receive Onboarding Request")
capture = Transition(label="Capture Master Data")
screen = Transition(label="Run Sanctions Screening")
verify = Transition(label="Verify Bank Account")
classify = Transition(label="Classify Risk")
request_clar = Transition(label="Request Clarification")
receive_clar = Transition(label="Receive Clarification")
edd = Transition(label="Enhanced Due Diligence")
manager_ok = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
compliance_ok = Transition(label="Compliance Approval")
reject = Transition(label="Reject Vendor")
activate = Transition(label="Activate Vendor")
pay = Transition(label="Release First Payment")
close = Transition(label="Close Onboarding")

clar_cycle = partial_order([request_clar, receive_clar], [(request_clar, receive_clar)])
clar_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), clar_cycle])

intake = partial_order(
    [capture, screen, verify, classify, clar_loop],
    [(capture, classify), (screen, classify), (verify, classify)],
)

high_risk = partial_order([edd, compliance_ok], [(edd, compliance_ok)])
approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[manager_ok, high_risk])

change_1 = Transition(label="Change Bank Details")
suspend_1 = Transition(label="Suspend Vendor")
reverify = Transition(label="Verify Bank Account")
amend_1 = partial_order([change_1, suspend_1, reverify], [(change_1, suspend_1), (change_1, reverify)])
amend_loop_1 = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), amend_1])

change_2 = Transition(label="Change Bank Details")
suspend_2 = Transition(label="Suspend Vendor")
amend_2 = partial_order([change_2, suspend_2], [(change_2, suspend_2)])
amend_loop_2 = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), amend_2])

accepted = partial_order(
    [approval, activate, amend_loop_1, pay, amend_loop_2],
    [
        (approval, activate), (activate, amend_loop_1), (amend_loop_1, pay), (pay, amend_loop_2),
        (approval, amend_loop_1), (approval, pay), (approval, amend_loop_2),
        (activate, pay), (activate, amend_loop_2),
        (amend_loop_1, amend_loop_2),
    ],
)

outcome = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[reject, accepted])

procedural_model = partial_order(
    [receive, intake, outcome, close],
    [
        (receive, intake), (intake, outcome), (outcome, close),
        (receive, outcome), (receive, close), (intake, close),
    ],
)

BACKBONE_ALPHABET = {
    "Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data", "Run Sanctions Screening",
    "Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk", "Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification",
    "Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval", "Reject Vendor",
    "Activate Vendor", "Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor", "Release First Payment",
    "Close Onboarding",
}


def check_procedural(log):
    """Alignments on the projection of the log onto the backbone alphabet."""
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(procedural_model)
    projected = log[log["concept:name"].isin(BACKBONE_ALPHABET)]
    return pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(projected, net, initial_marking, final_marking)
```

**3. Explanation**

- **Concurrency.** The three checks are unordered nodes of the `intake` partial order; each has an explicit edge to Classify Risk, which gives the AND-join of backbone step 3. T1, T2 and T3 use three different check orders and all fit.
- **Clarification loop.** `loop(τ, Request → Receive)` is a fifth, unordered node of `intake`. Clarification cycles can interleave with the checks and with classification, repeat any number of times, and must be closed before the outcome block starts. This is an approximation and it is stricter than R3: a cycle that is still open during an approval but closed before activation satisfies R3 and DECLARE but is a deviation here. A clarification node cannot be ordered against Reject Vendor and Activate Vendor without also being ordered against the approvals, because those activities sit inside the XOR block. DECLARE stays authoritative for R3.
- **Choices.** The outer XOR separates rejection from acceptance; the inner XOR separates Manager Approval from Enhanced Due Diligence → Compliance Approval. The choice is not guarded by the risk class because the log does not contain it (A11).
- **Terminal outcomes.** Reject Vendor leads directly to Close Onboarding. On the accepted branch, Close Onboarding is reachable only after Release First Payment. Each of Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor and Release First Payment lies outside any loop, so R10 holds in the model's language.
- **Post-activation behavior included.** `amend_loop_1` (between activation and first payment) requires each bank change to be followed by a suspension and a new Verify Bank Account before payment, in either order. `amend_loop_2` (between payment and closure) requires only the suspension, since R10 excludes a later first payment. Verify Bank Account therefore appears twice as a label.
- **Deliberately excluded.** Sanctions Escalation and Obtain Legal Opinion (A5): their position cuts across the AND-join, the choice and possibly the activation, so a hierarchical model would either duplicate them in many places or over-constrain them. Without the projection, T6 and T7 would each show two log moves. Also excluded: bank changes before activation and anything after Close Onboarding. A rejection after Enhanced Due Diligence is not allowed, following backbone step 4 literally.

# 4. DECLARE Model


In every binary key the first element is the source A and the second the target B.

| Template | Key (A, B) or activity | Semantics | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| init | Receive Onboarding Request | The case starts with this activity. | Backbone 1 |
| exactly_one | Receive Onboarding Request, Classify Risk, Close Onboarding | Occurs exactly once per closed case (A1). | Backbone |
| precedence | (Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation) | B may occur only if A occurred earlier. Triggered by B, looks backward. | R7 |
| precedence | (Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval) | No Compliance Approval without earlier due diligence. | Backbone 4 |
| precedence | (Activate Vendor, Release First Payment) | No payment without earlier activation. | Backbone 5 |
| response | (Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) | Every A is eventually followed by a B. Triggered by A, looks forward. | R4 |
| response | (Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor) | Every bank change is eventually followed by a suspension. Unconditional, so stricter than R11 for a pre-activation change. | R11 (redundant) |
| altresponse | (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | Every request is followed by a response, and no second request occurs before it. | R3 |
| altprecedence | (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | Every response is preceded by a request, with no other response in between. | A4 |
| noncoexistence | (Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor) | A and B never both occur in a case, in any order. | R1 |
| noncoexistence | (Manager Approval, Compliance Approval) | Same, symmetric. | R2, R8 |
| noncoexistence | (Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval) | Same, symmetric. | R8 |
| noncoexistence | (Reject Vendor, Manager Approval), (Reject Vendor, Compliance Approval) | No approval in a rejected case. | R2 |
| nonsuccession | (Activate Vendor, Request Clarification), (Activate Vendor, Receive Clarification), (Reject Vendor, Request Clarification), (Reject Vendor, Receive Clarification) | B never occurs after A. B before A stays allowed. | R3 |

**Distinctions that matter here**

- **Response vs. precedence.** R4 is an obligation created by the escalation, so it is `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`. R7 is a precondition of the escalation, so it is `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)`. Swapping them would be wrong in both cases: `response(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` would force an escalation into every case, and `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` would forbid a legal opinion without escalation, which nobody asked for.
- **Alternate response vs. chain response.** `chainresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` would require the response to be the very next event. T2 happens to satisfy that, but the concurrent checks may legitimately fall between request and response. `altresponse` forbids only a second request before the response, which is exactly R3's "before the next clarification request".
- **Noncoexistence vs. nonsuccession.** R1 is symmetric, so it is `noncoexistence`; `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor)` would miss Reject → Activate. "No clarification after a final outcome" is directional, so it is `nonsuccession`; `noncoexistence(Activate Vendor, Request Clarification)` would wrongly fail T2.
- **How R3's "before any final outcome" is obtained.** No request may follow an outcome, every request needs a later response, and no response may follow an outcome. Hence every response lies before the outcome.

**Not encoded, on purpose.** R5 (three activities), R10 (no at-most-once template), the disjunctive half of R2, and all of R6, R9, R12.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
def sym(*pairs):
    """Both orientations of each pair, for the symmetric relations."""
    return {p for a, b in pairs for p in ((a, b), (b, a))}

```

**Orientation of every relation**

- `equivalence (A, B)`: A and B occur equally often in the case. Symmetric, so both orientations are stored.
- `always_before (A, B)`: if A occurs, B occurred somewhere **before** it. The dependent activity comes first. This is the reverse of the DECLARE key: `always_before("Sanctions Escalation", "Run Sanctions Screening")` corresponds to `precedence("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")`.
- `always_after (A, B)`: if A occurs, B occurs somewhere **after** it. Same orientation as DECLARE `response(A, B)`.
- `never_together (A, B)`: A and B do not co-occur. Symmetric, both orientations stored. Corresponds to `noncoexistence`.
- `activ_freq`: allowed occurrence counts per case.
- `directly_follows (A, B)`: every A is immediately followed by B. Left empty: no requirement demands immediacy, and the free-floating activities (clarifications, escalation, legal opinion, bank changes) can fall between almost any two backbone steps. This is the Log-Skeleton counterpart of refusing `chainresponse` in Section 4.

**Role, overlap and disagreement with the other layers**

- **Authoritative for R10 only.** `activ_freq` {0,1} for Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor and Release First Payment is the one thing neither DECLARE nor the temporal profile can state. Everything else here is a cheap, redundant screen.
- **Overlap with DECLARE.** `never_together` repeats all five `noncoexistence` rules (R1, R2, R8). `always_after` repeats the two `response` rules. `always_before` repeats the three `precedence` rules.
- **Weaker than DECLARE on R3.** Equal counts plus "some response later" accept `Request, Request, Receive, Receive`, which `altresponse` rejects. The skeleton also cannot say "before the final outcome".
- **Overlap with the backbone.** The `always_before` pairs around Classify Risk restate the AND-join. `always_after`/`equivalence` on (Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval) and (Activate Vendor, Release First Payment) restate backbone steps 4 and 5 for closed cases. They disagree with DECLARE in one situation: Enhanced Due Diligence followed by Reject Vendor passes DECLARE but fails the skeleton and the backbone. If the business confirms that due diligence may end in rejection, these pairs and the backbone must be changed together.
- **Disjunction.** "Activate Vendor is preceded by Manager Approval or Compliance Approval" cannot be an `always_before` pair. Only the common ancestor Classify Risk is listed.
- **Loops.** Verify Bank Account is `{1..5}` and is not in `equivalence` with the case start, because R11 allows re-verification. A frequency of exactly 1 would contradict R11.
- **Artificial caps.** `activ_freq` needs finite sets. The value 5 for clarifications, escalations, opinions, bank changes, suspensions and verifications is a technical cap, not a requirement. Exceeding it should be reviewed, not treated as a policy breach.
- **Conditional rule.** `always_after(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` is unconditional and therefore, like the DECLARE rule, stricter than R11 for a change before activation.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * HOUR, 24 * HOUR),   # (172800, 86400)
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * HOUR, 6 * HOUR),      # (43200, 21600)
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * HOUR, 36 * HOUR),         # (259200, 129600)
}

zeta = 2.0

temporal_result = None  # pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta)
```

A pair instance is flagged when `|elapsed − mean| > zeta × std`. With `zeta = 2.0` the accepted bands are 0–96 h, 0–24 h and 0–144 h.

**Why deviations are warnings, not violations**

- R12 itself says "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject".
- Mean and standard deviation describe typical behavior; a 2σ rule flags a share of perfectly legitimate cases by construction.
- The profile says nothing about order or existence. A late legal opinion that still precedes activation (T7) is slow, not forbidden. Keeping the two apart prevents a timing outlier from masking or imitating a control-flow breach.
- Hard deadlines are a different kind of rule. R6's 24 hours is a deadline with a fixed anchor and is checked in Section 7. It happens to equal the upper band of the escalation pair, but it is measured from the activation, not from the escalation.

**Limitations.** All three lower bounds are 0 h, so only lateness can be flagged. A pair that never completes (T5's unanswered request) produces no measurement and no warning. PM4Py measures every eventually-follows instance of a pair, so two normal clarification cycles can produce a spurious warning between the first request and the last response.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Four checks live outside the activity-only models: both segregation-of-duties rules (R9), the disjunctive half of R2, the R5/R6 legal gate, and the R11 bank-change monitor. The last two need only activity and order, but they are conditions over three activities and so cannot be stated in the dictionaries above.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"

POLICY = "R5_STRICT"                     # implemented default; alternative: "R6_EXCEPTION"
EMERGENCY_DEADLINE = pd.Timedelta(hours=24)


def finding(case, check, status, at=None, detail=""):
    return {"case": case, "check": check, "status": status, "at": at, "detail": detail}


def ordered(trace):
    """Events of one case in timestamp order; None if the order cannot be trusted."""
    if TS not in trace.columns or trace[TS].isna().any():
        return None
    return trace.sort_values(TS, kind="stable")


def norm(resource):
    if resource is None or pd.isna(resource) or not str(resource).strip():
        return None
    return str(resource).strip().casefold()


def check_sod(case, trace, first, second, check):
    """R9: no event of `first` may share its resource with an event of `second`, in any order."""
    if RES not in trace.columns:
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="org:resource column missing")
    a = trace[trace[ACT].isin(first)]
    b = trace[trace[ACT].isin(second)]
    if a.empty or b.empty:
        return finding(case, check, "pass", detail="vacuous: pair not instantiated")
    ra, rb = [norm(r) for r in a[RES]], [norm(r) for r in b[RES]]
    clash = (set(ra) & set(rb)) - {None}
    if clash:
        at = b[b[RES].map(norm).isin(clash)][TS].min() if TS in b.columns else None
        return finding(case, check, "fail", at, f"same resource on both sides: {sorted(clash)}")
    if None in ra or None in rb:
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="resource missing on a relevant event")
    return finding(case, check, "pass")


def check_approval_before_activation(case, trace):
    """R2, disjunctive half: activation needs Manager Approval OR Compliance Approval before it."""
    check = "R2_APPROVAL_BEFORE_ACTIVATION"
    ev = ordered(trace)
    if ev is None:
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="timestamp missing")
    approved = False
    for act, ts in zip(ev[ACT], ev[TS]):
        if act in ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"):
            approved = True
        elif act == "Activate Vendor" and not approved:
            return finding(case, check, "fail", ts, "activation without approval")
    return finding(case, check, "pass")


def check_legal_gate(case, trace, policy=POLICY, authorizations=None):
    """R5 / R6: monitor with states clear -> escalated -> clear, guarding Activate Vendor.

    authorizations: optional dataframe from the external authorization register with the
    columns case:concept:name, emergency (bool), authorizer_1, authorizer_2.
    """
    check = f"LEGAL_GATE[{policy}]"
    ev = ordered(trace)
    if ev is None:
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="timestamp missing")
    gate = ev[ev[ACT].isin(["Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor"])]
    if gate[TS].duplicated(keep=False).any():
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="tied timestamps on gate events")
    pending, breach = None, None
    for act, ts in zip(gate[ACT], gate[TS]):
        if act == "Sanctions Escalation" and pending is None:
            pending = ts
        elif act == "Obtain Legal Opinion":
            pending = None
        elif act == "Activate Vendor" and pending is not None:
            breach = ts
            break
    if breach is None:
        return finding(case, check, "pass")
    if policy == "R5_STRICT":
        return finding(case, check, "fail", breach,
                       f"activated while the escalation of {pending} had no legal opinion")

    opinions = gate[(gate[ACT] == "Obtain Legal Opinion") & (gate[TS] > breach)][TS]
    if opinions.empty or opinions.min() - breach > EMERGENCY_DEADLINE:
        return finding(case, check, "fail", breach, "no legal opinion within 24 h of the activation")
    row = None
    if authorizations is not None:
        hit = authorizations[authorizations[CASE] == case]
        row = hit.iloc[0] if len(hit) else None
    if row is None or pd.isna(row.get("emergency")):
        return finding(case, check, "policy-dependent", breach,
                       "opinion arrived in time; emergency flag and joint authorization are not in the data")
    signers = {norm(row.get("authorizer_1")), norm(row.get("authorizer_2"))} - {None}
    if bool(row["emergency"]) and len(signers) == 2:
        return finding(case, check, "pass", breach, "documented emergency, two authorizers, opinion in time")
    return finding(case, check, "fail", breach, "emergency claim not backed by two distinct authorizers")


def check_bank_change(case, trace, case_closed=True):
    """R11: post-activation bank change -> suspension; re-verification before a later payment."""
    check = "R11_BANK_CHANGE"
    ev = ordered(trace)
    if ev is None:
        return finding(case, check, "not evaluated", detail="timestamp missing")
    active = need_suspend = need_verify = False
    for act, ts in zip(ev[ACT], ev[TS]):
        if act == "Activate Vendor":
            active = True
        elif act == "Change Bank Details" and active:
            need_suspend = need_verify = True
        elif act == "Suspend Vendor":
            need_suspend = False
        elif act == "Verify Bank Account":
            need_verify = False
        elif act == "Release First Payment" and need_verify:
            return finding(case, check, "fail", ts, "payment released on unverified new bank details")
    if need_suspend:
        status = "fail" if case_closed else "not evaluated"
        return finding(case, check, status, detail="bank change not followed by a suspension")
    return finding(case, check, "pass")


def run_external_checks(log, policy=POLICY, authorizations=None):
    out = []
    for case, trace in log.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        out.append(check_sod(case, trace, ["Verify Bank Account"],
                             ["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"], "R9A_VERIFIER_NOT_APPROVER"))
        out.append(check_sod(case, trace, ["Capture Master Data"],
                             ["Activate Vendor"], "R9B_CAPTURER_NOT_ACTIVATOR"))
        out.append(check_approval_before_activation(case, trace))
        out.append(check_legal_gate(case, trace, policy, authorizations))
        out.append(check_bank_change(case, trace))
    return pd.DataFrame(out)


def four_eyes_cross_check(log):
    """Cross-check of R9 with the built-in filter; returns the violating case ids."""
    violating = set()
    for first, second in [("Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval"),
                          ("Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval"),
                          ("Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor")]:
        hit = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, first, second, resource_key=RES, keep_violations=True)
        violating |= set(hit[CASE].unique())
    return violating
```

**Required attributes and behavior when they are missing**

| Check | Requirement | Required attributes | If missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R9A_VERIFIER_NOT_APPROVER` | R9, first sentence | `org:resource` on every Verify Bank Account and on the approval | `not evaluated`, routed to a data-quality queue. A proven clash is still `fail` even if other resources are missing. Never `pass` by default. |
| `R9B_CAPTURER_NOT_ACTIVATOR` | R9, second sentence | `org:resource` on Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor | Same. |
| `R2_APPROVAL_BEFORE_ACTIVATION` | R2 | Activity, timestamp | `not evaluated` without timestamps. |
| `LEGAL_GATE[R5_STRICT]` | R5 | Activity, timestamp | `not evaluated` on missing or tied timestamps of the three gate activities. |
| `LEGAL_GATE[R6_EXCEPTION]` | R6 | As above, plus from an external register joined by case id: `emergency`, `authorizer_1`, `authorizer_2` | `policy-dependent` if the 24-hour condition holds but the evidence is absent; `fail` if the 24 hours are missed, whatever the evidence says. |
| `R11_BANK_CHANGE` | R11 | Activity, timestamp, and the knowledge that the case is closed | Open case with pending suspension: `not evaluated`, not `fail`. |

`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is used only as a cross-check. The dataframe check is authoritative because it handles the "either approval" disjunction, repeated verifications and missing resources explicitly, and because it returns a status instead of a filtered log. In the vacuous case (no approval or no activation, as in T3) the check returns `pass` with the note "pair not instantiated".

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement across layers**

- R1 is enforced three times (backbone XOR, `noncoexistence`, `never_together`); R2/R8 likewise; R3, R4 and R7 twice. One root cause in T8 yields at least five findings across layers. Findings must be grouped by requirement id, with the authoritative layer shown first.
- Duplicates drift. If the business later allows rejection after Enhanced Due Diligence, the backbone and two Log-Skeleton relations must change together, while DECLARE needs no change. A register that maps each requirement to its layers is needed to keep them aligned.
- Redundant layers can be stricter than the authoritative one. A clarification answered during the approval but before activation satisfies R3 and DECLARE, yet the backbone reports two log moves. This must be shown as a model deviation, not as a policy breach.

**Constraints that become inconsistent under loops or post-activation changes**

- Verify Bank Account: "exactly once" (natural from the backbone) contradicts R11's re-verification. The frequency is therefore `{1..5}`, the label appears twice in the POWL model, and R9 becomes ambiguous about which verification is meant. All of them are checked (A8).
- R10 versus R11: R11 speaks of "any later Release First Payment" while R10 allows only one. The re-verification clause can only apply to a change between activation and first payment. Later payments are outside this log and unprotected.
- Suspend Vendor before the first payment: the alphabet has no reinstatement activity, so the model lets a payment follow a suspension once the account is re-verified. Whether the vendor is still suspended is unknown.
- Clarification loops: the Log Skeleton accepts interleavings such as Request, Request, Receive, Receive. The temporal profile pairs every request with every later response, so two normal cycles (39 h and 41 h) produce a 100-hour pair (z = 2.17) and a spurious warning.
- Multiple escalations: `response` is satisfied by one opinion after the last escalation. Whether each escalation needs its own opinion is unspecified (A6).
- R5 versus R6 is a true contradiction, not a redundancy. It is kept visible as two named policies. R6's 24 hours and R12's 24-hour upper band look alike but differ in anchor and in force.

**Vacuity in declarative rules**

- Most rules are conditional and are satisfied trivially when their trigger is absent. In T3, every response, precedence and nonsuccession rule about approvals, escalations or bank changes is vacuous. In T1, R3, R4, R7 and R11 are all vacuous.
- The DECLARE model alone accepts the trace ⟨Receive Onboarding Request, Classify Risk, Close Onboarding⟩. Only the backbone and `activ_freq` reject it. A DECLARE `pass` therefore means "no rule was broken", not "the process was followed".
- Reports should separate "activated and satisfied" from "not activated", in particular for R4, R5 and R9.

**Missing case attributes**

- Emergency flag and joint authorization (R6): absent, so Resolution B cannot reach `pass` from the log alone.
- Risk class: the result of Classify Risk is not recorded. A high-risk vendor approved through the manager route is undetectable in every layer.
- Outcome status, suspension status and closure status are all inferred from activities.
- Resource identity: "Cora" is assumed to be one person. Shared accounts, role mailboxes and deputies defeat R9 silently. A missing resource must give `not evaluated`, never `pass`.

**Timestamp-only limitations**

- There are no start/complete lifecycle events, so true overlap of the concurrent checks and working time versus waiting time cannot be told apart.
- Hour granularity produces ties. Order-sensitive checks (precedence, the legal gate, alignments) then depend on file order; the gate check refuses to decide on ties.
- Timestamps record when something was logged. An opinion issued verbally before activation but logged later looks exactly like T6.
- The temporal profile cannot see missing events (T5) and, with all lower bounds at 0 h, cannot flag suspiciously fast steps (payment 2 h after activation in T7).
- Mean and standard deviation assume a roughly symmetric distribution; elapsed times are usually skewed, and calendar effects (weekends) are ignored.

**Support/confidence metadata as normative truth**

- Here `support = 1.0` and `confidence = 1.0` are constants set by the modeler. They carry no empirical meaning, and the conformance call checks every rule regardless of them.
- In a mined model the same numbers are frequencies from a historical log. A rule with confidence 0.97 is a habit, not a policy, and a policy that is broken in 30% of cases is still a policy.
- Mixing mined and decreed rules in one dictionary makes them indistinguishable. Each rule should carry its source (requirement id or mining run), and confidence must never be used to down-weight a violation of a decreed rule. The same applies to the temporal profile: its figures are "normative enough to flag" because Compliance said so, not because the log shows them.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation.** The POWL model of Section 3 is the single source for the backbone. The accepting Petri net used for alignments is generated from it with `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net` and is never edited by hand. It is always applied to the log projected onto the backbone alphabet.

**Authoritative policy layer.** A requirement register keyed R1–R12 is the authority, and each entry names one deciding layer:

- DECLARE decides R1, R3, R4, R7, R8 and the exclusion parts of R2.
- The Log Skeleton decides R10.
- The external checks decide R5 (or R6, depending on the active policy), R9, R11 and the disjunctive part of R2.
- The temporal profile decides R12, with warnings only.
- The backbone is the reference for the standard flow. Its deviations are diagnostic unless they coincide with a failed requirement.

The active R5/R6 policy is a named, versioned configuration value owned by Compliance, not a code change.

**Order of checks.** All layers always run; nothing short-circuits, so the report is complete.

1. Data-quality gate: known activity labels, timestamps present, ties on order-sensitive events, `org:resource` present, case closed. Failures here produce `not evaluated` cells downstream.
2. DECLARE and Log Skeleton: cheap, linear, and they catch the decisive outcome conflicts (R1, R2, R3, R10).
3. External resource/data checks: segregation of duties, legal gate, bank-change monitor.
4. Alignments against the backbone: the most expensive step, used to localize where the standard flow was left.
5. Temporal profile: warnings.

Verdict precedence is `fail` > `policy-dependent` > `not evaluated` > `warning` > `pass`.

**Surfacing contradictory findings**

- One row per requirement and case, showing the result of every layer side by side with the authoritative layer marked. Duplicate findings are collapsed under their requirement id.
- If the authoritative layer passes and a redundant layer fails (the clarification-during-approval example), show "model deviation, no policy breach" and send it to process owners, not to Compliance.
- If the authoritative layer fails and the others pass (T4, T6), show the violation together with the note that the other layers cannot see this kind of rule, so that a row of green cells is not read as reassurance.
- For the legal gate, always show both policy verdicts with the policy name and version, for example "R5_STRICT: fail; R6_EXCEPTION: policy-dependent, opinion 10 h after activation, authorization evidence missing". Offer a waiver workflow that attaches the evidence and records who accepted it. The two verdicts are never merged into one status.
- Temporal findings appear in a separate warning channel with the observed value, the band and the z-score, and never change a `pass` or `fail` of another layer.
- Vacuous passes and `not evaluated` cells are displayed as such, so that the absence of evidence is not presented as compliance.
