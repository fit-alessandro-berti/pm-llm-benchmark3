## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is not discovered in the OCEL; it is imposed by choosing one object type whose instances become traces. Flattening on, for example, Order means each of O1–O4 becomes a case and every event directly related to that order is copied into its trace, in timestamp order. Different choices therefore answer different questions: Order asks “how is an order fulfilled?”, Package asks “how is a parcel built and shipped?”, Invoice asks “how is a billed amount settled?”. No one of these is the process; each is a projection of a many-to-many event–object graph containing 31 events, 4 orders, 6 items, 4 packages, 3 deliveries, 3 invoices and 4 payments.

**Convergence.** I use convergence for the situation in which one event is related to several objects of the chosen type and is therefore copied into several cases. E.g. flattened on Order, e13 *Pack Package* (O2,O3,I21,I31,P3) appears in both O2 and O3; e16 *Dispatch* and e24 *Confirm Delivery* appear in O1, O2 and O3. The flattened log then contains more event instances than the OCEL (31 unique events), and activity frequencies, DFG edge counts and throughput aggregates count that one real occurrence several times.

**Divergence.** I use divergence for the opposite distortion inside one case: because a case aggregates events concerning several distinct objects of *other* types, unrelated sub-lifecycles are interleaved and the DFG invents directly-follows relations between them. E.g. in the O1 trace, e11 *Pack Package* (P1/I11) is followed by e12 *Pack Package* (P2/I12), not because P1’s packing causes P2’s, but because O1 is split across P1 and P2. Likewise O3 contains I31 packed in P3 (e13) and I32 packed in P4 (e14), dispatched in D2 (e16) and D3 (e17) respectively; a single O3 trace splices two largely independent fulfilment branches.

**Copied shared events: both evidence and duplication.** A copied event is real evidence that the case participated in that occurrence — O2 genuinely was involved in e16 via I21/P3 — so deleting all shared events would falsify participation. But it is duplication if the analyst interprets flattened frequencies as counts of distinct real-world occurrences, or treats the copies as independent observations for statistics or conformance. I therefore report two numbers throughout: copied event instances (flattened log size) and unique event IDs (OCEL truth, here 31), and I flag every DFG edge whose support comes mainly from shared events e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27.

**Permitted transformations.** By default I use only the stated E2O relations; I do **not** silently propagate through O2O. Thus *Close Order* e28–e31 relate only to orders, and *Record Payment* e21 relates to O1, V1, M1 but not, by default, to I11/I12 or P1/P2. I permit one explicitly labelled sensitivity variant (Section 7) in which relations are propagated along named O2O qualifiers (Order–contains–Item, Package–packs–Item, Delivery–carries–Package, Invoice–bills–Order, Payment–settles–Invoice), because e.g. an Item-flattened analyst may want I11 to inherit e15 via P1. Any result from that variant must be reported as “propagated”, never merged with direct-E2O results.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**1. Cross-order package P3 — Order flattening (convergence).** e13 *Pack Package* (O2,O3,I21,I31,P3) is copied into O2 and O3. In an Order DFG, *Pack Package* gains frequency 2 from one physical packing of P3, and each of O2/O3 shows an *Add Item  Pack Package* edge although the two items were packed simultaneously into one parcel. Harmful if read as two packing operations or two independent hand-offs; informative if read as “both orders participated in P3”. Context-dependent, must be annotated with P3.

**2. Cross-order package P4 and order O3’s split — Order and Package flattening (divergence + convergence).** O3 is packed twice: I31 in P3 (e13, with O2’s I21) and I32 in P4 (e14, with O4’s I41). The O3 trace therefore shows Pack (e13)  Pack (e14)  Dispatch D2 (e16)  Dispatch D3 (e17)  Confirm (e24)  Confirm (e27), producing PackPack and DispatchDispatch self-loops and an apparent sequence between the P3-branch and P4-branch that never causally existed. Conversely, the P4 trace merges O3’s I32 and O4’s I41 into one parcel story. Harmful for root-cause analysis on O3 (which branch was late? — e24 at Feb 3 16:00 vs e27 at Feb 4 10:00), informative for detecting consolidation.

**3. Consolidated delivery D2 — Order and Delivery flattening.** e16 *Dispatch* and e24 *Confirm Delivery* each relate to O1,O2,O3, I12,I21,I31, P2,P3, D2. Order-flattened, the identical 11:0516:00 interval (4h55) is credited to three orders; O1 looks slow even though its other branch P1/D1 was already confirmed at 12:30–14:00 (e15, e23). Averaging order delivery times thus triple-counts one trip and biases O1 upward. Delivery-flattened, D2 is a clean two-event trace, but the DFG edge DispatchConfirm carries frequency that silently aggregates three orders and two packages. Context-dependent: true for the trip, false as per-order performance.

**4. Consolidated invoicing V2 — Order flattening.** e19 *Issue Invoice* (O2,O3,V2) is copied to both orders, and V2’s amount cannot be split from the given data. An Order DFG shows identical invoice behaviour for O2 and O3, while an Invoice-flattened V2 trace is the honest unit. If analysts compute “time from invoice to order close” per order, O2 and O3 inherit the same e19 timestamp and the same payment events, manufacturing correlation between two distinct orders. Harmful for per-order billing KPIs; informative about consolidation policy.

**5. Partial payments for V2 — Order, Invoice and Payment flattening.** V2 is settled by M2 (e22, Feb 3 12:40, *partially_settles*) and M3 (e25, Feb 4 09:00, *completes_settlement*). Order-flattened, both O2 and O3 show two *Record Payment* events and a Record Payment  Record Payment edge, although each order did not pay twice — the invoice was paid in two instalments, and attribution per order is undefined. Invoice-flattened, V2 correctly shows Issue  Payment  Payment, but a naive variant analysis may call this a “loop/rework” variant versus V1’s single payment (e18e21). Payment-flattened, M2 and M3 are indistinguishable singletons, hiding that one is partial and one completing — that distinction lives only in O2O qualifiers, not events. Harmful if instalments are read as duplicate payments or rework.

**6. Near-singleton lifecycle — Payment flattening.** Every payment M1–M4 participates in exactly one event (e21, e22, e25, e26 respectively). Flattening yields four traces of length 1, a DFG with one activity and no edges, 100% “variant” triviality, and undefined throughput. Any discovery algorithm output here is an artefact of the case notion, not behaviour. Harmful if presented at all as a process model; the correct treatment is to use Payment as an object/attribute dimension of Invoice or Order traces.

**7. Short lifecycle — Delivery flattening.** D1, D2, D3 each have exactly Dispatch and Confirm Delivery (e15/e23, e16/e24, e17/e27). The DFG is a single edge per case; there is no packing, payment or closing context, so D3’s confirmation at Feb 4 10:00 (e27) cannot be connected to the fact that O4 was only paid at 09:30 that day (e26). Moreover e28–e31 *Close Order* never appear, so a Delivery-based “end-to-end” duration understates order lead time by roughly a day for O3/O4. Harmful for end-to-end claims; fine for carrier transit-time comparison (D1: 11:0014:00; D2: 11:0516:00; D3: 11:15next-day 10:00).

**8. Split order O1 — spurious sequencing and performance mixing.** O1’s items travel separately: I11/P1/D1 (e11, e15, e23, confirmed Feb 3 14:00) and I12/P2/D2 (e12, e16, e24, confirmed 16:00). The flattened O1 trace interleaves them as PackPackDispatchDispatch…Confirm (e23) Confirm (e24)  Record Payment? — in fact payment e21 (12:30) falls between dispatches and confirmations, so the DFG gains edges such as DispatchRecord Payment and Confirm DeliveryRecord PaymentConfirm Delivery. O1’s “delivery lead time” depends entirely on whether the analyst takes first or last Confirm; neither is wrong, but a single timestamp per activity hides bimodality. Context-dependent and a classic divergence trap.

**9. Frequency inflation of shared logistics events — all multi-order flattenings.** Unique events total 31, but Order-flattening copies e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27 into two or three cases. *Dispatch* and *Confirm Delivery* each occur 3 times uniquely (e15–e17, e23/e24/e27) yet appear in up to 7 order-cases (e16 and e24 each in three). A DFG weighted by flattened frequency will rank Dispatch/Confirm as more “central” and frequent than Create Order (4 unique, 4 copies — no inflation), distorting bottleneck and conformance statistics. Harmful unless frequencies are normalised back to unique event IDs.

**10. Missing commercial context in Item/Package flattening — lifecycle truncation at the end.** No item or package is directly related to e18–e22 or e25–e31. Item traces end at Confirm Delivery (e23, e24, e27) and can look “successfully completed” for I21/I31 at Feb 3 16:00, while their orders O2/O3 were only fully paid Feb 4 09:00 (e25) and closed Feb 4 11:05 (e29, e30). Symmetrically, Order traces show *Close Order* as final, hiding that O3’s last delivery (e27, Feb 4 10:00) preceded closing by only an hour. Harmful for “completion” semantics; each flattening silently redefines what “done” means.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs still worth creating.** (i) **Order-flattened**: safe for order-level questions that tolerate participation semantics — e.g. which activity *types* touch an order before e28–e31 closing, order lead time from Create (e01–e04) to Close, and whether any order closes before its last Confirm/Payment (check O4: e26 payment 09:30 and e27 delivery 10:00 on Feb 4 vs e31 close 11:10). It is unsafe for counting physical operations or attributing D2/V2 performance to individual orders. (ii) **Invoice-flattened**: safe for settlement analysis — V2’s two-instalment pattern (e19, e22, e25) versus V1/V3 single settlement; unsafe for order attribution. (iii) **Package-flattened**: safe for parcel contents and pack-to-confirm durations (P1–P4), especially cross-order consolidation in P3/P4; unsafe for billing state. (iv) **Item-flattened**: safe for branch-level fulfilment timing per item. I would *not* publish Delivery- or Payment-flattened DFGs; I would export D1–D3 and M1–M4 as object tables (transit duration; partial vs completing qualifier from O2O) joined as attributes.

**Questions requiring object-centric views.** Anything about consolidation, sharing or interaction — How many orders share a package/delivery/invoice? Which objects co-occur at e16 or e24? Does closing wait for all branches? — needs an **OC-DFG** (activity counts per object type, showing e.g. *Pack Package* with Order=2, Item=2, Package=1 at e13-type events), an **OTG** (object-type lifecycle models: Delivery has 2 activities, Payment 1), an **ET–OT graph** (which event types touch which object types — immediately exposing that Payment touches only Record Payment and Delivery never touches Issue Invoice), an **object-interaction summary** (O2–O3 interact via P3, V2 and D2; O3–O4 via P4/D3; O1–O2/O3 via D2), and, if variant comparison at OC level is wanted, **equivalent-execution clustering** to group objects with genuinely equivalent executions rather than artefacts of copying, e.g. distinguishing O1’s split-branch execution from O2’s single-item one.

**Reconciling contradictions.** When flattened and object-centric results disagree — e.g. Order-DFG says *Record Payment* is frequent and loopy, OC-DFG says it involves one payment object at a time — I treat the OCEL E2O sets as ground truth and audit in this order: (1) recount using unique event IDs vs copied instances; (2) list the shared events supporting the disputed edge (usually e16/e24 or e22/e25) and their full object sets; (3) check whether the edge survives within a single non-shared branch (e.g. I11’s e11e15e23); (4) report both numbers with the semantics labelled (“participations” vs “occurrences”). A contradiction is resolved by narrowing the claim, never by averaging the two views.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Decision: B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles** — Order as primary lead, with Package, Invoice and Item as mandated secondary lifecycles, and Delivery/Payment demoted to object-attribute tables.

**Decision criteria.** (1) End-to-end coverage of the business outcome (order closed, paid, delivered); (2) minimal convergence/divergence damage, or at least damage that is detectable; (3) trace length sufficient for discovery; (4) interpretability for the fulfilment business question; (5) existence of a unique start/end anchor.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Only Order touches every phase: Create (e01–e04), Add Item, Pack, Dispatch, Issue Invoice, Record Payment, Confirm Delivery and Close (e28–e31) all relate to at least one order, and every order has a clean start (Create) and end (Close). Item/Package traces lose the commercial half (no e18–e22, e28–e31), Invoice traces lose fulfilment, Delivery traces have 2 events and Payment traces 1. Order is also the unit of the stated outcome “Close Order”, and O1–O4 each close exactly once.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order is precisely where convergence and divergence bite hardest: D2 (e16/e24) copies one trip into O1,O2,O3; V2 copies billing into O2,O3; O1 and O3 each splice two package branches, inventing PackPack and DispatchDispatch edges. An Order-only DFG would misstate operation counts, delivery times and payment behaviour for exactly the interesting consolidated cases, and choosing Order risks reifying it as “the case” for finance and logistics stakeholders whose real units are V2 and P3.

**Residual risk.** Even with secondaries, Order-first reporting can anchor audiences: frequencies in the lead DFG remain participation counts, and per-order attribution of V2’s instalments (M2/M3) and P3/P4 packing effort remains undefined in the data — no secondary view can create information the E2O/O2O relations do not contain.

**Conditions to change.** I would move to C (no lead) if the analysis purpose shifts to network/consolidation optimisation (how P3, P4, D2, V2 sharing arises), where OC-DFG/OTG should lead and flattening is only illustrative. I would move to A (Order only) only if shared events were rare or removed — contradicted here by e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27 — or if the sole deliverable were a simple order lead-time report with duplicated-event caveats accepted. I would promote Invoice to co-lead if the study becomes primarily about settlement risk around V2-style consolidation.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**1. Down-weighting/removing copied shared events.** Rebuild the Order log three ways: (a) as-is; (b) keeping each shared event (e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27) in only one designated case; (c) removing shared events entirely. Compare DFG edges and activity frequencies. Expected: PackPack and DispatchDispatch self-loops and the inflated Dispatch/Confirm frequencies collapse in (b)/(c), while genuine branch edges (e11e15 for I11/P1) survive in Item/Package logs. Report (a) as participation and the unique-event count as occurrence truth; never report (c) as behaviour, since O2 really did participate in P3.

**2. Direct E2O versus propagated relations.** Create a labelled variant propagating along O2O: Item inherits events of its Package (I11 inherits e15/e23 via P1) and Delivery (via carries), Order inherits Payment via Invoice–bills–Order, etc. Compare trace lengths and new edges: Item traces gain Dispatch context they already partly have, but Orders may gain duplicated payment paths and Items may spuriously gain Issue Invoice via their Order. Any edge appearing only in the propagated variant (e.g. Item–Issue Invoice) is flagged as transform-induced and excluded from headline findings.

**3. Changing the lead object.** Repeat discovery for all six flattenings and tabulate, per activity, unique-event frequency versus copied-instance frequency and per-case trace length. The decision in Section 5 is validated only if conclusions claimed as “end-to-end” (e.g. “orders close after final payment and delivery”) hold in the Order log *and* are not contradicted by Invoice (V2 paid e25 Feb 4 09:00, before e29/e30 closes 11:05) or Package views; conclusions that flip between leads (e.g. payment “loops”) are reclassified as consolidation artefacts.

**4. OC-DFG frequency semantics.** In the OC-DFG compare three annotations for the same activity: number of distinct events, number of unique objects, and total objects. E.g. at *Dispatch*, events = 3 (e15–e17) but total Order-objects = 6 participations (e16 alone contributes O1,O2,O3) and total Item-objects = 6, Package-objects = 4, Delivery-objects = 3. If a bottleneck claim depends on which annotation is used — D2 events look rare by event count but dominant by order participations — the claim must be restated per object type.

**5. Performance re-computation per branch.** Recompute delivery and lead times at Item and Package granularity (I11: e05e23; P3: e13e24; D2: e16e24) and compare with Order-level aggregates. Specifically test whether O1’s apparent slowness is an artefact of crediting D2’s 11:0516:00 interval to it despite P1/D1 confirming at 14:00 (e23), and whether O3’s duration is bimodal across P3/D2 (confirmed e24) versus P4/D3 (confirmed e27 next day).

**6. Qualifier-aware payment check.** Re-derive settlement status from O2O qualifiers (*settles* M1V1, M4V3; *partially_settles* M2V2; *completes_settlement* M3V2) and verify that no flattened DFG edge is interpreted as “paid in full” before e25 for O2/O3. This guards against the most consequential business error available in this log: closing the books on V2 at e22.
