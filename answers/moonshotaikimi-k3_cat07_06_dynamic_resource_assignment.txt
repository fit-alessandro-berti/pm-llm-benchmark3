# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy should optimize **sustainable effective throughput**: cases completed correctly and on time, not merely the number of activities started. There is no single obvious optimum because the objectives conflict. For example, the fastest specialist assignment may weaken resilience, while a training assignment may increase short-term service time.

**Objectives**

1. **SLA protection:** prioritize cases with urgent deadlines and low probability of completion before the deadline.
2. **Quality and error avoidance:** match complexity and risk to demonstrated skill.
3. **Effective throughput:** reduce waiting, avoid bottlenecks, and use permitted batching.
4. **Workload fairness:** consider open cases, estimated effort, remaining availability, and overtime risk—not only case counts.
5. **Skill development:** use suitable normal/low-risk work for supervised development.
6. **Continuity:** retain the current owner when competent, especially where reassignment counts, complexity, or handover costs are high.
7. **Resilience:** avoid dependence on one specialist, maintain backup capacity, and limit exposure to absence.

**Safety and compliance constraints**

These must not be traded away by a higher score:

- Skill level `0` means the resource is not eligible to perform the activity.
- Security Review:
  - Skill level 3 may perform it independently.
  - Skill level 1–2 may perform it only with explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.
  - Skill level 0 is never eligible, even as the assigned performer.
- Final Approval:
  - The finalizer must have Approval skill 2–3.
  - Skill level 1 may prepare but must not finalize.
  - Skill level 0 is not eligible.
- Urgent/high-priority work must not be assigned to skill level below 2 unless a qualified supervisor is explicitly assigned and available.
- No resource may receive more than four named assignments in the decision batch. Primary, trainee/preparer, and supervisor roles should each count conservatively as one assignment.
- Quality Check may be batched only when priority is normal/low and more than 24 hours of SLA remain.
- A supervised assignment is valid only if the supervisor is named, available, and has enough capacity to review the work.

**Operational guardrails**

- Do not assign non-urgent work to a resource with `overtime risk = high` when a compliant, safer alternative exists. An urgent exception requires a documented reason.
- At least 20% of normal/low-priority case decisions should support a documented training priority when SLA risk remains acceptable.
- If no compliant assignment can meet the SLA, choose the least-risk compliant option and trigger escalation rather than silently accepting the miss.

**Preference trade-offs**

These are balanced rather than treated as absolute:

- Specialist skill versus specialist bottleneck.
- Current-owner continuity versus skill mismatch or overload.
- Immediate speed versus supervised learning.
- Workload balance versus team or customer continuity.
- Batching efficiency versus responsiveness.
- Short-term output versus future capability.

SLA feasibility should be treated as a risk estimate, not as false certainty. Where estimates are unreliable, the policy should prefer a conservative assignment for urgent or high-complexity work.

# 2. Assignment policy

Use a **guardrail-first, context-weighted policy**. Hard rules determine who may be assigned; scores only choose among compliant alternatives.

**Inputs**

For each case:

- Activity and required skill
- Priority and SLA deadline
- Complexity
- Current owner
- Reassignment count
- Handover-cost band
- Customer or case notes

For each resource:

- Skill matrix and team
- Current open cases and estimated effort
- Hours remaining and working calendar
- Overtime risk
- Documented training priority
- Current number of assignments in the decision batch
- Availability and absence information

Historical service, waiting, handover, and rework behavior can be learned from the event log.

**Case urgency score**

Use both priority and deadline pressure:

\[
U(c)=0.6 \times Priority(c)+0.4 \times DeadlinePressure(c)
\]

where:

- `Priority`: urgent = 1.0, high = 0.75, normal = 0.45, low = 0.20.
- `DeadlinePressure = 1 - min(1, SLA remaining / activity reference horizon)`.
- The reference horizon can be a historical percentile for the same activity or an agreed planning horizon.

Cases are considered in descending urgency, then by SLA deadline.

**Resource-case scores**

For a compliant resource or supervised pair:

1. **SLA fit**

\[
SLAFit(r,c)=P(start_r+service_{r,c}+handover_{r,c}\leq deadline_c)
\]

Use historical service and waiting-time distributions. For urgent cases, use a conservative percentile rather than an average.

2. **Skill fit**

- Set target skill to 3 for urgent, specialist-mandated, or high-complexity work.
- Otherwise, target skill is normally 2.
- Penalize shortfalls; do not let workload or training compensate for a hard skill violation.
- A supervised trainee receives a lower immediate skill score but may receive a training benefit.

3. **Continuity and handover cost**

Give an owner-retention bonus scaled by:

- Handover-cost band: none = 0, low = 0.25, medium = 0.60, high = 1.00.
- Case complexity.
- Existing reassignment count.

Subtract the expected cost of changing owner. Historical evidence—such as the median 2.7-hour delay for high-complexity handovers—should calibrate this estimate. Also subtract the expected cost of retaining a weakly skilled owner, such as the historical 3.3-hour service-time increase.

4. **Workload and overtime fairness**

Consider:

- Open cases and their estimated remaining effort.
- Hours remaining today.
- Assignments already made in the current batch.
- Role weight: primary = 1.0, trainee/preparer  0.5, supervisor  0.25, calibrated locally.
- Overtime risk.
- Team-level load.

High overtime risk is a strong penalty and a hard exclusion for non-urgent work when a safer compliant alternative exists.

5. **Training and development**

Give the highest training score when:

- The required skill matches the resource’s documented training priority.
- The case has sufficient SLA slack.
- Complexity is suitable.
- A qualified supervisor is explicitly assigned.
- The worker has agreed to the development assignment.

Give a smaller score for cross-training that supports resilience but is not the worker’s documented priority.

6. **Resilience and bottleneck avoidance**

Penalize assignments that:

- Concentrate a scarce skill in one person.
- Leave no available specialist backup.
- Put too much urgent work on one resource.
- Create excessive dependence on one team.

Give a bonus for assignments that create a usable backup, provided they do not compromise safety or SLA.

7. **Batching and throughput**

Give a batching bonus only where permitted. For Quality Check, the case must be normal/low priority and have more than 24 hours of SLA remaining.

**Example weighting**

| Component | Urgent/high weight | Normal/low weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| SLA fit | 0.30 | 0.15 |
| Skill fit | 0.25 | 0.15 |
| Continuity/handover | 0.15 | 0.15 |
| Workload/overtime | 0.10 | 0.20 |
| Resilience | 0.15 | 0.15 |
| Training | 0.00–0.03 | 0.15 |
| Batching/throughput | 0.05 | 0.05 |

For high-complexity, high-handover cases, increase the continuity weight—for example by 0.10—and reduce non-safety weights proportionally. For scarce skills, increase the resilience weight.

**Pseudocode**

```python
def assign_batch(queue, resources, history, policy_version):
    state = build_point_in_time_state(resources, history)
    assignments = {}

    ordered_cases = sorted(
        queue,
        key=lambda c: (-urgency(c), c.sla_deadline)
    )

    for case in ordered_cases:
        candidate_units = []

        for resource in resources:
            # Single-resource candidates
            if hard_eligible(case, resource, state):
                candidate_units.append((resource, None))

            # Supervised candidates, where allowed
            for supervisor in possible_supervisors(case, resource, resources):
                if hard_eligible_pair(case, resource, supervisor, state):
                    candidate_units.append((resource, supervisor))

        if not candidate_units:
            escalate(
                case,
                reason="NO_COMPLIANT_RESOURCE",
                allowed_actions=[
                    "manager_review",
                    "deadline_renegotiation",
                    "approved_external_help",
                    "approved_overtime_if_lawful"
                ]
            )
            continue

        scored = []
        for resource, supervisor in candidate_units:
            components = {
                "sla": urgency(case) * sla_fit(case, resource, supervisor),
                "skill": skill_fit(case, resource, supervisor),
                "continuity": continuity_fit(case, resource),
                "workload": workload_fit(case, resource, supervisor, state),
                "training": training_fit(case, resource, supervisor),
                "resilience": resilience_fit(case, resource, supervisor, state),
                "batching": batching_fit(case, resource, state)
            }

            weights = context_weights(case)
            total = sum(weights[k] * components[k] for k in components)
            scored.append((total, components, resource, supervisor))

        # Continuity overlay for high-complexity/high-handover cases
        owner_option = find_current_owner_option(scored, case.current_owner)
        if (
            case.case_complexity >= 0.75
            and case.handover_cost_band == "high"
            and owner_option is not None
            and owner_option.sla_fit >= minimum_urgent_sla_fit(case)
            and owner_option.skill_is_adequate
        ):
            chosen = owner_option
        else:
            chosen = max(scored, key=lambda x: x.total)

        assign(case, chosen.resource, chosen.supervisor)
        update_state(state, case, chosen)

        write_assignment_reason(
            case=case,
            assigned=chosen.resource,
            supervisor=chosen.supervisor,
            score_components=chosen.components,
            exception_code=chosen.exception_code,
            policy_version=policy_version
        )

    # Batch-level training floor
    while documented_training_share(assignments) < 0.20:
        replacement = best_training_improvement(
            assignments,
            max_sla_risk=TRAINING_SLA_THRESHOLD,
            max_score_loss=TRAINING_TRADE_OFF_TOLERANCE
        )
        if replacement is None:
            break  # Record that the target was infeasible
        apply_replacement(replacement)

    return assignments
```

**Tie-breaking**

When scores are materially equal:

1. Fewer policy exceptions.
2. Higher SLA-completion probability.
3. Better continuity for high-handover cases.
4. Lower projected weighted workload.
5. Lower overtime risk.
6. Fewer assignments already made in the batch.
7. Transparent rotation among tied workers, avoiding repeated assignment of undesirable work to the same person.

**Exception handling**

- **No eligible resource:** escalate; never assign a skill-0 resource.
- **Supervisor unavailable:** do not make the supervised assignment.
- **All compliant options miss the SLA:** choose the least-latency compliant option, notify the case owner/manager, and record the exception.
- **High-overtime resource is unavoidable:** allow only for urgent SLA risk, with a named approver and reason.
- **Continuity override:** record whether the reason was skill risk, SLA infeasibility, overload, or absence.
- **Worker challenge:** allow the worker to request review. A compliant alternative should be considered without treating the challenge as a performance problem.
- **Dynamic updates:** rerun the policy on new arrivals, absence, completion, overtime-status changes, or SLA threshold breaches. Avoid repeatedly reshuffling stable assignments.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

Under the stated specialist-only tie-break, Celine would likely receive C003, C007, C009, C014, and C016—five assignments, violating the batch cap. Farid would likely receive four, and Hugo three. This illustrates the specialist bottleneck described in the historical evidence.

First-available routing could assign C002 to Jonas because he has the fewest open cases and Approval skill 1. That would violate the Final Approval rule if the baseline truly filters only skill 0.

# 5. Explanation to workers

> **How today’s assignments were chosen**
>
> Assignments are not a ranking of people and are not intended as criticism. We first apply agreed safety rules: only qualified people can perform Security Review or finalize Approval, and higher-risk work cannot be given to a trainee without named support.
>
> We then look at the deadline and complexity of the case, who already knows it, how much work each person currently has, remaining availability, overtime risk, and whether the case is a suitable learning opportunity.
>
> For example, C001 moves from Celine to Hugo because it is an urgent, high-complexity Security Review with only three hours remaining. This is not a judgment about Celine’s overall work; the case requires a Security specialist immediately. Celine’s knowledge should still be captured through a structured handover.
>
> C007 stays with Celine because she already owns the case, it has had several reassignments, and another handover would probably create more delay and risk.
>
> C003 and C009 give Amal supported diagnosis experience because that is his documented development priority. Celine provides review and support; Amal is not being left to carry the risk alone.
>
> Bruno, David, and Iris receive no new assignments in this batch because they are already at high overtime risk. This is a temporary workload-protection decision, not a signal that their work is less valued.
>
> No one receives more than four new assignments. Where a trainee or supervisor is involved, that role is visible in the assignment reason. If an assignment conflicts with your availability, development preferences, or knowledge of the case, you can ask for a review. Any change must still satisfy the same safety and SLA rules, and the reason will be recorded.

The worker-facing display should show the main reasons in plain language, such as “urgent deadline,” “current owner retained,” “qualified supervisor assigned,” or “overtime-risk protection,” rather than exposing only a numerical score.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

| Indicator | Event-log calculation | Interpretation and action |
|---|---|---|
| **Handover frequency** | Count consecutive changes in `org:resource` within each `case:concept:name`; normalize by completed cases. Stratify by activity, complexity, team, and handover-cost band. | Rising handovers may explain waiting and quality problems. Compare actual handover paths with the approved policy. |
| **Formal reassignment count** | Use the maximum or change in `reassignment_count` per case; report the share moving above two reassignments. | Cases above two reassignments are historically associated with higher rework and appeal rates and should receive review. |
| **Continuity retention rate** | Percentage of assignments where `org:resource = current_owner`, stratified by complexity and handover band. | Low retention may indicate unnecessary churn; very high retention may indicate that skill or overload risks are being ignored. |
| **Skill mismatch rate** | Join `org:resource` and `required_skill` to the skill matrix. Flag skill 0, skill below the activity minimum, or skill below 2 on high-priority work without a valid supervisor. | Unauthorized mismatch should be zero. Separate unauthorized mismatch from approved supervised development. |
| **Supervision validity** | Check that supervised Security work names R7_Gita or R8_Hugo and that Approval preparation has a skill 2–3 finalizer. | Detects nominal supervision that did not actually have qualified support. |
| **Workload distribution** | Assignments per resource and decision batch; weighted open work; distribution of estimated hours; Gini coefficient or coefficient of variation by resource and team. | Identifies persistent concentration and cap violations. Do not interpret raw case counts without case mix and effort. |
| **Overtime-risk exposure** | New assignments and estimated hours assigned to resources with high overtime risk; split urgent approved exceptions from non-urgent assignments. | Non-urgent high-overtime assignments should be rare and explainable. |
| **SLA misses and near misses** | Compare case completion time with `sla_deadline`; report by priority, activity, resource, team, complexity, and assignment type. | Identify whether SLA failures come from waiting, service time, handovers, or impossible deadlines. Use only closed cases or mark open cases as right-censored. |
| **Rework or appeal after assignment** | Detect later `Rework Correction` or appeal events within an agreed window. Compare cases with similar priority and complexity. | Tests whether assignments are associated with downstream quality outcomes. Use as a learning signal, not automatic individual blame. |
| **Training assignment share** | Normal/low-priority case decisions where required skill matches the resource’s documented training priority and supervision is recorded, divided by all normal/low-priority decisions. | Should meet the agreed 20% floor when feasible. Also monitor whether training opportunities are distributed fairly. |
| **Training outcome** | Later changes in skill level, service time, rework, and independence after supervised assignments. | Determines whether training assignments are developing capability rather than merely adding a supervisor. |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | Queue length, median waiting time, service time, utilization, and share of scarce-skill work assigned to each skill-3 resource. Network centrality can identify key-person dependency. | Persistent concentration in Security or Approval indicates a structural capacity problem that assignment rules alone cannot solve. |
| **Quality batching compliance** | For discovered Quality Check batches, verify that every case is normal/low priority and has more than 24 hours of SLA remaining at batch creation. | Detects batching efficiency being prioritized over deadline compliance. |
| **Policy deviation rate** | Recompute the eligible and recommended set using the point-in-time snapshot. Compare it with the actual assignee and classify approved exception, unapproved exception, or missing data. | Distinguishes sensible human judgment from undocumented policy bypasses. |
| **Reason-code completeness** | Percentage of assignments with a structured `assignment_reason`, policy version, supervisor/finalizer where relevant, and exception code. | Missing reasons make the policy unauditable even if the assignment was appropriate. |
| **Worker challenge and override rate** | Count worker-requested reviews, manager overrides, and subsequent changes. | A very low rate may indicate lack of psychological safety; a high rate may indicate poor policy fit. Interpret with qualitative feedback. |

Historical associations should be case-mix adjusted. For example, complex cases may receive both more handovers and more rework, so the audit should not automatically attribute rework to the individual who accepted the handover.

For reliable auditing, the assignment service should store an append-only decision record containing the decision time, policy version, eligible resources, recommended ranking, score components, assigned role, supervisor/finalizer, exception code, and approver. The existing `assignment_reason` field can contain a structured reason string, but free text alone is difficult to audit.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ------------------------------------------------------------
events = pd.read_csv("service_process_events.csv")
events[TS] = pd.to_datetime(events[TS], utc=True)
events["sla_deadline"] = pd.to_datetime(events["sla_deadline"], utc=True)

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id=CASE,
    activity_key=ACT,
    timestamp_key=TS
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover resource and organizational structures
# ------------------------------------------------------------
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
organizational_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
discovered_batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)

# Interactive use:
# pm4py.view_sna(handover_net)
pm4py.save_vis_sna(handover_net, "handover_network.html")

# Interpretation:
# - handover_net: frequent resource-to-resource transfers.
# - working_together_net: whether claimed supervisor/trainee pairs actually co-occur.
# - resource_similarity: possible backup resources, subject to human validation.
# - organizational_roles: compare discovered work profiles with the official skill matrix.
# - discovered_batches: audit whether Quality Check batches satisfy the batching rule.

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Learn service, waiting, and path-performance behavior
# ------------------------------------------------------------
service_time = pm4py.get_service_time(log)
log_sw = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)

performance_dfg, start_activities, end_activities = (
    pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_sw)
)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log_sw)

# Example cohort filters
security_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log_sw,
    attribute_key="required_skill",
    values=["Security"],
    retain=True
)

urgent_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log_sw,
    attribute_key="case_priority",
    values=["urgent"],
    retain=True
)

security_performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(security_log)

# Cases completed within 24 hours; use a separate filter for long-running cases.
fast_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
    log_sw,
    0,
    24 * 60 * 60
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover and reassignment indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------
e = log_sw.copy()
e = e.sort_values([CASE, TS])

e["previous_resource"] = e.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift()
e["is_handover"] = (
    e["previous_resource"].notna()
    & e[RES].notna()
    & (e[RES] != e["previous_resource"])
)

handover_events = e[e["is_handover"]]
handovers_per_case = handover_events.groupby(CASE).size()

case_reassignment_max = (
    e.groupby(CASE)["reassignment_count"]
    .max()
    .rename("max_reassignment_count")
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join assignments to the skill matrix
# ------------------------------------------------------------
skill_columns = [
    "Eligibility", "Diagnosis", "Negotiation", "Security",
    "Approval", "Quality", "Rework"
]

skills = skill_matrix.rename(columns={
    "Resource": RES,
    "Training priority": "training_priority",
    "Overtime risk": "overtime_risk"
})

skills_long = skills.melt(
    id_vars=[RES, "training_priority", "overtime_risk"],
    value_vars=skill_columns,
    var_name="required_skill",
    value_name="skill_level"
)

# Prefer one explicit assignment-decision event per assigned role.
a = e[e[RES].notna()].copy()

# Recommended structured assignment_reason format:
# "policy=v1;role=trainee;supervisor=R3_Celine;exception=none"
reason = a["assignment_reason"].fillna("")
a["assignment_role"] = reason.str.extract(
    r"(?:^|[;|])role=([^;|]+)", expand=False
).fillna("primary")
a["supervisor"] = reason.str.extract(
    r"(?:^|[;|])supervisor=([^;|]+)", expand=False
)
a["finalizer"] = reason.str.extract(
    r"(?:^|[;|])finalizer=([^;|]+)", expand=False
)
a["exception_code"] = reason.str.extract(
    r"(?:^|[;|])exception=([^;|]+)", expand=False
)

a = a.merge(skills_long, on=[RES, "required_skill"], how="left")

approval_skill = (
    skills_long[skills_long["required_skill"] == "Approval"]
    .set_index(RES)["skill_level"]
    .to_dict()
)

def hard_compliant(row):
    role = str(row.get("assignment_role") or "primary")
    skill = row.get("skill_level")
    priority = str(row.get("case_priority", "")).lower()
    required = row.get("required_skill")
    supervisor = row.get("supervisor")

    if role == "supervisor":
        return True  # Validate supervisor qualification separately.

    if pd.isna(skill) or int(skill) == 0:
        return False

    if required == "Security":
        return (
            int(skill) == 3
            or (
                int(skill) in (1, 2)
                and supervisor in {"R7_Gita", "R8_Hugo"}
            )
        )

    if required == "Approval":
        if role == "preparer":
            finalizer = row.get("finalizer")
            return (
                int(skill) >= 1
                and approval_skill.get(finalizer, 0) >= 2
            )
        return int(skill) >= 2

    if priority in {"urgent", "high"} and int(skill) < 2:
        return pd.notna(supervisor)

    return True

a["hard_compliant"] = a.apply(hard_compliant, axis=1)
unauthorized_assignments = a[~a["hard_compliant"]]

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Workload, overtime, and batch-cap indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Replace this derived batch with the real decision-batch ID if available.
a["decision_batch"] = a[TS].dt.floor("15min")

assignments_per_batch = (
    a.groupby(["decision_batch", RES])
    .size()
    .rename("new_assignments")
)
cap_violations = assignments_per_batch[
    assignments_per_batch > 4
]

high_ot_nonurgent = a[
    (a["overtime_risk"] == "high")
    & (~a["case_priority"].str.lower().eq("urgent"))
]

def gini(values):
    values = np.asarray(values, dtype=float)
    values = values[~np.isnan(values)]
    if len(values) == 0 or values.sum() == 0:
        return np.nan
    values = np.sort(values)
    n = len(values)
    index = np.arange(1, n + 1)
    return ((2 * index - n - 1) * values).sum() / (n * values.sum())

numeric_workload = pd.to_numeric(
    a["workload_snapshot"], errors="coerce"
)
workload_gini = gini(numeric_workload)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. SLA, rework, and appeal outcomes
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Use a proper case-status field if available; here the last event is a proxy.
closed_cases = (
    e.sort_values(TS)
    .groupby(CASE)
    .tail(1)
    .copy()
)
closed_cases["sla_miss"] = (
    closed_cases[TS] > closed_cases["sla_deadline"]
)

sla_miss_by_activity = (
    closed_cases.groupby(ACT)["sla_miss"]
    .mean()
    .sort_values(ascending=False)
)

rework_times = (
    e[e[ACT] == "Rework Correction"]
    .groupby(CASE)[TS]
    .min()
)

a["later_rework"] = a.apply(
    lambda row: (
        row[CASE] in rework_times.index
        and row[TS] < rework_times.loc[row[CASE]]
    ),
    axis=1
)

appeal_times = (
    e[e[ACT].str.contains("appeal", case=False, na=False)]
    .groupby(CASE)[TS]
    .min()
)

a["later_appeal"] = a.apply(
    lambda row: (
        row[CASE] in appeal_times.index
        and row[TS] < appeal_times.loc[row[CASE]]
    ),
    axis=1
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Documented training share
# ------------------------------------------------------------
a["documented_training"] = (
    a["required_skill"].str.casefold()
    == a["training_priority"].fillna("").str.casefold()
) & reason.str.contains("training", case=False)

normal_low = a[
    a["case_priority"].str.lower().isin(["normal", "low"])
]

# Count one case-activity decision, not every supervisor row.
decision_keys = [CASE, ACT, TS]
normal_low_decisions = normal_low.drop_duplicates(decision_keys)
training_cases = set(
    normal_low.loc[normal_low["documented_training"], CASE]
)

training_share = (
    normal_low_decisions[CASE].isin(training_cases).mean()
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Specialist bottleneck indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------
waiting_columns = [
    c for c in e.columns
    if "waiting" in c.lower()
]

if waiting_columns:
    waiting_col = waiting_columns[-1]
    waiting_by_required_skill = (
        e.groupby("required_skill")[waiting_col]
        .median()
        .sort_values(ascending=False)
    )

specialist_assignments = (
    a[a["skill_level"] == 3]
    .groupby(["required_skill", RES])
    .size()
    .rename("specialist_assignments")
)

    historical_state = reconstruct_state_as_of(row[TS])
```

**How the PM4Py outputs support the policy**

- `discover_handover_of_work_network` identifies frequent and potentially costly ownership transitions.
- `discover_working_together_network` verifies whether named supervisors and trainees actually work together.
- `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` can suggest backup resources, but similarity must not override the formal skill matrix.
- `discover_organizational_roles` can reveal unofficial specialization or drift from documented roles.
- `discover_batches` supports the Quality Check batching audit.
- `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` provide historical inputs for SLA-fit estimates.
- `discover_performance_dfg` shows where time accumulates in Security, Approval, urgent cases, or other filtered cohorts.
- `get_variants` helps identify repeated diagnosis–rework loops and unusual reassignment paths.
- `filter_event_attribute_values` supports cohort audits by required skill, priority, team, assignment reason, or handover band.
- `filter_case_performance` separates short and long cases for deeper bottleneck analysis.
- `view_sna` or `save_vis_sna` makes resource networks inspectable by process owners and worker representatives.

All learning and audit results should be reviewed with process owners and worker representatives. Process-mining measures can be affected by missing events, case mix, calendars, and changing demand, so they should guide discussion and improvement rather than serve as automatic individual performance judgments.
