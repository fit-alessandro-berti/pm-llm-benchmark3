## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (given, not assumed).** GPIP-4 is a board-approved global policy effective 2026-01-01 for all entities and purchases. DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6 is a controlled Aachen SOP effective 2025-03-01 for maintenance and production-support purchases. ECD-2 is an incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander, effective only 2026-07-14 09:00–2026-07-18 18:00 CEST, scoped to “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.” The CFO email of 2026-07-15 07:20 is an uncontrolled ordinary email. The CRO was unavailable and did not ratify ECD-2. Incident Command’s charter does not call members budget owners. The CFO owns the Finance control framework. GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to the CRO. The organization has treated signed incident directives as temporary operational overlays, but not for new-supplier Compliance clearance. ERP ON-44 was down 2026-07-14 08:30–13:00 CEST and recovered timestamps may lag real acts by up to 12 minutes.

**Assumptions (interpretive, stated so they can be rejected).**

1. The legally relevant “supplier commitment” is the act `Commit_to_Supplier` (or an equivalent binding communication evidenced by supplier acknowledgment), not `Create_PO` and not a later ERP write.
2. Rules are applied at the time of that act. A later approval can mitigate consequences (A6) but does not convert a missing pre-commitment control into a timely one.
3. “Unapproved supplier” (local register) is not automatically a “new supplier” (A3). A3’s new-supplier prong is applied only when the case states a new supplier.
4. ECD-2 is **not** an A7 CRO exception. Silence in A7 plus absence of CRO signature or later ratification is treated as failure of the GPIP-4 exception path, not as implied authorization.
5. ECD-2 **can** displace ordinary *local* timing and supplier-register steps (B1, B3, B7, C1, C2, C4) when the purchase is inside ECD-2’s time window **and** purpose scope.
6. ECD-2 scope is read from the text “required to contain the current contamination event,” not from after-the-fact labels. Same-day / ≤24-hour need on the affected sterilization line is in scope. Multi-day backup stock and non-production site services are out of scope. Operations’ broader “backup = critical” reading is noted as a live alternative, not adopted.
7. Local B4 emergency (≤6-hour stoppage or imminent safety harm) is a standing SOP track. It waives PO-before-commit only. It does not waive Finance, the approved-supplier rule, or GPIP-4.
8. After 2026-07-15 07:20, D1 is a contemporaneous CFO interpretation of *Finance/operational* approvals for in-scope ECD-2 purchases **up to EUR 150,000**. It is not a waiver of A1 or A3 (D3, D4). It has no retroactive effect on 14 July. Above EUR 150,000, D1’s sufficiency statement does not apply; D2 adds Legal clearance and does not replace A2.
9. A person who is requester or committer and also one of two joint “final” authorizers fails A1. Joint authorization is still final business approval.
10. Where an exception, overlay, verbal extension, or ECD-2 scope claim is asserted, the organization bears the burden. Unrecorded oral extension of a written, time-limited directive fails that burden.
11. A late ERP timestamp is not conclusive if telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, or supplier acknowledgments show an earlier real act (ON-44 and the P-505 clock note). Conversely, absence of any contemporaneous record means the control is unproven.

**Decision method.** For each case: (i) fix the commitment time using the best evidence; (ii) test ECD-2 window and purpose scope, then B4 emergency, then standing SOP and GPIP-4; (iii) apply issue-specific override (section 2), not a single document-beats-document ranking; (iv) refuse to treat emergency status, COO signature, recency, or strong wording as a universal trump; (v) classify under the primary interpretation even when a credible alternative exists (section 5). Primary labels are used as follows: **COMPLIANT** = standing rules or a written, in-scope SOP emergency track were met at the time of the act; **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** = a time-limited overlay (ECD-2, and after 15 July D1 within its cap) validly displaced the specific local/Finance mechanics that were skipped, while non-waivable integrity controls that were triggered were met on time; **NONCOMPLIANT** = a required control was missing or late at commitment and no valid exception covers that issue; **INDETERMINATE** = the facts or the authority question are too thin to choose (used only if the record cannot support a primary call).

**Working stack by issue.** Integrity (A1, A3): GPIP-4 unless written CRO A7. Money dual-control (A2): GPIP-4, with D1 able only to interpret the Finance-Controller *mechanics* after 15 Jul 07:20 for in-scope amounts ≤EUR 150k; the independent-budget-owner title remains imperfect and is accepted only as a justified incident substitute when C3 dual IC authorization is timely and SoD is clean. Local sequencing and register (B1, B3): ECD-2 in window and scope, else B4 if its definition is met, else SOP. Informal email never outranks a board policy on SoD or Compliance.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 → NONCOMPLIANT** if committing EUR 38k is itself a Plant-level act, so B4’s second limb (absent PM + active delegation) was a condition of *commitment*, not only of `Plant_Approve`. Premise change: treat SHIFT-1’s 08:50 commit as an unauthorized Plant substitution. Credible because the case file stresses that the PM was reachable and did not delegate. Weaker than the primary reading because B4’s grammar attaches the delegation clause to Plant-Manager substitution, while PO sequencing is a separate permission, and a later distinct Plant_Approve exists.

**P-502 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** if C4’s “supplier-onboarding requirements” is read to include GPIP-4 A3 new-supplier Compliance, and B7 is read to let ECD-2 swallow Finance/Plant as “ordinary local workflow steps.” Premise change: ECD-2 becomes a de facto A7 for onboarding-plus-approvals despite CRO non-signature. Credible because C2 expressly allows an off-register supplier and the buy is core containment. Weak because A4/A5/A7 and the organization’s own history *exclude* this exact Compliance pattern, D1 did not yet exist, and A6 is explicit.

**P-503 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (or at least a narrower NONCOMPLIANT)** if Operations’ C6 reading is adopted (backup-line components are “critical”) **and** joint IC authorization is treated as C3 + D1 Finance substitute **and** A1 is read to require only that the *second* authorizer be independent. Premise change: scope liberalized and SoD reduced to “not sole approver.” The scope half is credible (IC labeled the pumps critical contingency). The SoD half is weak: PROC-3 cannot be requester, committer, and co-authorizer of the same EUR 140k commitment without emptying A1. A3 would still be unmet unless C4 is also over-read as in P-502.

**P-505 → NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE.** NONCOMPLIANT if the ERP commit stamp 18:06 is treated as conclusive, so the act fell after ECD-2 expiry and D1 died with it; or if D1 is denied any power over A2. INDETERMINATE if one refuses to choose between supplier-ack 17:59:31 and ERP 18:06 without a certified clock offset. Premise change: evidential ranking of ERP over supplier acknowledgment, or refusal to let an uncontrolled CFO email touch A2. The ERP-first variant is weak against the instruction to prefer contemporaneous external evidence. The “D1 is a nullity” variant is credible (uncontrolled email, D4 caveat, IC not budget owners) and is the best attack on the primary JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

**P-507 → NONCOMPLIANT** if A2 is held fully live (no D1 displacement) or if the authorization record is the ERP 14:30 entry rather than the 12:40 PDF, breaking C3’s four-hour recording rule. Premise change: either D1 is non-normative, or logging time is record time. Both are credible. The D1-nullity variant is the stronger; the PDF is a contemporaneous signed artifact created before 12:55, so the late-ERP attack is weak.

**P-508 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** if operational necessity (“only available pump,” affected line) plus timely A3, D2 Legal, clean SoD, and C3 are treated as collectively satisfying the purpose of A2, with the EUR 5,000 excess over D1 treated as de minimis. Premise change: purpose-and-necessity override of a numeric cap the CFO himself wrote. Credible on facts and process purpose; weaker as legal method, because the same email that eases Finance below 150k *creates* the 150k line, and the team proved it could obtain Compliance and Legal in minutes.

**P-506 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** only if an unrecorded verbal IC extension can continue ECD-2. That premise is weak: it contradicts written time limits, C3’s recording rule, A7’s writing requirement, and the claimant’s burden. Not adopted.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement clause (for GPIP-4 A4/A7 + local SOP + incident playbook)**

1. **Hierarchy.** Board policy (GPIP-4) controls integrity: segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, and the existence of dual money approval. A controlled local SOP may add stricter thresholds and evidence. An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander may vary *only* ordinary local sequencing (PO vs commit) and *local* approved-register onboarding, and only inside its written window and purpose. An uncontrolled email may interpret Finance *evidence*, not waive GPIP-4.

2. **Exception authority.** Only the Chief Risk Officer may switch off A1, A2, or A3, and only by a written, scoped, time-limited instrument naming the site, lot/purpose, amount cap, suppliers or supplier classes, start/end (timezone), and which clauses are paused. A COO+IC directive is not an A7 instrument unless the CRO countersigns or ratifies in writing within 72 hours. Absence of the CRO is not implied consent.

3. **Purpose and expiry.** “Critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the event” means parts without which the affected line, or a line already in overflow containment, cannot run, and that are needed within 24 hours. Backup stock with more than 24 hours of cover, and non-production site services, are out of scope. The directive ends at the written end-time. Extensions must be written and jointly signed before expiry. Verbal continuation has no effect.

4. **Thresholds during a valid overlay.** Competitive tendering may be waived. Commit-before-PO and off-register supply may be allowed. Dual Incident Command authorization (two people who are not the requester or committer) must precede commit; if verbal, a signed record is due within four hours. For amounts ≥ the GPIP-4 Finance trigger, that dual authorization may stand in for Plant routing and for the Finance *Controller mechanism* up to a stated cap (e.g. EUR 150,000) **only if** the CFO has issued a controlled interpretation. Above the cap, Legal clearance **and** a Finance Controller action are required before commit. Compliance clearance remains required before commit for every new supplier and every purchase above EUR 100,000.

5. **Segregation.** Requester and committer are ineligible as Incident Command authorizers on the same purchase. Incident Command membership does not, without more, make a person the independent budget owner.

6. **Records and clocks.** Supplier acknowledgments, recorded calls, and signed PDFs outrank ERP timestamps when systems are down or skewed. A late database write of a timely artifact is a logging defect, not a late approval. A late approval of an early commit is not a timely approval.

7. **Safeguard against emergency normalization.** Each incident directive must state that it is not standing policy, auto-expires, and will be followed within ten business days by a CRO/Internal Audit sample of every commit above EUR 25,000 in a window starting six hours before the directive and ending 48 hours after expiry. Any purchase whose purpose is “recovery,” “contingency,” or “site continuity” after expiry is processed under standing GPIP-4 with no overlay residual. A third incident directive in any rolling twelve months that repeats the same clause variances requires prior CRO approval, specifically to prevent emergency practice from becoming the default procurement path.
