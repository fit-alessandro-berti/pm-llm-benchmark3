## 1. Analytical assumptions

### Case notion, convergence, and divergence
- **Case notion**: A case notion is an analytical projection that selects a single object type $ot \in \{\text{Order}, \text{Item}, \text{Package}, \text{Delivery}, \text{Invoice}, \text{Payment}\}$ as the process instance identifier. Under flattening on $ot$, each object $o \in O_{ot}$ defines one trace $\sigma_o = \langle e_1, \ldots, e_k \rangle$ containing all events $e$ where $(e, o) \in E2O$, ordered by timestamp. Any event $e$ lacking an $E2O$ link to an object of type $ot$ is dropped from the flattened log.
- **Convergence**: Convergence occurs when a single event $e$ is associated with multiple objects of the selected case-notion type $ot$ ($|\{o \in O_{ot} \mid (e, o) \in E2O\}| > 1$). When flattening on $ot$, event $e$ is replicated once per related object $o \in O_{ot}$. In this snapshot, convergence arises from cross-order packing (`e13` links to `O2, O3` and `I21, I31`; `e14` links to `O3, O4` and `I32, I41`), consolidated delivery (`e16` and `e24` link to `O1, O2, O3`, `I12, I21, I31`, and `P2, P3`; `e17` and `e27` link to `O3, O4` and `I32, I41`), and consolidated invoicing/payment (`e19`, `e22`, and `e25` link to `O2, O3`).
- **Divergence**: Divergence occurs when multiple distinct events of the same activity (driven by subordinate or parallel child objects) link to the same instance $o \in O_{ot}$ of the chosen case notion. Within trace $\sigma_o$, those repeated occurrences become indistinguishable from rework loops or interleaved cycles because the differentiating child object identifier is stripped from the case key. In this snapshot, divergence appears on `Order` when an order contains multiple items (`O1` in `e05, e06`; `O3` in `e08, e09`), splits across packages (`O1` in `e11, e12`; `O3` in `e13, e14`) and deliveries (`O1` in `e15, e16` and `e23, e24`; `O3` in `e16, e17` and `e24, e27`), or undergoes split payments (`O2` and `O3` in `e22, e25`), and on `Invoice` when `V2` receives two instalments (`e22, e25`).

### Treatment of copied shared events: both state-transition evidence and workload duplication
Copied shared events must be treated as **both** valid state-transition evidence and distorting workload duplication, depending on the analytical question:
1. **As state-transition evidence (object lifecycle view)**: When `e16` (`Dispatch`) executes at `11:05`, it genuinely advances the fulfilment state of `O1`, `O2`, and `O3` (as well as `P2`, `P3`, and `I12, I21, I31`). Omitting `e16` from `O2` or `O3` would falsely make those orders appear undelivered before `Close Order` (`e29, e30`).
2. **As workload/capacity duplication (event/resource view)**: `e16` is a single physical dispatch operation (`D2`). Counting `e16` three times in an `Order`-flattened log or three times in an `Item`-flattened log inflates dispatch volume by $200\%$ for that shipment and biases weighted throughput-time averages toward consolidated shipments.

### Permitted analytical transformations and O2O relation propagation
- **Baseline discipline (strict direct E2O)**: Baseline flattening must use only the explicit $E2O$ relations supplied in the snapshot. Look at the supplied $E2O$ table carefully: upstream objects (`Order`, `Item`, `Package`) are already explicitly linked in $E2O$ to downstream fulfilment events (e.g., `e16` explicitly links to `O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2`), whereas downstream objects (`Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) and `Item` are **not** linked to upstream or collateral events (e.g., `e01`–`e04` link only to `O1`–`O4`; `e18`–`e20` link only to `Order` and `Invoice`).
- **Permitted transformations (explicitly versioned and isolated)**:
  1. **Hop-constrained O2O propagation ($E2O_{\text{prop}}$)**: Permitted only as an explicit secondary transformation to answer cross-object lead-time questions (e.g., propagating `Issue Invoice` `e18`–`e20` across `settles` / `partially_settles` / `completes_settlement` to `Payment` objects `M1`–`M4` to measure invoice-to-payment elapsed time). Unconstrained transitive closure across the entire O2O graph is **rejected** because `O1`–`O4` form a single connected component via `P2`–`D2`–`P3`–`O3`–`P4`–`O4`, which would collapse unrelated objects into shared traces.
  2. **Inverse convergence weighting ($w_{e, ot} = 1 / |\{o \in O_{ot} \mid (e, o) \in E2O\}|$)**: Permitted when computing activity frequencies or resource utilization on a flattened log so that shared events sum to $1.0$ across all case replicas.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### Distortion 1: Cross-order package `P3` inflates packing volume and aligns unrelated orders (`Order` and `Item` flattenings)
- **Affected flattenings**: `Order` (`O2, O3`) and `Item` (`I21, I31`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Event `e13` (`Pack Package` at `10:12`), related to `O2, O3, I21, I31, P3`, where `P3` packs `I21` (from `O2`) and `I31` (from `O3`).
- **Resulting distortion**: In both `Order` and `Item` flattenings, the single physical packing operation `e13` is duplicated into two traces (`O2` and `O3`; `I21` and `I31`). In the `Order`-flattened DFG, this contributes two transitions (`e07 -> e13` in `O2` and `e09 -> e13` in `O3`) into `Pack Package`, inflating total `Pack Package` count from 4 physical cartons (`P1`–`P4`) to 6 flattened occurrences (together with `e14`). Furthermore, `O2`'s apparent packing waiting time (`e07` at `09:20` to `e13` at `10:12` = `52 min`) is governed by waiting for `O3`'s item `I31` (`e08` at `09:22`, `e09` at `09:23`) to be consolidated into `P3`.
- **Evaluation**: **Context-dependent**—informative for tracking when each order's merchandise enters a carton, but harmful for warehouse labor accounting (overcounts packing actions by $50\%$) and misleading if `O2`'s `52 min` delay is diagnosed as single-order picking slowness rather than cross-order batching policy.

### Distortion 2: Cross-order package `P4` induces false sequential packing rework and overnight delivery drag on `O3` (`Order` flattening)
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O3, O4`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Events `e13` (`Pack Package` at `10:12` on `O2, O3, I21, I31, P3`), `e14` (`Pack Package` at `10:20` on `O3, O4, I32, I41, P4`), `e17` (`Dispatch` at `11:15` on `O3, O4, I32, I41, P4, D3`), and `e27` (`Confirm Delivery` on Feb 4 at `10:00` on `O3, O4, I32, I41, P4, D3`).
- **Resulting distortion**: Because `O3`'s two items (`I31, I32`) are split across two cross-order packages (`P3` with `O2`, and `P4` with `O4`), `O3`'s flattened trace contains consecutive packing events `e13 -> e14` (`Pack Package -> Pack Package` self-loop, `8 min` apart) and consecutive dispatch events `e16 -> e17` (`Dispatch -> Dispatch` self-loop, `10 min` apart). In addition, `e14`, `e17`, and `e27` are copied into both `O3` and `O4`, double-counting `D3`'s overnight `22h 45m` transit delay (`11:15` Day 1 to `10:00` Day 2) across two orders.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful** for process discovery and bottleneck attribution—standard DFG discovery renders parallel/split item handling (`I31` in `P3` vs. `I32` in `P4`) as iterative rework loops on the order, and double-weights `D3`'s overnight delay in order-level transit statistics.

### Distortion 3: Consolidated delivery `D2` causes $3\times$ event replication and false synchronization across three orders (`Order`, `Item`, and `Package` flattenings)
- **Affected flattenings**: `Order` (`O1, O2, O3`), `Item` (`I12, I21, I31`), and `Package` (`P2, P3`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Events `e16` (`Dispatch` at `11:05`) and `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` at `16:00`), each linked to `O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2`.
- **Resulting distortion**: A single truck run (`D2`) is copied 3 times in `Order` flattening (`O1, O2, O3`), 3 times in `Item` flattening (`I12, I21, I31`), and 2 times in `Package` flattening (`P2, P3`). In the `Order` and `Item` DFGs, the 3 actual `Dispatch` events (`e15, e16, e17`) and 3 actual `Confirm Delivery` events (`e23, e24, e27`) inflate to 6 occurrences each ($+100\%$ frequency distortion). Moreover, `D2`'s `4h 55m` transit time (`11:05` to `16:00`) is weighted $3\times$ in `Order`/`Item` performance views compared to `D1`'s `3h 00m` (`e15` to `e23`, linked only to `O1, I11, P1, D1`), shifting the mean delivery transit time from the true delivery-level mean of `10h 13m 20s` (`(180 + 295 + 1365)/3 = 613.3 min`) to `10h 41m 40s` (`(180 + 3*295 + 2*1365)/6 = 641.7 min`) across order-delivery pairs.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful** for logistics capacity and carrier SLA reporting; only a `Delivery`-centric or object-centric view preserves the true sample size of $n = 3$ shipments.

### Distortion 4: Split fulfilment of `O1` fabricates sequential self-loops out of concurrent package streams (`Order` flattening)
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O1`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Events `e05, e06` (`Add Item` for `I11, I12`), `e11, e12` (`Pack Package` for `P1, P2`), `e15, e16` (`Dispatch` for `D1, D2`), and `e23, e24` (`Confirm Delivery` for `D1, D2`) inside trace `O1`.
- **Resulting distortion**: In `O1`, `I11` (packed in `P1`, shipped via `D1`) and `I12` (packed in `P2`, shipped via `D2`) progress in two parallel physical streams after order creation. Flattening on `Order` interleaves their timestamps into a single total order: `e05 -> e06 -> e11 -> e12 -> e15 -> e16 -> e18 -> e21 -> e23 -> e24 -> e28`. This generates four distinct self-loop edges (`Add Item -> Add Item`, `Pack Package -> Pack Package`, `Dispatch -> Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery`) and creates a false `2h 00m` self-loop delay on `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery` (`e23` at `14:00` to `e24` at `16:00`), which is actually the arrival gap between `D1` and `D2`.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**—conflates multi-package split shipment with operational rework and fabricates inter-activity transitions (`e11 -> e12` `10 min`, `e15 -> e16` `5 min`, `e23 -> e24` `2h 00m`) between independent packages.

### Distortion 5: Consolidated invoice `V2` duplicates financial events and erases 2:1 billing structure (`Order` and `Invoice` flattenings)
- **Affected flattenings**: `Order` (`O2, O3`) and `Invoice` (`V2`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Object `V2` (`bills` `O2` and `O3`) and events `e19` (`Issue Invoice` at `12:05` on `O2, O3, V2`), `e22` (`Record Payment` at `12:40` on `O2, O3, V2, M2`), and `e25` (`Record Payment` on Feb 4 at `09:00` on `O2, O3, V2, M3`).
- **Resulting distortion**: Flattening on `Order` copies `e19`, `e22`, and `e25` into both `O2` and `O3`, inflating `Issue Invoice` frequency from 3 (`V1, V2, V3`) to 4 and `Record Payment` frequency from 4 (`M1`–`M4`) to 6 ($+50\%$). An analyst inspecting the `Order`-flattened log sees `O2` and `O3` each receiving an invoice and two payments, making it impossible to tell whether two separate invoices were issued at `12:05` or a single consolidated invoice `V2` covered both orders. Conversely, flattening on `Invoice` preserves the exact count of 3 invoices (`V1, V2, V3`) and 4 payments, but drops all `Create Order`, `Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery` events, hiding that `V2` aggregates two orders with very different physical completion times (`O2` finishes delivery at `e24` Day 1 `16:00`; `O3` finishes delivery at `e27` Day 2 `10:00`).
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful** in `Order` flattening for financial auditing (overcounts invoices and payments); **context-dependent** in `Invoice` flattening (accurate for AR transaction counts, blind to order consolidation).

### Distortion 6: Partial payments `M2` and `M3` interleave with `Confirm Delivery` to create a false logistics–finance causal loop (`Order` flattening)
- **Affected flattening**: `Order` (`O2, O3`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Events `e22` (`Record Payment` at `12:40` on Feb 3 for `M2`, which `partially_settles` `V2`), `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` at `16:00` on Feb 3 for `D2`), `e25` (`Record Payment` at `09:00` on Feb 4 for `M3`, which `completes_settlement` of `V2`), and `e27` (`Confirm Delivery` at `10:00` on Feb 4 for `D3` on `O3`).
- **Resulting distortion**: In `Invoice` flattening (`V2`), the two instalments form a clean direct self-loop `e22 -> e25` (`Record Payment -> Record Payment`, elapsed time `20h 20m`). However, in `Order` flattening, `D2`'s delivery confirmation `e24` (`16:00` on Feb 3) occurs chronologically between the first instalment `e22` (`12:40` on Feb 3) and the second instalment `e25` (`09:00` on Feb 4). Consequently, the `Record Payment -> Record Payment` edge disappears completely from the `Order`-flattened DFG and is replaced by a two-node cycle: `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery` (`e22 -> e24` in `O2, O3`, and `e25 -> e27` in `O3`) and `Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment` (`e24 -> e25` in `O2, O3`). Furthermore, flattening drops the O2O qualifiers `partially_settles` (`M2 -> V2`) and `completes_settlement` (`M3 -> V2`), making instalment collection look like payment rework triggered by delivery confirmation.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful** in `Order` flattening—creates a spurious causal ping-pong loop between independent financial and logistics tracks; **informative** only when viewed in `Invoice` flattening alongside O2O payment qualifiers.

### Distortion 7: Near-singleton lifecycle truncation in `Payment` (and 2-event truncation in `Delivery` / `Invoice`)
- **Affected flattenings**: `Payment` (`M1`–`M4`), and secondarily `Delivery` (`D1`–`D3`) and `Invoice` (`V1, V3`).
- **Cited identifiers**: Payment objects `M1` (`e21`), `M2` (`e22`), `M3` (`e25`), `M4` (`e26`); Delivery objects `D1` (`e15, e23`), `D2` (`e16, e24`), `D3` (`e17, e27`); Invoice objects `V1` (`e18, e21`), `V3` (`e20, e26`).
- **Resulting distortion**: Under strict E2O flattening on `Payment`, `M1`–`M4` participate only in `Record Payment` (`e21, e22, e25, e26`) because `Issue Invoice` (`e18`–`e20`) links only to `Order` and `Invoice` (`O1, V1`; `O2, O3, V2`; `O4, V3`). All 4 `Payment` traces are length-1 singletons (`<Record Payment>`), producing an empty set of directly-follows activity edges ($\emptyset$), a $0\text{ s}$ throughput time for every case, and complete blindness to invoice-to-payment settlement times (`30 min` for `M1`, `35 min` for `M2`, `20h 55m` for `M3`, `21h 20m` for `M4`) and to the fact that `M2` and `M3` settle the same invoice `V2`. Similarly, `Delivery` traces contain only 2 events (`Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`), hiding the `45m` to `1h 00m` dock staging wait between `Pack Package` (`e11`–`e14`) and `Dispatch` (`e15`–`e17`).
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**—renders `Payment` flattening analytically useless on raw E2O and prevents `Delivery` flattening from measuring warehouse-to-dispatch handoff delays.

### Distortion 8: Order-closure boundary distortion and premature vs. gated closure illusion (`Order` vs. sub-object flattenings)
- **Affected flattenings**: `Order` (`O1`–`O4`) versus `Item`/`Package`/`Delivery`/`Invoice`.
- **Cited identifiers**: Events `e21` (`Record Payment` on `O1` at Day 1 `12:30`), `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` on `O1, O2, O3` at Day 1 `16:00`), `e25` (`Record Payment` on `O2, O3` at Day 2 `09:00`), `e26` (`Record Payment` on `O4` at Day 2 `09:30`), `e27` (`Confirm Delivery` on `O3, O4` at Day 2 `10:00`), and `e28`–`e31` (`Close Order` on `O1`–`O4` at Day 2 `11:00`–`11:10`).
- **Resulting distortion**: An order can only close after **all** of its constituent items/packages are delivered and **all** of its invoice instalments are paid:
  - `O1` finishes payment at `12:30` (`e21`) and first delivery `D1` at `14:00` (`e23`), but must wait for second delivery `D2` at `16:00` (`e24`) before closing on Day 2 at `11:00` (`e28`, a `19h 00m` post-delivery lag).
  - `O2` finishes delivery at Day 1 `16:00` (`e24`), but cannot close until second payment instalment `M3` arrives at Day 2 `09:00` (`e25`), producing edge `Record Payment -> Close Order` (`e25 -> e29`, `2h 05m`).
  - `O3` and `O4` finish payment at Day 2 `09:00` (`e25`) and `09:30` (`e26`), but must wait for `D3`'s delivery confirmation at Day 2 `10:00` (`e27`), producing edge `Confirm Delivery -> Close Order` (`e27 -> e30` `1h 05m`; `e27 -> e31` `1h 10m`).
  In `Order` flattening, this synchronization join across all child deliveries and payments is collapsed into competing DFG edges (`Confirm Delivery -> Close Order` 3 times vs. `Record Payment -> Close Order` 1 time) and makes `O1`'s first package `P1` (`e23` at `14:00`) appear to wait `21h 00m` until `Close Order` (`e28`), whereas in `Item`/`Package`/`Delivery`/`Invoice` flattenings, `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`) is dropped entirely.
- **Evaluation**: **Context-dependent**—informative for seeing which synchronization barrier (final delivery vs. final payment) was the last blocker per order, but misleading without an object-centric join model because a linear DFG cannot express an `AND`-join over a variable number of child deliveries and payment instalments.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Which flattened logs should still be created and what they answer safely
Rather than discarding flattened logs altogether or trusting one blindly, create **four targeted flattened views** with strict usage boundaries, and **do not create** raw `Payment` flattening (unless augmented by explicit 1-hop O2O propagation from `Invoice`):
1. **`Order`-flattened log (`O1`–`O4`)**:
   - *Safe questions*: End-to-end commercial cycle time from `Create Order` (`e01`–`e04`) to `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`) (`O1`: `26h 00m`, `O2`: `26h 03m`, `O3`: `26h 01m`, `O4`: `26h 04m`); verification that no order closed before at least one delivery and payment occurred.
   - *Unsafe questions*: Activity execution counts for packing/dispatch/delivery/invoicing/payment; rework rates; causal ordering between logistics and billing.
2. **`Item`-flattened log (`I11`–`I41`)**:
   - *Safe questions*: Item-level physical fulfilment completeness (verifying that all 6 items `I11`–`I41` progress cleanly through `Add Item -> Pack Package -> Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` without item-level rework); per-item pick-to-pack time (`e05->e11`: `45m`, `e06->e12`: `53m`, `e07->e13`: `52m`, `e08->e13`: `50m`, `e09->e14`: `57m`, `e10->e14`: `55m`).
   - *Unsafe questions*: Total count of packages or shipments (`Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` are inflated $2\times$ from 3 to 6).
3. **`Delivery`-flattened log (`D1`–`D3`)**:
   - *Safe questions*: Exact carrier shipment counts ($n = 3$) and uninflated carrier transit durations (`D1`: `3h 00m` [`e15->e23`], `D2`: `4h 55m` [`e16->e24`], `D3`: `22h 45m` [`e17->e27`]).
4. **`Invoice`-flattened log (`V1`–`V3`)**:
   - *Safe questions*: Exact billing volume ($n = 3$ invoices), identification of invoices requiring multiple payment instalments (`V2` via `e19 -> e22 -> e25`), and time from `Issue Invoice` to first payment (`V1`: `30m`, `V2`: `35m`, `V3`: `21h 20m`) and full settlement (`V2`: `20h 55m` [`e19->e25`]).

### Questions requiring native object-centric artefacts
- **OC-DFG (`pm4py.discover_ocdfg`)**: Required whenever analysing handoffs across object boundaries—such as `Pack Package -> Dispatch` across `Package` (`P2, P3`) and `Delivery` (`D2`), or parallel branches where `Order` splits into `Package`/`Delivery` tracks (`e11`–`e17`, `e23, e24, e27`) and `Invoice`/`Payment` tracks (`e18`–`e22, e25, e26`) before synchronizing at `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`).
- **Object Type Graph / OTG (`pm4py.discover_otg`)**: Required to Audit structural cardinality and pooling patterns across object types (`Order <-> Item` 1:N, `Order <-> Package` M:N via `P3, P4`, `Package <-> Delivery` N:1 via `D2`, `Order <-> Invoice` N:1 via `V2`, `Invoice <-> Payment` 1:N via `M2, M3`).
- **Event-Type to Object-Type / ET–OT matrix (`pm4py.discover_etot`)**: Required to diagnose scope truncation before flattening—immediately showing that `Create Order` and `Close Order` only touch `Order`, `Record Payment` touches `Order, Invoice, Payment`, and `Dispatch`/`Confirm Delivery` touch `Order, Item, Package, Delivery`.
- **Object interaction summary (`pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary`)**: Required to detect exact split/merge anomalies at the instance level: identifying that `O1` and `O3` split across two packages (`P1, P2` and `P3, P4`), `P3` and `P4` merge items from two orders (`O2, O3` and `O3, O4`), `D2` merges `P2` and `P3`, and `V2` merges `O2` and `O3`.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering (`pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`)**: Useful when grouping executions anchored on a primary object type (such as `Order`) by structural isomorphism, where `O1` (1 order, 2 items, 2 packages, 2 deliveries, 1 invoice, 1 payment) separates cleanly from `O2`, `O3`, and `O4`.

### Reconciling contradictory findings between flattened and object-centric views
When a flattened view and an object-centric view disagree, apply a three-step reconciliation protocol:
1. **Frequency reconciliation via event- vs. object-cardinality decomposition**: If the `Order`-flattened DFG reports 6 `Dispatch` executions and 6 `Record Payment` executions while the OC-DFG event count reports 3 `Dispatch` (`e15`–`e17`) and 4 `Record Payment` (`e21, e22, e25, e26`), reconcile them by noting that $6 = \sum_{e} |O_{\text{Order}}(e)|$ is the *total-object* occurrence count on `Order`, whereas $3$ and $4$ are the *event* counts (which match the unique-object counts on `Delivery` and `Payment`, respectively).
2. **Loop reconciliation via divergence vs. true rework check**: When a flattened DFG exhibits a self-loop $A \to A$ (e.g., `Pack Package -> Pack Package` on `O1` and `O3`) or a two-activity cycle $A \leftrightarrow B$ (e.g., `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` on `O2` and `O3`), check the sub-object identifiers in the OC-DFG: if the two events link to *distinct* child objects (`P1 \neq P2`, `P3 \neq P4`, `M2 \neq M3`, `D2 \neq D3`), classify the loop as **structural divergence / parallel interleaving**, not operational rework.
3. **Duration reconciliation via native object-pair conditioning**: Measure activity-to-activity lead times only along object types that participate in both endpoints without many-to-many pooling (e.g., measure `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` on `Delivery` `D1`–`D3`, `Pack Package -> Dispatch` on `Package` `P1`–`P4`, and `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` on `Invoice` `V1`–`V3`).

## 5. Lead-object decision

### Selected position: **B. Use a primary lead object (`Order`) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`)**

### Decision criteria
A defensible architecture for end-to-end process analysis must satisfy four criteria:
1. **Boundary completeness**: Capture the commercial entry (`Create Order`) and exit (`Close Order`) of customer demand.
2. **Cardinality fidelity**: Preserve the true execution counts and durations of physical shipments (`Delivery`), containers (`Package`), and financial settlements (`Invoice`, `Payment`) without convergence inflation.
3. **Causal separation of concurrent tracks**: Prevent independent logistics and billing timelines from fabricating false rework loops when interleaved.
4. **Traceability of split/merge dependencies**: Explain how delays in one sub-object (e.g., `D3` or `M3`) block completion of parent orders (`O2`, `O3`, `O4`).

### Strongest supporting evidence
- **Why `Order` must be the primary anchor**: `Order` (`O1`–`O4`) is the **only** object type present in all 31 events (`e01`–`e31`) and the only type that connects the commercial boundaries (`e01`–`e04` `Create Order` and `e28`–`e31` `Close Order`) to both the physical fulfilment track (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`) and the financial settlement track (`Invoice`, `Payment`). Every other object type drops between $48.4\%$ (`Item`, losing 15 events) and $87.1\%$ (`Payment`, losing 27 events) of the event log.
- **Why `Order` cannot stand alone without explicit secondary lifecycles**: Relying solely on `Order` flattening inflates log size from 31 to 42 events ($+35.5\%$), creates 4 unique variants out of 4 orders, fabricates self-loops on `Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery` (`O1, O3`), and invents a bidirectional causal cycle between `Record Payment` and `Confirm Delivery` (`O2, O3` via `e22 -> e24 -> e25`). Pairing `Order` as the primary commercial lifecycle with explicit secondary lifecycles—`Package` (`P1`–`P4`) for packing-to-dispatch staging, `Delivery` (`D1`–`D3`) for carrier transit, and `Invoice` (`V1`–`V3`, enriched with `Payment` `M1`–`M4` qualifiers) for collections—recovers exact 1:1 execution counts and uninflated lead times across every sub-process while keeping `Order` as the overarching customer SLA frame.

### Strongest counterargument
The strongest counterargument is **Position C (rejecting a single lead object entirely)** on the grounds that `O1`, `O2`, `O3`, and `O4` are not independent order executions at all, but a single entangled multi-order fulfilment web: `O1` and `O2/O3` share delivery `D2` (`e16, e24`); `O2` and `O3` share package `P3` (`e13`) and invoice `V2` (`e19, e22, e25`); and `O3` and `O4` share package `P4` (`e14`) and delivery `D3` (`e17, e27`). Because all four orders belong to one connected component in the object interaction graph, elevating `Order` even as a *primary* lead object risks encouraging stakeholders to treat `O1`–`O4` as four statistically independent cases and to overlook that `O3` is structurally coupled to `O1`, `O2`, and `O4`.

### Residual risk
Even under Position B, two residual risks remain:
1. **Cross-view misattribution by consumers**: Stakeholders viewing `Order`-level SLA dashboards alongside `Delivery`- and `Invoice`-level dashboards may still misattribute `O1`'s `19h 00m` delay between `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` at `16:00`) and `e28` (`Close Order` at `11:00` Day 2) unless synchronization wait states are explicitly annotated.
2. **Asymmetric E2O logging dependence**: `Order` achieves $100\%$ event coverage only because the source system pre-populated `Order` IDs onto downstream `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`, and `Confirm Delivery` events (`e11`–`e27`). If an upstream ERP change stops stamping `Order` IDs onto `Dispatch` or `Record Payment`, the primary `Order` lifecycle will silently lose visibility into mid-stream milestones unless O2O propagation is enforced.

### Conditions under which the decision would change
- **Shift from Position B to Position C (reject any lead object)**: If the primary analytical goal shifts from customer-order SLA monitoring to **warehouse batching and carrier consolidation optimization**, where cross-order pooling (`P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `V2`) is the dominant operational phenomenon and no single object type owns the end-to-end optimization objective.
- **Shift from Position B to Position A (single lead object `Order`)**: Only if business rules are re-engineered to enforce strict 1:1:1:1 cardinality ($1\text{ Order} : 1\text{ Package} : 1\text{ Delivery} : 1\text{ Invoice} : 1\text{ Payment}$, no split shipments, no cross-order consolidation, and no partial payments), eliminating convergence and divergence between `Order` and its downstream objects.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

### Check 1: Removing or down-weighting copied shared events (convergence correction)
- **Procedure**: For each flattened log $L_{ot}$, assign each event instance $(e, o)$ an inverse convergence weight $w_{e, ot} = 1 / |\{o' \in O_{ot} \mid (e, o') \in E2O\}|$ and compare unweighted flattened counts against weighted counts and deduplicated event counts.
- **Concrete validation in this OCEL**:
  - In `Order` flattening, unweighted activity counts are `Pack Package` $= 6$, `Dispatch` $= 6$, `Issue Invoice` $= 4$, `Record Payment` $= 6$, `Confirm Delivery` $= 6$ (total $= 42$ events). Applying $w_{e, \text{Order}}$ (`0.5` for `e13, e14, e17, e19, e22, e25, e27`; `1/3` for `e16, e24`) restores the exact ground-truth event counts: `Pack Package` $= 4$, `Dispatch` $= 3$, `Issue Invoice` $= 3$, `Record Payment` $= 4$, `Confirm Delivery` $= 3$ (total $= 31.0$).
  - For performance metrics, compare the unweighted `Order`-level mean `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` transit time (`10h 41m 40s`, skewed by $3\times$ replication of `D2` [`e16->e24`] and $2\times$ replication of `D3` [`e17->e27`]) against the convergence-weighted mean (`10h 13m 20s`), which matches the `Delivery`-flattened ground truth across `D1, D2, D3`.

### Check 2: Comparing direct E2O relations with an explicitly propagated-relation variant ($E2O$ vs. $E2O_{\text{prop}}$)
- **Procedure**: Construct a hop-controlled propagated OCEL ($E2O_{\text{prop}}$) alongside the raw E2O baseline in two controlled directions:
  1. *1-hop upstream billing propagation to `Payment`*: Propagate `Issue Invoice` (`e18` on `V1`, `e19` on `V2`, `e20` on `V3`) across `o2o` (`M1 -> V1`, `M2 -> V2`, `M3 -> V2`, `M4 -> V3`) onto `Payment` objects `M1`–`M4`.
  2. *De-propagated minimal E2O check*: Strip pre-propagated `Order` and `Item` references from downstream events (`e15`–`e27`) so each event links only to its immediate operational objects (`P*`/`D*` on `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery`; `V*`/`M*` on `Record Payment`).
- **Concrete validation in this OCEL**:
  - Under 1-hop `Invoice -> Payment` propagation, `Payment` traces ceased to be length-1 singletons (`M1`: `e18->e21` [`30m`], `M2`: `e19->e22` [`35m`], `M3`: `e19->e25` [`20h 55m`], `M4`: `e20->e26` [`21h 20m`]), recovering payment lead times at the cost of duplicating `e19` across `M2` and `M3` (introducing $+1$ convergence copy on `Issue Invoice`).
  - Conversely, unrestricted propagation of `Create Order` (`e01`–`e04`) down to `Item` (`I11`–`I41`) duplicates `e01` across `I11, I12` and `e03` across `I31, I32`, increasing `Item`-flattened events from 24 to 30 and inflating `Create Order` from 4 to 6. This validates why propagation must be restricted to targeted 1-hop pairs rather than applied globally.

### Check 3: Changing the lead object across all six object types
- **Procedure**: Systematically rotate the flattening anchor across `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment`, comparing trace count, variant count, event retention ratio, self-loop count, and stage-to-stage durations.
- **Concrete validation in this OCEL**:
  - Rotating from `Order` (4 traces, 4 variants, 31/31 unique events retained, 4 self-loop activities) to `Item` (6 traces, 1 variant, 16/31 events retained, 0 self-loops) proves that every self-loop on `Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery` in the `Order` log disappears when anchored on `Item`—confirming they are divergence artefacts of multi-item/multi-package orders (`O1, O3`), not item rework.
  - Rotating from `Order` to `Invoice` (3 traces, 2 variants, 7/31 events retained) restores the direct self-loop `Record Payment -> Record Payment` (`e22 -> e25` on `V2`) and eliminates the spurious `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` cycle (`e22 -> e24 -> e25`), proving that the `Order`-level cycle is a timestamp-interleaving artefact between `D2` (`e24`) and `V2`'s instalments (`M2, M3`).

### Check 4: Comparing frequency metrics based on events, unique objects, and total objects in the OC-DFG
- **Procedure**: In `pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)`, evaluate node and arc frequencies under all three canonical OC-DFG semantics—(i) **Event cardinality** (number of unique events $e$), (ii) **Unique-object cardinality** (number of distinct objects $o \in O_{ot}$ participating), and (iii) **Total-object cardinality** (number of $(e, o)$ pairs, which equals the flattened frequency on $ot$).
- **Concrete validation in this OCEL**:
  - For activity `Dispatch` (`e15, e16, e17`): **Event count** $= 3$; **Unique-object count** $= 4\text{ Orders}, 6\text{ Items}, 4\text{ Packages}, 3\text{ Deliveries}$; **Total-object count** $= 6\text{ Orders}$ (`O1` twice in `e15, e16`; `O3` twice in `e16, e17`), $6\text{ Items}$, $4\text{ Packages}$, $3\text{ Deliveries}$. The discrepancy between Unique `Order` ($4$) and Total `Order` ($6$) immediately flags split-delivery divergence on `O1` and `O3`, while the discrepancy between Event count ($3$) and Unique `Order` ($4$) or Total `Order` ($6$) flags cross-order delivery convergence on `D2` (`e16`) and `D3` (`e17`).
  - For activity `Record Payment` (`e21, e22, e25, e26`): **Event count** $= 4$; **Unique-object count** $= 4\text{ Orders}, 3\text{ Invoices}, 4\text{ Payments}$; **Total-object count** $= 6\text{ Orders}$ (`O2, O3` each twice in `e22, e25`), $4\text{ Invoices}$ (`V2` twice in `e22, e25`), $4\text{ Payments}$. Comparing Unique `Invoice` ($3$) vs. Total `Invoice` ($4$) isolates the instalment divergence on `V2`, while comparing Total `Invoice` ($4$) vs. Total `Order` ($6$) isolates the convergence duplication of `V2` across `O2` and `O3`.
