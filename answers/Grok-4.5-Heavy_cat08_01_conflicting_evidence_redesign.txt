**1. Current-process diagnosis**

The dominant path in the frequency DFG (Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval 2550 cases; Fast approval → Dispatch replacement 2300 cases) is fast and initially cheap: Intake → Auto mean 0.8 h / median 0.5 h, Auto → Fast mean 1.0 h, Fast → Dispatch mean 3.0 h. Pre-rework cost is 31 % lower than alternatives. However, this path produces markedly worse quality and compliance outcomes. Fast-approval cases show a 14.1 % customer-complaint/reopen rate versus 3.3 % on the Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device path; overall reopen rate is 9.2 %. After accounting for complaints, reshipments and service credits the cost advantage shrinks to only 9 %. Alignment diagnostics reveal frequent move-on-log deviations—Fast approval before evidence completeness (violating R2) and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices (violating R1)—producing only 68 % fitting traces despite log fitness of 0.91 and low model precision.

Evidence loops are another major drag: Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26 % of cases that enter it; the edge Request → Manual eligibility review appears 890 times with mean 34 h; 320 claims are abandoned. The 22 % unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment has a median cycle time of 140 h and the highest abandonment. Temporal-profile deviations further highlight Dispatch → Customer complaint/reopen (mean 96 h, p90 180 h) linked to incorrect replacement-part selection, and Manual eligibility → Repair quote often too slow for Premium expectations.

Segment outcomes amplify the contradiction. Premium high-value devices (14 % share) show 22 % complaints on the fast path versus 5 % on the reviewed/repair path, with high compliance risk if R1 or R2 is broken; Standard high-value (21 %) shows 18 % versus 6 %. Low-risk accessories (Premium 18 %, Standard 25 %) tolerate the fast path far better (6–8 % complaints) and Premium low-risk is sensitive to >24 h service-credit exposure. Resource constraints compound the problem: Manual eligibility (7 reviewers) queues sharply above 82 % utilisation; Fraud/risk review (2 specialists) bottlenecks on Mondays and after campaigns; Escalation board is limited to one 45-minute daily slot; Quality check (2 inspectors) is under-used except after repair campaigns. Wrong-replacement expected cost is EUR 95 (low-risk) versus EUR 410 (high-value).

Thus the most frequent path is attractive on pure speed and pre-rework cost yet systematically worse on rework, compliance exceptions and customer experience—especially for high-value and Premium cases—while the slower reviewed and repair paths deliver superior quality and compliance at the expense of cycle time, capacity and cost. No single path dominates across segments and risk classes.

**2. Redesign A: Segment-Risk Stratified Auto-Rules with Selective Conformance and Pre-Dispatch Gates**

Redesigned flow: Intake claim → Enhanced auto eligibility screen (segment-, defect-type- and value-aware rules, deployable in ~2 weeks).  
- Low-risk accessories (Premium or Standard, ~43 % share): Auto → Fast approval only if evidence complete (R2 exception retained for Premium low-risk) → Dispatch replacement → Quality/compliance close.  
- High-value devices or claims > EUR 400 (~35 %): Auto forces diversion to Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review (strict R1); evidence completeness confirmed via portal if needed; only then Fast approval or (preferred) Repair quote → fulfilment → selective lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate (part-selection check, using currently under-used Quality-check capacity) for high-value replacements → close.  
- Unclear / missing-evidence cases: portal-triggered evidence request before re-routing.  
Reopens continue to enforce R3 (Manual before final disposition).

Targets primarily the high-volume Fast path on low-risk accessories (preserve speed/cost) while removing high-value and high-risk cases from it; also attacks R1/R2 deviations and the long-tail Dispatch → complaint pattern.

Mechanism: Updated auto rules correct the outdated, non-segment-aware logic that currently drives many alignment deviations and low precision. Removing high-value volume from Fast approval directly reduces the 14.1 % complaint rate and EUR 410 error exposure. The selective pre-dispatch gate catches incorrect part selection that produces the 96 h mean complaint edge. Evidence-portal assistance reduces the 26 % repeat-request rate. Conformance fitness and percentage of fitting traces rise because normative rules are hard-wired into the automated routing.

Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: Accepts a 15–35 % increase in Manual-review inflow (risk of queues above 82 % utilisation); high-value cycle times lengthen toward current reviewed medians (96–120 h); residual repair-rework rate (12.5 % on high-value devices) is not directly attacked; Escalation-board scalability remains limited; potential abandonment increase if the portal is not assisted for low-digital-literacy customers.

**3. Redesign B: Evidence-Portal-First Parallel Streams with Literacy Fallbacks and Status Visibility**

Redesigned flow: Intake claim → Immediate digital evidence portal for any incomplete or unclear case (especially the 22 % unclear-defect segment). Customers upload/confirm evidence; a parallel stream runs Auto eligibility (and light Fraud screening) on whatever data is already available. Once evidence is complete, routing proceeds by segment/risk: low-risk complete → Fast approval → Dispatch; high-value or R1 cases → Manual/Fraud → decision. Low-digital-literacy customers (detected or after timeout) receive assisted phone/chat evidence collection instead of pure self-service. All customers receive real-time status visibility. Reopens still force Manual (R3); Premium cases receive priority within the parallel queues.

Targets primarily the unclear/missing-evidence segment (highest median cycle time, abandonment and repeat requests) and the sequential evidence loops visible in the frequency and performance DFGs; secondarily improves data quality feeding all downstream decisions and Standard-customer tolerance.

Mechanism: Portal reduces repeated Request-missing-evidence instances by the stated 30–45 %. Parallelism collapses the current sequential mean of 34 h (Request → Manual). Cleaner upstream evidence reduces the incomplete cases that currently feed Fast-approval deviations (R2) and later complaints. Status visibility exploits the observation that Standard customers tolerate up to 72 h when visibility is good, lowering reopen rates after close. Literacy fallbacks limit the abandonment risk explicitly flagged in the prompt.

Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: Residual risk that some low-literacy customers still abandon despite assistance; parallel processing raises short-term Manual starts and requires IT support for concurrent case states; high-value Fast-path volume is reduced only indirectly, so some R1 compliance exposure and wrong-replacement cost remain; Fraud specialist and Escalation capacity constraints are not structurally relieved; repair-rework statistics are largely untouched.

**4. Redesign C: High-Value Repair-Default with Premium Priority Lanes and Capacity Rebalancing**

Redesigned flow: Intake → light auto triage (soft R1 flag only).  
- Low-risk accessories: retain current Auto → Fast → Dispatch path (preserve volume and speed where complaints are already low).  
- High-value devices (~35 %): after eligibility and evidence completeness, default to Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device → Quality check → close. Dispatch replacement becomes the exception, allowed only after explicit risk clearance.  
- Premium cases (any value): dynamic priority lanes that reserve capacity in Manual eligibility, Fraud/risk review, Repair quote and Escalation board; real-time status updates protect the <24 h service-credit threshold.  
Under-used Quality-check inspectors are reallocated or cross-trained to support the increased repair/quality volume; Manual utilisation is monitored continuously and flexed when approaching 82 %. Soft auto-rule refresh occurs only for obvious R1 violators. Reopens enforce R3 with Premium preference into Escalation.

Targets high-value complaint differentials (18–22 % fast vs 5–6 % repair), wrong-replacement cost (EUR 410), Premium service-credit exposure, and under-utilised Quality-check capacity.

Mechanism: Explicitly exploits the rework statistics—repair-path complaint rate of 3.3 % versus 14.1 % on Fast approval—and the post-rework cost finding that Fast is only 9 % cheaper once downstream costs are included. Priority lanes protect Premium cycle-time and credit outcomes. Reallocation of under-used inspectors absorbs the extra quality load without immediate headcount growth. Soft R1 flagging plus repair default together improve conformance without a full auto-rule overhaul that would spike Manual inflow.

Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: Increased load on the three Repair-quote technicians (already delayed by part-price confirmation, mean 30 h); high-value cycle times lengthen relative to today’s fast medians (20–28 h); residual outdated auto-rule precision issues remain; Escalation-board daily slot is still capacity-constrained even with priority; short-term fulfilment cost rises because more repairs replace cheap replacements; evidence-loop frequency (26 % repeats) is addressed only indirectly.

**6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks**

**Redesign A**  
- Manual-review queue explosion if the 15–35 % inflow increase materialises while utilisation is already >82 %, producing longer queues than the current performance DFG shows.  
- Pre-dispatch gate under-resourced or producing false positives, adding the full 6–18 h without catching enough wrong parts, or portal abandonment rising for low-literacy customers.  
Bottleneck migration: pressure shifts from Auto/Fast path onto Manual eligibility and (secondarily) Fraud/risk review; Repair path may also absorb diverted high-value volume.

**Redesign B**  
- Residual abandonment spike despite literacy fallbacks, especially in the 22 % unclear segment, or parallel processing creating inconsistent case states that generate new reopens.  
- Portal support demand or concurrent Manual starts overwhelm existing capacity before evidence completeness is achieved.  
Bottleneck migration: new demand appears at portal assistance / digital-support resources and at the parallel Manual starts; Fraud specialists may still spike on Mondays.

**Redesign C**  
- Repair-quote technicians (only three) become overloaded; part-price confirmation delays lengthen beyond the current 30 h mean, eroding the quality gain.  
- Premium priority starves Standard Manual/Fraud capacity, raising Standard complaint or abandonment rates; residual outdated auto rules continue to allow some R1/R2 leakage.  
Bottleneck migration: load moves from Dispatch/Fast path onto Repair quote and Quality check (even after reallocation); Escalation board remains a latent constraint for reopens.

**7. Recommended experiment sequence**

Stage 1 – first pilot: Implement Redesign A on low-risk accessories only (or a single geographic/product subset) for 3–4 weeks. Rules update is the fastest lever (~2 weeks). Hold out a pure-control cohort of identical low-risk cases.

Metrics to monitor (segment-stratified):  
- Complaint/reopen rate (target relative drop ≥20–25 % on the Fast path).  
- Percentage of fitting traces and residual R1/R2 deviations.  
- Manual eligibility utilisation and queue time (keep sustained utilisation <85–88 %).  
- Median and p90 cycle time by segment; service-credit exposure for Premium.  
- Evidence-repeat rate and abandonment.  
- Downstream 30-day reopen and reshipment costs (not merely end-to-end cycle time).

Stop/go thresholds (qualitative ranges):  
- Go if complaint rate falls ≥20 % relative on low-risk with Manual utilisation remaining <88 %, Premium cycle-time/credit metrics not degrading >10–15 %, and no rise in compliance exceptions.  
- Stop or narrow if Manual queues explode, abandonment rises sharply, or high-value pilot cells (if included) show compliance regressions.  
- Conditional continue if quality gains appear but resource pressure is borderline—then add temporary flex capacity before scaling.

Sequencing logic: A is lowest-complexity and fastest to deploy, protecting the largest volume (low-risk) while testing the core trade-off. If Stage 1 succeeds, Stage 2 pilots Redesign B on the unclear/missing-evidence segment (or a parallel region) to attack the 140 h median and 26 % repeats. Stage 3 introduces Redesign C elements (repair default + Premium priority) on high-value cohorts once capacity data from the first two stages is known.  

Learning real improvement versus downstream shift: Maintain control cohorts throughout; track full 30–60-day reopen, reshipment, service-credit and compliance-exception costs rather than upstream cycle time alone; examine whether reduced Fast-path complaints simply migrate into longer repair queues or Escalation volume. Segment-stratified dashboards prevent average-cycle-time optimisations that hide high-value or Premium deterioration. Only after stable segment-level gains and no net cost increase at the 60-day horizon should any redesign be rolled out organisation-wide.
