# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1: Event-order convention**
- For simultaneous rounded timestamps (e.g., 08:05), we assume the following priority: (1) activity completions, (2) activity starts, (3) arrivals. This reflects the physical constraint that a resource cannot start a new task until the current one is completed.

**A2: Rounding treatment**
- All timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes, so the true time lies in [t-2.5, t+2.5] minutes. For calculations, we use the reported time as the central estimate and ±2.5 minutes as bounds for sensitivity analysis.

**A3: Capacity assumptions**
- S1 has 2 resources (I1, I2), and S2 has 2 resources (C1, C2), except C2 is unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10.
- Resources cannot execute two cases simultaneously, but apparent conflicts due to rounding or logging delays are allowed.

**A4: Missing start imputation**
- For MISSING starts, we use the earliest possible start time (constrained by resource availability and prior work) as the lower bound and the latest possible start time (constrained by completion time and nonnegative service time) as the upper bound. The central estimate is the midpoint of this interval.

**A5: OPEN activities**
- For OPEN activities, the elapsed service time is calculated from the start time to 14:00. The final service time is unknown and treated as right-censored.

**A6: Queue entry**
- A case enters the S2 queue immediately upon completion of S1. If S1 completes at time t, the case is in the S2 queue at t.

**A7: Non-bypass**
- No case bypasses S1 or S2. All cases must pass through both stations in order.

**A8: Censoring**
- Cases with state_at_14_00 = WAITING_FOR_S1, WAITING_FOR_S2, or OPEN_IN_S1/S2 are right-censored. Their total sojourn time is lower-bounded by the time from arrival to 14:00.

**Notation**
- Waiting time (W): Time from arrival at a station to start of service.
- Service time (S): Time from start to completion of service.
- Sojourn time (J): Time from arrival at a station to completion of service (W + S).
- WIP: Work in progress (number of cases in the station, either waiting or in service).
- Throughput: Number of completed cases per unit time.
- Utilization: Busy time / available time for a resource or station.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

### Definitions
1. **Queue waiting time (W)**:
   - For S1: `s1_start - arrival_s1` (if `s1_start` is MISSING, use imputed bounds).
   - For S2: `s2_start - s1_end` (if `s2_start` is MISSING, use imputed bounds).

2. **Service time (S)**:
   - For S1: `s1_end - s1_start` (if `s1_start` is MISSING, use `s1_end - imputed_s1_start`).
   - For S2: `s2_end - s2_start` (if `s2_end` is OPEN, use `14:00 - s2_start` as elapsed service time; final service time is unknown).

3. **Sojourn time (J)**:
   - For S1: `s1_end - arrival_s1` (W + S).
   - For S2: `s2_end - s1_end` (if `s2_end` is OPEN, use `14:00 - s1_end` as lower bound).
   - Total sojourn time for completed cases: `s2_end - arrival_s1`.

4. **WIP**:
   - At any time t, WIP at a station = number of cases in the queue + number of cases in service.
   - For rounded timestamps, we use the priority in A1 to resolve ties.

5. **Throughput**:
   - Arrivals: Count of cases arriving at a station in a time interval.
   - Departures: Count of cases completing service at a station in a time interval.

6. **Utilization**:
   - For a resource: `total_busy_time / available_time`.
   - For a station: `sum(resource_busy_times) / (number_of_resources * available_time)`.

7. **Little's Law**:
   - `WIP = throughput * sojourn_time`. Applied only in intervals where the system is stable (no trends in WIP) and throughput is measurable.

### Estimation approach
- For MISSING starts, we derive bounds using:
  - Earliest possible start: Max(completion time of prior work on the same resource, arrival time at the station).
  - Latest possible start: Completion time (since service time  0).
- For OPEN activities, we treat the service time as right-censored and report elapsed service time as a lower bound.
- For queue lengths, we use the event-order convention (A1) to resolve simultaneous events.

### Uncertainty handling
- Report ranges for imputed values (e.g., [lower, upper]).
- For central estimates, use midpoints of ranges.
- For sensitivity analysis, compare optimistic (shortest possible waiting/service times) and pessimistic (longest possible) scenarios.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

### Evidence
1. **S1 Queue Growth**:
   - Early morning (08:00-10:00): S1 waiting times are short (0-40 min), and S1 completes cases faster than S2 (6 completions vs. 6 arrivals at S2).
   - Mid-morning (10:00-12:00): S1 waiting times increase (25-85 min), and S1 completions (6) match S2 arrivals (6), but S2 completions lag (5).
   - Afternoon (12:00-14:00): S1 waiting times are long (45-125 min), and S1 completions (4) < S2 arrivals (6), but S2 completions are only 2 (many OPEN).

2. **S2 Queue Growth**:
   - Early morning: S2 waiting times are short (5-25 min), and S2 keeps up with S1 completions.
   - Mid-morning: S2 waiting times increase (20-75 min), especially during C2 outage (10:55-11:10). S2 completions drop to 5 vs. 6 arrivals.
   - Afternoon: S2 waiting times are highly variable (0-85 min), with many cases still in S2 at 14:00 (OPEN).

3. **C2 Outage Impact**:
   - C2 is unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10, reducing S2 capacity by 50%. During this period:
     - P08 S2 starts at 11:15 (delayed by outage).
     - P11 S2 start is constrained by C2 availability (earliest 11:10).
   - The outage likely caused a temporary bottleneck at S2, increasing S2 waiting times.

4. **S1 vs. S2 Bottleneck**:
   - Before 10:00: S1 and S2 are balanced. S1 waiting times are short, and S2 keeps up.
   - 10:00-12:00: S2 becomes a bottleneck due to the C2 outage and increasing S1 completions. S2 waiting times increase, and S2 completions lag.
   - After 12:00: S1 becomes the bottleneck. S1 waiting times are very long (45-125 min), and S1 completions (4) < S2 arrivals (6). S2 has many OPEN cases, but this is due to S1 not feeding enough cases to S2.

### Hypotheses
- **Primary Bottleneck**: S2 is the primary bottleneck during 10:00-12:00 due to the C2 outage. Outside this period, S1 and S2 are roughly balanced, but S1 becomes the bottleneck after 12:00 as S1 waiting times grow.
- **Secondary Bottleneck**: S1 may have been a secondary bottleneck earlier due to high arrival rates, but the C2 outage exacerbates S2's bottleneck.

### Temporal Interpretation
- **08:00-10:00**: System is stable. S1 and S2 are balanced.
- **10:00-12:00**: S2 becomes the bottleneck due to C2 outage. S2 waiting times increase, and S2 completions lag.
- **12:00-14:00**: S1 becomes the bottleneck. S1 waiting times are very long, and S1 completions drop. S2 has many OPEN cases, but this is due to S1 not feeding enough cases.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

### Scenarios
1. **Optimistic Imputation**:
   - MISSING starts: Use earliest possible start time (minimizing waiting time).
   - OPEN activities: Assume service time equals elapsed time (minimizing service time).
   - Impact: Underestimates waiting and service times, leading to lower WIP and utilization estimates.

2. **Central Imputation**:
   - MISSING starts: Use midpoint of possible start time range.
   - OPEN activities: Use elapsed time as lower bound for service time.
   - Impact: Balanced estimates for waiting and service times.

3. **Pessimistic Imputation**:
   - MISSING starts: Use latest possible start time (maximizing waiting time).
   - OPEN activities: Assume service time is longer than elapsed time (e.g., +50%).
   - Impact: Overestimates waiting and service times, leading to higher WIP and utilization estimates.

### Stable Conclusions
- S2 is the primary bottleneck during 10:00-12:00 due to the C2 outage. This is robust across all scenarios because the outage is a hard constraint.
- S1 becomes the bottleneck after 12:00, as S1 waiting times are consistently long across scenarios.
- The system is stable before 10:00, with no clear bottleneck.

### Unstable Conclusions
- Exact waiting and service times for cases with MISSING or OPEN timestamps vary significantly across scenarios.
- Utilization estimates for S1 and S2 after 12:00 are sensitive to imputation choices due to many OPEN cases.
