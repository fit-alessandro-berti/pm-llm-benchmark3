## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is a business-risk judgment, not a conformance-cost score. I assess severity by asking, in order:

1. **Control objective**: Was a required control skipped, bypassed, or executed after its control point?
2. **Exposure**: What amount was paid or could have been paid, and did fraud indicators exist?
3. **Reversibility**: Can the payment or decision be recovered? Bank transfers usually cannot be cleanly reversed.
4. **Timing**: Did the deviation occur before/after approval or payment?
5. **Customer/regulatory harm**: Were notifications, denials, or withdrawals mishandled in a way that affects claimants or compliance?
6. **Recurrence**: Is this a one-off or a systemic pattern?
7. **Evidence reliability**: Is proof from authoritative timestamps, stable IDs, external documents, or only mutable/free-text fields?
8. **Model scope**: Is the case even inside the payable-claim model? If it is out of scope, the alignment cost measures model mismatch more than business risk.

Confidence is separate from severity. Confidence expresses how sure we are that the deviation is real, the cause is correct, and the evidence supports the story. A severe finding with weak evidence still has high severity but lower confidence. A harmless event with abundant evidence can have high confidence and low severity.

Raw alignment cost is a technical edit distance, not a risk score. It does not know policy constraints, payment irreversibility, fraud red flags, or whether a missing event is truly missing or only missing from one system.

- **Cases**: CL-603; 74 cases in the month have `L(Notify Claimant)` immediately after a synchronous notification with same message ID.

- **Cause**: One retrying connector without idempotency/deduplication.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Owner**: Integration/platform team.

### Pattern 2: External adjuster assessment not ingested
- **Cases**: CL-606; 9 high-value cases in the month have `M(Assess Loss)` with external adjuster documents; 2 lack stable document-to-case IDs.
- **Business risk**: Medium. Substantive loss assessment may have occurred, but core audit trail is incomplete. If the external document is not stable, compliance cannot prove assessment happened before approval.
- **Cause**: External adjuster system not integrated; document links are weak.
- **Confidence**: Medium-high for occurrence; lower for evidence reliability.
- **Owner**: Claims operations + IT integration.

### Pattern 3: Label/terminology mismatch
- **Cases**: CL-611.
- **Business risk**: Low, but conformance false positives obscure real findings. It may also cause regulators to think a mandatory activity was skipped.
- **Cause**: Legacy label `Assess Damage` versus canonical `Assess Loss`; mapping table updated after the case.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Owner**: Data governance / process excellence.

### Pattern 4: Data-aware auto-approval violations
- **Cases**: CL-609, CL-610; four cases in the month with fully synchronous Auto Approve paths that violate P1/P2.
- **Business risk**: High. Auto approval is being used where policy requires manual/fraud controls, including a bank-change fraud red flag.
- **Cause**: The process tree encodes a structural choice, but not the data-aware guard; the workflow system does not enforce P1/P2.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Owner**: Risk/compliance + workflow automation.

### Pattern 5: Denied/withdrawn cases outside the model
- **Cases**: CL-607, CL-612; denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with raw cost >=4.
- **Business risk**: Low for direct loss, but conformance reporting is distorted, and customers/claimants may still need correct notification/communication.
- **Cause**: Model scope is payable claims only; denial/withdrawal branches are missing or should be filtered.
- **Confidence**: High on facts; scope decision is business-driven.
- **Owner**: Process owner + model owner.

### Pattern 6: Payment/approval control bypass with high exposure
- **Cases**: CL-602, CL-604.
- **Business risk**: Critical. Missing fraud screening and payment before approval in high-value claims can cause irrecoverable financial loss and regulatory action.
- **Cause**: Control steps are not enforced as hard gates; overrides/urgent paths are not audited.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Owner**: Internal audit / control owner.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is a uniform, token-level edit distance. It does not understand policy, exposure, irreversibility, evidence quality, or customer harm.

- **CL-602** has raw cost 1 but is among the most severe cases: a EUR 480k HIGH-risk claim with a bank change was paid without evidenced fraud screening. The cost function rewards one missing event substitution with a cost of 1, but the business risk is enormous.
- **CL-604** has raw cost 3, which is higher than CL-602, but both are severe. The cost 3 partly comes from moving an early payment event and a missing later payment event; it does not encode that the payment happened before approval and cannot be cleanly reversed.
- **CL-609** and **CL-610** have raw cost 0 because the model structure matches the log. Yet they violate data-aware policy rules. Zero alignment cost is therefore consistent with major policy failure. This is the clearest proof that alignment cost cannot substitute for business risk.
- **CL-605** has raw cost 0 because the hidden tau transition is fitted. Hidden transitions are silent model mechanics; they are not evidence that the underlying business activity was correct or incorrect.
- **CL-607** and **CL-612** have raw cost 4, but they are low-risk scope/model issues. The high cost comes from a model that expects payable activities that never should happen after denial or withdrawal.
- **CL-601** and **CL-603** add cost from UI telemetry and retry duplicates. Those are harmless technical noise, not business violations.
- **CL-606** and **CL-608** have raw cost 1 from a missing core event, but the activity may have occurred externally. The raw cost cannot express whether the evidence is trusted, weakly linked, or immutable.
- **CL-611** has raw cost 2 because of a naming mismatch between `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss`. That is an activity-granularity and label-mapping issue, not a control failure.

Thus, alignment cost can point to areas needing attention, but it must be adjusted for policy constraints, evidence reliability, financial amount, fraud indicators, timing, reversibility, and model scope.
