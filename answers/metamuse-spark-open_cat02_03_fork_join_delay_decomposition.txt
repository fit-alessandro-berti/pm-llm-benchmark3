# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

**Indexes and primitives.**
`c  {F01…F12}` case; `i  {A,B,C}` branch. All times are *complete-event* timestamps accurate to 1 min on 2026-05-19. No pre-emption assumed.

**Assumptions labelled:**

* **A1 – Fork permission:** `fork_release(c)` is earliest time any branch *may* start. A branch may start later due to its own queue.
* **A2 – No pre-emption:** `service_i(c) = end_i(c)-start_i(c)` is contiguous active work.
* **A3 – Join logic:** `join_ready` is *not logged*; under stated process rule it is derived as latest branch completion. No additional inspection delay.
* **A4 – Final station:** `final_start(c)  join_ready(c)`; downstream waiting is `final_start-join_ready` if A3 holds.

To be scrutinized: arrival may contain prior queue; fork_release may be controlled or merely first start; `join_ready = max(end_i)` must be validated against logs; final waiting may be queue *or* release policy/batching.

**Per-case decomposition (minutes):**

```
upstream_elapsed(c)      = fork_release(c) - arrival(c)
branch_queue_i(c)        = start_i(c) - fork_release(c)  0, for i=A,B,C
branch_service_i(c)      = end_i(c) - start_i(c)
branch_completion_i(c)   = end_i(c) - fork_release(c) = queue_i+service_i
join_ready(c)            = max_{i{A,B,C}} end_i(c)               [derived]
critical_branch(c)       = argmax_i end_i(c)                       [deterministic tie-break]
sync_wait_i(c)           = join_ready(c) - end_i(c)  0, 0 if i is critical
elapsed_fork_join(c)     = join_ready(c) - fork_release(c)
                         = max_i [ branch_queue_i(c)+branch_service_i(c) ]
downstream_queue(c)      = final_start(c) - join_ready(c)         [0 if A3/A4 hold]
final_service(c)         = final_end(c) - final_start(c)
total_flow_time(c)       = final_end(c) - arrival(c)
                         = upstream_elapsed + elapsed_fork_join + downstream_queue + final_service
```

Ties: if `|{i:end_i = join_ready}|>1`, critical set has >1 member. For reproducibility choose lexicographically smallest `min{A<B<C}`. Report tie as joint critical; ranking counts it as shared.

**Why summing synchronization waits double-counts:**

Elapsed fork-join time is a *max*, not a sum. Branches run in parallel. The time line `fork_release  join_ready` is covered once.

```
sync_sum(c) = _i sync_wait_i(c) = _i (join_ready - end_i)
            = n·join_ready - _i end_i         with n=3
elapsed_fork_join(c) = join_ready - fork_release = max_i (end_i - fork_release)
```

In general `sync_sum  elapsed_fork_join` and can be larger or smaller. Example F07: ends 10:30,10:55,10:03, join 10:55  sync waits 25+0+52=77 min while elapsed fork-join = 75 min. Summing would count the interval 10:3010:55 once for A, once overlapping for C, and miss that B itself defines the elapsed. Correct decomposition counts *elapsed* once via `max`, and uses sync waits only to explain *which branch waited how long*, not to add to total. Formally:

```
total_flow  upstream + _i (queue_i+service_i+sync_i) + downstream + final
total_flow  = upstream + max_i(queue_i+service_i) + downstream + final
            = upstream + (queue_k+service_k) + downstream + final  for k=critical
            = upstream + (queue_i+service_i+sync_i) + downstream+final  for any i, but not summed over i
```

Summing all `sync_i` would add the same wall-clock waiting 2-3 times.

**Reasoned influence ranking:**

1. **B – most influential.** Highest completion time on average, 2/3 of cases last-completing, lowest mean sync (closest to join). Reducing B queue+service moves join in 8/12 cases. 2. **A and C – tied for second, context-dependent, equal.** Both 16.7% critical. A is deterministic floor 50 min; no queue, so improvement requires faster lab work, affects only A-critical cases. C is usually non-critical but can become critical via administrative queue spikes (F04 57, F08 55). Mean sync confirms C spends most time waiting. No statistically meaningful difference between A vs C with n=12; honest tie.

**Hypothesis vs estimate:** Critical frequency is estimate subject to sampling error (12 cases, single day). If another day had different B staffing, ranking could shift. Bound: even with uncertainty B remains >50% probability most influential.

### 1. Reduce A service by 20% (5040 min)

*Direct benefit:* A completion 40 min. New join = max( fork+40, B_end, C_end).
*Zero-benefit cases:* 10 of 12 where B or C remains later than shortened A. E.g. F02 B09:25 still >A09:05; F04 C09:52 >A09:35.
*Benefiting cases:* F01 saves 5 min (AB critical switch, join 09:0008:55). F11 saves 10 min (11:3011:20). Mean 1.25 min naive.
*Secondary effects:* Critical branch shifts from A to B in those 2 cases, exposing B as new bottleneck. No reduction in B queue variance. Total flow tail (F07,F12) unchanged. If downstream queue fixed, naive savings become downstream waiting increase: e.g. F01 final_start 09:08 still > new join 08:55  downstream 813, total flow unchanged (masked). Realistic saving in [0, 1.25] mean depending on final capacity. Utilization of A lab would drop ~20% but fork-join stage not perceptibly improved – high risk of wasted investment if judged by mean service alone.

### 2. Reduce B queue delay by 50% (queue×0.5)

*Direct benefit:* B_end' = B_end -0.5·queue. Mean queue 19.29.6, mean B completion 56.747.1 (<A floor). Implemented via adding B operator, better scheduling, priority for long-wait B jobs.
*Zero-benefit cases:* 4 of 12: F01 A still critical, F04 C critical, F08 C critical, F11 A critical. Their branch acceleration irrelevant to join.
*Benefiting cases:* 8 of 12 with 5-15 min join saving. Shifts criticality to A in F05, F10 (B no longer last). Tail cases improve most: F07 15, F12 15, F06 15. Total naive 92.5 min, mean 7.7.
*Secondary effects:* Reveals A as next bottleneck (50 min floor). Need to then address A or variability remains. Reducing B queue may increase work-in-process before final, potentially increasing downstream queue if final not expanded – but here downstream average 14.7 so buffering possible. Best leverage: attacks both mean and 90th percentile.

### 3. Add final-station capacity / reduce downstream queueing

*Direct benefit:* Currently downstream queue mean 14.7 min, max 20, present in every case despite 33% idle time on F1/F2 (see §7) suggesting policy/dispatch cause, not pure capacity shortage. Eliminating waiting: saving = downstream_queue per case, sum 176 mean 14.7, all 12 cases benefit. Halving queue saves 88 mean 7.3.
*Zero-benefit analysis inverse:* This counterfactual is *always* beneficial to total flow, but *masks* branch improvements if not done: Example F02 B improvement 10 min naive translates to 0 total if final_start stays 09:40 – early join just waits longer downstream. Bounds for CF2 realistic total saving = max(0, join - (resource_idle?)). Empirically F03 join 09:45 final10:00 downstream15 but preceding F1 free since 09:28 – 17 min idle before F03 start contradicts pure queue, so branch savings likely partially masked by non-queue policy.
*Secondary effects:* Adding a third identical server (23) would increase idle time (66%~45% utilization) without guaranteeing zero wait if dispatch is FCFS-by-join with fixed assignment – data shows assignment not earliest-available. Better to redesign dispatch to pooled earliest-available, allow overtaking (F08 before F07 already does), and review release rule causing 10-20 min downstream hold. Throughput may increase modestly because final service 20 min is constant; flow-time variance will shrink more than mean. Risk: overcapacity raises cost, underutilization, may hide upstream quality issues.

**Summary trade-off:** CF2 attacks fork-join stage (dominant for long flows), CF3 attacks post-join stage (dominant on average). CF1 is weak. Best combined: B queue reduction + pooled final dispatch yields additive savings where both stages contribute; either alone partially masked (Bdownstream, finalstill leaves B tail).

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### Redesign A – Prioritize B queue reduction (parallel B capacity + queue discipline)

**What:** Add one flexible B technician or cross-train, implement shortest-remaining or priority for cases where `B_queue` >15 (6 of 12 cases exceed). Target 50% queue cut modelled in CF2, keep A,C unchanged. Pilot with dedicated B monitoring for one week, measure `B_queue` distribution and end-to-end `join_ready`.

*Throughput:* Fork-join stage mean drops 60.652.9 min (13%). System throughput may increase modestly because cases arrive every 15 min, B service 37.5 is not system bottleneck vs arrival, but tail reduction frees downstream earlier.
*Flow-time tail:* 90th percentile total flow 125~112 (tail dominated by B 7560). Variance drops. Mean total -7.7 naive, realistic 3-7 after downstream masking.
*Utilization:* B resource utilization currently implicit (queue suggests single B server overloaded). Adding capacity will lower B utilization but increase A utilization relative. Final station utilization unchanged initially; if B improvement floods final, final may become new bottleneck.
*New bottlenecks / synchronization:* After cut, A floor 50 becomes critical more often (4-5 cases vs 2 now). Gains saturate; further B cuts yield diminishing returns (see zero-benefit cases F01,F04,F08,F11). Synchronization wait shifts: A sync mean 11~8, C sync ~30~? not much change.
*Risks:* Speeding non-critical B in C-critical cases wastes effort; need dynamic routing not blanket speedup. If B capacity added without final dispatch fix, early joins increase downstream waiting (masking). May create incentive to batch.
*Pilot/measurement:* Log B queue start reason (operator busy vs material), capture B resource calendar, track `fork_releaseB_start` every case, re-run decomposition weekly, control chart for B_queue 90th percentile, A/B test with 50% of cases prioritized.

### Redesign B – Fix final-station dispatch to pooled earliest-available + optional third buffer

**What:** Keep B as-is initially, convert final F1/F2 from fixed assignment to pooled FCFS by `join_ready` with earliest-available server, eliminate idle gaps (e.g. F06F07 gap 30 min idle on F1). This implements CF3 without necessarily hiring: policy change, small buffer for hand-off. If idle persists, add on-call third reviewer for peaks (expected utilization <30%).

*Throughput:* No change in service time (20 min) but effective flow time mean -14.7 if queue eliminated, -7.3 if halved. Throughput limited by final service 20 min with 2 servers = 6/hr; current arrival 4/hr, so capacity adequate – improvement is latency not capacity.
*Flow-time tail:* Downstream queue variance (8-20) collapses, tail reduction 10-20 min in worst cases F10,F11 (20 min). More predictable release than B redesign.
*Utilization:* Final utilization may rise from 66% to ~80% if idle eliminated, but still <100%; no underutilization crisis. Fork branch utilizations unchanged.
*New bottlenecks / synchronization:* Makes branch improvements visible; after fix, B dominance becomes fully realized in total flow, so next step is B redesign – complementary. If only final fixed, fork-join tail (B=75) still limits max flow 75+downstream0+20 =95+upstream vs current 130, so residual tail remains.
*Synchronization effects:* Early branch completions no longer mask downstream policy wait; sync waits correctly attributed to true parallelism. Risk of overtaking causing out-of-order releases may violate business rule requiring order – check policy.
*Risks:* If downstream queue is actually quality gate (not capacity), removing it may increase defect escape; must audit why downstream wait exists despite idle servers (observed gaps). Adding capacity without fixing dispatch wastes cost.
*Pilot/measurement:* Instrument `join_ready` proxy vs `final_start`, log resource idle time per interval, run discrete-event simulation with pooled vs dedicated assignment for 4 weeks, measure downstream queue distribution, interview operators for release triggers.

**Combined note:** Either redesign alone leaves 50% of potential on table. Phased approach: first fix dispatch (low cost, reveals true branch effects), measure, then target B queue. Avoid investing in A service, which data prove non-influential despite largest mean service.

# 7. Limits of inference

**What cannot be identified from these timestamps alone:**

* **Causality vs correlation:** Queues correlate with criticality (B queue spikes co-occur with B-critical cases) but timestamps do not prove *why* B queued (operator, material, priority). No intervention data.
* **Branch resource capacity and discipline:** Number of parallel A/B/C workers, queueing rule (FIFO, priority, batching), and simultaneous multi-case handling are not logged. A_queue always 0 suggests A may have ample capacity or dedicated per case, but could also be data artifact.
* **Work pre-emption / interruptions:** We assume `end-start` is active service. Pauses, rework, or split work would be invisible; service time would be over-estimated.
* **True join vs logged max:** `join_ready` is *inferred* as `max(end_i)`. If an unlogged inspection or assembly step exists between branch end and final start, `join_ready` is later than max and downstream queue is overstated and sync waits understated.
* **Whether final-station waiting is truly a queue rather than a release policy:** Evidence of idle gaps (e.g., F1 idle 09:2810:00 while F03 waits 15 min; F1 idle 10:4011:10 while F07 waits 15 min) contradicts pure capacity queue with pooled servers. Fixed assignment vs earliest-available, batching, shift breaks, or order-enforcement policy could create same pattern. Without resource calendars, cannot separate.
* **Fork-release enforcement and arrival process:** `arrival  fork_release` gap is 15 min for F01 then 20 min for rest; upstream cause unknown (single preparation station? travel?). Cannot infer upstream bottleneck or whether fork can be released earlier.
* **Final resource assignment logic:** Observed F1/F2 assignment is not earliest-available optimal, suggesting either dedicated customer/resource affinity or data error. With 2 servers, pooled simulation would give shorter waits; observed longer waits imply policy not just capacity.
* **Sample size and temporal scope:** 12 cases on one morning (3.5 h) cannot generalize to other shifts/days, seasonality, learning effects, or rare failures. Confidence intervals on critical frequencies are wide ( ±25% for 66% estimate). Tail inference (90th percentile) unstable.
* **Timestamp resolution and synchronisation:** 1-min accuracy creates artificial ties and may hide sub-minute ordering affecting critical branch determination. Clock skew across branches/servers not logged.
* **Throughput vs flow time confounding:** All final services exactly 20 min suggests engineered or rounded data. No variation, failures, or re-entrance observed, so throughput implications of redesigns are extrapolations.
* **Counterfactual realism:** Naive savings assume downstream duration constant and resources instantly available. Real system feedback (WIP increase after B speedup causing final congestion) cannot be estimated without queueing model and arrival forecast.

**Needed before acting:** Log per-branch resource IDs and busy intervals, final server calendars and dispatch rule, explicit join event, and upstream preparation queue. Pilot small controlled changes with A/B measurement rather than assuming proportional savings.
