# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Definitions used below:

- **Nominal waiting time** = `start - queue_arrival`; **nominal service time** = `complete - start`. These are field-based observations, not necessarily physical waiting or work.
- Intervals are treated as half-open, `[start, complete)`, so adjacent events do not overlap.
- Confidence refers to the leading explanation for the temporal pattern, not confidence that all lifecycle fields represent physical execution.
- `sequence_no` is evidence about source insertion order only.
- The additional context is supporting evidence, not assumed causal evidence.

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

The following are direct calculations from the supplied fields. They should not be read as physical-work estimates. Maximum concurrency uses positive half-open intervals.

| G1 | 4.5 | 3.0 | 4 | Fine-precision common cycle |
| G2 | 12.5 | 17.5 | 4 | Common start creates nested elapsed durations |
| G3 | 19.0 | 16.5 | 4 | Common completion/folder submission |

| G8 | 48.0 | 7.0 | 1 | Long pre-start backlog followed by uninterrupted serial work |

No resource calendar or analysis window is supplied, so absolute utilization percentages cannot be calculated. The discussion below concerns the direction of busy-time and bottleneck bias.

- **G1**
  - **Waiting:** Queue-to-cycle-start waiting is plausibly meaningful if 09:00 is the fixture-cycle start. It is not necessarily waiting to an individual label actuation.
  - **Service and utilization:** Summing four 3-minute durations yields 12 item-minutes, while the fixture is occupied for only 3 wall-clock minutes. Twelve minutes may be meaningful as slot-occupancy, but it would overstate single-machine busy time fourfold.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Four completions at 09:03 may be a real output burst. Treating that as four independent executions would misstate per-item capacity; the proper unit may be one four-position cycle.
  - **Conformance:** Same-time ordering based on `sequence_no` would be artificial. A batch-aware or capacity-four model is more appropriate than four serial robot transitions.

- **G2**
  - **Waiting:** Waiting to the group start is observable. If later items are actually inspected serially, using 10:00 as every work start understates their waiting to physical inspection.
  - **Service and utilization:** Nominal service totals 70 minutes, but the union of intervals is only 27 minutes. Under a validated serial interpretation, the completion increments—8, 6, 7 and 6 minutes—would account for the 27-minute run. Until validated, those are estimates rather than corrections.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Staggered completions may be usable throughput timestamps, but the intervals falsely suggest four concurrent long inspections. This can exaggerate workload or lead to an incorrect parallel-capacity model.
  - **Conformance:** A miner may infer concurrent execution after a common start, whereas the UI may simply have put four records into an active state.

- **G3**
  - **Waiting:** The staggered starts may be useful if they are actual review starts, but this requires validation. The common completion does not itself alter nominal waiting.
  - **Service and utilization:** Nominal durations total 67 minutes over a 28-minute union. Earlier reviews include time between their likely physical completion and the 11:30 folder submission, inflating elapsed service and apparent overlap.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Four completions at 11:30 create a false short-window throughput burst and can make release review look like a highly concurrent bottleneck. The total number completed over a long window remains four, but its temporal distribution is wrong.
  - **Conformance:** Individual review completion and folder release are different process semantics. Conflating them can create false synchronization and lifecycle-ordering behavior.

- **G4**
  - **Waiting:** The increasing waits—7, 10, 13, 16 and 19 minutes—are consistent with a real FIFO backlog where arrivals are faster than 4-minute service.
  - **Service and utilization:** Sum and union are both 20 minutes, supporting a continuously busy single server during 12:05–12:25.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Throughput is one completion every 4 minutes during this fragment. This is local evidence of overload relative to the supplied arrivals, not sufficient evidence that packing is the system-wide bottleneck.
  - **Conformance:** Calling the chain a PM4Py “sequential batch” is harmless as a temporal label but can be semantically misleading: ordinary continuous queue service needs no batch rule.

- **G5**
  - **Waiting:** Queue-to-pool-start waits are consistently about 15–16 minutes and may be meaningful at pool level. They cannot be assigned to an individual analyst.
  - **Service and utilization:** Nominal service totals 70 minutes over a 30-minute union. Treating `analyst_pool` as one person creates impossible concurrency; using only the union, however, can undercount labor performed by several analysts. Summed elapsed durations may overcount touch time if analysts multitasked.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** The different completion times look like independent concurrent work, not a physical batch. Pool throughput can be measured, but analyst utilization and capacity require badge identities and a denominator of up to three analysts.
  - **Conformance:** A resource-constrained model could falsely conclude that one analyst multitasks four cases. Conversely, a model that assumes three independent analysts still cannot explain four-way overlap without multitasking or imprecise work boundaries.

- **G6**
  - **Waiting:** Nominal waits range from 35 to 175 minutes, with a 110-minute median. If 17:00 is an administrative closure time rather than a work start, these values include work already performed but not entered and are inflated relative to actual execution waiting.
  - **Service and utilization:** Every service duration is zero. This hides all physical labor and produces near-zero measured utilization. Some algorithms may instead treat seven same-time records as an instantaneous capacity spike.
  - **Why inflated waiting and near-zero service coexist:** Both effects follow from putting the same late administrative timestamp into `start` and `complete`. The entire interval from queue arrival to the bulk timestamp is allocated to waiting, while none is allocated to service. This is a field-semantics problem, not a paradox in the operation.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Seven completions at 17:00 imply a false one-minute burst. The data can simultaneously make the queue appear to be the bottleneck and the closing resource appear almost unused.
  - **Conformance:** Zero-duration, simultaneous closure can satisfy simplistic ordering rules while violating expected minimum processing times and resource-capacity constraints. Insertion order is not execution order.

- **G7**
  - **Waiting:** Four negative waits are direct evidence that point-valued queue waiting is invalid under the current rounding and possible clock uncertainty. Clipping them to zero would create an unsupported downward bias.
  - **Service and utilization:** Every service time is exactly one 15-minute bucket, yielding 75 summed minutes over a 15-minute union. Actual durations could be shorter, longer, serial or overlapping; the bias direction is not identifiable without the rounding rule.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** Five completions at 14:15 are a quantization burst, not reliable minute-level throughput. Bottleneck and concurrency detection are therefore unreliable.
  - **Conformance:** Rounded starts before queue arrivals create false causality violations, while common endpoints create a false simultaneous-batch label. Interval-censored conformance is required.

- **G8**
  - **Waiting:** The 38–58 minute waits likely contain a genuine unavailable-capacity component, but cross-system clock offsets could shift them. Even if total waiting is real, attributing it specifically to the safety check is not yet justified.
  - **Service and utilization:** Five 7-minute scanner intervals form a 35-minute union and a 35-minute sum, strongly supporting serial busy time from 15:38 to 16:13. Availability and utilization must be distinguished: the preceding period may be unavailable rather than idle.
  - **Throughput and bottlenecks:** The system first accumulates five jobs and then completes one every 7 minutes. A naive analysis will label repair triage as the bottleneck, whereas the main cause of the queue may be temporary capacity loss.
  - **Conformance:** If the safety check is a prerequisite, unsynchronized clocks can create false ordering violations. The serial repair order itself is well supported by scanner timestamps.

Because each supplied case has only one fragment event, full control-flow conformance cannot be assessed here. The stated effects concern lifecycle, temporal, resource-capacity and ordering conformance when these events are joined to fuller case traces.

# Current PM4Py versions return tuples of
# (activity, resource, number_of_batches, {batch_type: instances}).
# The normalizer also includes a fallback for minor representation differences.
cluster_lookup = {
    (str(r.activity), str(r.resource)): r.cluster
    for r in df[["cluster", "activity", "resource"]].drop_duplicates().itertuples(index=False)
}

def category_name(value):
    return str(getattr(value, "value", value))

def normalize_batches(discovered, merge_distance, min_batch_size):
    rows = []

    def emit(activity, resource, reported_count, by_type):
        cluster = cluster_lookup.get((str(activity), str(resource)), "UNMAPPED")
        if isinstance(by_type, dict):
            for batch_type, instances in by_type.items():
                try:
                    n_instances = len(instances)
                except TypeError:
                    n_instances = np.nan
                rows.append({
                    "cluster": cluster,
                    "activity": str(activity),
                    "resource": str(resource),
                    "merge_distance": merge_distance,
                    "min_batch_size": min_batch_size,
                    "category": category_name(batch_type),
                    "instances": n_instances,
                    "reported_batches": reported_count
                })
        else:
            rows.append({
                "cluster": cluster,
                "activity": str(activity),
                "resource": str(resource),
                "merge_distance": merge_distance,
                "min_batch_size": min_batch_size,
                "category": "UNPARSED:" + repr(by_type)[:100],
                "instances": np.nan,
                "reported_batches": reported_count
            })

    if isinstance(discovered, dict):
        for key, value in discovered.items():
            activity, resource = key if isinstance(key, tuple) and len(key) >= 2 else (key, "")
            if isinstance(value, tuple) and value and isinstance(value[-1], dict):
                emit(activity, resource, value[-2] if len(value) >= 2 else np.nan, value[-1])
            else:
                emit(activity, resource, np.nan, value)
    else:
        for record in discovered:
            if isinstance(record, (tuple, list)) and len(record) >= 4 and isinstance(record[3], dict):
                emit(record[0], record[1], record[2], record[3])
            elif (isinstance(record, (tuple, list)) and len(record) >= 3
                  and isinstance(record[0], tuple) and len(record[0]) >= 2
                  and isinstance(record[-1], dict)):
                emit(record[0][0], record[0][1], record[-2], record[-1])
            else:
                rows.append({
                    "cluster": "UNMAPPED",
                    "activity": "",
                    "resource": "",
                    "merge_distance": merge_distance,
                    "min_batch_size": min_batch_size,
                    "category": "UNPARSED:" + repr(record)[:100],
                    "instances": np.nan,
                    "reported_batches": np.nan
                })
    return rows

# merge_distance is in seconds. min_batch_size=5 deliberately tests whether
# four-event clusters disappear; merge_distance=1 versus 900 tests episode merging.
settings = [(1, 2), (1, 5), (900, 2), (900, 5)]

    discovered = pm4py.discover_batches(
        formatted,
        merge_distance=merge_distance,
        min_batch_size=min_batch_size
    )
    batch_rows.extend(
        normalize_batches(discovered, merge_distance, min_batch_size)
    )
batch_df = pd.DataFrame(batch_rows)

def equality_stats(series):
    counts = series.dropna().value_counts()
    if counts.empty:
        return 0, 0
    return int(counts.max()), int((counts >= 2).sum())

def aligned_fraction(series, precision_s):
    values = series.dropna()
    if values.empty or pd.isna(precision_s):
        return np.nan
    return float(np.mean([
        (int(ts.value // 1_000_000_000) % int(precision_s)) == 0
        for ts in values
    ]))

def resource_diagnostics(group):
    group = group.sort_values("sequence_no")
    valid = group.dropna(subset=["start", "complete"]).copy()

    pair_total = 0
    pair_overlap = 0
    for left, right in combinations(valid.index, 2):
        pair_total += 1
        overlap = (
            max(valid.loc[left, "start"], valid.loc[right, "start"])
            < min(valid.loc[left, "complete"], valid.loc[right, "complete"])
        )
        pair_overlap += int(overlap)
    overlap_rate = pair_overlap / pair_total if pair_total else np.nan

    positive = valid.loc[valid["complete"] > valid["start"]].copy()
    if positive.empty:
        max_concurrency = 0
    else:
        max_concurrency = max(
            int(((positive["start"] <= t) & (positive["complete"] > t)).sum())
            for t in positive["start"]
        )

    ordered = positive.sort_values(["start", "sequence_no"])
    if len(ordered) >= 2:
        adjacency = np.mean(
            ordered["start"].iloc[1:].reset_index(drop=True)
            == ordered["complete"].iloc[:-1].reset_index(drop=True)
        )
    else:
        adjacency = np.nan

    start_eq_max, start_eq_groups = equality_stats(group["start"])
    end_eq_max, end_eq_groups = equality_stats(group["complete"])

    lag_min = (
        (group["recorded_at"] - group["complete"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    ).dropna()
    wait_min = (
        (group["start"] - group["queue_arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    ).dropna()

    first_start = group["start"].min()
    arrivals_before_first_start = (
        int((group["queue_arrival"] <= first_start).sum())
        if pd.notna(first_start) else np.nan
    )
    arrival_span = (
        (group["queue_arrival"].max() - group["queue_arrival"].min()).total_seconds() / 60
        if group["queue_arrival"].notna().any() else np.nan
    )

    wait_order = group.assign(wait_min=(
        group["start"] - group["queue_arrival"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60).dropna(subset=["wait_min"]).sort_values("sequence_no")
    wait_slope = (
        float(np.polyfit(np.arange(len(wait_order)), wait_order["wait_min"], 1)[0])
        if len(wait_order) >= 2 else np.nan
    )

    precision_s = int(group["precision_s"].mode().iat[0])
    endpoints = pd.concat([group["start"], group["complete"]], ignore_index=True)

    return pd.Series({
        "n": len(group),
        "lifecycle_flag": " / ".join(sorted(group["lifecycle_flag"].unique())),
        "identity_flag": " / ".join(sorted(group["resource_identity_flag"].unique())),
        "precision_s": precision_s,
        "start_eq_max": start_eq_max,
        "start_eq_groups": start_eq_groups,
        "end_eq_max": end_eq_max,
        "end_eq_groups": end_eq_groups,
        "start_unique_ratio": group["start"].nunique(dropna=True) / max(group["start"].notna().sum(), 1),
        "end_unique_ratio": group["complete"].nunique(dropna=True) / max(group["complete"].notna().sum(), 1),
        "endpoint_grid_share": aligned_fraction(endpoints, precision_s),
        "recorded_grid_share": aligned_fraction(group["recorded_at"], precision_s),
        "pairwise_overlap_rate": overlap_rate,
        "max_concurrency": max_concurrency,
        "adjacency_rate": adjacency,
        "recording_lag_median_min": lag_min.median() if not lag_min.empty else np.nan,
        "recording_lag_min_min": lag_min.min() if not lag_min.empty else np.nan,
        "recording_lag_max_min": lag_min.max() if not lag_min.empty else np.nan,
        "wait_median_min": wait_min.median() if not wait_min.empty else np.nan,
        "wait_min_min": wait_min.min() if not wait_min.empty else np.nan,
        "wait_max_min": wait_min.max() if not wait_min.empty else np.nan,
        "negative_wait_share": float((wait_min < 0).mean()) if not wait_min.empty else np.nan,
        "arrivals_before_first_start": arrivals_before_first_start,
        "arrival_span_min": arrival_span,
        "wait_slope_per_sequence_min": wait_slope
    })

diagnostics = (
    df.groupby(["cluster", "resource"], dropna=False)
      .apply(resource_diagnostics)
      .reset_index()

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### If resource IDs are trusted

- G2 and G3 become difficult to interpret as literal execution intervals: one trusted worker would appear to process four overlapping jobs. That strengthens common-start and common-end logging explanations.
- G4 and G8 strongly support serial single-server execution.
- G5 would require one person to have four active analyses at once. Multitasking is possible, but the supplied shared-login fact is a more direct explanation.
- G6 would show seven simultaneous zero-duration actions by one resource, strengthening the bulk-form diagnosis.
- G7 would imply five simultaneous manual measurements by T-G, but the documented rounding and causality violations make that interpretation weak.
- G1 is special: one resource can legitimately have capacity four because it is an automated fixture, so a trusted ID does not imply capacity one.

### If resource IDs may be shared

- G5’s overlaps no longer imply one analyst’s multitasking, although four-way overlap still exceeds the stated pool size of up to three and therefore remains partly unexplained.
- G2 could represent several inspectors sharing one tablet. The common-start UI remains strong evidence, but serial execution becomes less certain.
- G3 could mix several reviewers submitting through one desktop account, weakening the inference of sequential review by T-C.
- For G4 and G8, shared use could produce multiple workers, but the exact non-overlapping chains and scanner lags still support serial station-level execution.
- Shared accounts do little to explain G6’s common zero-duration lifecycle or G7’s quarter-hour quantization.
- The RBT-7 identifier denotes an automated cell rather than a human login, so generic shared-account reasoning is not the leading explanation for G1.

### If timestamps are exact

Exactness strengthens the distinction among the PM4Py temporal categories, especially for the 1-second PLC/scanner sources. It does not prove execution semantics:

- G1 remains a common fixture cycle, but within-cycle actuation may still be serial.
- G4 and G8 have compelling serial adjacency.
- G2’s exact group start and G3’s exact common end still likely come from interface semantics.
- G6 can be exactly recorded and still semantically false as a work interval.

### If timestamps are interval-censored by stated precision

**Sensitivity assumption:** because most rounding rules are not supplied, treat each displayed timestamp as locating the event only within a bucket whose width is its documented precision. This is a robustness model, not a claim about the actual rounding algorithm. G7 may require wider one-sided alternatives because its rule can round down or up.

- One-second uncertainty does not materially alter the multi-minute G1, G4 and G8 patterns, although cross-system clock offset can still bias queue waiting.
- One-minute uncertainty can perturb exact equality for G2, G3, G5 and G6. It cannot explain G5’s 10–20 minute overlaps, nor can it recover G6’s missing physical work history.
- G6’s apparent zero duration could become an interval of at most roughly one precision bucket under a pure resolution explanation, but the manual bulk-form context still indicates that earlier work may be wholly absent.
- G7 changes fundamentally. Actual starts and ends could differ materially within or around the displayed quarter-hour boundaries; equality, overlap, service duration and queue causality all become set-valued rather than point-valued.
- Queue waiting combines upstream `queue_arrival` with a local start clock. An unknown source-clock offset adds uncertainty to every queue wait, even when local service duration is stable because start and complete came from the same source.

### Same pattern, multiple physical explanations

At least the following patterns are intrinsically ambiguous:

1. **Equal starts and equal ends:** G1 supports a four-position automated cycle, G6 supports bulk administrative closure, and G7 supports timestamp quantization. The same PM4Py “simultaneous” label therefore maps to three different physical stories.
2. **Equal starts with different ends:** G2 may be a shared setup followed by serial work, several genuinely parallel inspections, or a UI state transition that does not represent work start.
3. **Different starts with equal ends:** G3 may be delayed folder submission, genuine synchronized completion at a deadline, or multitasking with a common business release.
4. **Overlapping intervals under one resource:** G5 may reflect multiple people behind a shared account, one person multitasking, or elapsed case ownership that includes inactive time.
5. **Exact adjacency:** G4 and G8 may be operationally defined sequential batches or merely ordinary single-server busy periods. A temporal detector cannot determine whether a batch concept exists in the operation.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

1. **Separate operational semantics before increasing timestamp precision.** Capture distinct fields or events for queue arrival, item work start, pause, resume, item work end, group/setup start, group/setup end, business completion and form submission. A precise submission timestamp must not populate a work-start field.
2. **Retain execution and recording clocks independently.** Store source event time, client submission time, server receipt time, database commit time and any later correction time. Preserve the original value when corrections are made.
3. **Synchronize and monitor source clocks.** Use a common time service where possible; record source clock ID, observed offset, synchronization status and uncertainty. Cross-system comparisons such as queue arrival versus local start or safety check versus triage should include offset bounds.
4. **Recover resource identity at the appropriate level.** Retain a privacy-preserving badge or worker identifier separately from device, login, workstation, role and pool. Explicitly mark shared accounts and record the number of simultaneously staffed analysts.
5. **Add group and physical batch identifiers.** For G1-like work, record fixture-cycle ID, fixture position and configured capacity. For G2-like work, record group ID and distinguish group start from item start. Folder IDs should link G3 reviews to the later submission without replacing individual completion times.
6. **Record workstation capacity and availability states.** Capture planned capacity, actual staffing, downtime, safety checks, maintenance and capacity reductions. This allows waiting caused by unavailability to be separated from waiting caused by insufficient processing rate.
7. **Capture pause/resume and active-touch information where elapsed ownership is long.** This is particularly important for analytical work and multitasking. Preserve both elapsed case duration and, where operationally justified, accumulated active time.
8. **Make precision metadata field-specific and machine-readable.** Store resolution, rounding mode, bucket anchoring, clock accuracy and whether a timestamp is observed, defaulted, inferred or manually entered. For coarse sources, retain lower and upper temporal bounds instead of presenting one rounded point as exact.
9. **Validate with periodic operational samples.** Compare source events against fixture controllers, scanner traces, badge observations or audited work samples. The purpose is to determine semantics and bias, not merely to measure database completeness.

# 7. Final adjudication

- **High-confidence temporal or data-quality diagnoses**
  - **G1:** one four-item automated fixture cycle is strongly supported, although simultaneous per-item label actuation is not proven.
  - **G2:** common group-start semantics are strongly supported.
  - **G3:** common completion/folder submission is strongly supported.
  - **G4:** a genuine serial busy period is strongly supported; calling it an operational batch is not.
  - **G5:** the single resource ID is a shared-account artifact, although the true distribution of work among analysts is unresolved.
  - **G6:** the lifecycle fields are bulk administrative timestamps rather than defensible physical service intervals.
  - **G7:** timestamp coarsening is certain enough to invalidate point-duration analysis.
  - **G8:** serial clearance after a backlog is clear; the cause of the preceding capacity loss is only moderately supported.

- **Exclude or interval-censor in queue and duration analysis**
  - Exclude **G6** from execution-based queue, service and utilization estimation until actual work timestamps are recovered.
  - Treat **G7** as interval-censored; do not use its point waiting or service times and do not clip its negative waits.
  - For **G2**, 10:00 supports waiting to group admission, not necessarily waiting to individual work start.
  - For **G3**, the starts may be usable after validation, but the common completion should not be used for individual service duration.
  - Analyze **G5** only at pool level unless badge identities and capacity are recovered.
  - Use **G8** queue waits with clock-offset sensitivity and do not attribute them to the safety check until clocks and capacity states are reconciled.

- **Operational validation required before physical-performance conclusions**
  - G1 needs fixture-cycle and within-cycle actuation validation if per-item labor or robot utilization is required.
  - G2 needs individual inspection-start evidence.
  - G3 needs individual review-end evidence.
  - G5 needs badge identity, pool staffing and multitasking information.
  - G8 needs clock alignment and a confirmed capacity-loss reason.
  - G6 and G7 require source redesign or external evidence rather than a purely statistical correction.

Overall, G4 is the cleanest fragment for direct single-server performance analysis. G1 is usable as a capacity-four cycle. G6 and G7 should not be “fixed” by tie-breaking or sequence-number ordering, and no PM4Py batch category should be treated as proof of physical batching.
