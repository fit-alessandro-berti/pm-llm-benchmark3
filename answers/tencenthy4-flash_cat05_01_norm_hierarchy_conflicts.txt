## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts used as given**
- GPIP-4 is board-approved, globally effective from 2026-01-01, and applies to all purchases.
- Aachen SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6 is a controlled local procedure for maintenance/production-support purchases.
- ECD-2 was a signed COO/Incident Commander incident directive effective only from 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST.
- The CFO email was sent during the ECD-2 window but was not entered into the controlled policy repository.
- The CRO did not issue or later ratify an A7 exception.
- The ERP outage affects evidentiary weight for timing; it is not itself a normative exception.

**Working assumptions**
1. **No total ordering is assumed.** Authority is issue-specific. GPIP-4 is strongest for integrity controls; ECD-2 is strongest for expressly stated incident timing/supplier-onboarding overrides; the CFO email is strongest only within the CFO’s finance-control domain.
2. **ECD-2 is not treated as an A7 CRO exception.** It is a COO/Incident Commander directive. GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to the CRO; the CRO was unavailable and did not ratify. Prior practice treating signed directives as temporary overlays is relevant but not enough where GPIP-4 integrity controls are untouched.
3. **ECD-2 can override ordinary local workflow only within its scope and window.** It can excuse SOP sequencing and approved-supplier rules where “critical sterilization-line lots” is established. It does not expressly waive Compliance_Clearance, segregation of duties, Finance approval under GPIP-4, the EUR 100,000 threshold, or CRO authority.
4. **The CFO email is given limited contemporary effect.** Because D3 states the CFO owns Finance controls, D1/D2 are treated as a contemporaneous emergency interpretation/relaxation of operational finance approvals during ECD-2: joint Incident Command authorization is sufficient up to EUR 150,000, and with Legal clearance above EUR 150,000. This does **not** waive Compliance controls, GPIP-4 segregation of duties, or the CRO exception requirement. This is a contested interpretation because A4 could be read to require a CRO exception for any GPIP-4 control relaxation.
5. **Later-in-time instruments do not automatically defeat earlier ones.** The CFO email is later than ECD-2 but lower in authority and narrower in subject matter. Later approvals do not cure missing pre-commitment controls unless the controlling rule says so.
6. **Emergency necessity has two separate tests.** SOP emergency status requires imminent safety harm or production stoppage within six hours. ECD-2 scope requires “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.” Satisfying one does not automatically satisfy the other.
7. **Retrospective evidence is weighed by proximity and reliability.** Supplier recordings, acknowledgments, signed PDFs, and access logs may outweigh ERP timestamps where the outage or clock issue is documented. ERP timestamps are rebuttable, not conclusive.
8. **Burden of proof.** The party invoking an exception must establish its scope, timing, and conditions with contemporaneous or strong equivalent evidence. Ambiguity in high-integrity controls is resolved against the exception; ambiguity in mere logging may be handled as an evidentiary defect.
9. **Classification convention.** `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` means the baseline rule would have been breached but a valid, scoped exception or emergency overlay was satisfied. It is not a finding that ordinary baseline procedure was followed.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

- **P-501 — alternative: COMPLIANT or LOW-LEVEL EXCEPTION.**  
  Premise required: treat SOP B2 approval timing as not expressly pre-commitment and treat the later ECD-2 as superseding “ordinary local workflow steps” broadly enough to validate the earlier emergency sequence. This is credible because B2 does not contain the words “before commitment,” and B7 is undefined. It is weak because the emergency occurred before ECD-2 began, the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate, and B5 preserves Finance approval.

- **P-502 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
  Premise required: read ECD-2 C2/C4 “supplier-onboarding requirements” and CFO D1 “all operational approvals” as also waiving A3 new-supplier Compliance_Clearance. This is credible if one views supplier onboarding as including compliance vetting and contamination containment as requiring rapid new-supplier use. It is weak because C5 expressly says ECD-2 does not mention Compliance clearance, D3 says the CFO cannot waive Compliance controls, and the organization’s prior overlay practice did not involve new-supplier Compliance clearance.

- **P-503 — alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
  Premise required: accept Operations’ broad scope for backup-line components and interpret PROC-3’s IC authorization as not a “final business approval” but only one part of a collective incident authorization. Even then, the missing A3 Compliance_Clearance for EUR 140,000 remains fatal unless A3 is also waived. This alternative is weak because A1 and A3 are express board controls and no CRO exception exists.

- **P-505 — alternative: NONCOMPLIANT.**  
  Premise required: treat the ERP commitment timestamp 18:06 as controlling, placing commitment after ECD expiry at 18:00. This is credible because ERP is the formal system of record. It is weaker on these facts because the supplier call began at 17:57, the supplier acknowledgment arrived at 17:59:31, and the ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast. The alternative also strengthens if CFO D1 is rejected as a waiver of A2 finance precommitment.

- **P-507 — alternative: NONCOMPLIANT.**  
  Premise required: hold that “recorded within four hours” means entered in ERP, not merely captured in a signed PDF, or reject CFO D1 as incapable of relaxing GPIP A2. This is credible because ERP showed authorization only at 14:30 and A4 reserves non-tendering controls to a CRO exception. It is weak on the record-language point because a signed PDF created at 12:40 is strong contemporaneous evidence of timely recording; it is stronger on the A2/CRO point.

- **P-508 — alternative: NONCOMPLIANT.**  
  Premise required: read D1/D2 narrowly so that above EUR 150,000 Legal clearance is additional but does not make joint IC authorization sufficient for finance/budget approval, or hold that only a CRO exception can relax GPIP A2. **Distinctions**

- **Policy breach:** P-502 new-supplier Compliance after commitment; P-503 self-authorization and missing >100k Compliance; P-504 unapproved supplier outside ECD and non-emergency; P-506 post-expiry purchase without valid extension; P-501 invalid/emergency-limited approval sequence.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement clause — Emergency Procurement Overlay and Exception Hierarchy**

1. **Authority.** GPIP-4 controls remain in force unless suspended by a written, scoped, time-limited CRO exception. A COO/Incident Commander directive, CFO email, or verbal incident instruction may not amend GPIP-4. Operational directives may override only identified local SOP steps and must cite the SOP clauses superseded.

2. **Scope.** An emergency procurement directive must define covered items by named product, line, use case, and containment purpose. “Critical sterilization-line lots” includes only items necessary to prevent imminent safety harm, restore the affected sterilization line, or avoid loss of containment within a stated period. Backup-line or contingency items require express written inclusion based on documented necessity.

3. **Timing and expiry.** A directive is valid only during its stated window. Expiry is absolute unless extended in writing by the same issuing authority and, where GPIP-4 is affected, by the CRO under the exception clause. Verbal extensions are invalid.

4. **Finance/operational approvals.** During a valid directive, purchases up to EUR 150,000 may be approved by two distinct Incident Command authorizers, at least one of whom must be a designated budget delegate and one a designated finance delegate. Above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is also required before commitment. This does not alter segregation or Compliance requirements.

5. **Compliance and new suppliers.** Purchases above EUR 100,000 and purchases from any new supplier require Compliance_Clearance before commitment. Supplier-onboarding exceptions do not waive Compliance_Clearance. Only the CRO may issue a written exception to this requirement. Pre-qualified emergency suppliers should be maintained before incidents.

6. **Segregation of duties.** The requester or person committing the company may not be the final business approver or one of the required independent authorizers. Joint authorization is valid only if all authorizers are distinct from the requester and committer.

7. **Retrospective records.** Verbal authorization is permitted only where the rule allows it and must be recorded within four hours. Signed PDFs, access logs, call recordings, and supplier acknowledgments are acceptable evidence of timely action where ERP timestamps are affected by outages or clock error. Outage notices affect evidentiary weight only.

8. **No emergency normalization.** An emergency cannot be used repetitively for the same supplier, item class, or non-immediate need. Any third use within 30 days, any use beyond 72 hours after incident declaration, or any “site continuity” rationale requires CRO review and documented necessity. Post-incident review must classify each event as emergency, ordinary purchase, logging defect, or control breach.
