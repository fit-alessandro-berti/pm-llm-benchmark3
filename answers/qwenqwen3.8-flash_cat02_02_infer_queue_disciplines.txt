# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

The following reconstruction treats a “decision opportunity” as any event that could plausibly trigger a dispatch choice: service completion, a job becoming visible, a resource becoming available, an appointment time, an interruption, or a resumption.

**Working assumptions**

- **Assumption A1 — Visibility:** A job is plausibly eligible if `arrival + 0–2 min  decision time`. When a decision occurs within 2 minutes of arrival, I mark eligibility as uncertain.
- **Assumption A2 — Priority:** Priority 1 is highest, priority 3 lowest. The “Priority” column gives the non-preemptive priority choice at the decision time. If preemptive priority were in force, a higher-priority arrival during service should also have caused an earlier interruption.
- **Assumption A3 — SPT:** SPT uses `est_min` for waiting jobs. For interrupted jobs, the remaining time is unknown to dispatch unless explicitly tracked; for B01 I use the inferred remaining time as a bound.
- **Assumption A4 — Appointments:** An appointment job is a candidate at or after its appointment time. Exact appointment time versus appointment window is unknown.
- **Assumption A5 — Skill-based routing:** Skill constraints filter eligibility but do not by themselves determine order among eligible jobs.
- **Assumption A6 — Data quality:** Timestamps are accurate to ±1 minute; missing interruption reasons do not prove that no operational reason existed.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable queue: QA.**  
QA has a single resource, no skill ambiguity, and a strong FIFO pattern among non-appointment jobs. The appointment exception at A03 is clear, and the calibration window explains the major idle gap. The main weakness is that A06’s appointment start is also FIFO-consistent, but the overall diagnosis is relatively stable.

**Least identifiable queue: QC.**  
QC is the most ambiguous because several mechanisms are observationally entangled: SPT explains C02, C03, and C06; priority/deadline explains C05; aging/oldest-job explains C01; and C04’s selection is highly sensitive to whether C06 was visible at 10:32. The queue could plausibly be described as SPT with SLA/aging overrides, but the evidence is not clean enough for high confidence. QD is also weakly identifiable because of the unexplained D-F idle period, but it has more consistent FIFO-under-skill-constraints episodes.

**Most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone: pure priority, especially preemptive priority.**  
The data contain a few high-priority starts or preemptions—QB B02 and B05, QC C05—but many priority-1 jobs are delayed: QA A04 and A08, QD D08 and D10, and QB B01/B04 when lower-priority or older jobs are selected. These patterns are better explained by FIFO with urgent exceptions, SLA-triggered overrides, skill constraints, or appointment reservations than by a consistent priority discipline. Inferring preemptive priority from the isolated priority-1 events would be especially misleading.
