## 1. Conformance semantics  

**Forbidden behavior** – Only actions that are explicitly marked *forbidden* in the normative edges or DFG relations constitute violations. In our case, *Order  Cancel Order  Dispatch Package* (N3) and *Order object_cobirth Payment* (T6) are forbidden. Any observation of these edges must be treated as a hard, noncompliant breach regardless of context.  

**Missing mandatory behavior** – Relations or edges marked *mandatory* are expected whenever their scope applies. If the observed log contains zero instances of a mandatory relation (e.g., a missing *Create Order  Validate Order* for the two Orders that do not show it), this is a **gap** that signals noncompliance unless a legitimate openworld alternative can be accounted for (e.g., an unrecorded interim review). The openworld assumption permits missing events **only** when the scope does not strictly require them; mandatory items therefore become *missing mandatory behavior* when the abstraction is interpreted as “directlyfollows” without allowance for intermediate steps.  

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** – Any activity that the norm does **not** cover (status *unspecified* or absent from the norm) falls into this bucket. It is neither a violation nor a normative pattern; it simply reflects processes outside the captured policy (e.g., *Reopen Order  Close Order* (N6) or *Order object_interaction Customer* (T4)). These should be recorded for future policy enrichment.  

**Typicalpattern absence** – When a *typical* relation is missing, it may reflect legitimate exceptions permitted by the norm (e.g., the 14 Invoices with no observed successor, or the 1 Order that only performed a manual compliance review after creation). Such absences are **expected** under a typical status and need no corrective action.  

**Likely dataquality error** – Observations that are highly inconsistent with the process model or context (e.g., the single *Sensor Heartbeat* edge linked to an Invoice) are flagged as probable dataquality issues, especially when the link originates from a reused identifier or an anomalous attribute.  

**Abstraction mismatch** – This arises when a directlyfollows (chronotemporal) abstraction is too strict for a business rule that allows intermediate steps. The *Create Order  Validate Order* expectation (N1) suffers from this: two Orders lack a direct edge because a review or invoicing step may intervene, which the norm does not capture. Recognizing this mismatch prevents misclassification of noncompliance.  

**Insufficient evidence** – Situations where the observed log lacks critical attributes needed to evaluate a normative expectation (e.g., whether the lone *Record Payment* without an Invoice is “unapplied cash” or a genuine emptylink error). In such cases, the observation is recorded as “insufficient evidence” and requires audit data before classification.  

### Openworld vs. closedworld  

- **Closedworld** applies only to *forbidden* relations and *mandatory* items within their explicit scope. Observations missing these are violations.  
- **Openworld** governs all other statuses (typical, unspecified, and implicit permissive steps). Missing or unexpected behavior under an openworld clause is not automatically noncompliant; it may be a legitimate process variant or data gap. This distinction guides whether a gap is a hard breach (closedworld) or an area for improvement (openworld).

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family  

### OCDFG (ObjectCentric DirectFollows Graph)  

**What the model can establish**  
- Directlyfollows edges such as *Create Order  Validate Order* (N1) and *Pack Package  Dispatch Package* (N4) allow us to count exact transitions.  
- Counts (e.g., 98/100 Orders validated directly after creation) provide quantitative compliance metrics for mandatory relations.  

**What the model cannot establish**  
- It cannot infer **intermediate** steps that the openworld nature permits (e.g., a manual compliance review inserted between creation and validation). This abstraction mismatch can mislead us into labeling a compliant early review as a missing mandatory flow.  
- The model lacks context about why a *Cancel Order  Dispatch Package* edge appears; without event timestamps or business rationale, it cannot differentiate a process deviation from a datacapture error.  

**Counts and scopes**  
- *Create Order  Validate Order* is **mandatory**; the 2 Orders lacking it trigger the “missing mandatory behavior” classification only if we assume a **directlyfollows** interpretation. Recognizing the openworld aspect (possible review step) shifts it to “absence of typical pattern”.  
- *Validate Order  Issue Invoice* is not normative (N1N2 only cover validation  close), but the 72 occurrences are acceptable as they fall under typical postvalidation activities (unspecified by the norm).  

**Abstraction danger**  
Interpreting an “eventually expected” rule (validation before invoicing) as a strict directlyfollows rule can overpenalize legitimate early invoicing or compliance reviews. Hence, we must treat such gaps as **abstraction mismatches**, requiring business validation before flagging.  

### OTG (ObjectCentric Graph)  

**What the model can establish**  
- Edge types such as *(Order, object_interaction, Item)* (T1) and *(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)* (T2) give us counts of required interactions.  
- The weight of *(Order, object_interaction, Customer)* (100) shows a high volume, but because its status is *unspecified*, we cannot deem it a violation; it simply indicates an area where external policy may apply.  

**What the model cannot establish**  
- The *object_cobirth* edge *(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)* (T6) is **forbidden**; a single observed instance signals a hard breach, but the model alone cannot differentiate whether the edge arose from a dataquality error (ID reuse) or a genuine process shortcut.  
- *object_codeath* edges (Package–Delivery) are not covered by any normative relation, so they are classified as “unexpected but unregulated”.  

**Counts affect interpretation**  
- The high weight of *(Order, object_interaction, Item)* (1450) confirms adherence to mandatory item addition.  
- The solitary *(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)* edge, despite low weight, dominates severity ranking because forbidden status overrides any openworld allowance.  
- The unspecified *(Order, object_interaction, Customer)* edges are benign and require no corrective action, illustrating the openworld handling of status *unspecified*.  

**Additional considerations**  
- The *manual compliance review* events linking to both *RiskCase* and *Customer* illustrate crossobject interactions that the norm does not restrict; they fall under “unexpected but unregulated”.  

### ET–OT (EventType to ObjectType)  

**What the model can establish**  
- Eventobject mappings such as *Create Order  Order* (E1), *Pack Package  Package* (E4), and *Dispatch Package  Delivery* (E6) verify that every required event appears for its target object.  
- Counts match expectations (e.g., 200 *Dispatch Package* events align with 198 *Delivery* events, indicating a minor dataquality lag rather than a process error).  

**What the model cannot establish**  
- The *Record Payment  Payment* event (E7) appears with a count of 93, exceeding the 92 *Record Payment  Invoice* events, hinting at a stray “unapplied cash” payment. However, without explicit labeling, the model cannot confirm whether this is a permitted outlier or an erroneous link.  
- The lone *Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice* edge (not in ET–OT but in observed edges) is outside any normative mapping, making it a clear dataquality outlier.  

**Counts and scopes**  
- *Create Order* appearing 100 times with no missing *Validate Order* directly (except the 2 gaps) suggests the abstraction mismatch discussed earlier.  
- The mismatch between *Dispatch Package* (200) and *Delivery* (198) underscores a minor reporting discrepancy that can be remedied by ensuring delivery capture sync, not a process violation.  

**Abstraction danger**  
- Treated as a “directlyfollows” expectation, the *Create Order  Validate Order* relation might be incorrectly flagged; ETOT shows the create event is present, so the model must defer to OCDFG’s openworld clarification.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch  

```python
import pm4py

# Load the observed log (assumed OCEL format)
ocel = pm4py.read_log("observed_log.json")

# Norm definitions (as Python dicts / named tuples)
norm_ocdfg = {
    "N1": {"relation": ("Create Order", "Validate Order"), "status": "mandatory"},
    "N2": {"relation": ("Validate Order", "Close Order"), "status": "typical"},
    "N3": {"relation": ("Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"), "status": "forbidden"},
    "N4": {"relation": ("Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"), "status": "mandatory"},
    "N5": {"relation": ("Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"), "status": "typical"},
    "N6": {"relation": ("Reopen Order", "Close Order"), "status": "unspecified"}
}

norm_otg = [
    ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment", {"status": "typical"}),
    ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer", {"status": "unspecified"}),
    ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment", {"status": "forbidden"}),
    ("Order", "object_cobirth", "RiskCase", {"status": "unspecified"})
]

norm_etot = [
    ("Create Order", "Order", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Add Item", "Item", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Add Item", "Order", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Pack Package", "Package", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Dispatch Package", "Package", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Record Payment", "Payment", {"status": "mandatory"}),
    ("Record Payment", "Invoice", {"status": "mandatory"})
]

# Discover OCDFG
ocdfg_net = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)

# Discover OTG
otg_net = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)

# Discover ET–OT
etot_net = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Conformance checks (raw boolean results)
ocdfg_conformance = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, ocdfg_net, parameters={"return_type": "boolean"})
otg_conformance = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, otg_net, parameters={"return_type": "boolean"})
etot_conformance = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, etot_net, parameters={"return_type": "boolean"})

# ----- Attach normative metadata outside the bare graph structures -----
# Create a classification layer based on norm statuses
def classify_ocdfg(net, norm):
    violations = []
    for rel, status in norm.values():
        # Find if the relation is absent
        edge = (rel[0], '->', rel[1])
        if edge not in net.get_edges():
            if status == "mandatory":
                violations.append(("Missing mandatory", rel))
            elif status == "forbidden":
                violations.append(("Forbidden", rel))
    return violations

# Apply to OCDFG
ocdfg_violations = classify_ocdfg(ocdfg_net, norm_ocdfg)

def classify_otg(net, norm):
    violations = []
    for src, rel, dst, data in norm:
        edge = (src, rel, dst)
        if edge not in net.get_edges():
            if data["status"] == "mandatory":
                violations.append(("Missing mandatory", edge))
            elif data["status"] == "forbidden":
                violations.append(("Forbidden", edge))
    return violations

otg_violations = classify_otg(otg_net, norm_otg)

def classify_etot(net, norm):
    violations = []
    for evt, obj, data in norm:
        edge = (evt, '->', obj)
        if edge not in net.get_edges():
            if data["status"] == "mandatory":
                violations.append(("Missing mandatory", edge))
    return violations

etot_violations = classify_etot(etot_net, norm_etot)

# Combine results (prepend PM4Py raw booleans for completeness)
full_results = {
    "ocdfg_conformance_raw": ocdfg_conformance,
    "ocdfg_violations": ocdfg_violations,
    "otg_conformance_raw": otg_conformance,
    "otg_violations": otg_violations,
    "etot_conformance_raw": etot_conformance,
    "etot_violations": etot_violations,
}

# The raw diagnostic outputs are now stored; additional semanticclassification layer is separate.
print(full_results)
```

- **Mandatory/forbidden metadata** (`norm_ocdfg`, `norm_otg`, `norm_etot`) must be maintained **outside** the discovered graph objects; the code separately flags violations based on these dictionaries.  
- The `classify_*` functions implement the *openworld vs. closedworld* logic: mandatory absence triggers a violation, forbidden absence is impossible (so any observed forbidden edge is flagged), and unspecified edges are ignored for violation detection.  
- The raw Boolean conformance results are preserved for downstream analytical pipelines while the classification layer provides the businesslevel interpretation required by the task.  

## 6. Normcompleteness assessment  

1. **Gap: No explicit rule for “unapplied cash” payments**  
   - *Operational risk*: Financial reconciliation may miss payments, leading to delayed cash flow or overstated receivables.  
   - *Clarification*: Add a normative entry `Payment  Record Payment` with status *typical* and note “unapplied cash” as an allowable variant.  
   - *Classification*: **Typical** (since unapplied cash is a known exception).  

2. **Gap: No coverage for “reopen then close” order pattern**  
   - *Operational risk*: Administrative shortcuts could bypass risk checks or lead to inconsistent order histories.  
   - *Clarification*: State `Reopen Order  Close Order` as **unspecified** (leaving policy open) or, optionally, mark it as **typical** if allowed.  
   - *Classification*: **Unspecified** (currently)  can become **Typical** with policy decision.  

3. **Gap: No rule for “sensor heartbeat” linking to financial objects**  
   - *Operational risk*: System integration errors could produce spurious data, misleading analytics.  
   - *Clarification*: Explicitly forbid any *sensor*type event interacting with *Invoice* objects (`Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice` status *forbidden*).  
   - *Classification*: **Forbidden** (recommended).  

4. **Gap: Ambiguity around “object_cobirth” semantics**  
   - *Operational risk*: Simultaneous birth of related objects may violate dataintegrity constraints (e.g., payment and order cannot be created together in this system).  
   - *Clarification*: Confirm **Order  Payment** object_cobirth is **forbidden** as already noted, but extend to all other object pairs of the same class for completeness.  
   - *Classification*: **Forbidden** (already present, but should be enumerated for all object types).  

5. **Gap: No explicit “object_codeath” expectations**  
   - *Operational risk*: Incomplete lifecycle termination handling may leave orphaned objects, impacting audit trails.  
   - *Clarification*: If termination coupling is required, add a rule such as `Package  Delivery` with status *mandatory* for codeath scenarios.  
   - *Classification*: Could be **Mandatory** or **Typical** depending on business need; currently **unspecified**.  

6. **Gap: Lack of “eventual” vs. “direct” temporal distinction in OCDFG**  
   - *Operational risk*: Interpreting “eventually expected” as “must follow immediately” may penalize legitimate interim actions (review steps).  
   - *Clarification*: Introduce a metastatus *eventual* that explicitly permits intermediate steps, or explicitly list allowed intermediate nodes (e.g., `Create Order  Manual Review  Validate Order`).  
   - *Classification*: **Typical** or **Eventual**; currently **absent**, leading to abstraction mismatches.  

*Overall, the norm is sufficiently detailed for many transactional flows but leaves critical edgecases (unapplied cash, sensor integration, exact birth/death rules, and temporal expectations) open, exposing risks in reconciliation, data integrity, and policy adherence.*
