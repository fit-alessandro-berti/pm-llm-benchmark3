4. **Calibration–Machine–Job serial chain.** CAL-3 is shared by all jobs at TEST-1; the recalibration window 16:35–17:20 head-of-line blocks J6 even though TEST-1 itself is idle.
5. **Tool–Machine–Job blocking.** J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and waits while CNC-2 is partly idle 10:05–10:47.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Every value below is a *plausible* delay under an explicit counterfactual; none is proven causal.

**C1 — J1 synchronization in B1.**
t(J1) = 12:05  08:10 = **3 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: B1 launched at 08:10 (J1's readiness). Cost unknown because J3 would still have its own 3 h 45 min elsewhere.

**C2 — J2 synchronization in B1.**
t(J2) = 12:05  08:25 = **3 h 40 min**. Same counterfactual.

**C3 — FIX-A contribution to J4's wait.**
J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50. CNC-2 is independently idle 10:05–10:47 (42 min). FIX-A is released at 11:40. J4 starts at 11:45. Removing the CNC-2-attributable portion, the FIX-A-induced wait is roughly **53 min** (10:47  11:40). Counterfactual: FIX-A free from 09:50 onward; CNC-2 utilization then becomes the binding factor.

**C4 — J1's shipment consolidation delay.**
J1 packed at 13:30; S1 dispatches at 19:25. t = **5 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: dispatch immediately on packing, or reassign J1 to S2 (which departs 25 min after its last pack).

**C5 — CAL-3 contribution to J6's wait.**
J6 ready at 16:05; CAL-3 in recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 starts at 17:25. t = **1 h 20 min**. Counterfactual: CAL-3 free at 16:05.

**C6 (supplementary) — RACK-7 cleaning gap for B2.**
OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 = 1 h 55 min. Of this, RACK-7 unavailable until 14:35, so ~**1 h 40 min** is plausibly tool-induced. Counterfactual: RACK-7 free at 12:55.

**C7 (supplementary) — J3 synchronization.**
12:05  11:55 = **10 min**. Confirms the batch held for the slowest, not vice versa.

**C8 (supplementary) — J2 policy override vs J5.**
L7 release at 13:50; J5 ready 14:40 (post-release). Hence the lot delay binds J2 but **not** J5 directly; J5's wait is dominated by RACK-7 cleaning and the B2 barrier.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job-flattened DFG.** Visible: clean Stage  Machine Process  Inspect  Pack  Dispatch chain per job. Hidden: that J1 sits 3 h 55 min at the batch barrier with no `Inspect` or `Rework` event to mark it. Misleading conclusion: "J1's cycle time looks normal; the median elapsed times per edge are all under 1.2 h."

**Machine-flattened summary.** Visible: utilization 44–58%, gap statistics. Hidden: that the 42-min CNC-2 idle gap coincides with J4 waiting on FIX-A, and that the 73-min OVEN-1 idle gap coincides with RACK-7 cleaning. Misleading: "OVEN-1 has spare capacity" hides the fact that adding more work to OVEN-1 is futile when RACK-7 is the binding tool.

**Batch flattening.** Visible: the `Batch Cure` event at 12:05. Hidden: how long each member waited inside the barrier. Misleading: a single cure event makes the batch look instantaneous.

**Tool flattening.** Visible (if implemented): RACK-7 cleaning 12:55–14:35 and CAL-3 recalibration 16:35–17:20. Hidden: which jobs were blocked by those intervals. No `Wait for Tool` activity exists in the event log.

**ComponentLot flattening.** Visible: Quarantine Lot at 10:20, Release Lot at 13:50 on L7. Hidden: that J2 was unblocked by an override recorded in a *separate* system and that J5's lifecycle depends on linking rules in the extraction pipeline. Misleading: depending on the pipeline, L7 either binds J2 and J5 tightly or binds neither visibly.

**Shipment flattening.** Visible: Create  Load  Dispatch in 1.1 h. Hidden: 5 h 55 min that J1 sits packed waiting for J4 to complete. The flattening counts *only* shipment-object events and never the time a packed job spends awaiting a partner.

**Why low utilization coexists with long waits.** Utilization measures machine-busy time. Synchronization barriers (batch, lot, tool cleaning, shipment consolidation) generate waiting *outside* any machine's busy window. A machine can be 54% utilized while 46% of wall-clock time is spent waiting for tools, lot releases, or partners. The resource accounting is decoupled from the constraint accounting.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — FIX-A  CNC-2  J4.**
*Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00, before J4 arrives at CNC-2 at 09:50. *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 becomes free at 10:47, FIX-A is still held until 11:40. *Policy constraint:* the system has no `Wait for Tool` activity, so blocking is silent. *Causal speculation:* J4's ~53 min wait after 10:47 is plausibly FIX-A-induced; the 5 min after FIX-A release is plausibly setup.

**Narrative B — L7 quarantine/release  J2, J5.**
*Temporal precedence:* Quarantine Lot at 10:20 precedes any L7-using work. *Resource feasibility:* J2 requires C2 (in L7) and J5 requires C5 (in L7); both are blocked while L7 is quarantined. *Policy constraint:* a supervisor override (in a separate system) unblocks J2 before the official Release Lot at 13:50; J5 has no such override. *Causal speculation:* J2's batch entry at 12:05 (inside B1) is policy-bypassed; J5's readiness at 14:40 *follows* release at 13:50, so the lot mechanism primarily manifests in J2's audit trail rather than in J5's wall-clock wait. This is a case where the policy bypass inflates the apparent effect on J2 and *hides* the intended effect on J5.

**Narrative C — CAL-3  TEST-1  J6.**
*Temporal precedence:* J5 final test 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 ready 16:05. *Resource feasibility:* TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25; CAL-3 unavailable 16:35–17:20. *Policy constraint:* every `Final Test` event requires CAL-3 (mandatory co-occurrence). *Causal speculation:* had CAL-3 been pre-calibrated off-line, J6 would have started near 16:05.

**Narrative D — B1  OVEN-1/RACK-7  J1  S1.**
*Temporal precedence:* J1 ready 08:10; B1 starts 12:05; J1 packed 13:30; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25. *Resource feasibility:* OVEN-1 free, RACK-7 cleaning 12:55–14:35. *Policy constraint:* S1 cannot dispatch until J4 is packed. *Causal speculation:* a staggered B1 (e.g., B1a = J1+J2; B1b = J3) plus a dynamic shipment reassignment could compress J1's flow by 8–9 hours.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Staggered sub-batches for B1.**
- *Target:* batch synchronization.
- *Benefit:* ~3 h 40 min saved on J2; J1 saves similar.
- *Helped:* J1, J2; potentially downstream OVEN-1/RACK-7 cycle.
- *Harmed:* OVEN-1 utilization may drop; cure chemistry may reject partial batches.
- *New cost:* validation, possible quality rework.
- *Perverse metric:* "batch throughput" could fall even if cycle time improves.
- *OCEL evaluation:* compare per-Job `cycle_time` and `Job-Batch wait interval` on J1/J2 before/after, using `pm4py.extract_ocel_features`.

**I2. Decouple RACK-7 cleaning from the OVEN-1 cycle (parallel cleaning station).**
- *Target:* batch-tool-machine triangle.
- *Benefit:* B2 starts ~1 h 40 min earlier.
- *Helped:* J4, J5.
- *Harmed:* capital expenditure; possibly quality if cleaning parameters differ.
- *New cost:* duplicate fixture, cleaning validation.
- *Perverse metric:* cleaning station idle rate.
- *OCEL evaluation:* `pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary` on (Batch, Tool) pairs; track gap between Batch end and next Batch start per Tool.

**I3. Pre-calibrate CAL-3 off the test loop (or duplicate CAL-3).**
- *Target:* calibration tool chain.
- *Benefit:* J6 starts ~1 h 20 min earlier; TEST-1 idle gap closes.
- *Helped:* J5, J6 and any subsequent jobs.
- *Harmed:* technician utilization; capital cost.
- *New cost:* calibration hardware or scheduled technician time.
- *Perverse metric:* "test bay utilization" might appear lower if calibration moves out.
- *OCEL evaluation:* compare Final Test start-to-start intervals and TEST-1 idle gaps on TEST-1 logs.

**I4. Dynamic shipment reassignment (allow J1 to ship via S2 if J4 not ready within X hours).**
- *Target:* shipment consolidation.
- *Benefit:* J1 saves 5 h 55 min.
- *Helped:* J1, customers expecting J1.
- *Harmed:* S1 may ship short (lost revenue/penalty); customer-side consolidation.
- *New cost:* policy/contract rework.
- *Perverse metric:* "consolidation rate" could drop, making dispatch look less efficient even as customer value rises.
- *OCEL evaluation:* Shipment-object life spans from `pm4py.extract_ocel_features` on `Shipment`; measure time from "last pack" to dispatch and from "first pack" to dispatch.

**I5. Strengthen ComponentLot event linkage and audit overrides.**
- *Target:* data-integration artifacts and policy compliance.
- *Benefit:* J2-style overrides become visible; J5's true binding constraint becomes diagnosable.
- *Helped:* audit/compliance; future diagnosis.
- *Harmed:* none to operations.
- *New cost:* logging discipline, ETL work.
- *Perverse metric:* none obvious.
- *OCEL evaluation:* compare `Job-ComponentLot` interaction counts before/after; flag every Quarantine/Release event lacking explicit Job relations.

**I6. Tool scheduling for FIX-A.**
- *Target:* fixture contention.
- *Benefit:* ~50 min saved on J4.
- *Helped:* J4.
- *Harmed:* J3 if pre-emption required.
- *New cost:* scheduling overhead.
- *Perverse metric:* "tool utilization" might rise sharply.
- *OCEL evaluation:* measure J3J4 hand-off interval on FIX-A acquire/release events.

def otype_pair_counts_from_events(ocel):
    obj_type = dict(zip(ocel.objects["ocel:id"],
                        ocel.objects["ocel:type"]))
    c = Counter()
    for ev in ocel.events:
        ros = list(ev.related_objects)
        types = sorted({obj_type[o] for o in ros if o in obj_type})
        for i in range(len(types)):
            for j in range(i+1, len(types)):
                c[(types[i], types[j])] += 1
    return c

ev_counts = otype_pair_counts_from_events(ocel)
print("OCEL event-cooccurrence pair counts:", ev_counts)

# Job-flattened frequency: counts of 'uses' edges only
flat_job = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
flat_jm = sum(1 for ev in flat_job
              if ev["concept:name"] in ("Stage Component",
                                        "Machine Process"))
print("Job-Machine uses from flattening:", flat_jm)

# Note: do NOT infer waiting from machine-event gaps.
# Use the OCEL event-cooccurrence counts AND the per-object

The block computes (a) event-cooccurrence object-type pair counts (every pair of object types that appear together in the *same* event), and (b) Job-flattened Machine-use counts. Differences between these two metrics surface integration choices: events whose object set includes types outside the flattening's source type (e.g., the `Batch Cure` event relates to B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3 — many more than the Job flattening records). Crucially, no waiting is inferred from inter-event gaps; waiting is read from explicit timestamps like "Ready for batch at" and acquire/release pairs.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken Rank 1 (batch synchronization):**
1. B1 actually started at 08:10, not 12:05 (timestamp error in the `Batch Cure` event).
2. J1's 3 h 55 min was actually rework after Inspect, not batch waiting.
3. J1 and J2 were intentionally held to align with shipment S1 (a deliberate shipment-coordination policy, not a synchronization bug).
4. OVEN-1 was actually processing another unrecorded job between 08:10 and 12:05.
5. The "Ready for batch at" timestamps reflect the moment components were physically staged, not when the job could legally enter the batch.
6. B1 was a logical grouping, not a physical synchronization barrier; jobs can leave the oven independently and the `Batch Cure` is just a reporting artifact.

**Observations that would weaken Rank 2 (ComponentLot release coupling):**
1. The Quarantine Lot event at 10:20 was administrative and never physically blocked work.
2. J2's components were pulled from a different physical lot despite the L7 logical grouping.
3. The supervisor override is normal procedure under the company's policy, not an exception.
4. J5's "ready for batch" at 14:40 only occurred after a wholly unrelated rework; L7 release timing is coincidental.
5. The Release Lot at 13:50 was for documentation only; physical release happened earlier.
6. J2 entered B1 only because of unrelated pre-batch staging, not because of an override.

**Additional fields/event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**
1. A `Wait for Tool` or generic `Blocked` activity type with resource references.
2. Tool state events (`Free`, `Reserved`, `Cleaning`, `Quarantine`, `Calibrating`) for FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.
3. Per-component physical availability timestamps (`Component Available`).
4. Per-shipment last-ready signals (a "ready to dispatch" notification with object linkage).
5. OVEN-1 detailed states (Loading / Heating / Curing / Unloading).
6. Supervisor override events with policy-ID and approver, in the same log.
7. Logical-vs-physical lot identifiers.
8. Resource calendars (shifts, breaks, maintenance).
9. Failure/rework root causes linked to objects.
10. Customer-imposed shipment deadlines with object linkage.
11. Per-batch partial-load policies (does the oven accept <N jobs?).
12. Tool-cleaning cycle definitions (duration, prerequisites).

With these, one could distinguish, for example, whether J1's wait was synchronization-induced (then J3's presence matters), shipment-induced (then S1's policy matters), or data-induced (then the extraction pipeline matters).
