# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered front door (policy-encoded routing)

**Redesigned flow.** Intake classifies every claim on three axes already present in the data — customer tier, device value tier, defect clarity — and routes into one of four lanes *before* eligibility:

1. **Premium low-risk accessory:** auto screen  fast approval  dispatch, unchanged (the normative model already grants this R2 exception; this lane is fast *and* compliant).
2. **Standard low-risk accessory:** auto screen with an evidence-completeness check embedded at intake; fast approval only fires once completeness is confirmed (closes the R2 gap for Standard).
3. **High-value device (both tiers):** auto approval disabled above EUR 400 — Manual eligibility review is the default, Fraud/risk review is reserved for anomaly-flagged cases only (protecting the 2 specialists from funnel overload). R1 satisfied by construction.
4. **Unclear defect:** routed out of the generic queue into the evidence lane (developed fully in Redesign C).

**Targets.** The 2,550 AutoFast-approval edges and the 2,300 FastDispatch edges, specifically the high-value subpopulation (35% of volume: 14% Premium + 21% Standard high-value) that the alignment diagnostics flag as dispatching "without risk review."

**Mechanism of improvement.** This is conformance *by construction*, not by policing. Low model precision exists because the process lets any case combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in any order; a routing matrix collapses the variant space so the executed model and the normative model coincide. The economics work because the trade is favorable *only where applied*: moving high-value cases onto the reviewed path should shift their complaint rates from 18–22% toward the 5–6% the reviewed path already achieves, removing reshipments, €410 part errors, and R1/R2 exposure — while leaving the low-risk lanes' 8–13h medians untouched. The rule update is deployable in two weeks.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.**
- High-value median cycle time moves toward the reviewed path's 96–120h. A accepts this *unless* B and C compress the reviewed path; A alone makes high-value slower and righter.
- The known side effect — the updated auto rules push +15–35% more volume into manual review — collides with the 7 reviewers' 82% utilization cliff. A therefore requires a **utilization governor**: staged admission of high-value segments, thin-scope reviews (eligibility/risk decision only, since evidence chasing moves to intake), and pre-committed reviewer capacity. A does not fix the wrong-part root cause, the part-price quote delay, or the evidence loop.

# 3. Redesign B: Right-first-time fulfillment (pre-dispatch verification + part-data integrity)

**Redesigned flow.** Routing stays as-is. Two interventions inside fulfillment:
1. A **lightweight pre-dispatch gate** before Dispatch replacement: verifies part-selection-vs-claim match, device/address match, and evidence completeness (which incidentally enforces R2 at the point of dispatch). 2. **Part-price decoupling for Repair quote:** pre-agreed price bands for the top defect types so quotes no longer wait on part-price confirmation, attacking the 30h mean / 80h p90 quoteapproval edge.

**Targets.** The Dispatch replacement  complaint edge (260 cases, 96h mean / 180h p90, explicitly linked by the temporal profile to incorrect replacement part selection); the repair-quote wait; the 45 Quality check  Rework repair edges.

**Mechanism of improvement.** This redesign changes *where quality is detected*, not who goes where. Today a wrong part is discovered by the customer 96 hours later, triggering a complaint (40h to re-review, 54h to escalation), a reshipment, and a service credit. The gate converts that late, expensive signal into a 6–18h pre-emptive one, at near-zero marginal labor cost because inspector capacity is idle. **Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.**
- Every gated dispatch absorbs 6–18h of latency; B bets that a small certain delay beats a large probabilistic one. It does nothing about R1 (high-value cases can still be auto-approved without review) and only partially enforces R2.
- The gate treats the symptom; unless the part catalog/decision table is cleaned, gate load is permanent. Part-price bands require supplier negotiation and may fail for long-tail parts.

# 4. Redesign C: Evidence-once intake and a structured reopen lane (customer-channel loop elimination)

**Redesigned flow.**
1. **Evidence-once intake:** for unclear-defect/missing-evidence cases (22% of volume) and any case whose first evidence request would fire, evidence is captured *once* at intake — guided portal capture (photos, symptom scripts, device telemetry where the device is connected), a single consolidated request that never asks twice for the same item, and an **assisted fallback channel** (phone/retail) explicitly for low-digital-literacy customers. Cases cannot leave the evidence lane until completeness is confirmed.
2. **Structured reopen lane:** replaces the single 45-minute daily escalation slot. Reopens are auto-triaged into (a) status/resolution complaints handled by frontline under SLA, (b) substantive reopens forced through Manual eligibility review — R3 by construction — with a reviewer rotation, and (c) the escalation board reserved for genuinely board-worthy cases only.

**Targets.** The Request missing evidence  Manual review loop (890 return edges; 14h + 34h per iteration; 26% repeat rate); the 320 post-evidence abandonments; the 140h median cohort; the 120 reopens entering the unscalable board.

**Mechanism of improvement.** This is a demand-side redesign: it removes loop *edges* rather than speeding activities. The portal's evidenced 30–45% reduction in repeat evidence requests compounds — each avoided repeat eliminates a 14h request edge *and* a 34h customer-response edge *and* reviewer re-touch. Structured reopen triage converts R3 from an audit finding into a designed path and stops the board from being the de facto overflow valve. There is a secondary capacity effect: fewer evidence loops free the 7 reviewers, partially offsetting Redesign A's inflow increase — C is the natural companion to A.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.**
- Front-loading evidence makes intake slower for everyone in the cohort; the portal's documented risk — higher abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers — is real and only mitigated, not eliminated, by the assisted channel.
- C does nothing for R1 violations on high-value fast approvals. If upstream quality is unchanged, the reopen lane simply floods. Telemetry capture only works for connected devices.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A.**
- *Utilization cliff:* rule update (+15–35%) plus re-routed high-value volume pushes the 7 reviewers past 82%; queues explode and the reviewed path's 96–120h medians get worse, converting compliance gains into complaint-type "too slow" reopenings. *Bottleneck migration:* eligibility risk moves from policy violations to the reviewer queue, and the 890-edge evidence loop grows as more cases pass through manual review.
- *Misrouting:* defect-type/segment attributes are noisy, so high-value cases slip into the fast lane (compliance exposure persists, now harder to detect because the routing *looks* right) or low-risk cases get slowed into review (Premium service credits above 24h).
- *Fraud-review funnel:* anomaly-flag-only routing can still concentrate high-value flags into the 2 specialists' Monday/campaign bottleneck.

**Redesign B.**
- *Rubber-stamp or serial gate:* under campaign-peak dispatch volume, 2 inspectors can't sustain a serial 6h gate; it degrades into a tick-box and the 96h complaint tail returns.
- *Symptom treatment:* if the part catalog isn't fixed, the gate runs forever at full load and complaints partially migrate to repair-side rework (the Quality check  Rework repair loop). *Bottleneck migration:* from the customer-complaint channel to the gate queue and quality-check rework.
- *Latency breach:* if the gate is applied serially to Premium low-risk dispatches, 8h + up to 18h crosses the 24h service-credit threshold — the redesign must exclude or parallelize that lane.

**Redesign C.**
- *Portal-driven abandonment:* low-digital-literacy customers abandon at intake instead of at the evidence request — abandonment doesn't disappear, it migrates earlier and becomes less visible. *Bottleneck migration:* from the evidence loop to the assisted (phone/retail) channel, which needs its own staffing or it becomes the new queue.
- *Reopen-lane flooding:* if A and B aren't in place, the ~460 reopens keep arriving; the structured lane and its reviewer rotation then compete with A's inflow for the same 7 reviewers — a migration of pressure back onto manual review.
- *Strict completeness gating* can delay dispatch, interacting badly with R2 timelines if not coordinated with B's gate.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 — Instrumentation before intervention.** Define end-to-end metrics with a **post-close observation window**: complaints/reopens within 30 days of *close* (not of dispatch), reshipments, service credits, abandonment, repeat evidence requests, R1/R2/R3 violation counts via re-run conformance checking, reviewer utilization, and median **and p90** cycle time per segment (the p90s — 18h manual entry, 88h evidence return, 180h dispatch-complaint — carry the risk story that averages hide).

**Stage 1 — First pilot: pre-dispatch gate (B-lite) on Standard high-value dispatches only.** Chosen first because it is cheap, reversible, uses idle inspectors, shifts no inflow, and directly tests the causal claim that wrong part selection drives the 96h complaint tail.
- *Metrics:* gate latency distribution (target p90  8h parallel; stop-redesign if p90 > ~18h serial), wrong-part catch rate, 30-day post-close complaint rate vs matched control, cost per case including credits.
- *Stop/go:* go if catch rate is meaningfully non-zero and cohort complaints fall well below the 18% baseline (toward single digits); stop if complaints merely reappear as repair rework or gate catches nothing (theory falsified).

**Stage 2 — Auto-screen rule update (A) on Standard high-value first, not Premium.** Avoids the 24h service-credit exposure while learning; runs with a utilization governor (capped inflow, thin-scope reviews).
- *Metrics:* reviewer utilization and manual-entry queue (adjust or hold if utilization is sustained above the ~82% cliff or entry wait materially exceeds the current 7.5h/18h baseline), R1 violation rate on the routed cohort (target  0), cohort complaint rate vs control, median + p90 cycle time, abandonment.
- *Stop/go:* stop-and-redesign if the queue blows the cliff before complaint rates move; expand to Premium high-value only after the reviewed path holds.

**Stage 3 — Evidence portal + assisted channel (C) on the unclear-defect cohort, with a two-arm design** (portal-only vs portal+assisted) to isolate the portal's abandonment effect.
- *Metrics:* repeat evidence requests (expect 30–45%; stop if < ~15% relative), abandonment in each arm vs control (stop if portal-arm abandonment rises meaningfully above control), cohort cycle time vs the 140h median, downstream reopen volume.
- *Sequencing logic:* C relieves the reviewer load that Stage 2 increases, so C should be at least piloted before A scales beyond one segment.

**Stage 4 — Integration and re-mining.** Combine A (all high-value), B (gate extended across dispatches, parallelized), C (portal + reopen lane at scale); restructure the escalation board last. Re-run process mining: fitting-trace percentage and precision should rise (routing collapses the variant space), R1–R3 violations should approach zero, and the 31%9% pre/post-rework cost gap should narrow — the definitive test that fast-path savings are real.

**Detecting "shifted downstream" rather than fixed:** every stage is judged on post-close complaint incidence, reshipment, rework-repair, escalation load, and abandonment — not on stage-local KPIs like gate throughput or queue length. If a stage improves its local metric while 30-day post-close complaints, rework, or abandonment stay flat, the problem has migrated, and the stage is scored as a failure regardless of its local numbers.
