## 1. Drift diagnosis table

The unit of prediction is one case at its first valid diagnosis-completion event. Let X denote the information available at that instant and Y the eventual breach of the promised resolution date. A realized route or parts wait can explain an error retrospectively even when it was unknown at prediction time.

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January.** Establish a mature reference with 18% breaches, 19% mean risk, AUROC 0.81 and ECE 0.025. V3 already had greater breach risk (0.32) than V1 (0.16) or V2 (0.10).
- **February.** At full maturity, observed and predicted risk both averaged 0.19. AUROC was 0.82 and route shares moved by only one point. This provides a second reference for normal variation.
- **March.** AUROC was 0.81 with observed and predicted risk both at 0.18. The parts-edge median had crept from 28 h in January to 35 h, with no corresponding rise in mature V3 breaches. Use the three months together as baseline.
- **April.** Immediately after migration, mean risk jumped to 0.28 against 0.19 observed, Brier worsened to 0.184 and ECE to 0.110. Incorrect landmarks can change both the inputs and which cases get scored. The simultaneous unmapped-variant spike makes a business rerouting explanation premature.
- **May.** Following the 6 May patch, unknown activities fell to 2%, missing queue to 3%, AUROC recovered to 0.78 and ECE to 0.050. This partial reversal implicates the pipeline. Split the month at deployment and distinguish a corrected raw label from a regenerated prediction prefix.
- **June.** The 1 June policy expanded the relatively low-risk remote route. Its mature breach rate was 0.09, its execution time stayed stable, and the aggregate breach rate fell to 0.15 against 0.20 predicted. AUROC remained 0.79. The route-mix change can explain a lower base rate without requiring a new within-route risk relationship. Check whether diagnosis-time scores captured the policy change before attributing the five-point overprediction to mix alone.
- **July.** After the 10 July supplier change, the parts edge reached 65 h and V3 breaches reached 0.46. Overall predicted and observed risk were close (0.18 and 0.19), so aggregate calibration-in-the-large could mask growing V3 errors. Split by order date or actual supplier exposure as well as case-start date.
- **August.** V3 reached 35% of starts and its breach rate reached 0.64. The parts-edge median doubled from July to 130 h. V1 and V2 breach rates moved by only one point each. Both more parts cases and worse outcomes on that route matter. Even if every pending August case resolved on time, the final rate would be at least 0.93 × 0.28 ≈ 26%. Even if every pending July case breached, July would reach only about 21%. With the reported maturity and breach rates referring to the same starts, the aggregate rise survives censoring.
- **September.** The parts wait reached 148 h and V3 breaches remained elevated at 0.61. Z added a population concentrated in V3, helping raise V3's overall share to 42%. Observed breaches were 0.31 against 0.21 predicted, with AUROC 0.66 and ECE 0.140. Z's slower label maturation complicates attribution of this additional error to source, rurality or model behavior.
- **October.** The parts edge remained slow (155 h), V3 share was 41%, and Z share reached 27%. The outcome API preferentially omitted longer cases. With 55% labeled and 0.16 breaches among them, the rate among all starts can range from about 0.088 to 0.538 before making any assumption about the pending outcomes. Even the direction of the gap against 0.23 mean risk needs reassessment on matched denominators. AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205 and ECE 0.130 are provisional too.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test and comparison | Results that distinguish explanations |
|---|---|
| **Maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtests.** For each start month plot the fraction of all starts labeled by days since start, split by source, path and predicted-risk band. Save original scores, promised dates, outcome occurrence and API arrival times. Replay each cohort at identical ages such as 30, 60 and 90 days, then at verified final ascertainment. Reconcile open cases and closed cases awaiting ingestion separately. | Selection from the backlog predicts delayed long-path/Z curves, an increased occurrence-to-ingestion lag and revisions to the October estimates as labels arrive. A genuine risk change predicts a residual breach or calibration difference at equal follow-up that persists after completion. Equal age alone can leave bias if the API slowed. |
| **Paired canonical replay.** Use identical raw snapshots and frozen model weights. Compare as-served prefixes with replay after renaming, then after deduplication across all sources/dates, then after rebuilding the cache and every dependent feature. Check exactly one timely score per eligible case, missing/late scores, and metrics on the common scored cases plus coverage of all eligible cases. | A pipeline-driven loss predicts migration-specific timestamp/count corrections, restored coverage and substantial April score recovery; inspect both sides of 20 April and 6 May. Genuine conditional drift predicts errors that persist on correctly reconstructed, comparable prefixes. |
| **Calibration and discrimination.** On matched vintages report counts, positives, coverage, reliability curves with common bins, observed-minus-predicted risk, calibration intercept/slope, Brier, ECE and AUROC by month × source × variant. Use uncertainty intervals and flag sparse or single-class cells. Also evaluate score bands and groups actually known at diagnosis. | A mix change can move pooled calibration and AUROC while within-group curves and ranking stay stable. A supplier-related change predicts persistent underprediction within comparable V3 cases. Stable within-group AUROC with a shifted calibration intercept supports recalibration; poorer within-group ranking suggests a score update may be needed. |
| **Variants and DFGs.** On canonical traces compare route shares and diagnosis-outgoing edge frequencies per opportunity, plus counts and median/upper-quantile edge times. Use start cohorts for route outcomes and event/order dates for operational timing. Include pending orders and their elapsed ages alongside observed receipts. | Routing drift predicts a June increase on diagnosis → Remote Fix without a slower remote edge. A parts bottleneck predicts a later shift on Order Part → Part Received, especially for the affected supplier. If April's apparent route changes disappear on canonical replay, they were extraction artifacts. |
| **Conditional risk on the same path.** Within mature V3 compare pre/post July orders, matching or standardizing on diagnosis-time X, original promise slack, device class and source where supported. Estimate a period-by-V3 effect on breaches or frozen-score residuals. Compare unaffected suppliers and V1/V2 trends. Verify original promises, revisions and the breach rule against source records since no definition change was documented. | Persistent excess at like prefixes on the same route, concentrated among exposed orders, supports changed conditional risk. Disappearance after case-mix adjustment supports composition. Attenuation when actual parts wait is added retrospectively is consistent with delay as a mechanism; that wait is downstream and cannot enter the diagnosis-time score. |
| **Transport checks for Z.** Compare concurrent Z and non-Z first. On common support, reweight non-Z to Z's diagnosis-time covariate distribution, checking balance, extreme weights and effective sample size. Also standardize September to a fixed pre-Z mix. Repeat on mature labels; use realized route as a separate retrospective decomposition. | If composition explains the gap, transported mature risks and errors approach Z's, and standardization removes much of the aggregate change. Persistent within-support errors suggest omitted predictors or different conditional risk. A gap that vanishes with follow-up instead points to ascertainment. Where comparable rural/older-device cases are absent, report the unsupported share. |
| **Missingness and lineage.** Trace sampled case IDs through raw event, source schema, canonical activity, diagnosis timestamp, queue join, feature cache and served score. Reconcile hashes/counts by source, date and pipeline version, including cases without a score. Compare raw queue availability with derived missingness. | A broken join/cache predicts available raw values or correct event counts that disagree with served values, with a discontinuity at migration/patch dates. A true intake shift predicts changed raw distributions with faithful transformations. An outcome-only backlog predicts current valid features and scores but delayed label arrivals. |

For a quantitative attribution, use the same eligible, mature denominator for the group shares and rates. For illustration, June's within-route rates applied to June's route shares give 15.8%. Applying those same rates to August's shares gives 19.9%, about four points of mix effect. Then allow the within-route rates to vary. Apply the same standardization to prediction errors. All-start route shares and rates from currently labeled cases need reconciliation before their weighted average can be compared to the published overall rate.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Contain immediately.** Preserve original scores and model, mapping, feature and outcome-feed versions. Gate automated actions on a valid diagnosis prefix. Send cases with missing or ambiguous prefixes to the existing service workflow for review. Mark incomplete-cohort metrics provisional, recover delayed outcomes, and escalate the parts backlog operationally.
2. **Repair features before estimating a correction.** Replay both sources on both sides of the migration and patch, including duplicates before 20 April and stale April counts. Reconcile online and offline features at the exact prediction time. Evaluate both coverage recovery and score performance before replacing the serving pipeline.
3. **Recalibrate if ranking is adequate.** On clean, representative, mature data, test an intercept adjustment for a level shift or an intercept-and-slope correction if reliability curves warrant it. June is a candidate after routing has stabilized. Fit on earlier cohorts and validate forward on untouched cohorts within the intended regime. Require improvement in calibration, Brier and decisions at the operating threshold. Recalibration cannot repair lost ordering or reconstruct absent scores.
4. **Retrain if residuals persist.** If the repaired, recalibrated model continues to miss comparable parts cases or loses within-group ranking, evaluate a challenger with features known at diagnosis, such as an already recorded parts requirement, current supplier backlog as of scoring, original promise slack, travel distance and device age. Validate point-in-time availability. Prefer a pooled update with interactions or partially pooled calibration while Z history is short. A separate segment model needs a segment identifiable at scoring, sufficient mature positives and negatives, and reproducible forward benefit over the pooled model. A final V3 classification available only after ordering cannot select a diagnosis-time model.

**Cohort eligibility.** January–March are clean, mature reference/training candidates for the old regime. Quarantine April despite 100% label maturity until complete replay passes. Admit May only for verified clean intervals or replayed cases with validated lineage. June is fully mature and suitable for the new routing mix after checks. July and August contain valuable supplier-transition evidence; admit reconciled cohorts at adequate follow-up, retaining the change date. September needs representative, mature Z and non-Z outcomes before fitting a population update. Hold October out of supervised fitting, calibration and promotion decisions until the backlog is reconciled. Selecting only rows already labeled from a partial month would preserve the selection bias. Use chronological development and holdout windows; a clean baseline alone cannot validate a model for the later supplier or Z regimes.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Maintain a case-level ledger of eligibility, canonical diagnosis time, as-served prefix/features, score, versions, decision, intervention and eventual outcome, including the date the outcome became available. Preserve as-of snapshots so a backfill cannot overwrite what production actually saw.

| Monitor | Signal and alert dependency |
|---|---|
| Predictive performance | On fixed-vintage and mature cohorts track AUROC, Brier, recall/precision at the operating threshold, and eligible-to-scored coverage. Compare by month, source and diagnosis-time segment with intervals and sample sizes. |
| Calibration | Track mean prediction versus breach rate, reliability curves, intercept/slope and ECE with stable bins. Open a recalibration review when a sustained mature gap survives quality checks and ranking remains adequate. |
| Label maturity | Monitor arrival curves by cohort age, source, path length and score band, pending counts, and outcome-to-ingestion lag. A delay alarm pages the outcome-feed owner, marks affected metrics provisional and gates performance-triggered training. |
| Data quality | Alert on schema/mapping changes, unknown activities, duplicates, missing queues, absent or repeated landmarks, late predictions and cache/serving discrepancies. Route failures to pipeline repair and require replay validation. |
| Process variants | Compare normalized variant shares, unmapped/partial traces and diagnosis-outgoing transitions to a versioned reference. Split by source and completion status. Investigate policy changes with operations. |
| Performance DFG | Monitor transition counts, median and tail waits by order date and supplier, plus open-order ages. The parts edge can trigger a service response during a label outage; the remote edge provides a comparison. |
| Population | Track source proportions, feature distributions, overlap and missingness by source, including Z's rural and device-age mix where available. Compare raw and standardized mature errors before attributing a performance alert to intake. |
| Intervention and deployment | Timestamp routing policies, supplier incidents, thresholds, model/mapping releases, overrides and expedited service. Record who received an intervention and its outcome. Use a shadow challenger and a controlled rollout with rollback criteria because successful intervention can change the label being predicted. |

Route each alarm to its owner. A sustained decline on mature, quality-checked data initiates error analysis and a challenger evaluation. Promote a replacement only after forward validation. Set alert limits using baseline variability, volume and operational cost, with minimum support for segment metrics.

The sketch assumes an immutable as-of export, globally consistent case IDs, UTC timestamps, and a stable event ID within source and case where supplied. Rows tied on timestamp must have reliable source order. It retains events with missing IDs and counts them for investigation. Cross-source replicas need a verified identity mapping. Resource counts describe recorded assignments only. The cutoff is exclusive.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_evidence(cutoff):
    C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
    raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", dtype="string").rename(
        columns={"case_id": C, "activity": A, "timestamp": T}
    )
    if raw[[C, T]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Missing case ID or timestamp")
    raw[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw[T], utc=True, errors="raise")
    end = pd.to_datetime(cutoff, utc=True)
    raw = raw.loc[raw[T] < end].copy()
    raw[A] = raw[A].fillna("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").replace(
        {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
    )
    if "org:resource" not in raw:
        raw["org:resource"] = raw.get("resource", pd.NA)
    for col in ("source", "event_id"):
        if col not in raw:
            raw[col] = pd.NA
    key = ["source", C, "event_id"]
    identified = raw[key].notna().all(axis=1)
    conflicts = raw.loc[identified].groupby(key)[[A, T]].nunique()
    if conflicts.gt(1).any().any():
        raise ValueError("Conflicting event identity; reconcile with source")
    duplicate = identified & raw.duplicated(key)
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw.loc[~duplicate].copy(),
                                 case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)
    starts = log.loc[log[A].eq("Case Created")].groupby(C)[T].min()

    def edge_summary(frame):
        if frame.empty:
            return {}
        freq, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(frame)
        median_sec, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            frame, perf_aggregation_key="median"
        )
        return {"edge_counts": freq, "edge_median_seconds": median_sec}

    report = {}
    for lo in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS", tz="UTC"):
        if lo >= end:
            break
        hi = lo + pd.offsets.MonthBegin()
        fmt = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
        events = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, lo.strftime(fmt), hi.strftime(fmt),
                                         mode="events")
        events = events.loc[(events[T] >= lo) & (events[T] < hi)]
        ids = starts.index[(starts >= lo) & (starts < hi)]
        cohort = log.loc[log[C].isin(ids)]
        closed_ids = cohort.loc[cohort[A].eq("Case Closed"), C].unique()
        closed = cohort.loc[cohort[C].isin(closed_ids)]
        in_month = (raw[T] >= lo) & (raw[T] < hi)
        report[lo.strftime("%Y-%m")] = {
            "as_of_exclusive": end, "calendar_events": len(events),
            "duplicates_removed": int((in_month & duplicate).sum()),
            "unkeyed_events": int((in_month & ~identified).sum()),
            "unknown_events": int(events[A].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").sum()),
            "active_cases_without_start": events.loc[~events[C].isin(starts.index), C].nunique(),
            "calendar_edges": edge_summary(events),
            "calendar_temporal": pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
                events, grouper_freq="MS").to_dict("records") if len(events) else [],
            "started_cases": len(ids), "closed_by_cutoff": len(closed_ids),
            "start_cohort_edges": edge_summary(cohort),
            "closed_variants": pm4py.get_variants(closed) if len(closed) else {},
        }
    return report
```

Event-calendar summaries describe activity inside the month and omit transitions crossing a window boundary. Case-start cohorts retain the visible history of each start through the cutoff, including later receipts. Their edges and closed-case variant counts can change as open cases finish. Divide closed-variant counts by closed cases for a conditional share and report the open count alongside it. Re-extract snapshots at equal follow-up for comparisons; an event timestamp alone cannot reconstruct when a late event first became available.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Consequence and mitigation |
|---|---|
| Retrain on October's available labels or encode pending outcomes as on time. | Long, high-risk paths can be downweighted just when they need attention. Recover the backlog, verify outcomes against source records and evaluate at fixed vintages. |
| Calibrate around April's pipeline bug. | An offset can hide the average error while wrongly timed or absent scores persist. Repair the landmark, deduplication and cache first; compare original and replayed scores and coverage. |
| Treat June's mix change as permanent conditional deterioration. | A global adjustment can push already high-risk V3 cases in the wrong direction. Standardize to fixed route weights and validate within-group calibration under the routing policy. |
| Explain August entirely by route shares or Partner Z. | V3 breaches doubled relative to baseline before Z arrived. Inspect same-path residuals and supplier exposure, address delayed orders and test a supplier-aware challenger on mature outcomes. |
| Interpret Z's crude breach rate as proof of unfair scoring. | Different case mix, label delay and intervention access can produce a rate gap; they can also conceal a harmful decision disparity. Compare mature calibration, threshold errors, interventions and outcomes at comparable risk and support, with uncertainty for each group. |
| Use realized route, receipt delay or later data corrections as live features. | Retrospective performance will overstate what dispatch can achieve immediately after diagnosis. Reconstruct as-of prefixes and require online/offline agreement before rollout. |
