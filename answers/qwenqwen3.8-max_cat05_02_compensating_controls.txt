## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 should not be reduced to “Prepare_Payment resource  Release_Payment resource.” The underlying control objective is to prevent a single human actor from causing an erroneous or fraudulent payment to become irrevocable without an independent check. The table below decomposes that objective into threat scenarios and maps the compensating controls to prevention, detection, correction, or failure modes.

| Objective / threat scenario | What it means | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Prevent unilateral erroneous or fraudulent payment initiation/authorization | One person prepares and releases a payment without anyone else challenging the payee, amount, purpose, or bank details. | Prevents only covered technical anomalies: duplicate invoice, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank change, operator limit, basic PO match. Does not validate intent or commercial reasonableness. | Prevents if an independent manager reviews and can block before release or before irrevocable settlement. Rubber-stamp approval is weak. | Detects after release. Becomes partly corrective only if performed before the recall window closes. | ED-1 permits single-operator action only if strict safeguards exist. If conditions fail, ED-1 does not compensate. |
| 2. Prevent bank-account redirection, sanctions exposure, duplicate payment, or limit breach | Fraudster or error changes bank details, submits duplicate invoice, or exceeds authority. | Strong for covered checks, especially duplicate, sanctions, bank-change proximity, and operator limits. ## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating-control package should be evaluated by its ability to achieve the control objective at the relevant time, not merely by whether some control event appears in the log. The following dimensions are used:

- **Timing**: Did the control act before the payment became irrevocable? Controls before release are strongest; controls before settlement are potentially preventive if settlement can be held; controls after settlement are mainly detective.
- **Independence**: Was the reviewing person truly separate from the preparer/releaser, free from conflict, and not using shared or borrowed credentials?
- **Coverage**: Did the control cover the relevant threat? AV-2 covers bank-change, duplicate, sanctions, and limit issues, but not receipt of goods or collusion.
- **Overrideability**: Was a warning overridden? By whom? Was the override independent and justified? Self-override or conflicted override greatly weakens the control.
- **Recoverability**: Could the payment still be stopped or recalled when the defect was detected? Audit within the recall window has corrective value; audit after the window is mostly detective.
- **Evidence quality**: Are there logs, tickets, signed checklists, manager review notes, receipt evidence, and token records? Missing evidence reduces confidence.
- **Collusion resistance**: Are resource pairs rotated? Are workstations/devices separate? Are repeated pairings explained by approved organizational design?

Classification standard used below:

- **Fully equivalent / OBJECTIVE_MET**: The package provides a substantive preventive barrier comparable to the four-eyes objective, usually because an independent human gate acts before release or before irrevocable settlement, AV coverage is clean and not overridden, emergency conditions are satisfied, and timely audit/recall capacity exists.
- **Partially equivalent / OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET**: Important controls operate, but there are formal or substantive gaps. The package reduces risk meaningfully but is not fully equivalent to a clean four-eyes control or a fully valid ED-1 exception.
- **Merely mitigating**: Controls reduce likelihood or impact but cannot prevent the main threat before irrevocability. Examples include late ratification, late audit, or AV checks that do not cover the dominant risk.
- **Ineffective / OBJECTIVE_FAILED**: Critical controls are absent, overridden by a non-independent party, conflicted, too late, or unable to address the dominant threat. The control package does not materially reduce risk.
- **LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION**: The payment was blocked or canceled before funds left the company. The four-eyes objective for completed payments is not triggered, although the attempt may still warrant root-cause analysis.

“Same expected loss” is not the only standard because control quality must be assessed before the outcome is known. A payment that happens to be legitimate despite missing controls is a near miss, not proof of control sufficiency. Expected loss also ignores: irreversibility, independence, deterrence, evidence quality, fraud incentives, collusion risk, and the risk that the same weakness will later produce a large loss. Therefore, AP-208 can be a serious control failure even though the payment was legitimate, while AP-205 can be a successful preventive outcome despite same-user attempt behavior.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

The classification below uses the following semantics: a control is **preventive** if it can stop a loss before the payment becomes irrevocable; **detective** if it identifies a defect after the relevant event; and **corrective** if it can still stop, recall, or recover funds. A manager action after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement is treated as **hybrid**: it is detective with respect to the release decision because the release has already occurred, but preventive with respect to loss if the manager has actual authority and system capability to stop settlement. If the manager cannot stop settlement, or merely rubber-stamps after the fact, the action should be treated as weak detective/ratification.

| Control / mechanism | Primary timing | Preventive strength | Detective strength | Corrective strength | Key limitations / failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FEP-1 nominal segregation: different preparer and releaser | Before release | Strong when the releaser is independent and substantive; prevents one person from unilaterally completing payment | Weak by itself; only shows that two IDs acted | None by itself | Fails if users collude, share workstation/credentials, or act as a routine pair without independence |
| AV-2 automated validation | Before release | Strong for covered checks: duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank change, operator limit, basic PO match | Can produce warnings that indicate risk | None directly; can support later investigation | Does not validate receipt or commercial reasonableness; cannot detect collusion; can be overridden or unavailable |
| MG-4 independent manager approval before release | Before release | Strong if manager is independent, reviews invoice/bank details/receipt, and can block release | Moderate; review may reveal anomalies | None directly, but prevents settlement if used before release | Fails if manager is conflicted, rubber-stamps, lacks evidence, or approves after irrevocability |
| MG-4 manager approval after release but before settlement | Between release and settlement | Hybrid: preventive for loss if settlement can be held and manager can stop it | Detective for the release decision because release already occurred | Can stop settlement if defect found before settlement | Weak if settlement is automatic, manager cannot hold payment, or approval is perfunctory |
| MG-4 manager ratification after settlement | After irrevocability | None for loss prevention | Detective/ratification only | Usually none, except exceptional recovery | Creates false comfort if treated as approval; too late to prevent loss |
| RA-7 retrospective audit | After release/settlement | None by itself | Strong if timely and thorough; can find defects, patterns, and missing evidence | Partly corrective only if performed before recall/stopping window closes | Becomes merely historical after recall window; cannot compensate for missing preventive controls |
| ED-1 emergency single-operator permission | Exception framework around release | Not a control by itself; permits reduced segregation only when safeguards are present | Requires audit and manager evidence | Depends on RA-7 timing and recall window | Fails if any condition is missing; token after prepare, AV outage, bank change, high amount, late manager, or late audit invalidate the exception |

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

The following recommendations distinguish **alerts** from **proof of misconduct**. An alert justifies investigation; it does not by itself prove fraud or error.

| Metric / monitoring rule | Recommended evidence to collect | Alert condition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-user prepare/release rate | Case ID, preparer, releaser, timestamps, ED-1 token status, amount, vendor risk | Any same-user completed payment outside a fully satisfied ED-1 exception; elevated alert for high amount, new vendor, or recent bank change | Alert indicates nominal FEP-1 violation; not proof of loss or fraud |
| ED-1 exception completeness | Token start time, amount, vendor age, bank-change date, AV-2 result, manager approval time, settlement time, audit completion time | Any missing ED-1 condition: token not before prepare, amount  EUR 100,000, vendor < 180 days, bank change < 7 days, AV override/outage, manager after settlement, audit late | Exception failure; requires root-cause analysis and possibly retroactive risk assessment |
| AV-2 override rate | Warning type, overridden checks, override credential owner, user who performed override, justification, manager confirmation | Any self-override; override of bank-change plus operator-limit warning; override by non-independent manager; repeated overrides by same user | High-risk alert, especially when the override user is also preparer/releaser; not proof of misconduct |
| Late manager approval | Manager ID, independence, approval timestamp, release timestamp, settlement timestamp, review notes | Manager approval after release; stronger alert if after bank settlement; strongest if after recall window | Late approval is detective/ratification, not preventive; after settlement it provides little loss prevention |
| Audit before recall window | Settlement timestamp, recall deadline, audit start/end timestamps, auditor independence | Audit not completed before settlement plus 24 hours, or before the bank’s actual recall deadline if shorter | Late audit reduces corrective capacity; alert should trigger recovery feasibility check |
| Repeated resource pairs | Rolling 30-day count of preparer/releaser pairs, case volume, workstation/device IDs, rotation policy | Pair count above an organizational threshold, e.g., more than 10 paired payments in 30 days or a high share of one user’s payments; alert stronger if same workstation | Repeated pairing is an independence-risk indicator, not proof of collusion |
| Shared workstation/device usage | Device ID, IP address, terminal ID, login session, physical location | Prepare and release performed from the same device/session, or paired users consistently share devices | Shared device weakens segregation; requires operational explanation or remediation |
| Bank-change proximity | Vendor bank-change timestamp, payment timestamp, bank-ownership verification, vendor master-data change log | Payment within 7 days after bank-account change; stronger alert if combined with warning override, new vendor, or high amount | High fraud-risk indicator; independent verification should be mandatory |
| Control outages | AV-2 availability logs, manual checklist, signed approvals, fallback procedure | Release during AV-2 outage without signed manual checklist and independent secondary approval; especially high amount or recent bank change | Outage does not remove control objective; missing fallback evidence is a control failure |
| Manager conflict of interest | Org hierarchy, performance-rating relationships, conflict-of-interest declarations | Manager approving for direct report or otherwise conflicted person | Conflict undermines MG-4 independence; alert requires reassignment or additional review |
| Receipt/commercial evidence | Invoice, service report, goods receipt, PO match, manager review notes | High-value payment, new vendor, or emergency payment without receipt evidence | AV-2 pass is insufficient for receipt; missing evidence is a substantive control gap |

PM4Py behavior that cannot be guaranteed identically across versions:

- `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` may differ in whether it returns an `EventLog`, a `DataFrame`, or another filtered object. The helper function above handles both where possible.
- The filter may differ in whether it treats the presence of both activities by the same resource as a violation or returns the complementary set. The manual same-user check is included as a version-independent cross-check.
- The parameter name and acceptance of `resource_key` may vary. The code first tries passing `resource_key="org:resource"` and falls back if that raises `TypeError`.
- Case-ID attributes may be stored as `case:concept:name` or `concept:name` after conversion. The helper checks both.
- PM4Py’s four-eyes filter generally reasons over recorded activity/resource pairs and will not by itself understand `Attempt_Release`, `Automated_Validate_Block`, settlement irrevocability, emergency tokens, manager independence, or recall windows. Those judgments require the derived flags and the case-facts table.
