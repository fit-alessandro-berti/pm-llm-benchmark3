## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The estimands below distinguish interventions on the **policy response** to a flag from interventions on the flag itself. Setting a stored field to zero is not assumed to be a neutral label intervention: if the flag still triggers priority routing or mandatory review, it changes a policy bundle.

- **P0:** Current policy bundle.
- **P1:** Hide the flag label from human interfaces while leaving the risk score, other information, and already-triggered machine routing unchanged. This isolates human salience. If the score and threshold remain visible, humans may still infer the flag.
- **P2:** Retain priority triage but remove automatic mandatory manual review for flagged cases; discretionary or safety/compliance-triggered review remains.
- **P3:** Retain current routing and add incremental, protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases.
- **P4:** P4-C changes a prespecified escalation rule \(q\) while holding senior capacity fixed; P4-K adds senior capacity while holding \(q\) fixed. Criteria and capacity changes must not be combined without a factorial design.

Natural direct/indirect effects are not used because the flag is nearly determined by risk score, exposure and mediator confounding are unresolved, and there is no pre-policy counterfactual. Instead, I use **interventional dynamic-policy effects**, **controlled direct effects (CDEs)** for isolated pathway interventions, **interventional-indirect-effect analogues (IIEs)** for policy-induced shifts in mediator occurrence, and **separable effects** for own-case versus queue spillovers.

Interference is central: a case routed to review changes queue occupancy and therefore other cases’ waits; escalation similarly spills into shared senior work. Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding arises because the flag changes review occurrence, queue state, resource assignment, escalation, and possibly the decision. Conditioning on these descendants can block mediated paths or open collider paths. Treatment–confounder feedback can arise if queue pressure, altered by earlier flagged demand, changes later review, escalation, and service. Reviewer salience and senior capacity are also unobserved here. Thus arrows from the flag represent policy responses, not intrinsic causal effects. These data cannot recover natural direct or indirect effects; interventional contrasts require fixed routing rules, adequate overlap, and explicit interference assumptions.

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Priority acceleration remains for flagged cases; overall system congestion remains | High flagged review demand and shared senior bottleneck persist | Reference; observed flagged cycle is descriptively longer, not causally estimated | High review delays may encourage unsafe closure or excessive escalation; actual quality is unknown | Existing review and senior queues affect all cases | Descriptive only; no counterfactual | Retain solely as the comparator while collecting prospective data |
| P1 | Little direct triage effect if machine routing is unchanged; human behavior may alter discretionary routing | No direct capacity relief; fewer or more discretionary reviews could change queue demand | Likely near zero direct effect; direction depends on reviewer response | Hidden salience may reduce attention to risk; score may still reveal the flag | Any behavioral response is shared across the queue | Weak for total effects; plausible for salience mechanism | Use only as a randomized diagnostic, not as a safety intervention |
| P2 | Priority triage remains; no change to triage timing | Fewer automatic reviews should reduce manual demand, although voluntary review may replace some | Likely shorter cycle for flagged cases and possibly shorter unflagged waits; effect is not identified | Skipped review may miss risk; compliance rules must remain | Can free capacity, but reviewers may add discretionary review and shift work to escalation | Moderate for routing volume; weak for total and quality effects | Preferred guarded pilot with blinded adjudication and safety stopping rules |
| P3 | Priority triage remains unchanged | Protected capacity should shorten flagged manual waits; senior queue may become the new bottleneck | Likely shorter manual stage, but uncertain total-cycle effect if capacity is displaced | Preserves mandatory review, though staffing quality and bottlenecks matter | Incremental capacity limits displacement; a nonincremental implementation would crowd out other work | Moderate queue arithmetic; weak total and quality evidence | Scale only after measuring own-case benefit, spillovers, and senior-stage effects |
| P4 | No direct triage effect | Criteria changes affect senior demand; added senior capacity reduces senior waiting but not manual waiting | Tightening criteria may shorten cycle but can reduce quality; capacity expansion is less likely to do so | Criteria changes have a direct quality/compliance trade-off; capacity expansion need not | Added senior capacity can displace other senior work; tighter criteria may move work downstream | Low until criteria, capacity, and quality are specified | Test P4-C and P4-K separately; use capacity before criteria-only acceleration |

**Limited P2 pilot.**

- **Eligibility:** Flagged arrivals at participating sites for eight weeks, excluding prespecified statutory/compliance reviews, explicit senior referrals, and initially High-complexity cases. Priority triage remains; a pilot-eligible case can still receive discretionary review.
- **Assignment:** Cluster-randomize reviewer-team-day blocks 1:1 to P2 versus current P0, stratified by baseline manual backlog, complexity, and site. Cluster assignment prevents one arm’s review demand from contaminating the other.
- **Capacity guardrail:** Do not reduce baseline staffing. Ring-fence enough manual-review capacity for control-arm and nonpilot demand, with at least 20% of shift capacity retained as a safety buffer. Do not transfer pilot work into protected nonpilot slots.
- **Primary outcome:** Arrival-to-close time among flagged pilot cases.
- **Secondary outcomes:** Triage time, review occurrence, manual queue wait and service, escalation occurrence and senior wait, residual-to-close, P90 cycle time, unflagged queue waits, reviewer overrides, blinded major decision error, and 30-day adverse outcome.
- **Minimum data:** Assignment and display logs; score, complexity, and load; all stage start/end timestamps; reviewer/team/shift; queue occupancy; decision and escalation reasons; blinded re-adjudication; adverse outcomes; synchronized clock metadata.
- **Stopping:** Pause for a credible serious safety signal or materially higher major decision error than control; stop if median unflagged manual wait rises over 10% or senior waiting over 20% from the pre-pilot baseline for two consecutive days; operational futility if 50 evaluable cases per arm show less than 10% cycle improvement; also stop for event-data completeness below 95%.

cases["escalation_occurred"] = cases["escalation_start"].notna()

manual_rows = cases["manual_done"].notna()

last_completed.loc[escalation_rows] = (
    cases.loc[escalation_rows, "escalation_done"]

cases["residual_to_close"] = (
    cases["close"] - last_completed
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
cases["total_cycle"] = minutes_between("arrival", "close")

    "triage_service",

def add_event(case_id, concept, start, end, resource=""):

            {
                "case": case_id,

for flag_value, label in [(1, "FLAGGED"), (0, "UNFLAGGED")]:

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If a modest unmeasured risk factor explains both mandatory-review selection and long service times, the apparent downstream penalty of review may be case mix rather than a harmful policy response.
2. **Queue interference:** If protected capacity mainly displaces other cases, or if shared-queue demand rather than priority routing explains the waits, P3’s apparent own-case benefit would not represent a system-level improvement.
3. **Decision quality:** If blinded adjudication shows that removing mandatory review or tightening escalation criteria produces materially more harmful approvals or missed risks, the P2 pilot and cycle-time recommendation would reverse.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If starts and completions are batch-posting times, retrospective activity times, or affected by clock drift, the apparent triage acceleration and downstream queue delays may be measurement artifacts.
5. **Positivity or overlap:** If prospective data show no comparable flagged and unflagged cases near the score threshold, or effects concentrated in unsupported complexity/load strata, these 20 cases cannot support extrapolation to the target population.
6. **Reviewer behavior:** If hiding the flag causes reviewers to request more discretionary reviews or escalate more often—or if P2 leads to similar substitution—the assumed P1 neutrality or P2 queue relief would fail.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** In these 20 post-policy cases, flagged arrivals wait 14.4 minutes to triage versus 148.8 minutes for unflagged cases, while their mean total cycle is 990.6 versus 484.2 minutes. All flagged cases receive manual review, versus 4/10 unflagged cases; 6/10 flagged cases escalate versus 1/10 unflagged cases. Conditional manual waiting is much longer for flagged cases, 390 versus 112.5 minutes, while residual closing time is similar.

**Plausible mechanism.** The pattern is consistent with direct operational acceleration—priority triage—and with indirect delay from additional review demand, shared queues, and scarce senior escalation. It does not show that the flag itself causes either acceleration or delay. The flag is nearly determined by risk score, and complexity, imperfect risk, arrival load, reviewer assignment, and post-flag routing remain entangled.

**Unidentified.** These data cannot identify natural direct or indirect effects, the effect of hiding the display, the effect of mandatory versus discretionary review, or decision-quality tradeoffs. “Approve/Reject” is not a quality label, and no pre-policy or randomized counterfactual exists. Queue interference also violates no-interference assumptions.

**Next evidence.** A cluster-randomized, guarded P2 pilot—retaining priority triage but randomizing removal of automatic mandatory review—can estimate occurrence and queue pathways while measuring reviewer behavior, queue state, quality, and spillovers. P2 should advance only with blinded adjudication, fixed staffing, and prespecified safety stopping rules; P3 is the follow-up if protected capacity, rather than review occurrence, dominates.
