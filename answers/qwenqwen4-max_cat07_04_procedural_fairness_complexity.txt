# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

## 2.1 Define separate estimands (do not blend them)

1. **Total disparity** — group difference adjusting only for plausibly exogenous, non-mediated need: `household_size_band`, `region`, month, and *validated* eligibility-relevant facts. Excludes `risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`, `channel`.
2. **Conditional-on-need disparity** — additionally adjusts for the three suspect mediators. This is the preliminary model's estimand; it answers "given the same recorded risk/completeness/channel, are groups treated differently?"
3. **Within-path treatment disparity** — comparison inside an exact route (e.g., V1) among matched cases.
4. **Routing-compliance disparity** — group difference in *mandated-step delivery* and *non-mandated-step imposition*, conditional on the trigger.
5. **Waiting-time disparity** — group difference in agency-controlled queue time, decomposed by edge.

Reporting (1) and (2) side by side is essential: if the gap shrinks a lot when suspect mediators enter, that is evidence the mediators are *carrying* the disparity, not evidence that it is legitimate.

## 2.2 Matching / stratification variables

- **Exact-match keys (need triple):** `needs_language_support` × `accommodation_requested` × `risk_score_band` (plus `documentation_completeness` as a secondary key). Use coarsened exact matching or stratification rather than a single propensity score, because group and need are strongly correlated.
- **Overlap / positivity diagnostics:** support steps have almost no Group A overlap (A: 2% language  72 cases, 4% accommodation  144 cases). Those small cells are still analytically precious: compare **A-with-language-need vs B-with-language-need** and **A-with-accommodation vs C-with-accommodation**. If A's need-holders wait ~3.8d / ~4.7d too, the mechanism is *need-plus-capacity*; if they wait materially less, the mechanism is *group-linked resource allocation*. Report these as exploratory (small n, wide CIs).
- **Adjustment set for within-stratum models:** region, month/cohort, household size, prior-case count, channel; caseworker and region **cluster-robust SEs** (Group B's effective sample is closer to ~10 staff clusters than 1250 independent cases).
- **Do not adjust away** the support steps themselves when estimating total burden — that would define the problem out of existence.

## 2.3 Path / variant comparison

- Compute **activity-level incidence conditional on the trigger**, not variant shares: P(Language Support Contact | language need), P(Accommodation Review | request), P(Fraud Review | mandated band), P(Supervisor Review | no mandate). Variants are mutually exclusive strings and hide co-occurrence (a case needing both interpreter and fraud review appears in neither V4 nor V3).
- Compare **matched variant distributions** (within need triple × channel × region): does B still get V1 (5.8d happy path) less often than A when need is identical?
- Test **mandate-absent escalation**: Supervisor Review and Request Missing Documents among cases with complete documentation, low risk, no support need. This is the cleanest discrimination-relevant contrast available.
- Handle **right-censoring**: slower groups have more still-open cases at extraction, which truncates variants and biases durations. Report (a) closed-cohort results, (b) survival/competing-risk estimates on all cases with administrative censoring, and (c) an "activation-given-approval" rate.

## 2.4 Resource-assignment comparison

- Handover-of-work networks per group: handovers per case, **boomerang handovers** (general  specialist  general), and specialist touch share.
- Working-together and activity-based-resource-similarity networks to identify de facto roles; organizational-role discovery to confirm the **specialist/supervisor role overlap** for Group C.
- **Caseworker-composition decomposition:** how much of B's/C's adjusted gap is explained by *which* staff handle them (caseworker fixed effects / leniency effects) versus by differential treatment within the same worker? A worker-level "approval propensity adjusted for case mix" separates assignment-driven from conduct-driven disparity.
- Load proxies: concurrent open cases per worker per day, cases per worker per month, queue length at assignment. Acknowledge these are coarse (§5).

## 2.5 Waiting-time decomposition

Split each case's duration into: **agency queue time**, **agency service time**, **applicant-response time**, and **support-step time**. Then:

- **Edge-level decomposition:** for each DFG edge, compute per-group waiting quantiles (p50/p90), and attribute the total gap to (i) *path-mix* (groups take different routes) vs (ii) *within-edge delay* (same route, slower). A Kitagawa/Oaxaca-style decomposition of cycle time gives an interpretable split: e.g., "of B's +5.6d raw gap, X days from route composition, Y days from slower edges."
- **Counterfactual for `n/a` cells:** you cannot compare Language Support  Eligibility to a Group A edge that does not exist. Instead compare to the *functionally equivalent* elapsed segment for non-support cases (Completeness  Eligibility), and to policy targets (currently absent), and across need-holders in different groups/regions. Label this a structural, not a within-path, comparison.
- **Negative-control edges:** Decision Draft  Notification differs only 0.5 / 0.8 / 0.7 / 0.5d. If B were uniformly "harder" or globally deprioritized, this edge should also be much slower. Its near-parity argues for **localized capacity bottlenecks**, not generalized disadvantage — and it is a useful falsification test for the "case complexity explains everything" story.
- **Clock separation caveat:** the Request Missing Documents interval mixes agency lag-to-ask, applicant lag-to-respond, and agency lag-to-act. Without a "Documents Received" event these cannot be separated; treat the current 0.4 vs 1.6d figure as suggestive only.

## 2.6 Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates

- **Mediator-exclusion ladder:** report the disparity with (a) no suspect mediators, (b) + channel, (c) + documentation, (d) + risk band. Monotone attenuation localizes where disparity is transmitted.
- **Mismeasurement simulation:** assume `documentation_completeness` under-rates B/C by  (e.g., 5–20pp misclassification) and `risk_score_band` over-rates D by ; re-estimate. Report the  at which the adjusted disparity vanishes or doubles.
- **Bias-strength bounds:** E-values or Rosenbaum-type bounds for the residual approval gaps — how strong would an unmeasured confounder have to be to explain 5.5pp?
- **Yield / calibration tests (the strongest justification test):** substantiation rate of Fraud Review by group; overturn rate of Supervisor Review by group; share of document requests that actually changed the eligibility determination. Extra scrutiny with low yield is burden without legitimate payoff. These fields do not exist yet (§5) — request them.
- **Alternative measures:** replace subjective completeness with an objective document-count checklist; replace risk band with its audited components.

## 2.7 Subgroup checks

- Region × channel × group cells; the two regions with boomerang handovers and supervisor backlog are a natural **difference-in-differences** setting (regions with vs without bilingual capacity; before/after any staffing change).
- Intersectional cells (B  accommodation, C  minority language, D  in-person) with explicit small-n suppression and CIs.
- Multiplicity control (pre-register a small primary set: adjusted approval, adjusted cycle time, mandate delivery, non-mandated escalation, support-step wait; FDR for exploratory cells).

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

Each mechanism is paired with a distinguishing signature so the hypotheses are separable rather than rhetorical.

1. **Legitimate need-based routing colliding with unequal capacity (structural/indirect disadvantage).** Mandated steps exist; the pools serving them are ~10× smaller; utilization is high; waits grow nonlinearly. *Signature:* delays concentrated on support edges; near-parity on the negative-control edge (DecisionNotify, observed); A's small need-holder cells wait similarly to B/C; waits track daily queue length; no conditional excess in *decision* behavior once queues are accounted for.
2. **Biased measurement of mediators generating extra loops.** `documentation_completeness` is recorded by staff and may under-rate minority-language or accommodated applicants; `risk_score_band` may encode historical contact. This produces "legitimate-looking" triggers for document requests and escalation. *Signature:* adjusted Request Docs excess persists at equal recorded completeness; objective document counts differ less than subjective ratings; document requests rarely change the eligibility determination (low yield); disparity attenuates sharply when the mediator enters the model.
3. **Handover-induced information loss.** Boomerang transfers drop context, causing re-requests, contradictory notes, rework, and avoidable denials. *Signature:* rework and repeat document requests rise with handover count *within* strata; the two affected regions show the effect; boomerang cases have worse outcomes than specialist-owned cases with identical need.
4. **Discretionary escalation / statistical discrimination / implicit bias.** Supervisors and caseworkers treat language need or accommodation as a proxy for unreliability, adding scrutiny not required by policy. *Signature:* Supervisor Review excess concentrated among mandate-absent cases; low overturn yield of those reviews; worker-level heterogeneity in escalation rates unexplained by case mix; disparity persists after queue and handover controls.
5. **Workload-driven decision shortcuts.** Overloaded staff satisfice: default to denial or "request more documents" to defer work. Not animus, but produces the same disparate effect because load is unequally distributed. *Signature:* denial/doc-request rates rise with daily open caseload; effects strongest at month-end or peak intake; within-worker variation over time explains part of the between-group gap.
6. **Communication and explanation failure.** Decisions are correct but unexplained (or untranslated), producing appeals, rework, and non-take-up rather than genuine eligibility differences. *Signature:* high appeal volume with **low overturn** rates; V6 concentration; rework without eligibility change; activation-given-approval gaps. Requires appeal-outcome and notification-comprehension data.
7. **Risk-score feedback loop.** `prior_case_count` penalizes prior system contact, which is itself a product of past scrutiny; D is routed to fraud review, generating more contact, raising future scores. *Signature:* risk band predicts fraud *routing* strongly but fraud *substantiation* weakly for D; score inflation over successive cases; recalibration reduces routing without reducing detected fraud.
8. **Selection, censoring, and channel-access artifacts.** Slower groups have more open cases at extraction (truncated variants, missing Activation/Notification); applicants who abandon may be recorded as withdrawals, invisibly removing the worst experiences; channel reflects access, not preference. *Signature:* variant/duration distributions shift materially between closed-cohort and censored analyses; withdrawal rates differ by group; missing terminal events cluster in support variants.

Mechanisms 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 can produce substantial disparate treatment **without any discriminatory intent** — which is precisely why intent is not the test. Mechanisms 2 and 4 are the ones that would support a discrimination finding, and they are the ones the current attribute set is least able to isolate.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias in the adjustment variables.** `documentation_completeness` is plausibly a staff judgment contaminated by language and communication barriers; adjusting for it can *mask* the very mechanism at issue (over-control of a biased mediator). `channel` is partly a constraint (digital access, literacy, disability), not a free choice. Adjusted estimates therefore should not be read as "the fair share of the gap."
- **Historical bias in `risk_score_band`.** It may encode prior enforcement patterns and prior system contact. Treating it as neutral converts past disparity into present justification. Without substantiation outcomes, its calibration and predictive parity across groups are untested.
- **Missing applicant needs.** `language_preference`  actual interpretation need; `disability_accommodation_requested` captures *requests*, not needs — non-requesters may be undiagnosed, unaware, deterred, or unable to request. The log cannot distinguish "no need" from "need not voiced," which biases both the need denominators and any compliance test. Literacy, digital access, caregiving constraints, and household instability are absent.
- **Unobserved resource workload.** Daily granularity cannot capture intra-day queueing, interruption, case effort, or triage order. "Overloaded" is inferred from assignment counts, not measured. Consequently **capacity scarcity cannot be cleanly separated from differential prioritization within the same day** — the two require different remedies.
- **Appeal quality and outcomes are absent.** Only `appeal_flag` and Appeal Intake/Review activities exist. Without overturn rates, reasons, timeliness of appeal resolution, and language of appeal service, high appeal rates cannot be classified as error correction vs. explanation failure; low appeal rates cannot be read as satisfaction (possible suppressed voice, especially for C).
- **No applicant-side clock and no support-delivery detail.** No "Documents Received," "Interpreter Connected," "Accommodation Implemented," or "Support Requested" events. Agency delay and applicant delay are conflated; support steps are recorded as *contacts*, not as *adequately delivered rights*.
- **Outcome semantics are thin.** `case_outcome` omits withdrawal/abandonment, partial awards, benefit level, sanction, explanation quality, and dignity of treatment. Approval  benefit receipt (activation appears only in V1). Procedural justice — being heard, informed, and treated respectfully — is essentially unmeasured.
- **Group labels are coarse and non-exclusive.** A/B/C/D overlap in reality (a minority-language applicant requesting accommodation appears in two groups); intersectional disadvantage is likely understated. Group attributes also raise privacy and misuse concerns for monitoring.
- **Log artifacts.** Variant "path summaries" may be abridged; top-6 coverage differs by group (82% A vs 99% B), so cross-group variant comparisons are not like-for-like. Timestamp batching, right-censoring, and reopened-case stitching can all manufacture or hide disparities.
- **Inference limits.** Effective cluster counts are small (7 bilingual staff, 5 specialists, 2 problem regions); nominal n overstates precision for resource-mediated effects. Observational data cannot identify intent, and no design here randomizes treatment.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Option | Rationale | Side effects / trade-offs | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Add capacity for language support and accommodation review** (cross-train generalists, pooled/tele-interpreting, float specialists, surge rota) | Attacks the largest, clearest agency-controlled delays (3.8d / 4.7d) without touching rights | Cost; dilution of specialist expertise; interpretation-quality and confidentiality risks with ad-hoc vendors; training time temporarily reduces throughput; pooling may reduce cultural/linguistic matching quality | Certification standards for interpreters; minimum specialist share per case; measure *quality* alongside speed |
| **Service-level targets for support steps** (need-triggered clocks, e.g. interpreter contact 1d, accommodation decision 2d) | Closes the policy gap where rights-preserving steps are untimed and therefore unmanaged | Target gaming ("stop the clock," reclassifying cases as awaiting applicant); rushing accommodations into inadequate arrangements; cherry-picking easy cases; displacement of effort to unmeasured steps | Pair speed targets with quality/adequacy audits and re-request rates; audit clock-stop codes; never incentivize *skipping* the step |
| **Routing redesign to reduce handovers** (single accountable owner with specialist *consult* rather than transfer; warm handoffs with structured context; eliminate generalbilingualgeneral returns) | Reduces queueing, information loss, rework (22–24%) and repeat document requests | Generalists may make errors in specialist domains; specialists lose direct control; consult model can create hidden, unlogged work; ownership may reduce independent checking | Structured consult notes in the log; specialist sign-off for defined decision classes; monitor error/rework by routing model |
| **Decision-explanation improvements** (plain-language, translated reasons, oral explanation option, explicit appeal instructions in preferred language) | Targets appeals/rework driven by misunderstanding rather than eligibility; improves dignity and voice | Longer per-case handling time (short-run throughput loss); templated reasons degenerate into boilerplate; translation cost; potential legal exposure from more explicit reasoning | Comprehension testing with applicants; sample-based reason-quality review; track appeal-with-low-overturn as the target metric |
| **Monitoring adjusted disparities** (standing dashboard: adjusted approval, cycle time, mandate delivery, non-mandated escalation, support-step waits, by group × region × channel) | Makes indirect disadvantage visible and persistent rather than episodic | Goodhart effects and metric fixation; small-cell noise misread as signal; privacy risk from group attributes; risk of group data leaking into individual decisions; dashboard complacency | Cluster-robust CIs and FDR control; minimum cell sizes; strict purpose limitation and access control; prohibit group attributes in individual adjudication |
| **Audit `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`** (calibration and predictive-parity checks, substantiation-yield analysis, replace subjective completeness with objective checklist, review prior-case-count weighting) | Prevents biased mediators from laundering disparity into "legitimate routing" | Removing/weakening risk signals may increase fraud loss or shift staff to noisier informal heuristics (possibly worse); objective checklists can be gamed and may exclude legitimate evidence; re-audit is costly | Shadow-mode recalibration before deployment; measure fraud detection *and* false-positive burden jointly; human review of any downgrade |
| **Segregation of duties: specialists must not supervise their own cases** | Removes the self-review conflict in Group C's V5 chain | Fewer eligible reviewers  longer Supervisor Review queues unless capacity is added; loss of specialist insight in review | Add reviewer capacity simultaneously; log reviewer identity and independence flag |
| **Parallelize support with completeness/risk screening, with a hard gate before Decision Draft** | Cuts serial delay while *preserving* the support step | Eligibility work may need redoing once accommodations are known; risk of decisions drafted before accommodation is in place | Enforce a precedence constraint: no Decision Draft until accommodation/interpretation resolved |
| **Guarantee notification and activation; track abandonment** | Fixes the V4 notification gap and the approvalactivation gap; makes attrition visible | More logging/administrative burden; recording abandonment may look like worse performance | Treat abandonment as a *fairness* indicator, not a productivity penalty |
| **Proactive document assistance at intake; in-language appeal access** | Reduces downstream Request Docs loops and lowers the cost of voice | Longer intake; higher appeal volume and cost initially | Budget for appeal capacity; measure overturn rate to confirm value |

**Explicitly rejected:** removing, shortening, or "streamlining away" Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review to improve cycle time. That would improve the metric by destroying the right. The target is *the wait and the handovers around the step*, never the step.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Overall: partially unfair in identifiable procedural respects, with the discrimination question in adjudication remaining indeterminate.** The evidence does not support a claim of equal treatment, and it does not support a claim of proven discrimination.

**What can be concluded with reasonable confidence**

- Raw outcome gaps are **not** evidence of discrimination: roughly 63% (B), 76% (C) and 84% (D) of the raw approval gaps are statistically accounted for by measured case mix, and D's routing into fraud review is directionally consistent with an explicit policy mandate and a 51% high-risk share.
- Nevertheless, **procedural burden and delay are unequally distributed after adjustment**, and the largest burdens fall on the groups using rights-preserving steps. Group C's +4.1 days adjusted delay — the largest of any group — is plausibly attributable to a single 4.7-day specialist queue, and Group B faces a 3.8-day interpreter queue plus +9.5pp document requests and +8.0pp supervisor review conditional on recorded complexity. Neither pattern is explained by applicant behavior; both sit on agency-controlled clocks.
- The **resource structure is materially asymmetric** (~69 A cases per caseworker vs ~130–160 need-cases per specialist), and near-parity on the DecisionNotification control edge argues that these are *localized capacity bottlenecks*, not generalized case difficulty. This is a credible mechanism for indirect disadvantage **without any discriminatory intent** — and intent is not the standard.
- Governance defects are visible and hard to justify: two accommodation specialists performing supervisor review on their own pathway, boomerang handovers in two regions, and **no timeliness targets for mandated support steps**. A right without a clock or capacity is a right delivered unequally.

**What is unresolved**

- **Adjusted approval disparities:** B's 5.5pp is reasonably robust; C's 2.2pp and D's 3.1pp are within roughly 1–1.5 standard errors and should be reported as *not established*. Small cluster counts (7 bilingual staff, 5 specialists, 2 regions) further weaken precision.
- Whether B's conditional excess scrutiny reflects **biased measurement of the mediators, discretionary escalation, workload shortcuts, or genuinely warranted caution** cannot be determined without fraud-substantiation, supervisor-overturn, and document-request-yield data.
- Whether the apparent **under-delivery of mandated support** (B: ~57% need vs 25% of cases in variants that can contain the step; C: ~62% vs ~34%) and the **missing Notification/Activation** in V2–V6 are real rights failures or artifacts of abridged variant summaries and right-censoring. This is the single highest-priority verification: if real, it is the most serious finding in the log and would sharpen the assessment considerably.
- Whether D's fraud routing is **calibrated** — high routing volume with low substantiation would make the burden unjustified even though the routing rule is formally neutral. `risk_score_band` must not be treated as an exogenous justification.
- Whether elevated appeals in B and D reflect first-instance error or explanation failure, and whether C's *low* adjusted appeal gap reflects satisfaction or **suppressed voice**. Appeal outcomes are absent.

**Calibrated verdict by dimension:** timeliness and procedural burden — *unfair for B and C, moderate-to-high confidence, pending verification of log artifacts*; resource allocation — *structurally unequal, high confidence*; consistency of routing — *indeterminate, with concrete indicators of possible under-delivery that must be checked first*; quality/accessibility of support and effective remedy — *indeterminate and largely unmeasured*; outcome discrimination — *indeterminate for all groups, with B the most concerning residual*.

**What would change the verdict:** verified mandate-delivery rates near 100% with support waits explained by need rather than group (including in A's small need-holder cells), low non-mandated escalation after handover and caseload controls, and high yield of fraud/supervisor scrutiny would move this toward "burden differences largely justified, with a capacity problem to fix." Confirmed non-delivery of support or notification, group-linked waits among identical-need applicants, or low-yield excess scrutiny of B would move it toward a substantiated finding of procedurally unfair — and potentially discriminatory — treatment.

**Immediate no-regrets actions**, justified under every mechanism: add interpreter and accommodation capacity, set need-triggered service-level targets with quality guards, remove self-review, reduce boomerang handovers, guarantee notification and track activation and abandonment, and audit the risk score and completeness measure. None of these requires resolving the discrimination question, and none of them removes a rights-preserving step.
