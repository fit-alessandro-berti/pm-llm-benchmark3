# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

For a case \(i\), naïve waiting is \(W_i=start_i-arrival_i\), service is \(S_i=complete_i-start_i\), and interval-based busy time is the union of attributed service intervals—not their raw sum when they overlap. Throughput is generally safest when based on valid completions per elapsed wall-clock period, but administrative completions can shift throughput into artificial spikes. Conformance is also affected: equality can create arbitrary event order, fake parallelism, or impossible single-resource overlap.

### G1

- **Waiting:** The observed waits of 2–6 minutes are plausible waits until the fixture cycle begins. If analysts instead serialize the four records, later cases would be assigned invented additional waiting.
- **Service:** Three minutes per case is appropriate as elapsed cycle exposure, but summing to 12 worker-equivalent minutes would overstate cell busy time. The physical fixture is busy for about three wall-clock minutes.
- **Utilization:** Case-duration sums produce a fourfold overstatement for the cell unless utilization is capacity-normalized. Conversely, treating only one record as real would undercount item throughput.
- **Bottlenecks/throughput:** The cell produces four items per cycle, so item throughput can be high even when cycle duration is three minutes. A single-server queue model would incorrectly flag impossible overlap.
- **Conformance:** A model requiring serial labels would report false violations; a batch-capable activity or capacity-four resource is more suitable.

### G2

- **Waiting:** Using the common 10:00 group start gives waits of 6–18 minutes. If 10:00 is only group initialization, it underestimates item-level waiting for S02–S04 because their actual inspections may begin later.
- **Service:** Recorded durations of 8, 14, 21, and 27 minutes likely mix group-open elapsed time, queueing inside the group, and active inspection. Their sum severely overstates active labor under a serial interpretation.
- **Utilization:** Summing overlapping intervals can imply more than 100% utilization for T-B. Taking their union, 10:00–10:27, is a better upper-level occupied-window measure, but still may include internal waiting.
- **Bottlenecks:** Naïve durations can falsely identify inspection as a long-service bottleneck. The true bottleneck may instead be serial completion after a common setup.
- **Conformance:** PM4Py may call this simultaneous-at-start, but that is a UI/lifecycle pattern, not proof of four concurrent inspections.

### G3

- **Waiting:** The logged starts imply waits of 5–22 minutes. These may be usable only if start capture is genuine. If starts are remembered or entered later, both waiting and ordering are uncertain.
- **Service:** Durations of 6–28 minutes are likely upper bounds on elapsed case engagement because common 11:30 completion may be a folder submission boundary. Assigning all overlap as active work overstates labor.
- **Utilization:** A trusted single resource would appear heavily concurrent. Union time avoids double counting but cannot separate active work, pauses, and interleaving.
- **Bottlenecks:** Common completion creates a false throughput burst at 11:30 and can make release review look both long-running and highly parallel.
- **Conformance:** Same-end events can create arbitrary completion ordering and apparent parallel branches. Submission should be modeled as a distinct event if that is the operational reality.

### G4

- **Waiting:** Observed waits rise from 7 to 19 minutes as the queue builds. These are credible if `start` is true work start.
- **Service:** Four minutes per item is consistent and likely reliable. Boundary equality may hide sub-second handoff, but not materially alter minute-scale estimates.
- **Utilization:** The resource is continuously occupied from 12:05 to 12:25 under a no-gap interpretation. Summed service and interval union both equal about 20 minutes because there is no substantive overlap.
- **Bottlenecks:** This cluster supports a real single-server service pattern. Increasing waits despite stable service suggest arrival pressure or an initial setup delay, not service-time inflation.
- **Conformance:** It should fit a sequential resource-capacity model. PM4Py may detect a batching category based on adjacency, but “batch” here means a serially drained group, not simultaneous work.

### G5

- **Waiting:** The logged waits of 13–15 minutes may be valid at the pool level, but cannot be assigned to a specific analyst.
- **Service:** Durations of 10–25 minutes are case elapsed times, not necessarily continuous analyst effort. Four overlapping cases against a pool capacity of up to three imply either multitasking, approximate lifecycle times, or a capacity/account inconsistency.
- **Utilization:** Treating `analyst_pool` as one person yields impossible utilization. Treating it as capacity three is more defensible, but badge loss prevents exact person-level utilization and workload balancing.
- **Bottlenecks:** The pool could be a bottleneck, but no conclusion about individual productivity or overload is defensible.
- **Conformance:** Resource-based segregation-of-duty and workload rules cannot be tested at person level. Pool-level concurrency should not be reported as a single-worker violation.

### G6

- **Waiting:** Naïvely, every case waits from arrival until 17:00—35 to 175 minutes. That likely measures **time to administrative closure**, not queue waiting to operational work start.
- **Service:** Because every start equals every completion, naïve service is zero. The same logging design therefore creates **both inflated waiting and near-zero service**: real execution time and possibly post-work delay are collapsed into the pre-`start` interval, while the bulk action stamps an instantaneous lifecycle interval at closure.
- **Utilization:** Zero service implies zero resource busy time even though work must have occurred somewhere. If analysts compensate by treating arrival-to-close as service, utilization becomes grossly inflated. Neither is justified.
- **Bottlenecks:** Queue analysis would falsely identify pre-close waiting as the bottleneck and miss actual handling capacity. Throughput spikes artificially at 17:00.
- **Conformance:** Seven instant closures can appear as simultaneous execution or skipped work. The proper interpretation may be an administrative “close status” event rather than a complete lifecycle pair.

### G7

- **Waiting:** Recorded starts precede some arrivals, producing negative waits for M02–M05. This is direct evidence that point estimates are unsafe. With 15-minute rounding, start and completion must be treated as intervals whose exact bounds depend on the undocumented rule.
- **Service:** The common 15-minute duration could represent anything from a short action quantized to a bin to a genuine group operation. It is not a precise per-case service duration.
- **Utilization:** Summing five 15-minute intervals would claim 75 minutes of work in a 15-minute window; taking a 15-minute union may understate serial work hidden inside the bin. Only utilization bounds are defensible.
- **Bottlenecks:** Coarsening can create a false simultaneous batch and erase ordering. Apparent negative waits can also distort queue distributions and process models.
- **Conformance:** Ordering among cases inside the timestamp bin is unknown. Use partial orders or tied/interval-censored events rather than arbitrary row order as physical order.

### G8

- **Waiting:** Logged waits rise from 38 to 65 minutes as arrivals accumulate and the server drains the queue. These are credible from the repair source, but attribution of the initial delay to the safety event is uncertain because the clocks are not aligned.
- **Service:** Each seven-minute interval and short scanner lag strongly support consistent serial service.
- **Utilization:** T-H is continuously busy from 15:38 to 16:13 in the repair log. Cross-system utilization before 15:38 cannot be combined with the safety interval until clocks are synchronized.
- **Bottlenecks:** This is consistent with a backlog accumulated during unavailable capacity, followed by stable drainage. However, “safety caused the backlog” remains a hypothesis rather than an observation.
- **Conformance:** Within-source repair order is strong. A cross-system model may report a false overlap or three-minute idle gap depending on clock offset.

def flatten_keys(obj):
    """Compactly collect category-like dictionary keys across PM4Py versions."""
    keys = []
    if isinstance(obj, dict):
        for k, v in obj.items():
            keys.append(str(k))
            if isinstance(v, (dict, list, tuple)):
                keys.extend(flatten_keys(v))
    elif isinstance(obj, (list, tuple)):
        for x in obj:
            keys.extend(flatten_keys(x))
    return keys

# Pairwise overlap: positive-duration intersections only; touching boundaries
# are adjacency, not overlap.
def overlap_stats(g):
    x = g.sort_values(["start", "complete", "sequence_no"])
    pairs = overlaps = adjacent = 0
    for i in range(len(x)):
        for j in range(i + 1, len(x)):
            a, b = x.iloc[i], x.iloc[j]
            if pd.isna(a["start"]) or pd.isna(b["start"]):
                continue
            pairs += 1
            if min(a["complete"], b["complete"]) > max(a["start"], b["start"]):
                overlaps += 1
            if a["complete"] == b["start"] or b["complete"] == a["start"]:
                adjacent += 1
    return pd.Series({
        "pair_overlap_rate": overlaps / pairs if pairs else np.nan,
        "adjacent_pairs": adjacent
    })

def max_equality_group(g, col):
    counts = g[col].dropna().value_counts()
    return int(counts.max()) if len(counts) else 0

    z = g[col].dropna()

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

## Resource IDs trusted versus shared

| Cluster | If resource IDs identify one executor accurately | If resource IDs may represent a shared account or pool |
|---|---|---|

## Exact timestamps versus interval-censored timestamps

- **One-second sources (G1, G4, G8):** Treating timestamps as exact makes equality and adjacency strong evidence. Even so, accuracy can differ from documented precision; G8’s cross-system safety comparison remains clock-sensitive.
- **One-minute sources (G2, G3, G5, G6):** Equality is only known within the minute bins. Common starts/ends remain meaningful patterns, but boundary overlaps may be rounding artifacts. G6’s seven identical zero-duration records are still highly suspicious because both lifecycle fields and insertion waves coincide.
- **Fifteen-minute source (G7):** Point-time analysis is indefensible. Depending on the undocumented down/up rule, actual starts and completions occupy broad intervals, and case order may be unknown. A smaller PM4Py merge tolerance does not repair information already lost to quantization.

At least five patterns support multiple physical explanations:

1. **G1 identical starts and ends:** one four-item fixture cycle; four parallel sub-stations hidden behind a cell ID; or PLC emission of a cycle-level interval copied to all items.
2. **G2 common start:** common setup followed by serial inspection; several inspections genuinely launched together; or a UI-generated administrative start marker.
3. **G3 common end:** folder-level bulk submission; genuine coordinated completion; or one worker multitasking and intentionally closing all open reviews at a checkpoint.
4. **G5 overlap:** up to three analysts working concurrently; one or more analysts multitasking; or approximate lifecycle fields that include inactive elapsed time.
5. **G7 identical intervals:** genuine physical batch; serial work hidden within one quarter-hour; or rounding that maps distinct operations to the same bounds.
6. **G6 zero-duration equality:** end-of-shift data entry; bulk status transition after earlier work; or closure of tickets whose operational completion occurred elsewhere.
7. **G8 backlog:** safety-related capacity loss; another unavailable-capacity cause such as setup or break; or ordinary queueing before a scheduled repair run.

These ambiguities show why batch discovery should be interpreted as detection of temporal relations—simultaneous, start-grouped, end-grouped, or sequential—not direct classification of physical production modes.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

1. **Capture execution and administration as separate events.** Record `work_started`, `work_completed`, `saved`, `submitted`, and `status_closed` independently. Never populate a missing execution start from bulk-close time.
2. **Use source-generated immutable timestamps.** Capture execution timestamps at the workstation or machine at the moment of action, while retaining `recorded_at` as a separate ingestion timestamp. Include event IDs and correction/version history.
3. **Synchronize and monitor clocks.** Apply a common time standard, retain timezone and clock-offset metadata, log NTP/PTP status, and estimate drift. For G8-like cross-system analyses, store both local and normalized timestamps plus uncertainty.
4. **Retain badge-level or session-level identity.** Keep the displayed account, actual authenticated badge, workstation/session ID, and role separately. If privacy requires pseudonymization, use stable pseudonyms. Explicitly identify resource pools and their capacity.
5. **Add group and batch identifiers.** Capture fixture cycle ID, UI “begin group” ID, folder/submission ID, and bulk-operation ID. Link item-level events to the group while preserving item-specific starts and completions.
6. **Record workstation capacity and resource semantics.** Maintain effective capacity over time, distinguishing a unit-capacity worker, multi-slot machine, team, and shared pool. Record fixture slot and operator-machine assignment where relevant.
7. **Capture pause/resume and preemption.** For analytical work and inspections, log active intervals, pauses, handoffs, and reassignment. This separates elapsed case duration from active labor.
8. **Make timestamp precision machine-readable per event.** Store nominal resolution, rounding rule, accuracy estimate, and whether a time is observed, copied, inferred, or manually entered. Represent coarse times as uncertainty intervals rather than false exact points.
9. **Record availability and downtime causally.** Capture safety holds, failures, breaks, setup, maintenance, and recovery with affected resources and synchronized clocks. This permits queue accumulation to be attributed to capacity loss rather than inferred from shape alone.
10. **Add validation controls.** Flag starts before arrivals, zero-duration manual lifecycle pairs, impossible capacity overlap, excessive common timestamps, and delayed insertion waves. Preserve flagged records instead of silently rewriting them.

# 7. Final adjudication

**High-confidence temporal interpretations**

- **G1:** A genuine capacity-four machine batch is highly likely, though the interval remains a cell-cycle observation rather than proof of continuous item-level work.
- **G2:** High-confidence common-start/group-setup pattern; item-level service starts are not established.
- **G3:** High-confidence common-end or bulk-submission pattern; individual physical completion times are uncertain.
- **G4:** High-confidence sequential processing with reliable scanner-supported service intervals.
- **G5:** High-confidence shared-account artifact at person level; pool concurrency is real or at least plausible, but individual attribution is unavailable.
- **G6:** High-confidence administrative bulk closure, not valid execution lifecycle data.
- **G7:** High-confidence timestamp coarsening; low confidence about the underlying physical pattern.
- **G8:** High-confidence sequential repair chain; only moderate confidence that a safety-related capacity loss caused the backlog.

**Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis**

- **Exclude G6** from execution waiting/service estimation unless operational timestamps are recovered. It may be used only for time-to-administrative-closure.
- **Interval-censor G7** and avoid point waiting estimates, especially because several recorded starts precede arrivals.
- **Treat G2 item starts and G3 item completions as semantically censored** unless UI/form semantics are formally confirmed. Group-level timing remains usable.
- **Do not use G5 for individual-resource queue or utilization analysis.** It may be analyzed as a capacity-limited pool with explicit uncertainty.

**Usable with qualifications**

- **G1** is usable for fixture-cycle waiting and item throughput, provided capacity-four utilization replaces single-server duration summation.
- **G4** is the strongest cluster for conventional queue, service, and utilization analysis.
- **G8** is usable within the repair scanner’s clock domain; causal attribution to safety downtime requires clock alignment.

**Require operational validation before performance conclusions**

- G2: determine whether `start` means group opening or item work.
- G3: distinguish review finish from folder submission.
- G5: recover badge identity or confirm pool capacity and multitasking rules.
- G7: recover the rounding rule and raw timestamps.
- G8: synchronize the safety and repair clocks.
- G1: validate that the copied interval corresponds to one four-item fixture cycle if capacity-normalized utilization is to be reported.

Accordingly, PM4Py batch labels should be reported as temporal-pattern findings and paired with lifecycle quality, precision, identity, and capacity evidence. Only G1, G4, and the within-source portion of G8 support relatively direct operational interpretation; the other clusters primarily demonstrate logging semantics or unresolved resource identity rather than physical execution modes.
