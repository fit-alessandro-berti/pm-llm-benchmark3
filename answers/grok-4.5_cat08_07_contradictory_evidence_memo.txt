# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Leadership, Fiber Operations  
**From:** Process Analytics / Operating Model  
**Subject:** Decision on Redesign R — do not scale nationally; hybrid segment deployment with controls and measurement  
**Recommendation:** Alternative E

Redesign R improved the middle of the distribution and cut handovers, but it degraded tails, first-time-right, complaints, manual activation, and second visits. National scale (A) is not defensible on current evidence. Full rollback (B) would discard clear gains for simple low-risk houses. The right next move is a **hybrid**: keep coordinator ownership and merged validate/schedule **only where the data show net benefit**, suspend the risky shortcuts everywhere else, and buy the measurements that resolve whether partial scale can be expanded.

**What the evidence actually shows.** Median cycle time fell from 14.2 to 10.8 days and cases with 3+ handovers fell from 34% to 12%. Mean visible cost before complaint handling fell (EUR 184  161). Those are real operational wins. Against them: p90 rose 31  38.5 days and p95 45  62 days; first-time-right fell 82%  76%; complaints within 30 days 6.1%  9.8%; second visits 11%  18%; manual activation 7%  15%; SLA breach 13%  16%. Estimated cost including incomplete complaint/revisit handling rose to ~EUR 207. NPS slipped +21  +17. Coordinator utilization hit 93% in two regions while second-visit teams spiked to 92% and survey capacity sat idle (78%  52%).

**Segment split is decisive.** Simple low-risk houses (34% of pilot) gained ~6 days median, better p90, +3pp FTR, fewer complaints. Apartments medium-risk, old/permit-sensitive buildings, and to a lesser extent business and rural cases lost on tails, FTR, and complaints. Pilot regions had more apartments and older stock than baseline, so national extrapolation of the average is biased. Attribute coverage was incomplete before pilot week 3; cost data omit downstream complaint handling after week 4; start timestamps are sparse. We cannot treat pilot averages as unbiased national forecasts.

**Process-mining artifacts explain the pattern, they do not contradict it.** Pilot model fitness is high (0.94) because many cases follow the simplified coordinator path; precision is poor (0.51) because the discovered model permits too many combinations of skipped survey, repeated install, manual activation, and billing-before-activation. Alignments flag systematic deviations: billing start before successful activation test; install repeated without field survey; expected permit checks missing on older apartments; complaint escalation after billing. DFG shifts match the design (merged validate/schedule; less survey; more direct-to-install) and the failure modes (more failed visit  second visit; more manual activation  billing; more billing  complaint). Temporal profiles improved addressappointment for simple houses and worsened installactivation for apartments; billingcomplaint is a new long-tail relation. The handover network simplified, but work centralized on coordinators and activation specialists; absence of a coordinator stalled cases—fewer handovers, less resilience.

**Trade-off.** R bought speed and accountability on easy cases by skipping survey and allowing post-signal billing start. That same design under-serves complex buildings, loads coordinators past a robust operating point, and moves defects downstream (manual activation, “installed but not working,” billing disputes). Visible cost looks better because rework and complaint cost are partially off-book. Scaling the average would scale the tail and the compliance-like activation defect.

**Decision.**  
- **Scale now:** coordinator ownership + merged validate/schedule for **simple low-risk houses only**, with utilization caps and backup coverage rules.  
- **Pause:** predictive survey-skip and activation-after-billing (and any path that starts billing before successful activation test) outside that segment; do not nationalize full R.  
- **Add controls:** mandatory permit check path for old/apartment stock; conformance monitors on billing-before-activation and missing permit; stop-the-line if those rates breach agreed thresholds.  
- **Measure next (highest decision value):** full 60-day downstream cost (M4); reason codes for fail/manual/complaint/second visit (M3); validate building-risk rule vs survey outcomes (M6); randomized skip-by-stratum only inside a governed experiment (M1); start-timestamp instrumentation (M2); separate CX for speed vs working service (M8); absence/resilience test of ownership model (M5); live conformance on the two critical deviant patterns (M7).  

**Broaden rollout only if,** on a clean segment-stratified window: simple-house gains hold; apartment/old-building p90 and complaint deltas return to  baseline; billing-before-activation and missing-permit rates are near zero under controls; full loaded cost  baseline; coordinator utilization sustainably  ~85% with demonstrated backup. Until then, treat R as a segment-qualified pattern, not a company standard.

This preserves value already proven, stops the mechanisms driving tail and rework, and replaces opinion with targeted evidence before any wider commit.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness, low precision.** Fitness rose because a large share of pilot cases follow the new happy path (coordinator ownership, merged validate/schedule, occasional survey skip). The model therefore replays observed traces well. Precision fell because that same model tolerates many behavior combinations not intended as standard—skipped survey + repeated install, manual activation, billing before activation, complaint after billing. Alignments confirm those extras are frequent in the log. High fitness means “we explain what happened”; low precision means “the model (and the operating reality) allow too much deviance,” which matches worse tails and rework.

**Improved median, worse tail.** Simple houses (large share) moved left on cycle time via merged scheduling and fewer handovers, pulling the median down. Apartments, old/permit-sensitive buildings, and overloaded-coordinator paths produced long delays (missing permits, failed visits, second visits, activation disputes), stretching p90/p95. A design that accelerates the easy majority and destabilizes the complex minority routinely improves central tendency while harming tails and SLA breach.

**Fewer handovers, lower resilience.** Coordinator ownership collapsed multi-role chains (3+ handovers 34%  12%) and simplified the handover-of-work network. Context concentrated on the coordinator. When utilization hit ~93% or a coordinator was absent, cases stalled; the working-together network centralized on coordinators and activation specialists. Redundancy was removed with the handovers. Throughput accountability improved on the steady state; shock absorption worsened.

**Lower visible cost, higher rework/downstream cost.** Pre-complaint cost fell with less survey work and faster simple paths. Second visits, manual activation, complaint escalations, and billing disputes rose—costs that are partly excluded after week 4 and show up in the incomplete loaded estimate (EUR 207). Shifting activation checks after billing and skipping surveys moves defect discovery downstream, so “cheaper to install” and “more expensive to finish and remediate” coexist.

**Simple houses improve; apartments/old buildings deteriorate.** Merged validate/schedule and fewer handovers fit low-risk houses (survey often unnecessary; activation clean). The skip rule and weaker permit discipline misfire on apartments and older stock (alignments: missing permit; install without survey; install  activation worsens). Business customers see billing disputes from manual activation. Same intervention, opposite sign by segment—hence average metrics conflict with segment metrics, and pilot mix (more apartments/old buildings) makes the average a poor national sufficient statistic.

# 4. Decision alternatives

| Alt | Advantages | Risks | When reasonable |
|-----|------------|-------|-----------------|
| **A. Scale R nationally** | Fast capture of median/handover/visible-cost gains; simple change story | Exports worse tails, FTR, complaints, manual activation, SLA breach; precision/compliance-like billing-before-activation issues at scale; mix bias; coordinator overload; loaded cost likely up | Only if loaded cost, tails, and activation conformance already  baseline across segments—**not true now** |
| **B. Full rollback** | Stops tail/rework/complaint bleed immediately; restores survey and activation discipline | Gives up proven simple-house gains and handover reduction; ignores segment heterogeneity; demotivates local improvement | If hybrid controls impossible, coordinator model inseparable from harm, or regulatory exposure on billing-before-activation is intolerable and unfixable quickly |
| **C. Scale only coordinator ownership; suspend skip + post-billing activation** | Keeps accountability/handover benefit; removes two strongest harm mechanisms; simpler than full hybrid | Ownership alone untested; utilization/resilience issues remain; may keep some apartment friction if permits/paths not fixed; still needs measurement | Strong if leadership wants one national ownership model **and** will hard-gate activation-before-billing and reinstate survey/permit rules pending validation |
| **D. Controlled regional experiment + better segment/risk measurement** | Maximum learning; causal identification on skip and ownership; fixes data gaps before commit | Delays value for simple houses already winning; experiment ops cost; pilot fatigue | If political capital for any production scale is low, or attribute/cost instrumentation must precede any differential treatment |
| **E. Hybrid: R for simple low-risk houses; controls for apartments/old buildings; measure before wider scale** | Protects demonstrated winners; contains losers; explicitly uses PM artifacts (segments, alignments, DFG, resources); staged evidence gates | Requires reliable segment/risk classification at case start; two-speed ops complexity; risk of leakage of skip/billing practices into wrong segment | **Best fit to current evidence:** benefits are segment-specific, harms are mechanism-specific, data gaps are material but not total |

# 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended decision: E (hybrid), with C-like hard suspends and D-like measurement discipline.**

| Element | Action |
|---------|--------|
| **Immediate action** | Qualify cases at address validation into (i) simple low-risk house vs (ii) apartment / old-permit-sensitive / other. Apply coordinator ownership + merged validate/schedule to (i). Reinstate field survey and permit check expectations for (ii). **Prohibit billing start before successful activation test** on all segments. Stand up M7 conformance monitors and utilization caps (target coordinator 85% with named backup). |
| **What to pause** | National scale of full R; predictive survey-skip outside a governed experiment; activation-after-billing / billing-before-activation; any further region copy-paste of the bundled pilot. |
| **What to scale** | Coordinator ownership and merged validate/schedule **only for simple low-risk houses**, plus the simpler handover pattern, under backup/absence rules. Do not scale skip rule or post-billing activation. |
| **What to measure next** | Priority pack: **M4, M6, M3, M7** immediately; **M8, M2** in parallel; **M1** only inside risk-stratified experiment after M6; **M5** in two regions before expanding coordinator model. Fix attribute completeness and cost coverage that broke after pilot weeks 3–4. |
| **Thresholds for broader rollout** | On a segment-stratified holdout 6 weeks with complete attributes and 60-day cost: (1) simple-house median/p90/FTR/complaint gains hold; (2) apartment & old-building p90 and complaint rates  baseline; (3) billing-before-activation  0 and missing-permit on designated stock  0 under M7; (4) full loaded cost  baseline overall and per adverse segment; (5) FTR  baseline; (6) coordinator util. sustainably  ~85% with absence coverage proven (M5). If (2)–(5) fail after controls, freeze hybrid scope or move to B for adverse segments. If M6+M1 show safe skip only in low-risk stratum, reintroduce skip there only. |

Do not pretend R dominates all metrics. Do not nationalize while tails, rework, precision, and activation order are worse. Do not erase simple-house value with a blunt rollback. Segment the design, suspend the harmful mechanisms, and let the ranked measurements decide any next expansion.
