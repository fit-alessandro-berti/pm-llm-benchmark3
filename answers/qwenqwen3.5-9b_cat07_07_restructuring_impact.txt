# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring alters the underlying topology of the process network. The following map links specific structural changes to observable process-mining effects.

| Restructuring Action | Structural Change | Expected Process-Mining Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Merge local specialists** | Removal of `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, `E_Tech` resources; creation of `Shared_Tech` resource. | **Central queue formation:** Increased waiting time variance (`service_time` distribution widens). <br> **Resource similarity:** `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` will show lower similarity between local caseworkers and the shared service. |
| **Standardize routing rules** | Logic change: `required_specialty` determines `Shared_Service` vs `Local_Tech`. | **Nominal handover reduction:** Frequency of `local caseworker  local tech specialist` drops. <br> **Handover network:** Shift from local loops to `local caseworker  Shared_Tech  local caseworker`. |
| **Remove local supervisor** | Removal of `N_Supervisor`, `S_Supervisor`; creation of `Shared_Approval`. | **Role clarity:** `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` may detect ambiguity in `Shared_Approval` ownership. <br> **Resilience to absence:** Reduced local capacity to absorb staff absence (higher `reassignment_count` during spikes). |
| **Centralize Legal/Risk** | Consolidation of `Legal` and `Risk` resources into a shared queue. | **Specialist utilization:** `Shared_Tech` utilization increases (target 15% gain). <br> **Rework:** Potential spike in `rework_flag` if local triage fails to catch issues early. |
| **Keep Intake local** | `Bot_Intake`  `Local_Intake` remains unchanged. | **Queueing:** `Bot_Intake` handover wait remains stable. <br> **Fairness:** `customer_segment` variance in intake speed remains consistent. |
| **Shared approval queue** | `Local_Case`  `Shared_Approval` instead of `Local_Supervisor`. | **Cycle time:** `median_cycle_time` may decrease, but `90th_percentile_cycle_time` may increase due to queueing. <br> **Fairness:** Cross-team handovers decrease (`cross-team handover` frequency drops). |

# 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** Significant reduction in cross-team handovers (6  3). Local loops are replaced by efficient shared-service loops.
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** `median_wait` after handovers decreases for technical reviews due to better load balancing.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Tighter distribution; 90th percentile improves as East's bottlenecks are relieved.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework rate drops (8%  6%) due to standardized specialist review catching errors earlier. Quality check consistency improves.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Shared approval queue reduces decision variance; clear SLAs on shared resources.
*   **Fairness implications:** East team sees immediate cycle time improvement. North/South see stable performance.
*   **Early warning signs:** `reassignment_count` remains low; `local_expertise_flag` does not correlate with higher rework.

### Central Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** Nominal handover count drops, but complexity of handovers increases (more data transfer required for shared service).
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** `median_wait` improves, but `99th_percentile_wait` increases due to central queue congestion during peak times.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Average cycle time improves (6.8d  6.0d), but tail latency increases for complex cases requiring specialist intervention.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework rate stable but shifts location (more rework happens post-specialist review rather than pre-review).
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Shared approval creates ambiguity; `reassignment_count` for approvals rises slightly.
*   **Fairness implications:** North/South staff report lower satisfaction due to loss of local context; East gains.
*   **Early warning signs:** Increase in `local_expertise_flag` correlation with `rework_flag` in North/South teams.

### Adverse Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** `cross-team handover` frequency drops, but `local caseworker  Technical Shared Service` frequency spikes due to over-triage.
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Central technical service becomes a hidden bottleneck; median wait for technical review exceeds 4.0h.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Average cycle time stable, but 90th percentile cycle time degrades significantly (16.4d  22.0d) for complex cases.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework rate increases (8%  12%) as local specialists who absorbed "easy" tech issues are removed.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Shared approval queue leads to disputes; `reassignment_count` for approvals spikes.
*   **Fairness implications:** North/South customers experience higher wait times compared to East; SLA miss rate rises for North/South.
*   **Early warning signs:** `local_expertise_flag` drops in North/South, and `rework_flag` spikes for cases originating from those regions.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail Latency:** Average cycle time may improve due to faster simple cases, while high-complexity cases (requiring specialist review) face longer queues. The 99th percentile cycle time could double without affecting the mean.
2.  **High-Complexity Case Handling:** Local teams currently triage complexity informally. Centralization may route complex cases to the shared service, but if the shared service lacks the specific regional context, resolution time for these cases will increase.
3.  **Local Knowledge Loss:** Removing `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, `E_Tech` removes the immediate feedback loop where local staff learn from specialists. This reduces the team's ability to handle exceptions without specialist intervention in the long term (learning curve risk).
4.  **Fairness Across Teams:** The shared service may prioritize volume over specific regional needs. North and South teams may experience higher wait times for specialist advice compared to East, despite having similar case volumes.
5.  **Accountability Ambiguity:** The shared approval queue removes clear ownership. `reassignment_count` may increase for approvals as staff wait to see who is responsible for final decisions, leading to delays not captured in average time.
6.  **Exception Handling:** Local teams currently handle exceptions informally. A centralized model may fail to absorb sudden spikes in legal/risk cases, causing the shared queue to back up, whereas local teams could absorb the shock locally.

# 5. Pilot design

*   **Pilot Scope:** Roll out the shared-service model only for the **East Team** first. This team has the highest specialist utilization (82%) and longest cycle times (8.6d), making it the highest-risk candidate for improvement and the most likely to show benefit. North and South remain as the baseline.
*   **Comparison Group:** North and South teams (maintaining current embedded specialist model).
*   **Pre/Post Observation Window:** 8 weeks. 2 weeks pre-pilot for baseline stabilization, 6 weeks pilot, 1 week post-pilot for trend analysis.
*   **Segmentation:**
    *   **Team:** East (Pilot) vs. North/South (Control).
    *   **Complexity:** Split log by `case_complexity` (Low, Medium, High).
    *   **Specialty:** Monitor `required_specialty` (Legal, Risk, Tech, General).
    *   **Customer Segment:** Monitor satisfaction by `customer_segment`.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   90th percentile cycle time for East must not exceed baseline + 15%.
    *   `rework_flag` rate for East must not exceed baseline + 2%.
    *   `reassignment_count` for approvals must not increase by >20%.
*   **Staff Feedback Collection:** Weekly surveys on `outcome` (perceived workload) and role clarity.
*   **Rollback or Adjustment Triggers:**
    *   If `rework_flag` in East exceeds North/South average by >5%, pause rollout.
    *   If `service_time` for `Technical Shared Service` exceeds 5.0h median for 3 consecutive days, adjust queue capacity.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation:** **Pilot First.** Proceed with the phased rollout as designed in the Pilot Design section. Do not proceed to a full organization-wide restructuring without data validation.

**Reasoning:**
Management estimates suggest a 15% increase in specialist utilization and reduced handovers. However, process-mining analysis reveals that average cycle time improvements can mask tail latency and fairness issues. Specifically, the removal of local embedded specialists from North and South carries a high risk of reducing resilience to local exceptions and increasing rework due to loss of regional context.

**What Management Should Not Infer:**
*   **From Early Averages:** Do not infer success if the average cycle time drops but the 90th percentile cycle time rises. This indicates that while simple cases are faster, complex cases are suffering.
*   **From Utilization:** Do not infer success solely from `specialist_utilization` increases. High utilization in the shared service may indicate a bottleneck rather than efficiency.
*   **From Rework Rates:** Do not ignore `rework_flag` variance by team. If East's rework drops but North's rises significantly, the model is creating inequity.

**Action:** Monitor the `reassignment_count`, `90th_percentile_cycle_time`, and `customer_segment` satisfaction during the pilot. If the adverse scenario indicators (e.g., local expertise loss in North/South) appear in the data, revert the structural changes immediately.
