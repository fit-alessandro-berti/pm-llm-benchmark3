This enables true process discovery (path variants, bottleneck analysis) rather than aggregate inference.

**Spillover monitoring:** Because the fraud desk is centralized, monitor fraud-desk volume and wait time by branch of origin weekly. If Delta’s automation increases fraud-desk submissions and Epsilon’s wait times rise disproportionately, spillover is confirmed. Also track routing-campaign adjustments; freeze routing allocations for Delta/Epsilon during the evaluation window.

**Guardrails for customer harm:** Define a 4-week rolling average for `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h`. If median exceeds 110% of pre-period mean for 3 consecutive weeks, or p90 exceeds 120%, or `rework_pct` jumps >3 pp, trigger an automatic review board and suspend automation for high-complexity cases (revert to manual triage for risk class above median). Monitor `csat` weekly with mandatory response tracking (target n>50 per branch per week); if mean drops >0.15 points relative to rolling 4-week baseline, pause expansion.

**Decision rule for scale-up to full organization:** Approve full rollout only if, over months 3–6 post-deployment in Delta: (1) `median_cycle_h` is stable or lower than pre-period mean in 4 of 6 months; (2) `p90_cycle_h` does not exceed 105% of pre-period mean; (3) `automated_fast_path_pct` reaches 30%; (4) fraud-desk wait times for Epsilon (control) do not increase >15% relative to baseline; (5) case-level data confirms the automated fast path has lower loop frequency than manual fast path; and (6) staff feedback scores do not decline. If 4 of 6 criteria are met and no harm guardrail is triggered, approve Epsilon rollout with the same phased mechanism. If fewer than 4 are met, redesign the triage rules, retrain staff, and extend evaluation by 3 months. Do not approve immediate universal expansion based on these branch-month aggregates.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Branch-level automation availability (`automation`=1) began in April for Alpha/Beta, July for Gamma, and never for Delta/Epsilon; actual use (`automated_fast_path_pct`) built gradually and varied widely across branches and months; concurrent operational shifts included a March centralized fraud desk, an April/July routing campaign, staff changes, and a May product change that increased `high_complexity_pct`.

- `Suggestive:` For Gamma, median and tail cycle times improved after July adoption relative to its own pre-period, but this coincides with a sharp pre-adoption spike in May–June and a staff reduction in June. For Alpha and Beta, median cycle times in August were lower than in January–March, but never-treated branches (Delta, Epsilon) experienced substantial deterioration over the same calendar period, implying that automation may have mitigated—but not clearly reversed—a system-wide decline.

- `Conditionally causal:` If one conditions on the strong parallel-trends and no-spillover assumptions, and adjusts for observed staffing and case-mix changes, a staggered group-time difference-in-differences design could target an average treatment effect on the treated for median cycle time; however, the small cluster count (N=5), shared fraud-desk spillovers, endogenous treatment intensity, non-flat Gamma pre-trends, and missing case-level queue data mean this estimate remains highly uncertain and should not be reported as a precise “X% reduction.”

- `Not supported:` A single organizational statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible. Tail performance (`p90_cycle_h`) did not clearly improve in direct pre/post comparisons for Alpha/Beta; rework and escalation rates did not fall; customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`) cannot be causally interpreted due to unknown response selection and concurrent stress; and any precise point estimate collapses median and tail behavior that moved in opposite directions across cohorts.
