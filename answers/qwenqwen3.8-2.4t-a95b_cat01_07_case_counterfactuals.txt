The K-219 intervention changes routing by substituting D2 for D1, with timing and quality consequences. The K-337 intervention changes the allowed decision policy: it stops an information loop and forces a decision on incomplete evidence, so it is not merely a timing change. The K-411 intervention changes task creation and concurrency control, allowing Identity and Document work to overlap; it is primarily a routing/timing change with audit implications.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The case was delayed by three mechanisms: late escalation of sanctions ambiguity, a long D1 queue for complex corporate documents, and a final compliance review performed largely from scratch after Assessment. The factual path preserved dependencies but left compliance preparation until late.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create a preliminary escalation packet for S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot. S1 can begin advisory work while Identity and Document checks proceed. C1 or S1 can perform preliminary compliance screening, but final Compliance Review must still occur after the final Assessment.

Temporal propagation: The intervention does not materially change I1 or D1 service times. Its main effect is to move evidence organization, sanctions framing, and compliance preparation earlier. If S1 completes preliminary escalation before Assessment, A1 may receive a cleaner evidence package, but the largest plausible saving is in C1 final review time and queue wait. A reserved C1 slot after Assessment can move final compliance from Wednesday afternoon to Wednesday late morning. If preliminary work saves 1-3 hours and the reserved slot avoids several hours of queue wait, end-to-end completion may improve by roughly 2-10 hours.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible. Register and Triage occur first. Identity and Document both complete before Assessment. Because originals are not required, Identity and Document may proceed independently. High-risk/high-value status requires final Compliance Review after the latest Assessment, and the intervention preserves that. Four-eyes is preserved because A1 performs final Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance Review. Preliminary screening does not replace final review.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: S1 packet is complete by Monday, preliminary compliance work occurs Tuesday, Assessment still finishes Tuesday evening, C1 uses a reserved Wednesday morning slot, and Decision/Notify occur around midday Wednesday, saving roughly 8-10 hours. Base case: preliminary packet is partially complete, C1 final review is shortened by 1-2 hours, and the reserved slot is only slightly earlier, saving 3-6 hours. Worst case: packet is incomplete, C1 cannot use the reserved slot effectively, final compliance remains long, and other urgent cases are delayed by the reservation; net target-case gain may be near zero or negative for the system.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The conclusion would reverse if C1 remains the binding bottleneck despite reservation, if preliminary packets are usually incomplete, if reserved slots are frequently released too late, or if non-target urgent compliance cases suffer large delays. Evidence of C1 queue growth after reservations would be especially important.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The case was routed to D1 because handwriting can be difficult. D1 produced an accurate check but started later due to D1 queue. The factual delay was mainly queue-based, not rework-based.

Minimal intervention: Assign the Document Check to D2 as soon as Originals Receipt is complete, rather than waiting for D1. The routing changes from D1 to D2; the mandatory precedence of Originals Receipt before Document Check remains unchanged.

Temporal propagation: D2 may start earlier than D1 because D2’s queue is shorter in the snapshots. If D2 starts Monday night or early Tuesday, Document Check could finish before the factual D1 completion at Tuesday 13:30. However, D2 is slower on standard documents and may be more likely to request clarification on handwriting. If D2 finishes earlier, Assessment readiness moves earlier, but the actual Assessment start still depends on A2 queue. If D2 requests clarification, the path adds Request More Info, a new Document Check, and a new Assessment, which can dominate any queue saving.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible. Register and Triage occur first. Identity Check completes before Assessment. Originals Receipt precedes Document Check. Because the case is not high-risk or high-value, no Compliance Review is required. Decision follows Assessment and precedes Notify. If rework occurs, Rule 7 is satisfied by a new Document Check and new Assessment before Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: D2 starts soon after Originals Receipt, completes in 5-7 hours, Assessment starts earlier Tuesday, and Decision occurs late Tuesday or early Wednesday, saving 6-18 hours. Base case: D2 starts Tuesday morning, takes 6-8 hours, Assessment timing is almost unchanged, and cycle time changes by roughly -2 to +4 hours. Worst case: D2 cannot resolve handwriting, issues a clarification request, external response takes 8-36 hours, and repeated Document/Assessment work adds 12-48 hours or more.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The conclusion would reverse if D2’s actual queue is longer than forecast, if handwriting clarification rates are high, if D2 errors require rework, or if A2 queue prevents earlier Assessment despite earlier Document completion. Legibility scores or historical D2 rework rates would be decisive.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The case entered repeated rework because supplier evidence was inconsistent. Assessment 1 requested corrected information, and Assessment 2 requested another confirmation. The second external response crossed a weekend, causing a large calendar delay. Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 then occurred before Compliance Review and Decision.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop and require a decision using the evidence already available. The process may approve with conditions, reject, or formally accept risk, but it may not silently ignore the unresolved discrepancy.

Temporal propagation: The intervention removes the second external wait, Document Check 3, Assessment 3, and their queue exposures. Compliance Review can begin after Assessment 2 on Friday morning. Because one inconsistency remains, C1 may need 4-6 hours rather than the lower end of the complete-evidence range. If C1 and M1 can act Friday, Decision and Notify may occur Friday afternoon. If weekend work is unavailable or M1 requires escalation, Decision may move to Monday. The main propagation is therefore from avoided external delay and avoided rework to earlier compliance and decision, with a possible increase in decision complexity.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible under the intervention. The first Request More Info was followed by a new Document Check and new Assessment, satisfying Rule 7. High-risk/high-value status requires Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment; the counterfactual places C1 after Assessment 2 and before Decision. Four-eyes is preserved because A1 performs Assessment 2 and C1 performs Compliance Review. The intervention changes decision policy but does not remove mandatory compliance review.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: C1 reviews Friday afternoon, M1 decides Friday evening, and the case finishes about 3-4.5 business days earlier. Base case: C1 and M1 act Monday, saving roughly 2 business days. Worst case: the unresolved discrepancy makes compliance review longer, M1 requires senior risk acceptance, or the decision becomes rejection/conditional approval with appeal risk; cycle-time gain may shrink and post-decision exposure may rise.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The conclusion would reverse if the unresolved discrepancy is legally material, if regulators or contracts require supplier confirmation, if conditional approvals generate high appeal or loss rates, or if C1/M1 cannot defensibly decide without the second confirmation. Historical outcome data on conditional approvals would be critical.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The legacy system created only the Identity task, and a case lock prevented Document task ownership until Identity completed. Although no originals were required and concurrent verification was allowed, the case was processed sequentially.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create both Identity and Document tasks. Use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can own and work their tasks concurrently. Require real-time timestamps rather than later batch entry.

Temporal propagation: The intervention moves Document Check into the D2 queue earlier. I2 and D2 can work in parallel, so Document Check no longer waits for Identity completion. If both checks complete by Tuesday mid-afternoon instead of Tuesday evening, the case becomes assessment-ready earlier. However, the Assessment queue may be congested. The factual Assessment started Wednesday 09:30; the counterfactual may start Wednesday morning, but the gain may be limited if A2 queue is binding. Thus the intervention reduces verification serial time but may not reduce end-to-end time one-for-one.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible. Register and Triage occur first. No originals are required, so Identity and Document may run concurrently. Both checks complete before Assessment. The case is not high-risk or high-value, so no Compliance Review is required. Decision precedes Notify. Real-time timestamps preserve auditability; batch-entered timestamps would undermine the intervention.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: both checks complete by Tuesday 15:00-16:00, Assessment starts Wednesday 08:00-09:00, Decision occurs around midday Wednesday, saving 4-6 hours. Base case: checks complete a few hours earlier, Assessment start improves slightly, saving 1-3 hours. Worst case: assessment queue is saturated, workaround creates support issues, or logging is poor; cycle-time gain is zero or slightly negative due to audit hold.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The conclusion would reverse if D2 cannot actually take the task until Identity completes, if timestamps show backdating, if assessment queue depth increases without reducing final cycle time, or if the workaround produces audit exceptions. Concurrent-work evidence and assessment queue depth would be decisive.

If applied to 100 similar cases in one week, the interventions interact with the single certified compliance reviewer, two document checkers, and assessment queues. For K-104, reserving provisional C1 slots for many high-risk/high-value cases converts C1 from a first-come-first-served bottleneck into a reservation system. Target cases may finish final compliance earlier, but non-target urgent compliance cases are displaced into later slots. If preliminary packets are incomplete, reserved C1 time is wasted, reducing effective weekly C1 capacity. The policy is only beneficial when C1 slack exists or preliminary work materially shortens final reviews.

For K-219, shifting handwritten standard cases from D1 to D2 exploits D2's shorter queue but increases per-case document hours. At scale, D2 may become the new bottleneck, while D1 frees capacity for complex corporate documents. The net effect depends on handwriting quality: if D2 clarification rates rise, avoided D1 waiting is overwhelmed by rework loops, new document checks, and repeated assessments. A dynamic routing rule based on legibility score would be safer than blanket D2 assignment.

For K-337, suppressing a second information loop avoids weekend external delays and saves D1/A1 rework capacity. However, it changes the decision policy. Across 100 slow-supplier cases, some discrepancies will be material. Conditional approvals and rejections may reduce cycle time but increase compliance review complexity, manager decision time, appeals, and potential loss exposure. Weekend effects make the factual delay large, so measured cycle-time gains will be attractive, but tail risk may migrate to legal, monitoring, or supplier-relationship processes not visible in the event log.

For K-411, parallel verification can scale well for digital standard cases because it removes an artificial dependency. The main system-wide risk is synchronized arrivals to assessment. If 100 cases complete identity and document checks earlier in the same day, A1/A2 queues may grow, and the benefit becomes queue waiting rather than true labor savings. Real-time timestamps are essential; batch entry would create false concurrency and obscure whether the workaround actually increased simultaneous workload. The most transportable rollout is therefore K-411, with monitoring of assessment queue depth.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411, parallel verification with controlled task creation and real-time timestamps. It removes an artificial dependency in low-risk digital cases, does not change the decision policy, and preserves compliance rules. Its benefit is modest but likely positive, and the main adverse effect—earlier arrival at assessment—is observable and manageable through queue monitoring. It is also easy to reverse if the workaround creates audit issues.

The most dangerous intervention is K-337, suppressing the second rework loop. It can produce the largest apparent cycle-time reduction because it eliminates weekend external waiting and repeated D1/A1 work. However, it changes what decisions are allowed on incomplete evidence. The time saving can be offset by conditional approvals, rejections, appeals, loss exposure, and weaker compliance defensibility. Because the decision is hard to reverse after notification, this intervention needs strong governance rather than routine process acceleration.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104. In a single case, reserving a provisional C1 slot looks beneficial because the target case avoids the compliance queue. At system scale, C1 is a scarce bottleneck; reservations displace other urgent cases and can waste capacity if preliminary packets are incomplete. The local gain depends heavily on queue priority rules not visible in the case record.

The most valuable additional data are time-stamped queue entries and exits for each resource, reservation/no-show records for C1, reason codes for document rework and handwriting clarification, external supplier response times, assessment queue depth by hour, and audit logs showing whether concurrency timestamps reflect real work. Linking case outcomes—appeals, losses, condition breaches—to process paths would allow speed-quality trade-offs to be evaluated rather than inferred from cycle time alone.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. structural: The controlled workaround for K-411 permits both Identity and Document tasks to be owned concurrently without violating audit rules, provided timestamps are recorded in real time.

2. temporal: Queue snapshots are forecasts, not guarantees; actual start times depend on predecessor completion, resource availability, and queue displacement.

3. resource: C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer, so any provisional C1 reservation consumes scarce capacity and may displace other urgent compliance cases.

4. resource: D2 can start handwritten standard documents earlier than D1 but is likely to require more service time and may need clarification.

5. behavioral: S1 preliminary escalation packets are useful only if Triage provides enough sanctions evidence; incomplete packets will not materially reduce final C1 work.

6. quality: Suppressing the second Request More Info loop in K-337 does not eliminate the unresolved discrepancy; it converts the discrepancy into a documented conditional approval, rejection, or formal risk acceptance.

7. interference: Earlier completion of Identity and Document checks can shift waiting from verification queues into the assessment queue, especially when many cases are parallelized.

8. temporal: External supplier response and weekend non-working periods dominate rework cycle time, so avoiding a second Request More Info loop removes a large calendar delay.
