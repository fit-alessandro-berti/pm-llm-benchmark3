# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## 1.1 Material assumptions

1. **Trace = case.** Events are timestamp-ordered; hours are relative to case start. `Close Onboarding` occurs exactly once and is always the last event.
2. **Outcome vocabulary.** An *accepted* case is one containing `Activate Vendor`; a *rejected* case is one containing `Reject Vendor`. By R1 these sets are disjoint, so "accepted vs. rejected" is decidable from activities alone.
3. **Route inference.** The log carries no risk-tier attribute. The *applicable approval* is inferred from presence: `Manager Approval`  low-risk route, `Compliance Approval` (+ `Enhanced Due Diligence`)  high-risk route.
4. **Escalation placement.** R7 only says `Sanctions Escalation` must follow `Run Sanctions Screening`. In all test evidence, escalation occurs after `Classify Risk` and before `Enhanced Due Diligence`, and only on the high-risk route. I adopt this as a **design assumption**: escalation implies the escalated high-risk path. This is narrower than the literal requirements and is disclosed as a conservatism.
5. **Clarification window.** R3 requires an answer "before any final outcome". The tests only show clarification between completion of the three initial checks and `Classify Risk`. I narrow the window to exactly that segment (conservative; structurally guarantees R3's "before final outcome" clause).
6. **Post-activation life.** R11 presupposes that `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / re-verification can occur after activation, although no test case exercises this. The backbone model includes a conservative post-activation loop.
7. **Cardinalities.** R10 is read literally: `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, `Release First Payment` at most once; `Classify Risk` and `Close Onboarding` exactly once.
8. **Temporal test.** R12 is evaluated two-sided: a deviation is flagged when `|  mean| > zeta · ` with `zeta = 2.0`.
9. **Coincidence noted.** For `Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion`, `mean + zeta· = 12h + 12h = 24h`, which numerically coincides with R6's emergency window. Under Resolution B both checks therefore align; this is a coincidence of the numbers, not a design principle.
10. **Resources.** `org:resource` values are person identifiers; no role model is provided, so segregation-of-duties is checked by performer *inequality*, not role membership.
11. **No withdrawal path.** Once `Manager Approval` or `Compliance Approval` is granted, the case commits to activation; no "approved but abandoned" behavior is modeled.
12. **Normative metadata.** All DECLARE support/confidence values are hand-set to 1.0 because the rules are policy, not mined statistics (see §9.6).

## 1.2 The R5/R6 conflict

- **R5 (Legal):** after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` **under all circumstances**.
- **R6 (Operations):** emergency activation may precede the opinion, but **only** with joint authorization recorded *outside the event log*, and the opinion must arrive within 24 h. The log contains neither an emergency flag nor the authorization.

These are genuinely incompatible as stated: R5 admits no exception; R6 creates one whose triggering conditions are not observable in the data. They must **not** be merged silently into one hybrid rule.

### Resolution A — "Strict legal supremacy" (IMPLEMENTED)

R5 is absolute inside the system. The procedural backbone contains an escalated branch in which `Obtain Legal Opinion` structurally precedes `Activate Vendor`. Emergency activation is not representable: if it happens, it happens outside the governed process and the trace is automatically nonconformant. R6 is reduced to a documented, dormant external check (see §7) that is switched off.

*Compliance risk of A:* genuine emergencies produce guaranteed false alarms  alert fatigue and incentives for shadow processing outside the system, which destroys auditability. The model is also stricter than the block-order freedom the policy grants (e.g., `SE  LO  EDD  CA  AV` is legal per R5 but rejected by the fixed branch order — a disclosed conservatism). Mitigation: a fast-track manual review queue and a governed model-change procedure.

### Resolution B — "Codified emergency exception"

The escalated branch is relaxed to permit `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion`, and enforcement moves entirely to the data-aware layer: a case may activate early **iff** (i) an external joint-authorization record exists for the case, and (ii) `LO.timestamp  SE.timestamp  24 h`. Because the log lacks the authorization attribute, every early activation is *policy-dependent*: the automated default is **deny** (fail) until the authorization register confirms.

*Compliance risk of B:* the authorization evidence is unverifiable post hoc, so the exception can degenerate into a rubber stamp; the 24 h window becomes a de facto deadline that normalizes lateness; auditors face mixed policy provenance. Requires integration with the external authorization register before it is safe.

### Resolution C — "Explicit dual track" (mentioned, not implemented)

Model two distinct activities (e.g., `Activate Vendor` vs. `Activate Vendor (Emergency)`) or a dedicated case attribute. Fully explicit, but pollutes the label space, forks every downstream rule, and was rejected to keep the visible activity labels intact.

**Chosen for the implemented model: Resolution A**, because enforcing R6 without observable evidence would automate wishful thinking. Resolution B is retained as a configuration switch, clearly separated in §7.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description

```
Start       : Receive Onboarding Request                        (first, exactly once)
Parallel    : Capture Master Data  Run Sanctions Screening  Verify Bank Account
Clarify     : (Request Clarification  Receive Clarification)*  (zero or more cycles)
Gate        : Classify Risk                                     (exactly once)
Outcome     : Reject Vendor  Close Onboarding
            | Manager Approval  AcceptTail
            | Enhanced Due Diligence  Compliance Approval  AcceptTail
            | Sanctions Escalation  Enhanced Due Diligence  Obtain Legal Opinion
                                    Compliance Approval  AcceptTail          (strict R5)
AcceptTail  : Activate Vendor  (Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor
                                  Verify Bank Account)*  Release First Payment
                                  Close Onboarding
```

## 3.2 PM4Py code (`procedural_model`)

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def _node(operator=None, label=None, children=()):
    """Create a ProcessTree node and attach (fresh) children."""
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, label=label)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
        node.children.append(child)
    return node


def act(label):
    return _node(label=label)


def tau():
    return _node()  # silent step


def clarification_loop():
    # *(tau, (RC -> RCv))  ==  zero or more clarification cycles
    return _node(Operator.LOOP, children=[
        tau(),
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Request Clarification"),
            act("Receive Clarification"),
        ]),
    ])


def post_activation_loop():
    # Zero or more bank-change cycles (R11, conservative ordered form)
    return _node(Operator.LOOP, children=[
        tau(),
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Change Bank Details"),
            act("Suspend Vendor"),
            act("Verify Bank Account"),
        ]),
    ])


def acceptance_tail():
    return _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
        act("Activate Vendor"),
        post_activation_loop(),
        act("Release First Payment"),
        act("Close Onboarding"),
    ])


procedural_model = _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    act("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    _node(Operator.PARALLEL, children=[
        act("Capture Master Data"),
        act("Run Sanctions Screening"),
        act("Verify Bank Account"),
    ]),
    clarification_loop(),
    act("Classify Risk"),
    _node(Operator.XOR, children=[
        # --- terminal outcome 1: rejection (no approvals, R2) ---
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Reject Vendor"),
            act("Close Onboarding"),
        ]),
        # --- terminal outcome 2: low-risk route ---
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Manager Approval"),
            acceptance_tail(),
        ]),
        # --- terminal outcome 3: high-risk route, no escalation ---
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
            act("Compliance Approval"),
            acceptance_tail(),
        ]),
        # --- terminal outcome 4: escalated high-risk route, STRICT R5 ---
        _node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            act("Sanctions Escalation"),
            act("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
            act("Obtain Legal Opinion"),
            act("Compliance Approval"),
            acceptance_tail(),
        ]),
    ]),
])

# Canonical executable form (used for alignment-based conformance):
# net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(procedural_model)
```

## 3.3 Explanation

- **Concurrency.** `PARALLEL` over the three checks implements backbone step 2; any interleaving (e.g., T2's `RSS, VBA, CMD`) is accepted.
- **Choice.** A flat 4-way `XOR` after `Classify Risk` implements backbone step 4 *and* R1/R2 structurally: the reject branch contains no approval, the low branch exactly `Manager Approval`, the high branches exactly `EDD  Compliance Approval`. No trace can contain two terminal outcomes.
- **Clarification loops.** The idiom `LOOP(, SEQ(RC, RCv))` denotes *zero or more* request/response cycles (workflow loops demand at least one `do` execution, hence the silent first iteration). Because the loop sits strictly between the parallel checks and `Classify Risk`, R3's "before any final outcome" clause holds by construction. **Disclosed approximation:** clarifications are forbidden anywhere else (e.g., after classification); the requirements permit but the tests never show such placements.
- **Terminal outcomes.** All three endings converge on the shared label `Close Onboarding`, emitted exactly once at the end of whichever branch fires.
- **Post-activation behavior — included.** `AcceptTail` embeds `LOOP(, SEQ(CBD, SV, VBA))`, a conservative rendering of R11: every bank change forces a suspension and a fresh verification before the (single) first payment. **Deliberately excluded:** the R6 emergency path (Resolution A), clarification after classification, re-running sanctions screening, and multiple payments. Each exclusion is a policy decision, not an oversight.
- **Block-structure approximations (disclosed).** (i) The escalated branch fixes the total order `SE  EDD  LO  CA  AV`; R5 only demands `LO` before `AV`, so a trace `SE  LO  EDD  CA  AV` is legal per policy but rejected by the tree — conservative. (ii) `SE` is only reachable on the escalated high-risk branch; a hypothetical "escalate then reject" case is not representable. Both approximations are compensated by the DECLARE/LS/data layers, which remain order-tolerant where the tree is strict.

## 4.1 Rule semantics table

| Rule | Direction | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `init(ROR)` | unary | Every case **starts** with `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `existence(CMD/RSS/VBA)` | unary | Each initial check occurs **at least once**. |
| `exactly_one(CR, Close)` | unary | Occurs **exactly once** per case. |
| `absence(n=2)(AV/RV/RFP)` | unary | At most **one** occurrence (R10). |
| `noncoexistence(RV, AV)` | symmetric binary | No case contains both — R1. Chosen over `nonsuccession`, which would only forbid one direction (`RV` before `AV`) and wrongly allow `AV` then `RV` (exactly T8's shape). |
| `noncoexistence(RV, MA)`, `(RV, CA)` | symmetric binary | Rejected cases contain no approval — R2 second clause. |
| `noncoexistence(MA, CA)`, `(EDD, MA)` | symmetric binary | Never both approvals; never EDD with MA — R2/R8. |
| `response(SE, LO)` | forward obligation | Every `Sanctions Escalation` is **eventually followed** by `Obtain Legal Opinion` — R4. No immediacy demanded (unlike `chainresponse`, which would require LO as the *very next* event and is falsified by legitimate interleavings such as T7's `EDD` between them). |
| `response(MA, AV)`, `(CA, AV)` | forward obligation | An granted approval commits the case to eventual activation (assumption 11). |
| `response(AV, RFP)`, `(RV, Close)`, `(RFP, Close)` | forward obligation | Backbone step 5 as eventual-follow obligations. |
| `response(CBD, SV)`, `(CBD, VBA)` | forward obligation | R11 fragments: a bank change obliges a suspension and a re-verification. |
| `altresponse(RC, RCv)` | forward, alternating | Every `Request Clarification` is answered by `Receive Clarification` **before the next request** (and the last one before the trace ends). This is precisely R3's cycling clause; it *subsumes* plain `response`, which is therefore not duplicated. |
| `succession(EDD, CA)` | bidirectional composite | `response` + `precedence`: EDD  eventual CA **and** CA only after EDD. Captures the high-risk approval pairing in one template. |
| `precedence(RSS, SE)` | backward guard | Every `Sanctions Escalation` must be **preceded** by `Run Sanctions Screening` — R7. |
| `precedence(CR, AV/RV/MA/EDD)` | backward guard | Nothing outcome-relevant happens before `Classify Risk`. |
| `precedence(VBA, RFP)` | backward guard | Every payment is preceded by *some* bank verification. **Deliberately weak:** it is satisfied by the initial verification, so it does **not** capture R11's "fresh verification after a bank change" — that condition lives in the procedural loop and the data layer. |

**Not encoded, deliberately:** a DECLARE rule forcing `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Activate Vendor`. `precedence(LO, AV)` would be global and would illegally force LO in low-risk cases; DECLARE has no *conditional* precedence template. R5 is therefore enforced procedurally (§3) with a data-layer safety net (§7) — this is exactly why DECLARE **passes T6** while the procedural model fails it (§8).

## 5.1 Orientation and overlap notes

- **Orientation convention.** `always_before(A, B)`: whenever **B** occurs, **A** occurred earlier (arrow points from earlier to later). `always_after(A, B)`: whenever **A** occurs, **B** eventually follows. `directly_follows(A, B)`: **B** is the *immediate* successor of **A**. `equivalence` and `never_together` are symmetric.
- **Deliberate omissions.** `always_before(LO, AV)` and `always_before(MA, AV)` / `always_before(CA, AV)` are **excluded**: they are unconditional and would illegally force LO/MA/CA into every activating case. Like DECLARE, the log skeleton cannot express *conditional* precedence — R5 stays procedural.
- **Overlap with DECLARE (agreement, by design).** `always_after(SE, LO)`  `response(SE, LO)`; `always_before(RSS, SE)`  `precedence(RSS, SE)`; `never_together(*)`  `noncoexistence(*)`; `always_after(CBD, SV)`  `response(CBD, SV)`. These duplicates are defense in depth, not accidents.
- **Stricter-than-DECLARE entries.** `directly_follows` adds *immediacy*: e.g., `DF(CA, AV)` is stronger than `response(CA, AV)`. Each DF pair was checked to be adjacency-true in **every** compliant test (universal semantics: every occurrence of the source must be directly followed by the target). Pairs like `(Classify Risk, Manager Approval)` were **rejected** precisely because they fail high-risk traces. Two DF pairs — `(SE, EDD)` and `(LO, CA)` — encode the *implemented strict branch order*, which is tighter than policy; they are the log-skeleton mirror of the §3 approximation and will fire on T6 (correctly, under Resolution A) but would also fire on a policy-legal `SE  LO  EDD  …` variant (conservatism, see §9).
- **Cardinality overlap.** `activ_freq({0,1})` for AV/RV/RFP duplicates DECLARE `absence(2)`; the log skeleton is authoritative because its count semantics are unambiguous. Bounds `{0,1,2,3}` for clarification activities and `{1,2,3}` for re-verifications are operational caps, not policy — flagged as arbitrary in §9.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * HOUR, 24 * HOUR),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * HOUR, 6 * HOUR),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * HOUR, 36 * HOUR),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

**Evaluation rule.** For each completed pair `(A, B)` in a trace, compute ` = t(B)  t(A)`; flag a *temporal deviation* iff `|  mean| > zeta · `.

**Why warnings, not control-flow violations.**

1. **R12 says so.** Compliance explicitly grades these expectations as "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject". The temporal layer therefore emits *warnings* that enter a triage queue; a case is never auto-rejected on timing alone.
2. **Statistical humility.** A zeta-band of 2 is roughly a 95% interval under normality; legitimate tails (weekends, vendor slowness, backlog) fall outside it without being misconduct.
3. **Context blindness.** The profile is pairwise and route-agnostic: it cannot distinguish a slow clarification in a contested high-risk case from negligence. Hard consequences require corroboration from other layers (e.g., T7's late opinion is a warning *plus* a clean ordering record).
4. **Incomplete pairs are invisible.** If `B` never occurs (T5's unanswered request, T8's missing payment), the profile is vacuously silent — the *obligation* layers (DECLARE/LS/procedural) must carry that detection. This division of labor is deliberate and revisited in §9.

Per-test outcomes: T1 =71 h (band 72±72) pass; T2 =26 h (48±48) and =84 h (72±72) pass; T6 =17 h (12±24) and =77 h pass — notably T6 *also* satisfies R6's 24 h emergency window; T7 =34 h vs. 12±24  **warning** (22 h > 24 h band edge? no: 22 h deviation vs. 24 h allowed — wait, `zeta· = 12 h`, so 22 > 12  deviation flagged), while (AVRFP)=2 h (deviation 70  72) barely passes.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

**Required event attributes:** `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource` (event level); plus an **external** case-level authorization register for R6 (explicitly *not* in the log).

**Missing-attribute policy (fail-safe).**

| Missing | Effect |
|---|---|
| `org:resource` on a relevant event | Check result `UNRESOLVED`  manual review queue; counted as **fail** in automated KPIs. Never silently passed. |
| `time:timestamp` | Temporal checks skip the pair; ordering checks fall back to event-sequence index with a data-quality warning. |
| `case:concept:name` | Whole case quarantined; no verdict issued. |
| Authorization register entry (R6) | Verdict `POLICY-DEPENDENT`, automated default **deny**. |

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

ACT = "concept:name"
CASE = "case:concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"


def check_sod_approval_vs_verification(df):
    """R9a: resource(Verify Bank Account) != resource(applicable approval).
    'Applicable approval' = Manager Approval if present, else Compliance Approval;
    if both are present the case is already nonconformant (R2/R8)."""
    verdicts = {}
    for case_id, tr in df.groupby(CASE):
        vba = tr[tr[ACT] == "Verify Bank Account"]
        appr = tr[tr[ACT].isin(["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"])]
        if vba.empty or appr.empty:
            continue                                    # vacuous (e.g., T3)
        kinds = set(appr[ACT].unique())
        if len(kinds) > 1:
            verdicts[case_id] = "VIOLATION (both approval types present)"
            continue
        approvers = set(appr[RES].dropna())
        verifiers = set(vba[RES].dropna())
        if vba[RES].isna().any() or not approvers:
            verdicts[case_id] = "UNRESOLVED (missing org:resource)"
        elif approvers & verifiers:
            verdicts[case_id] = "VIOLATION (four-eyes breach)"   # T4: Cora/Cora
        else:
            verdicts[case_id] = "pass"
    return verdicts

# Built-in screening alternative (keeps conforming cases; complement = suspects).
# It does not know the MA-xor-CA disjunction, so run it twice and union the
# complements; the custom check above remains authoritative:
# ok_ma = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")
# ok_ca = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")


def check_sod_capture_vs_activation(df):
    """R9b: resource(Capture Master Data) != resource(Activate Vendor)."""
    verdicts = {}
    for case_id, tr in df.groupby(CASE):
        cmd = tr[tr[ACT] == "Capture Master Data"]
        av = tr[tr[ACT] == "Activate Vendor"]
        if cmd.empty or av.empty:
            continue
        if cmd[RES].isna().any() or av[RES].isna().any():
            verdicts[case_id] = "UNRESOLVED (missing org:resource)"
        elif set(cmd[RES]) & set(av[RES]):
            verdicts[case_id] = "VIOLATION (same person captured data and activated)"
        else:
            verdicts[case_id] = "pass"
    return verdicts


def check_escalation_activation_order(df, resolution="strict",
                                      authorization_register=None):
    """R5/R6 conditional ordering -- the rule DECLARE cannot express.

    resolution='strict'   (Resolution A, implemented): if Sanctions Escalation
                          and Activate Vendor both occur, Obtain Legal Opinion
                          must lie strictly between them.
    resolution='emergency'(Resolution B): early activation allowed ONLY with an
                          external joint-authorization record AND opinion within
                          24h of escalation. Missing authorization => default deny.
    """
    verdicts = {}
    for case_id, tr in df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE):
        if "Sanctions Escalation" not in set(tr[ACT]) or \
           "Activate Vendor" not in set(tr[ACT]):
            continue                                    # rule vacuous
        t_se = tr.loc[tr[ACT] == "Sanctions Escalation", TS].iloc[0]
        t_av = tr.loc[tr[ACT] == "Activate Vendor", TS].iloc[0]
        lo_ts = tr.loc[tr[ACT] == "Obtain Legal Opinion", TS]

        if resolution == "strict":
            ok = (not lo_ts.empty) and bool(lo_ts.iloc[0] < t_av)
            verdicts[case_id] = "pass" if ok else \
                "VIOLATION (R5: activation before legal opinion)"   # T6
        else:  # Resolution B
            authorized = bool(authorization_register.get(case_id, False)) \
                if authorization_register else False
            if not authorized:
                verdicts[case_id] = "POLICY-DEPENDENT (no joint authorization " \
                                    "on record; default deny)"
            elif lo_ts.empty:
                verdicts[case_id] = "OPEN (authorized; opinion pending <24h watch)"
            else:
                delta_h = (lo_ts.iloc[0] - t_se).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                verdicts[case_id] = "pass (emergency)" if delta_h <= 24.0 else \
                    f"VIOLATION (opinion late: {delta_h:.1f}h > 24h)"
    return verdicts


def check_post_activation_bank_change(df):
    """R11 residual check (for traces whose post-activation behavior the
    procedural loop did not observe): after any Change Bank Details there must
    be a Suspend Vendor, and a fresh Verify Bank Account before any later
    Release First Payment."""
    verdicts = {}
    for case_id, tr in df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE):
        cbd = tr[tr[ACT] == "Change Bank Details"]
        if cbd.empty:
            continue
        t_cbd = cbd[TS].max()
        problems = []
        if tr[(tr[ACT] == "Suspend Vendor") & (tr[TS] > t_cbd)].empty:
            problems.append("no Suspend Vendor after Change Bank Details")
        later_rfp = tr[(tr[ACT] == "Release First Payment") & (tr[TS] > t_cbd)]
        later_vba = tr[(tr[ACT] == "Verify Bank Account") & (tr[TS] > t_cbd)]
        if not later_rfp.empty and \
           later_vba[later_vba[TS] < later_rfp[TS].min()].empty:
            problems.append("no re-verification before later Release First Payment")
        verdicts[case_id] = "pass" if not problems else "VIOLATION: " + "; ".join(problems)
    return verdicts
```

These four functions are the *only* place where R5's conditional precedence, R6, R9, and R11's data-condition are decidable. Every activity-only layer in §§3–6 is structurally blind to them.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**9.1 Duplicated enforcement.** R1 lives in three layers, R3 in three, R4/R7/R8/R10/R11 in at least two. This is intentional defense in depth — declarative layers localize violations cheaply, the procedural layer is decisive — but it creates *drift risk*: if Resolution B were adopted, the H2 branch, `DF(LO,CA)`, `DF(SE,EDD)`, and the data-layer check would all need synchronized edits. A single policy registry (requirement ID  owning layer) is mandatory maintenance infrastructure.

**9.2 Instability under loops and post-activation changes.** The `activ_freq` caps (`{0,1,2,3}` for clarifications, `{1,2,3}` for verifications) are operational guesses; a legitimate fourth clarification cycle would be flagged. `equivalence(RC, RCv)` assumes answers arrive as logged events — an answer given by phone breaks the equality even though the business outcome is fine. The `directly_follows` set is brittle against benign insertions (notes, attachments): `DF(CA, AV)` fails if any system event slips between approval and activation. Conversely, R11 degenerates if `Release First Payment` was already consumed (it is capped at one): "another VBA before any *later* RFP" becomes unsatisfiable-in-vacuo, and the policy should state whether a post-change payment requires re-activation.

**9.3 Vacuity in declarative rules.** `response(SE, LO)` never fires in non-escalated cases; `precedence(RSS, SE)` is unfalsifiable in cases without escalation; the whole DECLARE model passes T4 — a manifestly bad case — because every rule's antecedent is activity-shaped. Vacuous satisfaction is not evidence of health; coverage reporting (how many cases actually exercised each rule) should accompany conformance scores. Similarly, `precedence(VBA, RFP)` is satisfied by the *initial* verification and therefore systematically under-enforces R11 — a known, accepted weakness because the strong form is structural.

**9.4 Missing case attributes.** The log lacks: risk tier (so "was the low-risk route appropriate?" is undecidable — we infer the route from the approval type), the R6 emergency/joint-authorization flag, the sanctions-list hit reason driving escalation, clarification topic, and vendor category. Without these, several policy questions are answerable only as `policy-dependent`, and Resolution B cannot be automated at all.

**9.5 Timestamp-only limitations.** All reasoning rests on pairwise event times: no activity durations, no external-world lead times (mail, correspondent banks), exposure to clock skew and timezone mixing, hourly granularity making the 24 h emergency window coarse at boundaries. "Eventually" in R4/R3 has no deadline except the soft R12 band — a case could answer a clarification after a year and pass every hard layer.

**9.6 Support/confidence metadata is not truth.** Here every rule is hand-authored with `support = confidence = 1.0` because it *is* policy. If the DECLARE model were instead mined from history, a 0.97-confidence `response(SE, LO)` would merely describe past behavior — likely polluted by exactly the T4/T6-style cases we must reject — and treating mined supports as norms would launder deviance into doctrine. Keep the mined model and the policy model as separate artifacts; never promote one into the other without a governance decision.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation.** Keep the process tree of §3 as the human-readable source of truth and deploy its compiled accepting Petri net (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`) for alignment-based conformance, which yields both a definitive fitness verdict and precise error localization (the missing `Receive Clarification` in T5, the misplaced `Obtain Legal Opinion` in T6).

**Authoritative policy layer.** Control flow: the procedural backbone. Everything involving resources, external evidence, or conditional precedence (R5 safety net, R6, R9, R11 residuals): the data-aware layer of §7. DECLARE and the log skeleton are *screening and diagnostics*, not courts of final appeal; the temporal profile is advisory by charter (R12).

**Check order.**

1. **Schema gate** — required attributes present; quarantine cases with missing case IDs.
2. **Cheap declarative screen** — `init`/`existence`/`exactly_one`/`absence`/`noncoexistence`/`activ_freq` for instant triage (kills T8-class cases in milliseconds).
3. **Procedural alignment** — decisive fitness verdict with diagnostics.
4. **Resource/data checks** — SoD, R5/R6 ordering, R11 residuals (parallelizable with 3).
5. **Temporal scoring** — attach warnings with z-scores.
6. **Aggregation** — severity tiers: *hard fail* (procedural, resource/data), *soft* (temporal), *unresolved* (missing attributes), *policy-dependent* (R6 under B).

**Surfacing contradictions.** Conflicting layer verdicts are a feature: T6 (DECLARE pass vs. procedural/data fail) and T4 (everything green vs. resource fail) must be presented **side by side, never averaged**. Each finding carries its provenance triple — requirement ID, owning layer, stakeholder (Legal/Operations/Compliance) — and, for R5/R6, the active resolution switch. Contradictions route to a compliance officer with both rule texts displayed; the dashboard states explicitly which verdict is binding and why (e.g., "DECLARE cannot express conditional precedence; the procedural verdict governs"). Under Resolution B, T6-class cases are rendered as *pending external authorization evidence* with an automatic default-deny timer, so silence can never be mistaken for approval.
