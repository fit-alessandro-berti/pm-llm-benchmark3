The raw summary shows large group differences, but raw parity is the wrong target on its own: groups differ in case mix, and the log contains no ground-truth measure of substantive eligibility. The assessment therefore has to separate *outcome parity*, *adjusted outcome disparity*, and — most importantly here — *procedural* dimensions, which the outcome table does not measure at all.

| Dimension | What the evidence shows | Fairness reading |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome parity (raw) | Approval 78 / 63 / 69 / 58 % (AD) | Not interpretable as fairness; case-mix shares differ sharply (high risk 18 %51 %, incomplete docs 21 %46 %, accommodation 4 %62 %) |
| Adjusted outcome disparity | Approval 5.5 / 2.2 / 3.1 pp; cycle time +2.6 / +4.1 / +0.9 days; appeal +4.4 / +1.1 / +5.7 pp | Residual gaps persist after standard controls; size and precision differ by metric |
| Procedural burden | V4 = 24 % of B (1 % of A), V5 = 31 % of C (2 % of A); +9.5 pp Request Missing Documents (B), +8.0 pp Supervisor Review (B); extra handovers for B and C | Clear group-linked burden differences; burden is concentrated on rights-preserving steps |
| Timeliness | Adjusted +2.6 d (B), +4.1 d (C), +0.9 d (D); support-step queues of 3.8 d and 4.7 d with no A counterpart | Largest and most precise residual disparities are in time, not in approvals |
| Consistency of routing | DV3 fraud routing 28 % vs 9 %, consistent with 51 % high risk; BV4 and CV5 routing follows stated rights | Mostly consistent with policy — *but* consistency must be checked for under-routing too (needs recorded, step not taken) |
| Quality / accessibility of support | Language support and accommodation review exist and are used; no attribute captures explanation quality, comprehension, dignity, or whether support was *effective* | Essentially unmeasured — a major blind spot, not a pass |
| Appeal / rework burden | Appeal 6 / 13 / 10 / 16 %; rework 11 / 22 / 24 / 20 %; D appeal residual +5.7 pp | B and C carry ~2× the rework burden with no adjusted analysis; D appeals most even after risk control |
| Resource-allocation fairness | A spread over 52 caseworkers; B concentrated in 7 bilingual + 3 regional officers; C in 5 specialists (2 also supervising) | Non-substitutable specialist pools with higher implied per-person load and no queue protection |

**Outcome parity.** Approval gaps of 15–20 pp raw are *not* evidence of discrimination, for two reasons: (i) documented complexity differs by roughly a factor of three on several axes; (ii) there is no merit benchmark in the log — `case_outcome` is a decision, not a verified eligibility label. Equal approval rates could itself be unfair if true eligibility prevalence differs. Parity on approval is therefore an alert trigger, not a verdict.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** Adjustment absorbs much of the raw gap (B: 15  5.5 pp; D: 20  3.1 pp), which is the expected signature of case-mix differences. Residuals remain. Rough precision checks (model standard errors were not reported, so these are indicative only): with n = 1250 vs 3600, a 5.5 pp approval difference is on the order of 3–4 standard errors and unlikely to be noise; for Group C (n = 820), 2.2 pp is on the order of 1 standard error and may be indistinguishable from zero. By contrast, the **cycle-time** residuals are very likely real: +4.1 days with n = 820 vs 3600 is many standard errors even with a highly dispersed duration distribution. **Conclusion: the timeliness findings are the statistically strongest; the small approval findings are the weakest.** This must be stated explicitly rather than presenting all cells of the adjusted table as equally reliable.

A further caution: the adjusted model controls `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`, both of which are flagged as potentially biased/endogenous (Section 5). Controlling for a mediator or a biased proxy can *shrink* the very disparity one is trying to measure. The adjusted table should therefore be read as one point in a sensitivity range, not as "the" disparity.

**Procedural burden.** This is where the evidence is strongest and where outcome-focused analysis is blind. Group B and Group C cases are routed into additional steps at rates 10–15× Group A's rate for V4/V5, those steps have the longest median cycle times in the log (19.2 d, 21.6 d vs 5.8 d for V1), and those steps have no counterpart wait for Group A. Crucially, the policy itself classifies these as *rights-preserving* — so the disparity is not in whether the right is granted, but in what the right *costs the applicant in time, handovers, and re-explanations.*

**Timeliness.** Post-decision handling is near parity (Decision  Notification: 0.5 / 0.8 / 0.7 / 0.5 d), so the disparity is not a notification or activation failure — it is concentrated upstream, in intake and assessment queues. Two waits have no Group A analogue at all: Language Support Contact  Eligibility (3.8 d) and Accommodation Review  Eligibility (4.7 d). Those are the single largest procedural differentiators in the dataset.

**Consistency of routing.** Group D's routing (V3, 28 %) matches the stated rule that high `risk_score_band` requires Fraud Review, and Group C's V5 matches the accommodation rule. Consistency in this direction is a point in the process's favour. However, *consistency of routing into a step* is only half the test: the log must also be checked for cases with a recorded need where the step is absent (under-routing / unmet right) and for cases routed into the step without a recorded need (possible mis-routing or bias in recording). Group A's 2 % language-need and 4 % accommodation shares are low enough to raise — but not settle — the question of whether need is detected uniformly across groups.

**Quality and accessibility of support.** The log records that Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review *occur*, not whether they were competent, timely in the applicant's terms, in the applicant's language, or sufficient for the applicant to act. An applicant may "receive" a support step and still be unable to supply documents. This dimension is effectively unmeasured; absence of a disparity here cannot be claimed.

**Appeal and rework burden.** Rework (22 % / 24 % for B and C vs 11 % for A) is *not* included in the adjusted table at all — a gap in the analysis. It is a direct burden measure: it means the applicant must act again. Group D's +5.7 pp adjusted appeal probability despite only +0.9 days adjusted cycle time is an instructive dissociation: **time parity is not procedural parity.** D cases are processed about as fast as expected but are far more likely to be contested, which is consistent with either (a) contested risk/fraud routing and dissatisfaction with reasons, or (b) genuinely lower substantive merit. The log cannot distinguish these — `appeal_flag` carries no grounds, no outcome, and no timing.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** Order of magnitude: language-support need in B  57 % × 1250  712 cases over 7 bilingual caseworkers  **102 cases per bilingual caseworker**; accommodation  62 % × 820  508 cases over 5 specialists  **102 per specialist** (two of whom also carry Supervisor Review); Group A  3600 / 52  **69 per general caseworker**. Even allowing that these denominators are approximate and that other staff also touch these cases, the specialist pools appear to carry roughly **1.5× the per-person load**, are **non-substitutable** (a general caseworker cannot absorb a language-support or accommodation case), and are **not protected by any service-level target.** That combination — high mandatory load, zero substitutability, zero SLA — is a structural fairness problem independent of any individual decision-maker's behaviour.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The goal is to classify every observed gap into one of four buckets: **(1) legitimate routing**, **(2) residual disparity of unclear origin**, **(3) disparity consistent with unfair treatment**, **(4) statistical uncertainty.** No single model does this; a model *ladder* plus path- and queue-level analysis does.

## 2.1 Model ladder (nested, explicitly reported)

| Model | Covariates | Interpretation of the group coefficient |
|---|---|---|
| M0 | group only | Total observed disparity (raw gap) |
| M1 | + risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, month | "Case-mix-adjusted" disparity — *the preliminary table* |
| M2 | + language_preference, disability_accommodation_requested | **Removes legitimate rights-preserving routing** from the coefficient; the M1M2 drop quantifies how much of the gap is *explained by legitimate routing* |
| M3 | + variant / path fixed effects, + assigned resource pool, + region×month backlog | **Within-path, within-resource disparity** — the residual most suggestive of treatment differences rather than routing differences |

The key reporting rule: **report M1 and M2 side by side.** As specified, the preliminary model does **not** control for `language_preference` or `disabilty_accommodation_requested` — i.e., it deliberately (or accidentally) folds legitimate rights-preserving routing into the group coefficient. That inflates the B and C coefficients with justified work. M2 separates them. Conversely, M3 risks over-controlling: variant is partly *caused* by the disparity mechanism, so the M1M3 shrinkage is a bound on the "explained" share, not proof of fairness.

## 2.2 Matching / stratification variables

- **Exact strata:** region × month × channel × risk_score_band (coarsened to 3–4 bands).
- **Coarsened exact matching (CEM)** or propensity-score matching on: `risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness` (binned), `channel`, `household_size_band`, `prior_case_count`, `region`, `month`.
- **Primary matched comparison:** match each Group B/C case to one or more Group A cases on the above *and* on observed complexity, then compare **cycle time, waiting time, handover count, and number of activities**, not approval alone.
- **Within-variant matching:** compare B vs A *within V2* (both took the Request-Documents path) and C vs A *within V5* (if any A cases exist there; if not, report "no common support" rather than forcing a comparison).
- **Intersectional cells:** BC, Bhigh-risk, Cincomplete-docs. The groups overlap (variant "Group shares" sum to 99–113 %, and total cases 3600+1250+820+980 = 6650 may exceed the unique case count), so **the four rows are cohorts, not a partition**; single-axis analysis can mask a much worse intersection.

## 2.3 Path / variant comparison

- Enumerate the full variant set per group; compute per-group variant distributions with confidence intervals (multinomial).
- Compare **within-variant** outcomes: if Group B cases in V2 have the same cycle time as Group A cases in V2, then the group gap is *purely compositional* (routing), and the fairness issue is *who gets routed where*, not *how the path is executed*. If B is slower within V2, the issue is execution/resource.
- Use variant coverage explicitly: top-6 variants cover 82 % of A but 99 % of B — renormalise or use the full set before comparing shares.
- Identify "support-path" variants (V4, V5) as a class and report their population-weighted contribution to each group's mean/median duration.
- Check for *orphan* paths: support step started but never completed; appeal intake without review; decision without notification.

## 2.4 Resource-assignment comparison

- Compute per case: number of activities, distinct resources, resource handovers, whether an overloaded/region-backlog resource touched the case, and the assigned pool (general / bilingual / accommodation / fraud / supervisor).
- Compare distributions by group **after** stratifying on variant — this isolates "same path, different handler" effects.
- Build and compare the **handover-of-work network** and **working-together network** per group; extract per-case handover counts, network density, and whether Group B/C cases traverse more *cluster-bridging* edges (generalspecialistgeneral), which implies repeated re-explanation.
- Use **activity-based resource similarity** to test whether the same activity (e.g., Eligibility Assessment) is performed by comparably skilled/similar resource sets across groups, or whether support-path activities are executed only by a narrow, distinct pool.
- Use **organizational roles** to test role coverage: does any role in Group B's path exist in sufficient numbers, and do roles overlap (2 of 5 accommodation specialists also supervise  contention).

## 2.5 Waiting-time decomposition

Decompose each group's cycle-time gap into **service time** and **waiting time** at three levels:

1. **Case level:** insert service/waiting time; compare `waiting / duration` ratio by group. A disparity that is mostly *waiting* points to capacity and queue design (addressable, and not applicant-driven); a disparity that is mostly *service* points to more substantive work per case (more plausibly legitimate).
2. **Edge level:** performance DFG per group  difference in mean duration per directly-follows edge; rank edges by excess seconds. Expect the ranking to be dominated by Language SupportEligibility and Accommodation ReviewEligibility.
3. **Stage-level (Oaxaca-style):** allocate each group's total gap to (a) *composition* (different variant shares, using Group A's edge times), (b) *edge-level execution* (same edge, different wait). This answers "how much of the +4.1 days for C is 'the accommodation step exists' versus 'the accommodation queue is slow'?" — and only the second component is an unambiguous defect.

Back-of-envelope sanity check (order of magnitude only — variant shares do not sum to 1 and medians are not means): C's 31 % in V5 at 21.6 d vs A's 2 % at 19.2-equivalent excess  0.31×(21.65.8)  0.02×(21.65.8)  **4.6 days**, very close to the reported **+4.1 adjusted days**. B: 24 % in V4 (19.2 d) plus excess V2 share  **3+ days** gross, consistent in magnitude with **+2.6 adjusted days**. So the adjusted residuals are *plausibly fully accounted for by support-path routing plus its queue* — which is exactly why the service/waiting split is decisive.

## 2.6 Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates

Run the full ladder with variants of each suspect control and report the *range* of group coefficients:

- **`risk_score_band`:** (a) omit entirely; (b) re-derive from current-period outcomes only (strip historical inputs); (c) replace with a "clean" proxy (e.g., verifiable current facts only); (d) stratify by band rather than linearly adjust. If the B/C/D coefficients move a lot, the headline number is model-dependent.
- **`documentation_completeness`:** omit; treat as a **mediator** rather than a confounder (language barriers  incomplete documents  Request Missing Documents  delay). Controlling for it *removes* part of the harm channel and biases the B coefficient **toward zero**. Report both "total effect" (without it) and "direct effect" (with it), and label them clearly.
- **`channel`:** channel is partly a *choice* and partly an *assignment*; check whether channel itself differs because support is only available in person (selection into channel = a fairness outcome, not a confounder).
- **Bounded/unmeasured-confounding analysis:** report how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to nullify each residual (E-value style), and a simple tipping-point analysis.
- **Functional form:** replace linear terms with flexible splines/interactions; use coarsened strata; check that residuals are not driven by extrapolation into sparse cells.

## 2.7 Subgroup checks by region and channel

- Report adjusted gaps **by region**: the waiting table already attributes Supervisor Review backlog to *two regions* — if B's supervisor excess disappears within those two regions, it is a regional capacity problem, not a group problem; if it persists everywhere, it is more concerning. Note that region is in M1, so a region-driven gap should already be attenuated — check whether it is.
- Report by **channel**: in-person vs online; and **language need × channel** cells (B in-person + language need is likely the worst cell and may be a small n — report intervals).
- Check **period effects**: whether disparities widened over time (staffing changes) or are stable.
- Report **n and CIs in every cell**; small cells (C by region) will be underpowered — state that rather than reporting a point estimate alone.

## 2.8 Decision rules (how the four buckets are assigned)

| Observed pattern | Classification |
|---|---|
| Gap vanishes under M2 (adding language/accommodation flags) and is explained by edge-time of the mandated step | **Legitimate routing** (but see §3: the *queue* around it is still a defect) |
| Gap persists within-variant and within-resource, is mostly waiting time, and is concentrated on non-substitutable pools | **Residual disparity / structural burden** — actionable even if not discriminatory |
| Gap persists within-variant, within-resource, within-region, within-period, on *service* time or on approval, and is robust to omitting suspect covariates | **Consistent with unfair treatment** — warrants escalation; still not proof of intent |
| Gap small relative to its CI, or sign/magnitude unstable across specifications | **Uncertainty** — report as inconclusive |
| A recorded need with the mandated step absent | **Rights non-fulfilment** — separate, more serious defect |

## 3.2 What is justified

- **Group D's fraud and supervisor routing** is mandated and tracks measured risk; its *presence* is not a fairness defect. (Its *fairness* still depends on whether `risk_score_band` is itself unbiased — see §5.)
- **Group B's Language Support Contact** and **Group C's Accommodation Review** as *steps* are rights-preserving and must not be removed or discouraged to improve cycle time. Their existence is a fairness *feature*.
- **Higher Request-Missing-Documents rates** are partly genuine: B's incomplete-documentation share is 46 % vs 21 %.
- **Case-mix explains a large share of raw gaps**: adjustment moves B's approval gap from 15 pp to 5.5 pp and D's from 20 pp to 3.1 pp. Dismissing the process entirely on raw numbers would be wrong.

## 3.3 What remains concerning even after granting all of that

1. **The waiting time *inside* rights-preserving steps is not mandated by the right.** A 3.8-day or 4.7-day queue between a support contact and the next substantive assessment is queueing, not work. The policy statement is explicit that language support and accommodation review are rights-preserving, yet **service-level targets contain no timeliness threshold for them** — i.e., the steps most likely to slow applicants are the steps least measured. That is a design-level procedural fairness gap.
2. **The burden is concentrated exactly where the rights are exercised.** V4 and V5 are the two slowest non-appeal paths and are 10–15× over-represented for B and C. Whatever the intent, *using a right reliably costs time*: that is the definition of a chilling burden on a right.
3. **Non-substitutable pools with high load and no SLA.** General caseworkers can absorb Group A volume; nobody can absorb a bilingual or accommodation case. The resource design therefore converts a policy right into a queue position.
4. **Handover-mediated burden.** Each handover implies re-statement of the applicant's situation; B and C traverse generalspecialist(general/supervisor) in two regions. Handovers are a *cost paid by the applicant*, invisible in approval statistics.
5. **Residual adjusted gaps are largest exactly where the support steps are.** C has the biggest cycle-time residual (+4.1 d) and B the biggest approval residual (5.5 pp) — the two groups whose defining features trigger support steps. Even if each residual has an innocent explanation, the *pattern* (rights-exercising groups are slower after controls) is a legitimate finding in its own right.
6. **Rework for B and C is double Group A's and has never been adjusted for.** Unexplained doubling of applicant effort is a procedural burden finding that the current analysis simply does not address.
7. **D's time/appeal dissociation.** Near-parity processing time combined with the highest appeal rate suggests the *content* of decisions (reasons, risk routing, fraud stigma) may be contested in ways cycle-time metrics cannot see.

**Important caveat:** none of the above requires, or supports, an inference about intent. Specialist scarcity, missing SLAs, and backlog are organisational design failures that produce unequal burden without anyone treating an applicant differently.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**M1 (structural / not applicant-specific): Non-substitutable specialist capacity bottleneck — legitimate step, unfair queue.**
Seven bilingual caseworkers and five accommodation specialists are the only personnel permitted to perform mandated steps, carry roughly 1.5× the implied per-person case load of general staff, and have no service-level target. When a right can only be exercised through one small pool, queueing is mechanically guaranteed. This produces B's 3.8 d and C's 4.7 d waits, the V4/V5 concentration, and much of the adjusted cycle-time residual — **without any differential treatment of individuals.** *Legitimate in origin, concerning in effect, and fully addressable.*

**M2 (legitimate): Genuine complexity-driven routing.**
Higher risk  Fraud Review  Supervisor Review  longer V3 (18.4 d) and higher appeal propensity for D; higher incomplete-documentation share  more Request-Missing-Documents loops for B. Adjustment absorbs much of this (D: 20  3.1 pp). This is the mechanism the process would *defend itself with*, and it is partly correct.

**M3 (concerning, compounding): Language-mediated documentation loop.**
Language barrier  documents supplied incompletely or in an unexpected format  `documentation_completeness` lower  Request Missing Documents (+9.5 pp for B)  another applicant round-trip  +1.6 d at that edge  further delay. Here the "documented complexity" used to justify the disparity is itself partly *produced* by the disparity. Controlling for `documentation_completeness` then removes evidence of the harm channel. This mechanism would produce exactly the observed signature: higher request-documents probability, higher rework (22 %), longer cycles, *and* an adjusted coefficient biased toward zero.

**M4 (concerning): Repeated handover and re-explanation burden.**
General  bilingual/accommodation  general/supervisor handovers force the applicant to re-establish facts. Each handover adds latency (waiting for the next handler) and a comprehension risk (mis-translated or dropped context), and may contribute to both rework and appeals. This is an applicant-borne cost of organisational structure, not of case complexity.

**M5 (concerning, institutional): Unmeasured rights-preserving steps become deprioritised "soft" queues.**
Because service-level targets cover ordinary assessment steps but not language support or accommodation review, backlog pressure is rationally absorbed in the unmeasured steps. Supervisors and notification staff are held to implicit norms (DecisionNotification is near parity at 0.5–0.8 d); the un-targeted queues drift. This is a Goodhart-style mechanism: **what is not measured is not managed**, and what is not managed is where the delay lands.

**M6 (possible but unproven): Differential discretionary thresholds.**
After equivalent information, assessors may apply subtly different approval thresholds, ask for more evidence, or escalate more readily for some groups — producing residual approval gaps (B 5.5 pp) and extra supervisor reviews (+8.0 pp) that persist within-variant. This is the mechanism that *would* constitute unfair treatment. The current evidence is **consistent with it but does not identify it**, because the adjusted model does not yet condition on variant, assigned resource, and region-period backlog simultaneously (that is M3 in §2.1).

**M7 (possible, not fairness-neutral): Endogenous risk scores.**
If `risk_score_band` reflects historical enforcement patterns rather than current facts, then D's fraud routing and the attenuation of D's coefficients are partly *mechanisms of historical bias being laundered through a control variable*. This mechanism works in the opposite direction from M6: it can make a biased process look adjusted-and-fine.

**M8 (feedback): Delay  applicant disengagement  incomplete responses  rework  longer delay.**
A slow, multi-handover process raises the chance an applicant misses a document request or a contact attempt, feeding M3 and inflating rework. Self-reinforcing, and it would disproportionately affect applicants with the least slack time — a group attribute the log does not contain.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Measurement bias in the "complexity" controls.**
Both key controls are suspect as *measures*:
- `documentation_completeness` is plausibly partly an outcome of language barriers, channel (in-person staff help complete forms; online does not), and form accessibility for applicants with disabilities. Treating it as exogenous complexity converts a *disparity effect* into an *explanation*.
- `channel` is simultaneously a preference and an availability constraint (support may only be reachable in person for some applicants). Adjusting for it may adjust away an access disparity.

**Historical bias in `risk_score_band`.**
If the band is trained on or derived from prior enforcement outcomes, prior denials, or prior fraud referrals, it can encode earlier disparity. Controlling for it then (a) attributes historically-produced risk to the applicant, and (b) shrinks group coefficients toward zero. Two specific risks: (i) D's *adjusted* 3.1 pp may understate the true disparity; (ii) fraud routing for D may be partly circular — high band  fraud review  more findings  higher band. Without knowing the band's construction, **no adjusted estimate in the table can be called "the" disparity.**

**No merit ground truth.**
`case_outcome` records the decision, not verified eligibility. Without an independent eligibility label (or an audit subsample with expert re-adjudication), it is impossible to know whether Group B's 5.5 pp adjusted approval gap reflects lower substantive merit, different error rates, or different thresholds. This is the single largest obstacle to any discrimination claim — in *either* direction.

**Missing applicant needs and treatment quality.**
Absent from the log: severity of accommodation need, actual language proficiency, whether the support contact reached the applicant, comprehension, whether explanations were intelligible and respectful, whether the applicant understood the reasons for denial, whether they could respond in time, and applicant satisfaction. `case_outcome` cannot capture **quality of explanation or dignity of treatment** — so the fairness dimension most directly about *procedural* justice is unobservable. A group could receive perfectly equal times and rates and still be treated worse.

**Unobserved resource workload.**
Workload is observed only at **daily granularity**: no hourly arrival/departure, no absences, vacancies, training time, overtime, caseload-per-worker over time, skill matrix, or concurrent-task load. Waiting times therefore cannot be cleanly attributed to *capacity* versus *scheduling versus prioritisation versus deliberate deferral*. The 102-vs-69 load estimate above rests on assumptions the data does not confirm.

**Appeal quality is unmeasured.**
`appeal_flag` is binary. There is no appeal outcome (upheld/overturned), no grounds (procedural vs substantive), no appeal timing, and no record of whether the appellant had representation. **Overturn rate is the natural discriminator test** — a high overturn rate for B or D would be strong evidence of initial-decision error; its absence from the data is a critical gap. Likewise, appeals may be suppressed by inaccessibility: a *low* appeal rate can indicate empowerment, not satisfaction.

**Reopen/rework lacks an adjusted model**, and `case_outcome` likely collapses partial approvals, benefit amounts, and emergency/interim payments — so "approval parity" may mask large differences in *how much* and *how soon* money arrives, which is the outcome that matters to a household.

**Structural data issues:**
- The four groups are **overlapping cohorts, not a partition** (variant shares sum to 99–113 %); single-axis analysis can hide worse intersections (e.g., minority language + accommodation).
- Group-level "shares" mix case counts and event counts ambiguously; variant coverage differs by group (82 % for A vs 99 % for B), so tail paths are unevenly observed.
- Ongoing/incomplete cases are likely censored, and censoring may be differential (slow groups have more open cases), biasing duration estimates **downward** for exactly the groups of concern.
- No counterfactual: applicants who never applied (deterred by known slowness) are invisible — selection on the dependent variable.
- Region-specific backlog means group effects and region effects may be collinear in cells with few cases.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

### 6.1 Add capacity for language support and accommodation review
**Action:** Increase bilingual caseworker count (7  a level sized to the 57 % need share), add accommodation specialists, remove the overlap where two of five accommodation specialists also perform Supervisor Review, and cross-train general staff on intake-level support tasks (while keeping *decision* authority appropriately separated).
**Expected effect:** Directly shortens the 3.8 d and 4.7 d queues — the two largest identified edge waits — and reduces V4/V5 cycle times and handover counts.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** Recurring cost and long hiring/training lead time (delayed benefit); bilingual and accommodation specialists risk becoming *permanent* single points of failure, and their concentration may invite new bottleneck or tokenism dynamics; cross-training general staff raises competence questions and possible quality variation; if supervisor duties remain coupled to specialist duties, contention merely shifts. **Do not** solve capacity by discouraging requests — that would convert a queue problem into a rights-access problem.

### 6.2 Introduce explicit service-level targets for support steps
**Action:** Add SLA thresholds for SubmitIdentity Verification, Language Support ContactEligibility, Accommodation ReviewEligibility, CompletenessRequest Documents, and Supervisor ReviewDecision Draft, with breach alerts and escalation.
**Expected effect:** Makes visible what is currently invisible; the data already shows ordinary steps behave near-parity while untargeted steps drift. This is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost item because it changes *measurement*, not structure.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** **Goodhart risk** — targets can push staff to close support contacts quickly without achieving comprehension, or to record the step as complete prematurely; targets can cause *delay displacement* (hitting the support SLA while the delay moves to the next untargeted edge); measurement and reporting cost; defining "service delivered" for language support requires a comprehension criterion that is hard to operationalise; small regional samples make SLA breaches noisy and may trigger over-reaction. Pair every SLA with a **quality** check, not just a clock.

### 6.3 Routing redesign to reduce handovers
**Action:** Assign a bilingual or accommodation-literate caseworker **at intake** with end-to-end ownership (removing generalspecialistgeneral round trips); use a structured, standardised handoff packet when transfer is unavoidable; consider co-locating language support with completeness checking so the documentation loop is resolved in one contact rather than two.
**Expected effect:** Fewer re-explanations, less lost context, lower rework (currently 22–24 % for B/C vs 11 %), and likely shorter cycles independent of any capacity increase.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** End-to-end ownership reduces flexibility and can create *new* single-owner bottlenecks and absence vulnerability; concentrating language-literate staff on their own group can produce ghettoisation rather than integration; **do not merge Fraud Review with Eligibility Assessment** — separation between risk investigation and benefit determination is a legitimate control, and collapsing checks to save time would reduce integrity; a standardised handoff packet has its own privacy and comprehension requirements.

### 6.4 Decision-explanation improvements
**Action:** Structured, plain-language, bilingual reasons for every decision and every Request-Missing-Documents prompt; state what is needed, by when, in which format, and what happens if not received; include a named contact and the appeal route with timelines.
**Expected effect:** Reduces avoidable rework and misdirected appeals, and directly addresses the *quality/dignity* dimension the log cannot currently see. It is the only listed mitigation that improves fairness on an axis entirely absent from the data.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** Per-case time cost; **short-term appeal rates may rise** as clearer reasons make appeals easier to formulate — this is not a failure (an appeal rate is not a target to be minimised); standardisation can reduce justified discretion in unusual cases; producing and verifying translated material requires translation capacity, which interacts with mitigation 6.1; if reasons are clearer *and* thresholds differ, better explanations can expose rather than fix disparity.

### 6.5 Monitor adjusted disparities continuously
**Action:** Standing dashboard reporting raw and adjusted gaps (M0–M3 ladder) with confidence intervals for approval, cycle time, waiting/service split, handover count, rework, appeal and appeal-outcome, by group, region, channel and month; publish the specification and the sensitivity range; pre-specify escalation thresholds; add equivalence/non-inferiority tests so "no significant difference" is not mistaken for "no difference."
**Expected effect:** Detects drift (e.g., backlog widening in two regions) and keeps covariate sensitivity visible rather than buried.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** **Model risk** — a dashboard built on biased covariates (`risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`) can provide *false comfort*, making an unfair process look adjusted-and-fine; Goodhart effects once targets attach to the metrics; small subgroups (Group C n = 820; further split by region) yield unstable estimates and can trigger spurious alarms or mask real ones; publication creates governance and interpretive burden; monitoring must be accompanied by the sensitivity analyses in §2.6 or it should not be labelled "adjusted fairness."

### 6.6 Audit `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`
**Action:** Document exactly how each is constructed; test whether the band predicts *current-period* verifiable facts or merely reproduces historical referrals/denials; re-run the entire model ladder omitting each, replacing each with a clean proxy, and re-deriving the band from current-period outcomes only; audit documentation requirements for language and accessibility bias (form language, digital-only formats, acceptance of alternative proofs).
**Expected effect:** Establishes whether the reported adjusted disparities are an artefact of the controls; may *increase* measured disparities (which would be a more honest number), or confirm they are robust.
**Side effects / trade-offs:** If the band is found biased and recalibrated, **fraud-review volume for D will change**, requiring capacity planning and possibly affecting integrity outcomes — a real trade-off, not a free win; loosening documentation requirements reduces burden but requires alternative verification pathways to protect programme integrity; recalibration may be resisted on legal/policy grounds; the audit itself may reveal past unfairness with governance implications.

### 6.7 Additional options worth considering
- **Wait-time alerts at applicant level:** notify applicants when a support step exceeds a threshold, with a named contact — reduces disengagement-driven rework (M8). *Cost:* notification volume; risk of normalising long waits.
- **Interim/accelerated decisions** for applicants whose only pending item is a support-step queue, where policy permits. *Risk:* may bypass a right-preserving review — must not be used to skip accommodation or language review.
- **Independent adjudication subsample** to create the missing merit ground truth and appeal-overturn data. *Cost:* expense; *benefit:* without it, no approval-gap finding can ever be interpreted.

**Explicit constraint honoured:** none of these proposals involves removing, discouraging, or de-prioritising Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review. Those steps are the fairness *feature*; the defect is the queue, handover and measurement structure built around them.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Overall: procedurally mixed — with specific, evidenced procedural fairness defects. Unequal burden: supported. Discrimination: not established, not excluded.**

**What the evidence supports.** A substantial share of the raw gaps is genuinely explained by case mix: adjustment moves Group B's approval gap from 15 pp to 5.5 pp and Group D's from 20 pp to 3.1 pp, and Group D's fraud/supervisor routing tracks its 51 % high-risk share in line with stated policy. Legitimate routing is therefore doing real explanatory work, and reading the raw table as discrimination would be wrong.

At the same time, **the process is measurably less favourable to experience for the groups that exercise rights.** Group B is ~10–15× over-represented in the slowest non-appeal path (V4, 19.2 d vs 5.8 d) and Group C in V5 (21.6 d); the two queues attached to rights-preserving steps — 3.8 days and 4.7 days — have no Group A counterpart and no service-level target; specialist pools carrying those steps appear to hold roughly 1.5× the per-person load while being non-substitutable; Groups B and C traverse extra handovers; rework runs at ~2× Group A's (22 %/24 % vs 11 %) and has never been adjusted for. Residual adjusted gaps remain and are largest exactly where support steps apply: +4.1 days (C), +2.6 days (B) in cycle time, 5.5 pp (B) in approval, +4.4/+5.7 pp (B/D) in appeals. A back-of-envelope check suggests the cycle-time residuals are of the same order as the support-path contribution — which is itself the finding: **using a right reliably costs time, and that cost is unmeasured by the organisation's own targets.** Post-decision handling is near-parity (max 0.3 d), so this is an upstream assessment-and-queue problem, not a notification problem.

**What the evidence does not support.** It does not support any claim of intent, differential treatment of like cases, or discrimination. The strongest-sounding numbers are also the weakest: the timeliness residuals are very likely real, but Group C's 2.2 pp approval gap is on the order of one standard error and may be noise. The evidence does not establish that the process *is* fair either — the controls used to adjust the comparison (`risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`) are themselves flagged as potentially historically biased or language-driven, so the "adjusted" figures may understate disparity rather than remove it. And the dimension most central to *procedural* fairness — quality of explanation, comprehension, dignity of treatment, appeal outcomes — is simply not in the log, so no conclusion, favourable or unfavourable, can be drawn about it.

**Calibrated classification:**
- **Disparity:** established (multiple metrics, robust magnitudes, likely precise for time).
- **Procedural burden:** established — concentrated on rights-preserving steps, driven by capacity, queueing, and handover design rather than by case complexity alone.
- **Legitimate routing:** substantially supported for Group D's risk pathway and partly for documentation loops; **not** a complete explanation, and the *queue around* a legitimate step is not itself legitimate.
- **Discrimination:** indeterminate. The pattern (rights-exercising groups slower after controls) is consistent with differential thresholds, but is equally consistent with specialist scarcity and un-SLA'd queues; distinguishing them requires the within-variant, within-resource, within-region analysis in §2 and, critically, appeal-outcome and adjudication-audit data that do not exist yet.
- **Priority actions:** instrument the support steps (SLAs plus a quality criterion), add specialist capacity, reduce handovers, improve explanations, and audit the two suspect covariates — all of which improve fairness regardless of how the discrimination question resolves, and none of which requires touching the rights-preserving steps themselves.
