### 1. Executive Memo

**To:** Executive Management
**From:** Process Analytics & Strategy Team
**Date:** 24 May 2024
**Subject:** Decision Recommendation: Fiber Installation Redesign R

**Recommendation:** Adopt **Option E (Hybrid Deployment)**. Scale Redesign R exclusively for "Simple House/Low Risk" segments immediately. Suspend survey-skipping and post-billing activation for all other segments. Implement coordinator load-balancing controls. Commission targeted validation of the predictive risk rule and full downstream cost measurement before any further expansion.

**Rationale:**
Redesign R is a segmented success. Process-mining evidence confirms significant efficiency gains for low-risk cases (median duration reduced by 42%, handovers cut by 65%, visible cost down 12%). However, the redesign introduces unacceptable risks in complex segments. Aggressive skipping of field surveys and permit checks, combined with billing-before-activation sequencing, has caused the tail duration to explode (p95 up 38%), first-time-right rates to drop, and complaints to rise. The process model's low precision (0.51) and alignment diagnostics reveal high variability and compliance violations, particularly "Billing start before Activation test" and missing permit checks in older buildings.

Furthermore, the coordinator-ownership model, while reducing handovers, has created a bottleneck (93% utilization) and a single point of failure; coordinator absence now stalls cases due to lack of shared context. Scaling nationally (Option A) would amplify tail risks, complaint volumes, and compliance exposure. Rolling back (Option B) discards proven value for the largest and most efficient segment.

**Trade-offs:**
*   **Efficiency vs. Robustness:** We gain speed and accountability for simple cases but lose resilience and quality for complex cases if controls are not added.
*   **Cost Shifting:** Upstream cost savings are being eroded by downstream rework and complaints. The estimated total cost is higher, though data is incomplete.
*   **Centralization vs. Flexibility:** Coordinator ownership improves accountability but reduces team resilience and creates utilization constraints.

**Next Steps:**
1.  **Immediate:** Deploy R only for simple low-risk houses. Revert survey-skipping and post-billing activation for apartments, old buildings, and business customers.
2.  **Controls:** Implement coordinator utilization caps and a fallback protocol for coordinator absence.
3.  **Measurement:** Prioritize validation of the building-risk predictive rule (M6) and full downstream cost tracking (M4).
4.  **Review:** Re-evaluate expansion to medium-risk segments only when the predictive rule accuracy exceeds 95% and tail metrics stabilize.

This approach captures immediate value where the redesign works, contains risk where it fails, and generates the information needed to safely expand.

### 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

*   **High Fitness but Low Precision:** Fitness measures how well the model covers observed cases; precision measures if the model predicts only observed behavior. The pilot process introduced high variability (many exception paths due to failed skip rules and manual activations). The discovered model captures this chaos (high fitness) but becomes overly permissive, allowing combinations of events not seen in reality (low precision). This indicates the process under R is less stable and more prone to uncontrolled variation than the baseline.
*   **Improved Median but Worse Tail:** The median reflects the central tendency, dominated by the "Simple House" segment where the redesign works well. The tail reflects the worst-performing cases, which are concentrated in complex segments (apartments, old buildings) where the redesign's assumptions fail. The distribution has shifted: the left tail shrank, but the right tail elongated significantly due to rework, missing permits, and coordinator bottlenecks.
*   **Fewer Handovers but Lower Resilience:** Handover count measures workflow fragmentation; resilience measures robustness to disruptions. Coordinator ownership reduced handovers by consolidating responsibility, which improved accountability. However, this centralization created a single point of failure. When a coordinator is absent, the case lacks shared context, causing stalls. The network analysis confirms centralization around coordinators, reducing the distributed resilience of the baseline team structure.
*   **Lower Visible Cost but Higher Rework:** Visible costs exclude downstream activities. The redesign reduces visible costs by skipping field surveys and moving activation checks. However, these skipped checks lead to higher failure rates (FTR down, second visits up, manual activation up). The cost is not eliminated; it is shifted downstream to rework and complaint handling. The incomplete cost data masks this shift, but the rise in second visits and complaints confirms the trade-off.
*   **Improvement for Simple Houses but Deterioration for Apartments/Old Buildings:** The redesign assumes low risk and standard conditions. Simple houses match these assumptions, benefiting from merged tasks and reduced handovers. Apartments and old buildings have higher variance (permit needs, access issues). The predictive skip rule fails here, leading to missing surveys and permits. The "one-size-fits-all" application of R ignores segment heterogeneity, causing performance degradation where risk is higher.

### 6. Final Recommendation

**Recommended Decision:** **Option E (Hybrid Deployment)** with immediate controls and targeted measurement.

*   **Immediate Action:** Deploy Redesign R **only** for "Simple House, Low Risk" cases. For all other segments, revert to baseline survey scheduling and activation-before-billing sequencing.
*   **What to Pause:** Pause the predictive survey-skip rule for medium/high-risk segments. Pause post-billing activation for all segments until conformance monitoring (M7) confirms compliance risk is acceptable. Pause national scaling of coordinator ownership until utilization controls are in place.
*   **What to Scale:** Scale the merged validation/scheduling for simple cases. Scale coordinator ownership for simple cases, subject to load management.
*   **What to Measure Next:**
    1.  **M6:** Validate and refine the building-risk predictive rule.
    2.  **M4:** Implement full downstream cost tracking to quantify cost-shifting.
    3.  **M7:** Monitor conformance for billing sequence and permit checks.
    4.  **M5:** Test coordinator fallback protocols to address resilience.
*   **Thresholds for Broader Rollout:**
    *   Predictive rule accuracy for medium-risk segments > 95%.
    *   Tail duration (p95) for expanded segments  Baseline p95.
    *   Complaint rate for expanded segments  Baseline complaint rate.
    *   True total cost (including downstream)  Baseline total cost.
    *   Coordinator utilization capped at 85% with effective fallback protocol.

This recommendation secures efficiency gains where the process is robust, mitigates risks where assumptions fail, and builds the evidence base for safe expansion.
