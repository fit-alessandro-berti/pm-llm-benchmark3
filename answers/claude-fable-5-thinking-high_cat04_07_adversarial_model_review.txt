# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

I distinguish four intended uses, because the acceptable fitness/precision trade-off is different for each:

1. **Exploration / discovery communication.** The model is a descriptive map for analysts and business users. High fitness and simplicity matter; low precision is tolerable because the model is not used to declare anything compliant or non-compliant. Model S was apparently built for this use (F7).
2. **Conformance enforcement / audit.** The model is a normative specification. Precision against *policy* (not merely against observed traces) is critical, and fitness matters only for behavior that is actually legitimate. A model that certifies a check-skipping trace as fitting (Model S on B3) is disqualifying regardless of its scores. A model that rejects legitimate unseen combinations (Model C on B2/B5) produces false alarms that erode audit credibility.
3. **Simulation / what-if.** The model must reproduce realistic behavior distributions; both structural over-permissiveness (S generates traces without any checks) and enumeration (C can only replay eight variants) make simulated populations misleading.
4. **Stakeholder communication and governance sign-off.** Comprehensibility and explicit representation of decisions (e.g., the correction/recheck decision of F6) matter more than any conformance number.

Key assumptions I make explicit:

- The reported metrics are **not reproducible as reported** (no seeds, no preprocessing code, no alignment costs, no held-out case list). I treat them as weak evidence of tendencies, not as measurements.
- The behavior probes B1–B8 express current business policy and outrank the metrics wherever they conflict.
- P1–P7 are the governing requirements; where the log lacks attributes (external/internal in P3, affected-check in P6), a model must represent the *decision point* honestly and flag the attribute gap, rather than silently absorbing it into permissiveness or silently forbidding it.
- "Accepts" below means a perfect (cost-zero) alignment is possible; partial alignment fitness > 0 does not count as acceptance.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

Model S is `->( Register, +( X(CC,tau), X(TR,tau), X(SR,tau), X(ER,tau) ), X(MA, AA), Release )` — abbreviating Register Dossier (R), Credit Check (CC), Technical Review (TR), Supplier Review (SR), Expedite Review (ER), Manager Approval (MA), Auto Approval (AA), Correct Dossier (CD), Release Order (RO).

**Strongest case for S.** It is honest about uncertainty: with the external/internal attribute missing (P3) and the affected-check attribute missing (P6), S refuses to invent constraints it cannot verify. It is robust to missing events (each review skippable), replays essentially all real behavior (holdout fitness 0.981), is fast, sound-by-construction (block-structured), and business users can actually read it (4.4). For pure exploration and for a first conversation with stakeholders, it is a defensible artifact — provided it carries a "not for conformance" label.

**Strongest case against S.** It optionalizes *mandatory* behavior and erases a whole activity. Its fitness and generalization scores are purchased with policy violations. Concrete admitted or mishandled behaviors:

1. `⟨R, MA, RO⟩` (= B3): both mandatory checks skipped, approval granted. Violates P2. Accepted with cost zero.
2. `⟨R, ER, CC, TR, MA, RO⟩` (= B4): Expedite Review before either mandatory check. Violates P4. Accepted — the parallel operator cannot express "ER only after CC and TR."
3. `⟨R, CC, ER, TR, AA, RO⟩`: ER interleaved between the checks (after CC, before TR). Also violates P4; shows the flaw is structural, not an edge case.
4. `⟨R, SR, AA, RO⟩`: Supplier Review performed, both mandatory checks skipped, auto-approved. Violates P2; a plausible fraud pattern that S certifies as perfectly fitting.
5. `⟨R, ER, MA, RO⟩`: an "expedited" order with no checks at all. Violates P2 and P4 simultaneously.
6. `⟨R, CC, TR, CD, TR, MA, RO⟩` (= B6): **Correct Dossier is not in the model's alphabet at all.** Every correction trace forces a log-move; S cannot even describe the correction/recheck behavior of P6, let alone the P7 suspicion rule. Conversely, S also *forbids* legitimate rechecks: each check can occur at most once (each `X(a,tau)` fires once inside `+`), so `⟨R, CC, TR, CD, CC, TR, MA, RO⟩` cannot fit either.

**Intentional noise tolerance vs. accidental semantic flaws.** Making SR skippable is *intentional and defensible* noise tolerance: the conditioning attribute is missing (P3) and F4 says some SR events may be missing from the log. Making CC and TR skippable is *intentional but indefensible for any normative use*: it converts "robust to missing events" into "mandatory controls are optional." The ER-ordering freedom (items 2–3) and the complete omission of Correct Dossier (item 6) are *accidental semantic flaws*: the chosen operators simply cannot express P4 and P6, and no one noticed. The distinction matters for remediation: the first is a labeling/usage problem, the latter two require a different model structure.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

Model C is an XOR over eight fully enumerated sequences.

**Strongest case for C.** For an auditor, every accepted trace is one of eight explicitly approved end-to-end variants; there is no emergent behavior. Precision on observed behavior is high (0.941), deviations are guaranteed to surface as alignment moves, and the advocate's stance (F8) — unseen behavior is noncompliant until approved — is a legitimate control philosophy in regulated approval processes. C also at least *contains* Correct Dossier, which S does not.

**Strongest case against C.**

- **Branch enumeration.** C hardcodes variants instead of composing rules. The policy space is combinatorial: 2 check orders × SR∈{0,1} × ER∈{0,1} × 2 approvals is already 16 correction-free variants, before any correction/recheck combinations. C covers 8 cherry-picked cells. Every newly approved combination requires editing the model (F2), and nothing in the structure documents *why* a branch exists.
- **Duplicate labels.** 33 duplicates (definition unknown, but consistent with each activity reappearing per branch) mean one business activity maps to many transitions. Impact analysis ("what happens if Credit Check changes?") requires touching many places (F3); alignments become slower and ambiguous (which duplicate did the trace hit?); and precision/generalization metrics are distorted (Section 6).
- **Unseen combinations of known behavior.** C rejects B2 (`TR, CC` order + Auto Approval — both features individually approved, never combined in training) and B5 (SR + ER + AA). It never allows SR together with AA, never allows ER with the TR-first order, and never allows ER with a correction. These are not new activities or new orderings — they are cross-products of behavior C itself endorses. Rejecting them is memorization, not strictness.
- **Correction handling.** C admits exactly one correction pattern: a single CD that always repeats *both* checks and always ends in MA. P6 explicitly says only the affected checks should repeat; F6 says the decision (CC only / TR only / both) is real. C misclassifies the policy-conditional B6 (CD followed by TR only) as noncompliant unconditionally, forbids repeated corrections ("may repeat", P6), and forbids correction followed by Auto Approval.
- **Holdout degradation.** 0.973 → 0.744 is the classic memorization signature. F4 confirms the failing holdout cases are largely *recombinations* of known behavior plus some missing SR events. If even a modest share of those are legitimate, C's operational false-alarm rate is unacceptable for audit use.
- **Maintainability.** Eight parallel sequences with 33 duplicated labels, a 0.281 simplicity score, a 1.9 comprehension score, and 25× alignment runtime. Every policy change is an O(branches) edit with high risk of inconsistency between branches.
- **Is the 0.941 precision an artifact?** Largely yes. It was computed on the *training* log only, against *positive traces* only (F5). A lookup table of training variants would score similarly. High precision against observed behavior, with no negative suite and collapsing holdout fitness, is precisely the signature of memorizing training variants rather than encoding policy.

# 5. Behavior-Probe Matrix

"Accepts" = a cost-zero alignment exists. Semantics used: in a process-tree `+` block each child executes exactly once (so each `X(a,tau)` yields at most one occurrence of `a`); Model S has no Correct Dossier label anywhere; Model C accepts exactly its eight listed sequences and nothing else.

(ii) For C, acceptance is certain because XOR-of-sequences has unambiguous language; the only caveat is that duplicate labels can make *diagnostic* alignments ambiguous about which branch was matched, not whether a match exists.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely impact on the governance decision:

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior (S).** S's headline numbers (0.992/0.981 fitness) are generated by tau-skips on CC/TR — i.e., by the exact defect F1 flags. This is the single most decision-distorting pathology: it makes the unsafe model look best on the most-quoted metric.
2. **Positive-log-only precision (C, and S's comparison).** Per F5, precision was computed only against observed positive traces. It cannot detect that C rejects valid unseen combinations (B2, B5) or that S accepts forbidden ones the log happens not to contain. Without a negative suite, "precision 0.941" is not evidence of audit-readiness.
3. **Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity.** No seeds, no case IDs, no preprocessing code; "same 80/20 split" is a claim, not a fact. C's 0.744 vs S's 0.981 holdout gap could shrink or grow under a different split, and variant-level leakage (same variant in train and test) inflates both models' holdout fitness. All cross-model comparisons inherit this doubt.
4. **Missing-event bias.** F4 says some Supplier Review events may be missing. Missing events *reward* S (tau-skips absorb them silently, inflating fitness and hiding data-quality problems) and *punish* C (a missing SR turns a branch-3 trace into a non-matching variant). The two models' scores are therefore biased in opposite directions by the same data defect.
5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness (S).** Token-based generalization structurally favors small models whose parts fire in every trace. S's 0.907 measures reuse, not correct extrapolation; it would also score high for a flower-like model.
6. **Duplicated-label effects (C).** 33 duplicates enlarge the alignment search space (runtime 0.440 s), depress token-based generalization (each duplicate used by one branch only), complicate escaping-edge computation for precision, and break the transition↔activity correspondence auditors and IT rely on (F3). The metric definition itself is undocumented — a reproducibility failure in its own right.
7. **Silent-transition effects (S).** Taus inflate fitness (free skips), depress precision estimates unevenly (escaping edges from invisible states), and hide from stakeholders that "optional" was chosen for mandatory activities. The 0.584 precision for S is itself of uncertain accuracy because of tau handling.
8. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Costs were not published. Under standard unit costs, S's skip of CC+TR costs nothing (model has tau paths), while C pays 2+ for any unseen combination. Non-standard costs (e.g., cheap log-moves) could materially move both fitness numbers; median-only runtime reporting can likewise hide heavy tails on C.
9. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** Informal 1–5 with no protocol, no rater identification, no comprehension tasks; possibly each team scored its own audience. Also confounds "easy to read" with "fit for purpose" — S is easy to read partly because it omits the hard parts (CD, P4 ordering).
10. **Runtime confounding by model size.** The 25× runtime gap reflects duplicate labels and branch count, not model quality; decision-relevant only for online conformance at volume, and only after costs/hardware are fixed and documented.

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy:** a block-structured procedural backbone (parsable process tree, convertible to a sound workflow net) that hard-codes the *undisputed* structure P1, P2, P4-ordering, P5, and P6's recheck loop, plus a small declarative/data layer for the rules the log's missing attributes prevent the backbone from enforcing (P3 conditionality, P7 audit rule, affected-check consistency). This is deliberately *not* "everything optional": only Supplier Review and Expedite Review are optional, each for a stated reason (missing attribute / genuinely optional per policy), and mandatory checks cannot be skipped.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

```text
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ),                -- both mandatory, either order (P2)
  *( tau,
     ->( 'Correct Dossier',
         X( 'Credit Check',
            'Technical Review',
            +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ) ) ) ),-- 0..n corrections; each repeats CC only, TR only, or both (P6)
  +( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) ), -- SR optional (attribute missing, P3); ER optional, after all checks, before approval, either order w.r.t. SR (P4)
  X( 'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval' ),               -- exactly one (P5)
  'Release Order'                                          -- last (P1)
)
```

Loop semantics: `*(tau, body)` executes the tau "do" part, then zero or more times executes `body` (Correct Dossier followed by the chosen recheck set) and returns. Design decisions made explicit: (a) a correction must be followed by **at least one** recheck (the inner `X` has no tau branch) — P6's "the checks affected by the correction should repeat" presupposes at least one affected check; a CD with no recheck is treated as a deviation to be flagged, not silently allowed; (b) ER and SR are placed after the correction loop, encoding "ER follows the *final* state of both mandatory checks" — see limitations in 7.5.

## 7.2 Self-contained PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py

model_third_str = """
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ),
  *( tau,
     ->( 'Correct Dossier',
         X( 'Credit Check',
            'Technical Review',
            +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ) ) ) ),
  +( X( 'Supplier Review', tau ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ),
  X( 'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval' ),
  'Release Order'
)
"""

model_third = pm4py.parse_process_tree(model_third_str)

# Petri-net form for alignments / soundness checking
net3, im3, fm3 = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
pm4py.check_soundness(net3, im3, fm3, print_diagnostics=True)
```

(An equivalent POWL formulation is straightforward — a `StrictPartialOrder` with Register → {CC, TR} → correction loop → {optional SR, optional ER} → approval XOR → Release, using `OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, ...)` and `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, ...)` nodes and `.order.add_edge(...)` for the precedence arcs; the process-tree form is used here because it parses directly and converts to a sound net.)

## 7.3 Declarative layer and external checks

The backbone cannot see attributes. The following layer makes the attribute gaps *auditable* instead of invisible.

```python
# DECLARE-style constraint dictionary (pm4py declare-model dict format;
# usable with pm4py.conformance_declare or as documentation of intent)
declare_layer = {
    "existence":   {"Register Dossier": {"support": 1.0},
                    "Credit Check":     {"support": 1.0},
                    "Technical Review": {"support": 1.0},
                    "Release Order":    {"support": 1.0}},
    "exactly_one": {"Register Dossier": {"support": 1.0},
                    "Release Order":    {"support": 1.0}},
    "precedence":  {("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"):     {"support": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Expedite Review"):  {"support": 1.0},
                    ("Credit Check", "Manager Approval"):     {"support": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Manager Approval"): {"support": 1.0},
                    ("Credit Check", "Auto Approval"):        {"support": 1.0},
                    ("Technical Review", "Auto Approval"):    {"support": 1.0}},
    "not_coexistence": {("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"): {"support": 1.0}},
}

# External data-aware checks the backbone cannot express
def check_p7_suspicious_repeats(trace_activities):
    """P7: any repeated mandatory check must be preceded by a Correct Dossier
    that occurred after the previous execution of that same check."""
    findings = []
    last_seen = {}
    last_correction_idx = -1
    for i, a in enumerate(trace_activities):
        if a == "Correct Dossier":
            last_correction_idx = i
        if a in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
            if a in last_seen and last_correction_idx < last_seen[a]:
                findings.append((i, a, "repeat without intervening Correct Dossier"))
            last_seen[a] = i
    return findings

def check_p3_supplier_review(trace_activities, is_external):
    """P3: once the external/internal attribute is logged, SR presence must
    match it. Until then, is_external=None and the check only reports."""
    has_sr = "Supplier Review" in trace_activities
    if is_external is None:
        return [("unknown-sourcing", has_sr)]          # audit note, not a verdict
    if is_external and not has_sr:
        return [("missing Supplier Review on external order", True)]
    if (not is_external) and has_sr:
        return [("Supplier Review on internal order", True)]
    return []

def check_p6_affected_checks(trace_activities, affected_checks_per_correction=None):
    """P6: when the affected-check attribute becomes available, verify each
    correction's recheck set equals the affected set. Until then, report the
    observed recheck set per correction for manual review."""
    reports, i, n = [], 0, len(trace_activities)
    corr_no = 0
    while i < n:
        if trace_activities[i] == "Correct Dossier":
            corr_no += 1
            rechecks = set()
            j = i + 1
            while j < n and trace_activities[j] in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
                rechecks.add(trace_activities[j]); j += 1
            expected = (affected_checks_per_correction or {}).get(corr_no)
            reports.append((corr_no, sorted(rechecks), expected,
                            None if expected is None else set(expected) == rechecks))
            i = j
        else:
            i += 1
    return reports
```

## 7.4 Explicit semantics per requirement

- **Mandatory checks (P2):** `+( CC, TR )` — both must occur before anything downstream; either interleaving order; no tau escape. B3 is rejected structurally.
- **Optional Supplier Review (P3):** `X(SR, tau)` — optional *because the conditioning attribute is absent*, not because the policy is optional. The `check_p3_supplier_review` hook turns this from silent permissiveness into a tracked open item, and becomes enforcing the day the attribute is logged.
- **Expedite Review ordering (P4):** ER sits in a block strictly after the mandatory-check block (and correction loop) and strictly before the approval XOR; the parallel wrapper with SR permits ER-before-SR and SR-before-ER. B4 and its interleaved variants are rejected.
- **Exactly-one approval (P5):** `X(MA, AA)` between the review block and Release Order; double approval or no approval cannot fit.
- **Correction/recheck (P6, P7):** a dedicated loop makes each correction and its recheck set explicit and repeatable. The inner `X(CC, TR, +(CC,TR))` represents the F6 decision transparently: repeat CC only, TR only, or both. Repeats *without* a correction are structurally impossible in fitting traces (each check occurs once in the mandatory block and otherwise only inside a correction iteration), and `check_p7_suspicious_repeats` gives an explainable audit finding for non-fitting traces instead of a bare alignment cost.

## 7.5 Limitations from the missing attributes

- **External/internal (P3):** the model cannot *require* SR for external orders or *forbid* it for internal ones; SR remains optional for every case, so an external order that skipped SR still fits the backbone. This is a declared residual risk, mitigated by the P3 check and by the recommendation (Section 10) to start logging the attribute. If a proxy exists (e.g., supplier master data), the honest upgrade path is a *small family of attribute-conditioned models*: the same backbone with `X(SR,tau)` replaced by `SR` (external variant) or removed (internal variant).
- **Affected-check (P6):** the model lets the recheck set be freely chosen per correction; it cannot verify the set matches what the correction actually touched. B6 therefore fits the backbone, and its "valid only if the correction affected TR alone" condition is delegated to `check_p6_affected_checks` — undecidable until the attribute is logged, and reported as such rather than guessed.
- **ER/SR vs. corrections:** placing SR/ER after the correction loop forbids traces where ER occurs, then a correction happens, without ER being repeated. P4's text does not settle whether an expedite review survives a correction; I chose the stricter reading (ER reflects the final checked state). If stakeholders rule otherwise, the SR/ER block can be moved inside the loop scope — a one-block change, unlike Model C where it would spawn new branches.
- The model also deliberately duplicates CC/TR labels once (initial vs. recheck occurrence). This is a *meaningful* duplication (two distinct roles of the same activity), bounded and localized — but it will register on any duplicate-label count and slightly enlarge the alignment search space; that trade-off is accepted to represent P6 at all.

# 9. Validation Experiments

An evaluation program the governance board can rerun end-to-end:

1. **Reproducible splits.** Case-level 80/20 split with a published integer seed, published case-ID lists, and versioned preprocessing code (event filtering, lifecycle handling, timestamp repairs). Additionally a *variant-disjoint* split (no variant appears in both train and test) to measure true extrapolation, and 5× repeated splits to report variance, not a single number.
2. **Attribute-stratified holdout.** Stratify test cases by best-available proxies: presence of SR, presence of ER, approval type, presence/count of CD, check order. Report fitness/precision per stratum. This directly tests F4's claim that failures concentrate in SR+AA+alternate-order combinations.
3. **Curated negative trace suite.** Encode B3, B4, B8, X2, X4 and systematic mutations (drop one mandatory check; move ER before a check; duplicate a check without CD; append activity after Release Order; both approvals; approval before checks). *Required result: 0% of negative traces obtain cost-zero alignments.* This is the antidote to F5.
4. **Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation.** Randomly delete SR (and, separately, each other activity) from p ∈ {5%, 10%, 20%} of fitting traces; randomly duplicate CC/TR without CD. Plot fitness degradation per model. S is predicted to be pathologically flat (absorbs deletions silently — itself a finding); C to be brittle; Third should degrade gracefully *and* report explainable findings via the P7 check.
5. **Unseen-combination tests.** Generate the full cross-product of individually-approved features (check order × SR × ER × approval × correction patterns), label each cell with the policy verdict from P1–P7, and measure each model's accept/reject agreement with the labels. This replaces token-based generalization as the generalization measure.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Recompute fitness under (a) standard unit costs, (b) elevated model-move costs on CC/TR/approvals, (c) reduced log-move costs; report the ranking stability of the models across cost settings.
7. **Soundness and reachability.** `check_soundness` on all three nets; verify no dead transitions, and that every activity (esp. both CC/TR occurrences in Third, and every duplicate in C) is coverable.
8. **Separate metric reporting.** Fitness, precision (positive log *and* negative suite), unseen-combination accuracy, and simplicity reported side by side, never aggregated; duplicate-label count reported with an explicit definition (e.g., visible transitions per label in the converted net).
9. **Stakeholder comprehension protocol.** ≥8 raters from the four stakeholder groups; each answers 10 concrete yes/no behavior questions per model ("can approval happen without Technical Review?") plus a 1–5 confidence score; report accuracy, time, and inter-rater agreement — comprehension measured as *correct prediction of model behavior*, not likability.
10. **Rejection decision rule.** A model is rejected regardless of aggregate metrics if any of: (R1) any negative-suite trace fits at cost zero; (R2) >5% of policy-labeled-valid unseen combinations are rejected; (R3) the net is unsound; (R4) stakeholder behavior-prediction accuracy <70% for the model's intended audience; (R5) results not reproducible from the published seed + code within tolerance 0.005.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

- **Model S — exploration only, with a mandatory warning label; prohibited for conformance.** It may continue to serve as a communication sketch, but every artifact must state that it (a) certifies check-skipping traces as conformant (B3), (b) permits Expedite Review before the checks (B4), and (c) contains no Correct Dossier at all. Its fitness/generalization numbers must not be quoted in compliance contexts. If it is used for exploration, add Correct Dossier to it first; a map that omits an entire activity misleads even explorers.
- **Model C — retire as an executable model; retain as documentation.** Its eight branches are a useful *catalog of observed frequent variants* and can seed the negative/positive test suites, but as a conformance model it over-rejects legitimate compositional behavior (B2, B5, holdout 0.744), hard-wires accidental couplings (SR never with AA), handles corrections wrongly per P6/F6, and is unmaintainable (F2, F3). Its 0.941 precision is a training-log artifact (F5) and must not be cited as audit-readiness.
- **Neither original model may be used for compliance decisions today.**
- **Third alternative — conditional deployment for conformance.** Preconditions before any enforcement use: (1) passes the full Section 9 program, including 0% negative-suite leakage and ≥95% agreement on policy-labeled unseen combinations; (2) the governance board explicitly ratifies the two documented design decisions (ER/SR after corrections; correction requires ≥1 recheck) or the model is amended; (3) the external/internal attribute starts being logged, with the P3 check promoted from "audit note" to enforced rule within one release cycle, and the same commitment for the affected-check attribute; (4) stakeholder comprehension re-measured under the Section 9 protocol with ≥70% behavior-prediction accuracy; (5) alignment costs, seeds, and preprocessing published alongside the model. Until (1)–(5) hold, the Third model runs in *monitoring mode*: it flags deviations for human review but does not auto-reject cases.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.** This review is too comfortable rejecting the only model an auditor can actually defend. In a regulated approval process, F8's stance is correct: unseen behavior *should* be noncompliant until explicitly approved, and Model C is the only submission that implements that default. The review calls C's rejections of B2 and B5 "memorization," yet neither trace is confirmed legitimate — "plausible valid" is the reviewer's judgment, not evidence, and treating probe labels as ground truth while calling metrics "only evidence" is asymmetric skepticism. Meanwhile the proposed third model quietly reintroduces the very laxities it condemns: Supplier Review is optional for everyone, so an external order skipping a required review fits perfectly; the recheck choice after a correction is unverifiable, so a manipulated dossier can be "corrected" with the cheapest recheck and pass; and its two-layer design (procedural backbone plus external Python checks) has never been shown to stakeholders — its comprehension score is an assumption. Retiring C also discards the one artifact whose accepted language is fully enumerable and hence fully certifiable. The cautious governance move is the opposite of the recommendation: keep C in enforcement with a fast branch-approval process for new variants, and use the third model only as a monitoring overlay until it earns trust.

**What would change my decision.** Evidence that the failing holdout cases are predominantly deviant rather than legitimate (case-level adjudication by the process owner); a demonstrated branch-governance process that updates C within days, not months; negative-suite and unseen-combination results showing C's policy accuracy exceeding the third model's; or stakeholder tests showing the two-layer third model is *less* correctly understood than C's explicit variant list. Conversely, my recommendation would harden if the external/internal attribute, once logged, confirms SR-skipping external orders that only the third model's audit layer surfaces.
