## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **E1** | Policy regime $P_0$ (current flag display + routing) vs. $P_1$ (flag suppressed, underlying score retained) | Case $i$ arriving during active operational hours | Total cycle time ($T_{close} - T_{arrival}$) | Total Policy Effect | Yes | Concurrent queue state changes across cases (SUTVA violation via queue spillover) |
| **E2** | Assigning flag $F=1$ vs $F=0$ under fixed triage queue policy | Case $i$ at Triage stage | Triage queue wait ($T_{triage\_start} - T_{arrival}$) | Controlled Direct Effect (CDE) | Yes | Differential priority lane capacity and arrival load selection |
| **E3** | Mandating manual review ($M=1$) vs triage-discretionary routing ($M=M(0)$) under $F=1$ | Case $i$ conditional on $F=1$ | Total cycle time | Interventional Indirect Effect via stage occurrence | Partially | Exposure-induced confounding by triage-discovered risk and complex case mix |
| **E4** | Injecting 1 additional flagged case into manual review queue | Shared Manual Review Queue shift-period $s$ | Queue wait time for subsequent unflagged cases | Spillover / Interventional Disproportionate Effect | Yes | Unobserved queue length at case arrival and resource scheduling variability |
| **E5** | Lowering escalation threshold under $F=1$ vs baseline escalation policy | Case $i$ entering Manual Review | Escalation queue wait ($T_{esc\_start} - T_{man\_done}$) + cycle time | Interventional Indirect Effect via escalation path | Yes | Senior team capacity constraints and unobserved case complexity $U_{complexity}$ |
| **E6** | Policy regime $P_2$ (priority triage, optional manual review) vs. $P_0$ | Case $i$ arriving under system load $L$ | End-to-end duration ($T_{close} - T_{arrival}$) | Total Interventional Policy Contrast | Yes | Spillover interactions across shared review resources between policy regimes |
| **E7** | Policy regime $P_0$ vs $P_1$ | Case $i$ reaching final decision | Binary decision accuracy / outcome rate (`outcome`) | Total Policy Effect on Quality | Yes | Latent ground-truth risk $U_{risk}$ is unobserved; outcome depends on reviewer fatigue |

### Choice of Causal Framework
We adopt the **Interventional Disproportionate/Mediated Effects** and **Separable Effects** framework (Didelez, 2019; Vansteelandt et al., 2012) rather than classic Natural Direct/Indirect Effects (NDIE). NDIE relies on cross-world counterfactuals ($Y(x, M(x'))$), which require no exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding and strict non-interference (SUTVA). In process-mining and queueing environments:
1. **Interference is inherent:** Priority routing for Case $A$ increases queue delay for Case $B$ via shared service resources.
2. **Exposure-induced confounding is present:** The flag triggers mandatory manual review, which uncovers case-level details that alter escalation decisions and queue states downstream.

Interventional effects evaluate well-defined policy interventions on accessible system mechanisms (e.g., routing rules vs queue priority vs flag display) without requiring cross-world counterfactual independence.

### Path Dynamics and Confounding (152 words)
Interference arises because triage, manual review, and escalation queues are shared multi-server resources. Bypassing triage wait via `priority_lane` accelerates early completion but injects cases into `manual_queue_wait` at a higher arrival rate, inducing downstream congestion for concurrent cases. 

Exposure-induced confounding occurs because the `flag` forces entry into `manual_review`. In manual review, reviewers process `complexity` and unmeasured case risk ($U_{unmeasured\_complexity}$), which directly determines `escalation` to the scarce senior team. Thus, `manual_review` acts as a policy-mandated mediator that exposes the case to downstream escalation confounders. 

Treatment-confounder feedback occurs when accelerated triage under high `arrival_load` creates temporal batching in the manual review queue, elevating queue wait times, exhausting manual reviewer capacity ($U_{reviewer\_fatigue}$), and increasing subsequent escalation rates.

### Mechanistic Analysis of Observed Contrasts (148 words)
The observed contrasts show an operational trade-off. Flagged cases ($F=1$) experience priority triage queue acceleration, waiting an average of only 0.24h compared to 2.48h for unflagged cases. However, downstream bottlenecking severely reverses this advantage: $F=1$ cases wait an average of 6.50h to enter manual review (versus 1.88h for unflagged cases that undergo manual review) and 7.00h in the escalation queue. 

These empirical contrasts are not automatically causal representations of the flag alone. The flag operates bundled with mandatory policy routing (100% of $F=1$ cases undergo manual review vs 40% of $F=0$) and selection bias ($F=1$ cases disproportionately carry High complexity: 50% vs 30%). Thus, the cycle time inflation (16.50h vs 8.17h) reflects a combination of policy-mandated stage addition, queue congestion spillovers, and underlying case-mix complexity rather than direct physical slowing caused by the flag attribute.

| **1. | **2. Queue-state-aware analysis** | Queue-conditioned Discrete Event Simulation / Structural DAG | Instantaneous queue length $Q(t)$, worker availability, `arrival_load` | No unobserved queue-level prioritization overrides | Test effect of flag on manual wait conditioning on exact queue length $Q(t)$ upon arrival | If flag effect on wait disappears given $Q(t)$, wait delay is entirely queue-structural |
| **3. Policy-change / Encouragement** | Stepped-wedge cluster rollout or randomized flag suppression ($P_1$) | Time trends, shift fixed effects, `arrival_load` | SUTVA across shift clusters; stable policy implementation | Placebo test on pre-intervention period cases | Significant "effect" during placebo period indicates unobserved temporal confounding |
| **4. Mediation analysis** | Interventional / Separable Effects decomposition | Baseline `complexity`, `arrival_load`, `risk_score` | Sequential ignorability for mediator given observed confounders | Test for interaction between flag and unobserved complexity using sensitivity parameters | High sensitivity to hidden confounders ($E\text{-value} < 1.5$) invalidates mediation split |
| **5. Negative-control test** | Negative-control exposure (future load) & outcome (pre-triage registration time) | `arrival_load`, arrival time of day | Negative control outcome is uninfluenced by flag policy | Estimate flag effect on pre-arrival registration duration (must equal 0) | Non-zero effect indicates unobserved system-level temporal confounding |
| **6. | **7. Interference-aware design** | Cluster-randomized trial across independent temporal shifts | Shift-level arrival load, staffing levels | No spillover of queues across shift boundaries (inter-shift isolation) | Test for balance in unflagged case wait times across treatment vs control shifts | Unflagged wait time increases during high-flag shifts, confirming queue interference |

## 6. Policy comparison

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### Limited Pilot Specification: Policy P2 (242 words)

#### Eligibility
All cases arriving during weekday operational shifts (08:00–20:00) with active triage staffing.

#### Randomization Rule
Cluster-randomized by 4-hour shift blocks. Treatment shifts apply **P2** (triage discretion: triagers view flag and risk score, but manually check review requirement); Control shifts apply **P0** (mandatory manual review for $F=1$).

#### Queue-Capacity Guardrail
If the manual review queue length exceeds 8 cases or wait time exceeds 4.0 hours during a P2 shift, the system automatically reverts to P0 routing for the remainder of the shift.

#### Primary & Secondary Outcomes
- **Primary:** Total case cycle time ($T_{close} - T_{arrival}$) and manual review queue wait time ($T_{man\_start} - T_{tri\_done}$).
- **Secondary:** Final decision accuracy / error rate (audited post-hoc on a 20% random sample) and escalation rate.

#### Minimum Process Data Needed
Timestamps for `arrival`, `triage_start`, `triage_done`, `manual_start`, `manual_done`, `escalation_start`, `escalation_done`, `close`; `risk_score`; `complexity`; assigned `triage_resource` and `review_resource`.

#### Stopping Conditions
1. Statistically significant increase in false approval rate ($> 2.5\%$ absolute increase vs control, $p < 0.05$).
2. Escalation queue wait exceeding 10.0 hours due to delayed surge arrivals.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden Risk Confounding:** If unobserved case risk ($U_{risk}$) accounts for $> 85\%$ of the variation in escalation decisions and manual processing difficulty, then suppressing the flag ($P_1$) or removing mandatory review ($P_2$) will fail to reduce total cycle time because reviewers will manually re-route those exact cases to review and escalation anyway.
2. **Queue Interference & Dynamic Congestion:** If queue congestion is non-linear and dominated by arrival spikes rather than flag volume, then opening dedicated manual review capacity ($P_3$) will shift queue bottlenecks back to triage start, increasing overall system cycle time for unflagged cases.
3. **Decision Quality & Downstream Harm:** If mandatory manual review under $F=1$ reduces catastrophic false-approval errors by $> 5\%$ compared to discretionary review, then any cycle-time reduction achieved under $P_2$ is outweighed by downstream financial/regulatory risk costs.
4. **Timestamp Semantics:** If `manual_start` records the timestamp when a case is assigned to a reviewer batch rather than active human working time, then recorded "manual queue wait" is inflated work time, and true queue waiting time is located elsewhere in the system.
5. **Positivity / Overlap Violation:** If high-complexity cases under $F=1$ have zero empirical probability of bypassing manual review under operational policy, then controlled direct effects conditioning on $M=0$ for $F=1$ are non-identifiable without structural extrapolation assumptions.
6. **Reviewer Behavioral Adaptation (Goodhart’s Law):** If reviewers learn that unflagged cases bypass mandatory audit and selectively delay unflagged cases to perform discretionary background checks, then removing mandatory review ($P_2$) will induce systemic slowing across unflagged cases.

## 9. Final synthesis

### Directly Observed Data
The event log empirically shows that cases carrying the high-risk flag ($F=1$) experience drastically shorter initial queue wait times at triage (mean 0.24h vs 2.48h for unflagged cases) due to priority lane assignment. However, $F=1$ cases exhibit double the average total cycle time (16.50h vs 8.17h). Mechanistically, 100% of $F=1$ cases enter manual review (waiting an average of 6.50h vs 1.88h for reviewed $F=0$ cases) and 60% are escalated to a scarce senior team (waiting an additional 7.00h).

### Plausible Mechanism
The paradox is driven by a policy bundle and downstream queue dynamics rather than physical processing sluggishness caused by the flag itself. The flag acts as a deterministic routing policy trigger, forcing cases into downstream manual review regardless of triager assessment. Because flagged cases disproportionately arrive during high system load and carry higher complexity, rushing them through triage creates downstream queue batching. The shared manual and escalation queues become bottlenecked, compounding idle waiting time.

### Unidentified Elements
Without experimental or structural interventions, observational data cannot isolate how much of the escalation wait is caused by flag-induced confirmation bias versus true unobserved case risk ($U_{risk}$). Furthermore, cross-case queue spillover (interference) prevents traditional estimation of natural direct and indirect effects.

### Optimal Next Intervention
The most informative next step is a 4-hour shift-level **cluster-randomized pilot of Policy P2** ( priority triage with triage-discretionary manual review). This pilot directly tests whether removing mandatory review bottlenecks downstream cycle times without compromising decision accuracy, generating unconfounded, queue-aware evidence for permanent policy design.
