## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated Facts (Established by Evidence):**
1. **CS9 Requirement (N1):** Every supplier must undergo Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation; Manual_Procurement_Approval is mandatory before Activate_Supplier; requester and approver must be different humans.
2. **PD3 Pilot (N2):** For LOWrisk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, Auto_Approve may replace Manual_Procurement_Approval; Quality_Review can occur up to two business days after provisional activation.
3. **FD7 Emergency (N3):** For critical relief suppliers under flooding, provisional activation may precede Quality_Review and manual procurement approval; Sanctions_Check must occur beforehand; no sanction on spend above EUR20,000 until retrospective approval.
4. **CRM Migration (N4):** Notification now occurs via CRM; ~70% of notifications are captured with stable `notification_id`; older links are mutable text fields.
5. **Stakeholder Practice (N5):** Operations frequently uses Request_More_Info cycles; there is a desire for a permanent lowrisk autoapproval branch.
6. **Observed Cases:**  
   - SUP701 follows M0 perfectly (compliant).  
   - SUP702 uses Auto_Approve within PD3 eligibility (potentially compliant under pilot).  
   - SUP703 uses pilot outside eligibility (noncompliant).  
   - SUP704 follows FD7 (potentially compliant under emergency).  
   - SUP705 operates after FD7 expiry but flags continued emergency (ambiguous).  
   - SUP706 lacks notification (dataquality defect).  
   - SUP707 includes request loops (loop not in M0).  
   - SUP708 activates before Sanctions_Check (clear violation).  
   - SUP709 rejects early (scope ambiguity).  
   - SUP710 is a reactivation lacking Register_Supplier and Quality_Review (model gap).  
   - SUP711 duplicated Register_Supplier (logging artifact).  
   - SUP712 requester/approver are same (resource rule violation).  
   - SUP713 renamed Quality_Review to QA_Clearance (label equivalency).  
   - SUP714 uses Auto_Approve after PD3 expiry (oversight).  
   - SUP715 involves related approver and reviewer (conflictofinterest).  

**Disputed Propositions (Depend on Authority, Scope, or Assumptions):**
1. Whether **rejected leads** (e.g., SUP709) are governed by M0. CS9 is silent on this, creating a grey area.
2. Whether **SUP705** remains under FD7’s implied authority after its formal expiry; operations claim the flood emergency persisted sans formal extension.
3. Whether **SUP702’s Quality_Review** being performed the next business day satisfies the pilot’s “up to two business days” condition (borderline but generally acceptable).
4. Whether **SUP714’s** Auto_Approve usage constitutes a permissible extension or a violation of PD3’s limited window.
5. Whether **SUP708**’s presanctions activation reflects a procedural slip or a deliberate emergency shortcut.

**Burden of Proof:**
- **Noncompliance (Prosecution):** Must demonstrate that a case clearly violates a formally codified rule (e.g., CS9, FD7, PD3) or a mandatory resource rule. The burden is preponderance of evidence that the violation is material and not justified by a legitimate exception.
- **Exception Acceptance (Defense):** Must show that all deviations are covered by an explicit pilot, emergency, or recognized practice, and that the underlying normative rule (CS9) is appropriately overridden. The burden shifts to proving that the deviation is minor, documented, and did not expose material risk.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially noncompliant because several observed traces directly breach normative controls and mechanisms that are either explicitly required or critically implied by CS9, PD3, and FD7. The prosecution focuses on three pillars: **formal authority expiry**, **obvious controlflow violations**, and **highrisk misuse of informal practices**.

### 2.1 Formal Authority & Expiry Violations
- **SUP703**: Uses Auto_Approve outside PD3’s eligibility window (ineligible LOWrisk DE supplier). Without a documented pilot extension, this breaches PD3’s scope, exposing the organization to procurement fraud (autoapprove without justified risk reduction).
- **SUP710**: Reactivation lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review, violating CS9’s “every activation requires quality review.” The absence of a reactivation procedure in CS9 does not excuse the missing control; systemic risk of reintroducing unreviewed suppliers rises.
- **SUP714**: Auto_Approve executed after PD3 expired (two days past 20July31). No permanent policy amendment exists, making this a clear lapse in compliance management.

### 2.2 ControlFlow Deviations Hiding by M0
M0 omits crucial concurrency constraints and branching logic that are essential for CS9 enforcement:
- **Sanctions_Check & Quality_Review concurrency**: While M0 models them in parallel (`StrictPartialOrder`), CS9 mandates *both* be completed *before* activation. The model does not enforce a hard **AND** gate; an operator could inadvertently skip one, as seen in **SUP708** (activation before sanctions check).
- **Manual_Procurement_Approval**: M0 treats it as a linear step after registration, but CS9 explicitly requires distinct humans. **SUP712** (same human as requester/approver) and **SUP715** (related spouses) slip through because M0 lacks a resource rule check.

### 2.3 Misuse of Informal Practices as Policy
Stakeholderdriven behaviors (e.g., Request_More_Info loops, permanent autoapproval desire) are not codified, leading to:
- **SUP707**: Loop of Request_More_Info is beneficial but unsupported by M0, risking incomplete information gatherings that could enable noncompliant activations.
- **Cultural normalization of exceptions**: 312 Auto_Approve cases in JuneJuly, 289 of which are indeed PD3eligible, but 14 are clearly ineligible. This suggests systemic laxity, not just isolated incidents.

### 2.4 Aggregated Risk
- **Aggregated Deviations**: 47 Request_More_Info cases are safe but indicate procedural gaps; 6 postpilot Auto_Approve cases after PD3 expiry suggest **operational drift**.
- **Audit Findings**: Internal Audit flagged **SUP703** and **SUP708** as potentially severe, confirming that noncompliant deviations can materially impact procurement integrity and regulatory exposure.

**Conclusion (Prosecution):** The operation is materially noncompliant in several respects, primarily due to breaches of formally mandated procedural gates and an overreliance on undocumented exceptions. The existing M0 model fails to enforce essential controls, exposing the organization to regulatory and financial risk.

## 3. Defense Brief  

The existing normative model M0 is obsolete, incorrectly scoped, and insufficiently expressive because it does not accommodate the dynamic policies (pilots, emergencies, reactivations) and business realities observed in recent operations. The defense argues that many deviations are **justified exceptions** or **systemic data/logic defects** that expose no material risk when properly understood.

### 3.1 M0’s Scope & Model Limitations  
1. **Concurrency Without Enforcement:** While M0 places Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrently, it does **not** enforce a mutualexclusion gate that both must complete before the exclusive choice (`decision`). This omission allows traces like **SUP708** where activation occurs before sanctions clearance—an artifact of model misinterpretation rather than a deliberate noncompliance.  
2. **Lack of Pilot/Emergency Overlays:** M0 lacks conditional branches for PD3, FD7, or CS9exception mechanisms. Consequently, the pilot’s lowrisk autoapproval and FD7’s provisional activation are **not modeled**, compelling operators to rely on adhoc judgment (e.g., **SUP702**, **SUP704**). When these traces adhere to the pilot’s explicit constraints, they are *legitimate* exceptions, not breaches.  
3. **Missing Dataaware Rules:** M0 does not incorporate eligibility criteria, transaction caps, or expiration dates. For instance, **SUP703** falls outside PD3’s eligibility; however, the model cannot flag it, leaving the operator to assume it’s compliant—a defect of the **model’s dataawareness**, not the operator’s intent.  
4. **Resource & ConflictofInterest Rules:** CS9 demands distinct humans for requester and approver, yet M0 offers no enforcement. The **SUP712** and **SUP715** cases illustrate that such violations are **policylevel** (resource rule) rather than **processlevel** compliance failures, thus not constituting noncompliance under a properly scoped M0 that acknowledges delegated authority.

### 3.2 Justified Exceptions & Operational Realities  
- **Pilot & Emergency Integration:** Cases **SUP702**, **SUP704**, and **SUP705** (subject to FD7’s implicit postexpiry operation) follow the *pilot* or *emergency* rules as they were intended. These are **documented exceptions** (PD3, FD7) that M0 cannot encode but are **legally permissible** within their effective windows. Their adherence to the respective constraints (e.g., quality review within two days for FD7) validates that the deviation is **justified**, not a breach.  
- **Reactivation & Duplicate Registration:** **SUP710** and **SUP711** demonstrate that the model’s omission of reactivation logic is a *data* deficiency rather than evidence of noncompliance. In practice, reactivations are governed by a separate procedural annex (outside CS9) that M0 does not need to capture. This is a **scope limitation**.  
- **Request_More_Info Loops:** While M0 lacks a loop construct, the extra cycles in **SUP707** are *desired* by stakeholders and, when completed before the decision, align with CS9’s spirit of thorough review. The absence of a formal loop does not implicate noncompliance; it merely reflects **model expressiveness**.  
- **Label Equivalence & Renaming:** **SUP713**’s rename from “Quality_Review” to “QA_Clearance” illustrates benign semantic change that does **not** affect control flow. M0’s label sensitivity is a *technical* constraint that does not affect the underlying policy compliance.  

### 3.3 Risk vs. Deviation  
- **Material Risk:** The prosecution’s highlighted cases (**SUP703**, **SUP708**) are the only ones where the deviation directly threatens regulatory compliance with *no* documented exception. Even there, **SUP703** breaches PD3’s eligibility, yet the organization’s internal audit treated it as “potentially severe,” implying the risk was **nontrivial** but *documentable*.  
- **DataQuality Issues:** **SUP706** and **SUP712** reflect logging or alias resolution defects. These are **dataquality** problems, not procedural noncompliance. Correcting the CRM linkage or alias consolidation would resolve them without altering the underlying policy.  
- **Normalization of Exceptions:** The surge in Auto_Approve usage (312 cases) shows that when pilots and emergencies are **effectively managed**, the process adapts. The lack of a permanent autoapproval policy for lowrisk suppliers is a governance gap, but it does not invalidate the compliance of the *eligible* subset.

**Conclusion (Defense):** M0 is fundamentally **obsolete** for the operational landscape because it fails to capture pilots, emergencies, resource constraints, and dataaware eligibility. Most observed deviations are **justified exceptions** or **model/data defects** that do not, by themselves, constitute material noncompliance. The organization’s risk exposure is mitigated when the operational context (pilot, emergency, reactivation) is explicitly overlayed onto M0, and data quality (notification IDs, alias resolution) is remediated.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture  

### 6.1 Layered Structure  

| Layer | Purpose | Key Components |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **ControlFlow Layer (POWL)** | Formalizes base onboarding steps and overlays for pilots/emergencies. | - `BasePOWL`: Register_Supplier  Concurrent(Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review)  Decision(Manual_Procurement_Approval vs Reject)  Notify  Close. <br> - `PilotOverlay`: Conditional branch for AUTO_APPROVE when PD3 eligibility satisfied. <br> - `EmergencyOverlay`: Conditional branch allowing provisional activation before Quality_Review when FD7 active. |
| **DataAware Rule Layer** | Enforces eligibility, caps, dates, and resource constraints. | - **Eligibility Engine**: Evaluates supplier risk, corporate group membership, and pilot eligibility flags. <br> - **Expiration Manager**: Checks effective dates of pilots/emergencies. <br> - **ConflictofInterest Checker**: Verifies distinct human actors and relationship disclosures. |
| **EventLogging & Monitoring Layer** | Guarantees traceability and supports compliance evidence. | - **Notification Service**: Stable `notification_id` linkage via CRM, with immutable timestamp. <br> - **Audit Trail**: Stores approval delegation, decision timestamps, and policy version IDs. |
| **Governance Layer** | Provides policyoverlay governance and exception workflows. | - **Policy Registry**: Stores CS9, PD3, FD7, and any permanent autoapproval policies with versioning. <br> - **Exception Workflow**: Formal Request_More_Info loop, autoreject triggers, and escalation paths. |
| **Operational Interface** | Connects business users to the model through UI/automation. | - **UI Canonical Steps**: Aligns user actions with POWL transitions, highlights active overlays. <br> - **Automation Hooks**: Allows PM4Py to ingest live event streams for realtime compliance checks. |

### 6.2 Selected Mechanisms  

1. **Conditional Branches (PM4Py)**:  
   ```python
   pilot_branch = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.AND,
                               children=[EligibilityCondition("PD3_Eligible", value=True),
                                         TimeCondition("PD3_Effective", start="2026-06-01", end="2026-07-31")])
   emergency_branch = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.AND,
                                   children=[TimeCondition("FD7_Effective", start="2026-07-10", end="2026-07-12"),
                                             AmountCondition("SpendCap", max=20000)])
   ```
2. **Resource Constraint**: A DECLAREstyle rule (not POWL) to enforce distinct actors:
   ```prolog
   distinct_human(Requester, Manual_Approver).
   conflict_of_interest(Approver, Reviewer) => require_disclosure.
   ```
3. **Loop for Request_More_Info** (approximate via silent transitions):
   ```python
   request_more_info = SilentTransition(label="Request_More_Info")
   while_loop = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_more_info, Quality_Review])
   while_loop.add_edge(Quality_Review, request_more_info)   # feedback edge
   ```

## 7. PM4Py Model & Data Proposal  

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, Transition, OperatorPOWL
from pm4py.algo.discovery.directed_petrify import factory as petri_factory
# Core control flow (BasePOWL)
base = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=["Register_1"])
register = Transition(label="Register_1")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
base.add_node(register)
base.add_node(sanctions)
base.add_node(quality)

# Concurrent checks
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
checks.add_edge(sanctions, quality)  # implicit AND: both required
# Enforce order: Register  ConcurrentChecks  Decision
base.add_edge(register, sanctions)   # implicit ordering for first check; assuming sequential init
# Decision node using XOR for approved vs reject
approved_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=["Manual_Approval", "Activate"])
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_01")
approved_branch.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

reject = Transition(label="Reject_01")
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approved_branch, reject])
base.add_edge(quality, decision)

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

base.add_edge(decision, notify)
base.add_edge(notify, close)

# Pilot overlay (PD3)
pilot_cond = Transition(label="Pilot_Eligibility_Condition")
pilot_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=["Auto_Approve_01", "Provisional_Activate"])
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate")
pilot_branch.add_edge(auto_approve, provisional_activate)

pilot_overlay = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.IF,
                             children=[pilot_cond, pilot_branch])
# Link eligibility condition before base decision
base.add_edge(decision, pilot_cond)   # postdecision check for pilot eligibility

# Emergency overlay (FD7)
emergency_cond = Transition(label="Emergency_Eligibility")
emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=["Provisional_Activate_Emergency"])
# Note: provisional activation precedes Quality_Review in emergency path
emergency_branch.add_edge(activate, quality)   # after provisional activate, quality still required but later
emergency_overlay = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.IF,
                                 children=[emergency_cond, emergency_branch])

# Example placement: after quality check, evaluate emergency overlay
base.add_edge(quality, emergency_cond)

# Request_More_Info loop (approximation via silent transition)
request_info = SilentTransition(label="Request_More_Info")
quality.add_edge(request_info, quality)   # feedback loop

# Data overlay placeholders (to be linked from event store)
# 1. Eligibility flags (PD3, FD7, conflictofinterest)
# 2. Notification ID association (via separate data layer)
# 3. Amount and timing caps enforced in rule engine (outside POWL)

# Final model object
model = base
print(model)
```

### Event/Collection Proposal
- **Source Streams**: Register events, sanctions results, quality outcomes, manual approvals, autoapprove decisions, emergency flags, CRM notifications.
- **Stable IDs**: Each event carries a UUID; `notification_id` is a foreign key linking CRM to POWL trace (`trace_id`).
- **Immutable Timestamps**: `event_time` stored in UTC, never altered.
- **Derived Attributes**: Compute `is_eligible_PD3`, `is_in_emergency_window`, `is_distinct_human`, `has_disclosure`.
- **Monitoring**: Realtime check that if `Activate_01` fires, then either `Manual_Approval` occurred **or** `Pilot_Eligibility_Condition` true **or** `Emergency_Eligibility` true, otherwise reject.

This architecture ensures **clear separation of controlflow (POWL) from dataaware policies**, enabling both compliance enforcement and flexibility for pilots/emergencies while preserving auditability.
