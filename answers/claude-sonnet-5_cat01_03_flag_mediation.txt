## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework note: Because the flag is generated from `risk_score`+`complexity` and is *policy-bundled* (flag  lane, flag  mandatory review referral are near-deterministic in this data: 10/10 flagged cases were reviewed, 0/10 unflagged-Low-complexity cases were flagged), classic natural direct/indirect effects (NDE/NIE) require a cross-world counterfactual ("flagged but mediator takes its unflagged-world value") that is not observed and may not even be realizable given the deterministic routing rule. I therefore rely on **controlled direct effects (CDE)**, **interventional (stochastic) direct/indirect effects** in the VanderWeele sense (mediator distribution set by an external hypothetical policy, not by cross-world matching), and **separable-effects** style decompositions that split the bundled "flag" treatment into distinguishable manipulable components (display vs. routing-rule vs. review-mandate vs. escalation-rule). Pure NDE/NIE are flagged as *not* identified.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | flag=1 vs flag=0, current policy P0 (observational contrast, *not* a pre/post-introduction comparison — no baseline period is in the data) | case, all cases under P0 | cycle_time | Total associational effect | Partially — well-defined as a contrast, not automatically causal | confounding by complexity/underlying_risk; positivity violation (no Low-complexity flagged cases) |
| E2 | P0 vs P1 (suppress flag display only, keep score/human info) | case | triage_wait + triage_service | Controlled direct effect of *display* only | Well-defined as a policy contrast; not identified from this data alone | unclear whether lane assignment is automated off `risk_score` or off the *displayed* flag — ambiguity in mechanism, not just data |
| E3 | flag=1 vs flag=0, mediator `manual_review` set to its P0-unflagged distribution (interventional) | case | cycle_time, manual_queue_wait | Interventional indirect effect via review occurrence | Not well-defined via NDE/NIE (positivity: P(review=0\|flag=1)0); the interventional-distribution version is definable but requires assuming transportability of the unflagged referral mechanism to flagged cases | near-deterministic mediator given flag  no empirical support for counterfactual arm |
| E4 | flag=1 vs flag=0, mediator `manual_queue_wait` fixed via P3-style added capacity (do-intervention on queue capacity, not on wait itself) | case | cycle_time | Interventional (separable) effect via queueing mechanism | Well-defined as a *capacity* intervention effect; ill-defined as a "set wait to x" effect because wait is jointly determined by other cases (SUTVA violation) | shared-queue interference; cannot "set" one case's wait without affecting others |
| E5 | flag=1 vs flag=0, mediator `escalation` set via P4 criteria change | case | cycle_time, decision_outcome | CDE at fixed escalation-criteria policy | Well-defined as a policy-level CDE | escalation almost perfectly tracks `complexity`, so flag's marginal contribution is weakly identified separately from complexity |
| E6 | P0 vs P3 (protected review capacity for flagged) | case + shared queue (cluster) | total cycle_time | Total interventional effect of a capacity policy | Well-defined if randomized at shift/queue-cluster level | interference across clusters if flagged cases can be redirected between clusters |
| E7 | P0 vs P2 (remove mandatory review) | case | decision_outcome (Approve/Reject) as proxy for decision quality | Total effect on a quality proxy | Weakly well-defined — no ground-truth "correct decision" field exists, only a proxy | outcome misclassification / no rework or audit field to validate quality |
| E8 | flag=1 vs flag=0, restricted to cases near the flagging threshold (risk_score  0.55–0.67) | case, local sample near cutoff | triage_wait, manual_queue_wait, cycle_time | Local (quasi-RD) total effect | Well-defined locally, but small-n and complexity imbalance near the cutoff weakens it | complexity is unbalanced even locally (e.g., R08 Medium/flag1 vs R09/R17 High/flag0 near the same score) |


**Interference / confounding / feedback (180 words):** The manual-review and escalation queues are shared resources, so routing many flagged cases into review (a direct consequence of the flag policy) raises `manual_queue_wait` for *other* cases waiting in the same queue — a violation of the no-interference (SUTVA) assumption that undermines simple flag-vs-no-flag comparisons of waiting time. This is also **exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding**: `arrival_load` and contemporaneous queue occupancy affect both whether/how fast a case is reviewed (the mediator) and the final cycle time (the outcome), and that occupancy is itself partly caused by the flag policy operating on other cases. There is also a **treatment-confounder feedback loop**: `manual_queue_wait` for case *i* depends on prior escalation/review decisions for other flagged cases, which were themselves shaped by the same flag policy, so mediators are not exogenous with respect to the treatment applied fleet-wide, not just to the focal case.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Absent stages are coded `NA` at the case level and **excluded, not zero-filled**, when computing conditional means for `manual_service`, `manual-done-to-escalation-start`, and `escalation_service` (the "n" for that column is reported separately when smaller than the subgroup n). Mixing structural absence with continuous duration would bias downstream means toward zero and hide the occurrence-mediation effect.

(All values in minutes, medians/means from the 20-case sample; small n noted in parentheses.)

Two contrasts are consistent with "faster triage, slower downstream": (1) flag=1 vs flag=0 shows triage wait dropping ~10× (14914 min) while manual-queue wait rises ~3.5× (113390 min); (2) within High complexity, flagged and unflagged both wait long for review, but flagged cases wait even longer, suggesting congestion from volume rather than pure severity. These are **not automatically causal**: triage speed differs partly because flagged and unflagged cases use disjoint resource pools (T1/T2 vs T3/T4), a staffing artifact bundled with the flag policy, not a "pure" flag effect; and complexity is a near-perfect predictor of both flag status and downstream duration (no Low-complexity flagged cases exist), so much of the "downstream delay" may reflect case mix rather than flag-triggered routing. The manual-queue gap could also reflect selection: the four unflagged reviewed cases may be atypical (human-judgment fast-tracked), not representative of what unflagged review would look like at flagged volumes.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does flag status matter independent of score/complexity near the decision boundary? | Local threshold / fuzzy-RD design using cases with risk_score 0.55–0.67 | complexity, arrival_load as covariates in bandwidth | Threshold is not perfectly confounded with complexity locally | Check covariate balance (complexity) just above/below cutoff; here it is *not* balanced (R08 Medium vs R09/R17 High) | If local balance can be achieved (e.g., pooling more data) and gap persists, supports a genuine flag effect beyond score |
| Is the manual-queue delay driven by contemporaneous congestion rather than "flag" per se? | Queue-state-aware regression: predict manual_queue_wait from concurrent queue occupancy at triage_done time, plus flag | concurrent occupancy, arrival_load, hour-of-day | Occupancy at triage_done is measurable from overlapping start/end intervals | If flag's coefficient shrinks to ~0 after conditioning on occupancy, congestion (not flag) explains the delay | If flag remains a strong predictor after full occupancy control, supports a genuine queue-discipline effect from the flag |
| What happens if mandatory review is relaxed? | Randomized encouragement / staggered policy pilot: randomly exempt a subset of flagged cases from mandatory review | None needed if randomized; use as instrument for review occurrence | Exclusion restriction: encouragement affects outcome only via review occurrence | Compare cycle_time and reject rate across arms | If exempted cases show similar reject rates but much shorter cycle time, supports P2; if reject rate rises, do not pursue P2 |
| How much of the total effect is occurrence- vs. wait- vs. escalation-mediated? | Sequential g-formula / natural-effects-style decomposition with measured mediators | complexity, risk_score, arrival_load at each stage | Sequential conditional ignorability (likely violated by unmeasured U_reviewer_judgment) | Sensitivity analysis varying assumed unmeasured confounding; check decomposition stability | If decomposition flips sign or magnitude under modest sensitivity perturbation, treat any single-mediator claim as unreliable |
| Is there uncontrolled confounding leaking into unrelated stages? | Negative-control outcome: residual-to-close (pure post-decision admin overhead) should not depend on flag | none (test itself is the control) | Negative control genuinely unaffected by flag except through confounding | Compare residual-to-close by flag (67 vs 71 min — nearly identical) | If residual-to-close differed sharply by flag, it would indicate hidden confounding contaminating "administrative" stages, undermining the whole framework |
| How much hidden risk could explain the gap? | E-value/bias-factor sensitivity analysis on the within-High-complexity flag gap (flag=1 High mean 1344 vs flag=0 High mean 924) | complexity-stratified comparison | Unmeasured confounder roughly independent of complexity given data | Compute confounder-exposure/-outcome association needed to null the ~420 min residual gap | If a plausible unmeasured factor (e.g., regulatory hold, prior offense) could easily produce that magnitude, weaken causal claims |
| Does flagged-case volume spill over onto unflagged cases? | Interference-aware design: compare unflagged wait across shifts/queue-clusters with different flagged density (cluster or shift randomization) | shift/time-block as cluster unit, cluster-robust SEs | Spillover contained within cluster (no cross-cluster leakage) | Dose-response: unflagged wait vs. concurrent flagged density | If unflagged wait is insensitive to flagged density, interference is negligible; if strongly dose-responsive, confirms shared-queue interference |
