## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Each release is a separate decision point. I reconstruct the case state from its history up to and including that release, rather than using the last row of the case.

For each case, order events lexicographically by `(time:timestamp, event_index)`. A nonblank attribute cell updates that attribute at its event; a blank cell leaves the preceding value unchanged. Before an attribute's first supplied value, its state is UNKNOWN. Forward-fill only within a case and only forward in this order. Updates attached to a release apply to that release. An exemption identifier is a reference to evidence, not proof that an exemption is valid. The jurisdiction on `Sanctions_List_Update` identifies the jurisdiction added to the shared list; it is not, by itself, a change to a transaction's jurisdiction.

An event at an earlier timestamp precedes a later event. At the same timestamp, the lower `event_index` precedes the higher one. Thus TX-311's Enhanced_Review at `(16:20, 3)` reviewed US; the jurisdiction update at `(16:20, 4)` then made the transaction DE. “Before” and “after” below mean this strict event order, not strictly different wall-clock timestamps. List changes are shared information and must also be compared with affected cases' screens and releases. A cross-system equal-time comparison without a comparable sequence requires additional ordering evidence.

I use the following interpretation of materiality:

- Actual jurisdiction, risk, and customer-type changes are material to review context, applicable triggers, or scope. A review must address the applicable context; a jurisdiction change to DE can remove R1 at release.
- For amount-dependent review validity, R4 governs: an increase exceeding 20% of the amount associated with the relevant review invalidates it. Exactly 20% does not. A decrease alone does not invalidate it, and TX-306's 4.55% increase does not automatically require another review or approval.
- I use the reviewed amount as the amount baseline, including cumulative exposure above that baseline. Once invalidated, a review is not restored merely by a later decrease. A new review supplies a new baseline. Unknown baselines or changes from an unknown amount require evidence rather than a fabricated percentage.
- Trigger thresholds and exemption scope are nevertheless recalculated at release after every update, including small increases and decreases. Review reuse does not freeze the transaction amount or preserve an exemption that has gone out of scope.

The policy does not exhaustively define non-amount materiality or every possible percentage-baseline scenario. These are stated operational assumptions; the supplied cases do not depend on choosing between stepwise and review-baseline percentages for an invalidating increase.

Four different times must remain separate:

| Time | Meaning and application |
|---|---|
| Event time | When the business activity occurred; ordinarily also when its attribute update became effective. |
| Effective time | When a fact or authorization applies. Use a different effective time only when explicitly supported. An exemption's `effective_from` is a validity condition, not proof of earlier approval. |
| Record time | When information was inserted into a database. Both exemption rows were inserted at 11:00; this does not establish their approval times. |
| Evidence time | When an independent artifact attests, was signed, or was observed. Preserve those distinct timestamps when available. EX-77 has a qualified signature at 09:05. EX-88 has a certificate-creation time at 10:30, which is not an 08:50 signature timestamp. The time the assessor received either artifact is unspecified. |

At audit time, late-arriving evidence may establish a fact that already existed before release. It cannot make a genuinely later approval exist earlier. EX-77's verified signature and documented outage support earlier approval; EX-88's displayed date and interested witness do not conclusively establish it. TX-305's EUR 150,000 settlement amount is reliable on April 7, but is not evidence of an identical release amount on April 6 without a supported linkage.

I assume the supplied activity history is complete enough to identify missing required activities, except where the question expressly introduces evidential uncertainty. I do not assume missing attributes are low risk, or assume unlogged sanctions updates. Findings cover R1–R7, not an additional universal screening duty or a legal prohibition on transfers to XZ.

Formal pseudo-logic uses three truth values `T`, `F`, and `U` (unknown). `AND` is false if any operand is false; otherwise it is unknown if any operand is unknown. `OR` is true if any operand is true; otherwise it is unknown if any operand is unknown.

Definitions:

- `a ≺ b`: event a strictly precedes event b under `(UTC timestamp, event_index)`. `a ≼ b` permits equality. Shared-list comparisons need comparable source order.
- `S(c,e)[k]`: the last explicit update to attribute k in case c at an event `u ≼ e`; UNKNOWN if none exists. A blank is not an update. Explicitly substantiated different effective times replace the default event-effective ordering for that fact.
- `ER(e)`, `SR(e)`, `CA(e)`, `SS(e)`: completed enhanced review, completed standard review, compliance approval, and completed sanctions screen. The log's review/screen activity occurrences are treated as completions for this assessment.
- `M(c,r)`: latest known material change at or before release r, under the materiality interpretation above. An initial snapshot is a lower bound, not a review invalidation.
- `Relevant2(e,r)`: enhanced review e covers the risk/PEP context applicable at r. In this log, a review after the latest risk/type change establishes the chronological part of that coverage. `Covers(a,e)` means approval a covers e; an approval of only a standard packet does not satisfy it. Where explicit IDs are absent, chronological linkage is an operational assumption subject to the notes.

```
R4:
  InvalidAmount(q,r) = T if there is a known amount update u such that
      q ≺ u ≼ r,
      A(u) > A(u immediately before), and
      100*A(u) > 120*A(q).
  AmountFit(q,r) = F if InvalidAmount(q,r) = T;
                  U if the reviewed amount is unknown or an intervening
                    increase cannot be classified because its origin is unknown;
                  T otherwise.
  Invalidation persists for q; a new review q' has its own snapshot and baseline.
  This invalidation concerns amount-dependent use, not automatically R2.

R5:
  ValidExemption(x,rule,r) =
      explicitly_waives(x,rule)
      AND approved_by_Head_Compliance(x)
      AND supported_approval_existed_by(x,r)
      AND effective_from(x) <= time(r) < expiry(x)
      AND jurisdiction_scope_matches(x,S(c,r).jurisdiction)
      AND customer_scope_matches(x,S(c,r).customer_type)
      AND amount_scope_matches(x,S(c,r).amount).
  supported_approval_existed_by is an evidential predicate, not an inference
  from database insertion time or a displayed backdated approval field.
  Equal-time approval/release requires ordering evidence as well.

R1:
  Need1(r) = [S(c,r).jurisdiction != DE] AND [S(c,r).amount >= 100000].
  Fresh1(e,r) = ER(e) AND e ≺ r
               AND [M absent OR M(c,r) ≺ e]
               AND AmountFit(e,r).
  If Need1(r)=T, require EXISTS e: Fresh1(e,r)=T
                    OR EXISTS x: ValidExemption(x,R1,r)=T.
  Uncertain material changes after e leave Fresh1 uncertain unless other
  evidence resolves them. SR(e) never substitutes for ER(e).

R2:
  Need2(r) = [S(c,r).risk_level = HIGH] OR [S(c,r).customer_type = PEP].
  If Need2(r)=T, require EXISTS e,a:
      ER(e) AND CA(a) AND Relevant2(e,r) AND Covers(a,e)
      AND e ≺ a ≺ r.
  This is an existential qualifying chain, not a rule requiring every
  later enhanced review to receive a separate approval.

R3:
  For each authoritative addition u of S(c,r).jurisdiction with u ≺ r,
  if an applicable screen existed before u, require EXISTS s':
      SS(s') AND covers_current_jurisdiction(s',r) AND u ≺ s' ≺ r.
  Equivalently, an addition later than the latest applicable screen leaves
  an outstanding rescreening obligation at release.
  A missing applicable screening baseline is an evidential gap, not a
  license to invent a universal initial-screen requirement.

R6:
  Never replace UNKNOWN with a low-risk value.
  If a missing fact leaves activation, satisfaction, or exception validity
  decisive and unresolved, return INDETERMINATE unless another established
  violation already determines NONCOMPLIANT.

R7:
  For every release r, construct S(c,r) by ordered, forward-only replay.
  Use ≺, including event_index, for all prior/subsequent requirements.
  A future state or record supplies an earlier fact only with independent
  evidence establishing its earlier effective/existence time.
```

A proven active, unsatisfied, unwaived obligation determines NONCOMPLIANT. With no proven violation, a decisive unresolved question determines INDETERMINATE. If a valid waiver is needed to permit release, use JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; if all active obligations are independently fulfilled, use COMPLIANT. A redundant valid exemption does not force the exception classification. These are findings under the supplied policy, rather than claims about all possible regulatory obligations.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-305 — missing release amount.** An immutable release payload or a contemporaneous independently verified message establishing an amount below EUR 100,000 would exclude R1 and support COMPLIANT under these rules. If the release amount was at least EUR 100,000, evidence must also establish that the 11:15 review covered the applicable amount and remained valid. Proof of an invalidating pre-release increase without a replacement enhanced review would establish NONCOMPLIANT. A release/settlement linkage can establish the historical amount, but settlement value alone cannot establish the reviewed amount or the change history. The current evidence supports neither a fabricated EUR 150,000 release state nor a zero amount.

**TX-310 — disputed historical approval.** Independent contemporaneous evidence identifying Head_Compliance's approval, scope, and timing before 10:00 would support JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, because no enhanced review exists. Proof that approval first occurred after release would establish NONCOMPLIANT. The certificate created at 10:30 cannot establish the displayed 08:50 qualified approval, but it does not logically disprove a distinct earlier oral approval. R5 does not specify whether oral approvals are admissible or the exact evidential threshold for accepting them. I therefore leave historical compliance INDETERMINATE. Under a strict documented-proof reading of “evidence supports,” rejecting the conflicted oral claim means no usable waiver has been demonstrated and yields NONCOMPLIANT for failure to establish authorization. That is a defensible alternative; it should be identified as a proof-standard choice, rather than presented as certainty that oral approval never happened.

**TX-302 — timely approval recorded late.** The qualified signature at 09:05 supports approval before the 09:40 obligation trigger. The 11:00 insert is record time, not approval time. Treating all late inserts as post-release approvals would be wrong here. COMPLIANT versus JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION depends on whether a redundant exemption changes the reporting label. Even if the signature became unreliable, the enhanced review would still independently satisfy R1. A finding-changing discovery would need to defeat that route too—for example, verified evidence of a later material increase to EUR 260,000 before release, which would both invalidate the 180,000 review and exceed EX-77's amount scope. The supplied record establishes no such change.

**TX-311 — ordering and deactivation.** Index 3 precedes index 4, so the enhanced review saw US and the release saw DE. The review predates a material jurisdiction change, but R1 is evaluated at release and is false there; prior foreign/high-value status does not create an independent obligation persisting after deactivation under the supplied wording. Reversing those two indices would change the review snapshot, but would still leave this release domestic and compliant. If credible evidence established that DE became effective only after release, the release would be US; the existing 16:20 review would still satisfy R1 on the supplied history. In contrast, evidence that the post-review change was actually to another foreign jurisdiction would leave R1 active and the review before the material change, requiring a new review or valid waiver. These distinctions prevent assuming that every changed timestamp interpretation must change the verdict.

**TX-306 — small changes and approval reuse.** The amount increase is 10,000/220,000 = 4.55%; it does not invalidate the reviewed amount under R4. Nothing in R2 requires reapproval after every amount edit. Evidence of an actual pre-release increase above EUR 264,000 without a new review would defeat R1 review validity. A separate policy requiring approval of every final amount would change the assessment, but is not one of the supplied rules. A packet showing that the 12:25 approval covered only a standard review would also defeat the qualifying R2 chain.

**TX-308 — decreases and persistent invalidation.** The direct decrease does not invalidate the review, and EUR 100,000 activates R1 exactly at the threshold. If additional reliable history showed a 140,000 → 175,000 increase after review, followed by the decrease to 100,000, the intervening +25% would have invalidated the review; the final decrease would not itself restore it. A replacement review could restore compliance. No intervening increase is supplied.

For future logs, specify whether “more than 20%” means a single update relative to its predecessor, cumulative increase from the review snapshot, or a formally defined exposure measure after decreases. The chosen review-baseline interpretation prevents several individually small increases from silently escaping review. TX-301 and both invalidating increases in TX-309 exceed 20% under both the immediate-step and relevant-review baselines, so this ambiguity does not change their findings.

def key(e):
    return (e[T], int(e["event_index"]))

def missing(x):
    return bool(pd.isna(x))

def eq(x, y):
    return None if missing(x) or missing(y) else bool(x == y)

def and3(*xs):
    return False if any(x is False for x in xs) else (None if any(x is None for x in xs) else True)

def or3(*xs):
    return True if any(x is True for x in xs) else (None if any(x is None for x in xs) else False)

def ge(x, limit):
    return None if missing(x) else bool(x >= limit)

def exemption_valid(r):
    if missing(r["exemption_id"]):
        return False
    if missing(r["ex_exemption_id"]):
        return None  # referenced approval is missing from supplied evidence
    scope = and3(eq(r["ex_approved_by"], "Head_Compliance"),
                 bool(r["ex_waives_r1"]),
                 eq(r["jurisdiction"], r["ex_scope_jurisdiction"]),
                 eq(r["customer_type"], r["ex_scope_customer"]),
                 None if missing(r["amount"]) else bool(r["amount"] <= r["ex_max_amount"]),
                 bool(r["ex_effective_from"] <= r[T] < r["ex_expires_at"]))
    timely = None
    if r["ex_evidence_kind"] == "qualified_signature_hash_valid":
        timely = bool(r["ex_signature_at"] <= r[T])
    # A late certificate and a conflicted oral claim do not prove timely approval.
    # They also do not conclusively disprove a separate earlier oral approval.
    return and3(scope, timely)

def obligation(trigger, fulfilled, waived=False):
    if trigger is False:
        return "NOT_APPLICABLE"
    if trigger is None:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    if fulfilled is True:
        return "PASS"
    if waived is True:
        return "WAIVED"
    if fulfilled is None or waived is None:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    return "FAIL"

    previous = dict.fromkeys(ATTRS)

    def amount_assessment(review, release_key):
        if review is None:
            return None, False, False
        baseline = review["amount"]
        uncertain = missing(baseline)
        invalidated = uncertain_increase = False
        for ch in amount_changes:
            if not (key(review) < ch["key"] <= release_key):
                continue
            if missing(ch["old"]):
                uncertain = uncertain_increase = True
            elif ch["new"] > ch["old"]:
                if missing(baseline):
                    uncertain = uncertain_increase = True
                elif ch["new"] * 100 > baseline * 120:
                    invalidated = True
        fitness = False if invalidated else (None if uncertain else True)
        r4_flag = True if invalidated else (None if uncertain_increase else False)
        return fitness, r4_flag, uncertain_increase

        k = key(e)
        if e[A] != "Sanctions_List_Update":
            if e[A] == "Create_Transaction":
                last_material = dict(e, change_kind="initial_snapshot")
                last_context_change = e
            else:

                    if not missing(e["u_" + attr]) and eq(previous[attr], e[attr]) is not True:
                        last_material = dict(e, change_kind=attr)
                        if attr in ["risk_level", "customer_type"]:

                if not missing(e["u_amount"]) and eq(previous["amount"], e["amount"]) is not True:
                    old, new = previous["amount"], e["amount"]
                    amount_changes.append(dict(key=k, old=old, new=new))
                    candidates = [latest[x] for x in ["Standard_Review", "Enhanced_Review"]

        if e[A] == "Enhanced_Review":

        if e[A] == "Compliance_Approve":

        if e[A] != "Release_Funds":

This is a checking scaffold, not a general evidence-verification engine. Production use needs structured approval-to-review IDs, explicit review completion/lifecycle semantics, signed scopes, authoritative effective-time records, and an agreed definition of materiality. In particular, the standard-packet note check is suitable for this supplied evidence, whereas free text should be replaced with structured packet metadata. Cross-case list updates at an identical timestamp and identical index are flagged for ordering evidence instead of arbitrarily choosing a case-name order.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** Assigning final case attributes to every event would give TX-305 an unsupported release amount of 150,000 and make TX-311's US review appear domestic. A trace of activity names cannot reconstruct which values governed an earlier event.

2. **Post-hoc exemptions and evidence quality.** TX-302 and TX-310 both reference an exemption before a late `Record_Exemption`. An activity-order checker cannot distinguish a verified earlier signature from a displayed backdated field and a conflicted witness. Conversely, forbidding every record after release falsely rejects genuine earlier authorization.

3. **Material changes after review.** `Enhanced_Review` before `Release_Funds` is true in TX-309, yet the review was invalidated by the subsequent increase. The same sequence with a small increase in TX-306 or a decrease in TX-308 preserves validity. Data values and reviewed baselines determine the result.

4. **Conditional activation and deactivation.** A universal enhanced-review precedence constraint overstates R1 for domestic or low-value releases. A universal approval constraint overstates R2 for non-HIGH, non-PEP cases. Conversely, LOW risk does not deactivate PEP-triggered R2 in TX-307, and the move to DE deactivates R1 in TX-311. The obligations are predicates over release state, not unconditional patterns.

5. **Unknown values need three-valued reasoning.** A trace checker generally returns pass/fail or a fitness score. It cannot establish whether TX-305 crossed the threshold, and treating blank amount as zero invents compliance. Known HIGH can still activate R2 despite an unknown customer type; uncertainty is not simply equivalent to false.

6. **Equal timestamps require source order.** Timestamp-only sorting can reverse TX-311's review and jurisdiction update or treat them as unordered. That changes the review snapshot and whether it follows a change. PM4Py's internal row index should not replace the supplied `event_index`.

7. **Approval and review coverage require linkage.** Distinguishing Standard_Review from Enhanced_Review helps, but an approval activity alone cannot establish which review, packet, or data version it approved. TX-307's standard-packet approval does not create the required enhanced-review chain.

8. **Sanctions changes are shared, versioned facts.** A release trace may contain no list-update activity even though a shared list changed after its screen. Screening freshness requires the current jurisdiction, the authoritative update's effective time, and the list version screened. Per-case control flow can miss this cross-case dependency.

Control-flow discovery or DECLARE checking remains useful for coarse sequencing and detecting absent activities. A data-aware monitor must supplement it with event-time state, conditional activation, review invalidation, scoped exceptions, and evidence status. A single trace-fitness score is not a compliance verdict.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- **Persist an immutable release payload.** At every attempted and completed release, store amount and currency, risk, jurisdiction, customer type, exemption references, transaction/state version, payload hash, source message ID, and the actual release timestamp. Preserve the snapshot rather than reconstructing it from mutable final attributes. Link settlement to that payload explicitly without assuming equal amounts.

- **Separate effective, record, approval, signature, and observation times.** Store `effective_at`, `recorded_at`, actual approval time, trusted signature time, certificate issuance/creation time, and evidence receipt time. Keep original UTC timestamps and provenance for corrections. A backdated field must not silently rewrite history; a correction should preserve the original record and its supporting evidence.

- **Record review completion and the reviewed state.** Distinguish review start from completion, assign immutable review IDs, and retain the reviewed amount, currency, risk/type/jurisdiction, scope, and state version. State which review baseline is used for the 20% test and which later changes invalidated it. Define cumulative increases and decreases explicitly in the rule specification.

- **Bind approval to its evidence.** A Compliance_Approve should name the Enhanced_Review ID, packet type/version/hash, approved data version, approver identity and role at that time, and completion time. This makes an approval of a standard packet distinguishable without interpreting prose notes.

- **Make exemption scope explicit and verifiable.** Record the exact rules waived, jurisdiction and customer-type scopes, amount bounds and currency, validity interval, revocation history, Head_Compliance authority, approval ID, signed content/hash, and independent verification result. If oral approval is allowed, define admissibility and require contemporaneous independent evidence. Preserve outage and delayed-insertion provenance separately from approval evidence.

- **Version the shared sanctions list.** Log authoritative additions/removals, their effective and publication times, jurisdiction identifiers, and globally comparable sequence/version numbers. Each screen should record the list version and covered subject/jurisdiction. Propagate relevant updates to affected releases and invalidate screening freshness until a new screen completes.

- **Capture change provenance and unknowns.** Store old and new values, actor, source system, reason, effective time, and correction linkage. Use explicit statuses for unknown, unavailable, unchanged, and cleared values; keep blank-as-no-update behavior unambiguous. Include the evidence supporting jurisdiction or PEP reclassification.

- **Preserve stable ordering.** Retain immutable event IDs, case sequence, source-system order, and causal links alongside UTC timestamps. Provide a shared ordering mechanism for list updates and release authorization. Detect duplicate or missing sequence information rather than letting incidental dataframe order decide the result.

- **Version and persist the decision itself.** Record the rule version, configured thresholds/materiality definition, state/list versions used, each trigger's truth value, the required and satisfied evidence IDs, exception decision, unknown-value reasons, and decision time. Evaluate and authorize release against an atomic snapshot so a data/list change between checking and release cannot evade the controls. Route decisive unknowns or conflicting exemption evidence for resolution before authorizing release, while allowing independently verified timely approval to survive a database-insertion outage.
