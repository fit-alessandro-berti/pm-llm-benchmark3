# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope (gate-to-gate, order fulfillment).** The unit of analysis is a spare-part **order** (flattened case), from `Receive order` to `Close` or `Cancel due to delay`. Impact is attributed with the enriched event attributes already in the log.

**Inside the boundary**
- Process energy: `energy_kwh` on `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, `inspection_cell`, and emergency production.
- Transport: `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode` (`parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`), including the extra leg from partial shipment / backorder.
- Materials: `material_scrap_kg` (especially rework loops), `package_material_kg` (standard vs reinforced, plus packaging rework), and **obsolescence** of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days.
- Hidden loops: `rework_flag` production (`Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC`) and `Rework packaging`.
- Empty or poorly utilized dedicated-van capacity, insofar as it shows up in high `transport_kgco2e` on the Critical path.

**Outside the boundary**
- Upstream extraction and supplier manufacturing of **already-stocked** Standard-stock items (except when this process triggers MTO or emergency production).
- Customer use of the installed part, and end-of-life of the spare after installation.
- Corporate buildings, IT, and corporate travel.
- Carrier-network emissions beyond the order’s assigned `route_mode` (e.g., other customers filling a truck).

**Assumptions**
- Promised lead time (`promised_lead_time_hours`) is the service contract that must not be unacceptably breached; “unacceptable” means a clear rise in late delivery for Service-contract or Critical-downtime, or loss of an alternative route (policy R1).
- Critical-downtime is **not** homogeneous: the 3% stock/ground exception already shows a lower-impact feasible path for some of this segment.
- Energy per item falls with batch size, but batch wait, late risk, inspection rush, and obsolescence are first-class costs, not externalities.
- The discovered model has low precision on disruption combinations; estimates are directional and path-based, not precise LCAs.

**Remaining uncertainty**
- Empty return legs of dedicated vans are only indirectly observed.
- Forecast-error obsolescence (6% of large-batch items) is not fully case-linked in the flattened order log.
- Damage returns after `Close` may be under-counted relative to in-process packaging rework.
- Heat-treatment `energy_kwh` allocation to waiting cases vs. the items actually fired in the batch.
- Scope 3 of purchased stock and of cancelled orders fulfilled by another supplier (displacement).

# 3. Intervention options

## S1 — Green shipping recommender (lead time, reliability, segment)

- **Target path / segment:** Ship choice after `Package standard` / `Package reinforced`, especially Standard-stock Service-contract (air-heavy, 7.8 kgCO2e vs 4.2 Regular) and MTO air (6% path, 23 kgCO2e).
- **Environmental mechanism:** Enforces SP1: `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` when P(meet promise) > 85%. Air is 4–6× kgCO2e and is only ~5% of stock variants but dominates their transport footprint (18.5 vs 3.6).
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Service holds **if** the 85% model is calibrated; quality unchanged. Resilience requires keeping air and an alternative carrier for Service-contract (R1)—the recommender ranks, it does not delete modes.
- **PM evidence:** `route_mode` × remaining slack × `promised_lead_time_hours`; SP1 violation rate; late delivery by segment; `transport_kgco2e`; `expedite_flag`.

## S4 — Priority pick + ground instead of default air for Standard-stock Service-contract

- **Target:** Stock → standard pack → air (5%) and the 16% Standard-stock Service-contract segment (median 18 h, p90 41 h, late 5%). Evidence: these orders are often flown even when promise still allows ground plus priority picking. SP1 violated in 22% of this segment.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Removes 4–6× air emissions where the 24 h air advantage is not required. Ground stock path is already 3.6 kgCO2e at 8% late.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Late must stay near 5%. Use air when p90/slack is tight or during carrier disruption. Quality unchanged. Do **not** apply this to true Critical-downtime.
- **PM evidence:** Service-contract stock cases with `route_mode=parcel_air` and slack > ground p90; post-change late %, `expedite_flag`, pick waiting time.

## S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck for non-urgent stock

- **Target:** `Package standard -> Ship ground` (5100) and Standard-stock Regular (39%, median 31 h, late 7%). Not default for Service-contract or Critical.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Consolidation cuts transport kgCO2e ~35–55% vs individual ground parcels. Twice-daily departures cap the 12–24 h consolidation wait.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Regular stock can absorb ~12 h more wait for many orders (p90 62 h). Service-contract and Critical must stay on parcel/van. Resilience: keep `parcel_ground` as R1 backup so one missed truck does not strand Service-contract volume.
- **PM evidence:** `consolidated_truck` share; dock-wait hours; Regular-stock late %; `transport_kgco2e`; spillover expedites.

## S9 + S8 — Split Critical into true downtime vs precautionary; replace *some* vans with shared emergency lanes

- **Target:** Critical-downtime Service-contract (11%; transport 38 kgCO2e). Dominant path: emergency production → reinforced pack → dedicated van (8%, 14 h, 3% late, **52 kgCO2e**). Contrast: Critical stock/ground exception (3%, 23 h, 9% late, **4.8 kgCO2e**). SP2 justification missing on 31% of vans.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Dedicated vans often return empty. Precautionary “urgent” cases move to priority stock + ground or a shared courier lane. True plant-down cases keep van/emergency. Shared lanes cut empty-return kgCO2e without banning vans.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** True downtime must stay near 3% late / 15 h median. Precautionary can tolerate the 9% late of the stock/ground exception. Resilience **improves** if unjustified vans stop consuming scarce emergency capacity; R1 still requires an alternate mode. Do **not** force all Critical onto ground.
- **PM evidence:** New subtype × `route_mode` × SP2 justification event; Critical late % split by subtype; van empty-return proxy; emergency-production count (760 baseline).

## S2 — Dynamic CNC / heat-treat batching with segment wait caps

- **Target:** `Plan production -> Batch wait` (3020) and MTO paths. Efficient path: batch → CNC_A → heat → inspection → reinforced → ground (13%, 16 kWh, 5.8 kgCO2e) **if batch ≥ 12**. Immediate CNC → air (6%) is 24 kWh and 23 kgCO2e.
- **Environmental mechanism:** CNC_A energy/item 2.8 → 1.3 kWh from tiny to 12–20 batches; heat-treat energy/item lowest above batch 10. Caps stop waiting from 4 h to 38 h and the 1.7× late risk on heat-treat waits. Cap batch size to limit 6% obsolescence.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Caps by segment: MTO Regular can wait more (already 94 h median, 18% late); MTO Service-contract tighter; Critical/emergency never wait for a green batch. Risk: large batches + rushed `inspection_cell` miss defects. Do not starve emergency CNC slots.
- **PM evidence:** `batch_id`/`batch_size`, Batch-wait duration, `energy_kwh`/item, late %, 14-day unshipped rate, inspection queue time.

## S3 — Prefer CNC_A over CNC_B when queue and due-date slack allow

- **Target:** MTO → batch wait → **CNC_B** → inspection → rework → ground (5%, 160 h, **32% late**, **32 kWh**, **1.8 kg scrap**). CNC_B is faster but 18% rework vs 9% on CNC_A.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Rework dominates impact on this path (energy and scrap far above the CNC_A+heat+ground path at 16 kWh / 0.70 kg). Avoiding the 390 CNC re-entries removes a full extra production cycle.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Quality improves. Cycle time may rise if CNC_A is batched; only shift when slack covers CNC_A wait. Resilience: keep CNC_B for tight due dates and as surge capacity so A-queues do not force emergency production.
- **PM evidence:** `machine_group` mix on MTO; `rework_flag`; `material_scrap_kg`; `energy_kwh`; late %; CNC_A queue time.

## S7 — Inspection quality gate on high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target:** `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) and inspection-driven packaging/customer returns. Evidence: inspection queues cause rushed inspections that miss defects.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Prevents the lose-lose rework path (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late) and later packaging rework. Slightly more inspection energy is small versus a full CNC remake.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Adds inspection time but removes 160 h-class rework tails. Quality and (usually) lateness both improve. Resilience: gate must not block Critical emergency flow—fast-track those with recorded risk acceptance.
- **PM evidence:** Rework rate by `machine_group` × part; inspection sojourn; scrap; post-delivery return proxy; Q1 outcomes.

## S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check before ship (and enforce Q1)

- **Target:** Rework packaging (260 from standard + 360 from reinforced); Q1 (MTO >8 kg must be reinforced unless inspection certifies low damage risk). Reinforced pack cuts damage-related returns ~40% for heavy MTO but doubles package material.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Stops pack/route/geometry mismatches that create extra `package_material_kg`, delay, and possible reship. Enforcing Q1 avoids return loops that dwarf the extra pack material. Do not put reinforced pack on light Standard-stock.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Removes a delay loop; damage returns fall. Resilience unchanged if check is a short gate, not a new queue.
- **PM evidence:** Packaging-rework rate; Q1 conformance; `package_material_kg`; `route_mode`; return/rework correlation.

## S11 — Tighten availability-check rules to cut unjustified partial shipments

- **Target:** `Pick -> Partial shipment` (1010); path stock → partial → ground + backorder (7%, 79 h, **24% late**, 1.4 kWh, 7.2 kgCO2e, 0.08 scrap). 220 backorder waits; 60 cancels.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Second shipment roughly doubles transport versus clean stock-ground (7.2 vs 3.6) and adds contact/repack. Hold for complete availability on Regular stock unless the customer values the first piece.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Regular late should fall from the 24% partial path. **Keep partials for Critical-downtime and many Service-contract lines** (first piece can stop a line). Blindly banning partials would raise cancels and displace demand to emergency/air.
- **PM evidence:** Partial rate by segment; second-leg `transport_kgco2e`; late %; `Cancel due to delay`; backorder wait.

## S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO only

- **Target:** Made-to-order Regular (22%, 94 h median, 18% late, 18.5 kWh, 8.0 kgCO2e).
- **Environmental mechanism:** A slightly longer, honest promise enables batch ≥12 on CNC_A/heat and ground instead of immediate CNC + air (24 kWh, 23 kgCO2e). Also reduces cosmetic “late” when 180 h p90 was never compatible with a tight promise.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Only Regular, and only with customer acceptance. Service-contract and Critical promises stay. Resilience: does not remove expedite capacity.
- **PM evidence:** Change in `promised_lead_time_hours`; acceptance rate; shift from immediate/air variant to batched/ground; late % against the **new** promise; obsolescence.

## S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1 / SP2 / Q1 overrides

- **Target:** All air, dedicated-van, and non-reinforced heavy-MTO shipments.
- **Environmental mechanism:** Makes the 22% SP1 and 31% SP2 gaps visible and reviewable; unjustified overrides decline.
- **Service / quality / resilience:** Enables justified exceptions (true Critical van, disruption air) so policy does not become a brittle ban. Required for R1.
- **PM evidence:** Presence of justification events; override reason codes; subsequent late/rework/emissions of excepted cases.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound (energy vs wait, late, obsolescence).** Pushing CNC_A/heat-treat to batch 12–20 cuts kWh/item but can move Batch wait from 4 h to 38 h, multiply heat-treat late risk by 1.7, and leave 6% of items unshipped in 14 days—scrap and warehousing that erase the energy win.

2. **Transport modal-shift rebound.** Ground/consolidation that then misses the promise trains Service-contract customers to demand air/`expedite_flag` on the *next* order, raising air share above today’s 5% stock-air variant.

3. **Packaging displacement.** Universal reinforced pack cuts MTO damage ~40% but doubles `package_material_kg`. Applied to Standard-stock (already 0.04–0.06 scrap, 3–4% rework) it adds material with little return benefit. Conversely, chasing pack-kg by skipping Q1 on heavy MTO reopens return loops.

4. **Rework / machine displacement.** Shifting MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A without a queue cap congests A, forcing tight jobs onto B or Emergency production (760)—the high-energy path the shift was meant to avoid.

5. **Obsolescence from “efficient” over-production.** Dynamic batching that ignores demand confidence recreates the 6% 14-day unshipped waste. Flattened order logs hide this until stock ages out.

6. **Customer-behavior rebound (S10 / greener promises).** Regular MTO customers offered longer carbon-aware dates may reclassify orders as Service-contract or Critical, or place earlier/larger orders. That inflates emergency and van demand (38 kgCO2e segment) and inventory.

7. **Priority-pick displacement.** Giving Standard-stock Service-contract priority pick so they can stay on ground delays Regular stock, lifting Regular late above 7% and triggering compensatory air—the emissions move from SC to Regular.

8. **Shared-lane / van contention.** Replacing vans with shared emergency couriers helps empty returns in normal weeks but can queue several simultaneous downtime events, then force extra emergency CNC + air. R1 fails if the shared lane is the only remaining alternative.

9. **Partial-shipment displacement.** Holding Regular orders for full availability cuts second-leg kgCO2e, but if the rule hits Critical or scarce multi-line orders, cancels (already 530) and off-log spot buys rise—impact leaves the log, not the planet.

10. **Inspection-gate rebound.** Stricter gates lengthen `inspection_cell` queues, which the log already links to rushed inspections and missed defects. Without capacity, the gate recreates the rework loop it was meant to close.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Implement a **segmented operating system**, not a single “greenest path.” Lowest emission options (all-ground, max batch, no vans, no partials) violate Critical service (4% late / 15 h), SP2/R1, and create obsolescence and rework. The package below attacks high-impact, low-justification variants while preserving true emergency capability.

**Package (deploy together)**

1. **Shipping control plane (S1 + S4 + S5 + S12)**  
   - Regular Standard-stock: default twice-daily `consolidated_truck` or `parcel_ground`.  
   - Service-contract Standard-stock: **priority pick + ground** unless slack fails the SP1 85% test; air is an exception with a logged reason (today 22% of this segment violates SP1).  
   - True Critical-downtime: dedicated van or emergency courier remains legitimate.  
   - Air and van stay in the mode set for R1.

2. **Critical triage (S9 + selective S8)**  
   Split Critical into **true downtime** vs **precautionary urgent**. Only true downtime auto-starts Emergency production + dedicated van (8% path, 52 kgCO2e). Precautionary uses the already-observed stock/ground exception (3% path, 4.8 kgCO2e, 9% late). Require SP2 justification on every van (fix 31% gap). Move *some* precautionary vans to shared emergency lanes; do not abolish vans.

3. **Production quality-first, then batch (S3 + S7 + S2, S10 on Regular MTO)**  
   - Prefer CNC_A when slack exists; treat the CNC_B → rework path (5%, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg, 32% late) as a defect, not a valid “fast” option.  
   - Inspection gate on high-rework machine/part pairs; fast-track true Critical.  
   - Dynamic batches with **hard wait caps**: looser for Regular MTO (optionally after S10 promise negotiation so the 13% batched-ground path at 16 kWh / 5.8 kgCO2e is attainable), tight for Service-contract MTO, **zero extra wait** for emergency. Cap batch size / require demand confidence to hold 14-day unshipped near or below 6%.

4. **Close waste loops (S6 + S11 + Q1)**  
   - Compatibility check so pack type matches `route_mode` and geometry (620 packaging reworks). Reinforced pack **required** for MTO >8 kg unless inspection certifies; **not** for light stock.  
   - Availability check: suppress convenience partials on Regular stock (7% path, 24% late, extra 3–4 kgCO2e). Keep partials for Critical and for Service-contract when the first line item has downtime value.

**Why this is balanced**
- **Environment:** Removes the largest *unjustified* burdens—SC-stock air, unjustified vans, CNC_B remakes, double shipments, pack/route rework—using the path table’s own contrasts (18.5 vs 3.6 transport; 52 vs 4.8; 32 vs 16 kWh; 1.8 vs 0.7 scrap).  
- **Service:** Does not put true Critical on slow consolidation; does not use 38 h batch waits on contracted lead times; priority pick protects SC-stock when air is removed.  
- **Quality:** CNC_A + inspection gate + Q1 attack the only paths where rework *dominates* impact.  
- **Resilience:** Emergency production, air, and vans remain, but are reserved; R1 alternatives stay explicit in the exception log. Capacity is freed *for* real disruptions by clearing 31% unjustified vans and some emergency jobs that were precautionary.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Design.** Phase by warehouse or region. Keep a control group. Stratify every metric by the five case-mix segments plus the new Critical subtypes. Do not judge success on a blended 12,000-order average (Critical transport would hide stock gains, and vice versa).

**Event-log validation (same attributes as today)**
- Path shares: stock–standard–ground (42% baseline), stock–air (5%), partial+backorder (7%), MTO batch–CNC_A–heat–ground (13%), MTO immediate–air (6%), MTO CNC_B–rework (5%), Critical–van (8%), Critical–stock/ground (3%).
- Means already in the log: `energy_kwh`, `transport_kgco2e`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, `rework_flag`, `expedite_flag`, cycle time, late vs `promised_lead_time_hours`.
- Batching: `batch_size` vs wait vs kWh/item vs 14-day unshipped.
- DFG frequencies: Emergency production (760), Rework production (470), Rework packaging (620), Cancel (530), dedicated van (620), ship air (1420).

**Guardrails (stop or roll back a lever if breached)**
- Critical true-downtime late delivery stays ~4% (median ~15 h); precautionary may move toward the 9% exception path, not worse.  
- Standard-stock Service-contract late stays ~5%; Regular stock not materially above ~7–9%.  
- MTO Regular late should not rise above today’s 18% after S10 promise changes (measure against the **agreed** promise).  
- CNC_B rework path share and scrap should fall; CNC_A queue must not push emergency count up.  
- 14-day batch obsolescence must not rise above the current 6% warning level.

**Conformance monitoring**
- **SP1:** % of orders with P(on-time | ground/consolidation) > 85% that still used air. Target: well below 22% on Standard-stock Service-contract. Pair with late % so the 85% model is recalibrated, not gamed.
- **SP2:** every `dedicated_van` has a Critical-downtime (true) justification event. Target: missing justification << 31%, ideally exceptional only.
- **Q1:** MTO >8 kg without reinforced pack and without inspection waiver. Track waiver quality against later returns.
- **R1:** on carrier-disruption days (tag in the log), Service-contract cases still show at least one alternative `route_mode` offered/used.

**Segment-level service cockpit**
Weekly: median/p90 cycle time, late %, cancel %, partial %, expedite % for each segment. Overlay energy, transport, scrap. Review the low-precision disruption combinations (emergency × partial × air) as their own stratum so they are not averaged away.

**Exception governance (S12)**
- Override reasons: slack mis-estimate, carrier disruption, true downtime, customer-accepted Regular MTO date, Q1 inspection waiver.
- Weekly review of air, van, and Q1 exceptions; monthly audit of whether excepted cases were actually late or damaged.
- Feedback into the shipping recommender and batch wait caps.
- Explicit ban on “silent” policy breaks: if the green option is infeasible, log it; do not silently fly SC-stock or dispatch an empty van.

**Model hygiene.** Raise discovered-model precision by logging Critical subtype, SP2 justification, pack–route check result, and batch demand-confidence. Re-mine the DFG after 1–2 months; if emergency and partial variants still explode combinatorially, report them as a disruption subprocess rather than forcing them into the happy path.
