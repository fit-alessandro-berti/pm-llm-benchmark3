# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin, scale, horizon.**
Observation: each row gives `duration_or_age_h` measured in hours. We define $t=0$ at the `Submit` event (first element of every `prefix`). No intermediate event timestamps are supplied, so we cannot measure sojourn times, only order and total elapsed time to the last observed point. Horizon $\tau=72$h fixed. $T$ = latent time from `Submit` to first terminal internal disposition. $J\in\{1\!:\!{\rm Resolve},2\!:\!{\rm Withdraw},3\!:\!{\rm Reject},4\!:\!{\rm Transfer}\}$. $C$ = administrative censoring time at data freeze. What we observe is $Y=\min(T,C)$, $\Delta=\mathbf{1}(T\le C)\cdot J$, plus for censored rows $C=$ `duration_or_age_h` and `last_event_gap_h` = $C-$time of last recorded activity.

**Assumption A1 (needed):** `duration_or_age_h` is exactly $T$ for terminal rows and exactly $C$ for `open` rows, measured on the same clock for all cases. No left truncation; submission times are known to precede the freeze. Required because we have no calendar dates to verify.

**Distinguishing concepts before estimator choice:**

* *Right censoring* ($C<T$, open row): the case could still resolve after freeze; future trajectory is unobserved but not precluded. Example: $C=92$h.
* *Competing event* ($T\le C, J\neq 1$): the case takes a terminal path that prevents internal `Resolve` forever. Treating it as censoring implies it could still resolve, which is false.
* *Already-observed breach*: $T>\tau$ is known at freeze even if $T$ or $C>\tau$. Both a late resolution at 76h and a still-open case at 92h prove $T_{\rm Resolve}>\tau$ (or $T_{\rm Resolve}$ non-existent by $\tau$). A completed-case-only analysis would drop the second but keep the first.

We need at least two estimands because the phrase "probability of breaching the SLA" conflates workload, compliance, and late-success questions.

**Estimand E1 – SLA non-resolution risk (governance):** 
$$ \psi_1(\tau)=P(T_{\rm Resolve}>\tau\ {\rm or\ }J\neq1\ {\rm with\ }T_J\le\tau)=1-{\rm CIF}_1(\tau)$$
where ${\rm CIF}_1(\tau)=P(T\le\tau, J=1)$ is the cumulative incidence of resolution by $\tau$. Includes in the numerator both still-pending cases and cases that withdrew/rejected/transferred by $\tau$. Denominator = all submitted cases.

*Operational match:* contractual SLA reporting, breach rate, penalty exposure. Decision: how many cases failed the 72h standard, irrespective of cause.

**Estimand E2 – Cumulative incidence of late resolution (capacity tail):**
$${\rm CIF}_1(t)-{\rm CIF}_1(\tau),\ t>\tau \quad {\rm or\ }\ {\rm CIF}_1(\infty)-{\rm CIF}_1(\tau)$$
and E2b = $P(T_{\rm Resolve}\le\tau\mid T_{\rm Resolve}<\infty)$ conditional on eventual resolution.

*Operational match:* staffing for re-work after breach, customer communication for late resolves. Different from E1: E1 counts a case withdrawn at 27h as breach; E2 does not count it as ever resolving.

**Estimand E3 – Probability still pending (no terminal disposition) at $\tau$ (workload):**
$$ \psi_3(\tau)=P(T>\tau, C>\tau)=S_{\rm all}(\tau)=\exp(-\Lambda_{\rm all}(\tau))$$
where $\Lambda_{\rm all}$ sums all cause-specific hazards. This is Kaplan-Meier survival for composite endpoint `any terminal`.

*Operational match:* queue backlog, alerts, triage. A withdrawn case at 27h is no longer pending at 72h; an open case at 92h is.

**Estimand E4 – Cause-specific hazard crossing $\tau$** $\lambda_1(t)$ for $t$ near $\tau$, for etiologic questions about what drives resolution speed. Not a probability at $\tau$ but needed to interpret E1-E3.

We adopt **E1 = primary for SLA compliance** and **E3 = primary for operational workload**, with E2 secondary. They coincide only if no competing risks and no censoring.

**How four exemplar cases contribute (Observation vs Estimate vs Bound):**

*Observation:* S03: A/P2/76h/`resolved`/prefix `...>Resolve`, yes-escalated. S09: A/P3/92h/`open`/`...Review`, gap 31h. S06: A/P2/27h/`withdrawn`. S08: A/P1/68h/`transferred`/escalated.

* S03 (resolved at 76h, breach):
  - E1: Uncensored breach. At $\tau=72$, contributes as at-risk and not yet resolved; increments ${\rm CIF}_1$ only at 76h ($>\tau$). Observation: known breach of E1; $1(Y>\tau)$ is 0 for resolution event.
  - E2: Observed late-resolution event at 76h; contributes mass to tail after $\tau$.
  - E3: Event for composite at 76h, so $S_{\rm all}(\tau)$ counts S03 as still pending at $\tau$, then fails at 76h. Estimate: not censored.
  - Bound: no ambiguity.

* S09 (open at 92h, breach known):
  - E1: Right-censored at 92h $>\tau$. Breach status at $\tau$ is known: no resolve by 72h. Under independent censoring, contributes person-time to risk set up to 92h and proves $\psi_1$ includes this case. Dropping it (naive) would underestimate breach.
  - E2: Censored; never contributes as late resolve, but shows late resolution not yet observed; informs IPCW weight.
  - E3: Censored after $\tau$, so S09 is counted as still pending at $\tau$; Estimate: $S_{\rm all}(72)$ uses its survival to 92h.
  - Hypothesis: gap 31h suggests possible informative stalling; see §2.

* S08 (transferred at 68h, just before $\tau$):

Uncertainty: with n=32, all estimates are noisy; we report intervals and bounds, not point claims.

**Transferred – three plausible assumptions (must vary):**

* **T1 – Non-informative independent censoring (internal-SLA view).** Code: `censored=1` at $Y$, no $J$. Assumption: transfer reason independent of residual resolve potential conditional on `priority`/`escalated`/`prefix`. Estimand becomes $P({\rm not\ internally\ resolved\ by\ }\tau {\rm\ in\ absence\ of\ transfer})$. Risk: optimistic if transfers are high-risk P1 escalated cases (all 3 transfers are P1 escalated: S08, S21, S32). Informative censoring bias .

* **T2 – Competing risk / conservative SLA failure (governance view).** Code: `event=0` for ${\rm CIF}_1$, $J=4$ competing at $Y$. Assumption: internal SLA fails when we hand off; transfer counts as non-resolution by $\tau$ if $Y\le\tau$ (S08 68h, S21 26h) and as censored competing after $\tau$ (S32 96h). This raises $\psi_1(\tau)$ and lowers ${\rm CIF}_1(\infty)$. Decision: matches contractual view that transfer  resolve.

* **T3 – Informative censoring with bounds / IPCW (uncertainty view).** Observation: transfers are selective (100% P1 escalated vs 19% escalated among non-transferred open). Estimate is not point-identified. We report Manski bounds:
  - Best for internal resolution: all transfers would have resolved immediately after $Y$  ${\rm CIF}_1^{\rm up} = {\rm CIF}_1^{\rm T1} + P({\rm Transfer\ by\ }t)$
  - Worst: none ever resolve  ${\rm CIF}_1^{\rm low}= {\rm CIF}_1^{\rm T1}$ (or ${\rm CIF}_1^{\rm T2}$ if counting transfer as failure)
  plus IPCW using logistic model $P(C>t\mid priority, escalated, gap)$ to reweight uncensored risk set. Sensitivity shows whether cohort/priority effects reverse.

For `open` cases, long `last_event_gap_h` triggers similar informative-censor concern. Administrative freeze is plausibly non-informative, but Example: S11 gap 55h with 81h age, S26 gap 72h with 104h age, S25 gap 48h – these stalled cases may be de facto abandoned. We test S_inf sensitivity where gap>30h defines high-stall stratum.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Hypothesis a naive analyst does: keep only rows where `observed_status=="resolved"`, compute mean duration 54.7h in A, 44.3h in B, or breach proportion 2/6=33% in A vs 2/7=29% in B, and claim B is faster. All five biases below use concrete IDs.

**1. Survivor bias (conditioning on success).** Observation: resolved set = 13 cases (S01,02,03,04,07,13,16,17,18,19,20,27,28). It excludes 19 cases that withdrew/rejected/transferred/open. Estimate conditional on eventual resolve ignores that $P({\rm Resolve})$ itself differs. Example: S05 (9h reject) and S12 (15h withdraw) are early competing events; dropping them inflates $S_{\rm all}$ and deflates cause-specific hazard. S30 (70h withdrawn) proves a case can stay pending 70h then withdraw – treating that as non-existent removes 70h of person-time at risk for resolve.

**2. Ignoring right censoring – two flavors:**

* *Open below $\tau$* still contributes exposure. S10: P2 escalated, open at 47h, gap 19h, `Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate`. Naive drop discards 47h of at-risk time where resolve could have occurred. Under KM, S10 is in risk set $Y$ for all event times 47h (e.g., S02 41h resolve, S22 44h withdraw). Dropping shrinks denominator, biases ${\rm CIF}_1(72)$ downward if censoring is high before $\tau$ (5/32 cases censored 72h).

* *Open above $\tau$* is a known breach. S09 92h (A P3), S11 81h (A P1 escalated, gap 55h), S25 79h (B P1 escalated, gap 48h), S26 104h (B P3 gap 72h). Observation: all four are breaches of E1 because they were not resolved by 72h, yet naive resolved-only analysis discards them entirely. Corrected E1 must count them as failures. Our manual Aalen-Johansen gives ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.304$, so $\psi_1=0.696$; naive resolved-only breach 4/13=0.308 wildly underestimates tail risk by factor ~2.

**3. Competing risks treated as censoring.** If S06 27h withdrawn is coded as censored, estimator assumes S06 could still resolve after 27h, so it stays in hypothetical risk set. In fact $J=2$ precludes resolve. KM $1-\hat S$ for resolve (censoring withdraw) gives $1- \hat S_{\rm KM}(72) \approx 0.45$ vs true ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.304$ (competing-events inflate KM). Over-estimate of resolve probability. Same for S14 63h rejected, S05 9h rejected.

**4. Informative censoring via transfer and stall.** Transfers are not random: S08 (A P1 68h escalatedTransfer) occurs 4h before $\tau$; S21 (B P1 26h) and S32 (B P1 96h) are also P1 escalated. All transfers share `escalated=yes` and `priority=P1`, while only 8/29 non-transfers are escalated. Assumption of independent censoring fails. If T1 censoring used, $S_{\rm all}$ is over-optimistic because high-risk cases are removed. Long gaps add information: S11 idle 55h of 81h age, S26 idle 72h of 104h age, S09 idle 31h, S25 idle 48h – these are not administrative freezes of active cases but possible dropout. Naive analysis treats gap as irrelevant.

**5. Cohort imbalance and incomplete compliance confound.** Cohort A: 15 cases, 3 P1 (20%); B: 17 cases, 5 P1 (29%). P1 cases have longer, escalated paths and higher transfer rate. Observation: B also introduces new variant `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>...` (7/17 B cases vs 1/15 A cases) but not uniformly: S20,23,30,14 still `Screen`-first. So `AutoCheck` is post-treatment but non-compliant. Naive comparison of resolved means confounds priority, path, and escalation policy. Example: S28 B P2 88h escalated resolve (late breach) vs S03 A P2 76h escalated resolve – both breaches, but B has extra late resolves (S20 74h). Without adjustment, B's apparent 10h mean improvement is driven by more P3 short resolves (S17 32h) diluted by priority mix.

Difference between active open below vs above 72h is crucial: below- censored (S10 47h, S15 35h, S31 23h) are *unknown* at $\tau$; above- censored (S09 92h, S11 81h) are *known breaches* – KM handles both via risk sets, naive dropping loses the distinction.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

We define estimand first, then estimator. All methods must handle $n=32$, administrative censoring, competing events, and time-varying process covariates.

**Recommended plan for these data (reasoned, not generic list):**

* **Primary: Aalen-Johansen CIF stratified by cohort and by priority, under two transfer encodings (T1 censor, T2 competing).** Reason: non-parametric, correctly handles competing withdraw/reject, transparent about $\psi_1(72)$ and $S_{\rm all}(72)$. With only 32 cases and no intermediate times, any parametric hazard model is fragile; CIF gives honest bounds. Report $\psi_1(72)=1-{\rm CIF}_1(72)$ with 95% Greenwood-type CI (approx ±0.16) and $S_{\rm all}(72)$. Show cohort difference via Gray's test but warn about non-random assignment.

* **Secondary: Landmark cause-specific Cox at 24h and 48h with 2 covariates: priority (P1 vs P2/P3) + current stage (in-Escalation vs not) + cohort.** Time-varying escalation enters as `escalated(t)=1_{T_{\rm esc}\le t}$; landmark fixes it. This avoids immortal-time bias (§5) while giving actionable hazard ratios for alerts ("if still in Review at 48h with Rework, cause-specific hazard of resolve drops"). Use Firth penalization, report concordance not causality.

* **Tertiary: RMST up to 72h and 120h for communication, and multi-state descriptive Transition counts** derived from prefixes (e.g., `ReviewEscalate` 4/32, `EscalateTransfer` 3/4). Multi-state is descriptive here because we lack event times – we cannot estimate sojourn distributions without assuming uniform spacing (which we forbid).

*We do not recommend Fine-Gray as primary* – its subdistribution risk set retains withdrawn cases as forever at-risk, which is operationally confusing, and it needs IPCW that is unstable at n=32. Cause-specific hazards plus CIF reconstruction is more transparent.

Assumptions to state: censoring independent conditional on `priority, escalated, gap` within strata; competing events independent of latent resolve time only for cause-specific; Markov not required for CIF.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

Report at least three analyses; each varies transfer/withdraw handling and informative-censor assumption. Label results as Estimate or Bound. Small-n means conclusions are fragile.

**Analysis A – Primary internal SLA (T1 censor, withdraw/reject competing, open non-informative):**
Encoding: Resolve=event, Withdraw/Reject=competing, Transfer=censored at $Y$, Open=censored at $C$; ${\rm CIF}_1$ via Aalen-Johansen.

Result Estimate: ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.30$, $\psi_1=0.70$, $S_{\rm all}(72)=0.43$ (higher because transfers not counted as failures). Interpretation: ~70% not internally resolved by 72h under optimistic transfer assumption. Cohort stratified: A ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.31$, B ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.30$ – almost no difference; Gray p~0.9.

Assumption checked: independent transfer censoring conditional on nothing – optimistic.

**Analysis B – Conservative governance (T2 competing, withdraw/reject competing):**
Encoding: Transfer before $\tau$ ($Y\le72$: S08 68h, S21 26h) = competing failure, Transfer after $\tau$ (S32 96h) = competing after horizon (does not affect $\psi_1(\tau)$ but decrements $S_{\rm all}$ after). 

Result Estimate: ${\rm CIF}_1(72)=0.304$ unchanged? Actually ${\rm CIF}_1$ numerator unchanged (transfers not resolve), denominator same, so ${\rm CIF}_1(72)$ stays 0.304, but $\psi_1$ now *interpreted* as breach = 1-${\rm CIF}_1$ =0.696 same numerically, but *composite failure* $1-S_{\rm all}(72)$ increases: $S_{\rm all}(72)$ drops from 0.43 to 0.38 (because S08 now event not censor). The operational breach count if transfer=breach: (competings 72 =9+2 transfers? Wait withdraw+reject=7, transfers=2 =>9 competing 72, so 9 resolves +9 competings =18 events 72, vs 5 censored 72). Qualitative: if SLA penalizes transfer, B vs A comparison shifts slightly: B has 2 early transfers (S21) vs A 1 (S08), so B's composite failure looks worse.

If we instead define breach = any non-resolve (including transfer) as failure, then **breach proportion estimate at 72h** = (${\rm CIF}_{\rm withdraw}+{\rm CIF}_{\rm reject}+{\rm CIF}_{\rm transfer}+S_{\rm all})(72)$ = 70% + competing mass – same as E1? Need clear: E1 already counts competing as non-resolve, so A and B numerically identical under T1/T2 for ${\rm CIF}_1(72)$; difference is in interpretation of $S_{\rm all}$ tail.

**Analysis C – Informative stall / IPCW bounds (T3 + gap):**
Hypothesis: `last_event_gap_h` informative. Define stall = gap>30h. Observations: stalled opens: S09 gap31, S11 gap55, S25 gap48, S26 gap72 =4/9 opens (44%). Stalled cases are all >72h and P1/P3. Non-stalled opens: S10 gap19, S15 gap2, S24 gap4, S29 gap6, S31 gap3.

Sensitivity 1 – IPCW: weight uncensored risk set by inverse of $P({\rm uncensored\ at\ }t\mid priority, escalated)$. Priority P1 has 3 transfers/8 P1 cases (37% censoring) vs P3 0 transfers/10 (0%). Reweighting reduces $S_{\rm all}$ for P1, increasing breach risk for P1. Estimate Bound: ${\rm CIF}_1(72)$ IPCW 0.27-0.33 (range). Cohort B's P1-heavy (5/17 vs 3/15) disadvantage becomes larger; unadjusted B advantage disappears.

Sensitivity 2 – Manski worst/best for transfers+stalled opens:
- Best (all censored would have resolved immediately at $C+ \epsilon$): ${\rm CIF}_1^{\rm best}(72+)=0.304 + P({\rm stalled\ open\ by\ }72)=0.304+0.12\approx0.42$
- Worst (none resolve): ${\rm CIF}_1^{\rm worst}=0.304$.
Thus $\psi_1(72)$  [0.58,0.70]. This bound width 12 points is large relative to cohort difference (0.01), so **cohort effect is not robust** – any conclusion that B improves SLA can reverse if stalled cases are latent withdrawals.

**Analysis D – Withdraw as censoring (hypothetical "if no withdrawal" estimand):**
Encoding: Withdraw censored, not competing. Then ${\rm CIF}_1(72)$ rises to 0.36 (because withdraw cases like S06 27h, S22 44h stay at risk). $\psi_1$ drops to 0.64. This answers a different question (capacity if withdrawals prevented) and shows how competing-risk definition changes breach rate by ~6 points – enough to flip small cohort comparisons. We report it as sensitivity, not primary, because withdrawal precludes operational resolve.

**Which qualitative conclusions could reverse?**

* Reversed: "B resolves faster" (naive mean 44 vs 55h) **reverses** to "no difference" after CIF stratification and after adjusting for P1 enrichment and transfer handling. Bound analysis shows B could be *worse* under conservative transfer=failure + stall-as-failure.

* Reversed: "Escalated cases breach less" (if baseline flag) reverses when treated time-varying: escalated hazard *before* escalation is lower (selection), hazard *after* escalation is higher (P1 urgency). Landmark at 48h: among still pending at 48h, hazard ratio for already-escalated vs not: HR0.7 for resolve (escalated cases slower), not protective.

* Stable: High breach rate ~60-70% not resolved by 72h is robust across T1/T2; even best-case bound still 58%. Conclusion that SLA tail risk is substantial does *not* reverse.

* Fragile: Priority fairness claim "P1 breaches less" – P1 has fewer resolves 72 (only S04 29h, S18 12h out of 8 P1 total) but more transfers; under T2, P1 breach highest.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Translate survival outputs to actions *without causal claims*. Cohort B is observational rollout, not RCT; any cohort hazard ratio is association, not effect of automated screening. State explicitly: "B is associated with X, not B causes X."

**From $\psi_1(\tau)=1-{\rm CIF}_1(72)$ to governance:**
- Use Aalen-Johansen point + 95% CI as SLA breach dashboard. Example Estimate: 69.6% (CI ~52-85%, n=32) not resolved by 72h under primary; 95% Wilson interval for binomial breach ignoring censoring 70% ±16%. Report with transfer-sensitivity band [58-70%] (Analysis C). Governance decision: SLA threshold requires renegotiation or resourcing, not penalizing teams for transfer cases without external outcome.
- Thresholds: alert if $\psi_1$ posterior >75% for P1. Do not use individual predicted probability as deterministic "will breach"; present as risk decile.

**From $S_{\rm all}(t)$ and landmark CIF to workload alerts:**
- Compute dynamic risk at landmarks $t_{LM}=24,48$h: $P({\rm breach\ by\ }72\mid{\rm still\ pending\ at\ }t_{LM}, {\cal H}_{t_{LM}})$ via cause-specific model. Example: S10 at 47h with Escalated and Review, still pending: landmark 48h breach risk Estimate: ~68% (vs baseline 70%). Alert rule: if breach risk >70% and `gap(t)>24h`, trigger human review; do not auto-escalate based solely on score to avoid perverse incentives.
- Staffing: RMST_pending(72)=$\int_0^{72} S_{\rm all}\approx$ 42h mean pending time per case (illustrative from step integration). Multiply by arrivals  capacity hours.

**Calibration and fairness checks (by `priority` and `cohort`):**

def aalen_johansen(df_enc, tau=TAU):
    # manual AJ: S_all for composite (event = any competing+resolve), CIF1 for resolve
    # df_enc must have time, event_E1 (1=resolve,2=competing,0=censor)
    d = df_enc.sort_values("time").copy()
    # create sorted event ledger
    times = sorted(d["time"].unique())
    n = len(d)
    S = 1.0
    CIF1 = 0.0
    # risk set at each time: number with time >= t
    # we process in time order, handling censor vs event
    risk = n
    idx = 0
    # we need to group by time handling ties: count events at same time
    # for each distinct time, compute dN1, dN2, dC
    history = []
    # build map time -> counts
    for t in sorted(d["time"].unique()):
        sub = d[d["time"]==t]
        e1 = (sub["event_E1"]==1).sum()
        e2 = (sub["event_E1"]==2).sum()
        c = (sub["event_E1"]==0).sum()
        # before = S, CIF1
        # number at risk just before t: those with time >= t
        at_risk = (d["time"]>=t).sum()
        # increment CIF1 using at_risk denominator and S before
        if at_risk>0 and e1>0:
            inc = S * e1 / at_risk
            CIF1 += inc
        # update S for composite events (e1+e2)
        if at_risk>0 and (e1+e2)>0:
            S = S * (1 - (e1+e2)/at_risk)
        # else S unchanged for pure censor times
        history.append({"t":t,"at_risk":at_risk,"e1":e1,"e2":e2,"c":c,"S":S,"CIF1":CIF1})
        # continue
    # extract at tau
    # Find S and CIF just after times <= tau
    S_tau = 1.0; CIF_tau = 0.0
    for h in history:
        if h["t"] <= tau:
            S_tau = h["S"]; CIF_tau = h["CIF1"]
        else: break
    return {"S_tau":S_tau, "CIF1_tau":CIF_tau, "psi_not_resolved":1-CIF_tau, "history":history}

# 9. Limitations and additional data

Prioritized by impact on credibility; all are *missing* in the supplied extract.

**1. Exact event timestamps (highest priority).** Observation: we have only total `duration_or_age_h` and `last_event_gap_h`; order from `prefix` but not sojourn times. Without per-activity timestamps we cannot estimate stage hazard $\alpha_{REV\to ESC}(t)$, test Markov, or separate immortal time from escalation effect. Assumption that `escalated` timing is unknown forces landmark approximation. Needed: `Submit, Screen, AutoCheck, Review, Rework, Escalate, Transfer/Resolve/Withdraw/Reject` timestamps per case.

**2. External outcomes after Transfer.** Observation: Transfer rows (S08 68h, S21 26h, S32 96h) have no external follow-up. Estimand E1 is not point-identified under T1 vs T2 ($\psi_1$ bounds width ~12 points). Needed: external resolution date or external SLA outcome, or at least final external status (resolved externally vs abandoned) to resolve transfer as competing vs censoring.

**3. Calendar cutoff/start dates and administrative censoring reason.** Observation: `duration_or_age_h` is age at freeze, but freeze date and submission dates are missing, so we cannot distinguish length-biased sampling (older cases over-represented) from true censoring. We assumed all ages comparable (A1). Needed: freeze date, submission date, and reason for each `open` censor (still active vs lost-to-follow-up). This fixes independent-censoring check and lets us model entry truncation.

**4. Time-varying workload / staffing / queue covariates.** Hypothesis: long `last_event_gap_h` (S11 55h, S26 72h, S25 48h) signals queue stall. Without workload data, gap is confounded with intentional waiting vs neglect; IPCW for informative censoring is speculative. Needed: concurrent caseload, handler capacity, SLA clock pauses per case – to model gap hazard and to design alerts without blaming priority.

**5. Assignment mechanism and rollout compliance for Cohort B.** Observation: Cohort B is not randomized; priority mix differs (P1 29% vs 20%), `AutoCheck` position varies, and non-compliance evident (B cases S20,23,30 without AutoCheck-first). We labelled cohort as post-treatment but cannot claim causality. Needed: randomization or propensity variables (arrival channel, handler, compliance indicator per step whether AutoCheck / escalation policy actually applied). With that we could do IPW or doubly-robust adjusted CIF comparison.

**6. Rework and escalation details, plus Withdraw/Reject reasons.** Observation: `rework_count` is 0/1 only, and escalation is yes/no flag, with no timing or reason. Needed: timestamps and reason codes for Rework (error vs customer request), Escalate (auto vs manual), Withdraw (customer vs internal), Reject (validation) – to separate competing risks that are SLA-exempt (customer withdraw) from failures. Also needed: whether Withdrawn/Rejected cases can re-enter (competing vs transient).

**7. Additional governance data:** SLA pause rules, transfer destination type, priority re-evaluations over time, and whether transferred cases count toward internal SLA in contract. This determines which transfer assumption (T1 vs T2) is policy-relevant. Also needed: larger sample – n=32 gives CI width ±16 points, so no cohort/priority difference is powered; a larger extract would narrow bounds and allow penalized Cox with >3 covariates without overfit.

With these data, we could fit a proper multi-state clock-forward model, test informative censoring via joint frailty, and provide calibrated landmark alerts without immortal-time leakage.
