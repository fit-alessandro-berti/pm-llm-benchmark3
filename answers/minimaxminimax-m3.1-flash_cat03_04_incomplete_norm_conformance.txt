# 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is **partially closed-world, and the closure is asymmetric**. Only two analytic commitments are supplied: (a) relations explicitly marked **forbidden** are closed-world — any observed instance is a violation regardless of counts, and (b) the **mandatory** columns are closed-world *within their stated scope*, meaning absence of the expected edge/role is assessable — but only if the scope condition and the denominator are observable in the log.

I distinguish six deviation classes:

* **Forbidden behavior** — an observed relation explicitly marked forbidden (N3, T6, E10). Closed-world: instance = violation. Severity depends on business impact and confidence that the relation is genuine, not on frequency.
* **Missing mandatory behavior** — an expected edge/role is absent for identified entities whose scope demonstrably applies (N1, N4, E1–E8, T1, T2). Requires a verifiable denominator and a scope check; otherwise it degrades to *abstraction mismatch* or *insufficient evidence*.
* **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** — an observed relation with no norm entry and no forbidden status (e.g., `Manual Compliance Review  Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, `Reopen Order  Close Order` under N6). Under open-world semantics this is **not** a violation; it is a candidate for explicit `unspecified` declaration.
* **Typical-pattern absence** — absence of a relation marked **typical** (N2, N5, T3) is *never* a violation. N5 itself names credit notes, write-offs, and open credit terms as legitimate exceptions, so `Issue Invoice  Record Payment` = 92 of 110 with 4 `Issue Invoice  Write Off` and 14 invoices with no observed successor is expected variation. It may still be a *monitoring signal*, not a finding.
* **Likely data-quality error** — the observation is probably an artifact of extraction, joins, typing, or tool configuration (the `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice join on a reused numeric identifier; the unapplied-cash event lacking a typed label; the two `Dispatch Package` events keeping only a carrier attribute). These are *repair* items, not process breaches.
* **Abstraction mismatch / insufficient evidence** — the norm states a lifecycle or horizon intent while the graph encodes direct succession (N1); or the norm's own exception trigger is unobservable in this snapshot (E8); or a PMI-style discovery filter suppresses rare pairs.

**Open-world vs closed-world.** Outside the forbidden list and the scoped mandatory columns, presence of an unreferenced relation is permitted (open world), and absence of a typical relation is permitted. The corollary matters operationally: *absence from the norm is not noncompliance*, and *presence in the log beyond the norm is not itself reportable* — it is only reportable if a forbidden relation or a mandatory scope applies.

Severity ladder used below: **Critical** (forbidden, live business impact plausible, no mitigating evidence)  **High** (forbidden or mandatory gap with material control impact)  **Medium** (bounded/recoverable mandatory gaps, or diagnostics that mask gaps)  **Low** (documentation, taxonomy, monitoring)  **Informational** (conforming, or unregulated and benign).

# 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

## OC-DFG

**What it can establish.** The OC-DFG is the right family for *entity-level lifecycle* questions: whether an Order that was created ever reached validation, whether a cancellation and a dispatch co-occur on the same Order, whether a packed Package left the building. N3 (`Cancel Order  Dispatch Package`, 2 Orders) is a direct instance test and is closed-world: it is a violation irrespective of any threshold. Likewise N4's `Pack Package  Dispatch Package` (198 of 200) and N1's `Create Order  Validate Order` (98 of 100) are assessable as per-entity completeness, provided scope is checked.

**What it cannot establish.** It cannot tell you *why* a relation is missing, nor whether an absent edge is a process failure, an unmodeled intermediate event, a typing inconsistency, or a discovery threshold artifact. PM4Py's `discover_ocdfg` applies `variant_threshold`/`activity_threshold` filters that can drop rare pairs — plausible for the 198/200 figures — so the diagnostic must be re-run with thresholds neutralized before any gap is charged to the process. It also cannot resolve *scope*: N4 applies only to packages "intended for external shipment," and the summary's notion of "externally shipped Packages" is an analyst label, not necessarily a log field. Nor can it confirm causation: the `Cancel Order  Dispatch Package` co-occurrence could be a cancelled-then-replaced fulfilment reused under the same Order id.

**Counts and scopes.** `Issue Invoice  Record Payment` = 92 of 110 must be read against N5's *typical* status and its named exceptions: 4 `Issue Invoice  Write Off` are explicitly legitimate, and the 14 invoices with no observed successor are plausibly open credit terms (or unrecorded payments). None of the 18 is a violation. `Create Order  Manual Compliance Review` (3 Orders) and `Validate Order  Issue Invoice` (72) are not in any normative edge set, but N2 explicitly says intermediate invoice/review events may occur and are incompletely modeled — so they are conforming *in spirit*, and `unspecified`/unregulated, not violations. `Reopen Order  Close Order` (1) falls under N6 `unspecified`: outside coverage entirely.

**The danger of reading "eventually" as "directly follows."** N1's scope explicitly states that every newly created Order "should eventually enter validation," and that the summary *approximates* this with a directly-follows expectation. Conflating the two converts an atemporal lifecycle obligation into a synchronous adjacency test, which (a) flags legitimate orders whose validation is preceded by an unrecorded review event, (b) misses late validation entirely (the 2 orders may have validated on day 5 — fully conformant), and (c) inflates apparent noncompliance by construction. The correct treatment: model N1 as a *reachability-with-horizon* constraint over the Order's lifecycle, not an edge. Only after confirming both orders lack any descendant validation event should a missing-mandatory classification be raised against N1. This is a textbook case where a conforming-at-the-right-abstraction verdict differs from the naive one.

                         and case.get("validated_within_horizon", False)
            cls = _classify(status, observed_counts.get(edge, 0), scope_ok, dq, horizon_ok)
            findings.append({
                "model_family": "OC-DFG", "relation": edge, "norm_status": status,
                "deviation_class": cls, "severity": SEVERITY[cls],
                "case_id": case.get("case_id"),
                "contracted_by": case.get("order_id") or case.get("package_id"),
            })
    return findings

# 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 says "eventually" but has no horizon, no successor tolerance, and no lifecycle model.** *Risk:* false noncompliance on every Order whose validation is preceded by an unrecorded event or occurs late; simultaneously blind to never-validated Orders. *Clarification:* define validation as reachability within a bounded horizon (e.g., 30 days) with a permitted-intermediate-event whitelist; keep it **mandatory**, and downgrade the raw directly-follows proxy to **typical** as a monitoring heuristic.
2. **Scoping of N4 ("intended for external shipment") is not observable.** *Risk:* two Packages cannot be judged; analysts will assume either violation or compliance by labelling fiat. *Clarification:* require a log-visible shipment-intent attribute or package class, then keep N4 **mandatory** with the scope tied to that marker.
3. **E8's exception depends on a type label that does not exist in the snapshot.** *Risk:* every legitimate unapplied-cash payment becomes a formal breach; teams either stop coding unapplied cash or ignore the panel. *Clarification:* mandate explicit `payment_kind = unapplied_cash` typing (and a nullable invoice reference); keep E8 **mandatory** with a machine-readable exception, else reclassify as **typical**.
4. **N3 does not define cancellation terminality or "same Order".** *Risk:* dispatch-after-cancel-request, re-created fulfilment reusing an id, and clock skew all generate false positives on a Critical rule. *Clarification:* specify that cancellation is terminal until a *new* Order id is created, define equality on order id, and exclude dispatch-before-cancel ordering by timestamp — **forbidden**, with the boundary specified.
5. **T6 does not define "same business event"/transaction boundary.** *Risk:* a coarse time-window grouping manufactures cobirth edges (or clears real ones). *Clarification:* define the event/transaction boundary (single event object vs. session) — **forbidden** within that definition, otherwise **unspecified**.
6. **Typical relations have no metric, window, or exception taxonomy.** *Risk:* 18 of 110 invoices (4 write-offs + 14 unexplained) cannot be adjudicated; "unexplained" becomes a permanent grey zone. *Clarification:* define exception concepts (credit note, write-off, dispute, unapplied cash), a payment-term horizon, and whether a reporting threshold exists — status **typical** with an explicit exception list, or leave **unspecified** if it is purely descriptive.
7. **The norm never distinguishes per-instance vs. statistical obligation, and states no denominators.** *Risk:* "98 of 100" vs "1448 of 1450" may be entity-anchored or event-anchored; denominators can be gamed. *Clarification:* state per-entity closure for lifecycle rules and per-event closure for ET–OT rules, and define denominators (unique Order id, unique event id) — **mandatory** metric conventions.
8. **Several frequently produced relations have no explicit status** (`Manual Compliance Review  Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, `Create Order  Manual Compliance Review`, `Validate Order  Issue Invoice`). *Risk:* inconsistent analyst treatment; drift toward "anything unlisted is a problem". *Clarification:* declare them **unspecified** (or **typical** where the interaction is expected) rather than leaving them implicit.
9. **No clause governs error-type relations (E10) other than the prohibition.** *Risk:* the sensorinvoice artifact shows the prohibition alone does not protect financial objects from bad joins; the pipeline has no positive requirement to use disjoint id namespaces. *Clarification:* add a **mandatory** data-engineering rule (globally unique, non-reused identifiers across object families) with E10 remaining **forbidden**.

# 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment (ranks 1 and 2 first).**
- *N3 (2 Orders)*: put the two Orders on a shipment hold; verify, from the carrier/pick records, whether physical goods left the site and on what date; if they shipped, open credits/returns and quantify exposure.
- *T6 (1 edge)*: freeze the single order/payment pair for finance review; until the boundary semantics are confirmed, add a temporary rule blocking order creation in payment-capture transactions (segregation-of-duties control).
- *E10*: even though the likely cause is a technical artifact, the resulting financial-object linkage is a real data-integrity exposure; quarantine the affected invoice from downstream reporting until the join is repaired.
- Add alerting for the three forbidden patterns for the duration of the investigation.

**Data repair.**
- Rebuild the `Sensor Heartbeat``Invoice` join using disjoint, namespaced identifiers (E10).
- Materialize `Delivery` objects for the two `Dispatch Package` events that carry only a carrier attribute, restoring E6/T2 coverage at 200.
- Type the single unapplied-cash Payment (add `payment_kind`, invoice reference = null), then re-evaluate E8.
- Reconcile the 1450/1448 `Add Item` denominator discrepancy; re-run all discoveries with thresholds neutralized to rule out filter artifacts in the 198/200 figures.
- Recover the unrecorded review events that may explain the 2 missing `Create Order  Validate Order` cases (check capture coverage of review/scan event types).

**Process investigation.**
- *Why can a dispatch be scheduled against a cancelled Order?* Identify the system allowing the state transition and whether cancellation is advisory only; that is a control-design question, not a data question.
- *Why does a payment capture appear to create an Order?* Inspect the payment interface and transaction grouping.
- Invoice-level reconciliation of the 14 invoices with no successor (credit terms, disputes, pending write-offs) and of the 2 packed-but-not-dispatched Packages (returns, damage, backorder). Note: 4 write-offs need only reason-code retention.

**Norm revision.** Issue the clarifications from Section 6: N1 horizon + successor whitelist; N4 scope marker; E8 typed exception; N3 cancellation terminality; T6 boundary definition; per-instance vs. statistical closure with denominators; explicit `unspecified` declarations for the unregulated relations in Section 4.

**Monitoring.** (i) Forbidden-pattern dashboards (N3, T6, E10) with per-case drill-down; (ii) per-entity lifecycle reachability (N1 horizon, N4) rather than directly-follows proxies; (iii) ET–OT mandatory-role coverage percentages per event type (E2, E6, E8) with typed exception tallies; (iv) an idle-open-invoice metric decomposed into legitimate exceptions, so that "typical" relations produce monitoring rather than false alarms; (v) CI regression on discovery outputs (threshold-neutral) to detect rare-pair disappearance.

Deliberately *not* escalated: `Manual Compliance Review` (3, E9 typical-rare), `Package object_codeath Delivery` (7), `Reopen Order  Close Order` (1, N6 unspecified), `Create Order  Manual Compliance Review` (3), and the write-off/invoicing detours. Rarity without a forbidden or mandatory-with-scope trigger is not a finding.

# 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust:** the two `Order` instances with `Cancel Order  Dispatch Package` (N3) and the single `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge (T6) are norm-forbidden and require immediate case-level verification regardless of any refinement; the ET–OT shortfall of 2 deliveries out of 200 (E6) is arithmetically firm, as is the single `Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice` edge (E10); and the classification of `Validate Order  Issue Invoice` (72), `Reopen Order  Close Order` (1), `Manual Compliance Review  Customer` (3), and the 7 `Package object_codeath Delivery` edges as non-violations is robust because they are `typical`, `unspecified`, or simply outside the norm's coverage. **Dependent on norm incompleteness:** the 2 Orders lacking `Create Order  Validate Order` (N1) and the 2 Packages lacking `Pack Package  Dispatch Package` (N4) — both become conforming or out-of-scope once horizons and shipment intent are defined; and the single `Record Payment` without an Invoice (E8) depends on typing that does not yet exist. **Dependent on suspected data quality:** the E10 edge rests on a documented identifier-reuse join; the E6 gap rests on carrier-reference attributes; the 1448/1450 `Add Item` discrepancy may be a denominator artifact. **Not decidable from graph summaries alone:** whether the cancelled Orders physically shipped, whether the cobirth edge reflects a genuine event boundary, why 14 invoices lack a payment successor, whether `Manual Compliance Review` substitutes for validation, and whether any of the 198/200 ratios is a PMI threshold artifact. These require event-level case inspection, source-system semantics, and finance reconciliation — not more graph mining.
