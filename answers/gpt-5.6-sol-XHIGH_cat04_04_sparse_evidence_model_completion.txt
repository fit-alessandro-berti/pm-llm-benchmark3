# 2. Shared Assumptions

Let:

- `R` = Register Order
- `C` = Credit Check
- `E` = Engineering Review
- `S` = Supplier Confirmation
- `X` = Expedite Review
- `A` = Approve Configuration
- `P` = Release Production
- `Z` = Archive Case

The following assumptions are common to all three candidates:

1. **Visible events represent recorded completions.** A sequence in a trace is a total order of logged completion events, including arbitrary ingestion-index ordering for equal timestamps. It is not a direct record of activity starts, durations, overlap, or causality.
2. **Endpoints and production gate are enforced.** `R` is first, `Z` is last, and `A  P  Z` is mandatory.
3. **Both principal checks are mandatory before approval.** All candidates require one `C` and one `E` before `A`, based on D3 and their presence in all 30 cases.
4. **No rework loops are modeled.** Each visible label occurs at most once per case. This is strongly supported for this sample but remains an extrapolation for Supplier and the principal checks; only Expedite has an explicit at-most-once rule.
5. **A missing Supplier event has two possible meanings:** a legitimate stocked-component skip or an integration omission. Since the external/stocked attribute and a case-level missingness indicator are absent, the models cannot separate these meanings.
6. **Optionality is language-level, not probabilistic.** In POWL, optional activities are represented by `XOR(, activity)`, where `` is silent. In the conservative ProcessTree, optionality appears only through explicitly enumerated branches.
7. **The silent Supplier alternative means “no recorded Supplier event.”** It must not be interpreted as proof that Supplier work did not occur operationally.
8. **No candidate encodes observed frequencies as probabilities.** The counts affect the evidential assessment but not the accepted language.
9. **Unordered POWL nodes express absent precedence, not proven simultaneous execution.** Their linear extensions allow either completion order without asserting physical concurrency.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The conservative candidate uses a closed-world, trace-enumeration bias: only the seven distinct observed visible traces are accepted. It maximizes sample precision and minimizes unsupported behavior, but it treats ordering artifacts and sparse combinations as if they were process constraints. This is consequently a descriptive baseline rather than a strong predictive process hypothesis.

**Compact formal expression.**

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{conservative}}=\operatorname{XOR}\{&
RCESAPZ,\ REC SAPZ,\ RCEAPZ,\ RECAPZ,\\
&RCSEAPZ,\ RCESXAPZ,\ RECXSAPZ\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here `RECSAPZ` is written without spaces as `R,E,C,S,A,P,Z`; the branch counts are not duplicated because frequency is not part of the language.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py

_conservative_variants = [
    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation",
     "Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration",
     "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
     "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],

    ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check",
     "Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation",
     "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
]

_branches = [
    "->(" + ", ".join(repr(label) for label in trace) + ")"
    for trace in _conservative_variants
]
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "X(" + ", ".join(_branches) + ")"
)
```

The same visible labels occur in multiple XOR branches. PM4Py can represent this, but Petri-net conversion produces multiple transitions sharing a label, and alignments may have several structurally different paths for the same prefix. The representation scales poorly: adding variants duplicates long common fragments unless the tree is manually refactored, and the size can grow with the number and length of observed variants.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- Every unobserved check/Supplier/Expedite combination, even if allowed by D5 or D6.
- Supplier completing after Engineering but before Credit: `R,E,S,C,A,P,Z`.
- Expedite without a recorded Supplier event.
- Most legal-looking Expedite placements, such as `R,C,E,X,S,A,P,Z`.
- Any repetition, missing principal check, early approval, alternative endpoint, or activity after approval other than Release and Archive.
- Alternative linearizations caused by tied timestamps unless that exact linearization was observed.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- It cannot generalize to stocked expedited orders, even though that combination is plausible.
- It turns sparse Expedite observations into accidental ordering constraints.
- It may reject legitimate linearizations produced by timestamp ties.
- It cannot generalize Supplier placement after Engineering but before Credit.
- It encodes no shared causal abstraction for the seven branches and will become unwieldy as variants accumulate.
- It conflates actual Supplier skips with missing events while accepting only the two no-Supplier orders already observed.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The balanced candidate uses a partial-order bias: it preserves strong gates and explicit domain prerequisites while generalizing over completion orders that the evidence cannot reliably distinguish. Credit and Engineering are both mandatory but unordered. Supplier is a no-recorded-event alternative or, when visible, is constrained to complete after at least one principal check. Expedite is independently optional, follows both checks, and is unordered relative to Supplier. This favors a compact causal explanation over exact trace memorization without allowing Supplier to be the first completed core activity.

**Compact formal expression.**

Let \(X?=\operatorname{XOR}(\tau,X)\). Then:

\[
R \prec
\operatorname{XOR}
\begin{cases}
PO(\{C,E,X?\},\ C\prec X?,\ E\prec X?)\\
PO(\{C,E,S,X?\},\ C\prec S,\ C\prec X?,\ E\prec X?)\\
PO(\{C,E,S,X?\},\ E\prec S,\ C\prec X?,\ E\prec X?)
\end{cases}
\prec A\prec P\prec Z.
\]

The two Supplier branches implement a disjunctive prerequisite: `C before S` **or** `E before S`. If both checks precede Supplier, both branches can derive the same visible trace; that is harmless for the accepted language but can affect path multiplicity in simulation.

The sequence/concurrency/partial-order choices are:

- **Credit versus Engineering:** no precedence edge. This is a partial-order uncertainty decision, not a claim of physical concurrency. It is chosen over a fixed sequence because both completion orders occur and D7/S7 prevent causal inference.
- **Supplier:** optional as a recorded event. When present, the model uses two alternative partial orders so that at least one check completion precedes it. This approximates D5 at completion level and is therefore stronger than the original start/readiness rule.
- **Expedite:** optional and at most once, with both `C  X` and `E  X`, implementing D6/S6.
- **Supplier versus Expedite:** no edge. Each must satisfy its own prerequisites, and both are completed before approval.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def _balanced_core_branch(supplier_after=None):
    """
    supplier_after:
      None       -> no recorded Supplier Confirmation
      "credit"   -> Credit Check precedes Supplier Confirmation
      "engineering" -> Engineering Review precedes Supplier Confirmation
    """
    credit = Transition("Credit Check")
    engineering = Transition("Engineering Review")

    optional_expedite = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [SilentTransition(), Transition("Expedite Review")]
    )

    nodes = [credit, engineering, optional_expedite]
    supplier = None

    if supplier_after is not None:
        supplier = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
        nodes.append(supplier)

    core = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)

    # Expedite, if visible, requires both principal checks.
    core.order.add_edge(credit, optional_expedite)
    core.order.add_edge(engineering, optional_expedite)

    # Disjunctive Supplier prerequisite is implemented by XOR branches.
    if supplier_after == "credit":
        core.order.add_edge(credit, supplier)
    elif supplier_after == "engineering":
        core.order.add_edge(engineering, supplier)

    return core


_no_recorded_supplier = _balanced_core_branch(None)
_supplier_after_credit = _balanced_core_branch("credit")
_supplier_after_engineering = _balanced_core_branch("engineering")

_balanced_core = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [
        _no_recorded_supplier,
        _supplier_after_credit,
        _supplier_after_engineering,
    ]
)

_register = Transition("Register Order")
_approve = Transition("Approve Configuration")
_release = Transition("Release Production")
_archive = Transition("Archive Case")

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[_register, _balanced_core, _approve, _release, _archive]
)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_register, _balanced_core)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_balanced_core, _approve)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_approve, _release)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_release, _archive)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- Supplier as the first completed core activity, before both `C` and `E`.
- Expedite before either principal check has completed.
- Approval before both principal checks, or before a selected Supplier/Expedite activity completes.
- Missing Credit or Engineering events.
- Supplier or Expedite after approval.
- Repetition, rework, cancellation, and multiple Supplier or Expedite events.
- A strict universal `C  E` or `E  C` order.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- Supplier might legitimately complete before both check completions if it started after an unlogged readiness milestone while a check remained in progress.
- The model cannot distinguish a true stocked skip from a missing Supplier event.
- It requires both principal events to be recorded and cannot accommodate missing Credit or Engineering logs.
- Disjunctive Supplier prerequisites are approximated through XOR branches, causing duplicate derivations when both checks precede Supplier.
- It does not represent activity starts, overlaps, component-level Supplier confirmations, or rework.
- If S1 is the actual binding control rather than a disputed stakeholder statement, this model is too permissive rather than underfitting.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The permissive candidate uses an open-world but bounded generalization bias. It retains the strong endpoints, approval gate, mandatory checks, and Expedite compliance rule, but it places no completion-order edge between Supplier and either principal check. This admits Supplier as the first completed core activity because completion-only data cannot rule out a check having started and produced sufficient information before its own completion. It is intended for exploration and sensitivity analysis, not as direct proof that all admitted orders are operationally authorized.

**Compact formal expression.**

Let \(S?=\operatorname{XOR}(\tau,S)\) and \(X?=\operatorname{XOR}(\tau,X)\). Then:

\[
R \prec
PO(\{C,E,S?,X?\},\ C\prec X?,\ E\prec X?)
\prec A\prec P\prec Z.
\]

There are no edges among `C`, `E`, and `S?`; `X?` follows both checks but is unordered relative to Supplier.

The boundary of reasonable generalization is:

- **Admitted:** any completion order of Credit, Engineering, and an optional recorded Supplier event; optional Expedite after both checks; either Supplier–Expedite order.
- **Still forbidden:** Supplier before registration or after approval; Expedite before both checks; missing principal checks; release before approval; repeated activities; alternative start/end; and post-approval core work.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

_credit = Transition("Credit Check")
_engineering = Transition("Engineering Review")

_optional_supplier = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [SilentTransition(), Transition("Supplier Confirmation")]
)
_optional_expedite = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [SilentTransition(), Transition("Expedite Review")]
)

_permissive_core = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        _credit,
        _engineering,
        _optional_supplier,
        _optional_expedite,
    ]
)

# Expedite, if selected, remains compliance-constrained.
_permissive_core.order.add_edge(_credit, _optional_expedite)
_permissive_core.order.add_edge(_engineering, _optional_expedite)

_register = Transition("Register Order")
_approve = Transition("Approve Configuration")
_release = Transition("Release Production")
_archive = Transition("Archive Case")

model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[_register, _permissive_core, _approve, _release, _archive]
)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_register, _permissive_core)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_permissive_core, _approve)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_approve, _release)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_release, _archive)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- Approval without both principal checks.
- Expedite before completion of both checks or more than once.
- Supplier or Expedite after approval.
- Release before approval or Archive before Release.
- Any activity before Register or after Archive.
- Repeated checks, Supplier confirmations, approval cycles, and production rework.
- Missing Credit or Engineering events, including possible logging omissions of those events.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- Real orders may have multiple external components and therefore multiple Supplier confirmations.
- Rejected, canceled, or reworked cases are not represented.
- A principal check might be missing from the log even though it occurred; this model still rejects such a trace.
- The model does not represent case attributes that condition Supplier mandatory status.
- It cannot express Supplier readiness based on a partial, unlogged result from an in-progress check.
- Even this candidate may underfit exceptional post-approval corrections or repeated approvals, while simultaneously overgeneralizing Supplier completion order.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

The abbreviations from Section 2 are used below; every row denotes a complete visible trace from `R` through `Z`.

The conservative ProcessTree admits **no unobserved visible trace at any length**: its language is exactly the seven enumerated traces. This follows from its finite XOR-of-sequences construction, not from sampled playout.

def trace_tuple(trace):

def sampled_playout(model, net, im, fm, requested_traces=200):
    """
    Try common PM4Py high-level signatures. The returned set is only
    a sample. Duplicate visible traces are collapsed for reporting.
    """
    attempts = [
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(model, no_traces=requested_traces),
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(
            model, parameters={"num_traces": requested_traces}
        ),
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(
            net, im, fm, no_traces=requested_traces
        ),
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(
            net, im, fm,
            parameters={"num_traces": requested_traces}
        ),
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(model),
        lambda: pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm),
    ]

    errors = []
    for attempt in attempts:
        try:
            simulated_log = attempt()
            sampled = [
                trace_tuple(trace)
                for trace in simulated_log
            ][:requested_traces]
            return set(sampled)
        except Exception as exc:
            errors.append(f"{type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")

    raise RuntimeError(
        "No PM4Py playout signature succeeded:\n" + "\n".join(errors)
    )

def exact_trace_accepts(net, initial_marking, final_marking, labels):
    """
    Exact acceptance of one finite visible trace in an ordinary
    accepting Petri net. Invisible transitions are explored by BFS.

    This is not a general language-inclusion algorithm.
    """
    place_number = {
        place: index for index, place in enumerate(net.places)
    }

    def marking_key(marking):
        return tuple(sorted(
            (place_number[place], int(tokens))
            for place, tokens in marking.items()
            if tokens
        ))

    def silent_closure(markings):
        reached = {
            marking_key(marking): marking
            for marking in markings
        }
        stack = list(reached.values())

        while stack:
            marking = stack.pop()

            for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
                if transition.label is not None:
                    continue

                next_marking = semantics.execute(
                    transition, net, marking
                )
                key = marking_key(next_marking)

                if key not in reached:
                    reached[key] = next_marking
                    stack.append(next_marking)

        return list(reached.values())

    current = silent_closure([initial_marking])

    for label in labels:
        next_markings = {}

        for marking in current:
            for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
                if transition.label == label:
                    fired = semantics.execute(
                        transition, net, marking
                    )
                    next_markings[marking_key(fired)] = fired

        if not next_markings:
            return False

        current = silent_closure(list(next_markings.values()))

    current = silent_closure(current)
    final_key = marking_key(final_marking)

    return any(marking_key(marking) == final_key for marking in current)

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate use:** `model_balanced`.

It has the best evidence-to-generality trade-off. It accepts every observed case, enforces the approval and Expedite controls, does not force an unsupported order between the principal checks, and admits Supplier after either check without allowing it to be the first completed core activity. It should be deployed with an explicit warning that the no-Supplier branch means “no recorded Supplier event,” not necessarily “Supplier was not executed.”

**Sensitivity analysis:** `model_permissive`.

Use it to quantify how conformance results change when Supplier completion is allowed before both principal check completions. Cases accepted only by the permissive model should be investigated rather than automatically classified as compliant. The conservative model should also be retained as a descriptive baseline for detecting wholly new variants, but it is not recommended as the principal operational model.

A concrete data-collection plan is:

1. **Export lifecycle timestamps.**
   - Collect `start`, `complete`, and preferably intermediate status/readiness timestamps for Credit, Engineering, Supplier, and Expedite.
   - Include activity-instance identifiers so starts and completions can be paired.
   - This directly tests S1 versus S2 and distinguishes sequential execution from overlap.

2. **Preserve event-order provenance.**
   - Export source event time, ingestion time, ingestion index, timestamp precision, source system, and correction/replay indicators.
   - Add an explicit flag for same-timestamp tie resolution.
   - This identifies cases where apparent order is only a logging artifact.

3. **Export the missing case condition.**
   - Add `component_source  {stocked, external, mixed}`.
   - Add `supplier_confirmation_required` and the rule version that produced it.
   - For mixed orders, collect component-level identifiers and whether multiple Supplier confirmations are expected.

4. **Instrument missingness.**
   - Add source message IDs, integration delivery status, retry counts, and reconciliation results.
   - Create an expected-event table so a required-but-missing Supplier event can be distinguished from a legitimate skip.
   - Periodically compare source-system Supplier records with the process log.

5. **Collect decision and compliance attributes.**
   - Record approval outcome, check outcome, check waiver, Expedite reason, Expedite authorization, and responsible role.
   - Capture whether S1 is a legally binding rule, a local policy, or merely a normal operating convention.

6. **Seek maximally informative future cases.**
   - A case where Engineering **starts before Credit completes** would directly contradict the strict S1 sequence and support overlap.
   - Cases with both start intervals overlapping, especially with both completion orders, would distinguish physical concurrency/overlap from an XOR of sequential orders.
   - A controlled external-component case with `E,S,C` completions would support the balanced disjunctive Supplier prerequisite.
   - A case with Supplier completing before both checks, while lifecycle data shows one check had already started and produced a readiness milestone, would favor the permissive interpretation.
   - An expedited stocked-component case with no Supplier event would test independence between Supplier optionality and Expedite optionality.
   - Required-Supplier cases exhibiting both `S,X` and `X,S` after both checks would strengthen the claim that no Supplier–Expedite order is required.
   - A stocked case with a reconciled absence of Supplier confirms true optionality.
   - An external-required case with no exported Supplier event but a source-system Supplier record confirms integration missingness.
   - Any repeated Credit, Engineering, Supplier, Expedite, or approval event would falsify the current no-rework assumption.

After collecting enough cases with known component source and lifecycle intervals, compare at least four explicit hypotheses: strict `CE`, strict `EC`, XOR between two sequential orders, and an unordered/overlapping partial order. Completion variants alone cannot reliably distinguish these hypotheses.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

The confidence levels below are judgments about the current evidence, not statistical confidence intervals.

| Modeling decision | Calibrated confidence | Basis and remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Register is first and Archive is last | High | D1 and all 30 traces agree. Exceptional cancellation paths may simply be absent from the sample. |
| Approval precedes Release, which precedes Archive | High | D2 and invariant observed tail. Immediate adjacency outside this sample is less certain than precedence. |
| Both Credit and Engineering precede approval | Medium-high | D3 and all 30 traces agree. The word “should” leaves some possibility of waivers or missing events. |
| Credit and Engineering should have no inferred mutual completion-order edge | Medium | Both completion orders occur, and D7/S7 undermine causal inference. This does not establish physical concurrency. |
| Credit and Engineering physically run in parallel | Low-to-medium | S2 supports it, but no start intervals are available and S1 directly conflicts. |
| A no-recorded-Supplier alternative is required | High | Seven traces lack the event. The meaning of the absence remains uncertain. |
| Supplier is truly optional for stocked components | Medium-high | S4 is credible and consistent with S3’s external-component condition, but the conditioning attribute is missing. |
| Some Supplier absences are logging omissions | Medium | S5 is credible, but no affected cases are identified. |
| Balanced rule that Supplier completion follows at least one completed check | Low-to-medium | V5 and D5 are suggestive, but D5 concerns Supplier start/readiness rather than completion. |
| Permissive allowance for Supplier completion before both checks | Low-to-medium | It is compatible with overlap and completion-only logging, but no such trace was observed. |
| Expedite requires both checks and precedes approval | Medium-high | D6 and S6 agree, and both observed Expedite cases conform. The sample contains only two such cases. |
| Supplier and Expedite have no mutual order | Medium | S6 explicitly supports both orders and the log contains one of each, but evidence remains sparse. |
| Activities occur at most once | Medium for Expedite; low-to-medium for others | Explicit for Expedite and observed for all activities, but only 30 completed production cases were sampled. |
| Conservative model is a faithful descriptive model | High | Its visible language is exactly constructed from the observed variants. |
| Conservative model is a good predictive process model | Low | It is intentionally overfit and encodes accidental order combinations. |
| Balanced model is the best immediate hypothesis | Medium | It reconciles most evidence, but the Supplier prerequisite and check concurrency remain unresolved. |
| Permissive model is safe for compliance decisions | Low | It intentionally admits weakly supported Supplier-first completion orders. |

A material alternative interpretation is that **S1 is a binding control and the 11 Engineering-first completions are entirely caused by logging, timestamp, or case-correlation defects**. If lifecycle timestamps confirm that Credit always finishes before Engineering starts, the balanced partial order would be too permissive for compliance. The recommendation would then change to a sequence-based compliance model—`R  C  E  …  A  P  Z`—with out-of-order observations handled through a separate observation-error model rather than admitted as legal process behavior.

Another material alternative is that **Supplier is mandatory in almost all 30 cases and the seven absences indicate a substantially worse integration failure than estimated**. In that situation, Supplier should be mandatory in the latent operational model, while missing Supplier records should be modeled through an explicit observation/missing-data layer rather than through ordinary control-flow optionality.
