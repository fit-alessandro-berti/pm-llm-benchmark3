# Process Redesign and Decarbonization Proposal for Industrial Spare-Parts Distribution

---

## 1. System Boundary and Assumptions

### Environmental Scope & Lifecycle Boundary
To evaluate decarbonization opportunities without introducing unmonitored external impacts, the operational boundary is defined as a **Gate-to-Gate + Downstream Transport Lifecycle Scope**:

*   **Inside Boundary:**
    *   **Direct In-House Energy Consumption (`energy_kwh`):** Machine electrical energy during production and heat treatment (`CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`).
    *   **Direct Material Scrap (`material_scrap_kg`):** Raw metal scrap generated during primary CNC machining, emergency production, and rework loops (`Rework production`).
    *   **Packaging Material Mass (`package_material_kg`):** Materials consumed across standard packaging (`Package standard`), reinforced packaging (`Package reinforced`), and packaging correction cycles (`Rework packaging`).
    *   **Downstream Freight Emissions (`transport_kgco2e`):** Tank-to-wheel / Well-to-wheel greenhouse gas emissions across all transport modes (`parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`).
    *   **Waste from Obsolescence:** Embodied carbon and material loss associated with batch-produced parts that remain unshipped (>14 days) due to forecast inaccuracies.

*   **Outside Boundary:**
    *   Upstream supplier extraction, raw material refining, and component manufacturing prior to warehouse arrival.
    *   Customer-side operation and end-of-life disposal/recycling beyond customer delivery confirmation (`Confirm delivery`).
    *   Facility overheads (e.g., administrative building HVAC, ambient warehouse lighting) not tied to specific order event logs.

### Key Assumptions & Data Uncertainties
1.  **Transport Emission Factors:** Emission values in `transport_kgco2e` reflect mode-specific carbon intensities where `parcel_air` (~18.5–23.0 kgCO2e/order) carries a 4–6$\times$ higher impact than `parcel_ground` (~3.6–5.8 kgCO2e/order), and `dedicated_van` (~52.0 kgCO2e/order) incurs severe empty-return (deadhead) penalties.
2.  **Rework Scrap Overhead:** Each `Rework production` cycle (470 log events) consumes an average of 1.8 kg scrap and 32.0 kWh on `CNC_B` paths compared to 0.70 kg scrap and 16.0 kWh on streamlined `CNC_A` paths.
3.  **Part Weight and Geometry Distribution:** Part weight determines compliance with Quality Policy Q1 (>8 kg requiring reinforced packaging). Data assumes ~35% of Made-to-order (MTO) items fall into this heavy category.

## 2. Sustainability-Related Process Indicators

To track environmental, service, quality, and resilience performance across event paths, the following multi-criteria indicators are established:

| Indicator Category | Metric Name | Definition & Event Log Calculation | Target Direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Transport Emissions** | **Transport Carbon Intensity ($I_{trans}$)** | Total `transport_kgco2e` / Total shipped orders (by segment and route mode) | $\downarrow$ Minimize |
| **Energy Efficiency** | **Specific Machining Energy ($E_{unit}$)** | $\sum \text{energy\_kwh} / \text{completed order line items}$ across `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat` | $\downarrow$ Minimize |
| **Material Efficiency** | **Scrap Rate per Order ($M_{scrap}$)** | Average `material_scrap_kg` per completed case across primary and rework paths | $\downarrow$ Minimize |
| **Packaging & Waste** | **Packaging Material Intensity ($P_{mat}$) & Rework Rate** | Average `package_material_kg` per case + Count of `Rework packaging` events | $\downarrow$ Minimize |
| **Inventory Risk** | **Batch Obsolescence Rate ($O_{batch}$)** | % of batch-produced MTO parts residing in inventory >14 days before dispatch | $\downarrow$ Minimize |
| **Service Performance** | **On-Time Delivery Rate ($SLA_{ontime}$)** | % of orders where actual cycle time $\le$ `promised_lead_time_hours` | $\uparrow$ Maximize ($\ge 95\%$) |
| **Quality & Defect** | **First-Pass Yield ($FPY$)** | % of cases reaching `Confirm delivery` without `Rework production` or `Rework packaging` | $\uparrow$ Maximize |
| **Resilience & Slack** | **Delivery Buffer Index ($RBI$)** | Remaining lead time slack ratio before carrier cutoff: $(T_{promised} - T_{elapsed} - T_{transit}) / T_{promised}$ | Maintain $\ge 15\%$ safety margin |

### Intervention Details

#### Option S1: Green Shipping Recommender (SP1 Policy Automation)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract (16% share) and Regular (39% share) cases currently triggering unnecessary `parcel_air` shipments (5% overall share, 18.5 kgCO2e/order).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Automatically evaluates remaining promised lead time at `Pick from stock`. If SLA compliance probability for `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` exceeds 85%, air shipping is locked out.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Eliminates up to 80% of air freight emissions on stock items. Service levels are preserved because only orders with sufficient slack are shifted. Quality is unaffected. Resilience is maintained by leaving air dispatch as an automated escalation path if picking is delayed.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Monitor transition duration from `Availability check` to `Package standard`, tracking ratio of SP1 compliance vs. lead time slack remaining.

#### Option S2: Dynamic Batching with Segment-Based SLA Waiting Caps
*   **Target Path / Segment:** MTO Regular (22% share) and MTO Service-contract (12% share) passing through `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production`.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Replaces static batch thresholds with dynamic waiting caps. Large batches on `CNC_A` reduce energy from 2.8 kWh (batch 1–3) to 1.3 kWh (batch 12–20). The dynamic cap limits `Batch wait` to max 12h for Service-contract and 24h for Regular orders.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Prevents `Batch wait` times from reaching the current 38h median, directly addressing the 21% late-delivery rate in MTO ground paths and reducing the 6% obsolescence rate.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Track `Batch wait` event durations correlated with `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, and downstream late delivery flags.

#### Option S3: Smart Routing from CNC_B to CNC_A Based on Due-Date Slack
*   **Target Path / Segment:** MTO orders routed to `CNC_B` (5% path share, experiencing an 18% rework rate vs 9% on `CNC_A`).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Routes MTO parts to `CNC_A` whenever queue slack permits. Avoiding `CNC_B` rework cuts average energy per order from 32.0 kWh to 16.0 kWh and material scrap from 1.8 kg to 0.70 kg.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Significantly reduces late deliveries on MTO parts (down from 32% on the `CNC_B` rework path). Machine load increases on `CNC_A`, requiring load-balancing rules to prevent queue bottlenecks.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Track `machine_group` assignment, `rework_flag` frequency, and cycle time deltas between `CNC_A` and `CNC_B`.

#### Option S4: Priority Pick + Ground Replacement for Service-Contract Stock Air Freight
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders (16% share) experiencing SP1 policy non-conformance (currently 22% of these orders use air freight despite having ground slack).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Implements a priority queue at `Pick from stock` for Service-contract orders. Rapid picking frees up 12–16h of transit slack, allowing 85%+ of these orders to shift from `parcel_air` (7.8 kgCO2e avg) to `parcel_ground` (4.2 kgCO2e avg).
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** On-time delivery remains high (protecting the 18h median SLA) while drastically reducing transport emissions. Warehouse picking capacity must be dynamically managed.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** DFG latency from `Receive order` to `Pick from stock` and subsequent `route_mode` selection logs.

#### Option S5: Bi-Daily Consolidated Truck Dispatch for Non-Urgent Stock Orders
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock Regular orders (`Package standard -> Consolidated truck` / `parcel_ground`).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Schedules fixed truck consolidations twice daily (12:00 and 17:00). Consolidated trucks reduce transport carbon by 35–55% compared to individual parcel ground shipping.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Introduces a controlled 6–12h consolidation wait. Because Standard-stock Regular has a generous lead-time window (median cycle time 31h vs p90 of 62h), late-delivery risk remains low.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Hold time at `Package standard` prior to `Consolidated truck` departure and resulting total `transport_kgco2e`.

#### Option S6: Automated Packaging-Route Compatibility Gate (Q1 Policy Integration)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** MTO heavy parts (>8 kg) triggering `Rework packaging` events (260 standard pack rework + 360 reinforced pack rework = 620 total rework packaging events).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Systematically enforces Quality Policy Q1 prior to packaging execution. MTO parts >8 kg are directed immediately to `Package reinforced`, avoiding single-use packaging destruction, repackaging waste, and transport damage returns.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Eliminates 620 unnecessary repackaging steps, cuts packaging rework cycle time delays by 8–14h, and reduces customer transit damage by 40%. Increases initial packaging material mass slightly, but reduces total lifecycle packaging waste.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Frequency of `Rework packaging` events, compliance rate with Q1 rules, and `package_material_kg` consumption.

#### Option S8: Shared Emergency Courier Pools for Critical Downtime
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders (11% share) using `dedicated_van` (620 cases, 52.0 kgCO2e/order).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Replaces single-order dedicated vans with a regional shared emergency courier network or requires mandatory return-leg backhaul integration (SP2 policy compliance).
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Cuts dedicated transport emissions by 40–50% without compromising the strict 15h median cycle time required for critical plant-downtime scenarios.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** SP2 policy exception logs, `dedicated_van` trip fill factors, and time-to-delivery for Critical-downtime cases.

#### Option S9: Two-Stage Critical Downtime Triage Protocol
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Critical-downtime segment orders initiating `Emergency production` (760 cases) or `Dedicated van` transport (620 cases).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Establishes a triage verification step at `Availability check` to separate true machine-unavailability crises from precautionary customer rush orders. Precautionary rush orders are re-routed to standard stock picking or scheduled MTO production.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Curbs excessive high-carbon emergency processing (7.0 kWh energy, 52.0 kgCO2e transport). Protects emergency production capacity for true customer crises, improving overall system resilience.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Ratio of `Availability check -> Emergency production` vs. `Availability check -> Pick from stock / Plan production`.

#### Option S11: Stock Reservation Rule Revision to Prevent Partial Shipments
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Orders executing `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010 cases) and subsequent `Backorder wait` (220 cases).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Changes stock reservation logic from immediate partial dispatch to short-window consolidation holding (max 24h) when line items are missing, unless customer explicitly requests partial delivery.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Eliminates duplicate transport emissions (7.2 kgCO2e on backorder path vs 3.6 kgCO2e standard ground) and cuts cycle time for backordered cases from 79h median. Reduces customer contact overhead.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence:** Count of `Partial shipment` transitions and duplicate shipping trackings per order ID.

## 5. Rebound and Displacement Risks

Decarbonization interventions can induce unintended secondary effects across the lifecycle:

```
                            REBOUND & DISPLACEMENT MECHANISMS
  
   Intervention Push               Secondary Phenomenon                  Negative Rebound Result
                
 Maximize Batch Size   >   Extended Batch Wait     >   SLA Breaches & Air Escalation  
 (Target: Low kWh)             (Up to 38h median)              (High Transport CO2e)          
                
                
 Strict Ground Shift   >   Buffer Depletion in     >   Downstream Transit Delays &    
 (Target: Low Freight)         Warehouse Processing            Late Delivery Penalties        
                
                
 Reduce Packaging Mass >   Lower Heavy Part        >   Transit Damage, Full Scrapping 
 (Target: Low Mat kg)          Protection (>8kg MTO)           & Emergency Re-manufacturing   
                
                
 Strict Inspection     >   Inspection Bottlenecks  >   Inspection Bypass / Rushed     
 Quality Gates                 & Processing Queues             Packaging Rework Loops         
                
```

1.  **Batching Rebound (Energy vs. Air Freight Escalation):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Maximizing batch sizes on `CNC_A` (12–20 units) minimizes energy per unit (1.3 kWh), but increases median `Batch wait` time to 38h.
    *   *Displacement Result:* The extended waiting time consumes order SLA buffer, causing late deliveries (up to 21%) and forcing logistics managers to escalate downstream transport from ground to `parcel_air` or `dedicated_van`, net-increasing total carbon.

2.  **Transport Mode Displacement (Consolidation Delays vs. Delivery Failure):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Shifting Standard-stock Regular orders to `consolidated_truck` introduces a 12–24h consolidation wait window.
    *   *Displacement Result:* If warehouse picking encounters minor delays, the combined picking and consolidation hold pushes the shipment past the promised delivery window, triggering customer penalty fees or order cancellations (`Cancel due to delay`).

3.  **Packaging Rebound (Material Reduction vs. Damage Scrap):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Attempting to minimize `package_material_kg` by substituting standard packaging for heavy MTO parts (>8 kg).
    *   *Displacement Result:* Insufficient structural integrity leads to a 40% increase in transit damage returns. Scrapping a damaged MTO part wastes 18.5–20.0 kWh of manufacturing energy and 0.85–0.90 kg of metal scrap, completely offsetting small packaging material savings.

4.  **Rework Displacement (Inspection Queue Bottleneck):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Mandating intensive 100% manual verification at `Inspection_cell` to prevent `CNC_B` defect propagation.
    *   *Displacement Result:* Queue congestion at `Inspection_cell` increases overall cycle time. Rushed inspectors miss non-conformance issues under throughput pressure, resulting in downstream `Rework packaging` (620 events) or customer returns.

5.  **Obsolescence Risk from Production Over-Batching:**
    *   *Mechanism:* Aggregating predicted MTO demand into large manufacturing runs to optimize thermal energy efficiency during `heat_treat` (optimal at batch size >10).
    *   *Displacement Result:* 6% of batch-produced items remain unshipped after 14 days due to demand variations. Unshipped parts become obsolete stock, wasting embodied material and energy.

6.  **Customer Behavior Rebound (Priority Gaming):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Implementing carbon-aware lead-time extensions or ground routing for Regular customer orders.
    *   *Displacement Result:* Regular customers bypass longer ground lead times by misclassifying routine orders as `Critical-downtime` or requesting `expedite_flag`, artificially inflating the high-emission `Emergency production` (760 cases) and `dedicated_van` (620 cases) streams.

## 6. Balanced Recommendation

To maximize environmental reduction while safeguarding service levels, quality, and supply chain resilience, isolated interventions are organized into a integrated four-pillar redesign package.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 BALANCED REDESIGN ARCHITECTURE                                    |
+-------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
| PILLAR 1: Intelligent Order Triage & Routing    | PILLAR 2: Dynamic Production & Quality Control  |
| • S1: Lead-Time Carbon Recommender (SP1)        | • S2: Segment-Capped Dynamic Batching           |
| • S4: Priority Picking for Service Contracts    | • S3: Slack-Based Routing to High-Yield CNC_A   |
| • S9: Two-Stage Critical Downtime Triage        | • S7: Inspection Quality Gates for CNC_B        |
+-------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
| PILLAR 3: Packaging & Transport Efficiency      | PILLAR 4: Dedicated Logistics Optimization      |
| • S5: Scheduled Bi-Daily Truck Freight          | • S8: Shared Emergency Courier Networks (SP2)   |
| • S6: Automated Packaging-Route Compliance (Q1) | • S12: Sustainability Exception Governance      |
| • S11: Hold-for-Complete Order Dispatch Logic   |                                                 |
+-------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------+
```

### Pillar 1: Intelligent Order Triage & Carbon-Aware Routing (Interventions S1, S4, S9)
*   **Operational Integration:** Replace manual mode selection with an automated engine at `Availability check`. True `Critical-downtime` cases proceed to fast-track dispatch. `Standard-stock` orders are dynamically evaluated: if priority picking creates $>85\%$ ground SLA confidence, the order is locked to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck`, eliminating policy SP1 violations (currently 22%).

### Pillar 2: Dynamic Production & Quality Balancing (Interventions S2, S3)
*   **Operational Integration:** Implement dynamic batching thresholds in `Plan production -> Batch wait`. MTO Service-contract orders are capped at 12h wait; Regular MTO capped at 24h. Shift MTO parts from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` whenever SLA slack is positive. This cuts manufacturing rework by 45%, lowers energy from 32.0 to 16.0 kWh on shifted orders, and prevents excessive batching obsolescence.

### Pillar 3: Packaging & Freight Consolidation Optimization (Interventions S5, S6, S11)
*   **Operational Integration:** Enforce Quality Policy Q1 systematically: MTO parts >8 kg are routed directly to `Package reinforced`, preventing 620 packaging rework events and transit damage. Transition standard stock dispatches to bi-daily consolidated truck departures. Modify stock reservation to hold incomplete multi-item orders up to 24h, eliminating partial shipment emissions (7.2 kgCO2e down to 3.6 kgCO2e).

### Pillar 4: Dedicated Logistics Efficiency & Exception Governance (Interventions S8, S12)
*   **Operational Integration:** Enforce Policy SP2 for `dedicated_van` dispatches. Require explicit critical downtime logs and transition to a shared emergency courier pool to eliminate empty return legs. All policy overrides are logged in a central exception database for continuous governance.

### Performance Balance Analysis
*   **Environmental Impact:** Reduces transport carbon by 35–45% overall, cuts machining energy per MTO order by 20–30%, and reduces material scrap by >35%.
*   **Service & Quality Protection:** On-time delivery for Service-Contract cases remains $\ge 95\%$. Median cycle times for critical downtime remain under 15h. Packaging and machining rework loops are reduced by over 40%.
*   **Resilience Assurance:** Maintains operational flexibility by retaining express air shipping as an automated fallback if picking or production delays threaten SLA breach.

### 3. Sustainability Exception Governance (Intervention S12)
*   **Workflow Exception Logging:** Any operational override of policies SP1, SP2, or Q1 requires explicit operator authorization in the system, generating a mandatory `Policy_Override_Log` event attribute containing the override reason code (e.g., `Carrier_Disruption`, `Severe_SLA_Risk`, `Customer_Direct_Pay`).
*   **Weekly Governance Review:** A cross-functional team (Logistics, Quality, Operations, and Sustainability) reviews exception logs weekly. Repeated policy bypasses trigger automatic process adjustments (e.g., recalibrating ground transit time tables or adjusting CNC queue priorities).
*   **Resilience Safeguard:** If primary ground transit channels experience regional disruptions (monitored via route reliability data), the automated rule engine temporarily suspends SP1 restrictions for impacted geographic zones, restoring express air options to protect customer operations.
