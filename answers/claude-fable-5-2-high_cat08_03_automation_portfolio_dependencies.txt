## 1. Candidate matrix

**Ranking criterion.** Candidates are ranked by process-level value per scarce first-year slot (four modules, two deep integrations in the first six months), adjusted for prerequisites and control risk. Frequency is one input, not the criterion.

**Evidence behind the ranking.** The figures below are derived from the activity table (frequency × mean service time, frequency × median wait). They are rough indicators only: the observation period is not stated, medians do not add up across concurrent branches, and the selected directly-follows (DFG) counts do not fully reconcile with activity counts (for example, the listed inflows to Credit analysis and Committee packaging exceed their frequencies). I use them for relative comparison, not for ROI.

- **Registration is small.** Register application is about 1,580 h of touch time, roughly 3% of all touch time (about 52,800 h) and about 1% of recorded waiting. Its rework rate is 4%, it sits in position 2 in 98% of cases, and the back office is under 65% utilised after 15:00.
- **Analysts are the loaded pool.** Financial document review, Normalize financial statements and Credit analysis total about 29,700 h, roughly 56% of all touch time, on 9 FTE. The back office carries about 4,800 h on 12 FTE. Per FTE, the analyst load is about eight times the back-office load.
- **Waiting dominates.** On a High-exposure Equipment-finance path, median waits sum to about 258 h against about 7 h of touch time, so waiting is about 97% of elapsed time. Registration contributes 1.5 h of that.
- **Where the waiting sits.** Customer signing holds about 20% of recorded waiting, Credit analysis about 16%, Contract preparation and Financial document review about 11% each, and external valuation about 9% (concentrated on 2,350 cases at 58 h each).
- **Loops.**
  - Financial document review → Request missing documents occurs 3,300 times, with 2,800 returns. That is about 2,100 analyst hours of re-review, more than the whole registration activity.
  - KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch occurs 1,750 times, with 1,160 returns to KYC check.
  - Contract preparation → Remediate control issue occurs 650 times, with 510 returns.
- **Concurrency.** KYC check and Financial document review run concurrently in practice. The financial branch (waits of 18 h + 20 h + 28 h) is normally longer than the KYC branch (4 h). KYC becomes critical only when an entity mismatch occurs (31 h wait, 38% rework, critical path in 82% of those cases).

## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

```
A2 (correct product/exposure) ──► A5 checklist content, A7 early valuation trigger, control path, committee routing
A5 (complete documents) ──► A6 (extraction quality) ──► Credit analysis ──► A8 (pack assembly)
A4 (entity resolved) ──► KYC clearance ──► A9 (KYC gate, decision match) ──► A10 ──► A11
A7 (valuation on time) ──► Committee packaging join ──► weekly Committee decision
A9 (prevents mismatches) ──► shrinks the inflow that A12 would process
A1 (faster intake) ──► more work-in-progress at Classify, KYC check, Financial document review
```

**Upstream data quality dependencies**
- **A6 depends on A5.** Extraction fails on incomplete or low-quality documents. The portal should collect statements in preferred formats, so A5 raises the straight-through share of A6.
- **A5 depends on classification.** Checklists are specific to product and exposure, and classification errors run at 8%. Until A2 exists, A5 should use the product declared on the portal plus a simple rule check, and flag doubtful cases to the back office.
- **A8 depends on A6, A4 and A7.** Committee packaging waits for KYC status, normalized statements, valuation and credit notes. Automating assembly while those inputs are late or inconsistent speeds up nothing and embeds the 15% packaging rework.
- **A9 depends on structured decision data.** The committee decision (amount, tenor, pricing, covenants, conditions) must be captured as data, not free text, before contract terms can be checked against it.

**Legal and compliance controls**
- **KYC before signing.** A9 can enforce this as a gate when the contract is released. A10 and A11 must not be deployed ahead of that gate, because they would make provisional signing faster and its consequences harder to reverse.
- **Contract matches decision.** A9's rule library is the control. Legal must own it and version it, and Compliance must review it. A12 is a fallback for residual cases, not a substitute.
- **Four-eyes.** The violation (5.8% of High-exposure cases at month end) sits at Credit analysis and Committee packaging. Its cause is analyst overload, which A6 relieves. Enforcement needs a segregation-of-duties rule at pack submission, which can run in the same rule engine as A9.
- **Audit trail.** Every module that makes a suggestion (A2, A3, A4, A6, A9) must log the suggestion, the human override and the final decision. Responsibility for the decision stays with the named human role.

**Human escalation capacity**
- **Compliance specialists (3 FTE, two seniors as hub).** This is the tightest escalation pool. A3 with conservative thresholds would initially send more hits to it. A4 should come before A3, so that specialists verify prepared evidence instead of researching it.
- **Credit analysts (9 FTE).** Low-confidence extractions from A6 create a new validation queue for analysts. It must be sized and given a service target, otherwise the 20 h normalization wait simply moves.
- **Back office (12 FTE, slack after 15:00).** This pool can absorb document chasing and A5 exceptions. Shifting that work away from RMs and analysts uses capacity that exists today.
- **Legal ops (4 FTE, month-end overload).** Faster upstream flow raises their inflow. A9 is what lets them absorb it.

**External valuation dependency**
- **Late trigger.** The request is raised only after Credit analysis (Credit analysis → Collateral valuation request: 2,400). The 58 h external wait therefore starts after about 66 h of internal queueing on the financial branch.
- **Parallelization.** Triggering at classification for likely-collateral cases (71% of Equipment finance) lets the valuation run alongside document review and credit analysis.
- **Limits.** Early triggering depends on correct classification (A2) and costs fees on cases later declined, so it should be limited to cases with a high need probability. Valuer performance stays outside direct control: A7 needs partner SLAs and a second valuer for late cases (19% late).

**Core-banking integration constraints**

The case does not state integration depth, so these are my assumptions.
- **Deep:** A1 (customer master), A9 (decision data, KYC status, product parameters), A11 (accounts, limits, disbursement) and, conservatively, A6 (customer data and the credit-model feed).
- **Light or read-only:** A4, A5, A7 and A10 (using the existing e-sign tool).
- **Medium, outside core banking:** A2, A3, A8, A12.
- **Consequence:** only two deep integrations fit in the first six months. A1 and A11 would each consume one of them for little end-to-end benefit.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Portfolio 1: Quick win (A1, A9, A10, A11)**
- **Optimizes:** visible labour savings and delivery confidence. All four are high feasibility, and A9 satisfies the control requirement.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - It leaves the analyst pool, the missing-document loop, entity mismatches and the committee join untouched.
  - The labour it frees is mostly in pools that are not constraining (back office, Operations).
  - It needs three deep integrations, so only two can start in the first six months.
  - A11 must wait until A9's gates are proven, which removes the "quick" from one of the four.

**Portfolio 2: Bottleneck and synchronization (A4, A5, A6, A7)**
- **Optimizes:** end-to-end cycle time and its variability, especially for High-exposure and collateral cases. It gets all inputs of the Committee packaging join in on time and releases analyst capacity.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - It adds no preventive control, so it does not clearly meet Compliance's condition.
  - It leaves the contract-remediation loop in place.
  - It shifts load onto Legal ops at month end and onto the signing step.
  - A7 introduces a partner dependency.

**Portfolio 3: Compliance first (A3, A4, A9, A12)**
- **Optimizes:** control strength and auditability on KYC, signing and contract terms.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - It gives almost no relief to the financial branch, which is the critical path for most cases.
  - A3 carries the most severe model risk in the list and a long validation period.
  - A12 overlaps with A9 (one prevents, the other processes the same loop).
  - Analyst overload, the root of the four-eyes violations, stays.

**Portfolio 4: Flow and control (A5, A6, A4, A9), recommended**
- **Optimizes:** the two real constraints (analyst capacity and the entity-mismatch block) plus the control point where upstream acceleration would otherwise create new violations and a new bottleneck.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - The 44 h signing wait (A10) and the 58 h valuation wait (A7) are left for year 2.
  - Back-office labour savings from A1 are forgone.
  - Committee packaging effort is not reduced directly.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Portfolio 1: Quick win**
- **Cycle time:** gains at the tail only (Contract preparation → Customer signing → Loan activation). Micro and Working-capital cases without committee or collateral benefit most. High-exposure cases barely change, because their time sits between Classify and Committee decision.
- **Waiting time:** the 1.5 h registration wait disappears. The 22 h contract wait and 44 h signing wait fall. Work arrives sooner at Classify → KYC check (7,250) and Classify → Financial document review (3,940), so the 18 h and 28 h analyst queues grow.
- **Rework:** Contract preparation → Remediate control issue (650) falls. The missing-document loop (3,300 / 2,800) and the mismatch loop (1,750 / 1,160) are untouched.
- **Handovers:** fewer manual handovers inside the back office and between Legal ops, RMs and Operations. The KYC ops → senior specialist dependency is unchanged.
- **Resource demand:** capacity is freed in the back office (already slack), Legal ops and Operations, and none for analysts or compliance specialists. Activation exceptions become a new queue for Operations.
- **Control violations:** contract mismatches fall. Provisional signing is blocked by A9's gate, but the blocked cases then sit in the 31 h mismatch queue with nothing done to shorten it. Four-eyes violations are unchanged or worse, because intake reaches analysts faster.

**Portfolio 2: Bottleneck and synchronization**
- **Cycle time:** the largest reduction of the four portfolios, concentrated in High-exposure SME and Equipment finance. Fewer packs miss the weekly committee slot, which costs up to a week each time.
- **Waiting time:** lower waits before Resolve entity mismatch (31 h), Normalize (20 h) and Credit analysis (28 h). The external valuation wait (58 h) moves off the critical path when the request is triggered early.
- **Rework:** fewer Financial document review → Request missing documents loops and lower repeat rate (52%). Less mismatch rework (38%) and normalization rework (16%), and indirectly less packaging rework (15%).
- **Handovers:** fewer RM → customer → back office → analyst cycles. Less dependence on two senior specialists. More, and earlier, handovers between the Collateral team and valuers.
- **Resource demand:** analysts are relieved. Specialists shift from research to verification. Legal ops receives more cases sooner and its month-end overload worsens. The signing wait becomes the dominant remaining delay.
- **Control violations:** pressure for provisional signing falls (380 cases go Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation, meaning everything else was waiting on the mismatch). Nothing prevents it, though, and the 8.5% contract-remediation rate stays.

**Portfolio 3: Compliance first**
- **Cycle time:** modest improvement. The KYC branch and the contract loop shorten, but the financial branch still determines cycle time in most cases.
- **Waiting time:** mismatch and contract waits fall. The 18 h, 20 h and 28 h analyst waits are unchanged.
- **Rework:** the remediation loop is both prevented (A9) and processed faster (A12). KYC exceptions (11%) are triaged better. The document loop is untouched.
- **Handovers:** KYC ops → specialist handovers become structured. Legal ops ↔ remediation handovers fall.
- **Resource demand:** specialists carry model validation and, initially, more escalations from A3. Analysts get no relief.
- **Control violations:** the strongest effect on KYC-before-signing (6.5% provisional, 1.1% remediated) and contract match (8.5%, 14% for renewals). Four-eyes violations persist because their cause is month-end analyst overload. A3 adds false-negative risk.

**Portfolio 4: Flow and control (recommended)**
- **Cycle time:** a large reduction on the financial branch for all segments that need normalization (5,200 executions) and on the 1,750 mismatch cases. It is smaller than Portfolio 2 for collateral cases, because the valuation wait is not automated. The tail improves through shorter contract preparation.
- **Waiting time:** lower waits before Financial document review, Normalize, Credit analysis, Resolve entity mismatch and Contract preparation. The signing wait (44 h) and valuation wait (58 h) remain and become the next targets.
- **Rework:** the three largest loops are all addressed: missing documents (3,300 / 2,800), mismatch (1,750 / 1,160) and contract remediation (650 / 510).
- **Handovers:** document chasing moves from RMs and analysts to the portal and back office. Specialist work is pre-assembled. Legal ops receives structured decision data instead of re-keying it.
- **Resource demand:** analysts are relieved by more hours than registration consumes in total: a 25% reduction in normalization effort already equals the whole registration activity. Analysts take on a new extraction-validation queue. The back office takes on document follow-up in its afternoon slack. Legal ops absorbs higher inflow through generation.
- **Control violations:** KYC-before-signing and decision match become preventive controls. Mismatch cases blocked by the gate are resolved faster through A4. Four-eyes pressure falls with analyst load and is enforced by a segregation-of-duties rule.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Where it arises | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of auditability | A4, A6, A9, and any later model (A2, A3) | Immutable log of input, model or rule version, suggestion, human override with reason code, and final decision by a named role; periodic sampling by Compliance |
| False negatives | A4 (wrong entity accepted), A9 (rule passes a non-matching term), later A3 | Human confirmation for every entity match; A9 rules fail closed; independent sample review of passed items; A3 in shadow mode only until validated |
| False positives | A5 (documents requested unnecessarily), A9 (valid contracts blocked), A4 (spurious mismatches) | Track block and override rates; fast override path with justification; monthly threshold review; RM feedback channel for checklist items |
| Model and rule drift | A6 (new statement formats), A4 (registry changes), A9 (policy or product changes) | Monitor field-level override rates and confidence by document type; revalidate on a schedule and on every policy change; Legal sign-off and effective dates on each rule version |
| Weak exception handling | Low-quality PDFs, atypical customers, non-standard clauses, valuation disputes | Confidence thresholds route to named manual queues with service targets; the manual path is kept staffed; no silent fallback or auto-completion |
| Four-eyes violations | Credit analysis and Committee packaging in High-exposure cases at month end | System rule: analyst and pack completer must differ. A person validating an extraction counts as involved in the analysis. Month-end capacity plan |
| Automation bias | Analysts accepting extracted figures, specialists accepting proposed matches | Mandatory checks of key ratios against source; seeded test cases; overrides reviewed as a quality signal, not penalized |
| Bottleneck shift | Legal ops at month end, signing, Committee packaging | Weekly work-in-progress and queue monitoring per pool; release caps if a downstream queue exceeds its limit; A9 timed before upstream gains peak |
| Gate turns violations into waiting | Hard KYC gate on the roughly 6.5% of cases signed provisionally today | A4 live in the same phase; priority lane for mismatch cases that already have a decision; escalation rule with Compliance approval only |
| Customer friction and RM resistance | A5 checklists and nudges | Co-design with RMs; segment-specific checklists; a "cannot provide" option that routes to a person; RM controls the tone and timing of reminders |
| Key-person dependency | Two senior compliance specialists | A4 captures their resolution patterns as guidance; cross-train the third specialist; monitor the share of cases handled by each |
| Outdated legal content | A9 templates and rule library | Single owner in Legal; change log; regression test set of past contracts before each release |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 (month 1): baseline and prerequisites**
- **Work:**
  - Fix event logging so that concurrent KYC and document-review work is recorded as concurrent.
  - Baseline cycle time by segment and product (median and 90th percentile).
  - Define the structured committee-decision record and mismatch reason codes.
  - Agree the audit-log standard and model governance with Compliance.
- **Gate to proceed:** baselines signed off; owners named for every exception queue; rule-library ownership accepted by Legal.

**Phase 1 (months 2 to 4): A5 and A4 live, A9 control rules, A6 in shadow mode**
- **A5 (light integration):** checklists per product and segment, RM instruction templates, portal nudges. This is the visible early win.
- **A4 (read-only integration):** evidence gathering and proposed resolution, with specialist sign-off.
- **A9 step 1 (deep integration 1):** rule engine only. The KYC-clearance gate is blocking from the start because it is a legal requirement. Decision-match and four-eyes rules run in detective mode.
- **A6 (deep integration 2, build):** runs in shadow mode on clean digital statements, compared against analyst output.
- **Metrics:**
  - Share of reviews leading to a document request (baseline about 36%), repeat rate (52%), and requests that never return (about 15%).
  - Portal drop-off and RM complaints.
  - Wait before Resolve entity mismatch (31 h), mismatch rework (38%), and share of Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check returns (about 66%).
  - Provisional signing (6.5%) and gate blocks.
  - Detective hits on decision match.
  - A6 field-level agreement with analysts.
- **Gate to proceed:**
  - The repeat-request rate is clearly falling with no rise in abandonment.
  - The mismatch wait is falling with no accepted wrong match in sampling.
  - A6 shadow accuracy meets the threshold set by Credit Risk on key fields.
  - The specialist queue is stable.

**Phase 2 (months 5 to 7): A6 live with validation, A9 generation for renewals**
- **A6:** live for digital statements, with human validation of low-confidence fields.
- **A9 step 2:** contract generation for Credit-line renewal first, because it has the highest remediation rate (14%) and the simplest templates.
- **Metrics:**
  - Normalize service time (75 min) and wait (20 h).
  - Credit analysis wait (28 h) and analyst utilisation.
  - Override rate by document type.
  - Contract preparation → Remediate control issue rate for renewals.
  - Legal ops month-end backlog.
- **Gate to proceed:**
  - The analyst queue does not grow despite cleaner inflow.
  - The override rate is stable.
  - Renewal remediation is clearly below baseline for two consecutive month ends.
  - No audit-log gaps in the Compliance sample.

**Phase 3 (months 8 to 10): scale**
- **Work:**
  - Extend A6 to scanned and lower-quality documents, with a manual fallback.
  - Extend A9 to Working-capital and Equipment finance.
  - Switch the decision-match and four-eyes rules to blocking.
- **Metrics:**
  - Overall remediation rate (8.5%).
  - Four-eyes violations (5.8%).
  - Missed committee slots.
  - 90th-percentile cycle time for High-exposure cases.
  - Wait at Customer signing and Committee packaging, to detect bottleneck shift.
- **Gate to proceed:** blocking rules show an acceptable false-block rate; no downstream queue exceeds its agreed limit.

**Phase 4 (months 11 to 12): stabilise and decide year 2**
- **Work:**
  - Re-mine the process and confirm where the constraint has moved (expected: signing and external valuation).
  - Confirm the year-2 order: A10 first (the gate now exists), then A7 with A2, then A8, then A1 and A11. A3 stays in shadow validation.
- **Changes that need no module:** pilot the early valuation request for Equipment finance manually in year 1, and keep pushing adoption of the existing e-sign tool.

**Integration check.** Only A9 and A6 need deep integration in the first six months. A5 and A4 are light. The four modules land on four different pools (back office and RMs, compliance specialists, analysts, Legal ops), so the change load is spread.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend **Portfolio 4: A5, A6, A4 and A9**.

**Why not the highest-frequency activity first**
- **The benefit is small.** Registration is about 3% of touch time and about 1% of waiting. It is performed by a pool with spare capacity and has a 4% rework rate. Receive application is even more frequent, but it is already 72% digital.
- **It adds load to the constraint.** Faster registration saves hours where none are scarce and delivers work sooner to classification (8% errors), KYC check (11% exceptions) and document review (24% rework), in front of the pool that is already the constraint.
- **It uses a scarce slot.** It would also consume one of only two deep-integration slots.
- **Timing.** A1 is a sound year-2 module, once downstream can absorb the flow. The visible early win management wants is better delivered by A5, which is equally feasible and customer-facing.

**Why these four**
- **A6 and A5 work on the real capacity constraint.** Analysts hold more than half of all touch time, and the document loop alone costs them more hours than registration takes in total. The two modules must go together: A5 without A6 delivers clean files faster into an unrelieved queue, and A6 without A5 extracts from incomplete documents.
- **A4 is the low-frequency, high-impact case.** It covers only 1,750 cases, but with a 31 h wait, 38% rework and critical-path status in 82% of them. It is also the pressure behind provisional signing and the dependence on two people.
- **A9 is the control module.** It turns two of the three control findings into preventive checks and relieves Legal ops before upstream improvements push more volume into month end. The third finding (four-eyes) is addressed by lower analyst load plus a rule in the same engine.

**Trade-off accepted**
- **Left for year 2:** the 44 h signing wait and the 58 h valuation wait remain. Both are large, and A10 was the closest alternative to A4.
- **Why A4 before A10:** I prefer A4 in year 1 because the KYC gate makes mismatch resolution the binding step for affected cases, and because faster signing is only safe once that gate has been shown to hold.
- **What would change the order:** if phase 1 shows that A4 does not shorten the mismatch wait, A10 should take its place. If classification errors turn out to undermine the A5 checklists, A2 should be brought forward.
- **Responsibility:** in every case, compliance responsibility stays with the named human roles. The modules prepare, check and record; they do not decide.
