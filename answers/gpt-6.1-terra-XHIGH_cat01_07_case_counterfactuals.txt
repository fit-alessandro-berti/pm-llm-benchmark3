## 1. Feasibility model

The relations below are precedence constraints, not a single prescribed sequence. `A_f` and `C_f` denote the final assessment and final or explicitly reaffirmed compliance review.

```text
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check
Originals Receipt < Document Check                    [if originals required]
Identity Check complete < Assessment
Document Check complete < Assessment
Assessment < Request More Info                       [when assessment requests it]
Compliance Review < Request More Info                 [when compliance requests it]
Request More Info/response complete < new Document Check < new Assessment
A_f < C_f < Decision                                 [if high risk or high value]
A_f < Decision                                       [all cases]
Decision < Notify
Triage < preliminary escalation / preliminary compliance screen [if used]
```

```text
- Identity Check and Document Check may overlap when their inputs and resources are available. Identity need not wait for originals.
- If originals are required, each document check that requires them must wait for their receipt; a rework check must also wait for the relevant response.
- Each Request More Info requires a subsequent new Document Check and new Assessment; a high-risk or high-value case then requires a new or explicitly reaffirmed review after that assessment and before Decision.
- Preliminary compliance work may overlap other checks but cannot substitute for C_f. The final assessor and final reviewer must be different people.
- A resource cannot serve overlapping intervals; a reservation must correspond to actual available capacity. Other cases may be displaced.
- A decision must address material unresolved evidence under the applicable decision policy. A prohibition on a request does not establish that the evidence is adequate for approval.
- Recorded timestamps must reflect actual task execution; forecasts and provisional slots are uncertain, and external response times are not controlled by internal queues.
```

K-104 advances advisory and preliminary compliance work and reserves capacity, changing timing and priority while retaining final review. K-219 changes document-resource routing and timing. K-411 changes task creation and locking so existing verification work can overlap. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by forbidding a further information request; its outcome and risk can therefore change as well as its timing.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage spotted the ambiguity at Mon 08:50, but S1 was not consulted until after A1's Tue 19:10 assessment. C1 began Wed 13:10 and spent 4.5 h reviewing evidence. The factual elapsed time was 58 h 30 min.

**Minimal intervention:** Keep registration, triage, identity, D1's complex document check and A1's assessment as recorded. Send the packet to S1 at triage, then request C1 preparation and a provisional later final slot. Preserve final review after A1 and let C1 make an independent assessment of the complete evidence.

**Temporal propagation:** Illustratively S1 advises Mon 09:00–11:00, and C1 screens the incomplete packet Tue 08:00–10:00 while D1's check is already complete. C1 provisionally holds Wed 09:00–12:00 for final review after A1 finishes Tue 19:10; M1 decides Wed 12:15–12:45 and notifies by 12:55. This saves 5 h 45 min if those slots exist and preparation cuts final review from 4.5 to 3 h. The held slot is a priority assumption, not implied by the snapshots: the Wed 08:00 C1 forecast of 8 h could instead imply a later start, and S1 or C1 may be unavailable. Earlier preparation alone does not make the case ready for final review before assessment.

**Process-feasibility check:** Both checks precede A1; C1's final review follows it and precedes M1. A1 and C1 are different people. The preliminary screen is separate, and the example assumes no subsequent information request; if one occurs, repeat document check and assessment, then renew or reaffirm compliance.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: useful preparation and a held slot reduce both review time and waiting. Base: some preparation helps but C1 remains the bottleneck, so the saving is modest. Worst: evidence changes or urgent work displaces the slot, preparation is repeated, and the case finishes later or requires rework.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Actual C1 reservation and utilization logs showing no usable earlier final slot, frequent discarded preparation, or added urgent-case delays larger than the target benefit would undermine a favorable conclusion; evidence of systematic early detection could strengthen it.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** D1 did not start until Tue 09:30 although originals arrived Mon 17:30. The accurate check ended Tue 13:30, and A2 began at 20:00. Factual elapsed time was 47 h 50 min.

**Minimal intervention:** Keep the original receipt and identity intervals; send the document task to D2 at receipt rather than waiting for D1. D2 may start only when both originals and D2 capacity are available. Keep the factual outcome only if the handwriting is read reliably.

**Temporal propagation:** For example, D2 works Mon 18:00–Tue 00:30, identity having finished Mon 16:20. If an A2 slot and its queue permit Tue 08:00–10:30, M1 could decide Tue 11:00–11:20 and notify by 11:30, 21 h 30 min earlier than factual. This optimistic illustration slightly exceeds the rounded typical no-rework range in the table; it requires favorable assessment and manager capacity. Earlier document completion moves the case into a different queue at a different time, but neither the Tue 08:00 assessment forecast nor the document completion guarantees that slot. If clarification is requested, an external response and a new document check and assessment can erase the gain.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals precede D2's check; identity and documents both finish before assessment. A request from assessment or compliance triggers the full required recheck and reassessment. This low-risk, low-value case does not require the specified high-risk compliance review.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 reads the handwriting correctly, finishes overnight and catches an earlier assessment slot. Base: D2's slower work and queues reduce the gain, with no outcome change. Worst: clarification takes 8–36 h or longer, or an error is missed, causing delay, quality loss or a changed decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2's actual handwriting accuracy, clarification rate and queue at receipt could show that D1's speed or accuracy outweighs D2's earlier start; an unavailable A2 slot could eliminate the expected completion benefit.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** A remaining inconsistency prompted the second request Fri 10:30. Supplier confirmation arrived Mon 09:00; D1 and A1 then repeated their checks before C1 review. Factual elapsed time was 238 h 10 min, including the weekend.

**Minimal intervention:** Preserve all events through A1's second assessment Fri 10:30. Instead of requesting another confirmation, record the unresolved discrepancy and its implications; C1 reviews the assessed evidence, and M1 makes a permitted, defensible decision such as rejection. Conditional approval is another possibility only if the relevant policy permits it and conditions address the risk.

**Temporal propagation:** If C1 is available Fri 15:00–19:00 and M1 Fri 19:30–20:00, notification at 20:10 would finish 109 h earlier than factual. That is an optimistic scenario outside the table's illustrative 48–100 h range, contingent on evening availability and no queue displacement. Alternatively, C1 or M1 queues, weekend hours and the seriousness of the discrepancy may delay the decision into the next week. Removing the external wait and internal repeats does not remove the need for final review or establish an earlier available C1 slot.

**Process-feasibility check:** The first information request is followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. C1's final review follows that assessment; A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes, and decision precedes notification. No further request is silently omitted: the remaining discrepancy is recorded and addressed. If neither rejection nor a conditional decision is permitted on this evidence, this proposed path is infeasible under the governing rules.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: an early, defensible rejection or conditional approval avoids the weekend loop. Base: rejection changes the factual approval and saves time at the cost of a possible appeal. Worst: a poorly supported approval causes loss or compliance failure, or an unjustified rejection harms the applicant; a required further request would make the intervention incompatible with the rules.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Decision-policy requirements, materiality of the discrepancy, evidence that the supplier's confirmation routinely changes outcomes, appeal and loss data, or lack of C1 capacity could overturn the claimed benefit or feasibility.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The lock kept D2's task from being owned until identity finished Tue 13:20; the document check finished at 19:30, followed by an overnight assessment wait. Factual elapsed time was 29 h 30 min.

**Minimal intervention:** At triage create both tasks and grant separate, controlled ownership. Retain I2's Tue 11:30–13:20 identity interval; assume D2 is free to perform the digital document check Tue 11:30–16:00, using actual contemporaneous start and completion records.

**Temporal propagation:** Once both checks are complete at 16:00, A2 might take the case Wed 08:30–11:00 rather than Wed 09:30–12:00; if M1 is available Wed 11:30–11:50, notification at 12:00 saves 2 h 30 min. Earlier readiness alone does not guarantee an earlier assessment or manager slot; a congested queue may absorb the gain or move other cases back. A same-day assessment is possible only with actual capacity.

**Process-feasibility check:** Triage precedes both checks; their actual service intervals overlap on distinct resources, and assessment begins only after both completions. The workaround needs access controls and consistent evidence, not merely retrospective timestamps. No high-risk compliance review is mandated for the stated attributes.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 and A2 have earlier slots and the case completes materially sooner. Base: assessment or manager queues absorb much of the verification gain. Worst: contention or inconsistent records forces correction, delays other cases, or creates apparent rather than real concurrency.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Resource-level start and completion logs, version history and task-ownership records could show that D2 rarely has capacity during identity, the assessment queue absorbs all gains, or the workaround degrades evidence quality.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation and preparation | Adds early S1 and C1 work; may shorten final C1 service or duplicate it; reserves priority capacity. | Potentially final-review waiting for prepared targets. | C1 for unreserved urgent and ordinary cases; S1 if scarce. | Other high-risk cases, especially urgent cases displaced by held slots. | Ambiguous; positive only if useful preparation and prioritization exceed duplication and displacement costs. | C1 near saturation, urgent arrivals and unused reservations can sharply increase waiting. |
| K-219 D2 routing | Shifts standard handwritten checks from D1 to slower D2; possible extra clarification and rework. | D1 document queue. | D2, and document/assessment queues if rework rises. | D2's standard cases and D1's complex cases. | Ambiguous; depends on utilization, quality and routing mix. | Small error-rate changes can generate external loops and repeated internal work. |
| K-337 no second request | Avoids one external request and document/assessment cycle; still needs C1 and decision, potentially earlier; shifts work to monitoring or appeals. | Supplier-response, D1 and A1 rework queues. | C1 and M1 earlier; appeals and post-decision controls potentially later. | Other D1/A1 cases, C1 cases and applicants facing altered decisions. | Ambiguous or negative when quality and compliance costs dominate time saved. | Weekend crossings and decision thresholds produce discontinuous time and risk effects. |
| K-411 concurrent verification | Similar identity and document service demand, but moves D2 demand earlier and creates simultaneous work. | Idle time between identity and document readiness; possibly target verification delay. | Earlier document arrivals and assessment queue; potential correction queue. | Other D2 and A2 cases and users of the shared record. | Ambiguous; potentially positive if real idle capacity exists, otherwise mainly redistribution. | Near-full assessment capacity magnifies an arrival burst; timestamp artifacts can mimic concurrency. |

For a rollout of 100 similar cases in one week, per-case gains cannot simply be multiplied by 100. These are separate policy scenarios, not an assumption that all four interventions apply to the same cases. The snapshots give queue forecasts at a few times, not full workload, service calendars, arrival distributions or capacity headroom. A simulation or staged trial needs those inputs and must include non-target cases.

For K-104, 100 reservations compete for the sole certified reviewer. If preparation saves one to three hours of final review but consumes more capacity than it saves, C1's total load rises; even useful preparation can displace urgent cases. Reservations need expiry, release and priority rules. Unused slots and concentrated arrivals can create idle periods followed by queues. S1 may become a constraint too.

For K-219, shifting work from D1 frees capacity for complex documents but loads D2, whose standard service is generally slower. A higher clarification rate adds external collection and repeat document and assessment work; missed handwriting errors may not appear as rework at all. The net result depends on the case mix and both queues, not just one early start.

For K-337, avoiding 100 second loops may release D1 and A1 time, but it brings cases to C1 sooner and can increase rejection, appeal, loss or monitoring demand. Friday requests and slow external responses make weekend crossings disproportionately important. A ban is only viable where the resulting decision is lawful and defensible; observed speed is insufficient evidence of success.

For K-411, parallel work changes arrival times more than total service demand. Earlier, clustered completions can move congestion into assessment and displace other cases, especially near full utilization. Real-time event records, resource assignments and shared-record versions must establish actual overlap: batch entry or backdated timestamps could otherwise make a serial process look concurrent. Measure both target and non-target outcomes over the full week, including work that spills beyond it.

## 6. Pilot designs

Intervention 1 is K-411; intervention 2 is K-219. Randomize within eligible cases, preserve normal escalation and stop if safety or quality deteriorates. Define thresholds before launch using baseline data and operational owners' requirements.

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | Low-risk, low-value cases with standard digital documents, no originals requirement, and a controlled shared-record workaround. | Low-risk, low-value cases with originals received and standard handwritten documents; exclude cases requiring D1 expertise or with unreadable evidence. |
| assignment rule | Randomly assign eligible cases at triage to concurrent task creation or existing serialized flow, stratified by arrival day and queue load. | Randomly route eligible cases at originals receipt to D2 or usual D1, stratified by handwriting difficulty and queue load; start when the assigned resource is free. |
| sample/support logic | Run a feasibility stage, then size for a prespecified cycle-time effect using baseline variance and an intraday or weekly cluster design if queues are shared; include several weeks and non-target cases. | Use baseline rework and error rates to size for both time and quality; if rare errors cannot be estimated precisely, report intervals and continue monitored rollout only with sufficient support. |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify elapsed time, including weekends, analyzed by assigned group with median and tail. | Register-to-Notify elapsed time, analyzed by assigned group with median and tail. |
| process-path outcome | Actual verification overlap, readiness-to-assessment wait, repeated work and deviations from prerequisites. | Receipt-to-check start, document service time, clarification loops, reassessments and decision changes. |
| quality outcome | Independent blinded audit of evidence completeness, record conflicts and decision corrections. | Blinded independent reread of handwriting and audit of missed discrepancies, decisions and later corrections. |
| queue-spillover outcome | A2 waiting and completion times for non-target cases, plus D2 utilization and wait by arrival cohort. | D1 and D2 wait, utilization and completion times for both target and non-target cases; measure assessment rework load. |
| logging validation | Compare real-time task events to resource activity, access/version history and independent samples; reject inferred or backdated overlap. | Reconcile receipt, resource assignment, actual service intervals, clarification events and audit records; check timestamps against source systems. |
| stopping rule | Suspend for a confirmed access-control or evidence-integrity breach, or a prespecified excess in errors or non-target waiting; review any affected cases. | Suspend for a confirmed serious missed discrepancy or prespecified excess in errors, rework or non-target D2 waiting; audit affected decisions. |

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust for a controlled pilot. It removes a lock-induced dependency between two checks that are allowed to run concurrently, while leaving the assessment and decision standards intact. Its benefit is nevertheless limited by actual D2 availability and by assessment and manager queues. The key test is whether service intervals truly overlap and whether earlier readiness improves completion time without shifting excessive waiting to other cases.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potentially large speed gain. The second request produced evidence that supported the factual approval; suppressing it leaves a material discrepancy unresolved. A documented rejection or conditional approval might be defensible under the applicable rules, but the factual approval cannot simply be carried forward. The effect must include applicant harm, appeals, losses and any post-decision controls, not just the avoided weekend.

K-104 is especially difficult to transport from one favorable case to a system-wide reservation policy because C1 is the only certified reviewer. A slot that makes one case faster can delay an urgent case, and preparation based on incomplete evidence may consume capacity without shortening final review. K-219 and K-411 also redistribute queues: moving many checks to D2 or advancing many assessments can exhaust the headroom that made a single-case example attractive.

The most valuable additional data are actual resource-level service intervals and availability, queue entry and exit times, priorities and reservation releases, and non-target waiting times. Link those records to evidence versions, clarification requests and external response times, final decision changes, independent quality audits and appeals. Real-time logs should distinguish genuine concurrent work from retrospective timestamp entry. Include weekend calendars and follow cases beyond the trial week so delayed work is not mistaken for eliminated work.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural:** The stated precedence and rework rules apply to each case, and no unreported mandatory activity changes the paths.
2. **Temporal:** Illustrative service can occur overnight or on Friday evening only where the relevant resource calendar permits it; otherwise completion may move later.
3. **Resource:** Illustrative S1, C1, D2, A2 and M1 slots are available after accounting for all other work; snapshot queues do not establish those slots.
4. **Behavioral:** Staff actually use the early advice or concurrent-task workaround and record task events when work occurs.
5. **Quality:** D2's handwriting performance and the value of preliminary C1 preparation are uncertain and must be measured rather than inferred from service times.
6. **Structural:** K-337's governing decision rules permit a documented rejection or conditional decision on the available evidence; if they require another request, the proposed intervention is infeasible.
7. **Interference:** Other cases compete for shared resources, and reservations or earlier arrivals can displace them even when a target case improves.
8. **Temporal:** External responses and weekend operating patterns can vary substantially from the factual cases, so elapsed-time scenarios are not deterministic forecasts.
