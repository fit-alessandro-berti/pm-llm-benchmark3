# Independent Conformance Review — Supplier Onboarding (Model M0 vs. CS-9 / PD-3 / FD-7)

## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 Stipulated facts (not reasonably contestable on the record given)

S1. **M0's formal semantics.** M0 is a `StrictPartialOrder` with a total chain `Register_Supplier → checks → decision → Notify_Requester → Close_Case`. Inside `checks`, `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are *unordered* nodes of a strict partial order — in POWL semantics this means **concurrency/interleaving in any order**, not a fixed sequence. Inside `approved_branch`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier` is ordered. `decision` is an **XOR**: exactly one of {approved_branch, Reject_Supplier} executes.

S2. **M0's language.** M0 accepts exactly the traces:
`Register_Supplier`, then `{Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review}` in either order, then either (`Manual_Procurement_Approval`, `Activate_Supplier`) or (`Reject_Supplier`), then `Notify_Requester`, then `Close_Case`. There is **no** `Auto_Approve`, **no** `Request_More_Info`, **no** loop, **no** silent skip, **no** `QA_Clearance`, and **no** provisional-activation ordering. Every activity is mandatory in its branch.

S3. **CS-9 is the only controlled, formally approved normative source.** It is effective 2025-01-01 onward and has not been amended.

S4. **PD-3 is not a policy amendment.** It is steering-committee minutes plus an email, explicitly "not incorporated into CS-9 or M0," with a hard window 2026-06-01 → 2026-07-31.

S5. **FD-7 is time-boxed** 2026-07-10 00:00 → 2026-07-12 23:59, scoped to critical relief suppliers, capped at EUR 20,000, and **expired automatically**. No written extension exists.

S6. **Data-collection break.** From 2026-07-01 `Notify_Requester` moved to CRM; ~70% mirrored; notification missingness rose 2% → 29%. Linkage via `notification_id` is reliable only where the ID is stable; older links are **mutable text**.

S7. **Aggregate counts.** 312 `Auto_Approve` cases in Jun–Jul (289 apparently eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 indeterminate); 47 `Request_More_Info` cases, **none** activated before evidence arrived; 6 post-pilot `Auto_Approve` cases in the first week of August.

S8. **Observed orders are as tabulated.** SUP-708's activation precedes `Sanctions_Check` by 40 minutes; SUP-703's first purchase was EUR 180,000; SUP-704 spend EUR 18,000 (within cap); SUP-705 spend EUR 12,000 on 2026-07-13 (outside FD-7 window).

S9. **CS-9 contains a resource rule** (requester ≠ manual approver) that **M0 does not encode**. This is a stipulated *model incompleteness*, independent of any case outcome.

### 1.2 Disputed propositions (and what they turn on)

| # | Proposition | Turns on |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | PD-3 validly authorizes `Auto_Approve` in place of `Manual_Procurement_Approval` | **Authority.** CS-9 is controlled; a steering committee's minutes normally cannot derogate from a controlled standard. The Compliance observer's "monitored exception" vs. her later "I did not approve weakening CS-9" is a genuine authority conflict. |
| D2 | FD-7 validly suspends the approval-before-activation control | **Authority + scope.** FD-7 reads as a properly issued emergency directive; its internal compensating controls (cap, monitoring, retrospective approval) suggest deliberate, authorized risk acceptance. |
| D3 | Rejected leads (SUP-709) are in M0's scope | **Scope.** Process owner and model owner disagree; the scope document is ambiguous. M0 itself *contains* `Reject_Supplier`, which cuts against the model owner. |

### 1.3 Unproved / indeterminate on the present record

U1. Whether SUP-706's requester was in fact notified.
U2. Whether SUP-712's alias resolution is correct (and whether the shared identity is a genuine segregation-of-duties breach or an identity-management artefact).
U3. Whether the 9 `Auto_Approve` cases with missing eligibility attributes were eligible.
U4. Whether any Compliance-level ratification of PD-3 exists beyond the observer's ambiguous email.
U5. Whether SUP-705's activation was ratified by any post-hoc emergency authority.

### 1.4 Burden of proof

I adopt an **asymmetric two-standard** approach, which is the standard defensible posture for an independent reviewer:

**(a) To declare a case NONCOMPLIANT**, the reviewer bears the burden and must establish, on **clear and convincing evidence**:
1. a normative rule that was **in force, in scope, and applicable to the case at its event time** (not merely a modelled control-flow edge);
2. **conduct** that breached it, established by evidence that is not an artefact of logging, labelling, identity resolution, or model incompleteness; and
3. that no **validly authorized** overlay excused the conduct.
Where the only "rule" breached is an *edge in M0 that CS-9 does not require*, the correct finding is **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, not noncompliance. Where the decisive fact is unknowable from the log, the finding is **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE** — a reviewer must not convert missing evidence into a violation.

**(b) To accept an exception (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**, the **operation** bears the burden and must show, on the **balance of probabilities but with a documentary floor**:
1. an **identifiable authority instrument** (rule ID, issuer, approval record);
2. the case falls inside its **effective window and eligibility scope**;
3. the instrument's **compensating controls were actually satisfied** (cap, monitoring, retrospective approval, deadlines); and
4. the deviation was **contemporaneously recorded as an exception**, not silently normalized.
Absent (1) or (2), good outcomes, good faith, and stakeholder consensus are **not** substitutes. This is the "no retroactive amendment by practice" rule, and it does the decisive work in SUP-705, SUP-714, and the six August cases.

**(c) A tie-break principle.** Where prosecution and defense are both coherent, I resolve toward the reading that (i) does not treat *model silence* as a prohibition, and (ii) does not treat *practice* as authority. These two principles are symmetric constraints — one disciplines the prosecution, the other the defense.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Thesis: the operation is materially noncompliant. Not uniformly — but at the points where noncompliance matters most, and in a pattern that shows control erosion rather than isolated error.**

### 2.1 Formal authority: CS-9 is the only instrument that can change CS-9

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved (N1). PD-3 is, by its own description, "meeting minutes and follow-up email; **not incorporated into CS-9 or M0**" (N2). In every mature control framework, a controlled standard is derogated only by an instrument of equal or greater standing. A steering committee is a *sponsoring* body, not the standard's owner. The Compliance observer's phrase "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception" is, at its highest, a tolerance of *monitored* deviation — and she has since stated **she did not approve weakening CS-9**. That is not the record of a valid derogation; it is the record of an ambiguity that Operations resolved in its own favour.

Consequence: `Auto_Approve` never lawfully replaced `Manual_Procurement_Approval` under CS-9's mandatory rule ("**every** activation requires Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier"). At best PD-3 created a *tolerated, monitored, time-boxed* deviation whose acceptance was conditional on eligibility, monitoring, and the window. It never created a right.

### 2.2 Expiry is not a formality — it is the entire compensating control

Time-boxing *is* the risk control for both overlays. FD-7 expired 2026-07-12 23:59; PD-3 expired 2026-07-31.

- **SUP-705** (2026-07-13) activated before quality review and before manual approval, **one day after FD-7 expired**. Operations' answer — "the flood emergency still factually existed" — is precisely the argument that time-boxing exists to defeat. A directive that self-extends whenever the operator judges the underlying condition persists is not a directive; it is a delegation of the emergency power to the line. No written extension exists. The low spend (EUR 12,000) mitigates severity; it does not supply authority.
- **SUP-714** (2026-08-02) and the **six August `Auto_Approve` cases** (aggregate) used the pilot flow **after PD-3 expired**, on a belief that "permanent approval was imminent." Belief in imminent approval is not approval. This is textbook **normalization of deviance**: an exception mechanism becomes the default route, and the organization's control posture silently degrades to the exception.

The aggregate is the aggravating fact. One post-expiry case is an error; **six in one week, plus SUP-714**, is a process that no longer knows the rule expired. Nobody stopped it, which means the detective control also failed.

### 2.3 The pilot itself was not operated within its own terms

Even taking PD-3 at face value, Operations did not honour it:

- **14 clearly ineligible cases** out of 312 used `Auto_Approve`. That is not a rounding error; it is a **~4.5% eligibility-control failure rate on the very gate that made the pilot tolerable**, plus **9 more** cases where eligibility attributes are missing — meaning the pilot ran without capturing the data that proves its own legality (U3).
- **SUP-703** is the emblem: a **HIGH-risk new foreign supplier**, auto-approved on 2026-06-20 because "the operator selected 'pilot' despite ineligibility," with a **first purchase of EUR 180,000**. PD-3 covers LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group. Every eligibility dimension fails: risk, geography, group membership. There was no manual procurement approval by any human, and no cap. This is not a control-flow annotation problem — it is an **unapproved commitment of EUR 180,000 to an unvetted high-risk foreign counterparty**. Internal Audit is right to flag it as potentially severe. The defense cannot reach this case: no reading of M0's obsolescence makes an ineligible high-risk supplier eligible.

### 2.4 Deviations that M0 *hides*, not deviations M0 invents

The defense will say M0 over-flags. The prosecution's sharpest point is the opposite: **M0 under-detects**, because it is purely control-flow.

- **SUP-712** is a **perfect conformance fit** and simultaneously a breach of the one CS-9 rule that exists to prevent self-dealing: requester and approver are the same human behind two aliases. A conformance regime that grades SUP-712 as clean while grading SUP-707 (a case where *extra diligence* was performed) as deviant is inverted. That inversion is an argument for *more* control, not less.
- **SUP-715** fits M0 and involves a quality reviewer and manual approver who are **spouses in the same reporting line**, with **no disclosure recorded** though the conflict-of-interest policy requires it. Even accepting that no rule expressly prohibits the relationship, the **disclosure obligation was breached**. That is a real, provable control failure, invisible to M0.
- **SUP-708** is the second severe case: a **HIGH-risk** supplier **activated 40 minutes before `Sanctions_Check`**, with **no** pilot or emergency in scope. Sanctions screening before activation is the one requirement common to CS-9, PD-3 *and* FD-7 — every instrument, without exception. It is the control whose breach carries legal, not merely procedural, exposure. That no transactions occurred before clearance is genuine mitigation of *impact*; it is not mitigation of the *control breach*, and it was luck rather than design — nothing in the observed process prevented a transaction in that window. Note also that M0's concurrency inside `checks` means M0 would not even flag a `Quality_Review`-before-`Sanctions_Check` ordering; what makes SUP-708 detectable is the **activation** boundary. Under a naive "M0 is obsolete, relax it" programme, SUP-708 is exactly the case that would be lost.

### 2.5 Desirable outcomes do not amend policy

`Request_More_Info` (SUP-707, 47 cases, none activating early) is genuinely desirable, and the prosecution concedes it (see 2.7). But the *inference* Operations draws is invalid: "good behaviour that the model cannot express" and "prohibited behaviour that the model correctly excludes" are different categories, and Operations has been merging them to argue that its overall deviation rate is benign. `Auto_Approve` post-expiry is not analogous to `Request_More_Info`; one bypasses a mandatory human control, the other adds diligence before the control. The defense's strongest evidence (the 47 clean `Request_More_Info` cases) is being used to launder its weakest (the 14 + 6 + 1 unauthorized auto-approvals).

Likewise, low realized loss is not compliance. SUP-705's EUR 12,000 and SUP-704's EUR 18,000 look alike; only one had authority behind it. Compliance is measured at the moment of decision on the information and authority then available, not by the outcome.

### 2.6 The data problem is itself a compliance finding

N4 is not merely an excuse for missing events; it is a **failure to maintain the audit trail of a controlled process**. Notification missingness tripled-plus (2% → 29%) and the migration shipped with **mutable text-field linkage** for older records. In SUP-706 the CRM message was linked **two days later** into an editable field — evidence that cannot be relied on precisely because it can be rewritten. The organization changed a system in a way that destroyed the provability of a required step and did not compensate. Whoever wins the SUP-706 case-level argument, the **migration** is a finding against the operation.

### 2.7 Prosecution's mandatory concessions (genuine model/data defects)

Intellectual honesty requires the prosecution to concede these, and they are conceded without reservation:

1. **M0 is materially incomplete.** No `Request_More_Info` loop, no `Auto_Approve`, no provisional/emergency route, no resource rule, no versioning against CS-9 revisions, no reactivation or pre-screen scoping.
2. **SUP-707 and the 47 `Request_More_Info` cases are not misconduct.** They are model defects. Flagging them as deviations is a *false positive attributable to the reviewer's own instrument*.
3. **SUP-711** (duplicated `Register_Supplier`, same source transaction ID) is a **migration artefact**, not behaviour. Any claim built on it would be spurious.
4. **SUP-713** (`QA_Clearance`) is a **label/mapping-governance** defect. The substance was performed; audit documents show equivalence. Late approval of the mapping is a documentation-control finding at most.
5. **SUP-706** cannot be charged as a missing notification on a 29%-missingness log. The burden is the prosecution's, and it is not met.
6. **Concurrency**: M0 correctly makes sanctions and quality concurrent; any conformance tooling that reported "wrong order" for `Quality_Review` before `Sanctions_Check` misread POWL semantics, and the prosecution disclaims such findings (relevant to SUP-708 — the real charge there is *activation before sanctions*, nothing else).
7. The **high raw deviation rate is not itself evidence of misconduct** and the prosecution does not rely on it.

**Prosecution's residual case after concessions** — and it is the case that matters: SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, the 14 ineligible pilot cases, the 6 August cases, SUP-712's alias breach, SUP-715's non-disclosure, and the CRM audit-trail degradation. Removing every model-defect artefact leaves a **coherent pattern of authority erosion plus two individually severe control breaches**.

## 3. Defense Brief

**Thesis: M0 is an obsolete, under-specified, mis-scoped artefact applied to a degraded log. Most reported "deviations" are defects of the measuring instrument, not of the operation — and treating them as noncompliance would be a methodological error.**

### 3.1 M0 was never a faithful model of the intended process

M0 encodes a 2025 idealization and has not been maintained. It cannot express:
- **iteration** — `Request_More_Info`, used in 47 cases, all of them *strengthening* the evidence base before activation. The corrected inference is not "47 violations" but "**M0's language is a strict subset of the sanctioned process**";
- **optionality** — every activity is mandatory, so any legitimate skip (no quality review for a pre-screen rejection; no re-registration for a reactivation) is mechanically a violation;
- **overlays** — PD-3 and FD-7 both existed and both governed real cases; a model with no policy-version dimension cannot even represent the question "which rule applied on this date?";
- **labels** — a renamed activity (`QA_Clearance`) breaks the model though nothing in the business changed;
- **anything non-control-flow** — resources, amounts, risk class, dates.

A conformance instrument that flags *added diligence* (SUP-707), *renaming* (SUP-713), *migration duplication* (SUP-711), and *system-migration logging gaps* (SUP-706) as noncompliance while passing genuine self-approval (SUP-712) and undisclosed conflicts (SUP-715) is **not measuring compliance**. Its alignment with the compliance construct is poor in both directions. Precision and recall are both bad. That is the defense's central methodological point: **a high deviation rate against a defective model is evidence about the model.**

### 3.2 Semantics are being misread in the operation's disfavour

M0's `checks` block is a `StrictPartialOrder` with **no edge** between `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` — they are **concurrent**. Any finding of the form "quality review was performed before sanctions check" is void on its face. This matters for **SUP-708**, where the *only* legitimate concern is `Activate_Supplier` preceding `Sanctions_Check`; the `Quality_Review`-first ordering is fully conformant and must be dropped from the charge. It also disposes of any generalized "checks out of order" statistic.

Similarly, `Reject_Supplier` **is a node inside M0**. The model owner's position that M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance is inconsistent with M0's own construction: if rejections are out of scope, the XOR branch is dead code. **SUP-709** therefore reveals a scope contradiction *inside the normative artefact itself*.

### 3.3 The overlays were real governance, not freelancing

- **FD-7** is a properly issued directive with a defined scope, a hard expiry, a **EUR 20,000 cap**, mandatory daily monitoring, and a Quality_Review deadline. That is a **designed, risk-accepted, compensated** derogation — exactly what a competent organization does in a flood. **SUP-704** satisfies every one of its terms: critical pump supplier, 2026-07-11, sanctions before activation, EUR 18,000 (under cap), daily monitoring present, quality and approval next business day. Grading SUP-704 as noncompliant because it deviates from M0 would penalize the organization for **following a valid instrument that the model failed to represent**. It is a paradigm JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION and a paradigm model defect.
- **PD-3** was a monitored pilot with eligibility criteria and a Compliance observer in the room who wrote that it "may proceed as a monitored exception." **289 of 312** cases were eligible — a ~93% adherence rate on a novel route, with the eligibility data captured. **SUP-702** is exemplary: LOW-risk DE, approved corporate group, in-window (2026-06-15), sanctions before activation, quality review next business day per PD-3's explicit allowance, complete eligibility fields, and CRM notification at +2 hours with a **stable** `notification_id`. Nothing about SUP-702 is a compliance concern; it is a **model-coverage** concern. Note also that SUP-702's "missing" `Notify_Requester` is provable — the stable ID is exactly the evidence quality the prosecution demands elsewhere.

### 3.4 The log cannot support the findings being asked of it

Post-2026-07-01 the process's notification step **left the observed system**. ~30% of it is simply not in the onboarding log; missingness went 2% → 29%. In this regime, "no `Notify_Requester` event" carries almost no information. **SUP-706** must not be charged: there *is* affirmative CRM evidence of a message at +3 hours. The link's mutability lowers its evidential weight, but "weak positive evidence plus a known 29% systematic gap" cannot clear a clear-and-convincing bar. The correct finding is **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, and the corrective action is on the **data pipeline**, not on the case team.

**SUP-711** is even starker: the duplicate `Register_Supplier` rows share a **source transaction ID**. That is a deterministic, provable ETL artefact. Charging it would be charging the data warehouse.

**SUP-712** deserves care too: "resolve to the same human through two aliases" is an *identity-resolution inference*. If it holds, it is serious. But the organization has no actor-alias master data (that is exactly what the defense proposes to build), so the finding rests on the same weak identity infrastructure the prosecution criticizes elsewhere. It should be treated as a **substantiated-pending-confirmation** finding, not a closed one.

### 3.5 Scope was never defined, so scope violations cannot be attributed

- **SUP-709** (rejected lead, no quality review): CS-9 explicitly permits an "abbreviated pre-screen process" for rejected leads and explicitly **does not say** whether such leads are M0 cases. When the normative text concedes its own ambiguity, penalizing the operation for choosing one reading is unfair. There is no risk here at all: a lead that failed sanctions was rejected. That is the control **working**.
- **SUP-710** (reactivation, no `Register_Supplier`, no `Quality_Review`): CS-9 has **no reactivation procedure**. The prior quality certification remained valid — re-performing a valid certification is waste, not control. Sanctions was re-run (correctly, since sanctions status changes over time) and manual approval was obtained. The operation applied sound judgment in a governance vacuum. The gap is CS-9's.
- **SUP-713**: procedure and UI were formally renamed; audit documents show equivalent content; the mapping was approved a week later. The substance was identical. The finding belongs in *mapping/version governance*, not conformance.

### 3.6 Trace deviation ≠ business misconduct

The defense's organizing distinction: a deviation is a *difference between an observed trace and a model*. Misconduct requires (i) a rule in force, (ii) breached, (iii) with realized or exposed harm, (iv) without authority. Under that test, of the fifteen cases, deviations attributable to model, label, ETL, scope, or logging defects are the **majority**: SUP-702 (model coverage), 704 (overlay not modelled), 706 (log), 707 (no loop), 709 (scope), 710 (scope), 711 (ETL), 713 (label). Operations' claim that "M0's high deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope" is, on this record, **substantially correct as to volume**.

### 3.7 Defense's mandatory concessions (genuine risk and the limits of informal practice)

The defense does not defend everything, and says so plainly:

1. **SUP-703 is indefensible.** Ineligible on every criterion, HIGH-risk, foreign, new, EUR 180,000 with no human approval. The pilot's eligibility gate was the reason Compliance tolerated it; bypassing the gate voids the tolerance. This is a **severe** finding and the defense joins Internal Audit.
2. **SUP-708 is indefensible.** Activation before sanctions screening breaches CS-9, PD-3 and FD-7 alike. No overlay applied. The absence of pre-clearance transactions is fortunate, not exculpatory.
3. **Expiry must bind.** SUP-705, SUP-714 and the six August cases are **not** justified exceptions. The defense's own best argument — that FD-7 and PD-3 were legitimate *because* they were bounded and compensated — collapses if the bounds are optional. Operations' "the emergency still factually existed" and "permanent approval was imminent" are the two sentences that turn a governed exception into an ungoverned one. The defense concedes these as noncompliant, mitigated in severity by small amounts and good faith, and treats them as **evidence of a missing control**: nothing in the system enforced the expiry date.
4. **The 14 ineligible and 9 unknown-eligibility pilot cases** are real control failures, and the 9 reflect a data-capture obligation the pilot itself imposed.
5. **The resource rule must be enforced.** SUP-712's alias issue and SUP-715's undisclosed spousal relationship are genuine integrity exposures that the defense's own proposed architecture is designed to catch. Arguing "M0 doesn't encode it" is a reason to *build* it, not to excuse it.
6. **The CRM migration is a self-inflicted audit-trail failure.** The defense uses it to block inferences *against* cases; it must also accept it as a finding *against* the organization's data governance.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**On operational compliance: materially noncompliant in a specific, bounded way — not pervasively.**

The organization is *not* broadly out of control. Ordinary standard-route processing (SUP-701, 713, and the bulk of volume) works. The genuinely defensible reading of the aggregate is that the *raw* deviation rate is dominated by model obsolescence — that part of Operations' argument is correct, and I accept it.

But three findings survive every defense and together constitute material noncompliance:

1. **Two severe individual control breaches.** SUP-708 (activation before sanctions screening, HIGH-risk, no authority) and SUP-703 (EUR 180,000 to an ineligible high-risk foreign supplier with zero human approval). Either alone would justify an adverse finding. Both indicate that the *system permitted* what policy forbade — no preventive gate existed.
2. **Systemic erosion of exception boundaries.** SUP-705 (FD-7 + 1 day), SUP-714 and six further August cases (PD-3 + 2 days onward), and 14 ineligible in-window pilot cases with 9 more unprovable. The failure mode is uniform: **overlays had no enforced expiry and no enforced eligibility gate.** This is the most important systemic finding because it is a *design* failure, not an operator failure — and it will recur with the next pilot.
3. **Invisible non-control-flow breaches.** SUP-712 (self-approval via aliases) and SUP-715 (undisclosed conflict) both passed conformance checking cleanly. The organization currently has **no detective control** for the CS-9 rule it can least afford to lose.

**On model validity: M0 is invalid as a conformance instrument and must be withdrawn from that use.**

M0 is unfit on four independent grounds: (i) **incomplete language** — no loop, no `Auto_Approve`, no provisional route, no optional activities; (ii) **no overlay/versioning dimension** — it cannot represent that different rules governed 2026-06-15, 2026-07-11 and 2026-08-02; (iii) **wrong altitude** — it is control-flow-only, so it cannot express the resource, amount, risk, timing, and identity rules that carry most of the actual risk; (iv) **undefined scope** — rejections, reactivations, and withdrawals are unresolved, and M0 internally contradicts the model owner's stated scope by containing `Reject_Supplier`.

Both conclusions hold simultaneously, and neither cancels the other. **M0's invalidity does not excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-712, SUP-714, or SUP-715**, because those breach **CS-9 and the overlays' own terms**, not merely M0's edges. Conversely, **the operation's noncompliance does not rescue M0**, because most flagged deviations were the model's fault.

### 5.3 Where I reject the prosecution

- **Rejected: "PD-3 is void, therefore every one of the 289 eligible pilot cases is a violation."** This proves too much. Compliance sat in the room, wrote "may proceed as a monitored exception," and did not stop the pilot for two months. Operators who followed a documented, monitored, eligibility-gated, time-boxed pilot within its terms acted reasonably. The authority defect is real, but it is an **institutional** finding against the steering committee and Compliance function — not a case-level sanction against ~289 line executions. Punishing operators for an ambiguity created above them is unfair and would destroy the incentive to run pilots transparently.
- **Rejected: SUP-704 as a deviation requiring sanction.** FD-7 was valid and every compensating condition was met. Flagging this penalizes correct behaviour.
- **Rejected: SUP-706 as a missing-notification violation.** With 29% systematic missingness and affirmative CRM evidence, the prosecution's own clear-and-convincing standard is unmet. Missing evidence is not evidence of a missing step.
- **Rejected: SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, SUP-713 as misconduct.** Loop absence, scope ambiguity, governance vacuum, ETL duplication, and approved renaming are, respectively, five different kinds of instrument failure.
- **Rejected: any "checks performed out of order" finding.** M0's `checks` block is concurrent. Half the intuitive complaint about SUP-708 is a semantic misreading and I discard it; the surviving charge is activation-before-sanctions alone.
- **Rejected: the raw deviation rate as evidence of misconduct.** It measures the model.

### 5.4 Where I reject the defense

- **Rejected: "the flood emergency still factually existed" (SUP-705).** The time-box *is* the control. If operations may extend an emergency directive by its own assessment of the underlying facts, the directive imposes no limit at all and FD-7's careful cap-plus-monitoring design becomes decorative. An extension was available and was not sought.
- **Rejected: "permanent approval was imminent" (SUP-714, six August cases).** Anticipated authority is not authority. This is the exact mechanism by which a monitored exception becomes an unmonitored default, and the six-case cluster shows it already had.
- **Rejected: "low realized loss means low severity" for SUP-705 and SUP-708.** SUP-705's EUR 12,000 and SUP-708's zero pre-clearance transactions are fortunate, not designed. Compliance is judged at the decision point; a control that happened not to be tested is still a control that was absent.
- **Rejected: "M0 doesn't encode the resource rule" as any answer to SUP-712 or SUP-715.** CS-9 states the rule explicitly. Model silence is the reviewer's problem, never the operation's defense.
- **Rejected: "obsolescence explains the deviation rate" as a global exculpation.** It explains volume, not the residual. After removing every artefact the defense correctly identifies, an unapproved EUR 180,000 commitment, a pre-sanctions activation, and a cluster of post-expiry exception use remain.
- **Rejected: the CRM migration as a purely neutral fact.** It is invoked (correctly) to block inferences against SUP-706; it is simultaneously a finding *against* the organization for degrading a controlled process's audit trail without compensating controls.
- **Rejected: "stakeholders regard it as desirable" as a source of norm (SUP-707's loop, the permanent auto-approval branch).** Desirability justifies **amending the model and the standard** — which I recommend below — not acting as though they were already amended.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The core design error is that **one artefact (M0) was asked to carry control flow, policy versioning, data conditions, identity rules, and scope**. The fix is layered separation, with each layer expressed in the formalism that can actually enforce it.

### Layer 0 — Scope and case-notion definition (organizational control; binding on all layers)

A controlled **Scope Rule Document (SRD-1)** defining, with a `process_variant` attribute stamped on every case at intake:

| Variant | Definition | Governing model | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ONBOARDING_FULL` | New supplier proceeding past pre-screen | Revised M1 | Core case |
| `PRESCREEN_REJECT` | Lead rejected at or before sanctions pre-screen | M1's rejection sub-path (in scope, reduced obligations) | Resolves SUP-709: rejections **are** in scope (M0 already contained `Reject_Supplier`) but carry no quality-review obligation |
| `REACTIVATION` | Previously onboarded supplier, dormant, certification still valid | M1's reactivation route | Resolves SUP-710 |
| `WITHDRAWAL` | Requester or supplier abandons before decision | M1's withdrawal route | Currently unmodelled; will otherwise generate false deviations forever |

**Rule Z-1 (scope closure):** every case must carry exactly one `process_variant`; conformance is evaluated only against that variant's route. Unassigned variants are a **data** finding, never a compliance finding.

### Layer 1 — Control flow (POWL — model M1)

POWL carries only what is genuinely order/choice/concurrency:
- `Register_Supplier` (skippable for reactivation via a silent transition);
- a **loop** for `Request_More_Info` (POWL `Operator.LOOP`), placed after registration and before the decision — this legalizes SUP-707 and the 47 cases;
- concurrent `Sanctions_Check` ∥ `Quality_Review` for the standard route (preserving M0's correct concurrency), with `Quality_Review` skippable in the pre-screen-reject and reactivation routes;
- an XOR over **four approval routes**: standard manual, low-risk auto, emergency/provisional, and rejection;
- `Notify_Requester` before `Close_Case`, with notification made **optional-in-log but mandatory-in-fact** (silent transition in POWL + a DECLARE/data obligation, because the event now lives in CRM — see Layer 2 and Layer 5).

**What POWL must not be asked to do:** amounts, risk classes, dates, identities, authority, eligibility. Encoding these as extra transitions would produce a combinatorial, unmaintainable model that still could not evaluate `spend ≤ 20000`.

### Layer 2 — Declarative / log-skeleton constraints (DECLARE or log-skeleton, evaluated per case)

These express cross-cutting invariants that a single partial order cannot, especially *negative* and *conditional* ones:

| ID | Constraint | Type | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | `Sanctions_Check` **precedes** `Activate_Supplier` | DECLARE `precedence` / log-skeleton *always-before* | **All** variants, all overlays — the universal invariant. Catches SUP-708 |
| C2 | `Activate_Supplier` requires a preceding approval event ∈ {`Manual_Procurement_Approval`, `Auto_Approve`, `Emergency_Provisional_Authorization`} | `precedence` (disjunctive) | All activation routes |
| C3 | If `Auto_Approve` occurs, `Manual_Procurement_Approval` is **absent** and vice versa | `not co-existence` | Prevents double-route ambiguity |
| C4 | `Quality_Review` **responds to** `Activate_Supplier` when it did not precede it (deadline handled in Layer 3) | `response` | Provisional routes (SUP-702, 704) |
| C5 | `Notify_Requester` precedes `Close_Case`; a notification **fact** (log or CRM) must exist before closure | `precedence` + data obligation | All variants |
| C6 | `Reject_Supplier` **not co-existent** with `Activate_Supplier` | `not co-existence` | All |
| C7 | `Emergency_Provisional_Authorization` implies a later `Manual_Procurement_Approval` (retrospective) | `response` | Emergency route (SUP-704) |
| C8 | At most one `Register_Supplier` per case **after** ETL dedup on `source_transaction_id` | log-skeleton *equivalence/cardinality* | Catches and correctly attributes SUP-711 |

Log-skeleton is the better fit for C1 and C8 because it natively expresses *always-before* and *activity cardinality* over noisy logs; DECLARE is the better fit for the response/precedence obligations with deadlines.

### Layer 3 — Data-aware rules (pandas / SQL checks over the enriched event log)

Everything numeric, temporal, or categorical:

| ID | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | `Auto_Approve` ⇒ `risk_class == 'LOW'` ∧ `country == 'DE'` ∧ `corporate_group_approved == True` ∧ `eligibility_snapshot` complete | PD-3 |
| D-2 | `Auto_Approve` ⇒ event date ∈ [rule.effective_from, rule.effective_to] of the cited `rule_version_id` | PD-3 window — **catches SUP-714 and the six August cases** |
| D-3 | Emergency route ⇒ event date ∈ FD-7 window ∧ `supplier_criticality == 'RELIEF_CRITICAL'` — **catches SUP-705** | FD-7 |
| D-4 | Emergency route ⇒ cumulative spend ≤ EUR 20,000 until `Manual_Procurement_Approval` timestamp | FD-7 |
| D-5 | Emergency route ⇒ `daily_monitoring_flag` present for each day between activation and retrospective approval | FD-7 |
| D-6 | Provisional activation ⇒ `Quality_Review` within 2 **business** days (business-day calendar, not 48h) | PD-3, FD-7 |
| D-7 | Any auto-approved case ⇒ first-purchase amount ≤ the pilot's value threshold — **would have caught SUP-703's EUR 180,000 at transaction time, not at audit time** | Recommended addition; PD-3 lacked a cap, which is itself a design defect |
| D-8 | Every deviation from the standard route must cite a `rule_version_id` **and** an `exception_record_id` recorded **before or at** the deviating event | New governance rule |

### Layer 4 — Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (identity-resolved data checks + organizational controls)

| ID | Rule | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| R-1 | `requester_person_id ≠ approver_person_id`, evaluated on **resolved person IDs**, not raw actor strings — **catches SUP-712** | Preventive (block at approval time) + detective |
| R-2 | Actor-alias master data maintained in the IdP; every event carries `actor_alias` **and** resolved `person_id` with a `resolution_confidence` | Data engineering; low-confidence resolutions route to manual review rather than to a finding |
| R-3 | Quality reviewer and approver must be independent: not in the same direct reporting line, and any declared relationship requires a recorded, accepted disclosure — **catches SUP-715** | Detective check against HR relationship register + mandatory disclosure attestation at approval |
| R-4 | Delegation must be evidenced: `delegation_id`, grantor, grantee, scope, validity window | Organizational + data |
| R-5 | Four-eyes on HIGH-risk activations regardless of route | Preventive |

R-3 requires an organizational step first: the conflict-of-interest policy must state the prohibition explicitly, since today it only requires disclosure. Until then, only the disclosure omission is chargeable — which is exactly how I classified SUP-715.

### Layer 5 — Policy-overlay governance (organizational control + versioned rule registry)

The single most important structural fix. A **Rule Registry** table, machine-readable, with every overlay carrying:
`rule_id`, `version`, `type` (STANDARD | PILOT | EMERGENCY), `issuer`, `approver_role`, `compliance_signoff_ref`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `scope_predicate`, `compensating_controls`, `superseded_by`, `record_timestamp` (immutable).

Governance rules:
- **G-1 (authority):** no overlay may derogate from a controlled standard without a **countersignature from the standard's owner** (Compliance). PD-3 would have failed this check on day one — which is the correct outcome, and would have forced the ambiguity into the open before 312 cases ran.
- **G-2 (hard expiry):** the workflow system reads `effective_to` and **disables the route automatically** at expiry. No SUP-705, no SUP-714, no August cluster. Expiry must be a *system* property, not a memo.
- **G-3 (eligibility gate at selection time):** the "pilot" option is not selectable unless D-1 evaluates true on the live record. SUP-703 becomes impossible.
- **G-4 (exception record):** every overlay use writes a contemporaneous exception record (D-8), making the deviation visible in real time rather than at audit.
- **G-5 (sunset review):** every pilot must be either codified into CS-9 or terminated before expiry, with a named owner. The permanent low-risk auto-approval branch that Operations wants is a **legitimate request** — it should be pursued through a CS-9 amendment, on the strength of the 289 clean cases, not through continued informal use.

### Layer 6 — Event and data-collection remediation

- Immutable `notification_id` propagated from CRM at send time, bound to `case_id` in an **append-only** join table; retire mutable text-field linkage and backfill where a defensible key exists.
- Explicit `notification_source` (LOG | CRM | NONE) so that "not observed" is distinguishable from "did not occur" — the distinction SUP-706 turns on.
- `eligibility_snapshot` (JSON, immutable) captured at decision time, not recomputed later.
- `source_transaction_id` used for ETL dedup before conformance checking (SUP-711).
- Approved **activity-label mapping table** with `valid_from`/`valid_to`, so renames (SUP-713) are resolved by mapping rather than reported as deviations — and so that mapping approvals are themselves dated and controlled.
- Dual timestamps everywhere: `effective_timestamp` (when it happened) and `record_timestamp` (when it was recorded, immutable), enabling detection of late/backdated entries such as SUP-706's two-days-later link.

### Which layer gets what — summary

| Concern | Layer / formalism | Why not elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| Order, choice, concurrency, iteration | **POWL (M1)** | Native; this is what POWL is for |
| Universal "sanctions before activation" invariant, cardinality, negative constraints | **DECLARE / log-skeleton** | Must hold across *all* routes and overlays; embedding it in each POWL branch duplicates it and invites drift |
| Amounts, dates, risk class, eligibility, deadlines | **pandas/SQL data checks** | POWL and DECLARE have no data perspective at all |
| Identity, segregation of duties, conflicts, delegation | **Identity-resolved data checks + HR/IdP controls** | Requires master data POWL cannot see |
| Overlay authority, expiry, sunset | **Rule registry + organizational controls, enforced in the workflow system** | Must be *preventive*; a conformance checker finds these only after the money is gone |
| Case notion and scope | **SRD-1 (organizational), stamped as data** | A model cannot define its own scope without circularity |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Stated limitations before the code

1. **POWL cannot express data conditions.** Risk class, country, corporate-group membership, EUR 20,000 cap, EUR 180,000 purchase, business-day deadlines, and overlay windows are **absent from M1 by design** and live in Layers 2–5. Encoding them as transitions would explode the model and still not evaluate an inequality.
2. **The four routes are modelled as a single XOR.** Route *selection* is data-driven (risk, eligibility, emergency status); POWL can only offer the choice, not police it. The `route_selection` guard is therefore an overlay rule (D-1/D-3), and I state this rather than pretending the XOR is a control.
3. **`Request_More_Info` loop.** PM4Py's process-tree `Operator.LOOP` with children `[do, redo]` means *do, then optionally (redo, do)\**. I use `do = SilentTransition()` and `redo = Request_More_Info`, giving zero-or-more `Request_More_Info` executions — the correct semantics for SUP-707 (two cycles) and for the majority of cases with none. An unbounded loop cannot express "at most N cycles" or an SLA; that bound is a Layer-3 data check, not a control-flow property. I flag this rather than hide it.
4. **Concurrency vs. provisional ordering.** In the standard route sanctions and quality are concurrent (preserving M0's correct semantics). In the auto and emergency routes `Quality_Review` legitimately follows activation, so those routes carry their **own** ordering. The invariant `Sanctions_Check → Activate_Supplier` is enforced in **every** route by an explicit edge, *and* redundantly as DECLARE C1 — belt and braces on the one rule that must never fail.
5. **Optional activities** (`Register_Supplier` for reactivation, `Quality_Review` for pre-screen rejection, `Notify_Requester` observability) are modelled with `SilentTransition` inside XORs. This is the standard, honest way to express optionality in POWL; it necessarily makes the model more permissive, so the corresponding *obligations* are pushed into DECLARE/data checks that can be conditioned on `process_variant`.
6. **A single POWL cannot represent time-varying policy.** M1 is the union of routes ever permitted; **which routes are legal for a given case is decided by `rule_version_id` at Layer 5.** This is the deliberate separation that M0 lacked, and it is why SUP-705 and SUP-714 are noncompliant even though M1 accepts their traces. I state this plainly: **M1 accepting a trace is necessary but not sufficient for compliance.**

### 7.2 Executable model and overlay/data proposal

```python
"""
Revised supplier-onboarding normative architecture.

LAYER 1 (this file, `M1`): control flow only, in POWL.
LAYER 2-5 (this file, as data): declarative constraints, policy overlays,
          data-aware rules, resource rules, and the event-collection proposal.

No external files are read. All tables are inline literals.

IMPORTANT SEMANTIC NOTE
-----------------------
M1 is the UNION of every route the organization has ever been permitted to run.
Acceptance of a trace by M1 is NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT for compliance:
which route is legal for a given case is decided by the policy overlay
(effective window + eligibility predicate) and the data-aware rules below.
This separation is the central fix relative to M0, which conflated the two.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# LAYER 1 - CONTROL FLOW (POWL)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# --- 1a. Intake: Register_Supplier is skippable for REACTIVATION cases -------
# Rationale: SUP-710 (reactivation) legitimately has no Register_Supplier.
# Scope rule Z-1 decides which variant a case belongs to; POWL only offers
# the option.
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
intake = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[register, SilentTransition()])


# --- 1b. Request_More_Info: zero-or-more cycles ------------------------------
# PM4Py LOOP(do, redo) == do, then zero or more (redo, do).
# do = silent, redo = Request_More_Info  =>  Request_More_Info repeated 0..n.
# Legalizes SUP-707 (2 cycles) and the 47 aggregate cases.
# LIMITATION: POWL cannot bound the number of cycles or attach an SLA;
# see data rule D-9 below.
rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                        children=[SilentTransition(), rmi])


# --- 1c. STANDARD ROUTE ------------------------------------------------------
# Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review (concurrent - preserves M0's correct
# semantics), both before Manual_Procurement_Approval, which precedes
# Activate_Supplier.
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality, std_manual, std_activate]
)
# No edge between std_sanctions and std_quality => they remain CONCURRENT.
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_sanctions, std_manual)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_quality, std_manual)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_manual, std_activate)
# Invariant C1 holds transitively: sanctions -> manual -> activate.


# --- 1d. LOW-RISK AUTO-APPROVAL ROUTE (PD-3 pattern) -------------------------
# Sanctions_Check and automated master-data validation before activation;
# Quality_Review may follow activation (PD-3: within 2 business days).
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_mdv = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_mdv, auto_approve, auto_activate, auto_quality]
)
# Sanctions and master-data validation are concurrent with each other,
# but BOTH must precede the auto-approval decision.
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_sanctions, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_mdv, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_quality)
# C1 satisfied: sanctions -> approve -> activate.


# --- 1e. EMERGENCY / PROVISIONAL ROUTE (FD-7 pattern) ------------------------
# Provisional activation may precede Quality_Review and manual approval,
# but NEVER precedes Sanctions_Check. Retrospective approval is mandatory
# (constraint C7), as is monitoring.

emg_auth = Transition(label="Emergency_Provisional_Authorization")

emg_monitor = Transition(label="Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
emg_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
emg_retro = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[emg_sanctions, emg_auth, emg_activate,
           emg_monitor, emg_quality, emg_retro]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_sanctions, emg_auth)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_auth, emg_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_activate, emg_monitor)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_activate, emg_quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_activate, emg_retro)
# Quality_Review and the retrospective approval are concurrent with each
# other and with monitoring - all merely required to FOLLOW activation.
# Their DEADLINES (2 business days) are data rule D-6, not control flow.
# C1 satisfied: sanctions -> auth -> activate.


# --- 1f. REJECTION ROUTE (incl. PRESCREEN_REJECT) ----------------------------
# SUP-709: a lead failing the sanctions pre-screen is rejected with NO
# quality review. Quality_Review is therefore optional on this route.
rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_quality = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Transition(label="Quality_Review"), SilentTransition()],
)
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_sanctions, rej_quality, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_sanctions, rej_quality)
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_quality, reject)


# --- 1g. WITHDRAWAL ROUTE ----------------------------------------------------
# Previously unmodelled; without it, every abandoned case is a false
# deviation forever.
wdr_sanctions = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"), SilentTransition()],
)
withdraw = Transition(label="Withdraw_Request")

withdrawal_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[wdr_sanctions, withdraw])
withdrawal_route.order.add_edge(wdr_sanctions, withdraw)


# --- 1h. Decision: exclusive choice over the five routes ---------------------
# LIMITATION: POWL offers the choice; it cannot enforce WHICH route a case
# is entitled to. Route entitlement is overlay rule D-1/D-3 (Layer 3/5).
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route,
              reject_route, withdrawal_route],
)


# --- 1i. Notification: mandatory in fact, optional in the ONBOARDING log -----
# Post-2026-07-01 the event lives in CRM and is mirrored only ~70% of the
# time (N4). Making it mandatory in POWL would manufacture ~29% false
# violations. The OBLIGATION is preserved as declarative constraint C5 plus
# data rule D-10, evaluated against the CRM join - not against log presence.
notify = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Transition(label="Notify_Requester"), SilentTransition()],
)
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")


# --- 1j. Assemble M1 ---------------------------------------------------------
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[intake, rmi_loop, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(intake, rmi_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(rmi_loop, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

### 7.3 How the revised architecture re-adjudicates the fifteen cases

| Case | Under M0 | Under M1 + overlays | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 701 | fits | COMPLIANT | standard route |
| 702 | deviant | fits M1; JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION | auto_route + PD-3.v1 in window, D-1 passes |
| 703 | deviant | fits M1 but **fails D-1, D-7, R-5** → NONCOMPLIANT | eligibility gate G-3 would have blocked it |
| 704 | deviant | fits M1; JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION | emergency_route + FD-7 in window, D-4/D-5/D-6/C7 pass |
| 705 | deviant | fits M1 but **fails D-3** → NONCOMPLIANT | window check; G-2 would have prevented it |
| 706 | deviant | fits M1; decidable via D-10 | immutable notification_id ends the indeterminacy |
| 707 | deviant | **COMPLIANT** | RMI loop; D-9 bounds abuse |
| 708 | deviant | **violates C1** → NONCOMPLIANT | the universal invariant, enforced in every route |
| 709 | deviant | **COMPLIANT** | PRESCREEN_REJECT variant, optional Quality_Review |
| 710 | deviant | **COMPLIANT** | REACTIVATION variant, skippable intake |
| 711 | deviant | disappears | D-12 dedup before checking |
| 712 | **fits** | **violates R-1** → NONCOMPLIANT | resolved person IDs; blocked preventively |
| 713 | deviant | **COMPLIANT** | D-11 label mapping |
| 714 | deviant | fits M1 but **fails D-2** → NONCOMPLIANT | expiry check; G-2 prevents |
| 715 | **fits** | **violates R-3** → NONCOMPLIANT | independence + disclosure check |

The decisive property: the two cases that M0 rated **clean** (712, 715) are now caught, and the six cases M0 rated **deviant for no good reason** (707, 709, 710, 711, 713, and 706's evidential gap) are resolved — while every genuinely severe case (703, 705, 708, 714) is caught **preventively rather than forensically**. That inversion — catching real risk and stopping the manufacture of false positives — is the measure of whether this revision was worth doing.
