## 1. Conformance semantics

The classification distinguishes between **objective norm violations**, **benign deviations due to model incompleteness or abstraction looseness**, **likely data-quality errors**, and **insufficient evidence**. The closed-world assumption applies *only* to relations explicitly marked `forbidden` and to `mandatory` relations *within their stated scope*; all other relations are open-world. This means:
> - A `forbidden` relation observed in the data is always a **violation** (regardless of context), because the norm explicitly disallows it and the closed-world assumption leaves no exception.
> - A `mandatory` relation missing within its stated scope *may* be conforming if:
>   - The norm’s wording allows for reasonable delay or intermediate steps (e.g., N1’s "eventually enter validation" vs. the "directly-follows" approximation), and the observed deviation aligns with documented business practice (e.g., unrecorded review events); or
>   - The abstraction mismatch (e.g., summarizing "eventually" as "directly-follows") makes the summary stricter than the norm. Absence of the summarized edge does *not* imply non-compliance with the norm’s actual scope ("eventually").
> - A `typical` relation’s absence or irregularity is **never a violation**; it is expected that exceptions occur (e.g., intermediate invoice/review events for N2).
> - A `unspecified` relation’s presence or absence is **outside norm coverage**; no classification as violation, typical, or unexpected—it is regulated by other policies (e.g., Customer relations, Risk review).
> - **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: Presence of a relation not in the norm (e.g., `object_codeath`, `object_interaction` with Customer/RiskCase) where the norm says `unspecified`. This is benign if consistent with business practice and not contradicted by other norms.
> - **Likely data-quality error**: An observed relation that contradicts a `forbidden` norm *only* when evidence suggests it results from a modeling artifact (e.g., reused identifier causing Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice link). Classification as "error" requires: (a) clear suspicion (e.g., reused ID pattern), (b) no contradictory business intent (e.g., no legitimate sensor-financial linkage), and (c) no explicit norm exception. It is *not* a violation if treated as an error; instead, it signals a data repair need.
> - **Abstraction mismatch**: When the OC-DFG summary uses "directly-follows" language (e.g., "Order should eventually enter validation approximated with directly-follows"), but the norm states "eventually". Missing the summarized edge (e.g., 2/100 Orders missing `Create Order  Validate Order`) may reflect a legitimate intermediate review step not captured in the summary. This is not a violation; it reflects summarization looseness.
> - **Insufficient evidence**: When observed data is inconclusive (e.g., a single `Record Payment` without an observed Invoice successor; no event attribute indicating "unapplied" status). Classification defaults to "typical exception possible" until evidence (e.g., event attributes, business documentation) clarifies intent.

Open-world assumptions dominate interpretation: we never infer missing mandatory behavior as intentional non-compliance; we only flag observed violations of `forbidden` norms or observed `mandatory` gaps *when the scope clearly applies and no reasonable intermediate/delay aligns with business context*. Closed-world assumptions are strictly limited to `forbidden` (always violation) and `mandatory` within scope (only violation if observed absence contradicts "eventually" + no plausible intermediate per context).

Assuming `object_codeath` implies violation would be wrong (norm unspecified), but `object_cobirth` directly uses "born in same event", so the edge count for cobirth must be checked against the forbidden rule. The summary’s OTG edge list includes `object_codeath`, which is safe to interpret as unregulated (since norm silent), but `object_cobirth` must be treated strictly due to "forbidden" status.

### ET–OT
- **What the model can establish**: Counts of event-object associations (e.g., Create Order  Order, Dispatch Package  Package/Delivery). It matches event types to object types.
- **What it cannot establish**: Whether a Sensor Heartbeat event was intentionally linked to an Invoice (e.g., via ID reuse) or accidentally linked due to data error; whether a Record Payment event without Invoice successor represents unapplied cash (typical) or a missed Invoice (violation). It sees weights but not event attributes (e.g., no `is_unapplied` flag in summary).
- **Counts and scope effect**: E1–E5, E7–E8 are mostly satisfied (e.g., Create OrderOrder 100/100, PackPackage 200/200, DispatchPackage/Delivery 198/200). E8’s "mandatory except explicitly typed unapplied cash" is critical: the summary notes "none are so typed" (no event labeled unapplied cash), but finance says "unapplied cash is possible". Thus, the single Record Payment without observed Invoice could be an untyped unapplied cash event. Since the norm lacks guidance on *how* to type it (e.g., attribute name), the observed absence of Invoice link is inconclusive—hence "potential violation due to incompleteness" (not automatic violation). E10 (Sensor HeartbeatInvoice) has weight 1 and is explicitly forbidden. The additional context suspects ID reuse ("reused numeric identifier"), making it a likely data-quality error rather than intentional compliance failure. E6 (DispatchDelivery) is satisfied (198/200), and the 2 lacking Delivery have carrier refs—consistent with Delivery confirmation possibly occurring alongside dispatch; norm does not forbid concurrent confirmation, so benign.
- **Abstraction mismatch danger**: E8’s exception ("Except events explicitly typed as unapplied cash") creates ambiguity when no typing exists. Interpreting the single Record Payment without Invoice as a violation would ignore the norm’s own exception mechanism and the observed business practice (unapplied cash possible). Thus, classification must reflect uncertainty ("potential violation due to incompleteness"), not a definite violation. Similarly, treating Sensor HeartbeatInvoice as a violation without checking ID reuse suspicion would over-policing; the norm says "must never be linked", but if the link is accidental (data error), the business intent (prevent meaningful sensor-financial linkage) is not breached.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

> *Rule: If an observation matches a relation type (e.g., Order: Cancel  Dispatch) and the norm marks that relation type as `forbidden`, it is a **forbidden violation** regardless of context (closed-world for forbidden). If it matches a `mandatory` relation within scope and the observed absence contradicts "eventually" + no plausible intermediate (per context), it is a **mandatory violation**; otherwise, it is **conforming** (scope applies, "eventually" allows delay). `typical` absence/irregularity is **always conforming**. `unspecified` presence/absence is **always unregulated/benign**. `likely data-quality error` is assigned only to forbidden relations observed via probable ID reuse/artifact; it is *not* a violation but a repair need. `abstraction mismatch` applies to mandatory relations summarized as "directly-follows" when context shows intermediates (e.g., review)."

- **Mandatory behavior (conforming or violation)**:
  - N1 (`Create Order  Validate Order`): Mostly observed (98/100); missing 2 due to possible review (context confirms unrecorded review). **Conforming** (N1 says "eventually"; abstraction mismatch acceptable). *Violation only if absence contradicts "eventually" with no intermediate—not the case here.*
  - N4 (`Pack Package  Dispatch Package`): 198/200 observed  **Conforming** (mandatory met).
  - E1–E5, E7–E8 (basic event-object links): Mostly observed (e.g., Create OrderOrder 100/100; DispatchPackage/Delivery 200/198); **Conforming**. E8’s single Record Payment without Invoice is *not* mandatory violation due to norm incompleteness (no typed exception); treat as uncertain.
  - *Mandatory violation*: None for "mandatory" relations *other than forbidden*; N1 missing 2 is not a mandatory violation because "eventually" allows delay and context supports review.

- **Forbidden behavior (violation or data-quality error)**:
  - N3 (`Cancel Order  Dispatch Package`): 2 observed  **Forbidden violation** (norm explicitly disallows; no intermediate can satisfy "no package may be dispatched after cancellation" if dispatch occurs post-cancel).
  - T6 (`Order, object_cobirth, Payment`): 1 observed  **Forbidden violation** (norm "cannot be born in same business event"; edge directly implies co-birth; ID reuse unlikely for cobirth as it’s a relation type, not ID collision).
  - E10 (`Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice`): 1 observed  **Likely data-quality error** (norm forbids meaningful link; suspicion of reused ID makes it accidental; *not* a violation until confirmed; classification as "error" avoids over-policing). *Note: Only Sensor HeartbeatInvoice is classified as error; N3/T6 are violations because no evidence suggests ID reuse or artifact for them.*
  - *When is a forbidden observation a data-quality error? Only if clear suspicion of identifier reuse/graph artifact (e.g., Sensor Heartbeat ID reused as Invoice ID) exists AND no business intent to link sensor to finance. For N3/T6, the edge types (CancelDispatch, cobirth) reflect business logic actions/births, not ID reuse; thus violations, not errors.*

- **Typical behavior (always conforming)**:
  - N2 (`Validate Order  Close Order`): 83/100 observed; 17 missing due to invoice/review intermediates. **Conforming** (N2 says "Intermediate invoice, review, or fulfilment events may occur"; absence of direct ValidateClose is expected).
  - N5 (`Invoice: Issue Invoice  Record Payment`): 92/110 observed; 4 write-offs, 18 no Record (including 4 write-offs + 14 no successor). **Conforming** (N5 allows credit notes/write-offs/open terms).
  - Manual Compliance Review links (RiskCase and Customer): 3 events observed; **Conforming** (N4/T4/T5 are `unspecified`; norm does not govern Customer relations here).
  - Invoice no successor (14 Invoices): **Conforming** (N5 typical allows open invoices).
  - Dispatch without Delivery relation (2 Packages): **Conforming** (Delivery may be confirmed concurrently; norm silent on codeath; T2 requires Delivery for Dispatch, which is met via carrier ref; no direct follow requirement).
  - Reopen Order  Close Order (1 observed): **Conforming** (N6 is `unspecified`; norm does not cover reopening).

- **Unregulated/unspecified behavior (always benign/unspecified)**:
  - T2 (`Package, object_interaction, Delivery`): Delivery edge present (200/200 Dispatch), plus 7 `object_codeath` edges. **Unregulated**: Norm says nothing about `object_codeath`; concurrent Package–Delivery end is likely normal (carrier confirms with package).
  - T4 (`Order, object_interaction, Customer`): 100 observed; **Unspecified** (covered by other policy).
  - T5 (`Order, object_interaction, RiskCase`): 3 observed; **Unspecified** (allowed per norm).
  - `object_codeath` edges (7): **Unregulated** (norm silent; consistent with concurrent lifecycle end).
  - Manual Compliance Review  Customer (3): **Unspecified** (covered elsewhere).
  - Reopen  Close (N6): `unspecified` by norm; behavior governed by other policy.
