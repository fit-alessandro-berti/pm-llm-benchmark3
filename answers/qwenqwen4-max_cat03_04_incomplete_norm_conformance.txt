## 1. Conformance semantics

I separate seven deviation classes, because they carry different remediation owners and different evidential burdens.

- **Forbidden behavior (FB):** an observed relation matches an explicitly `forbidden` norm row within its stated scope (N3, T6, E10). Closed-world applies here: absence of permission is irrelevant, presence of the pattern is decisive *if the observation is real and in scope*.
- **Missing mandatory behavior (MM):** a `mandatory` relation is absent for at least one in-scope object/event (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Closed-world applies only *within the stated scope*; scope must be verified before a gap becomes a finding.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior (UU):** a relation exists that the norm neither requires nor prohibits (ValidateIssue Invoice, ReopenClose, MCRCustomer, Package–Delivery codeath). Open-world: **not a violation**, at most a norm-coverage gap.
- **Typical-pattern absence (TA):** a `typical` relation is missing (N2, N5, T3). Legitimate exceptions are pre-authorized by the norm text; absence is a *review signal*, not noncompliance.
- **Likely data-quality error (DQ):** the relation is implausible as business behavior and has a stated artifact mechanism (identifier-reuse join for Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice; carrier reference present in an attribute but no Delivery object link).
- **Abstraction mismatch (AM):** the norm's semantics ("eventually") are stronger/looser than the summary's semantics (directly-follows, aggregated edge weights). N1 explicitly warns of this.
- **Insufficient evidence (IE):** the summary cannot discriminate between the above (14 invoices with no successor could be open credit terms, right-censoring, or genuine collection failure).

Open-world reasoning governs everything except explicitly forbidden rows and in-scope mandatory rows. Consequently, rarity alone never creates a violation, and a raw conformance score is a *graph-theoretic* statement, not a business verdict.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**Can establish:** which activity transitions were observed per object type, and their frequencies; existence of a forbidden transition pattern (N3) and existence of paths the norm never mentions.

**Cannot establish:** eventual reachability, temporal distance, concurrency, object identity across edges, or whether an absent successor means "never" versus "not yet". A directly-follows edge is a *projection*, and interleaved events on the same object destroy it.

**Counts and scope:** 98/100 for N1 is a directly-follows rate, while N1 says "eventually enter validation" and explicitly admits the summary is an approximation. With 3 Orders going CreateManual Compliance Review, an unrecorded or interleaved review can break direct adjacency while validation still occurs later. Therefore N1's 2-order gap is **AM/IE first, MM only if reachability fails**. N4's 198/200 is scoped to *externally shipped* packages; if the scope flag is derived from the presence of a dispatch, the denominator is circular and the gap may be an artifact. N5's 18 non-payment invoices decompose into 4 authorized write-offs and 14 unknown; only the 14 warrant review, and censoring must be excluded first.

**Explicit danger:** re-reading "eventually" as "directly follows" converts legitimate multi-step traces into false noncompliance, inflates violation counts, and pushes reviewers toward the wrong remediation (forcing a procedural shortcut) instead of fixing the model. N1 must be evaluated with reachability/eventually semantics; the DF count is only a cheap upper bound on suspicion.

**Cross-abstraction tension:** CancelDispatch is benign-looking as a mere edge but is *forbidden* by N3 — here the abstraction is adequate to raise the alarm, though not to prove the shipment belonged to the cancelled order.

### OTG

**Can establish:** that a typed object-to-object relationship exists somewhere in the log, with a weight; existence of forbidden structural patterns (T6 cobirth, weight 1).

**Cannot establish:** *which event* created the relationship, per-event completeness, cardinality constraints, or temporal ordering. OTG weights aggregate over event types.

**Counts and scope:** Package–Delivery interaction weight 200 looks like full T2 satisfaction, yet ET–OT shows only 198 Delivery links at Dispatch Package. Both can be true: two Delivery links may be attached at other event types. This is the clearest demonstration that **a relation can be conforming at the OTG abstraction and deficient at the ET–OT abstraction**. T1 (1450) and T3 (92) are consistent with their ET–OT counterparts; T4/T5 and the codeath edge are open-world and must not be scored as violations. Codeath in particular is a *derived* lifecycle relation whose semantics depend on timestamp granularity — seven instances is a modeling curiosity, not misconduct.

**T6** is closed-world: one cobirth edge is enough. Its plausible link to the single invoice-less Record Payment (rank 9) is a hypothesis to test by event ID, not a conclusion.

### ET–OT

**Can establish:** per-event-type object participation — the finest-grained mandatory check available here (E1–E8), and the exact locus of the E10 forbidden link.

**Cannot establish:** whether a missing link reflects absent business reality or absent extraction; whether an event is correctly typed (critical for E8's exception); or intra-event ordering.

**Counts and scope:** the arithmetic gaps are precise and small: E2 1448 vs 1450 (2), E6 198 vs 200 (2), E8 92 vs 93 (1). Each has a stated non-behavioral candidate explanation — carrier attribute for E6, unlabeled unapplied cash for E8. For E8 the norm's exception is *type-conditional* and no event is so typed, so strictly the gap is MM; but the correct verdict is "MM under current typing, reclassify to legitimate exception if finance confirms and the type is populated." That is a norm-instrumentation defect as much as a process defect. E10 is forbidden regardless of frequency; yet because the stated cause is identifier reuse, the *assertion* is a forbidden-relation instance while the *behavior* is almost certainly not — it must be reported as a data-integrity incident, not a compliance breach by a business actor.

Note also that three separate "2"s occur (N1 orders, N4 packages, E6 dispatches, plus E2 items). Nothing in the summary licenses treating them as the same objects; object-level joins are required before any combined root-cause narrative.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 abstraction is undefined** ("eventually" vs directly-follows). *Risk:* systematic false positives (2/100 here) and pressure to remove legitimate review steps. *Clarification:* define N1 as reachability within the order lifecycle, with an explicit tolerance for interleaved events. **Mandatory** (semantics: eventually).
2. **N4 scope predicate "intended for external shipment" is not operationalized.** *Risk:* circular denominators; unverifiable 2-package gap. *Clarification:* derive scope from an order/shipment-type attribute independent of the dispatch event. **Mandatory** scope definition.
3. **E8's exception depends on an event label that is never populated.** *Risk:* every legitimate unapplied-cash payment is scored as a violation; staff lose trust in monitoring. *Clarification:* require an explicit `unapplied_cash` type or an application-status attribute at payment creation. **Mandatory** data requirement, with the exception itself **typical**.
4. **No right-censoring rule for open lifecycles.** *Risk:* the 14 successor-less invoices and in-flight packages are misread as failures. *Clarification:* exclude objects whose observation window is shorter than the normative completion horizon. **Mandatory** evaluation rule.
5. **Codeath and other derived lifecycle relations are unaddressed.** *Risk:* ad-hoc violation calls on 7 benign Package–Delivery codeaths; inconsistent tooling behavior. *Clarification:* declare codeath out of scope, or define permitted confirmation-event semantics. **Explicitly unspecified** (recommended), pending evidence of harm.
6. **Identifier namespaces and join provenance are ungoverned.** *Risk:* the E10 artifact recurs and can fabricate forbidden financial links; conversely real violations get dismissed as "DQ". *Clarification:* require globally qualified object IDs and prohibit cross-namespace joins into financial objects. **Forbidden** join practice; **mandatory** ID qualification.
7. **N3 lacks a temporal/identity definition and an override policy.** *Risk:* disputes about whether a backdated cancellation or a re-created order counts; no defined emergency-release path. *Clarification:* "no Dispatch event whose timestamp exceeds the effective cancellation timestamp on any Package linked to that Order," with a logged, approved exception mechanism. **Forbidden**, with a **mandatory** override record.
8. **No frequency/tolerance policy and no severity mapping.** *Risk:* rarity-driven escalation of the 3 risk reviews or 1 reopen. *Clarification:* state that unspecified/typical patterns are never escalated on frequency alone; define severity by harm, not count. **Mandatory** evaluation policy.
