# 1. Objectives and constraints

A sustainable resource-assignment policy for a knowledge-intensive service process cannot be reduced to a single mathematical optimum. Short-term throughput, zero-defect quality, worker well-being, case continuity, and long-term skill building actively pull against one another:

* Assigning a trainee slows immediate service time (historically +3.3h median for weak-skill ownership) but reduces future specialist bottlenecks.
* Preserving ownership avoids handover delays (+2.7h median on high-complexity cases) and rework spikes (1.8x rework and 1.4x appeals after >2 reassignments), yet keeping an under-skilled or burned-out owner can harm both SLA and quality.
* Shielding high-overtime workers prevents attrition and errors, even when those workers happen to hold scarce skills.

Accordingly, the policy treats assignment as a **governed multi-objective trade-off**, separating non-negotiable safety/compliance gates from tunable operational preferences.

## Optimization objectives

1. **SLA adherence and timely throughput:** Complete urgent and high-priority cases within deadline without starving normal/low-priority work.
2. **Quality and rework prevention:** Match task complexity and risk to sufficient skill (or explicit supervision) to prevent loops, rework, and customer appeals.
3. **Continuity of case ownership:** Minimize disruptive handovers, particularly on high-complexity cases and cases approaching or exceeding two reassignments.
4. **Workload fairness and overtime protection:** Distribute work equitably across available shift capacity and protect workers already exposed to high overtime risk.
5. **Structured skill development:** Allocate a protected share of lower-risk cases to documented training priorities with appropriate supervision or shadowing.
6. **Organizational resilience and bottleneck avoidance:** Preserve scarce specialist capacity (especially Security and Approval) for work that truly requires it, while cross-training adjacent staff and batching eligible routine checks.

## Hard constraints (safety, compliance, and capacity gates)

These rules act as strict filters before scoring; no preference score may override them:

| ID | Constraint | Category |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Prohibited skill (`skill == 0`):** A resource with score `0` may never execute the activity (they may only *shadow/observe* a qualified executor as a non-executing learner). | Compliance / Safety |
| **H2** | **Batch assignment cap (Rule 1):** No resource may receive more than **4** new assignments in a single decision batch (counting primary, supervisory, and structured co-working roles). | Safety / Fatigue |
| **H3** | **High/urgent priority skill floor (Rule 2):** No `urgent` or `high` priority case may be assigned to `skill < 2` unless under explicit supervision by a qualified specialist. | Quality / SLA Safety |
| **H4** | **Security Review authorization (Rule 6):** Must be executed by `skill == 3` (`R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`), or by `skill in {1, 2}` **only** under explicit supervision by `R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`. `skill == 0` cannot execute even under supervision. | Compliance / Security |
| **H5** | **Final Approval authority (Rule 7):** Must be finalized by `skill in {2, 3}`. `skill == 1` may prepare the dossier but cannot finalize. | Compliance / Governance |
| **H6** | **Quality Check batching gate (Rule 8):** Quality Check cases may be batched only when `priority in {normal, low}` and `SLA_remaining > 24h`. | SLA Safety |
| **H7** | **Shift capacity feasibility:** A resource must have enough remaining shift hours to absorb urgent/same-day commitments without immediate infeasibility. | Operational Feasibility |

## Soft constraints and preference trade-offs

These rules shape the scoring weights and require documented rationale whenever bent:

| ID | Preference / Governance Rule | Trade-off Logic |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **Overtime protection (Rule 4):** Avoid assigning new work to `overtime_risk == "high"` resources unless SLA risk is `urgent` and no safer compliant alternative exists. | Treated as a near-hard gate; overrides continuity and specialist preference on all non-urgent work. |
| **S2** | **Continuity on high-complexity/high-handover cases (Rule 5):** Keep current owner unless owner has `skill in {0, 1}` and supervision is infeasible (e.g., SLA too tight for +3.3h learning overhead). | Balances the +2.7h handover delay and >2-reassignment rework risk against the +3.3h weak-skill penalty. |
| **S3** | **Training quota (Rule 3):** At least **20%** of `normal`/`low` priority assignments should support documented `training_priority`, provided SLA risk remains acceptable. | Trades short-term speed for long-term resilience; triggers structured coaching or compliant shadowing. |
| **S4** | **Specialist conservation:** Avoid spending scarce `skill == 3` capacity (notably Security and Approval) on routine work or activities where `skill == 2` resources with lower load are available. | Prevents queue buildup at single points of failure (e.g., `R6_Farid` in Approval). |
| **S5** | **Socio-technical explainability & worker agency:** Every assignment must emit a plain-language `assignment_reason` and permit human override with logged justification. | Prevents algorithmic opacity and captures tacit context (e.g., sudden customer call, fatigue). |

# 2. Assignment policy

The proposed **Explainable Multi-Criteria Dynamic Assignment (EMCDA)** policy operates in batches (or event-driven micro-batches). Rather than using an opaque black-box model, it computes an additive, auditable score over six transparent components whose weights shift systematically by case urgency.

## Policy pseudocode

```python
FUNCTION assign_queue_batch(cases, resources, config):
    # 1. Queue ordering: urgent/high SLA risk first, then complex/handover-sensitive,
    #    then normal/low cases (enabling training and batching with remaining capacity).
    ordered_cases = SORT(cases, key=(
        priority_rank(c.priority),          # urgent=0, high=1, normal=2, low=3
        c.sla_remaining_hours,              # ascending
        -c.complexity                       # descending
    ))

    # Track batch state dynamically so each decision reflects prior allocations
    batch_state = INIT_STATE(resources)     # new_count=0, committed_hours=0
    normal_low_total = COUNT(c for c in cases if c.priority in {"normal", "low"})
    training_target = CEIL(0.20 * normal_low_total)
    training_assigned = 0
    decisions = []

    # Pre-identify eligible Quality Check batch candidates (Rule H6)
    qc_batch_pool = [c for c in ordered_cases
                     if c.activity == "Quality Check"
                     and c.priority in {"normal", "low"}
                     and c.sla_remaining_hours > 24]

    FOR case IN ordered_cases:
        # 2. Candidate generation & HARD eligibility filtering
        candidates = []   # Each candidate is (executor, supervisor_or_shadow, mode)

        FOR r IN resources:
            IF batch_state[r].new_count >= 4: CONTINUE                # H2
            skill = r.skill[case.required_skill]
            IF skill == 0: CONTINUE                                   # H1

            # --- Solo execution eligibility ---
            solo_allowed = TRUE
            IF case.priority in {"urgent", "high"} AND skill < 2:
                solo_allowed = FALSE                                  # H3
            IF case.activity == "Security Review" AND skill < 3:
                solo_allowed = FALSE                                  # H4
            IF case.activity == "Final Approval" AND skill < 2:
                solo_allowed = FALSE                                  # H5
            IF NOT has_feasible_hours(r, case, batch_state):
                solo_allowed = FALSE                                  # H7

            IF solo_allowed:
                candidates.APPEND((executor=r, partner=NONE, mode="solo"))

                # Compliant non-executing shadowing for skill==0 documented trainees
                # (e.g., Security training when trainee still holds skill 0)
                IF case.priority in {"normal", "low"} AND skill == 3:
                    FOR learner IN resources:
                        IF (learner != r
                            AND batch_state[learner].new_count < 4
                            AND learner.overtime_risk != "high"
                            AND learner.training_priority == case.required_skill
                            AND learner.skill[case.required_skill] == 0):
                            candidates.APPEND((executor=r, partner=learner, mode="shadowing"))

            # --- Supervised execution / dual-role eligibility ---
            # (a) Trainee execution with supervisor (H3, H4, or coaching on normal/low)
            IF skill in {1, 2}:
                FOR sup IN eligible_supervisors(case, resources, batch_state):
                    IF sup == r: CONTINUE
                    IF case.activity == "Final Approval" AND skill == 1:
                        # H5: skill 1 prepares, skill 2-3 finalizes
                        candidates.APPEND((executor=r, partner=sup, mode="prepare_and_finalize"))
                    ELIF case.activity == "Security Review":
                        # H4: supervisor must be R7_Gita or R8_Hugo
                        IF sup.id in {"R7_Gita", "R8_Hugo"}:
                            candidates.APPEND((executor=r, partner=sup, mode="supervised"))
                    ELSE:
                        # Coaching/supervision for trainee on complex or high-priority work
                        IF sup.skill[case.required_skill] >= 2 AND skill == 1:
                            candidates.APPEND((executor=r, partner=sup, mode="supervised"))

        # Soft overtime filter (S1): exclude high-OT candidates unless urgent & no safer option
        safe_candidates = [cand for cand in candidates
                           if cand.executor.overtime_risk != "high"
                           and (cand.partner is NONE or cand.partner.overtime_risk != "high")]

        IF safe_candidates IS NOT EMPTY:
            pool = safe_candidates
        ELIF case.priority == "urgent" AND candidates IS NOT EMPTY:
            pool = candidates
            FLAG_EXCEPTION(case, "HIGH_OT_URGENT_FALLBACK_REQUIRES_REBALANCE")
        ELSE:
            # Exception handling: escalate to team lead for deferral or cross-shift rebalance
            decisions.APPEND(ESCALATE_TO_LEAD(case, reason="No compliant non-overtime candidate"))
            CONTINUE

        # 3. Context-sensitive weights based on SLA urgency
        urgency_score = compute_sla_urgency(case)   # in [0, 1]
        w = dynamic_weights(urgency_score)
        # Higher urgency -> higher weight on skill & SLA speed, lower weight on training.
        # Lower urgency  -> higher weight on fairness, training, batching, resilience.

        # 4. Score every feasible candidate across 6 transparent dimensions
        scored = []
        FOR cand IN pool:
            s_sla      = score_sla_fit(case, cand, batch_state)
            s_skill    = score_skill_match(case, cand)
            s_cont     = score_continuity_handover(case, cand)
            s_fair     = score_workload_fairness(cand, batch_state)
            s_train    = score_training_value(case, cand, training_assigned, training_target)
            s_resil    = score_resilience_bottleneck(case, cand, qc_batch_pool, batch_state)

            total = (w.sla * s_sla + w.skill * s_skill + w.cont * s_cont
                     + w.fair * s_fair + w.train * s_train + w.resil * s_resil)

            reason = generate_explainable_reason(case, cand, s_sla, s_skill, s_cont,
                                                 s_fair, s_train, s_resil)
            scored.APPEND((total, cand, reason))

        # 5. Select winner with deterministic, fair tie-breaking
        best = ARGMAX(scored, tie_breaker=(
            lambda x: -batch_state[x.cand.executor].new_count,   # 1. fewest new assignments
            lambda x: int(x.cand.executor == case.current_owner),# 2. preserve continuity
            lambda x: -x.cand.executor.open_cases,               # 3. lowest existing load
            lambda x: -overtime_rank(x.cand.executor),           # 4. lowest OT risk
            lambda x: x.cand.executor.hours_remaining            # 5. most shift hours left
        ))

        # 6. Commit assignment & update state
        UPDATE_STATE(batch_state, best.cand, case)
        IF supports_documented_training(case, best.cand):
            training_assigned += 1
        IF is_high_handover_switch(case, best.cand):
            ATTACH_PROTOCOL(best, "MANDATORY_15MIN_WARM_HANDOVER")

        decisions.APPEND(best)

    RETURN decisions
```

## Scoring component definitions

1. **SLA urgency (`compute_sla_urgency` & `score_sla_fit`):**
   * Urgency combines priority tier and remaining time buffer relative to expected service duration (`expected_hours = base_duration(activity, complexity) + weak_skill_penalty(3.3h if unsupervised skill 1) + handover_wait(2.7h if high-complexity switch)`).
   * Candidates whose expected completion time risks breaching `sla_remaining_hours` receive a steep negative score.
2. **Skill-match (`score_skill_match`):**
   * Rewards alignment between required complexity and skill level rather than blindly maximizing skill.
   * For `complexity >= 0.70` or `urgent`: `skill 3 = +1.0`, `skill 2 = +0.5` (if continuity holds), `supervised skill 1 = +0.1` (only if SLA allows).
   * For routine cases (`complexity < 0.40`, `normal/low`): `skill 2 = +0.9`, `supervised/coached skill 1 = +0.8`, `skill 3 = +0.5` (mild over-qualification discount to curb specialist waste).
3. **Continuity and handover-cost (`score_continuity_handover`):**
   * Keeping `current_owner` earns a bonus scaled by `(handover_cost_band, complexity, reassignment_count)`.
   * Switching owner when `reassignment_count >= 2` incurs a heavy penalty (reflecting the empirical 1.8x rework / 1.4x appeal jump).
   * Switching on `complexity >= 0.70` and `handover_cost == "high"` incurs an additional delay penalty unless the owner has `skill <= 1` and SLA precludes supervised coaching (Rule S2).
4. **Workload and overtime fairness (`score_workload_fairness`):**
   * Penalizes high `projected_open_cases = open_cases + new_count` and low `remaining_hours_per_open_case`.
   * Applies a strong soft penalty to `overtime_risk == "medium"` once projected open cases exceed team median, and filters out `overtime_risk == "high"` via S1 unless urgent fallback is triggered.
5. **Training and development (`score_training_value`):**
   * Active only when SLA buffer is healthy (`normal`/`low` priority, or safe supervised co-handling).
   * Highest bonus when `learner.training_priority == case.required_skill` and `training_assigned < training_target` (Rule S3).
   * Secondary bonus for building cross-skill redundancy in known bottleneck activities (Approval, Security), even when not the worker's primary listed training goal.
6. **Resilience and bottleneck avoidance (`score_resilience_bottleneck`):**
   * Computes activity scarcity (`1 / count_of_available_specialists`). Because Approval has only **1** specialist (`R6_Farid`) and Security has only **2** (`R7_Gita`, `R8_Hugo`), using them on non-bottleneck or routine tasks incurs a scarcity penalty.
   * Awards a batching bonus when assigning multiple `qc_batch_pool` cases (e.g., `C006` and `C012`) to the same low-load Quality resource (Rule H6).
7. **Exception handling and human governance:**
   * If an urgent case forces a high-OT fallback, the system automatically flags a manager to offload 1–2 low-priority cases from that worker.
   * Any human override is allowed but requires selecting a reason tag (`customer_context`, `unplanned_absence`, `fatigue_protection`, `coaching_request`) written into `assignment_reason` for auditing.

### Key trade-off takeaway

There is no free lunch across these policies:
* **First-available** wins if the sole goal is immediate pick-up latency in a homogeneous, low-risk process—conditions that **do not hold** here due to compliance rules and asymmetric handover/rework penalties.
* **Specialist-only** wins if the horizon is a single isolated hour with no fatigue, no absences, and unlimited specialist capacity—conditions that break down within a single shift in Approval and Security.
* **EMCDA** consciously gives up minor short-term speed on `C003`, `C009`, and `C015` and accepts deliberate handovers away from high-OT workers (`C003`, `C006`, `C010`, `C014`) in exchange for compliance, lower multi-shift rework, worker sustainability, and steadily shrinking bottlenecks.

---

### How work is assigned on our team

Our goal with case assignment is simple: **keep promises to customers while keeping workloads fair, protecting people from burnout, and helping everyone grow into new skills.**

Because no single rule works for every situation, our assignment tool balances six practical factors every time new work comes in:

1. **Protecting your workload and overtime:** If you already carry a heavy caseload or are flagged at high overtime risk, the system actively shields you from new work—even if you are the current owner or a specialist—unless there is a true urgent emergency with no safer backup.
2. **Respecting safety and compliance rules:** Some activities (like Security Reviews and Final Approvals) have strict sign-off rules. The system will never put you in an uncomfortable position by assigning high-stakes work solo before you are certified for it.
3. **Avoiding frustrating handovers:** We know how much time is lost when a complex case bounces between people. If you already own a complex case and have the skills to finish it, we keep it with you so you do not lose momentum. When a handover *is* needed (for example, to protect someone's overtime or bring in a specialist on an urgent deadline), we build in time for a brief warm handover rather than dropping the case cold.
4. **Making real space for learning:** At least 1 in 5 normal- or low-priority cases is set aside to support your stated training goals—paired with a mentor, a light checkpoint review, or structured shadowing—so growth does not only happen "when things are quiet."
5. **Preventing bottlenecks:** Rather than sending every routine case to the same one or two specialists, we use batching for routine Quality Checks and dual-prep workflows so specialists stay available for genuinely urgent problems.
6. **Transparency, not surveillance:** Assignments are **not** a performance ranking. Not receiving a case may mean the system is protecting your shift hours; receiving a complex case with a co-worker means we are pairing complementary strengths to solve a tricky issue together.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The Python sketch below demonstrates how to ingest the process event log with PM4Py, compute process-mining audit indicators (organizational networks, roles, similarity, batches, service/waiting times, performance DFGs, and rework variants), and feed empirical evidence back into policy calibration.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ==============================================================================
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ==============================================================================
def load_and_format_log(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    df["sla_deadline"] = pd.to_datetime(df["sla_deadline"], utc=True)

    # Standardize columns for PM4Py
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    return log


# ==============================================================================
# 2. Organizational mining: handovers, collaboration, similarity, roles, batches
# ==============================================================================
def audit_organizational_structure(log: pd.DataFrame, out_prefix: str = "audit"):
    # Handover-of-work network (detects excessive bouncing & handover hubs)
    hw_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, beta=0)
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(hw_network, f"{out_prefix}_handover_network.png")
    # For interactive notebooks: pm4py.view_sna(hw_network)

    # Working-together network (verifies mentoring/supervision pairs & team silos)
    wt_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(wt_network, f"{out_prefix}_working_together.png")

    # Activity-based resource similarity (identifies cross-training candidates
    # and measures whether skill silos are narrowing over time)
    sim_network = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)

    # Organizational roles (flags narrow specialist bottlenecks, e.g., Approval/Security)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)

    # Batch discovery (verifies Quality Check batching per Rule H6 and checks for
    # unintended batching on urgent/high-priority work)
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)

    return {
        "handover_network": hw_network,
        "working_together_network": wt_network,
        "resource_similarity": sim_network,
        "organizational_roles": roles,
        "detected_batches": batches,
    }


# ==============================================================================
# 3. Performance, SLA, bottleneck, and rework variant auditing
# ==============================================================================
def audit_performance_and_bottlenecks(log: pd.DataFrame):
    # Insert case-level service and waiting times (adds @@case_service_time, @@case_waiting_time)
    enriched_log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log.copy())

    # Activity-level service times (used to verify +3.3h weak-skill hypothesis)
    avg_service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(log)

    # Performance DFG (median/mean transition times between activities; spots
    # waiting queues before Security Review and Final Approval)
    perf_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

    # Filter urgent/high-priority cases for focused SLA & handover inspection
    urgent_high_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        enriched_log,
        attribute_key="case_priority",
        values=["urgent", "high"],
        level="case",
        retain=True,
    )

    # Filter slow cases (e.g., total throughput time > 24 hours) to inspect causes
    slow_cases_log = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
        enriched_log,
        min_performance=24 * 3600,
        max_performance=365 * 24 * 3600,
    )

    # Discover process variants to quantify rework/diagnosis loops
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(enriched_log)
    rework_variants = {
        v: count
        for v, count in variants.items()
        if ("Rework Correction" in v) or (v.count("Technical Diagnosis") > 1)
    }

    return {
        "enriched_log": enriched_log,
        "avg_service_times": avg_service_times,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "urgent_high_log": urgent_high_log,
        "slow_cases_count": slow_cases_log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "rework_variant_share": sum(rework_variants.values()) / max(sum(variants.values()), 1),
    }


# ==============================================================================
# 4. Policy KPI audit & empirical calibration of EMCDA scoring weights
# ==============================================================================
def compute_policy_kpis_and_calibrate(enriched_log: pd.DataFrame, skill_matrix: dict):
    # Case-level summary
    case_end = enriched_log.sort_values("time:timestamp").groupby("case:concept:name").last()
    sla_miss_rate = (case_end["time:timestamp"] > case_end["sla_deadline"]).mean()
    high_reassignment_rate = (case_end["reassignment_count"] > 2).mean()

    # Workload fairness (Gini coefficient of assigned events per resource)
    res_counts = enriched_log["org:resource"].value_counts().values
    sorted_c = np.sort(res_counts)
    n = len(sorted_c)
    gini = (2 * np.sum(np.arange(1, n + 1) * sorted_c) / (n * np.sum(sorted_c))) - (n + 1) / n

    # Training share on normal/low cases & policy deviation rate
    normal_low = enriched_log[enriched_log["case_priority"].isin(["normal", "low"])]
    training_share = normal_low["assignment_reason"].str.contains("TRAINING|SHADOW", na=False).mean()
    override_rate = enriched_log["assignment_reason"].str.contains("OVERRIDE", na=False).mean()

    # Empirical re-estimation of handover delay on high-complexity cases
    # (Used by governance committee to recalibrate continuity penalty, not blind auto-tuning)
    high_comp = enriched_log[enriched_log["case_complexity"] >= 0.70]
    switched = high_comp[high_comp["org:resource"] != high_comp["current_owner"]]
    kept = high_comp[high_comp["org:resource"] == high_comp["current_owner"]]

    empirical_handover_wait_hrs = (
        (switched["@@case_waiting_time"].median() - kept["@@case_waiting_time"].median()) / 3600.0
    )

    # Check Quality Check batch compliance (Rule H6): ensure no urgent/high QC batches
    qc_urgent_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        enriched_log, "concept:name", ["Quality Check"], level="event", retain=True
    )
    qc_urgent_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        qc_urgent_log, "case_priority", ["urgent", "high"], level="event", retain=True
    )
    illegal_qc_batches = pm4py.discover_batches(qc_urgent_log)

    return {
        "sla_miss_rate": round(float(sla_miss_rate), 4),
        "cases_over_2_reassignments": round(float(high_reassignment_rate), 4),
        "workload_gini": round(float(gini), 4),
        "normal_low_training_share": round(float(training_share), 4),
        "policy_override_rate": round(float(override_rate), 4),
        "empirical_high_comp_handover_wait_hrs": round(float(empirical_handover_wait_hrs), 2),
        "illegal_urgent_qc_batches_detected": len(illegal_qc_batches),
    }


# Example governance pipeline execution
if __name__ == "__main__":
    log = load_and_format_log("service_process_events.csv")
    org_audit = audit_organizational_structure(log)
    perf_audit = audit_performance_and_bottlenecks(log)
    kpi_report = compute_policy_kpis_and_calibrate(
        perf_audit["enriched_log"], skill_matrix={}
    )
    print("Monthly EMCDA Audit Report:", kpi_report)
```

### Closing the socio-technical feedback loop

Rather than allowing the code above to silently auto-mutate assignment weights, the PM4Py outputs feed a **monthly joint review** involving operations leads, risk/quality owners, and worker representatives:
* `discover_handover_of_work_network` and `discover_performance_dfg` visually pinpoint whether handover delays or Security/Approval queues are growing.
* `discover_organizational_roles` and `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` verify whether cross-training (`C003`, `C009`, `C010`, `C015`) is genuinely expanding role overlap and reducing single-specialist fragility over time.
* Comparing outcomes on `OVERRIDE` events against policy-followed events surfaces tacit operational rules that should be formalized into the next version of EMCDA.
