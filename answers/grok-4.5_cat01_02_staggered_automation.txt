## 1. What is observed

Automation availability is a branch-month binary flag that turns on in April 2026 for Alpha and Beta and in July 2026 for Gamma; Delta and Epsilon remain unavailable through August. Availability is not the same as use: `automated_fast_path_pct` rises only gradually after go-live (roughly mid-20s to high-40s for early adopters), and non-zero baseline values appear even pre-rollout, so intensity is a continuous, partial, and learning-dependent exposure. Selection into rollout was managerial and performance-targeted: Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and historically weak cycle times; Gamma followed after a readiness pilot; no branch was randomized. Several large operational changes coincide with rollout—centralized fraud-desk launch in March (raising escalation everywhere), a routing campaign that steered roughly 10% more arrivals to Alpha/Beta (and later ~5% to Gamma), FTE losses at Alpha and Gamma and a gain at Beta, and a May product change that lifted `high_complexity_pct` in multiple branches. Outcomes are branch-month aggregates: medians and p90s of cycle time, rework and escalation percentages, and CSAT means from surveys with 20–45% response rates but no branch-month response counts, so nonresponse composition is unknown. Managers could also alter local work practices during rollout. These facts describe co-movement of availability, intensity, selection, confounders, and coarse measurement; they do not by themselves establish what automation caused.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: staggered event-study / Sun-Abraham-style cohort DiD | Relative month to first availability; cohort = Apr vs Jul adopters | Not-yet-treated + never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) in each calendar month | high_complexity_pct, fte, log arrivals, month FE; report raw and adjusted | E1, E3, E4, E7 (ITT availability) | Conditional parallel trends in outcomes absent adoption; no anticipatory changes; limited interference; effect heterogeneity handled by cohort-time weights | Pre-trend event-study coefficients; balance on mix/staffing; leave-one-cohort-out | Targeted selection + common May shock + fraud-desk spillovers break parallel trends |
| Alt A: treatment-intensity panel (dose) with branch FE | continuous automated_fast_path_pct (and lags) | Within-branch over time; never-treated as pure controls | same + automation flag | E2, dose on median/p90/rework | Conditional ignorability of intensity given FE and covariates; stable version of treatment | Lag structure; coefficient stability dropping May–Jun; Hausman-style FE vs FD | Intensity chosen in response to queues/mix (simultaneity) |

| Alt C: interrupted time series per branch with control series | Segmented level/slope at go-live | Own pre-trend + parallel control ITS | complexity, FTE, arrivals | Local ATT on median/p90 | Stable pre-trend; discontinuity attributable to automation not concurrent shocks | Pre-trend fit; control series break tests in May | March fraud desk and May product change create multiple breaks |

Primary strategy preference. A cohort-aware staggered event-study is the best primary design because adoption is explicitly staggered (April vs July), never-treated branches exist, and leadership’s policy question is about making the system available at a branch—not about a hypothetical full-intensity mandate. Unlike naïve TWFE, cohort-time or Sun–Abraham / Callaway–Sant’Anna-type aggregations avoid contaminating early×late comparisons when effects vary over time or cohort. The design can display pre-trends month-by-month, separate median from p90, and be reported both raw and covariate-adjusted for complexity, FTE, and arrivals. Intensity models and SCM remain essential complements because actual use ramps slowly and donor pools are tiny; they do not replace the ITT availability contrast that matches the expansion decision. With only three treated clusters, all strategies are fragile: the event-study’s value is disciplined description and transparent pre-trend failure, not precise ATT standard errors.

**1. Adopter-specific pre/post windows (transparent definitions)**  
- Early adopters (Alpha, Beta): pre = mean Jan–Mar; post_early = mean Apr–May; post_later = mean Jul–Aug (after intensity ramp).  
- Gamma: pre = mean May–Jun (recent pre); post = mean Jul–Aug.

Arithmetic facts: Gamma shows large favorable level shifts on median and p90 after July; Alpha/Beta show little median improvement in the first two months and clear p90 degradation through mid-post, with partial median recovery by Jul–Aug as intensity rises.

**2. Comparison to not-yet / never-treated**  
Over May–Jun (post Alpha/Beta, pre Gamma), never-treated Delta/Epsilon and not-yet Gamma also saw median and p90 rise with the May complexity shock (e.g., Epsilon median Jan–Mar ~40.3  May–Jun ~48.0; Delta ~34.5  ~42.6). Alpha/Beta did not uniquely escape the shock; their post-April paths are not cleanly better than controls’ concurrent deterioration. Gamma’s Jul–Aug improvement occurs while Delta/Epsilon medians ease only modestly from their May peaks (still above early-year baselines). Difference-in-differences sketch (Gamma Jul–Aug vs May–Jun minus average Delta/Epsilon same contrast) still favors Gamma on median/p90, but the control improvement and Gamma’s concurrent ~5% routing and prior FTE loss keep this descriptive.

**3. Treatment intensity**  
Across adopter branch-months, higher `automated_fast_path_pct` co-moves with later calendar time. Simple within-adopter comparison: months with auto_fast 40 (Alpha/Beta Jun–Aug) vs first adoption month (~25–27) show Alpha median 53.543.3 and p90 119.9101.2; Beta median ~4945 and p90 ~10799. Intensity is entangled with learning, summer arrival declines, and mix. Never-treated months stay at auto_fast=4 with no intensity variation to identify a pure dose curve.

**4. Pre-trends and regression to the mean**  
Alpha and Beta were selected for poor historical cycle times. Their Jan–Mar medians are stable-to-slightly-improving (not sharply worsening), so classical RTM from a single bad pre-month is unlikely, but selection on levels still biases toward expecting mean reversion relative to better branches. Controls’ May shock shows organization-wide nonstationarity—parallel trends in calendar time are doubtful without covariate adjustment. Gamma’s pre-trend already worsened in May–Jun with complexity before improving at adoption—consistent with either automation benefit or recovery from the product shock.

**5. Sensitivity / bounded conclusions**  
- Median: scenarios range from mild harm (+2 h) in early Alpha/Beta windows under full confounding attribution, to ~5 to 10 h beneficial for Gamma-like later adoption after intensity ramp. A cautious pooled ITT interval of roughly **[8 h, +3 h]** on median is consistent with the descriptive contrasts; zero is inside.  
- p90: early post periods support **[+5 h, +20 h]** deterioration under load/FTE stress; later Gamma-like windows support **[15 h, 0]**. Net sign for organization-wide rollout **unidentified**; tail risk cannot be signed the same way as median.  
- Rework/CSAT: bounds include zero after May shock and survey nonresponse; do not support a leadership “X% improvement” claim.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Do not issue a global “automation reduces cycle time by X%” statement, and do not expand immediately to both Delta and Epsilon as a pure operations rollout. Expand with an embedded evaluation that treats the next phase as both capacity deployment and a design that restores identifiability.

Which unit next. Phase Epsilon first or a random 50% of teams/queues within Delta and Epsilon (preferred: within-branch stepped wedge at queue or team level if operationally feasible). Epsilon has higher baseline complexity and cycle times closer to early adopters, so it is more informative about external validity to “weak” environments; Delta is healthier and more useful as a longer pure control if only one branch can stay untreated for 2–3 months. If branch-level assignment is mandatory, randomize order: one of {Delta, Epsilon} at T0, the other at T0+10–12 weeks, retaining the earlier adopters as high-intensity mature sites.

Assignment mechanism. Use explicit randomization or a public priority score plus lottery to break managerial selection. Hold routing campaigns fixed or pre-register any volume shifts. Freeze concurrent staffing changes in the evaluation window or pre-specify them as strata. Define treatment as availability plus a minimum supported training path so intensity is not pure self-selection; still measure `automated_fast_path_pct` weekly.

Minimum measurement window. Collect at least 8–12 weeks pre and 12 weeks post for each new wave, with weekly (not only monthly) aggregates. Do not decide scale-up before week 8 post go-live, when learning curves in Alpha/Beta were still immature.

Process-mining and queue data to collect. Case-level event logs with path variant (manual fast, manual extended, automated fast, automated escalated), timestamps, risk class at registration, rework loop counts, specialist and fraud-desk wait times, reason codes for escalation, and whether automated score was overridden. Queue-state snapshots (desk backlog, specialist WIP). Branch-week FTE productive hours and arrival sources (organic vs routed). CSAT with response counts and, if possible, link to case_id and cycle time.

Spillover monitoring. Shared fraud-desk waiting time and utilization by sending branch; cross-branch routing volumes; escalation rates at never-treated units when treated intensity rises.

Guardrails. Stop-rules if p90_cycle_h or complaint rate exceeds pre-period p90 bound by a pre-set margin for two consecutive weeks, or if fraud-desk SLA breaches. Cap routing increases into new sites during the evaluation. Require human override audit on automated fast-path cases.

Scale-up decision rule. Scale to full remaining branches only if (i) cohort-time ATT on median_cycle_h is favorable with pre-trends passing placebo, (ii) p90 does not worsen after covariate adjustment for mix and FTE, (iii) intensity reaches a pre-specified fidelity floor (e.g., automated fast path stable above an operational target without rework spike), and (iv) desk spillovers remain within SLA. Otherwise pause and redesign triage thresholds or staffing before further expansion.

    ("Beta", pre_ab, post_ab_late, "later"),
    ("Gamma", pre_g, post_g, "post"),
]:
    rec = {"branch": branch, "window": label}
    for c in OUTCOMES + ["automated_fast_path_pct", "fte", "high_complexity_pct", "arrivals"]:
        rec[f"d_{c}"] = mean_window(branch, post, c) - mean_window(branch, pre, c)
    rows.append(rec)
contrasts = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print(contrasts.round(2).to_string(index=False))

# Control contemporaneous change (May shock awareness)
print("\n=== Never-treated May-Jun vs Jan-Mar (common shocks) ===")
for b in ["Delta", "Epsilon"]:
    rec = {c: mean_window(b, pd.to_datetime(["2026-05", "2026-06"]), c)
           - mean_window(b, pre_ab, c) for c in OUTCOMES}
    print(b, {k: round(v, 2) for k, v in rec.items()})

# --- 4. Relative-time indicators for staggered adoption ---
df["rel_time"] = (
    (df["month"].dt.year - df["adopt_month"].dt.year) * 12
    + (df["month"].dt.month - df["adopt_month"].dt.month)
)
# never-treated: leave rel_time as NaN
print("\n=== Relative time counts (treated only) ===")
print(df.loc[df["ever_treated"] == 1].groupby(["cohort", "rel_time"]).size())

# Event-study style dummies (omit -1 as reference among treated path)
# For descriptive panel we build rel_time bins for ever-treated observations
for k in range(-3, 5):
    if k == -1:
        continue  # reference
    df[f"rt_{k}"] = ((df["rel_time"] == k) & (df["ever_treated"] == 1)).astype(int)

# --- 5. Descriptive panel model + treatment-intensity model ---
# ASSUMPTION (explicit): Parallel trends conditional on covariates; no interference;
# automation flag ignorable given branch FE + covariates — LIKELY FALSE given targeted rollout.
# These are associative fits for transparency, not proof of causality.

warnings.warn(
    "Only 5 branches (clusters). Cluster-robust SEs are not reliable; "
    "do not interpret p-values as confirmatory."
)
warnings.warn(
    "Outcomes are branch-month aggregated medians/p90s/means — "
    "not case-level effects; composition changes can move aggregates."
)

if HAS_SM:
    # Model A: binary availability + covariates + branch FE + month FE (descriptive TWFE-like)
    # NOTE: Naïve TWFE can be biased under heterogeneous staggered effects;
    # reported only as a descriptive benchmark, not primary causal estimator.
    print("\n=== Model A: descriptive two-way FE (automation binary) ===")
    print("# Causal comment: does NOT identify ATT under HTE/staggered timing/selection.")
    for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct"]:
        formula = (
            f"{y} ~ automation + high_complexity_pct + fte + log_arrivals "
            f"+ C(branch) + C(month)"
        )
        res = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
            cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
        )
        print(f"\n--- {y} ---")
        print(res.params[["automation", "high_complexity_pct", "fte", "log_arrivals"]].round(3))
        print("cluster SE automation:", float(res.bse["automation"]).round(3))
        print("N=", int(res.nobs), "clusters=5 WARNING small G")

    # Model B: treatment intensity (automated fast path %)
    print("\n=== Model B: treatment intensity (automated_fast_path_pct) ===")
    print("# Causal comment: intensity is endogenous (learning, mix, queues).")
    for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
        formula = (
            f"{y} ~ automated_fast_path_pct + high_complexity_pct + fte + log_arrivals "
            f"+ C(branch) + C(month)"
        )
        res = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
            cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
        )
        print(f"\n--- {y} ---")
        cols = ["automated_fast_path_pct", "high_complexity_pct", "fte", "log_arrivals"]
        print(res.params[cols].round(3))
        print("cluster SE intensity:", float(res.bse["automated_fast_path_pct"]).round(3))

    # Model C: simple event-study dummies + covariates (no month FE to reduce collinearity in tiny panel)
    print("\n=== Model C: relative-time dummies (descriptive event study) ===")
    print("# Causal comment: pre-period rt_* coefficients should be ~0 if parallel trends; often fail here.")
    rt_terms = " + ".join([f"rt_{k}" for k in range(-3, 5) if k != -1])
    formula = (
        f"median_cycle_h ~ {rt_terms} + high_complexity_pct + fte + log_arrivals + C(branch)"
    )
    res = smf.ols(formula, data=df.fillna({f"rt_{k}": 0 for k in range(-3, 5) if k != -1})).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    rt_coefs = {k: res.params.get(k, np.nan) for k in res.params.index if str(k).startswith("rt_")}
    print(pd.Series(rt_coefs).sort_index().round(2))

# --- Intensity vs outcome: adopter months only scatter summary ---
print("\n=== Adopter months: intensity tertile means (descriptive) ===")
ad = df[df["ever_treated"] == 1].copy()
ad["int_bin"] = pd.qcut(ad["automated_fast_path_pct"], 3, labels=["low", "mid", "high"])
print(ad.groupby("int_bin", observed=True)[OUTCOMES + ["automated_fast_path_pct"]].mean().round(2))

print("\n=== DONE. Remember: 5 clusters; aggregated medians; endogenous staggered adoption. ===")
print("Do not report a single causal 'X% cycle-time reduction' from these fits alone.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation availability was rolled out first to historically slow, high-volume branches, actual automated-fast-path use rose only gradually after go-live, and median versus p90 cycle times, rework, escalation, and CSAT moved under simultaneous fraud-desk, routing, staffing, and case-mix shocks—with Gamma showing more favorable post-July median/p90 shifts than Alpha/Beta’s early post period.
- `Suggestive:` After intensity ramps and in the later adopter, higher automated-fast-path share is compatible with modest median cycle-time improvement when staffing is not cut, while early post-adoption months under added volume often show p90 deterioration rather than tail improvement.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends held after adjusting for complexity, FTE, and arrivals and interference through the shared fraud desk were negligible, the ITT effect of branch availability on median cycle time would likely lie near zero to modestly negative (improvement), but those assumptions are not credible enough in these data to support a single organization-wide percentage claim.
- `Not supported:` “Automation reduces cycle time by X%” (or reliable gains in p90, rework, or CSAT) as a defensible, transportable effect for immediate expansion to Delta and Epsilon.
