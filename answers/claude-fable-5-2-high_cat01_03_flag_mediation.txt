## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is a deterministic function of `risk_score` (in this extract the highest unflagged score is 0.61 and the lowest flagged score is 0.64), so "set the flag to zero" is not one intervention. I treat the flag policy as a bundle of three components an organization can actually switch:

- **L**: entry to the priority triage lane;
- **R**: the rule that sends flagged cases to manual review by default;
- **D**: display of the label to triagers, reviewers and the senior team.

P0 is (L=1, R=1, D=1). P1 switches off D, and with it L and R unless staff rebuild them from the visible score. P2 switches off R only. P3 and P4 leave the bundle alone and change the queues behind it.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 versus P1, applied to the whole system for a period | System-period (shift or day block); all arriving cases, flag-eligible or not | Mean, median and P90 cycle time; throughput | Total policy effect, including spillovers on unflagged cases | Yes, once P1 states what staff still see (score, complexity) and how lanes are staffed | No concurrent P1 period; before/after comparison is confounded by case mix and load; no score overlap between flag groups |
| E2 | L on versus L off, with R and D held at their P0 setting | Case; flag-eligible cases (score at or above threshold), plus ineligible cases for displacement | Arrival to triage completion, split into `triage_wait` and `triage_service` | Separable effect of the lane component on the first stage | Yes | Lane, flag and triage resources coincide perfectly (T1/T2 serve only flagged cases, T3/T4 only unflagged), so no within-log contrast exists |
| E3 | P0 versus P2: review mandated versus review left to triager judgment | Case; flag-eligible cases, reported by complexity | Probability of `manual_review`; cycle time | Effect mediated by manual-review occurrence, expressed as the separable effect of component R (controlled-direct analogue when review is set to "none") | Yes for the policy contrast; "set review to none" is only meaningful where skipping review is permissible | 10 of 10 flagged cases were reviewed, so flagged-and-unreviewed has zero support; under P2 the reviewed set is selected by judgment |
| E4 | P0 versus a regime that gives flagged reviewed cases the waiting-time distribution of unflagged reviewed cases of the same complexity (implemented by P3 or a queue-discipline change), occurrence unchanged | Regime-level; flagged cases that are reviewed under both regimes | `manual_queue_wait`; cycle time | Interventional (randomized-analogue) indirect effect through manual-review waiting | Partly: defined as a capacity or discipline regime, not as "set this case's wait to w" | Wait is a system outcome with interference; queue state at entry is itself changed by the lane; backlog from cases outside the extract is unseen |
| E5 | P0 versus P4a (escalation criteria changed or label hidden at the escalation decision) and versus P4b (senior capacity added) | Case for occurrence, regime for waiting; reviewed cases | Probability of `escalation`; `escalation_queue_wait`; cycle time | Interventional indirect effect through escalation occurrence (P4a) and through senior waiting (P4b) | Yes for P4b; P4a only once the new criteria are written down | Escalation follows review findings, which the flag bundle affects; flagged High cases are escalated 5 of 5, so no overlap |
| E6 | Small shift of the flag threshold under otherwise unchanged P0 | Case; cases with scores near the threshold | Total cycle time (close minus arrival) | Local total effect of receiving the whole bundle, for marginal cases only | Yes | Few cases near the cutoff; complexity is unbalanced there (unflagged neighbours are High, flagged neighbours are Medium); says nothing about scores near 0.9 |
| E7 | P0 versus P2 (and versus P4a) | Case; flag-eligible cases, audited after close | Decision error found by blinded audit; reopen or reversal rate; reject rate as a secondary descriptor | Total effect of the routing change on decision quality | Yes if an audit standard exists; "Reject" alone is not quality | No ground truth in the log; outcomes are recorded only after the routing they depend on |
| E8 | Share of eligible cases receiving the bundle set to 0, 50 or 100 percent | System-period; unflagged cases | `triage_wait`, `manual_queue_wait`, cycle time of unflagged cases | Spillover (interference) effect | Yes at regime level | Requires variation in saturation, which a single observed regime does not give |
| E9 | Label shown versus hidden to reviewers and seniors, routing and queue position identical | Case; flagged reviewed cases | `manual_service`, escalation probability, decision | Separable effect of the information component D | Yes with blinded work lists | Reviewers can infer the flag from the score or the lane; behaviour may change once they know labels are hidden |

**Framework.** The primary estimands are total effects of whole policies (E1, E6, E7, E8), because those are what the organization can implement. For decomposition I use separable effects of the components L, R and D (E2, E3, E9) and interventional indirect effects, in which a mediator's distribution is shifted by a describable regime (E4, E5). I do not use natural direct and indirect effects. They need cross-world independence between the mediator a case would have had unflagged and its outcome when flagged. That fails here for two reasons: triage findings and the queue state met after triage are caused by the flag bundle and affect both later mediators and cycle time; and a case's waiting time is not a property of the case, because it depends on how every other case is routed. Controlled direct effects are used only where the mediator value can be imposed by rule (review yes or no), never for waiting times.

```text
                    |           | yes

                    |                                               about 7h for flagged and unflagged alike

```

Interference: `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` depend on other cases' flags and routing through `U_manual_queue_state` and `U_senior_queue_state`, and the priority lane can delay unflagged triage. "This case unflagged, everything else unchanged" is therefore not the policy counterfactual; effects have to be defined for regimes or saturation levels.

Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding: `U_triage_findings`, the time triage ends, and the queue state met at that moment are consequences of the flag bundle and causes of `manual_review`, both waits, `escalation` and `cycle_time`. `U_review_findings` plays the same role for escalation. Natural direct and indirect effects are therefore not identified even if there were no hidden risk.

Treatment-confounder feedback: review occurrence changes review findings and the senior backlog, which change escalation, which changes the backlog and the referral habits later cases meet. Conditioning on "reviewed" or "escalated" also opens collider paths through `U_underlying_risk`, because unflagged cases reach those stages only by human judgment.

**Handling of stages that do not occur.** An absent stage is missing, not zero. Stage statistics are computed only over the k cases in which the stage occurred, and `NA` is shown when k = 0. Residual-to-close is measured from the completion of the last stage that actually occurred (escalation, else manual review, else triage). Total cycle is close minus arrival for every case, and the occurring intervals sum exactly to it.

**Accounting split of the 8.34 h gap in mean cycle time (flag 1 minus flag 0).** Here an absent stage adds nothing to that case's cycle time, which is an identity on the cycle and not a claim about a zero-length stage. Each optional stage is split into an occurrence part (more cases reach it) and an intensity part (cases that reach it spend longer). The split is order-dependent and descriptive.

- Triage wait: −2.24 h. Triage service: −0.35 h.
- Manual-review wait: +5.75 h, of which occurrence +1.12 h and intensity +4.62 h.
- Manual service: +1.60 h, of which occurrence +1.65 h and intensity −0.05 h.
- Escalation wait: +2.90 h, of which occurrence +2.60 h and intensity +0.30 h.
- Escalation service: +0.75 h, of which occurrence +0.80 h and intensity −0.05 h.
- Residual-to-close: −0.07 h.

**Interpretation.** Acceleration at triage shows in every flagged case: waits of 0.1 to 0.4 h against 1.5 to 3.5 h, and shorter triage service. Delay downstream shows in three places. All flagged cases enter review, against four of ten unflagged. Reviewed flagged cases wait 6.5 h for a reviewer, against 1.9 h. Six of ten are escalated, against two, and each escalation costs about 7 h of waiting. The pattern survives within Medium and within High complexity. None of this is automatically causal. The flag groups do not overlap on `risk_score`, no Low case is flagged, and triagers coincide perfectly with the flag. The downstream columns condition on post-flag routing, so reviewed unflagged cases are a set selected by human judgment. Waits are produced in shared queues, and part of the unflagged triage wait may be displacement caused by the lane itself. Twenty cases from one 36-hour window cannot separate shift calendars or unseen backlog from capacity.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does receiving the bundle change triage time, review, escalation and cycle time for marginal cases? | Sharp regression discontinuity in `risk_score` at the flag threshold (between 0.61 and 0.64 here), local linear fits, standard errors clustered by arrival block. Identifies E6, a local total effect of the bundle, not a mediated effect | Complexity, arrival hour and queue state at arrival, used as balance checks and for precision | Staff cannot manipulate the score; potential outcomes are continuous at the cutoff; complexity does not jump there | Density test at the cutoff; covariate balance; placebo cutoffs at 0.50 and 0.75; the same cutoff applied to pre-introduction cases | No jump in review wait or cycle time at the cutoff, only a smooth rise with score, would mean the delay is risk and complexity rather than flag handling. In this extract the neighbours (R09, R17, R20 against R15, R08) differ in complexity, so it is uninformative |
| Is the long review wait explained by queue state rather than by the flag? | Rebuild, from the full log, the number waiting and in service per reviewer pool at each case's `triage_done`; model review wait on that state, flag, complexity and hour; replay the queue under FIFO and compare with observed start order | Queue length and work in progress at entry, reviewer pool, shift, complexity. In this sample flagged Medium cases had 0 of 5 rejections and 1 of 5 escalations, which is far too few to rely on | Positive for the review queue; possible extra pressure on the senior queue | Weak to moderate: the occurrence contrast is clear, the referral rate without the mandate is unknown | Pilot, restricted to Medium complexity, randomized and audited |
| P3: protected review capacity for flagged cases | None | Review wait for flagged cases falls toward the 2 h seen for unflagged referrals. Flagged High cases then arrive at the senior queue sooner. | P4: change escalation criteria or capacity | None | More senior capacity cuts the 7 h wait for all escalated cases. Tighter criteria cut escalation volume | Capacity: perhaps 3 to 6 h shorter for the 8 of 20 escalated cases. Each avoided escalation saves about 9 h | Highest for criteria changes: all four rejections came through escalation. The shortest expected cycle time probably comes from combining P2 with P4 criteria tightening, which is also the combination with the most quality risk and the least evidence.

**Pilot: P2, restricted.**

- **Eligibility.** Flagged cases with Medium complexity and a score between the threshold and 0.80. High complexity and any category with a regulatory review requirement are excluded.
- **Assignment.** Case-level randomization at registration, stratified by `arrival_load`. Arm A keeps mandatory review. Arm B keeps priority triage, and review happens only if the triager records a reason code. The treated share alternates between 30 and 70 percent in day blocks, giving a saturation contrast for spillovers.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail.** No capacity is added or removed. Enrolment pauses if the median senior-queue wait, or the review wait of non-eligible cases, rises more than 20 percent above baseline for two consecutive blocks.
- **Primary outcomes.** Arrival-to-close time (median and P90) for eligible cases, and decision error rate from a blinded post-close audit of every unreviewed Arm B case and an equal Arm A sample.
- **Secondary outcomes.** Each stage interval, review and escalation rates, triage service, 30-day reopenings, and waits of non-eligible cases.
- **Minimum data.** Start and completion timestamps per stage, queue-entry and assignment times, resource identifiers including seniors, arm, reason code, score and complexity at intake, queue-length snapshots, staff calendars, audit results.
- **Stopping conditions.** Any severe missed-risk case in Arm B; an audited error rate in Arm B exceeding Arm A by a pre-set margin; the guardrail breached twice; or triagers referring more than 90 percent of Arm B cases, which ends the pilot for futility.

Notes on the code. It was run against PM4Py 2.7 with pandas 3. `discover_performance_dfg` on a dataframe measures completion-to-completion time between directly-following events, so its `Triage -> Manual Review` edge contains review waiting plus review service; the explicit stage table is the source for waiting and service times. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` gives one service, waiting and sojourn total per case and is used only as a consistency check. The code computes descriptive intervals and an accounting split. It does not estimate natural direct or indirect effects. Timestamps are treated as a single local clock; PM4Py labels them UTC without shifting them.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If pre-introduction cases carrying a shadow flag already showed the same review waits and cycle gap, or audit proxies show a large risk imbalance within complexity strata, the delay is case mix rather than flag handling. P2 would then be dropped in favour of recalibrating the score.
2. **Queue interference.** If the saturation blocks show that review capacity released by P2 is absorbed at once by other work, or that High-complexity and unflagged cases wait longer when more cases are treated, P2 only moves delay. The preferred lever becomes capacity or queue discipline (P3).
3. **Decision quality.** If the blinded audit finds that mandatory review catches material errors in mid-band cases above the agreed tolerance, the longer cycle time is the price of correct decisions. The recommendation reverses to keeping the mandate and shortening its wait.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If `manual_start` records first opening after a batch assignment, if waits follow shift calendars or time-zone offsets, or if `triage_done` in the priority lane is logged at hand-off while work continues, the stage split is wrong. The triage gain may be an artefact and the remedy is scheduling.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap.** If a larger log still has no flagged unreviewed cases and no score overlap, or eligible Medium cases are too few, no mediation claim is supportable and the pilot cannot inform the High-complexity cases that carry most of the delay. Priority then shifts to P4.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers.** If triagers under P2 refer nearly every case anyway or lengthen triage to compensate, or reviewers escalate more defensively once reviews are discretionary, P2 yields no gain or a loss. The better levers are then escalation criteria and senior capacity.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** Flagged cases start triage about 2.2 h sooner and finish it faster, yet close 8.3 h later on average (16.5 h against 8.2 h). Arithmetically, the gap sits in the manual-review queue (+5.8 h per case, mostly longer waiting rather than more reviews), manual service (+1.6 h, all from more reviews) and escalation (+3.7 h, mostly from more escalations). Unflagged referrals overtake flagged cases in the review queue. Service times, the closing step and rejection counts (two of ten in each group) do not differ.

**Plausible mechanism.** The flag bundle buys speed at triage and spends it downstream. Automatic referrals arrive early and in bulk, with a thinner triage work-up, in a queue that gives them no priority. High-complexity cases then meet a scarce senior team that delays every case it receives.

**Unidentified.** How much downstream delay would remain without the bundle is unknown, because scores never overlap between groups and omit risk. Whether the waits reflect capacity, queue discipline, preparation or shift calendars is open. So are natural direct and indirect effects, spillovers on unflagged cases, and whether the extra reviews improve decisions.

**Most useful next evidence.** A randomized, audited pilot of P2 in Medium-complexity flagged cases, with the treated share varied by block, creates the missing flagged-but-unreviewed cases, measures the quality cost and exposes queue spillovers. P3 and P4 should be sized from its results.
