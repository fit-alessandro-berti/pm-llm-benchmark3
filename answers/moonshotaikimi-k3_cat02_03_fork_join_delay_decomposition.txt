# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let `c` index cases and `i  {A,B,C}` index branches. All quantities are minutes; timestamps are completeevents (1minute resolution).

- `upstream_elapsed(c) = fork_release(c)  arrival(c)`
- Branch queue: `q_i(c) = start_i(c)  fork_release(c)`  (a branch may start only at/after `fork_release`; a positive gap is that branch's own queue)
- Branch service: `s_i(c) = end_i(c)  start_i(c)`
- Branch latency from release to completion: `L_i(c) = q_i(c) + s_i(c) = end_i(c)  fork_release(c)`
- Join instant (derived, not logged): `join_ready(c) = max_i end_i(c) = fork_release(c) + max_i L_i(c)`
- Critical branch: `i*(c) = argmax_i end_i(c)` (ties broken by a fixed priority ABC; see §3/§6 — no exact ties occur in the baseline)
- Branch synchronization wait: `w_i(c) = join_ready(c)  end_i(c)` (so `w_{i*}(c)=0`)
- Elapsed fork–join stage: `FJ(c) = join_ready(c)  fork_release(c) = max_i L_i(c)`
- Downstream queueing: `dq(c) = final_start(c)  join_ready(c)`
- Final service: `fs(c) = final_end(c)  final_start(c)` (=20 for all c)
- Total flow: `T(c) = final_end(c)  arrival(c) = upstream_elapsed(c) + FJ(c) + dq(c) + fs(c)`

Why summing branch synchronization waits double counts. For every branch, `q_i + s_i + w_i = (end_i  fork_release) + (join_ready  end_i) = join_ready  fork_release = FJ(c)`. So each branch's `q_i+s_i+w_i` already equals the *same* elapsed fork–join time `FJ(c)`. Summing over the three branches gives `_i(q_i+s_i+w_i) = 3·FJ(c)`, i.e. it counts the fork–join interval three times. In this data `_i L_i = 1647` and `_i w_i = 549`, whose sum `2196 = 3 × 732 = 3·_c FJ(c)`. The only nonredundant fork–join measure is the **max** (`FJ = max_i L_i`); the `w_i` of noncritical branches are slack, not additive elapsed time.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**R1 — Level/dedicate B capacity to cut B's releasetostart queue (target 50%).**

**R2 — Audit and remove the fixed postjoin offset; revisit the odd/even lane assignment at the final station.**

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality is not identified.** B's queue correlates with criticality, but both could stem from a common upstream cause (e.g., the preparation step releasing work in bursts). The counterfactual savings are *ceteris paribus* calculations, not proven causal effects; accelerating B could induce feedback (arrivals refilling the queue per Little's law, or pacing changes).
- **Branch resource capacity is unobserved.** We see releasestart gaps but not how many A/B/C servers exist or their busy intervals. That `A_queue  0` could mean A is dedicated, overprovisioned, or simply first in line; we cannot compute A/B/C resource utilization or distinguish a capacity shortage from a scheduling policy for B and C.
- **Preemption and work content are invisible.** Completeevents only: a 50minute A "service" may include preemption, waiting for a subresource, or rework. We assume `endstart` is valueadding service; if it embeds hidden waits, the service/queue split for A (and the constancy of A=50) is misleading.
- **Finalstation waiting may not be a queue.** The postjoin gap is 8–20 min for *every* case while the two servers run at 57% utilization, and F01 waits 8 min with both servers idle — inconsistent with a workconserving queue. Moreover `final_resource` alternates strictly by case parity (oddF1, evenF2), and a nooverlap-plusconstanttransfer model does not fit (F05/F09/F11 start exactly at the lane predecessor's end while F01/F03/F07 carry positive offsets). The gap is therefore as consistent with a **release/QA hold or fixed transfer policy** as with queuing; CF3's large savings are conditional on the gap being waste.
- **`join_ready` is derived, not logged.** It equals `max(A_end,B_end,C_end)` only under the stated ANDjoin rule. If F actually required only a subset (e.g., A and B) or had its own release logic, the critical branch and all downstream conclusions change.
- **Resolution and sample size.** Oneminute rounding makes ±1min ties/gaps ambiguous (the CFinduced ties at F11/F02/F06 are within rounding). Twelve cases on one day cannot establish that the parity assignment, A's constant 50, or the offset distribution are stable rather than coincidental; tail statements (max=75/130) rest on two cases.
- **No arrival process for resources.** Without resourceclaim timestamps we cannot separate "branch queues independently" from shared hidden resources, and we cannot validate that synchronization waits (`w_i`) actually free the branch resource for other work.
