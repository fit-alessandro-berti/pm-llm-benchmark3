The key calendar effect is that the 62-hour interval from Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00 is not available for routine chemistry, while Sunday is not available for routine QA. Therefore an apparently small service remainder can create a large elapsed-time jump. For every ordinary path, the correct order is: finish each branch under its own calendar, take the later usable result, wait for QA capacity, perform QA and any conditional rework, and only then perform the terminal recording step.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Microbiology readiness and readout staffing — calendar and service-time uncertainty.** The 46–54 hour incubation window straddles Sunday and Monday, and the difference between on-call and routine staffing can move the usable result by many elapsed hours.

2. **The unknown temperature excursion — observation, dependence, and path uncertainty.** Confirmation changes more than one branch: it raises both chemistry-rerun and microbiology-contamination risks and also moves QA into the higher investigation-probability regime. The effects should not be multiplied as independent events.

3. **Chemistry queue discipline and the 18:00 close — queue and calendar uncertainty.** FIFO can leave B-417 behind all four waiting samples, while risk-priority can move it ahead of ordinary work. Crossing the close creates a long weekend pause even when only a small amount of service remains.

4. **The future QA queue — queue uncertainty.** Five current batches, stochastic arrivals at 0.35 per opening hour, substantial overdispersion, and changing reviewer capacity make the queue at the actual join time uncertain; this is more important than fine distinctions within the 1.5-hour ordinary QA service time.

5. **QA exception type and duration — path and calendar uncertainty.** A 4–12 working-hour query is materially different from an 8–20 working-hour documentation investigation, and the 3–7 calendar-day laboratory investigation creates the severe tail. The conditional investigation probability is itself much higher after a rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed excursion.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The most forecast-changing next observation is the **microbiology primary readout being recorded, including whether it shows a contamination indication**. A normal result, especially one read Monday morning or Monday afternoon, removes the extra 20–30 hour confirmation clock and leaves a likely Monday-to-Tuesday release window. A contamination indication adds continuous confirmation incubation and another readout, usually moving the forecast to Tuesday–Wednesday and increasing the chance of a QA investigation. A late readout timestamp also directly reveals whether the staffing/calendar branch—not test duration—is controlling the join.

The secondary update trigger is **resolution of the upstream temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation should shift probability toward a chemistry rerun, microbiology confirmation, and QA investigation together; refutation should pull mass back toward the smooth-path Monday/Tuesday release forecast.
