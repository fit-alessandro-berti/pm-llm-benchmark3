## 1. What is observed  
During Jan–Aug 2026 five branches produced 64 monthly summaries.  

• Automation availability – the binary indicator `automation` switches on for Alpha and Beta in Apr-26 and for Gamma in Jul-26; Delta and Epsilon remain 0.  

• Actual use – the percentage of cases that truly flowed through the automated fast path (`automated_fast_path_pct`) starts near 25-27 % in the first month of availability and climbs to roughly 50 % for Alpha and Beta and to the low-30 % range for Gamma, confirming heterogeneous and time-varying treatment intensity.  

• Branch selection – Alpha and Beta were chosen first because they historically lagged on cycle time and volume; Gamma was considered “ready” later; Delta and Epsilon served as implicit controls but faced different volumes and staffing. None of these choices were randomized.  

• Concurrent operational changes – March introduced a new fraud desk; April shifted 10 % more arrivals toward the first adopters; May brought a product change that raised high-complexity share; multiple one-FTE staffing shocks occurred; managers could also tweak local processes. All of these are plausible confounders.  

• Measurement limits – outcomes are branch-month aggregates, so within-branch dispersion and queue states are unobserved. `csat_1to5` is based on surveys with unknown and varying response rates, making non-response bias and sampling error hard to gauge. Fraud-desk wait-times and specialist-queue backlogs are missing. No event-level data are supplied, preventing precise path decomposition.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|---------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Event-study / staggered adoption (primary) | Relative month dummies centred on go-live | Not-yet-treated & never-treated months | arrivals, fte, high_complexity_pct, month FE | ITT-cycle, Tail-effect, Rework-diff | 1. Parallel pre-trends conditional on controls 2. No concurrent shocks correlated with treatment | Pre-trend plots, placebo leads, branch-specific trends, robustness to dropping one treated branch | Confounding by routing and staffing shocks coincident with adoption |
| Synthetic control per adopter | Separate donor pool for each treated branch | Remaining branches weighted to match pre-period | Same as above | ITT-cycle | 1. Good pre-fit 2. No interference 3. Structural stability | RMSPE ratio tests, in-space placebo, leave-one-out donors | Very small donor pool & shared shocks (fraud desk) break counterfactual |
| Interrupted time-series with matched controls | Level & slope change at branch-specific adoption date | Best-matching never-treated branch | arrivals, fte, high_complexity_pct | ITT-cycle | 1. Stable pre-period slope 2. Control captures calendar shocks | Durbin-Watson for autocorr., sensitivity to window choice | Regression to mean given selection on poor performers |

The event-study framework is preferable because: (a) it leverages all 64 observations and both adopters and non-adopters; (b) it lets us visualise dynamic effects and test for pre-trend violations; (c) it easily incorporates time-varying confounders (arrivals, FTE, complexity). Synthetic control and ITS are informative cross-checks but are under-powered with only two untreated branches and many simultaneous system-wide shocks (fraud desk, product change). Robustness will still be limited by three treated clusters, yet event-study transparently displays that limitation.

2. Difference vs. not-yet-treated (Delta+Epsilon) shows that Delta/Epsilon median rose 3-5 h after May, so Alpha’s flatness may mask an avoided increase, but Beta’s rise exceeds that counterfactual.

3. Intensity model (simple within-treated correlation): across adopter months, every 10 pp higher fast-path share is associated with 0.6 h median (SE0.4) and +0.4 pp escalation (SE0.2). Association, not causal.

4. Pre-trend check: Alpha & Beta had slightly improving medians Jan–Mar; Gamma was flat; Delta/Epsilon improved until May downturn. Parallel-trend assumption is fragile; regression-to-mean is plausible because adopters were chosen as poor performers.

5. Sensitivity ranges  
• Median cycle time ITT effect lies between 4 % and +4 % depending on specification; we cannot rule out 0.  
• Escalation increase of 2-3 pp is consistent across designs unless we adjust for complexity, suggesting a robust upward shift.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Selection into early adoption | Alpha & Beta were slow branches | Could exaggerate improvements (regression) or mask harm (they lacked slack) | Pre-trend placebo, compare to later adopters | Misleading statement of benefit |
| Non-parallel trends | Product change hit all branches in May | Confounds ITT estimates | Include month FE, use synthetic control | Estimates unstable |
| Time-varying confounding (routing) | Extra 10 % arrivals to adopters | Likely biases against automation (cycle time ) | Adjust for arrivals, simulate load-neutral counterfactual | Understate benefit |
| Staffing shocks | Alpha lost FTE, Beta gained | Could create cycle-time noise | Control for FTE or hours worked | Wrong sign attribution |
| Treatment intensity endogeneity | Staff choose which cases to automate | High fast-path % in easy weeks | Bias toward favourable effect | Instrument by shift-level availability logs (future) | Misjudge machine capability |
| Fraud-desk spillover | Shared queue could slow specialists | Bias tails upward for adopters | Gather desk wait times | Attribute congestion to automation wrongly |
| Case-mix drift | Complexity  5–8 pp | Inflates cycle time independently | Control for complexity, interact | Mask small automation gains |
| Anticipation / training | Staff prepare before go-live | Improves pre-trend, breaks parallelism | Placebo leads | Overstate effect |
| Survey non-response | CSAT changes could be artefactual | Unknown | Track response counts | Incorrect customer conclusion |
| Aggregation bias | Medians summarise broad distribution | Hides within-branch heterogeneity | Use raw event logs | Miss tail harms |
| Few clusters | Only 3 treated, 2 control | Standard errors unreliable | Wild bootstrap, randomisation inference | False precision |
| Dynamic effects | Learning over months | Short post for Gamma | Need longer window | Incorrect long-term inference |

## 8. Rollout recommendation  
Recommendation: `expand with an embedded evaluation`

To balance operational needs and credible learning, proceed with a stepped-wedge expansion in which Delta adopts in 2026-11 and Epsilon in 2027-02, contingent on pre-specified guardrails.

Design  
• Randomly assign the order of Delta vs. Epsilon if leadership is flexible; otherwise hold Epsilon as control for one extra quarter.  
• Maintain at least 3 full pre-months of comparable data (Aug-Oct 2026) and 6 post-months after each go-live to capture learning.  
• Collect event-level logs: exact timestamps for each activity, auto-triage confidence scores, specialist queue entry/exit, fraud-desk wait times.  
• Measure queue states (WIP, backlog) daily.  
• Record survey invitation counts and responses to compute response rates.  
• Monitor spillovers: if specialist backlog or fraud-desk median wait exceeds 20 % above pre-rollout baseline for two consecutive weeks, trigger a pause.  
• Customer guardrails: if branch-level p90_cycle_h exceeds 120 h for two consecutive months or csat drops by 0.15 points, halt new auto-triage pushes.  

Decision rule for full scale-up  
After 6 months post-Epsilon go-live, compute difference-in-differences between treated and the last pre-treatment months adjusting for arrivals, complexity, and FTE. Approve wider deployment only if:  
1. Median cycle time non-inferior (+2 %) and p90 not worse than +5 %;  
2. Escalation_pct increase 3 pp or is offset by documented fraud prevention benefit;  
3. CSAT non-inferior (0.05).  

This staged approach supplies a comparison window, enriches the causal data set, and still moves the organisation toward network-wide automation within a year.

## 10. Final claim ladder  
• Observed: branches with higher automated-fast-path percentages tend to show slightly lower median cycle times in the same month but higher escalation rates.  
• Suggestive: automation availability has not yet reduced median cycle time overall and may have increased 90th-percentile cycle time in early adopter branches.  
• Conditionally causal: if staffing and routing shocks are adequately adjusted, automation appears to raise specialist escalations by roughly 2-3 percentage points.  
• Not supported: a blanket statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X %” for future branches.
