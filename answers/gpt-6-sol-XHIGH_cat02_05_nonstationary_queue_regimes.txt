# 1. Data audit and estimands

Let (A_d) and (C_d) be arrivals and completions during the 12 open hours of day (d). Their daily average intensities are (A_d/12) and (C_d/12) cases per open hour; these averages do not imply constant hourly rates. Let (B_d) be the number of unfinished cases at 07:00 and (L_d) the mean in-system count from the 15-minute snapshots. If every arrival and completion belongs to this queue, the accounting identity is (B_{d+1}=B_d+A_d-C_d). The unknown initial (B_d) prevents recovery of absolute backlog from these rows, although changes in backlog are observable. “Service time” is active handling time; “flow time” includes waiting. The reported daily medians and p90s describe within-day service-time distributions, not a pooled service distribution or mean flow time.

For a regime, estimates below are arithmetic means of daily counts, WIP, and concurrent FTE; complex-case share is weighted by arrivals. Arrival/FTE/day is a *workload proxy*, not utilization: mean handling time, staff schedules, parallel work, and effort by variant are unavailable. All per-hour rates use 12 scheduled open hours, including the three outage hours on April 15.

Threats to naive inference include: unknown opening and closing backlog and overnight carry-over; the 180-minute outage and its recovery; day-of-week staffing and volume cycles; within-day arrival peaks; a possible change in case mix *or its coding*; completed cases selected from earlier arrivals; serially dependent daily observations; aggregate quantiles that cannot be averaged into a case-level service distribution; WIP measured from snapshots only during opening; and intervention timing on April 29 that is not recorded. There is also a direct data conflict: on the four representative dates, the hourly vectors put only 16.8%, 18.1%, 9.6%, and 11.8% of arrivals in the 08:00–09:59 bins, respectively, versus the daily field's 31%, 32%, 47%, and 22%. The vector totals do match daily arrivals and completions. I use the vectors for hourly shape and regard the reported `peak_08_10_pct` trend as provisional until its definition or extraction is reconciled.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different segmentation follows **backlog state** across calendar days, treating day-of-week and FTE as covariates rather than regime boundaries:

| Window | Backlog-state interpretation | Observed net change |
|---|---|---:|
| Apr 6–14 | Approximately balanced, low inventory | +5 |
| Apr 15–19 | Outage shock, partial clearance, residual carry-over | +17, comprising +47 on Apr 15 and −30 on Apr 16–17 |
| Apr 20–28 | Persistent accumulation during high-complexity operation | +106 |
| Apr 29–May 3 | Initial drawdown after staffing change, with transition uncertainty | −18 |

This view captures hysteresis: output on a given day depends on earlier unfinished work, and a high completion count may reflect clearing that stock. It is useful for a backlog-control model. Its weakness is that mixing weekdays with weekends produces misleading mean arrival and completion rates; the April 15 outage and April 29 partial-day intervention also deserve separate treatment. Conversely, the primary segmentation resolves schedule and mix better but has small cells and does not fully condition on opening backlog. The data support both views, not a unique latent-state model.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

Using sample variance across the 12 hourly arrival bins divided by their mean (Fano/index of dispersion), and hourly standard deviation divided by mean (CV), gives:

| Representative day | Arrival Fano | Arrival CV | Largest hourly arrival count | Actual 08:00–09:59 vector share versus daily field |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apr 7, ordinary weekday candidate | 3.23 | 0.62 | 20 | 16.8% versus 31% |
| Apr 15, outage | 4.14 | 0.69 | 22 | 18.1% versus 32% |
| Apr 21, high-mix weekday | 10.12 | 1.03 | 34 | 9.6% versus 47% |
| Apr 25, weekend | 1.29 | 0.55 | 8 | 11.8% versus 22% |

Apr 21 has 34 arrivals versus 13 completions at 10:00–10:59, and 24 versus 12 at 13:00–13:59: two hours alone add 33 cases before other hours offset them. The unusually high Fano factor is evidence of an uneven hourly profile, not by itself a test of stochastic overdispersion: a time-varying Poisson rate, bursts, or recording artifacts can all produce it. The daily `peak_08_10_pct` series increases after Apr 20, but its conflict with all four vectors prevents a reliable claim about that *specific* two-hour slot.

The daily sequence also violates an independent, identically distributed assumption. In these 28 observations, lag-one correlations are approximately 0.41 for arrivals, 0.61 for WIP, and 0.93 for complex percentage; lag-seven arrival correlation is about 0.99. These are descriptive: the weekly schedule, level shifts, and short sample generate much of the apparent dependence. The cumulative net count rises by +47 on the outage day, falls by 30 over its two recovery days, then rises by +106 from Apr 20–28. The clearest candidate changes are Apr 15 (known outage), Apr 20 (complex share jumps from 23% on Sunday to 41% on Monday, with the same-weekday comparison also rising strongly), and Apr 29 (FTE and net-flow change). The exact onset and mechanisms of the mix shift are not identified. Four selected hourly profiles cannot estimate a full arrival process, queue discipline, service distribution, or intraday autocorrelation across all days.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law concerns a consistently defined system in long-run rate balance, (L=\lambda W), with mean **flow** time (W). For a finite 12-hour-per-day window, (L/(C/12)) is at best a diagnostic estimate of *open-clock* time in system if cases and WIP use the same boundary, opening/closing inventory effects are small, and the window is approximately stable. It excludes closed overnight hours; it is not calendar elapsed time. Using completions as throughput is natural when estimating time for cases leaving the system; arrivals are similar only when (A\approx C). Fifteen-minute snapshot WIP approximates, but does not equal, the time integral of queue length.

| Candidate window | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow time, if defensible | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 6–10 early weekdays | Net zero across five consecutive days; endpoints still unknown | Completions = arrivals = 491/5 = 98.2/day | Mean open-hour WIP 19.6 from snapshots | Unknown opening and overnight stock | Most plausible locally stable window; five days only | (19.6/(98.2/12)=2.40) **open hours**, provisional | approximate only |
| Apr 11–12 early weekend | Net +1 over two days | Completions 79/2 = 39.5/day | Mean open-hour WIP 13.0 | May inherit Friday stock; no night measurements | Same staffing, but only two days | (13.0/(39.5/12)=3.95) **open hours**, very provisional | approximate only |
| Apr 15 outage | Net +47; severe boundary change | Neither arrival nor completion rate represents steady throughput | WIP 47 changes during outage | Large induced carry-over | Known service interruption | — | not defensible |
| Apr 16–17 recovery | Net −30 | Completion rate inflated by prior arrivals | WIP 52 then 35 | Large and deliberate clearance | Transient | — | not defensible |
| Apr 20–28 high-mix, including weekends | Net +106 | Arrivals and completions diverge | WIP and FTE vary by day type | Growing stock | Mix shift and accumulation | — | not defensible |
| Apr 29 transition | Net −5, treatment starts at unknown hour | Full-day completion rate mixes states | WIP 43 only a day mean | Inherited backlog | Within-day intervention boundary unknown | — | not defensible |
| Apr 30–May 3 later period | Net −13 overall; weekday drawdown and weekend balance | Completions exceed arrivals overall | WIP 36 → 19 across day types | Backlog being depleted | Short and nonstationary | — | not defensible |
| All 28 days | Net +110 | 2,519 arrivals versus 2,409 completions | Open-only WIP pooled across incompatible regimes | Substantial | Weekly cycle, outage, mix shift, staffing change | — | not defensible |

The two provisional numbers are *not* mean active service times and cannot be checked against the supplied medians. Even their ordering may depend on unmeasured carry-over and closed-hour waiting. The arithmetic pooled ratio (29.86/(86.04/12)\approx4.16) hours is deliberately **not** reported as an implied flow time because its assumptions fail.

# 7. Operational implications

- Plan weekday and weekend staffing separately, and model high-complexity demand with its longer active-service tail. The high-mix weekday workload proxy rises from 16.9 to 19.2 arrivals/FTE/day while completions barely increase; monitor actual workload hours and queue-age distributions before turning that proxy into a utilization target.
- Treat outage response as an explicit backlog-recovery plan. Track opening backlog, age, and daily net change; estimate clearance against new arrivals. The −30 net change on Apr 16–17 recovers only part of the outage day's +47, and subsequent high-mix growth dominates the period.
- Investigate the April 21 spikes and reconcile the contradictory `peak_08_10_pct` field before moving staff to a named 08:00–10:00 slot. If timestamps confirm the spikes, stagger starts, reserve cross-trained capacity around the actual peak hours, or offer appointment/smoothing options; assess effects on waiting and abandonment, not just daily completions.
- A policy based on the pooled 90 arrivals/day might remove weekday capacity or schedule 08:00–10:00 coverage from the unverified peak field. That can look efficient in aggregate yet is risky against 114 high-mix weekday arrivals, large within-day spikes, and accumulating backlog.
- Collect case-level arrival, activity start/end, completion, variant, priority, and transfer timestamps; opening/closing backlog and queue-age snapshots; actual staffed minutes and outage start/end; and versioned definitions of `complex_pct` and the peak field. These permit arrival-to-completion cohorts, calendar-time and open-time waits, effort by variant, peak-specific capacity, and a properly conditioned intervention analysis. For the April 29 change, record implementation time and compare matched weekdays while controlling for inherited backlog and mix.

d = pd.read_csv(StringIO(daily_csv), parse_dates=["date"])

d["net"] = d.arrivals - d.completed

def regime(row):
    t = row.date
    if t == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15"):
        return "outage"
    if pd.Timestamp("2026-04-16") <= t <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-17"):
        return "recovery"
    if t == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29"):
        return "intervention transition"
    if row.weekend:
        if t < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20"):
            return "early weekend"
        if t < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29"):
            return "high-mix weekend"
        return "later weekend"
    if t < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20"):
        return "early weekday"
    if t < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29"):
        return "high-mix weekday"
    return "later weekday"

d["regime"] = d.apply(regime, axis=1)

hourly = pd.DataFrame([
    ("2026-04-07", [3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4],
     [4,7,10,14,15,11,9,8,7,6,5,3]),
    ("2026-04-15", [4,6,13,22,17,11,9,7,6,4,4,2],
     [5,7,10,8,0,0,0,4,6,7,6,5]),
    ("2026-04-21", [2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4],
     [3,4,7,13,10,8,12,10,8,9,9,8]),
    ("2026-04-25", [1,2,4,7,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,2],
     [1,2,3,5,6,6,5,4,4,3,2,2]),
], columns=["date", "arrivals_by_hour", "completions_by_hour"])

    return {
        "group": name, "days": len(x),
        "arr/day": x.arrivals.mean(), "comp/day": x.completed.mean(),
        "net/day": x.net.mean(), "WIP": x.mean_wip.mean(),
        "FTE": x.staff_fte.mean(),
        "complex%": (x.arrivals * x.complex_pct).sum() / x.arrivals.sum(),
        "arr/FTE/day": x.arrivals.sum() / x.staff_fte.sum(),
        "median_min_range": f"{x.median_service_min.min()}-{x.median_service_min.max()}",
        "p90_min_range": f"{x.p90_service_min.min()}-{x.p90_service_min.max()}",
    }

         "high-mix weekday", "high-mix weekend", "intervention transition",
         "later weekday", "later weekend"]

    summary("all", d), summary("weekdays", d[~d.weekend]),
    summary("weekends", d[d.weekend]),
    summary("Apr 20-28", d[d.date.between("2026-04-20", "2026-04-28")]),
    summary("Apr 29-May 3", d[d.date.between("2026-04-29", "2026-05-03")]),
])

    burst_rows.append({
        "date": r.date.date(), "arrivals": int(a.sum()), "completed": int(c.sum()),
        "arrival_Fano": a.var(ddof=1) / a.mean(),
        "arrival_CV": a.std(ddof=1) / a.mean(),
        "max_2h_share%": 100 * a.rolling(2).sum().max() / a.sum(),

# at least 15 percentage points on three consecutive days flags a candidate

               & high_step.shift(-2, fill_value=False), "date"]

      starts.iloc[0].date() if len(starts) else "none flagged")

ll_rows = []
for label, lo, hi in [
