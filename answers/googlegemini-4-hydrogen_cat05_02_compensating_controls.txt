## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package cannot be judged solely by whether controls "occurred." Sufficiency must be evaluated across seven structural dimensions:

1. **Timing:** Controls operate *before release* (preventive), *after release but before irrevocable settlement* (pre-settlement stop), or *post-settlement* (recall-dependent vs. purely forensic).
2. **Independence:** Actors must be distinct humans with separate credentials, no hierarchical/performance-rating dependency (e.g., direct supervisor vs. independent L3+), and no shared workstation/session contamination.
3. **Coverage:** Controls must close the *same* threat vectors left open by single-user release—specifically substantive receipt/service verification and bank-account authenticity, not just syntactic PO checks.
4. **Overrideability:** Hard system blocks outperform soft warnings; warnings overridden by the same operator (or via shared credentials) negate the control.
5. **Recoverability:** If a defect slips past release, can a stop-payment occur before clearing, or does audit fall within the ~24h recall window?
6. **Evidence Quality:** Controls require verifiable artifacts (pre-existing emergency token, timestamped substantive review of invoice/bank/receipt, active AV-2 logs), not unsigned checklists or unverified assertions.
7. **Collusion Resistance:** Controls must resist both single-actor impersonation (shared terminals) and two-actor collusion (independent third-party MG-4 + behavioral monitoring).

### Sufficiency Classification Rubric

| Package Tier | Required Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Fully Equivalent** | All seven dimensions hold at a level matching or exceeding nominal four-eyes: hard AV-2 coverage operational without override; independent L3+ MG-4 with documented substantive review (invoice, bank, receipt) *before release* (or pre-settlement under strict ED-1); valid pre-existing emergency justification when same-user; and RA-7 audit within the recall window. |
| **Partially Equivalent** | Core risk is substantially contained before irrevocable loss (e.g., clean AV-2 + independent pre-settlement MG-4 + timely RA-7), *but* one structural safeguard is degraded (e.g., missing emergency precondition, MG-4 occurred post-release/pre-settlement without verified review depth, or MG-4 preceded AV-2 by minutes). |
| **Merely Mitigating** | One layer reduces statistical error (e.g., basic AV-2 pass or post-settlement audit) while leaving major threat vectors open (e.g., unverified service delivery, shared workstations, no independent pre-settlement human gate, or audit outside the 24h recall window). |
| **Ineffective** | Preventive and pre-settlement gates failed, were bypassed, or lacked independence (e.g., AV-2 unavailable or self-overridden, conflicted/post-settlement manager ratification, audit after recall window), leaving full unrecoverable exposure regardless of whether fraud happened to occur in that specific case. |

### Why "Same Expected Loss" Is Not the Only Standard
Equating controls solely by *expected loss* ($\mathbb{E}[L] = p \times \text{Impact}$) is flawed for internal control governance:
- **Tail Risk and Catastrophic Variance:** A EUR 900,000 unvalidated transfer (like AP-208) with a low base defect probability may have a modest mathematical expected loss, yet a single failure causes severe liquidity or balance-sheet damage that violates risk appetite.
- **Non-Repudiation and Accountability:** Controls must prove *who* authorized funds and *why*. Shared credentials or workstations destroy forensic attribution even if expected loss appears unchanged.
- **Normalization of Deviance (Moral Hazard):** Permitting "convenience" same-user releases (AP-201) without emergency tokens signals that rules are optional, eroding deterrence and raising future override probabilities across the organization.
- **Unmeasurable Knightian Uncertainty:** When key evidence is missing (unsigned checklists, missing org-design docs, unknown credential sharing), failure probabilities cannot be credibly estimated; "same expected loss" becomes an arbitrary guess.
- **Regulatory and Fiduciary Compliance:** Statutory/audit obligations (e.g., SOX/internal governance) require demonstrable preventive segregation and valid authorization trails, not merely favorable historical loss averages.

### Semantics of Manager Action After `Release_Payment` but Before `Bank_Settlement`
A manager action occurring between `Release_Payment` and `Bank_Settlement` (e.g., AP-201 at 08:30 vs 10:00; AP-207 at 14:40 vs 16:00) is best classified as **hybrid**:
- **Detective relative to internal release:** It did not prevent the operator from executing `Release_Payment` or committing the instruction to the outbound payment queue.
- **Preventive / Corrective relative to irrevocable economic loss:** Because internal control treats `Bank_Settlement` as the point of irrevocability, an independent manager review prior to clearing can still intercept and cancel a fraudulent or erroneous instruction before funds leave the company's control.
- **Critical operational caveat:** This pre-settlement preventive capacity is genuine *only* if the payment gateway enforces a clearing cut-off or pending-ratification hold; otherwise, a brief processing window exposes the firm to settlement racing ahead of review.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

1. **AP-202 (EUR 480,000; New vendor, 2h bank change, self-override)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Insider-assisted vendor diversion—u23 created or redirected a new vendor's bank details to a relative's account and bypassed both operator limits and bank-change warnings using m09's override credential.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Enforcing non-shared MFA/hardware-token authentication on override credentials *and* hard-blocking same-user self-overrides at the system level, backed by mandatory pre-release L3+ independent MG-4 verification of bank ownership.
   - **What remains exposed:** Even with separate credentials, if a direct supervisor (m09) is negligent or collusive due to reporting ties, pre-release approval fails; only independent L3+ segregation and timely RA-7 remain.
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Residual risk drops from **Realized/Critical to Low**. An independent releaser without conflict would see a new vendor, a 2-hour-old bank change, and missing receipt confirmation, refusing release absent verified documentation.

2. **AP-204 (EUR 220,000; Consulting payment, fabricated receipt, shared workstation)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Either (a) collusion between u12 and u13 to push through a fictitious consulting invoice using a forged service receipt, or (b) u12 (or u13) exploiting an unlocked session on the shared workstation to self-approve.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Eliminating shared-workstation session reuse (per-action re-authentication) breaks unilateral impersonation; independent MG-4 verification with the business-unit recipient (confirming consulting deliverables) breaks two-clerk collusion.
   - **What remains exposed:** AV-2 remains blind to service delivery; without business-owner receipt confirmation, two colluding AP clerks could still pass nominal four-eyes and AV-2 checks.
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Residual risk drops from **High (Realized defect) to Low–Moderate** against single-actor impersonation, *provided* the independent releaser verifies authentic service-recipient sign-off rather than trusting an unverified PDF receipt.

3. **AP-206 (EUR 95,000; Production outage, token 2 min late, MG-4 before AV-2)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Opportunistic exploitation of an operational outage to push an unauthorized or inflated vendor charge while emergency protocols are rushed and control sequence is inverted.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Requiring AV-2 completion *before* MG-4 approval (so the manager sees automated flags) and enforcing hard system gating so an emergency token must exist *prior* to `Prepare_Payment`.
   - **What remains exposed:** Because m11's review depth is undocumented, an under-pressure rubber-stamped approval could miss an inflated outage invoice even if vendor/bank master data are clean.
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Residual risk shifts from **Low–Moderate to Low**; a second independent operator eliminates emergency single-user reliance entirely and removes token-timing ambiguity, though receipt verification still requires commercial diligence.

4. **AP-207 (EUR 35,000; Safety repair, post-release/pre-settlement MG-4)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Timing race condition—a compromised or erroneous payment released by u61 clears via an early bank batch before m15 completes ratification at 14:40.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Technical settlement hold (or pre-release MG-4 as in AP-203) ensuring funds cannot enter bank clearing prior to m15's sign-off.
   - **What remains exposed:** Narrow 35-minute pre-ratification window if payment rails lack a hold mechanism, plus residual collusion risk between u61 and m15 (mitigated here by m15 independence and <24h RA-7 audit).
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Residual risk narrows from **Low (timing-dependent) to Negligible–Low**, eliminating the post-release/pre-settlement gap altogether.

5. **AP-208 (EUR 900,000; AV-2 unavailable, 3-day bank change, post-settlement MG-4)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Business Email Compromise (BEC) or vendor-impersonation bank diversion exploiting the AV-2 outage to route EUR 900,000 to an attacker-controlled account changed three days earlier.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Fail-closed AV-2 design (blocking high-value or recent-bank-change releases during outages unless an independent L3+ manager signs a verified out-of-band callback checklist *before* release).
   - **What remains exposed:** With fail-open execution, unsigned checklists, and post-settlement ratification, the firm carries **Critical** unmitigated exposure—only luck prevented a EUR 900,000 loss.
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Residual risk drops from **Critical to Moderate** (still Moderate unless the second releaser executes mandatory out-of-band bank-change verification to compensate for the AV-2 outage, since two operators can both miss a spoofed bank change without callback verification).

6. **AP-209 (EUR 42,000; Repeated u12/u13 pair on shared workstation)**
   - **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Structural erosion of segregation—if u12/u13 routinely share a terminal without step-up re-authentication, one operator can initiate and release payments under both IDs, masking unilateral fraud behind nominal compliance.
   - **Which control breaks the chain:** Disabling shared-workstation cross-login without hardware/biometric re-authentication at `Release_Payment`, plus periodic rotation of operator pairs.
   - **What remains exposed:** Until organizational design and workstation session logs are verified, every clean-looking u12/u13 payment carries **Moderate** latent uncertainty (highlighted by AP-204's fabricated receipt).
   - **Effect of a truly independent second releaser:** Moving release to an unassociated operator on a separate workstation reduces residual risk from **Moderate (Indeterminate) to Negligible–Low**.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Effective governance requires distinguishing **automated alerts** (risk indicators warranting investigation) from **proof of misconduct** (corroborated forensic evidence establishing intent, policy breach, or falsification).

|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Same-User Release Rate** | Alert on any completed same-user payment without a pre-existing valid ED-1 token; track % per operator/unit weekly (spike $>2\%$ or repeat non-emergency convenience like AP-201). | Emergency token creation timestamp vs. `Prepare_Payment`, outage ticket, ED-1 checklist. | **Alert:** Same-user execution without token (could be logging delay as in AP-206 or honest mistake). **Proof:** Confirmed non-emergency convenience or deliberate token backdating. |

Override Rate & Self-Override** | Real-time alert on any AV-2 override; **critical block/alert** if override credential owner != active releaser or override precedes same-user release (AP-202). | Override credential MFA logs, IP/terminal ID, manager approval ticket, bank-change log. | **Alert:** Elevated override frequency or mismatched user/credential. **Proof:** Auth logs/admissions showing credential sharing or unauthorized use of supervisor token. |

### PM4Py Version Behavior Notes
1. **`filter_four_eyes_principle` polarity and activity scope:** In standard PM4Py (2.7+), `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")` returns the subset of **violating** cases where both activities occur and share the same `org:resource`. Cases lacking `Release_Payment` (such as AP-205, which ends at `Cancel_Payment`) are excluded by the filter rather than flagged as attempted violations—hence the separate check on `Automated_Validate_Block` / `Attempt_Release`.
2. **DataFrame vs. `EventLog` compatibility:** In PM4Py 2.3+, `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` accepts a formatted pandas `DataFrame` directly via the top-level API. Older PM4Py 2.2.x releases may require converting with `pm4py.convert_to_event_log(log)` or importing from `pm4py.algo.filtering.pandas.ltl.ltl_checker`.
3. **Timestamp & grouping nuances:** `pm4py.format_dataframe` enforces UTC-normalized timestamps and sorts events chronologically, while pandas `groupby(...).apply(..., include_groups=False)` behaves cleanly in pandas 2.2+ (if running pandas `<2.2`, remove `include_groups=False`). Notice also that for AP-208, no explicit emergency token timestamp exists in the CSV or table, so the script uses a placeholder pre-prepare timestamp (`14:50`) solely to test how `ed1_eligible_prelim` still rejects AP-208 on amount, bank change, and AV-2 unavailability.
