| `claimant_channel` | Proxy / lineage verification | medium | Not in global top-12; likely registration-time, but no lineage note. May proxy access, broker behavior, or process routing. | Verify point-in-time registration capture; keep only with fairness/robustness review; avoid causal claims. |
| `final_decision` | Post-`t0` outcome leakage | critical | Settlement decision occurs after evidence, fraud investigation, and damage assessment; unknown at `t0`. | Remove from `t0` explanations. Use only as an outcome/label. |
| `initial_reserve` | Registration-time if frozen; otherwise proxy/leakage | medium | If initial reserve is set at registration and never revised before `t0`, it is available. If mutable, it may leak later adjustments. | Verify timestamp and freeze semantics. If valid, use as a severity proxy with fairness/robustness review. |
| `risk_score_current` | Mutable untimestamped feature | critical | May have been recalculated after `t0`; no feature timestamp was stored. | Store `risk_score_at_t0` with as-of timestamp; exclude the current value from `t0` explanations. |
| `last_resource_role` | Complete-trace future leakage | critical | Means the last role in the complete case, not the role at `t0`. | Replace with `last_resource_role_before_t0` or exclude. |
| `base value and other features` | Opaque aggregate / auditability | medium | Aggregated residual hides feature-level leakage or proxies and cannot be audited. | Require full local decomposition and prefix-only recomputation before interpretation. |

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should test both data lineage and process semantics.

**Prefix-time availability**
- Reconstruct the admissible prefix `P_t0` as all events with timestamp `< t0`, plus events at `t0` only if their sequence index precedes or equals the first Coverage Check Completed event.
- Feature computations must be as-of joins against `P_t0`: no event after `t0`, no complete-trace aggregate, no future snapshot.
- Test each feature definition for hidden time travel: `total`, `anywhere`, `final`, `full_trace`, `last`, `current`, and closure-populated fields are red flags.
- Concrete SQL checks: `claim_created_at < t0`, not `<= extraction_date`; document counts must use document event time `<= t0`; queue snapshots must have snapshot time `<= t0` or explicitly tolerated near-`t0` lag.
- Required output: per-feature as-of timestamp, source event-time column, and pass/fail for `event_time <= t0`.

**Activity-order feasibility**
- Use a normative BPMN/DFG/footprint model and replay each prefix with alignment conformance checking.
- For each explanation feature or pattern, label involved activities as completed, enabled, future-possible, or impossible at `t0`.
- After first Coverage Check Completed, completed activities are Claim Registered and Coverage Check Completed. Damage Assessment Completed and Documents Requested may be enabled depending on route. Fraud Investigation Opened may be future-possible after a trigger. Settlement Decision Recorded, Appeal, and Claim Closed are not enabled at that point.
- Example: `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is not process-impossible in general, but it is future and only legitimate after a Partial/Deny settlement followed by an appeal. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is process-inconsistent in that order and must be rejected unless there is overwhelming sequence-index evidence, which would still need semantic review.
- Limitation: frequent DFG edges are not normative rules. A DFG may show common co-occurrence but cannot prove enablement. Alignment can over-penalize parallel fraud investigation or valid repeated cycles.

**Same-timestamp ambiguity**
- Require a reliable sequence index for events with identical timestamps.
- If the sequence index is missing, treat same-timestamp events as partially ordered sets, not as direct-follows.
- Test any direct-follows explanation under all total orders consistent with the timestamps. If the explanation depends on an arbitrary order, reject it.
- Case Z is the canonical failure: Claim Closed and Damage Assessment Completed share a timestamp, so `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` cannot be trusted.

**Complete-trace feature detection**
- Scan feature lineage for full-case aggregates: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, and closure-populated `final_handler_team`.
- Recompute prefix-only equivalents where possible: `num_document_requests_before_t0`, `prefix_variant_id`, `last_resource_role_before_t0`, `handler_team_at_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`.
- If no point-in-time equivalent exists, exclude the feature from `t0` explanations.

**Explanation enabled/impossible/future-possible checks**
- Map every local SHAP item and sequence pattern to the process state at `t0`.
- Reject explanations whose highest contributions are activities that are impossible or merely future-possible at `t0`.
- Distinguish “future-possible” from “enabled”: Fraud Investigation may be future-possible, but it is not known unless opened before `t0`. Appeal is not enabled until after Partial/Deny settlement.
- Do not present predictive associations as causal or actionable. A feature can be important but still not interventable at `t0`.

**Repeated Settlement Decision events**
- Validate that a second Settlement Decision Recorded is semantically legitimate only when preceded by an Appeal and an earlier Partial or Deny decision.
- Check ordering: initial Settlement Decision -> Appeal -> second Settlement Decision.
- Flag a repeated Settlement Decision without Appeal as a duplicate, data-quality issue, or same-timestamp ambiguity.
- Do not treat the repeated decision label as a single impossible activity; it can be legitimate, but only in the correct appeal sequence.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal**
- Examples: `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and `initial_reserve` if frozen at registration.
- May claim: at `t0`, these variables are associated with breach risk and may improve ranking or triage.
- May not claim: they cause breach or that changing them is operationally actionable. Complexity, prior history, and reserve are case descriptors, not proven delay mechanisms.

**Process-state or congestion signal**
- Examples: `queue_length_at_t0`, repaired `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `num_document_requests_before_t0`, `prefix_variant_id`, and `last_resource_role_before_t0`.
- May claim: current workload, evidence availability, or prefix path state is associated with breach risk and may support operational monitoring.
- May not claim: queue length or a frequent DFG edge causally determines breach. These are state indicators, not policy levers unless tested causally.

**Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review**
- Examples: `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and possibly `handler_team_at_t0`.
- May claim: these are predictive proxies that may reflect access, network coverage, socioeconomic context, routing, or case severity.
- May not claim: region or channel causes delay. Use requires calibration/error review across groups, proxy-risk analysis, and mitigation if adverse operational decisions are derived.

**Drift/calendar proxy**
- Examples: `calendar_week`, employee experience if used, holiday-adjacent periods, and policy-change periods.
- May claim: these capture seasonal load, holiday effects, staffing, or operating-policy changes.
- May not claim: a particular week is a stable causal driver of breach. Monitor drift and avoid week-specific causal narratives.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**
- Examples: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, original `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, original `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, original `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and `last_resource_role`.
- May claim: nothing at `t0` until recomputed with point-in-time semantics.
- After repair, some may move into defensible predictive or process-state classes. Others, such as complete-trace outcomes, should remain excluded from early prediction.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Enforce a prediction-time contract.** Define `t0`, the admissible prefix, and a formal list of allowed feature families. Separate labels and post-`t0` outcomes from predictors in the feature store and model pipeline.

2. **Build a point-in-time feature store.** Store event time, ingestion time, and as-of timestamp for every feature. Require as-of joins for queue snapshots, risk scores, handler/team assignment, document counts, prior claim counts, and resource roles.

3. **Recompute all features prefix-only.** Replace complete-trace features with prefix equivalents: `num_document_requests_before_t0`, `prefix_variant_id`, `last_resource_role_before_t0`, `handler_team_at_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`, and `documents_received_before_t0`. Exclude features that cannot be made point-in-time.

4. **Add process-conformance tests.** Use BPMN/DFG/footprint and declarative checks to validate activity order, enabledness, and impossible edges. Treat frequent DFG edges as observations, not normative rules. Add same-timestamp partial-order checks and repeated Settlement Decision checks.

5. **Recalibrate after feature removal.** Removing leaked features changes score distributions. Re-estimate calibration curves, threshold behavior, and subgroup error rates using time-based validation.

6. **Test explanation stability.** Recompute local explanations under bootstrap resampling, timestamp tolerance, and feature perturbations. Reject explanations that depend on missing sequence indexes, near-`t0` snapshots, or future activities.

7. **Run proxy and fairness analysis.** Audit `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and any team-allocation proxy. Measure calibration and error across groups, test proxy substitutions, and avoid causal or adverse operational claims without evidence.

8. **Monitor schema and process drift.** Alert on missing sequence indexes, late-arriving events, snapshot lag, changed SQL semantics, holiday/policy effects, queue definition changes, and new activity labels. Revalidate explanations whenever the process model or feature lineage changes.
