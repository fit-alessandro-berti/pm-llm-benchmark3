## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm distinguishes four relation statuses, and each demands a different diagnostic posture.

- **Forbidden behavior** is the only closed-world category besides mandatory columns in scope. If the norm forbids an edge (N3, T6, E10) and the observation shows it, we have a violation unless there is strong evidence of a data-quality artifact. Confidence must be high before severity is high.
- **Missing mandatory behavior** is a violation only when the scope clearly applies. Counts of *Nk* versus *N* matter: 98/100 (N1) is not the same failure mode as 0/100, because open-world nuances (unrecorded intermediate events, abstraction mismatch) can legitimately swallow a single directly-follows hop.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** covers edges that appear in the log but are not mentioned by any norm ID (e.g., `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Issue Invoice -> Write Off`). These are observations, not violations.
- **Typical-pattern absence** is *not* a violation by itself. N2, N5, and T3 are explicitly “typical,” meaning the norm licenses legitimate exceptions (intermediate invoice events, write-offs, unapplied cash).
- **Likely data-quality error** is a separate epistemic bucket. The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice join, the unlabeled unapplied Payment, the two Dispatch events without Delivery relations, and the missing `Add Item -> Order` edge on two events are all plausible data-quality or join artifacts that the norm cannot resolve.
- **Abstraction mismatch** applies when a mandatory relation exists in reality but is invisible to the directly-follows summary (e.g., a Create Order  Review  Validate Order chain that omits the intermediate hop).
- **Insufficient evidence** applies when only aggregated counts are available and per-case inspection is required to distinguish a violation from a data error.

Open-world assumptions dominate: anything not explicitly forbidden, or mandatory within its scope, is admissible. Closed-world assumptions apply narrowly to N3, T6, E10, and to mandatory ET–OT edges in their stated scopes.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

**OC-DFG.** The directly-follows abstraction collapses chains that the business treats as “eventually expected.” N1 (Create Order  Validate Order) is mandatory, but the 98/100 ratio is consistent with the stated possibility of an unrecorded review event between creation and validation; a directly-follows gap is therefore not yet a mandatory violation, only a candidate. N2 (Validate Order  Close Order) is *typical*: the 83 Orders that close after validation are a subset, the remainder presumably invoiced first or cancelled. The 2 Orders with `Cancel Order  Dispatch Package` is the only directly-follows pattern that maps cleanly onto a forbidden rule (N3) and is the strongest OC-DFG signal in the log. N6 (Reopen  Close) is explicitly unspecified, so its occurrence carries no normative weight.

**OTG.** The object-interaction graph shows high-weight edges that are mandatory (T1, T2) or typical (T3). The 1450-weight T1 edge and 1448 of those also producing an Order edge (E2) is a small but real gap: 2 `Add Item` events relate an Item but not the Order they belong to. This is a likely ETL artifact rather than a business rule break. The 200-weight T2 edge vs 198 Delivery objects (E6) is a stronger mandatory gap: 2 Dispatch events lack a Delivery object, but the context says carrier attributes are present, which makes a tracking/join alternative plausible. T6 (Order  cobirth Payment, weight 1) is the only forbidden OTG edge and warrants immediate inspection. T4 and T5 are explicitly unspecified, so their presence (100 Customer edges, 3 RiskCase edges) is informational, not normative.

**ET–OT.** The ET–OT table surfaces the two highest-impact findings outside the OC-DFG: E10 (Sensor Heartbeat  Invoice, weight 1) and the 92 vs 93 gap on Record Payment. The norm is closed-world on forbidden ET–OT edges (E10) and on mandatory ET–OT columns in their stated scope, so E10 looks like a hard violation until the documented identifier-join hypothesis is examined. The Record Payment gap is a typical-pattern candidate because the additional context says unapplied cash is possible, but E8 is mandatory *except* events explicitly typed as unapplied cash, and none are so typed; that is a precise rule with a precise exception, and the unlabeled status is the issue, not the absence.

The recurring danger is treating “eventually expected” as “directly-follows.” N1 is a mandatory *eventual* relation that the OC-DFG approximates; the abstraction can miss intermediate review steps, and 2/100 is statistically small. Likewise, T2 is mandatory *for every Dispatch Package*, but a Delivery object could legitimately be created in a downstream system that this log does not cover. The right response is to look at the case-level trace, not to convict the abstraction.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# --- Discovery ---
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)              # returns (ocdfg, start_activities, end_activities)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)                  # object-centric temporal graph
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)                # event-to-object tuples

# --- Conformance diagnostics ---
# Each call returns a diagnostics object. We preserve the raw output
# rather than overwriting it with semantic labels.
cd_diag = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
otg_diag = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
etot_diag = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

# --- Semantic classification layer ---
# norm_ocdfg / norm_otg / norm_etot are expected to be dictionaries
# whose VALUES carry a 'status' field (mandatory | forbidden |
# typical | unspecified) and a 'scope' field. PM4Py's own graph
# structures do not encode these statuses; they must be maintained
# alongside, not inside, the discovered graphs.

# Helper: bucket an observed edge using the normative status.
def classify_edge(edge, norm_table, observed_count):
    spec = norm_table.get(edge)              # may be None (unregulated)
    if spec is None:
        return "unregulated", observed_count
    status = spec["status"]
    if status == "forbidden" and observed_count > 0:
        return "forbidden_violation", observed_count
    if status == "mandatory" and observed_count == 0:
        return "missing_mandatory", 0
    if status == "typical" and observed_count == 0:
        return "typical_absence", 0
    if status == "mandatory" and observed_count > 0:
        return "mandatory_satisfied", observed_count
    if status == "typical" and observed_count > 0:
        return "typical_satisfied", observed_count
    return "unregulated", observed_count

# Build classification tables without touching PM4Py outputs.
ocdfg_classification = {
    edge: classify_edge(edge, norm_ocdfg, cdfg_count)
    for edge, cdfg_count in ocdfg["edges"].items()
}

otg_classification = {
    edge: classify_edge(edge, norm_otg, w)
    for edge, w in otg["edges"].items()
}

etot_classification = {
    edge: classify_edge(edge, norm_etot, w)
    for edge, w in etot["items()
}

# Note: forbidden edges that survive a data-quality filter can still
# be downgraded to 'suspected_data_quality' here, leaving the raw
# PM4Py diagnostics untouched for audit purposes.
```

The block keeps the raw PM4Py diagnostic output (`cd_diag`, `otg_diag`, `etot_diag`) intact and layers a separate semantic classification on top, keyed off the `status` field that lives in the normative tables, not inside the discovered graphs.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 (Create Order -> Validate Order) scope.** The norm says “every newly created standard Order” but does not define *standard* (e.g., draft vs. standard, B2B vs. consumer). Operational risk: false positives during draft-cancellation flows. Clarify by enumerating order subtypes; status: *mandatory* for standard orders, *unspecified* otherwise.
2. **N2 (Validate Order -> Close Order) intermediate events.** The norm names “intermediate invoice, review, or fulfilment events” but does not enumerate them. Operational risk: any new intermediate event looks suspicious by default. Clarify with an allow-list; status: *typical* with an explicit allow-list.
3. **N3 (Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package).** “Same Order” is ambiguous about time windows (cancel-then-reverse-cancel, partial cancellations). Operational risk: misclassification of complex cancellation flows. Clarify by defining temporal scope; status: *forbidden* with a clear window definition.
4. **N5 (Issue Invoice -> Record Payment) exceptions.** “Credit notes, write-offs, and open credit terms” are listed, but the procedure for *labeling* an event as unapplied cash is not standardized. Operational risk: E8-style gaps recur. Clarify that unapplied-cash events must be typed explicitly; status: *mandatory* that every Record Payment be typed, *typical* that most eventually match an Invoice.
5. **T2 (Package, object_interaction, Delivery) carrier-only scenarios.** The norm says every Dispatch Package needs a Delivery, but the additional context shows carrier references can substitute. Operational risk: false alarms on carrier-only dispatch. Clarify whether a carrier attribute counts as a Delivery proxy; status: *mandatory* unless carrier reference exists.
6. **Codeath and reuse semantics.** `object_codeath` (Package–Delivery, 7 cases) and identifier-join artifacts (Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice) are not addressed. Operational risk: spurious forbidden-edge alarms. Clarify by either *forbidding* codeath between Package and Delivery or marking it *unspecified*; recommend *unspecified* with a separate identity-management policy.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment (prioritize Ranks 1–2).**
- Quarantine the 2 Orders with `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`; freeze further shipments on those packages and recall or reroute any in transit. Inspect the 1 Order–Payment cobirth case for fraud/compliance referral.

**Data repair.**
- Investigate the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge; almost certainly an identifier-join collision (per context). Refactor the join to use a composite key.
- Audit the 2 Dispatch events lacking Delivery objects; reconcile carrier references and, if a Delivery is created downstream, backfill.
- Audit the 1 unlabeled unapplied Payment; either relabel or attach an Invoice.
- Audit the 2 Add Item events lacking an Order binding; reconcile Item-to-Order mapping.

**Process investigation.**
- Case-level inspection of the 2 Orders missing `Create Order -> Validate Order` to determine whether validation happened through a path not captured by the directly-follows summary.
- Confirm with finance that the 18 Invoices without `Record Payment` are legitimate exceptions (write-off, open credit terms, or genuine overdue).

**Norm revision.**
- Define *standard Order* for N1.
- Enumerate allowed intermediate events for N2.
- Add temporal scope to N3 (window within which dispatch after cancel is forbidden).
- Formalize unapplied-cash typing to make E8 enforceable.

**Monitoring.**
- Track N3, T6, and E10 as hard controls with zero-tolerance dashboards (with a data-quality override path).
- Track N1, T2, E2, E8 at near-zero tolerance but with scope awareness.
- Track N5 and T3 at typical-pattern tolerances; alert only on aging breaches.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

- **Robust conclusions:** the 2 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` edges are a clear norm N3 violation; the 4 Write Offs and 14 open Invoices are legitimate typical-pattern absences under N5; the 83/98 split on ValidateClose / CreateValidate is within an acceptable abstraction margin; the 3 Manual Compliance Review–Customer edges and the 7 Package–Delivery codeath edges are not violations.
- **Conclusions dependent on norm incompleteness:** the 2 missing `Create Order -> Validate Order` edges, the 2 missing Delivery objects on Dispatch, and the 1 unlabeled unapplied Payment are conditional on clarifications to N1, T2, and E8 that the norm has not yet made.
- **Conclusions dependent on suspected data-quality issues:** the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge (likely a join artifact), the 1 Order–Payment cobirth (possible coinherited id), and possibly the 2 Add Item events without Orders (ETL gap) all carry meaningful alternative explanations.
- **What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** per-case validation of the 2 missing CreateValidate edges, the precise scope of the 2 CancelDispatch cases (label error vs. real violation), the truth status of the Order–Payment cobirth, and the carrier-vs-Delivery policy on the 2 Dispatch events. These require case-level inspection, not aggregated counts.
