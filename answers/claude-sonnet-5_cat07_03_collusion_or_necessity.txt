# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Create PO + Approve PO four-eyes violations (Carla/Dan/Gita)**
- Benign 1: Legitimate emergency delegation — emergency_flag=True in 88%/79% of these cases suggests an authorized rush channel. *Supporting evidence:* documented emergency-approval policy naming Gita/Hugo as delegated approvers; timestamps aligned with genuine incident/repair tickets.
- Benign 2: Specialization — Carla (medical) and Dan (facilities) are the designated buyers for narrow categories with few alternate approvers available out-of-hours. *Supporting evidence:* roster/on-call schedules showing limited approver pool on weekends.
- What would refute: absence of any emergency ticket/ITSM reference, or override use for routine low-value purchases without justification.

**Fast approvals (CarlaGita, DanHugo)**
- Benign 1: Genuine urgency (medical stockouts, weekend facility failures) shortens legitimate review time.
- Benign 2: Pre-negotiated/standing terms for recurring emergency vendors reduce review workload.
- What would refute: fast approvals on high-value, non-emergency-flagged cases, or approvals with no supporting documentation attached.

**Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval (Lena/Kim, vendor family V-77x)**
- Benign 1: Documented mass-maintenance migration to a new banking platform explains clustered bank-detail changes. *Supporting evidence:* migration project ticket, vendor notification records, platform cut-over date matching event timestamps.
- Benign 2: Regional role coverage — Lena/Kim may be the only staffed pair for that vendor family/region, creating unavoidable overlap. *Supporting evidence:* staffing roster, regional assignment matrix.
- What would refute: bank changes to accounts not linked to the migration project, or third-party/personal account numbers, or absence of vendor confirmation records.

**Subcontracting loops (GitaCarla, HugoDan, LenaKim)**
- Benign 1: Iterative clarification (query raised, buyer responds, approver finalizes) is a normal workflow for complex/urgent cases.
- Benign 2: System design requires a round-trip step (e.g., comment resolution) regardless of risk.
- What would refute: loops that always end in approval without any substantive comment/change logged, or loops used to circumvent a rejection.

**Shared account "Approver_Gita" / Bot_PO mapping error**
- Benign 1: Shared login is a known IT/access-management shortcut for a small team, not necessarily concealment.
- Benign 2: Legacy ERP mapping table bug is a documented, systemic data issue affecting many unrelated cases equally.
- What would refute: evidence the shared account was used specifically to obscure who authorized specific high-risk transactions (e.g., selectively used only for the V-77x cases).

# 3. Case prioritization

**High priority (targeted case-level review)**
- VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x (44 cases, especially the 9 four-eyes violations and 16 fast approvals within manual_upload).
- Reason: combines a genuine SoD breach type (bank change + payment approval), vendor concentration, manual-system prevalence, and a tight subcontracting loop — the only cluster where multiple independent risk indicators stack.
- Documents to inspect first: bank-change audit trail (old vs. new account numbers), vendor confirmation/callback records, mass-maintenance migration ticket and its linkage (or lack thereof) to each case ID, entered_by vs. approved_by identity verification, source_system logs for manual_upload entries.

**Medium priority (sample-based review)**
- Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification same-person cases (17), especially the 12 batch-entered at quarter close.
- Buyer_Carla+Gita and Buyer_Dan+Hugo emergency PO clusters (126 and 104 cases) — sample a subset of non-emergency-flagged or high-amount-band cases within these clusters rather than the whole population.
- Reason: control-design weaknesses and possible data-quality artifacts (batch timestamps) rather than concrete SoD/financial-diversion risk; still warrants confirming emergency justification is genuine and timely.
- Documents to inspect first: original justification source records (pre-migration), ITSM/incident tickets, timestamps of underlying events vs. system-entry timestamps, delegation-of-authority policy documents.

**Low priority / monitoring only**
- Buyer_Aline/Approver_Elena and Buyer_Boris/Approver_Felix routine clusters (510/495 cases) — high volume but low emergency flag, low amount band, low late-justification rate.
- VendorMaster_Marc + AP_Iris (41 cases) — ordinary onboarding pattern, low manual-system share, low vendor concentration.
- Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details same-person pattern (31 cases) — likely role design; flag for policy review rather than case-level audit.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern:**
- Bank-detail changes in the Lena/Kim cluster that do not map to the known V-77x platform migration ticket (i.e., unexplained changes to unrelated vendors or non-published account numbers).
- Payment amounts routed to accounts not matching vendor master records, or post-change payments exceeding pre-change historical averages.
- Fast approvals (Carla/Gita, Dan/Hugo) occurring on cases with emergency_flag=False or low-severity purchase_category.
- Evidence that the shared "Approver_Gita" account was used selectively to mask which individual approved specific high-risk (non-emergency) transactions.
- Retrospective justifications that are substantively identical/copy-pasted text across unrelated cases, beyond timestamp clustering.
- Any case where Bot_PO/human mapping error coincides with unusually high-value approvals rather than routine ones.

**Would reduce concern:**
- A verifiable, timestamped migration project record linking each V-77x bank-detail change to an approved mass-maintenance ticket.
- On-call/roster documentation confirming Gita and Hugo were the only authorized emergency approvers during the flagged windows.
- IT confirmation that the legacy_erp mapping error is systemic and affects a random cross-section of cases, not concentrated on sensitive transactions.
- Vendor confirmation callbacks or secondary authentication logs for bank-detail changes.
- Access logs showing distinct login sessions/IP addresses for "assistant" users under the shared Gita account, with an audit-friendly reason for the shared credential.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

This review examined procurement four-eyes exceptions, timing anomalies, and resource-network patterns. No direct evidence of fraud or collusion was found; the observed patterns are consistent with a mixture of legitimate emergency handling, informal role specialization, and data-quality limitations, alongside a smaller number of control exceptions warranting targeted follow-up.

The subgroup involving VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim (vendor family V-77x) is the most concerning: it combines a same-person bank-detail-change/payment-approval sequence, high vendor concentration, a tight approval-correction loop, and heavy reliance on manual data entry. A documented platform migration plausibly explains much of this pattern, but the missing linkage between maintenance tickets and case records prevents confirmation and should be treated as a control gap requiring remediation regardless of intent.

Emergency-related four-eyes exceptions (Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita, Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo) appear largely attributable to legitimate rush-handling of medical and facilities emergencies, evidenced by high emergency-flag rates; these should be distinguished as control exceptions rather than misconduct, pending confirmation of delegation authority.

Data-quality issues — a shared approver account, a legacy-system mapping error conflating bot and human resources, and batch-entered justification timestamps — materially limit confidence in several derived statistics and should be corrected before further quantitative conclusions are drawn. We recommend targeted document-level review of the V-77x cluster, confirmation of emergency delegation policy, and remediation of the identified data-mapping issues, with no findings currently supporting an allegation of misconduct against any individual.
