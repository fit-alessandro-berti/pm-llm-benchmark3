## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 puts a second human decision between payment preparation and release. For that decision to reduce risk, the reviewer must be able to challenge the instruction, verify the evidence, and stop the transfer. ED-1 permits specified single-operator payments while retaining independent oversight and timely review.

| Objective or threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent one individual from initiating and authorizing a payment, including through another person's credentials | Can block an instruction that fails a covered check. A pass says nothing about who controlled the accounts or who authorized the transfer. | Prevents unilateral authorization when a qualified, independent manager authenticates separately, reviews the specific instruction, and can withhold it. | Can detect credential misuse or self-approval if access and transaction records support attribution. Can enable cancellation or recall while available. | Limits single-operator use and requires independent approval. Token, account use, and review require separate evidence. |
| Prevent duplicates, basic PO mismatches, out-of-limit payments, and sanctions violations | Screens all four before release. A block is preventive. A warning identifies a concern whose effect depends on the override control. | Can challenge a flagged item against source evidence and withhold approval. A manager approval alone gives no assurance that each screening check ran. | Can detect escaped defects through re-performance and help recover the funds if time permits. A prohibited transfer already made remains a breach. | Requires a full pass without override and caps the amount. It adds no screening coverage of its own. |
| Prevent payment to the wrong or diverted bank account | Checks account ownership and changes in the previous seven days. Results depend on the quality of account and change data. | Can verify the account against a trusted source and stop payment before commitment. Reviewing the same compromised record adds little assurance. | Can detect diversion through independent verification or reconciliation and initiate a timely recall. | Excludes recent account changes and vendors active for fewer than 180 days. An established vendor can still be impersonated. |
| Pay only for genuine obligations, including actual delivery and commercially reasonable terms | A basic PO match helps identify inconsistencies. Receipt authenticity, actual delivery, and commercial reasonableness are outside coverage. | Can check service or receipt evidence and challenge terms using independent sources. A cursory review or fabricated supporting record can defeat it. | Can confirm delivery or test the source of receipts. Recovery depends on the bank window or subsequent legal and commercial remedies. | Requires approval and audit but adds no direct check of receipt authenticity or commercial terms. |
| Resist collusion, rubber-stamping, and conflicts of interest | Still screens objective parameters. It cannot identify an agreement to approve a sham expense. | An independent reviewer with separate access and evidence can challenge the instruction. A conflicted reviewer or common control of credentials undermines that protection. | Can detect linked patterns, common devices, or forged evidence if the auditor has reliable records and can corroborate them. Patterns alone cannot establish collusion. | Requires independence and prompt audit. Permission and recorded activity names do not establish substantive review or separate human control. |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Assess three results separately. Record literal FEP-1 conformance on completed payments. For same-user payments, test ED-1 as a conjunction of all seven conditions and record each as evidenced, failed, or unresolved. Then assess the actual protection provided against the material risks in the case. A valid exception can meet the objective while the literal four-eyes actor test still shows a same-user payment.

1. **Timing and authority.** Establish when the payment became irrevocable, whether the reviewer could stop the exact instruction, and whether review happened while that power was usable. For audits, check completion and escalation time against the bank's actual deadline as well as the business-day requirement.
2. **Independence and collusion resistance.** Verify the human actors, reviewer level and authority, separate credentials, and any unresolved conflicts. Check whether the reviewer used evidence the operator could not unilaterally fabricate or amend. A different login is weak evidence where account sharing or coordinated approval is plausible.
3. **Coverage and enforcement.** Map the case's credible loss routes to specific checks. Verify validation scope, result, outage handling, and who can override. Confirm the reviewer inspected the relevant invoice, bank information, and receipt, and that the clearance applied to the released instruction, including any changes after approval.
4. **Recoverability.** Establish who could stop, recall, or recover the payment, how much time they had, and whether there was enough time to investigate and act. Distinguish theoretical recall eligibility from successful recovery. A timely audit alone does not establish either.
5. **Evidence quality.** Prefer authenticated, transaction-linked records, contemporaneous supporting documents, and bank acknowledgments. Distinguish a missing record from evidence that a condition failed. A legitimate outcome or a review with no findings cannot establish that the controls would have stopped an improper payment.

| Package rating | Decision rule |
|---|---|
| Fully equivalent | Evidence shows the package gives materially comparable protection to a genuinely independent second releaser for the relevant threats. Independent authority and effective stop capability operate before irrevocability, verification covers the key payment facts, and bypass and collusion risks are controlled to a comparable degree. |
| Partially equivalent | At least one material part of independent prevention is in place, but coverage, enforceability, timing, or evidence falls short in another material area. State which loss route remains open and whether the case-specific exposure is tolerable. |
| Merely mitigating | Validation, review, or recall reduces particular risks or potential loss without providing an adequate independent decision before commitment. A proven reduction in duplicate risk, for example, leaves unilateral approval for other instructions intact. |
| Ineffective | The package fails against the material threat because the relevant checks were absent, bypassed, conflicted, perfunctory, or too late to affect the outcome. Meaningful detection after the practical recovery window can still inform investigation. |

Equal expected loss can mask very different tail exposures, concentrations of override power, and risks of repeated abuse. It also cannot establish that mandatory approval rights, sanctions restrictions, or ED-1 conditions were respected. Record uncertainty where the evidence cannot support a material judgment.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive function | Detective function | Corrective function | Evidence that determines strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | Enforced pre-release blocks stop covered defects. Warning routes can preserve prevention if independent, authorized override is required. | Identifies covered anomalies and can log blocks, warnings, overrides, and service outages. | A block allows correction of the instruction or cancellation before release. The service itself does not retrieve funds. | The actual checks run, their inputs and results, override authentication, and the system's behavior when unavailable. |
| MG-4 | Effective before release or while the manager still has a reliable means of withholding settlement. | Substantive review can find discrepancies before or after release. Late ratification may detect issues but cannot change the authorization already exercised. | A manager can direct suspension or initiate recall while those options remain available. | Level, independence, reviewed evidence, exact payment approved, and enforceable stop authority. |
| RA-7 | Findings may inform future prevention. An audit between release and settlement can stop the current payment if it triggers an effective hold. | Audit can detect errors, unsupported payments, and policy breaches, including across cases. | A defect can be corrected if there is time and authority to stop, recall, or otherwise recover funds. | Completion, scope, bank cutoff, elapsed response time, and confirmation of recovery. |
| ED-1 | An enforced permission gate can limit access to the single-operator route using conditions that are knowable at the time. | Follow-up can identify missing approval or audit, late tokens, and repeated or inappropriate reliance on emergencies. | Any corrective capacity comes from the underlying stop or recall mechanisms. | Prior token activation, all eligibility facts, approval before settlement, and audit completion by the business-day deadline. |

Use the consequence as the reference point for prevention. A manager review after Release_Payment is detective with respect to the act of release. It is also preventive with respect to settlement when the manager can reliably hold or reject the exact transaction before the cutoff. That makes it a hybrid in a bank queue with a proven stop mechanism. A timestamp before settlement supplies evidence of opportunity. The queue rules, authority, and lead time determine whether that opportunity could prevent loss. After settlement, recall is corrective and depends on bank terms and response time.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

- Measure the **same-user rate** as same verified preparer and releaser divided by completed payments with attributable human actors. Display unattributable or shared-account cases separately and report their share of all completions. Break out ED-1 qualified, failed, and unresolved cases. Count blocked attempts separately. Retain token issue and activation times, approver identities and levels, and a versioned link between approval and the bank instruction.
- Track **override rate** per AV-2 warning and as a share of released payments, by failed check, operator, authenticated approver, and reason. Alert on override use by the preparer, a mismatch with the credential owner, or any override on the ED-1 route. Retain the original results and independently authenticated override record.
- Monitor **late manager approval** against both release and settlement. Isolate approvals after settlement, missing approvals, and approvals in the post-release interval where stop authority is unverified. Collect approval scope, conflicts, exact transaction details, bank queue status, and hold or reject permissions.
- Measure the share of applicable payments with **audit completed and actionable before the bank recall cutoff**. Flag audits started but unfinished near the cutoff, late escalations, and absent cutoff data. Monitor ED-1's one-business-day completion requirement separately. Collect bank acceptance, settlement, recall deadlines and responses, and audit start, completion, finding, and escalation times.
- Flag **repeated resource pairs** using ordered pair counts and their shares of each actor's work. Review concentration over comparable shifts, workload, vendor groups, and payment types. Investigate **shared workstations** with access records, separate authentication, and session attribution. Obtain staffing and payment-cell documentation and verify supporting receipts independently where the pattern overlaps with known defects.
- Monitor **bank-change proximity** using verified change and release timestamps. Flag any within-seven-day change on the ED-1 route and give changes shortly before release particular attention in ordinary processing. Preserve the account history, how ownership was verified, and what independent evidence linked the account to the vendor.
- Record **control outages** by duration, affected checks, and attempted, blocked, and completed payments. Alert on any release during an outage with no evidenced authorized alternative through the regular process. For each affected release, determine which verification steps ran and whether payment can still be held or recalled. A payment without fully operational validation is ineligible for ED-1.

An alert identifies a case for examination. A repeated pair can reflect assigned work, a workstation can be legitimately shared, and a token or device anomaly can reflect an attribution problem. Resolve the electronic records against authorization, staffing, and transaction evidence before attributing intent or misconduct.

        ("AP-208", 900000, True, True, "active, start unspecified", "unavailable",
         None, False, None),
        ("AP-209", 42000, True, False, "absent", "full pass",

    columns=[
        CASE, "amount_eur", "vendor_180d", "bank_change_7d", "token_evidence",
        "av_result", "manager_independence_supported", "audit_within_business_day",

for column in [
    "vendor_180d", "bank_change_7d", "manager_independence_supported",

    facts[column] = facts[column].astype("boolean")

manager = evidence["Manager_Approve"].combine_first(evidence["Manager_Ratify"])

def before(first, second):
    known = first.notna() & second.notna()
    return first.lt(second).astype("boolean").where(known)


evidence["manager_before_release"] = before(manager, evidence[RELEASE])
evidence["manager_before_settlement"] = before(manager, settlement)
day = pd.Timedelta(hours=24)
evidence["audit_event_before_release_plus_24h"] = before(audit, evidence[RELEASE] + day)
