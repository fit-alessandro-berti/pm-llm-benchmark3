## 1. Interaction-graph description

The evidence is best represented as a typed, temporal object-interaction graph rather than one flattened case graph.

Important node types:

- `Job`: J1–J6.
- `Component`: C1–C6.
- `ComponentLot`: L7, L8.
- `Machine`: CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1.
- `Tool`: FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.
- `Batch`: B1, B2.
- `Shipment`: S1, S2, S3.
- Optional but useful: `Event/Activity` nodes such as `Ready for batch`, `Batch Cure`, `Quarantine Lot`, `Release Lot`, `Acquire Tool`, `Release Tool`, `Final Test`, `Packed`, `Dispatch`.

Important edge types and qualifiers:

- Structural requirement edges: `requires`, `contains`, `batches`, `consolidates`.
- Resource-use edges: `uses`, `acquires`, `releases`, `occupies`, `calibrated_by`.
- Lifecycle edges: `ready_for`, `starts`, `packed`, `quarantined`, `released`, `dispatches`.
- Inferred coupling edges: `waits_for`, `blocked_by`, `synchronized_with`, `gated_by`.

Directly observed edges include the listed O2O relations and event-to-object relations such as the `Batch Cure` event relating B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3. Inferred edges include J4 waiting for FIX-A, J6 waiting for CAL-3, J1 waiting for S1’s late partner J4, and J5 being gated by L7.

Higher-order motifs:

1. Batch-barrier motif: `Batch` connects multiple `Job` objects, a `Machine`, and a `Tool`. B1 couples J1, J2, J3 to OVEN-1 and RACK-7; B2 couples J4, J5 to the same resources.
2. Lot fan-out motif: `ComponentLot` L7 contains C2 and C5, thereby coupling J2 and J5 through a quarantine/release event even when the job-flattened log lacks those events.
3. Shipment consolidation motif: `Shipment` S1 consolidates J1 and J4, so dispatch depends on the latest packed job, not the earliest.
4. Tool-mediated machine starvation motif: CNC-2 may be idle while J4 cannot start because FIX-A is held by J3.
5. Calibration interlock motif: TEST-1 is available, but `Final Test` requires CAL-3; recalibration blocks J6 after J5.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job flattening**  
A job flattening makes each job look like an independent case: Release Job  Stage Component  Machine Process  Inspect  Pack/Rework  Dispatch. It shows job-level durations and rework loops. It hides batch synchronization, tool waits, lot quarantine/release, and shipment-partner waiting. It can duplicate shared events across jobs or drop events that are not directly job-related. A misleading conclusion is that no job is abnormal because no total duration exceeds 1.8 standard deviations, while J1 still waits 5h 55m after packing and J1/J2 wait hours before B1.

**Machine flattening**  
A machine flattening shows machine utilization, sequences, and gaps. It makes CNC-2, OVEN-1, and TEST-1 look underutilized but not severely bottlenecked. It hides why machines are idle: waiting for FIX-A, RACK-7 cleaning, CAL-3 recalibration, batch synchronization, or shipment policy. A misleading conclusion is that low utilization implies excess machine capacity. In fact, OVEN-1 can be idle from 12:55 to 14:50 while J4 waits because RACK-7 and B2 synchronization constrain the start.

**Batch flattening**  
A batch flattening would show B1 and B2 lifecycles: load, cure, unload. It makes batch duration visible but hides the dispersion of job readiness before batch start. It may duplicate the single `Batch Cure` event across J1, J2, J3 or B2 jobs. A misleading conclusion is that batch processing is efficient because the cure event itself is short, while J1 and J2 waited almost four hours before the cure began.

**Tool flattening**  
A tool flattening would show FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3 holds, releases, cleaning, and calibration. It makes tool occupancy visible but may hide the jobs waiting outside the tool lifecycle. For example, FIX-A held by J3 from 09:00 to 11:40 appears as legitimate use, not as a blockage for J4. A misleading conclusion is that tools are not bottlenecks because their active use times are reasonable.

**ComponentLot flattening**  
A lot flattening would show L7 quarantine and release, and perhaps L8 status. It makes lot hold duration visible but may not connect lot events to jobs if integration rules are inconsistent. J2’s override may live in another system. A misleading conclusion is that lot events are quality-side events with little production impact, even though L7 couples J2 and J5 and may gate J5.

**Shipment flattening**  
A shipment flattening shows Create Shipment  Load  Dispatch. It makes shipment handling appear fast: 0.8h and 0.3h medians. It hides packed jobs waiting for consolidation partners. A misleading conclusion is that shipment is not a bottleneck, while J1 is packed at 13:30 and cannot dispatch until S1 leaves at 19:25.

Low machine utilization can coexist with long job waiting because the constraint is not machine busy time but synchronization, auxiliary tools, lot policy, or shipment consolidation. Machines may be starved by non-machine objects.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: J1 from component lot to batch to shipment

Objects: J1, C1, L8, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, S1, J4.

- Temporal precedence: J1 requires C1, which belongs to L8. J1 is ready for B1 at 08:10. B1 starts at 12:05. J1 is packed at 13:30. S1 dispatches at 19:25 after J4 is packed at 19:10.
- Resource feasibility: OVEN-1 and RACK-7 are part of the B1 cure event. No rack cleaning is recorded before B1, so the main observed feasibility constraint is batch readiness.
- Policy constraints: B1 batches J1, J2, J3 together. S1 consolidates J1 and J4. Both policies force early objects to wait for later objects.
- Causal speculation: J1’s long lead time is plausibly caused by batch synchronization before cure and shipment consolidation after packing, not by J1’s own processing difficulty.

### Narrative 2: J4 from fixture contention to batch delay to late shipment

Objects: J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A, J3, B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7, S1.

- Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 but starts machine processing at 11:45. J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10. RACK-7 is cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. J5 is ready at 14:40. B2 starts at 14:50. J4 is packed at 19:10 and S1 dispatches at 19:25.
- Resource feasibility: CNC-2 appears partly idle, but FIX-A is unavailable. OVEN-1 is idle before B2, but RACK-7 is not ready until 14:35.
- Policy constraints: No explicit `Wait for Tool` activity exists. B2 requires J4 and J5. S1 requires J1 and J4.
- Causal speculation: J4’s lateness may cascade from J3’s fixture hold, then from RACK-7 cleaning and B2 synchronization, and finally affect S1, which also delays J1.

### Narrative 3: J5 and J6 from lot quarantine to calibration delay

Objects: J5, C5, L7, J2, C2, B2, TEST-1, CAL-3, J6, S3.

- Temporal precedence: L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50. L7 contains C2 for J2 and C5 for J5. J2 enters B1 before release via override. J5 does not proceed until after release and is ready for B2 at 14:40. B2 starts at 14:50. J5 is ready for final test at 16:00 and tests 16:10–16:35. CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20. J6, ready at 16:05, tests at 17:25.
- Resource feasibility: TEST-1 is available during the calibration interval, but CAL-3 is not. J5’s progress appears gated by L7 release.
- Policy constraints: Lot release is required except where a supervisor override exists. Final test requires CAL-3. S3 consolidates J5 and J6.
- Causal speculation: L7 couples J2 and J5 asymmetrically; CAL-3 couples J5 and J6 by making J6 wait after J5’s test.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Dynamic batch release or split batching**  
   - Target mechanism: Batch synchronization.  
   - Expected benefit: Reduce J1’s 3h 55m and J2’s 3h 40m pre-batch waits; reduce J4’s B2 wait where feasible.  
   - Helped: J1, J2, J4, future early-ready jobs.  
   - Potentially harmed: J3 or J5 if left for a later batch; oven schedule stability; batch traceability.  
   - New cost/risk: More batch runs, higher energy/setup cost, possible quality or compliance issues with partial batches.  
   - Perverse metric: Maximizing oven utilization or batch fullness could recreate long synchronization waits.  
   - Object-centric evaluation: Compare readiness-to-batch-start waiting per job, batch size, batch count, and cure event relations before/after using OCEL batch-job-tool graphs.

2. **Auxiliary tool availability program**  
   - Target mechanism: Shared-tool contention for FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.  
   - Expected benefit: Reduce J4’s fixture-associated wait, B2’s rack-associated wait, and J6’s calibration-associated wait.  
   - Helped: J3, J4, J5, J6, CNC-2, TEST-1, OVEN-1.  
   - Potentially harmed: Jobs scheduled during tool maintenance or calibration windows if tools are taken offline.  
   - New cost/risk: Extra fixtures, racks, or calibration units; maintenance labor; risk of using uncalibrated or unqualified tools.  
   - Perverse metric: Tool utilization at 100% may encourage hoarding or delayed release.  
   - Object-centric evaluation: Track acquire/release/clean/calibrate events on tools and infer `waits_for` edges; compare machine idle time linked to tool unavailability.

3. **Shipment consolidation time-box or split shipment policy**  
   - Target mechanism: Shipment consolidation.  
   - Expected benefit: Reduce J1’s 5h 55m packed-to-dispatch wait.  
   - Helped: J1 and other early-packed jobs in consolidated shipments.  
   - Potentially harmed: Customers expecting consolidated delivery; transportation cost; warehouse loading schedules.  
   - New cost/risk: More partial shipments, higher freight cost, more complex order promises.  
   - Perverse metric: On-time shipment to a promised date can hide excessive early-job waiting if promises are padded.  
   - Object-centric evaluation: Measure packed-to-dispatch waiting per job, shipment partner lateness, consolidation ratio, and split-shipment cost using shipment-job edges.

4. **Improved logging and integration, not operations**  
   - Target mechanism: Data-integration artifacts and hidden waits.  
   - Expected benefit: Make batch, tool, lot, and shipment coupling visible; reduce managerial surprise.  
   - Helped: All jobs indirectly through better scheduling and diagnosis.  
   - Potentially harmed: Operators or systems burdened by extra logging; short-term throughput may dip.  
   - New cost/risk: Incomplete, delayed, or falsified event entry.  
   - Perverse metric: Percent events logged may encourage checkbox logging without accurate timestamps.  
   - Object-centric evaluation: Use interaction summaries to compare missing edge types before/after; validate inferred waits against explicit `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Batch`, and `Wait for Lot` events.

5. **Component-lot release policy redesign**  
   - Target mechanism: Component-lot release coupling.  
   - Expected benefit: Reduce hidden coupling between J2 and J5 and avoid asymmetric overrides.  
   - Helped: J5, J2, and future jobs using shared lots such as L7 or L8.  
   - Potentially harmed: Jobs in lots that are held longer under stricter quality rules; quality department workload.  
   - New cost/risk: Increased inspection cost, risk of releasing defective components, regulatory exposure.  
   - Perverse metric: Reducing quarantine cycle time alone may increase quality escapes.  
   - Object-centric evaluation: Link lot quarantine/release events to jobs and components; measure lot-hold-to-job-readiness time, override frequency, and quality escape rate.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. J1 or J2 “ready for batch” timestamps are only staging timestamps, and C1/C2 were not actually available until near 12:05.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 had an unlogged maintenance, qualification, or cleaning hold from 08:10 to 12:05.
3. J3’s readiness at 11:55 was itself caused by an upstream lot hold, tool hold, or inspection delay, making batch policy secondary.
4. Partial curing of B1 is technically or regulatorily impossible, so waiting for all three jobs is not avoidable.
5. J1 and J2 had long unrecorded waits before the “ready for batch” event, so batch synchronization is not the dominant delay.
6. B1 started at 12:05 because of a planned shift break, operator availability, or scheduled setup, not because J3 was late.
7. B2’s delay is mainly due to RACK-7 quarantine rather than waiting for J5, weakening pure batch synchronization as the mechanism.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. Explicit `Wait for Batch`, `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Lot`, and `Wait for Shipment` events with related job/tool/lot/shipment identifiers.
2. Tool state events for FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3: acquire, release, clean, quarantine, calibrate, reason code, and responsible operator.
3. Batch composition history: planned batch size, minimum batch size, batch split/merge events, and batch authorization reason.
4. ComponentLot status history linked to jobs: quarantine reason, release approver, override identifier, and alternate component substitution.
5. Shipment item-level events: shipment creation reason, promised ship date, customer consolidation preference, carrier availability, load start/load end per job.
6. Machine state reason codes: idle, setup, planned maintenance, unplanned downtime, operator absence, and starvation reason.
