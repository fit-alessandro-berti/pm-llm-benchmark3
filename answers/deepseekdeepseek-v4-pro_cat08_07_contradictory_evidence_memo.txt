# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness but low precision**  
Fitness measures how much of the event log can be replayed by the process model; high fitness means most observed cases can be reproduced. Precision measures how many paths allowed by the model are actually observed. The pilot model has high fitness because the coordinatordriven flow dominates the log. However, its low precision means it permits many extra paths — such as billing before activation, repeated installation without survey, or manual activation loops — that rarely appear in a disciplined process. In effect, the new process “fits” reality but is far too permissive, allowing harmful shortcuts that increase variation and rework.

**Improved median but worse tail duration**  
The median improved because simplified routing and fewer handovers accelerated the majority of cases, especially straightforward ones. The tail worsened because a subpopulation of cases — particularly apartments and older buildings — encountered new failure modes (wrongly skipped surveys, missing permits, premature billing). These failures triggered second visits, manual activations, and complaint escalations, creating extreme durations that show up in p90 and p95 while leaving the median relatively unaffected.

**Fewer handovers but lower resilience**  
Handovers fell sharply because a single coordinator owns each case. However, resilience suffered: context became concentrated in one person. When that coordinator was absent, no one else had the full picture, leading to stalls. The processmining generalization metric (0.56) and the centralized “workingtogether” network both signal that the system lost its ability to absorb disruptions — a classic tradeoff between lean flow and fragility.

**Lower visible cost but higher rework or downstream cost**  
Direct installation cost dropped by removing handovers and field surveys. But the redesign shifted work to later, costlier stages: more second visits, manual activations, and complaint handling. Because the pilot’s cost tracking excluded complaint costs after week 4, the visible cost improvement was misleading. The estimated total cost (EUR 207) exceeds baseline (EUR 198), indicating that the total cost of poor quality more than offsets the upfront savings.

**Improvement for simple houses, deterioration for apartments/old buildings**  
Simple lowrisk houses have predictable installations and no permit hurdles; the skip rule and merged tasks work well. Complex segments require field surveys to uncover buildingspecific constraints and to trigger permit checks. Skipping them introduces unknowns that surface during installation, causing failures, rework, and complaints. The segmentlevel data show a clear inversion: the redesign amplifies the inherent risk differential, benefiting the safe segment while punishing the risky ones.

*Advantages*: Maximises learning, resolves key uncertainties before national commitment, minimal risk of broad damage.  
*Risks*: Delays benefit capture; may lose organisational momentum.  
*Reasonable if*: the business can afford a deliberate, evidencefirst path and the pilot is considered too confounded to act on.

**E. Hybrid: deploy R only for simple lowrisk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, and measure before wider scale**  
*Advantages*: Captures early gains in the proven segment, protects vulnerable segments, creates a natural experiment for learning, builds stakeholder confidence.  
*Risks*: Operational complexity of running dual processes; requires reliable segment identification.  
*Reasonable if*: the organisation can accurately segment cases and is committed to factbased scaling — this is the most defensible choice given the conflicting evidence.

# 5. Valueofinformation analysis

Ranked by decision value (how much a measurement could change the scaling decision):

1. **M1 — Randomized pilot of survey skipping by buildingrisk stratum**  
   Directly tests whether a refined skip rule can be safely applied to mediumrisk apartments (29% of cases). A positive result would unlock significant additional benefit, dramatically changing the expansion calculus.

2. **M4 — Full downstream cost for 60 days after billing start**  
   Resolves the cost conflict conclusively. If total cost remains above baseline, the economic case for the redesign (except the simplehouse segment) collapses, regardless of speed gains.

3. **M6 — Validate the buildingrisk predictive rule against fieldsurvey outcomes**  
   Essential for rebuilding a reliable skipdecision model. Without it, any expansion of survey skipping would be guesswork and likely repeat the pilot’s failures.

4. **M3 — Structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation, Second visit**  
   Allows systematic rootcause diagnosis, distinguishing whether failures are due to the skip rule, coordinator knowledge gaps, or external factors — directly informing remedial actions.

5. **M2 — Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, Manual activation**  
   Pinpoints exactly where tail delays accumulate, enabling targeted cycletime reduction without sacrificing control. Important for operational improvement, but secondary to cost and skiprule validation.

6. **M8 — Separate customer experience for speed to appointment vs. working service after installation**  
   Provides customercentric tradeoff data that could influence branding and communication strategies, though the operational metrics already flag the problem well.

7. **M7 — Conformance monitoring for billing before activation and missing permit checks**  
   Crucial as a live safeguard to prevent harm while decisions are pending, but it does not change the scaling decision itself; it is an operational control.

8. **M5 — Compare coordinator ownership with sharedteam ownership under coordinator absence**  
   Relevant for scaling the coordinator model to more segments, yet current evidence on utilisation and centralisation already warns us sufficiently. This experiment would finetune staffing, not alter the nearterm deployment choice.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended decision: Option E — Hybrid segmented deployment with targeted measurements**

- **Immediate action:** Deploy the coordinatorownership model and the merged addressvalidation/ scheduling step for **simple lowrisk houses only**. Ensure these cases follow the streamlined path without survey skipping or postbilling activation.
- **What to pause:** Halt survey skipping and postbilling activation for all other segments. Reinstate mandatory field surveys for apartments and older buildings, and enforce activation before billing. Do not expand coordinator ownership to other segments yet.
- **What to scale:** Coordinator ownership for simple lowrisk houses can be scaled regionally under close monitoring of SLA, firsttimeright, complaints, and coordinator utilisation.
- **What to measure next:** Launch, in priority order, M1 (randomized surveyskipping trial), M4 (full downstream cost), M6 (validate risk rule), and M3 (failure reason codes). Simultaneously implement M7 (conformance monitoring) as an operational safety net.
- **Threshold for broader rollout:** Expand to mediumrisk apartments **only if** the refined surveyskipping rule (from M1 and M6) yields firsttimeright  82% and complaint rate  baseline, **and** M4 shows total cost  EUR 198. Further expansion to other segments requires evidence that coordinator utilisation can be kept below a sustainable threshold (e.g., <85%) and that resilience mechanisms (backup coverage) are in place. Until these conditions are met, the hybrid model stays.
