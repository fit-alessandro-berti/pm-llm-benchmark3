## 1. Executive causal assessment
**Provisional verdict:** *Partly causal through mechanisms* (with strong confounding)
**Confidence:** 75/100

**Strongest evidence for:**
- Within-complexity strata, Orion’s mean cycle time is *lower* than Nova’s for Low (7.5 vs. 9.6 h) and Medium (17.2 vs. 19.4 h), suggesting Orion’s *direct* effect may be beneficial when case mix is controlled.
- Orion handles 92.9% of night cases (higher queueing risk) and 64.3% of compliance-required cases (longer wait_before_review_h), yet still achieves comparable or better service times in stratified comparisons.

**Strongest evidence against:**
- Crude comparison shows Orion’s mean cycle time is 44% higher (21.96 vs. 15.24 h) due to case-mix imbalance (42.9% high-complexity vs. Nova’s 12.5%).
- Orion’s higher rework rate (28.6% vs. 18.8%) and longer queue waits (19.47 h vs. 11.12 h in High-complexity cases) suggest mediated mechanisms (compliance, rework) may offset direct benefits.

**Why the proposed 50% reassignment may fail or backfire:**
- Blind reassignment ignores that Orion’s *selection* for difficult cases is adaptive: removing 50% of Orion’s cases may shift Nova’s workload toward higher-complexity cases (unobserved selection on unobserved risk), worsening Nova’s performance.
- The crude mean difference is driven by Orion’s role as a "high-risk" sink; reassigning without addressing queueing capacity or compliance bottlenecks risks increasing cycle times in Nova.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

|------|------------|-------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------|

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|-------|--------------|------------------|-----------------|------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| 1 | Small randomized routing pilot (10% of cases) | Resource_group assignment | Case-level (stratified by complexity and shift) | Exclude cases with compliance_required; cap Orion load to 50% of night capacity | DFG discovery per group, cycle_time, queue_wait, review_service, rework | Estimate direct effect of Orion vs. Nova; test for interaction with complexity | Stop if Orion’s queue_wait exceeds 25 h or rework rate exceeds 35% |
| 2 | Compliance capacity expansion (add 1 FTE) | Compliance queue bottleneck | Shift-level (target night shift) | Monitor arrival_load and queue_wait; do not change routing rules | DFG before/after; compare compliance_required cases’ queue_wait and cycle_time | Test whether compliance mediation explains Orion’s longer waits | Rollback if compliance queue_wait does not decrease by 20% within 4 weeks |
| 3 | Queueing optimization (dynamic routing based on load) | Shift/load imbalance | Case-level (real-time) | Exclude high-complexity cases from dynamic routing; monitor quality outcomes | DFG per load bin; compare cycle_time and quality by load quartile | Test whether shift/load drives the Orion effect | Stop if quality outcomes (30-day appeal) worsen by >5% |

**Final recommendation:**
Do not proceed with the 50% reassignment. Instead, run a small randomized pilot (Phase 1) to estimate the *direct* effect of Orion vs. Nova, stratified by complexity and shift. Simultaneously, address the compliance bottleneck (Phase 2) to test whether mediation explains Orion’s longer cycle times. If the pilot shows Orion is faster for matched cases, expand Orion’s capacity rather than reassigning work. If compliance is the bottleneck, expand compliance capacity before reassigning. This approach learns the true mechanisms while limiting operational risk and avoids shifting unobserved risk to Nova.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved case risk:** The routing engine may select cases into Orion based on unobserved risk factors (e.g., fraud likelihood, policy ambiguity). *Additional data:* Include a risk score or case notes in the dataset.
2. **Reviewer skill heterogeneity:** Orion reviewers may have different skill sets than Nova reviewers, but this is unobserved. *Additional data:* Link reviewers to cases and include reviewer ID and certification level.
3. **Compliance performance:** The data assumes compliance is always performed when required, but performance time is unobserved. *Additional data:* Record compliance start/end times and performer ID.
4. **Queueing dynamics:** Arrival_load is coarse; dynamic queueing effects (e.g., bursty arrivals) are unobserved. *Additional data:* Timestamped arrival and assignment logs.
5. **Selection bias in completed cases:** The dataset only includes completed cases, which may exclude high-risk cases that take longer or are abandoned. *Additional data:* Include abandoned cases or a flag for case abandonment.
