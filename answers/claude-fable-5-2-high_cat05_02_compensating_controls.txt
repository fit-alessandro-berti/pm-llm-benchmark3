## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 states one purpose: no single person should both initiate and authorize an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. That purpose bundles eight distinct objectives, and the compensating controls cover them very unevenly.

| # | Objective / threat scenario | AV-2 (automated validation) | MG-4 (independent manager) | RA-7 (audit and recall) | ED-1 (emergency permission) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Unilateral insider fraud**: one person creates and releases a payment to an account they benefit from | Prevents only where the fraud trips a covered check (recent bank change, ownership mismatch, limit, PO mismatch); a warning can be overridden | Prevents if independent, substantive, and before release or settlement; only detects if later | Detects; corrects only inside the 24-hour recall window; deters | Does not prevent; caps exposure (below EUR 100,000, established vendor, no bank change) and makes MG-4 and RA-7 mandatory |
| O2 | **Honest error**: wrong amount, payee, bank details, or duplicate that a second reader would catch | Prevents duplicates, limit breaches, PO and account-ownership mismatches; misses a plausible but wrong amount | Prevents if the manager reads invoice and bank details | Detects; correction is easier than for fraud because a genuine vendor can refund | Caps the size of an error |
| O3 | **External deception of the operator**: changed bank details, impersonated vendor | Strongest control here (bank change in seven days, ownership, sanctions); fails if unavailable or overridden, and misses older changes | Partly prevents: the manager sees the same falsified documents but adds a second sceptical reader | Detects; recall is the main remedy and is time-critical | Conditions 3, 4, and 5 exclude the riskiest configurations |
| O4 | **Identity integrity**: two user IDs must be two humans (shared or borrowed credentials, service accounts, compromised accounts, one session on a shared workstation) | Fails to address; explicitly cannot establish who controlled an account | Addresses only if the manager acts in their own authenticated session; fails if the manager's credential is the one misused | Can detect through session and device forensics, after the fact | Fails to address; the token only attributes the exception |
| O5 | **Substantive entitlement**: goods or services received, documents genuine, payment commercially reasonable | Fails to address (basic PO match only) | Prevents if the manager reviews the receipt or service report; a rubber stamp does not | Detects (for example, a fabricated receipt), usually late | Relies entirely on MG-4 |
| O6 | **Collusion**: preparer with releaser, or operator with manager | Fails to address | Adds a third person only if independent of both; fails under a hierarchical or personal conflict | Main control: pattern analysis and deep sampling detect it; rarely corrects | Fails to address |
| O7 | **Abuse of the exception path**: convenience bypass, manufactured urgency, retroactive token, self-override, release during a control outage | A block cannot be waived; a warning can; an outage gives no assurance at all | Prevents if the manager challenges the necessity, not just the payment | 100% audit within one business day detects misuse | This is ED-1's purpose: token before preparation, exposure limits, mandatory approval and audit |
| O8 | **Irrevocability and accountability**: an independent decision must be evidenced before the point of no return, and a defect must be recoverable | Gives timestamped evidence for covered checks only | Meets it before settlement; after settlement it is only a record | Corrective within 24 hours; recovery is uncertain afterwards | Conditions 6 and 7 tie approval to settlement and audit to one business day |

Three consequences follow.

- **O4 and O6 are the gaps.** No compensating control covers them well, and FEP-1 itself only raises the cost of collusion; it does not resist it. A distinct-user pair is therefore not automatically an independent pair.
- **A clean AV-2 pass says nothing about O5 or O6.** It is strong evidence for the six checks it runs and no evidence for anything else.
- **ED-1 is a meta-control.** It does not reduce risk by itself; it limits exposure and makes the other controls compulsory.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

The test is applied to every case, including cases where two different users acted. It has four steps.

**Step 0 — Completion.** If no payment was released, classify the case as prevented and assess the attempt separately.

**Step 1 — Legitimacy of the deviation.** Was single-operator handling permitted (all seven ED-1 conditions) and necessary? This is separate from risk reduction: a package can control the risk well and still be an unauthorized bypass.

**Step 2 — Seven criteria**, each rated strong, degraded, or absent.

| Criterion | Question | Strong | Degraded | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Did an independent decision precede the point of no return? | Before release: nothing moves without approval | After release but before settlement: the payment proceeds unless someone intervenes | After settlement or after the recall window |
| Independence | Is the second decision-maker a separate, unconflicted human of the required level, using their own credential? | Independent L3+ manager in their own session | Independence stated but level or session not evidenced | Actor, shared credential, service account, or conflicted supervisor |
| Coverage | Does the package address the objectives (O1–O8) that matter for this case's risk profile? | Mechanical checks by AV-2 plus document review by a human | One relevant objective uncovered | The dominant threat for the case is uncovered (for example, a bank change with no ownership check) |
| Overrideability | Can the controlled person waive the control? | Hard block, or override only by an independent party | Override possible but independently logged and reviewed promptly | Self-override, or release continues when the control is down |
| Recoverability | If the control is late, can funds still be stopped, recalled, or recovered? | Stop before settlement | Audit inside the 24-hour recall window | Irrevocable and outside the window |
| Evidence quality | Is the review contemporaneous, attributable, and documented as to what was examined? | Documents reviewed are recorded | Review asserted but scope unknown | Unsigned, missing, or reconstructed afterwards |
| Collusion resistance | How many independent people must cooperate to defeat the package, and is pairing monitored? | At least two unconflicted people, with pattern monitoring | Two people, no monitoring or unproven independence | One person, or one person with a borrowed credential |

**Step 3 — Combination rule.** The criteria are not additive.

- Timing and independence are knock-out criteria: a prompt audit cannot offset an approval made by a conflicted party after settlement.
- Required strength scales with inherent exposure: amount, recency of a bank change, vendor age, irrevocability, and whether AV-2 was working. The same package can be enough for a EUR 1,800 payment to a six-year vendor and inadequate for EUR 900,000 to a recently changed account.

**Resulting tiers**

| Tier | Definition | Classification it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Fully equivalent | An independent, qualified human reviewed the underlying evidence and decided before irrevocability; AV-2 passed without override; no criterion absent; the exception was legitimately invoked | OBJECTIVE_MET |
| Partially equivalent | An independent decision before irrevocability exists, but one or two criteria are degraded (unproven precondition, undocumented review depth, unauthorized bypass) | OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET |
| Merely mitigating | No independent human decision before irrevocability; remaining controls lower likelihood or loss size for some objectives only | OBJECTIVE_FAILED (lower severity) |
| Ineffective | A knock-out applies: controls were late, performed or overridden by the actor or a conflicted party, unavailable, or blind to the dominant threat | OBJECTIVE_FAILED |
| Unresolved | A knock-out criterion cannot be rated on the available evidence | INDETERMINATE |

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard**

- **Tails.** Expected loss averages away rare, large, unrecoverable losses. A package with the same average but a EUR 900,000 irrevocable worst case is not equivalent.
- **Adversaries adapt.** Fraud probabilities are not stable; attackers move to the weakest path, so loss rates measured on honest history understate the risk of a weaker route.
- **Outcome is not control quality.** AP-208 lost nothing and was uncontrolled; AP-204 satisfied the form and passed a fabricated receipt. Controls must be judged on what was known and in place before the money moved.
- **Coverage by threat.** Equal aggregate loss can hide an objective left entirely uncovered, such as identity or collusion.
- **Assurance and accountability.** The organization must be able to show who decided, on what evidence, and when. This matters to auditors and regulators, and it protects honest operators from suspicion.
- **Recoverability and detection time.** Two packages with equal expected loss differ if one loses money irrevocably and the other leaves it recallable.
- **Erosion.** Tolerating convenient bypasses because each is cheap weakens the control for the expensive cases.

I therefore use threat-coverage dominance as the primary standard: the package must be at least as strong as an independent releaser on every objective relevant to the case. Exposure and recoverability set severity, and legitimacy of the deviation is assessed separately.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control and timing | Nature | Covers well | Does not cover | Independence and overrideability | Corrective capacity | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct releaser (FEP-1 baseline) | Preventive | O1, O2; O3 and O5 if the releaser reads the documents | O4 unless identity is verified; O6 | Independent only if two real, unconflicted people act | None by itself | AP-204, AP-209 |
| AV-2 block | Preventive, cannot be waived | Duplicates, sanctions, ownership mismatch, limit, PO mismatch | O4, O5, O6 | Machine-independent; not overridable | Stops before release | AP-205 |
| AV-2 warning with override | Preventive only if the override is independent | Recent bank change, limit | Everything above, plus the override itself | As strong as the overrider; worthless if the operator overrides | None | AP-202 |
| AV-2 unavailable | No control | Nothing | Everything | Release continued without it | None | AP-208 |
| MG-4 before release | Preventive | O1, O2, O5, O7; partly O3 | O6 if conflicted; O4 if the credential is misused | Independent if L3+, unconflicted, own session; weak if rubber-stamped | Full: nothing has moved | AP-203, AP-206 |
| MG-4 after release, before settlement | Hybrid (see below) | Same objectives, if substantive | Same, plus dependence on timely action | Same conditions | Stop before settlement, if a stop mechanism exists | AP-201, AP-207 |
| MG-4 after settlement | Detective only | Accountability record | Prevention of anything | Independence still matters for credibility | Only within the recall window | AP-202, AP-208 |

| ED-1 | Permission and exposure limiter | O7; bounds loss size | Does not itself check anything | Depends on who issues tokens | None | AP-203, AP-207; disputed in AP-206; misapplied in AP-208 |

**A manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement is a hybrid.** I classify controls by their position relative to the harm, not relative to the workflow step. The harm is the irrevocable transfer, which occurs at settlement.

- **Relative to the rule breach** (a single person released), the manager's action is detective: the release has already happened.
- **Relative to the loss**, it is preventive, provided the payment can still be stopped and the manager acts with enough margin.

It is still weaker than approval before release, for three reasons.

1. **Default outcome.** Before release, nothing moves without approval. After release, the payment settles unless someone intervenes, so an absent or slow manager means no control.
2. **Window.** The protection lasts only as long as the gap to settlement (80 to 90 minutes in AP-201 and AP-207) and disappears with instant payment rails.
3. **Framing.** Ratifying a completed act invites less challenge than deciding an open request.

I therefore treat it as sufficient only where exposure is bounded and the exception is legitimate (AP-207), as partial where it is not legitimate (AP-201), and as purely detective once settlement has passed (AP-202, AP-208).

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Evidence to collect**

- **Identity:** authentication records per event (credential owner, session user, device, location, second factor), so that a credential and a human can be told apart.
- **Tokens:** request time, issue time, issuer, justification, and expiry, logged as events in the payment case with synchronized clocks.
- **Manager review:** which documents were opened, and a recorded statement of what was checked; the manager's level and any declared conflict.
- **Settlement and recall:** settlement time for every payment (missing for four cases here), recall requests and outcomes, and whether a stop mechanism exists between release and settlement.
- **AV-2:** result per check, not only pass or warning; service health and outage periods; override records with reason.
- **Vendor master data:** bank-change events with requester, approver, and call-back verification.
- **Receipt:** confirmation of goods or services from the business owner, independent of the payment team.
- **Organization:** design documents for any dedicated payment cell, including rotation rules.

**Monitoring rules** (thresholds are illustrative and should be calibrated on history)

| Indicator | Definition | Alert rule | What an alert means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-user rate | Completed payments with one human as preparer and releaser, divided by completed payments; split by token and no token, per user and unit | Every no-token case; per-user rate well above peers; any service account as releaser | A bypass occurred. Not evidence of fraud; may reflect staffing gaps |
| Override rate | Overridden warnings divided by warnings; flag overrides in the operator's own session or where credential owner differs from session user | Every self-session override; any credential mismatch; rising rate per user or manager | The control was waived. A mismatch is a security incident to investigate, not proof of who acted |
| Late manager approval | Manager action after release; minutes of margin before settlement; action after settlement | Every post-settlement action; margin under 30 minutes; rising share of post-release approvals | Timing weakness. Says nothing about review quality |
| Audit before recall window | Share of same-user payments audited within 24 hours of release | Every miss | Lost corrective capacity. "One business day" and "24 hours" differ: AP-201 met the first and missed the second, and a Friday release audited on Monday meets the rule 72 hours late. Align the RA-7 deadline with the recall window |
| Repeated resource pairs | Share of a user's payments released by the same partner; reciprocal pairing; joint count per 30 days | Concentration above a set share without a documented cell; any pair linked to a confirmed defect | Opportunity for collusion. A dedicated cell produces the same pattern innocently |
| Shared workstations | Preparation and release from one device or address within a short interval | Every occurrence on payments above a value threshold; repeated occurrence for one pair | Possible single actor with two logins. Hot desks and shared terminals are innocent explanations |
| Bank-change proximity | Days between a vendor bank change and release | Hold every payment within seven days; flag within 30 days; hard stop when combined with same-user release or an override | Elevated deception risk; most such changes are genuine |
| Control outages | Payments released while AV-2 was unavailable; unsigned manual checklists | Every occurrence | A design failure: release should not be possible. Not evidence against the operator |
| Threshold proximity and token timing | Amounts at 90–100% of the ED-1 ceiling or operator limit; tokens issued after preparation | Repeated near-ceiling amounts per user or vendor; every late token | Possible sizing to the limit or retroactive justification; a logging delay is also possible |
| Blocked-then-attempted releases | Release attempts after an AV-2 block | Repeated attempts per user | May be probing; more often confusion about the block |

**An alert is not proof.** An alert is a reason to look: it shows that a pattern consistent with a weakness occurred. Proof of misconduct needs corroboration from identity records, documents, money flows, and interviews, and it must exclude the innocent explanations noted above. In practice:

- Route alerts to someone independent of the people flagged; AP-202 shows why a supervisor is the wrong recipient.
- Record the disposition of each alert, including "explained".
- Take no adverse action against a person on an alert alone. AP-204 and AP-209 currently justify an investigation of u12 and u13, not a conclusion.
- Treat a missing alert as weak reassurance: AP-208 produced no loss and no fraud signal, yet was uncontrolled.

**Design changes the cases point to**

1. Block same-user release technically unless a valid token exists.
2. Stop releases when AV-2 is unavailable.
3. Require warning overrides in the manager's own authenticated session.
4. Make manager ratification a condition of settlement, so a late manager delays the payment.
5. Apply two-person control and call-back to vendor bank changes.

def to_pm4py_log(frame):
    """Blank resource -> SYSTEM, UTC timestamps, pm4py.format_dataframe, stable ordering."""
    frame = frame.copy()
    frame[RES] = frame[RES].fillna("").astype(str).str.strip().replace("", "SYSTEM")
    frame[TS] = pd.to_datetime(frame[TS], utc=True)
    with warnings.catch_warnings():
        warnings.simplefilter("ignore")  # format_dataframe may emit deprecation warnings
        out = pm4py.format_dataframe(frame, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
    out[TS] = pd.to_datetime(out[TS], utc=True)
    return out.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)


def case_ids(obj):
    if isinstance(obj, pd.DataFrame):
        return set(obj[CASE].astype(str).unique())
    return {str(trace.attributes["concept:name"]) for trace in obj}  # legacy EventLog object

**What the script reports.** I ran it on PM4Py 2.7.23.4 with pandas 3.0.5.

- Nominal violators among completed payments: AP-201, AP-202, AP-203, AP-206, AP-207, AP-208. The PM4Py result, the `keep_violations=True` call, and the pandas check agree.
- Nominally conforming: AP-204 and AP-209, both with the pair u12 and u13.
- Blocked attempt: AP-205, same user for preparation and attempt.
- ED-1 flags: AP-203 and AP-207 have no failed condition; AP-206 fails condition 1 on logged times; AP-208 fails conditions 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7.
- Audit timing: AP-201 is inside one business day but 24.9 hours after release, which is why the two audit flags are kept separate.

**PM4Py behavior I cannot guarantee across versions**

- **Polarity of `filter_four_eyes_principle`.** In the version tested, the default call returns the cases that satisfy the principle and `keep_violations=True` returns the violators. The docstring wording has varied, and older releases lack the `keep_violations` parameter. The script therefore makes the required default call, determines its polarity from a two-case probe with a known answer, and derives the violators accordingly.
- **Cases missing one of the two activities.** Such cases (AP-205) appear in neither the conforming nor the violating result in the tested version. The script identifies them separately and does not rely on that behavior.
- **Multiple occurrences and ordering.** The tested implementation compares the sets of resources for the two activities within a case. I cannot guarantee how other versions treat repeated activities or their order.
- **Return type.** The script accepts a DataFrame or a legacy `EventLog` object.
- **`format_dataframe`.** It may emit deprecation warnings, add index columns, re-sort rows, or re-parse timestamps depending on the version. The script suppresses warnings, re-applies UTC parsing, and sorts explicitly.
- **Import output.** Some versions print a banner on import; it is unrelated to the results.
- **No concept of a human.** PM4Py compares resource strings. It does not know that a service account is not a person, that a credential may be used by someone else (AP-202), or that two user IDs may share a workstation (AP-204). Those points come from the facts table and from judgment.
