# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

The distinction below uses four criteria at once: (i) frequency within the resource's own profile, (ii) the resource's share of the activity across the organization, (iii) the complexity of cases in which the resource performs the activity, and (iv) whether the resource holds the formal authority or certification the activity implies. An activity is "core" only when it scores on frequency and share and matches the resource's mandate; otherwise it is peripheral, occasional, substitutional or shadow.

**R-A Automated intake (Nora).** Core: A1 and A2 for low-complexity, presumably rule-based entitlement checks. There is no peripheral activity, which is itself informative: the 150 registrations Nora performs without a matching validation define the automation boundary where human judgement starts. Authority is nil; Nora executes rules configured elsewhere, so any "role" it appears in should be labelled as a system boundary rather than a team.

**R-B Permanent front office (Amira, Ben).** Core: A2, A4, A12 and A1 (together roughly 85 % of their events), overwhelmingly on low and medium cases. Peripheral but structurally important: A3 Diagnose (135 events between them) and A8 Negotiation (67) show first-line triage before handover to R-D or R-E; A13/rework after temp-closed cases reopen (fragment 6) is an unpaid externality from R-C. Amira's 18 A7 events and 42 negotiations on medium/high cases suggest a senior-generalist sub-tier without pricing certification; they should be treated as occasional and reviewed, not as evidence of commercial competence. Authority: entitlement decisions only.

**R-C Temporary intake (Kai, Lina).** Core: the same four activities as R-B but restricted to low complexity (0 critical, 6 high cases combined). There are no genuine peripheral activities; the handful of A3/A8 events are probably mis-routed contacts. The substitution evidence is decisive: 17 reopened cases traced to their Validate Entitlement notes means their core activity is performed at a lower quality standard, and the cost is recorded under other resources. Authority: none formally (no certifications), which makes their 520 A2 events an uncertified entitlement decision stream.

**R-D Technical resolution (Chen, Dina).** Core: A3, A5, A10 (about 80 % of each profile). Peripheral: A13, A14, A8, A11, A12 in the 10-30 range, consistent with owning the technical part of a case end to end. Chen's 24 A6 Security Reviews are the exception: 16 % of all security reviews, done on the late shift when Erik (day shift) is absent, without a security certification. For Chen this is a recurring shadow activity, not an occasional one; for Dina (6 events) it is occasional. Complexity separates the two members more than activities do: Chen's median 0.71 and 42 critical cases vs Dina's 0.48 and 9. Authority: configuration certification for both; no security or approval authority.

**R-E Commercial exceptions (Fatima).** Core: A7 and A8 (73 % of her events, 52 % and 27 % of the organization's). Peripheral: A2 (22) and A12 (18) as pre-checks and closures on her own cases. Her 16 A9 approvals are the ambiguous item: either delegated approval for pricing exceptions below a threshold, or an event-labelling artefact where "submit for approval" is logged as A9. Substitution: her six-day absence doubled median pricing duration despite Giorgio's coverage, which means her core competence is not fully replicable. Authority: pricing certification; approval authority unverified.

**R-F Hybrid senior agent (Giorgio).** He has two cores, and both are legitimately core by frequency and share: front-office intake/closure (A2/A4/A12/A1, about 68 % of events) and commercial (A7/A8, 20 % of events but 31 % and 26 % of organizational volume). The 41 baseline pricing reviews outside weeks 3-4 mean the commercial role is routine, not merely substitutional. Peripheral: A3, A13, A5/A10. Shadow: 20 A9 approvals without approval certification. Complexity 0.58 with 156 high/critical cases is above the front office and below Fatima, consistent with him taking the complex front-office cases that need pricing discussions. Authority: entitlement and pricing certifications; no formal approval mandate.

**R-G Security assurance (Erik).** Core: A6 (56 % of his events, 66 % of the organization's). Everything else is peripheral follow-through on his own cases: A3, A10, A5, A14, A11. The peripheral events matter for role interpretation because 44 of 170 events overlap with R-D activities, but on cases with median complexity 0.86; they document that security findings are implemented under his eye, not that he is a technical analyst. Substitution: nobody substitutes for A6 formally; Mateo filtered referrals and Chen absorbs late-shift reviews. Authority: security certification, unique.

**R-H Supervisory authority (Hana).** Core: A9 (56 % of her events, 63 % of the organization's). Peripheral with authority content: A13 Reopen (24) as management-initiated reopenings, A8 (22) on escalated negotiations, A11 (12) as spot checks. The week-5 recheck of 13 of 21 of Mateo's preliminary approvals shows that the A9 label is not equivalent to a decision: the same label covers final approvals (Hana) and preliminary ones (Mateo), and the log needs escalation_level or a lifecycle attribute to tell them apart. Authority: approval certification, the only holder.

**R-I Escalation broker (Mateo).** Core by function rather than by label: A13 Reopen (30 % of all reopens) and the coordination touches that the log records as A2/A4/A12 because there is no explicit "Coordinate" activity (fragments 3 and 5 name it, the activity list does not). A9 preliminary approvals (44) are core in volume but split: 21 in week 5 were substitution, and 62 % were rechecked; the remaining ~23 (about 2.6 per week) are routine pre-screening. Peripheral: A3, A8, A11, A6, A5, A14 in the 16-35 range, each on high/critical cases. Complexity is the strongest signal: 76 critical cases, more than Hana or Erik. Authority: escalation certification, which is a routing mandate, not a decision mandate.

**R-J Quality gate (Ivan, Julia).** Core: A11 and A14 (about 75 % of events; near-monopoly organizationally). Peripheral: A10 and A5 (roughly 60 events each) and A12 (about 30 each). Two readings of the A10/A5 events are discussed in Section 4; either way they are peripheral by frequency but not by risk, because the same person checking and changing a solution weakens the gate. Complexity is medium, meaning the quality gate is applied broadly rather than only to complex work. Authority: quality certification; no configuration certification, so the A5/A10 events are uncertified.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

"Shadow" here means work that is routinely performed outside the formal title or certification, and "bridging" means work whose value lies in connecting formal roles. Neither is improper by default; each is assessed for benefit and risk.

**3.1 R07_Giorgio - commercial shadow role.** Evidence: 72 A7 (31 % of all pricing reviews), 88 A8 (26 %), 20 A9; ~41 pricing reviews occur outside Fatima's absence, so the shadow role is standing, not episodic; similarity 0.71 to Fatima and 0.84 to Amira; 156 high/critical cases. Benefits: a second pricing-capable person exists at all, which is why a six-day absence produced a slowdown rather than a stoppage; complex front-office cases with a pricing component are resolved without a handover; he is certified for pricing, so the competence has a formal basis. Risks: pricing-exception duration doubled under substitution, indicating either lower proficiency on hard exceptions or capacity conflict with his front-office load; his 20 A9 events are approvals without approval certification, so pricing exceptions may in effect be reviewed and approved within the same informal channel; his role is undocumented, so capacity planning for the Commercial team understates true demand by about a third. Recommendation: formalise as a designated backup with a scope limit (e.g., exception value or escalation_level), and separate A9 events he records into "prepared" vs "approved".

**3.2 R13_Mateo - cross-functional escalation broker.** Evidence: presence in all 14 activities, 30 % of reopens, 20 % of approval labels, the largest critical caseload (76), 0.54 similarity to Hana and 0.39 to Erik, central position in fragments 3, 5 and 6, and two substitution episodes. Benefits: he is the organization's routing memory for cases that leave the happy path; the weeks 7-8 triage of security referrals reduced false escalations and protected Erik's scarce capacity; the week-5 preliminary approvals kept the pipeline moving during Hana's absence. Risks: the week-5 recheck rate of 62 % shows that his approvals are not decisions, so treating A9 counts as authority would overstate his mandate; the weeks 7-8 filter delayed two critical cases beyond two days, which is precisely the failure a filter in front of a specialist can cause; he is a single point of coordination whose activity is not even modelled as a distinct activity, so his contribution cannot be measured or backed up. Recommendation: add an explicit coordination activity or lifecycle transition to the log, define triage criteria for security referrals jointly with Erik, and record preliminary approvals with a distinct escalation_level so the recheck rate can be monitored.

**3.3 R05_Erik - rare but critical specialist.** Evidence: 170 events (lowest in the log) but 66 % of A6, median complexity 0.86, 83 % of cases high/critical, day shift only. Benefits: concentration of security judgement in one certified person gives consistency and clear accountability; his follow-through on A3/A10 for his own cases shortens the loop between finding and fix. Risks: a frequency-based role model can literally drop him; there is no certified substitute, so late-shift security reviews fall to Chen (24 events) and triage falls to Mateo (16 events), both uncertified; demand spikes (weeks 7-8) translate directly into waiting time for critical cases. Recommendation: treat him as a capacity constraint in the process model, certify Chen as a formal secondary reviewer for the late shift, and monitor A6 waiting time by escalation_level rather than A6 volume.

**3.4 R14_Nora - automation boundary role.** Nora's 760 A1 / 610 A2 define where automated entitlement pre-checks end and human validation begins. Benefit: about 27 % of validations need no human effort, and fragments 1-2 show clean automated openings. Risks: the 150 registrations Nora cannot validate are the hardest entitlement cases, and they land disproportionately on whoever is next in queue, including the uncertified temps; if Nora's rules drift, error propagates at 24/7 scale; in network analyses Nora's centrality swamps the human handover structure. Recommendation: exclude Nora from human role discovery, analyse Nora-to-human handovers separately, and check whether temps receive a disproportionate share of Nora's non-validated registrations.

**3.5 R11_Kai and R12_Lina - temporary-workforce role.** Their activity vector is the front office's, but their complexity is one tier lower and their downstream quality is measurable: 17 reopens traced to their Validate notes, with the rework performed by Amira, Ben and Mateo. Benefits: they absorbed seasonal volume (about 1,700 events) and kept the permanent staff free for medium and high cases. Risks: uncertified entitlement decisions at scale; quality externalities that do not appear in their own metrics; a mined "front-office role" that includes them would overstate the permanent team's capacity and understate its rework. Recommendation: keep them as a separate role in the model with a complexity ceiling and a mandatory validation-note template.

**3.6 R09_Ivan and R10_Julia - quality gatekeeping with fix-forward.** They own 89 % of quality checks and 77 % of KB updates, but also perform about 120 configuration/implementation events. Benefits: fix-forward avoids a handover back to R-D for trivial defects, and the KB work turns quality findings into reusable knowledge; the day/late shift split gives the gate continuous coverage. Risks: checker-implements-fix weakens separation of duties and can hide defect rates from the technical team; uncertified configuration changes. Recommendation: log fix-forward explicitly (e.g., a lifecycle attribute or a "minor fix" activity) so that the quality gate's independence can be verified.

**3.7 R03_Chen - late-shift security shadow (bridging R-D and R-G).** 24 A6 events on the late shift when the only certified security reviewer is off shift, plus the highest critical caseload in the technical team. Benefit: security work is not blocked overnight. Risk: uncertified security decisions on high-complexity cases; a similarity score of 0.94 with Dina makes this invisible to the process owner. Recommendation: certify or formally restrict.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_events.csv", sep=";")

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S%z",
)
df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)
df["org:resource"] = df["org:resource"].astype(str)
df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
df["temporary_substitution"] = df["temporary_substitution"].fillna(0).astype(int)
df["escalation_level"] = pd.to_numeric(df["escalation_level"], errors="coerce").fillna(0)

KEYS = dict(
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
)
SNA_KEYS = {k: v for k, v in KEYS.items() if k != "activity_key"}

# Three views of the same log. Roles are mined on each and compared,
# so that substitution episodes and the bot do not drive the structure.
humans = df[df["org:resource"] != "R14_Nora"]                     # bot excluded
baseline = humans[humans["temporary_substitution"] == 0]         # routine work only
subst_only = humans[humans["temporary_substitution"] == 1]       # substitution episodes only

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Organizational role discovery (activity groups + resource weights)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def roles_to_frame(roles, label):
    """pm4py returns Role objects (activities, originator_importance);
    older versions return [activities, {resource: importance}] pairs."""
    rows = []
    for i, r in enumerate(roles):
        acts = getattr(r, "activities", None) or r[0]
        imp = getattr(r, "originator_importance", None) or r[1]
        for res, w in imp.items():
            rows.append({"view": label, "role_id": f"{label}_{i}",
                         "activities": " | ".join(sorted(acts)),
                         "resource": res, "importance": float(w)})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

roles_full = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df, **KEYS)
roles_hum = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(humans, **KEYS)
roles_base = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(baseline, **KEYS)

# Optional: sensitivity of the activity-merging threshold (default 0.65)
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_alg
roles_strict = roles_alg.apply(
    pm4py.convert_to_event_log(baseline),
    parameters={roles_alg.Parameters.ROLES_THRESHOLD_PARAMETER: 0.80},
)

R = pd.concat([roles_to_frame(roles_full, "full"),
               roles_to_frame(roles_hum, "humans"),
               roles_to_frame(roles_base, "baseline")], ignore_index=True)

# Multi-membership: a resource whose importance is spread over several
# activity groups is a bridging or hybrid candidate (Giorgio, Mateo).
R["share"] = R["importance"] / R.groupby(["view", "resource"])["importance"].transform("sum")
membership = (R[R["view"] == "baseline"]
              .groupby("resource")["share"]
              .agg(n_roles="count", top_share="max",
                   entropy=lambda s: float(-(s * np.log2(s)).sum()))
              .sort_values("top_share"))
print(membership)   # low top_share / high entropy = does not fit one cluster

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity, on counts and complexity-weighted
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sim_full = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(humans, **KEYS)
sim_base = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(baseline, **KEYS)

def sim_to_frame(d, name):
    s = pd.Series(d, name=name)
    s.index = pd.MultiIndex.from_tuples(s.index, names=["r1", "r2"])
    return s

SIM = pd.concat([sim_to_frame(sim_full, "sim_all"),
                 sim_to_frame(sim_base, "sim_baseline")], axis=1)

def cosine_matrix(m: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = m.values.astype(float)
    x = x / np.clip(np.linalg.norm(x, axis=1, keepdims=True), 1e-12, None)
    return pd.DataFrame(x @ x.T, index=m.index, columns=m.index)
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.** Plan capacity per role, not per activity count. The front office's true permanent capacity is Amira, Ben and roughly two-thirds of Giorgio; the temps add throughput on low-complexity cases only and generate rework elsewhere, so their contribution should be netted against the 17 reopens and the A13 events of R01/R02/R13. Commercial demand is Fatima's 291 events plus Giorgio's ~160 pricing/negotiation events, about 50 % larger than the Commercial team's own count suggests. Security capacity is one day-shift person with a late-shift shadow; if Enterprise Security volume grows, waiting time on critical cases will grow before any volume metric shows stress.

**Training and certification.** The mined shadow roles point to where certification lags practice: Giorgio (approval scope), Chen (security review), Mateo (preliminary approvals), Ivan/Julia (configuration), temps (entitlement). The choice is either to certify and formalise, or to route the work away; leaving it informal is a decision too, and should be recorded as one. Training temps on validation-note quality has a measurable target (reopen rate on temp-closed cases vs permanent agents on the same complexity band).

**Risk control.** Three controls follow from the structure: (1) separate preliminary from final approval in the log, and report the recheck rate for every non-supervisor approver; (2) define triage criteria for security referrals with Erik so that Mateo's filter has an explicit escalation-level floor below which nothing is delayed (the two > 2-day waits in weeks 7-8 are the failure case); (3) make fix-forward by quality analysts visible so the gate's independence can be audited.

**Resilience.** The organization already has resilience through its shadow roles; the question is whether it is deliberate. Named backups with scope limits for Commercial (Giorgio), Approval (Mateo, with defined final authority for low escalation levels) and Security (Chen for late shift) would convert improvised coverage into planned coverage, and the substitution episodes give baseline numbers for what degraded service looks like (pricing duration x2, 62 % recheck, two delayed critical cases).

**Cautions before changing staffing on mined roles.**

1. Substitution is not competence. Giorgio covered pricing for six days with doubled cycle time, and 62 % of Mateo's preliminary approvals were rechecked. Reassigning headcount on the assumption that these substitutes can carry the role permanently would lock in slower and less reliable decisions.
2. Similar activity vectors are not similar risk. Chen and Dina (0.94) and Amira and Kai (near-identical vectors) work different complexity tiers; treating them as interchangeable pools would push critical and high cases to people who do not currently handle them.
3. Low volume is not low importance. Erik (170 events) and Hana (245) are the two people whose absence stops the process for critical cases; any model that ranks roles by event share will underinvest in them.
4. The log under-records coordination. Mateo's brokerage appears as scattered A2/A4/A12 events because there is no coordination activity; removing or redistributing his position on the basis of his "low" activity share would remove the routing memory for every off-path case.
5. Ten weeks with a seasonal peak and three absence episodes is a short and atypical window; roles should be re-mined on a longer baseline before structural changes, with the substitution flag used to separate routine from episodic behaviour.
