## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is an actor-separation rule, but its underlying objective has several components. “P,” “D,” and “C” below mean preventive, detective, and corrective.

| Objective or threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Prevent unilateral initiation and authorization by one person** | **P, partial:** can stop listed anomalies, but does not provide a second human judgment or establish who controlled an account. | **P:** strong if an authenticated, independent manager substantively approves before release or irrevocable settlement. After release but before settlement it is hybrid. | **D; sometimes C:** can identify self-authorization later, but only supports correction while stop, recall, or recovery remains feasible. | **P framework:** permits single-operator handling only with multiple gates. It does not itself create independence; it relies on AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7. |
| **2. Prevent duplicate, mis-keyed, excessive, or basic PO-mismatch payments** | **P, strong within scope:** duplicate, amount-limit, and basic PO checks directly address these risks. A warning is weaker than a hard block. | **P/D:** substantive review may identify errors missed by automation. Rubber-stamping provides little coverage. | **D/C:** detects after release; correction depends on settlement and recall timing. | Requires a full, non-overridden AV-2 pass, reducing these risks for an eligible emergency. |
| **3. Prevent payment to a changed, unowned, or sanctioned bank account** | **P, relatively strong:** checks bank ownership, recent changes, and sanctions. It still cannot establish who controlled a compromised user or all underlying evidence. | **P:** useful if the manager independently verifies bank details and recent changes, preferably out of band. | **D/C:** can detect diversion, but recovery becomes uncertain after the recall window. | Excludes bank changes within seven days and requires AV-2 to pass, making this a central eligibility condition. |
| **4. Establish that goods or services were actually received and the payment is commercially reasonable** | **Fails or is weak:** basic PO matching is not receipt confirmation or commercial-reasonableness review. | **P if substantive:** invoice, receipt, service report, and commercial context must actually be reviewed. | **D:** audit may find fabricated receipts or unsupported services; late detection may not recover funds. | Does not directly cover this threat beyond requiring MG-4. |
| **5. Resist warning overrides, credential misuse, and compromised accounts** | **Conditional P:** hard blocks help; overrideable warnings may merely record risk. AV-2 does not establish who used a credential. | **P only with authenticated independence:** the manager must use their own credentials and must not be conflicted. Later ratification cannot validate an earlier credential misuse. | **D:** access and override forensics can expose misuse; **C** only if action is timely. | Requires a pass without override, but does not independently prove credential custody. |
| **6. Resist collusion, rubber-stamping, or a sham second actor** | **P, limited:** objective checks may catch some collusive transactions, but not fabricated receipt evidence or commercially plausible fraud. | **P, conditional:** an independent third actor improves resistance; a conflicted or perfunctory manager does not. | **D:** pair concentration, shared-device analysis, and receipt testing can reveal patterns. | Does not eliminate collusion risk. Its required manager must still be genuinely independent and substantive. |
| **7. Preserve an opportunity to stop or recover an erroneous payment** | **P:** a block before release avoids the recovery problem entirely. | **P/hybrid:** before release is preventive; after release but before settlement is preventive of external loss only if the payment can actually be stopped. | **D plus possible C:** timely audit can support stop or recall. After approximately 24 hours, it is generally detective only. | Requires approval before settlement and audit within one business day, but actual recall feasibility remains relevant. |
| **8. Prevent abuse of emergency exceptions and preserve accountability** | Does not verify that an emergency token was timely or justified. | Can challenge the emergency rationale and supporting documents. | Detects retrospective exception abuse and recurring operators or managers. | Provides the authorization framework: prior token, amount/vendor/bank conditions, full AV-2, independent MG-4, and timely RA-7. Failure of a condition means the permission is unavailable. |

A clean AV-2 pass therefore supports only its enumerated checks. It is not evidence that services were received, that a transaction was commercially reasonable, or that two logged accounts represented independent human judgment.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should be evaluated against the following dimensions.

| Dimension | Sufficiency question |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | Did the independent challenge occur before `Release_Payment`, or at least before an enforceable, still-reversible settlement boundary? A review after settlement cannot be preventive. |
| **Independence** | Was the reviewer a different authenticated human, L3 or above where MG-4 is invoked, outside unresolved conflicts, and using their own credentials? |
| **Coverage** | Did the package address the risks salient to the case—bank change, receipt, vendor legitimacy, amount, duplicate, commercial rationale—not merely pass unrelated checks? |
| **Overrideability** | Was the control a hard block, a warning subject to override, or a control unavailable in practice? Who authenticated the override? |
| **Recoverability** | If detection followed release, was there a reliable hold, stop, or recall opportunity? Detection after the ordinary recall window is not equivalent to prevention. |
| **Evidence quality** | Is there evidence of what was reviewed, not merely an approval event? Relevant evidence includes signed documents, review fields, authentication logs, timestamps, and bank status. |
| **Collusion resistance** | Could the same individual control both accounts, could two users be colluding, or was approval concentrated in a habitual pair? A third independent reviewer, rotation, and separate authentication improve resistance. |
| **Exception eligibility** | If ED-1 is relied upon, did every condition hold? A generally strong package does not retroactively make an ineligible emergency exception formally authorized. |
| **Risk fit** | Does the package cover the transaction’s actual loss mechanism? Amount matters for exposure, but not as a substitute for independence, coverage, or timing. |

### Equivalence levels

- **Fully equivalent:** The package preserves an effective independent veto before the irreversible point, has authenticated independence, substantively covers the material case risks, cannot be casually overridden, has reliable evidence, and provides reasonable collusion resistance. An ED-1 package satisfying all conditions can be functionally equivalent to FEP-1’s loss-prevention objective even though the same operator performed both logged activities.
- **Partially equivalent:** Important elements are strong, but there is a material timing, eligibility, review-depth, authentication, or coverage gap. Examples include a substantive manager review before settlement but after release without evidence of a hard bank hold.
- **Merely mitigating:** The controls reduce some risks but do not recreate the independent authorization objective. A narrow AV-2 pass plus a late audit is generally mitigating rather than equivalent.
- **Ineffective:** Controls were unavailable, bypassed, conflicted, perfunctory, or too late for recovery, especially where the salient warning was overridden.

“Same expected loss” is not the only valid equivalence standard. Two controls with similar average loss could differ materially in:

- catastrophic tail exposure;
- legal or policy requirements for actor separation;
- deterrence and accountability;
- common-mode credential compromise;
- collusion resistance;
- certainty of recovery;
- frequency of irreversible losses; and
- auditability and stakeholder confidence.

A detective control with uncertain recovery is therefore not automatically equivalent to a preventive veto merely because historical average losses appear similar.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control or timing | Preventive role | Detective role | Corrective role | Principal limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AV-2 hard pass/block before release** | Strong for its enumerated checks; a block prevents completion | Produces evidence of warnings, changes, and failed checks | Usually none needed if blocked | Does not validate receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion, or actual credential controller |
| **AV-2 warning with override** | Conditional and potentially weak | Records that a risk was identified and overridden | None by itself | Effectiveness depends on override authorization and whether the warning receives independent review |
| **MG-4 before release** | Strong independent veto | May identify preparer errors or fraud during review | Can require correction before release | Weak if conflicted, unauthenticated, below L3, or rubber-stamped |
| **MG-4 after release but before settlement** | Preventive of external loss only if there is an enforceable hold or practical stop right | Detective relative to the already-completed `Release_Payment` event | Can stop settlement or initiate rapid correction | Does not cure nominal FEP-1; effectiveness depends on actual stop authority and review substance |
| **MG-4 after settlement** | None for the completed transfer | Detective/ratifying only | May support legal recovery, but not ordinary prevention | Ratification cannot retroactively authorize a release or repair missing independence |
| **RA-7 inside recall window** | Not preventive of release | Strong detective role | Partly corrective if stop or recall remains practical | Recovery is not certain and audit quality matters |
| **RA-7 after recall window** | None | Detective and accountability role | Usually weak; legal recovery may still be possible | Cannot be treated as equivalent to a pre-release veto |
| **ED-1** | Preventive exception framework through eligibility gates | Requires timely audit and documented manager review | Relies on stop/recall capacity rather than creating it | A permission, not a stand-alone control; all conditions must hold |

### Semantics for post-release, pre-settlement manager action

Two different boundaries matter:

1. **Process semantics:** Once `Release_Payment` occurs, a later manager action is detective or ratifying relative to FEP-1. It cannot make the logged preparer and releaser different.
2. **Loss semantics:** If settlement has not occurred and the bank instruction is genuinely stoppable, the same manager action can prevent funds from leaving irreversibly.

Accordingly, such an action is best called **hybrid**. It is detective relative to the release event and potentially preventive/corrective relative to settlement. AP-201 and AP-207 receive credit on this basis, but only conditionally; an approval timestamp alone does not prove that the manager had a substantive review or enforceable stop capability.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### Evidence to retain

1. **Identity and authentication**
   - Individual user-to-human mappings, MFA records, session IDs, device IDs, IP addresses, and workstation identifiers.
   - Override credential owner, actual authenticator, reason code, and approval chain.
   - Concurrent-session and account-sharing evidence.

2. **Manager approval**
   - Manager level, reporting relationship, conflict declarations, and delegated authority.
   - Exact documents reviewed: invoice, bank details, receipt/service report, PO, bank-change evidence.
   - Signed decision, timestamp, comments, and proof that the manager could stop settlement.
   - Distinguish substantive approval from a workflow click.

3. **Emergency authorization**
   - Token creation and activation timestamps, system clock synchronization, expiry, reason, and issuer.
   - Evidence that the token was active before `Prepare_Payment`.
   - Automatic enforcement of amount, vendor-age, bank-change, AV-2, manager, and audit conditions.

4. **AV-2 operation**
   - Service uptime, rule version, input data, individual check results, warnings, blocks, and overrides.
   - Fail-open versus fail-closed behavior.
   - Evidence of out-of-band bank ownership validation for recent bank changes.

5. **Settlement, recall, and audit**
   - Bank submission, hold, settlement, stop, recall request, and recall-result timestamps.
   - Audit start and completion times, scope, findings, and recovery action.
   - Local business calendar used to calculate one business day.

6. **Receipt and vendor evidence**
   - Goods receipt, service acceptance, responsible business owner, vendor master history, beneficial ownership, and bank-change authorization.
   - Independent confirmation for unusual or recent bank changes.

### Monitoring rules

| Metric or rule | Recommended interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Measure same-user completed payments divided by all completed payments. Split into ED-1-eligible, conditionally eligible, and ineligible cases. A low rate does not excuse an ineligible event. |
| **Override rate** | Measure overrides divided by warnings, by operator, manager credential, rule type, vendor, and bank-change status. An override by someone other than the credential owner should be high priority. |
| **Late manager approval** | Report separately: after release but before settlement, after settlement, and settlement time unknown. These categories have materially different control value. |
| **Audit before recall window** | Track emergency same-user payments audited within one business day and, separately, within 24 hours. The latter better reflects ordinary recall capacity. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Monitor ordered preparer–releaser pairs, pair concentration, rotation, defect rate, and repeated use over rolling periods. Compare with documented team design. |
| **Shared workstations** | Alert on repeated pairs using one device, overlapping sessions, rapid user switching, absent MFA, or one device operating both roles. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Escalate payments within seven days of a bank change, especially same-user releases, overrides, new vendors, or validation outages. Consider a hard hold pending out-of-band verification. |
| **Control outages** | Count payments prepared, released, or settled while AV-2 is unavailable. Monitor unsigned manual fallbacks and require explicit risk-owner authorization. |
| **Emergency-token timing** | Alert where token activation is not earlier than preparation, where tokens are backdated, or where token issuance clusters around particular operators. |
| **Blocked attempts** | Retain and review attempted releases after an AV-2 block, even though they are not completed-payment FEP violations. |
| **Manager concentration and conflicts** | Monitor managers repeatedly approving the same operator, approvals using shared credentials, and reporting or family relationships. |
| **Audit defects by pair or workstation** | Correlate fabricated receipts, bank anomalies, and duplicate attempts with resource pairs and devices rather than reviewing cases in isolation. |

These are **alerts, not proof of misconduct**. Repeated pairing may reflect a documented two-person cell, and a shared workstation may have legitimate shift or secure-terminal explanations. Conversely, a clean audit does not prove independence. Alerts should trigger preservation of evidence and independent investigation, not automatic allegations.

PM4Py behavior cannot be guaranteed identically across all versions. In particular, versions may differ on whether the four-eyes filter retains violating or satisfying traces and whether it returns a DataFrame or event-log object. The code therefore checks the returned completed-case IDs against a direct resource-label comparison and uses the exact complement only when the polarity is unambiguous.

The direct comparison is valid for this embedded data because each completed case has one preparation and one release. For logs with multiple preparations or releases, the pairing semantics should be explicitly defined. PM4Py also establishes only equality or inequality of resource labels; it cannot determine whether accounts represent independent humans, whether credentials were shared, whether a manager performed a substantive review, or whether collusion occurred.
