# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Observation boundary.** The supplied data describe expected arrivals, service-time ranges, skills, shifts, and policy constraints. They do not contain realized timestamps, initial backlog, correlations, or empirical distributions. Therefore, the capacity calculations below are analytical expectations or lower bounds; the policy-performance ranges are model-based estimates, not observations.

**A1 — arrivals.** For expected-load calculations, fractional expected arrivals are retained. For the simulation, arrivals in each two-hour block are Poisson with the stated mean and uniformly distributed within the block. Priority and case type are sampled independently conditional on the block. This independence is necessary because no priority-by-type joint distribution is supplied.

For U1 and U5, the eight-percentage-point increase in P1 share is taken from P2 and P3 proportionally to their original shares. Other reallocations would produce slightly different SLA results but identical total workload.

**A2 — service-time distributions.** Each ordinary and complex-tail range is modeled as uniform within its stated endpoints. Thus, for activity (a),

[
E[S_a]=(1-p_a)\frac{l_a+u_a}{2}
+p_a\frac{l_a^{tail}+u_a^{tail}}{2}.
]

This gives the following pre-multiplier means:

| Activity   | Base mean, min | U4 mean, min |
| ---------- | -------------: | -----------: |
| Intake     |           6.94 |         6.94 |
| Mechanical |          26.30 |        29.60 |
| Electrical |          32.82 |        37.14 |
| Software   |          38.50 |        44.50 |
| Validation |          15.74 |        15.74 |

The U4 column doubles only the diagnostic tail probabilities. These means are not sufficient to infer queueing tails; the simulation samples the full mixtures.

**A3 — calendars and utilization.** Break time is unavailable. A task can pause over a break without a restart penalty. At the end of the 30-minute overtime window, unfinished work may be handed off, incurring the normal handover delay if another resource resumes it. Overtime is excluded from the 90% planned-utilization fairness limit and treated as contingency capacity.

The base simulation starts with an empty queue at 08:00 and permits a no-arrival clearance day. This makes its delays optimistic. Since the base expected workload already exceeds daily capacity, a repeated-day system without added capacity has no stable steady-state queue.

**A4 — overhead interpretation.**

* A seven-minute handover applies when either a stage or an interrupted stage moves to another resource.
* The additional five-minute team-transfer delay applies between Mechanical and Electrical/Software diagnostics when different resources are involved.
* A five-minute setup applies when a resource changes diagnostic family between cases.
* Handover, transfer, and setup are conservatively treated as receiver-occupied time in the simulation. If they are pure delay rather than resource effort, elapsed time remains affected, but the capacity penalty is smaller.
* The eight-minute parallel coordination time is elapsed delay before Validation and does not occupy a diagnostic resource.
* Only diagnostic work is preemptible. An interrupted case can be preempted once, and 10% of its remaining active work is added as restart work.

**A5 — parallel versus sequential work.** For a two-branch case, compare predicted validation-ready times:

[
C_{\text{parallel}}
=\max(EFT_1,EFT_2)+8+\text{relevant handovers},
]

[
C_{\text{sequential}}
=\min_{r\text{ cross-skilled}}
\left(EFT_r+d_{1r}+d_{2r}\right).
]

At mean service times and with immediately available fast resources, before handovers:

* ME parallel diagnostic completion is approximately
  (\max(26.30,32.82)+8=40.82) minutes.
* ME sequential on Deepa is approximately
  (26.30(1.25)+32.82=65.70) minutes.
* ES parallel is approximately
  (\max(32.82,38.50)+8=46.50) minutes.
* ES sequential on Chen is approximately
  (32.82+38.50(1.25)=80.95) minutes.

Parallelism is therefore attractive when both resources are promptly available, but it can be inferior when the second branch waits, when it consumes protected P1 capacity, or when it releases a wave into an already congested Validation queue.

**Decision variables** are:

1. the resource assigned to every Intake, diagnostic, and Validation task;
2. the queue order and dispatch time;
3. whether an ME/ES case is parallel or sequential and, if sequential, its branch order;
4. whether a lower-priority diagnostic is preempted;
5. how much capacity is protected for P1;
6. when cross-skilled overflow is enabled;
7. whether overtime or contingency staffing is activated.

Two defensible objectives lead to different recommendations.

**Tail/SLA-risk objective.**

[
J_{\text{risk}}=
1000(0.90-S_{P1})*+
+100(0.90-S*{P2})*+
+10(0.90-S*{P3})_+
0.02P95(T)
+0.005E[T]
+0.1H,
]

where (S_p) is SLA attainment for priority (p), (T) is case throughput time, and (H) is overhead minutes per case. The weights make P1 non-attainment much more expensive than average delay.

**Robustness-and-fairness objective.**

[
J_{\text{robust}}=
\max_{s\in{\text{base},U1,\ldots,U5}}
\left[
J_{\text{risk},s}
+\lambda\max_r(U_{rs}-0.90)_+
+\gamma(1-\operatorname{Jain}(U_s))
\right].
]

This minimax objective prefers graceful degradation across shocks and penalizes concentration of overload. A full-pooling policy can be preferable under a mean-delay objective; the hybrid policy is preferable under the risk/robustness objective.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

For each block, expected diagnostic counts are:

* Mechanical count = M + ME;
* Electrical count = E + ME + ES;
* Software count = S + ES.

Multiplying these counts by the service means gives the base offered work below. “Regular capacity” is the sum of on-shift minutes after breaks. The fluid ratio is the minimum possible maximum resource utilization from a fractional skill-assignment relaxation:

[
\min z
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_r x_{ra}=W_a,\qquad
\sum_a m_{ra}x_{ra}\le zC_r,
]

with (x_{ra}=0) for unqualified assignments. It ignores queue order, precedence, handover, setup, coordination, and integer cases, so it is a lower bound.

### Base bottleneck migration

Electrical is the largest diagnostic workload at approximately 798 minutes. However, Software is more fragile because only Chen, Emil, and Farah qualify. Before Emil starts at 12:00, base Software arrivals generate approximately 154 minutes of nominal work. If Chen is absent, Farah’s entire four-hour shift supplies only (240/1.40\approx171) base Software minutes even if she does nothing else. Intake, Electrical, and generic work therefore force a Software backlog until Emil arrives.

Pooling can move the bottleneck rather than eliminate it. Fast diagnostic completion creates a concentrated Validation queue. Validation represents about 661 minutes, or 24% of all nominal work, and after 16:00 the generic-capacity set contracts sharply.

### U1

Morning work rises from about 1,031 to 1,752 nominal minutes. The local fluid ratios rise to roughly 1.68 in 08:00–10:00 and 1.74 in 10:00–12:00. Expected P1 arrivals increase from 5.44 to approximately 9.05 per day. A non-reserving pooled policy can have all fast resources occupied by P2/P3 work just before the P1 burst.

### U2

Total workload changes only slightly because doubling E and ES weights is followed by renormalization. The operational problem is composition: Electrical rises to about 861 minutes while Mechanical falls to about 423 minutes. Strict queues strand Mechanical capacity while Electrical waits. This is the scenario in which controlled pooling has the clearest advantage.

### U3

Losing Chen removes 450 regular minutes and the best Electrical resource plus an important Software resource. The fluid lower bound rises from 109% to 136%. Full pooling can use Emil, Deepa, Ada, and Farah more flexibly, but it cannot replace Chen’s pre-noon Software availability.

### U4

Mean diagnostic work rises by about 242 minutes, but the more important effect is variance. Long tasks occupy scarce resources across breaks and shift boundaries, increase handoff probability, and produce correlated Validation arrivals. Policies optimized to mean service times will under-reserve capacity.

### U5

U5 combines the two most damaging mechanisms: approximately 11.2 extra arrivals and a 450-minute capacity loss. The Intake outage briefly withholds cases and then releases a burst into already overloaded diagnostic queues. Routing alone cannot make this scenario target-preserving.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy S — strict specialization with fixed queues

**Eligibility.**

* Intake: Ada, Farah, Emil.
* Mechanical: Ada, Ben.
* Electrical: Chen, Deepa.
* Software: Chen, Emil.
* Validation: Ben, Deepa, Emil.

A qualified resource outside the fixed lane is not used, even when its own lane is idle.

**Priority rule.** Within each lane, use P1 before P2 before P3, then FIFO. Continuity is only a tie-breaker.

**Reservation.** None. P1 obtains queue priority but no protected idle or borrowable capacity.

**Preemption.** A P1 diagnostic may preempt only a lower-priority task in the same diagnostic lane. Preempt only if the P1 is projected to miss its deadline, the victim has not previously been preempted, and the expected net time saved after the 10% restart penalty is at least 20 minutes.

**Parallelization.** ME and ES are parallel by default because their branches enter separate queues. Sequential execution is used only if one branch lane is unavailable until the next shift.

**Handover minimization.** Retain the previous owner only when that owner belongs to the fixed lane and is tied on queue order. The policy otherwise accepts the resulting handovers.

**Overflow trigger.** There is no cross-lane overflow. The operational fallback is overtime or next-shift carryover.

This policy is highly explainable and limits diagnostic-family switching. Its weakness is severe capacity stranding under U2 and U3 and predictable late-day carryover.

## Policy P — full pooling with transparent earliest-risk dispatch

**Eligibility.** Any resource with a finite multiplier for the activity is eligible.

**Priority and assignment rule.** At each assignment opportunity:

1. classify tasks as at-risk P1, other P1, at-risk P2, other P2, then P3;
2. within the highest class, choose the task-resource pair with the earliest predicted case completion;
3. predicted completion includes the resource multiplier, current queue, seven-minute handover, five-minute team transfer, five-minute setup, remaining branches, and Validation;
4. prefer the current owner if predicted completion differs by less than five minutes;
5. use lower cumulative utilization as the final tie-breaker.

**Reservation.** None. Every idle qualified resource is immediately used.

**Preemption.** A P1 diagnostic can preempt any lower-priority qualified diagnostic when the P1 would otherwise miss 120 minutes and the predicted gain exceeds the restart penalty by at least five minutes. This is deliberately aggressive.

**Parallelization.** Select the smaller of predicted parallel and sequential validation-ready times. Parallelism is allowed whenever it is even marginally faster after the eight-minute coordination cost.

**Handover minimization.** Handover and setup are included in predicted completion, but an earlier finish may still justify switching.

**Overflow trigger.** Pooling is always active; there is no distinction between normal and overflow assignments.

This policy should minimize base-case mean delay and respond well to U2. It is more exposed to estimate errors, unnecessary skill switching, and P1 capacity exhaustion during U1.

## Policy H — P1-reserved hybrid with controlled overflow

**Primary lanes.**

* Mechanical: Ada and Ben.
* Electrical: Chen and Deepa.
* Software: Chen and Emil.
* Farah is primarily an Intake and surge-buffer resource.
* Validation is pooled, subject to a P1 guard band.

**Priority score.** Start with predicted SLA slack:

[
\text{slack}=\text{deadline}-\text{now}-\text{predicted remaining time}.
]

Dispatch lexicographically by at-risk P1, other P1, at-risk P2, other P2, and aged P3. Within a class, minimize predicted completion plus:

* seven or twelve minutes for relevant handover/team transfer;
* five minutes for setup;
* a fairness penalty for cumulative utilization above the team median;
* a continuity credit when the same owner remains qualified.

**Borrowable P1 reserve.** Protect one currently available fast resource in each active constrained family:

* Mechanical: Ada early, Ben through 16:00, Deepa after 16:00;
* Electrical: Chen early, Deepa from 10:00–18:00, Emil thereafter;
* Software: Chen before 12:00 and Emil thereafter.

A protected resource may run P2/P3 only when there is no queued P1 and the borrowed task is expected to finish within 20–30 minutes. Borrowed work is the first candidate for permitted P1 preemption. This avoids pure idling while preserving response capability.

**Controlled overflow.** Use a non-primary qualified resource when at least one condition holds:

* a P1 is waiting;
* oldest P2 diagnostic wait exceeds 30 minutes;
* queued expected work divided by primary-family capacity over the next hour exceeds 0.85;
* the predicted wait reduction exceeds handover, setup, and a ten-minute margin.

Farah’s 1.40 diagnostic assignments are permitted only for P1 or when family queue cover exceeds 60 minutes.

**Preemption.** Preempt only when:

* predicted P1 completion without preemption exceeds 105 minutes;
* the victim is diagnostic P2/P3 and has not been preempted;
* at least 15 minutes remain after accounting for the 10% restart penalty;
* no idle eligible resource can meet the same need.

**Parallelization.** Parallelize ME/ES only if it improves predicted validation-ready time by at least 12 minutes after coordination and handovers and does not consume the last protected family resource. When Validation queue cover exceeds 30 minutes, the threshold rises because faster diagnostics would only transfer congestion downstream.

**Fairness.** Under normal operation, avoid new assignments that would take a resource above 90% planned utilization when a similarly capable resource is below 80%. When every eligible resource is saturated, rotate undesirable overflow and overtime according to cumulative burden rather than claiming the 90% target is still achievable.

This policy trades a small amount of base mean performance for better P1 protection, continuity, fairness, and shock behavior.

The most defensible comparative conclusions are:

* Full pooling generally has the lowest mean delay, especially in U2.
* The hybrid generally has the best P1/worst-scenario trade-off.

* No policy reliably reaches both the P1 and P2 90% targets under the stated base capacity.
* Under U5, the choice is not between “good” and “bad” routing; it is between different ways of rationing insufficient capacity.

# 5. Robust recommendation

The primary recommendation is **Policy H, the P1-reserved hybrid**, evaluated under the robustness-and-fairness objective rather than base mean delay.

It does not dominate full pooling on every metric. Full pooling is likely to produce a slightly smaller base mean and performs particularly well in U2. The hybrid is preferable because it:

* preserves a borrowable response channel for P1;
* uses pooling precisely where strict lanes create imbalance;
* avoids habitual use of slow 1.40 assignments;
* includes continuity and setup in the assignment decision;
* raises the threshold for parallelism when Validation is congested;
* distributes overflow and overtime more fairly;
* degrades more gracefully under Chen’s absence and long-tail days.

## Normal-to-stress trigger thresholds

Activate stress mode if any condition is met:

1. a critical resource such as Chen is absent;
2. rolling 60-minute expected work divided by effective available capacity exceeds 0.95 for any diagnostic family;
3. Electrical or Software queue cover exceeds 45 minutes;
4. oldest P1 Intake or diagnostic wait exceeds 10 minutes;
5. any P1 has predicted SLA slack below 25 minutes;
6. at least six arrivals occur in a rolling morning hour, indicating a rate close to the U1 regime;
7. an Intake outage or comparable gating failure begins.

## Stress-mode actions

* Reserve the fastest available Mechanical and E/S response channels for P1; P2/P3 borrowing is limited to short, explicitly preemptible tasks.
* Keep Farah on Intake until Intake queue cover falls below ten minutes. Use her 1.40 diagnostic skills only for at-risk P1 or a family queue over 60 minutes.
* From 12:00, prioritize Emil for Software/ES and Deepa for Electrical or Mechanical according to the larger P1 workload ratio.
* Stop starting new P3 diagnostics while a P1 is at risk, but continue aging P3 cases so that the pause cannot become indefinite.
* Parallelize P1 ME/ES when the net predicted improvement is at least 12 minutes. Prefer sequential same-owner execution for lower-priority cases when a branch would consume the last protected slot or Validation queue cover exceeds 30 minutes.
* Allow preemption only under the 105-minute P1 guard, one interruption per victim, and a 15-minute net-benefit threshold.
* Request contingent capacity rather than trying to disguise an overload as a dispatch problem.

## Capacity implication

For base service alone, at least 533 additional regular productive minutes are needed to clear expected work while keeping planned utilization at 90%. An allowance of 10–15 overhead minutes per case adds approximately 420–630 minutes. Slower overflow assignments add further effort. A practical base planning range is therefore approximately **1.8–2.7 speed-one resource-days**, depending on whether handover delays occupy staff.

For U1, the service-only addition needed at a 90% utilization ceiling is approximately

[
3442/0.9-2490\approx1335
]

productive minutes. For U5, after Chen’s absence reduces regular capacity to 2,040 minutes, the corresponding service-only gap is approximately

[
3442/0.9-2040\approx1785
]

minutes. It is not economical to permanently staff every rare combined shock, but a robust operating design needs on-call cross-skilled coverage, negotiated lower-priority deferral, or both.

Full pooling is preferable when workload is known to be safely below capacity, service estimates are well calibrated, and mean flow time is the dominant objective. Strict specialization is preferable only when cross-skill execution is operationally prohibited or setup/quality risks are substantially larger than those supplied here.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

**Estimate gaming.** A dispatch rule based on predicted complexity can encourage inflated estimates to avoid undesirable assignments or to obtain P1 protection. Store the estimate made at queue entry, every subsequent revision, the estimator identity, and the actual residual. Calibrate by activity, resource, and case type rather than using a single global error factor.

**P3 starvation.** Strict priority in an overloaded system can make P3 delay unbounded. Include an aging term and a minimum service share. One implementable safeguard is to promote a P3 case after six elapsed hours and reserve at least 15% of non-P1 diagnostic starts for aged P3 whenever no P1 has less than 30 minutes of slack.

**Skill erosion.** Strict specialization may erode secondary skills, while indiscriminate pooling may overload cross-skilled workers. Track execution counts by primary versus secondary skill and schedule controlled practice windows. Farah should not become the permanent slow overflow worker merely because she is broadly qualified.

**Excessive preemption.** Aggressive pooling can create repeated disruption whose queueing cost exceeds the apparent P1 gain. Besides the one-preemption-per-case rule, limit preemption when less than 15 minutes remain, record the estimated and realized gain, and review false-positive preemptions.

**Coordination overhead.** A policy that parallelizes every ME/ES case can consume two scarce resources, add handovers, and synchronize arrivals at Validation. Parallelization decisions should be based on validation-ready time, not diagnostic completion alone.

**Validation as the new bottleneck.** Validation has the second-largest stage workload after Electrical and is required by every case. Monitor queue cover and protect a P1 Validation channel. When Validation is congested, reduce lower-priority parallel starts and consider temporary WIP limits at the diagnostic-to-Validation boundary.

**Fairness.** Jain utilization can look excellent because every remaining worker is saturated. Fairness monitoring must also include overtime, undesirable-skill assignments, P1 interruptions handled, cross-team transfers, and consecutive high-complexity cases. The 90% threshold should generate a capacity alert, not an individual-performance criticism.

**Human interpretability.** Operators should see a small set of reason codes, such as `P1_RISK`, `PRIMARY_LANE`, `CONTINUITY`, `OVERFLOW_QUEUE_45`, `BORROWED_RESERVE`, `SETUP_AVOIDED`, and `FAIRNESS_ROTATION`. A large opaque numerical score should be retained for audit but translated into these reasons.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Each assignment decision should produce an auditable decision record, while each execution segment should produce an interval event.

## Required event and decision attributes

| Category          | Attributes                                                                                                            |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity          | `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `task_id`, `branch_id`, `parallel_group_id`                                      |
| Time              | `queue_entry_timestamp`, `assignment_timestamp`, `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp`                                  |
| Resource decision | `org:resource`, `assignment_decision_id`, `assignment_reason`, `eligible_resource_set`, `candidate_scores`            |
| Skill             | `required_skill`, `skill_used`, `resource_multiplier`, `primary_or_overflow_skill`                                    |
| Service           | `planned_service_minutes`, `actual_active_service_minutes`, `remaining_service_at_assignment`                         |
| Pause/resume      | `pause_timestamp`, `resume_timestamp`, `pause_reason`, `preemption_count`, `restart_penalty_minutes`                  |
| Handover/setup    | `from_resource`, `to_resource`, `handover_minutes`, `team_transfer_minutes`, `setup_minutes`, `previous_skill_family` |
| Branch decision   | `parallel_or_sequential`, `predicted_parallel_completion`, `predicted_sequential_completion`, `coordination_minutes`  |
| Policy state      | `policy_version`, `reservation_state`, `overflow_trigger`, `stress_mode`                                              |
| Shock context     | `scenario_or_shock_flag`, `absence_flag`, `outage_flag`, `arrival_rate_signal`                                        |
| SLA clock         | `case_arrival_timestamp`, `sla_deadline`, `elapsed_sla_minutes`, `predicted_completion`, `predicted_slack`            |
| Workload          | queue lengths and expected queue work by skill, resource utilization snapshot, Validation queue cover                 |

## Deployment indicators

At minimum, monitor by priority, type, arrival block, policy version, and shock flag:

* SLA attainment and confidence intervals;
* case throughput mean, p90, and p95;
* queue-entry-to-assignment waiting by stage;
* active service and residual error by activity/resource;
* setup, handover, team-transfer, and coordination minutes per case;
* number and realized value of preemptions;
* parallelization rate and actual parallel-versus-sequential benefit;
* busy/available utilization, overtime, Jain index, and undesirable-skill burden;
* aged P3 WIP and maximum P3 wait;
* Validation queue cover and diagnostic-to-Validation arrival bursts;
* backlog at shift end and age of carried-over work.

## PM4Py interpretation

`pm4py.format_dataframe` should standardize the interval log using the case, activity, completion, and start timestamp keys.

`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` produces **case-level interval-derived service, sojourn, and waiting measures**. Those values are not queue-specific stage waits. In parallel cases, case-level active interval coverage also differs from total resource effort because overlapping branch minutes should not be double-counted in elapsed case service but must be counted when measuring labor capacity.

Therefore:

* calculate queue-specific waiting directly as `assignment_timestamp - queue_entry_timestamp`;
* sum those waits by task when a task is requeued after preemption or shift handoff;
* calculate resource effort by summing interval durations across resources;
* calculate elapsed case service separately from resource effort.

`pm4py.get_service_time` can expose service-time drift by activity. The result should be segmented further by resource, case type, shock flag, and policy version to determine whether apparent drift is caused by case mix or changed resource assignments.

`pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` can show increasing transfer burden and concentration on cross-skilled resources. Its directly-follows interpretation must be used cautiously for parallel branches, where timestamp ordering does not necessarily imply a business handover. Explicit `from_resource` and `to_resource` records should be treated as the authoritative handover measure.

Bottleneck migration is best detected by combining:

* stage-specific queue p90;
* expected queued work in minutes rather than case count;
* service-time drift;
* resource utilization by skill;
* handover/setup burden;
* Validation arrival and queue-cover profiles.

A movement from high Electrical queue time to high Validation queue time after a pooling change is not a system improvement merely because the Electrical queue fell.

def multiplier(resource: str, activity: str) -> float:
    value = SKILLS.loc[resource, activity]
    return float(value) if pd.notna(value) else math.inf


def qualified(resource: str, activity: str) -> bool:
    return math.isfinite(multiplier(resource, activity))


def regular_capacity(resource: str) -> int:
    row = SHIFTS.loc[resource]
    break_minutes = (
        0
        if pd.isna(row.break_start)
        else int(row.break_end - row.break_start)
    )
    return int(row.regular_end - row.shift_start - break_minutes)


def on_calendar(resource: str, minute: int, scenario: str) -> bool:
    row = SHIFTS.loc[resource]
    m = minute % 1440

    if not (row.shift_start <= m < row.overtime_end):
        return False
    if (
        pd.notna(row.break_start)
        and row.break_start <= m < row.break_end
    ):
        return False

    # Chen returns on the clearance day; U3/U5 specify a full scenario-day absence.
    if (
        minute < 1440
        and scenario in {"U3", "U5"}
        and resource == "Chen"
    ):
        return False

    return True


def intake_outage(minute: int, scenario: str) -> bool:
    m = minute % 1440
    return (
        scenario == "U5"
        and minute < 1440
        and 90 <= m < 120
    )


def diagnostic_group(activity: Optional[str]) -> Optional[str]:
    if activity == "Mechanical":
        return "mechanical"
    if activity in {"Electrical", "Software"}:
        return "electrical_software"
    return None


def scenario_block(
    block: pd.Series,
    scenario: str,
) -> tuple[float, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    lam = float(block.expected_arrivals)
    priorities = block[
        ["P1_pct", "P2_pct", "P3_pct"]
    ].to_numpy(dtype=float)
    types = block[
        ["M_pct", "E_pct", "S_pct", "ME_pct", "ES_pct"]
    ].to_numpy(dtype=float)

    if scenario in {"U1", "U5"} and block.start_minute < 240:
        lam *= 1.7
        priorities[0] += 0.08
        priorities[1:] *= (
            (1.0 - priorities[0]) / priorities[1:].sum()
        )

    if (
        scenario == "U2"
        and 240 <= block.start_minute < 480
    ):
        types[[1, 4]] *= 2.0
        types /= types.sum()

    return lam, priorities, types


def draw_service(
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    activity: str,
    scenario: str,
) -> float:
    row = SERVICE.loc[activity]
    tail_probability = float(row.tail_probability)

    if scenario == "U4" and activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
        tail_probability = min(1.0, 2.0 * tail_probability)

    if rng.random() < tail_probability:
        return float(rng.uniform(row.tail_low, row.tail_high))

    return float(rng.uniform(row.ordinary_low, row.ordinary_high))


def generate_cases(seed: int, scenario: str) -> list[CaseState]:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    cases: list[CaseState] = []
    case_counter = 0

    for _, block in DEMAND.iterrows():
        lam, priority_prob, type_prob = scenario_block(block, scenario)
        count = int(rng.poisson(lam))
        arrivals = np.sort(
            rng.integers(
                int(block.start_minute),
                int(block.end_minute),
                size=count,
            )
        )

        for arrival in arrivals:
            priority = str(
                rng.choice(["P1", "P2", "P3"], p=priority_prob)
            )
            case_type = str(
                rng.choice(["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"], p=type_prob)
            )
            sampled = {
                activity: draw_service(rng, activity, scenario)
                for activity in [
                    "Intake",
                    *TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case_type],
                    "Validation",
                ]
            }

            case_id = f"{scenario}-{seed}-{case_counter:04d}"
            cases.append(
                CaseState(
                    case_id=case_id,
                    arrival=int(arrival),
                    priority=priority,
                    case_type=case_type,
                    sampled_service=sampled,
                    deadline=int(arrival) + DEADLINE[priority],
                )
            )
            case_counter += 1

    return cases


def add_task(
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    next_task_id: int,
    case: CaseState,
    activity: str,
    ready_time: int,
    required_resource: Optional[str] = None,
) -> int:
    base = float(case.sampled_service[activity])
    tasks[next_task_id] = TaskState(
        task_id=next_task_id,
        case_id=case.case_id,
        activity=activity,
        total_base_minutes=base,
        remaining_base_minutes=base,
        ready_time=int(ready_time),
        queue_entry_time=int(ready_time),
        required_resource=required_resource,
    )
    return next_task_id + 1


def overhead_items(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    task: TaskState,
    case: CaseState,
) -> list[list[float | str]]:
    items: list[list[float | str]] = []

    # A resumed activity can itself change resource.
    source_resource = (
        task.last_resource
        if task.last_resource is not None
        else case.last_owner
    )
    if (
        source_resource is not None
        and source_resource != resource_state.resource
    ):
        items.append(["Handover", float(HANDOVER_MINUTES)])

    old_group = diagnostic_group(case.last_diagnostic)
    new_group = diagnostic_group(task.activity)
    if (
        old_group
        and new_group
        and old_group != new_group
        and case.last_owner != resource_state.resource
    ):
        items.append(["TeamTransfer", float(CROSS_TEAM_MINUTES)])

    if (
        task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS
        and resource_state.last_diagnostic is not None
        and resource_state.last_diagnostic != task.activity
        and resource_state.last_diagnostic_case != case.case_id
    ):
        items.append(["Setup", float(SETUP_MINUTES)])

    return items


def estimated_overhead(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    task: TaskState,
    case: CaseState,
) -> float:
    return sum(
        float(item[1])
        for item in overhead_items(resource_state, task, case)
    )


def next_calendar_minute(
    resource: str,
    minute: int,
    scenario: str,
) -> float:
    for candidate in range(minute, minute + 1441):
        if on_calendar(resource, candidate, scenario):
            return float(candidate)
    return math.inf


def projected_free(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    minute: int,
    scenario: str,
) -> float:
    if resource_state.task_id is None:
        return next_calendar_minute(
            resource_state.resource,
            minute,
            scenario,
        )

    task = tasks[resource_state.task_id]
    remaining = sum(
        float(item[1])
        for item in resource_state.overhead_queue
    )
    remaining += (
        task.remaining_base_minutes
        * multiplier(resource_state.resource, task.activity)
    )

    return max(
        next_calendar_minute(
            resource_state.resource,
            minute,
            scenario,
        ),
        minute + remaining,
    )


def queue_pressure(
    activity: str,
    minute: int,
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
) -> float:
    queued_base = sum(
        task.remaining_base_minutes
        for task in tasks.values()
        if (
            task.status == "pending"
            and task.ready_time <= minute
            and task.activity == activity
        )
    )
    effective_servers = sum(
        1.0 / multiplier(resource, activity)
        for resource in PRIMARY_LANES[activity]
        if qualified(resource, activity)
    )

    return queued_base / max(effective_servers, 1e-9)


def protected_resource(
    activity: str,
    minute: int,
    scenario: str,
) -> Optional[str]:
    m = minute % 1440

    if activity == "Mechanical":
        candidate = (
            "Ada"
            if m < 120
            else ("Ben" if m < 480 else "Deepa")
        )
    elif activity == "Electrical":
        candidate = (
            "Chen"
            if m < 120
            else ("Deepa" if m < 600 else "Emil")
        )
    elif activity == "Software":
        candidate = "Chen" if m < 240 else "Emil"
    else:
        return None

    return (
        candidate
        if on_calendar(candidate, minute, scenario)
        else None
    )


def is_eligible(
    policy: str,
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    task: TaskState,
    case: CaseState,
    minute: int,
    scenario: str,
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
) -> bool:
    resource = resource_state.resource
    activity = task.activity

    if (
        task.required_resource is not None
        and task.required_resource != resource
    ):
        return False

    if not qualified(resource, activity):
        return False

    if policy == "strict":
        return resource in STRICT_LANES[activity]

    if policy == "pool":
        return True

    # Hybrid: primary lanes first, controlled overflow later.
    if activity not in DIAGNOSTICS:
        return True

    if resource in PRIMARY_LANES[activity]:
        protected = protected_resource(
            activity,
            minute,
            scenario,
        )
        duration = (
            task.remaining_base_minutes
            * multiplier(resource, activity)
        )

        # Borrowable reserve: long lower-priority work waits briefly.
        if (
            case.priority != "P1"
            and protected == resource
            and duration > 30
            and minute - task.queue_entry_time < 20
        ):
            return False

        return True

    wait = minute - task.queue_entry_time
    pressure = queue_pressure(activity, minute, tasks)

    if case.priority == "P1":
        return True

    if resource == "Farah" and pressure < 60:
        return False

    return wait >= 30 or pressure >= 45


def expected_case_remaining(
    case: CaseState,
    task: TaskState,
    resource: str,
) -> float:
    remaining = (
        task.remaining_base_minutes
        * multiplier(resource, task.activity)
    )

    if task.activity == "Intake":
        remaining += sum(
            case.sampled_service[a]
            * min(
                multiplier(r, a)
                for r in SKILLS.index
                if qualified(r, a)
            )
            for a in TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type]
        )
        remaining += case.sampled_service["Validation"]

        if len(TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type]) == 2:
            remaining += PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES

    elif task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
        for activity in TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type]:
            if (
                activity != task.activity
                and activity not in case.diagnostics_done
            ):
                remaining += (
                    case.sampled_service[activity]
                    * min(
                        multiplier(r, activity)
                        for r in SKILLS.index
                        if qualified(r, activity)
                    )
                )

        remaining += case.sampled_service["Validation"]

        if case.diagnostic_mode == "parallel":
            remaining += PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES

    return remaining


def dispatch_score(
    policy: str,
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    task: TaskState,
    case: CaseState,
    minute: int,
    scenario: str,
) -> float:
    resource = resource_state.resource
    overhead = estimated_overhead(resource_state, task, case)
    duration = (
        task.remaining_base_minutes
        * multiplier(resource, task.activity)
        + overhead
    )
    slack = (
        case.deadline
        - minute
        - expected_case_remaining(case, task, resource)
        - overhead
    )
    waiting = minute - task.queue_entry_time
    continuity = 1.0 if case.last_owner == resource else 0.0
    priority_weight = {
        "P1": 3000.0,
        "P2": 1000.0,
        "P3": 0.0,
    }[case.priority]

    if policy == "strict":
        return (
            priority_weight
            + waiting
            - 0.1 * duration
            + 8.0 * continuity
        )

    score = (
        priority_weight
        + 500.0 * (slack < 15)
        + 2.0 * waiting
        - 2.0 * duration
        + 15.0 * continuity
    )

    if policy == "hybrid":
        score -= (
            120.0
            * resource_state.busy_minutes
            / max(regular_capacity(resource), 1)
        )

        if (
            task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS
            and resource not in PRIMARY_LANES[task.activity]
        ):
            score -= 20.0

        if (
            case.priority != "P1"
            and protected_resource(
                task.activity,
                minute,
                scenario,
            ) == resource
            and duration > 30
        ):
            score -= 180.0

    return score


def choose_diagnostic_mode(
    policy: str,
    case: CaseState,
    minute: int,
    resources: dict[str, ResourceState],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    scenario: str,
) -> tuple[str, Optional[str], Optional[list[str]]]:
    activities = TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type]

    if len(activities) == 1:
        return "single", None, None

    if policy == "strict":
        return "parallel", None, None

    # Parallel estimate: branches must use distinct resources.
    best_parallel = math.inf
    for first_resource in SKILLS.index:
        for second_resource in SKILLS.index:
            if first_resource == second_resource:
                continue
            if (
                not qualified(first_resource, activities[0])
                or not qualified(second_resource, activities[1])
            ):
                continue

            finish_1 = projected_free(
                resources[first_resource],
                tasks,
                minute,
                scenario,
            )
            finish_1 += (
                case.sampled_service[activities[0]]
                * multiplier(first_resource, activities[0])
            )

            finish_2 = projected_free(
                resources[second_resource],
                tasks,
                minute,
                scenario,
            )
            finish_2 += (
                case.sampled_service[activities[1]]
                * multiplier(second_resource, activities[1])
            )

            best_parallel = min(
                best_parallel,
                max(finish_1, finish_2)
                + PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES,
            )

    # Sequential estimate: one cross-skilled owner retains the case.
    best_sequential: tuple[float, Optional[str]] = (
        math.inf,
        None,
    )
    for resource in SKILLS.index:
        if all(
            qualified(resource, activity)
            for activity in activities
        ):
            finish = projected_free(
                resources[resource],
                tasks,
                minute,
                scenario,
            )
            finish += sum(
                case.sampled_service[a]
                * multiplier(resource, a)
                for a in activities
            )

            if (
                case.last_owner is not None
                and case.last_owner != resource
            ):
                finish += HANDOVER_MINUTES

            if finish < best_sequential[0]:
                best_sequential = (finish, resource)

    # Full pooling accepts any positive predicted gain.
    # Hybrid requires a 12-minute guard after coordination/overhead.
    required_gain = 0 if policy == "pool" else 12

    if best_parallel + required_gain < best_sequential[0]:
        return "parallel", None, None

    order = sorted(
        activities,
        key=lambda a: case.sampled_service[a],
        reverse=True,
    )
    return "sequential", best_sequential[1], order


def close_interval(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    minute: int,
    events: list[dict],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    release_reason: Optional[str] = None,
) -> None:
    if (
        resource_state.active_start is None
        or resource_state.active_label is None
        or resource_state.task_id is None
    ):
        resource_state.active_start = None
        resource_state.active_label = None
        return

    if minute <= resource_state.active_start:
        return

    task = tasks[resource_state.task_id]
    case = cases[task.case_id]

    events.append(
        {
            "case:concept:name": case.case_id,
            "task_id": task.task_id,
            "concept:name": resource_state.active_label,
            "start_minute": resource_state.active_start,
            "end_minute": minute,
            "org:resource": resource_state.resource,
            "priority": case.priority,
            "case_type": case.case_type,
            "queue_entry_minute": task.queue_entry_time,
            "assignment_minute": task.last_assignment_time,
            "sla_deadline_minute": case.deadline,
            "release_reason": release_reason,
        }
    )

    resource_state.active_start = None
    resource_state.active_label = None


def release_running_task(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    minute: int,
    events: list[dict],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    reason: str,
    restart_penalty: bool,
) -> None:
    if resource_state.task_id is None:
        return

    task = tasks[resource_state.task_id]
    close_interval(
        resource_state,
        minute,
        events,
        tasks,
        cases,
        release_reason=reason,
    )

    if restart_penalty:
        task.remaining_base_minutes *= (
            1.0 + PREEMPTION_RESTART_FRACTION
        )
        cases[task.case_id].preemptions_suffered += 1

    task.status = "pending"
    task.ready_time = minute
    task.queue_entry_time = minute
    resource_state.task_id = None
    resource_state.overhead_queue = []


def try_one_preemption(
    policy: str,
    minute: int,
    resources: dict[str, ResourceState],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    scenario: str,
    events: list[dict],
) -> bool:
    pending_p1 = sorted(
        [
            task
            for task in tasks.values()
            if (
                task.status == "pending"
                and task.ready_time <= minute
                and task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS
                and cases[task.case_id].priority == "P1"
            )
        ],
        key=lambda task: cases[task.case_id].deadline,
    )

    for p1_task in pending_p1:
        p1_case = cases[p1_task.case_id]

        # Do not preempt if an eligible resource is already idle.
        if any(
            rs.task_id is None
            and on_calendar(rs.resource, minute, scenario)
            and is_eligible(
                policy,
                rs,
                p1_task,
                p1_case,
                minute,
                scenario,
                tasks,
            )
            for rs in resources.values()
        ):
            continue

        candidates: list[tuple[float, ResourceState]] = []

        for rs in resources.values():
            if (
                rs.task_id is None
                or rs.active_label not in DIAGNOSTICS
            ):
                continue

            current = tasks[rs.task_id]
            current_case = cases[current.case_id]

            if (
                current_case.priority == "P1"
                or current_case.preemptions_suffered >= 1
            ):
                continue

            if not is_eligible(
                policy,
                rs,
                p1_task,
                p1_case,
                minute,
                scenario,
                tasks,
            ):
                continue

            if (
                policy == "strict"
                and current.activity != p1_task.activity
            ):
                continue

            remaining_current = (
                current.remaining_base_minutes
                * multiplier(rs.resource, current.activity)
            )
            p1_finish_without_preemption = (
                minute
                + remaining_current
                + p1_task.remaining_base_minutes
                * multiplier(rs.resource, p1_task.activity)
            )

            safety_buffer = {
                "strict": 10,
                "pool": 0,
                "hybrid": 15,
            }[policy]

            if (
                p1_finish_without_preemption
                <= p1_case.deadline - safety_buffer
            ):
                continue

            net_saved = (
                remaining_current
                * (1.0 - PREEMPTION_RESTART_FRACTION)
            )
            minimum_net_saved = {
                "strict": 20,
                "pool": 5,
                "hybrid": 15,
            }[policy]

            if net_saved >= minimum_net_saved:
                candidates.append((remaining_current, rs))

        if candidates:
            _, chosen = max(
                candidates,
                key=lambda item: item[0],
            )
            release_running_task(
                chosen,
                minute,
                events,
                tasks,
                cases,
                reason="P1_preemption",
                restart_penalty=True,
            )
            return True

    return False


def dispatch(
    policy: str,
    minute: int,
    resources: dict[str, ResourceState],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    scenario: str,
    assignments: list[dict],
) -> None:
    while True:
        pairs: list[
            tuple[float, ResourceState, TaskState]
        ] = []

        for rs in resources.values():
            if (
                rs.task_id is not None
                or not on_calendar(rs.resource, minute, scenario)
            ):
                continue

            for task in tasks.values():
                if (
                    task.status != "pending"
                    or task.ready_time > minute
                ):
                    continue

                if (
                    task.activity == "Intake"
                    and intake_outage(minute, scenario)
                ):
                    continue

                case = cases[task.case_id]
                if is_eligible(
                    policy,
                    rs,
                    task,
                    case,
                    minute,
                    scenario,
                    tasks,
                ):
                    pairs.append(
                        (
                            dispatch_score(
                                policy,
                                rs,
                                task,
                                case,
                                minute,
                                scenario,
                            ),
                            rs,
                            task,
                        )
                    )

        if not pairs:
            return

        _, rs, task = max(
            pairs,
            key=lambda item: item[0],
        )
        case = cases[task.case_id]

        task.status = "running"
        if task.first_assignment_time is None:
            task.first_assignment_time = minute
        task.last_assignment_time = minute

        rs.task_id = task.task_id
        rs.overhead_queue = overhead_items(rs, task, case)
        task.last_resource = rs.resource

        eligible_set = tuple(
            r.resource
            for r in resources.values()
            if (
                on_calendar(r.resource, minute, scenario)
                and is_eligible(
                    policy,
                    r,
                    task,
                    case,
                    minute,
                    scenario,
                    tasks,
                )
            )
        )

        assignments.append(
            {
                "case:concept:name": case.case_id,
                "task_id": task.task_id,
                "concept:name": task.activity,
                "queue_entry_minute": task.queue_entry_time,
                "assignment_minute": minute,
                "org:resource": rs.resource,
                "priority": case.priority,
                "case_type": case.case_type,
                "eligible_resource_set": eligible_set,
                "assignment_score": dispatch_score(
                    policy,
                    rs,
                    task,
                    case,
                    minute,
                    scenario,
                ),
            }
        )


def finish_task(
    resource_state: ResourceState,
    minute: int,
    events: list[dict],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    resources: dict[str, ResourceState],
    policy: str,
    scenario: str,
    next_task_id: int,
) -> int:
    assert resource_state.task_id is not None

    task = tasks[resource_state.task_id]
    case = cases[task.case_id]

    close_interval(
        resource_state,
        minute,
        events,
        tasks,
        cases,
    )
    task.status = "done"

    if task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
        resource_state.last_diagnostic = task.activity
        resource_state.last_diagnostic_case = case.case_id
        case.last_diagnostic = task.activity

    case.last_owner = resource_state.resource
    resource_state.task_id = None
    resource_state.overhead_queue = []

    if task.activity == "Intake":
        mode, sequential_resource, order = (
            choose_diagnostic_mode(
                policy,
                case,
                minute,
                resources,
                tasks,
                scenario,
            )
        )
        case.diagnostic_mode = mode

        if mode == "single":
            next_task_id = add_task(
                tasks,
                next_task_id,
                case,
                TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type][0],
                minute,
            )

        elif mode == "parallel":
            for activity in TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type]:
                next_task_id = add_task(
                    tasks,
                    next_task_id,
                    case,
                    activity,
                    minute,
                )

        else:
            assert (
                sequential_resource is not None
                and order is not None
            )
            case.sequential_second = (
                order[1],
                sequential_resource,
            )
            next_task_id = add_task(
                tasks,
                next_task_id,
                case,
                order[0],
                minute,
                sequential_resource,
            )

    elif task.activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
        case.diagnostics_done.add(task.activity)

        if (
            case.diagnostic_mode == "sequential"
            and case.sequential_second is not None
        ):
            activity, sequential_resource = (
                case.sequential_second
            )
            case.sequential_second = None
            next_task_id = add_task(
                tasks,
                next_task_id,
                case,
                activity,
                minute,
                sequential_resource,
            )

        elif (
            len(case.diagnostics_done)
            == len(TYPE_ACTIVITIES[case.case_type])
        ):
            ready = minute + (
                PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES
                if case.diagnostic_mode == "parallel"
                else 0
            )
            next_task_id = add_task(
                tasks,
                next_task_id,
                case,
                "Validation",
                ready,
            )

    elif task.activity == "Validation":
        case.completion = minute

    return next_task_id


def process_one_minute(
    policy: str,
    scenario: str,
    minute: int,
    resources: dict[str, ResourceState],
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState],
    cases: dict[str, CaseState],
    events: list[dict],
    assignments: list[dict],
    next_task_id: int,
) -> int:
    # Planned release at the end of each overtime window.
    for rs in resources.values():
        if (
            rs.task_id is not None
            and minute % 1440
            == int(SHIFTS.loc[rs.resource, "overtime_end"])
        ):
            release_running_task(
                rs,
                minute,
                events,
                tasks,
                cases,
                "shift_end",
                restart_penalty=False,
            )

        # The U5 outage releases Intake tasks so staff can do other work.
        if (
            rs.task_id is not None
            and tasks[rs.task_id].activity == "Intake"
            and scenario == "U5"
            and minute < 1440
            and minute % 1440 == 90
        ):
            release_running_task(
                rs,
                minute,
                events,
                tasks,
                cases,
                "IT_outage",
                restart_penalty=False,
            )

    # At most three preemptions are evaluated at one timestamp.
    for _ in range(3):
        if not try_one_preemption(
            policy,
            minute,
            resources,
            tasks,
            cases,
            scenario,
            events,
        ):
            break

        dispatch(
            policy,
            minute,
            resources,
            tasks,
            cases,
            scenario,
            assignments,
        )

    dispatch(
        policy,
        minute,
        resources,
        tasks,
        cases,
        scenario,
        assignments,
    )

    completed_resources: list[ResourceState] = []

    for rs in resources.values():
        if (
            rs.task_id is None
            or not on_calendar(rs.resource, minute, scenario)
        ):
            if rs.active_start is not None:
                close_interval(
                    rs,
                    minute,
                    events,
                    tasks,
                    cases,
                )
            continue

        task = tasks[rs.task_id]

        if (
            task.activity == "Intake"
            and intake_outage(minute, scenario)
        ):
            if rs.active_start is not None:
                close_interval(
                    rs,
                    minute,
                    events,
                    tasks,
                    cases,
                )
            continue

        label = (
            str(rs.overhead_queue[0][0])
            if rs.overhead_queue
            else task.activity
        )

        if rs.active_label != label:
            close_interval(
                rs,
                minute,
                events,
                tasks,
                cases,
            )
            rs.active_label = label
            rs.active_start = minute

        rs.busy_minutes += 1.0
        if minute < 1440:
            rs.busy_minutes_day0 += 1.0

        if rs.overhead_queue:
            rs.overhead_minutes += 1.0
            rs.overhead_queue[0][1] = (
                float(rs.overhead_queue[0][1]) - 1.0
            )

            if float(rs.overhead_queue[0][1]) <= 0:
                close_interval(
                    rs,
                    minute + 1,
                    events,
                    tasks,
                    cases,
                )
                rs.overhead_queue.pop(0)

        else:
            task.remaining_base_minutes -= (
                1.0
                / multiplier(rs.resource, task.activity)
            )

            if task.remaining_base_minutes <= 1e-9:
                close_interval(
                    rs,
                    minute + 1,
                    events,
                    tasks,
                    cases,
                )
                completed_resources.append(rs)

    for rs in completed_resources:
        next_task_id = finish_task(
            rs,
            minute + 1,
            events,
            tasks,
            cases,
            resources,
            policy,
            scenario,
            next_task_id,
        )

    return next_task_id


def simulate(
    seed: int,
    policy: str,
    scenario: str,
) -> tuple[dict, pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    case_list = generate_cases(seed, scenario)
    cases = {
        case.case_id: case
        for case in case_list
    }

    arrivals: dict[int, list[str]] = defaultdict(list)
    for case in case_list:
        arrivals[case.arrival].append(case.case_id)

    resources = {
        resource: ResourceState(resource)
        for resource in SKILLS.index
    }
    tasks: dict[int, TaskState] = {}
    events: list[dict] = []
    assignments: list[dict] = []
    next_task_id = 0

    for minute in range(SIMULATION_HORIZON_MINUTES):
        for case_id in arrivals.get(minute, []):
            next_task_id = add_task(
                tasks,
                next_task_id,
                cases[case_id],
                "Intake",
                minute,
            )

        next_task_id = process_one_minute(
            policy,
            scenario,
            minute,
            resources,
            tasks,
            cases,
            events,
            assignments,
            next_task_id,
        )

    for rs in resources.values():
        close_interval(
            rs,
            SIMULATION_HORIZON_MINUTES,
            events,
            tasks,
            cases,
        )

    # Unfinished cases are right-censored at the finite horizon.
    flow_times = np.array(
        [
            (
                case.completion
                if case.completion is not None
                else SIMULATION_HORIZON_MINUTES
            )
            - case.arrival
            for case in case_list
        ],
        dtype=float,
    )

    def sla_counts(
        priority: str,
        target: int,
    ) -> tuple[int, int]:
        selected = [
            case
            for case in case_list
            if case.priority == priority
        ]
        met = sum(
            (
                case.completion is not None
                and case.completion - case.arrival <= target
            )
            for case in selected
        )
        return int(met), len(selected)

    p1_met, p1_n = sla_counts("P1", 120)
    p2_met, p2_n = sla_counts("P2", 480)
    p3_met, p3_n = sla_counts("P3", 1440)

    present_resources = [
        resource
        for resource in SKILLS.index
        if not (
            scenario in {"U3", "U5"}
            and resource == "Chen"
        )
    ]
    utilizations = np.array(
        [
            resources[resource].busy_minutes_day0
            / regular_capacity(resource)
            for resource in present_resources
        ]
    )
    jain = (
        utilizations.sum() ** 2
        / (
            len(utilizations)
            * np.square(utilizations).sum()
        )
    )

    event_df = pd.DataFrame(events)
    assignment_df = pd.DataFrame(assignments)

    if event_df.empty:
        overhead_total = 0.0
    else:
        overhead_rows = event_df[
            event_df["concept:name"].isin(
                OVERHEAD_ACTIVITIES
            )
        ]
        overhead_total = (
            overhead_rows["end_minute"]
            - overhead_rows["start_minute"]
        ).sum()

    metrics = {
        "scenario": scenario,
        "policy": policy,
        "seed": seed,
        "cases": len(case_list),
        "mean_delay": (
            float(flow_times.mean())
            if len(flow_times)
            else np.nan
        ),
        "p90_delay": (
            float(np.quantile(flow_times, 0.90))
            if len(flow_times)
            else np.nan
        ),
        "p95_delay": (
            float(np.quantile(flow_times, 0.95))
            if len(flow_times)
            else np.nan
        ),
        "P1_met": p1_met,
        "P1_n": p1_n,
        "P2_met": p2_met,
        "P2_n": p2_n,
        "P3_met": p3_met,
        "P3_n": p3_n,
        "completion_rate": (
            float(
                np.mean(
                    [
                        case.completion is not None
                        for case in case_list
                    ]
                )
            )
            if case_list
            else np.nan
        ),
        "max_resource_utilization": (
            float(utilizations.max())
            if len(utilizations)
            else np.nan
        ),
        "utilization_fairness_jain": float(jain),
        "handover_setup_minutes_per_case": (
            float(overhead_total / max(len(case_list), 1))
        ),
        "preemptions_per_case": (
            float(
                sum(
                    case.preemptions_suffered
                    for case in case_list
                )
                / max(len(case_list), 1)
            )
        ),
    }

    return metrics, event_df, assignment_df


def wilson_interval(
    successes: int,
    trials: int,
    z: float = 1.96,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    if trials == 0:
        return np.nan, np.nan, np.nan

    p = successes / trials
    denominator = 1.0 + z * z / trials
    center = (
        p + z * z / (2 * trials)
    ) / denominator
    radius = (
        z
        * math.sqrt(
            (
                p * (1 - p)
                + z * z / (4 * trials)
            )
            / trials
        )
        / denominator
    )

    return (
        p,
        max(0.0, center - radius),
        min(1.0, center + radius),
    )


def summarize_runs(runs: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []

    for (scenario, policy), group in runs.groupby(
        ["scenario", "policy"],
        sort=False,
    ):
        p1 = wilson_interval(
            int(group.P1_met.sum()),
            int(group.P1_n.sum()),
        )
        p2 = wilson_interval(
            int(group.P2_met.sum()),
            int(group.P2_n.sum()),
        )
        p3 = wilson_interval(
            int(group.P3_met.sum()),
            int(group.P3_n.sum()),
        )

        rows.append(
            {
                "scenario": scenario,
                "policy": policy,
                "mean_delay_mean": group.mean_delay.mean(),
                "mean_delay_q10": group.mean_delay.quantile(.10),
                "mean_delay_q90": group.mean_delay.quantile(.90),
                "p90_delay_mean": group.p90_delay.mean(),
                "p90_delay_q10": group.p90_delay.quantile(.10),
                "p90_delay_q90": group.p90_delay.quantile(.90),
                "P1_SLA": p1[0],
                "P1_CI_low": p1[1],
                "P1_CI_high": p1[2],
                "P2_SLA": p2[0],
                "P2_CI_low": p2[1],
                "P2_CI_high": p2[2],
                "P3_SLA": p3[0],
                "P3_CI_low": p3[1],
                "P3_CI_high": p3[2],
                "max_resource_utilization_mean": (
                    group.max_resource_utilization.mean()
                ),
                "utilization_fairness_jain_mean": (
                    group.utilization_fairness_jain.mean()
                ),
                "handover_setup_minutes_per_case_mean": (
                    group.handover_setup_minutes_per_case.mean()
                ),
                "completion_rate_mean": (
                    group.completion_rate.mean()
                ),
            }
        )

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def add_timestamps(
    event_df: pd.DataFrame,
    assignment_df: pd.DataFrame,
    policy: str,
    scenario: str,
) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    origin = pd.Timestamp(
        "2026-01-05 08:00:00",
        tz="Europe/Berlin",
    )

    log = event_df.copy()
    log["start_timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            log.pop("start_minute"),
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    log["time:timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            log.pop("end_minute"),
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    log["queue_entry_timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            log.pop("queue_entry_minute"),
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    log["assignment_timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            log.pop("assignment_minute"),
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    log["sla_deadline"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            log.pop("sla_deadline_minute"),
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    log["policy_version"] = policy
    log["scenario_or_shock_flag"] = scenario

    assignments = assignment_df.copy()
    assignments["queue_entry_timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            assignments.queue_entry_minute,
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    assignments["assignment_timestamp"] = (
        origin
        + pd.to_timedelta(
            assignments.assignment_minute,
            unit="m",
        )
    )
    assignments["queue_wait_minutes"] = (
        assignments.assignment_minute
        - assignments.queue_entry_minute
    )
    assignments["policy_version"] = policy
    assignments["scenario_or_shock_flag"] = scenario

    return log, assignments

    for scenario in scenarios:
        for policy in policies:
            for replication in range(N_REPLICATIONS):
                seed = 10_000 + replication
                metrics, event_df, assignment_df = simulate(
                    seed,
                    policy,
                    scenario,
                )
                all_metrics.append(metrics)

                if (
                    scenario == "U5"
                    and policy == "hybrid"
                    and replication == 0
                ):
                    representative = (
                        event_df,
                        assignment_df,
                    )

    runs = pd.DataFrame(all_metrics)
    comparison = summarize_runs(runs)

    compact_columns = [
        "scenario",
        "policy",
        "mean_delay_mean",
        "p90_delay_mean",
        "P1_SLA",
        "P2_SLA",
        "P3_SLA",
        "max_resource_utilization_mean",
        "utilization_fairness_jain_mean",
        "handover_setup_minutes_per_case_mean",
        "completion_rate_mean",
    ]

    print(
        "\nPolicy/scenario comparison; delays are minutes. "
        "Empty-start makes these optimistic on an overloaded "
        "repeated-day system."
    )
    print(
        comparison[compact_columns]
        .round(3)

    )

    uncertainty_columns = [
        "scenario",
        "policy",
        "mean_delay_q10",

        "p90_delay_q10",
        "p90_delay_q90",

        "P1_CI_high",

        "P2_CI_high",
    ]

    print(

        "percentiles; SLA uses pooled Wilson 95% intervals."
    )
    print(
        comparison[uncertainty_columns]
        .round(3)
        .to_string(index=False)
    )

    # Emit an interval event log and assignment-decision table.
    assert representative is not None
    interval_log, assignment_log = add_timestamps(
        *representative,
        policy="hybrid",
        scenario="U5",

    required_columns = [
        "case:concept:name",
        "concept:name",
        "start_timestamp",

        "org:resource",

    print("\nRepresentative interval log:")

    interval_log.to_csv(

    # Queue-specific waiting is queue-entry -> assignment.
    # Reassignments after preemption/shift release contribute

    # PM4Py post-analysis:
    # - inserted service/waiting values are case-level interval measures;

        activity_service = pm4py.get_service_time(

        print("\nPM4Py activity service-time summary:")

        handover_network = (

            )
        )
        print(
            "\nPM4Py handover-of-work network "

        print(handover_network)

    except ImportError:
        print(

        )

# 9. Limits and validation plan

The following cannot be concluded from the supplied expectations and ranges alone:

* the true arrival process, burstiness within blocks, or day-to-day autocorrelation;
* the joint distribution of priority, type, complexity, and arrival time;
* whether service distributions are uniform, multimodal, or resource-dependent beyond the stated multipliers;
* the backlog present at 08:00;
* whether handover and setup delays occupy one worker, two workers, or neither;
* how often workers can change tasks at breaks or shift boundaries;
* whether workers slow down under repeated switching, overtime, or interruption;
* whether ME/ES branches are technically independent enough for all nominal parallel executions;
* the empirical relation between the Intake outage and work performed by affected staff;
* a stationary p90 or p95, because expected base workload exceeds expected capacity;
* exact SLA attainment from only about five expected P1 cases per base day.

The code is therefore a transparent experimental scaffold, not a calibrated digital twin. Its empty-start clearance horizon should be replaced by multi-day historical replay with observed carryover.

## Validation sequence

**1. Instrumentation and shadow mode.** For several operating cycles, run the three dispatch rules in shadow mode while actual assignments remain unchanged. Record their eligible sets, predicted completion, branch decision, reserve state, and reason code. This identifies disagreements without risking P1 cases.

**2. Calibrate distributions.** Estimate arrival dispersion, priority/type dependence, active-service distributions, residual service after pauses, handover occupancy, setup frequency, and the realized 10% restart assumption. Compare model-generated and observed queue waits, variants, resource workloads, and shift-end backlog.

**3. Historical replay.** Replay complete multi-day arrival streams, including the observed opening backlog and subsequent-day demand. A policy should not receive an artificial no-arrival clearance day. Test parameter uncertainty, not only fitted central values.

**4. Matched operational pilot.** Because cases share queues and resources, randomizing individual cases would create interference. Use matched team-days or time blocks, alternating the current policy and the hybrid under comparable demand. Run full pooling initially in shadow mode or in lower-risk blocks rather than exposing all P1 cases immediately.

**5. P1 protection guardrails.** During the pilot, automatically enter hybrid stress mode whenever a P1 has less than 25 minutes of predicted slack, waits more than ten minutes for Intake/diagnostics, or lacks an available protected skill family. No experimental assignment should override that guard.

**6. Pre-specified comparison.** Compare:

* P1 SLA with a confidence interval and a non-inferiority guard;
* P2 and P3 SLA;
* stage queue p90/p95;
* carryover work at the next 08:00;
* overhead and preemption benefit;
* Validation bottleneck migration;
* utilization, overtime, and fairness burden;
* prediction calibration.

**7. Stop conditions.** Revert to the protected fallback if P1 misses cluster, a resource exceeds the overtime rule, aged P3 WIP grows monotonically, or the policy repeatedly predicts a gain from preemption that is not realized.

The principal validation question is not merely whether the hybrid lowers mean delay. It is whether it preserves more P1 cases and limits worst-scenario deterioration without transferring unacceptable delay to P3, creating a Validation bottleneck, or chronically overloading the cross-skilled workers.
