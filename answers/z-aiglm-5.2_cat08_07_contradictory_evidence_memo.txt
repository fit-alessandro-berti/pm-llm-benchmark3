1. Executive memo

To: Telecom Management Board
From: Process Analytics Team
Subject: Decision on Fiber-Installation Redesign R

Redesign R has yielded a highly polarized outcome. While it successfully accelerated simple fiber installations, it introduced significant operational and compliance risks for complex cases. We recommend a hybrid approach (Alternative E): scaling the redesign exclusively for simple, low-risk houses while reverting to baseline controls for apartments and older buildings, paired with targeted measurement before any broader rollout.

The evidence shows that Redesign R improved the median cycle time from 14.2 to 10.8 days and reduced handovers for 34% of cases classified as simple, low-risk houses. In this segment, first-time-right installations improved by 3 percentage points, and the p90 cycle time dropped by 8 days. However, the redesign’s core changes—skipping field surveys based on a predictive rule and moving activation checks after billing—created severe downstream issues. For apartments and old buildings, the tail duration exploded (p95 worsened from 45 to 62 days overall). The move-on-log diagnostics reveal that billing often starts before successful activation, and installations are repeated without preceding field surveys, leading to a spike in second visits (up from 11% to 18%) and complaints (up from 6.1% to 9.8%). 

The process discovery confirms this chaos: the pilot model shows high fitness (0.94) because the log is dominated by the new coordinator path, but extremely low precision (0.51) due to erratic combinations of skipped surveys, manual activations, and post-billing complaints. Furthermore, centralizing ownership around coordinators has driven their utilization to 93%, reducing resilience; when a coordinator is absent, cases stall because context is not shared. 

We cannot scale this nationally without inviting a surge in SLA breaches, complaint escalations, and compliance failures related to missing permit checks. Conversely, rolling back entirely would discard a proven 6-day median improvement for simple houses, unnecessarily sacrificing visible cost savings and customer satisfaction in that segment. 

Management should immediately adopt a bifurcated process. We will scale Redesign R for the 34% of cases that are simple, low-risk houses, where the benefits are unambiguous. For all other segments, we must pause survey skipping, enforce permit checks, and require activation tests before billing. Before expanding the redesign to complex segments, we must validate the building-risk predictive rule, measure the true downstream cost of rework, and establish conformance monitoring to prevent billing-before-activation.

3. Explanation of metric conflicts

*   **High fitness but low precision:** The pilot model fits the observed log well (fitness 0.94) because most cases did follow the new, simplified coordinator path. However, precision is low (0.51) because the redesign allowed too many uncontrolled variations—such as skipping field surveys, manual activations, and billing before activation. The model essentially allows the process to "do anything," making it fit the messy reality but rendering it useless for strict standardization.
*   **Improved median but worse tail duration:** The redesign successfully accelerated the bulk of routine, simple cases, pulling the median down. However, for complex cases (apartments, old buildings), skipping surveys and moving activation checks created rework. This rework (second visits, complaint escalations) created severe delays, dramatically worsening the p90 and p95 cycle times.
*   **Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Centralizing case ownership under a single installation coordinator eliminated sequential handovers, streamlining normal cases. However, this created a single point of failure. With utilization at 93%, when a coordinator was absent, cases stalled because the context was not shared across a team, reducing the network's overall resilience.
*   **Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:** Redesign R reduced upfront operational steps (fewer surveys, merged scheduling), lowering visible mean cost to EUR 161. However, this shifted the cost downstream. The increase in second visits, manual activations, and complaint handling drove the estimated total cost to EUR 207, exceeding the baseline.
*   **Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings:** The merged scheduling and predictive survey-skipping are safe for simple houses with predictable layouts. For apartments and older buildings, where wiring is complex and permits are required, skipping these steps led to blind spots during installation, causing failed visits, missing permit checks, and customer complaints.

4. Decision alternatives

*   **A. Scale Redesign R nationally.**
    *   *Advantages:* Fast to execute; immediately captures median cycle time and visible cost improvements across the board.
    *   *Risks:* Would explode tail duration, complaint volumes, and SLA breaches nationally. Severe compliance risks due to missing permit checks.
    *   *Conditions:* Only reasonable if the company is willing to accept massive downstream rework and compliance penalties to prioritize speed. (Not recommended).
*   **B. Roll back to the baseline process.**
    *   *Advantages:* Eliminates the risks of billing-before-activation, missing permit checks, and high coordinator burnout. Restores baseline resilience.
    *   *Risks:* Discards the 6-day median improvement and cost savings for the 34% of cases that are simple houses. 
    *   *Conditions:* Reasonable only if the organization lacks the capability to run a bifurcated process or if compliance risks pose an existential legal threat.
*   **C. Scale only the coordinator-ownership part, but suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation.**
    *   *Advantages:* Keeps the accountability and handover reduction while removing the most damaging process changes (skipping surveys, billing before activation).
    *   *Risks:* May not achieve the same median speed improvements if merged scheduling is suspended. Does not solve the coordinator overload (93% utilization) issue.
    *   *Conditions:* Reasonable if coordinator ownership is proven to be the sole driver of speed, independent of the skipped steps.
*   **D. Run a controlled regional experiment with better segment/risk measurement.**
    *   *Advantages:* Provides rigorous, randomized data on the redesign's effects without confounding variables.
    *   *Risks:* Delays the immediate benefits of the redesign for simple houses. Slow to implement.
    *   *Conditions:* Reasonable if current segment data is deemed too unreliable to act upon safely.
*   **E. Hybrid: deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, and run measurement before wider scale.**
    *   *Advantages:* Captures immediate benefits where they are proven, protects complex cases from rework, and maintains a baseline for comparison. Operationally pragmatic.
    *   *Risks:* Running two process variations simultaneously increases short-term operational complexity and training costs.
    *   *Conditions:* Highly reasonable given the strong segment-specific evidence already available from the pilot. (Recommended).

4.  **M5. Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence:** (Medium Value) Resolves the resilience bottleneck. If shared-team ownership can maintain speed without the 93% utilization stall, it provides a scalable path for the coordinator model.

Start timestamps would add precision to duration measurement but are unlikely to change the core strategic decision.

6. Final recommendation

*   **Recommended Decision:** Alternative E (Hybrid approach).
*   **Immediate action:** Deploy Redesign R exclusively for cases classified as "Simple house, low building risk" (approx. 34% of volume). For all other segments, immediately revert to the baseline process: require field surveys for medium-risk apartments, enforce permit checks for old buildings, and mandate activation tests before billing start. 
*   **What to pause:** Suspend the predictive rule for skipping field surveys on medium-risk apartments. Suspend the practice of moving activation checks after billing start for all segments.
*   **What to scale:** Scale the installation coordinator ownership model, but implement a shared-team backup protocol to prevent cases from stalling when a specific coordinator is absent, mitigating the 93% utilization bottleneck.
*   **What to measure next:** Prioritize M6 (validate the building-risk rule), M4 (measure 60-day downstream cost), and M7 (conformance monitoring for billing/permit checks). 
*   **Threshold/evidence for broader rollout:** Expand the redesign to apartment segments only after M6 shows the building-risk predictive rule achieves >95% accuracy, M7 confirms zero billing-before-activation events, and the pilot data demonstrates that the complaint rate for apartments falls back below 7% and p90 cycle time drops below 35 days.
