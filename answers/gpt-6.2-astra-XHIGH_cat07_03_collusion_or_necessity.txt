# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The quarterly log identifies potential separation-of-duties exceptions in purchasing and payments. The highest-priority finding concerns vendor bank maintenance and payment approval. Nine cases with a recorded same-person exception involve vendor family V-77x. Separately, the VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim subgroup includes 16 unusually rapid bank-change-to-approval cases, all recorded by manual upload. The extent of overlap requires case-level reconciliation. The recent banking-platform migration provides a plausible operational explanation for concentrated activity, although the log lacks links to the maintenance tickets.

Routine buyer and approver pairings are consistent with team assignments and workload. Emergency medical procurement and weekend repair coverage could also explain short approval intervals and repeated returns for clarification. Expedited processing still requires evidence of valid authority and timely independent retrospective justification. Shared approval credentials, spreadsheet migration and a legacy bot mapping error limit confidence in the apparent identities and elapsed times. The available records do not establish collusion or intent.

Internal Audit should first trace the flagged bank changes to authorized migration instructions, authenticated changes, payment approvals and the beneficiaries actually paid. For urgent purchases, reviewers should compare incident and delegation records with original signed justifications and the applicable working-day deadline. Confirmed failures of independent review should be reported as control exceptions. Cases with unresolved attribution should remain open pending source evidence, with any decision to investigate misconduct based on corroborated case findings.
