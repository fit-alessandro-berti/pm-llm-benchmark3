## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound (S2):** Cap-driven smaller batches raise energy per item (CNC_A goes from 1.3 kWh/item at batch 12–20 to 2.8 kWh/item at batch 1–3). Mitigation: cap must be segment-specific; never apply MTO caps to Standard-stock.
2. **Ground-shift rebound (S1, S4):** If priority pick is not actually faster, late-delivery will rise and customers may move to competitors with air, *increasing* future transport emissions (rebound through customer churn). Mitigation: monitor late-delivery weekly; only shift when reliability model supports it.
3. **Packaging reduction rebound (S6):** Removing reinforcement on heavy parts to save material will *raise* damage returns; reverse logistics emissions are not in our boundary, so the company "wins" on the dashboard but loses in the field. Mitigation: enforce Q1 for >8 kg MTO parts; do not let S6 override Q1.
4. **CNC load rebound (S3):** Routing more MTO to CNC_A may create a new queue at CNC_A, replicating the CNC_B problem. Mitigation: S3 must be conditional on CNC_A queue depth and due-date slack.
5. **Consolidation rebound (S5):** 12–24 h wait on consolidated_truck makes the 4% Critical-downtime late baseline vulnerable if the consolidation window overlaps with peak demand. Mitigation: do not route Critical-downtime or sub-24 h slack to consolidated truck.
6. **Obsolescence rebound (S2, S3):** The prompt reports 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days. Aggressive batching to maximize energy-per-item savings will inflate this hidden waste. Mitigation: track obsolescence rate as a primary KPI, not a side metric.
7. **Rework-suppression rebound (S7):** A stricter inspection gate may itself become a queue, increasing cycle time and pushing more cases to air freight to compensate. Mitigation: time-box the gate and parallelize where possible; monitor cycle time on the inspection cell.
8. **Carrier consolidation rebound (S5, S8):** Reducing the number of distinct carriers to capture consolidation gains can violate R1 (alternative route). Mitigation: enforce R1 by region before consolidation is approved.
9. **Customer-behavior rebound (S10):** Longer promised lead times may cause Regular MTO customers to order earlier or split orders, raising batch sizes elsewhere. Mitigation: only offer the longer promise when it is genuinely the lowest-impact route, and track order-pattern changes.
10. **Dedicated-van displacement (S8):** Replacing dedicated vans with shared courier lanes may *raise* per-stop handling and packaging requirements, partially offsetting the empty-return savings. Mitigation: confirm net transport kgCO2e/order reduction in a controlled pilot.
