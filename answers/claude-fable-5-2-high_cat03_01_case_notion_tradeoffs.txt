## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is a projection rule, not a property of the process. Flattening on type T makes each T-object a case whose trace is the time-ordered set of events *directly* related to it in the E2O table. I judge each flattening by the question it can answer, not by trace length.

**Convergence.** One event is related to several objects of the case type and is therefore copied into several cases (e16 into O1, O2 and O3). Counts, workload and durations attached to that event are multiplied.

**Divergence.** One case contains several events of the same activity that belong to different objects of another type (O1 has Pack Package for P1 and for P2). Events that are causally independent become sequential, producing self-loops and false directly-follows edges.

**Deficiency.** Events with no object of the chosen type vanish (e18 has no Item). I treat this as a third effect, because lifecycle truncation matters as much here as the other two.

**Copied shared events are both evidence and duplication.** A copy is evidence that the object really took part: O2's goods did leave in e16. It is duplication for anything measured in events: there were 3 dispatches, not 6. Copies are therefore admissible for per-object lifecycle questions and inadmissible for volume, workload or cost.

**Transformations.** The baseline is the direct E2O table. Two observations shape what I permit:

- The supplied E2O is already "downstream-propagated" for Order. Order appears in every Pack, Dispatch, Invoice and Payment event, although the O2O table has no direct Package→Order or Payment→Order relation. Order's 31/31 event coverage is thus partly a logging design choice.
- The objects form a single connected component (O1–D2–P3–O3–P4–O4, plus O2–V2–O3). A connected-component case notion would give one case of 31 events, so no partition into independent cases exists.

I permit O2O propagation only as a separately named log variant, one qualifier at a time, compared against the baseline:

- Acceptable upstream propagations: Delivery onto the Pack events of the packages it `carries`; Payment onto the Issue Invoice event of the invoice it settles.
- Not acceptable as a default: propagating Invoice over `bills` onto all order events. V2 would become a 16-event interleaving of O2 and O3, with e29 and e30 tied at 11:05.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Cross-order package P3, Order flattening (frequency inflation).** e13 is copied into O2 and O3 and e14 into O3 and O4, so Pack Package appears 6 times against 4 real events. O2 and O4 show a plain single-pack trace, and nothing reveals that their package is shared with O3. Harmful for workload; informative for "was my order packed?".

2. **Cross-order package P4, Order flattening (false edge and timing bias).** In O3 the sequence is e13 (P3), e14 (P4), e16 (D2), e17 (D3). The edge Pack→Dispatch is realised as e14→e16 (45 min), although P4 is not on D2. In O1 the same edge is e12→e15 (50 min), pairing P2's packing with P1's dispatch. The order-level mean is 50.75 min (50, 53, 45, 55); the package-level truth is 55.75 min (60, 55, 53, 55). Harmful: the edge exists, but its timing mixes objects.

3. **Cross-order packages, Package flattening (hidden mixing).** P3 and P4 produce the same trace as P1. The view cannot say that P4's late confirmation (e27, 02-04 10:00) holds up both O3 and O4, nor that O3 depends on two packages. Context-dependent: fine for packing-station performance, misleading for customer impact.

4. **Split order O1, Order flattening (false rework).** Dispatch→Dispatch (e15→e16, 5 min) and Confirm Delivery→Confirm Delivery (e23→e24, 2h) read as re-dispatch or repeated confirmation. They are two different deliveries, D1 and D2. O3 shows the same Pack→Pack→Dispatch→Dispatch prefix for a different reason (two mixed packages), so the variant prefix conflates split shipment with consolidation. Harmful if read as a loop; informative as a multiplicity signal.

5. **Consolidated delivery D2 (weighting bias).** e16 and e24 appear three times in the Order log and three times in the Item log (I12, I21, I31). Mean Dispatch→Confirm Delivery is 10h13 per delivery, 8h54 per package and 10h32 per item. Each figure is correct for its unit, so the trap is quoting one without the unit. In the Order DFG the edge does not exist at all, because Issue Invoice and Record Payment always fall between the two events (e16→e18 in O1). The most physical dependency in the process is invisible in the most complete log. Context-dependent for the averages; harmful for the missing edge.

6. **Consolidated delivery, Delivery flattening (near-singleton).** D2 looks identical to D1: two events, one edge. The 4h55 transit cannot be related to load (two packages, three orders), and no pre-dispatch waiting is measurable because the lifecycle starts at Dispatch. Harmless for transit KPIs; useless for anything else.

7. **Consolidated invoice V2 (count inflation and lost link).** In the Order log e19 is copied into O2 and O3, giving 4 Issue Invoice occurrences for 3 invoices. Summing invoice amounts per order case would double-count V2. In the Invoice log the reverse happens: V2's link to two orders disappears, so "invoiced before delivery completed?" cannot be asked (e19 at 12:05 precedes e24 for O2, and both e24 and e27 for O3). Harmful for financial totals.

8. **Partial payments, Order flattening (spurious cycle).** e22 and e25 are each copied into O2 and O3, so Record Payment appears 6 times for 4 payments, and two instalments look like four. O3's trace contains Record Payment (e22) → Confirm Delivery (e24) → Record Payment (e25) → Confirm Delivery (e27), which a DFG renders as a two-activity loop. It is an interleaving of V2's instalments with deliveries D2 and D3, which share no object except the order. Harmful.

9. **Partial payments, Invoice flattening (first payment versus settlement).** Issue Invoice→Record Payment takes the first payment: V1 30 min, V2 35 min, V3 21h20, mean 7h28. Time to full settlement is 30 min, 20h55 and 21h20, mean 14h15. The Order log gives 5h45, because V2's fast first instalment counts twice. The self-loop e22→e25 (20h20) is informative, but flattening drops the O2O qualifiers, so "partial" is only inferable from repetition.

10. **Near-singleton Payment.** Four cases of one event each (e21, e22, e25, e26), with no edges and zero cycle time. M2 and M3 are detached from the invoice they jointly settle. Harmful if presented as a process model; harmless as a count.

11. **Item flattening (deceptively clean).** All six items follow Add Item→Pack→Dispatch→Confirm Delivery. The 100% single variant is not evidence of a well-behaved process. Deficiency removes 15 events, and the 1:1 item–package–delivery structure removes all divergence. The spread from 4h45 (I11) to 24h37 (I32) is real, but its cause (D3) is visible only through another type.

12. **Order cycle time (masking by a batched end event).** Create→Close is 26h00, 26h03, 26h01 and 26h04. O1's last substantive event is e24 (02-03 16:00), 19h before e28. O3 and O4 finish at e27, about an hour before closing. The closures at 11:00–11:10 look batched, but the log cannot confirm that. A uniform KPI here hides real variation.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs worth creating**

- **Order log:** a navigation and conformance log. It answers whether every order was created, invoiced once, paid and closed, and in what temporal order finance and logistics touched it. It is not used for activity counts or for Pack/Dispatch/Confirm timings.
- **Package log:** Pack→Dispatch waiting and package-level delivery time.
- **Delivery log:** transit time per shipment, kept because it is the only duplication-free logistics unit.
- **Invoice log:** time to first payment, time to settlement, number of instalments.
- **Item log:** only as a cross-check of the physical chain or for line-level SLAs.
- **Payment:** no log, only a table joined to Invoice.

**Questions requiring object-centric artefacts**

- **OC-DFG:** where lifecycles synchronise (Pack joins Order, Item and Package; Record Payment joins Order, Invoice and Payment), and whether an edge is carried by one type or several.
- **OTG:** type-level cardinalities, namely Order–Package many-to-many (O1, O3 against P3, P4), Delivery–Package 1:n (D2), Invoice–Order 1:n (V2) and Payment–Invoice n:1 (M2, M3).
- **ET–OT graph:** which activities touch which types. It predicts truncation before any flattening: Payment touches one activity, Delivery two, Order all eight.
- **Object interaction summary:** the concrete sharing objects (P3, P4, D2, V2) and their blast radius, for example that D3's delay reaches O3 and O4.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering:** whether order-centred neighbourhoods recur (own-package split as in O1, shared-package as in O2 and O4, double-shared as in O3).

**Reconciling contradictions**

1. Counts are settled at event level: 4 packs, 3 dispatches, 3 invoices, 4 payments.
2. A duration is owned by the type that holds both endpoints: Pack→Dispatch by Package, Dispatch→Confirm by Delivery, Issue→settlement by Invoice, Create→Close by Order.
3. A flattened edge is a behavioural claim only if the two events share an object besides the case object. Otherwise it is labelled temporal coincidence (Record Payment→Confirm Delivery).
4. If both views remain valid, both are reported with units: "two orders late, one delivery late".

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: Order as the primary lead object, with Package/Delivery and Invoice as explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria**

- Coverage of activities and events.
- Presence of business start and end events.
- Alignment with the question stakeholders ask.
- Amount of convergence and divergence.
- Whether a duration's endpoints are owned by the type.

**Strongest supporting evidence**

- Order is the only type with Create and Close, the only one related to all 31 events, and the only one that connects logistics (e11–e17, e23, e24, e27) with finance (e18–e22, e25, e26).
- Every other type loses at least 15 events.
- The secondary lifecycles are clean exactly where Order is dirty: Delivery and Invoice have zero duplication, and Package has two extra copies.

**Strongest counterargument**

- Order's completeness is partly an artefact of E2O design.
- 9 of its 31 events are shared, and its log has 35% more case-events than events (42 against 31).
- All four orders sit in one connected component, so order cases are not independent executions.
- O2 retains only e02, e07 and e29 if shared events are removed.

This is a serious case for position C. I stay with B because stakeholders still need a per-order narrative, and B confines Order to that role.

**Residual risk**

- Order-log frequencies or the Record Payment⇄Confirm Delivery cycle may be read as process facts.
- Four mutually distinct variants may be over-interpreted from four orders.

**Conditions for changing the decision**

- To C: sharing dominates at scale (most orders coupled through packages or invoices), or the question is about resources, capacity or network effects.
- To A (Order): consolidation is rare, so that almost all orders resemble an unshared O1.
- To a different primary: the analysis is scoped to a single domain (Invoice for cash collection, Package for warehouse).

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

OBJECT_TYPES = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
LEAD_TYPE = "Order"                                   # position B: primary lead
SECONDARY_TYPES = ["Package", "Delivery", "Invoice"]  # explicitly secondary lifecycles

# ------------------------------------- 1. convergence / deficiency ledger (E2O)

objects_per_event_and_type = (

       .rename("n_objects").reset_index()
)
duplication_ledger = objects_per_event_and_type.groupby("ocel:type").agg(
    unique_events=("ocel:eid", "nunique"),
    case_events=("n_objects", "sum"),
    shared_events=("n_objects", lambda s: int((s > 1).sum())),
)
duplication_ledger["extra_copies"] = (
    duplication_ledger["case_events"] - duplication_ledger["unique_events"]

duplication_ledger["missing_events"] = len(ocel.events) - duplication_ledger["unique_events"]

flat_log_skeletons, flat_variants, flat_case_durations = {}, {}, {}
flat_event_couple_dfgs = {}

def event_couple_dfg(flat_log):
    """Directly-follows counts where each (source event, target event) pair
    counts once, however many cases it was copied into."""
    df = flat_log.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "ocel:eid"]).copy()
    grouped = df.groupby("case:concept:name")
    df["next_eid"] = grouped["ocel:eid"].shift(-1)
    df["next_activity"] = grouped["concept:name"].shift(-1)
    couples = df.dropna(subset=["next_eid"]).drop_duplicates(["ocel:eid", "next_eid"])
    return couples.groupby(["concept:name", "next_activity"]).size().to_dict()

for object_type in OBJECT_TYPES:
    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    flat_logs[object_type] = flat_log

    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    flat_dfgs[object_type] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_activities, "end": end_activities}

    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_log)
    flat_perf_dfgs[object_type] = performance_dfg

    flat_log_skeletons[object_type] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_log, noise_threshold=0.0)
    flat_variants[object_type] = pm4py.get_variants(flat_log)
    flat_case_durations[object_type] = pm4py.get_all_case_durations(flat_log)
    flat_event_couple_dfgs[object_type] = event_couple_dfg(flat_log)

# edges whose raw count exceeds their event-couple count are carried by copied events
inflated_edges = {

        for edge in flat_dfgs[object_type]["dfg"]

    }
    for object_type in OBJECT_TYPES
}

# self-loops are divergence candidates, to be explained with object ids, not by looking
self_loop_candidates = {

    for object_type in OBJECT_TYPES
}

# ------------------------------------------------- 3. object-centric artefacts

interaction_pairs = interactions_summary.drop_duplicates(["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"])
interaction_fan_out = (

sharing_objects = interaction_fan_out[

# ------------------------------- 4. explicit, named propagation variant of E2O
def propagate_e2o(source_ocel, o2o_qualifiers):
    """Add each O2O source object to the events of its target object,
    only for the listed qualifiers. Returns a new OCEL."""
    o2o = source_ocel.o2o[source_ocel.o2o["ocel:qualifier"].isin(o2o_qualifiers)]
    o2o = o2o.rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "src", "ocel:oid_2": "tgt", "ocel:qualifier": "o2o_q"})
    type_of = source_ocel.objects.set_index("ocel:oid")["ocel:type"]
    added = source_ocel.relations.merge(o2o, left_on="ocel:oid", right_on="tgt")
    added["ocel:oid"] = added["src"]
    added["ocel:type"] = added["src"].map(type_of)
    added["ocel:qualifier"] = "propagated:" + added["o2o_q"]
    added = added[source_ocel.relations.columns]
    propagated = copy.deepcopy(source_ocel)
    propagated.relations = (
        pd.concat([source_ocel.relations, added])
          .drop_duplicates(["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"])
          .sort_values(["ocel:timestamp", "ocel:eid"])
    )
    return propagated

secondary_cluster_sizes = {

reconciliation_activity_counts = pd.DataFrame({

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Removing versus down-weighting copied events.** Dropping the nine shared events from the Order log leaves O2 with e02, e07, e29 and O3 with e03, e08, e09, e30, while O1 loses only e16 and e24. Removal therefore destroys the consolidated orders and biases the model toward O1. I prefer weighting each copy by 1/k, or counting unique event couples (`flat_event_couple_dfgs`). Weighted totals must return to 31 events, with 4 Pack, 3 Dispatch and 4 Record Payment. Any edge whose rank changes under weighting is flagged as convergence-driven.

2. **Direct versus propagated E2O.**
   - Under `carries`, D2 should gain e12 and e13, and Delivery should gain a Pack→Pack self-loop for D2 only. That is acceptable, and it exposes consolidation.
   - Under the settlement qualifiers, M2 and M3 both gain e19. This introduces convergence into a previously clean type.
   - Under `bills`, V2 becomes a 16-event mixture of O2 and O3.
   
   A finding counts as robust only if it survives in the direct variant. Propagated variants generate hypotheses and are never the reporting basis.

3. **Changing the lead object.** Re-run with Package, then Invoice, as lead and compare conclusions.
   - "The D3 leg is slow" recurs under Order (O3, O4), Item (I32, I41), Package (P4) and Delivery (D3), so it is robust.
   - "Payment precedes delivery confirmation" (e21 before e23) appears only under Order. It is a true temporal fact with no shared-object support, so it is reported as ordering, not as dependency.
   - "Orders take 26h" exists only under Order and is dominated by Close timing.

4. **Event, unique-object and total-object metrics in the OC-DFG.**
   - Dispatch for Order: 3 events, 4 unique orders, 6 total objects.
   - Dispatch for Delivery: 3, 3, 3.
   - Record Payment for Invoice: 4 events, 3 unique invoices, 4 total objects.
   - Order edge Record Payment→Confirm Delivery: 4 event couples, 4 unique orders, 5 total objects (O3 twice).
   
   Events fewer than unique objects indicates convergence (D2). Total above unique indicates divergence or repetition (O1, O3, V2). The three metrics are reported together.

5. **Timestamp ties and granularity.** e29 and e30 share 11:05. This is harmless in the direct Order log but order-dependent in any merged or propagated view. Re-run with both tie orders, and with timestamps coarsened to the hour, to see which edges are artefacts of minute-level ordering (e15→e16 is five minutes apart).

6. **Small-sample discipline.** With four orders and four variants, percentages are not generalisable. Validation consists of tracing every claimed edge back to event identifiers and confirming the consolidation rules (P3, P4, D2, V2) with process owners.
