1. Protect urgent/highpriority cases from SLA breaches.
2. Ensure compliance with hard safety and skill rules.
3. Minimise avoidable rework and appeals (handover cost, skill mismatch).
4. Maintain workload fairness and protect staff from overtime burnout.
5. Preserve continuity of ownership where beneficial.
6. Promote skill development through documented training priorities.
7. Build resilience by avoiding specialist bottlenecks and crosstraining.

**Hard constraints (safety/compliance)**
- Any assignment must have the required skill 1 (0 = not allowed).
- Security Review: only skill level 3, or level 1–2 under explicit supervision of R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.

# 2. Assignment policy

The policy is applied to the pending queue, sorted by decreasing **priority criticality** (urgent > high > normal > low) and then by SLA time remaining. For each case, the following steps are executed:

**Step 1 – Eligibility filtering**
- Remove any resource with required skill = 0.
- For Security Review: retain only resources with skill = 3, or skill = 12 where R7_Gita or R8_Hugo is available as a supervisor (supervised pair).
- For Final Approval: retain only skill = 23.
- For highpriority cases (urgent, high): if skill = 1, the resource is only eligible as part of a supervised pair with a supervisor of skill 2 in that activity.
- If the current owner meets the eligibility and constraint5 conditions, they are kept as a candidate (continuity preferred).
- Remove resources that have already received 4 new assignments in this batch.

**Step 2 – Softfilter on overtime risk**
- If the case is not urgent and there exists at least one eligible resource with overtime_risk  “high”, exclude resources with overtime_risk = “high”.
- If the case is urgent and all eligible resources have high overtime risk, keep them but flag the necessity.

**Step 3 – Score each eligible candidate (single resource or supervised pair)**
Each candidate receives a weighted sum of the following scores (weights calibrated from historical impact):

| Score component | Description | Formula / logic |
|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **SLA urgency** | Higher for tighter deadlines | `score_urg = max(0, 1 – remaining_time / threshold)`, where threshold is 24h for normal/low, 8h for high, 3h for urgent. |
| **Skill match** | Higher skill reduces defect risk | `skill_eff = max(trainee_skill, 2 if supervised)`. `score_skill = skill_eff / 3`. |
| **Continuity / handover cost** | Rewards keeping owner, penalises high handover cost changes | `score_cont = +0.5` if candidate is current owner, `0.4` if handover_cost=high and candidateowner, `0.3` if reassign_count>2 and candidateowner. |
| **Workload & overtime fairness** | Penalises high open cases and high overtime risk | `score_wl = – (open_cases_before + assigned_this_batch) / max_workload` (normalised); add `0.3` if overtime_risk = “high”. |
| **Training / development** | Encourages documented skill growth on normal/low cases | `score_train = +0.3` if priority in {normal, low} and candidate.training_priority = required_skill and SLA risk acceptable (remaining_time > 2× p95 service time). If supervised pair creates a learning opportunity, `+0.15`. |
| **Bottleneck avoidance** | Reserve exclusive specialists (R7/R8 for Security, R6/Eva/Farid for Approval) | `score_res = 0.2` if resource is the only available skill3 for that activity and there is at least one other eligible candidate with skill 2 (supervised). |

Total score:  
`total = w_urg * score_urg + w_skill * score_skill + w_cont * score_cont + w_wl * score_wl + w_train * score_train + w_res * score_res`  
Default weights: `w_urg=0.30, w_skill=0.25, w_cont=0.15, w_wl=0.15, w_train=0.10, w_res=0.05`.

**Step 4 – Ranking and tiebreaking**
Candidates are ranked by total score descending. Ties are broken by:
1. lower overtime risk (low > medium > high)
2. lower current open cases + batch assignments
3. higher effective skill
4. continuity bonus (if any)

**Step 5 – Assignment and counter update**
- Select the topranked candidate.
- If the candidate is a supervised pair, both members receive a “soft” load count (0.5 for supervisor, 0.5 for trainee) but only the responsible resource’s newassignment limit is incremented.
- Update `assigned_this_batch` for the responsible resource(s).
- If no candidate survives filtering, raise an exception for manual allocation.

**Step 6 – Postbatch evaluation**
After queue processing, verify that constraint3 is met (20% training assignments). If not, select the safest normal/low cases and reassign to trainingpriority resources under supervision until the threshold is reached.

# 5. Explanation to workers

We want everyone to understand the logic behind today’s assignments. No decision is meant to judge anyone’s ability; it’s about balancing the pressure we all feel.

- **Urgent and critical customer cases** (like the enterprise security review or VIP negotiation) go to colleagues who are fully qualified and have capacity, so we meet promises without cutting corners. That sometimes means moving a case from a lessspecialist owner, not because they couldn’t do it, but because the risk of delay or rework is too high for the customer.
- **Workload fairness and burnout prevention** – you can see that colleagues with the highest overtime risk and already many open cases have almost no new work today. This protects them and the service; overloading anyone leads to errors and exhaustion. We spread the remaining work across those who still have healthy capacity.
- **Keeping ownership where it matters** – when a case is complex and you already know the history, we keep it with you, as long as that doesn’t put the deadline in danger. That’s why Eva and Celine and Farid kept their complex cases.
- **Learning and growth** – some routine, lowerpriority work is matched to your personal development goals. Amal gets diagnosis cases because that’s her stated training priority, and Jonas is observing an approval to broaden his skills. These assignments are safe because they have enough time and low complexity. Learning happens without risk to the customer.
- **If your name isn’t here much today**, it’s because your current caseload is already high or you are our specialist in an area where we need to keep you free for future urgent work. That’s a sign of trust, not a punishment.

If you see a case you feel you could handle better, talk to your team lead – this is a dynamic system and we always listen to human insight.

# 6. Processmining audit indicators

Auditing the deployed policy will rely on the following indicators, computed from event logs:

| Indicator | How to measure | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Handover frequency** | Count of `org:resource` changes for the same `case:concept:name` divided by total case transitions. | Detects excessive reassignment; should stay stable or drop. |
| **Reassignment count** | Distribution of `reassignment_count` field; proportion of cases exceeding 2. | Values >2 correlate with 1.8× rework – must be monitored. |
| **Skill mismatch** | Difference between assigned resource’s skill level in the required skill (from resource matrix) and the level needed (e.g., <2 for high priority). Flag assignments where skill <2 on highpriority cases without supervision. | Direct measure of policy compliance and error risk. |
| **Workload distribution** | Gini coefficient or standard deviation of open cases across resources, recorded in `workload_snapshot`. | Ensures fairness constraints are respected; no resource frequently overloaded. |
| **Overtime risk exposure** | Proportion of assignments given to resources with overtime_risk = “high”, with subfilter for nonurgent cases. | Checks compliance with safe workload constraint. |
| **SLA misses** | Percentage of cases where `time:timestamp` of last activity exceeds `sla_deadline`. | Overall timing health; should differ by policy. |
| **Rework / appeal after assignment** | Frequency of “Rework Correction” or “Customer Negotiation” activities occurring after an initial completion, per assignment. | Proxies for quality of initial assignment. |
| **Training assignment share** | Proportion of normal/lowpriority assignments where `assignment_reason` includes “training” or the assigned resource’s `training_priority` matches the required skill. | Measures adherence to the 20% development rule. |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | Average waiting time before Security Review and Final Approval; length of unassigned queue for these activities over time. | Detects if specialists are overcentralised. |
| **Deviations from assignment policy** | Compare actual `org:resource` against the recommendation that the policy would produce under the same log state. Record cases where a different resource was chosen and check `assignment_reason`. | Identifies manual overrides and potential noncompliance. |
