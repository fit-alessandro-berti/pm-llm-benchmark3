# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality.** A1 only produces a faster, more complete case header. Classification (A2) then decides the control path: KYC intensity, whether collateral is required, and whether committee/four-eyes apply. Misclassification therefore poisons A3/A4, A7, A8, and A9. Document completeness (A5) is a prerequisite for A6; A6 is a prerequisite for reliable credit analysis and for any increase in clean inflow to the 9 analysts. Committee packaging (A8) is a join: it waits for KYC status, normalized statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit notes. Automating A8 before A4/A5/A6/A7 is GIGO. Contract generation (A9) needs the same reconciled objects plus the committee decision. Activation (A11) is last and is not safe if any of those objects are provisional.

**Legal and compliance controls.** Customer signing cannot legally occur before KYC clearance, yet 6.5% of cases show provisional signing and 1.1% later need remediation. A10 therefore depends on A3/A4 actually improving clearance integrity, not just speed. Contract terms must match the committee decision (8.5% remediation overall, 14% on credit-line renewal); A9 is the preventive control and A12 the detective/corrective one. High-exposure SME cases require four-eyes between credit analysis and committee packaging (5.8% violations in the month-end rush). A8 and any credit-side assist (A6) must preserve distinct actors and an auditable trail of model suggestion, human override, and final decision.

**Human escalation capacity.** KYC ops already hand work to two of three compliance specialists. A3 without spare specialist capacity, or A4 that merely surfaces more ambiguous matches, will lengthen the 31h mismatch queue. Credit analysts are the center of the working-together network: A1/A5/A3 that increase clean inflow without A6 will move the queue from back office (12 FTE, utilization already falling after 15:00) onto the 9 analysts (110 min credit analysis, 28h wait). Legal ops (4 FTE) is starved when KYC is slow and flooded at month end; A9 without smoother KYC/decision arrival patterns recreates that month-end spike.

**External valuation.** Wait for external valuation is the longest internal idle (58h, 19% late) and a synchronization point into committee packaging (1,980 DFG). A7 can triage and parallelize but cannot command partner cycle time. It depends on A2 so that only true equipment-heavy and selected working-capital cases enter the valuer network. Starting valuation earlier, in parallel with FDR/KYC (already concurrent in reality), is the process lever; a bot that only “requests faster” does not remove the join.

**Core-banking integration.** IT can deeply integrate only two modules in the first six months. A1, A9, and A11 are the obvious core-banking candidates (booking, contract, activation). A2, A6, and A10 may need partial core hooks. A3/A4 (compliance), A5 (portal), A7 (valuer routing), A8 (document assembly), and A12 (case workbench) can live on satellite platforms. Year-1 design should spend the two deep slots on control-critical systems (prefer A9 over A1/A11) and keep synchronization tools outside the core.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

**P1 — Quick-win (A1, A5, A9, A10).** Optimizes visible volume, feasibility, and a political “we automated the front door” narrative, while A9 meets the compliance-must-strengthen-controls constraint. Sacrifices the true synchronization constraints (entity mismatch 31h, external valuation 58h, analyst normalization 20h/75 min) and will likely expose classification, KYC, and FDR queues. A10 also advances signing before KYC reliability is improved.

**P2 — Bottleneck / synchronization (A4, A5, A6, A7).** Optimizes critical-path waits and join points: mismatch clearance, missing-doc loops, statement readiness, and valuer idle time that all feed committee packaging. Sacrifices a crisp contract-control module (A9/A12), the high-frequency registration win, and e-sign volume. Control strengthening is real (KYC-before-sign) but less visible to legal-ops auditors than contract-rule checks.

**P3 — Compliance-first (A3, A4, A9, A12).** Optimizes auditability, KYC escalation discipline, contract–decision match, and remediation transparency. Sacrifices the largest volume waits (signing 44h, valuation 58h), analyst capacity (no A6), and the FDR rework engine (no A5). Cycle-time gains will be modest and specialist load may rise.

**P4 — Recommended hybrid (A4, A5, A6, A9).** Optimizes the rework and control joins that actually dominate end-to-end performance, protects analyst demand, and puts a preventive control (A9) in the first wave. Sacrifices the registration vanity metric (A1), external-valuation partner work (A7), e-sign adoption (A10), and committee-pack cosmetics (A8). Those become phase-two candidates once inputs are trustworthy.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**P1 Quick-win.** Cycle time falls mainly in the already-short Receive → Register → Classify head (11,880 and 11,680 DFG) and later in Contract prep → Customer signing (7,300). Waiting time at registration (1.5h) shrinks; signing wait (44h) shrinks only where e-sign is adopted. Rework: A5 should cut FDR → Request missing documents (3,300) and the 2,800 return edge; A9 should cut Contract prep → Remediate (650). Handovers from portal to back office decline, but handovers into KYC (7,250) and FDR (3,940) rise in rate. Resource demand: back-office utilization, already <65% after 15:00, falls further; credit analysts and compliance specialists receive a denser stream of still-dirty cases. Control violations: A9 helps contract–decision match, but A10 without stronger KYC can increase provisional signing beyond 6.5%.

**P2 Bottleneck / synchronization.** Cycle time falls on the variants that currently sit on the critical path: Resolve entity mismatch (1,750; 82% critical when present), the FDR completeness loop, Normalize → Credit analysis (5,000), and Wait for external valuation → Committee packaging (1,980). Waiting time: 31h mismatch, 18h FDR, 20h normalize, 28h credit, 58h valuation, and then 26h packaging should all compress because the join arrives more nearly together. Rework: 38% mismatch and 52% repeated document requests decline; 16% normalize exceptions decline if extraction is reviewed. Handovers: fewer KYC ↔ specialist ping-pongs (1,750 / 1,160 loop) and fewer RM-triggered document loops. Resource demand: the 3 specialists spend less time hunting matches; the 9 analysts lose 75 min normalize work and can absorb cleaner credit files; collateral team routes rather than chases. Control violations: KYC-before-sign should improve; four-eyes and contract-match are largely untouched.

**P3 Compliance-first.** Cycle time improves mainly after committee: Contract prep (22h) and the 650/510 remediation loop, plus some KYC (4h) and mismatch (31h). Waiting time elsewhere (valuation, signing, FDR) is almost unchanged. Rework: KYC 11% and contract 12% (14% credit-line) fall; FDR 24% does not. Handovers: more structured KYC-ops → specialist edges, fewer legal-ops fire drills. Resource demand: specialists and legal ops become the constrained pools; analysts and back office unchanged. Control violations: four-eyes still needs a packaging/credit design change; provisional signing and contract mismatch should drop if A3 is conservative and A9/A12 are live.

**P4 Hybrid (recommended).** Cycle time: material on mismatch-critical cases, document-loop cases (~3,300), normalize/credit (5,200 / 8,900), and contract prep (7,600). Waiting time: 31h + 18h + 20h + 22h are the primary internal targets; 58h valuation and 44h signing remain, but packaging (26h) improves because KYC status and normalized statements arrive sooner (committee packaging’s other two wait conditions). Rework: FDR loop 3,300/2,800, mismatch loop 1,750/1,160, and contract remediation 650/510. Handovers: fewer RM → back office → analyst completeness chains; fewer legal remediations. Resource demand: back-office chase work down; specialist time refocused on true hits; analyst demand shifts from 75 min normalize to 110 min judgment—net capacity gain, not overload. Control violations: KYC-before-sign and contract–decision match both improve; four-eyes is not solved but is not worsened if A6 remains assistive and A8 is deferred.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability.** Every recommender (A2, A3, A4, A6, A9) must store model version, input hash, suggestion, override reason, actor, and final decision. A8/A11 must not write a completed state without those artifacts.
- **False negatives (severe).** A3 KYC pre-clear and A4 “no mismatch” labels need high-recall thresholds, mandatory specialist review of all hits and a sampled share of clears, and a block on A10/A11 when KYC status is not final.
- **False positives / customer friction.** A5 checklists that over-request documents will inflate the 52% repeat rate and trigger RM resistance. Compensate with product-specific minimum sets, RM preview before customer send, and a “reason for extra request” field.
- **Model drift.** A2 and A6 will drift as products and statement formats change; A9 drifts when credit-line covenants change. Compensate with monthly champion/challenger samples, a rule-library owner in legal/compliance, and freeze-and-review after any product-policy change.
- **Exception handling and shifted demand.** Do not staff-reduce back office 1:1 after A1/A5; afternoon slack is already unused, while specialists (3 FTE) and analysts (9 FTE) are the real scarce nodes. A3/A4 require a published escalation roster so work does not collapse onto two seniors. A6 must keep analyst review of low-confidence extractions so credit analysis is not fed silent errors.
- **Four-eyes.** A6 and any future A8 must refuse the same user id on credit analysis and committee packaging for High-exposure SME, with a month-end overtime alert (today’s 5.8% violation window).
- **Provisional signing and activation.** A10/A11 are gated: no e-sign packet and no activation bot if KYC is open, a control-remediation case is open, or contract ≠ committee. That encodes the 6.5% / 1.1% / 4% findings rather than automating past them.
- **External partner quality (A7).** Dual-valuer sample on high-exposure and a dispute SLA so routing speed does not buy systematically optimistic collateral.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 (weeks 0–6) — measure and constrain.** Baseline: cycle time by product/exposure; waiting time at mismatch, FDR, normalize, valuation, packaging, signing; DFG loop counts 3300/2800, 1750/1160, 650/510; four-eyes 5.8%; provisional signing 6.5%; contract mismatch 8.5%/14%. Stand up the audit-log schema before any model goes live.

**Phase 1 (months 1–6) — two satellite modules, one deep core slot.**  
1) A5 on the customer portal / RM desktop (not deep core).  
2) A4 on the compliance case system (not deep core), with specialist escalation.  
3) A9 as the first deep core-banking integration (contract + control-rule checks).  
Gate to expand A5/A4: missing-doc repeat rate down, mismatch wait down, no increase in provisional signing. Gate for A9: contract-remediation rate down, 100% suggestion/override logging, legal sign-off on the rule library. The second deep slot is reserved, not spent on A1 or A11.

**Phase 2 (months 6–12) — fourth module and demand rebalancing.** Deploy A6 (second deep integration if the credit engine sits on core; otherwise keep the second slot for a later A2/A10 hook). Retune analyst capacity before celebrating “saved” normalize minutes—reinvest them in High-exposure judgment, not headcount cut. Optional thin add-on: duplicate detection from A1 without full auto-registration STP.

**Phase 3 (year 2, not in the four-module cap) — only after gates.** A7 once product class is trustworthy; A8 once join inputs are complete; A10 once KYC-before-sign violations fall; A2 with mandatory override on High-exposure; A12 to industrialize residual remediations; A11 last.

**Monitoring metrics.** End-to-end cycle time by Micro / SME / High-exposure and by Working-capital / Equipment / Credit-line; wait at Resolve entity mismatch, Request missing documents, Normalize, Wait for external valuation, Committee packaging, Customer signing; loop frequencies above; specialist and analyst utilization and after-15:00 back-office utilization; four-eyes breaches; provisional-signing rate; contract–decision mismatch; override rates and sampled error rates for A4/A6/A9.

**Gate criteria.** No phase advance if (a) specialist queue wait rises, (b) analyst wait rises after a completeness improvement, (c) four-eyes or provisional-signing rates worsen, or (d) audit-trail completeness < 100% on automated suggestions.

# 7. Final recommendation

Recommend **P4 (A4, A5, A6, A9)** as the first-year portfolio, rolled out as A5 + A4 + A9 in the first six months and A6 in the second half, using only one (or at most two) deep core-banking integrations.

Frequency is a poor ranking key. Register application is almost universal (11,880; position 2 in 98% of cases) and very automatable, but its 8 min service and 1.5h wait are not what stretch cycle time. Automating it first (A1) would raise arrival rate into Classify → KYC (7,250) and Classify → FDR (3,940), onto a 3-FTE specialist bottleneck and a 9-FTE analyst pool that is already central in the working-together network. That is the textbook frequency trap the evidence warns about.

The activities that dominate end-to-end performance are lower-frequency or mid-frequency constraint points: entity mismatch (1,750; 31h; 38% exceptions; critical path in 82% when present; blocks KYC and signing), the missing-document loop (3,300/2,800; 52% repeated; RM-triggered incomplete instructions), statement normalization (5,200; 75 min; 20h wait; prerequisite to credit), and contract-control failure (650/510; 14% on credit-line renewal). A4, A5, A6, and A9 attack those joins directly, include a genuine control-strengthening module (A9), preserve human escalation, and avoid activating a downstream bot (A11) or e-sign (A10) while KYC-before-sign is still violated in 6.5% of cases.

A1 should wait. It is a legitimate later hygiene step (duplicates, straight-through for the 72% already digital) once classification, KYC, and document quality can absorb a faster head-of-process. Highest frequency is a good dashboard story; it is the wrong first automation.
