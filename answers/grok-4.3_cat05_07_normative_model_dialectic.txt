## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

Stipulated facts: CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01) mandates Sanctions_Check + Quality_Review before activation and Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier; the resource separation rule is explicit but absent from M0. PD-3 and FD-7 were time-bounded with documented expiry. CRM migration (N4) caused ~70% notification capture with mutable links. Request_More_Info occurred in 47 cases without premature activation. 312 Auto_Approve uses occurred in June–July, of which 289 met PD-3 eligibility criteria on paper.

Disputed propositions: Whether M0 applies to rejected leads or reactivations; whether post-expiry continuation of FD-7 patterns constitutes noncompliance; whether pilot normalization after 2026-07-31 is justified by operational need; whether label changes (Quality_Review  QA_Clearance) or duplicate Register events are data defects versus control-flow deviations.

Burden of proof: Noncompliance is declared only when a case violates an effective, documented rule (CS-9, PD-3 while active, FD-7 while active) with traceable evidence and without documented overlay authority. An exception is accepted only when contemporaneous authority (pilot minutes, emergency directive, or explicit scope exclusion) exists and the deviation stays within the overlay’s stated limits. Logging gaps shift the burden to data-quality classification rather than automatic noncompliance.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

M0 and CS-9 remain the controlling instruments. PD-3 and FD-7 were explicitly temporary; their expiry restored CS-9. SUP-703 applied the pilot to a high-risk ineligible supplier with EUR 180 k spend, violating both eligibility and amount caps. SUP-708 executed Activate_Supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check outside any emergency or pilot window. SUP-705 continued the FD-7 pattern two days after automatic expiry with no written extension. SUP-714 used Auto_Approve after PD-3 lapsed. Aggregate data show 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and 6 post-pilot uses, evidencing normalization of exceptions. Desirable outcomes (SUP-707) do not amend policy; resource-rule violations (SUP-712, SUP-715) remain unmodeled but still breach CS-9. M0’s silence on Request_More_Info does not authorize its omission from enforcement.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is incomplete and incorrectly scoped. It lacks any representation of Request_More_Info loops (SUP-707), low-risk auto-approval (SUP-702, SUP-714), emergency provisional activation (SUP-704), reactivation (SUP-710), or rejected-lead pre-screen exclusion (SUP-709). The CRM migration renders Notify_Requester unreliable (SUP-706), turning apparent deviations into data artifacts. SUP-708 performed sanctions before any transaction, so the trace deviation is low-risk. Label equivalence (SUP-713) and duplicate registration (SUP-711) are mapping and migration issues, not control-flow misconduct. Concurrency assumptions in M0 are violated by legitimate overlays; treating all deviations as noncompliance conflates model obsolescence with operational risk.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Control-flow in revised POWL; eligibility/amount/time rules in data-aware constraints; resource and conflict-of-interest rules in organizational controls; policy-overlay governance via versioned tables; scope rules explicit in intake definitions.

- POWL: core control flow with standard, auto, emergency, and rejection branches plus repeatable Request_More_Info.
- DECLARE/log-skeleton: sanctions-before-activation, quality-before-final-approval (except emergency), notification-before-close.
- pandas/data checks: risk/eligibility snapshot, amount caps, date windows, actor separation, notification_id stability.
- Organizational controls: conflict-of-interest disclosure, pilot/emergency authority logging, reactivation procedure.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])

manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
approved = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual, activate])
approved.order.add_edge(manual, activate)

auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
emergency = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate")

more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
more_info_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), more_info])

reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approved, auto, emergency, reject])

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, checks, more_info_loop, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, checks)
M1.order.add_edge(checks, more_info_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(more_info_loop, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

Policy-overlay table (normalized): columns = (rule_version, effective_from, effective_to, risk_class, approval_type, max_amount, quality_timing, authority_source).

Event/data-collection proposal: mandatory stable notification_id, rule_version_id, eligibility_snapshot JSON, actor_alias_map, delegation_evidence, emergency_or_pilot_id, immutable effective_timestamp and record_timestamp.
