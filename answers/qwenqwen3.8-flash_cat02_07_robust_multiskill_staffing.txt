# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Demand and service assumptions

- The supplied `expected_arrivals` are treated as mean arrivals per two-hour block. For simulation I use a Poisson count with that mean and uniform arrival times within the block. This is an approximation, not an observation.
- Priority and case type are independent draws within a block unless a scenario changes them.
- Service-time ranges are converted to a two-component mixture:
  - ordinary service time: uniform on the ordinary range;
  - complex-tail service time: uniform on the complex-tail range;
  - complex-tail probability as supplied.
- For analytical load estimates I use the midpoint of each range as the mean service time. For bounds I use the high end of the complex-tail range.

Mean base service times, before resource multipliers:

| activity | ordinary mean | complex-tail mean | complex-tail probability | base mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intake | 6.5 | 12.0 | 0.08 | 6.94 |
| Mechanical | 23.0 | 45.0 | 0.15 | 26.30 |
| Electrical | 28.5 | 52.5 | 0.18 | 32.82 |
| Software | 32.5 | 62.5 | 0.20 | 38.50 |
| Validation | 14.0 | 28.5 | 0.12 | 15.74 |

Upper-bound single-case service times, before multipliers, are the high ends of the complex-tail ranges: Intake 14, Mechanical 55, Electrical 65, Software 80, Validation 35 minutes.

## Resource, handover, setup, and parallelism assumptions

- A resource multiplier is applied to the sampled base service time. A multiplier of 1.25 means 25% longer than the base draw.
- Handover, setup, cross-team, and coordination delays are modeled as explicit elapsed/resource overheads:
  - resource change on Intake, diagnostic, or Validation: 7 minutes;
  - movement between mechanical team and electrical/software team unless the same cross-skilled resource retains the case: 5 minutes;
  - resource switching diagnostic skill between consecutive cases: 5 minutes;
  - parallel ME or ES branches: 8 minutes coordination before Validation;
  - sequential ME or ES: no 8-minute coordination, but the two diagnostic activities are serialized.
- For tractability, handover and setup are added to the assigned resource’s occupied time in the simulation. This is conservative for utilization and approximate for elapsed delay.
- Overtime is represented as a 30-minute extension at the end of each shift. The simulation does not explicitly cost overtime or enforce a hard daily overtime budget beyond this simplified availability.
- Breaks are hard unavailability intervals; tasks do not span breaks in the simulation. This may overstate delay for long tasks around break times.
- Preemption is modeled only for P1 diagnostic activities interrupting lower-priority diagnostic activities. The interrupted task is requeued with remaining actual service multiplied by 1.10. A case can be preempted at most once.
- The simulation horizon extends into the next day to allow P3 cases to complete, but there is no overnight staffing. Evening P2 cases can therefore fail structurally if not completed by the end of the first operating window. This is a real operational risk implied by the supplied shifts.

## ME/ES parallelism decision

Parallelism is a policy decision variable, not a fixed process fact.

- Strict specialization: sequential by default, to preserve fixed queues and reduce coordination.
- Full pooling: parallel when qualified resources can take both branches, because pooling can expose enough eligible resources.
- Hybrid: threshold-based parallelization; P1/P2 cases may run parallel, P3 cases are more often sequential unless queue risk is high.

Sequential execution avoids the 8-minute coordination delay but lengthens the critical path and may increase handover if the same cross-skilled resource is unavailable. Parallel execution shortens the diagnostic critical path but increases coordination, handover, and resource fragmentation.

## Objectives

### Objective 1: tail/SLA-risk objective

Let \(S_{P1}\), \(S_{P2}\), \(S_{P3}\) be SLA attainment rates and \(D_{90}\) be the 90th percentile case delay.

A defensible SLA-risk objective is:

\[
\min J_{\text{SLA}} =
w_1(1-S_{P1})^2 +
w_2(1-S_{P2}) +
w_3\frac{D_{90}}{120} +
w_4\frac{\text{preemption penalty}}{\text{completed cases}}
\]

The squared P1 miss term reflects that P1 failure is more severe than linear P2/P3 miss. In the recommendation below, \(w_1\) is largest.

### Objective 2: robust fairness objective

Let \(U_r\) be resource utilization, \(CV(U)\) be the coefficient of variation of utilizations, and \(R_s\) be the P1 SLA attainment in scenario \(s\).

\[
\min J_{\text{robust}} =
\max_s(1-R_s) +
\lambda \cdot CV(U) +
\mu \cdot \max(0, \max_r U_r - 0.90) +
\nu \cdot \text{handover burden per case}
\]

This objective explicitly penalizes:

- worst-case P1 failure across shocks;
- unfair utilization;
- fragility when any resource exceeds 90%;
- coordination overhead.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy A: Strict specialization with fixed queues

Operational rule:

- Intake and Validation are staffed from all qualified resources.
- Diagnostic queues are fixed:
  - Mechanical queue: Ada, Ben;
  - Electrical queue: Chen, Deepa;
  - Software queue: Emil.
- Farah is used for Intake/Validation and emergency overflow only if a dedicated queue is empty.
- ME and ES cases are processed sequentially.
- No P1 preemption.
- No dynamic skill-family overflow.
- Dispatch within each queue is FIFO by priority class, but without cross-queue rebalancing.

Advantages:

- Simple to audit.
- Low handover and setup burden.
- Preserves skill specialization.

Disadvantages:

- Structurally fragile when a dedicated resource is absent or not on shift.
- Poor for ME/ES because it forces cross-team handover and sequential critical paths.
- Fails badly under U1, U3, and U5.

## Policy B: Full pooling with priority, SERPT, and preemption

Operational rule:

- Every diagnostic task may be assigned to any qualified resource.
- Dispatch score:
  1. priority rank: P1 > P2 > P3;
  2. ready time / age;
  3. deadline slack;
  4. shortest expected remaining processing time (SERPT), using the fastest eligible resource multiplier.
- P1 diagnostic tasks may preempt one lower-priority diagnostic activity if P1 is waiting and SLA risk is material.
- ME and ES cases run parallel by default if both branches can be assigned; sequential fallback is used when no two resources are available or when a cross-skilled resource can retain the case.
- Handover minimization:
  - prefer the previous resource for the next task if it is qualified and available;
  - penalize resource switches in the dispatch score.
- Setup minimization:
  - penalize switching a resource between diagnostic skill families between consecutive cases.

Advantages:

- Best base-case mean delay.
- Absorbs absence shocks better than strict specialization.
- Improves P1 SLA through priority and preemption.

Disadvantages:

- Higher handover/setup burden.
- More complex to explain.
- Can overload Validation and late-shift resources if P1 preemption cascades.
- May underutilize slower resources or overuse fast resources unless fairness is added.

## Policy C: Hybrid reserved P1 capacity with controlled overflow

Operational rule:

- Eligibility is pooled, but P1 capacity is reserved dynamically.
- Reserved diagnostic resources while a P1 diagnostic queue exists:
  - Mechanical: Ben;
  - Electrical: Deepa;
  - Software: Emil.
- P2/P3 tasks cannot use reserved resources unless:
  - the P1 queue for that skill is empty, or
  - the P2/P3 task has waited more than 15 minutes, or
  - the reserved resource is idle for more than 5 minutes.
- Preemption:
  - P1 may preempt lower-priority diagnostic work only if P1 has waited at least 5 minutes or its remaining SLA clock is under 30 minutes.
  - Choose the victim with the largest slack.
  - Apply the 10% restart penalty.
- Parallelization:
  - P1 and P2 ME/ES cases are parallelized when both branches can start within 10 minutes.
  - P3 ME/ES cases are sequential unless queue risk is high.
- Overflow triggers:
  - if any skill queue exceeds 3 cases or any P2/P3 wait exceeds 60 minutes, release reserved capacity temporarily;
  - if any resource utilization exceeds 90%, reduce parallelization and increase sequential retention;
  - if handover burden exceeds 10 minutes per completed case, prefer same-resource continuation and cross-skilled sequential retention.

Advantages:

- Protects P1 tail risk.
- Reduces unfair utilization relative to unmanaged pooling.
- More auditable than full SERPT because reservation rules are simple.

Disadvantages:

- Can delay P2/P3 if reservations are too rigid.
- Requires clear release triggers.
- May reserve capacity that is not immediately useful if P1 arrivals are lumpy.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary recommendation: Hybrid reserved P1 capacity with controlled overflow

The hybrid policy is recommended as the primary operating policy under the robustness objective:

\[
\max \text{worst-case P1 SLA} \quad \text{subject to} \quad CV(U) \leq 0.25,\ \max U_r \leq 0.90 \text{ in normal states}
\]

It is not the best policy if the sole objective is base-case mean delay. Full pooling should win on average delay in calm states because it uses all qualified capacity and can parallelize more often. However, the scenario evidence shows that pooling’s advantage erodes under U1, U3, and U5, where P1 SLA and fairness degrade.

Hybrid dominates because:

- It protects P1 tail risk through reserved capacity and disciplined preemption.
- It retains most pooling resilience against absence and mix shocks.
- It reduces handover relative to unconstrained pooling by favoring same-resource continuation and threshold-based parallelization.
- It gives operators a simple audit story: “P1 work is protected; lower-priority work can use reserved capacity only after a wait threshold.”

## Fallback mode: Full pooling with SERPT

Use full pooling as a fallback when:

- no P1 diagnostic is waiting;
- P2/P3 queues exceed 3 cases or wait exceeds 60 minutes;
- reserved capacity has been idle for more than 5 minutes;
- the objective for the current period is backlog clearance rather than P1 protection.

## Strict specialization: not recommended except as a degraded manual mode

Strict specialization should be used only if the dispatch system fails and operators need a simple manual fallback. It is too fragile under Chen absence, morning surges, and late mechanical gaps.

## Mode-switch triggers

| Condition | Mode |
|---|---|
| P1 diagnostic queue > 0 and P1 deadline < 60 minutes | Hybrid protection mode |
| No P1 queue for 15 minutes and P2/P3 queue > 3 cases | Release reservations, switch to pooling/SERPT |
| Any resource utilization > 90% for 30 minutes | Reduce parallelization; increase sequential retention |
| Handover burden > 10 minutes/case | Prefer same-resource continuation; restrict cross-team overflow |
| Chen absent and software queue > 2 cases | Allow Farah/Emil overflow immediately; reserve Emil only for P1 |
| Complex-tail probability doubled | Raise preemption threshold; preempt only if P1 deadline < 45 minutes |

## When another policy is preferable

- If the organization’s objective is purely mean delay under stable staffing and low P1 mix, full pooling is preferable.
- If skill development and auditability are paramount and SLA targets are relaxed, strict specialization may be acceptable.
- If P1 volume is very low and P2/P3 backlog is the main problem, dynamic release of reserved capacity should make the system behave like pooling.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## Gaming of estimates

If dispatch uses expected remaining processing time, resources or planners may inflate estimates to avoid undesirable assignments. Mitigation:

- log planned versus actual service;
- audit persistent overestimation by resource and skill;
- use median actual service over a rolling window, not self-reported estimates alone.

## Starvation of P3 work

Reserved P1 capacity can starve P3. Mitigation:

- aging: after 15 minutes, P2/P3 may use reserved capacity;
- daily fairness cap: no resource should be reserved for P1 for more than a specified fraction of its shift;
- P3 SLA is long, but not infinite; monitor P3 queue age.

## Skill erosion

Strict specialization preserves depth but creates single points of failure. Full pooling may reduce depth if resources are constantly interrupted across skills. Hybrid should use controlled cross-skill overflow:

- allow cross-skill only when queue risk is high;
- track skill-family switching frequency;
- schedule deliberate cross-training without making it the default dispatch rule.

## Excessive preemption

Preemption can create restart penalties and reduce throughput. Mitigation:

- P1 preempts only diagnostic work;
- at most one preemption per case;
- preempt only if P1 wait > 5 minutes or deadline risk < 30 minutes;
- choose the victim with largest slack.

## Coordination overhead

Parallel ME/ES branches add 8 minutes and often increase handover. Threshold-based parallelization should avoid parallelizing P3 work when sequential cross-skilled retention is possible.

## New bottleneck at Validation

Pooling can concentrate Validation on fast resources. Under U1/U5, Validation may become a secondary bottleneck after diagnostic surge. Monitor:

- Validation queue age;
- Intake-to-Validation elapsed time;
- Validation resource utilization;
- handover edges into Validation.

## Fairness

Farah has a short shift and slow multipliers. Full pooling may underuse Farah because faster resources finish earlier. Hybrid fairness penalties should avoid pushing all slow work to Farah while also avoiding overloading Ada/Ben/Chen/Deepa/Emil.

## Human interpretability

Operators need a simple score. A practical dispatch score could be:

1. P1 first;
2. oldest ready task;
3. shortest expected remaining time;
4. same-resource preference;
5. lower setup/handover penalty;
6. lower current utilization.

This is more explainable than a black-box optimization.

   - `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` reveals which resource pairs exchange work and how often.

4. **Drift detection**
   - Compare service-time distributions by skill and resource over time.
   - Monitor whether complex-tail frequency increases.
   - Track preemption count, restart penalty, and P1 SLA attainment.

5. **Bottleneck migration**
   - Compute stage-specific queue age by skill and time block.
   - Track utilization by resource and skill.
   - Use handover network and waiting-time trends to identify whether the bottleneck is moving from Software to Electrical, Mechanical, or Validation.
