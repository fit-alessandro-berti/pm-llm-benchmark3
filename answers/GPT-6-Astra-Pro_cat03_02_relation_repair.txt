## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Select Bundle A for the repaired baseline; retain Bundle B as a sensitivity scenario.** The fragment supports a particularly strong packing inference, but not a uniquely determined delivery or financial reconstruction.

* **Hard invariants:** Existing identifiers and object types are preserved; every relation endpoint must exist. O2O qualifiers retain their direction: `Delivery carries Package`, not its reverse. This repair is additive: no events, objects, or observed relations are removed or invented.
* **Strong defaults:** Apply the supplied activity cardinalities, including one Item and one Package for ordinary packing, and one Payment per payment-recording event. Missing partners trigger investigation, not automatic completion. Packing-before-dispatch and dispatch-before-confirmation are plausibility checks, assuming a common clock, not infallible ordering constraints.
* **Weak heuristics:** Identifier similarity, temporal proximity, customer, route, and amount compatibility support hypotheses. The adjacent `PK8-1` and `PK8-2` scans are much more discriminating than the shared `N-42` prefix. Both deliveries have the same route; both orders have the same customer. Package weights are uninformative without item weights.
* **Evidence against a repair:** Competing identifier matches, allowable portal identifier loss, unapplied cash, possible invoice adjustments, unsupported transitive propagation, and assumptions introduced merely to eliminate incomplete lifecycles.

Existing O2O structure constrains interpretation: **ORD-A has items in both packages**, so its presence at ev11 does not distinguish the corresponding delivery candidates.

The equalities \(130+100=230\) and \(230-100=130\) support consolidated invoicing and PAY-2 application, respectively. Neither proves either relation. The payment residual argument uses the observed invoice and PAY-1 allocation; it does **not** require assuming that ORD-B is billed.

Confidence scores below are subjective plausibility judgments—not calibrated probabilities, independent evidence weights, or values that must sum to one.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair — selected.**
Apply P01–P02 only. ev08 becomes a normal packing event, and PK-8 contains items from two orders. Leave ev10’s delivery unresolved, with DEL-Y preferred. Preserve ev11 as the permitted order-only acknowledgment. Do not add ORD-B to INV-44 or INV-44 to PAY-2. These omissions remain unknown rather than being classified as definitively unbilled or unapplied. DEL-Y remains isolated. “Minimum-change” means the smallest substantive repair adopted here, not a mathematical optimum over all possible repairs.

**Bundle B: two deliveries, joint acknowledgment, consolidated billing.**
Apply P01–P04 and P07–P12. ev08 packs IT-B1 into PK-8; ev10 dispatches PK-8 through DEL-Y. ev11 acknowledges both DEL-X and DEL-Y. INV-44 bills both orders, and PAY-2 applies to INV-44. This yields two dispatch–confirmation lifecycles and an invoice followed by two payments whose recorded amounts total 230. No direct ev11–ORD-B relation or payment-to-order allocation is added. Shared acknowledgment, consolidated billing, and payment application are distinct assumptions, not mutually confirming observations.

**Bundle C: shared delivery, legitimate financial exceptions.**
Apply P01–P02 and P05–P07. Both packages use DEL-X; ev11 acknowledges X only. Treat DEL-Y as unused/planned within this hypothesis. INV-44 bills ORD-A alone, requiring an assumed net 100 adjustment; PAY-2 remains unapplied. This is coherent but less persuasive: it requires discounting `N-421?` and explaining two financial coincidences. The unique ev11 assignment is conditional on this bundle, not established globally.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** Both orders have creation and item-addition events. ORD-A’s two items occupy different packages, which is not prohibited. ORD-B’s directly observed history ends at Add Item; that is incomplete coverage, not an orphan or proven process failure. Order–Item ownership is represented in both observed E2O and O2O.

**Item.** All three items have Add Item followed by Pack Item. After A, each has one observed package assignment. This does not impose lifetime single-package membership: repacking is neither observed nor forbidden. IT-B1’s new package association has only one underlying evidential basis.

**Package.** PK-7 has one packing event and dispatch; PK-8 has two packing events and dispatch. The latter combines two orders, but no supplied rule prohibits that cardinality. Existing PK-7/IT-A1 and PK-8/IT-A2 associations appear in both channels. P02 does not have such independently supplied corroboration.

**Delivery.** DEL-X has dispatch and an observed `carries` relation, supported by ev09. It lacks direct confirmation under A. DEL-Y has neither E2O nor O2O participation and is the sole fully isolated object. Its existence does not oblige us to attach it to ev10.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has issuance and one directly associated payment; its ORD-A billing relation appears in both channels. Its observed applied-payment balance is 130, but that is not proof of an unpaid balance because PAY-2’s allocation is unresolved. The declared-value discrepancy is not a hard violation.

**Payment.** Each payment has exactly one recording event. PAY-1’s invoice application is represented in E2O and O2O. PAY-2 is not an event-orphaned object: it has ev14, but no established invoice allocation.

There are **no orphan events**. A changes 22 E2O/8 O2O relations to **23/9**, preserving all 14 events and 12 objects.

**Circularity warning:** P02 cannot independently validate P01. The same applies to P03/P04, P09/P10, and P11/P12. Even agreement between *observed* E2O and O2O does not prove independent collection; their provenance is unspecified.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** These predictions use directly related events per object, without propagating events through O2O paths—the relevant distinction in PM4Py’s discovery implementation. 

Relative to the unrepaired log, A changes PK-8 from `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` to `Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package`. The old event pair ev07→ev10 disappears, replaced by ev07→ev08 and ev08→ev10. The activity-level Pack→Dispatch edge remains; its PK-8 interval changes from 56 to 55 minutes. The new self-loop represents **different items**, not necessarily rework.

B additionally introduces Order-level `Add Item → Issue Invoice` for ORD-B, Delivery-level `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` for both deliveries, and Invoice-level `Record Payment → Record Payment`. It does not create Package-level confirmation or Order-level payment events.

**OTG.** Distinguish a qualified O2O summary from PM4Py’s default OTG, which aggregates event-derived interaction, descendants, inheritance, cobirth, and codeath graphs. It is not simply a display of stored `packs`, `carries`, or `bills` edges. 

For qualified O2O counts, unrepaired→A→B gives: `packs` **2→3→3**, `carries` **1→1→2**, `bills` **1→1→2**, and `settles` **1→1→2**.

In the event-derived interaction graph, A adds IT-B1–PK-8. B adds, among others, Order–Order interaction through ev12 and Delivery–Delivery interaction through ev11. These are co-participation relationships, not new business qualifiers. 

Extending DEL-X’s observed lifecycle to ev11 also removes its apparent co-ending with PK-7 at ev09. Such inferred lifecycle boundaries describe observed coverage, not physical creation or destruction. 

**ET–OT graph.** PM4Py’s classic implementation weights activity–type edges by relation rows. Thus, A changes Pack Item–Package from **2 to 3**. B additionally changes Dispatch Package–Delivery **1→2**, Issue Invoice–Order **1→2**, and Record Payment–Invoice **1→2**. Confirm Delivery–Delivery appears with weight **2**, although there is only **one confirmation event**. 

**Flattenings.** Ordinary flattening uses direct E2O membership, not automatic transitive O2O inheritance. 

Order flattenings are unchanged by A. B appends Issue Invoice to ORD-B and represents ev12 in both order traces. Package flattenings gain only the extra PK-8 packing occurrence. Delivery flattening has one singleton DEL-X trace in unrepaired/A; B has two dispatch–confirmation traces, sharing ev11. Invoice flattening changes from issuance plus one payment to issuance plus two payments in B. Payment flattenings remain two singleton Record Payment traces in every bundle.

**Activity frequencies.** Distinguish distinct events, distinct participating objects, and distinct event–object incidences—the basis of PM4Py’s corresponding activity metrics. 

Global activity-event counts remain unchanged: **14 events**. For Pack Item restricted to Package, `(events, unique objects, total incidences)` changes **(2,2,2)→(3,2,3)→(3,2,3)**. For Confirm Delivery restricted to Delivery, it changes **(0,0,0)→(0,0,0)→(1,2,2)**. O2O additions alone do not increase these E2O-based counts.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** PM4Py’s current implementation extracts ancestor/descendant neighborhoods from event-derived interactions and then compares normalized execution descriptions; it is not merely connected-component clustering. 

Unrepaired ORD-B has a small disconnected execution. A connects its item through PK-8, changing both orders’ extracted neighborhoods. B adds shared billing and acknowledgment structure and may bring payment events into both rooted executions. Sharing a component does **not** establish equivalence: different item counts and ORD-A-only direct confirmation can preserve distinct signatures. With two orders, cluster-frequency conclusions would be fragile.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

The pandas validation was exercised on the supplied identifiers and relations, including negative duplicate, endpoint, and direction tests. The PM4Py calls were not executed here. The constructor line follows the checked source signature and is explicitly version-dependent. 

```python
import pandas as pd

# Assumes canonical OCEL column names; arbitrary event/object attributes survive.
original = {name: frame.copy(deep=True) for name, frame in {
    "events": events, "objects": objects, "relations": relations, "o2o": o2o
}.items()}
e, o, r, oo = [original[k].copy(deep=True)
               for k in ("events", "objects", "relations", "o2o")]

EI, OI, OI2 = "ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"
AC, TM, TY, QU = "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier"

def require(df: pd.DataFrame, cols: list[str], name: str) -> None:
    missing = set(cols) - set(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"{name}: missing columns {sorted(missing)}")
    if df[cols].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError(f"{name}: null required values")

def no_duplicates(df: pd.DataFrame, keys: list[str], name: str) -> None:
    if df.duplicated(keys).any():
        raise ValueError(f"{name}: duplicate relation/identity keys {keys}")

require(e, [EI, AC, TM], "events")
require(o, [OI, TY], "objects")
require(r, [EI, OI], "relations")
require(oo, [OI, OI2, QU], "o2o")
no_duplicates(e, [EI], "events")
no_duplicates(o, [OI], "objects")

e[TM] = pd.to_datetime(e[TM], errors="raise")
# Common-clock assumption only: do not invent a timezone or change tied timestamps.
r[QU] = r[QU].fillna("") if QU in r else ""
event_index, object_index = e.set_index(EI), o.set_index(OI)

def checked(rel: pd.DataFrame, objrel: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    require(rel, [EI, OI], "relations")
    require(objrel, [OI, OI2, QU], "o2o")
    for values, known, label in [
        (rel[EI], e[EI], "E2O event"),
        (rel[OI], o[OI], "E2O object"),
        (objrel[OI], o[OI], "O2O source"),
        (objrel[OI2], o[OI], "O2O target"),
    ]:
        bad = values[~values.isin(known)]
        if not bad.empty:
            raise ValueError(f"{label}: dangling IDs {bad.tolist()}")
    no_duplicates(rel, [EI, OI, QU], "E2O")
    no_duplicates(objrel, [OI, OI2, QU], "O2O")
    out = rel.copy(deep=True)

    # Check supplied denormalized metadata before filling missing metadata.
    expected = {
        AC: out[EI].map(event_index[AC]),
        TM: out[EI].map(event_index[TM]),
        TY: out[OI].map(object_index[TY]),
    }
    for col, values in expected.items():
        if col in out:
            actual = (pd.to_datetime(out[col], errors="raise")
                      if col == TM else out[col])
            if (actual.notna() & actual.ne(values)).any():
                raise ValueError(
                    f"E2O metadata disagrees with master table: {col}"
                )
        out[col] = values

    signature = {
        "contains": ("Order", "Item"),
        "packs": ("Package", "Item"),
        "carries": ("Delivery", "Package"),
        "bills": ("Invoice", "Order"),
        "settles": ("Payment", "Invoice"),
    }
    for src, dst, qual in objrel[[OI, OI2, QU]].itertuples(
        index=False, name=None
    ):
        if qual in signature:
            actual = (object_index.at[src, TY], object_index.at[dst, TY])
            if actual != signature[qual]:
                raise ValueError(
                    f"Wrong directional/type signature: {src}, {qual}, {dst}"
                )
    return out

r = checked(r, oo)  # Validate observed data before any addition.

# Selected Bundle A only. Provisional/unresolved candidates are not appended.
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([{EI: "ev08", OI: "PK-8", QU: "related"}])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([{OI: "PK-8", OI2: "IT-B1", QU: "packs"}])

# Sidecar provenance: these are not additional OCEL relation fields.
repair_provenance = pd.DataFrame([
    {"proposal": "P01", "kind": "E2O", "confidence": 0.94,
     "justification": "Adjacent PK8-1/PK8-2 scanner sequence; source fragment."},
    {"proposal": "P02", "kind": "O2O", "confidence": 0.92,
     "justification": "Same packing hypothesis as P01; not independent evidence."},
])

r = pd.concat([r, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
oo = pd.concat([oo, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)
r = checked(r, oo)

# Cardinality counts ignore multiple qualifiers for the same event-object pair.
incidence = r[[EI, OI, TY]].drop_duplicates()
cardinality = (
    incidence.groupby([EI, TY])[OI].nunique().unstack(fill_value=0)
    .reindex(index=e[EI], columns=o[TY].unique(), fill_value=0)
)
print("Cardinality profile (review defaults; do not enforce completeness):")
print(cardinality)
print("Events without E2O:", sorted(set(e[EI]) - set(r[EI])))
print("Objects without E2O:", sorted(set(o[OI]) - set(r[OI])))
print("Multiple-qualifier event/object pairs:",
      r.loc[r.duplicated([EI, OI], keep=False), [EI, OI, QU]])

# Stable ID tie-breaking is computational, not evidence of temporal precedence.
e = e.sort_values([TM, EI], kind="mergesort").reset_index(drop=True)
r = r.sort_values([TM, EI, OI], kind="mergesort").reset_index(drop=True)

import inspect
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Completing both deliveries from ev11** could turn a partial or order-only acknowledgment into false proof of two completed shipments.
2. **Propagating ev11 to ORD-B through PK-8** could fabricate an order acknowledgment that the portal never recorded.
3. **Treating simultaneous dispatches as a bulk event** could duplicate PK-8 participation or create a misleading zero-duration Dispatch→Dispatch sequence.
4. **Treating arithmetic as allocation proof** could conceal invoice adjustments, unapplied cash, or unsupported PAY-1–ORD-B and PAY-2–ORD-A allocations.
5. **Reversing qualifiers or materializing every O2O path as E2O** could create invalid associations and artificial packing, delivery, and payment events in Order flattenings.
6. **Forcing every lifecycle to finish** could erase the legitimate possibility of unused DEL-Y, incomplete extraction, and unapplied PAY-2. A cleaner-looking process graph would then be evidence of the repair policy, not necessarily the process.
