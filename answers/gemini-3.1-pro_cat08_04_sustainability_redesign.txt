# 1. System boundary and assumptions
The environmental assessment scope encompasses the order fulfillment lifecycle within the distributor's direct control. 
**Inside the boundary:** Energy consumption from manufacturing machines (CNC_A, CNC_B, heat treatment), material scrap resulting from manufacturing defects and rework, packaging materials (standard and reinforced), and outbound transport emissions across all route modes (parcel ground, air, dedicated van, consolidated truck).
**Outside the boundary:** Raw material extraction, upstream supplier manufacturing, facility overhead (HVAC, lighting) unless tied to the `energy_kwh` order attribute, customer use-phase of the spare parts, and end-of-life disposal.
**Assumptions:** We assume that obsolescence is directly tied to excessive batch sizes driven by forecasting errors; that "returns empty" for dedicated vans implies unused backhaul capacity that shared couriers could mitigate; and that promised lead time acts as a hard constraint for service level calculations. 

# 2. Sustainability-related process indicators
*   **Energy:** Average `energy_kwh` per order; Energy per item by `machine_group` and `batch_size`.
*   **Transport:** Average `transport_kgco2e` per order; Percentage of standard-stock orders shipped by air or dedicated van.
*   **Materials:** Average `material_scrap_kg` per order; Average `package_material_kg` per order.
*   **Rework & Returns:** Rework rate (%) by machine group; Packaging rework loop frequency (events per order).
*   **Batching:** Median batch wait time; Obsolescence scrap rate (target < 6%).
*   **Service:** Median and p90 cycle time by segment; Late delivery rate (%).
*   **Quality:** Inspection failure rate; Defect-driven return/rework rates.
*   **Resilience:** SP1, SP2, and Q1 policy conformance rates; Alternative carrier availability for Service-contract orders.

# 3. Intervention options
*   **S1. Green shipping recommender:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Standard-stock and Made-to-order fulfillment.
    *   *Mechanism:* Shifts transport from air to ground or consolidated truck dynamically when calculated lead time slack permits.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Maintains service levels by acting only on slack; enhances resilience by standardizing carrier selection criteria.
    *   *Required evidence:* Monitor `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, and cycle time.
*   **S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC / Heat treatment.
    *   *Mechanism:* Balances energy efficiency of large batches (e.g., dropping to 1.3 kWh for size 12-20) against the 6% obsolescence scrap risk and downstream transport expediting caused by excessive queueing.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Reduces p90 cycle times and late delivery risks (currently 1.7x higher for heat treatment waits).
    *   *Required evidence:* `batch_size`, batch wait times, `late_delivery` flag, `material_scrap_kg`.
*   **S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Made-to-order production queues.
    *   *Mechanism:* Routes volume to CNC_A to drastically reduce the material scrap and energy waste associated with CNC_B's higher rework rate (18% vs 9%).
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* CNC_A is slower, so it requires queue monitoring to avoid service deterioration, but significantly improves first-pass quality.
    *   *Required evidence:* `machine_group`, `rework_flag`, queue lengths, and `material_scrap_kg`.
*   **S4. Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Standard-stock Service-contract (currently averaging 7.8 kgCO2e).
    *   *Mechanism:* Swaps air transport (4-6x more kgCO2e) for ground by accelerating warehouse picking to consume the time buffer.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Protects contracted service levels while reducing policy SP1 violations (currently 22%).
    *   *Required evidence:* `route_mode`, Picking duration, and SP1 conformance flags.
*   **S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Standard-stock Regular segment.
    *   *Mechanism:* Reduces transport emissions by 35-55% via volume consolidation, accepting a deliberate 12-24h wait.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Slightly increases cycle time but remains well within the segment's generous slack (31h median).
    *   *Required evidence:* `route_mode` (consolidated_truck), wait times, and `transport_kgco2e`.
*   **S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging loops.
    *   *Mechanism:* Eliminates duplicate packaging materials (reducing `package_material_kg`) and wasted handling energy.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Speeds up outbound operations and eliminates handling errors, improving resilience.
    *   *Required evidence:* Rework packaging transition frequency and `package_material_kg`.
*   **S8. Replace dedicated vans with shared emergency couriers:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Critical-downtime Service-contract (currently averaging 52.0 kgCO2e).
    *   *Mechanism:* Eliminates empty return trips of dedicated vans by sharing logistics networks.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* Maintains urgent response capabilities (14h cycle time) while dramatically lowering carbon intensity.
    *   *Required evidence:* `route_mode`, `case_type` (Critical-downtime), and `transport_kgco2e`.
*   **S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules:** 
    *   *Target path/segment:* Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder.
    *   *Mechanism:* Cuts transport kgCO2e and packaging materials by waiting to ship a single, complete order instead of splitting it.
    *   *Service/quality/resilience effect:* May increase median cycle time but reduces customer friction and handling errors associated with split deliveries.
    *   *Required evidence:* Partial shipment transition frequency, cycle time, `package_material_kg`.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks
1.  **Batching vs. Transport Displacement:** Maximizing production batch sizes reduces manufacturing energy per item, but the resulting queue delays (e.g., 38h wait for CNC_A) can consume lead-time slack, forcing downstream logistics to use high-emission express air transport to avoid late deliveries.
2.  **Batching vs. Obsolescence:** Over-batching to save energy generates a 6% obsolescence rate. Producing unused parts displaces the energy savings with wasted raw materials and embedded carbon.
3.  **Quality Bottleneck Displacement:** Shifting too much volume from CNC_B to CNC_A to reduce scrap could create a massive bottleneck, forcing "Emergency production" paths and dedicated vans to satisfy MTO deadlines.
4.  **Packaging vs. Transport Weight:** Applying Q1 (reinforced packaging) strictly reduces rework and damage scrap, but doubles packaging weight, thereby incrementally increasing transport emissions.
5.  **Inspection Rushing Rebound:** If strict queue-time caps (from dynamic batching) are enforced, the inspection cell may rush, missing defects that later cause packaging incompatibility or customer returns, paradoxically increasing total cycle time and material waste.
6.  **Consolidation vs. Resilience:** Relying heavily on consolidated trucks reduces routing flexibility. If a consolidated truck is delayed or misses a departure, a much larger batch of orders is impacted, threatening Service-contract SLA resilience.

# 6. Balanced recommendation
The recommended redesign package is **"Smart Flow & Slack Management,"** combining S1 (Green recommender), S2 (Dynamic batching), S3 (Shift to CNC_A cautiously), S4 (Priority pick + ground), and S6 (Packaging checks). 
*Why it is balanced:* This package addresses the root causes of waste rather than treating symptoms. It prevents the displacement risk of "efficient" large batches causing downstream express shipping (S2). By accelerating internal warehouse operations (S4, S6), it creates the time slack necessary to utilize lower-emission transport modes (S1) without deteriorating the service levels promised to Service-contract customers. Furthermore, shifting to CNC_A (S3) sacrifices local machine speed to improve systemic quality, cutting the high material scrap and hidden energy waste of rework loops, proving that slower, high-quality production is greener overall. 

# 7. Validation and governance plan
*   **Event-Log Validation:** Continuously ingest the event log to monitor specific transitions. Success will be indicated by a reduced frequency of `Package standard -> Rework packaging` and `Availability check -> Emergency production` transitions. 
*   **Policy Conformance Monitoring:** Implement automated dashboards tracking SP1 (flagging Service-contract orders shipped by air when `promised_lead_time_hours` > required ground + pick time), SP2 (flagging `dedicated_van` without a Critical-downtime justification), and Q1 (flagging MTO parts > 8kg in standard packaging without a certified inspection event).
*   **Segment-Level Service Monitoring:** Track p90 cycle times specifically for the MTO segments to ensure the shift to CNC_A does not breach SLAs.
*   **Exception Governance:** Enact intervention S12 by maintaining a sustainability exception log. Any SP1/SP2 overrides must be tagged with a reason code, reviewed monthly by process owners against actual emission impacts, and used to continuously calibrate the S1 Green shipping recommender's parameters.
