# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

## Assumptions (labelled)

| ID | Assumption | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | `join_ready` = latest of A_end, B_end, C_end (the stated rule); no unlogged approval step. | The join time is not logged, so it must be derived. |
| A2 | All three branches are released together at `fork_release`, so branch queue = start  fork_release. | Given in the scenario. |
| A3 | Timestamps are minute-accurate, so every duration carries about ±1 min of rounding. | The smallest "critical margin" found below is 5 min, so no conclusion depends on a 1-min difference. |
| A4 | Counterfactuals are ceteris paribus: other durations stay fixed, and queue length does not feed back into service time. | Needed for a static what-if. |
| A5 | The downstream wait can be read in two ways, and both are shown. **Pass-through:** the observed lag is case-specific and is preserved, so join savings carry 1:1 into flow time. **Capacity replay:** F is a 2-server FIFO, non-idling station with 20-min service. | A6 | Fractional minutes are allowed in counterfactuals (50% of 5 min = 2.5 min). | Avoids arbitrary rounding. |
| A7 | n = 12 cases on one day. Statistics are descriptive, not inferential. | Sample size. |

## Notation

Case c, branch i  {A, B, C}, and `t(·)` is a timestamp.

- Upstream elapsed: `U_c = fork_c  arrival_c`
- Branch queue: `Q_ic = start_ic  fork_c`
- Branch service: `S_ic = end_ic  start_ic`
- Branch completion offset: `T_ic = end_ic  fork_c = Q_ic + S_ic`
- Join ready: `J_c = max_i end_ic`
- Branch synchronization wait: `W_ic = J_c  end_ic  0`
- Fork-join elapsed time: `FJ_c = J_c  fork_c = max_i T_ic`
- Critical branch: `k*_c = argmax_i end_ic`. Ties are broken deterministically in the order A < B < C, and all tied branches are also reported. `W_k*c = 0`.
- Critical margin: `M_c = J_c  (second-latest end)`. This is the most that accelerating only the critical branch can save at the join.
- Downstream queue: `D_c = final_start  J_c`
- Final service: `G_c = final_end  final_start`
- Total flow time: `Flow_c = final_end  arrival`

## Non-double-counting partition

The flow time is additive only along the critical path:

`Flow_c = U_c + FJ_c + D_c + G_c = U_c + Q_k*c + S_k*c + D_c + G_c`

Noncritical branches' `Q`, `S` and `W` are off-critical-path information, not additive flow components. `W_ic` is slack.

## Why summing all branch synchronization waits double counts

For every branch, the identity `FJ_c = Q_ic + S_ic + W_ic` holds because every branch spans the same interval from `fork_c` to `J_c`. Summing over the three branches gives:

`_i (Q_ic + S_ic + W_ic) = 3·FJ_c`

If all branch components were added to `U + D + G`, the stage would be counted three times, an overcount of `2·FJ_c`.

Example F02:
- Branch rows are A: 0+50+10 = 60, B: 20+40+0 = 60, C: 5+20+35 = 60, which totals 180 = 3×60.
- Sync waits alone sum to 10+0+35 = 45. Adding these to the critical path (20+40) would give 105, but the true `FJ` is 60.
- Only the critical branch's `Q + S` is on the clock. The other branches' waits ran concurrently with it.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

All savings are in minutes of total flow time. "Pass-through" keeps each case's observed downstream lag. "Capacity replay" uses a 2-server FIFO final station with 20-min service, measured against its own baseline.

## CF1: A service 20%

- **Direct benefit:**
  - F01 saves 5 (join 5045). The saving is capped by B, which completes at offset 45.
  - F11 saves 10 (5040). It is now tied with B at 40.
  - Together these saturate A's standalone cap of 15 min, so accelerating A any further gains nothing.
- **Zero benefit, 10 cases:** F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, F12 (B critical) and F04, F08 (C critical). Shortening a noncritical branch only increases its sync wait.
- **Capacity replay:** F11's 10-min gain is absorbed. Its new join (11:20) ties with F10, and with two servers busy it still starts at 11:35. Only F01's 5 min survive. This result depends on the tie-break (F10 before F11), so it is an illustration, not a robust estimate.
- **Secondary effects:** mean A work-in-process falls by about 20%. The floor on the fork-join stage drops from 50 to 40, so B becomes the sole limit in more cases.

## CF2: B queue 50%

- **Direct benefit:**
  - Eight B-critical cases benefit: F02 10, F03 10, F05 10, F06 15, F07 15, F09 12.5, F10 5, F12 15, for a total of 92.5.
  - Without the A floor the gain would be 100, so A absorbs 7.5 min (F05 2.5, F10 5).
- **Zero benefit, 4 cases:** F01 and F11 (A critical) and F04 and F08 (C critical).
- **Criticality shift:** after CF2, A is critical or tied in F01, F02, F05, F06, F10 and F11 (6/12 with the A < B < C tie-break). B remains critical in 4 cases and C in 2. A becomes the new bottleneck.
- **Tail:** the maximum flow falls from 130 to 115 and P90 from 124.5 to 114.5.
- **Capacity replay:** there are no capacity waits after the change, and F11 gains an extra 5 min from spillover because earlier joins clear the queue. The total is 97.5.
- **Secondary effects:** B sync waits increase in the cases where B is no longer last. Work in progress falls (Little's Law, total flow over the 285-min window: about 4.85  4.53 cases). Join times compress, so final-station arrivals get more regular.

## CF3: downstream queueing

- **CF3a, third resource (capacity replay):**
  - Only F11 benefits, by 5 min (total 5, mean 0.42).
  - If the observed wait is not a server constraint, the effect is zero.
  - Final-station utilization is 240 server-min over 424 available (57%), and both servers were busy at `join_ready` in only 3/12 cases (F05, F09, F10).
  - Adding capacity is therefore unlikely to be the lever.
- **CF3b, halve the observed lag (policy or handover):**
  - Every case gains 0.5·D, for a total of 88 (mean 7.33) and a maximum flow of 122.5.
  - Eliminating the lag entirely would save 176 min (mean 14.67, mean flow 100.58).
  - This is the second-largest addressable block after B's queue.
- **Masking question:**
  - Under capacity replay there is almost no masking, except in the F11 tie case in CF1.
  - Under a release-slot floor (final start fixed regardless of join), every branch improvement is fully masked and gives zero benefit. The timestamps cannot exclude this reading.
  - The honest range for CF1 and CF2 is therefore 0 up to the pass-through figure.

## CF4 and CF5

- **CF4 (A 20% and B queue 50%):** the total saving is 117.5, against 107.5 for the two alone. The extra 10 min is super-additive because A and B cap each other (F01 +2.5, F05 +2.5, F10 +5).
- **CF5 (C queue 50%):**
  - F04 saves 7 and F08 saves 5, because A (50) becomes the limit.
  - This confirms C's low standalone leverage.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

## Redesign 1: Cut B's queue by queue-aware dispatch and release scheduling

For example, dispatch the B test first, or give a priority lane to cases whose B wait is already aging, and use C's slack to schedule around B.

- **Throughput:**
  - Arrivals are about every 15 min, so throughput will mostly be unchanged unless capacity binds. Capacity is not identifiable (section 7).
  - Work in progress and flow time fall.
- **Flow-time tail:**
  - This is the largest lever in the analysis. Pass-through P90 goes from 124.5 to 114.5 and the maximum from 130 to 115.
  - The benefit stops at A's 50-min floor.
- **Resource utilization:**
  - The utilization of B's (unknown) resources is not observable. Observed overlap reached at least 4 concurrent B tests, so parallel capacity exists.
  - Halving queue without adding capacity requires better sequencing or release rules, not just more work in parallel. Overload risk on B resources must be checked.
- **New bottlenecks:**
  - A becomes critical or tied in about half the cases.
  - Further B gains then yield nothing without an A change.
  - C also stays critical in F04 and F08.
- **Synchronization effects:**
  - B sync wait rises while A and C waits shrink or stay the same.
  - Deprioritizing C must respect its 22-min minimum observed slack.
- **Pilot and measure first:**
  - Start by measuring why B cases wait: resource calendars, setup, sample availability, and priority rules.
  - Record B resource identity and capacity.
  - Pilot on a subset with pre-registered B queue and completion-offset targets, and track the share of cases where A becomes critical.

## Redesign 2: Replace the fixed or alternating final-stage handover with join-triggered, first-available dispatch

For example, F starts automatically when the third branch completes, on whichever of F1 or F2 is free.

- **Throughput:** little change, since F utilization is about 57% and the replay predicts almost no capacity queue.
- **Flow-time tail:**
  - Potential saving is 14.67 min on average, and up to 176 min in total (the capacity-replay bound is only 5).
  - The real benefit depends on whether the lag is a release policy or a true constraint (section 7).
- **Resource utilization:** only a small rise is expected. Watch for clustering of joins once Redesign 1 compresses them. The capacity replay shows no waiting even then.
- **New bottlenecks:**
  - Final service (20 min) becomes a fixed floor, and U and G remain.
  - If the lag is a compliance or review step, removing it is a risk.
- **Synchronization effects:** none directly. Earlier join use makes sync waits more visible.
- **Pilot and measure first:**
  - Diagnose the lag: check whether F1/F2 were idle, the assignment rule (F1 and F2 alternate exactly by case order), and whether there is a hand-off notification or approval step.
  - The observed pattern of idle servers at join time (9 of 12 cases) is suggestive but not causal.
  - Measure `final_start  join_ready` alongside server state before and after.

**Sequencing:** pilot Redesign 2 in parallel as a diagnostic, because it is cheap. Scale Redesign 1 first, then revisit A, given the CF4 complementarity.

def max_conc(starts, ends):
    e = [(t, 1) for t in starts] + [(t, -1) for t in ends]
    e.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))     # ends before starts at equal times
    cur = mx = 0
    for _, d in e:
        cur += d
        mx = max(mx, cur)
    return mx

def replay(join_min, service, n_servers):
    """Non-idling FIFO multi-server replay (ties broken by case order)."""
    order = sorted(range(len(join_min)), key=lambda i: (join_min[i], i))
    free = [0.0] * n_servers
    heapq.heapify(free)
    start = np.zeros(len(join_min))
    for i in order:
        s = max(join_min[i], heapq.heappop(free))
        start[i] = s
        heapq.heappush(free, s + service[i])
    return start

def run(name, q_fac=None, s_fac=None, d_fac=1.0, n_servers=2, use_pass=True, use_cap=True):
    q_fac, s_fac = q_fac or {}, s_fac or {}
    off = np.column_stack([Qm[:, k] * q_fac.get(b, 1.0) + Sm[:, k] * s_fac.get(b, 1.0)
                           for k, b in enumerate(BR)])
    fj = off.max(axis=1)                                   # max-based fork-join
    sav_pass = flow0 - (U + fj + D_obs * d_fac + G) if use_pass else np.full(n, np.nan)
    if use_cap:
        jn = fork_min + fj
        st = replay(jn, G, n_servers)
        sav_cap = flow_cap0 - (U + fj + (st - jn) + G)
    else:

    new_ci = np.isclose(off, off.max(1, keepdims=True)).argmax(1)
    s = dict(scenario=name)
    if use_pass:
        nf = flow0 - sav_pass
        s.update(pass_total=np.nansum(sav_pass), pass_mean=np.nanmean(sav_pass),
                 pass_n_benefit=int((sav_pass > 1e-9).sum()),
                 new_mean=nf.mean(), new_p90=p90(nf), new_max=nf.max())
    if use_cap:
        s.update(cap_total=np.nansum(sav_cap), cap_mean=np.nanmean(sav_cap),
                 cap_n_benefit=int((sav_cap > 1e-9).sum()), cap_n_worse=int((sav_cap < -1e-9).sum()))
    s["crit_counts_after"] = {b: int((new_ci == k).sum()) for k, b in enumerate(BR)}
    return s, sav_pass, sav_cap

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality.**
  - The data are one day of 12 cases.
  - Critical-branch frequency and counterfactual savings are descriptive. They show what the observed timestamps imply under the max-rule, not why queues occurred.
  - A branch's queue may reflect case priority, sample availability, batching or staffing that is not logged.
  - Static counterfactuals ignore feedback, such as an earlier join raising congestion for later cases.
- **Branch resource capacity.**
  - Branch resources are not logged. Observed concurrency was at least 4 for A, at least 4 for B and at least 3 for C (by hand check, printed by the code).
  - B has more than 4 tests in progress at times while later cases still wait. A simple fixed-server queue therefore does not explain B's waiting.
  - B's queue could reflect setup, shared equipment, skills or calendars. The data cannot separate these.
  - A's queue is always 0, which is consistent with ample capacity, dedicated resources or start-on-release. It is not evidence that A is fast.
- **Work preemption and interruption.**
  - Only start and end are logged. If a branch was paused, `S` includes waiting inside the activity, and branch "service" would be overstated.
  - Tests like B and A may also involve unobserved overlap with other work.
- **Is final-station waiting truly a queue?**
  - A strict queue would mean servers were busy when the case was ready. In 9 of 12 cases, fewer than two F services were in progress at `join_ready` (from logged F activities only).
  - Both servers were busy in only 3 cases (F05, F09, F10).
  - A FIFO two-server replay with 20-min service predicts about 5 min of queueing against 176 observed.
  - F1 and F2 alternate exactly with case order, and the lag is fairly regular (8–20 min, mean 14.7). This suggests a handover delay, a fixed release or review step, or pre-assigned routing rather than a capacity queue.
  - These patterns are consistent with a release policy but do not prove one. If the lag is a policy, branch improvements could be fully masked (zero benefit) or fully passed through, and the data cannot say which.
- **Join definition.**
  - `join_ready` is assumed to be the latest branch completion (A1). If F needs any other unlogged trigger, such as approval or availability of inputs, part of the "downstream queue" is a precedence wait, not queueing.
- **Timestamp precision and sample size.**
  - Durations carry about ±1 min rounding error. The smallest margins (5 min) survive this, but the A vs C near-tie in the ranking (2 critical cases each) cannot be resolved with n = 12.
  - Tie-breaking (A < B < C) only affects labels in counterfactual states and some replay orderings. The F10/F11 tie in CF1 shows order sensitivity.
- **Generalization.**
  - Arrival spacing is almost constant (15 min) on one day. Behaviour under heavier load, or when arrivals are bursty, is not observable.
