The waiting sets below use the recorded arrival scans. A later recorded arrival is not evidence that a job was ineligible if it is shown as arrived before the dispatch decision. No appointment tolerance is supplied. “Exact appointment” means a rule that targets the stated minute; a flexible appointment-window rule can predict earlier or later starts only if its tolerance permits them.

For the tables, priority predictions assume higher priority first, with arrival order as a tie-break. SPT uses the visible estimate; for suspended B01 it uses estimated remaining productive work. FIFO predictions for a suspended job depend on whether the dispatcher retains its original queue position. Skill-based routing predicts resource eligibility, not a global service order. All jobs in QA, QB, and QC require general skill, so that rule does not distinguish their order.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

- **QA — FIFO with appointment-aware insertion.** A02, A05, A06, and A07 start in arrival order after the A03 exception; A02 at 08:32 also beats newer A04 despite A04’s priority and shorter estimate. A03 supports appointment scheduling because it starts exactly at 08:20, ahead of older A02. It also challenges FIFO, but does not require SPT: A04 is shorter and would have been selected by SPT. A08 starts last despite priority 1; this contradicts strict priority and has no known resource explanation. A06 starts five minutes after its stated time, consistent with a small appointment tolerance or ordinary FIFO, not proof of an appointment rule. The appointment explains A03 if the slot was reserved; otherwise it requires an undocumented override.

- **QB — preemption plus resume preference.** B02 supports preemptive priority: it is priority 1, interrupts B01, and the recorded reason is “urgent arrival.” B05 later starts ahead of B04, supporting priority selection at 10:00. B01’s resumptions at 09:31 and 10:14 support a resume-first or continuation rule, but contradict strict priority and SPT because B03, B04, B06, and B07 are respectively higher priority or shorter in the relevant sets. B03 starting at 09:48 is compatible with priority over B04, but conflicts with the recorded B05 if B05 remained eligible. The stated reassignment may explain why B01 was not resumed then; it does not, by itself, explain B05. The explicit first interruption is supported by a recorded operational reason; the remaining exceptions need a resumption/reassignment rule or another unrecorded reason.

- **QC — SPT with an override or aging component.** C02, C03, and C04 are the shortest jobs at 10:10, 10:18, and 10:32 respectively; C06 is shortest at 11:34. These support SPT using the arrival-time estimates. C05 challenges pure SPT at 10:54, but its priority 1 and already-passed deadline could trigger an override. That is an exception, not clear evidence of an SLA-protection switch: it could not meet its deadline at dispatch time. C01 at 11:40 challenges SPT, priority, and earliest-deadline-first; it is consistent with FIFO or an anti-starvation/aging rule. The record does not tell whether C01 had been aged into a special rank. The C05 selection has a plausible known priority/deadline explanation; C01 requires aging, a tie/eligibility fact not shown, or an undocumented exception.

- **QD — skill-constrained routing with possible capacity reservation.** D02, D05, and D07 support routing by skills: firmware requires D-F, firmware plus electrical requires D-F, and mechanical plus electrical requires D-X. D03 on D-F while X is free, and D07 on X while D-F is free, are consistent with assigning flexible jobs to a more general resource and reserving scarce capability. D06’s exact 11:30 start supports an appointment reservation. D08’s delay despite priority 1, short duration, and potential eligibility on idle X challenges a pure priority or SPT policy; D04 on D-M while X was also qualified likewise shows skill eligibility does not explain every assignment. These are not violations if X was reserved for D06 or for combined-skill work, but no reservation record is supplied. If resources are dedicated by task class despite their listed skills, that assignment restriction is also undocumented.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

1. **QA at 08:20: appointment insertion versus SPT.** Both explanations select A03 at 08:20, so that start alone cannot distinguish them. After A03 completes at 08:30, SPT predicts A04 (estimate 7) ahead of A02 (20), while an appointment insertion into an otherwise FIFO queue predicts A02, absent another due appointment. A02 is recorded starting at 08:32, favoring the appointment-plus-FIFO explanation. A dispatch record showing whether A03 had a reserved slot, together with the configured early/late tolerance and the 08:20 candidate list, would discriminate the policies directly.

2. **QC at 10:54: SPT versus priority/overdue recovery.** SPT predicts C06 (6 minutes) ahead of C05 (40); priority and an overdue-job recovery rule predict C05. C05 is selected, but its deadline had already passed, so this does not show an SLA could still be met. The decisive extra observable is the dispatch-time rank/reason, including displayed deadline slack and whether priority 1 or overdue status changes the scoring rule. At 11:34, SPT predicts C06 while priority or earliest deadline predicts C07, and C06 is selected; that later choice argues against a simple persistent priority or EDF rule.

3. **QD at 11:20–11:30: appointment reservation versus work-conserving skill routing.** Once D-X becomes available at 11:20, D07 is waiting and needs X; D08 and D09 are also eligible for X, although they can use D-M. A work-conserving FIFO/skill dispatcher could start eligible waiting work on X before 11:30. An appointment-reservation policy can hold X for D06 and start it exactly at 11:30. The observed idle interval followed by D06 is consistent with the reservation explanation. A resource reservation calendar or dispatch log showing whether X was deliberately held for D06 would separate these explanations.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from strongest direct support in this record to weaker alternatives:

1. **Resource availability and skill restrictions (strongest for QD).** D-M, D-F, and D-X have different skills and availability. D-F’s meeting and D-X’s start time create real eligibility constraints, and some jobs have only one qualified resource. This explains D05’s wait until D-F returns and D07’s use of X. It makes “skill-based routing” plausible, while leaving the ranking among jobs eligible for multiple resources unresolved.

2. **Resume/continuation preference and switching costs (strong for QB).** B01’s work is segmented and it resumes twice; one stop has an explicit urgent-arrival reason and one has a reassignment reason. Restart setup, lost work, handoff overhead, or a rule that suspended jobs retain their place could rationally make B01 resume ahead of a newly waiting job. None of those costs or rank rules is measured, so they explain observed choices only as hypotheses. A no-cost strict preemptive-priority model does not fit the resumption choices.

3. **Appointment reservation or a flexible appointment window (moderate for QA and QD).** A03 and D06 start at their exact stated appointment times; A06 starts five minutes after hers. These fit reservation or tolerance rules. B07 starts 26 minutes after its time, which challenges punctual appointment service unless the allowed window is unusually wide or its slot is subordinate to other work. Recording the window and whether resources are held for slots could shift QA and QD diagnoses toward appointment scheduling.

4. **A priority/overdue override or a changing policy in QC (plausible but weakly established).** C05 is priority 1 and already overdue when selected, which could elevate it above SPT. However, it had been passed over at 10:32, and C07’s earlier deadline and higher priority do not move it ahead of C06 or C01 later. A rule that changes policy when an SLA is endangered would need a stated trigger and state transitions; the events support at most an isolated override, not a verified policy switch.

5. **Arrival-scan delay and minute-level timestamps (material for close comparisons, not a general explanation).** Recorded scan times can lag queue entry by up to two minutes, changing close FIFO order and the apparent amount of waiting. Minute precision can also make a start and completion at the same minute appear simultaneous. These effects matter for close boundaries such as starts separated from arrivals by only a minute or two; they cannot plausibly reverse the much wider A03/A02 or QC order differences. Since a scan delay makes true entry earlier than the recorded time, it does not make a recorded-before-decision job disappear from the candidate set.

6. **Estimate provenance, noise, or strategic misreporting (alternative to taking SPT literally).** In this extract every estimate equals the sum of the observed productive segments, an unusually clean alignment. If dispatch really had those same estimates at arrival, the early QC choices are good SPT evidence. If estimates were overwritten, copied from actual durations, or not the values dispatch saw, the alignment is hindsight evidence and SPT becomes less identifiable. Estimate snapshots, timestamps, and revisions would decide whether the apparent SPT pattern is genuine.

7. **Unrecorded setup, restart, or handoff time (possible across queues).** Service segments record productive work but may omit preparation, teardown, or restart time. Such costs can change the true expected remaining time, especially in QB, and may explain why dispatch resumes B01 or preserves D-X. No such costs should be silently added to numerical SPT calculations without event data.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized collection plan:

1. **Decision snapshots for every dispatch and preemption.** Record dispatch-decision time, selected job, all candidates considered, each candidate’s eligibility or exclusion reason, and the policy version/score that ranked them. This would resolve the central ambiguity between a policy choice and an unobserved candidate-set constraint.

2. **Queue entry and scan timestamps as separate fields.** Capture physical/operational queue-entry time, arrival-scan time, eligibility time, and dispatch-visible time. Preserve sub-minute precision. This is needed to validate FIFO claims and determine whether a job was actually visible at the QB 09:48 decision.

3. **Resource state at each decision.** Record busy/idle, calendar availability, meetings/calibration, current job, skills valid at that time, setup state, reserved intervals, and why an otherwise qualified resource was not used. For QD, this must include whether D-X was held for D06 or protected for combined-skill jobs.

4. **Appointment contract fields.** Store appointment window open/close, exact target time if any, early/late tolerance, whether appointment jobs can be started early, reservation/hold status, and miss reason. This distinguishes the A03 and D06 alignments from coincidence and evaluates A06 and B07.

5. **Estimate and remaining-work history.** Store the exact estimate visible at arrival and at every dispatch decision, estimate source/model, revisions, expected remaining time after partial work, and actual productive versus setup/restart time. This is essential for valid SPT comparisons and B01 resume decisions.

6. **Preemption and resumption events.** Record stop decision time, reason code and free-text reason, initiating actor/event, whether work was suspended or reassigned out of the queue, progress saved or lost, restart cost, and resume rank. This resolves whether QB is truly preemptive priority or an urgent-arrival exception layered over resume-first behavior.

7. **Priority and deadline state as seen by dispatch.** Capture priority changes, deadline type (hard or soft), service-level target, displayed slack/lateness at each choice, and any escalation or policy-switch trigger. This is needed to tell whether C05 was chosen for priority, lateness recovery, or an SLA rule.

8. **Policy configuration and audit history.** Retain effective-dated rules, tie-breakers, appointment overrides, preemption thresholds, resource reservation rules, and manual dispatcher actions. A policy name alone is insufficient; the version active at each decision is necessary to test whether queues switch policies under load or risk.

# 7. Final synthesis

QB is the most identifiable queue for the presence of preemption: B01 is visibly interrupted for an urgent arrival and B02 immediately starts. The evidence supports a preemptive event, but not a simple, strict preemptive-priority ranking rule because B01 later resumes ahead of shorter or higher-priority work. QC has the clearest repeated ordinary ranking signal: several choices match SPT, with credible but incomplete exceptions.

QD is least identifiable as a dispatch discipline. It has multiple resources, overlapping eligibility, and explicit availability constraints, so a service start often reflects which resource can do the work or was held for another job rather than the queue’s global ranking rule.

The most dangerous inference would be to label QB as strict preemptive priority from the single urgent-arrival interruption and apply that rule to every dispatch. The observed resume choices and the 09:48 selection contradict that extrapolation. Across all queues, the best-supported diagnoses are hybrids: QA FIFO with appointment-aware insertion, QB preemption with resume/reassignment behavior, QC SPT with an override or aging effect, and QD skill-constrained routing with possible resource reservation.
