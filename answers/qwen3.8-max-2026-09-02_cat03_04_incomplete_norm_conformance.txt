## 1. Conformance semantics

I separate **normative behavior**, **data quality**, and **model incompleteness** as follows:

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation explicitly marked `forbidden` appears inside its stated scope. It is the strongest signal, but it still requires validation of object identity, timestamps, and scope. If the edge is demonstrably an artifact, it is reclassified as a data-quality error rather than business noncompliance.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: a mandatory relation is absent for an object/event that clearly falls inside the mandatory scope. Absence is not automatically noncompliance; scope, eventual semantics, alternative evidence, and logging completeness must be checked.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: an observed relation is absent from the norm but not forbidden. Because the norm is open-world outside forbidden and scoped mandatory relations, this is not a violation.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: a `typical` relation is not observed for some cases. This may warrant monitoring but is not noncompliance, because exceptions are legitimate.
- **Likely data-quality error**: the observed edge is implausible as business behavior and has a credible extraction/join/identifier explanation.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the normative intent is not faithfully represented by the mined graph, e.g. an “eventually happens” rule checked as directly-follows.
- **Insufficient evidence**: the summary cannot determine scope, temporal order, object identity, finality, or business justification.

The norm is **closed-world only for forbidden relations and mandatory relations within their stated scope**. For everything else it is **open-world**: absence from the normative edge set does not imply noncompliance. Therefore, unlisted edges such as `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` or `Package object_codeath Delivery` are not violations merely because they are unmodeled.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG summary can establish **frequently observed directly-follows relations between event types on object types**. It is useful for spotting expected process flow and obvious forbidden directly-follows patterns. It cannot establish eventual compliance, causal responsibility, business intent, or whether intermediate events were omitted.

Counts and scopes matter. N1 is mandatory but described as an eventual expectation approximated by directly-follows. The 98/100 count for `Create Order -> Validate Order` therefore does not prove that the two missing Orders violate the norm. They may have unrecorded review events, later validation, or nonstandard scope. Treating an **eventually expected** rule as a directly-follows rule creates false positives whenever intermediate activities are legitimate or missing from the log.

N3 is different: `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` is forbidden. The two observed Orders are the strongest OC-DFG concern, but even here we need case-level timestamps and cancellation finality. If cancellation was reversed, or if the dispatch belongs to another order due to identifier confusion, the finding becomes a data or modeling issue.

N4’s mandatory scope applies only to externally shipped Packages. The 198/200 direct `Pack -> Dispatch` count may miss intermediate handling events. Thus the two missing cases are mandatory-edge gaps, but they may be abstraction mismatches rather than process violations.

N5 is typical, so the 92/110 payment rate and the 14 invoices with no successor are not violations. They are monitoring candidates. N6 is unspecified, so `Reopen Order -> Close Order` is benign unless another policy governs reopening.

### OTG

The OTG can establish **object-type association patterns**: interaction, cobirth, codeath, and similar structural relations. It cannot establish event chronology, whether a relation was produced by a particular event type, or whether the relation is business-justified.

T1 and T2 are mandatory object interactions. The observed weights are high, but aggregate weights can conceal event-level gaps. The 1450 Order–Item interactions must be reconciled with the ET–OT observation of 1450 Add Item–Item links and 1448 Add Item–Order links. The OTG alone cannot say which two events lack Order links.

T6 is forbidden: one `Order object_cobirth Payment` edge is a high-severity structural signal. However, cobirth extraction can be wrong if a shared event identifier is reused. The finding is therefore forbidden behavior until source evidence shows it is a data-quality artifact.

Codeath is not governed. The seven `Package object_codeath Delivery` edges are not violations. They may be perfectly normal when both lifecycles end in the same confirmation event.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT model can establish **whether event types are linked to required object types**. It cannot establish whether the linked object instance is correct, whether the business exception is legitimate, or whether missing links reflect process failure or logging failure.

E2/E3 show a mismatch: 1450 Add Item–Item links but 1448 Add Item–Order links. This suggests two Add Item events may lack Order context. E6 shows 198/200 Dispatch–Delivery links, while the context says the two missing cases have carrier references. This is a mandatory gap at the object-graph abstraction, but business dispatch may still have occurred. E8 shows one Record Payment without Invoice. Because no event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash, this is a mandatory gap under the current norm, but finance context makes unapplied cash plausible.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction and suspicious at another. For example, the OTG shows 200 Package–Delivery interactions, suggesting T2 is satisfied, while ET–OT shows only 198 Dispatch–Delivery links, suggesting two dispatch events lack Delivery. Similarly, `Create Order -> Validate Order` may be missing as a directly-follows edge but satisfied eventually. This is why raw diagnostics need a separate semantic layer.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 eventual versus directly-follows semantics**
   - **Risk**: false positives for Orders validated after intermediate review events.
   - **Clarification**: `Create Order` must eventually be followed by `Validate Order` for standard Orders; intermediate events are allowed. Directly-follows is only a monitoring approximation.
   - **Status**: mandatory, with explicit eventual semantics.

2. **N4/E6 external shipment scope**
   - **Risk**: internal packages or carrier-referenced dispatches are incorrectly treated as mandatory violations.
   - **Clarification**: define “external shipment” using a shipment flag or Delivery object. If carrier reference is acceptable, specify equivalence; if not, require Delivery creation.
   - **Status**: mandatory for external shipment; explicitly unspecified for internal movement.

3. **E8/T3 unapplied cash exception**
   - **Risk**: legitimate unapplied cash is flagged as mandatory violation, or hidden unapplied cash escapes control.
   - **Clarification**: `Record Payment` may omit Invoice only when the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash. The typing itself should be mandatory.
   - **Status**: mandatory labeling; Invoice linkage typical or mandatory except typed unapplied cash.

4. **N3 cancellation finality**
   - **Risk**: dispatch after canceled-but-reopened orders is incorrectly treated as forbidden, or true post-cancellation dispatch is missed.
   - **Clarification**: define `Cancel Order` as final unless an explicit reversal/reopen event occurs. Dispatch after final cancellation is forbidden.
   - **Status**: forbidden for final cancellation; reversal behavior should be specified separately.

5. **Cobirth and codeath semantics**
   - **Risk**: T6 cobirth violations are overcalled due to identifier reuse; codeath edges are misread as violations because they are unregulated.
   - **Clarification**: keep Order–Payment cobirth forbidden, but define extraction rules for event identity. State that Package–Delivery codeath is not governed unless used for lifecycle closure controls.
   - **Status**: forbidden for Order–Payment cobirth; explicitly unspecified for Package–Delivery codeath.

6. **N6 reopening governance**
   - **Risk**: uncontrolled reopening may hide fraud or rework, but current norm says nothing.
   - **Clarification**: require `Reopen Order` to carry a reason code and subsequent closure. Treat reopening as legitimate but monitored.
   - **Status**: typical, with mandatory reason metadata if the organization wants stronger control.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

### Immediate containment

Prioritize the two highest-severity findings:

1. **N3: two Orders with `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`**
   - Identify the orders and packages.
   - Check whether cancellation was final.
   - If final, stop further dispatch, notify operations, and assess whether goods were wrongly released.

2. **T6: one Order–Payment cobirth edge**
   - Isolate the event.
   - Verify whether a payment was actually created with the order.
   - If real, block automatic posting and route to finance controls.

Also contain E10 by disabling the suspected sensor-to-invoice join until identifier reuse is fixed.

### Data repair

- Add or correct the missing `Add Item -> Order` links for the two suspected events.
- Create Delivery objects or canonical carrier mappings for the two Dispatch events lacking Delivery.
- Label the unapplied-cash payment explicitly if finance confirms that interpretation.
- Remove or rekey the erroneous `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` edge.
- If validation or pack events exist in source systems but were not logged, backfill with provenance.

### Process investigation

- For N3, investigate warehouse timing, cancellation propagation, and interface latency between order management and dispatch.
- For T6, investigate whether payment creation is embedded in order creation and whether that is allowed by financial control.
- For mandatory gaps N1, N4, E2, E6, and E8, distinguish process noncompliance from logging/abstraction problems before corrective action.
- Do not escalate rare but unregulated behavior such as Manual Compliance Review, RiskCase interaction, Reopen–Close, or codeath solely because it is rare.

### Norm revision

- Add eventual semantics to N1.
- Define external shipment scope for N4/E6.
- Add explicit unapplied-cash typing for E8/T3.
- Define cancellation finality for N3.
- Clarify cobirth/codeath extraction and governance.
- Decide whether reopening should remain unspecified or become typical/controlled.

### Monitoring

- Alert immediately on forbidden edges after scope validation.
- Monitor mandatory gaps with aging, not as binary violations.
- Track typical exceptions such as unpaid invoices, write-offs, and unapplied cash.
- Add data-quality dashboards for orphan events, reused identifiers, and missing object links.
