## 1. Conformance semantics

We distinguish deviation classes by reference to the explicit status of each normative relation, the open- versus closed-world stance of the norm, the stated scope of each rule, and the external context that accompanies the observed summary.

- **Forbidden behavior** is the positive observation of any relation marked forbidden (N3, T6, E10). Because the norm is closed-world for these edges, presence alone constitutes noncompliance; frequency is irrelevant. Severity is then calibrated by business impact and residual uncertainty about object identity or logging artefacts.

- **Missing mandatory behavior** is the absence of a relation marked mandatory inside its declared scope (N1 for newly created standard Orders, N4 for packages intended for external shipment, E1–E8 for the listed event–object pairs). Scope must be verified before a gap is declared noncompliant. Small absolute gaps (2/100, 2/200) are interpreted only after confirming that the objects fall inside the scope predicate.

- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is the presence of any relation that the norm neither forbids nor requires (Create Order → Manual Compliance Review, Order–RiskCase, Manual Compliance Review–Customer, Package–Delivery codeath, Reopen Order → Close Order, Validate Order → Issue Invoice, Order–Customer). Under the open-world default these edges are permitted; they become findings only if they collide with a forbidden or mandatory rule.

- **Typical-pattern absence** is the incomplete realisation of a relation marked typical (N2, N5, T3). The norm itself characterises the missing successors (invoice, review, fulfilment, credit notes, write-offs, open credit, unapplied cash) as legitimate; therefore their absence is not noncompliance.

- **Likely data-quality error** is an edge that formally matches a forbidden or missing-mandatory pattern yet is explained by the supplied context as an artefact (Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice produced by a join on a reused numeric identifier; possible missing Delivery objects when a carrier reference exists as an event attribute; possible missing Order links on two Add Item events). The DQ hypothesis is preferred when the context supplies a concrete mechanism; residual process risk is retained until the artefact is confirmed or repaired.

- **Abstraction mismatch** arises when the observed graph relation is a coarser or finer representation than the business rule it approximates. The clearest case is N1: the norm states an eventual-validation expectation yet the summary records a directly-follows count. An intermediate unrecorded review can satisfy the business rule while violating the DF abstraction. Similar mismatches appear when a carrier attribute stands in for a Delivery object or when multi-object events are projected onto binary edges.

- **Insufficient evidence** covers cases in which the aggregate counts alone cannot decide scope applicability, object identity, eventual versus direct succession, or the legitimacy of an unlabeled exception. The two missing Create → Validate links, the two missing Pack → Dispatch links, and the single unlabeled Record Payment without Invoice fall into this category until case-level traces or attributes are examined.

Open-world versus closed-world assumptions are decisive. The norm is closed solely for relations explicitly marked forbidden and for the mandatory columns inside their stated scopes. Everywhere else it is open: non-presence of a typical or unspecified edge is not a finding, and presence of an unlisted edge is not a violation. This prevents the false positives that would arise from treating an incomplete model as exhaustive. Statistical tolerance bands are deliberately absent; rarity therefore never automatically elevates an observation to a violation.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

**OC-DFG.**  
The family establishes directly-follows succession frequencies on a shared object. It therefore supplies strong evidence for the forbidden path N3 (Cancel → Dispatch = 2) and for the approximate coverage of mandatory DF edges (N1 98/100, N4 198/200). Typical edges (N2 83, N5 92) correctly display the variation the norm anticipates.  

It cannot establish true eventual reachability. N1 itself states that the summary approximates an eventual-validation rule with a DF expectation; consequently the two missing DF links may hide unrecorded intermediate reviews. OC-DFG also cannot speak to duration, concurrency or multi-object causality, nor can it confirm object-identity integrity across events.  

Counts must be read inside scope: 2/100 is material only if the two Orders are standard and never validated; 2/200 for N4 is material only if both packages were intended for external shipment. The danger of treating an “eventually expected” business rule as a strict DF rule is acute for N1. A process that always reaches Validate after an intermediate review is fully conforming at the business level yet appears non-compliant under the DF abstraction. Validate → Issue Invoice (72) and Validate → Close (83) can both be conforming under N2’s allowance for intermediates; the DF model simply omits those successors.

**OTG.**  
OTG establishes the existence and multiplicity of object-to-object relationships (interaction, cobirth, codeath) independently of event order. T1 (Order–Item 1450) and T2 (Package–Delivery 200) corroborate volume expectations; T6 (Order–Payment cobirth = 1) is a direct forbidden structural anomaly; the seven codeath edges are observed yet unregulated.  

It cannot establish the temporal or causal context of those relationships, nor whether an interaction is required for a particular event type (that is ET–OT territory). Weight 1 for a forbidden edge is already a finding; the norm supplies no frequency tolerance. Cross-model inconsistency appears when OTG reports 200 Package–Delivery interactions while ET–OT reports only 198 Dispatch–Delivery participations, indicating either missing objects or attribute-only representations.

A relation may look conforming in OTG (Package interacts with Delivery for every shipped package) while ET–OT reveals two Dispatch events that lack the Delivery object—conforming at the structural level, suspicious at the participation level.

**ET–OT.**  
ET–OT establishes which object types participate in which event types and the observed cardinalities. Mandatory participations E1–E7 are nearly complete; the residual gaps (E6 198/200, E8 92/93, E2 1448/1450) are small. E10 (Sensor–Invoice = 1) is a closed-world forbidden edge.  

It cannot decide whether a missing participation is a true process error, an untyped legitimate exception, or a join failure, nor can it interpret multi-object events that legitimately involve extra types (Manual Compliance Review linked to both RiskCase and Customer). The single unlabeled Record Payment without Invoice is formally missing under E8, yet finance staff state that unapplied cash is possible; the carrier attributes on the two Dispatch events likewise suggest that a Delivery object may be replaceable by an attribute under a richer norm.  

An event can satisfy its ET–OT mandatory constraints while failing an OTG interaction or an OC-DFG succession, and vice versa. The Sensor–Invoice edge is isolated to ET–OT and therefore most naturally explained as a data-quality artefact.

Taken together the three families are complementary: OC-DFG for behavioural order, OTG for structural object relations, ET–OT for event participation. Diagnostics must be interpreted jointly with scope, abstraction level and external context; they are never a complete legal or business judgement.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 “eventually” versus DF approximation.**  
   Operational risk: false positives on missing DF when legitimate intermediate events exist.  
   Clarification: state whether the mandatory is eventual (with optional latency bound) or strict DF; if eventual, require path or timestamp analysis.  
   Status after clarification: mandatory (eventual).

2. **Scope predicate for “packages intended for external shipment” (N4).**  
   Operational risk: mandatory applied to internal or cancelled packages, generating noise.  
   Clarification: define an explicit attribute or object-type filter.  
   Status: mandatory within the clarified scope.

3. **Unapplied-cash labelling for E8 / N5 / T3.**  
   Operational risk: legitimate finance practice flagged as missing mandatory, or genuine missing Invoice links hidden.  
   Clarification: require an explicit event subtype or attribute for unapplied cash.  
   Status: typical (when labelled) or mandatory only for applied payments.

4. **Delivery object versus carrier attribute (E6 / T2).**  
   Operational risk: carrier-ref-only dispatches appear non-compliant.  
   Clarification: declare whether a Delivery object is required or an attribute is sufficient.  
   Status: mandatory object, or typical attribute alternative.

5. **Codeath (and broader lifecycle synchronisation) beyond the single forbidden cobirth T6.**  
   Operational risk: seven observed codeath edges remain uninterpreted; future co-termination rules cannot be enforced.  
   Clarification: explicitly mark codeath as unspecified or elevate to typical for confirmation events.  
   Status: explicitly unspecified (or typical if co-termination is desired).

6. **Whether Manual Compliance Review satisfies or replaces Validate Order.**  
   Operational risk: the three review cases may be double-counted against the two N1 gaps, or validation may be silently skipped.  
   Clarification: state that Manual Compliance Review is an alternative path that still requires eventual formal validation, or that it substitutes.  
   Status: typical alternative path (still mandatory Validate eventually).

7. **Reopen lifecycle (N6 currently unspecified).**  
   Operational risk: uncontrolled reopens may bypass later mandatory controls.  
   Clarification: either leave unspecified or forbid selected post-reopen paths.  
   Status: keep explicitly unspecified unless risk policy demands otherwise.

8. **Absence of frequency / statistical tolerance bands.**  
   Operational risk: a 2 % gap is treated identically to a systemic failure.  
   Clarification: optional soft typical frequency bands for monitoring.  
   Status: typical (soft).

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**  
- Quarantine the two Orders that exhibit Cancel → Dispatch (N3) and any associated Packages; halt further logistics actions and preserve full audit trails.  
- Isolate the single event that produced the Order–Payment cobirth (T6) and freeze related financial postings until identity and typing are verified.  
- Quarantine the Invoice linked by the Sensor Heartbeat edge (E10) pending join-key validation.

**Data repair**  
- Correct the ETL join that produced the Sensor–Invoice edge; re-run uniqueness checks on the numeric identifiers.  
- For the two Dispatch events that lack Delivery objects, either instantiate the missing objects from the carrier attributes or document the attribute-only design.  
- Resolve the two Add Item events missing an Order link and the single unlabeled Record Payment; label unapplied cash explicitly if confirmed.

**Process investigation**  
- Reconstruct full traces (not DF summaries) for the two Orders missing Create → Validate to determine whether intermediate reviews exist or validation was truly skipped.  
- Confirm the business intent and destination attributes of the two Packages missing Pack → Dispatch; decide whether they fall inside N4 scope.  
- Examine the single cobirth event for concurrent-system or logging artefacts.

**Norm revision**  
- Rewrite N1 as an eventual rule with an optional latency bound and an explicit statement that DF is only an approximation.  
- Add a precise scope predicate for N4 and a labelling requirement for unapplied cash.  
- Decide the status of codeath, Manual-Compliance-as-Validate-substitute, and reopen paths; record the decisions as mandatory, forbidden, typical or explicitly unspecified.

**Monitoring**  
- Retain the three Manual Compliance Review cases, the seven codeath edges, the single reopen, the Write-Off / open-invoice set, and the Order–Customer links under light frequency monitoring only.  
- Do not escalate these benign or unregulated observations merely because they are rare.

The two highest-severity findings (N3 Cancel → Dispatch and T6 cobirth) receive immediate containment and process investigation; E10 is treated first as data repair given the strong DQ signal. The mandatory gaps N1 and N4 proceed to scoped process investigation rather than automatic escalation.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are the two Cancel → Dispatch instances (N3) as true forbidden behavioural deviations and the single Order–Payment cobirth (T6) as a forbidden structural anomaly that requires investigation; typical-pattern absences (N2, N5) and all unspecified relations (Reopen, RiskCase, codeath, Customer links) are not violations under the open-world reading of the norm.  

Conclusions that depend on the norm’s incompleteness are the interpretation of the two N1 gaps (eventual versus DF), the precise scope of the two N4 gaps, the legitimacy of the unlabeled Record Payment without Invoice, and the status of codeath.  

Conclusions that depend on suspected data-quality issues are the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge (almost certainly artifactual), the possible missing Delivery objects (carrier attributes present), and the two missing Order links on Add Item events.  

What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone is whether the two N1 gaps contain unobserved intermediate events, the true business intent of the two N4 packages, the root cause of the cobirth (logging versus process), and whether any forbidden cases share object-identity errors. Full OCEL event inspection, attribute analysis and domain confirmation are required.
