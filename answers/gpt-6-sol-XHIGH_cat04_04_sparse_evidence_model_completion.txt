# 1. Evidence Ledger

The ledger concerns *recorded completions*. In the tables and formulas below, `R`, `C`, `E`, `S`, `X`, `A`, `P`, and `Z` abbreviate Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, and Archive Case.

| Evidence ID | What it supports | What it does not prove | Reliability | Modeling implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1: 12 `RCESAPZ` | Common `C,E,S` completion order | That `C` caused `E`, or `E` caused `S` | High as an observed variant | Include this trace. |
| V2: 7 `RECSAPZ` | Reversed principal-check completion order | That the checks ran concurrently | High as an observed variant | Do not impose `C` before `E` on all cases. |
| V3: 4 `RCEAPZ` | Recorded cases without `S` | That supplier work was truly unnecessary | High for the record; ambiguous for the process | Permit an observed `S`-absent path. |
| V4: 3 `RECAPZ` | `S`-absent cases also occur with `E` first | That either review order causes supplier omission | High for the record | Preserve this combination. |
| V5: 2 `RCSEAPZ` | `S` can be recorded between the two check completions | That `S` started only after `C` completed | High for completion order | A model requiring both checks before `S` underfits. |
| V6: 1 `RCESXAPZ` | Recorded `X` after both checks and after `S` | That this is the only valid position for `X` | High for the record; low for generalization | Allow this placement; one case gives weak frequency evidence. |
| V7: 1 `RECXSAPZ` | Recorded `X` after both checks and before `S` | That `X` causes `S` | High for the record; low for generalization | Allow the other `X`/`S` order. |
| DFG | Both `C→E` (17) and `E→C` (11); also `S→E` (2) | Causality, overlap, or a concurrency relation | High as an aggregate of the 30 traces | Treat arcs as adjacency counts only. The remaining two `C`-before-`E` cases have `S` between them. |
| D1 | Intended `R` start and `Z` end | That every real case was logged completely | High domain confidence | Fix the visible start and end. |
| D2 | `A` is a prerequisite for `P` | That the log contains every approval event | High domain confidence | Enforce `A≺P`. |
| D3 | Both `C` and `E` precede `A` | A sequence between `C` and `E` | Medium to high | Require both checks before approval. |
| D4 | Required `S` precedes `A` | Which cases require `S` | Medium | Put any visible `S` before approval. |
| D5 | `S` may begin when either check supplies enough information | An observable completion dependency, or that `S` cannot begin from order data | Low to medium | Use an either-check completion proxy in the balanced model; test a relaxed proxy separately. |
| D6 | Optional, at-most-once `X` after both checks and before `A` | A dependency between `X` and `S` | Medium | Constrain `C,E≺X≺A`; leave `X` versus `S` unordered where both occur. |
| D7 | Check overlap versus sequencing is unresolved | Actual concurrency from opposite completion orders | Unknown | Represent the uncertainty explicitly. |
| S1 | Finance claims `C` finishes before `E` starts | That this rule was followed in the 11 `E`-first records | Contested stakeholder statement | Investigate policy versus practice and timestamp ties; do not force it into the balanced observed-event model. |
| S2 | Engineering reports usual parallel work | That all cases overlap, or that ties reflect real simultaneity | Plausible but unverified | Supports an unordered pair as a working hypothesis. |
| S3 | External components require `S` | Which cases were external | Plausible conditional policy | Add component type before enforcing conditional mandatory `S`. |
| S4 | Stocked components can skip `S` | That all seven recorded `S`-absent cases were stocked | Plausible conditional policy | A skip path is plausible. |
| S5 | Component type is absent; about 5–10% of `S` events may be missing | Which individual `S` events are absent, or that 5–10% of cases omit `S` | Credible data-quality warning; rate approximate | Keep true skips and recording loss separate in interpretation. |
| S6 | Compliance allows `X` on either side of `S`, after both checks | That all other placements are observed | Medium policy evidence | Allow both `X/S` orders without allowing early `X`. |
| S7 | Only completion times are recorded; equal times use ingestion order | That adjacency is operational precedence | High as a logging description | Obtain lifecycle times before making start-order or overlap claims. |

The two DFG directions for `C` and `E` show variation in *recorded adjacency*, not concurrency. Sequential policies that differ by case, overlapping work, and ingestion ordering of tied completions can all produce them. Conversely, S1's strict finish-before-start claim conflicts with a genuinely earlier `E` completion, but the available summary does not reveal whether some apparent reversals were ties or timestamp errors.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three models describe complete, visible **completion-event traces**, with each named activity appearing at most once per case. They encode constraints on completion order. A completion-order edge does not establish a start-time dependency or prove that activities overlapped. `R` begins every modeled complete case; `Z` ends it. Both checks precede `A`, and `A≺P≺Z`. Any visible `S` precedes `A`; any visible `X` follows both checks and precedes `A`.

The external/stocked attribute is unavailable. Seven of 30 cases lack a recorded `S`, but that is an observational fact, not an estimate of true stock use: some external-component cases may have lost an `S` event. An optional `S` branch is therefore a **control-flow abstraction of the observed log** combining legitimate skips and missing records. It cannot enforce S3 per component type or repair an omitted event. Optional `X` likewise means either one visible event or none; no model infers an unrecorded `X`. POWL partial orders admit linearized completion traces; they do not assert measured simultaneity. No frequency is encoded as a routing probability.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Memorize the seven observed complete variants, including the rare ones, and admit no other visible completion order. This is suitable as a strict reference for observed behavior or a high-precision anomaly screen, with the cost that valid future combinations will be rejected. In particular, the model refuses to infer concurrency from reversed order or optionality beyond the exact combinations seen.

**Compact expression.** With `;` denoting sequence and `⊕` exclusive choice,

`model_conservative = ⊕{RCESAPZ, RECSAPZ, RCEAPZ, RECAPZ, RCSEAPZ, RCESXAPZ, RECXSAPZ}`.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

names = {
    "R": "Register Order", "C": "Credit Check",
    "E": "Engineering Review", "S": "Supplier Confirmation",
    "X": "Expedite Review", "A": "Approve Configuration",
    "P": "Release Production", "Z": "Archive Case",
}
variants = ["RCESAPZ", "RECSAPZ", "RCEAPZ", "RECAPZ",
            "RCSEAPZ", "RCESXAPZ", "RECXSAPZ"]

def node(operator, children):
    parent = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = parent
    return parent

model_conservative = node(
    Operator.XOR,
    [node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [ProcessTree(label=names[c]) for c in trace])
     for trace in variants],
)
```

**Deliberately excluded behavior.** Every new ordering or new combination is excluded, including `E,S,C`, `X` without `S`, `X` after `S` when `E` finishes first, and `S` after only `E` but before `C`. Early approval, early expedite, repetition, and incomplete cases are also excluded.

**Known underfitting risks.** This likely rejects valid review interleavings, lawful optional expedites, and external-versus-stock paths absent from this small sample. The XOR repeats the same visible labels in seven branches; those are distinct tree nodes with identical labels, not repeated work in a trace. That inflates the net, can complicate alignments and explanations of a particular branch, and scales poorly as variants multiply. No sharing or general rule is learned from the repeated prefixes and suffixes.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Generalize only where the evidence and medium-confidence rules jointly support it. Leave `C` and `E` unordered, but require an `S` completion to follow at least one of them. Use optional `X` after both checks, independently of `S`. The model can explain all 30 cases while preserving the disputed supplier timing as a specific, auditable boundary.

**Compact expression.** `R ; (B₀ ⊕ B_C ⊕ B_E) ; A ; P ; Z`, where `PO` denotes all linear extensions of a strict partial order and `X?` denotes `τ⊕X`:

- `B₀ = PO(C,E,X?; C≺X?, E≺X?)` (no visible `S`);
- `B_C = PO(C,E,S,X?; C≺S, C≺X?, E≺X?)`;
- `B_E = PO(C,E,S,X?; E≺S, C≺X?, E≺X?)`.

An edge to `X?` also precedes its silent skip. The two `S` branches can generate some of the same visible traces; their XOR is an alternative explanation of the *prerequisite*, not two supplier events in one case.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)

names = {
    "R": "Register Order", "C": "Credit Check",
    "E": "Engineering Review", "S": "Supplier Confirmation",
    "X": "Expedite Review", "A": "Approve Configuration",
    "P": "Release Production", "Z": "Archive Case",
}

def po(nodes, edges):
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes.values()))
    for before, after in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(nodes[before], nodes[after])
    return result

def optional_expedite():
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(names["X"])],
    )

def check_block(supplier_after=None):
    nodes = {"C": Transition(names["C"]),
             "E": Transition(names["E"]), "X": optional_expedite()}
    edges = [("C", "X"), ("E", "X")]
    if supplier_after is not None:
        nodes["S"] = Transition(names["S"])
        edges.append((supplier_after, "S"))
    return po(nodes, edges)

middle = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[check_block(), check_block("C"), check_block("E")],
)
nodes = {"R": Transition(names["R"]), "M": middle,
         "A": Transition(names["A"]), "P": Transition(names["P"]),
         "Z": Transition(names["Z"])}
model_balanced = po(nodes, [("R", "M"), ("M", "A"),
                            ("A", "P"), ("P", "Z")])
```

**Sequence, concurrency, and partial-order decision.** I use sequence for `R` before the work block and `A≺P≺Z` because D1–D4 and all cases support those observable boundaries. I do **not** choose `C≺E` or `E≺C`: 19 cases finish `C` first and 11 finish `E` first, while D7 and S7 leave the execution relation unknown. An unordered POWL pair is a compact hypothesis allowing either completion order; it is not a claim that every instance was concurrent. I do not make `S` fully independent: V5 and D5 support completion after either `C` or `E`, expressed by the two XOR partial-order branches. I leave `S` and `X` unordered once their prerequisites are met, because V6, V7, and S6 allow both orders. D6 supplies the stricter `C,E≺X`; `X` remains optional and at most once.

**Deliberately excluded behavior.** A visible `S` before both check completions, `X` before either check completes, `A` before both checks or before a visible `S`/`X`, repeated events, and an incomplete case are excluded.

**Known underfitting risks.** If procurement can confirm from registration data alone, the `S` prerequisite is too strong. The XOR cannot resolve which check actually enabled `S` when both finished first. True start-time dependencies, component-type rules, and missing-event mechanisms are outside the observable model. The optional `X` without `S` path and several `S/X` interleavings may be overgeneralizations, though they follow the stated rules.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Preserve the strong approval and expedite safeguards, but allow the weaker D5 supplier-timing claim to be wrong or too narrow. After registration, `S` may be observed before, between, or after the two check completions, or be absent. This tests a plausible early-procurement hypothesis while keeping a clear compliance boundary.

**Compact expression.** `R ; PO(C,E,S?,X?; C≺X?, E≺X?) ; A ; P ; Z`, with `S?=τ⊕S` and `X?=τ⊕X`. The whole partial-order block completes before `A`.

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)

names = {
    "R": "Register Order", "C": "Credit Check",
    "E": "Engineering Review", "S": "Supplier Confirmation",
    "X": "Expedite Review", "A": "Approve Configuration",
    "P": "Release Production", "Z": "Archive Case",
}

def po(nodes, edges):
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes.values()))
    for before, after in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(nodes[before], nodes[after])
    return result

def optional(letter):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(names[letter])],
    )

work = {"C": Transition(names["C"]), "E": Transition(names["E"]),
        "S": optional("S"), "X": optional("X")}
work = po(work, [("C", "X"), ("E", "X")])
nodes = {"R": Transition(names["R"]), "M": work,
         "A": Transition(names["A"]), "P": Transition(names["P"]),
         "Z": Transition(names["Z"])}
model_permissive = po(nodes, [("R", "M"), ("M", "A"),
                              ("A", "P"), ("P", "Z")])
```

**Deliberately excluded behavior.** `X` before both checks complete; approval without either check; a visible `S` or `X` after approval; release before approval; multiple checks, supplier confirmations, or expedites; and truncated cases remain forbidden. The reasonable-generalization boundary is early `S` after `R`, perhaps using registration or procurement data. It does **not** relax D1–D4 or D6. Early `S` is a sensitivity assumption that conflicts with a strict reading of D5; it needs lifecycle and material-source evidence before operational adoption.

**Known underfitting risks.** This still omits legitimate rework loops and multiple supplier confirmations if those exist, and it cannot express external-component conditionality or recover missing events. Its larger risk is **overfitting the hypothesis space**: early `S` and many interleavings have no observed support, so the model may accept a real policy violation as ordinary behavior.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on 30 cases | All 30 traces fit if parsed as given | All 30 fit | All 30 fit |
| Expected precision risk | Lowest observed-language overacceptance; very high brittleness | Moderate: some unseen legal-looking combinations | Highest: early `S` and more unseen combinations |
| Generalization potential | Low | Moderate to high within D5 | High, including a disputed D5 relaxation |
| Structural simplicity | Simple idea, large duplicated XOR tree | More complex XOR of partial orders | Compact single partial order |
| Explainability | Exact variant membership is easy; branch duplication is cumbersome | Dependencies are explicit; supplier-branch overlap needs explanation | Very clear broad constraints; early `S` requires policy caveat |
| Robustness to missing `S` records | Only observed omission combinations fit | Omission can combine with valid check and expedite orders | Same, plus unconstrained early visible `S` |
| Handling unseen interleavings | Rejects all | Admits those with `S` after either check and lawful `X` | Admits those plus `S` before both checks |
| Compliance versus exploration | Strict observed-baseline screen, not a complete policy model | Best interim monitoring hypothesis | Sensitivity analysis and exploratory discovery |

These are qualitative expectations. Fitting the 30 cases cannot establish that any candidate describes the underlying process; the conservative model's apparent precision is partly memorization.

# 8. Validation Code

The following standalone script rebuilds all three models and the 30-case log. It uses PM4Py's comma-separated trace-string parser, then maps one-character event codes to full activity labels. It reports available metrics and enumerates accepting Petri-net runs up to eight visible events or a state cap. A complete bounded search supports the listed trace checks **within that bound**. If the cap is hit, negative membership checks are inconclusive. Bounded or sampled playout cannot prove language inclusion for an arbitrary infinite or cyclic model; alignment fitness and precision are likewise sample-based.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import deque
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

N = {"R": "Register Order", "C": "Credit Check",
     "E": "Engineering Review", "S": "Supplier Confirmation",
     "X": "Expedite Review", "A": "Approve Configuration",
     "P": "Release Production", "Z": "Archive Case"}
variants = ["RCESAPZ", "RECSAPZ", "RCEAPZ", "RECAPZ",
            "RCSEAPZ", "RCESXAPZ", "RECXSAPZ"]
counts = [12, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1]
encoded_cases = [",".join(v) for v, n in zip(variants, counts)
                 for _ in range(n)]
df = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(encoded_cases)
df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].map(N)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
assert len(log) == 30

def tree_node(operator, children):
    parent = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = parent
    return parent

model_conservative = tree_node(
    Operator.XOR,
    [tree_node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [ProcessTree(label=N[c]) for c in v])
     for v in variants],
)

def po(nodes, edges):
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes.values()))
    for before, after in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(nodes[before], nodes[after])
    return result

def optional(letter):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(N[letter])],
    )

def wrap(work):
    nodes = {"R": Transition(N["R"]), "M": work,
             "A": Transition(N["A"]), "P": Transition(N["P"]),
             "Z": Transition(N["Z"])}
    return po(nodes, [("R", "M"), ("M", "A"),
                      ("A", "P"), ("P", "Z")])

def balanced_branch(supplier_after=None):
    nodes = {"C": Transition(N["C"]), "E": Transition(N["E"]),
             "X": optional("X")}
    edges = [("C", "X"), ("E", "X")]
    if supplier_after is not None:
        nodes["S"] = Transition(N["S"])
        edges.append((supplier_after, "S"))
    return po(nodes, edges)

model_balanced = wrap(OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[balanced_branch(), balanced_branch("C"),
              balanced_branch("E")],
))
permissive_work = {"C": Transition(N["C"]),
                   "E": Transition(N["E"]),
                   "S": optional("S"), "X": optional("X")}
model_permissive = wrap(po(permissive_work,
                           [("C", "X"), ("E", "X")]))
models = {"conservative": model_conservative,
          "balanced": model_balanced,
          "permissive": model_permissive}

def labels(code):
    return tuple(N[c] for c in code)

def bounded_language(net, initial, final, max_visible=8, max_states=100000):
    queue = deque([(initial, ())])
    accepted = set()
    processed = 0
    while queue and processed < max_states:
        marking, trace = queue.popleft()
        processed += 1
        if marking == final:
            accepted.add(trace)
            continue
        for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            next_trace = trace + ((transition.label,)
                                  if transition.label is not None else ())
            if len(next_trace) <= max_visible:
                next_marking = semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)
                queue.append((next_marking, next_trace))
    return accepted, bool(queue), processed

suite = {
    "conservative": {
        "positive": variants,
        "negative": ["RESCAPZ", "RCEXAPZ"],
    },
    "balanced": {
        "positive": variants + ["RCEXAPZ", "RESCAPZ", "RCSEXAPZ"],
        "negative": ["RSCEAPZ", "RCXESAPZ"],
    },
    "permissive": {
        "positive": variants + ["RSCEAPZ", "RSECAPZ", "RSCEXAPZ"],
        "negative": ["RCXESAPZ", "RCESPAZ"],
    },
}

for name, model in models.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    soundness = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm,
                                     print_diagnostics=False)
    is_sound = soundness[0] if isinstance(soundness, tuple) else soundness
    print(name, "sound:", is_sound)
    metrics = {
        "fitness": lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "precision": lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "generalization": lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
        "simplicity": lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm),
    }
    for metric, compute in metrics.items():
        try:
            print(name, metric, compute())
        except Exception as exc:
            print(name, metric, "unavailable:", repr(exc))

    accepted, truncated, states = bounded_language(net, im, fm)
    print(name, "bounded accepted traces:", len(accepted),
          "states explored:", states, "state cap hit:", truncated)
    for code in suite[name]["positive"]:
        assert labels(code) in accepted, (name, "missing positive", code)
    if not truncated:
        for code in suite[name]["negative"]:
            assert labels(code) not in accepted, (name, "accepted negative", code)
        if name == "conservative":
            assert accepted == {labels(v) for v in variants}
    else:
        print(name, "negative and exact-language checks inconclusive")
```

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use **`model_balanced` for immediate, provisional process monitoring** and retain `model_permissive` for sensitivity analysis of early supplier work. Keep the conservative enumeration as the observed-variant baseline: a case rejected by it but accepted by the balanced model is a candidate new interleaving, not automatically a violation. For compliance decisions, apply the high-confidence approval and release rules directly and investigate missing `S` before judging a supplier skip.

Collect a stable case ID; event ID, source system, ingestion time, event time, and timestamp precision; lifecycle **start and completion** times for `C`, `E`, `S`, and `X`; and the order's external-versus-stocked component status, component lines, supplier requirement, and any waiver. Record whether `S` was required, whether it was actually performed, and integration delivery or failure status. Add the policy version and a reason code for expedite. Reconcile supplier-system records against the order log to estimate missingness by component type and time period, rather than applying the 5–10% estimate indiscriminately.

Prospectively sample cases with `C` and `E` starts and completions that establish actual overlap, `C`-then-`E` without overlap, and `E`-then-`C` without overlap. Those separate concurrent execution from case-dependent sequence. Observe a genuine `S` start and completion after only `E`, after only `C`, and, if one occurs, before either check completes; these discriminate the balanced and permissive prerequisites. Capture `X` both before and after `S`, plus `X` in a verified stocked case with no required `S`. For every `S`-absent case, verify stocked status or a missing supplier-system event. These cases discriminate true optionality from logging loss and determine whether the XOR paths should instead be conditional on material type.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

Confidence is **high** in the recorded seven variants, `R`/`Z` boundaries for complete cases, and `A≺P` as a domain rule; **medium to high** in requiring both checks before approval; **medium** in visible `S≺A` and `C,E≺X≺A` with at most one `X`; **low to medium** in the balanced model's supplier prerequisite; and **low** in any claim that the checks are actually concurrent rather than merely unordered in completions. Confidence that every recorded `S` absence is a legitimate skip is **low**. These are qualitative judgments, not posterior probabilities from 30 cases.

A material alternative is that a strict credit-before-engineering *start* policy exists and most reversed completion records are tied timestamps or faulty data. Verified lifecycle times supporting that explanation would favor a sequential policy model and targeted log repair over the balanced partial order. Another material alternative is that all seven `S`-absent cases are external-component cases with missing events; then the observed-log skip abstraction remains useful for conformance, but a business-process model should require supplier confirmation conditionally on external components and treat the seven records as data defects.
