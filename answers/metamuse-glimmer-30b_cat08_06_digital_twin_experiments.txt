## 1. Model scope and state variables

**Boundary**
Municipal building-permit process from portal submission to Issue permit / Reject application. Applicant-driven delays are inside boundary, third-party consultant delays are modelled as external service times. Emergency cases removed.

**Entities**
* Case: building permit application with attributes case_type, district, architect_id, application_value, requires_fire, requires_environment, requires_public_notice, submission timestamp.
* Activity instances with planned start, actual start, completion, resource, service duration, waiting duration.
* Resources: Clerks, Planners, Fire reviewers, Environmental reviewers, Inspectors, Legal officers, Scheduler, System. Each with FTE, calendar, skill set and case-type eligibility.
* Queues: per activity and per resource, with priority rules for aging cases.
* Cross-case links: architect_id sharing, district sharing for inspection batching, public-notice objection spill-over.

**State variables**
* Case state: current activity, next activity, timestamps, remaining reviews, rework counters, bundled inspection group id.
* Resource state: busy/idle, next available time per calendar, utilization.
* System state: arrival backlog per day/week, queue lengths per activity, number of cases in public-notice, number of cases waiting for applicant response, district travel backlog for inspectors.
* Demand state: non-stationary arrival intensity by month and weekday, architect burst state.

Calendars and non-stationarity are first class: public holidays, council meeting weeks, Monday portal spikes, seasonal 1.7x demand.

## 2. Parameter-estimation plan

PM4Py is used only for log/model handling, not for simulation.

* Log preparation: `pm4py.format_dataframe` with `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`. Convert to event log.
* Variants and routing: `pm4py.get_variants`, `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`. Branching probabilities per case_type from variant shares and from conditional probabilities in DFG conditioned on attributes.
* Performance: where start timestamps missing, compute inter-event durations as waiting+service proxy. Service time distributions are fitted from activities with both start and complete or from empirical medians/p90s provided. Waiting times are inferred as residual after subtracting median service.
* Arrivals: first event per case per day/week/month. Estimate non-homogeneous Poisson intensity with seasonal factor 1.7 for Mar-May and Sep-Nov, weekday factor 2.2 for Monday vs Wednesday, architect burst factor from top 20 architect_id submission clusters.
* Resource calendars: taken from HR reconstruction, not log. Validate utilization against performance DFG waiting.
* Rework: probability of rework after Planning review, Inspection, Legal check, Appeal from variant shares and from loop counts in variants. Rework delay distribution from loop duration.
* Dependencies: co-occurrence of cases with same architect_id within 7 days, district-level inspection travel times from D3/D5 longer tails. Public-notice objection proxy: excess duration over 14d minimum.

All estimates stored in a parameter table separate from PM4Py objects.

## 3. Assumptions

**arrivals**
Arrival process is renewal with time-varying rate. Architect bursts are modeled as a Hawkes-like cluster on top of baseline seasonal rate. No inter-arrival dependence beyond architect and weekday effects.

**service times**
Service times are independent of queue length. Distributions are heavy-tailed; lognormal or Weibull fitted to median/p90. Activities with missing start timestamps: service is sampled from fitted distribution, waiting is the modelled queue delay.

**routing**
Routing follows the process tree with XOR for optional reviews. Fire-safety review triggered by requires_fire or area threshold imputed from case_type. Environmental review triggered by requires_environment or district sensitivity with partial missing data, so probability is estimated per case_type/district.

**resources/calendars**
Resources are pooled with deterministic calendars. No absenteeism modelled beyond calendar. Legal officers unavailable during council meeting weeks except urgent cases. Fire reviewers work Tue-Thu only. Environmental reviewers 40% non-permit load modeled as reduced effective capacity.

**rework**
Rework is memoryless and independent of prior waiting. Rework reason is not observed, so rework probability is a case-type specific rate.

**applicant/third-party response**
Receive missing documents and applicant appeal delays are exogenous heavy-tailed, estimated from inter-event times between Request and Receive. Not influenced by municipal resources.

**cross-case dependencies**
Same architect cases can be batched for inspection with travel saving but first case waits for batch formation window. Neighborhood objection effect is unobserved; modelled as random extension of public-notice beyond 14d with probability estimated from duration tail.

**non-stationary demand**
Seasonal and weekday multipliers are fixed to historic averages. No trend extrapolation beyond 18 months.

**missing data**
Start timestamps missing  service/waiting split is partly inferred, uncertainty propagated via sensitivity. Objection events missing  public-notice duration used as proxy, treated as latent. Rare emergency decisions removed, so tail risk is underestimated.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

* Warm-up: discard first 3 months of simulated time to remove initial empty system bias. Warm-up validated by stabilization of resource utilization and queue lengths.
* Run length: 18 months simulated, matching log period, plus 2 months for tail completion.
* Replications: 30 independent replications per experiment with different random seeds. Common random numbers across policies for variance reduction.
* Uncertainty: empirical distributions with bootstrap resampling of service/waiting parameters. Heavy-tailed durations handled via lognormal/Weibull with fitted shape and 95% percentile checks. Report median and 90th percentile with 95% confidence intervals or Bayesian credible intervals from replication means.
* Sensitivity: Latin Hypercube over key uncertain parameters: arrival multiplier, missing-doc response tail, objection extension probability, inspector travel saving.

## 6. Validation strategy

* Face validation with process owners on process tree, calendars and routing rules.
* Event-log replay validation: simulate historical demand profile and compare simulated activity frequencies, variant shares, and performance DFG waiting/service distributions to log.
* Distributional validation: Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for case duration by case_type, and for waiting times per activity.
* Resource utilization validation: simulated utilization vs reconstructed utilization from log and HR calendars.
* Bottleneck validation: queue lengths and wait times rank ordering matches log.
* Out-of-sample validation: calibrate on first 12 months, validate on last 6 months of log for arrival intensity and duration tails.

## 7. Decision criteria

* Median and p90 case duration by case_type.
* SLA compliance: % cases  target days per case_type.
* Fairness: duration disparity across districts D3/D5 vs others, and across high-volume architects vs others.
* Resource utilization and overtime risk, especially Legal and Inspectors.
* Rework rate and rework-induced waiting.
* Backlog at end of horizon and maximum queue length.
* Robustness: coefficient of variation of p90 duration across stress scenarios S1-S3.

Intervention preferred if it improves median and p90 without increasing max disparity beyond 10% and keeps resource utilization <85% sustained.
