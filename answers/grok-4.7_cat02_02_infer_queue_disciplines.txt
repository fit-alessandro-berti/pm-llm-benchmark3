# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Reading conventions used below. Priority 1 is highest. A resource can run a job only if it covers every token in `skill_required` (`+` means both are required). `est_min` is the dispatch-time size input. Arrival timestamps are scan times and may lag true arrival by up to two minutes, so a job is treated as visible by its scan time and only uncertainly visible in the preceding two minutes. Segment breaks are service interruptions; a one- to two-minute gap with no recorded block is treated as setup or scan lag. Assumption A1: outside recorded exceptions, A1, B1, and C1 are continuously available, D-M is available only 11:00–12:30, D-X only 11:20–13:00, and D-F is available except for the meeting 12:00–12:20. Assumption A2: small uncoded gaps are not separate dispatch decisions.

Computed check, used throughout: for every one of the 32 jobs, the sum of segment lengths equals `est_min` exactly (B01 is 14+17+14=45). In this extract, SPT was implementable without estimation error. Disagreement with SPT is a behavioral fact, not an artifact of noisy `est_min`. Preemption of B01 adds no visible rework: the original estimate still accounts for all busy minutes.

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Eligible sets include jobs visible by scan time, still unfinished, and skill-compatible with the resource that is acting. Suspended B01 is listed while it is unfinished. Predictions are for a myopic rule evaluated at that instant, with FIFO as the tie-break inside priority. “Appointment” means: if some waiting job has appointment time at or before the decision, pick the earliest such job; otherwise the rule abstains. QC has deadlines instead of appointments, so earliest due date (EDD) and least slack, slack = deadline − now − estimate, are reported in that column.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

Supports FIFO as the base rule: A02 runs before A04 and A05 (08:32); A05 runs before A06, A07, and A08 (09:05); A07 runs before priority-1 A08 (09:32). The 09:05 release is the clean one, because priority and SPT each name a different job. A04 and A08 also fail to preempt, which supports non-preemption. Supports an appointment override: A03 starts at 08:20, ahead of FIFO, priority, and SPT together, and the note says the appointment arrived early. The exact minute and the triple disagreement make a coincidence unlikely.

Challenges a strong appointment policy: A06, appointment 09:15, starts at 09:20 because A05 was allowed to start at 09:05. That is a known consequence of myopic non-preemptive FIFO, not an unexplained violation of the hybrid stated above. It does challenge any reading in which appointments reserve future capacity. The same episode does not challenge FIFO. A04’s wait across calibration is explained by the recorded 08:52–08:58 block.

No QA case requires an undocumented priority or SPT exception. The only decision that requires something beyond FIFO is A03.

## QB

Supports the hybrid: B02’s preemption is labeled urgent and is exactly the priority-1 arrival; B03 does not preempt higher-priority B02; B01 resumes at 09:31 ahead of priority-2 B03; B05 at 10:00 jumps older B04 and suspended B01; B01 resumes at 10:14 ahead of priority-2 B06; B04 precedes B06 at 10:28; B06 precedes shorter, appointment-overdue B07 at 10:36. B07 is a direct challenge to appointment scheduling and to SPT, and the note already calls the appointment missed. It is not a challenge to FIFO.

The decision that the hybrid does not absorb is 09:48: B03 starts, B05 and B04 wait, and the reason is dispatcher reassignment. Scan lag cannot remove B03, B04, or B01 from that set. It can only move B05’s visibility by about two minutes, which is why this remains the contradictory episode. If the reassignment was committed before B05 was visible, the selection of B03 can be a manual FIFO pull of the longest-waiting unserved job, and B05’s failure to preempt B03 is then a separate fact: priority 1 preempted priority 3 and did not preempt priority 2. That limit is estimated from one pair of jobs, so it is a hypothesis, not a settled predicate. Nothing in the trace shows priority 2 ever reordering FIFO.

## QC

Supports an SPT-like opening: C02 at 10:10 and C03 at 10:18. Both conflict with FIFO, with priority, and with deadline urgency. C03 is especially strong because C05 is already unable to finish by 10:50 under the stated estimate. Supports non-preemption: C04 runs through the 10:50 deadline. Supports a late catch-up of an already-missed priority-1 deadline: C05 is the 10:54 choice, where priority, EDD, and least slack agree and FIFO and SPT disagree. The note says the deadline was already at risk when C05 was selected. C06 at 11:34 supports a fresh SPT comparison among the jobs then waiting and conflicts with FIFO, priority, and EDD.

Challenges dynamic SPT: C04 over C06 at 10:32, and C01 over C07 at 11:40. The first can be explained by an unrecorded hold on C06, or by a list frozen at 10:10; neither explanation is in the event data, so both stay hypotheses. The second is not a scan-lag or calendar effect. C07 is eligible, feasible, shorter, higher priority, and more urgent. Keeping C01 ahead of C07 matches FIFO and matches “finish the deferred opening-backlog job after one short insertion.” It conflicts with SPT, priority, and both deadline rules. It is the decision that blocks any claim of a stable QC discipline. C05’s long wait from 10:18 to 10:54 also blocks any claim that negative slack or priority 1 is sufficient to seize the server before the deadline clock has actually passed.

## QD

Supports skill eligibility as a real constraint: D02 and D05 run only on D-F; D07 runs only on D-X; D01, D04, D08, and D09 run on D-M. Supports per-resource FIFO: D04 before D08 on D-M, and D07 before D10 on D-X. Both conflict with priority and with SPT, and the size gaps are exact ex post (10 vs 8, and 28 vs 12). Supports a hard appointment hold: D-X idles from the start of its shift until 11:30 while D07, which would run through the appointment, is waiting, and D06 then starts on the appointment minute. D08’s failure to preempt D01 or D04 supports non-preemption on D-M. The meeting and the two shift windows explain D05’s 12:20 start once 11:36 has been reached, and they explain why D10 at 12:16 cannot move to D-F. The D10 note is accurate for 12:16. It does not explain 11:36–12:00.

Challenges a globally skill-aware policy: at 11:21 D-F uses itself on shareable D03 and thereby loses the only window in which exclusive D05 fits before the meeting (margin about nine minutes, robust to a one-minute clock). The later D-F idle, with D10’s 12-minute estimate fitting easily, challenges both “never idle if a compatible job fits” and dynamic reallocation. Sticky local queues explain that idle without an extra exception, so it challenges flexible skill-based routing and not sticky FIFO. It can also be explained by an unrecorded block on D-F or on D10. No such block is in the data. Scarce-skill-first is not rescued by the appointment, because D05 was the job that fit and D03 was the job that was chosen.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## QB at 09:48 — B03 was started, B05 was the priority-1 arrival

The two strongest accounts predict different next jobs.

- Automatic priority-1 preemption, the same mechanism as B02 at 09:16, predicts B05. B04 would win only under SPT. Suspended B01 would win only under immediate stack resume with no priority-1 jump.
- A manual reassignment to the longest-waiting job that has not yet started, which is what the reason text says, predicts B03. FIFO among jobs other than the interrupted one also predicts B03.

The trace follows B03, so the labeled override is the better account of this single release. It is still ambiguous because B05’s scan is two minutes earlier, inside the stated scan lag of the stop. The two accounts also diverge on the next priority-1 arrival during a priority-2 service. Under a gap rule, priority 1 does not preempt priority 2, so that arrival waits. Under “any priority-1 arrival preempts whatever is running,” it cuts in immediately.

The discriminating observable is a decision record with three fields that this log collapses into one reason phrase: decision time, the set of jobs visible at that time, and the target job of the reassignment. If B05 was not yet in the visible set when B03 was committed, the override is FIFO-like and the non-preemption of B03 is a separate policy fact. If B05 was visible and was not the target, the center overrode priority even for priority 1, and QB’s priority-1 clause is weaker than the other six releases suggest.

## QC at 10:32 — C04 was started, C06 was the shortest waiting job

- Dynamic SPT on every scanned, unfinished job predicts C06 (est 6, against C04 at 22, C01 at 36, and C05 at 40).
- A list frozen at 10:10, when the visible jobs were C01–C04, predicts C04. The same prediction is produced by an unrecorded hold that leaves C06 ineligible. Priority and EDD predict C05 instead, so they are not the close competitors for the job that was actually chosen.

The trace follows C04. That favors the frozen list or a hidden ineligibility constraint, and it conflicts with dynamic SPT under the assumption that C06 was eligible. Those two survivors make the same prediction here, so this episode cannot separate them. They separate at the next release where a short newcomer is unquestionably eligible and no deadline has passed. That later release is 11:34, where C06 does jump C01, which a fully frozen list would not do, and 11:40, where C07 does not jump C01, which dynamic SPT would do.

The discriminating observable is the eligibility snapshot at 10:32: queue-entry time, any hold or parts flag, and whether C06 was shown to the dispatcher. If it was shown, dynamic SPT is refuted at 10:32 and the later C06 start has to be a second regime. If it was not released until after C05, the opening discipline remains SPT through C04, and the instability begins at 11:40 rather than at 10:32.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from most to least plausible. Each is labeled as an exception or a data-quality alternative, not as a finding.

1. The 09:48 stop is a real manual override. Plausibility is high because the log already names “dispatcher reassignment,” distinct from B02’s “urgent arrival.” Treating that one release as outside the standing rule leaves QB coherent: FIFO, priority 1 jumps or preempts priority 3, interrupted work otherwise keeps its place, priority 2 does not reorder, and B07’s appointment is ignored. Treating it as the standing rule makes QB an undocumented hybrid with three different selection principles in one hour.

2. Arrival scans lag true arrival by up to two minutes. Plausibility is high because the prompt states the limit. It can move B02’s and B05’s visibility into the preemption minutes, and it can place C05 inside QC’s 10:10 set. In that set, SPT would still select C02, so the main QC opening result is stable; priority would be rejected even more clearly if C05 was already visible and was not chosen. The same lag makes the D-X gap too tight to prove that D08 or D04 could have been inserted before 11:30. It does not repair A03 over A04, A05 over A07, B04 over B06, C01 over C07, or D04 over D08. Those separations are many minutes wide.

3. A job can be unscanned-as-waiting for an operational reason that was not coded. Plausibility is moderate, and the prompt warns that a blank reason is not proof of a blank cause. The two places this would change the diagnosis are C06 at 10:32 and D-F’s idle 11:36–12:00 while D10 waits. A hold on C06 would let dynamic SPT survive through C04 and would move QC’s breakdown to the C05 insertion and the C01-before-C07 choice. A block on D10 or on D-F would make the idle non-diagnostic for sticky routing. It would not create positive evidence for priority or for appointments. This alternative is less plausible for D10 than for C06, because D10 then runs at once when D-X frees, for exactly its estimate, with no coded delay reason.

4. A03 is a one-off courtesy rather than a standing appointment override. Plausibility is moderate. It is a single release, and the rest of QA is FIFO. It is less attractive than a standing idle-time override because the choice simultaneously beats FIFO, priority, and SPT and lands on the appointment minute. If it is only a courtesy, QA simplifies to non-preemptive FIFO, and the center has no demonstrated appointment mechanism except QD. A06’s lateness would then be expected rather than a second regime.

5. Dispatchers distrusted `est_min`, so SPT comparisons are aimed at the wrong numbers. Plausibility is low in this extract. Every job’s busy time equals the arrival estimate, including B01 after two stops. There is no second estimate in the data that would reorder the violated comparisons: A04 is shorter than A02, A07 is shorter than A05, C06 is shorter than C04, D08 is shorter than D04, and D10 is shorter than D07. Distrust could explain why a dispatcher refused SPT. It cannot turn the observed choices into SPT under some other size ranking that these data contain.

A further boundary, not a sixth policy: C05 is only borderline-infeasible at its scan (39 minutes available versus an estimate of 40). By the next decision the miss is robust. QC’s refusal to take C05 at 10:18 should be read from that later, robust deficit, not from the arrival-minute arithmetic.

1. Decision journal for every release and every non-preemption. Fields: decision time, server, eligible set actually shown, job chosen, and whether the act was an override. This is the only record that separates QB’s 09:48 target from B05’s coincidental scan, and that separates “C06 was rejected” from “C06 was not yet in the set” at 10:32.

2. Visibility times, kept distinct. Physical arrival, scan time, queue-entry or release time, and any hold reason. The two-minute scan bound is already known to be active; what is missing is which of the borderline jobs had entered the choice set. That single distinction reclassifies C05 at 10:10, B05 at 09:48, and D10 during D-F’s idle.

3. Resource state as a timeline, not a shift note. States should include busy, idle, reserved-for-appointment, and blocked, with a reason. The states that the present table cannot settle are D-X from 11:20 to 11:30 (reserved versus unexplained idle) and D-F from 11:36 to 12:00 (available and refusing work, reserved for D05, or blocked). A1’s calibration and D-F’s meeting are already informative because they were recorded; the unrecorded gaps are the problem.

4. Appointment contract. Scheduled instant, earliest acceptable start, latest acceptable start, and a flag for whether a server is reserved before the instant. That separates QA’s idle-time override from QD’s ten-minute hold, and it shows whether B07 was ineligible before 10:30 or was eligible and skipped. A06’s five-minute lateness should be scored against the same tolerance, not against a blank field.

5. Preemption record. Trigger job, target job, estimated remaining minutes at the stop, reason code from a fixed list (urgent arrival, reassignment, calendar, unknown), and whether resume is mandatory. QB’s two stops already show that free-text reasons carry different meanings. Remaining time at 09:16, 09:31, and 09:48 is what a future SRPT or threshold rule has to be tested against.

6. Routing offer, for QD only. For each job, the resources that were considered qualified, the resource queue it was placed in, and whether a later jockey was allowed. Skill tags on people are not a substitute: D-X’s unused mechanical skill and D-F’s refusal of shareable D10 are otherwise permanent non-events.

7. Deadline state at the decision, computed and stored then. Clock overdue, projected slack under the estimate the dispatcher had, and SLA band. QC cannot be interpreted until “deadline passed” is distinguished from “estimate no longer fits.” C07 at 11:40 is the control case: still feasible, still skipped.

8. The processing-time number on the dispatch screen at the decision, plus estimated remaining for the job in service. Arrival `est_min` happened to match the eventual total for all 32 jobs, which is unusually strong, but it is still not direct evidence of what the dispatcher looked at when refusing the short job.

9. Policy identifier and version per queue and per decision. The QC sequence is what a mid-shift change would look like. Without a version stamp, a hybrid and a changed policy remain observationally equivalent.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue. On one server, with exact estimates, three releases pick the FIFO job when priority and SPT name someone else (A02 at 08:30, A05 at 09:05, A07 at 09:32), priority-1 arrivals do not preempt, and the single anti-FIFO release is the appointment job at the appointment minute. The remaining uncertainty is narrow: whether that appointment behavior is a standing idle-time override, and whether an overdue appointment would have beaten FIFO. A06 cannot answer the second question because FIFO selected A06 anyway.

QC is the least identifiable. The first two releases look like SPT and conflict with FIFO, priority, and deadline urgency. The next contested release looks like a frozen opening list or a hidden hold. The release after C05’s deadline looks like priority or EDD. The last contested release looks like FIFO. Deadlines and priority are equivalent on C05 and are both wrong on C03 and on C07. No honest single label survives the whole morning.

QD is identifiable as a constraint structure — unique skills are respected, D-X is held for D06, and each staffed resource then looks like FIFO — and is not identifiable as a dynamic skill optimizer. QB is identifiable only after the labeled 09:48 reassignment is set aside, and even then the exact priority-1 preemption predicate rests on two jobs.

The most dangerous inference from these observations alone is a center-wide preemptive or static priority policy. The episodes that invite it, B02’s urgent stop and C05’s eventual selection, are real and salient, and they do not survive the eligible-set tests: priority 1 waits behind FIFO work on QA and on D-M, priority 1 D10 waits behind D07, priority 2 never reorders QB, and C05 is skipped at 10:18 while already unable to meet its deadline. A priority policy read off the dramatic episodes would mis-state who waits and would overstate how urgency is handled. The near competitor for the same mistake is to treat QC’s first two releases as evidence that the center runs SPT, or to treat A03 and D06 as evidence that appointments are honored as a system. QB’s B07, started 26 minutes after a six-minute appointment, is enough to reject that last claim on its own.
