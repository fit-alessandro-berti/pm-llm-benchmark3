## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants**

- **H1 — Referential integrity and no invention.** Every relation endpoint must already exist in `OBJECTS`/`EVENTS`; a repair may only append rows to `ocel.relations` and the O2O table. A "repair" that needs `PK-9` or a second invoice object is not a repair, it is fabrication. This is what kills the "IT-B1 must have *some* package, therefore create one" move.
- **H2 — Directionality and set-uniqueness of O2O (domain rule 8).** `DEL-X carries PK-7` is a typed, directed tuple. Storing the reverse tuple, or both, creates a 2-cycle in the object graph and doubles every downstream traversal.
- **H3 — Temporal causality.** An event cannot act on an object before that object's first lifecycle step. Because Package, Delivery, Invoice and Payment have no explicit `Create` events here, this check is only binding on Order/Item chains (e.g. no `Dispatch Package` may touch an item not yet packed).

**Strong defaults (defeasible cardinality/type templates)**

- **S1 — Activity cardinality templates (rules 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7):** `Create Order`  1 Order; `Add Item`  1 Order + 1 Item; `Pack Item`  1 Item + 1 Package; `Dispatch Package`  1 Package + 1 Delivery; `Issue Invoice`  1 Invoice + 1 Order; `Record Payment`  1 Payment + normally 1 Invoice. The documented exceptions (scanner outage, bulk dispatch, multi-delivery acknowledgment, fees/taxes, unapplied cash) mean a deviation is *tolerable* but must be named, not silently absorbed.
- **S2 — Qualifier type signatures:** `contains` OrderItem, `packs` PackageItem, `carries` DeliveryPackage, `bills` InvoiceOrder, `settles` PaymentInvoice. Anything outside these signatures is rejected outright.
- **S3 — E2O/O2O agreement.** Objects co-occurring in a typed event normally have the matching typed O2O edge. Crucially, an E2O row and the O2O row *derived from the same hypothesis* are **one** evidence strand, not two (see §5).

**Weak heuristics**

- **W1 — Identifier/code similarity** (`PK8-2``PK-8`, `N-421``carrier_ref=N-421`, `order_hint`, `route_hint`). Strength rises sharply when the codebook is calibrated *inside the log* by 2 exemplars (here `PK7-1``PK-7` and `PK8-1``PK-8` give two).
- **W2 — Timestamp coincidence** (`ev09`/`ev10` both at 10:00 = one dispatch wave).
- **W3 — Amount decomposition** (230 = 130+100; 230100 = 130). Value-level only; rule 6 explicitly downgrades this to *evidence, not proof*.
- **W4 — Shared customer / route** (C17, NORTH).
- **W5 — Lifecycle-pressure heuristics** (an item sitting at the pack stage "wants" a dispatch link).

**Evidence against a repair**

- **A1 — Explicit uncertainty markers** (`N-421?`, `N-42*`): the operator did not trust the transcription; this weakens W1 for those rows specifically.
- **A2 — Cardinality creep:** a repair that hands `DEL-X` a second package, `INV-44` a second order or a second payment raises the many-to-many risk and each extra link needs its own evidence.
- **A3 — Completeness-only motivation:** "otherwise the object has no end" is counterevidence (rule 10 sanctions leaving things unresolved).
- **A4 — Non-discriminating attributes:** gross weights 3.1 vs 2.9 with no item weights cannot distinguish one-item from two-item packages; that is *absence* of evidence, not support.
- **A5 — Customer-level attributes cannot identify orders** when the customer has two open orders (`portal_customer=C17` cannot pick between ORD-A and ORD-B).

**Combination model.** I treat the repair as abductive scoring over declared strands. **Accept** = 2 quasi-independent strands (e.g. an in-log calibrated code *and* an orphaned event) with no hard conflict. **Provisional** = one strand, no conflict. **Unresolved** = strands conflict, or the only support is arithmetic/wildcard/customer-level. **Reject** = would violate H1–H3 or reverse/re-type a qualifier. Independence is enforced by naming strands explicitly, so that an inferred O2O edge is never counted as confirmation of the E2O edge that produced it.

If instead ev08PK-7 (Bundle B), that self-transition moves to PK-7 and PK-8 keeps a clean `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`. *Bundle B and C* add `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` on the Delivery type (DEL-X in B; both deliveries in C). *Bundle B* additionally gives ORD-B the shortcut `Create Order  Issue Invoice` on the Order type (no `Confirm Delivery`), a lifecycle fragment that should not be read as normal order-to-cash.

**OTG.** Worth stating plainly: at **object-type** granularity the OTG topology is identical in all three bundles — OrderItem (`contains`), PackageItem (`packs`), DeliveryPackage (`carries`), InvoiceOrder (`bills`), PaymentInvoice (`settles`) all already exist. What changes is **instance-level multiplicity**: A adds `PK-8IT-B1` and `DEL-YPK-8`; B replaces those with `PK-7IT-B1` and `DEL-XPK-8`, leaving `DEL-Y` as an isolated node; C is A plus nothing new. Any analysis that aggregates the OTG by type will therefore be blind to the repair — only instance-level OTG or E2O-driven views show it.

**ET–OT graph.** Unrepaired, `Pack ItemPackage` is 2/3 and `Dispatch PackageDelivery` is 1/2 and `Record PaymentInvoice` is 1/2. A/C raise Pack ItemPackage to 3/3 and Dispatch PackageDelivery to 2/2 while leaving Record PaymentInvoice at 1/2 (2/2 in B) and Confirm DeliveryDelivery at 0/1 (1/1 in B, 2/2 incidences in C).

**Flattenings.** On **Order** (E2O-based): ORD-A = ev01, ev03, ev04, ev11, ev12; ORD-B = ev02, ev05 (+ ev12 in B). Payment events never reach Order-level flattenings without O2O expansion, so "orders get paid" is invisible in A. If one expands over `bills`/`settles`, Bundle B drags ev13/ev14 into both orders, lengthening cases 57 and adding `Record Payment` to the Order alphabet. On **Package**: PK-8's case grows by ev08 in A/C. On **Delivery**: unrepaired DEL-Y yields an *empty case*; after A it is `[ev10]`; after B it stays empty (must be filtered before variant analysis); after C both cases contain ev11  the flattening duplicates one event into two cases and inflates `Confirm Delivery` to 2 occurrences from 1 event. On **Invoice**: A/C = `[Issue Invoice, Record Payment]`, B = `[Issue Invoice, Record Payment, Record Payment]`. On **Payment**: one-event cases; PAY-2 is a case with no invoice in A/C.

**Activity frequencies.** By events, `Record Payment` = 2 everywhere. By unique objects: 3 (PAY-1, PAY-2, INV-44) unlinked/A, 3 in B. By total object touches: 3 links in A/C vs 4 in B. `Confirm Delivery` = 1 event in all bundles, but 1 object (A), 2 objects (B) and 3 object links (C) — the three metrics diverge exactly where the repair is uncertain.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired and in A/C, ORD-A and ORD-B fall into different classes (full order-to-cash vs. truncated), yielding two clusters and a spurious "abandoned order" cluster. In Bundle B, once both orders share PK-7, DEL-X, INV-44 and both payments, O2O-expanded flattening gives them nearly identical event sets and they merge into one equivalent-execution class — the split-shipment behaviour disappears from the cluster view. Clustering should therefore be run per bundle and compared.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Linking ev08PK-8 and then citing the resulting `PK-8 packs IT-B1` as proof of the link** (single strand counted twice) silently asserts co-loading of two orders' items in one package, creating false partial-fulfilment and split-shipment behaviour on PK-8.
2. **Attaching ev11 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y "for completeness"** duplicates one `Confirm Delivery` event into two Delivery cases, inflating its frequency 12 and manufacturing a `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` arc on the Delivery OC-DFG that the portal data never asserted.
3. **Linking PAY-2INV-44 purely on 230100=130** destroys the unapplied-cash behaviour that rule 7 says is real business behaviour; INV-44 then looks 230/230 settled and any genuine open balance or misapplied payment becomes undiscoverable.
4. **Linking INV-44ORD-B purely on 130+100=230** merges two order lifecycles into one invoice, gives ORD-B a phantom invoice/payment tail in expanded flattenings, and hides a possible fee/freight/tax component on ORD-A.
5. **Treating `N-421?` and `N-420` as one reference, or DEL-X/DEL-Y as one object** merges two carrier bookings, orphan-erases DEL-Y, and turns `Dispatch Package` into a many-to-many (2 packages × 2 events) that will read as duplicated dispatch.
6. **Inventing PK-9 for IT-B1** creates an object with no creation, no dispatch and no delivery, corrupting every object-count metric and the ET–OT graph with a zero-degree type instance.
7. **Assigning ev11 to ORD-B on `portal_customer=C17`** uses a customer-level attribute as an object identifier and can close ORD-B's delivery stage before its item is even dispatched — a lifecycle inversion that order-level conformance checking will then blame on the process.
8. **Adding `PAY-1 settles ORD-A` or `ev13ORD-A`** drags payment events into Order flattenings, bypassing the Invoice stage and creating spurious `Issue Invoice`-skipping order variants and inflated order-level case lengths.
