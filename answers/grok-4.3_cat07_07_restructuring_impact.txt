# 1. Mechanism map
Restructuring actions map to observable effects as follows:

- Centralizing N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech into Technical Shared Service + routing all high-complexity and {legal,risk,security} cases produces nominal handover reduction (fewer local-specialist loops) but central queue formation (single queue replaces three embedded specialists).
- Shared approval queue and removal of one local supervisor increase specialist utilization (broader routing) while lengthening median waits on approval handovers.
- Routing difficult Quality Check and specialist reviews centrally raises rework when local knowledge is unavailable, because early informal advice disappears.
- Loss of local expertise appears as higher cross-location handovers and degraded local_expertise_flag values on complex cases.
- Role clarity declines when local caseworkers no longer own specialist steps, visible in increased reassignment_count and ambiguous org:resource values on shared queues.
- Resilience to absence falls because cross-team cover previously handled by local supervisors now routes through overloaded central queues.
- Fairness across teams/customer segments emerges when East (previously overloaded) gains capacity while North/South lose regional accommodation, measurable by segmenting outcome and SLA miss by location and customer_segment.

# 2. Scenario forecasts
Optimistic scenario: Handover pattern shows reduced local loops and stable central waits; queue/waiting-time pattern remains under 4 h median; cycle-time distribution tightens with lower 90th percentile; rework and quality improve via standardization; role clarity increases because queues make ownership explicit; fairness improves for East and high-complexity cases; early warning signs are minimal variance in service time by team.

Central scenario: Handover pattern shifts from local-embedded to shared-service edges with modest frequency increase on Technical Shared Service; queue/waiting-time pattern shows 10–20 % longer waits on specialist and approval edges; cycle-time distribution improves on median but widens on high-complexity tail; rework rate stays flat while quality variance rises by customer_segment; role clarity erodes as local caseworkers lose visibility into shared steps; fairness improves for East but declines for regional-language customers; early warning signs appear as rising reassignment_count within 30 days.

Adverse scenario: Handover pattern shows new central bottlenecks and increased cross-team transfers; queue/waiting-time pattern exceeds 10 h on Legal/Risk and Technical Shared Service; cycle-time distribution lengthens for high-complexity cases despite stable median; rework and quality worsen because local_expertise_flag cases require extra loops; role clarity collapses with duplicated approvals; fairness deteriorates for North/South and non-standard segments; early warning signs include sudden rise in rework_flag after shared-queue handovers and SLA misses concentrated in East and high-priority cases.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time
Stable or improved average cycle time can mask: (1) tail latency on high-complexity cases routed to overloaded central queues; (2) degradation of local knowledge that previously prevented unnecessary specialist referrals, visible only in variants filtered by local_expertise_flag; (3) fairness erosion when North/South regional customers experience more handovers while East improves; (4) loss of on-the-job learning as local staff no longer handle technical steps, measurable by declining activity-based resource similarity within teams; (5) reduced accountability when shared approval queues produce ambiguous org:resource ownership and higher rework_flag; (6) weakened exception handling and absence resilience once embedded specialists are removed, shown by increased cross-team handover waits and SLA misses during peak periods.

# 5. Pilot design
Pilot scope: North and East teams only, routing 30 % of high-complexity and specialty cases through Technical Shared Service while South remains unchanged. Comparison group: South team plus historical pre-pilot data from North/East. Pre/post observation window: 8 weeks baseline, 12 weeks pilot, segmented weekly. Segmentation: by team (North/East vs South), case_complexity, required_specialty, and customer_segment. Guardrail metrics: specialist utilization < 85 %, median wait on shared queues < 6 h, rework_flag increase < 2 pp. Staff feedback collection: weekly structured interviews with local caseworkers and specialists plus anonymous pulse on role clarity. Rollback or adjustment triggers: any guardrail breach for two consecutive weeks or > 15 % rise in SLA misses for any customer_segment.

# 6. Recommendation
Pilot first, limited to North and East with the guardrails above. Proceed to broader rollout only if leading indicators (handover network stability, reassignment_count, shared-queue waits) remain within thresholds for eight weeks and no fairness deterioration appears by customer_segment. Management must not infer success from early average cycle-time improvements, because these can improve while tail latency, local expertise loss, and role-clarity erosion accumulate; decisions require segmented variant, waiting-time, and role-discovery analyses before scaling.
