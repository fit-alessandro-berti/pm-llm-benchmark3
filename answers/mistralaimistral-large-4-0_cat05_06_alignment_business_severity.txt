## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is a business-risk judgment, not a technical alignment-cost ranking. I use a 1–5 scale that considers:

- **Control objective**: whether the activity protects money, fraud controls, customer communication, or regulatory/audit obligations.
- **Amount/exposure**: EUR exposure, especially above EUR 50,000 or involving beneficiary-bank changes.
- **Reversibility**: payments already released are much more severe than missing documentation or model noise.
- **Timing**: payment before approval, notification outside 24 hours, or bank change immediately before payment.
- **Customer/regulatory harm**: fraud loss, incorrect denial, missing claimant notification, or weak audit evidence.
- **Recurrence**: patterns across the month, not just one case.
- **Evidence reliability**: whether a missing log event means the activity probably did not happen, or merely that evidence is external, unlinked, or mutable.
- **Model scope**: whether the case is a payable claim, denied lead, withdrawn claim, or otherwise outside the designed process tree.

| Severity | Label | Typical criteria |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 | Critical | Irreversible payment or control failure; high exposure; fraud/regulatory/customer harm; policy violation with payment already made. |
| 4 | Major | Significant policy or control failure with material exposure, but potentially reversible or limited in scope. |
| 3 | Moderate | Evidence/audit-trail gaps, notification/recordkeeping issues, external evidence unlinked, or label mismatch on high-value cases; no direct payment-control failure. |
| 2 | Minor | Process-model mismatch, scope mismatch, harmless telemetry, duplicate events, or label noise with no business impact. |
| 1 | Informational | Fully conforming or technical noise only. |

**Confidence** is separate from severity. Confidence expresses how certain the interpretation is, given missing evidence, scope disputes, and possible alternative explanations. A case can be **severity 5 with medium confidence** if the log is missing but external evidence suggests the activity may have occurred. A case can be **severity 1 with high confidence** if the deviation is clearly harmless telemetry.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a technical edit-distance measure under a chosen cost function. It is not a risk score. The supplied cost function assigns cost 1 to every visible log/model move, with no business weighting. That design choice alone makes raw cost misleading.

- **CL-610 has raw cost 0 but is a severe fraud-risk failure.** The model fully synchronizes because the process tree permits Auto Approve. But P1 and P2 are violated: the beneficiary bank changed one hour before payment. The model has no data conditions, so a zero-cost alignment hides a critical policy failure.

- **CL-609 has raw cost 0 but violates P1.** EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk should not be auto-approved. The process tree permits the branch, so alignment cost is zero even though the business rule is broken.

- **CL-605 has raw cost 0 and is genuinely harmless.** The `T(tau,c=0)` silent move is a hidden routing transition. Zero cost here is informative because the silent move is technically fitting and no business deviation exists.

- **CL-601 has raw cost 3 but is harmless.** The cost comes from `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, and `Close Workbench` UI telemetry. Activity granularity matters: these are not business activities under P5.

- **CL-603 has raw cost 1 but is harmless.** The duplicate `Notify Claimant` is a retry artifact with the same message ID. Duplicated telemetry inflates cost without business harm.

- **CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4 but no payment risk.** They are denied and withdrawn claims. The high cost is a process-model scope mismatch, not an operational failure. Scope matters: the model was designed for payable claims.

- **CL-602 has raw cost 1 but is critical.** A missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a recent bank change is far more serious than the cost suggests. Missing evidence is not proof the activity did not occur, but here there is no fraud evidence at all, and the payment is already made.

- **CL-604 has raw cost 3 but is critical.** Payment before approval is irreversible. The alignment can “repair” the sequence cheaply by treating the early Pay Claim as log-only and inserting a model-only Pay Claim later, but the business failure remains severe.

- **CL-606 and CL-608 have raw cost 1 but are evidence-reliability issues.** In CL-606, an external adjuster report exists; in CL-608, a CRM secure-portal message exists. Missing core-log events do not prove the activities did not happen, but they do weaken auditability.

- **CL-611 has raw cost 2 but is low risk.** The cost comes from an event-label mismatch (`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`), not from a payment-control failure.

In short, raw cost answers: “How far is this trace from the model under this cost function?” It does not answer: “What is the business risk?” Business risk requires the policy overlay, amounts, reversibility, timing, recurrence, evidence reliability, and model scope.
