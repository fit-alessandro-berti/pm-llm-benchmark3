## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Shared skeleton
  Register ≺ Triage ≺ Identity Check
  Triage ≺ Document Check
  Identity Check ≺ Assessment_latest
  Document Check_latest ≺ Assessment_latest
  Assessment_latest ≺ Decision ≺ Notify

Originals required (K-219, K-337)
  Triage ≺ Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check
  Identity Check || Originals Receipt

Originals not required (K-104, K-411)
  Identity Check || Document Check

High-risk or high-value (K-104, K-337)
  Assessment_latest ≺ Compliance Review_final ≺ Decision
  resource(Assessment_latest) ≠ resource(Compliance Review_final)

K-104 added relations
  Triage ≺ Escalation Advice_prelim || Identity Check || Document Check
  Triage ≺ Compliance Screen_prelim || Identity Check || Document Check
  Compliance Screen_prelim does not satisfy Compliance Review_final
  Escalation Advice_prelim ≺ Assessment is advisory, not a prerequisite

K-219 added branch
  Document Check is performed by D2
  optional Document Check_1 ≺ Request More Info ≺ Document Check_2 ≺ Assessment_latest

K-337 deleted branch and replacement
  remove Assessment_2 ≺ Request More Info_2 ≺ Document Check_3 ≺ Assessment_3
  Assessment_2 ≺ Compliance Review_final ≺ Decision
  Decision ∈ {approve-with-conditions, reject} with the discrepancy recorded

K-411 release constraint
  Triage ≺ Document Check
  start(Document Check) is independent of complete(Identity Check)
```

```text
C1. Originals required ⇒ Originals Receipt precedes Document Check. Otherwise Identity Check and Document Check may overlap.
C2. High-risk or high-value ⇒ a final Compliance Review sits after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
C3. A preliminary compliance screen never discharges C2.
C4. Every Request More Info is followed by a new Document Check and a new Assessment before Decision. High-risk or high-value cases also need a new or explicitly reaffirmed final compliance review.
C5. Four-eyes: the final assessor is not the final compliance reviewer. C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer in this window.
C6. A resource has at most one service interval at a time. S1's advice may overlap other people's checks; S1 cannot overlap S1.
C7. Published queues are forecasts. Ready time plus residual queue is a band, not a start time.
C8. Service bands are uncertain: identity 1.5–3.5 h; D1 document 3–6 h standard / 5–9 h complex; D2 4–8 h standard / 8–14 h complex; assessment 2–5 h; complete-evidence compliance 3–6 h, with 1–3 h avoidable only if earlier work is reusable; external recollection 8–36 h.
C9. K-337 overrides the usual freedom to open another Request More Info. The residual discrepancy must be decided explicitly.
C10. K-411 concurrency counts only if the timestamps are live. Later batch entry is false concurrency.
C11. Reserving C1 removes capacity from other compliance cases; it does not create a second reviewer.
```

K-411 changes release timing only: the rules already allow concurrent checks, and the same resources and the same decision policy remain. K-219 changes routing from D1 to D2 and therefore timing; the decision policy is unchanged, though a clarification can open an extra rework branch. K-104 changes routing and timing of preparation: escalation and a non-final compliance screen move ahead of assessment, and a scarce slot is reserved. It does not relax the final compliance gate. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy. The shorter path exists because a permitted information loop is forbidden and a terminal approve-with-conditions or reject is required on evidence the factual assessor did not accept.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The long pole is D1's complex check, then A1's backlog, then C1's singleton backlog. From document completion Tue 05:40, A1's residual queue accounts for the wait until 15:10; the 4 h assessment is service, not queue. S1 then occupies 2 h on the critical path, and C1 does not start until Wed 13:10, a wait of about 16 h after S1, consistent with the still-long Tue/Wed compliance forecasts. The 4.5 h review matches a from-scratch reading. Identity was never on the critical path after mid-Monday.

Minimal intervention: Keep T1, I1, D1, and A1. Add one S1 advisory just after triage and one preliminary C1 screen on whatever exists then. Hold a provisional final slot for C1. Do not treat that screen as the review required after assessment, and do not switch the assessor.

Temporal propagation: Document ready time stays Tue 05:40, so the earliest assessment start stays near Tue 15:10. A pre-scoped sanctions note can trim assessment service by roughly 0–1 h, which moves the end from 19:10 toward 18:00–19:10. Removing S1 from the post-assessment path saves those 2 h only if C1 would otherwise have been idle; here C1 would not start Monday or Tuesday night. The reservation changes the Wednesday start. The Wed 08:00 forecast was 8 h, and the factual start at 13:10 shows the forecast was soft. A protected morning slot can move the start from 13:10 toward 08:30–11:30, a gain of about 2–5 h, unless the two urgent cases added by Mon 16:00 take it. Reusable notes can cut the 4.5 h service by about 1–2 h. Incomplete notes cut almost nothing and can add a 2–3 h preliminary touch earlier in the week. Decision and notify remain a short M1 tail immediately after final compliance, as in the factual close.

Process-feasibility check: Register and triage stay first. Identity and document still both finish before assessment and may overlap because originals are not required. Final compliance is after assessment and before decision. A1 assesses and C1 reviews, so four-eyes holds. S1 overlaps other pools' work and is a single interval for S1. The preliminary screen is extra control-flow, not a substitute gate.

Best/base/worst case: Best, the slot survives and notes are reused: final compliance Wed 08:30–11:00, notify around Wed 11:30, cycle near 51 h, about −7 to −8 h, with sharper conditions. Base, urgent cases take part of the slot and only about 1 h is reused: notify Wed mid-afternoon, about −2 to −5 h, same decision type. Worst, Monday's preliminary read is discarded and the preemption pushes the final start to Wed late or Thu morning: cycle about +8 to +14 h, and other urgent files move with it.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A C1 calendar showing real idle time on Monday afternoon would make the preliminary screen cheap and the savings larger. Evidence that S1 was fully booked until Tuesday evening, or that the triage packet contained no usable sanctions material, would push the result to the worst band. A same-day A1 cancellation would matter more than the compliance reservation, because A1's queue, not C1, holds the file through Tuesday.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Originals at Mon 17:30 met a D1 backlog that included the overnight complex file. D1 started only Tue 09:30 and needed 4 h. The subsequent wait to A2 at 20:00 is an assessment-queue or shift gap of about 6.5 h, and the wait from Tue 22:30 to Wed 08:30 is a manager-batch gap. Handwriting did not cause rework once D1 had the file.

Minimal intervention: At 17:30 place the case on D2 behind work already queued or started. Leave identity, A2, and M1's rules unchanged. Do not preempt D2.

Temporal propagation: At Mon 16:00 D2's forecast was 5 h and had been rising all day. By 17:30 the residual wait is about 4–6 h, so service starts Mon 21:00–23:30 rather than Tue 09:30. D2 on difficult handwriting is plausibly 5–10 h and, in a bad draw, toward the complex band of 8–14 h, against D1's observed 4 h. A clean 6–8 h service finishes around Tue 04:00–08:00 and enters A2's morning queue, whose Tue 08:00 forecast was 6 h. Assessment can then run late Tuesday morning or early afternoon instead of Tue 20:00. That is an 8–14 h earlier assessment start only if no clarification appears. If M1 still signs at the next morning batch, a Tuesday-afternoon finish waits until Wed 08:30 and the customer-visible gain collapses to about zero. If M1 can sign late in the day, as happened on the other Wednesday file, the decision moves to Tuesday evening and most of the gain survives. A clarification at the end of D2's first pass adds 8–36 h external time plus a second D2 service and a second A2 service, which pushes the close to Wednesday evening or Thursday.

Process-feasibility check: Originals precede document check. Identity may, and factually does, finish earlier. Assessment waits for both. No compliance gate applies. Any request for clarification restarts document check and assessment before decision. D2 is not double-booked if the new interval starts after D2's current work.

Best/base/worst case: Best, 5–6 h accurate D2 work and a Tuesday M1 signature: close Tue 15:00–18:00, about −14 to −18 h, same approval. Base, accurate but slower D2 work, with the signature held for Wed 08:30: about −1 to −6 h. Worst, a long D2 pass plus a slow external clarification: close Thu morning, about +18 to +32 h, or a fast path that misreads the handwriting and approves the wrong content.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A D2 handwriting sample with accuracy and speed close to D1 would make the no-rework best case the default. Proof that M1 had no Tuesday decision window would cap the gain near zero even when D2 is fast. A D2 queue already above about 10 h at 17:30 would remove the start-time advantage.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Internal work through Assessment 2 ends Fri 10:30. The second request then waits on the supplier across the weekend until Mon 09:00. Only after that do Doc3 (3.5 h), a wait into Tuesday, Assessment 3 (2.5 h), a C1 review Tue 15:30–19:30, and a Wednesday morning decision occur. Most of the 214 h cycle is external delay and the queues around the third pass, not the first-pass service.

Minimal intervention: Leave the path unchanged through Assessment 2. Delete Request More Info 2 and everything that existed only to process its answer. Require C1's final review of the Friday file and an explicit M1 decision. The discrepancy stays visible in the conditions or in a rejection.

Temporal propagation: The Friday 10:30 ready time for compliance replaces the Tuesday 10:30 ready time. Thursday's C1 forecast was already down to 5 h and clearing, so a Friday start about 0–4 h after 10:30 is plausible, with a 4–8 h review because the evidence is incomplete rather than the factual 4 h on a clean file. A Friday finish allows a Friday evening decision. If C1 or M1 will not close a high-risk discrepancy on Friday afternoon, the file waits through the weekend and closes Monday, still well before the factual Wednesday. The removed internal work is about 6–8 h of D1 and A1 service plus their queues; the removed calendar time is dominated by the supplier weekend. Moving the review forward loads Friday's C1 queue and frees the factual Tuesday afternoon slot for someone else.

Process-feasibility check: Originals still precede the document checks that remain. Both information loops already completed stay valid, including the rework after the first request. No third assessment is required because no new request is issued. Final compliance still sits between Assessment 2 and Decision, performed by C1 rather than A1. The policy change is the ban on another information request and the duty to record the gap. A silent approval would violate the intervention even though it would look fast.

Best/base/worst case: Best operational close, Fri 17:00–18:30, about −105 to −115 h, either a tightly conditioned approval or a reject. Base, Monday decision around 12:00–16:00, about −40 to −70 h, same altered decision class. Worst on risk, a conditioned approval that leaves the supplier discrepancy economically open, or a reject that an appeal reopens into a full confirmation loop, returning the horizon toward or beyond 214 h. Those quality branches can share the fast calendar path.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A supplier correction already sitting unread in the Friday file would make the ban almost costless and the factual extra loop unnecessary. A loss history showing this discrepancy class is immaterial would support conditioned approval. Evidence that C1 will not sign either outcome without the confirmation would mean the intervention is operationally infeasible under the certification constraint, not merely slower.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Triage ended Tue 09:35 with only an identity task. I2's start at 11:30 matches a roughly 2 h identity queue. The document task did not exist until identity completed at 13:20, and D2 started at 15:00, so about 1.5 h of that wait was queue and about 3.5 h was the lock. Document service was 4.5 h. The file then waited from Tue 19:30 into an assessment queue that was still forecast at 5 h on Wed 08:00, started 09:30, and waited again until M1 at 14:00.

Minimal intervention: Create the D2 task at 09:35 and record both intervals as they happen. Do not change pools, skip either check, or move assessment priority.

Temporal propagation: Identity's start and service stay about Tue 11:30–13:20 because its queue is unchanged. D2's Tue 08:00 forecast was 4 h, so a task released at 09:35 starts around 12:00–14:30, overlapping the end of identity rather than starting at 15:00. With a 4–6 h service, document completion moves from 19:30 toward 16:30–19:00. The case can join A2 only at the later of the two completions, so the critical-path gain is about 1.5–3.5 h of readiness. The Wed 08:00 assessment forecast of 5 h already contains this file under the factual arrival. An arrival 2 h earlier gets ahead of some overnight arrivals and can pull A2's start from 09:30 toward 08:15–09:00, after which the 2.5 h service ends mid-morning. The factual 2 h gap before M1 may still hold the signature near 14:00. End-to-end gain is therefore the part of the readiness gain that survives both queues, often about 1 h and only in a light queue about half a shift. A failed workaround that creates a second official task after the lock can erase the gain and add coordination delay.

Process-feasibility check: No originals, so concurrency is permitted. Both checks still precede assessment, and decision still precedes notify. No compliance or four-eyes issue arises. Real overlap requires the two service intervals to be true, not reconstructed after the fact. I2 and D2 remain on one task each.

Best/base/worst case: Best, D2 starts near 12:00, finishes near 16:30, and a short A2 queue plus an earlier M1 slot close the file Wed late morning: about −3 to −4.5 h, same approval. Base, document finishes Tue early evening and assessment still runs Wed 08:30–11:30, with M1 near 13:00–14:00: about −0.5 to −2 h. Worst, the workaround double-creates the task or the overlap is only a backfilled stamp: cycle about 0 to +4 h, with an audit trail that overstates concurrency.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Access or workstation logs with no overlapping D2 activity would turn the apparent gain into false concurrency. An assessment queue under about 1 h at Tue 17:00 would make almost all of the readiness gain visible in cycle time. A D2 queue above about 8 h at 09:35 would leave the document start near the factual 15:00.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early S1 packet and C1 reservation | Extra 1.5–3 h of C1 and 1.5–2.5 h of S1 up front; final C1 falls by 0–3 h only if notes are reused | This file's wait for final C1, and only while the slot is honored | C1 for everyone else; S1 if advisories pile up | Urgent compliance files already in the Mon 16:00 snapshot | Positive for one well-documented file; negative once reservations exceed slack | Singleton capacity: the second reserved hour costs more than the first because the backlog was already 16–20 h |

| K-411 release both checks at triage | Almost no change in total service; both pools receive the demand earlier | Identity-to-document lock time; sometimes this file's assessment wait | Assessment, if many files become ready together; D2 for arrivals just behind the new task | The next assessment cases and D2 cases released between 09:35 and 13:20 | Small positive per case while assessment has slack; flat or negative when A2 is the bottleneck | Gains add while assessment slack remains, then pile up as queueing delay |

Applying each policy to 100 similar cases in one week changes effects that look helpful on one trace. Compliance reservation reverses first. A single K-104 slot can be taken from a C1 backlog that was already 16–20 hours on Monday and Tuesday, and a good early packet can trim the final review by about 1–3 hours. One hundred reservations require the only certified reviewer to hold capacity and to reread incomplete files, while S1 cannot absorb one hundred preliminary advisories. Second touches raise C1 hours, and urgent cases already in the Monday 16:00 snapshot slip. The case-level gain disappears once reserved hours exceed slack, which is scarce before midweek.

D1/D2 substitution is a threshold. K-219 is attractive only while D1 is far behind D2. One hundred handwritten files add D2's slower service on every case and a clarification loop on the cases D2 cannot read, so D2 and repeat assessment grow while D1 drifts toward idle. The policy stops helping when D2's wait, extra service, and expected rework exceed D1's wait. That margin flipped between Monday 08:00 and Monday 16:00. Complex corporate files that reward D1 are then stuck behind the slower generalist queue.

Cutting the second information request removes mostly external weekend time. Internal relief is about one document check and one assessment per file, which helps D1 and A1. Final C1 review remains mandatory and becomes longer because the discrepancy is unresolved. One hundred harder reviews, pulled forward toward Friday, can refill compliance while upstream queues shrink. Appeals and loss events form a delayed queue that a cycle-time report misses.

Parallel release of identity and document work adds almost no service time. One K-411 file is ready one to three hours sooner and usually still waits behind four to seven hours of assessment. One hundred files arrive as a tighter wave, so verification waits fall and assessment, then the manager's decision batch, become binding. Batch-entered timestamps would show concurrency the resources did not perform and would send later staffing toward a fictional bottleneck.

Run together, the D2 routing rule and parallel release both advance completions into assessment, while early escalation and suppressed rework both concentrate harder files on C1. C1, S1, and assessment are the seats that saturate. Weekend supplier delay and handwriting rework look like local slack until volume multiplies them.

## 6. Pilot designs

Intervention 1 is K-411. Intervention 2 is K-219.

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is parallel release of identity and document tasks when originals are unnecessary, as in K-411. The rules already allow that overlap. A legacy lock created the document task only after identity finished. Opening both at triage, with live timestamps, advances readiness by about one to three hours. Assessment still held several hours of work, so cycle time likely falls by less than four hours and sometimes barely moves. The policy is reversible and auditable from timestamps.

The dangerous intervention is the ban on a second information request in K-337. It can cut about 40 to 115 hours by dropping an external weekend, a third document check, and a third assessment, with a close on Friday or Monday instead of the following Wednesday. The new rule forces approve-with-conditions or reject, records the gap, and still requires a final compliance review by someone other than A1. Added loss, a weaker file, or an appeal can wipe out the clock-time gain.

The single-case result that travels worst is K-104's early packet plus a reserved C1 slot. One free S1 hour and one protected window can move a single review forward when notes are reused. At one hundred similar files that plan consumes the only compliance reviewer and the only escalation adviser. C1 was already heavily queued on Monday and Tuesday, and two urgent cases arrived by Monday 16:00. Reservations then delay other work, and thin packets create second reads.

The highest-value added data are residual queues at task release, C1 and S1 calendars split into idle and preemptable time, proof the final reviewer used the early notes, D2 handwriting clarification and misread rates, M1's decision-batch timing, and a live-versus-backfilled timestamp flag. For suppressed rework, loss, override, and appeal events past one week matter more than cycle time.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Structural. A preliminary C1 screen and an S1 note do not discharge the final compliance gate. K-104 and K-337 still need a post-assessment review by C1, not by A1.
2. Temporal. The morning queue figures can miss by several hours before a case joins, so every predicted start is a band around residual work.
3. Resource. During the Monday–Tuesday backlog, a reserved C1 hour is taken from other compliance cases. S1 has no second adviser behind the empty queue snapshot.
4. Behavioral. Assessors and reviewers reuse an early packet only when it already contains the evidence they later rely on. A thin sanctions or supplier packet is reread.
5. Temporal. M1's observed gaps, overnight before K-219's signature and from 12:00 to 14:00 on K-411, can absorb an earlier technical finish.
6. Quality. Difficult handwriting is more likely to need clarification, or to be misread, with D2 than with D1, who finished K-219 in four clean hours.
7. Quality. The inconsistency left after K-337's second document check can affect loss and defensibility. That is why Assessment 2 asked for another confirmation.
8. Interference. Joining a queue does not delay cases already ahead. It delays later arrivals, and at a volume of one hundred those later arrivals include the policy's own future cases.
