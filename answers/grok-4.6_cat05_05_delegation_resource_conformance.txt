## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 (drafter/approver separation).** Let `R(c, a)` be the alias-normalized human who performed activity `a` on case `c`. If both `Draft_Change_Request` and `Approve_Change` exist, require `R(c, Draft) ≠ R(c, Approve)`. A service account is not a human and cannot occupy either side unless attributed first. Emergency status does not waive RR1.

**RR2 (implementer/verifier separation).** If both `Implement_Change` and `Verify_Implementation` exist, require `R(c, Implement) ≠ R(c, Verify)` after alias normalization and after any reliable service-account attribution. RR2 is a same-human prohibition. It does not, by its text, require the verifier to hold the Independent Verifier title. Eva’s move to Quality Engineer on 2026-08-12 therefore does not itself fail RR2, but it weakens the **control objective** of independent verification and must be reported separately from the technical check.

**RR3 (high-risk approval authority).** If `risk = HIGH`, `Approve_Change` is conforming only when the actor at the event timestamp is (i) Asha in her standing QM role, or (ii) a QM delegate whose delegation interval covers the timestamp, whose scope covers the change (equipment class / document class), who does not subdelegate when prohibited, and whose principal-absence (or formal unavailability) condition is met **when the instrument requires it**. Standing QE, Site Director, Independent Verifier, Shift Supervisor, or “current HR master says Quality Engineering” is not QM authority.

**RR4 (emergency substitution).** A HIGH-risk emergency may proceed on IC or valid IC-delegate approval, but only as a justified exception: the emergency flag and a credible safety rationale must exist, and a QM or valid QM delegate must complete `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days of the emergency implementation (or, if implementation is missing, the emergency approval). Failure of the retrospective clock, scope, or identity means the exception is not perfected and the case is not QM-conforming.

**Business-day count.** The site calendar has no holiday in the window. 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 are weekend days, so business days are Monday–Friday. The event date is day 0 if it is a business day. The deadline is 23:59:59 UTC on the second following business day. Example: implementation on Tuesday 2026-08-11 → deadline Thursday 2026-08-13 23:59:59 UTC. Saturday/Sunday do not count. CHG-505’s Friday 2026-08-14 review is therefore late. CHG-510’s Wednesday 2026-08-12 review is on time.

**RR5 (identity evidence).** `svc_qms` cannot satisfy a human-role rule by itself. Weak session evidence (shared terminal, badge-in-room only, no re-authentication) is insufficient to map the act. Strong multi-factor attribution (badge + FIDO on a named verifier station) may be used, with residual shared-station risk disclosed. Aliases of one human are merged before SoD.

**RR6 (suspicion versus proof).** Repeated Clara–Ben pairs, reciprocal LOW-risk approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators. They are not proof of collusion. Network metrics inform investigation; they do not classify a case as collusion.

**Principal acting while on leave.** Leave is an availability fact, not a revocation of standing role. Asha remains QM on leave. If she personally performs `Approve_Change`, RR3 can be satisfied by the principal. That same presence is relevant to any delegation that is conditioned on her absence: a documented personal session shows she was not uniformly unavailable that morning, but a short VPN window does not by itself end the leave record for the rest of the day. Delegate acts still require the instrument’s absence condition to be evaluated at **their** timestamps.

**Later database entry.** A QMS row created 2026-08-12 09:15 cannot, by itself, prove that D-QM-01 was electronically effective before that instant. It also cannot create authority retroactively. A signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09 18:00 is independent, earlier evidence of the same instrument. For approvals before 2026-08-12 09:15 (notably CHG-501), validity rests on the PDF’s authenticity, not on the migrated row. For approvals after that stamp, PDF and database agree. Delayed master-data recording is a control weakness, not automatic invalidity of a genuine prior wet-ink/PDF delegation.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Repeated collaboration is not classified as collusion.

**Clara drafts / Ben approves (18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes in 60 days; this log continues that pair on CHG-501, 503, 506).**
- Benign: they share a production cell; Ben is the named D-QM-01 delegate during Asha’s leave; no pair-rotation policy exists, so concentration is the default routing.
- Adverse: rubber-stamp cell capture; Ben approves Clara’s HIGH changes as a standing practice, including when he is not actually present (CHG-506).
- Discriminating evidence: independent re-performance of a sample of Ben’s approvals; whether cycle time from draft to approve collapses to a near-constant (suggesting non-review); whether CHG-506’s office-IP session is Clara’s or a third person’s workstation; cell roster versus actual reviewer notes.

**Reciprocal LOW-risk training pattern (Ben drafts, Clara often approves; CHG-508 instead shows Ben self-approving via alias).**
- Benign: Ben authors training content in his QE capacity; Clara is a process-knowledge reviewer; CHG-508 is an alias/SoD defect, not a favor trade.
- Adverse: you-approve-mine reciprocity that later contaminates HIGH-risk production approvals.
- Discriminating evidence: whether Clara’s training approvals contain substantive comments; whether HIGH-risk production approvals ever flow Clara → Ben with documented technical challenge; HR/finance review for any benefit (none is known).

**Shared workstation WS-DM-04 (CHG-503 verify).**
- Benign: a named verifier used a shared shop-floor terminal after Diego opened the room; service-account design forced a generic login.
- Adverse: Diego verified his own implementation, defeating RR2.
- Discriminating evidence: interactive user, MFA, CCTV, or even a typed verifier identity in the QMS payload. Badge-in-room without re-auth cannot discriminate.

**Asha leave versus VPN (CHG-504).**
- Benign: QM personally handled one urgent HIGH change, consistent with her statement and the 25-minute VPN window.
- Adverse: credential sharing so that work continued under the principal’s ID while she was away; would also undermine every D-QM-01 “Asha is absent” conclusion if she was in fact directing the queue.
- Discriminating evidence: FIDO/device binding, calling-tree corroboration, whether any other `u_as` events exist outside 09:15–09:40, and Asha’s detailed recollection of CHG-504 content.

**Ben sick leave versus account use (CHG-506 at 13:00 on 08-13; also CHG-505 review at 16:00 on 08-14).**
- Benign: Ben attended site despite the certificate (against policy, but still Ben); or a documented break-glass by a second person that was logged under the wrong ID.
- Adverse: delegate-credential use while the delegate was certified unavailable — compromise, sharing, or an attempt to keep D-QM-01 “green.”
- Discriminating evidence: physical access logs for Ben those afternoons, compare office IPs across CHG-506 and CHG-505, MFA logs, and a forensic image of the office workstation.

**Prohibited subdelegation (D-IC-04, CHG-510) adjacent to a valid IC window (D-IC-03, CHG-505).**
- Benign: messy emergency paperwork; Farid tried to cover a second cooling change while occupied and used email.
- Adverse: drafter granting herself emergency approval, then seeking a paper trail.
- Discriminating evidence: timestamps of the D-IC-04 email versus the 14:20 approval, whether Farid was occupied on CI-44, and whether anyone in QA rejected the subdelegation in real time.

**Network interpretation.** A dense Clara→Ben handover edge and a high working-together weight are **expected** under cell assignment plus D-QM-01. They raise monitoring priority. They do not, without the identity breaks above, prove an agreement to evade SoD. The actionable network signal is the **intersection** of that dense edge with (i) alias self-approval, (ii) sick-leave account use, and (iii) a void IC subdelegation — not the edge alone.

def second_business_deadline(ts):
    """Day-0 = event calendar date if Mon-Fri; add two weekdays; end of that day UTC."""
    d = ts.date()
    if d.weekday() >= 5:
        while d.weekday() >= 5:
            d += timedelta(days=1)
        added = 0
    else:
        added = 0
    while added < 2:
        d += timedelta(days=1)
        if d.weekday() < 5:
            added += 1
    return datetime(d.year, d.month, d.day, 23, 59, 59, tzinfo=UTC)

def sod_violations(frame, resource_col, a1, a2):
    """Cases that contain both activities and fail four eyes on resource_col."""
    tmp = frame.copy()
    tmp["org:resource"] = tmp[resource_col]
    have = (
        tmp.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"]
        .agg(lambda s: set(a1 if x == a1 else a2 if x == a2 else None for x in s) >= {a1, a2})
    )
    cases_both = set(have[have].index)
    conforming = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(tmp, a1, a2)
    conf_cases = set(conforming["case:concept:name"].unique()) if len(conforming) else set()
    return sorted(cases_both - conf_cases)

## 6. Control Improvements

**Delegation creation.** Forbid email-only or informal onward passes. Require a controlled form that states principal, delegate, interval to the minute (UTC), in-scope change classes, named incidents if any, absence rule, and an explicit “no subdelegation” flag that the workflow engine can read. Reject D-IC-04-style instruments automatically when the parent forbids subdelegation.

**Effective-time recording.** The legally effective timestamp is the signed instrument, not the migration insert. Write the effective interval into the QMS **before** first use, with the PDF hash bound to the row. If a migration creates the row later, store both `instrument_time` and `system_record_time` and block use until the row exists, or accept the PDF under a documented break-glass that QA reviews. Never allow a later insert to back-date electronic proof without an immutable signature.

**Principal availability.** Feed leave, sick notes, on-call, and VPN/badge into the same decision service that evaluates D-QM-01/02. If the principal has an active interactive session, prompt whether the absence condition still holds. If the delegate is on certified sick leave, disable the delegate credential for QM acts. A principal who chooses to work from leave should use a break-glass that records intent, so CHG-504 is not ambiguous.

**Alias management.** One human, one SoD key. Map `b.koch` and `u_bk` in the IdP and in PM analytics before maker-checker. CHG-508 should be impossible at submit time.

**Individual authentication.** Retire shared `svc_qms` for Verify_Implementation, or force step-up (FIDO) that writes the human identity into the event. Shared shop-floor terminals must re-authenticate per action. Badge-in-room is not attribution.

**Emergency review.** Compute the RR4 clock in the workflow (second business day, site calendar). Escalate if Retrospective_QM_Review is missing or late. Scope emergency IC approval to a registered incident ID (CI-44) stored on the case, so cooling_system alone is not guessed later. Require a short safety-rationale field before IC approval can close.

**Pair rotation.** Clara–Ben concentration is an organizational default, not proof of collusion. Impose sampled second-QM review or periodic approver rotation on HIGH production-equipment changes, especially during a standing delegation.

**Network monitoring.** Use handover and working-together graphs as **watchlists**: alert when a dense pair coincides with leave/sick conflicts, service-account verifies, or alias collisions. Do not auto-open a misconduct case from the edge weight (RR6).

**Evidential retention.** Keep signed delegations, IdP alias maps, MFA/FIDO assertions, VPN session records, badge logs, and workstation assignment for the retention period of the change record. Preserve CCTV or justify why a shared terminal used for GxP verification has none. Retain Ben’s sick certificate against the CHG-506 / CHG-505 Friday sessions.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Apparent nonconformance is a **mixture**, not a single failure mode. The clearest **genuine authority failures** are CHG-507 (Eva approved HIGH production work with no QM role or delegation), CHG-510 (void subdelegation plus drafter self-approval on an emergency), CHG-502 (plain RR1), CHG-508 (RR1 after alias merge), and CHG-505’s unperfected RR4 path (late, out-of-scope, sick-leave retrospective). **Identity and data quality** explain a large share of the residual: `svc_qms` on CHG-503 cannot support a human SoD pass; Ben’s account on CHG-506 (and possibly the Friday CHG-505 review) is not reliably Ben; Asha’s CHG-504 is authorized in role but not closed in identity; D-QM-01’s migrated row is later than its first use; Eva’s current QE title must not be read as QM. **Organizational-pattern risk** is real but unproven as collusion: Clara–Ben dominance is consistent with cell design and a written delegation, and RR6 forbids treating that density as misconduct. Uncertainty is highest wherever a credential, a service account, or an absence condition stands between the named role and the person at the keyboard.

Three highest-priority investigations: (1) **who used Ben’s credential** after 2026-08-13 12:00 (CHG-506, and the 2026-08-14 CHG-505 review) — possible compromise of a live QM-delegate account; (2) **CHG-510 / D-IC-04** — prohibited IC subdelegation and self-approval of a HIGH emergency; (3) **CHG-503 `svc_qms` verification** — possible implementer self-verify on a shared terminal, which would be an RR2 breach the raw log cannot see.
