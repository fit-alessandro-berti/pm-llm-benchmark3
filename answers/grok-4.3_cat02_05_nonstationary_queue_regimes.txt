# 1. Data audit and estimands
Queueing quantities are defined over the 12-hour opening window 07:00–19:00. Arrivals and completed are integer counts of case entries and case exits during that window. Median_service_min and p90_service_min are observed active handling times (excluding queue wait). Mean_wip is the arithmetic average of 15-minute snapshot occupancy counts. Staff_fte is average concurrent full-time-equivalent capacity. Complex_pct and peak_08_10_pct are percentages of daily arrivals. Outage_min records total minutes of system downtime.

Five threats to naïve inference are: (1) 07:00 backlog is never supplied, so daily completed can include overnight carry-over; (2) completed on day t need not equal arrivals on day t; (3) median and p90 service times cannot be substituted for mean flow time; (4) complex_pct rise may be a true mix shift, a coding change, or both; (5) successive daily rows are serially dependent through backlog and staffing decisions.

# 3. Alternative segmentation
An alternative segmentation collapses all pre-04-20 weekdays into one “ordinary” regime and isolates only the 04-15 outage plus the post-04-29 staffing change. This story treats the complex_pct jump as a permanent new steady state rather than a distinct transitional regime. Its strength is simplicity and direct focus on the logged intervention; its weakness is that it masks the capacity-pressure increase visible between 04-20 and 04-28 and therefore understates the value of the later staffing increase.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics
Pooled weekday arrival intensity (8.7 h¹) hides the post-mix rise from 8.5 to 9.5 h¹. Pooled median service (30 min) conceals the jump from 25 min to 35 min after 04-20. Pooled staff_fte (5.3) masks the deliberate increase from 6.0 to 7.0 after 04-29 while simultaneously averaging weekends (2.5–3.0) with weekdays. The 04-15 outage inflates pooled WIP; excluding it lowers mean WIP by 8 cases on weekdays.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity
The 2026-04-07 vector shows mild intra-day variation (CV0.6). The 2026-04-21 vector exhibits strong burstiness (peak 34 arrivals in one hour, Fano factor >3). Weekend vectors are flatter. Daily arrival series displays a clear level shift after 04-20 and a second shift in completions after 04-29. Serial dependence is visible in the recovery period (04-16–04-19) where completions exceed arrivals. Four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model because they omit weekend-to-weekday transitions, the exact outage timing, and any overnight backlog state.

# 7. Operational implications
Staffing should be raised on post-mix weekdays to 7.0–7.5 FTE and a short post-outage recovery buffer of 0.5 FTE should be scheduled. Peak smoothing via appointment slots in the 08–10 window is indicated by the 40–50 % peak share after 04-20. A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages—uniform 6.0 FTE staffing—appears risky once segmented: it under-staffs the high-complexity regime and over-staffs weekends. Data collection should record 07:00 backlog and exact outage start/stop timestamps.
