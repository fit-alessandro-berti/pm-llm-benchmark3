## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts

- **M0** is the current normative POWL model: `Register_Supplier  (Sanctions_Check  Quality_Review)  XOR(Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier)  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`.
- **CS-9** is controlled, formally approved, and effective from 2025-01-01. It requires:
  - Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation.
  - Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier.
  - Requester and manual approver must be different humans.
  - Rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen, but CS-9 does not state whether those leads are governed by M0.
- **PD-3** is a steering-committee pilot decision, not incorporated into CS-9 or M0, effective 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It allows Auto_Approve for LOW-risk DE suppliers already in an approved corporate group, with sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation, and Quality_Review up to two business days after provisional activation. The Compliance observer wrote “pilot may proceed as a monitored exception” but later said she had not approved weakening CS-9.
- **FD-7** is a flood emergency directive, effective 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59. It allows provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual procurement approval, but requires Sanctions_Check before activation, spend capped at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, daily transaction monitoring, and Quality_Review within two business days. It expired automatically.
- **N4**: From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to a CRM service. About 70% of notifications are mirrored into the onboarding log. CRM evidence can be linked by `notification_id`, but older links are mutable text fields.
- **N5**: Operations commonly uses one or more Request_More_Info cycles before a decision. Compliance agrees this is desirable, but M0 has no loop and no explicit activity for it. Operations wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch; no permanent policy amendment exists.
- The observed cases and aggregate evidence are as stated in the prompt.

### Disputed or unproved propositions

- Whether PD-3 is a valid authorization to deviate from CS-9/M0 despite not being incorporated into them.
- Whether the Compliance observer’s “monitored exception” statement constitutes approval of a CS-9 weakening.
- Whether FD-7’s factual emergency after 2026-07-12 can justify continued provisional activation absent a written extension.
- Whether rejected leads such as SUP-709 are inside M0 scope.
- Whether reactivations such as SUP-710 are governed by CS-9/M0.
- Whether QA_Clearance in SUP-713 is semantically equivalent to Quality_Review and whether post-hoc mapping approval suffices.
- Whether a CRM notification with a mutable text link satisfies Notify_Requester when the onboarding log lacks the event.
- Whether Request_More_Info is a deviation, a desirable extension, or a model defect.
- Whether resource and conflict-of-interest rules are part of M0 or external to it.
- Whether the duplicate Register_Supplier in SUP-711 is a data-quality defect or a real duplicate registration.
- Whether the high M0 deviation rate reflects noncompliance, model obsolescence, or both.

### Burden of proof

- **For declaring noncompliance**, the accuser must prove:  
  (i) a rule was in force at event time;  
  (ii) the case is in scope;  
  (iii) a required event or condition was absent, or a prohibited event occurred;  
  (iv) no valid overlay or authority applies; and  
  (v) the evidence reliably links the events to the case.  
  Where evidence is incomplete, the correct classification is not “noncompliant” but “indeterminate” or “model/scope defect.”

- **For accepting an exception**, the proponent must prove:  
  written authority, temporal validity, scope, eligibility conditions, and required safeguards. Informal belief, past practice, or operational need is insufficient.

- **For claiming a model defect**, the proponent must show that M0 cannot express behavior that is either required by policy or normatively desirable and not prohibited.

- **For claiming a data-quality defect**, the proponent must show that the underlying event probably occurred but linkage, timestamp, label, or duplication issues prevent reliable conformance assessment.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### 1. Formal authority is hierarchical

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. M0 is the normative control-flow model. PD-3 is a steering-committee decision that was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. FD-7 is a time-limited emergency directive that expired automatically. Exceptions must be written, scoped, time-bound, and authorized. The Compliance observer’s “monitored exception” statement is ambiguous and was later disclaimed as approval to weaken CS-9. Therefore, deviations under PD-3 or FD-7 are not automatically compliant; only cases that satisfy every condition of a valid overlay are justified.

### 2. The core controls remain valid

CS-9’s core requirements are not obsolete: sanctions before activation, quality before activation on the standard route, manual approval before activation, and requester/approver separation. These controls protect against high-risk suppliers, sanctions exposure, and self-approval. A model that cannot express them is a model defect; it does not repeal the underlying policy.

### 3. High-risk cases are noncompliant

- **SUP-703**: HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, ineligible for PD-3, used Auto_Approve. First purchase was EUR 180,000. This violates CS-9 and PD-3.
- **SUP-708**: HIGH-risk supplier activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check. No emergency or pilot applied. No transactions before sanctions clearance does not cure the activation-before-sanctions violation.
- **SUP-712**: Trace fits M0, but requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through two aliases. This violates CS-9’s segregation-of-duties rule.
- **SUP-715**: Trace fits M0, but quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line, and no conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded.

These are not model defects. They are control failures.

### 4. Expiry and normalization of exceptions

- **SUP-705**: Same pattern as FD-7 but on 2026-07-13, after FD-7 expired. No written extension exists. A continuing factual emergency does not create formal authority.
- **SUP-714**: LOW-risk eligible supplier on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 expired. Operations believed permanent approval was imminent, but no permanent amendment existed.
- Aggregate evidence: 312 cases used Auto_Approve; 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 have missing eligibility attributes. Six post-pilot cases used Auto_Approve in the first week of August.

This is normalization of exceptions. Informal practice cannot amend policy.

### 5. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy

SUP-707’s Request_More_Info loop is desirable, and Compliance agrees. But M0 has no loop. That is a model defect, not a violation. However, the desirability of Request_More_Info does not authorize other deviations. It supports model revision, not unilateral departure from CS-9.

### 6. Data quality does not excuse missing controls

Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after the CRM migration. SUP-706 has no Notify_Requester in the onboarding log; the CRM message exists but the case link is a mutable text field entered two days later. That is weak evidence. SUP-702 has a stable `notification_id`, so its log gap is a data-quality issue, not a process failure. But the prosecution should not treat every missing log event as proof of noncompliance; it should treat it as indeterminate unless the CRM link is reliable.

### 7. M0’s high deviation rate partly reflects obsolescence, but the remedy is formal amendment

The prosecution acknowledges that M0 is incomplete: it lacks Request_More_Info, auto-approval, emergency routes, reactivation scope, rejected-lead scope, label mapping, resource rules, and data constraints. Those defects explain some deviations. But they do not excuse high-risk violations, post-expiry deviations, or segregation-of-duties failures. The correct response to an obsolete model is formal revision, not silent noncompliance.

### 8. Aggregate evidence supports material noncompliance

Internal Audit identified SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe. The aggregate data show 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases, 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes, and 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases. These are not mere trace mismatches; they are unauthorized control bypasses.

## 3. Defense Brief

### 1. M0 is incomplete

M0 cannot express several behaviors that are either operationally necessary or normatively desirable:

- Request_More_Info loops (SUP-707).
- Low-risk auto-approval (SUP-702, SUP-714).
- Emergency provisional activation (SUP-704, SUP-705).
- Reactivation without full re-registration (SUP-710).
- Rejected leads with abbreviated pre-screen (SUP-709).
- Label equivalence such as QA_Clearance (SUP-713).
- Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (SUP-712, SUP-715).

Therefore, raw trace deviation from M0 is not equivalent to noncompliance.

### 2. Concurrency is a modeling choice, not a business rule

M0 models Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review as concurrent. CS-9 only requires both before activation. Sequential execution of sanctions and quality may be acceptable if both occur before activation. However, SUP-708 activates before sanctions, so it remains a violation. But the concurrency itself is not a business requirement; it is a POWL modeling artifact.

### 3. Pilot and emergency overlays are real operational authorities

PD-3 and FD-7 were formal steering-committee and emergency decisions. They show that M0 is obsolete for certain supplier classes. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are substantively compliant under those overlays. The Compliance observer’s “monitored exception” statement supports the pilot. FD-7 was a formal directive. The fact that these overlays are not reflected in M0 is a model-governance defect, not operational noncompliance.

### 4. CRM migration explains missing notifications

N4 states that from 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to a CRM service and only about 70% of notifications are mirrored. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. SUP-702 has a CRM notification with a stable `notification_id`. SUP-706 has a CRM message but a mutable link. Missing log events are data-collection defects, not necessarily process failures.

### 5. Reactivation is out of scope

SUP-710 lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review because it is a reactivation, not a new onboarding. Prior quality certification remained valid. CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. Applying M0’s full new-onboarding path to reactivation is incorrect scope.

### 6. Rejected leads are intake cases

SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen. CS-9 allows rejected leads to use an abbreviated pre-screen. The process owner says rejected leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance. The scope document is ambiguous. Applying M0’s full checks to rejected leads is a scope error.

### 7. Label equivalence is a data-dictionary issue

SUP-713 uses QA_Clearance instead of Quality_Review. The procedure and UI renamed the activity. Audit documents show equivalent content. The mapping was approved one week after the case. This is a label-mapping defect, not a substantive control failure.

### 8. Duplicate register is a data-quality defect

SUP-711 has two Register_Supplier events, but both rows have the same source transaction ID. This is a migration artifact. Deduplication yields a compliant trace. It is a data-quality defect, not a process deviation.

### 9. Trace deviation is not business misconduct

Many deviations from M0 are due to model or data defects, not control failures. The 47 Request_More_Info cases had no activation before the requested evidence arrived, showing control intent. The 289 PD-3-eligible Auto_Approve cases were within pilot conditions. The 9 missing eligibility attributes are data-quality issues, not automatically noncompliant. The 14 clearly ineligible cases are noncompliant, but they are a minority.

### 10. Desirable behavior should be incorporated into the model

Request_More_Info is desirable and should be incorporated. Operations wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch. This suggests M0 should be revised, not enforced rigidly.

### Defense acknowledgment of genuine risk

The defense must acknowledge that SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715 are not excused by model defects. Informal practice, operational need, or desirable outcomes do not amend policy. The defense also cannot claim that all deviations are model defects: the 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and the 6 post-pilot cases are noncompliant, and missing eligibility attributes are not automatically compliant.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 6.1 Revised control-flow model (POWL)

POWL should handle activity ordering and concurrency only. The revised control-flow model has four routes:

- **Standard route**:  
  `Register_Supplier  (Sanctions_Check  Quality_Review)  Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`

- **Low-risk auto-approval route**:  
  `Register_Supplier  Sanctions_Check  Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`

- **Emergency/provisional route**:  
  `Register_Supplier  Sanctions_Check  Activate_Supplier  (Quality_Review  Manual_Procurement_Approval)  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`

- **Rejection route**:  
  `Register_Supplier  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`

- **Request_More_Info**: an optional repeatable loop placed before route selection. This is an approximation because POWL cannot cleanly interleave a loop inside a concurrent branch.

### 6.2 Data-aware eligibility/amount/time rules (pandas/DECLARE)

These cannot be fully encoded in POWL. Use pandas/data checks and DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints.

Examples:

- `precedence(sanctions_check, activate)` for all routes.
- `response(register, sanctions_check)`.
- Standard route: `precedence(quality_review, activate)` and `precedence(manual_approval, activate)`.
- Auto route: eligibility snapshot must show `risk_level == LOW`, `country == DE`, `approved_group == True`, `policy_overlay_id == PD-3`, and `event_time` within the PD-3 window. Quality_Review must occur within two business days after activation.
- Emergency route: `emergency_directive_id == FD-7`, `event_time` within the FD-7 window, `spend <= 20000`, `daily_monitoring == True`, and Quality_Review within two business days.

### 6.3 Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (organizational + data checks)

These are not POWL. Use actor alias resolution and data checks.

- Requester and manual approver must be different canonical humans.
- Quality reviewer and manual approver must not be spouses or in the same reporting line without a recorded disclosure.
- Delegation evidence is required for any delegate approver.
- Organizational controls: COI disclosure workflow, segregation-of-duties enforcement in the UI.

### 6.4 Policy-overlay governance

Pilots and emergencies must be structured overlays with:

- version IDs,
- effective windows,
- scope,
- eligibility conditions,
- required safeguards,
- expiry,
- authority.

No informal normalization. Expiry must be enforced by data checks. Overlays must be approved by Compliance and formally recorded. Post-expiry cases fail unless a new overlay exists.

### 6.5 Scope rules

- **Rejected leads**: define whether they are intake cases with abbreviated pre-screen or M0-governed cases requiring full checks. The current ambiguity is a defect.
- **Reactivations**: separate procedure. Require sanctions, manual approval, and valid prior quality certification. Do not require full Register_Supplier and Quality_Review if prior certification is valid.
- **Withdrawals**: define a closure path. Require notification and closure. No activation.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- Activities ---
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# --- Optional repeatable Request_More_Info loop ---
# POWL loop semantics: do -> (redo | exit).
# Here do=Request_More_Info, redo=tau (loop again), exit=tau.
# XOR with tau gives zero or more Request_More_Info events.
request_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[request_more_info, SilentTransition(), SilentTransition()]
)
request_more_info_opt = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[SilentTransition(), request_loop]
)

# --- Standard route: concurrent sanctions and quality, then manual approval ---
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])  # no edges => concurrent
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks, manual_approval, activate])
standard_route.order.add_edge(checks, manual_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# --- Low-risk auto-approval route (PD-3 overlay) ---
# Sanctions before Auto_Approve; Quality_Review may follow activation.
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto_approve, activate, quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)

# --- Emergency/provisional route (FD-7 overlay) ---
# Sanctions before Activate; Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval
# may follow activation and are concurrent with each other.
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, activate, quality, manual_approval])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, manual_approval)

# --- Rejection route ---
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, reject)

# --- Route decision ---
route_decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route]
)

# --- Top-level partial order ---
# Limitation: the Request_More_Info loop is placed before route selection.
# POWL cannot cleanly interleave a repeatable loop inside the concurrent
# checks branch; this is the most defensible approximation.
M = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, request_more_info_opt, route_decision, notify, close])
M.order.add_edge(register, request_more_info_opt)
M.order.add_edge(request_more_info_opt, route_decision)
M.order.add_edge(route_decision, notify)
M.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# M is the revised POWL model.
```

### Event/data-collection proposal

Each event must carry:

- `case_id`
- `activity`
- `canonical_label`
- `event_time` (immutable)
- `record_time`
- `source_system`
- `transaction_id`
- `actor_canonical_id`
- `actor_alias`
- `policy_overlay_id`
- `rule_version_id`
- `eligibility_snapshot` (JSON)
- `amount`
- `notification_id` (stable UUID)
- `delegation_id`
- `emergency_directive_id`
- `pilot_id`
- `coi_disclosure_id`

Additional requirements:

- Notification events must be mirrored to the onboarding log with a stable `notification_id`; CRM links must be immutable.
- Eligibility snapshots must be captured at decision time, not reconstructed later.
- Actor aliases must be resolved to canonical human IDs; delegation evidence must be recorded.
- Emergency/pilot authority must be recorded as structured fields, not free text.
- Immutable effective/record timestamps: `event_time` vs `record_time`; late-arriving events must be flagged.

### DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints (examples)

- `precedence(sanctions_check, activate)` for all routes.
- `response(register, sanctions_check)`.
- Standard route: `precedence(quality_review, activate)`, `precedence(manual_approval, activate)`.
- Auto route: `eligibility_snapshot[risk_level] == LOW`, `country == DE`, `approved_group == True`, `policy_overlay_id == PD-3`, `event_time` within window; `quality_review` within 2 business days after activate.
- Emergency route: `emergency_directive_id == FD-7`, `event_time` within window, `spend <= 20000`, `daily_monitoring == True`, `quality_review` within 2 business days.
