# INDEPENDENT REVIEW: SUPPLIER ONBOARDING CONFORMANCE

---

## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts (high confidence, direct evidence)

1. **Normative policy exists**: CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01) mandates Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, and Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier.
2. **Pilot authorized**: PD-3 (steering-committee decision, effective 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31) permits Auto_Approve for eligible LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved groups; Quality_Review may occur up to +2 business days.
3. **Emergency issued**: FD-7 (effective 2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12 23:59) permits provisional activation before approval/review for critical suppliers, capped at EUR 20,000.
4. **Pilot was monitored**: Compliance observer recorded pilot exception status but explicitly did not approve weakening CS-9.
5. **Data migration occurred**: CRM change (2026-07-01) reduced notification visibility from ~98% to ~71% in log; older links are mutable text fields.
6. **High deviation rate**: Approximately 312 cases used Auto_Approve during pilot; 289 appear eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 incomplete.
7. **Desired behavior lacks policy**: Request_More_Info is operationally common (47 cases, none activated early) and stakeholder-approved but absent from M0 and CS-9.
8. **Resource rule not encoded**: CS-9 requires distinct requester and approver; M0 does not represent this constraint.

### Burden of Proof

**For declaring noncompliance**:
- Control-flow deviation must not be explained by authorized pilot, active emergency, or explicit scope boundary.
- Timing of policy/rule amendment relative to case execution is critical: if rule was not effective when case executed, retrospective judgment is invalid.
- Missing evidence (e.g., notification) is insufficient to declare control-flow noncompliance if the activity occurred and CRM linkage is recoverable.
- Resource violations (identity, relationship, separation of duty) require identity resolution and explicit rule at the time of decision.

**For accepting an exception**:
- Pilot or emergency must be formally authorized by steering committee or equivalent before case execution.
- Exception must be scoped (eligibility, amount, duration, monitoring).
- Monitoring compliance (e.g., daily transaction reviews in FD-7) must be demonstrated.
- Post-expiry behavior requires retrospective authorization or must be reclassified as noncompliant.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### Normative Authority and Formal Obligation

CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01) is a controlled compliance standard. It establishes mandatory control-flow requirements:
- Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation.
- Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier.

M0 encodes this requirement. The standard is unambiguous and predates the pilot and emergency by months. **There is no grandfathering clause; CS-9 applies to all cases onward.**

### High-Risk Cases: SUP-703 and SUP-708

**SUP-703** (2026-06-20, HIGH-risk foreign supplier, EUR 180,000):
- Operator selected Auto_Approve despite ineligibility (not LOW-risk DE; not in approved group).
- PD-3 explicitly limits auto-approve to LOW-risk DE suppliers in corporate groups.
- First purchase was EUR 180,000, exceeding any reasonable low-risk threshold and vastly above the emergency cap (EUR 20,000).
- **Prosecution interpretation**: Willful or negligent violation of pilot scope. The HIGH-risk classification and foreign origin mean sanctions and quality review were especially critical. Absence of Manual_Procurement_Approval before activation represents loss of deliberative control. This is **severe compliance failure**.

**SUP-708** (timing unclear, but HIGH-risk supplier, activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check):
- Sanctions_Check is mandatory before activation per CS-9.
- No emergency was declared; no pilot applied.
- Activation occurred before the critical control point (sanctions clearance).
- **Prosecution interpretation**: Even though no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance, the activation event is *recorded* before the check. This violates the sequencing requirement and the control-flow precondition. The absence of pre-activation transaction is a mitigating fact but does not erase the process violation. **Material noncompliance.**

### Normalization of Exceptions Post-Expiry

PD-3 expired 2026-07-31 23:59. Six cases used Auto_Approve in the first week of August 2026:
- **SUP-714** (2026-08-02) is post-expiry.
- Operations claim normalization and pending permanent amendment.
- **No permanent amendment exists.** The steering committee did not issue a follow-on decision.
- Between expiry and any amendment, these cases violate CS-9.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: This is **unauthorized use of an expired exception.** Operators cannot unilaterally extend a pilot; the absence of a permanent policy amendment is dispositive. Even if these cases were LOW-risk-eligible, they proceeded under a procedure (Auto_Approve) that had no authority after 2026-07-31.

### Emergency Exception Abuse: SUP-705

FD-7 expired 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-705 is dated 2026-07-13:
- Operations claims the flood emergency "factually existed."
- **FD-7 was a time-limited directive; it had a formal end date.**
- CS-9 requires Manual_Procurement_Approval before activation. SUP-705 executed activation before approval.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: Absence of a written extension is dispositive. Operators cannot unilaterally extend an emergency directive based on factual conditions. If the emergency continued, the proper channel was a formal written amendment or a new directive. **This is unauthorized use of an expired emergency provision.**

### Ineligible Pilot Use: Aggregate Data

Of 312 auto-approve cases in the pilot window:
- 14 are clearly ineligible (wrong country, wrong risk tier, or both).
- 9 have missing eligibility attributes (cannot confirm eligibility).
- **23 cases proceeded without confirmed eligibility.** Even if some are later confirmed, the absence of eligibility evidence at decision time means operators cannot demonstrate they were entitled to auto-approve.

### FD-7 Overreach

SUP-704 (2026-07-11, within FD-7 window):
- Activation occurred before Manual_Procurement_Approval.
- FD-7 permits this *for critical relief suppliers.*
- Daily transaction monitoring and Quality_Review within +2 business days are required.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: SUP-704 may be justified by FD-7 if critical status is confirmed and monitoring is verifiable. The conditions are narrow. But the mere fact that an emergency was active does not license all deviations; it must be case-specific.

### Resource Rule Violations

**SUP-712**: Requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through aliases.
- CS-9 mandates different humans.
- M0 does not represent this constraint, but CS-9 does.
- M0's silence does not override CS-9.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: This is a **control-flow violation in practice**, even though M0 cannot detect it. The approvals carry reduced integrity if the same person was both requester and approver.

**SUP-715**: Quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line. No undisclosed conflict-of-interest.
- Conflict-of-interest policy requires disclosure.
- Absence of disclosure at decision time is a governance failure.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: Process control was compromised; independence of review is in doubt. This is a **compliance failure** under the conflict-of-interest rule, even though M0 does not encode it.

### Notification Defects

27% of cases lack notification evidence in the onboarding log post-migration (from ~2% pre-migration).
- Notify_Requester is a mandatory step (M0 and CS-9).
- Mutable text-field links are not reliable evidence.
- **Prosecution interpretation**: For cases like SUP-706, the mutable-field link does not constitute proof that notification occurred. The increase in missingness suggests process degradation. Absence of audit trail is a **compliance failure in transparency and auditability.**

### Design Obsolescence is Not Excuse

Stakeholders want Request_More_Info loops and auto-approve as permanent features. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy. M0 may be incomplete, but:
- Cases executed under CS-9, not under a new (nonexistent) amendment.
- If Request_More_Info is desired, CS-9 and M0 should have been amended before cases executed.
- Instead, **informal practice preceded formal authorization.** This is backwards.

### Summary: Prosecution Case

- **SUP-703, SUP-708**: Severe noncompliance, material risk.
- **SUP-705, SUP-714**: Unauthorized use of expired exceptions.
- **SUP-704**: Conditional on verifiable critical status and monitoring.
- **SUP-712, SUP-715**: Resource rule violations outside M0 but within CS-9 scope.
- **SUP-706 and ~80 others**: Notification defects reduce audit integrity.
- **Aggregate**: 14–23 ineligible auto-approve cases; 6+ post-expiry cases; 2+ severe resource violations.
- **Operational posture**: Normalization of exceptions without authority; informal practice ahead of policy.

**Recommendation**: Enforce CS-9; require amendments before authorizing new variants; investigate high-risk cases for transactional damage.

## 3. Defense Brief

### M0 is Incomplete by Design

M0 encodes control-flow sequencing but explicitly does not represent:
- Resource rules (distinct requester/approver; conflict-of-interest).
- Risk stratification (LOW vs. HIGH tier auto-approve eligibility).
- Amount caps (EUR 20,000 emergency limit).
- Time windows (FD-7 expiry, +2 business day allowances).
- Request_More_Info loop (47 observed cases, none violating activation rules).

**Defense interpretation**: M0 is a control-flow skeleton. Deviations from M0 are not ipso facto noncompliance unless they also violate CS-9 and active policy overlays. Many of the flagged cases fail M0 but succeed under authorized overlays (PD-3, FD-7) or valid scopes (reactivation, rejected leads).

### Pilot (PD-3) Authorization and Scope

PD-3 is formally authorized (steering-committee decision) for 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31. It explicitly permits:
- Auto_Approve (not Manual_Procurement_Approval) for LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved groups.
- Quality_Review up to +2 business days after activation.

**Defense interpretation**: 
- **SUP-702** (2026-06-15, LOW-risk DE in approved group, Quality_Review at +1 business day): **Fully compliant with PD-3.** Pilot eligibility fields are complete. Notification is recorded (stable notification_id in CRM). This is textbook pilot usage.
- **SUP-703** (2026-06-20, HIGH-risk foreign, EUR 180,000): Operator error in pilot selection, yes. But does this refute the pilot's legitimacy? No. It shows operator training was insufficient. The breach is in *eligibility verification*, not in the existence of the pilot.
- **SUP-714** (2026-08-02, LOW-risk eligible): The case occurred two days after pilot expiry. Prosecution says this is unauthorized. But operations was under reasonable belief that a permanent amendment was imminent (email evidence suggests active discussion). The case is borderline. If permanent policy was substantially finalized, the two-day gap is a timing artifact, not willful violation. **Data: lack of formal amendment decision is a legitimate hole.** But absence of written decision does not prove violation at time of execution if the belief in authorization was documented and reasonable.

**Defense concession**: Post-expiry cases (SUP-714 and others) require retrospective authorization or reclassification. But pilot success (289 of 312 cases eligible) suggests the underlying process is sound; the issue is administrative timing.

### Emergency Authorization (FD-7) and Justification

FD-7 (2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12 23:59) is formally issued, time-limited, and risk-calibrated:
- Permits activation before approval/review for critical suppliers.
- Requires Sanctions_Check before activation (control point retained).
- Spend capped at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval.
- Daily monitoring and Quality_Review within +2 business days.

**SUP-704** (2026-07-11, EUR 18,000, daily monitoring, quality/approval at +1 business day):
- **Defense interpretation**: This case exemplifies proper emergency use. It is entirely consistent with FD-7. Activation before approval is authorized; spend is within cap; monitoring is verifiable. **COMPLIANT with FD-7.**

**SUP-705** (2026-07-13, after expiry, EUR 12,000):
- Operations claims the flood factually persisted; no written extension exists.
- **Defense interpretation**: Here, prosecution is right. FD-7 had an explicit end date; no extension authority exists; thus SUP-705 is out-of-scope for FD-7. *However*, if operations was under emergency protocol by a different informal mechanism, or if the case was mislabeled-executed but should have been classified as a standard case, the control-flow violation might be administrative rather than material. Did transactions occur before sanctions clearance? Unknown from the trace. If yes, material risk; if no, process failure but mitigated. **Data: operational emergency status and transaction timeline are missing.**

### Scope Ambiguities: Rejected Leads and Reactivation

**SUP-709** (rejected lead, no quality review):
- M0 includes Reject_Supplier.
- Prosecution assumes all suppliers flow through M0.
- **Defense interpretation**: Process documentation suggests intake pre-screening gates entry into M0. If SUP-709 is a pre-screen case that never entered M0, then absence of Quality_Review is not a deviation from M0. The scope question is: does M0 apply to leads that fail pre-screen, or does M0 begin after pre-screen acceptance?
- **Evidence**: Process owner says pre-screen is upstream; model owner says M0 is universal. CS-9 scope clause is ambiguous ("onboarding requires...").
- **Reasonable interpretation**: If pre-screen is part of intake triage, not onboarding, then SUP-709 is out-of-scope for M0. But CS-9 might still require Quality_Review for final acceptance. The ambiguity is real.

**SUP-710** (reactivation without Register or Quality_Review):
- CS-9 mandates Quality_Review before activation.
- SUP-710 lacks both Register_Supplier and Quality_Review.
- **Defense interpretation**: Reactivation is a distinct process from new onboarding. Prior quality certification remained valid; risk profile was already known. A minimal reactivation flow (Sanctions check only, if status has changed) is operationally reasonable and may be justified by CS-9's silence on reactivation.
- **Data**: Missing is whether re-sanctions check is part of the "quality review" for an existing supplier, or whether CS-9 applies to reactivation at all.
- **Reasonable interpretation**: CS-9 says "supplier onboarding requires...". Does reactivation count as onboarding? Grammatically, reactivation is not initial onboarding. But CS-9 does not explicitly define the boundary.

### Data Quality Issues Confound Control-Flow Assessment

**Notification Migration (SUP-706 and ~80 others)**:
- CRM migration (2026-07-01) caused ~27% notification log missingness.
- CRM contains Notify_Requester evidence (SUP-706: message at +3 hours).
- Mutable text-field link is a data integrity problem, not a control-flow problem.
- **Defense interpretation**: The activity *occurred*; the logging is defective. Data recovery (cross-referencing CRM by date, supplier, approver) can likely reconstruct notification events. Missing log evidence  missing activity.
- **Reasonable interpretation**: Notification missingness is a **data quality defect**, not a control-flow violation. The proper remedy is data cleanup and reconciliation, not retroactive compliance finding.

**Migration Artifact (SUP-711)**:
- Register_Supplier appears twice; identical transaction ID.
- **Defense interpretation**: This is a migration duplicate, not a double registration. The event should be deduplicated. **Data artifact, not process violation.**

**Label Equivalence (SUP-713)**:
- QA_Clearance vs. Quality_Review.
- **Defense interpretation**: Procedure authorized the rename; audit confirmed equivalence; one-week post-case approval is administratively messy but substantively valid. This is a **data integration issue** (label mapping), not a control-flow issue. If QA_Clearance is functionally identical to Quality_Review and was approved, then SUP-713 is compliant.

### Resource Rules are Outside M0 Scope

M0 does not encode resource constraints (distinct actors, separation of duty, conflict-of-interest). CS-9 does.

**SUP-712** (requester and approver same human via aliases):
- **Defense interpretation**: M0 is not designed to enforce resource rules. This is a data/identity issue, not a control-flow deviation. The defect is in alias resolution, not in control flow. Prosecution correctly notes that M0 should incorporate resource rules or that data should enforce them. But the absence of such rules in M0 means you cannot say M0 was violated; you can only say CS-9's resource requirement was violated, and M0 was not designed to catch it.
- **Reasonable interpretation**: M0 is incomplete for full compliance, but SUP-712 is a **data integrity issue** (alias resolution) and a **governance defect** (no resource rule in model), not a control-flow violation of M0.

**SUP-715** (undisclosed conflict-of-interest):
- Conflict-of-interest policy requires disclosure.
- Relationship was not disclosed.
- **Defense interpretation**: This is a **violation of the conflict-of-interest policy**, which is orthogonal to M0. It should have been caught at the governance layer (review of approvers before decision). M0 cannot represent relationship checks; this is a human-decision defect. The process control allowed it to slip through, but that is a governance/data failure, not a control-flow failure.

### Request_More_Info is Operationally Sound and Should Be Modeled

47 cases include Request_More_Info iterations. None activated before requested evidence arrived. Operations and Compliance agree it is desirable.

**Defense interpretation**: 
- These loops do not violate control-flow sequencing; they occurred *within* the decision-making phase, before activation.
- The loop is operationally sound and policy-compliant.
- M0's absence of the loop is a **modeling gap**, not an operational noncompliance.
- The remedy is to revise M0 to include the loop as an optional path before the decision point.

### Aggregate Eligibility: 289 of 312 is Success

312 auto-approve cases in pilot window; 289 eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 incomplete.
- **92.6% eligibility rate** during the pilot.
- 14 ineligible cases is an issue (operator training, UI design).
- 9 incomplete cases require investigation (were they later verified?).
- **Defense interpretation**: This is not wholesale noncompliance; it is a training/UI gap within a largely successful pilot. The remedy is eligibility screening (UI hardening, operator training), not cancellation of the pilot model.

### Summary: Defense Case

- **M0 is incomplete**: It omits resource rules, risk stratification, amount caps, time windows, and the Request_More_Info loop. Deviations from M0  noncompliance if authorized overlays or valid scopes apply.
- **PD-3 is well-scoped and mostly compliant**: 289 of 312 cases are eligible. SUP-703 is operator error, not pilot design failure. Post-expiry cases (SUP-714) are borderline (evidence of imminent amendment exists).
- **FD-7 authorization is valid**: SUP-704 is a textbook compliant emergency case. SUP-705 is borderline (factual emergency may justify extension if documented; data is missing).
- **Scope ambiguities are real**: Rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivation (SUP-710) may be out-of-scope for M0 if CS-9 scope is narrower than "all supplier cases."
- **Data quality issues confound assessment**: CRM migration (notification missingness), label equivalence (QA_Clearance), migration artifacts (SUP-711) are data problems, not control-flow violations.
- **Resource violations (SUP-712, SUP-715) are governance defects, not M0 violations**: M0 does not encode resource rules; governance/data should. Absence from M0 is a modeling gap.
- **Prosecution conflates control-flow deviation with noncompliance**: Many flagged cases are deviations from M0 but compliant with authorized overlays or outside scope.

**Recommendation**: Revise M0 to incorporate overlays and scopes; invest in data quality (CRM reconciliation, alias resolution, conflict-of-interest checks); investigate 14–23 borderline cases for transactional damage; investigate SUP-708 for timing facts.

#### Operational Assessment

1. **The operation has material noncompliance risks**: SUP-703 (HIGH-risk supplier, EUR 180k, ineligible auto-approve, no manual approval) and SUP-708 (HIGH-risk, activation before sanctions) are severe. Both represent loss of critical control points.

2. **Pilot (PD-3) was mostly successful but had weak eligibility gating**: 289 of 312 cases eligible; 14–23 ineligible. The defect is in operator training and UI design, not the pilot model itself.

3. **Emergency (FD-7) was well-scoped and exemplified in SUP-704**: Expiry was enforced (at least formally); post-expiry cases (SUP-705) are unauthorized unless retrospectively justified.

4. **M0 is incomplete by design but not invalidated**: M0 omits resource rules, risk stratification, Request_More_Info loop, reactivation, and rejected-lead scope. These omissions created blind spots (SUP-712, SUP-715 not detected; SUP-707 classified as deviation; SUP-709, SUP-710 ambiguous).

5. **Data quality issues undermine confidence in audit trail**: CRM migration (27% notification missingness), mutable-field links, and alias resolution problems mean that compliance status depends on forensic data recovery.

6. **Scope ambiguities are real and must be resolved**: CS-9 does not explicitly define pre-screen, reactivation, or the boundary between intake and M0. Process documentation is ambiguous.

#### Prosecution Case: Where It Is Right

- **SUP-703 is indefensible**: HIGH-risk, ineligible, EUR 180k, no manual approval. This is a compliance failure and material risk.
- **SUP-708 activation-before-sanctions is a control-flow violation**, regardless of whether transactions were deferred. The precedence rule was violated.
- **Resource rules (CS-9 requirement for distinct actors) are not optional.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 violate this, even though M0 does not represent it.
- **Expired procedures cannot be unilaterally extended.** PD-3 and FD-7 have formal expiry dates; post-expiry use without written amendment is unauthorized.
- **Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.** Request_More_Info is operationally sound, but its absence from M0/CS-9 is a policy gap, not an excuse.

#### Defense Case: Where It Is Right

- **M0 is not designed to enforce resource rules, risk stratification, or amount caps.** Deviations from M0 are not ipso facto noncompliance if authorized overlays (PD-3, FD-7) apply or if the case is out-of-scope.
- **PD-3 pilot was largely successful (92.6% eligibility).** The 14–23 ineligible cases are an operator/UI defect, not a pilot design failure.
- **FD-7 SUP-704 is textbook compliance.** Emergency procedures work as designed when applied to eligible cases with adequate monitoring.
- **Data quality issues are real confounds.** CRM migration, mutable links, and alias resolution are data problems, not control-flow violations. Notifications likely occurred; logging is defective.
- **Scope ambiguities are real and unresolved.** Pre-screen, reactivation, and rejected-lead boundaries are not defined in CS-9 or M0. Until clarified, cases like SUP-709 and SUP-710 cannot be unambiguously classified as noncompliant.

#### Prosecution Case: Where It Is Wrong

- **It conflates M0 deviation with CS-9 noncompliance.** Many flagged cases (SUP-702, SUP-704) are M0 deviations but CS-9-compliant due to authorized overlays.
- **It underweights the success of PD-3 and FD-7.** The majority of pilot cases (289/312) were eligible; the emergency case (SUP-704) is exemplary. These are not systemic failures.
- **It ignores data quality problems as potential explanations.** CRM migration, mutable links, and alias resolution are serious issues that may explain apparent deviations without implying process noncompliance.

#### Defense Case: Where It Is Wrong

- **It hand-waves away serious violations.** SUP-703 (HIGH-risk, ineligible, EUR 180k) is not a training defect; it is a material breach. SUP-708 (activation before sanctions) violates sequencing regardless of transaction deferral.
- **It relies too heavily on "incomplete scope" as an excuse.** Even if scope is ambiguous, operators have a duty to apply rules conservatively (preconditions before main activity). SUP-708 is indefensible even with generous scope interpretation.
- **It treats post-expiry cases (SUP-705, SUP-714) as harmless timing artifacts.** Formal expiry dates exist for a reason; operations cannot unilaterally extend emergency or pilot procedures. The absence of written extension is dispositive.
- **It underweights resource violations.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 involve undisclosed conflicts and loss of independence; these are not mere governance gaps; they are compliance failures.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Design Principles

1. **Layered governance**: Control-flow (POWL), eligibility rules (data-driven), resource constraints (governance), and policy overlays (authorized exceptions with scope, duration, monitoring).
2. **Explicit scope boundaries**: Pre-screen (upstream intake, distinct process), new onboarding (M0 standard), low-risk auto-approve (PD-3 overlay), emergency activation (FD-7 overlay), reactivation (separate minimal flow).
3. **Immutable audit trail**: Stable IDs, versioned rules, actor identity resolution, delegation evidence, timestamps.
4. **Data-aware constraints**: Risk tier, amount caps, time windows, relationship/conflict checks.

### Layered Architecture Components

#### Layer 1: Control-Flow Model (POWL)

**Primary flow** (M0-revised):
- Register_Supplier  [Sanctions_Check  Quality_Review]  Decision  Notify_Requester  Close_Case

**Decision tree**:
- If eligible for low-risk auto-approve  Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier
- Else  Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier
- Or  Reject_Supplier (and close)

**Optional Request_More_Info loop**:
- Before decision, loop to gather additional evidence.

**Reactivation flow** (separate, minimal):
- Sanctions_Check (if status changed)  [Approve]  Activate_Supplier  Notify  Close

**Rejected-lead intake** (upstream, out-of-scope for M0):
- Pre-screen  [Quality_Pre_Check (optional)]  Reject or Pass to M0

#### Layer 2: Eligibility and Risk Rules (Data-Driven)

**Auto-Approve eligibility** (requires ALL):
- Risk tier = "LOW"
- Supplier country in approved list (e.g., DE for PD-3)
- Supplier in approved corporate group
- Transaction amount  EUR 50,000 (or risk-based threshold)
- No prior sanctions flags
- No conflict-of-interest disclosed

**Manual-Approve required** (default):
- Risk tier = HIGH or MEDIUM
- Country outside approved list
- Amount > EUR 50,000
- Conflict-of-interest or undisclosed relationship

**Emergency activation** (FD-7 or equivalent):
- Requires active emergency directive (active date/time window)
- Critical-supplier designation (defined in directive)
- Transaction amount  EUR 20,000 (or directive cap)
- Daily transaction monitoring during activation
- Quality_Review within +2 business days
- Manual_Procurement_Approval within +2 business days

#### Layer 3: Resource and Governance Rules

**Separation of duty**:
- Requester  Manual Approver (verify via authoritative identity source, not aliases)
- Quality Reviewer independent of approver (check relationship, reporting line, delegation)

**Conflict-of-interest disclosure**:
- Family relationship (spouse, sibling, parent-child) in same reporting line  requires disclosure and escalation
- Financial interest (equity, debt, contract)  requires disclosure
- Non-disclosure is a compliance failure

**Actor identity resolution**:
- Maintain canonical actor ID (SSO/LDAP, not aliases)
- Reconcile aliases to canonical ID at decision time
- Log canonical ID, not alias, in audit trail

#### Layer 4: Policy Overlays (Authorized Exceptions)

**Overlay structure**:
- Steering-committee or equivalent authority issues directive (e.g., PD-3, FD-7)
- Directive specifies: effective date, expiry date, scope (who is eligible), conditions (amount, monitoring)
- Directive is versioned and recorded immutably
- Deviations from directive scope are noncompliant

**PD-3 (Automation Pilot)**:
- Effective: 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31
- Scope: LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups
- Condition: Sanctions_Check before activation; Quality_Review within +2 days post-activation
- Monitoring: Eligibility verification (UI hardening, operator training)
- Expiry: Automatic, no extension authority given

**FD-7 (Flood Emergency)**:
- Effective: 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59
- Scope: Critical relief suppliers (list maintained by Operations)
- Condition: Sanctions_Check before activation; approval/quality within +2 days; daily monitoring
- Amount cap: EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval
- Expiry: Automatic; operations may request written extension with justification

**Post-PD-3 permanent low-risk auto-approve** (TBD):
- Requires steering-committee decision and amendment to CS-9 and M0
- Until amended, post-pilot auto-approve is unauthorized

#### Layer 5: Scope and Boundaries

**In-scope for M0**:
- New supplier onboarding (first registration)
- Leads that pass pre-screen

**Out-of-scope for M0**:
- Pre-screen intake triage (upstream, separate process)
- Reactivation of previously-registered suppliers (use minimal reactivation flow)

**Ambiguous (to be clarified)**:
- Should rejected leads be logged in M0's Reject_Supplier branch, or are they pre-screen artifacts?
- Should prior certification be sufficient for reactivation, or must Quality_Review be repeated?

#### Layer 6: Data Collection and Audit Trail

**Immutable identifiers**:
- Case ID (onboarding case, unique, immutable)
- Activity instance ID (each event occurrence)
- Stable notification_id (for CRM-onboarding log linkage)
- Actor canonical ID (not alias)
- Rule version ID (which version of CS-9/PD-3/FD-7 was applied?)

**Timestamps**:
- Activity execution time (when event occurred)
- Log record time (when event was logged; may differ due to async processing)
- Rule effective time (when policy/overlay became active)

**Metadata**:
- Risk tier (assessed at registration; evidence)
- Amount (transaction amount, caps applied)
- Delegation evidence (if approver was delegated, by whom, with what authority)
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure (date, disclosed parties, escalation outcome)
- Emergency directive reference (if activated under overlay, which directive?)

**Audit trail requirements**:
- All identity resolutions (alias  canonical ID) logged with timestamp
- All conflict-of-interest checks logged (disclosed, mitigated, or escalated)
- All policy/overlay applications logged (which rule version?)
- All eligibility verifications logged (passed/failed, evidence)

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Revised POWL Model

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ============================================================================
# LAYER 1: REVISED CONTROL-FLOW MODEL
# ============================================================================

# Standard onboarding flow with overlays

# Register supplier
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# Concurrent checks (Sanctions and Quality)
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# No order constraint; checks are concurrent (either order allowed)

# Optional Request_More_Info loop (TBD: POWL limitations; see explanation below)
# POWL cannot natively express arbitrary loops; we represent this as a choice:
# - Path 1: No additional info  proceed to decision
# - Path 2: Request_More_Info (one or more times)  proceed to decision
# 
# For simplicity, we model a "RequestInfo" silent transition that is optional.
# In practice, this would be a loop that captures real Request_More_Info events
# and logs them without blocking activation (which happens *after* decision).

request_more = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
no_request = SilentTransition(label="_skip_request")  # Silent: no request needed

request_optional = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[request_more, no_request]
)

# Decision point: standard manual approval vs. low-risk auto-approve
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")  # PD-3 overlay

# Both paths converge to activation
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

# Approved branch (manual): Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier
approved_manual = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
approved_manual.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# Approved branch (auto): Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier
approved_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate])
approved_auto.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)

# Decision: choose between manual, auto, or reject
# (In real execution, eligibility checks determine path; POWL allows both)
decision_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[approved_manual, approved_auto, Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")]
)

# Post-decision: Notify and Close
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Main flow: Register  Checks  Request (optional)  Decision  Notify  Close
main_flow = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, checks, request_optional, decision_choice, notify, close]
)
main_flow.order.add_edge(register, checks)
main_flow.order.add_edge(checks, request_optional)
main_flow.order.add_edge(request_optional, decision_choice)
main_flow.order.add_edge(decision_choice, notify)
main_flow.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# ============================================================================
# LAYER 1B: REACTIVATION FLOW (SEPARATE, MINIMAL)
# ============================================================================

# Reactivation assumes prior certification is valid; minimal sanity checks
reactivate_register = Transition(label="Reactivate_Supplier")
reactivate_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check_Reactivation")
reactivate_approve = Transition(label="Approve_Reactivation")
reactivate_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier_Reactivation")
reactivate_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Reactivation")
reactivate_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

reactivation_flow = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reactivate_register, reactivate_sanctions, reactivate_approve, 
            reactivate_activate, reactivate_notify, reactivate_close]
)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivate_register, reactivate_sanctions)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivate_sanctions, reactivate_approve)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivate_approve, reactivate_activate)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivate_activate, reactivate_notify)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivate_notify, reactivate_close)

# ============================================================================
# LIMITATION AND EXPLANATION
# ============================================================================

# POWL LIMITATION: Request_More_Info Loop
# POWL (Strict Partial Order Language) does not natively support arbitrary loops.
# The model above uses an XOR choice (either request or skip) to approximate a
# single iteration. In practice:
#
# 1. A full loop would require a state-machine or Petri-net representation.
# 2. Observed behavior (47 cases with 1-2 request iterations) is mostly linear
#    (request happens once or twice before decision).
# 3. WORKAROUND: Use a loop-unrolling approximation with OperatorPOWL(XOR) for
#    "0, 1, or 2 requests" (feasible for small iteration bounds).
# 4. BETTER APPROACH: Log Request_More_Info as a normal activity and use a
#    separate data-driven constraint (e.g., DECLARE rule: 
#    Request_More_Info may occur multiple times before decision; activation
#    only occurs after request is satisfied).
#
# For now, we model the XOR choice above and rely on data constraints (see below)
# to enforce the sequencing and satisfaction rules.

# More realistic loop approximation (unfold 1 iteration):
request_or_no = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        request_more,  # One request_more activity
        no_request     # Or no request
    ]
)

# We use this in main_flow above.

print("=" * 70)
print("REVISED POWL MODEL (PM4Py-style construction)")
print("=" * 70)

# Print main flow structure
print("\nMain Onboarding Flow:")
print("  Register_Supplier")
print("     [Sanctions_Check  Quality_Review]  (concurrent)")
print("     [Request_More_Info XOR Skip]        (optional loop approximation)")
print("     Decision (Manual_Approval XOR Auto_Approve XOR Reject)")
print("     Notify_Requester")
print("     Close_Case")

1. POWL MODEL (main_flow, reactivation_flow):
   - Encodes control-flow sequencing (activity ordering)
   - Represents choices (Manual vs. Auto vs. Reject)
   - Approximates Request_More_Info loop as XOR choice
   - Cannot encode resource rules, risk stratification, or amount caps
   
2. DATA-DRIVEN CONSTRAINTS (policy_rules, auto_approve_eligibility, etc.):
   - Eligibility checks (risk tier, country, group, amount)
   - Emergency directive scope and expiry
   - Resource rules (distinct actors, conflict-of-interest)
   - Enforcement at runtime via DataFrame lookups

3. AUDIT TRAIL (case_metadata, event_log, notification_log, conflict_log):
   - Immutable IDs (notification_id, event_id, case_id)
   - Canonical actor IDs (SSO/LDAP, not aliases)
   - Versioned rule IDs (which CS-9 version, PD-3 version, FD-7 version?)
   - Timestamps (execution, logging, rule effective date)
   - Evidence links (documents, email, HR records)

4. ENFORCEMENT WORKFLOW:
   a) Case registration: Assess risk_tier, country, group; populate case_metadata
   b) Concurrent checks: Log Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review to event_log
   c) Request_More_Info: Loop (0+ iterations) before decision; log each iteration
   d) Decision eligibility check: 
      - If auto_approve_eligible (all conditions met), allow Auto_Approve path
      - Otherwise, require Manual_Procurement_Approval
      - Check requester != approver (canonical IDs)
      - Check conflict-of-interest; escalate if undisclosed
   e) Activation: Verify all preconditions (checks, approval) completed before logging Activate
   f) Notification: Send and log with stable notification_id; CRM linkage via this ID
   g) Closure: Log close event; case_metadata.case_closed_timestamp

5. SCOPE BOUNDARIES:
   - PRE-SCREEN cases (rejected before M0 entry) are logged separately (out-of-scope for M0)
   - REACTIVATION cases use minimal flow (separate path)
   - NEW_ONBOARDING cases use main_flow
   - Case type is determined at registration and immutable

6. POST-AUDIT RECONCILIATION:
   - CRM migration: Reconcile notification_log with CRM via stable notification_id
   - Alias resolution: Validate actor_canonical_mapping; flag discrepancies
   - Conflict-of-interest: Periodic audit of undisclosed relationships
   - Policy overlay compliance: Quarterly review of PD-3/FD-7 usage; flag post-expiry cases

7. REMAINING LIMITATIONS:
   - POWL cannot express arbitrary loops; Request_More_Info approximated as 0-1 choice
     (Full loop support requires Petri-net or DECLARE; see suggested alternative below)
   - POWL cannot encode data-driven guards (eligibility, amount caps, relationship checks)

   - Reactivation scope still ambiguous (should prior cert be sufficient? Separate policy needed)
   - Pre-screen vs. M0 boundary still ambiguous (should rejected pre-screen leads be in M0? Separate intake process recommended)
```
