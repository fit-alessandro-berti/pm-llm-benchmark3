## 1. What is observed

The data are 40 branch-month summaries from five branches over January–August 2026. `automation` is an availability flag, not actual use: Alpha and Beta had the system from April, Gamma from July, and Delta/Epsilon had no availability during the observed window. Actual completion through the automated fast path rises gradually after adoption, from about 25–27% in the first adopted month to 48–50% by August. Oddly, nonadopters and pre-adoption months show a 4–6% value in `automated_fast_path_pct`; this suggests the variable may include manual fast-path completion or is otherwise not a clean automation-only dose measure.

Rollout was not random. Alpha and Beta were selected for high volume and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a short readiness pilot. Several operational changes coincided with adoption: a centralized fraud desk began serving all branches in March; routing directed about 10% more arrivals to Alpha/Beta from April onward and about 5% more to Gamma after July; FTE counts changed in several branches; and a May product change increased the recorded share of high-complexity cases. Outcomes are branch-month aggregates—median and p90 cycle time, rework, escalation, and mean CSAT. CSAT response rates varied between roughly 20% and 45%, but branch-month response counts are unavailable. No case-level route, queue-wait, fraud-desk wait, or complexity-at-case data were supplied.

## 2. Causal estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | population | time horizon | outcome | unit of analysis | interpretation | identifiable from supplied data? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Availability of automation: `automation=1` vs `0` in a branch-month, irrespective of actual fast-path use | All branches eligible for expansion, or the treated branch population | 1–4 months after availability | `median_cycle_h` | branch-month | Intention-to-treat-like effect of making automation available, including learning, imperfect use, and operational disruption | Only under staggered-DiD/parallel-trend assumptions; not experimentally identified |
| E2 | Same availability contrast as E1 | Same | 1–4 months | `p90_cycle_h` | branch-month | Effect on slow-tail cycle time | Same as E1; tail estimate is noisier |
| E3 | Same availability contrast as E1 | Same | 1–4 months | `rework_pct` | branch-month | Effect on repeated review/information-request loops | Weak; product-change and case-mix effects confound |
| E4 | Same availability contrast as E1 | Same | 1–4 months | `escalation_pct` | branch-month | Effect on share sent to specialist/fraud-desk review | Confounded by the March fraud-desk change |
| E5 | Same availability contrast as E1 | Same | 1–4 months | `csat_1to5` | branch-month | Effect on survey mean satisfaction | Not credible without response-count/nonresponse data |
| E6 | Increase actual `automated_fast_path_pct` by 10 percentage points | Branch-months with automation available | Same month | median/p90/rework/escalation/CSAT | branch-month | Dose-response effect of substituting automated fast-path completion for other routes | Only descriptive; dose is selected and time-confounded, no valid instrument |
| E7 | Cohort-specific availability effect by event time since adoption | Each adopting cohort | 0,1,2,3,4 months after adoption | `median_cycle_h` | branch-month | Dynamic learning/implementation effect | A staggered event study is possible but has very few treated cohorts |
| E8 | Spillover effect of adoption on never-treated branches through fraud desk/routing | Delta/Epsilon | From March onward | median/escalation/p90 | branch-month | Indirect network interference | Not identified: the fraud-desk and routing shocks affect all branches simultaneously |
| E9 | Per-case substitution of automated fast path for manual fast path | Cases eligible for fast path | Triage to close | cycle time | case | Ideal case-level treatment effect of automation | Not identifiable from branch-month aggregates; case-level routes are missing |

Interference and treatment-version issues are central. Availability is a “treatment version” whose content changes as teams learn and as the share of automated cases changes. The centralized fraud desk and routing campaign create spillovers: adopter arrival and referral patterns change the workloads and waiting times of other branches, violating stable-unit-treatment-value assumptions.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: staggered-adoption event study / cohort ATT, Callaway–Sant’Anna-style | Binary `automation=1` at first available month; relative-time indicators around adoption; no naive two-way fixed effects | Not-yet-treated branches: Gamma before July; Delta/Epsilon throughout; never-treated branches anchor month effects | Branch fixed effects; month fixed effects; optional secondary adjustments for complexity/arrivals/FTE only with caveats about post-treatment variables | Cohort-specific ATT of availability; pooled ATT; dynamic ATT by event time | Parallel trends conditional on branch/month; no anticipation; no interference/spillovers; no treatment-driven time-varying confounding; treatment effects allowed to be heterogeneous by cohort | Event-study pre-trend plot; cohort-specific estimates; placebo pseudo-adoption for Delta/Epsilon; sensitivity to dropping Gamma; wild-cluster bootstrap not reliable with 5 clusters | Too few branches; selection into adoption; simultaneous routing/product/fraud-desk changes; SUTVA violation |
| Alternative 1: synthetic control / augmented SCM | Calendar-time pre vs post adoption, separately for Alpha, Beta, Gamma | Weighted combination of never-treated donors (Delta/Epsilon; Gamma before July for its own counterfactual) matched on pre-period outcomes/covariates | Donor weights from pre-period outcome levels, trends, complexity, arrivals/FTE | ATT for each adopter | Donor combination reconstructs the counterfactual trend; no spillovers; adequate pre-period | Pre-treatment MSPE; placebo distributions; sensitivity to donor exclusion | Only 2–3 donors and 3–6 pre-periods; donor fit is poor for the selected weak branches |
| Alternative 2: comparative interrupted time series | Adoption as an immediate level and slope change in branch-specific linear trends | Branch’s own extrapolated pre-trend; nonadopters absorb common shocks | Branch fixed effects; linear month trend; optional controls | Immediate and gradual post-adoption changes | No other shocks at adoption; linear pre-trend; no anticipation | Lead/lag dummies; varying pre-window; compare with and without controls | Product change, routing, and fraud desk coincide with adoption; counterfactual extrapolation is fragile |

The primary strategy is preferable because it uses all branches, explicitly allows treatment effects to vary by cohort and calendar time, and avoids the biases of a single two-way fixed-effects coefficient when adoption is staggered and effects are dynamic. It also lets the never-treated branches absorb common shocks such as the product change and the fraud-desk launch. The main limitation is structural rather than technical: five branches is far too few for reliable cluster-robust inference, and the treated branches were selected for poor historical performance. Therefore, I treat the primary strategy as a disciplined descriptive comparison that can support ranges and qualitative conclusions, not as a precise causal machine.

## 4. Process-aware causal mechanisms

| mechanism | process artifact or metric affected | expected signature if beneficial | expected signature if harmful | evidence in supplied data | unresolved ambiguity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast-path substitution | `automated_fast_path_pct`; median cycle time | Fast-path share rises; median cycle time falls; no p90 increase | Fast-path share rises but errors/misrouting increase rework/escalation; median flat; p90 rises | Fast-path share rises from 4–6% to 25–50% in adopters; Alpha/Beta medians are roughly flat while p90 rises | Cannot separate route substitution from case-mix and volume changes |
| Specialist-queue congestion | `escalation_pct`; p90 cycle time | Automation sends only appropriate cases to specialists; no queue inflation | More borderline cases are sent to specialist/fraud desk; queue waits grow; tail times worsen | Escalation rises ~2–3 pp in all branches after the March fraud desk; Alpha/Beta p90 worsens; no queue-wait data | Fraud-desk waiting times are unrecorded |
| Learning and implementation fidelity | `automated_fast_path_pct` trajectory; event-time effects | Early effects are small and grow as use rises; later months improve | Early misconfiguration hurts outcomes; effects are negative before maturity | Fast-path use rises gradually; Alpha/Beta outcomes improve in August relative to May–June; Gamma is observed for only two post-months | Cannot separate learning from calendar shocks or case-mix composition |
| Case-mix or routing changes | `high_complexity_pct`; arrivals; p90 | Automation absorbs extra complexity/volume without tail degradation | Complexity and arrivals rise, creating longer cycles independent of automation | Complexity rises in May in all branches; Alpha/Beta/Gamma receive extra routed arrivals | No case-level complexity; the routing campaign changes both adopter and control workloads |
| Staffing pressure | FTE; arrivals/FTE; median/p90 | Automation reduces manual triage workload and offsets FTE losses | FTE cuts or volume growth overwhelm capacity; cycle times rise | Alpha loses 2 FTE while arrivals grow; Gamma loses 1 FTE; Beta gains 1; Delta gains 1 | Actual staff allocation to triage vs other work is unknown |
| Rework displacement | `rework_pct`; cycle time | Automated triage reduces manual information-request loops | Automation misroutes cases, causing later specialist rework | Rework drops in the first adopted month (Alpha 13.211.2; Beta 12.810.4; Gamma 14.612.6), then partly reverts | Rework definitions and error sources are not traced |
| Tail amplification | `p90_cycle_h`; median | Automation fast-paths easy cases, freeing specialists for complex work, so tail stays stable or improves | Automation cream-skims easy cases, leaving complex cases with fewer human resources, so p90 increases even if median falls | Alpha/Beta p90 increases after adoption; nonadopters also show p90 increases after the product change | Cannot distinguish “automation hurts tail” from “product change worsens tail” |
| Survey selection | CSAT mean | Satisfaction improves if fast-path cases are quicker and less burdensome | Nonresponse changes with automation; only satisfied or only dissatisfied customers respond | CSAT changes are small and mixed; response rates vary 20–45%; no branch-month counts | Nonresponse bias cannot be assessed |

Treatment intensity is analyzed descriptively with branch fixed effects. Because the fast-path share rises almost monotonically after adoption, this dose-response is not separately identifiable from calendar time. The within-branch slopes are approximately:

| Outcome | Slope per 1 pp `automated_fast_path_pct` |
|---|---:|
| `median_cycle_h` | 0.02 h |
| `p90_cycle_h` | +0.22 h |
| `rework_pct` | +0.01 pp |
| `escalation_pct` | +0.07 pp |
| `csat_1to5` | +0.0008 |

These slopes are descriptive associations, not causal dose-response estimates.

Pre-trend evidence is limited. Alpha/Beta do not show strong downward pre-trends in January–March, but they were selected for poor historical cycle times, so regression to the mean is a real possibility. Gamma has an increasing pre-adoption trend through June and then improves in July–August; however, Delta/Epsilon also show increasing then decreasing cycles over the same calendar period, suggesting a common shock from the product change/routing environment rather than a branch-specific regression artifact. With only three to six pre-periods and five branches, formal pre-trend tests have essentially no power.

Sensitivity ranges for the two central cycle-time outcomes:

| Scenario | Median-cycle implication | p90 implication |
|---|---|---|
| Assume control-branch changes are the correct counterfactual for Alpha/Beta | 6 to 3 h relative to control | 8 to 5 h relative to control, but own p90 rose |
| Assume Gamma’s own pre-trend extrapolation | about 3 h | about 1 h |
| Assume the May product change completely explains the control worsening and automation had no effect | 0 h | +10 h in adopters |
| Mature-use comparison, August vs pre-adoption | Alpha 5.6 h; Beta 0.9 h; Gamma 4.6 h | Alpha +2.7 h; Beta +6.3 h; Gamma 3.1 h |

These ranges show that a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” statement is not defensible. The median and p90 move differently and the causal identification is weak.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta were chosen for poor historical cycle times; Gamma came after a pilot | If poor performers regress to the mean, bias is toward apparent improvement; if they are chronically resource-limited, bias is unknown | Compare pre-period levels/trends; use never-treated branches; restrict claims to the selected profile | ATT is not generalizable to Delta/Epsilon |
| Nonparallel trends | No random assignment means treated and control branches may be on different trajectories | Unknown | Event-study pre-trend visualizations; synthetic control balance; sensitivity to dropping controls | Causal estimates are uninterpretable |
| Time-varying confounding / common shocks | Fraud desk, routing campaign, and product change coincide with adoption | Mixed: product change worsens all branches; routing increases adopter volumes; may mask or exaggerate effects | Month fixed effects; separate windows before/after product change; monitor arrivals/complexity | A single estimate conflates automation with operational changes |
| Treatment intensity / actual use | Binary availability hides gradual adoption; fast-path share is the real exposure | Binary estimates understate full-implementation effects; dose-response is confounded by time | Use `automated_fast_path_pct` as a secondary exposure; collect case-level route data | “Automation effect” is ambiguous |
| Spillovers through fraud desk/routing | Fraud desk is centralized; routing shifts cases into adopters; branch-level waits are absent | If adopters receive more complex/voluminous cases, bias is against automation; if controls are drained, controls look worse | Monitor referral volumes and queue waits by branch; model arrivals and referrals; avoid SUTVA-naive comparisons | Effects are contaminated by network interference |
| Staffing changes | Alpha lost 2 FTE; Gamma lost 1; Beta/Delta gained FTE | FTE losses bias toward worse cycle times; gains bias toward better outcomes | Adjust for FTE and arrivals/FTE; collect work allocation | Outcomes may reflect staffing rather than automation |
| Case-mix changes | High-complexity share rises in May across branches | Complexity increases lengthen cycles and tail times, masking benefits or worsening apparent harms | Stratify by complexity if case-level data exist; use registration-time risk class | Simpson’s paradox at branch-month aggregation |
| Anticipation / preparation | Gamma had a readiness pilot; managers may change practices before go-live | Pre-period improvements from anticipation make post effects look smaller | Check pre-trends; use “donut” estimates that drop the month before adoption | Event-time estimates are attenuated or pre-trends fail |
| Survey nonresponse | CSAT response rates vary 20–45%, counts unavailable | Unknown; happier respondents may be more likely to respond after automation | Collect response counts and sample frames; compare CSAT with complaint/administrative measures | CSAT effects are not identifiable |
| Measurement aggregation | Outcomes are branch-month medians/p90/percentages, not case-level records | Ecological bias; median changes may not reflect per-case effects; p90 is sensitive to few long cases | Require case-level timestamps and route flags; compute distributional summaries | “Automation reduces median cycle time” may not mean cases are faster |
| Small number of branches/clusters | Five clusters is far below the threshold for reliable cluster-robust inference | Standard errors are too small; false confidence | Use permutation/randomization inference or qualitative ranges; avoid p-values | Precise single-number claims are impossible |
| Dynamic and heterogeneous treatment effects | Effects likely vary by cohort and time since adoption; naive TWFE can be biased | Unknown | Estimate cohort-specific event-time effects; avoid pooling heterogeneous effects blindly | A pooled average may not represent any real policy effect |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

Choice: `expand with an embedded evaluation`.

Do not expand immediately to Delta and Epsilon at the same time. Instead, randomize the order of adoption between Delta and Epsilon, with Delta as the first candidate for operational safety and Epsilon as the simultaneous control. If leadership prefers a single next branch, start with Delta because its case mix is simpler and its outcomes are more stable; use Epsilon as the never-treated comparator for several months, then switch Epsilon after Delta has at least three post-adoption months. Randomizing which of the two goes first is still feasible and would materially improve causal learning.

Minimum measurements: use at least three pre-period months before any switch, ideally more, and at least three post-period months after each go-live. The existing data provide a retrospective baseline, but new prospective data should include case-level timestamps for registration, triage start/end, specialist review start/end, resolution, and closure; queue lengths and waiting times for specialist and fraud-desk review; the exact path taken by each case; registration-time complexity/risk class; survey response counts and denominator information; FTE allocation to triage and review work; and routing-source indicators. Process-mining analyses should be run on event logs, not on branch-month aggregates.

Spillover monitoring must be explicit. Track arrivals, high-complexity share, referral rates, and fraud-desk wait times in whichever branch is not yet treated. If the control branch’s outcome trends diverge sharply, the evaluation design is compromised. Guardrails: pause or dial back automation in the treated branch if p90 cycle time increases more than 15% from its pre-period average for two consecutive months, if CSAT falls by more than 0.2 points for two consecutive months, or if control-branch wait times or satisfaction deteriorate beyond pre-specified thresholds. The decision rule for full scale-up should be pre-registered: expand broadly only if the treated branch shows a median-cycle improvement that exceeds the control-branch change by at least the noise floor observed in the evaluation, with no deterioration in p90, rework, escalation wait times, or CSAT. Otherwise, redesign the triage configuration before scaling.

        row[f"post_{outcome}"] = post_df[outcome].mean()
        row[f"delta_{outcome}"] = post_df[outcome].mean() - pre_df[outcome].mean()
    return row

contrasts = [
    pre_post("Alpha", ["2026-01", "2026-02", "2026-03"], ["2026-04", "2026-05", "2026-06", "2026-07", "2026-08"]),
    pre_post("Beta", ["2026-01", "2026-02", "2026-03"], ["2026-04", "2026-05", "2026-06", "2026-07", "2026-08"]),
    pre_post("Gamma", ["2026-01", "2026-02", "2026-03", "2026-04", "2026-05", "2026-06"], ["2026-07", "2026-08"]),
]
contrast_df = pd.DataFrame(contrasts)
print("\nAdopter-specific pre/post contrasts")
print(contrast_df.to_string(index=False))

# Descriptive panel model with branch fixed effects and branch-clustered SEs.
# This is NOT a causal model. It only describes within-branch associations.
print("\nDescriptive branch-fixed-effects models (no calendar controls)")
for outcome in outcomes:
    formula = f"{outcome} ~ C(branch) + automated_fast_path_pct"
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    beta = model.params["automated_fast_path_pct"]
    se = model.bse["automated_fast_path_pct"]
    print(f"{outcome}: slope={beta:.4f}, cluster-SE={se:.4f}")

# Same, with calendar-month fixed effects. This is a more demanding descriptive
# model, but it is still not a causal estimate.
print("\nDescriptive branch + month fixed-effects models")
for outcome in outcomes:
    formula = f"{outcome} ~ C(branch) + C(month) + automated_fast_path_pct"
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    beta = model.params["automated_fast_path_pct"]
    se = model.bse["automated_fast_path_pct"]
    print(f"{outcome}: slope={beta:.4f}, cluster-SE={se:.4f}")

# Descriptive event-study panel model for median cycle time.
# It includes relative-time indicators and branch/month fixed effects.
# The rel_-1 indicator is omitted as the reference period.
print("\nDescriptive event-study model for median_cycle_h")
event_formula = (
    "median_cycle_h ~ C(branch) + C(month) + rel_-3 + rel_-2 + "
    "rel_0 + rel_1 + rel_2 + rel_3 + rel_4"
)
event_model = smf.ols(event_formula, data=df).fit(
    cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
)
print(event_model.params.filter(like="rel_").round(3))

# Explicit warnings.
print("\nWARNING: only 5 branches/clusters. Cluster-robust SEs are unreliable.")
print("WARNING: outcomes are branch-month aggregates, not case-level records.")
print("WARNING: these regressions are descriptive; they do not prove causation.")
print("WARNING: the automated_fast_path_pct column includes non-adopter baseline values,")
print("so it should not be treated as a pure automation dose without further data.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation was rolled out nonrandomly, with Alpha/Beta selected for high volume and poor historical cycle times and Gamma added after a pilot. In the raw data, Alpha/Beta median cycle times were roughly flat while their p90 times rose; Gamma’s median and p90 fell after July; nonadopters also worsened in May–June. This is arithmetic observation, not causal evidence.
- `Suggestive:` Compared with not-yet-treated and never-treated branches, adopter medians were about 3–6 hours better over the same calendar windows, and Gamma’s post-adoption improvement aligns with its July adoption. However, selection, routing, the fraud desk, staffing, and product changes make this only suggestive.
- `Conditionally causal:` Under strong assumptions—parallel trends conditional on branch and month effects, no anticipation, no spillovers, and no treatment-driven time-varying confounding—a staggered-adoption event study would identify an availability effect on median cycle time of roughly 4 hours, with wide uncertainty. The same assumptions do not support a causal effect on p90, rework, escalation, or CSAT.
- `Not supported:` The claim “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not supported, nor are claims that automation improved tail performance, reduced rework, changed customer satisfaction, or reduced escalation. The data are too aggregated, too few, and too confounded for a single quantitative causal statement.
