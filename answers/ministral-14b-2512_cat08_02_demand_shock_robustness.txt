# 1. Stress Scenarios
Four plausible demand/disruption scenarios that stress different resources and case types:

### **Scenario 1: Winter Infection Surge (Demand Mix Shift)**
- **Case mix**: Urgent (40%), Complex-panel (30%), Routine (20%), Repeat-after-failure (10%).
- **Disruptions**:
  - Courier arrival spikes at 10:00 AM and 5:00 PM (compression).
  - 1 medical validator absent (specialist-review queue doubles).
  - Assay machine M2 fails for 12 hours (M1 must handle all Urgent/Complex-panel).
- **Stress**: Pre-analytical check, medical validation, and assay machines (M1 overload).

### **Scenario 2: Equipment Outage + Staff Absence**
- **Case mix**: Urgent (25%), Complex-panel (25%), Routine (40%), Repeat-after-failure (10%).
- **Disruptions**:
  - Assay machine M1 out for maintenance (M2 handles all Urgent/Complex-panel).
  - Accession correction specialists both absent (recollection loops increase).
  - Courier delays push sample arrival times to 12–2 PM (batch fragmentation).
- **Stress**: Test preparation (batch waits), technical validation (rework), and medical validation (specialist review).

### **Scenario 3: Specialist Review Backlog**
- **Case mix**: Complex-panel (40%), Urgent (25%), Routine (25%), Repeat-after-failure (10%).
- **Disruptions**:
  - Specialist reviewer unavailable every Friday (queue builds Thursday).
  - 20% of Complex-panel cases require specialist review (vs. 13% normal).
  - Urgent cases bypass batching, fragmenting assay schedules.
- **Stress**: Medical validation, specialist review, and assay machines (repeats from poor validation).

### **Scenario 4: Courier Strike + Night Shift Repeats**
- **Case mix**: Routine (30%), Urgent (20%), Complex-panel (20%), Repeat-after-failure (30%).
- **Disruptions**:
  - Courier strike delays samples by 24 hours (arrival compression at 10:00 AM).
  - Night-shift assay runs have 30% higher failure rate (Repeat-after-failure spikes).
  - 1 technical validator cross-trained to accession correction (validation bottleneck).
- **Stress**: Pre-analytical check (recollection), assay machines (repeats), and technical validation.

# 2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis

2. **Secondary bottleneck**: **Medical validation** (Complex-panel cases need validation 3.1× more often).

3. **Tertiary bottleneck**: **Assay machine M1** (98% utilization).
   - Urgent cases bypass batching (58% in peak), fragmenting M1 schedule.

1. **Primary bottleneck**: **Specialist review** (87% utilization, 36h queue).

   - Night-shift failures double repeats; M2 overwhelmed.
   - *Relief*: Dedicate M1 to Urgent (I3)  but Routine cases queue for M2.
3. **Tertiary bottleneck**: **Technical validation** (cross-trained validators).
   - Validation queue grows (3.5h  8h) as repeats increase.

# 3. Robust Redesign Package
**Core interventions** (active in all scenarios):
1. **I6 (Early Pre-Analytical Triage) + I1 (Urgent-First Dispatch)**
   - *Why robust*: Reduces recollection loops (I6) and prevents Urgent starvation (I1).
   - *Assumptions*: Triage team can reject samples within 2h without adding delays to valid cases.
   - *Failure risk*: If triage is too strict, valid cases may be rejected (increases recollection).

2. **I3 (Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel) + I2 (Batch Routine to 24)**
   - *Why robust*: Protects Urgent SLAs (M1) while improving Routine efficiency (I2).
   - *Assumptions*: M2 can handle Routine without excessive repeats (monitor failure rates).
   - *Failure risk*: If M2 fails, Routine cases starve (use I4 to cross-train validators).

3. **I5 (Evening Medical-Validation Shift) + I10 (Outsource Complex-panel Overflow)**
   - *Why robust*: Covers specialist-review backlogs (I5) and reduces WIP (I10).
   - *Assumptions*: Partner lab can handle 20% of Complex-panel without violating Urgent SLAs.
   - *Failure risk*: Transport delay may cause Urgent cases to miss 18h SLA.

4. **I8 (Automate Registration for Digital Orders)**
   - *Why robust*: Reduces registration queue (68%  50%) and error rates.
   - *Assumptions*: 60% of orders are digital (monitor adoption rate).

**Critical dependencies**:
- **I6 + I1** must be paired to avoid Urgent starvation from triage delays.
- **I3 + I2** require real-time monitoring of M2 failure rates to adjust batch sizes.
- **I5 + I10** need a trigger for when to activate outsourcing (e.g., specialist queue > 12h).

# 6. Residual Vulnerabilities
1. **Redesign-Induced Fragility**:
   - **I3 (M1 dedication)** creates a single point of failure: if M1 fails, Urgent/Complex-panel cases stall.
   - *Mitigation*: Monitor M1 health in real-time; have a "failover" protocol to route Urgent cases to M2 (accepting longer runs).

2. **Unaddressed Weaknesses**:
   - **Night-shift assay failures**: Repeats spike after night shifts (Scenario 4), but no intervention targets this.
     - *Workaround*: Schedule high-reliability staff for night shifts or reduce batch sizes overnight.
   - **Physician communication**: Recollection loops (I6) may frustrate physicians if triage rejects valid samples.
     - *Workaround*: Automate rejection explanations with actionable feedback.

3. **Assumption Risks**:
   - **I10 (Outsourcing)** assumes the partner lab can handle 20% of Complex-panel without violating Urgent SLAs.
     - *Risk*: If transport delays exceed 12h, Urgent cases may miss SLAs.
   - **I2 (Batch Routine)** assumes M2 can handle Routine without repeats.
     - *Risk*: If M2 failure rate >5%, repeats will rise (monitor closely).

### **Key Insight**:
The redesign prioritizes **bottleneck migration awareness** (e.g., relieving pre-analytical check may overload medical validation) and **scenario-specific triggers** (e.g., outsourcing only when specialist queue exceeds 12h). Monitoring focuses on **leading indicators** (queues, failure rates) to preempt bottlenecks before they impact SLAs.
