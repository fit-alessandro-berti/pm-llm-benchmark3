# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Coverage and initial conditions.** I treat the 22 listed cases as the complete observed workload, with no unlisted cases or resource work. The nominal reconstruction starts empty immediately before 08:00. Under rounding, P01 may actually have arrived just before 08:00; that becomes initial WIP rather than an arrival within the window. Unlisted work would invalidate some missing-start bounds and make the reported busy times incomplete.

**A2 — Rounding and clock alignment.** Reported timestamps are nominal values. Each observed timestamp may move by approximately ±2.5 minutes, subject to process and resource constraints. Consequently, a difference between two observed timestamps generally has an uncertainty envelope of ±5 minutes; elapsed time measured against the fixed 14:00 cutoff has ±2.5-minute uncertainty. I assume no additional offset between the external-arrival clock and the operational clock. I do **not** assume independent or uniformly distributed rounding errors.

**A3 — Simultaneous events.** Queue and service intervals are half-open: \([q,s)\) and \([s,e)\). States are right-continuous, measured **after all events at a reported timestamp**. Completions release capacity, S1 completions simultaneously enter S2, and starts remove cases from queues. These changes are applied atomically; their processing order does not create artificial instantaneous peaks. Rate bins are also half-open, such as \([08{:}00,10{:}00)\).

**A4 — Service and capacity.** For the principal reconstruction, an activity occupies its named resource continuously from start to completion, without preemption, assistance, or multitasking. Thus its interval represents active service and resource busy time. Without this assumption, it represents an activity span, not necessarily hands-on work. Queues have no stated capacity limit, and I assume no upstream blocking. I do **not** impose FIFO or assume every waiting case is eligible for either resource.

**A5 — Maintenance.** The primary calculation treats C2’s 10:55–11:10 outage as an exact availability constraint. Available resource-minutes are therefore \(720\) at S1 and \(705\) at S2. Section 6 also considers rounded maintenance boundaries.

**A6 — Missing starts.** Missing starts are constrained by queue entry, preceding and following work on the same resource, completion, maintenance, and nonnegative service time. Three principal scenarios use the earliest feasible start, a **central five-minute-turnaround start**, and the latest feasible start. The central scenario is motivated by the many reported five-minute resource handovers; it is not an inferred probability distribution. A midpoint scenario is also shown. Zero service at the latest-start endpoint is a mathematical limiting bound, not a claim that a mandatory activity was skipped.

**A7 — Censoring.** An OPEN activity contributes service elapsed through 14:00, but its final service duration remains unknown. A waiting case contributes queue time accrued through 14:00, not a completed waiting duration. No future duration or independent-censoring assumption is imposed.

**A8 — PM4Py projection.** Case-level enrichment uses only completed cases, explicitly flagged central imputations, and a zero-duration marker at the supplied external arrival. That marker establishes the case boundary; it is not an additional service activity.

All durations below are in **minutes**. Unless explicitly described as rounding envelopes, numerical ranges vary missing starts while holding observed timestamps at their reported values.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case \(i\), let \(a_i\) denote external arrival, \(s_{ij}\) service start, and \(e_{ij}\) service completion at station \(j\). Queue entry is

$$
q_{i1}=a_i,\qquad q_{i2}=e_{i1}.
$$

For a completed station visit:

$$
W_{ij}=s_{ij}-q_{ij},\qquad
V_{ij}=e_{ij}-s_{ij},\qquad
Z_{ij}=e_{ij}-q_{ij}=W_{ij}+V_{ij}.
$$

These are queue waiting time, active service time, and station sojourn time, respectively. For a completed case:

$$
T_i=e_{i2}-a_i=Z_{i1}+Z_{i2}.
$$

For OPEN service, \(14{:}00-s_{ij}\) is elapsed service, not \(V_{ij}\). For a case still waiting, \(14{:}00-q_{ij}\) is accrued waiting, not final \(W_{ij}\).

### Missing-start constraints

With reported timestamps fixed, an isolated missing start satisfies

$$
L_i=\max(q_i,e_{\text{previous job}},\text{applicable availability boundary}),
$$

$$
U_i=\min(e_i,s_{\text{next job}}).
$$

The maintenance restriction is applied to the relevant uninterrupted availability interval.

| Missing start | Preceding completion | Following start | Feasible nominal start | Central start | Implied waiting / service ranges |
| ------------- | -------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------- |
| P05, S2/C1    | P03, 09:45           | P07, 10:20      | 09:45–10:15            | 09:50         | Waiting 20–50; service 0–30      |
| P07, S1/I1    | P05, 09:25           | P09, 10:05      | 09:25–10:00            | 09:30         | Waiting 40–75; service 0–35      |
| P11, S2/C2    | P08, 12:00           | P13, 12:30      | 12:00–12:25            | 12:05         | Waiting 55–80; service 0–25      |
| P15, S1/I1    | P13, 11:40           | P17, 12:20      | 11:40–12:15            | 11:45         | Waiting 90–125; service 0–35     |

For example, P11 cannot simply be assigned a start near its 11:05 queue entry: C2 is under maintenance and subsequently executes P08 through noon. Under A4, P11 must fit after P08.

Waiting and service ranges are **coupled**. For P05, \(W_{52}+V_{52}=50\): the largest wait and largest service cannot occur together.

### Queue, WIP, rates, and utilization

At time \(t\),

$$
Q_j(t)=\sum_i \mathbf1\{q_{ij}\le t<s_{ij}\},
\qquad
B_j(t)=\sum_i \mathbf1\{s_{ij}\le t<e_{ij}\},
$$

$$
N_j(t)=Q_j(t)+B_j(t),\qquad N_{\mathrm{system}}(t)=N_1(t)+N_2(t).
$$

Unobserved future starts/completions are beyond the cutoff, not replaced by completed events at 14:00.

Time averages are areas divided by window length:

$$
\bar Q_j=\frac{\int Q_j(t)\,dt}{360},\qquad
\bar N_j=\frac{\int N_j(t)\,dt}{360}.
$$

For interval \(I\), arrival and departure rates are counts divided by its duration in hours. S2 arrivals are **S1 completions**, not external arrivals or S2 starts.

Resource busy time is the length of the union of its service intervals intersected with the analysis window. Under the non-overlap constraints, this equals the sum of their clipped durations. Utilization is

$$
U_j=
\frac{\text{busy resource-minutes}}
{\int_I c_j(t)\,dt}.
$$

For rounding sensitivity, observed times become bounded variables and process/resource ordering becomes linear inequalities. Busy-time minima and maxima are optimized jointly; independently expanding every duration could otherwise create impossible overlaps.

For WIP, conservative pointwise envelopes can also be formed from timestamp intervals:

$$
N_j^-(t)=\sum_i\mathbf1\{q_{ij}^{+}\le t<e_{ij}^{-}\},
\quad
N_j^+(t)=\sum_i\mathbf1\{q_{ij}^{-}\le t<e_{ij}^{+}\}.
$$

These are marginal envelopes, not necessarily simultaneously attainable trajectories.

### Little’s-Law assessment

**No subinterval provides convincing evidence of equilibrium, stable flow, and negligible boundary effects together.** The process starts empty, queues build, maintenance changes capacity, and arrivals subsequently collapse. I therefore do not use \(L/\lambda\) to estimate a steady-state mean sojourn.

A finite-window area audit is nevertheless exact for the nominal reconstruction:

$$
\int N_1(t)\,dt
=20(92.25)+130+110
=2,085\text{ case-minutes},
$$

$$
\int N_2(t)\,dt
=14(70)+(105+85+70+50+35+15)
=1,340\text{ case-minutes}.
$$

The central waiting/service decomposition agrees:

$$
2,085=1,490+595,\qquad 1,340=780+560.
$$

For illustration, incorrectly combining S2’s completed-case mean sojourn with its departure rate gives

$$
(14/6)(70/60)=2.72,
$$

rather than measured mean WIP \(3.72\). The missing **1.00 case-equivalent** is exactly the unfinished visits’ \(360/360\) contribution. This is a boundary-accounting issue, not evidence that Little’s Law has failed.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**The evidence supports a time-varying combination: S1 is the dominant early queueing pressure point, while S2 becomes the more prominent downstream constraint. This does not prove a single structural bottleneck literally switches at a particular time.**

### Early morning: S1 absorbs an arrival surge

Between 08:00 and 10:00, external arrivals average **6.5/hour**, versus **3 S1 completions/hour**. S1’s queue grows substantially, and its waiting times rise from five minutes for P01/P02 to much longer waits for subsequent cases.

S2 initially has little work because no case reaches it before the first S1 completion. Consequently, low early S2 utilization partly reflects pipeline startup, not excess sustainable capacity.

### Mid-morning: both stations accumulate work

From 10:00 to noon, S1 receives eight cases and completes seven; S2 receives seven and completes five. Both remain pressured, but S2’s backlog increasingly matters.

Fully observed completed service intervals average **28.1 minutes at S1** versus **40 minutes at S2**. That supports a slower downstream service stage with the same nominal resource count. It does not establish sustainable capacity from a small, completion-selected sample.

For orientation, dividing 120 resource-minutes/hour by the central completed-service means gives approximately **4.25 cases/hour at S1** and **3.2 at S2**, before handover gaps, eligibility restrictions, maintenance, or changing case mix. These are service-only benchmarks, not measured capacity guarantees.

### Maintenance: a local amplifier, not the sole explanation

C2’s outage removes **15 resource-minutes**, halving S2’s available resource count during that interval. In the nominal reconstruction, its queue is already nonempty beforehand and contains two or three waiting cases during the outage.

C2 has a reported gap from **10:50 to 11:15**. Maintenance explains 15 of those 25 minutes; the remaining nominal ten minutes could involve preparation, scheduling around a nonpreemptive outage, logging effects, or ordinary handover.

Removing maintenance does **not** justify subtracting 15 minutes from every affected wait. The counterfactual start sequence and propagation to later cases are unknown.

### Afternoon: upstream drainage, persistent downstream congestion

After noon, only P22 arrives externally, while S1 completes seven services. Its backlog drains. S2 continues receiving those completions and finishes with six cases present: four waiting and two active.

The S2 queue is not monotonically increasing—its post-event queue happens to equal four at both noon and 14:00—but its hourly mean rises from approximately **3.17 during 12:00–13:00** to **4.17 during 13:00–14:00**.

There are also dispatching questions distinct from aggregate capacity. P09 starts S2 before earlier-queued P08, and P17 starts while earlier-queued P16 remains waiting. These are incompatible with strict station-wide FIFO under the supplied records, even allowing rounding. They may reflect eligibility, prioritization, or dispatching choices; the data do not identify which.

Finally, I2 finishes P20 at 13:45 while P22 remains waiting through 14:00. Rounding alone cannot eliminate that entire gap: its remaining length is approximately 12.5–17.5 minutes. Eligibility or unrecorded work could explain it, but simple work-conserving pooled service is not demonstrated.

**Recommendation:** validate dispatch eligibility, missing starts, and the final I2 idle period before interpreting nominal utilization as staffing efficiency. If the observed flow pattern recurs, protecting calibration availability and moderating the early arrival surge are more defensible tests than increasing inspection capacity alone.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

### Missing-start scenarios

“Early” is optimistic for waiting but produces more service work; “late” is pessimistic for waiting but approaches zero-duration missing activities. Neither is universally operationally optimistic.

| Scenario   | Missing-start rule             | S1 mean ended wait | S1 busy / utilization | S1 mean queue | S2 mean ended wait | S2 busy / utilization | S2 mean queue |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | -----------------: | --------------------: | ------------: | -----------------: | --------------------: | ------------: |
| Early      | Earliest feasible start        |               65.2 |           605 / 84.0% |          4.11 |               36.6 |           570 / 80.9% |          2.14 |
| Central    | Five minutes after lower bound |               65.7 |           595 / 82.6% |          4.14 |               37.2 |           560 / 79.4% |          2.17 |
| Midpoint   | Midpoint of feasible interval  |               66.9 |           570 / 79.2% |          4.21 |               38.3 |         542.5 / 77.0% |          2.22 |
| Late limit | Start approaches completion    |               68.6 |           535 / 74.3% |          4.31 |               40.0 |           515 / 73.0% |          2.29 |

**Stable across these scenarios:** station arrivals and completions, completed-case totals, station sojourns, nominal WIP trajectories, final populations, and the substantial buildup at both queues.

**Not stable:** the exact waiting/service decomposition, individual busy times, and utilization rankings. Missing starts can vary independently across stations; combining S1’s late scenario with S2’s early scenario already reverses their full-window utilization ordering.

A notable data-quality finding survives the nominal scenarios: P05 and P11 have shorter feasible calibration intervals than the fully observed calibration mean. That could be genuine case heterogeneity, but it also warrants checking whether “start” consistently denotes the beginning of the whole activity. There is no evidence supporting missing-at-random treatment.

Mean WIP is less sensitive: conservative marginal envelopes are approximately **5.50–6.08 at S1** and **3.49–3.96 at S2**. Their extremes need not be jointly attainable because an S1 completion is the same event as an S2 arrival.

Pointwise peaks deserve more caution than areas. Around 11:30, the S1 arrival and completion could occur in the opposite physical order, briefly producing WIP nine rather than the nominal peak eight. The reported equal-time C1 handover from P07 to P09 likewise must be constrained to avoid creating a spurious overlap. No supplied record forces genuine multitasking.

### Censoring and maintenance sensitivity

P15’s and P21’s 30 elapsed service minutes become **27.5–32.5** under rounding; P17’s five become **2.5–7.5**. None becomes an estimate of final service duration. P16’s accrued S2 wait becomes **82.5–87.5**, while its eventual wait remains unbounded above.

If the maintenance boundaries are also rounded, the outage could last approximately **10–20 minutes**, giving S2 **700–710 available resource-minutes**. Holding central busy time fixed, utilization becomes approximately **78.9–80.0%**, rather than 79.4%. This denominator sensitivity is much smaller than the full service-timestamp uncertainty.

T0 = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 08:00")
T1 = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 14:00")
OUTAGE_START = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 10:55")
OUTAGE_END = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 11:10")
DELTA = pd.Timedelta(minutes=2.5)
TIME_COLS = ["arrival_s1", "s1_start", "s1_end", "s2_start", "s2_end"]

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(DATA), keep_default_na=False)


def status(value: str) -> str:
    if value in {"MISSING", "OPEN"}:
        return value
    return "BLANK" if value == "" else "OBSERVED"


# NaT is accompanied by a status column: missing, open, and blank remain distinct.
for col in TIME_COLS:
    df[col + "_status"] = df[col].map(status)
    observed = df[col + "_status"].eq("OBSERVED")
    values = df[col].where(observed).map(
        lambda value: "2026-05-12 " + value
        if isinstance(value, str) else value
    )
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime(values, errors="raise")

rows = []
for r in df.to_dict("records"):
    for station in ["s1", "s2"]:
        entry = r["arrival_s1"] if station == "s1" else r["s1_end"]
        if pd.isna(entry):
            continue  # S2 not reached: do not create an S2 queue member.
        rows.append({
            "case": r["case"], "station": station.upper(),
            "entry": entry, "start": r[station + "_start"],
            "end": r[station + "_end"],
            "resource": r[station + "_resource"],
            "start_status": r[station + "_start_status"],
            "end_status": r[station + "_end_status"],
        })
base = pd.DataFrame(rows)
base["start_lo"] = base["start"]
base["start_hi"] = base["start"]

# Dataset-specific missing starts are isolated between known neighboring jobs.
for resource, group in base[base.resource.ne("")].groupby("resource"):
    order = (
        group.assign(stop=group.end.fillna(T1))
        .sort_values("stop").index.tolist()
    )
    for k, ix in enumerate(order):
        if base.at[ix, "start_status"] != "MISSING":
            continue
        previous_end = base.at[order[k - 1], "end"] if k else T0
        next_start = (
            base.at[order[k + 1], "start"] if k + 1 < len(order) else T1
        )
        lower = max(base.at[ix, "entry"], previous_end)
        upper = min(base.at[ix, "end"], next_start)
        if resource == "C2":
            lower = max(lower, OUTAGE_END)
        assert lower <= upper
        base.at[ix, "start_lo"] = lower
        base.at[ix, "start_hi"] = upper


def make_scenario(name: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = base.copy()
    missing = x.start_status.eq("MISSING")
    if name == "early":
        values = x.start_lo
    elif name == "late":
        values = x.start_hi  # Closure/limiting scenario, not physical zero work.
    elif name == "midpoint":
        values = x.start_lo + (x.start_hi - x.start_lo) / 2
    elif name == "central":
        values = pd.concat(
            [x.start_lo + pd.Timedelta(minutes=5), x.start_hi], axis=1
        ).min(axis=1)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown scenario: {name}")
    x.loc[missing, "start"] = values[missing]

    # Integration boundaries are separate from actual completion timestamps.
    x["stop"] = x.end.fillna(T1)
    x["wait_stop"] = x.start.fillna(T1)
    x["wait_min"] = (x.wait_stop - x.entry).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    x["service_min"] = (
        (x.stop - x.start).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    ).fillna(0)
    x["sojourn_min"] = (x.stop - x.entry).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    x["final_wait_min"] = x.wait_min.where(x.start.notna())
    x["final_service_min"] = x.service_min.where(x.end.notna())
    return x


def states(x: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    ticks = sorted(
        {T0, T1} | set(x.entry) | set(x.start.dropna()) | set(x.stop)
    )
    result = []
    for t in ticks:
        if not T0 <= t <= T1:
            continue
        row = {"time": t.strftime("%H:%M:%S")}
        for station, group in x.groupby("station"):
            # At T1, OPEN and waiting cases remain present.
            inside = (
                (group.entry <= t)
                & (group.end.isna() | (group.end > t))
            )
            active = inside & group.start.notna() & (group.start <= t)
            row[station + "_Q"] = int((inside & ~active).sum())
            row[station + "_B"] = int(active.sum())
            row[station + "_N"] = int(inside.sum())
        row["total"] = row["S1_N"] + row["S2_N"]
        result.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(result)


def summarize(x: pd.DataFrame, name: str) -> list:
    path = states(x)
    result = []
    for station, group in x.groupby("station"):
        started = group.start.notna()
        complete = group.end.notna()
        available = 720 if station == "S1" else 705
        result.append({
            "scenario": name, "station": station,
            "arrivals": len(group), "starts": int(started.sum()),
            "completions": int(complete.sum()),
            "mean_wait_started": group.loc[started, "wait_min"].mean(),
            "mean_service_completed":
                group.loc[complete, "service_min"].mean(),
            "mean_sojourn_complete":
                group.loc[complete, "sojourn_min"].mean(),
            "meanQ": group.wait_min.sum() / 360,
            "meanN": group.sojourn_min.sum() / 360,
            "peakQ": path[station + "_Q"].max(),
            "peakN": path[station + "_N"].max(),
            "busy": group.service_min.sum(),
            "util": group.service_min.sum() / available,
        })
    return result


# Joint rounding model: observed timestamps are bounded variables;
# missing starts are variables constrained by precedence and resource order.
keys, bounds = [], []
for r in df.to_dict("records"):
    for col in TIME_COLS:
        flag = r[col + "_status"]
        if flag == "OBSERVED":
            minute = (r[col] - T0).total_seconds() / 60
            keys.append((r["case"], col))
            bounds.append((minute - 2.5, minute + 2.5))
        elif flag == "MISSING":
            keys.append((r["case"], col))
            bounds.append((0, 360))

position = {key: i for i, key in enumerate(keys)}
A_ub, b_ub = [], []


def less_equal(left, right) -> None:
    """Add timestamp/constant constraint left <= right."""
    coefficients = np.zeros(len(keys))
    constant = 0
    for sign, term in [(1, left), (-1, right)]:
        if isinstance(term, tuple):
            coefficients[position[term]] += sign
        else:
            constant += sign * term
    A_ub.append(coefficients)
    b_ub.append(-constant)


for r in df.to_dict("records"):
    case = r["case"]
    if (case, "s1_start") in position:
        less_equal((case, "arrival_s1"), (case, "s1_start"))
    for station in ["s1", "s2"]:
        if (case, station + "_start") in position:
            end = (
                (case, station + "_end")
                if (case, station + "_end") in position else 360
            )
            less_equal((case, station + "_start"), end)
    if (case, "s2_start") in position:
        less_equal((case, "s1_end"), (case, "s2_start"))

for resource, group in base[base.resource.ne("")].groupby("resource"):
    sequence = (
        group.assign(stop=group.end.fillna(T1))
        .sort_values("stop").to_dict("records")
    )
    for previous, following in zip(sequence, sequence[1:]):
        less_equal(
            (previous["case"], previous["station"].lower() + "_end"),
            (following["case"], following["station"].lower() + "_start"),
        )
    if resource == "C2":
        # Given this dataset, every C2 job is unambiguously before or after
        # the outage; no mixed-integer selection of availability periods is needed.
        for r in sequence:
            case, station = r["case"], r["station"].lower()
            if pd.notna(r["end"]) and r["end"] <= OUTAGE_START:
                less_equal((case, station + "_end"), 175)
            else:
                less_equal(190, (case, station + "_start"))

A_ub, b_ub = np.array(A_ub), np.array(b_ub)


def optimize(terms: list, constant: float = 0) -> list:
    objective = np.zeros(len(keys))
    for key, coefficient in terms:
        objective[position[key]] += coefficient
    result = []
    for sign in [1, -1]:
        solution = linprog(
            sign * objective, A_ub=A_ub, b_ub=b_ub,
            bounds=bounds, method="highs"
        )
        if not solution.success:
            raise RuntimeError(solution.message)
        result.append(sign * solution.fun + constant)
    return result


def show(title: str, table: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    print("\n" + title)
    print(table.round(2).to_string(index=False))


summaries = []
for name in ["early", "central", "midpoint", "late"]:
    scenario = make_scenario(name)
    for resource, group in scenario[
        scenario.resource.ne("")
    ].groupby("resource"):
        group = group.sort_values("start")
        assert (
            group.stop.shift().dropna().to_numpy()
            <= group.start.iloc[1:].to_numpy()
        ).all()
        if resource == "C2":
            assert not (
                (group.start < OUTAGE_END) & (group.stop > OUTAGE_START)
            ).any()
    summaries.extend(summarize(scenario, name))

show("Nominal missing-start bounds", base[
    base.start_status.eq("MISSING")
][["case", "station", "entry", "start_lo", "start_hi", "end"]])
show("Station summaries: times in minutes; utilization is a fraction",
     pd.DataFrame(summaries))

central = make_scenario("central")
focus = ["P05", "P07", "P11", "P15", "P16", "P17", "P21", "P22"]
show(
    "Focused durations: service_min is elapsed for OPEN, zero accrued for BLANK",

        "case", "station", "wait_min", "service_min", "final_service_min",
        "sojourn_min", "start_status", "end_status"

)

path = states(central)
snapshots = [
    "08:00:00", "09:00:00", "10:00:00", "10:55:00", "11:10:00",
    "11:30:00", "12:00:00", "13:00:00", "13:45:00", "14:00:00"

show("Selected post-event states", path[path.time.isin(snapshots)])

period_rows = []
for offset in [0, 120, 240]:
    left = T0 + pd.Timedelta(minutes=offset)
    right = left + pd.Timedelta(minutes=120)
    for station, group in central.groupby("station"):
        arrivals = int(((group.entry >= left) & (group.entry < right)).sum())
        departures = int(((group.end >= left) & (group.end < right)).sum())

show("Half-open period rates", pd.DataFrame(period_rows))

resource_rows = []
for resource, group in base[base.resource.ne("")].groupby("resource"):
    terms, constant = [], 0
    for r in group.to_dict("records"):
        case, station = r["case"], r["station"].lower()
        terms.append(((case, station + "_start"), -1))
        if pd.notna(r["end"]):
            terms.append(((case, station + "_end"), 1))

    lower, upper = optimize(terms, constant)

area_rows = []
for station, group in central.groupby("station"):

# Direct station-specific pandas calculations are safer here than
# pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals: queue entries differ by station,
# MISSING is interval-valued, and OPEN is censored. stop=T1 must never be
# interpreted as an actual completion by a generic interval conversion.
try:
    import pm4py
except ImportError:
    print("\nPM4Py demonstration not run: install pm4py and rerun.")
else:
    def to_pm(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        usable = frame[frame.start.notna() & frame.end.notna()].copy()
        usable["start_imputed"] = usable.start_status.eq("MISSING")
        usable = usable.rename(columns={
            "case": "case:concept:name", "station": "concept:name",
            "start": "start_timestamp", "end": "time:timestamp",
            "resource": "org:resource",
        })
        usable["concept:name"] = usable["concept:name"].map({
            "S1": "Incoming Inspection", "S2": "Calibration"
        })
        return usable[[
            "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "start_timestamp",
            "time:timestamp", "org:resource", "start_imputed"
        ]]

    format_args = {
        "case_id": "case:concept:name", "activity_key": "concept:name",
        "timestamp_key": "time:timestamp",
        "start_timestamp_key": "start_timestamp",
    }

    # 30 fully observed completed interval events; incomplete cases can
    # contribute usable events, but are NOT treated as completed cases.
    interval_events = pm4py.format_dataframe(to_pm(base), **format_args)
    show("Observed PM4Py-compatible interval events (sample)",
         interval_events.head(4))

    completed = df[df.s2_end.notna()]
    projection = to_pm(
        central[central["case"].isin(completed["case"])]
    )
    markers = pd.DataFrame({
        "case:concept:name": completed["case"].to_numpy(),
        "concept:name": "External Arrival",
        "start_timestamp": completed.arrival_s1.to_numpy(),
        "time:timestamp": completed.arrival_s1.to_numpy(),
        "org:resource": "NO_RESOURCE",
        "start_imputed": False,
    })
    # Markers have zero service and encode the actual supplied case boundary.
    projection = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        pd.concat([projection, markers], ignore_index=True), **format_args
    )
    enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        projection.copy(), start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
    )
    enriched = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(
        enriched, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
        arrival_rate_column="case_interarrival_s",
        finish_rate_column="case_interfinish_s",
    )

    output = enriched.drop_duplicates(
        "case:concept:name"
    ).set_index("case:concept:name")
    output = output[[
        "@@service_time", "@@waiting_time", "@@sojourn_time",
        "case_interarrival_s", "case_interfinish_s"
    ]] / 60
    output.columns = [
        "case_service_min", "case_wait_min", "case_sojourn_min",
        "cohort_interarrival_min", "cohort_interfinish_min"
    ]
    # Helper boundary zeros do not establish genuine zero intercase gaps.
    first_arrival = completed.sort_values("arrival_s1").iloc[0]["case"]
    first_finish = completed.sort_values("s2_end").iloc[0]["case"]
    output.loc[first_arrival, "cohort_interarrival_min"] = np.nan
    output.loc[first_finish, "cohort_interfinish_min"] = np.nan

    expected = case_out.set_index("case").loc[output.index, "final_total_min"]
    assert np.allclose(output.case_sojourn_min, expected)
    show(
        "CASE-LEVEL completed-cohort annotations; gaps are MINUTES, not station rates",
        output.reset_index()
    )
