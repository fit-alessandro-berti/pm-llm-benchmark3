**Inside the boundary (cradle-to-gate of the distributor's own operations):**

| In-scope emission/resource driver | Event attributes and paths used |
|---|---|
| Production energy | `energy_kwh` on `CNC production`, `Heat treatment`, `Inspection`, `Rework production`; `machine_group` = CNC_A / CNC_B / heat_treat / inspection_cell |
| Outbound transport | `transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode` = parcel_ground / parcel_air / dedicated_van / consolidated_truck on `Ship ground`, `Ship air`, `Dedicated van`, `Consolidated truck` |

# 3. Intervention options

### S1 — Green shipping recommender (promised lead time × route reliability × customer segment)
- **Target path / segment:** `Package standard  Ship air` (620 cases) and `Package reinforced  Ship air` (800 cases), especially **Standard-stock Service-contract**, where SP1 is violated in **22%** of cases.
- **Mechanism:** Routes to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` whenever predicted on-time probability > 85% and remaining slack exceeds consolidation/pick time. Mechanically implements SP1 as a system suggestion instead of a paper policy.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Neutral-to-positive if the 85% rule holds — the prompt states many SS Service-contract orders "are shipped by air even when promised lead time still allows ground plus priority picking", i.e. air is currently *unearned*. Must not touch Critical-downtime air, and must not remove air capacity for Service-contract (R1 requires an alternative route during carrier disruption).
- **Evidence to monitor:** SP1 conformance (78%  target >95%), T2 avoidable-air share, segment late rates, `route_mode` choice vs `promised_lead_time_hours` slack distribution, override reasons from S12.

### S2 — Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with segment-specific wait caps
- **Target path / segment:** `Plan production  Batch wait  CNC production  Heat treatment  Inspection` (3,020 batch-wait cases; 13% of volume on the efficient path).
- **Mechanism:** Fill batches up to a size that still satisfies a **maximum wait cap** rather than maximising batch size: e.g. Critical = no batching / 4h; Service-contract MTO 12h; Regular MTO 24–30h. This captures most of the CNC_A gain (2.8  1.3 kWh/item between batch 1–3 and 12–20) without incurring the full 4h  38h wait, and keeps heat-treatment waits below the level that produces the 1.7× late risk.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Prevents the known service penalty of batching; explicitly caps obsolescence exposure (6% of batch items unsold at 14 days). Downside: smaller batches raise kWh/item (rebound, see §5) and more, smaller heat-treat runs may raise changeover scrap.
- **Evidence to monitor:** B1/B2/B3 (wait vs kWh per batch band), heat-treat wait vs late delivery, M4 obsolescence by `batch_id`, batch-size distribution shift, per-segment late rate.

### S3 — Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A where queue and due-date slack allow
- **Target path / segment:** MTO cases currently on the high-rework variant `MTO  CNC_B  inspection  rework  ground` (5% of volume, 160h median, **32% late**, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
- **Mechanism:** CNC_B rework rate is **18% vs 9%** on CNC_A. Halving rework attacks the dominant MTO material and energy driver simultaneously (MTO = ~87% of energy and ~85% of scrap in the portfolio) — this is the single most leverage-heavy environmental intervention, because it removes waste rather than shifting it.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** CNC_A is slower, so the shift is conditional on due-date slack; applied unconditionally it would push MTO p90 above 180h. Requires batching (S2) to be energy-efficient, otherwise CNC_A at batch 1–3 uses 2.8 kWh/item and *increases* energy — a genuine reversal risk. Adds dependence on CNC_A capacity  queue risk; CNC_B must stay warm as an alternative (resilience).
- **Evidence to monitor:** `machine_group` routing rate, `rework_flag` rate by machine, M1/Q1i/Q2, CNC_A queue length, energy per item vs `batch_size`, MTO p90 and late rate by machine.

### S4 — Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract when slack suffices
- **Target path / segment:** `Pick from stock  Package standard  Ship air` (Stockstandard packair, 5% of cases, 17h, 3% late, 18.5 kgCO2e vs 3.6 ground).
- **Mechanism:** Uses existing stock availability — the order is already in inventory — so the only lever needed is a **priority pick** to recover time, then `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck`. Directly closes the 22% SP1 violation.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Must be conditioned on slack: keep air where ground p90 (62h for stock) would breach `promised_lead_time_hours`. Priority pick adds picking congestion — monitor `Package standard` start delays. Quality neutral. Resilience: retain air as an available route for Service-contract (R1).
- **Evidence to monitor:** T2, SP1 conformance, p90 of the resulting variant, priority-pick queue time, `route_mode` × `customer_type` cross-tab, air-capacity utilisation for disruption buffer.

### S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders
- **Target path / segment:** `Package standard  Consolidated truck` (850 cases today) extended to eligible `Ship ground` cases (5,100 + 1,560 = 6,660 ground cases).
- **Mechanism:** Consolidated truck cuts transport CO2e by **35–55%** vs individual ground parcels; twice-daily cutoffs bound the 12–24h consolidation wait at ~12h average.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Cycle time rises 12–24h for consolidated cases — acceptable for the 42% low-impact stock path (34h median  up to ~58h) but **unacceptable for Critical-downtime and any case with < 24h slack**, so those are excluded by rule. Resilience: consolidation introduces a single daily/twice-daily bottleneck; parcel_ground must remain a live alternative (R1) during carrier disruption.
- **Evidence to monitor:** T3 coverage and consolidation wait, p90 per affected variant, late rate vs baseline 7–8%, cutoff-miss rate, route availability count for Service-contract (R1), carrier disruption events.

### S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment
- **Target path / segment:** edges `Package standard  Rework packaging` (260) and `Package reinforced  Rework packaging` (360); then the 550 loops back into `Package standard`/`Package reinforced`.
- **Mechanism:** Packaging rework occurs when packaging choice is incompatible with `route_mode` or part geometry. A pre-ship compatibility rule (part geometry × route × weight  pack spec) removes a **double-packaging cycle**: extra `package_material_kg`, extra handling time, and downstream damage if the wrong pack was used initially.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Saves cycle time (packaging rework adds a loop on cases already at 160h for MTO) and reduces material; low risk. Guard against over-application of reinforced packaging (it *doubles* material) — combine with Q1's certified low-damage exception.
- **Evidence to monitor:** M3 packaging-rework rate, `package_material_kg` per order and per kg shipped, compatibility-violation reason codes, damage/return proxy Q3, packaging loop depth.

### S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
- **Target path / segment:** `Inspection  Rework production` (470 cases) concentrated on CNC_B and heavy MTO parts; root cause is rushed inspection in `inspection_cell` queues.
- **Mechanism:** A dedicated gate (queue-priority rule or added check step) on the high-rework machine/part cells catches defects *before* packaging and shipping, converting late escapes into early, cheaper rework. Environmental gain comes from avoided scrap (1.8 kg on rework paths vs 0.70 on the good path), avoided second production cycle (32 kWh vs 16 kWh), and avoided packaging rework/returns.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Adds inspection queue time to affected MTO cases  must be offset by S2/S3 slack; if it delays, it can *cause* air shipment (rebound). Quality clearly improves. Resilience: gate must not become a single point of failure — allow a documented bypass under disruption with S12 logging.
- **Evidence to monitor:** Q1i escape rate by `machine_group`, Q2 loop depth, M1 scrap, MTO cycle time and late rate, inspection queue length, air-shipment rate of gated cases (rebound check).

### S8 — Replace *some* dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes
- **Target path / segment:** `Package reinforced  Dedicated van` (620 cases) within Critical-downtime Service-contract (11% of orders, **38 kgCO2e/order**, ~40% of total transport), excluding true-downtime emergencies identified by S9.
- **Mechanism:** Vans often return empty; a shared scheduled emergency lane amortises the leg across several urgent orders.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** **Only permissible with an SLA**: the shared lane must deliver within the Critical p90 (30h) and must not be the sole route (R1 requires 1 alternative for Service-contract). This is explicitly *not* a recommendation to move critical orders to ground-for-emissions reasons. SP2 conformance improves because justification is built into the lane-assignment decision.
- **Evidence to monitor:** T4 justification + load factor, Critical late rate (4%) and p90 (30h), shared-lane on-time rate, number of alternative routes available per Service-contract case (R1), SP2 override log.

### S9 — Split Critical-downtime into "true downtime" vs "precautionary urgent"
- **Target path / segment:** the whole `Critical-downtime Service-contract` segment (760 `Emergency production` cases; 8% of volume on emergency  dedicated van at 52 kgCO2e).
- **Mechanism:** A classification at `Receive order`/`Availability check` (customer-reported downtime flag, site availability status) routes precautionary-urgent cases to priority pick + ground/consolidated with an explicit promise, while true downtime keeps van/air. This is the highest-transport lever because 11% of orders carry ~40% of transport emissions.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** **Mis-classification is the key risk** — downgrading a true downtime case to ground creates a severe service failure (cancellation due to delay, contract penalty). Require a conservative default: unknown  treat as true downtime, and log every downgrade in S12. Quality unaffected; resilience depends on keeping the van/air option open.
- **Evidence to monitor:** classification accuracy vs observed outcomes (late rate per class), Critical late rate and p90 by class, downgrade override count and their outcomes, T5 emergency transport share, `Cancel due to delay` within Critical.

### S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for *Regular* Made-to-order customers only
- **Target path / segment:** `MTO  CNC immediately  inspection  reinforced pack  air` (6% of volume, 70h, 24 kWh, **23 kgCO2e**) and the MTO-Regular segment (22% of volume, **18% late**, p90 180h).
- **Mechanism:** Offer a later promised date (or a "green window") in exchange for ground shipping and normal batching instead of immediate production + air. Simultaneously attacks the worst combination: immediate CNC (no batching  high kWh), air transport (23 kgCO2e), and 18% lateness — promising a later date *and* batching usually improves both lateness and emissions.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Excludes Service-contract and Critical-downtime. Watch for metric gaming: an extended `promised_lead_time_hours` mechanically lowers the measured late rate without improving real delivery — report **both** promise-relative and fixed-horizon (e.g. 120h) lateness. May reduce demand if customers dislike longer promises; may shift them to expedite (rebound).
- **Evidence to monitor:** uptake rate by segment, `promised_lead_time_hours` distribution shift, fixed-horizon lateness, `expedite_flag` rate (rebound), air share in MTO, energy per order (batching effect), revenue/line loss.

### S11 — Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules
- **Target path / segment:** `Pick from stock  Partial shipment` (1,010 cases; 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e from two legs)  `Backorder wait` (220)  second pick (160) or cancellation (60).
- **Mechanism:** A smarter `Availability check` (net available minus reserved, plus a short hold to complete the line) produces one shipment instead of two — halving the transport leg for that 7% share and removing a customer-contact loop.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Mixed: holding for full availability adds wait, and the 60 backorder cancellations show that *some* customers prefer partial receipt. Apply only when the hold is short relative to `promised_lead_time_hours`; otherwise partial shipment remains the better service outcome. Do not use this as a major emissions lever — the gain is small (see §4).
- **Evidence to monitor:** partial-shipment rate, second-leg count, backorder  cancel conversion (60 today), late rate on the partial variant (24%), `Cancel due to delay` total (530), transport per fulfilled line.

### S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1 / SP2 / Q1 overrides
- **Target path / segment:** cross-cutting — every case where the recommender or policy is overridden.
- **Mechanism:** Turns policy violations into first-class events with reason codes (insufficient slack, carrier disruption, true downtime, geometry mismatch, quality certification). This is the **measurement backbone**: without it, "green" improvements cannot be separated from service failures, and the low model precision makes offline analysis unreliable.
- **Service / quality / resilience effect:** Adds negligible process overhead; improves auditability and the R2 indicator. It does not itself reduce emissions — its value is that it makes S1/S4/S5/S8/S9 safe to deploy.
- **Evidence to monitor:** override rate and reason mix per 1,000 cases; correlation of overrides with late delivery, rework, and emissions; trend of SP1 (22% violation), SP2 (31% missing justification), Q1 violations vs rework loops.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound (energy  service  obsolescence).** Capping batch size at, say, 12 to protect service raises CNC_A energy from 1.3 back toward 2.8 kWh/item on the 1–3 band, potentially erasing S2's energy gain — while *not* capping causes 4h  38h waits, heat-treatment 1.7× late risk, and 6% obsolescence. Partial batching can also increase **changeover scrap**, displacing energy savings into material. Must be managed as a joint frontier (B2), never optimised on one axis.

2. **Batching rebound via obsolescence displacement.** Large efficient batches produce items that are not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong (6%). The kWh/item number looks excellent while the material, storage, and eventual scrap of obsolete stock are invisible in `energy_kwh`. S2's cap therefore must be paired with an obsolescence metric (M4), otherwise environmental "savings" are booked against wasted inventory.

3. **Transport displacement: consolidation  air fallback.** If S5's 12–24h wait pushes an order past its promised date, the realistic recovery is `Ship air` (4–6× emissions), turning a 35–55% saving on the consolidated leg into a net increase. This is most likely at peak periods when the twice-daily cutoff is missed — exactly when model precision is lowest.

4. **Transport displacement: dedicated van  shared lane service failure.** Replacing vans (S8) with a shared courier lane saves on empty running but creates a shared dependency; if the lane is full, the fallback is either a late delivery (contract penalty, possible customer escalation to air) or a single-order van anyway — with worse service *and* no emissions saving. Similarly, dropping van capacity outright would violate SP2/R1 for Critical-downtime.

5. **Transport displacement across segments (green segment  green portfolio).** Aggressive air removal in Standard-stock Service-contract (S4) can leave total transport looking flat while emissions **migrate** to Critical-downtime, whose 11% share already carries ~40% of transport CO2e. Reported portfolio averages must be segmented (T1, T5), or S9/S8 progress will be masked — or, worse, critical cases will be quietly downgraded to look green.

6. **Packaging rebound: less material  more damage.** Reinforced packaging doubles `package_material_kg` but cuts damage-related returns by 40% for MTO > 8 kg. A material-reduction target applied naively (e.g. "reduce package mass by X%") will push MTO heavy parts back into standard packaging, raising returns, rework packaging loops, and — because a damaged delivery usually needs expediting — `Ship air` emissions. Q1 violations already correlate with return/rework loops; the packaging KPI must be *net* of damage.

7. **Packaging–route coupling.** Removing reinforced packaging to save material while keeping `dedicated_van`/`parcel_air` geometry expectations unchanged raises damage risk on the very routes where speed leaves no recovery time; conversely, S6's compatibility check can only work if packaging spec and `route_mode` are decided **together**, not sequentially.

8. **Quality-gate rebound: inspection time  expedited transport.** S7 adds inspection queue time on MTO cases already at 78–94h median. If that delay consumes slack, the order is shipped by air or produced immediately without batching — converting a scrap saving into 4–6× transport emissions plus higher kWh. The gate must be financed with slack from S2/S3, and the air-share of gated cases monitored explicitly.

9. **Machine-shift displacement.** Moving MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3) lowers rework (18%  9%) but loads CNC_A, lengthening its queue; if CNC_A becomes congested, cases either wait (batch energy gain but late risk) or fall back to CNC_B under pressure — reproducing the original rework rate while adding queue delay. Capacity, not preference, determines whether S3 delivers.

10. **Customer-behaviour rebound on promise negotiation (S10).** If Regular MTO customers learn that a later promise buys cheaper/greener shipping, some will *order earlier to preserve speed* (more total demand, more production energy), others will bypass the green window by selecting expedite (`expedite_flag`), restoring air. Also, extending `promised_lead_time_hours` mechanically improves measured lateness — a reporting artefact that can conceal real service deterioration.

11. **Partial-shipment rebound (S11).** Holding stock to ship complete reduces the two-leg transport of the 7% partial variant but increases inventory holding and the risk that the held order becomes a cancellation (`Backorder wait  Cancel due to delay`, 60 today), wasting the energy and material already invested in picked/produced items. Under disruption, holding also weakens the "ship what you have" resilience valve.

12. **Success-induced demand / scope slippage.** Lower per-order emissions can be read as licence to widen express promises or grow consolidated capacity, shifting impact outside the stated boundary (inbound freight, packaging embodied carbon, scrap disposal) that this analysis explicitly excludes. Boundary-stable intensity per fulfilled line item should be reported alongside absolute totals.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

**Recommend a layered redesign in which every environmental lever is bounded by a service, quality, or resilience constraint — and in which the waste-removal levers (rework, packaging, obsolescence) are pursued harder than the fuel-switching levers, because they improve all four objectives at once.**

### Layer 1 — Remove pure waste first (environment and service improve together)
- **S6 packaging–route compatibility** and **S7 inspection quality gate on high-rework cells**: these attack the 470 inspectionrework loops, the 620 packaging-rework loops, and the rework path that costs 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 160h and 32% lateness — versus 16 kWh / 0.70 kg on the good path. Nothing about these depends on asking the customer to wait longer or accept less.
- **S3 CNC_B  CNC_A, gated on due-date slack**: rework 18%  9% is a structural, not behavioural, improvement. Gate it: move only cases with slack  CNC_A queue + batch wait; keep CNC_B warm as the disruption alternative (resilience).

### Layer 2 — Make batching an explicit trade-off, not a maximisation
- **S2 with segment caps**: Critical 4h (essentially no batching), Service-contract MTO 12h, Regular MTO 24–30h; and a **heat-treatment batch-wait ceiling** chosen so the observed 1.7× late risk is not realised. Report the energy-per-item vs wait frontier (B2) and the 6% obsolescence rate (M4) side by side — a batch is only "green" if the item ships within 14 days.

### Layer 3 — Transport: decarbonise *with* slack, never *instead of* service
- **S1 + S4**: the recommender closes the 22% SP1 violation in Standard-stock Service-contract by using **priority pick + ground/consolidated when the 85% on-time rule holds**. Air is explicitly retained for (a) Critical-downtime, (b) any Service-contract case with <24h slack, and (c) the disruption buffer required by R1. This is not "ban air" — it is "make air earn its 4–6× emissions".
- **S5 twice-daily consolidation** for non-urgent stock only, with a hard exclusion list (Critical, partial/backorder cases, cases with <24h slack), and parcel_ground kept as a live parallel route so a missed cutoff never becomes a forced air shipment.
- **S9 then S8**: first classify Critical-downtime into true vs precautionary (conservative default: unknown  true downtime, every downgrade logged in S12); only then move *precautionary* urgent cases to a shared emergency lane **with an SLA at 30h p90 and at least one alternative route**. True downtime keeps the dedicated van/air. This is where the largest transport saving lives (11% of orders  40% of transport) and where the service downside would be most severe — hence the sequencing and the default.
- **S10 carbon-aware promises restricted to Regular Made-to-order**, with dual reporting (promise-relative *and* fixed-horizon lateness) so that date extension cannot masquerade as service improvement.

### Layer 4 — Measurement and exceptions as the backbone
- **S12 exception log** and **S11 partial-shipment tightening only where the hold is short**: S11 is deliberately not positioned as a major emissions lever (1–3% transport) because holding stock can convert a late delivery into a cancellation, destroying already-invested energy and material.

### Why this is balanced rather than "green at any cost"
| Objective | How the package protects it |
|---|---|
| **Environment** | Layers 1–3 attack MTO energy (87% of kWh), Critical transport (40% of CO2e), MTO scrap (85%), and 620 packaging-rework loops — the actual hot spots in the log, not generic measures. |
| **Service** | Every transport lever is slack-gated at an 85% on-time rule; batch waits are capped per segment; consolidation excludes urgent cases; the least-emitting observed variant (`Critical stock/ground`, 4.8 kgCO2e) is **not** recommended as a default because its 9% late rate and 23h median risk downtime service. |
| **Quality** | Rework is treated as a first-order impact (hidden material + energy), addressed upstream at inspection rather than by thinner packaging — reinforced packaging stays mandatory for MTO >8 kg unless certified. |
| **Resilience** | Air capacity, parcel_ground, and dedicated vans are retained as *available* options (R1 1 alternative route for Service-contract); CNC_B stays live as CNC_A fallback; bypasses under disruption are allowed but must be logged (S12), so green targets never become single points of failure. |

The unifying principle: **prefer interventions that reduce waste (rework, packaging, obsolescence, empty running) over interventions that shift emissions (route or machine swaps), and gate every shift behind a slack/SLA test.**

# 7. Validation and governance plan

### 7.1 Design of the validation
- **Phased rollout with a holdout.** Roll out by segment in sequence — (i) S6/S12 (near-zero service risk), (ii) S7/S3/S2 (MTO), (iii) S1/S4/S5 (Standard-stock transport), (iv) S9/S8/S10 (Critical and promise negotiation). Hold back a comparable set of cases per segment to serve as a control.
- **Segment-level before/after with difference-in-differences.** Compare each segment against its own baseline, since case mix (39/16/22/12/11%) drives absolute totals: baselines to hold are SS-Reg 31h/62h/7%/0.8 kWh/4.2 kg; SS-SC 18h/41h/5%/0.9/7.8; MTO-Reg 94h/180h/18%/18.5/8.0; MTO-SC 78h/156h/13%/20.0/12.0; Critical 15h/30h/4%/4.5/38.0.
- **Path-level, not only segment-level.** Re-mine the DFG after implementation and compare the performance/sustainability table per variant, because aggregate segment averages can hide a route shift (e.g. a saving in `Stock  ground` masked by growth in `MTO  air`). Track the six named variants as sentinel paths.
- **Guardrail thresholds (abort/revert triggers).** Any segment whose late-delivery rate rises >2pp over baseline, whose p90 exceeds its baseline (62/41/180/156/30h), or whose `Cancel due to delay` share rises above 4.4% triggers a rollback of the responsible intervention; Critical-downtime late rate >5% or p90 >30h triggers an immediate revert of S8/S9 regardless of emissions gain.
- **Attribution caution.** Model precision is low (emergency and partial paths recombine), so causal claims should rest on the intervention's own log signature (e.g. `batch_size` band change, `route_mode` change) rather than on overall averages.

### 7.2 Conformance monitoring for SP1 / SP2 / Q1
- **SP1 (ground/consolidated when on-time probability >85%):** compute per-week conformance on `route_mode` vs predicted probability and `promised_lead_time_hours`; target **78%  >95%** within two quarters. Every non-conforming case must carry an S12 reason code; track the *outcome* of non-conformances to calibrate the 85% threshold (if overridden cases are on time anyway, raise the threshold; if conforming cases turn out late, lower it).
- **SP2 (dedicated vans require Critical-downtime justification):** target **69%  >95%** justification coverage. Newly added attributes: justification event, van load factor, empty-return flag. Report the joint distribution: justified-and-loaded (keep), unjustified (process defect), justified-but-empty (target for S8 shared lanes).
- **Q1 (MTO >8 kg must use reinforced packaging unless certified low damage risk):** monitor violation count and, crucially, its correlation with `Inspection  Rework production` loops, `Rework packaging` edges, and the return proxy. Confirm that S6/S7 reduce violations *and* that any certified-low-damage exception does not raise returns — otherwise tighten the certification.
- **Standing report:** override rate per 1,000 cases (R2) with reason mix, reviewed monthly alongside the environmental KPI set.

### 7.3 Segment-level service monitoring
- Weekly dashboard per segment: median and p90 cycle time, late-delivery rate, promise adherence (S3), backorder rate (`Partial shipment  Backorder wait`), cancellation rate (`Cancel due to delay`, baseline 530 cases), and expedite rate (`expedite_flag` — the key rebound signal for S10).
- **Dual lateness reporting** for S10: promise-relative lateness *and* fixed-horizon lateness (e.g. delivered within 120h) so that promise-date extension cannot be scored as service improvement.
- Watch the **heat-treatment batch wait vs late delivery** relationship continuously to verify that the S2 cap actually holds the 1.7× risk at bay.
- Monitor `Package standard` / `Package reinforced` queue times to confirm that S7's inspection gate and priority picking (S4) do not create new congestion that re-triggers air shipping.

### 7.4 Quality and environmental co-monitoring
- Rework: `Inspection  Rework production` rate by `machine_group`, loop depth (`Rework production  CNC production`), rework-flag share of total energy (E3) and scrap (M1).
- Materials: `package_material_kg` per order **and per kg shipped**, packaging-rework rate (M3), damage/return proxy (Q3) — reject any packaging-mass reduction that raises returns.
- Batching: kWh/item by batch band, batch wait distribution, and **obsolescence by `batch_id` (14-day rule, baseline 6%)** — a batch that saves energy but raises obsolescence is scored as a net loss.
- Energy and transport reported as **intensity per fulfilled line item and in absolute totals per segment**, so that volume growth cannot flatter the intensity metric.

### 7.5 Resilience validation
- **R1 check:** for every Service-contract case, verify 1 alternative `route_mode` remained feasible — particularly after S5 (consolidation) and S8 (shared lanes) reduce route diversity.
- **Disruption drill:** simulate/observe a carrier disruption and measure time-to-re-route for Service-contract cases, emergency-path usage (`Emergency production`, `Dedicated van`), and the emissions/service outcome — accepting a temporary emissions rise if it protects downtime customers, and logging it as a justified exception.
- **Queue resilience:** verify CNC_A capacity headroom after S3, inspection-cell headroom after S7, and consolidation-cutoff miss rate after S5; each has a documented fallback (CNC_B, bypass-with-log, parcel_ground).
- **Classification audit for S9:** monthly review of precautionary-urgent downgrades and their actual outcomes; any true-downtime case downgraded and delivered late forces a review of the classification rule (conservative default = treat as true downtime).

### 7.6 Governance cadence
- **Weekly:** guardrail dashboard (service + quality) with automatic rollback triggers.
- **Monthly:** SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance and exception-log review; re-run DFG conformance and compare the six sentinel paths.
- **Quarterly:** re-mine the log, recompute the indicator set (§2), re-estimate the ranges in §4 against observed data, and re-test whether rebound risks in §5 are materialising (air share, obsolescence, package mass, expedite rate, CNC_A queue).
- **Annually:** revisit the system boundary (§1) to decide whether inbound transport and packaging embodied carbon should be brought inside, and re-baseline the segment targets.
