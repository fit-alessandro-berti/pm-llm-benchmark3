## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**Framework:** I use interventional and separable effects, not natural direct/indirect effects. The flag is a deterministic function of `risk_score`, so a cross-world counterfactual in which the same case has flag $=1$ for traces and flag $=0$ for mediators is not well-defined. More importantly, “set flag to zero” is not the same intervention as suppressing its display, removing priority routing, removing mandatory review, adding capacity, or changing escalation criteria. Queueing also creates interference and time-varying, exposure-induced confounding. Natural effects would require cross-world independence assumptions that are scientifically implausible here; controlled effects of a single flag toggle would be non-manipulable and often ill-defined. Policy-relevant interventional contrasts are therefore preferred.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 current flag bundle (priority triage plus usual manual/escalation routing) versus standard pre-flag routing with no priority and discretionary manual/escalation | Case, all arrivals in a defined period and capacity regime | Total cycle time, arrival to close | Total interventional policy effect | Yes, if bundle, staffing, and period are specified | Interference through shared queues, secular load trends, unobserved underlying risk |
| E2 | Priority-lane assignment versus standard triage queue for flagged cases, holding manual-review policy nominally fixed | Flagged case at triage entry | Arrival-to-triage-start and arrival-to-triage-done | Separable total effect of priority component | Partially; lane assignment is manipulable, but priority may change staff attention beyond queue position | Arrival load, time of day, triage resource, risk/complexity correlation with flag; positivity at score threshold |
| E3 | P0 versus P2 (retain priority, remove automatic mandatory manual review) among flagged cases | Flagged case at triage completion | Total cycle time | Interventional indirect effect through manual-review occurrence, plus remaining policy effect | Partially as a policy decomposition; occurrence distribution is intervenable | Exposure-induced confounding by complexity, human judgment, triage findings; reviewers may substitute discretionary review |
| E4 | P0 versus P3-like protected manual capacity, or a stochastic intervention that shifts manual waiting-time distribution while holding occurrence policy as fixed as possible | Case entering manual review | Total cycle time; manual queue wait | Interventional indirect effect through manual-review waiting, distinct from occurrence and service | Partially; waiting is emergent from capacity and arrivals, not directly set per case | Shared-queue interference, arrival-load measurement error, resource assignment, time-varying congestion |
| E5 | P0 versus P4 (changed escalation criteria and/or senior capacity), holding manual policy fixed | Case completing manual review | Total cycle time; escalation occurrence, wait, and service | Interventional indirect effect through escalation occurrence and escalation waiting/service | Partially, if new criteria and capacity are explicit | Selection by unobserved manual findings, scarce senior capacity, exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding |
| E6 | P3 protected manual-review capacity versus P0 for flagged cases, with unflagged spillover recorded | Case and arrival cohort; flagged and unflagged cases | Median and upper quantiles of total cycle time | Total system-level policy effect | Yes for a specified capacity increment, e.g. added review hours per day | Interference and capacity displacement, load trends, small sample, non-stationarity |
| E7 | P0 versus P2 or P4 among flagged cases | Flagged case with auditable decision | Observed Approve/Reject plus audited appropriateness, overturn, reopen, or harm | Total policy effect on decision label and on audited quality | Partially; observed label is well-defined, quality requires a reference standard | No gold standard in supplied data, hidden risk, reviewer anchoring and behavior change |
| E8 | P1 suppress flag display versus P0 display, holding risk score, priority algorithm, and nominal routing rules fixed | Flagged case exposed to triage/review staff | Cycle time, manual ordering, escalation ordering | Separable/controlled direct effect of information display | Partially; requires preventing leakage through visible score, queue position, or staffing | Staff can infer flag from score or lane, Hawthorne effects, noncompliance |

Interference arises because flagged cases share manual and senior queues: increasing flagged volume raises other cases' manual_queue_wait and escalation_queue_wait, violating SUTVA and making individual flag effects depend on arrival mix. Exposure-induced confounding arises because flag/priority affects triage timing and resource, which affect human judgment and findings, which then affect manual_review, escalation, and outcome; those post-flag variables confound mediator-outcome relations and cannot be conditioned on naively. Treatment-confounder feedback occurs over calendar time: earlier flagged cases congest queues, congestion affects arrival_load snapshots and later triage waits, which affect subsequent review decisions; complexity and unrecorded risk affect both flag threshold crossing and service needs. Hence static adjustment, natural effects, and case-independent DFG contrasts are fragile; queue state, arrival cohort, and time-varying history must be modeled explicitly.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Values are medians in hours. Each interval uses only cases where both endpoints occur; an absent manual or escalation stage is `NA` for that case and excluded, never coded as zero. Residual-to-close is close minus the last observed stage completion. Stage denominators therefore differ from subgroup $n$.

| subgroup | n | arrival-to-triage-start | triage service | triage-done-to-manual-start | manual service | manual-done-to-escalation-start | escalation service | residual-to-close | total cycle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Flag 0 (manual 4/10; escalation 2/10) | 10 | 2.5 | 1.0 | 1.8 | 3.0 | 6.5 | 2.0 | 1.1 | 5.8 |

| Complexity Low (manual 0/4; escalation 0/4) | 4 | 2.3 | 0.8 | NA | NA | NA | NA | 1.1 | 4.5 |

Flagged cases show much shorter arrival-to-triage-start (median about 0.2h vs 2.5h) and somewhat shorter triage service (0.6h vs 1.0h), consistent with prioritization. Yet they have longer triage-done-to-manual-start (6.8h vs 1.8h among reviewed), universal manual review (10/10 vs 4/10), and escalation in 6/10 vs 2/10, with escalation waiting around 7h. Consequently total cycle is longer (16.5h vs 5.8h), concentrated in flagged escalated cases (21.9h). These contrasts are not automatically causal: flag is derived from risk_score and complexity, with no low-complexity flagged cases and limited overlap; manual and escalation subgroups are selected by human judgment and findings; arrival_load, time of day, and shared queues differ; and stage medians condition on occurrence, so comparing them mixes occurrence, waiting, service, and selection effects. Small n=20 also makes medians unstable.

| pathway | candidate interpretation | observable evidence | required assumptions | competing explanation | intervention targeting this pathway | expected downstream side effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is there a discrete flag effect near the score threshold? | Local regression-discontinuity/matching in narrow risk_score bins around the inferred threshold (flagged minimum 0.64, unflagged maximum 0.61), stratified by complexity and load | Smooth risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, arrival time, triage resource | Continuity of underlying risk at threshold; no precise score manipulation; local overlap | Covariate balance just above/below threshold; placebo thresholds away from the real cut; pre-policy discontinuity check | If manual occurrence and waiting rise smoothly with risk_score with no jump at threshold, mandatory-flag routing is not the main driver |
| Does queue state explain downstream delay? | Event-history analysis at each stage start using queue length, recent arrivals, flagged share in queue, resource utilization, and time since last completion | Queue state, arrival_load, calendar time, resource, complexity, risk_score | Queue state is adequately reconstructed from timestamps and resources | Effect of flag attenuates after queue-state controls; future queue state should not predict current stage wait as a placebo | If flag-associated waiting disappears after queue-state adjustment, congestion rather than case-level mandatory handling is dominant |
| What is the effect of changing the policy bundle? | Stepped-wedge or randomized encouragement by team, day, or arrival block for P2 or P3, estimating intention-to-treat and complier effects | Block, load, resource, baseline risk/complexity; cluster-robust inference | Randomization as assigned; limited cross-block interference; no anticipation | First-stage change in manual occurrence/capacity; balance across blocks; spillover to unflagged cases | If encouragement changes manual occurrence but not cycle or quality, the occurrence pathway is weak |
| How much operates through occurrence, waiting, and escalation? | Sequential interventional mediation using g-computation or sequential regression with stochastic interventions on manual occurrence, then waiting distribution, then escalation | Baseline risk/complexity/load; time-varying triage result, resource, queue state, manual findings where measured | No unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding conditional on measured history; positivity; correct models | Cross-fitted model checks; negative-control mediators; compare interventional and policy-contrast estimates | If interventional effects are near zero while total policy effect remains, pathways are mis-specified or unmeasured confounding dominates |
| Are results driven by score artefacts? | Negative-control exposure using placebo thresholds below/above the real threshold and a negative-control outcome that should not respond to routing, such as pre-arrival complexity assignment timing if available | Same as threshold design | Placebo threshold has no policy meaning; negative-control outcome shares confounding but not treatment | Placebo jumps should be absent; flag should not predict pre-treatment variables after local adjustment | A similar placebo jump would undermine threshold-based causal interpretation |
| How strong must hidden risk be to explain the gap? | Sensitivity analysis with bias parameters, E-value-style bounds, and tipping-point scenarios for U_underlying_risk affecting review, escalation, and cycle | Observed risk_score, complexity, load; vary prevalence and effect of hidden risk | Hidden-risk structure is plausible and bounded by external clinical/process knowledge | Report how large hidden-risk effects must be to nullify manual-wait and cycle contrasts | If only implausibly large hidden-risk effects overturn the result, mechanism confidence increases; if modest hidden risk suffices, causal claims should be withdrawn |
| What are direct and spillover effects under shared queues? | Cluster randomization by half-day/shift with exposure mapping based on flagged share in the relevant queue window; estimate flagged, unflagged-spillover, and total system effects | Cluster, queue window, load, resource, calendar time | Partial interference within clusters; limited interference across clusters; exposure mapping approximately correct | Unflagged cases in high-flag blocks should show spillover; isolated low-volume blocks should show smaller waits | If effects are purely cluster-level with no within-cluster flagged contrast, individual pathway claims reverse |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current system | Fastest flagged triage; unflagged triage remains slow | Long manual and escalation queues for flagged cases | Longest flagged cycle, especially escalated cases | High compliance with flag rule, but anchoring and over-review risk | Flagged volume consumes review and senior capacity | Moderate descriptive evidence; causal evidence weak | Retain only as comparator |
| P1 suppress flag display | Little change if priority still uses hidden score; possible slower informal triage if staff lose signal | Manual ordering may fall only if display drives human judgment | Small and uncertain cycle reduction | Loss of alerting; staff may infer flag from score or lane | Minimal if routing unchanged | Weak; no display-only contrast in data | Do not deploy alone; useful only as an experimental component |
| P2 retain priority, remove automatic mandatory manual review | Preserves flagged triage advantage | Manual occurrence and queue load fall; unflagged waits may also fall | Likely largest cycle reduction among non-escalated flagged cases | Risk of missing hidden-risk cases unless explicit triggers and audit are used | Positive spillover by freeing review capacity | Moderate mechanistic plausibility from occurrence and waiting gaps | Select for limited pilot |
| P3 add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases | No direct triage change | Flagged manual wait falls if capacity is truly added; falls less if staff are merely relabelled | Moderate cycle reduction if waiting is capacity-driven | Preserves review compliance; may institutionalize over-review | Negative spillover if capacity is taken from unflagged queue | Weak to moderate; long waits are consistent but not proof of capacity shortage | Consider after pilot shows wait-sensitive benefit without quality loss |
| P4 change escalation criteria or senior capacity | No triage or manual effect | Escalation occurrence or senior wait falls depending on lever | Cycle falls mainly for the escalated subset, which has the longest cycles | Stricter criteria risk under-escalation; more capacity may invite over-escalation | Senior queue spillover to other urgent cases | Weak; escalated subgroup is highly selected | Second-stage pilot after manual-review pathway is tested |

**Pilot selection:** Pilot: P2 for four weeks. Eligibility: newly flagged cases in the local threshold band (risk_score 0.60–0.75) without documented mandatory-review triggers (e.g., High complexity plus senior-prespecified red flags); other flagged cases keep current routing. Assignment: randomize arrival blocks (half-days, stratified by load and triage resource) 1:1 to priority triage plus discretionary manual review versus priority plus automatic review, to limit queue interference within blocks. Queue guardrail: add temporary manual-review sessions if flagged manual queue wait exceeds 4 hours or unflagged triage wait exceeds 3 hours; pause enrolment if exceeded for two consecutive blocks. Primary outcome: total cycle time to close. Secondary: triage completion, manual occurrence/wait/service, escalation, unflagged spillover, audited decision appropriateness and 30-day reopen/harm if available. Minimum data: risk_score, complexity, flag, arrival_load, all start/done timestamps, queue length and resource at each stage start, reviewer, escalation reason, decision and audit label. Stopping: excess audited errors, escalation-related harm, queue guardrail breach, or futility after a prespecified sample; review weekly with blinded outcome audit.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If unrecorded underlying risk strongly predicts the need for manual review and escalation and is omitted from `risk_score`, then longer flagged cycles may reflect appropriate intensive handling, and removing mandatory review would reverse from beneficial to harmful.
2. **Queue interference:** If within-queue, cluster-aware analysis shows no flagged-case effect once queue state and flagged volume are accounted for, then the case-level mechanism interpretation reverses and the problem is system congestion rather than flag routing.
3. **Decision quality:** If blinded audit shows mandatory review or escalation prevents errors, harm, or costly reversals large enough to offset delay, then the cycle-time-based recommendation reverses.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If `manual_start`, `escalation_start`, or completion timestamps record batch release, system re-entry, or logging delay rather than actual queue entry and service, then the waiting decomposition is an artefact and the inferred mechanism reverses.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If there is no comparable support for flagged low-complexity or unflagged high-risk cases, threshold and stratified contrasts rely on extrapolation, and the preferred causal interpretation must be withdrawn or reversed.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If reviewers anchor on the flag, game the risk score, compensate when review is discretionary, or infer suppressed flags from score and lane, then pilot effects will not match the assumed P1/P2 mechanisms and the policy recommendation reverses.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** In these 20 cases, flagged cases reach triage start in about 0.2h versus 2.5h for unflagged, yet median total cycle is 16.5h versus 5.8h. All flagged cases receive manual review versus 4/10 unflagged, their triage-to-review interval is longer, and 6/10 escalate versus 2/10. Escalated flagged cases have the longest cycles. **Plausible mechanism:** The flag bundle likely accelerates triage through priority routing, then increases total time by routing cases into manual review and, for a selected subset, scarce senior escalation. Long manual and escalation waiting intervals, rather than longer service times alone, account for much of the downstream gap, although higher complexity and risk may also increase service needs. **Remains unidentified:** Because flag is a deterministic function of an imperfect risk_score, underlying risk is unobserved, manual/escalation selection depends on human findings, queues are shared, and subgroups are tiny, the data cannot separate case-mix, mandatory-review, queueing, reviewer, and decision-quality effects, nor identify natural direct or indirect effects. **Most useful next evidence:** A limited, block-randomized P2 pilot—priority triage retained, automatic manual review made discretionary for threshold-band flagged cases—with queue-state logging, unflagged spillover monitoring, and blinded decision audit. It directly tests whether removing mandatory review shortens cycle without harming decisions, while preserving the triage benefit.
