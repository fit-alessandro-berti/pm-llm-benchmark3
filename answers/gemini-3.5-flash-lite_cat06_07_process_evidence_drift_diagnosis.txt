## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

### January – March: Baseline Stability
* **Operational state:** The system operated under steady-state conditions with stable variant distributions (V1: ~45%, V2: ~35%, V3: ~20%), mature labels (100%), and reliable suppliers (part delivery median: 28–35h). 
* **Model performance:** High AUROC (0.81–0.82), low Brier scores (~0.130), and tight calibration (ECE ~0.023). This establishes our ground-truth baseline.

### April: The Pipeline and Schema Incident
* **Operational state:** A workflow system migration on 1 April renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. 
* **Phenomenon:** The feature pipeline failed to recognize the new label, causing missing prefix generations, 17% `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share, and 14% missing queue features. Although patched on 6 May, derived features (like pre-diagnosis event counts) were not fully backfilled, and duplicate event removal was only partially applied.
* **Metric impact:** Mean predicted risk spiked artificially to 0.28, AUROC collapsed to 0.67, and ECE jumped to 0.110. This is **not** concept or process drift; it is a pure data-pipeline failure and covariate mapping breakdown.

### May: Partial Recovery and Transition
* **Operational state:** Schema mapping patch deployed, stabilizing unknown activity share to 2.0% and missing queue features to 3%. 
* **Metric impact:** AUROC recovered to 0.78, but ECE remained elevated (0.050) because cached intermediate features were never recomputed for April, leaving lingering covariate artifacts.

### June – July: Process Drift (Remote-Routing Policy Change)
* **Operational state:** A new remote-routing policy launched on 1 June, driving V2 usage up to 52% in June and 50% in July.
* **Phenomenon:** V2 features low breach rates (~0.10). Because the model was over-indexed on V1/V3 baseline mixes, the sudden influx of V2 cases caused a mismatch between predicted risk (mean 0.20) and observed lower breach rates (0.15 in June). 
* **Metric impact:** Moderate degradation in calibration (ECE 0.060) driven by **process drift**, not model failure. Simultaneously, parts supplier issues began emerging in July (edge time increased to 65h).

### August – September: Compounding Concept Drift and Population Shift
* **Operational state:** 
  1. *Supplier failure:* Parts supplier delivery targets severely missed starting in August; median `Order Part -> Part Received` edge time blew out from 130h (Aug) to 148h (Sep). V3 matured breach rates surged to 0.64 in August.
  2. *Population shift:* Partner Z was onboarded in September, accounting for 24% of volume, bringing rural customers, older devices, and a 58% V3 routing share.
* **Phenomenon:** The model, blind to supplier bottlenecks and Partner Z's demographic covariates, under-predicted risk (mean predicted risk 0.19–0.21 vs observed breach 0.28–0.31). AUROC dropped to 0.66, and ECE worsened to 0.140. This is a severe confluence of **concept drift** and **population shift**.

### October: Label Delay and Censoring Confounding
* **Operational state:** The outcome API developed a severe backlog, pushing median label delay to 52 days. At the reporting cutoff, only 55% of October cases had matured.
* **Phenomenon:** Because breached cases take longer to resolve and close, immature cohorts are heavily censored toward non-breaches, dropping the observed breach rate to an artificial 0.16. 
* **Metric impact:** Apparent performance metrics (AUROC 0.64, ECE 0.130) are heavily distorted by **label censoring**. Treating October's observed breach rate as final or evaluating the model against immature outcomes will lead to false diagnostic conclusions.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-Maturity Curves and Fixed-Vintage Backtesting**
   * *Method:* Re-evaluate October and September cohorts at fixed maturity windows (e.g., 30, 60, 90 days post-case creation) using Kaplan-Meier censoring corrections.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Label Censoring):* Observed breach rates for recent cohorts will trend upward toward true historical levels as maturity windows expand.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - True Operational Improvement):* Breach rates remain low regardless of observation window.

2. **Recomputation After Activity-Normalisation and Duplicate Cleaning**
   * *Method:* Full historical data replay starting from 1 April 2026, forcing standardization of `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` back to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, purging duplicates, and recomputing all derived queue features.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Pipeline Failure):* April AUROC restores to ~0.80 and ECE drops back to baseline (~0.025).
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Algorithmic Concept Drift):* Performance remains degraded despite schema repair.

3. **Calibration and Discrimination Stratified by Variant, Source, and Month**
   * *Method:* Slice AUROC, Brier score, and ECE independently across V1, V2, and V3 variants, and separate Partner Z vs. non-Partner Z data across all months.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Variant/Population Mixing):* Stratified AUROC remains stable (~0.80) within V1, V2, and non-Partner Z pools, proving that global metric drop is a Simpson’s paradox driven by population and variant mix shifts.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Global Concept Drift):* Stratified models also show severe degradation across all variants.

4. **Process Variant and Performance-DFG Edge Analysis**
   * *Method:* Extract variant frequencies and measure median execution times across DFG edges (`Order Part -> Part Received`, `Diagnosis -> Repair`) monthly.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Process Drift / Supplier Bottleneck):* Dramatic latency inflation specifically on the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge corresponding with V3 breach spikes in Aug–Oct.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Internal Triage Delay):* Latencies remain flat across all edges.

5. **Conditional Outcome Relationship Tests (Stability of Feature Effects)**
   * *Method:* Fit a logistic regression on Jan-Mar data and score April-Oct data holding coefficients constant; test for residual deviance changes conditional on identical process paths and feature vectors.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Concept Drift):* Significant increase in residual deviance in Aug–Oct, proving the probabilistic mapping from features to breach outcomes shifted.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Covariate Shift Only):* Residual deviance remains stable; errors are entirely explained by input distribution shifts.

6. **Population Reweighting and Transport-Style Checks for Partner Z**
   * *Method:* Apply inverse probability weighting (IPW) based on covariate propensity scores to re-weight non-Partner Z data to match Partner Z's feature distribution (rural share, device age).
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Population Shift):* Weighted metrics align closely with Partner Z observed metrics, confirming population shift accounts for performance disparities.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Unmodeled Process Failure):* Weighting fails to bridge the performance gap, indicating uncaptured operational friction specific to Partner Z.

7. **Feature Missingness and Lineage Audits**
   * *Method:* Audit logging tables for feature store pipelines to track NULL rates and feature computation timestamps across all event logs.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis A - Pipeline Bug):* Spike in NULL values for derived queue features perfectly bounded between 1 Apr and 6 May, with orphaned feature caches persisting thereafter.
   * *Expected Result (Hypothesis B - Organic Data Absence):* Missingness correlates with specific customer types rather than calendar deployment dates.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Staged Decision Framework

* **Immediate Containment (Weeks 1–2):**
  * Freeze automated model retraining and deployment pipelines.
  * Implement rule-based risk overrides: For any V3 case where supplier part delivery delay exceeds 96 hours, override the predictive risk score to `High Risk` (breach probability > 0.80), bypassing the corrupted feature pipeline for those specific fields.
  * Quarantine all prediction logs generated between 1 April and 6 May.

* **Short-Term Recalibration (Weeks 3–4):**
  * Apply Platt scaling or Isotonic Regression recalibration to the existing model, fitted exclusively on June–July data (after pipeline repair, prior to severe supplier bottlenecks) to correct for the V2 remote-routing shift. Do not retrain model weights yet.

* **Feature Repair & Data Backfill (Weeks 5–6):**
  * Execute a complete data-pipeline backfill for April and May: re-normalize all renamed diagnosis labels, purge duplicate events, and recompute all derived queue features that were left stale by the 6 May patch.

* **Full Retraining Strategy (Month 2+):**
  * **Safe Training Windows:** January, February, March, May, June, and July are safe for historical baseline training *after* pipeline backfilling.
  * **Unsafe Training Windows:** April (unrecoverable logging artifacts without deep synthetic imputation), August, and September (active, severe concept drift driven by supplier failure and unmodeled Partner Z population shift). Training on Aug–Sep will bake supplier failure anomalies into baseline weights.
  * **Exclusion Criteria:** Octobers data must remain strictly excluded from training sets until full label maturity is reached (projected >90 days post-cutoff).

### Segment-Specific and Variant-Specific Modeling Justification
A single monolithic model is no longer justified. We recommend a **hybrid-segmented architecture**:
1. **Variant-Specific Sub-Models:** Split training into a **V2 Remote Resolution Model** (fast path, low breach variance) and a **V3 Parts-Dependent Model** (slow path, highly sensitive to external supplier SLAs). V1 Standard can share the base model.
2. **Partner-Specific Covariate Integration:** For Partner Z, introduce explicit source identifiers and rural/device-age features into the V3 sub-model. Do not build an entirely separate model for Partner Z unless data volume permits, but ensure feature pipelines explicitly ingest rural travel distance and device age.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

### Comprehensive Monitoring Suite
1. **Predictive Monitors:** Track mean predicted risk vs. rolling historical baselines to catch sudden population or schema shifts.
2. **Calibration Monitors:** Continuously track Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier scores across sliding 30-day windows.
3. **Label-Maturity Monitors:** Track median label delay and maturity percentage per cohort. Trigger automatic warnings when cohort maturity drops below 80%.
4. **Data-Quality Monitors:** Monitor event ingestion null rates, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` percentage, and schema adherence at the event-stream boundary.
5. **Process-Variant Monitors:** Track weekly volume shifts across V1, V2, and V3 variants to detect structural process routing changes.
6. **Performance-DFG Monitors:** Monitor median edge durations—specifically `Order Part -> Part Received`—to catch supplier SLA degradation in real time.
7. **Population Monitors:** Track covariate distribution shifts (e.g., Partner Z volume share, rural customer proportion, average device age).
8. **Intervention / Deployment Monitors:** Track downstream operational uptake of risk scores (e.g., technician re-allocation rates based on alerts).

### Monthly Process-Evidence Extraction Script
```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def audit_field_service_month(csv_path: str, target_year_month: str) -> dict:
    """
    Extracts auditable monthly process evidence from field service event logs.
    Handles schema normalisation, duplicate cleaning, and cohort separation.
    """
    # 1. Read event log
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    
    # Ensure standard timestamp formatting
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    
    # 2. Normalize renamed diagnosis activity (Pipeline migration fix)
    df['concept:name'] = df['concept:name'].replace({
        'Diagnostic Assessment Completed': 'Initial Diagnosis Completed'
    })
    
    # 3. Duplicate handling: Drop exact duplicate case-activity-timestamp events
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=['case:id', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])
    
    # 4. Format dataframe for pm4py
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, 
        case_id='case:id', 
        activity_key='concept:name', 
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # 5. Filter by case-start cohort vs event calendar month
    # Here we demonstrate event-calendar filtering for operational activity volume
    start_date = f"{target_year_month}-01"
    end_date = pd.to_datetime(start_date) + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(1)
    
    monthly_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        log, 
        start_date, 
        end_date.strftime('%Y-%m-%d'), 
        mode='events'
    )
    
    if len(monthly_log) == 0:
        return {"month": target_year_month, "cases": 0, "variants": {}, "edge_durations": {}}

    # 6. Extract process variants
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(monthly_log)
    variant_counts = {var: len(cases) for var, cases in variants.items()}
    
    # 7. Discover DFG and Performance DFG
    dfg_graph, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(monthly_log)
    perf_dfg_graph, _, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(monthly_log)
    
    # Extract specific critical edge duration (e.g., Order Part -> Part Received)
    target_edge = ('Order Part', 'Part Received')
    edge_duration_median = None
    if target_edge in perf_dfg_graph:
        # perf_dfg format typically stores summary stats or list of durations per edge
        edge_stats = perf_dfg_graph[target_edge]
        edge_duration_median = edge_stats.get('mean', None) # or extract median from distributions if available

    # 8. Return auditable summary dictionary
    summary = {
        "month": target_year_month,
        "total_events": len(monthly_log),
        "unique_cases": monthly_log['case:id'].nunique(),
        "variant_count": len(variants),
        "top_variant": max(variant_counts, key=variant_counts.get) if variant_counts else None,
        "order_part_received_latency": edge_duration_median
    }
    
    return summary
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on Immature Labels (October Confounding)**
   * *Failure Mode:* Training model weights on October data while label maturity is only 55%. Because unresolved (and often eventually breaching) cases are censored out of the positive label set, the model learns to associate risk factors with *non-breach*, destroying predictive validity.
   * *Mitigation:* Enforce a strict minimum maturity threshold (e.g., 90 days) before any cohort is permitted into training sets. Use right-censoring aware loss functions (survival analysis frameworks) if recent data must be utilized.

2. **Calibrating Around a Pipeline Bug (April Schema Artifacts)**
   * *Failure Mode:* Applying post-hoc recalibration (Platt scaling) to April's predictions to force alignment with observed breach rates, treating a schema migration mapping failure as natural drift. This bakes pipeline bugs into the scoring layer.
   * *Mitigation:* Decouple data engineering lineage audits from model calibration. Never adjust model parameters until event logs pass automated schema-integrity and completeness assertions.

3. **Ignoring Genuine Supplier-Driven Concept Drift (August–September V3 Surge)**
   * *Failure Mode:* Dismissing August and September performance drops as temporary noise or data-quality artifacts, failing to retrain or adjust risk thresholds while supplier part delivery times blow out to 155 hours. High-risk V3 cases go unflagged, resulting in SLA breach spikes.
   * *Mitigation:* Maintain separate operational DFG monitors. When physical process bottlenecks (like part delivery edge durations) degrade beyond operational thresholds, trigger mandatory risk-score overrides independent of machine learning predictions.

4. **Attributing Partner Z’s Population Shift to Unfair Model Behavior**
   * *Failure Mode:* Observing higher breach rates and calibration error for Partner Z in September and concluding the model exhibits bias or unfairness against rural partners, leading to rushed, uncalibrated model rewrites.
   * *Mitigation:* Perform stratified covariate analysis first. Once it is proven that Partner Z's higher breach rate is driven by unmodeled legitimate risk factors (rural travel distance, older device cohorts), update the feature store to ingest these covariates rather than tampering with model fairness constraints.
