## 1. Decision rule

**Step 0 — Stage eligibility (hard gate).** A case is eligible for a swarm only if it has not reached `Diagnosis Complete` and if at least ~60 minutes remain before the SLA deadline (a 45-minute swarm plus specialist hand-off must be able to finish while it can still matter). This gate is absolute: no breach probability, however high, buys a slot for an ineligible case. I-03 fails this gate regardless of its 0.82 prediction.

**Step 1 — Calibration robustification.** The raw `p_now` is observational and known to be miscalibrated by segment, so decisions use an adjusted interval, not a point:
- SME: add +0.04 to +0.09 (model underpredicts).
- Strategic Enterprise: subtract 0.05 to 0.12 (model overpredicts after the workflow redesign).
- Public Sector: use `p_now` as-is, but widen the interval modestly (moderate sample).
- Non-Native-Language Support: use `p_now` with a wide interval and treat both calibration and effect estimates as low-confidence; flag for human review rather than mechanically penalizing the case.
For go/no-go decisions on marginal cases, use the conservative end of the adjusted interval; for prioritization among clearly eligible cases, use the midpoint.

**Step 2 — Data-quality gate.** If a feature material to the score is missing/imputed (I-06's live queue field), a slot may not be committed until a 10–15 minute manual verification confirms the signal is real. Verification is cheap; a wasted slot is not.

**Step 3 — Alert persistence and minimum evidence change.** An alert becomes actionable only if (a) it has persisted across two consecutive 30-minute refreshes at an adjusted probability above the watch level, or (b) the deadline is close enough that waiting one refresh would push the swarm outside its effective window. A repeated alert on the same case is a *new* actionable alert only if a material new event has occurred (new artifact uploaded, stage transition, or an adjusted-probability jump ≥ 0.10 not attributable to imputation or refresh noise). Model refresh alone is never a new event.

**Step 4 — Priority score.** For each eligible, persistent, data-verified case:

S = Δ_adj × V − C_swarm − FA × (1 − p_adj) − E[spillover per slot]

where Δ_adj is the risk-reduction estimate shaded toward its lower half when calibration or effect evidence is weak (Strategic Enterprise, NNLS), V is the value of avoiding breach, C_swarm = €450, FA is the false-alarm burden weighted by the probability the case would not have breached anyway, and E[spillover per slot] ≈ 6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases × 0.01–0.03 added breach probability × their average breach value (≈ €15–25k), i.e. roughly **€900–3,600 per slot, ~€2,400 at base**. Charging every slot this externality is the core of the rule: it prevents spending slots on marginally positive cases.

**Step 5 — Portfolio constraints.** Intervene only where S > 0 at base *and* the adverse scenario is not catastrophic; never exceed the number of remaining slots; honor the management preference to hold one slot in reserve until ~15:00 unless a committed case's S clearly exceeds the option value of the reserve; respect the no-repeat-swarm rule absent a materially new event; apply the fairness floor in Section 5 so low-value segments are not permanently starved.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** after any alert is dispositioned (intervened, or explicitly declined), the case enters a 2-hour cool-down during which re-alerts are logged but not re-escalated.
- **Suppression:** during cool-down, alerts caused purely by the 30-minute model refresh — no new event, no stage transition, adjusted-probability change < 0.10 — are suppressed from the operator queue. Suppressed does not mean invisible: the case remains on a watchlist and its trajectory still counts toward persistence if a new event later occurs.
- **Escalation (re-entry):** a case re-enters the actionable queue immediately, overriding cool-down, if any of: (a) a materially new event (new customer artifact, stage transition, ownership change); (b) adjusted probability rises ≥ 0.10 for reasons traceable to real features, not imputation; (c) time-to-deadline falls below 2× (swarm duration + expected queue wait), i.e. the last realistic intervention window is opening/closing.
- **Repeat swarms:** forbidden on the same case unless a materially new event has occurred *and* a senior reviewer signs off — the default assumption is that a failed swarm means the bottleneck is elsewhere.
- **I-05 vs I-07 — the contrast the policy is built for.** Both are third alerts on SME cases. I-05's repeats are pure refresh noise: no new event since the first alert, probability nearly flat (0.55→0.57). It is suppressed; re-alerting adds no information and answering it would train operators to ignore alerts. I-07's repeat coincides with a new customer diagnostic log — a material new event — so it legitimately re-enters evaluation even though its point estimate *fell* (0.75→0.72): new evidence, not a higher number, is what earns re-entry. I-07 is then declined on economic grounds (low value, half its remaining 1.5h window consumed by the swarm itself, spillover cost) but receives the cheap escalation (senior log review, expedited triage). The asymmetry — suppress I-05, evaluate-then-decline-with-alternative I-07 — is deliberate: alert handling and slot allocation are separate decisions.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation.** The score S is value-weighted, and today it channels all slots to Strategic Enterprise and one NNLS case while every SME case (I-05, I-07, I-10) is declined. Left unchecked, SME and low-value Public Sector cases would *never* receive swarms. Safeguards: (a) a weekly **equity budget** — at least ~20% of swarm capacity (e.g., one slot per day) allocated by adjusted breach *risk* rather than euro value, drawn from the highest-risk eligible cases regardless of segment; (b) a monthly report of intervention rate, breach rate, and average time-to-resolution per segment, with a tripwire (e.g., SME breach rate exceeding its pre-policy baseline by >20%) that forces a rule review.
- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** Sparse validation data must not become a de facto denial-of-service: mechanically shading NNLS effects to zero would systematically exclude a group correlated with language. Safeguard: NNLS cases are decided with midpoint estimates plus mandatory human review (as done for I-09), every NNLS decision is logged with counterfactual notes, and NNLS cases are oversampled into the effect-estimation program in Section 6 until interval widths match other segments.
- **Differential calibration.** Uncorrected, the SME underprediction (−0.04 to −0.09) and Strategic Enterprise overprediction (+0.05 to +0.12) would silently shift capacity toward SE cases on top of their value advantage. The segment offsets in Step 1 are applied *before* scoring, are versioned, and are re-estimated monthly; any segment whose reliability curve drifts beyond the applied offset triggers an automatic widening of its decision interval (more human review, not silent automation).
- **Selective intervention corrupting labels.** Cases we swarm and save are recorded as non-breaches; a naively retrained model learns "high-risk-looking cases don't breach," suppressing exactly the alerts that worked. Safeguards: every case carries a treatment flag; retraining either excludes treated cases, uses only pre-intervention prefixes for them, or applies an explicit counterfactual correction; the training pipeline refuses to run if treatment flags are missing.
- **Spillover falling on untreated groups.** The 0.01–0.03 per-slot harm lands on whoever shares the diagnosis queue — and because we preferentially treat high-value cases, the harmed pool is systematically lower-value (and, given today's mix, disproportionately SME/Public Sector). Safeguards: (a) spillover is charged to the intervention score (already in Step 4) so it is never free; (b) untreated diagnosis-stage cases on swarm days are tracked as an explicit cohort, with their realized delay and breach rate compared to non-swarm days, broken down by segment; (c) if a segment absorbs a disproportionate share of measured spillover harm over a month, its cases receive a compensating priority uplift or the daily slot cap is reduced.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational monitoring (real-time / daily):** slots used vs. reserved; actual swarm duration vs. the 45-minute assumption; measured aggregate queue delay per used slot (to test the 8–20 min base vs. 15–30 min adverse assumption); reserve-slot outcomes (regulatory case arrived? reserve wasted?); alert volume, suppression rate, and operator override rate (rising overrides signal alert fatigue or a broken rule).
- **Predictive monitoring (weekly/monthly):** reliability curves and Brier scores per segment and per stage-prefix; population-stability checks on input features (the I-06 imputation failure should be caught by a feature-availability monitor, not by an analyst's suspicion); explicit tracking of whether the SME/SE offsets are shrinking or growing, with monthly re-estimation.
- **Causal-effect estimation (ongoing program):** the supplied Δ ranges are assumptions, not measurements. Estimate real effects by (a) **tie-break randomization**: among cases within a small band of the intervention threshold, randomize slot assignment and compare breach outcomes — this yields an unbiased local effect at exactly the margin where the policy decision lives; (b) regression-discontinuity analysis around the score threshold for non-randomized periods; (c) stratified effect estimates by segment and stage, with NNLS oversampled. Update the Δ ranges in the scoring rule quarterly, or immediately if the measured effect falls outside the assumed range.
- **Capacity and spillover monitoring:** breach rate and delay of the untreated diagnosis-stage cohort on swarm-heavy vs. swarm-light days; if measured spillover exceeds ~€3,000 per slot at typical congestion, lower the daily slot cap and raise the score threshold accordingly. Track specialist utilization to verify the "interrupted specialist" cost assumption.
- **Fairness monitoring:** the per-segment intervention/breach/spillover dashboards and tripwires from Section 5, reviewed monthly with authority to change the equity budget.
- **Policy-induced drift and label integrity:** monitor the correlation between predicted risk and realized breach among *treated* cases separately from untreated ones; a collapsing correlation among treated cases is expected (the intervention works) and must not be fed back into training uncorrected (treatment flags enforced in the pipeline).
- **Change triggers:** revise thresholds or the allocation rule when any of the following holds — measured intervention effect outside the assumed Δ range for two consecutive review periods; a segment's calibration offset drifting beyond its applied correction; measured spillover consistently in the adverse band; a fairness tripwire firing; or the false-alarm-to-true-alarm cost ratio shifting materially (e.g., new contractual penalty structures). All rule changes are versioned, dated, and applied prospectively so that effect estimation can distinguish policy epochs.
