## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 70/100.

Crude data show Orion cases take longer on average (21.96h vs 15.24h; +6.72h). But this is driven by who Orion receives: Orion handles far more High complexity (42.9% vs 12.5%), far more night work (92.9% vs 25%), and more compliance-required cases (64.3% vs 25%), all of which plausibly increase queueing and total cycle time. The key pattern is a reversal: within each observed complexity stratum Orion is faster (Low 7.50 vs 9.64; Medium 17.17 vs 19.38; High 31.57 vs 35.00). A simple complexity-standardization using the pooled mix implies Orion would be ~2.5h faster than Nova for a common case mix, suggesting the crude “Orion bottleneck” story is unsafe.

Against a pure-confounding explanation: within strata Orion also has lower mean queue wait and slightly lower review service, consistent with real operational mechanisms (skill/capacity). However identification is weak: several cells have 0–2 cases (e.g., no High-Day Orion; Orion-Day n=1), shift/load are entangled with group, and we only observe completed cases—selection could hide long Orion cases still open.

A blind 50% reassignment could succeed if Orion’s higher mean reflects overload at night and Nova can absorb capacity, but it can easily fail/backfire by moving difficult/night cases away from Orion’s certified capacity, increasing waits, rework, and quality risk while overloading Nova.

shift -> arrival_load, routing_rule, queue_wait
arrival_load -> routing_rule, queue_wait
complexity -> routing_rule, compliance_required, queue_wait, review_service, rework
routing_rule -> resource_group
resource_group -> unobserved_reviewer_skill, queue_wait, review_service, rework, case_completed, quality_outcome
unobserved_reviewer_skill -> review_service, rework, quality_outcome
compliance_required -> compliance_performed, queue_wait
compliance_performed -> queue_wait
rework -> review_service, cycle_time, quality_outcome
queue_wait -> cycle_time
review_service -> cycle_time
cycle_time -> case_completed, quality_outcome
```

Supported by prompt: routing assigns `resource_group` after `Classify`; Orion is chosen for difficult cases (so `complexity` affects `routing_rule`/`resource_group`) and covers most Night `shift` (so `shift` affects routing/assignment). `compliance_required` is policy-driven and (assumed) implies `compliance_performed`; `queue_wait` includes waiting around compliance; `review_service` includes repeated review after `rework`. Assumptions: staffing/skill make `resource_group` affect `queue_wait`/`review_service`; `arrival_load` affects both routing and waiting; `unobserved_case_risk` and `unobserved_reviewer_skill` exist. A static DAG suppresses feedback: routing changes future queue states (`arrival_load`/`queue_wait`), which then alter later `routing_rule` decisions over time.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mostly confounded: Orion looks slower only because it receives harder/night/compliance-heavy work | `routing_rule` routes high `complexity`/high `arrival_load`/Night cases to Orion; these elevate `queue_wait` and `cycle_time` | Orion has 42.9% High vs 12.5%; 92.9% Night vs 25%; 64.3% compliance-required vs 25%; crude vs within-complexity reversal; complexity-standardized contrast favors Orion (-2.51h) | Within-stratum comparisons still may be confounded (severity within “High”), and overlap is weak in some cells | Exact/PS matching or weighting on (`complexity`,`shift`,`arrival_load`,`compliance_required`) + overlap diagnostics; then sensitivity analysis for `unobserved_case_risk` | After adjustment, Orion effect on `cycle_time` is near 0 or negative; residual effect is highly sensitive to plausible unobserved risk |
| 2 | Beneficial Orion effect masked by case mix (Orion genuinely faster) | More certified reviewers → lower `review_service` and/or fewer `rework`; stronger night coverage → lower `queue_wait` | Within each complexity, Orion has lower wait and slightly lower service; Low/Medium rework not higher; High cycles shorter despite heavy compliance | Overall Orion mean is higher and overall rework pct is higher; one Orion appeal in High (small n) | Within-route or within-case-type comparisons (add richer case descriptors); if any reviewers work across groups, compare within reviewer; mediation-style decomposition of `resource_group`→(`queue_wait`,`review_service`,`rework`)→`cycle_time` | Matched cases show Orion has lower `review_service` and/or `queue_wait` without worse `quality_outcome`; mediated improvements explain most of any effect |
| 3 | Harmful Orion effect in night operations (fatigue/coordination/compliance bottleneck) | Night staffing gaps or fatigue increase `queue_wait`, increase `rework`, or slow `review_service` for Orion | Orion is overwhelmingly Night and High load; Orion-Night mean cycle (23.05) exceeds Nova-Night (17.50) | In the observed High-Night cell, Orion wait is lower than Nova (19.47 vs 22.75) and service is similar; Nova has few night cases | Quasi-experiment: staff Nova for additional night coverage and route a small fraction of Night/eligible cases to Nova; negative control: Night + No compliance + Low complexity | If harmful Orion-night effect exists, among Night and matched risk, Orion shows higher `queue_wait`/`review_service` and higher rework; otherwise differences shrink or reverse |
| 4 | Shift/load centered: `resource_group` is a proxy for overloaded regimes (time-varying confounding) | Routing reacts to real-time backlog; when load is high, Orion is chosen and `queue_wait` inflates | Many Orion cases have High `arrival_load`; `queue_wait` is a large share of `cycle_time` | Within complexity, Orion wait is lower, which is inconsistent with Orion simply representing “overload” | Queue-state reconstruction: add queue length/WIP at `Classify` and review start; estimate effect of `resource_group` conditional on queue state (and check if effect concentrates in `queue_wait`) | Conditioning on queue state largely eliminates any `resource_group` effect; residual differences mostly disappear and are concentrated in `queue_wait` |
| 5 | Compliance/rework mediation: delays are driven by compliance capacity and rework loops, not the group label | `compliance_required` triggers `compliance_performed` and waiting (counted in `queue_wait`); `rework` adds extra `review_service` | Compliance-required cases average 26.79h vs 11.95h; Orion has far more compliance-required cases; rework adds repeated Review | Within compliance-required cases, Orion and Nova means are similar; within complexity Orion still looks faster | Front-door-like mechanism analysis: separate “compliance waiting” from other waiting (needs timestamps); compare compliance turnaround by shift; check whether differences appear mainly in compliance-required variants | Most of the delay gap is explained by compliance-wait (and rework) measures; improving compliance capacity reduces `cycle_time` across groups |
| 6 | Selection/measurement: completed-only sample and composite wait metric bias comparisons | Conditioning on `case_completed` and using `queue_wait` that mixes multiple waits can create spurious group differences | Prompt explicitly states only completed cases; `queue_wait` is not pure queue; Orion handles harder cases (more likely to be censored) | No direct evidence of differential censoring in this extract | Include open/in-progress cases with extract date; survival analysis with censoring; negative-control timing (e.g., Register→Classify duration if available) that should not depend on post-Classify assignment | If selection bias is material, estimated group effects change substantially when censored cases are included; negative-control timing shows no group difference |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (1–2 wks) | Instrumentation + routing audit: log `routing_rule`, reviewer ID/certification, compliance start/end, and queue state at `Classify` and review start; include open cases in extracts | Reduce measurement/selection bias; enable queue reconstruction | system / all cases | No operational change; privacy review for reviewer IDs | Variant frequencies; group-specific DFGs; distributions of `queue_wait` split into (post-Classify queue vs compliance wait); completion/censoring rates | Enables valid adjustment/overlap checks and detects selection bias | Roll back only if logging harms performance or violates privacy constraints |
| Phase 2 (2–4 wks) | Target capacity: add Night compliance coverage or pooled compliance team with WIP limits and explicit SLAs; do not change group labels yet | Reduce compliance-related waiting inside `queue_wait` | shift (Night blocks) | Maintain certification requirements; monitor backlog spillover to Day | `queue_wait` for compliance-required variants; DFG stability (no unintended control-flow change); rework rate | Identifies whether “bottleneck” is compliance/queue capacity rather than reviewer group | Roll back if Night backlog (queue_wait) rises >20% or SLA breaches exceed agreed threshold for 3 consecutive days |
| Phase 3 (4–6 wks) | Small randomized routing pilot on an eligibility slice with overlap (e.g., Low/Medium complexity, clearly defined risk band; optionally Night-only): randomize `resource_group` at `Classify` (10–20% of eligible cases) | Identify total and mechanism-specific effects of assignment | case-level (at `Classify`) | Exclude extreme-risk cases; cap diversion rate; real-time monitoring of queue lengths; quality guardrails (appeals/rework) | ITT effect on `cycle_time`; mediation indicators (`queue_wait`,`review_service`,`rework`); variants/DFGs per arm; completion rates | Best available causal identification under operational constraints; quantifies trade-offs | Stop if pilot arm increases mean `cycle_time` by >3h or doubles rework/appeals relative to control for 2 consecutive weekly reads |
| Phase 4 (scale) | Adaptive routing policy: route based on predicted severity + real-time queue state (e.g., keep High with Orion unless Orion queue exceeds threshold; shift Low/Medium when Nova has slack) | Optimize queueing while preserving skill match | case-level policy | Staged rollout; fairness checks; capacity planning by shift | Continuous monitoring of standardized cycle time (by complexity/shift), DFG drift, and queue-state metrics | Converts learning into a robust policy and avoids brittle “50%” rule | Roll back if standardized `cycle_time` worsens in any complexity stratum by >10% for 2 weeks or quality deteriorates |

Do not implement a blanket “move 50% of Orion cases to Nova” policy based on crude means. First, change the KPI to a complexity- (and ideally shift-) standardized cycle-time metric and monitor queue_wait vs review_service separately; today’s data already show a Simpson’s-paradox reversal. Second, relieve the likely true bottleneck mechanisms (queueing around night work and compliance): add night compliance capacity and/or a shared compliance pool with explicit WIP limits, and measure the impact on queue_wait for compliance-required cases. Third, run a small routing pilot: for a clearly defined, moderate-risk eligibility set where both groups can legally process the work, randomize assignment at `Classify` (e.g., 10–20% of eligible cases for 4–6 weeks), with hard safeguards on backlog, rework, and appeals. Use process-mining outputs (variants/DFGs) to ensure you did not unintentionally change the control-flow. Only after the pilot should you scale targeted reassignment (e.g., shift low/medium cases when Orion’s queue exceeds a threshold) rather than a fixed 50% transfer.

def trace_to_activities(trace: str) -> list[str]:
    return [a.strip() for a in trace.split(\">\") if a.strip()]


def dfg_from_traces(traces: list[str]) -> Counter[tuple[str, str]]:
    c: Counter[tuple[str, str]] = Counter()
    for tr in traces:
        acts = trace_to_activities(tr)
        c.update(zip(acts[:-1], acts[1:]))
    return c

        res = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        dfg = res[0] if isinstance(res, tuple) else res
        items = sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: (-kv[1], kv[0]))
        print(f\"\\nGroup: {g}\")
        for (a, b), k in items:
            print(f\"  {a} -> {b}: {k}\")
except Exception as e:
    print(\"\\nPM4Py not available or failed; using order-only DFG above.\")
    print(\"Error:\", repr(e))
```

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Residual severity within `complexity` may drive both routing and time — add a quantitative case risk/severity score (or its key inputs) available at `Classify`.
2. `queue_wait` mixes post-Classify queueing with compliance waiting — add compliance start/end and review start timestamps to decompose waiting.
3. Group effects may actually be reviewer effects — add reviewer ID + certification/tenure and concurrent workload at assignment.
4. Time-varying backlog likely drives both routing and performance — add queue length/WIP snapshots at `Classify` and at review start.
5. Completed-only data can induce selection bias — include open/in-progress cases with an extract date to enable censoring-aware time-to-completion analysis.
