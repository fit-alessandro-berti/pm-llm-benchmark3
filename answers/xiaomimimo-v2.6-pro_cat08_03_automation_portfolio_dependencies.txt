## 7. Final recommendation

**I recommend Portfolio R — A5 (missingdocument checklist & nudging), A4 (entitymismatch resolution assistant), A6 (financialstatement extraction & normalization), A9 (contract generation with controlrule checks) — delivered in the phase order above.**

Why this set:

1. **It hits all three failure modes the evidence points at, instead of the biggest bar in the frequency chart.** Rework (3,300/2,800 missingdoc loop with 52 % repeated; 1,750/1,160 mismatch loop; 650/510 remediation loop), synchronisation/criticalpath delay (entity mismatch on the critical path in 82 % of its cases; the document leg of the *concurrent* KYC/document track; the packaging join inputs), and risk exposure (provisional signing 6.5 %; terms mismatch 8.5 %/14 %; the 380 cases running `Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation` with no traceable decision).
2. **It is anchored on the resource network, not just on activities.** A6 releases ~40 % of the analyst hub (which absorbs 45 % of its capacity on document handling and is central in the workingtogether network); A4 releases about one third of a 3FTE specialist pool whose handover network funnels through two people; A5 removes RMtriggered loops at their root cause; A9 removes 4,400 h of legal drafting and flattens the monthend peak that drives the foureyes breaches. Each module's freed capacity is *earmarked* for exception handling and for absorbing the inflow its siblings generate — this is what prevents "automationinduced queueing" at `Credit analysis` (28 h) and `Committee packaging` (26 h).
3. **It satisfies the compliance mandate genuinely.** A9 is a control module (decisionterms mapping, KYCclearance hard block at the earliest controllable point) and A4 is a second one (closes the decisiontraceability shortcut, forces reclearance). A11 is explicitly not automated until those controls are proven, and A10 is sequenced after them.
4. **It fits the integration and delivery constraints.** Two deep core integrations (A9, A4) inside six months, three sidecar/medium modules — no crowding out, and no dependency of a "downstream bot" on unreliable upstream controls: A9 ships only behind the Phase0 decisiondata gate ( 98 % traceable decisions, product rule library with Creditline renewal regression tests).

**Why the highestfrequency activity should *not* be automated first.** `Register application` is the largest activity by count (11,880) but carries only ~1 % of total waiting exposure (17,800 of ~1.57 M waithours/yr) and saves about **1 FTE** in a pool that is already **below 65 % utilisation after 15:00** — its queue is generated by arrival patterns, batching and handovers, not by a capacity shortage. Automating it removes labour that is not the binding constraint while pushing work-in-progress faster into `KYC check` (4 h wait) and `Financial document review` (18 h wait + 24 % rework), where the real constraints live; measured endtoend cycle time would barely move and could worsen. It also consumes one of only two deep coreintegration slots. By contrast `Resolve entity mismatch` appears in only 1,750 cases (14.6 %) yet is on the critical path in 82 % of them, holds 31 h of median wait and 38 % rework, consumes a third of the scarcest pool, and blocks both KYC clearance and the legal right to sign — 3 % of wait exposure, but a disproportionately large share of *endtoend* time and of control exposure. **Frequency allocates labour; dependencies, joins and loops allocate cycle time and risk.** We nonetheless take the one genuinely valuable part of A1 — duplicate/entity data quality — as a shared data service feeding A4, so the dataquality benefit is captured without spending a module or an integration slot.

**Deliberate sacrifice and its decision rule.** The largest single wait block (`Customer signing`, 44 h  20 % of wait exposure) is left to Year 2. That is intentional: the process already lets 6.5 % of cases sign before KYC clearance, so automating signature first would industrialise a control violation and pull cases into activation while the entitymismatch and contractterms defects upstream are still live — accelerating exactly the cases that generate the 1.1 % remediation and the 8.5 % contractremediation loops. If the board weights "time to funding" above capacity and rework, swap **A5  A10** (accepting the WIP and funnelconversion risk and compensating with a manual documentchasing cell built from backoffice slack); the reverse swap is my recommendation because loop removal protects the 59 % funnel conversion and improves the input quality on which A6 depends.
