# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining artifacts show a system that is locally efficient but globally unstable: the fastest path is also the most frequent, yet it creates downstream complaints, compliance exceptions, and hidden rework costs.

- **The fast path is dominant but risky.**  
  The frequency directly-follows graph shows that **3,600 of 5,000 cases go to Auto eligibility screen**, and **2,550 of those go directly to Fast approval**. Fast approval then sends **2,300 cases to Dispatch replacement**. The performance DFG explains why this path is attractive: **Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval has median 0.4 hours**, **Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement has median 1.8 hours**, and **Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close has median 3.2 hours**. But the same artifact family shows the long tail: **Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen has median 70 hours and p90 180 hours**.

- **The contradiction: cheap before rework, only slightly cheap after rework.**  
  The rework statistics state that **Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits**. Also, **customer complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases**, compared with **only 3.3% for the Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device path**. This is the core trade-off: the most frequent path improves average cycle time and upfront cost, but worsens total landed cost, quality, and compliance.

- **The slower reviewed/repair path is better for quality, but not universally acceptable.**  
  Segment outcomes show that for **Premium high-value devices**, complaint/reopen is **22% on the fast path versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path**; for **Standard high-value devices**, it is **18% versus 6%**. That supports stronger control for high-value claims. However, the reviewed path is slow: **Premium high-value median cycle time is 96 hours**, **Standard high-value is 120 hours**, and the performance DFG shows painful waiting in places such as **Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review: median 26 hours, p90 88 hours**. The temporal profile also flags that **Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote is often too slow for Premium cases**. Therefore, the slow path is not inherently better; it is better only where quality/compliance risk outweighs speed.

- **Evidence completion is a major structural weakness.**  
  The DFG shows large evidence-loop volume: **Auto eligibility screen -> Request missing evidence: 650**, **Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence: 780**, then **Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review: 890** and **Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned: 320**. The rework statistics add that **Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it**. The segment data identifies **Unclear defect / missing evidence as 22% of cases, with median cycle time of 140 hours** and the highest abandonment rate. This is not merely a speed problem; it is a customer-experience and process-stability problem.

- **Conformance is weaker than the headline fitness suggests.**  
  Token-based replay gives **log fitness 0.91**, but only **68% of traces fully fit** the normative Petri net. Alignment diagnostics show frequent move-on-log deviations such as **Fast approval before Evidence completeness**, **Customer complaint/reopen after close**, and **Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices**. The discovered model has **low precision**, because many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy. This indicates uncontrolled variant behavior, not just isolated exceptions.

- **Resource constraints make blunt optimization dangerous.**  
  Manual eligibility review has only **7 reviewers**, and the queue grows sharply above **82% utilization**. Fraud/risk review has only **2 specialists** and bottlenecks on Mondays and after campaigns. Repair quote delays are caused mostly by waiting for part-price confirmation. Quality check inspectors are underused except after repair campaigns, while the escalation board has only **one daily 45-minute slot** and poor scalability. These constraints mean that simply pushing more cases to manual review, or simply adding a gate everywhere, can create new bottlenecks.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Risk Tiered Eligibility with Guarded Fast-Track

## Redesigned process flow

Replace the current largely undifferentiated Auto eligibility screen with a **segment-aware and risk-aware routing engine** at Intake.

1. **Intake claim enrichment**  
   At Intake, classify each claim by:
   - customer segment: Premium vs Standard;
   - product risk class: low-risk accessory, mid-risk device, high-value device;
   - claim value, especially whether above EUR 400;
   - defect-type risk;
   - evidence completeness score;
   - fraud/risk score.

2. **Lane 1: Protected fast lane for low-risk, evidence-complete claims**  
   For Premium low-risk accessories and Standard low-risk accessories with sufficient evidence and low fraud risk:
   - Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close.
   - For Premium low-risk accessories, use the explicit R2 exception allowing dispatch before full evidence completion, but record the exception and perform post-dispatch audit.
   - For Standard low-risk accessories, require evidence completeness before dispatch unless policy explicitly allows a low-value exception.

3. **Lane 2: Reviewed lane for high-value and high-risk claims**  
   For Premium or Standard high-value devices, claims above EUR 400, fraud flags, or defect types with high wrong-replacement risk:
   - Auto eligibility screen may triage, but must not produce unconditional Fast approval.
   - Route to Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before any Fast approval, satisfying R1.
   - After review, route to Repair quote, gated replacement approval, or Claim denied.

4. **Lane 3: Evidence-deferred lane for unclear or incomplete claims**  
   For unclear defect / missing evidence cases:
   - Do not force a fast decision.
   - Route to structured evidence completion before technical decision.
   - If evidence remains insufficient, route to Manual eligibility review or controlled denial, not repeated generic requests.

5. **Embedded compliance blocks**
   - Block Fast approval for claims above EUR 400 unless Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review has occurred.
   - Block Dispatch replacement before evidence completeness except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.
   - Block final close or denial of a reopened claim unless Manual eligibility review has occurred, enforcing R3.
   - Store routing reason codes for audit and process mining.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign primarily targets:

- **Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen**;
- **Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval**;
- **Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement**;
- high-value Premium and Standard claims;
- Premium low-risk accessory claims needing sub-24-hour performance;
- Standard low-risk accessory claims that can tolerate up to 72 hours with visibility;
- compliance deviations related to R1, R2, and R3.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **routing control based on segment and risk**, not average-case optimization.

- It preserves the fast path where the evidence supports it: low-risk accessories, especially Premium low-risk claims where speed is contractually sensitive.
- It prevents the most damaging current behavior: high-value claims moving through Auto eligibility screen and Fast approval without proper review.
- It reduces low-precision variant explosion by forbidding illegal combinations such as auto-approved high-value dispatch without risk review.
- It improves conformance by making R1 and R2 enforceable in the workflow rather than relying on post-hoc detection.
- It should reduce downstream complaint/reopen for high-value segments, where the current fast path has complaint/reopen rates of 18–22%, compared with 5–6% on reviewed/repair paths.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It sacrifices speed for high-value and unclear claims. Those cases may move from fast medians of roughly 20–28 hours to reviewed/repair medians of 96–120 hours.
- It may increase manual review load. The prompt says updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by **15–35%**. With only 7 reviewers and queue sensitivity above 82% utilization, this is a real risk.
- It does not by itself solve repeated evidence requests, wrong replacement part selection, or repair quote delays.
- It does not fully solve Premium high-value customer experience unless paired with expedited review, better visibility, or a quality gate.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence Completion Factory with Assisted Customer Self-Service

## Redesigned process flow

Redesign the evidence loop as a dedicated, customer-facing evidence completion process rather than a generic Request missing evidence activity.

1. **Evidence requirement generation at Intake**  
   After Intake claim, use defect type, product class, customer segment, and claim value to generate a specific evidence checklist:
   - serial number;
   - proof of purchase;
   - photos or video of defect;
   - accessory/device identification;
   - symptom description;
   - diagnostic telemetry if available.

2. **Customer evidence portal with save-and-return**  
   If evidence is incomplete:
   - send a tailored portal link by SMS/email;
   - show required items, reason for request, and expected completion time;
   - allow customers to save progress and return later;
   - provide automated validation for photo quality, serial readability, and mandatory fields.

3. **Automated evidence validation**  
   Before returning the case to eligibility:
   - validate completeness;
   - validate readability and basic consistency;
   - match serial/product data to warranty status;
   - assign an evidence completeness score.

4. **Assisted capture for low digital literacy**  
   For customers who do not complete the portal:
   - offer phone-assisted upload;
   - retailer or service-point capture;
   - mail-in evidence kit for accessories;
   - agent-guided diagnostic session for unclear defects.

5. **Routing after evidence completion**
   - If evidence is complete and low-risk: route to Auto eligibility screen or Fast approval.
   - If evidence is complete but high-value/high-risk: route to Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review.
   - If evidence remains incomplete after assisted capture: route to controlled manual decision or abandonment prevention workflow.

6. **Premium low-risk accessory exception**  
   For Premium low-risk accessories, dispatch may occur under R2 exception while remaining evidence is collected post-dispatch, but only if identity/warranty validation is sufficient and audit logging is performed.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign targets:

- **Request missing evidence** loops;
- **Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review**;
- **Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned**;
- the **Unclear defect / missing evidence** segment, which is 22% of cases and has median cycle time of 140 hours;
- cases where evidence requests occur multiple times;
- Standard low-risk customers who tolerate 72 hours if status visibility is good;
- Premium customers where repeated evidence requests damage service expectations.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **front-loading structured evidence capture** so that eligibility and technical decisions are made on complete information.

- It reduces repeated evidence requests. The prompt states that a new evidence portal could reduce repeated evidence requests by **30–45%**.
- It reduces the long median 26-hour and p90 88-hour wait from Request missing evidence to Manual eligibility review by making evidence completion asynchronous and customer-driven.
- It reduces abandonment by making the request clearer, saveable, and assisted.
- It improves compliance with R2 by confirming evidence completeness before dispatch, except where Premium low-risk accessory rules explicitly allow an exception.
- It improves decision quality for Auto eligibility, Manual eligibility, and Fraud/risk review because the evidence pack is standardized by defect type.
- It can reduce downstream complaints caused by premature approval on incomplete evidence.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It shifts effort to the customer. If the portal is poorly designed or low digital literacy is not supported, abandonment may increase.
- It may increase initial case-handling complexity and requires technology investment.
- It does not directly prevent wrong replacement part selection once a claim is approved.
- It does not directly solve repair quote delays caused by part-price confirmation.
- If evidence completion is successful, it may increase inflow to Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review, shifting pressure rather than removing it.

# 4. Redesign C: Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate and Repair-First Fulfillment Cell

## Redesigned process flow

Redesign the fulfillment stage by inserting a risk-adjusted quality gate and making repair a more deliberate alternative to replacement.

1. **Decision routing after eligibility**
   After Fast approval, Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, or Repair quote, route the case by fulfillment risk:
   - low-risk accessory replacement;
   - mid-risk device replacement;
   - high-value device replacement;
   - repair candidate;
   - reopened/complaint case.

2. **Tiered pre-dispatch quality gate**
   Before Dispatch replacement:
   - For low-risk accessories: automated part match and inventory check; human inspection only by sampling.
   - For mid-risk devices: automated part match plus targeted human check for defect types with historically high wrong-part risk.
   - For high-value devices: mandatory lightweight quality gate using Quality check inspectors.
   - Gate checks replacement part selection, serial/defect compatibility, warranty coverage, evidence completeness, and R1/R2 compliance.
   - The gate should be designed to add approximately **6–18 hours**, as indicated in the prompt, not an open-ended inspection delay.

3. **Repair-first cell for high-value or repairable claims**
   Where repair is technically feasible and customer acceptable:
   - Repair quote using a pre-approved part-price catalog to reduce waiting for part-price confirmation;
   - Customer approval with clear time/cost trade-off;
   - Repair device;
   - Quality check;
   - Rework repair if needed;
   - Quality/compliance close.

4. **Premium repair experience offset**
   For Premium high-value devices, where the reviewed/repair path has median cycle time of 96 hours:
   - offer expedited repair slot;
   - provide proactive status updates;
   - consider temporary loan device or priority shipping where commercially justified.

5. **Closed-loop complaint and reopen handling**
   For Customer complaint/reopen:
   - enforce Manual eligibility review before final close or denial, satisfying R3;
   - classify root cause: wrong part, incomplete evidence, eligibility error, repair quality, logistics damage;
   - reserve escalation board for high-impact or Premium cases because it has only one daily 45-minute slot;
   - feed root causes back into auto-screen rules, evidence templates, and quality gate checks.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign targets:

- **Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement**;
- **Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen**;
- high-value Premium and Standard device claims;
- claims with high wrong-replacement cost, especially where wrong replacement costs EUR 410;
- repairable claims where the repair path has lower complaint/reopen;
- rework repair, especially for high-value devices where rework is 12.5%;
- underused Quality check capacity;
- repair quote delay caused by part-price confirmation.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **downstream error prevention and repair orchestration**.

- It catches wrong replacement selection before the customer receives the item, reducing dispatch-related complaints and reshipments.
- It uses the relatively underused Quality check inspectors to create a lightweight gate, rather than relying only on scarce manual reviewers or fraud specialists.
- It shifts suitable high-value claims toward repair, where complaint/reopen is much lower than on the fast replacement path.
- It reduces the long-tail complaint pattern where Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen has median 70 hours and p90 180 hours.
- It improves compliance by checking R1/R2 before dispatch and enforcing R3 on reopened claims.
- It can reduce total cost despite adding gate time, because avoiding one high-value wrong replacement saves an expected EUR 410 plus complaint handling and reshipment costs.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It deliberately sacrifices some speed for high-value replacement cases by adding 6–18 hours.
- If applied too broadly to low-risk accessories, it can damage Premium low-risk SLAs and create service credits.
- It may shift load to repair technicians and Quality check inspectors.
- It depends on accurate part catalogs and defect-type mapping; otherwise the gate may produce false positives or false negatives.
- It does not by itself solve evidence completion or initial eligibility misclassification.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A failure modes

1. **Manual eligibility review overload**  
   If updated auto-screen rules increase manual inflow by 15–35%, the 7-reviewer team may exceed the 82% utilization threshold. The queue could grow sharply, causing Premium high-value SLA breaches and longer Standard high-value cycle times.

2. **Misclassification at Intake**  
   If segment, value, defect type, or evidence completeness data are inaccurate, the routing engine may either block legitimate low-risk fast claims or allow risky high-value claims to pass. The first preserves speed but damages quality; the second recreates the current compliance problem.

3. **Fraud/risk review congestion**  
   More high-risk routing may worsen the existing Fraud/risk review bottleneck, especially on Mondays and after marketing campaigns, because only two specialists are available.

**Likely bottleneck migration:**  
Pressure migrates from Auto eligibility screen and Dispatch replacement to **Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review**. If those are constrained, escalation board demand may also rise.

---

## Redesign B failure modes

1. **Digital exclusion and increased abandonment**  
   The evidence portal may reduce repeated requests for digitally fluent customers but increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy. Current abandonment from Request missing evidence is already 320 cases in the DFG.

2. **Overly strict or poorly targeted evidence validation**  
   If validation rules are too rigid, customers may be stuck in a new portal loop: upload rejected, re-upload requested, no human help available. This would replace manual evidence loops with automated evidence loops.

3. **Incomplete defect-type evidence templates**  
   If evidence requirements are not well mapped to defect types, unclear-defect cases may still stall. The 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment would remain problematic despite the portal.

**Likely bottleneck migration:**  
Pressure migrates from manual evidence chasing to **customer support, assisted-capture channels, and evidence-validation exception handling**. It may also increase downstream Manual eligibility review once more cases become evidence-complete.

---

## Redesign C failure modes

1. **Quality gate becomes a new delay point**  
   If the gate is applied too broadly or inspectors are not scheduled flexibly, the 6–18 hour design target may become a much longer delay. Premium low-risk claims could accidentally be caught in the gate, triggering service credits if cycle time exceeds 24 hours.

2. **Repair-first overload**  
   If too many high-value claims are pushed to repair, repair technicians and Quality check may become congested. Repair quote delays could persist if the part-price catalog is incomplete.

3. **False positives/negatives in part matching**  
   If the gate incorrectly blocks valid replacements, cycle time rises without quality gain. If it incorrectly passes wrong parts, complaints and reshipments continue.

**Likely bottleneck migration:**  
Pressure migrates from Dispatch replacement and complaint handling to **Quality check inspectors, repair technicians, and repair quote preparation**. Escalation board demand may also rise if gate decisions are contested.

---

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

The experiments should be staged so that the organization does not simultaneously overload manual review, evidence support, and quality inspection.

## Stage 1: First pilot — Redesign A lite for high-value control and Premium low-risk protection

**Pilot scope:**  
Run a controlled pilot of segment-risk tiered eligibility for:

- Premium low-risk accessory claims, protecting the sub-24-hour expectation;
- high-value claims above EUR 400 or in high-risk defect categories, where Fast approval should require Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review.

Use a matched control group by product family, region, or intake cohort so current and redesigned paths can be compared.

**Why first:**  
The prompt says updating auto-screen rules could be deployed in two weeks. This is the fastest way to test the central contradiction: whether restricting risky fast approvals improves total outcome without destroying acceptable speed.

**Metrics to monitor:**

- segment-level median and p90 cycle time;
- share of high-value cases receiving Fast approval without required review;
- R1/R2 violations;
- complaint/reopen rate by segment;
- service credits for Premium low-risk cases exceeding 24 hours;
- Manual eligibility review utilization, especially against the 82% threshold;
- Fraud/risk review queue and Monday/campaign spikes;
- abandonment and escalation-board usage.

**Stop/go thresholds:**

- Go if high-value complaint/reopen moves materially from the current 18–22% range toward the reviewed-path range of 5–6%, without causing severe manual overload.
- Go if Premium low-risk median cycle time remains under 24 hours and service credits stay flat or fall.
- Pause or adjust if Manual eligibility review utilization consistently exceeds around 82–85% and queue time grows sharply.
- Pause if Fraud/risk review p90 worsens substantially, for example by more than 20–30%, without a corresponding quality gain.
- Stop if high-value cycle time becomes much worse than the current reviewed path, for example p90 beyond roughly 150–180 hours, while complaint rates do not improve.

**Pilot scope:**

- claims entering Request missing evidence;
- the unclear defect / missing evidence segment;
- selected low-risk accessory claims where evidence is incomplete but customer self-service is feasible.

**Why second:**  
Stage 1 may increase the need for clean evidence before manual or fraud review. If evidence loops remain, manual review will receive incomplete cases and the bottleneck will worsen.

**Metrics to monitor:**

- percentage of evidence-request cases with two or more evidence requests, baseline 26%;
- evidence completion rate;
- abandonment rate from evidence requests;
- median cycle time for unclear/missing-evidence cases, baseline around 140 hours;
- manual time spent chasing evidence;
- complaint rate after evidence request;
- portal drop-off by customer segment;
- assisted-capture volume and success rate.

**Stop/go thresholds:**

- Go if repeated evidence requests fall by roughly the prompt’s expected 30–45% range.
- Go if unclear/missing-evidence median cycle time falls materially, for example by 25–40%, without increasing abandonment.
- Go if abandonment stays flat or declines, especially for low digital literacy customers.
- Pause if abandonment rises meaningfully, for example more than 10–15% relative to baseline, or if portal rejection rates create a new loop.
- Stop if assisted-capture demand overwhelms support channels and cycle time worsens.

## Stage 3: Pilot Redesign C for high-value replacement and repair decisions

**Pilot scope:**  
Pilot the pre-dispatch quality gate and repair-first cell on:

- high-value replacement claims;
- defect types with known wrong-part risk;
- reopened claims involving dispatch errors;
- repair candidates where repair quote delay is currently caused by part-price confirmation.

Use the underused Quality check inspectors for the gate.

**Why third:**  
Once Stage 1 has improved routing and Stage 2 has improved evidence completeness, the quality gate can evaluate cleaner cases. This reduces the chance that inspectors waste time on incomplete or misrouted claims.

**Metrics to monitor:**

- wrong replacement rate;
- Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen rate;
- high-value complaint/reopen rate, baseline 18–22% on fast path;
- rework repair rate for high-value devices, baseline 12.5%;
- gate lead time, target 6–18 hours;
- repair quote cycle time;
- Customer approval -> Claim abandonment rate;
- Quality check utilization;
- repair technician load;
- escalation-board usage.

**Stop/go thresholds:**

- Go if high-value dispatch complaints and wrong replacements fall materially toward the lower reviewed-path complaint range.
- Go if gate lead time remains mostly within 6–18 hours and does not create a new severe bottleneck.
- Go if Quality check utilization rises from underused to a stable level without chronic overtime.
- Pause if gate delay regularly exceeds 18–24 hours or Premium service credits increase.
- Stop if repair-first routing creates a repair technician bottleneck with p90 repair cycle times materially worse than current reviewed-path performance.

## Sequencing logic

The sequence is deliberately staged:

1. **Stage 1 stops the most damaging fast-path behavior** by making eligibility segment- and risk-aware.
2. **Stage 2 fixes the information problem** so that cases entering manual review, fraud review, or technical decision are evidence-complete.
3. **Stage 3 fixes the fulfillment error problem** by adding a quality gate and making repair a controlled alternative.

This avoids overloading one resource at once. If all three were launched together, manual reviewers, support agents, quality inspectors, and repair technicians could all become congested simultaneously, making it impossible to identify the true cause of improvement or deterioration.

## How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

Each pilot must measure the full case lifecycle, not just the local activity being changed.

- Track **end-to-end cycle time**, including post-dispatch complaints, reopens, reshipments, and rework.
- Track **total landed cost per case**, including labor, replacement cost, repair cost, service credits, reshipment, and complaint handling.
- Track **abandonment and denial** separately from completed cases, because a faster process may look better only by pushing cases out of the funnel.
- Use **process mining replay and conformance checking** after each pilot to see whether deviations disappear or merely move to new paths.
- Monitor **resource utilization across the whole chain**, not only the pilot activity. If manual review improves but fraud review, quality gate, or repair quote becomes congested, the improvement has been displaced.
- Compare **segment-level outcomes**, because average improvements can hide damage to Premium low-risk accessories, high-value devices, or unclear-defect cases.
- Keep a **matched control group** or staged rollout so that changes can be compared against current behavior.
- Observe outcomes for a sufficient post-close window, because the current DFG shows that complaints can occur with median 50–70 hours after fast approval or dispatch and p90 up to 150–180 hours.

The key test is not whether one activity becomes faster or cheaper. The test is whether the redesigned process improves the full warranty claim journey: fewer compliance exceptions, fewer wrong replacements, fewer evidence loops, lower total rework cost, and better segment-appropriate customer experience.
