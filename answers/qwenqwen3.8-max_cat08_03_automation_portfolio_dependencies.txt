## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

The main automation dependency chain is:

**A1/A2  A3/A4  A5/A6  A7/A8  A9/A10  A11**

But the dependencies are not purely sequential. Several modules must be available together to avoid creating new bottlenecks or control failures.

### Upstream data quality dependencies

- **A1 Auto-registration** depends on duplicate detection and clean customer master data. If duplicates or entity mismatches are not caught early, they will surface later in KYC or contract preparation.
- **A2 Classification recommender** depends on accurate application, product, and exposure data. It is a prerequisite for safe routing into:
  - `Classify product and exposure -> KYC check` (`7250`)
  - `Classify product and exposure -> Financial document review` (`3940`)
- **A3 KYC hit pre-classifier** and **A4 entity-mismatch assistant** depend on reliable KYC screening data and entity resolution data. They also depend on a clear distinction between true mismatches, benign name variants, and unresolved compliance hits.
- **A5 Missing-document checklist** depends on product-specific document requirements and RM/customer data. It should reduce:
  - `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` (`3300`)
  - `Request missing documents -> Financial document review` (`2800`)
- **A6 Financial-statement extraction** depends on document completeness and document quality. It should not be deployed aggressively before A5 or equivalent document-intake controls are working.
- **A8 Committee pack auto-assembly** depends on KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. It is a downstream aggregator and will fail if upstream artifacts are incomplete.
- **A9 Contract generation** depends on clean KYC clearance, committee decision data, and a maintained contract-rule library.
- **A10 E-signature** depends on KYC clearance and contract readiness. The process variant allowing provisional signing is a control weakness and should not be automated without a hard gate.
- **A11 Loan activation bot** depends on all prior controls being complete. It should be the last module automated, not an early win.

### Legal and compliance control dependencies

Several automation candidates are unsafe unless specific legal controls are enforced:

- **KYC clearance must precede customer signing.**
  - A10 must not allow signing unless KYC clearance is confirmed.
  - Provisional signing should be blocked, alerted, or require explicit compliance approval.
- **Contract terms must match committee decision.**
  - A9 requires a governed rule library.
  - A12 should track contract-remediation loops:
    - `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` (`650`)
    - `Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` (`510`)
- **Four-eyes principle.**
  - Credit analysis and committee packaging should not be completed by the same person in High-exposure SME cases.
  - Automation should enforce role separation, especially during month-end rush when violations currently occur.
- **Auditability.**
  - Every model suggestion, human override, and final decision must be logged.
  - This applies strongly to A2, A3, A4, A6, A8, A9, and A11.

### Human escalation capacity

Automation will not remove human judgment; it will change where humans are needed.

- **Compliance specialists:** only 3 FTE and highly dependent on two senior specialists. A3 and A4 must not simply generate more unresolved hits. They must reduce specialist burden through triage, evidence assembly, and recommended resolution paths.
- **Credit analysts:** 9 FTE and central in the working network. A5 and A6 can increase clean inflow. Without WIP limits, this can overload analysts at `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging`.
- **Relationship managers:** A5 can reduce incomplete instructions, but if the checklist is too rigid, RMs may resist or bypass it.
- **Legal ops:** underutilized when KYC is slow but overloaded at month end. A9 could smooth this, but only if upstream KYC and decision data are reliable.
- **Back office:** currently visible but underutilized after 15:00. Automation can shift back-office work from registration to exception handling, document follow-up, and data remediation.

### External valuation dependency

Collateral valuation is a major synchronization point:

- `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` (`2400`)
- `Collateral valuation request -> Wait for external valuation` (`2350`)
- `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` (`1980`)

The mean wait for external valuation is **58 hours**, with **19% late**. This is especially important for Equipment finance, where collateral is required in about **71%** of cases.

Automation options:

- **A7** can triage and route valuation requests earlier.
- **A8** can prevent committee packaging from waiting unnecessarily for non-collateral cases.
- However, external valuer capacity remains outside direct bank control. Automation should be paired with valuer SLAs, parallel triggering where legally permissible, and exception escalation for late valuations.

### Core-banking integration constraints

Only **two modules** can be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months. The safest first two deep integrations are:

1. **A2 Product/exposure classification recommender**  
   Reason: it controls routing and exposure treatment early.

2. **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**  
   Reason: it strengthens KYC clearance and prevents a low-frequency but critical-path blocker.

Other modules can be staged differently:

- **A5** can use lightweight portal/document checklist integration.
- **A6** can integrate with document management and credit-file systems after the first six months, or through non-core APIs initially.
- **A9**, **A10**, and **A11** should wait until upstream control data is reliable.
- **A1** should not consume one of the first two deep-integration slots unless it is explicitly paired with A2 and downstream capacity protection.

### Concurrency note

The information system records **KYC check** and **Financial document review** sequentially, but in practice they are often concurrent. Automation should preserve or improve this concurrency. For example:

- A2 should allow parallel KYC and document tracks where risk policy permits.
- A5 and A6 should not force a serial document loop if KYC can proceed independently.
- A8 should recognize which artifacts are truly blocking committee packaging and which can be completed in parallel.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Recommended first-year portfolio

**A2, A4, A5, A6**

This portfolio is deliberately front-end, exception-focused, and control-aware. It does not automate the highest-frequency activity first. Instead, it prepares the process so that later automation of registration, contract generation, signing, and activation can be done safely.

### Phase 0: Guardrails and baseline — Month 0–1

**Purpose:** Create the conditions for safe automation.

Actions:

- Define control rules for:
  - KYC clearance before signing,
  - contract/committee decision matching,
  - four-eyes in High-exposure SME cases,
  - model suggestion/override audit trail.
- Establish baseline metrics for:
  - classification rework,
  - entity-mismatch waiting,
  - missing-document repeat rate,
  - normalization rework,
  - credit-analysis waiting,
  - contract remediation,
  - provisional signing,
  - four-eyes violations.
- Define human escalation capacity for compliance specialists and credit analysts.
- Agree WIP limits for credit analysts and compliance specialists.

Gate to proceed:

- Compliance approves audit and escalation design.
- Process owner approves capacity thresholds.
- Data owners confirm availability of application, KYC, entity, and document data.

---

### Phase 1: Controlled routing and document intake — Months 1–3

**Modules:** A2 and A5 pilot  
**Deep integration slot 1:** A2  
**Integration approach for A5:** lightweight portal/document checklist, not core-deep if possible.

Why this order:

- A2 prevents misrouting before volume is accelerated.
- A5 reduces RM-caused incomplete instructions and prepares better documents for A6.

Key metrics:

- Classification accuracy.
- Override rate and override reasons.
- Misroute rate between KYC and financial document review.
- High-exposure SME detection accuracy.
- Document completeness at first review.
- Repeated missing-document rate.
- RM adoption and bypass rate.

Gate criteria to expand:

- High-exposure cases are not misrouted.
- No meaningful increase in KYC waiting or compliance backlog.
- Document completeness improves and repeated missing-document requests decline.

## 7. Final recommendation

### Recommended portfolio

I recommend the **Bottleneck/Synchronization portfolio**:

1. **A2 Product/exposure classification recommender**
2. **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**
3. **A5 Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging**
4. **A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization**

This portfolio is the strongest process-level choice because it targets the activities that create the most harmful rework, waiting, and control exposure, rather than simply automating the most frequent task.

### Why the highest-frequency activity should not be automated first

`Register application` has high frequency: **11,880** cases, 8 minutes mean service time, and 1.5 hours median waiting. It is politically attractive and operationally simple. But frequency alone is misleading.

The process-mining evidence shows that the dominant problems are not primarily in registration:

- `Resolve entity mismatch` has only **1,750** cases but a **31-hour** median wait and is on the critical path in **82%** of cases where it appears.
- `Request missing documents` has a **52%** repeated exception rate.
- `Financial document review` has a **24%** rework/exception rate and an **18-hour** wait.
- `Normalize financial statements` has a **75-minute** mean service time, a **20-hour** wait, and a **16%** rework rate.
- `Wait for external valuation` has a **58-hour** wait and is a major synchronization point for Equipment finance.
- `Customer signing` has a **44-hour** wait and a **21%** reminder loop, but it cannot legally be automated safely unless KYC clearance is enforced.

If the bank automates `Register application` first, it will likely push more cases faster into:

- classification,
- KYC,
- entity resolution,
- financial document review,
- missing-document loops,
- credit analysis.

Without A2, A4, A5, and A6, this can increase queues, rework, and control violations. The bank would optimize a low-risk, low-delay activity while leaving the true constraints untouched.

### Why the recommended portfolio is better

The recommended portfolio does four things:

1. **Controls routing early** through A2.  
   This prevents cases from being sent down the wrong KYC, credit, or committee path.

2. **Removes a critical-path KYC blocker** through A4.  
   Entity mismatch is low frequency but high impact. It blocks KYC, contract preparation, and signing.

3. **Reduces document loops** through A5.  
   This addresses one of the highest rework rates in the process and reduces RM-caused incompleteness.

4. **Improves analyst-ready data** through A6.  
   This reduces normalization effort and prepares cleaner input for credit analysis and future committee-pack automation.

### Trade-off

The trade-off is explicit: the bank gives up the easiest high-frequency automation win in exchange for a safer, higher-impact reduction in end-to-end delay, rework, and control risk.

A1 can still be automated later, but only after:

- classification accuracy is stable,
- KYC/entity exception handling is under control,
- document completeness has improved,
- credit-analyst capacity is protected,
- audit and override logs are reliable.

### Final position

The bank should not start with the highest-frequency activity. It should start with the activities that control the flow, prevent exceptions, and protect downstream compliance. The recommended first-year portfolio is therefore:

**A2 + A4 + A5 + A6**

with later consideration of A7, A8, A9, A10, A1, and A11 only after the upstream process is stable, auditable, and capacity-protected.
