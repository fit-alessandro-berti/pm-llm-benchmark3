## 3. Causalabductive explanation

```
UI_release -> recorded_labels -> alignment_moves
policy_change -> true_process -> business_outcomes
ETL_change -> recorded_order -> alignment_moves
reviewer_behavior -> true_process -> measured_fitness
recorded_labels -> alignment_moves
recorded_order -> alignment_moves
normative_model -> alignment_moves
alignment_moves -> measured_fitness
business_outcomes <- true_process
```

Multiple mechanisms jointly generated the observed conformance collapse. The **UI release** renamed the activity label from **Classify** to **Risk Screen**, causing the **normative model** (which expects the former label) to misalign with the logged events—a classic **label/semantic logging change** (hypothesis3). Simultaneously, the **ETL pipeline** switched to warehouse ingestion timestamps, altering the perceived order of the **Decision** and **Notify** events (**timestamp/order defect**, hypothesis4). An additional technical event, **Queue Routed**, was emitted after risk screening, becoming an extra node in the event stream that the model did not anticipate (**technicalevent artifact**, hypothesis6). These changes reduced the **alignment fitness** dramatically, even though the underlying operational **process** (the way cases were actually handled) may have remained largely unchanged—only minor procedural tweaks (e.g., the **policy change** for EUR50k claims) introduced genuine behavioral variance (**real process change**, hypothesis1). Moreover, a small subset of reviewers might have **bypassed compliance checks** (**employee noncompliance**, hypothesis5), but stable approval and appeal rates suggest this is not a widespread issue. Because several factors improved once the technical artifact (Queue Routed) was stripped and labels were remapped, the most parsimonious explanation is that **technicalevent/extraction artifacts and label mismatches** chiefly drove the conformance drop, while **real process adjustments** and **possible lowlevel noncompliance** remain secondary concerns that could be addressed separately. The fact that business outcomes did not shift implies that any genuine noncompliance either has not yet materialized in measurable metrics or is being offset by other systemic factors, underscoring that unchanged outcomes neither confirm correct conformance nor eliminate the risk of hidden violations.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

The dominant driver of the observed conformance collapse appears to be **technicalevent/extraction artifacts combined with label/semantic mismatches**. The UI release (riskscreen label) and the subsequent ETL switch to warehouseingestion timestamps introduced both a **label mismatch** (`Risk Screen` vs. `Classify`) and an **ordering defect** (`Notify` preceding `Decision` and the extraneous `Queue Routed` event). These changes dramatically lowered alignment fitness, as evidenced by the steep drop in fitness from 93% (April–May) to 32% (June–July) and the subsequent improvement after applying a label mapping and removing the `Queue Routed` event. The fact that business outcomes (approval and appeal rates) remained stable suggests that while the conformance score suffered, the underlying operational behavior may have stayed largely intact; any real compliance lapses are likely limited and could be driven by **lowlevel noncompliance** (e.g., occasional bypasses of the `Compliance Check`), which are corroborated by the small number of traces lacking that step and the compliance memo reminder.  

**Confidence levels:**  
- **Primary explanation (technicalartifact + label change):** ~85% confidence. The evidence ledger logs strong support from alignment diagnostics, variant frequencies, and direct UI audit confirming the label shift.  
- **Secondary concern (real compliance problem):** ~45% confidence for widespread noncompliance, but ~65% confidence that isolated bypasses exist, given the policy memo and occasional missing compliance checks.  

**Immediate actions:**  
- **IT:** Reimplement the UI label as `Classify` in logs or apply a robust mapping during preprocessing; revert ETL timestamp source to the original eventtime for `Decision`/`Notify`; remove or correctly model the `Queue Routed` technical event.  
- **Process Owners:** Update the normative model to reflect the new UI label (or create a versioned model that accepts both labels) and consider adding an optional `Supervisor Signoff` node to cover the EUR50k policy change.  
- **Compliance:** Conduct a focused audit of cases lacking the `Compliance Check` step to quantify any genuine noncompliance, and issue targeted training to reinforce the reminder memo.  

**What must not be claimed yet:**  
- That the stable approval and appeal rates prove the process is fully compliant; they only indicate no *detectable* systemic drift.  
- That the optional signoff model fully captures the EUR50k requirement without additional data constraints (e.g., claimvalue attribute).  
- That the removal of `Queue Routed` alone will restore full conformance; residual misalignments may require additional labeling or ordering fixes.  

Overall, addressing the technical artifacts and semantic mismatches should restore conformance metrics, while a separate compliance audit will clarify whether latent noncompliance remains.

## 8. Modelgovernance recommendation

1. **Version every process model and eventsemantic contracts** – maintain a registry of LTS versions linked to release notes (e.g., LTSv1.0 = AprilMay; LTSv2.0 = postUI release).  
2. **Define explicit eventsemantic contracts** – specify required activity labels, optional technical events, and timestamp sources for each model version; enforce via automated schema validation before conformance checks.  
3. **Adopt a technicalevent policy** – classify technical events (e.g., `Queue Routed`) as “metadata only” and instruct mining pipelines to either drop them or model them explicitly; require signoff before introducing new such events.  
4. **Provision timestamp provenance** – always store both eventtime and ingestiontime; document the chosen timestamp source for alignment computations and allow runtime switching.  
5. **Implement dataaware constraints** – embed policy thresholds (e.g., EUR50k signoff) as constraints in the process model; enforce them as optional or mandatory edges based on case attributes; monitor constraint violation rates separately.  
6. **Establish postrelease validation** – run a lightweight conformance sanitycheck on a random sample immediately after any UI, ETL, or policy change; flag any fitness drop >10% for manual investigation before full deployment.  

These rules collectively prevent the conflating of semantic changes with process drift, ensure that new technical artifacts are accounted for, and provide a systematic way to keep process models aligned with both business policy and data realities.
