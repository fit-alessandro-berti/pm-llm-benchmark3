# 2. Integrated explanation

The trajectory of service times and error rates across a shift is not a simple story of workers warming up and then getting tired; rather, it is an emergent system dynamic driven by **shifting case composition early in the shift** colliding with **demand-induced queue pressure, behavioral workarounds, and experience asymmetries late in the shift**.

### Phase 1: The Early-Shift Backlog & Triage Absorbency (Positions 1–15)
At shift start, service times are at their slowest (18.8 min) and error rates are moderately elevated (3.4%). While a minor cognitive warm-up effect exists, the primary driver here is **case-mix and deliberate selection**. Shifts—especially Early (48% high-complexity in hour 1) and Night (56%)—inherit tough, high-priority carryover exceptions left unresolved by the prior shift. Senior staff actively pull these complex cases early to clear high-risk backlog, while Junior staff forced to take complex early items struggle (5.1% early error rate). As this complex carryover is digested over the first 90 minutes, the queue drains from 46 to 39, average complexity drops from 0.72 to 0.61, and both service times and error rates fall together.

### Phase 2: Mid-Shift Equilibrium (Positions 16–50)
Between roughly 2.5 and 4.5 hours into the shift, the center reaches operational stability. Queue lengths reach their nadir (31–33), case complexity normalizes (0.50–0.54), and workers are warmed up. Crucially, the resource-experience table proves that **pure time-on-task fatigue is weak when workload is manageable**: later in the shift under *low WIP*, Seniors (2.2% vs. 2.1% early) and Mid-level staff (2.9% vs. 2.8% early) show virtually zero quality degradation, and Juniors actually *improve* (4.4% vs. 5.1% early) once freed from complex carryover cases.

### Phase 3: Late-Shift Queue Pressure, Speed-Up, and Workarounds (Positions 51–76+)
The paradox of the late shift—where mean service time drops to 10.8 minutes even as errors triple to 7.9% and 7-day rework hits 13.7%—is explained by **endogenous queue pressure compounded by cherry-picking, batching, and measurement timing**:
1. **Speed–Quality Tradeoff under High WIP:** Late in the Day shift (and inherited by Late shift), arrival rates surge, pushing mean queue lengths to 62–88. Facing visible backlog (`wip_at_start` > 70), workers accelerate across *all* complexity bands (e.g., high-complexity service time drops from 22.4 min to 16.8 min; low-complexity drops from 12.7 min to 9.4 min). Rushing induces cognitive shortcuts: under high WIP, Mid-level errors more than double (to 6.8%) and Junior errorsnearly triple (to 12.4%).
2. **Easy-Case Selection & Sequential Batching:** Why does mean complexity fall to 0.43 at positions 76+? Under pressure to clear headcount metrics before clocking out, workers cherry-pick simpler cases (leaving complex ones to become the *next* shift's pos 1–5 carryover!) and Juniors (H and I) resort to sequential batching of 9–18 `Document Review` tasks—artificially depressing average service time while increasing slip-through mistakes.
3. **Automation Friction & Deferred Quality Checks:** When queues exceed 70, `Bot_Risk` classifications require more frequent human correction (either due to surge-specific claim anomalies or hasty downstream misinterpretation), adding hidden rework load. Simultaneously, because staff defer `Quality Check` activities to the final 45 minutes of the shift, errors committed *earlier* in the afternoon are detected and logged in a concentrated end-of-shift burst, exaggerating the apparent late-shift error spike in event-level timestamps.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To disentangle these overlapping mechanisms, we must combine event-log enrichment and process discovery in **PM4Py** with panel econometric/statistical modeling.

### Analytical Design

1. **Within-Resource & Within-Shift Position Effects:** Use resource-activity fixed-effects regressions (mixed-effects or panel FE) for `log(service_time)` and `logit(error_flag)` so Senior_A is only compared to Senior_A on the same `concept:name`. Model `minutes_since_shift_start` and `sequence_position_within_shift` using splines to separate non-linear warm-up (first 60 min) from late-shift time-on-task (>300 min).
2. **WIP/Queue Stratification & Complexity Adjustment:** Include smooth interaction terms between `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`, and `case_complexity_score`. Apply Propensity Score Weighting or Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition across shift bins to quantify how much of the 18.8 $\rightarrow$ 10.8 min drop is due to `case_complexity_score` falling (0.72 $\rightarrow$ 0.43) versus behavioral speed-up.
3. **Shift & Time-of-Day Controls (Circadian Disentanglement):** Exploit shift overlaps! Compare events occurring between **14:00–16:00** for Day-shift workers (`minutes_since_shift_start` $\in [300, 420]$) against Late-shift workers (`minutes_since_shift_start` $\in [0, 120]$) facing the exact same global `queue_length_at_start` and clock hour. Repeat for **06:00–08:00** (Night end vs. Early start).
4. **Batching Detection & Timestamp Attribution:** Run `pm4py.discover_batches` to flag sequential/concurrent batching per resource-activity pair. Create a "de-batched" indicator and re-estimate service-time regressions without batched `Document Review` events. Link `Quality Check` and `Rework Correction` flags back to the *originating* decision event (`Eligibility Decision` / `Document Review` / `Risk Classification`) within the same `case:concept:name` rather than attributing the error to the timestamp when `Quality Check` ran.
5. **Resource-Experience Interactions:** Interact `resource_experience_months` (or group tier) with `wip_at_start` and `case_complexity_score` to test differential resilience under load, plus model **case-picking selection** (conditional logit of selected case's complexity relative to the available queue distribution at `start_timestamp`).
6. **Downstream Rework & Bot_Risk Path Analysis:** Filter cases passing through `Bot_Risk` (`Risk Classification`), stratify by `queue_length_at_start` ($\le 70$ vs. $>70$), and use performance DFGs and rework tallies (`pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`) to trace whether high-queue bot cases loop disproportionately into `Rework Correction` or `Customer Callback`.

### PM4Py-Oriented Python Implementation / Pseudocode

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# 1. Format Event Log & Enrich Temporal / Workload Features
df = pd.read_csv("claims_event_log.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"])
df["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"])

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)

# Compute case-level service/waiting times, arrival/finish rates, and temporal features
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df)
temporal_feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

# Event-level service time in minutes
df["event_service_min"] = (
    df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# Verify overall per-activity service time summary via PM4Py
activity_srv_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

# 2. Visual Diagnostics: Dotted Chart & Event Distribution (Burst & Batching Clues)
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(
    df, "dotted_chart_resource_shift.png",
    attributes=["start_timestamp", "org:resource", "concept:name"]
)
qc_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "concept:name", ["Quality Check", "Rework Correction"], level="event"
)
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(
    qc_df, "qc_distribution_by_hour.png", distr_type="hours"
)

# 3. Automated Batching Discovery
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    merge_distance=15 * 60  # 15-minute proximity window in seconds
)
# Extract batched event indices to flag sequential batching (e.g., Junior_H / Junior_I)
batched_case_acts = set()
for act_res, batch_types in batches:
    for b_type, batch_instances in batch_types.items():
        for inst in batch_instances:
            if len(inst) >= 5:  # Flag runs of 5+ batched events
                for ev in inst:
                    batched_case_acts.add((ev["case:concept:name"], act_res[0], act_res[1]))

df["is_batched"] = df.apply(
    lambda r: int((r["case:concept:name"], r["concept:name"], r["org:resource"]) in batched_case_acts),
    axis=1
)

# 4. Downstream Rework & Bot_Risk High-WIP Path Comparison
rework_per_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)

high_q_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df[df["queue_length_at_start"] > 70], "org:resource", ["Bot_Risk"], level="case"
)
low_q_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df[df["queue_length_at_start"] <= 70], "org:resource", ["Bot_Risk"], level="case"
)

perf_dfg_high_q, sa_hq, ea_hq = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(high_q_cases)
perf_dfg_low_q, sa_lq, ea_lq = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(low_q_cases)

# Optional: Filter slow/problematic rework paths & cluster trace variants
slow_rework_paths = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df, ("Risk Classification", "Rework Correction"), min_performance=0, max_performance=86400*7
)
long_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, min_performance=3600*4, max_performance=86400*14)
trace_clusters = list(pm4py.cluster_log(df))

# 5. Circadian Overlap Filter (e.g., 14:00-16:00 Day Late-Phase vs. Late Early-Phase)
# Filter human events and trace-back errors from Quality Check to originating decision
human_df = df[df["org:resource"] != "Bot_Risk"].copy()
human_df["hour_of_day"] = human_df["start_timestamp"].dt.hour

# Within-Resource, Within-Activity Statistical Models (De-batched & Adjusted)
srv_model = smf.ols(
    "np.log1p(event_service_min) ~ C(org_resource) + C(concept_name) + C(hour_of_day) "
    "+ wip_at_start * case_complexity_score "
    "+ wip_at_start * resource_experience_months "
    "+ minutes_since_shift_start + I(minutes_since_shift_start**2) "
    "+ priority + is_batched",
    data=human_df.rename(columns={"org:resource": "org_resource", "concept:name": "concept_name"})
).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": human_df["shift_id"]})

err_model = smf.logit(
    "error_flag ~ C(org_resource) + C(concept_name) + C(hour_of_day) "
    "+ wip_at_start * case_complexity_score "
    "+ wip_at_start * resource_experience_months "
    "+ minutes_since_shift_start + priority + is_batched",
    data=human_df.rename(columns={"org:resource": "org_resource", "concept:name": "concept_name"})
).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": human_df["shift_id"]})
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Drawing individual worker performance conclusions from raw event-log aggregates in this center is scientifically invalid and operationally dangerous for five reasons:

1. **Endogenous Selection Bias & Asymmetric Role Expectations:** Resources do not face identical work. Seniors protect the center by pulling complex carryover cases in the first hour (keeping error rates at 2.1% despite `case_complexity_score` averaging 0.72), while Night staff handle urgent, high-complexity exceptions (56% first-hour share). Conversely, Juniors who get stuck with unpicked complex cases or surge-hour spillover look worse on raw metrics. Comparing unadjusted worker averages punishes cooperative behavior (taking hard cases) and rewards defensive cherry-picking.
2. **Timestamp & Attribution Measurement Error:** Event logs record *when an event was logged*, not necessarily when the cognitive work or error occurred. Because `Quality Check` is batched into the final 45 minutes of the shift, errors made at 13:30 may be flagged at 16:40—creating an illusion of late-shift carelessness or unfairly tagging the reviewer/closer rather than the upstream step. Similarly, sequential batching (`Document Review` by Junior_H/I) means `start_timestamp` to `time:timestamp` reflects bulk form-submission in the UI rather than true per-case reading time.
3. **Management-Created Queue Pressure:** Workers do not control claim arrival rates or shift staffing curves. The Day shift starts with a queue of 38 and ends at 75 because staffing fails to match afternoon demand. When `wip_at_start` crosses 70, error rates double across *every* experience tier (even Seniors jump from 2.2% to 4.9%). Blaming workers for speed-quality tradeoffs triggered by understaffed demand peaks shifts accountability for capacity planning onto frontline staff.
4. **Automation Degradation & Handoff Opacity:** `Bot_Risk` executes `Risk Classification` in milliseconds, inflating system throughput metrics, yet requires surging human corrections when `queue_length_at_start > 70`. If `Bot_Risk` misclassifies edge-case claims during surges, downstream human agents face anchoring bias and extra cognitive load under extreme time pressure—yet any missed override gets recorded as a *human* `error_flag` or `rework_flag`, masking upstream model drift.
5. **Toxic Perverse Incentives from Punitive Error Metrics:** Penalizing raw late-shift error rates violates Goodhart’s Law. Workers will respond rationally to protect themselves: avoiding high-complexity cases, refusing to pick up cases in the final two hours (exploding carryover queues), colluding to rubber-stamp `Quality Check` without logging `error_flag` (causing hidden defects to surface later as costly 7-day customer rework or regulatory breaches), and burning out. Process-mining metrics at the individual level should be used strictly for coaching, routing support, and ergonomic workload design—never raw punitive ranking.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

To manage the triage center safely without conflating workload, complexity, and worker capability, deploy an operational process-mining dashboard updated per shift and hourly window with the following **nine core indicators**:
