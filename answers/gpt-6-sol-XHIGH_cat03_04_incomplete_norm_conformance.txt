## 1. Conformance semantics

A **forbidden-behavior candidate** is an observed relation whose stated scope matches an explicit prohibition. N3, T6, and E10 supply closed-world prohibitions. The graph edge warrants prompt investigation; a confirmed business violation additionally requires valid object identities, event links, and timing. A **missing-mandatory candidate** is an expected link or step absent for an instance within a mandatory rule's scope. Its denominator must be the eligible events or objects, and a missing directly-follows edge need not mean the business step never happened. **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is an extra relation for which the norm says nothing, including relations marked unspecified. It is neither implicitly prohibited nor automatically approved by another policy. **Typical-pattern absence** is a departure from a common path; it calls for explanation, not an automatic noncompliance verdict.

A **likely data-quality error** is an edge plausibly created by incorrect joins, missing links, event typing, or incomplete extraction; it should be repaired or validated before attributing conduct to staff. An **abstraction mismatch** occurs when the graph representation, such as directly-follows or an aggregate type edge, is narrower or broader than the business rule. **Insufficient evidence** means the summary lacks the instance-level sequence, scope, or provenance needed to decide. These labels can coexist: E10 is a forbidden-edge candidate and a likely join error. The norm is closed-world for its forbidden edges and scoped mandatory requirements only. All other absent normative edges remain open-world; rarity and lack of a tolerance band create no new prohibition.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG shows activity succession along objects of a given type. It can flag N3's two cancellation-to-dispatch paths and the N1 and N4 direct-edge shortfalls. It cannot by itself establish why an edge is absent, whether all events were captured, or whether a successor occurred later. In particular, N1 says every newly created **standard** Order should *eventually* enter validation. The summary's 98/100 directly-follows count is only a proxy. If review lies between creation and validation, the business rule may be satisfied while the direct edge is absent. The three `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` observations are plausible intermediates, but counts alone cannot match them to the two gaps. Likewise, N4 applies only to externally shipped packages; the stated 198/200 denominator fits that scope, yet two missing direct edges do not prove that two packages were never packed. E4 and E5's 200 links each are consistent with packing and dispatch events, without proving which package had which sequence.

N2 and N5 are typical, not mandatory. The 83 direct Order closures and 72 invoice successors can coexist within traces; one cannot subtract either from 100 to declare failures. The 92/110 invoice payment path, four write-offs, and 14 without observed successor require terms, maturity, and extraction horizon. N6 leaves the single reopen-to-close path unregulated. A path can therefore be suspicious as a direct-edge anomaly while conforming as an eventual lifecycle; N1 is the clearest example. N3 remains urgent because its rule is a temporal prohibition, although the two edges still need identity and chronology checks.

### OTG

An OTG establishes that relationships between object types occurred, with weights under the discovery method's counting convention. It cannot show that *each* Add Item or Dispatch Package event carried the required pair, nor prove birth happened in one genuine business event without examining source events. T1's Order–Item weight 1450 and T2's Package–Delivery weight 200 support the existence of the required interactions, but E2 has only 1448 Order links and E6 only 198 Delivery links. The apparent disagreement is possible because OTG type interactions can be contributed by other events and weights need not count distinct events. T3's Invoice–Payment weight 92 is typical and does not excuse E8's unlinked Record Payment. T6's one cobirth edge is an explicit forbidden candidate and merits source-level verification. Order–Customer (100), Order–RiskCase (3), and Package–Delivery codeath (7) remain unregulated here; the latter is explained by shared confirmation events. Rarity is not a severity measure.

### ET–OT

ET–OT associates event types with object types. It can expose mandatory-link count gaps: E2's 1448 versus 1450 Add Item–Item, E6's 198 versus 200 Dispatch Package–Package, and E8's 92 versus 93 Record Payment–Payment. Those comparisons are candidates, assuming the counts reflect distinct events; inspect event IDs before asserting exactly two, two, and one defective events. It cannot establish whether a business step eventually occurred, whether an object is in scope, or whether a relationship was created by a bad join. The two carrier references do not supply a Delivery object under E6, though they may help repair the links. Finance's statement about unapplied cash does not meet E8's *explicitly typed* exception until the event is verified and typed. E10's single Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge conflicts with the prohibition in the graph, while the reused numeric identifier strongly suggests a join defect. The three review–RiskCase and three review–Customer links can be legitimate even though only the first appears as an unspecified norm row. Thus an OTG type edge may look conforming while an ET–OT event lacks a required participant, and a forbidden ET–OT edge may be a data artifact rather than real conduct.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory:** N1 has a 98/100 direct proxy, with two cases unresolved until standard-Order scope and eventual validation are checked. N4 has 198/200 direct edges for externally shipped packages, with packing order unresolved. T1 and T2 appear at weights 1450 and 200, but E2 (1448 Order links), E6 (198 Delivery links), and E8 (92 Invoice links against 93 Payment links) have candidate event-level gaps. E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 have the positive totals in the table. E8's exceptional event leaves this bucket only if verified and explicitly typed as unapplied cash; E6's two leave only if the required Delivery links are found or the scope is legitimately corrected.
- **Forbidden:** N3's two cancellation-to-dispatch Orders, T6's one Order–Payment cobirth, and E10's one Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link are prohibited *if the graph relations reflect real, correctly scoped business events*. E10 may instead be a join defect; suspected error changes the cause, not the need to remove the forbidden edge from trusted data.
- **Typical:** N2's 83 Validate-to-Close direct edges show a common path; other closure sequences can be legitimate. N5's 92 of 110 Issue-to-Payment paths coexist with four write-offs and 14 unresolved invoices. T3's 92 Invoice–Payment interactions support a common relationship; none imposes a minimum frequency.
- **Unregulated:** The 72 Validate-to-Issue paths, three Create-to-Manual-Review paths, one Reopen-to-Close path (N6), 100 Order–Customer interactions (T4), three Order–RiskCase interactions (T5), seven Package–Delivery codeaths, and three review links each to RiskCase (E9) and Customer are not violations under this norm. If another policy governs them, assess them under that policy; if a join is wrong, treat them as data defects.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1's time and representation:** Without a validation deadline and a choice between eventual and direct succession, interposed reviews generate false alarms and genuine omissions can linger. Clarify that standard Orders must reach validation by a defined milestone, irrespective of intermediate events: **mandatory**.
2. **N1's eligibility:** “Standard Order” is undefined, so 100 may be the wrong denominator. Define Order classes and exclusions at creation: **mandatory** within the specified class.
3. **N4's shipping scope and sequence:** “Intended for external shipment” and direct adjacency are ambiguous. Define when intent is fixed and require evidence of packing before dispatch, allowing documented intermediate scans: **mandatory**.
4. **N3's temporal and identity semantics:** The rule needs a canonical cancellation timestamp, shipment timestamp, and same-Order mapping; otherwise repairs or timezone errors appear as misconduct. Define these and retain the post-cancellation ban: **forbidden**.
5. **T6's birth semantics:** Imports or system migrations may manufacture apparent cobirth. Define business-event birth versus data-import timestamps, and prohibit genuine same-event Order–Payment birth: **forbidden**.
6. **E6/T2's Delivery identity:** A carrier reference may or may not resolve to a Delivery object, causing inconsistent compliance decisions. Require a resolvable Delivery object at dispatch, with a documented mapping procedure: **mandatory**.
7. **E8's unapplied-cash exception:** Staff knowledge alone cannot audit an untyped exception. Define the explicit subtype and its evidence, with Invoice links required for all other Record Payment events: **mandatory**.
8. **N2/N5's expected timing:** No closure or payment horizon means “no successor” may just be an open case. Specify reporting windows and acceptable intermediate or exception paths while retaining these as **typical**.
9. **Review and codeath relations:** The norm does not say whether Customer participation in review or joint Package–Delivery termination carries obligations. Document that these graph relations carry no conformance verdict here, leaving them **explicitly unspecified**.
10. **E10's identifier domain:** Cross-system numeric IDs can produce forbidden financial links. Require namespaced identifiers and validated joins; genuine Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice links remain **forbidden**.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are the reported graph counts, the existence of three forbidden-edge candidates under N3/T6/E10, and candidate mandatory-link gaps under N1/N4/E2/E6/E8. Whether the two direct-edge gaps mean missing business steps depends on scope and abstraction; N2, N5, and unregulated relations depend on the norm's intentional incompleteness. E10 and several missing links depend on suspected data-quality issues. Graph summaries alone cannot decide actual event order, same-object identity, exception validity, observation completeness, or final business noncompliance.
