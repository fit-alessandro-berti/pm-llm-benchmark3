# 1. Fairness dimensions

Procedural fairness here is multi-dimensional. The evidence supports different conclusions on different dimensions, so they are kept separate.

| Dimension | What it asks | What the evidence shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome parity** | Do groups receive approvals at similar raw rates? | A 78%, C 69%, B 63%, D 58%. Raw gaps to A are -9, -15 and -20 pp. | Nothing about discrimination. Groups differ strongly in risk band, documentation, channel and prior cases, and eligibility itself may differ. Raw parity is a descriptive screening signal only. |
| **Adjusted outcome disparity** | Do similar cases on measured complexity get similar outcomes? | Residual approval gaps are B -5.5 pp, D -3.1 pp, C -2.2 pp. Measured case mix removes roughly 63% (B), 85% (D) and 76% (C) of the raw gap. | No confidence intervals are given. On crude binomial standard errors (about 1.4–1.6 pp per group), B's gap is probably distinguishable from zero, D's is borderline and C's may not be. A residual is "unexplained by this model", not "caused by group membership". |
| **Procedural burden** | Do similar cases face more steps, requests, reviews and handovers? | After adjustment B has +9.5 pp more Request Missing Documents and +8.0 pp more Supervisor Review; C has +4.2 and +5.5 pp; D has +1.5 and +2.0 pp. Fast path V1 covers 42% of A but only 18–22% of the other groups. | Whether the extra steps were substantively needed in each case, for example a real document gap versus a translation or format issue. |
| **Timeliness** | Do similar cases wait equally long, including in the tail? | Adjusted cycle time is +4.1 d (C), +2.6 d (B), +0.9 d (D). Raw p90 is 38.8 d (C) and 34.2 d (B) against 21.4 d (A). Support queues take 3.8 d (language) and 4.7 d (accommodation). | Whether the time is queueing for staff, applicant response time or actual work. The log has one timestamp per event and workload is only daily. |
| **Consistency of routing** | Is the same trigger followed by the same routing in every group, and is extra routing always tied to a policy trigger? | Policy justifies Fraud Review, Accommodation Review and Language Support Contact. It does not obviously justify the extra Supervisor Review for B and C, or extra document requests at equal documented completeness. | Case-level compliance. The variant table is group-level and summarised, so trigger-to-step conformance must be checked in the log. |
| **Quality and accessibility of support** | Are rights-preserving steps delivered, on time, without added burden? | Support steps exist but have no timeliness targets, depend on 7 bilingual caseworkers and 5 specialists, and involve extra handovers in two regions. | Quality of interpretation, adequacy of accommodations, clarity of explanations and dignity of treatment. None of these are in the log. |
| **Appeal and rework burden** | Who has to fight for, or repeat, their case? | Appeal rates are 16% (D), 13% (B), 10% (C) against 6% (A); adjusted +5.7, +4.4 and +1.1 pp. Rework is 20–24% against 11% for A. | Appeal merit. Without overturn rates, a high appeal rate may mean more wrong first decisions, poorer explanations, or simply effective access to remedy. No adjusted rework figure is given. |
| **Resource-allocation fairness** | Is staffing proportionate to the demand each group generates? | A is spread over 52 caseworkers. B depends on 7 bilingual caseworkers plus 3 overloaded regional officers. C depends on 5 specialists, 2 of whom also do supervisor review. | Actual per-resource utilisation and queue lengths at the moment a case arrives. |

Two cross-cutting principles apply:

- **Equal treatment is not identical treatment.** Language support and accommodation review are meant to equalise access. They are fair only if they are delivered and do not cost the applicant materially more time and effort than similarly complex cases.
- **Legitimate purpose is not enough; proportionality matters.** A step can be legitimately triggered and still be unfairly implemented through under-capacity, missing service targets or unnecessary handovers.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

**Classification rules, fixed before looking at results**

| Label | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| **Disparity** | Any measurable group difference, raw or adjusted. Descriptive only. |
| **Legitimate routing** | The step is mandated by policy, triggered by its documented condition, applied at the same rate to all groups with that condition, and its delay is proportionate. |
| **Potential disparate treatment** | Similarly situated cases (same trigger attributes, region, channel, period) receive different routing, waits or outcomes, and no policy rule accounts for it. |
| **Structural or indirect disadvantage** | The rule is neutral or protective on its face, but its implementation (capacity, service targets, handovers) burdens the protected need. No individual bias is required. |
| **Uncertainty** | The estimate is imprecise, changes sign or size under reasonable covariate choices, or depends on covariates of doubtful validity. |

"Discrimination" in the legal or intentional sense cannot be established from an event log. The analysis can only produce evidence consistent or inconsistent with it and direct file audits.

**Matching and stratification variables**

- Tier 1, pre-treatment and relatively uncontroversial: household_size_band, region, month or intake week, and case type if available.
- Tier 2, legitimate but possibly biased: risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, prior_case_count and channel. Use them, but always report results with and without them.
- Tier 3, need indicators: language_preference and disability_accommodation_requested. The preliminary model omits these, so its group coefficients absorb the effect of the support steps. Use them to build a group × need design:
  - B with a language need versus B without one, versus A with a language need (2% of A, about 72 cases) and A without one.
  - C with an accommodation request versus C without one, versus A requesters (4%, about 144 cases).
  - This separates "being in the group" from "using the support step".
- Method: coarsened exact matching or fine stratification on Tier 1 and 2, with entropy balancing or propensity weighting as a robustness check. Report standardised mean differences and the share of cases without common support. High-risk D cases may have few comparable A cases; extrapolating there should be reported as uncertainty.
- Do not condition the total-disparity model on post-intake process variables such as Supervisor Review, number of handovers or assigned resource. These are mediators and belong in a decomposition.

**Path and variant comparison**

- Between-path composition: P(variant | group, stratum). Are groups over-represented on slow variants beyond what their triggers predict?
- Within-path rate: compare cycle time, waits and approval by group within the same variant, for example V2 for A versus B. A within-variant gap cannot be explained by "they needed a different procedure".
- Decompose each raw gap, Kitagawa/Oaxaca-Blinder style, into a composition effect (different path mix) and a rate effect (slower or harsher on the same path).
- Trigger conformance: P(Fraud Review | high risk), P(Accommodation Review | request) and P(Language Support Contact | language need) by group, plus the converse, such as Supervisor Review or Fraud Review without a trigger.

**Resource-assignment comparison**

- Compare cases per resource per day, open-case backlog at arrival and group mix per resource, using Gini or Herfindahl concentration.
- Fit a multilevel model with random effects for resource and region to separate "who handled it" from "which group". Check whether disparities persist within the same caseworker.
- Count handovers per case and A→B→A ping-pong patterns by group within strata, and test whether handover count mediates cycle time.
- Check separation of duties: is the accommodation specialist on a case also its supervisor reviewer?

**Waiting-time decomposition**

- Split cycle time into (a) queue wait for an internal resource, (b) applicant-side wait after Request Missing Documents, (c) handover wait, (d) rework loops and (e) service time.
- With a single timestamp per event, (a) and (e) cannot be separated cleanly. Treat inter-event time as an upper bound on waiting unless start/complete lifecycle data exist.
- Attribute each group's adjusted excess days to specific transitions. Use quantile regression (median and p90) and survival models with censoring for open cases.

**Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates**

- Fit nested models: M0 raw; M1 Tier 1; M2 adds prior_case_count and channel; M3 adds documentation_completeness; M4 adds risk_score_band. Report how the group coefficients move.
  - If documentation_completeness is partly caused by language barriers, controlling for it over-adjusts and M3/M4 understate B's disparity.
  - If legitimate complexity is unmeasured, all models overstate it.
  - The current adjusted figures are therefore neither upper nor lower bounds.
- Test risk-score calibration by group. Among cases sent to Fraud Review, compare the share later approved or not confirmed as fraud. A higher clearance rate for D at the same band suggests over-flagging, subject to the usual infra-marginality caveats.
- Re-measure documentation_completeness on a blind-coded audit sample to estimate differential measurement error by language and channel.
- Add omitted-variable bounds (E-values or Rosenbaum bounds), bootstrap confidence intervals clustered by resource and region, and a multiple-comparison correction.

**Subgroup checks by region and channel**

- Add group × region and group × channel interactions. The Supervisor Review backlog and the extra handovers are concentrated in two regions, so pooled estimates may be driven by them or may hide them (Simpson's paradox).
- Compare online versus in-person within each group. In-person intake already waits longer for Identity Verification.
- Check intersections, such as B with an accommodation request or D with a language need, with explicit small-cell warnings.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

**Most important disparities, ranked by concern**

1. **Support-step queues (B and C).**
   - Language Support Contact → Eligibility Assessment takes a median of 3.8 d; Accommodation Review → Eligibility Assessment takes 4.7 d.
   - Each is 65–80% of the entire median cycle time of fast path V1 (5.8 d). V4 (19.2 d) and V5 (21.6 d) are slower than the fraud path V3 (18.4 d).
   - The routing is legitimate; the delay is not justified by policy. The queues sit behind 7 and 5 people and have no timeliness target, so this is a design and capacity problem falling on rights-preserving steps.

2. **Extra document requests for B at equal documented completeness.**
   - Adjusted +9.5 pp. Completeness Check → Request Missing Documents waits 1.6 d for B against 0.4 d for A.
   - Request-docs variants (V2 + V4) cover at least 58% of B, while only 46% of B have incomplete documentation. For A the figures are about 25% and 21%.
   - V4 as summarised always includes Request Missing Documents, so language support and document requests appear bundled.
   - Part of this may be legitimate, such as translations or foreign documents. The part that remains after controlling for completeness is concerning: it suggests stricter scrutiny, a completeness measure that works differently for minority-language applicants, or unclear initial instructions.

3. **Extra Supervisor Review for B (+8.0 pp) and C (+5.5 pp).**
   - No stated rule links language or accommodation needs to supervisor review, and risk is already controlled.
   - V5 routes accommodation cases through Supervisor Review by default, and two of the five accommodation specialists are also supervisor reviewers.
   - The Supervisor Review → Decision Draft wait is also longer for B (1.9 d) and C (1.6 d) than for A (1.0 d).
   - This is the routing difference least supported by policy.

4. **Possible under-delivery of the support steps.** This is a hypothesis to verify, because the variant paths are summaries.
   - The six listed variants cover 99% of B, and Language Support Contact appears only in V4 (24%). At most about 25% of B cases show the step, while 57% need language support.
   - Likewise, at most about 33% of C cases show Accommodation Review, while 62% requested accommodation.
   - Either support is delivered informally or unlogged, the need flag differs from a formal request, or a substantial share of applicants do not receive the mandated step. The last would be a fairness problem in the opposite direction from delay.

5. **Handover chains and resource concentration.**
   - B has general → bilingual → general chains and C has general → specialist → supervisor chains, in two regions.
   - Rough arithmetic: about 710 language-need cases over 7 bilingual staff and about 510 accommodation requests over 5 specialists is roughly 100 cases per specialist. A has about 69 cases per caseworker across 52 staff. This ignores other duties and per-case effort, so it is indicative only.

6. **Fast-path access and intake waits.**
   - V1 covers 42% of A against 18–22% of the others. Much of this is plausibly case mix.
   - Submit → Identity Verification is slower for B (1.4 d against 0.8 d). This is linked to in-person intake, which is a channel effect that falls disproportionately on B and C.

7. **Appeals and rework.**
   - D (+5.7 pp) and B (+4.4 pp) appeal more after adjustment. V6, an appeal after an otherwise plain path, covers 13% of D and 8% of B against 4% of A.
   - Rework is about double A's rate for B, C and D.

**Largely consistent with legitimate complexity, conditional on the covariates being valid**

- **D's fraud routing.** V3 covers 28% of D against a 51% high-risk share, and 9% of A against 18%. That is about half of the high-risk share in both groups, so it is proportionate at group level.
- **D's fraud queue wait.** Risk Screening → Fraud Review is similar across groups (2.0–2.8 d), which suggests the fraud queue does not treat groups very differently.
- **D's residuals.** They are small: +0.9 d, +2.0 pp Supervisor Review and +1.5 pp document requests. Summing D's excess median waits at shared steps gives about 0.7 d, which matches the adjusted +0.9 d.
- **Most of the raw approval gaps.** Between 63% and 85% disappear with adjustment.
- **Decision Draft → Applicant Notification.** Differences are small (0.5–0.8 d).

D's result depends on risk_score_band being valid. If the score over-flags D, "explained by risk" becomes "transmitted through risk".

**What can and cannot be inferred**

- Can infer:
  - Raw disparities substantially overstate the like-for-like differences.
  - Residual differences point the same way, against B, C and D, on every metric.
  - For B and C the residuals fall on process features such as document requests, supervisor review, support queues and handovers, not mainly on final outcomes.
  - The largest delays are at capacity-constrained steps without service targets.
- Cannot infer:
  - Intent or individual caseworker bias.
  - Whether denials were substantively correct.
  - Whether the residuals are statistically robust, since no intervals are given.
  - Whether waits are internal queueing or applicant response time.
  - Whether the support was of good quality.
  - Whether the risk and documentation covariates are themselves fair.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**Legitimate or neutral mechanisms**

1. **Case-mix composition.** More high-risk bands, incomplete files and prior cases mechanically produce more document requests, fraud reviews and denials. This explains most of the raw gaps, especially for D.
2. **Policy-mandated routing.** Fraud Review, Accommodation Review and Language Support Contact add steps by design. Some added time is the inherent cost of doing them properly, such as scheduling an interpreter or assessing an accommodation.
3. **Unmeasured legitimate complexity.** Examples are foreign or translated documents, third-party medical evidence, and the complexity of prior cases beyond a count. These can raise document requests and review rates without unequal treatment.
4. **Regional and channel effects.** Backlogs in two regions and slower in-person intake affect whoever lives or applies there. They become a disparity only through uneven geographic and channel distribution.
5. **Applicant-side response time.** Time after Request Missing Documents may partly reflect how long applicants take to respond. This may itself be shaped by barriers.

**Concerning mechanisms**

6. **Capacity bottleneck on rights-preserving steps.** Too few bilingual and accommodation staff with no service target means these queues absorb delay unseen. This is a structural disadvantage tied directly to protected needs.
7. **Handover friction.** Each transfer adds queueing, context loss and repeated information requests. Returning the case to a general caseworker after language support may explain the co-occurrence with document requests and rework.
8. **Barrier-induced "incompleteness".** Forms, checklists and deficiency notices in the majority language yield more incomplete submissions and repeated requests. The documentation covariate is then a consequence of the barrier, and controlling for it hides part of the disparity.
9. **Defensive or heightened scrutiny.** Caseworkers who are less confident with interpreted interviews or accommodation cases may escalate to supervisors or ask for more evidence. Dual-role specialist/supervisors may make supervisor review a default for C.
10. **Risk-score feedback loop.** If risk_score_band uses prior_case_count or past denials and fraud referrals, past over-scrutiny raises today's score, which triggers more fraud review and denials, which feed future scores.
11. **Explanation quality.** Notifications that are untranslated, inaccessible or generic produce more appeals from B and D even when the decision is correct. Alternatively, higher appeal rates reflect more erroneous first decisions. The log cannot tell which.
12. **Workload spillover.** Overloaded regional officers and specialists working near capacity produce rushed decisions, more rework and longer tails.
13. **Unlogged or skipped support.** If support is sometimes not delivered (item 4 in section 3), affected applicants may go through standard paths without help. This raises document requests, denials and appeals while appearing "fast".

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias in covariates.**
  - documentation_completeness is recorded by staff and may depend on language, channel and the clarity of instructions. It is partly an outcome of the process, not a pure applicant characteristic.
  - channel may proxy for digital access, disability or language.
  - Coarse bands (risk, household size) leave residual confounding within bands.
- **Historical bias in risk scores.**
  - risk_score_band may be trained on earlier referral and denial decisions and may include prior_case_count. It can encode past disparities and may be differently calibrated across groups.
  - Controlling for it treats it as neutral ground truth, which it may not be. Not controlling for it ignores a real policy trigger. Both versions must be reported.
- **Missing applicant needs.**
  - disability_accommodation_requested captures requests, not needs; unrequested needs are invisible.
  - language_preference does not measure proficiency or literacy.
  - There is no data on digital access, representation or advocate support, health, housing instability, or the type and severity of accommodation.
  - Group labels are aggregates. Only 57% of B need language support and 62% of C requested accommodation, so group averages blur the affected sub-populations.
- **Unobserved resource workload.**
  - Workload is visible only daily. Queue position, concurrent caseload, absences, part-time status and non-case work are not.
  - Waiting cannot be attributed to "no one available" versus "deprioritised".
  - With one timestamp per event, service time and waiting time are confounded.
  - Work outside the system, such as phone calls and interpreter scheduling, is not logged.
- **Appeal quality.**
  - appeal_flag records that an appeal occurred. It does not record its grounds, its outcome (upheld or overturned), time to resolution, or whether the applicant understood the right to appeal.
  - Non-appeal is not satisfaction: barriers may suppress appeals in exactly the groups with most cause to appeal.
- **Outcome validity.**
  - case_outcome does not capture correctness against eligibility rules, benefit amount, quality of explanation, dignity of treatment or applicant effort (trips, calls, resubmissions).
  - Withdrawals and abandonment after long waits may be coded as denials or closures.
- **Selection and data-quality issues.**
  - Only submitted applications are observed; people deterred before applying are missing.
  - Open cases are right-censored.
  - Activity labels may be logged inconsistently across regions, and support steps may be performed but not recorded.
  - The variant table is summarised, and the top six variants cover 82% of A but 99% of B, so tail behaviour differs by group.
- **Statistical limits.**
  - The preliminary model reports point estimates without intervals, functional-form checks or overlap diagnostics.
  - Subgroups such as region × group × need become small.
  - Resource-level clustering inflates naive precision.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Mitigation | Intended effect | Side effects and trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **Add capacity for language support and accommodation review**: more bilingual caseworkers and specialists, cross-training, on-demand interpreters, overflow pooling across regions. | Shorten the 3.8 d and 4.7 d queues and relieve the 7 + 3 and 5-person bottlenecks. | Cost and recruitment time. Rapid cross-training may dilute quality. Reallocating staff may lengthen general queues. Remote interpretation may be worse for some applicants. Extra capacity may be absorbed by unlogged demand if coverage is currently incomplete. |
| **Service-level targets for support steps**: for example a maximum time from trigger to completed support contact, and an end-to-end target for supported cases no worse than comparable unsupported cases. Consider pausing clocks only for genuine applicant-side waits. | Make rights-preserving delay visible and owned. | Gaming, such as logging a token contact to stop the clock. Rushed or superficial support. Targets met by deprioritising other work. Clock-pausing rules can hide delay. Pair timeliness targets with quality checks. |
| **Routing redesign to reduce handovers**: single case owner, with the bilingual caseworker or specialist keeping the case through eligibility; support in parallel with Completeness Check; one consolidated document request in the applicant's language; no default Supervisor Review without a risk or policy trigger; separate the specialist and supervisor roles on the same case. | Remove ping-pong, context loss and bundled extra steps. | Concentrates more work on scarce specialists unless capacity grows. Less independent oversight if supervisor review is cut too far. Key-person dependency. Parallel steps may cause rework if the accommodation changes what evidence is needed. Transition costs. |
| **Decision-explanation improvements**: plain-language, translated and accessible notices with specific reasons, the evidence relied on and how to cure defects; a pre-decision "notice of intended denial" for fixable issues. | Fewer avoidable appeals and less rework; better perceived and actual fairness. | More drafting time per decision. Translation cost and risk of errors. Specific reasons may initially increase appeals by making errors visible, which is desirable for accuracy but raises workload. Templates can become boilerplate. |
| **Monitoring adjusted disparities**: a recurring dashboard of raw and adjusted gaps in approval, cycle time (median and p90), document requests, supervisor review, handovers, rework, appeals and overturns, by group × region × channel with confidence intervals. | Early detection; evaluation of the other mitigations. | Goodhart effects. Multiple-comparison false alarms. Small-cell privacy risks. Over-reliance on a model with biased covariates. False reassurance if adjusted gaps shrink only because biased covariates absorb them. Governance cost. |
| **Audit risk_score_band and documentation_completeness**: calibration and error rates by group; review features that proxy for group or past scrutiny; blind double-coding of completeness on a sample; review checklist language and accessibility. | Test whether "legitimate" covariates carry bias; fix scoring and measurement. | Recalibration may reduce fraud detection or shift false positives to other groups. Fairness criteria conflict (calibration versus equal error rates). Auditing takes time, and interim decisions rely on unverified tools. Findings may create legal exposure, which can generate resistance. |
| **Support-coverage conformance check**: verify that every case with a language need or accommodation request received the mandated step. | Detect under-delivery of rights. | May reveal higher true demand, lengthening support queues in the short term. Logging burden. |
| **Appeal-outcome and file-review sampling**: record overturn reasons; conduct matched-pair file audits. | Distinguish wrong decisions from poor explanations. | Labour-intensive. Reviewer bias. Small samples. |

Removing or thinning Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review to improve cycle time is not an acceptable mitigation. The aim is to make these steps fast and well resourced, not optional.

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
GROUP = "applicant_group"
DAY = 86400
SUPPORT = ["Language Support Contact", "Accommodation Review", "Fraud Review",
           "Supervisor Review", "Request Missing Documents", "Appeal Intake"]


def load(path):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
    return df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).reset_index(drop=True)


def n_cases(v):
    """get_variants returns counts or case lists depending on the version."""
    return v if isinstance(v, (int, np.integer)) else len(v)


def build_case_table(log):
    """One row per case: covariates, path flags, burden indicators."""
    g = log.groupby(CASE)
    attrs = [GROUP, "channel", "language_preference", "risk_score_band",
             "documentation_completeness", "household_size_band",
             "disability_accommodation_requested", "prior_case_count",
             "region", "case_outcome", "appeal_flag"]
    cases = g[attrs].first()
    cases["start"], cases["end"] = g[TS].min(), g[TS].max()
    cases["cycle_days"] = (cases["end"] - cases["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / DAY
    cases["month"] = cases["start"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    acts, ress = g[ACT].agg(list), g[RES].agg(list)
    cases["variant"] = acts.apply(tuple)
    for a in SUPPORT:
        cases["has_" + a.replace(" ", "_")] = acts.apply(lambda s, a=a: a in s)
    cases["n_events"] = acts.apply(len)
    cases["rework"] = acts.apply(lambda s: len(s) != len(set(s)))
    cases["reopen"] = acts.apply(
        lambda s: "Case Closure" in s and s.index("Case Closure") < len(s) - 1)
    cases["n_doc_requests"] = acts.apply(lambda s: s.count("Request Missing Documents"))

    def handovers(r):
        return sum(x != y for x, y in zip(r, r[1:]))

    def ping_pong(r):
        d = [x for i, x in enumerate(r) if i == 0 or x != r[i - 1]]
        return any(d[i] == d[i + 2] for i in range(len(d) - 2))

    cases["handovers"] = ress.apply(handovers)
    cases["ping_pong"] = ress.apply(ping_pong)
    cases["n_resources"] = ress.apply(lambda r: len(set(r)))
    return cases


def variant_shares(log, top=15):
    """Variant distribution per group: composition effect."""
    rows = []
    for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP):
        v = {k: n_cases(x) for k, x in pm4py.get_variants(sub).items()}
        tot = sum(v.values())
        rows += [{"group": grp, "variant": " > ".join(k), "share": c / tot}
                 for k, c in v.items()]
    tab = pd.DataFrame(rows).pivot_table(index="variant", columns="group",
                                         values="share", fill_value=0)
    return tab.loc[tab.max(axis=1).sort_values(ascending=False).index[:top]]


def within_variant_gap(log, variant):
    """Same path, different group: rate effect."""
    sub = pm4py.filter_variants(log, [variant], retain=True)
    out = {}
    for grp, s in sub.groupby(GROUP):
        d = np.array(pm4py.get_all_case_durations(s)) / DAY
        out[grp] = {"n": len(d), "median": np.median(d), "p90": np.percentile(d, 90)}
    return pd.DataFrame(out).T


def dfg_comparison(log, ref="Group A"):
    """Frequency per case and median duration of each edge, per group vs reference."""
    freq, perf = {}, {}
    for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP):
        n = sub[CASE].nunique()
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
        pdfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub)
        freq[grp] = {e: c / n for e, c in dfg.items()}
        perf[grp] = {e: m["median"] / DAY for e, m in pdfg.items()}
    f, p = pd.DataFrame(freq).fillna(0), pd.DataFrame(perf)
    return f.sub(f[ref], axis=0), p.sub(p[ref], axis=0)


def interval_waits(log, src, dst):
    """Eventually-follows interval, e.g. Language Support Contact -> Eligibility
    Assessment. This is not always a direct edge (see V4), so the DFG alone
    would miss it."""
    a = log[log[ACT] == src].groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("t_src")
    b = log[log[ACT] == dst][[CASE, TS]].merge(a, on=CASE)
    b = b[b[TS] >= b["t_src"]].groupby(CASE).agg(t_dst=(TS, "min"), t_src=("t_src", "first"))
    b["wait_days"] = (b["t_dst"] - b["t_src"]).dt.total_seconds() / DAY
    meta = log.groupby(CASE)[[GROUP, "region", "channel"]].first()
    return b.join(meta).groupby([GROUP, "region"])["wait_days"].describe(
        percentiles=[.5, .9])


def slow_cases(log):
    """Cases above the group-A p90 cycle time (21.4 d) and slow direct paths."""
    long_tail = pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, 21.4 * DAY, 10 ** 9)
    slow_docs = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
        log, ("Completeness Check", "Request Missing Documents"), 1 * DAY, 10 ** 9)
    slow_sup = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
        log, ("Supervisor Review", "Decision Draft"), 2 * DAY, 10 ** 9)
    share = lambda s: s.groupby(GROUP)[CASE].nunique() / log.groupby(GROUP)[CASE].nunique()
    return pd.DataFrame({"over_A_p90": share(long_tail),
                         "slow_doc_request": share(slow_docs),
                         "slow_supervisor": share(slow_sup)})


def service_waiting(log, start_key=None):
    """Service/waiting split. Only meaningful with a start timestamp; with a
    single timestamp, service time is ~0 and inter-event time is an upper
    bound on waiting."""
    kw = {"start_timestamp_key": start_key} if start_key else {}
    enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log, **kw)
    per_case = enriched.groupby(CASE)[
        [GROUP, "@@service_time", "@@waiting_time", "@@sojourn_time"]].first()
    by_group = per_case.groupby(GROUP).median(numeric_only=True) / DAY
    svc = {grp: pm4py.get_service_time(sub, aggregation_measure="median", **kw)
           for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP)} if start_key else None
    return by_group, svc


def transition_decomposition(log):
    """Contribution of each transition to excess cycle time per group."""
    d = log.copy()
    d["next_act"] = d.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift(-1)
    d["gap_days"] = (d.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(-1) - d[TS]).dt.total_seconds() / DAY
    d = d.dropna(subset=["next_act"])
    n = log.groupby(GROUP)[CASE].nunique()
    tot = d.groupby([GROUP, ACT, "next_act"])["gap_days"].sum().unstack(GROUP).fillna(0)
    return tot.div(n, axis=1)


def resource_analysis(log):
    """Handover, working-together, similarity, roles, daily workload."""
    out = {}
    for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP):
        hw = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(sub)
        wt = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(sub)
        n = sub[CASE].nunique()
        conn = pd.Series(hw.connections).sort_values(ascending=False)
        out[grp] = {"handover_edges_per_case": conn.sum() / n,
                    "top_handovers": conn.head(10),
                    "distinct_resources": sub[RES].nunique(),
                    "top10_resource_event_share":
                        sub[RES].value_counts(normalize=True).head(10).sum(),
                    "working_together_edges": len(wt.connections)}
    sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
    load = (log.assign(day=log[TS].dt.floor("D"))
               .groupby([RES, "day"])
               .agg(events=(ACT, "size"), cases=(CASE, "nunique")).reset_index())
    dual = (log[log[ACT].isin(["Accommodation Review", "Supervisor Review"])]
            .groupby([CASE, RES])[ACT].nunique().reset_index())
    dual_role_cases = dual[dual[ACT] == 2][CASE].nunique()
    return out, sim, roles, load, dual_role_cases


def conditional_routing_conformance(log, cases):
    """Policy triggers: is the mandated step present when triggered, and is the
    step absent when not triggered? Reported per group."""
    rules = {
        "fraud": ("risk_score_band", ["high"], "has_Fraud_Review"),
        "accommodation": ("disability_accommodation_requested", [True, "yes"],
                          "has_Accommodation_Review"),
        "language": ("language_preference", ["minority"], "has_Language_Support_Contact"),
    }
    res = {}
    for name, (attr, vals, flag) in rules.items():
        trig = cases[attr].isin(vals)
        res[name + "_coverage"] = cases[trig].groupby(GROUP)[flag].mean()
        res[name + "_untriggered"] = cases[~trig].groupby(GROUP)[flag].mean()
    res["supervisor_without_high_risk"] = (
        cases[cases["risk_score_band"] != "high"]
        .groupby([GROUP, "region"])["has_Supervisor_Review"].mean())
    high = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log, "risk_score_band", ["high"], level="case", retain=True)
    res["high_risk_variants"] = variant_shares(high)
    return res


def declarative_checks(log, ref="Group A"):
    """Log skeleton learned on the reference group, replayed on the others, plus
    explicit Declare constraints from policy."""
    ref_log = log[log[GROUP] == ref]
    skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(ref_log, noise_threshold=0.05)
    rule = {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}
    declare = {
        "init": {"Submit Application": rule},
        "precedence": {
            ("Identity Verification", "Eligibility Assessment"): rule,
            ("Decision Draft", "Applicant Notification"): rule,
            ("Applicant Notification", "Appeal Intake"): rule,
            ("Risk Screening", "Fraud Review"): rule,
        },
        "response": {
            ("Request Missing Documents", "Eligibility Assessment"): rule,
            ("Language Support Contact", "Eligibility Assessment"): rule,
            ("Accommodation Review", "Eligibility Assessment"): rule,
            ("Decision Draft", "Applicant Notification"): rule,
            ("Appeal Intake", "Appeal Review"): rule,
        },
    }
    mined = {grp: pm4py.discover_declare(sub, min_support_ratio=0.3,
                                         min_confidence_ratio=0.9)
             for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP)}
    out = {}
    for grp, sub in log.groupby(GROUP):
        sk = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(sub, skeleton)
        dc = pm4py.conformance_declare(sub, declare)
        out[grp] = {"skeleton_fit_share": np.mean([r["is_fit"] for r in sk]),
                    "declare_fit_share": np.mean([r["is_fit"] for r in dc]),
                    "declare_mean_fitness": np.mean([r["dev_fitness"] for r in dc])}
    return pd.DataFrame(out).T, mined


def adjusted_models(cases):
    """Nested models M0..M4 for sensitivity to possibly biased covariates, plus
    the group x need design, interactions, and a mediation-style model."""
    c = cases.copy()
    c["approved"] = (c["case_outcome"] == "approved").astype(int)
    c["appeal"] = c["appeal_flag"].astype(int)
    for col in ["has_Supervisor_Review", "has_Request_Missing_Documents",
                "has_Language_Support_Contact", "has_Accommodation_Review", "rework"]:
        c[col] = c[col].astype(int)
    g = f"C({GROUP}, Treatment('Group A'))"
    t1 = " + C(household_size_band) + C(region) + C(month)"
    specs = {
        "M0_raw": g,
        "M1_tier1": g + t1,
        "M2_plus_prior_channel": g + t1 + " + prior_case_count + C(channel)",
        "M3_plus_docs": g + t1 + " + prior_case_count + C(channel)"
                        " + C(documentation_completeness)",
        "M4_plus_risk": g + t1 + " + prior_case_count + C(channel)"
                        " + C(documentation_completeness) + C(risk_score_band)",
    }
    res = {}
    for y in ["approved", "has_Supervisor_Review", "has_Request_Missing_Documents",
              "appeal", "rework"]:
        for name, rhs in specs.items():
            m = smf.logit(f"{y} ~ {rhs}", c).fit(
                disp=0, cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": c["region"]})
            res[(y, name)] = m.get_margeff().summary_frame().filter(like=GROUP, axis=0)
    for q in (0.5, 0.9):
        for name, rhs in specs.items():
            res[("cycle_days_q%d" % (q * 100), name)] = (
                smf.quantreg(f"cycle_days ~ {rhs}", c).fit(q=q).params.filter(like=GROUP))
    full = specs["M4_plus_risk"]
    res["group_x_need"] = smf.ols(
        f"cycle_days ~ {g} * C(language_preference)"
        f" + {g} * C(disability_accommodation_requested)" + full[len(g):], c).fit()
    res["region_channel_interactions"] = smf.ols(
        f"cycle_days ~ {g} * C(region) + {g} * C(channel)" + full[len(g):], c).fit()
    res["mediation_style"] = smf.ols(
        f"cycle_days ~ {full} + handovers + has_Supervisor_Review + n_doc_requests"
        " + has_Language_Support_Contact + has_Accommodation_Review", c).fit()
    return res


def stratified_gaps(cases):
    """Exact stratification with common-support report, per region and channel."""
    keys = ["risk_score_band", "documentation_completeness", "channel", "region"]
    c = cases.assign(approved=(cases["case_outcome"] == "approved").astype(int))
    cell = c.groupby(keys + [GROUP]).agg(
        n=("approved", "size"), approval=("approved", "mean"),
        cycle=("cycle_days", "median"), handovers=("handovers", "mean"),
        rework=("rework", "mean")).unstack(GROUP)
    support = cell["n"].notna().all(axis=1).mean()
    return cell, support


def risk_score_outcome_test(cases):
    """Among fraud-reviewed cases in the same band, do groups differ in how
    often they are approved afterwards? Suggestive of mis-calibration."""
    fr = cases[cases["has_Fraud_Review"]]
    return (fr.assign(approved=(fr["case_outcome"] == "approved"))
              .groupby(["risk_score_band", GROUP])["approved"].agg(["mean", "size"]))


def main(path):
    log = load(path)
    cases = build_case_table(log)
    report = {
        "variant_shares": variant_shares(log),
        "dfg_freq_and_perf_vs_A": dfg_comparison(log),
        "language_queue": interval_waits(log, "Language Support Contact",
                                         "Eligibility Assessment"),
        "accommodation_queue": interval_waits(log, "Accommodation Review",
                                              "Eligibility Assessment"),
        "slow_cases": slow_cases(log),
        "service_waiting": service_waiting(log),
        "transition_decomposition": transition_decomposition(log),
        "resources": resource_analysis(log),
        "routing_conformance": conditional_routing_conformance(log, cases),
        "declarative": declarative_checks(log),
        "models": adjusted_models(cases),
        "strata": stratified_gaps(cases),
        "risk_outcome_test": risk_score_outcome_test(cases),
    }
    top = cases["variant"].value_counts().index[:6]
    report["within_variant"] = {v: within_variant_gap(log, v) for v in top}
    return report
```

Attribute values such as `"high"`, `"minority"` and `"approved"` are placeholders to be mapped to the actual codes in the log.

How each output feeds the assessment:

- `variant_shares` with `within_variant` separates path composition from the rate on the same path.
- `dfg_comparison`, `interval_waits` and `transition_decomposition` locate where the excess days arise.
- `resource_analysis` quantifies handover chains, staff concentration, dual-role conflicts and daily load.
- `conditional_routing_conformance` and `declarative_checks` test whether mandated steps are applied when triggered, whether extra steps occur without a trigger, and whether ordering rules hold equally for all groups.
- `adjusted_models` (M0–M4) shows how fragile the adjusted gaps are to the inclusion of risk_score_band and documentation_completeness.
- `stratified_gaps` and the interaction models show whether the disparities are system-wide or concentrated in two regions or one channel.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Overall: indeterminate on discrimination, with credible, specific indications of procedural unfairness for Groups B and C that case complexity does not explain on the current evidence.**

- **Raw outcome gaps are not evidence of discrimination.** Most of the approval gap (roughly 63–85%) is associated with measured case mix.
- **Case mix does not clear the process either.** After adjustment, B, C and D still have lower approval, longer cycle times, more supervisor review, more document requests and more appeals. These residuals are modest in size, come without uncertainty estimates, and rest on covariates that may themselves be biased. They could therefore be over- or understated.
- **Groups B and C: probably procedurally unequal in burden and timeliness.**
  - The strongest signals are not outcomes. They are extra document requests at equal documented completeness, extra supervisor review without a stated policy trigger, 3.8–4.7 day queues at rights-preserving steps with no service target, dependence on 7 and 5 specialised staff, and extra handovers.
  - The routing into support steps is legitimate. The burden attached to it appears disproportionate and structural: a design and capacity issue, not demonstrated individual bias.
  - Whether all applicants entitled to the support steps actually receive them should be verified.
- **Group D: largely consistent with risk-based routing, conditionally.** Process residuals are small and fraud routing looks proportionate to measured risk. This holds only if risk_score_band is valid and not a carrier of historical disparity, which has not been tested. D's elevated adjusted appeal rate (+5.7 pp) and residual approval gap (-3.1 pp) remain unexplained and warrant review of decision quality and explanations.
- **Not established:** intent, individual-level bias, correctness of decisions, quality or dignity of support, and whether the waits are internal queueing or applicant-side.

The process cannot be certified as procedurally fair, and it should not be labelled discriminatory. The defensible position is that support-related delay and scrutiny are a probable fairness deficit that can be acted on now through capacity, service targets and handover redesign, without removing the support steps. Stronger conclusions should wait for the routing-conformance, within-variant, sensitivity and risk-score audit analyses.
