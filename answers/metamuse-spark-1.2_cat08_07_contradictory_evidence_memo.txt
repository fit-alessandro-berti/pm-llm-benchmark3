# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Committee, Fiber Operations
**From:** Process Intelligence & Operations Advisory
**Date:** 10 Aug 2026
**Subject:** Decision on Redesign R Pilot - Do Not Scale Nationally; Deploy Selectively and Fix Before Expansion
**Recommendation:** Approve **Alternative E (Hybrid) with elements of D** - scale only the proven elements for a narrow segment while pausing harmful elements and investing in measurement.

**Situation:** Redesign R achieved its design goal for one segment but created systemic failure for others. On 9,200 pilot cases (6 weeks, 4 regions) vs 24,000 baseline cases (10 weeks), median cycle time fell 14.2 -> 10.8 days and handovers collapsed (34% -> 12% with 3+ handovers). Yet p90 rose 31.0 -> 38.5 days, p95 45.0 -> 62.0 days, first-time-right fell 82% -> 76%, complaints rose 6.1% -> 9.8%, and full estimated cost rose EUR 198 -> 207 despite lower visible cost (EUR 184 -> 161).

This is not a uniform success or failure. It is a **segment-biased redesign**: faster for simple houses, slower and more expensive for everyone else.

**What worked:** For simple houses / low-risk (34% of pilot), median -6.0 days, p90 -8.0 days, first-time-right +3pp, complaints -1pp. Merging validation+scheduling and single coordinator ownership clearly reduced waiting and handover friction. This proves the core mechanism can work when risk is low.

**What failed:** For apartments/medium-risk (-8pp first-time-right, +5pp complaints, p90 +12d), old permit-sensitive buildings (+25d p90, -11pp first-time-right, missing permit checks), and business/rural segments, the pilot traded speed for rework. Three design choices drove this:
1.  Skipping field surveys via an unvalidated predictive rule (Appointment->Field survey 31%->18%) led to more failed visits (13%->19%) and second visits (10%->17%).
2.  Moving activation checks after billing start when signal "looked acceptable" (Installation->Activation 78%->60%, Installation->Manual activation 8%->19%, Manual->Billing 6%->16%) created alignment violations: Billing before Activation, and a new long-tail Billing->Complaint relation (3%->8%).
3.  Single-coordinator ownership at 93% utilization created a bottleneck and single point of failure. Field-survey utilization fell 52% while second-visit teams spiked to 92%.

The discovered pilot model captures this: fitness 0.94 (it can replay what happened) but precision 0.51 (it allows far too many paths - skipped survey + manual activation + second visit + early billing in any combination) vs baseline 0.87/0.79. We bought fit to a messy reality at the cost of control and predictability.

**Trade-off:** Option A (scale nationally) locks in tail risk, compliance risk (permit checks, billing before service), and downstream cost. Option B (full rollback) throws away a proven 6-day median gain for 1/3 of our base. Neither is responsible.

**Decision:** 
Pause the two risky rule changes nationally. Keep coordinator ownership but redesign it for resilience. Deploy the full R only where it is proven to help, and use the next 8-10 weeks to generate the missing evidence before any broader rollout. Threshold for broader rollout must be met explicitly (see Section 6).

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness but low precision:** Fitness measures *can the model replay the log?* Pilot fitness 0.94 is high because the discovered model now includes all the shortcut paths Redesign R allowed - the simplified coordinator flow, the skip to Installation visit, the Manual activation->Billing route. Precision measures *does the model allow only what was seen?* Pilot precision 0.51 is low because that same model is too permissive - it allows any combination of those optional/skipped steps (e.g., skip survey *and* skip permit *and* manual activation *and* bill early) far beyond observed traces. Interpretation: the process became *more variable and less constrained*, so the model had to become vague to stay fit. This is not a modeling artifact to ignore; it mirrors the alignments (billing before activation, missing permit checks).

**Improved median but worse tail (p90/p95):** The distribution became bimodal. Simple low-risk cases (1/3) flow through a faster merged path with no handover delay, pulling the median down 3.4 days. Medium-risk, old-building, and business cases incur rework loops that were rare before: failed visit, second visit, manual activation, complaint escalation. Rework adds 10-25 days per affected case, inflating p90/p95. Because median is insensitive to tails, it hides this. Business impact is that average customer feels improvement, but the worst 10% - who drive complaints, SLA breaches, and cost - feel severe deterioration.

**Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Handovers fell 34%->12% because coordinator ownership concentrated context. The handover-of-work graph simplified, but the working-together graph centralized. With coordinators at 93% utilization, queueing theory predicts any absence or spike stalls cases because no one else holds the context. Field interviews confirm: when coordinator absent, case stalls. Reduced handovers improved accountability *when the coordinator is present* but removed redundancy and load balancing. Fewer handovers  more robust.

**Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:** Visible cost (to Billing start) fell 184->161 EUR because surveys (52% utilization) and handovers were cut. True cost to *working service* rose 198->207 EUR (and incompletely measured) because failed visits + second visits (second-visit team 66%->92%) and manual activations (7%->15%) and complaints (6.1%->9.8%) are downstream, labor-intensive, and partly after week 4 cut-off. We moved cost from upfront prevention to downstream correction, which is more expensive and NPS-damaging.

**Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings:** The predictive rule skipping field survey and the merged validation assume low variance. Simple houses are low variance - rule is usually right. Apartments/medium-risk and old permit-sensitive buildings are high variance (internal wiring, building access, permit needs). Skipping survey there is often wrong, causing Install->Failed visit, Install->Manual activation, and missing permit checks. This explains DFG shifts (Field survey->Install 29%->17%, Install->Activation down, Install->Failed/Manual up) and temporal finding Install->Activation worsened only for apartments. Benefits and harms are not contradictory; they are conditional on risk.

# 4. Decision alternatives

**A. Scale Redesign R nationally**
*Advantages:* Fastest median improvement nationally; immediate visible cost saving; leverages coordinator model.
*Risks:* Scales unproven predictive rule to high-risk building stock, amplifying tail delays (p95 +17d), SLA breach, complaints, and billing-before-activation compliance risk; locks in overload of coordinators/second-visit teams; low precision means process will be increasingly uncontrollable; full cost likely >207 EUR once 60-day complaints captured.
*Reasonable if:* Building stock were uniformly simple low-risk and coordinator capacity doubled - which it is not. **Not recommended given evidence.**

**B. Roll back to baseline process**
*Advantages:* Restores quality (82% FTR, 6.1% complaints), tail control, and compliance alignment; stops downstream cost bleed; buys time.
*Risks:* Sacrifices proven -6d median and -8d p90 for simple houses (34% share) and demotivates pilot teams; returns to high handover (34%) and waiting complaints; ignores that part of R demonstrably works.
*Reasonable if:* No capacity to fix coordinator resilience or validate risk rule, and leadership cannot tolerate any billing-before-activation exposure. Defensible but wasteful.

**C. Scale only coordinator ownership, but suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation**
*Advantages:* Retains handover reduction and accountability while removing two highest-risk rule changes; should preserve much of median gain with less quality loss; simpler to govern.
*Risks:* Still risks coordinator overload (93% utilization) if nationally scaled without staffing/back-up; survey savings lost; does not test whether *controlled* survey skipping could work for medium-risk; leaves precision issue partly unresolved.
*Reasonable if:* Management wants a fast, low-regret partial scale and can add coordinator backup capacity immediately. Better than A/B, but less targeted than E.

**D. Run a controlled regional experiment with better measurement (no scale yet)**
*Advantages:* Highest learning value; fixes data gaps (segments, start timestamps, full cost) before commitment; allows randomized test of survey skipping by risk stratum; de-risks compliance.
*Risks:* Delays benefit for simple houses 8-12 weeks; duplicate running costs; pilot fatigue in regions.
*Reasonable if:* Leadership prioritizes decision rigor and can tolerate short delay. Strong on evidence, weak on capturing immediate proven gains.

**E. Hybrid: Deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, and run measurement before wider scale (RECOMMENDED)**
*Advantages:* Captures proven segment benefit now; contains harm where evidence shows harm; enables targeted experiments for other segments; restores conformance (enforce permit check, block billing before activation for medium/high risk).
*Risks:* Requires accurate triage at address validation (needs risk rule validation); operational complexity of two process variants; needs coordinator load balancing and backup ownership definition.
*Reasonable if:* Organization can implement risk-based routing quickly and agrees to gate wider rollout on thresholds. **This balances value capture and harm prevention under uncertainty.**

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

Ranked by ability to change the scaling decision now.

**1. M4 - Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start**
*Resolves:* Whether visible saving (161 EUR) is illusory vs true total cost (already 207 EUR incomplete). Could flip cost justification for A vs B/E.
*Decision impact:* If 60-day total cost is 220-230 EUR, scaling without fix is indefensible. If it converges to 195 EUR, C becomes more attractive. High VOI because cost is executive gate.

**2. M6 - Validate building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes & M1 - Randomized pilot of survey skipping by risk stratum (tied)**
*Resolves:* Is the skip rule simply miscalibrated for medium-risk/old buildings or fundamentally flawed? M6 is observational validation; M1 is causal.
*Decision impact:* If rule accuracy <70% for medium-risk, E's restriction to simple houses is confirmed and permanent. If accuracy >85% with minor tuning, controlled re-expansion for medium-risk becomes possible. Directly decides scope of E.

**3. M7 - Conformance monitoring for Billing before Activation and Permit check absence**
*Resolves:* Frequency, intent, and risk of compliance-like deviations (move-on-log Billing->Activation, move-on-model permit). Determines regulatory/reputational exposure.
*Decision impact:* If >5% of bills precede successful activation, immediate IT guardrail to block billing is mandatory and scales to all options. Could force pause regardless of speed gains.

**4. M3 - Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint, Second visit**
*Resolves:* Why rework happens (e.g., no access vs. wrong material vs. signal fail vs. permit). Links DFG changes to root causes.
*Decision impact:* Cheap, fast way to diagnose which failures are rule-driven vs. capacity-driven; informs whether to fix rule (M1/M6) or staffing (M5).

**5. M2 - Instrument start timestamps for Installation, Activation, Manual activation**
*Resolves:* True waiting vs. processing time; sparse timestamps hide resource bottlenecks and handover delays.
*Decision impact:* Medium VOI - improves temporal profile and cost analysis, but decision can be made with completion-time tails and utilizations; necessary for longer-term capacity planning.

**6. M8 - Collect customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" vs "working service after installation"**
*Resolves:* NPS +21->+17 conflates two opposite experiences; complaints shifted from waiting to "installed but not working / billed before working."
*Decision impact:* Explains NPS trade-off but unlikely to change scale decision alone; supports communication and service design.

**7. M5 - Compare coordinator vs. shared-team ownership under coordinator absence**
*Resolves:* Resilience mechanism - does shared context restore flow when coordinator absent?
*Decision impact:* Important for designing sustainable ownership (backup, team inbox), but lower immediate VOI than risk-rule and cost truths; can be tested within E's rollout.

*Sequencing:* Start M4, M7, M3, M6 immediately (weeks 1-2, low cost). M1 requires randomization setup (weeks 3-10). M2/M5/M8 in parallel.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended Decision: E (Hybrid) + D's measurement discipline.** Do not scale R nationally. Do not fully roll back.

**Immediate action (next 2 weeks):**
- Enforce **risk-based routing at Address validation**: Full Redesign R (merged validation/scheduling + coordinator ownership) *only* for **simple houses, low-risk** (34% nationally, where pilot showed -6d median, -8d p90, +3pp FTR). All other segments revert to baseline flow with mandatory field survey and permit check path.

**What to pause:**
- **Pause** predictive field-survey skipping for apartments/medium-risk, old/permit-sensitive, business and rural segments.
- **Pause** post-billing activation: implement system block - no Billing start without successful Activation test or documented manual activation approval + customer notification. Treat current move-on-log Billing->Activation as defect.

**What to scale (with guardrails):**
- Scale **coordinator ownership** but redesign for resilience: cap utilization at 80%, define deputy/backup coordinator, create shared case context (team inbox/notes) to prevent stall on absence. Do not scale to >50% of regions until utilization target met.
- Keep merged validation/scheduling *only* for simple low-risk.

**What to measure next (8-week controlled window in 2 pilot + 2 control regions):**
- Prioritize M4 (60-day total cost), M7 (conformance blocks), M3 (reason codes), M6 (validate risk rule). Launch M1 randomized skip test within hybrid: randomize medium-risk apartments to survey vs. skip (with consent) to get causal estimate.
- Instrument M2 start timestamps and M8 split NPS. Monitor weekly: p90/p95, FTR, complaint, SLA breach, permit missing, billing-before-activation rate, coordinator/second-visit utilization, model precision.

**Threshold / Evidence to justify broader rollout beyond simple houses:**
Broader rollout requires **all** met in controlled experiment for that segment for 4 consecutive weeks:
- p90 31 days and p95 45 days (no worse than baseline)
- First-time-right 82% and complaint 6.5%
- Billing-before-Activation = 0% (enforced) and Permit check conformance 98% for old buildings
- Total 60-day cost EUR 198 (baseline)
- Discovered model precision 0.70 with fitness 0.85 (process is controllable)
- Coordinator utilization 80% and second-visit utilization 75%

If thresholds not met, retain hybrid segmentation and redesign the predictive rule before reconsidering. If thresholds met for medium-risk only, expand incrementally segment-by-segment, never nationally at once.

This path captures the proven gain, stops the tail and compliance bleed, and generates the information needed to earn the right to scale.
