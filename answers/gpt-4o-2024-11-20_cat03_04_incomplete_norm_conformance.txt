# 1. Conformance Semantics

To classify deviations, the following categories are defined:

### Forbidden Behavior
Observed behavior explicitly marked as forbidden by the norm. These are unequivocal violations.

**Closed-world assumption applies**: Any forbidden relation observed is noncompliant.

---

### Missing Mandatory Behavior
Mandatory patterns not observed in cases where their scope explicitly applies. These are gaps relative to the closed-world normative expectation.

**Closed-world assumption applies**: If a mandatory relation is absent in scope, it is a deviation.

---

### Unexpected but Unregulated Behavior
Behavior not explicitly specified in the norm. This includes legitimate extensions to the business process. The norm's open-world assumption permits such deviations when not explicitly forbidden or required.

**Open-world assumption applies**: These are not violations but require exploratory reasoning, especially regarding business interpretation.

---

### Typical-Pattern Absence
Typical patterns that are frequently expected but not strictly required. Their absence does not imply noncompliance but does warrant further investigation to contextualize the deviation.

**Open-world assumption applies**: Absences are suspicious if they undermine the intended process outcomes but are not formally incorrect.

---

### Likely Data-Quality Error
Suspicious or improbable relations that likely result from data recording or schema errors rather than actual process behavior. These are not violations unless confirmed to exist in the underlying system.

---

### Abstraction Mismatch
Observed discrepancies due to differences in granularity or generalization between normative expectations and recorded data.

---

### Insufficient Evidence
Cases where observed deviations cannot be tightly linked to clear normative rules, requiring additional investigation or data for robust classification.

---

### Open-World vs. Closed-World Assumptions
The classification hinges on the norm's explicit openness to unregulated patterns. **Mandatory** and **forbidden** relationships operate under a closed-world model (deviations are noncompliant). For **typical** or **unspecified** relations, the norm uses an open-world model, leaving room for flexibility.

# 3. Detailed Reasoning by Model Family

### a. OC-DFG Model
**What it establishes:** The model captures transition patterns and detects missing or anomalous directly-follows relations, subject to abstraction fidelity.

**What it cannot establish:** Cannot confirm intermediate states (e.g., additional states between `Create Order` and `Validate Order`); thus, defining "eventually expected" behavior requires contextual data.

**Counts and Scopes Impact:** Small gaps in counts (e.g., 98% compliance) must be contextualized as possible abstraction mismatches or rare operational variance.

Danger of misinterpretation: Equating directly-follows transitions (`Create -> Validate`) directly as mandatory steps ignores delays, subprocesses, or aggregation effects.

---

### b. OTG Model
**What it establishes:** Object-event interaction requirements, but edge-count mismatches can reflect either rule violations or data omission mismatches.

**What it cannot establish:** Cannot differentiate between optional and unregulated deviations without explicit normative naming.

Severe cases (e.g., `Order-Payment` cobirth) indicate transactional sequencing issues, while minor edge mismatches, especially typical-pattern absences, are less urgent.

---

### c. ET–OT Model
**What it establishes:** Mandatory event-object relationships, highlighting clear disallowed pairings (e.g., `Sensor Heartbeat`  `Invoice`).

**What it cannot establish:** Assumes integrity in event-object relationship metadata; relies on accurate mapping rather than deducing latent relationships.

Scope matters most: Unusual relations like the cobirth of orders and payments suggest systemic lapses or policy-endangering misalignment. Minor delivery-event gaps might result from external carrier triggers.

# 6. Norm-Completeness Assessment

1. **Intermediate `Create -> Validate` Events Oversight**  
   - **Risk**: False negatives due to abstraction bias.  
   - **Proposal**: Mandate the inclusion of modeled intermediate stages for diagnostic completeness.

2. **Unspecified Cobirth and Codeath**  
   - **Risk**: Ambiguous lifecycle overlaps lead to under-diagnosed norm deviations.  
   - **Proposal**: Require distinct birth/death treatment as typical.

3. **Carrier Reference Exception Handling**  
   - **Risk**: Gaps when delivery metadata diverges (e.g., external carriers).  
   - **Proposal**: Clarify external carrier rules explicitly as typical.

4. **Unapplied Cash Label Absence**  
   - **Risk**: Ambiguity in evaluating `Record Payment` events without invoices.  
   - **Proposal**: Mandate precise classification of unapplied cash.

5. **Rare Manual Review Specifications**  
   - **Risk**: Lack of scope clarity around rare but legitimate workflows.  
   - **Proposal**: Specify as **typical** behavior to avoid false positives.

6. **Sensor Join Ambiguity**  
   - **Risk**: Poor identifier hygiene can generate incorrect bindings.  
   - **Proposal**: Enforce stricter cross-domain reference rules as mandatory.
