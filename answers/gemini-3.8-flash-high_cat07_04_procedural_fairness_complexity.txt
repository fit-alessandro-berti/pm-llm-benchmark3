# 1. Fairness dimensions

Assessing public-benefits administration solely through the lens of raw outcome parity conflates distinct legal, ethical, and operational concepts. A comprehensive evaluation requires examining procedural fairness across at least eight interrelated dimensions:

- **Outcome parity:** This dimension reflects raw equality in final determinations across applicant groups ($P(\text{Approval} \mid \text{Group } A) = P(\text{Approval} \mid \text{Group } B)$). While raw approval rates exhibit wide gaps (Group A at 78% vs. Group B at 63%, Group C at 69%, and Group D at 58%), raw outcome parity is fundamentally inadequate on its own. In public-benefit systems, entitlement is governed by statutory eligibility criteria; blind enforcement of raw parity without accounting for legitimate eligibility variations would compromise statutory integrity. However, severe raw outcome disparities remain critical sentinel indicators that warrant deeper investigation.
- **Adjusted outcome disparity:** This measures differences in approval probability that persist after conditioning on observable, legitimate complexity and eligibility covariates (e.g., household size, region, channel, documented risk, and completeness). In the preliminary model, significant negative adjusted differences persist for Group B ($-5.5$ percentage points), Group D ($-3.1$ percentage points), and Group C ($-2.2$ percentage points) relative to Group A. While closer to isolating potential disparate impact or bias than raw rates, adjusted outcome parity can still mislead if the conditioning covariates are themselves intermediate products of biased procedures or encode historical inequities.
- **Procedural burden (administrative ordeal):** Procedural fairness requires that the administrative friction, cognitive overhead, and evidentiary demands placed on applicants are proportionate, necessary, and equitably distributed. The data reveals stark disparities in procedural hurdle rates: even after adjustment, Group B applicants face a $+9.5$ percentage point higher probability of receiving `Request Missing Documents`. Requiring repeated submissions and navigating confusing bureaucratic requests creates an "administrative ordeal" that disproportionately discourages vulnerable populations, inducing dropouts before adjudication.
- **Timeliness:** Fairness requires decisions to be rendered within a reasonable and equitable timeframe ("justice delayed is justice denied"). Delayed benefits cause acute harms, including eviction, food insecurity, and untreated health conditions. The raw median cycle time for Group A is $8.1$ days (90th percentile $21.4$ days), whereas Group B faces $13.7$ days ($34.2$ days at p90) and Group C faces $15.9$ days ($38.8$ days at p90). Even after controlling for all documented case-mix variables, Group C suffers an adjusted delay of $+4.1$ days and Group B $+2.6$ days.
- **Consistency of routing:** Procedural due process demands horizontal equity: similarly situated cases should traverse equivalent pathways and face uniform evidentiary thresholds. In this process, routing deviates systematically: adjusted probabilities of escalation to `Supervisor Review` are $+8.0$ percentage points higher for Group B and $+5.5$ percentage points higher for Group C compared to identically situated Group A peers. Such routing disparities indicate differential skepticism and inconsistent operational discretion.
- **Quality and accessibility of support:** Statutory protections frequently mandate accommodations for language barriers and disabilities. True procedural fairness requires that these rights-preserving support steps (`Language Support Contact`, `Accommodation Review`) provide substantive, dignified assistance without imposing structural penalties. When accessing a legal right exposes an applicant to multi-day waiting queues and fragmented routing, support functions in practice as an administrative penalty rather than an accommodation.
- **Appeal and rework burden:** Post-adjudication fairness requires accessible mechanisms to correct agency errors, but a fair primary process should minimize the need for appeals. Group D exhibits a $16\%$ appeal rate ($+5.7$ percentage points adjusted) and Group B a $13\%$ appeal rate ($+4.4$ percentage points adjusted), alongside rework/reopen rates exceeding $20\%$ (compared to $11\%$ for Group A). Forcing marginalized applicants into lengthy appeal cycles ($30.7$ days median in Variant V6) shifts the administrative burden of initial agency errors onto the applicant.
- **Resource-allocation fairness:** This dimension evaluates whether public administrative capacity—staffing, experience, and caseloads—is distributed equitably across service queues. The evidence indicates severe resource maldistribution: 52 general caseworkers handle predominantly Group A cases, while the specialized needs of Group B (1,250 cases; 57% requiring language support) are funneled through just 7 bilingual caseworkers, and Group C (820 cases; 62% requesting accommodation) depends on 5 specialists. Structural rationing of administrative resources produces disparate queueing times that violate institutional fairness.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

To rigorously isolate procedural discrimination from legitimate routing, genuine case complexity, and observational uncertainty, the analysis strategy must move beyond standard cross-sectional regressions to an integrated, quasi-experimental process mining framework.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               QUASI-EXPERIMENTAL STRATEGY                               |
|                                                                                         |
|  1. BASELINE MATCHING / STRATIFICATION                                                  |
|     Match on strictly pre-treatment covariates (Region, Channel, Household Size, Month) |
|     Isolate intermediate process variables (Mediators / Colliders)                      |
|                                                                                         |
|  2. WITHIN-VARIANT vs. ACROSS-VARIANT DECOMPOSITION                                     |
|     - Within identical paths (e.g., pure V1): test service/wait differences             |
|     - Across paths: test routing escalation probabilities (V1 -> V2, V3, V5)             |
|                                                                                         |
|  3. WAITING TIME DECOMPOSITION                                                          |
|     Total Cycle Time = Active Service Time + Agency Queue Time + Applicant Return Time  |
|                                                                                         |
|  4. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS & COVARIATE AUDITING                                           |
|     Assess endogeneity in risk_score_band & documentation_completeness                  |
|                                                                                         |
|  5. SUBGROUP STRATIFICATION                                                             |
|     Disentangle Region (Backlog vs. Non-backlog) & Channel (Online vs. In-Person)       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Matching and Stratification Variables
- **Strict Separation of Pre-treatment vs. Post-treatment Covariates:** Standard regression models that control for intermediate process steps (e.g., `Request Missing Documents` or `Supervisor Review`) suffer from mediator and collider conditioning bias, effectively masking procedural discrimination. 
- **Pre-treatment Covariates:** Exact or propensity-score matching must be conducted using: `household_size_band`, `region`, `channel`, `language_preference`, `disability_accommodation_requested`, `prior_case_count`, and application intake date/cohort.
- **Handling Endogenous Covariates:** `documentation_completeness` and `risk_score_band` must initially be quarantined from baseline matching because both reflect agency perceptions or potentially biased algorithms rather than pure applicant attributes.

### Path and Variant Comparison
- **Stratified Variant Matching:** Evaluate outcomes and cycle times conditional on specific execution paths. For example, comparing Group A and Group B cases that *both* followed pure Variant V1 (clean path) isolates whether caseworkers process identical clean cases at differing speeds or grant different approval rates.
- **Routing Probability Modeling:** Model the conditional transition probability from `Completeness Check` to `Request Missing Documents` (V2) or `Eligibility Assessment` (V1). By holding matched applicant characteristics constant, this isolates whether Group B's $+9.5$ percentage point excess document request rate reflects legitimate missing information or discretionary screening bias.

### Resource-Assignment Comparison
- **Caseworker Assignment Randomness / Balance Checks:** Test whether case assignment conditional on region and language preference satisfies conditional independence ($P(\text{Resource} = r \mid \text{Group}, \mathbf{X})$).
- **Caseworker Fixed-Effects and Leniency Analysis:** Construct an examiner leniency / judge-fixed-effects design. Compare cases randomly allocated across the 52 general caseworkers against those allocated to the 7 bilingual caseworkers and 3 overloaded regional officers. This separates caseworker-specific stringency and productivity from applicant merit.

### Waiting-Time Decomposition
Using event-level timestamp intervals, total cycle time must be decomposed into three mutually exclusive components:
1. **Active Service Time ($T_{\text{service}}$):** Duration during which a caseworker is actively performing an activity on the case.
2. **Agency Queue / Waiting Time ($T_{\text{wait, agency}}$):** Duration a case sits idle in an internal agency queue awaiting assignment or review (e.g., waiting for a bilingual worker or supervisor).
3. **Applicant Response Time ($T_{\text{wait, applicant}}$):** Duration between the issuance of `Request Missing Documents` and receipt of those documents.

This decomposition resolves whether delays experienced by Groups B and C are driven by applicant sluggishness or internal agency capacity bottlenecks.

### Sensitivity Analysis for Possibly Biased Covariates
- **Omitted Variable and Measurement Bias Audits:** Conduct Rosenbaum bounds and Oster sensitivity analyses to test how robust the adjusted outcome differences ($-5.5$ percentage points for Group B) are to unobserved confounders or measurement error in `risk_score_band`.
- **Dual-Model Benchmarking:** Estimate two parallel structural models:
  - *Model 1 (Exogenous Baseline):* Conditioning only on verified demographic and intake channel variables.
  - *Model 2 (Full Administrative Model):* Including `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`.
  Comparing Model 1 and Model 2 quantifies how much disparity is absorbed ("explained away") by variables that may themselves be infected with administrative bias.

### Subgroup Checks by Region and Channel
- **Regional Disaggregation:** The log notes that two specific regions suffer from severe backlogs and complex handovers. Estimating models with interaction terms ($\text{Group} \times \text{Region}$) establishes whether Group B and C disparities are systemic statewide or localized to specific dysfunctional regional offices.
- **Channel Stratification:** Group B has a $38\%$ in-person share (vs. $9\%$ for Group A). Because in-person cases wait longer for identity verification ($1.4$ days vs. $0.8$ days), running within-channel strata (online-only and in-person-only) isolates whether procedural delay is a feature of channel delivery friction or group-specific treatment.

### Path Disparities Across Variants
1. **Exclusion from the Clean Fast-Track (Variant V1):**
   - Variant V1 (`Submit` $\rightarrow$ `Verify` $\rightarrow$ `Completeness` $\rightarrow$ `Eligibility` $\rightarrow$ `Decision` $\rightarrow$ `Notify` $\rightarrow$ `Activate`) represents the gold-standard frictionless path, completing in a median of $5.8$ days.
   - While $42\%$ of Group A applicants experience this smooth path, access drops to $19\%$ for Group B, $22\%$ for Group C, and $18\%$ for Group D.
2. **Excess Evidentiary Loops (Variant V2):**
   - Variant V2 introduces `Request Missing Documents`, increasing median cycle time to $14.9$ days. Group B has the highest representation at $34\%$ (Group C: $29\%$, Group D: $26\%$, Group A: $24\%$).
   - Critically, the preliminary model shows that after controlling for documented completeness, risk score, channel, and demographics, Group B retains a $+9.5$ percentage point higher adjusted probability of document requests.
3. **The "Support Penalty" Paths (Variants V4 and V5):**
   - **Variant V4 (`Language Support Contact`):** Captures $24\%$ of Group B cases (and only $1-3\%$ of other groups) with an extensive median cycle time of $19.2$ days—over three times longer than V1.
   - **Variant V5 (`Accommodation Review`):** Captures $31\%$ of Group C cases (and only $2-3\%$ of others), with a median cycle time of $21.6$ days.
   - Rather than providing equal access, utilizing statutorily protected language or disability accommodations funnels applicants into the slowest standard paths in the entire system.
4. **Disproportionate Risk Routing and Appeals (Variants V3 and V6):**
   - Variant V3 (Fraud/Risk path: $18.4$ days) absorbs $28\%$ of Group D applicants, compared to $8-11\%$ for other groups.
   - Variant V6 (Appeals: $30.7$ days) absorbs $13\%$ of Group D and $8\%$ of Group B, compared to only $4\%$ of Group A.

### Waiting-Time Disparities and Internal Latencies
The waiting-time data demonstrates that group disparities are heavily driven by internal administrative queues rather than applicant delays:
- **Intake Channel Delay (`Submit` $\rightarrow$ `Identity Verification`):** Group B waits $1.4$ days, Group C $1.2$ days, and Group D $1.1$ days, compared to $0.8$ days for Group A. This gap is directly tied to in-person submission channels.
- **Document Request Preparation Delay (`Completeness Check` $\rightarrow$ `Request Missing Documents`):** Group B waits $1.6$ days compared to $0.4$ days for Group A—a $400\%$ increase. The data confirms this is because cases wait for bilingual staff simply to generate the request for missing documents.
- **The Language Support Chasm (`Language Support Contact` $\rightarrow$ `Eligibility Assessment`):** Group B applicants experience a median queue time of **$3.8$ days** purely waiting for eligibility assessment following language contact.
- **The Accommodation Review Chasm (`Accommodation Review` $\rightarrow$ `Eligibility Assessment`):** Group C applicants experience a staggering **$4.7$ days** median wait time following accommodation review.
- **Supervisor Review Delays (`Supervisor Review` $\rightarrow$ `Decision Draft`):** Group B waits $1.9$ days and Group C $1.6$ days, compared to $1.0$ days for Group A, compounding the delays in backlogged regions.

### Resource Allocation and Organizational Topography
- **Staffing Ratios:** Group A's 3,600 cases are distributed across 52 caseworkers ($\approx 69$ cases/worker). Group B's 1,250 cases—where $57\%$ ($712$ cases) require language assistance—are routed to only **7 bilingual caseworkers** ($\approx 102$ specialized cases/worker, on top of general duties). Group C's 820 cases—where $62\%$ ($508$ cases) request accommodation—depend on **5 accommodation specialists** ($\approx 101$ specialized cases/worker).
- **Dual-Role Bottlenecks:** Two of the 5 accommodation specialists also conduct supervisor reviews, creating an acute structural conflict where specialist resources are pulled away from frontline reviews to handle escalation queues.
- **Handover Frictions:** In two regions, Group B cases are subjected to a multi-handover cycle: `General Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `Bilingual Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `General Caseworker`. Group C cases follow: `General Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `Accommodation Specialist` $\rightarrow$ `Supervisor`. Every handover incurs queue re-entry, context loss, and accountability diffusion.

### Justified vs. Concerning Disparities
- **Justified by Complexity:**
  - Extra active service time required to perform specialized reviews (e.g., verifying accessibility needs or conducting fraud investigations for cases with genuine risk indicators) is legally and operationally warranted.
  - Group D's higher assignment to fraud review ($28\%$ in V3) aligns with a documented $51\%$ high-risk score band and $49\%$ prior-case history.
- **Concerning and Unjustified:**
  - **Queueing Inequity:** The $3.8$-day and $4.7$-day waiting times for language and accommodation support stem entirely from chronic administrative under-resourcing, not applicant complexity.
  - **Unexplained Excessive Document Requests:** Group B's adjusted $+9.5$ percentage point excess document request rate indicates that language barriers are being treated as documentation deficiencies.
  - **Unexplained Discretionary Escalations:** Adjusted $+8.0$ and $+5.5$ percentage point increases in supervisor reviews for Groups B and C point to institutional distrust and defensive administrative buck-passing.
  - **Asymmetric Appeal Burden:** Group D's $+5.7$ and Group B's $+4.4$ percentage point adjusted appeal probabilities reveal that the initial adjudication process fails disproportionately for these groups.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

The observed disparities can be explained by several competing and interacting institutional, behavioural, and procedural mechanisms:

```
                    PLAUSIBLE GENERATING MECHANISMS
                                   |
         +-------------------------+-------------------------+
         |                                                   |
   [LEGITIMATE]                                        [CONCERNING]
         |                                                   |
  +------+------+                             +--------------+--------------+
  |             |                             |              |              |
Legal Due   Inherent Evidentiary         Structural       Defensive     Handover &
Diligence   Complexity                   Capacity         Bureaucracy   Channel
(Risk/Fraud (Medical/Disability          Starvation       & Evidentiary Friction
 Audits)     Documentation)              (Understaffing)  Bias          (Ping-Pong)
```

1. **Structural Capacity Starvation of Rights-Preserving Services (Concerning):**
   The agency established formal compliance mechanisms for statutory rights (Title VI language access and ADA/disability accommodations) on paper, but failed to allocate matching operational capacity. Funneling over $700$ language-support cases through 7 bilingual workers and over $500$ accommodation requests through 5 specialists creates structural rationing. Because the agency lacks service-level targets (SLAs) for these intermediate steps, queue times balloon without triggering management intervention.
2. **Handover Fragmentation and the "Ping-Pong" Effect (Concerning):**
   In the two backlogged regions, process design segregates specialized tasks from general processing. Rather than empowering bilingual workers or specialists to adjudicate cases end-to-end, cases are repeatedly transferred (`General` $\rightarrow$ `Specialist` $\rightarrow$ `General` $\rightarrow$ `Supervisor`). Each handover requires re-reading files, rebuilding context, and waiting at the back of the next worker's queue. This handoff tax directly drives the $+2.6$ to $+4.1$ day adjusted cycle time inflation.
3. **Defensive Bureaucracy, Evidentiary Misattribution, and Discretionary Bias (Concerning):**
   When caseworkers encounter non-English documents, non-standard wage receipts, or unfamiliar disability verifications, they face cognitive burden and uncertainty. Under pressure to maintain error-rate metrics, caseworkers adopt risk-averse strategies: issuing a `Request Missing Documents` notice rather than deciphering the submission, or escalating the file to `Supervisor Review`. This explains why Group B suffers a $+9.5$ percentage point excess document request rate and $+8.0$ percentage point excess supervisor review rate even after controlling for baseline completeness.
4. **Legitimate Evidentiary and Substantive Complexity (Legitimate):**
   Adjudicating disability accommodations and complex benefit histories requires more procedural steps and active time than approving standard wage earners. Verifying medical necessity, evaluating accommodation feasibility, and auditing multi-year benefit records across prior cases cannot—and should not—be rushed into an automated $5.8$-day turnaround. To the extent that delays reflect thorough, active evaluation that protects applicant rights, they represent legitimate procedural diligence.
5. **Algorithmic Compounding and Surveillance Feedback Loops (Concerning):**
   Group D's elevated routing to fraud review ($28\%$ in V3) is driven by `risk_score_band` ($51\%$ high) and `prior_case_count` ($49\%$). If the risk scoring algorithm relies on historical enforcement data, prior investigations, or frequent address changes, it reflects historical surveillance bias rather than genuine fraud propensity. Past scrutiny generates higher risk scores, which triggers fraud review, which leads to denials or appeals, reinforcing future risk scores in an algorithmic feedback loop.
6. **Channel-Induced Friction and Asymmetric Administrative Ordeals (Concerning):**
   Group B's $38\%$ reliance on in-person channels reflects digital exclusion or language barriers in the online portal. In-person workflows are subject to physical office constraints, paper intake scanning, and delayed identity verification ($1.4$ days vs. $0.8$ days). Applicants forced into physical offices bear higher transportation costs, wait times, and administrative friction, creating an unmeasured systemic penalty.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

Relying exclusively on administrative event logs presents severe analytical vulnerabilities. Several critical limitations must qualify any conclusions:

- **Measurement Bias in `documentation_completeness`:**
  In the event log, `documentation_completeness` is an agency-assigned administrative label, not an objective state of reality. If online forms are optimized for native English speakers, non-native speakers may omit minor administrative fields or upload equivalent foreign documentation that automated checks flag as "incomplete." Treating this variable as a neutral control variable masks how portal and form design generate documentation failures.
- **Historical Bias and Endogeneity of `risk_score_band`:**
  `risk_score_band` is treated in regression models as an exogenous control. In reality, risk scores in public welfare systems are frequently trained on past fraud referrals, overpayment notices, or caseworker audit selections—metrics heavily confounded by historical enforcement bias against low-income and minority communities. Controlling for risk score conditions away the very mechanism through which systemic bias operates.
- **Unobserved Applicant Needs and Structural Heterogeneity:**
  The event log records coarse categorical variables (`language_preference`, `disability_accommodation_requested`) that flatten immense internal diversity:
  - It does not distinguish between literacy levels, non-English languages (e.g., Spanish vs. low-incidence indigenous languages), or dialect differences.
  - It collapses all disabilities into a binary accommodation flag, obscuring differences between simple requests (e.g., large-print notices) and complex psychiatric or cognitive accommodation needs.
  - It lacks data on applicant income volatility, housing instability, or access to broadband internet.
- **Unobserved Resource Workload and Intra-Day Dynamics:**
  Workload is observed only at daily granularity. The log does not capture:
  - Real-time caseworker queue depths, caseload turnover, or simultaneous assignments to other benefit programs.
  - Interruptions, training schedules, or caseworker tenure and experience.
  - Intra-day processing batches, which prevents determining whether delays reflect caseworker neglect, system downtime, or queue congestion.
- **Truncation and Ambiguity of Appeal Quality (`appeal_flag`):**
  The log records an `appeal_flag`, but omits the final outcome of the appeal:
  - If Group B and D appeals are overwhelmingly *overturned* (granted in favor of the applicant), the high appeal rate proves that initial adjudications were erroneous, premature, and discriminatory.
  - If appeals are overwhelmingly *upheld*, it suggests that initial adjudications were legally sound and that high appeal rates stem from desperation, miscommunication, or lack of clear explanation.
  Without appeal resolution data, the quality of initial decisions remains unobserved.
- **Absence of Experiential and Dignitary Metrics:**
  Administrative logs record what happened and when, but capture nothing of *how* applicants were treated. They cannot measure the dignity of interpersonal interactions, whether language interpreters were competent, whether denial notices provided comprehensible explanations, or the psychological stress inflicted by bureaucratic hurdles.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

To eliminate procedural inequities without undermining program integrity, the agency must implement targeted structural, operational, and algorithmic reforms:

| Mitigation Option | Intended Mechanism & Operational Reform | Potential Side Effects & Trade-Offs | Countermeasures & Guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Expand Capacity for Support Steps** | Hire and redeploy bilingual staff and accommodation specialists; expand bilingual caseworker pool from 7 to 20+, and accommodation team from 5 to 15+. Cross-train general staff. | High budgetary and hiring costs; risk that rapidly trained general caseworkers deliver low-quality, superficial translations or accommodation assessments. | Establish rigorous language proficiency and ADA competency certifications before granting review authority. |
| **2. Enforce Service-Level Targets (SLAs)** | Implement strict, binding maximum queueing thresholds for `Language Support Contact` ($\le 24$h) and `Accommodation Review` ($\le 48$h), integrated into operational dashboards. | Caseworkers may "game the metric"—conducting rushed, superficial reviews or closing tickets prematurely to stop the SLA clock, harming review quality. | Couple timeliness SLAs with independent quality audits; measure substantive satisfaction and rework rates alongside speed. |
| **3. End-to-End Pod Architecture** | Eliminate sequential "ping-pong" handovers. Form multidisciplinary "Pods" where bilingual workers and specialists manage assigned cases end-to-end from intake to decision. | Reduced economies of scale and resource pooling; potential workload imbalances between specialized pods and general processing pods during demand surges. | Implement dynamic cross-pod load balancing and floating caseworkers during regional demand spikes. |
| **4. Decision-Explanation & Pre-Denial Cure** | Redesign notices with plain-language, multilingual explanations; institute a mandatory 14-day "pre-denial cure period" with direct outreach before issuing formal rejections. | Increases overall cycle time for borderline applications; creates additional notification and tracking overhead for caseworkers. | Limit cure periods strictly to active applicants; automate multi-channel reminders (SMS, email, phone) to accelerate applicant curing. |
| **5. Continuous Fairness Monitoring** | Deploy automated process mining dashboards tracking adjusted cycle times, missing doc request rates, and supervisor escalations across demographic groups in real time. | Metric fixation and defensive decision-making: caseworkers might avoid legitimate fraud reviews or supervisor escalations out of fear of triggering bias flags. | Base monitoring on risk-adjusted, statistical process control (SPC) charts; use metrics for organizational learning rather than punitive caseworker scoring. |
| **6. Algorithmic and Form Auditing** | Commission an independent algorithmic audit of `risk_score_band` for disparate impact and predictive parity. Redesign digital forms using human-centered, multilingual UX testing. | Modifying risk models may temporarily disrupt fraud detection workflows; redesigning IT portals and intake systems requires capital expenditure and vendor lead time. | Run legacy and recalibrated risk models in parallel (shadow mode) to ensure fraud detection efficacy while eliminating discriminatory proxy features. |

*Explicit Policy Constraint Note:* Removing rights-preserving support steps (`Language Support Contact`, `Accommodation Review`) simply to shorten cycle times is legally and ethically unacceptable. Reforms must accelerate access to support, never eliminate it.

"""
Procedural Fairness and Complexity Assessment in Public Benefits
PM4Py Execution Pipeline
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils
from pm4py.algo.filtering.dfg import dfg_filtering
import statsmodels.api as sm

# STEP 1: LOG PREPARATION & ATTRIBUTE STANDARDIZATION
# ==============================================================================

def load_and_prepare_log(filepath: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Loads event log, parses timestamps, and standardizes PM4Py column schema."""
    df = pd.read_csv(filepath)
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df)
    
    # Format according to PM4Py standards
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    return df

# df_log = load_and_prepare_log("public_benefits_event_log.csv")

# ==============================================================================
# STEP 2: STRATIFIED LOG FILTERING & VARIANT ANALYSIS
# ==============================================================================

def analyze_variants_by_group(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Discovers and contrasts process variants across applicant groups."""
    groups = ['Group A', 'Group B', 'Group C', 'Group D']
    group_variants = {}
    
    for grp in groups:
        # Filter log by applicant group
        df_grp = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            df, 'applicant_group', [grp], level='cases'
        )
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(df_grp)
        total_cases = len(df_grp['case:concept:name'].unique())
        
        # Calculate variant shares
        variant_summary = []
        for var, cases in variants.items():
            variant_summary.append({
                'variant': var,
                'count': len(cases),
                'share': len(cases) / total_cases
            })
        
        group_variants[grp] = pd.DataFrame(variant_summary).sort_values(
            by='count', ascending=False
        )
        print(f"\n--- Top 3 Variants for {grp} (Total Cases: {total_cases}) ---")
        print(group_variants[grp].head(3))
        
    return group_variants

# ==============================================================================

def analyze_queue_performance(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Extracts Directly-Follows Graphs with performance metrics to isolate
    bottlenecks at rights-preserving steps and supervisor reviews.
    """
    groups = ['Group A', 'Group B', 'Group C', 'Group D']
    
    for grp in groups:
        df_grp = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            df, 'applicant_group', [grp], level='cases'
        )
        
        # Discover Performance DFG (median duration on edges)
        perf_dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            df_grp,
            case_id_key='case:concept:name',
            activity_key='concept:name',
            timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
        )
        
        print(f"\n================ Performance DFG Edges: {grp} ================")
        critical_edges = [
            ('Completeness Check', 'Request Missing Documents'),
            ('Language Support Contact', 'Eligibility Assessment'),
            ('Accommodation Review', 'Eligibility Assessment'),
            ('Supervisor Review', 'Decision Draft')
        ]
        
        for edge in critical_edges:
            if edge in perf_dfg:
                median_wait_days = perf_dfg[edge]['median'] / 86400.0
                case_count = perf_dfg[edge]['count']
                print(f"Edge {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: "
                      f"Median Wait = {median_wait_days:.2f} days (Cases: {case_count})")

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The empirical evidence indicates that the public-benefits case-management process is **demonstrably unfair in its procedural architecture and operational resourcing**, while remaining **indeterminate regarding intentional individual discrimination or underlying applicant eligibility**.

### Substantive Findings Supporting This Conclusion:
1. **Procedurally Unfair in Resource Allocation and Queue Latency:**
   The process creates severe structural bottlenecks at statutory, rights-preserving steps. While Group A cases move smoothly through a general pool of 52 caseworkers (completing clean paths in $5.8$ days), applicants who require language assistance or disability accommodations are segregated into severe queues—waiting a median of $3.8$ days for bilingual review and $4.7$ days for accommodation review. Funneling over $1,200$ language-support and accommodation cases through just 7 bilingual staff and 5 specialists, in the absence of intermediate service-level targets, constitutes institutional capacity rationing.
2. **Procedurally Unfair in Evidentiary Demands and Routing Discretion:**
   The process subjects minority-language applicants to unjustifiable procedural friction. Even after controlling for documented completeness, measured risk score, intake channel, household size, prior cases, and region, Group B applicants face a $+9.5$ percentage point higher adjusted probability of having their cases interrupted by `Request Missing Documents`, alongside a $+8.0$ percentage point higher adjusted rate of `Supervisor Review`. Treating language barriers as documentation deficiencies and imposing heightened supervisory scrutiny reflects defensive bureaucracy that violates horizontal equity.
3. **Severe Asymmetry in Downstream Appeal Burden:**
   The primary adjudication pipeline disproportionately fails Groups D and B, generating adjusted appeal probabilities $+5.7$ and $+4.4$ percentage points higher than for Group A. Forcing these groups to undergo a $30.7$-day appeal ordeal (Variant V6) to obtain benefits demonstrates that initial determinations are substantially less accurate and reliable for non-standard applicants.
4. **Indeterminate Dimensions:**
   The evidence does not substantiate deliberate disparate treatment by individual caseworkers, nor does it establish that approval gaps (Group B $-5.5$ percentage points adjusted) stem from bias rather than unobserved eligibility factors. Crucial context remains unobserved: the event log lacks appeal resolution outcomes (whether denials were overturned or affirmed), objective measures of form complexity, and audits of whether `risk_score_band` encodes historical surveillance disparities.

**Final Conclusion:**
The agency cannot defend the integrity of this process by pointing to case-mix complexity. While substantive complexity justifies additional *active review time*, it cannot justify *multi-day queueing latencies* and *excessive administrative ordeals* that stem directly from understaffed support queues and fragmented handovers. The process is procedurally unfair in operational execution, and requires immediate structural reform to resource allocation, routing design, and intake support.
