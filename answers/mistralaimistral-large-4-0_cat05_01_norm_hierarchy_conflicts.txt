## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts** are the source texts, timestamps, amounts, supplier statuses, and recorded events in the scenario. **Interpretations** are my readings of ambiguous terms such as “critical,” “ordinary local workflow steps,” “final business approver,” and “new supplier.” **Assumptions** are stated below and are not treated as facts. **Recommendations** appear only in Sections 6 and 7.

### Decision method

- **Authority first, not novelty or wording strength.** GPIP-4 is board-approved and globally scoped, so it is supreme for integrity controls: segregation of duties, pre-commitment approvals, Compliance clearance, and exception authority. A local SOP may be stricter but cannot silently weaken GPIP-4. An incident directive may adjust operational timing and supplier-onboarding within its stated scope and time, but it is not superior to GPIP-4 for integrity controls. A CFO email is uncontrolled and low-authority.
- **Specificity is issue-specific.** ECD-2 is more specific than the Aachen SOP for purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding during its window and scope. But specificity does not allow a lower-authority source to override a higher-authority source on a different issue.
- **Later-in-time is not decisive across authority levels.** ECD-2 is later than the SOP and GPIP-4, but later-in-time matters only where the sources have comparable authority and the later source actually covers the issue.
- **Emergency necessity is assessed at the time of the act.** Emergency status relaxes only what the applicable emergency rule expressly relaxes. Post-hoc labels such as “site continuity” or “critical contingency” do not create an emergency.
- **Retrospective evidence is admissible but not curative.** Contemporaneous signed PDFs, telephone recordings, supplier acknowledgments, and access logs can outweigh late ERP timestamps, especially where the ERP was down or the clock may be fast. But a later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval under GPIP-4 A6.
- **Burden of proof.** The person asserting compliance or an exception bears the burden of proving its elements: authority, scope, time, approvals, and segregation of duties. For required pre-commitment approvals, absence of evidence of a timely approval is treated as a violation because the approval had to exist before commitment.

### Stated assumptions

1. “Unapproved supplier” means a supplier outside the approved-supplier register. For GPIP-4 A3, I treat “unapproved” as “new supplier” only where the facts indicate a new or previously unqualified supplier; otherwise the B3 register violation is sufficient.
2. Plant_Approve is the local business approval. I do not assume the Plant Manager is an “independent budget owner” under GPIP-4 A2 unless evidence shows it.
3. Incident Command members are not assumed to be budget owners or Finance Controllers.
4. ECD-2 is **not** an A7 CRO exception for GPIP-4 integrity controls. GPIP-4 A7 reserves formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer; ECD-2 is signed by the COO and Incident Commander, and the CRO was unavailable and did not ratify it.
5. SOP B2 does not expressly impose a pre-commitment deadline. GPIP-4 A2 supplies the pre-commitment deadline for purchases of at least EUR 50,000.
6. ERP clock skew is possible but not proven; stronger contemporaneous evidence may establish actual time.
7. “Final business approver” includes any person who gives the final approval authorizing commitment. If Incident Command authorization is the only approval, an Incident Command member who is also requester or committer violates GPIP-4 A1.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 — currently COMPLIANT; alternative would make it NONCOMPLIANT.**  
The strongest alternative reads SOP B2 as imposing a pre-commitment approval requirement: because B1 requires Create_PO before Commit and approvals are normally part of PO creation, Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve had to exist before the 08:50 commitment. On that reading, the later 15:10 and 15:12 approvals are only mitigation under A6, not cure. The premise that must change is B2’s timing: silence would be read against GPIP-4 A2’s express “before the supplier commitment” language. This is credible because approval controls normally precede commitment. It is weak because B2 lacks express pre-commitment language, B4/B5 contemplate emergency commitment before PO with Finance_Approve still required but not necessarily before, and GPIP-4 A2’s threshold is EUR 50,000.

**P-502 — currently NONCOMPLIANT; alternative would make it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
The strongest alternative treats ECD-2 as an A7 CRO exception, or as retroactively ratified by the CRO, and treats CFO email D1 as a valid Finance-control interpretation waiving Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000. Then the missing pre-commitment Finance and Compliance approvals could be excused, and ECD-2 C1/C2/C3 would cover timing, supplier register, and authorization. The premise change is that a COO/IC incident directive counts as an A7 exception and that the CFO email can waive GPIP-4 A2. This is credible because ECD-2 is written, scoped, and time-limited, and the organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays. It is weak because A7 reserves exceptions to the CRO; the COO/IC directive is not the CRO; the CRO was unavailable and did not ratify; ECD-2 C5 omits GPIP-4 controls; prior practice did not involve new-supplier Compliance clearance; and D4 says the email is not meant to remove essential integrity controls.

**P-503 — currently NONCOMPLIANT; alternative would make it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
The strongest alternative reads ECD-2 scope broadly to include backup-line components as “critical” under the Operations interpretation, treats ECD-2 as an A7 exception, and reads A1 as not violated because PROC-3’s IC authorization is joint and operational rather than the sole “final business approver.” Then the missing Finance and Compliance approvals could be excused. The premise changes are: ECD-2 counts as A7; scope includes backup line; A1 does not apply to joint IC authorization. This is credible because C6 is undefined and Operations labeled the pumps critical. It is weak because A1’s purpose is segregation of duties, allowing a requester/committer to be one of two authorizers undermines it; ECD-2 cannot waive GPIP-4; and three-day stock is not “required to contain the current contamination event.”

**P-505 — currently NONCOMPLIANT; alternative would make it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
The strongest alternative treats the ERP clock as nine minutes fast, so commitment occurred before 18:00; treats CFO email D1 as validly waiving Finance_Approve and Plant_Approve for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000; and treats IC members as independent budget owners. Then A2 could be satisfied and ECD-2 would cover timing. The premise change is that the CFO email can waive GPIP-4 A2 and B2 approvals, and IC members are budget owners. This is credible because D1 says “all operational approvals” and the CFO has Finance interpretation authority, and the supplier acknowledgment supports a pre-expiry commitment. It is weak because GPIP-4 A2 is board-approved; D4 says the email is not meant to remove essential integrity controls; D3 does not give the CFO authority to waive GPIP-4 segregation of duties or Compliance; and the IC charter does not call them budget owners.

**P-507 — currently NONCOMPLIANT; alternative would make it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**  
The strongest alternative treats CFO email D1 as a valid Finance-control interpretation that substitutes IC authorization for Finance_Approve and Plant_Approve up to EUR 150,000, and treats IC members as independent budget owners. Then A2 could be satisfied; Compliance_Clearance was already before commitment; ECD-2 covers timing and supplier register. The premise change is that the CFO email can waive GPIP-4 A2 and B2 approvals, and IC members are budget owners. This is credible because D1 is explicit for amounts up to EUR 150,000 and the CFO owns Finance controls. It is weak because GPIP-4 A2 is board-approved; D4 is a disclaimer; D3 does not authorize waiving GPIP-4 segregation of duties or Compliance; and there is no evidence IC members are budget owners.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement hierarchy and exception clause**

1. **Hierarchy.** GPIP-4 prevails over local SOPs, incident directives, and emails for segregation of duties, pre-commitment approvals, and Compliance clearance. Local SOPs may impose stricter controls. Incident directives may adjust only operational timing and supplier-onboarding steps within their stated scope and time.
2. **Exceptions.** Only the Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped, time-limited exception to GPIP-4 controls. An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander is not an exception unless expressly endorsed in writing by the CRO. Verbal extensions are invalid.
3. **Thresholds.** Purchases of at least EUR 25,000 require Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve before commitment. Purchases of at least EUR 50,000 require independent budget owner approval and Finance Controller approval before commitment. Purchases above EUR 100,000, or any purchase from a new or unapproved supplier, require Compliance_Clearance before commitment. Legal clearance is additional for purchases above EUR 150,000 and does not replace Finance or Compliance approval.
4. **Segregation of duties.** Requester, committer, and final business approver must be distinct persons. No requester or committer may be an Incident Command authorizer for the same purchase.
5. **Emergency scope.** “Critical” means items or services needed to prevent imminent safety harm or production stoppage within six hours, or to contain an active contamination event on an affected line. Backup or contingency items with more than 24 hours of stock are not critical unless Incident Command documents a specific imminent need.
6. **Records.** Emergency authorizations must be recorded within four hours. Emergency forms must be recorded within 24 hours. ERP timestamps are presumptive, but contemporaneous signed, telephone, or email evidence prevails. Late entries must be annotated.
7. **Expiry and normalization safeguard.** Emergency directives expire automatically at the stated time. No verbal extension is valid. Any purchase after expiry requires normal controls or a new CRO exception. Emergency purchases may not exceed 5% of monthly purchase volume or continue for more than 10 consecutive days without CRO review. Routine maintenance and non-critical items are ineligible for emergency treatment.
