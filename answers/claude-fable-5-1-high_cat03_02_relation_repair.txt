## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

- R1 (hard invariant): every relation row references an existing `ocel:eid` and `ocel:oid`; no duplicate (eid, oid, qualifier) or (oid, oid_2, qualifier) rows.
- R2 (hard invariant): O2O qualifiers are directional (rule 8): `carries` Delivery -> Package, `packs` Package -> Item, `bills` Invoice -> Order, `settles` Payment -> Invoice, `contains` Order -> Item.
- R3 (hard invariant): lifecycle order (an item is added before it is packed; a package is packed before it is dispatched). All timestamps here comply, so R3 only prunes candidates.
- R4 (strong default): cardinalities from rules 1-7. Because rules 3-5 and 7 name exceptions (scanner outage, bulk dispatch, lost carrier id, unapplied cash), a missing partner is a prompt to search, not proof that a partner exists.
- R5 (strong default): an object already carrying a lifecycle fragment (PK-8 has Pack and Dispatch) is a better target than an eventless object, unless an identifier points at the eventless one.
- R6 (weak heuristic): identifier prefix/sequence matching (`PK8-2` after `PK8-1`; `N-421?` versus `N-421`; `N-42*`). A sequence number within one scanner prefix is more informative than a bare prefix.
- R7 (weak heuristic): temporal adjacency (ev08 one minute after ev07; ev14 thirty minutes after a partial payment) and shared attributes (customer C17, route NORTH).
- R8 (weak heuristic, easily over-weighted): arithmetic complement (130 + 100 = 230). Per rule 6 this is evidence, never proof.
- R9 (evidence against): an explicit doubt marker in the source (`N-421?`, `N-42*`) means the upstream system was itself unsure; repairing over it with high confidence contradicts the source.
- R10 (evidence against): two candidates with comparable support (ev11 -> DEL-X vs DEL-Y vs both). Symmetric evidence argues for leaving the relation unresolved.
- R11 (evidence against): lifecycle completion is not evidence. DEL-Y having no events or ORD-B having no invoice may be legitimate (cancelled delivery, invoice outside the extract window).

Combination. R1-R3 filter candidates. A survivor is added only if at least two independent channels agree: identifier (R6), time (R7), amount (R8), customer/route attributes, pre-existing O2O structure. An O2O edge I derive from an assumed E2O edge is not a channel. A candidate with a competing target of similar strength (R10), or resting on a doubt flag alone (R9), is capped at provisional or left unresolved. Confidence values are ordinal judgements (0.75 "would defend in review", 0.6 "plausible, sometimes wrong", 0.5 "annotation only"), not probabilities estimated from data.

- INV-44/ORD-B: provisional; customer identity is the primary channel, arithmetic secondary.

**Bundle B – consolidated shipment acknowledged together.** A plus P07, P08, P09 as provisional.

**Bundle C – skeptical: DEL-Y out of scope, cash unapplied.** Adds P06, `DEL-X carries PK-8`, P07; leaves ev08 unresolved; rejects P10-P13.
- ev08: kept item-only; IT-B1 may be back-ordered or packed outside the extract.

- ev11: DEL-X, the only live delivery.
- INV-44/ORD-B: 230 = 130 plus fees for ORD-A alone; ORD-B not yet invoiced.
- PAY-2: unapplied cash or another invoice.
Coherence: ORD-A complete; ORD-B stops at Add Item; IT-B1 has no package; DEL-Y and PAY-2 disconnected. Internally consistent, but it needs three separate "outside the extract" excuses, which is why I do not select it.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Order. ORD-A: Create -> Add -> Add -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice; plausible. ORD-B: Create -> Add -> Issue Invoice; plausible but incomplete. ev12 becomes one Invoice to two Orders, allowed by rule 6. `INV-44 bills ORD-B` exists only because I proposed P10; there is no independent O2O confirmation. The original `contains` edges agree with ev03-ev05 on both channels.

Item. All items: Add Item -> Pack Item. IT-A1/PK-7 and IT-A2/PK-8 are confirmed on both channels by original data; `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is a mirror of P01.

Package. PK-7: Pack -> Dispatch, one item. PK-8: Pack -> Pack -> Dispatch, two items from two orders. Package-to-Item one-to-many is normal; the Package-to-Order many-to-many that PK-8 introduces is the most consequential structural change and exactly what a scanner mis-key would fabricate.

Delivery. DEL-X: Dispatch, carries PK-7, independently supported by E2O and O2O in the source. DEL-Y: Dispatch only; its `carries` is a mirror of P04. ev11 remains a Confirm Delivery with zero Delivery objects, deliberately (rule 5, third case), not an orphan to be fixed.

Invoice. INV-44: Issue -> Record Payment -> Record Payment; 230 against 100 + 130. Two partial payments are legitimate. PAY-1's `settles` is original; PAY-2's is a mirror.

Payment. Each payment has exactly one Record Payment (rule 7). PAY-2 was unattached on the invoice side, not an orphan object.

Orphans after Bundle A: no eventless object, no object-less event, two unresolved candidates on ev11. Unrepaired, DEL-Y was the only eventless object and there were no object-less events.

Circular reasoning. (a) Every O2O addition is derived from its E2O twin and is never counted as confirmation of it. (b) P10 and P12 share the 100/130 arithmetic. If INV-44 covers ORD-A alone at 230, PAY-2 -> INV-44 still fits, so P12 does not strictly depend on P10; but arguing "payments were split by order, so the invoice covers both orders" would use one observation twice. (c) P08 depends on P04. (d) For ev10, R5 favours DEL-X while R6 favours DEL-Y; I sided with the identifier because the two dispatch rows are separate events, a fact not derived from any repair.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

OC-DFG. Unrepaired: Order has one long variant (ORD-A) and one truncated at Add Item (ORD-B); Package shows Pack -> Dispatch twice; Delivery shows a single Dispatch node from DEL-X, DEL-Y contributing nothing; Invoice shows Issue -> Record Payment. Bundle A adds the Package self-loop Pack Item -> Pack Item (PK-8), the Order edge Add Item -> Issue Invoice (ORD-B skips confirmation, a genuinely new edge), the Invoice self-loop Record Payment -> Record Payment, and raises the Delivery Dispatch frequency to 2. Bundle B adds Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery on Delivery (frequency 2) and Add Item -> Confirm Delivery for ORD-B, which removes the Add Item -> Issue Invoice edge A introduced. Bundle C gives DEL-X a Dispatch -> Dispatch self-loop followed by Confirm Delivery and leaves an item-only Pack Item outside the Package graph.

OTG. Unrepaired pairs: Order–Item, Item–Package, Package–Delivery, Order–Invoice, Invoice–Payment. A and C add no pair, only weights (Item–Package 2 -> 3, Package–Delivery 1 -> 2 in A). B adds Delivery–Order via ev11, absent from the source. Order–Package or Order–Delivery pairs appear only if the P15 shortcuts are added.

ET–OT. Confirm Delivery–Delivery is absent unrepaired and in A, present in B and C. Issue Invoice–Order and Record Payment–Invoice go 1 -> 2 in A/B. Pack Item–Package goes 2 -> 3 in A/B, stays 2 in C. Dispatch Package–Delivery goes 1 -> 2 in A/B, stays 1 in C.

Flattenings. Order: ORD-B's case grows from 2 to 3 events (A) or 4 (B); ev12 is duplicated into both order cases, in B ev11 as well. Package: PK-8 gains ev08 in A/B. Delivery: unrepaired yields one one-event case and drops DEL-Y; A yields two one-event cases; B two two-event cases with ev11 duplicated; C one three-event case. Invoice: 2 events become 3 in A/B. Payment: unchanged everywhere.

Activity frequencies. Pack Item: 3 events, 2 unique packages in all versions, total package relations 2 -> 3 in A/B. Dispatch Package: 2 events; unique deliveries 1 -> 2 (A/B) or 1 (C). Confirm Delivery: 1 event; total objects 1 (unrepaired, A), 4 (B), 2 (C). Issue Invoice: 1 event; objects 2 -> 3 in A/B. Record Payment: 2 events; 1 unique invoice throughout; invoice relations 1 -> 2 in A/B. Any "objects per event" statistic moves purely on repair decisions.

Equivalent-execution clustering around Order. Unrepaired, ORD-A's execution reaches IT-A1, IT-A2, PK-7, PK-8, DEL-X, INV-44, PAY-1, while ORD-B's is just ORD-B, IT-B1, ev08: two clearly different clusters. In A the executions become nearly the same connected component (via PK-8, INV-44, PAY-2) and cluster together or as two near-identical groups; in B they are isomorphic except for one Add Item. Whether the dataset shows one process pattern or two is an artefact of the repair, and any report must state which bundle it used.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Attaching ev11 to DEL-X, DEL-Y, ORD-A and ORD-B (Bundle B) turns a one-object event into a four-object event: every flattening duplicates ev11, Confirm Delivery counts by total objects quadruple, and DEL-Y acquires a Dispatch -> Confirm edge no source record supports.
2. Treating ev09 and ev10 as a bulk dispatch on DEL-X (P06) manufactures a Dispatch self-loop on DEL-X, permanently orphans DEL-Y, and hides the two-carrier-reference behaviour that `N-421?` hints at.
3. Adding ev11 -> ORD-B so that ORD-B "looks like" ORD-A collapses two order variants into one; clustering around Order reports a single cluster and conformance shows a completeness the analyst wrote in.
4. Deriving the O2O mirrors and then citing them as a second agreeing channel double-counts one guess; running `ocel_o2o_enrichment` afterwards hides the circle because derived edges then look system-generated.
5. Shortcut qualifiers such as `ORD-B shipped_in PK-8` or `ORD-A delivered_by DEL-X` (P15) add Order–Package and Order–Delivery pairs to the OTG and, if mirrored in E2O, put Dispatch/Confirm into Order traces, inventing a shipping sub-process inside the Order lifecycle.
6. Linking PAY-2 to ORD-A because 130 matches the declared value (P14) bypasses the invoice, puts Record Payment into Order flattenings, and destroys the invoice-centric settlement structure.
7. Forcing PAY-2 onto INV-44 with high confidence erases the unapplied-cash scenario rule 7 allows; if the extract exists to surface reconciliation problems, the repair deletes the finding.
8. Normalising `N-421?` or `N-42*` to clean identifiers destroys provenance; later analysts cannot see that P04 and P07/P08 rest on flagged values.
9. Accepting ev08 -> PK-8 and then reporting "mixed-order consolidation packing" as a discovered pattern turns one uncertain scanner-sequence inference into a process insight with a support of one.
