## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts taken from the record (not contested):**
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved policy in force for all purchases; it contains its own exception mechanism (A7, Chief Risk Officer only) and its own emergency rule (A4: emergency waives competitive tendering only).
- ECD-2 was signed by the COO and the Incident Commander, not the CRO; the CRO was unavailable and never ratified it. ECD-2 was in force 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST only.
- The CFO email (D) is uncontrolled, sent 2026-07-15 07:20, and by its own words is "not meant to remove essential integrity controls".
- The SOP (B7) itself contemplates that a corporate incident directive may supersede "ordinary local workflow steps".
- The ERP was down 07-14 08:30–13:00; recovery-entered timestamps can be up to 12 minutes *later* than the real act (never earlier). On 07-18 the ERP clock may have been ~9 minutes fast.
- The organization has treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays before, but never for new-supplier Compliance clearance.

**Decision method:**
1. *Authority is issue-specific, not a single ladder.* A source can only displace another source on an issue the displacing source has authority over. Formal rank (Board > COO directive > CFO within Finance > site SOP) is the starting presumption, but rank is cut down by scope, by the issuing body's competence, and by the displaced source's own reservation clauses (A5, A7, B5, D3, D4).
2. *Specificity* (lex specialis) operates within an authority level and downward, never upward: a specific incident directive beats a general local workflow step (B7), but does not beat a global integrity control that reserves its own exception route (A7).
3. *Later-in-time* only decides between sources of comparable rank on the same issue. ECD-2 and the CFO email are the newest and lowest-formality sources; recency gives them no power over GPIP-4.
4. *Emergency necessity* is a justification only where (i) some norm supplies an emergency route and (ii) the factual predicate of that route is evidenced at the time of commitment. Necessity mitigates severity elsewhere; it does not manufacture a rule.
5. *Time of act.* Pre-commitment controls are tested at the moment of Commit_to_Supplier, whose time is fixed by the best available evidence (supplier acknowledgment, recordings, signed PDFs, access logs) and only by ERP timestamps where nothing better exists or nothing impugns the ERP.
6. *Retrospective evidence.* A late record of an act that demonstrably happened on time is a logging defect. A late act is a breach. A later approval mitigates but does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval (A6).
7. *Burden of proof.* The log establishes baseline deviations. Whoever relies on an exception (emergency, ECD-2, CFO email, extension) bears the burden of evidencing its predicates: scope, window, authorization, and record. INDETERMINATE is reserved for cases where the classification-decisive fact cannot be established either way; it is not a reward for failing to document.
8. *Classifications.* COMPLIANT = baseline rules met without needing any exception. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION = baseline departed, but a valid exception route's predicates are met and evidenced. NONCOMPLIANT = at least one applicable rule breached with no valid exception. Severity: Critical (integrity control defeated on a high-value purchase), High, Moderate, Low.

**Interpretive assumptions (mine, contestable):**
- **I-1** ECD-2 is *not* an A7 exception and therefore cannot waive A1, A2 or A3. It *is* a valid corporate incident directive that, via B7/C4, displaces SOP-level "ordinary" steps (B1 PO-before-commit, B3 supplier register, Plant_Approve routing) within its scope and window. Basis: B7 is explicit, the COO can issue operational directives, prior practice treats such directives as overlays, and removing B3 while keeping A3 does not "silently weaken" GPIP-4 (A5).
- **I-2** The CFO email operates only as an interpretation/delegation of the *Finance Controller* element of A2/B2 (D3), effective from 07-15 07:20, only for ECD-2-scope purchases during the window, at ≤EUR 150k (above 150k: IC authorization plus Legal, reading "as well" in D2 literally), and only where the IC authorizers are independent of the requester/committer (D4). It cannot touch A1 or A3.
- **I-3** Two independent IC members acting under the COO's co-signed directive functionally satisfy the *business/budget approval* function (Plant_Approve; the "independent budget owner" of A2) for in-scope purchases. This is the assumption most likely to be reversed by a Board or CRO reading, because the IC charter does not call IC members budget owners.
- **I-4** "New supplier" in A3 includes any supplier outside the approved register unless there is evidence of prior approved use.
- **I-5** ECD-2 scope ("critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event") is read as a purpose-plus-urgency test: items whose absence within the directive window would prevent containment or restoration of the affected sterilization line(s). Backup-line spares with multi-day stock cover fall outside unless the IC documented a concrete containment link. This is nearer Internal Audit's view than Operations', but rejects a rigid 24-hour rule for a 4.5-day directive.
- **I-6** An extension of ECD-2 requires the same form as its issuance (written, jointly signed). A verbal extension has no normative effect.
- **I-7** The Aachen SOP covers P-504 (a site maintenance purchase), even though the item has no production effect.
- **I-8** B2 approvals are pre-commitment controls by purpose (B4's delegation clause presupposes Plant authority is exercised at the moment of emergency commitment; A6 confirms the logic). Below EUR 50k, where A2 is silent, a post-commitment Plant/Finance approval is a low-severity timing deviation rather than a missing approval.
- **I-9** Where approver identities are not reported, no SoD breach is presumed; it is listed as missing evidence.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise to change: B4's delegation clause governs only who may give *Plant_Approve*, not who may *commit*; B2 states no timing, so Plant and Finance approvals the same afternoon satisfy it. Under that reading SHIFT-1's commitment in a genuine four-hour emergency departs only from B1, which B4 permits. Credible because the SOP text is silent on the committer's identity and on B2 timing; weak because it makes the "absent Plant Manager / active delegation" safeguard pointless if a Shift Lead can bind the company while the Plant Manager is reachable, and because A6 treats pre-commitment approval as the norm.

**P-502 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise to change: (i) ECD-2's precedence over "supplier-onboarding requirements" (C4) is read to include the Compliance clearance that onboarding entails, treating the COO/IC directive as a de facto A7 overlay ratified by practice; and (ii) the CFO email is an interpretation that describes what Finance required throughout the window, including 07-14. Credible in part: the organization has a history of treating signed directives as overlays, and same-day Compliance clearance suggests the supplier was acceptable. Weak on the decisive point: A7 reserves exceptions to the CRO, C5 is silent on Compliance, the CRO never ratified, and the record says this overlay practice has *never* covered new-supplier Compliance. I would not adopt it; it would make A3 the one global control that a site incident could switch off.

**P-503 → no plausible reversal; alternative reading on scope only.** If Operations' reading of "critical" prevails, the purchase is within ECD-2 and the D1 Finance substitution formally applies. That does not help: A1 (PROC-3 as requester, committer and authorizer) and A3 (no Compliance at 140k) are breached under every reading. The only credible downgrade is from Critical to High if a third, independent IC member is shown to have authorized.

**P-505 → NONCOMPLIANT (low).** Premise to change: reject I-2 — the CFO may interpret Finance controls but cannot redefine the GPIP-4 A2 requirement that a *Finance Controller* approve before commitment; then Finance_Approve at 19:00 is a post-commitment approval (A6). Alternatively, reject the evidence hierarchy: if the 17:59:31 email acknowledges a quotation rather than an order, the commitment is the 18:06 ERP event and the purchase falls outside the window, removing the ECD-2 cover for B1 and Plant_Approve as well. The first alternative is credible (an uncontrolled email is a thin basis for varying a board policy); the second is testable and currently unsupported.

**P-506 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or INDETERMINATE.** Premise to change: an incident commander may extend an operational directive verbally, and the buyer's account is accepted as evidence. Then Finance approval before commitment plus a good-faith reliance on the extension leaves only a missing joint-authorization record. Weak: the directive derived its force from being jointly signed; an unrecorded oral extension by unnamed persons cannot be checked, and C3's record requirement was not met even on the buyer's account. INDETERMINATE is tempting but wrong under the burden rule: the fact is not unknowable, it is undocumented by the party who relied on it.

**P-507 → NONCOMPLIANT (low).** Premise to change: reject I-2 (as for P-505) so that the absence of any Finance event is an A2 breach; or treat the 4-hour record requirement as satisfied only by entry in the controlled system, making the 14:30 ERP record a breach of C3. The first is credible; the second is weak because C3 says "recorded", not "entered in ERP", and a signed PDF is stronger evidence than an ERP timestamp.

**P-508 → NONCOMPLIANT (low-moderate).** Premise to change: read D1's "up to EUR 150,000" as a hard cap on the IC-for-Finance substitution, with D2 adding Legal *on top of* ordinary Finance approval above the cap; then a 155k commitment with no Finance Controller event breaches A2, and Plant_Approve is also missing if I-3 is rejected. Credible: the cap is explicit and 155k exceeds it; the CFO's "as well" is genuinely ambiguous. Weak: the natural reading of "obtain Legal clearance as well" is additive to IC authorization, and every other integrity control (Compliance, Legal, SoD, independent pair) was in place before commitment.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed GPIP-4 clause A8 — Incident Overlays**

1. *Authority.* An Incident Continuity Directive is a temporary overlay to this policy only if signed by the COO and either the Chief Risk Officer or a pre-named CRO delegate. Where the CRO and delegate are unavailable, the COO and Incident Commander may issue a provisional directive valid for 48 hours, which lapses unless ratified in writing by the CRO within that period. Emails, verbal instructions and unsigned notes have no normative effect on this policy; the CFO may issue written Finance interpretations only through the controlled repository and only for the Finance Controller approval element.
2. *Scope.* A directive must list the affected lines or lots and state an objective urgency test (default: items required within 24 hours to contain or restore the named line). Items outside the list require ordinary process. Incident Command labels do not extend scope.
3. *What an overlay may alter.* PO sequencing (commitment may precede PO), local approval routing (two independent Incident Command members replace Plant approval), and the approved-supplier register (subject to paragraph 5). Competitive tendering is already waived by A4.
4. *What an overlay may never alter.* Segregation of duties (A1); the requirement that two approvers independent of the requester and committer authorize any purchase ≥ EUR 25,000 before commitment; Compliance clearance for new suppliers and purchases > EUR 100,000 before commitment; Legal clearance > EUR 150,000. During a directive, Compliance shall operate a two-hour express clearance so that this control never becomes the reason for delay.
5. *Thresholds and Finance.* Up to EUR 150,000, the two independent Incident Command authorizations satisfy the Finance Controller element if the CFO's written interpretation under paragraph 1 so provides; above EUR 150,000 a Finance Controller must approve before commitment in addition to Legal clearance.
6. *Records.* Verbal authorizations must be recorded in a signed form within four hours and entered in the ERP within 24 hours. Commitment time is the time of the first binding communication to the supplier, established by recordings, signed documents or supplier acknowledgment; ERP timestamps are secondary where an outage or clock deviation is logged. A later approval never cures a missing pre-commitment approval; it is recorded as mitigation only.
7. *Expiry and extension.* Directives expire automatically at the stated time, may not exceed five days, and may be extended once, in writing, by the same signatories. Commitments after expiry follow ordinary process regardless of any oral assurance.
8. *Safeguards against emergency normalization.* (a) Each directive carries a cumulative spend ceiling and a maximum number of commitments; exceeding either suspends the overlay until the CRO re-authorizes. (b) Any use of an emergency form outside a directive must cite a six-hour stoppage or imminent safety harm; forms citing other reasons are rejected and reported. (c) Internal Audit reviews every directive purchase within ten working days of expiry and reports to the Board Audit Committee the number of purchases, the number within one hour of expiry, threshold-proximate amounts, and any role concentration. (d) Any individual who acts as requester, committer and authorizer on the same purchase is automatically removed from authorizer roles pending review.
