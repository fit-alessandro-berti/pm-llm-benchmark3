## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### 1.1 Facts relied on (taken from the materials, not interpreted)

- F1. GPIP-4 is board-approved, effective 2026-01-01, and applies to all entities and purchases. A4 says an emergency waives competitive tendering only, and that other controls remain unless the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) issues a written exception under A7. A6 says a later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
- F2. The CRO was unavailable during the incident and did not later ratify ECD-2. A7 is silent on whether a directive signed by the COO and the Incident Commander counts as an A7 exception.
- F3. SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 rev. 6 is a controlled local procedure (effective 2025-03-01) covering Aachen maintenance and production-support purchases. B7 lets a corporate incident directive supersede "ordinary local workflow steps" but does not define them.
- F4. ECD-2 is signed by the COO and Incident Commander. Its window runs from 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST. Its scope ("critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event") is undefined. It claims precedence only over "ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements" and does not mention segregation of duties, Finance approval, Compliance clearance, the EUR 100k threshold or the CRO.
- F5. The CFO email was sent 2026-07-15 07:20 CEST. It is not in the controlled repository. The CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls. No document gives the CFO power over Compliance controls or segregation of duties, and the email itself disclaims removing "essential integrity controls".
- F6. The ERP was down on 2026-07-14 from 08:30 to 13:00 (outage ON-44). Entries made during recovery may carry a timestamp up to 12 minutes later than the real action, so the true time is at or before the ERP time. The notice changes how much weight the evidence gets; it is not a normative exception.
- F7. The case facts are as tabulated, including the corroborating evidence listed for P-502, P-505 and P-507.

### 1.2 Assumptions (conclusions would change if these are false)

- AS1. All times are CEST and fall in the same year.
- AS2. Commit_to_Supplier is the moment Northbridge becomes bound: the order is communicated and accepted. Where a call ends in a supplier acknowledgment, the commitment happened no later than the acknowledgment.
- AS3. At Aachen, Plant_Approve is the budget-owner approval, and a Finance Controller (or a higher Finance officer) issues Finance_Approve.
- AS4 (key contested assumption). The delegation-of-authority matrix that says who acts as budget owner and Finance approver belongs to the Finance control framework the CFO owns. The CFO can therefore designate who performs those approval roles, but cannot abolish the controls.
- AS5. The listed events are the full recorded trace. A missing event means "no record found". Where the table says "No separate X event", I treat that as a confirmed absence. A missing Record_Emergency_Form or recording artefact that the table does not mention is treated as missing evidence, not as an established breach.
- AS6. "Unapproved supplier" does not automatically mean "new supplier". P-502 is expressly new. Whether the suppliers in P-504, P-507 and P-508 are new is unknown; for P-507 and P-508 this does not matter because Compliance clearance was obtained anyway.
- AS7. The people recorded as Incident Command (IC) authorizers were real, distinct IC members.
- AS8. All cases are Aachen purchases within SOP scope. P-504 (cafeteria freezer repair) is treated as "maintenance". This is borderline, and the classification does not depend on it.

### 1.3 Interpretive positions (my reasoned choices)

- I1. Authority comes first and is issue-specific. A source can displace a control only if its issuer has power over that control: the board and CRO over GPIP-4, the COO and IC over operations, the CFO over Finance controls, and Aachen over stricter local controls and operational evidence (A5).
- I2. Express bridging clauses are the only doors between levels. B7 admits a corporate directive over ordinary local steps. A7 is the only route to GPIP-4 relief. ECD-2 is not an A7 exception because (a) the CRO did not sign or ratify it, (b) C5 shows it does not even purport to address GPIP-4 controls, and (c) the organization's past practice of treating directives as overlays has never covered new-supplier Compliance clearance.
- I3. The more specific rule wins only between sources at the same authority level, or where the higher source delegates. A specific lower source cannot defeat an express reservation such as A4 or A7.
- I4. The later rule wins only between sources of equal authority on the issue. ECD-2 and the CFO email being newer does not defeat GPIP-4. ECD-2 defeats the SOP on local timing and supplier-register issues because of B7 plus corporate authority, not because it is newer.
- I5. Strong wording is bounded by what it is about. C4 "takes precedence" over "ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements". I read "supplier-onboarding" as register admission, master data and qualification paperwork (B3 and related steps). I do not read it as the GPIP-4 A3 integrity clearance, which is a named board control that C5 confirms ECD-2 does not mention.
- I6. The CFO email is read so that it has effect within its author's competence and no further (D3, D4).
  - Within Finance, D1 is a written, prospective designation. For purchases that are in ECD-2 scope and window and up to EUR 150k, independent joint IC authorization fulfils the budget-owner and Finance approval roles of A2 and B2 (per AS4). This is the control being performed by designated alternates, not a waiver, so A4/A7 are not engaged.
  - D1 cannot reach Compliance clearance (A3) or segregation of duties (A1).
  - D1 requires the IC authorization to be independent of the requester and the committer (A1, D4).
  - D1 cannot operate for commitments made before 07-15 07:20, because a pre-commitment approval must exist before the commitment (A2, A6).
  - D2 is ambiguous. I read it narrowly: above EUR 150k, D1's sufficiency does not extend, and Legal clearance is added to the ordinary A2/B2 approvals.
- I7. ECD-2 scope (C6) is judged by purpose. An item is covered if it is needed to contain the event or restore the affected line, and it is needed sooner than an expedited ordinary process could supply it. Internal Audit's 24-hour test is a rebuttable presumption of coverage, not a hard limit. An IC label such as "critical contingency" is evidence but does not prove itself. Contingency stock with several days of cover is outside scope.
- I8. Time. Each rule is applied at the time of its trigger act, usually the commitment. Whether a case falls in the window depends on the true time of the commitment. Follow-up obligations attached to an in-window act (C3 recording, B6 form, PO) survive expiry. A commitment made after expiry gets no ECD-2 cover. An extension needs the same authority and form as the directive: written, and signed by the COO and IC.
- I9. There is no free-standing necessity defence. Emergency necessity works only through B4, ECD-2 or A7. Outside those routes it reduces severity only.
- I10. Post-hoc approvals never cure a missing pre-commitment requirement (A6). They matter for severity and remediation.

### 1.4 Evidence and burden of proof

- Best evidence for time, strongest first: independent contemporaneous records (supplier acknowledgment with verifiable mail headers, telephony logs or recordings, signed PDFs with validated signature timestamps, access logs); then ERP entries made during outage recovery or with known clock drift; then later self-serving statements.
- Burden. A NONCOMPLIANT finding requires the deviation to be shown on the balance of probabilities. Whoever relies on an exception must prove its conditions: scope, window, authorization and recording. Where a source requires writing (ECD-2's fixed window, A7), oral claims of authority do not meet that burden.
- INDETERMINATE is reserved for cases where the outcome turns on a fact that the available evidence cannot establish either way.

### 1.5 Classification and severity scale

- COMPLIANT: every applicable default rule is met without needing an exception.
- JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: at least one default rule is deviated from, and each deviation is displaced by a valid exception whose conditions are proven.
- NONCOMPLIANT: at least one applicable rule is violated at the relevant time with no valid exception.
- INDETERMINATE: see 1.4.

Severity is reported as inherent (the control failure at the time of the act) and residual (after later clearances or approvals). The levels are Low, Medium, High and Critical. The factors are: the purpose of the control that was harmed (integrity screening and segregation of duties weigh more than sequencing), the amount, any integrity red flags, genuine necessity, and whether the problem can be remediated.

Issue-specific orderings (these replace a single overall ranking):

1. PO before commitment: ECD-2 C1 (in scope and window) > B4 (qualifying emergency) > B1.
2. Supplier register: ECD-2 C2 (in scope and window) > B3. B4 gives no relief (B5).
3. Compliance for new suppliers or >EUR 100k: A3 > everything below it. Only an A7 CRO exception could displace it.
4. Segregation of duties: A1 > everything. It is a precondition for C3 and D1 to work at all.
5. Budget-owner and Finance approval:
   - Up to EUR 150k, in scope and window, after 07-15 07:20: D1 designation with independent IC authorization satisfies A2 and B2.
   - Otherwise: A2 (≥EUR 50k) and B2 (≥EUR 25k) apply by default.
   - Above EUR 150k: A2 and B2 apply, plus D2 Legal clearance.
6. Emergency authorization: C3 in the window, on top of GPIP-4. Outside the window, B4 and ordinary approvals.
7. Time of an act: independent contemporaneous evidence > adjusted ERP > unadjusted ERP > later statements.

Rules conditional on data (amount, supplier status, window, scope) are kept conditional. They are not collapsed into unconditional DECLARE precedence constraints such as `precedence(Finance_Approve, Commit_to_Supplier)`.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

- **P-501: NONCOMPLIANT becomes JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**

- **P-502: NONCOMPLIANT becomes JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.**

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

Four categories of finding:

- **Policy breach:** a required act was missing or out of order at the time of its trigger, with no valid exception. This covers P-501, P-502, P-503, P-504, P-506, and P-508 under the controlling reading.
- **Logging defect:** the system record is wrong or late although the underlying act complied. Examples: the P-505 ERP commitment timestamp (clock drift); the P-507 ERP authorization entry (queued document system); the ON-44 recovery entries in P-501 and P-502. None of these is a breach in itself.
- **Late evidence of a timely act:** the record was created or ingested late, but independent contemporaneous evidence proves the act was on time. Examples: the P-507 PDF at 12:40; the P-505 supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31. Evidence works both ways: the P-502 supplier recording proves the commitment time, which confirms the Compliance timing breach.
- **Inability to prove compliance:** required evidence is missing, although the act may have happened. Examples: emergency forms for P-502, P-503 and P-505 to P-508; the written C3 record for P-505; the A1 actor identities in P-505 and P-507; the claimed extension in P-506. This is not a breach until the deadline for producing the evidence passes.

Monitoring actions:

1. Run conformance checking on evidence-adjusted time. Each event should carry `ts_erp`, `ts_best`, `ts_source` and a confidence value. Commitments within 30 minutes of a window boundary get mandatory manual review.
2. Use conditional constraints rather than global ones:
   - if amount ≥ EUR 50k, approvals must precede the commitment;
   - if amount > EUR 100k or the supplier is new, Compliance must precede the commitment;
   - actor sets for requester and committer must be disjoint from the authorizers.
3. Alert on emergency purchases with no IC authorization, outside the window, or in categories outside scope (cafeteria, general facilities). Track free-text justifications such as "site continuity".

6. Detect concentration patterns: the same person across several roles; repeated emergency purchases from unregistered suppliers; spend clustered just below thresholds (EUR 24k, EUR 49k, EUR 99k, EUR 150k).
7. Monitor ERP clock synchronization, and keep a registry of outages for all evidence systems, not only the ERP.
8. Hold a post-incident review of 100% of ECD-2 purchases and report to the Audit Committee. Classify each retrospective CRO decision as affecting consequences only.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

Proposed GPIP-4 clause A8 (Emergency Procurement Hierarchy and Exceptions), to be placed in the controlled repository:

- **A8.1 Hierarchy.**
  - (a) Non-derogable controls: segregation of duties (A1), no self-approval, and pre-commitment integrity screening of new suppliers (sanctions, denied parties, beneficial ownership, conflict declaration). Only the Board Audit Committee may amend these.
  - (b) Other GPIP-4 controls may be varied only by a written, scoped, time-limited CRO exception. If the CRO cannot be reached within 2 hours, the General Counsel and CFO may jointly issue an interim exception with the same content requirements. The CRO must review it within 5 business days.
  - (c) An incident directive (COO + Incident Commander) may vary only: PO sequencing, approved-supplier register admission (subject to A8.4), local workflow steps, and tendering. It has no effect on GPIP-4 controls unless countersigned under (b).
  - (d) The CFO may issue emergency Finance delegations only through the controlled repository. Each delegation must name the delegates, set a per-purchase cap and an aggregate cap, and state its window. It cannot affect Compliance or segregation of duties.
  - (e) Stricter local rules continue to apply unless displaced by name.
- **A8.2 Scope.** A directive must name the covered assets or lines. It covers an item only if the item is needed to contain the incident or restore a named asset, and the documented need date comes before what an expedited ordinary process could deliver. Contingency or buffer stock is excluded unless stock cover is below 48 hours. A label applied by the requester or the IC is not enough on its own; the need date must be recorded before commitment.
- **A8.3 Emergency approval thresholds.**
  - Under EUR 50k: two independent IC members.
  - EUR 50k to 150k: two independent IC members plus an on-call Finance Controller.
  - Over EUR 150k: the above plus the CFO and Legal, with CRO notification.
  - Approvals may be verbal before commitment, but must be recorded within 4 hours. Aggregate spend under a directive may not exceed the cap stated in it.
- **A8.4 New suppliers.** The minimum screening in A8.1(a) must be completed before commitment, with a 1-hour on-call Compliance service level. Full onboarding must be completed within 10 business days, and payment is held until it is.
- **A8.5 Segregation of duties.** No person may be both requester or committer and an authorizer. The two authorizers must be independent of the requester, of the committer, and of each other's direct reporting line where feasible.
- **A8.6 Retrospective records.**
  - The time of an act is proven by the best available independent evidence. System timestamps are corrected by annotation, never overwritten.
  - A late record of an act proven to be timely is a logging defect, not a breach.
  - A post-hoc approval is a ratification. It affects consequences and never turns a breach into compliance.
- **A8.7 Expiry.**
  - Directives expire automatically at the stated time. Coverage depends on the evidence-adjusted time of commitment.
  - Extensions must be written, signed by the original signatories before expiry, and last at most 72 hours each. The total may not exceed 14 days without CRO and Audit Committee approval. Verbal extensions are void.
  - Follow-up obligations from in-window acts (recording, PO, emergency form, ratification) survive expiry.
- **A8.8 Safeguards against emergency normalization.**
  - Internal Audit reviews every emergency purchase within 30 days.
  - Emergency spend and exception counts are reported to the Audit Committee quarterly.
  - A second directive at the same site within 12 months requires a root-cause review and Audit Committee approval before any further directive is issued.
  - Only the listed emergency categories are valid. Free-text grounds such as "site continuity" are rejected by the system.
  - Nobody may declare an emergency for their own request.
