Remaining-time forecast for sterile batch B-417
Reference ("now") = Friday 18 September 2026, 15:30. All "remaining hours" (h+) are measured from this instant.

Calendar anchors used throughout (h+ from Fri 15:30):
Fri 18:00 = +2.5 | Sat 19 09:00 = +17.5 | Sat 13:00 = +21.5 | Sun 20 12:00 = +44.5 | Sun 20:00 = +52.5 |
Mon 21 08:00 = +64.5 | Mon 12:00 = +68.5 | Mon 16:00 = +72.5 | Mon 20:00 = +76.5 | Tue 22 08:00 = +88.5 |

Three structural findings drive everything below and are established before any scenario arithmetic:

(A) A hard calendar floor at Monday 08:00 (+64.5 h). Primary incubation cannot be technically ready before
Fri 14:00 + 46 h = Sunday 12:00 (+44.5 h). Microbiology readout needs a microbiologist, and QA needs a reviewer.
The only weekend QA window is Saturday 09:00-13:00, which closes 23 hours *before* the earliest possible
microbiology readiness. Therefore no usable microbiology result can meet a QA reviewer before Monday 08:00,
and no terminal event is physically reachable on Friday, Saturday or Sunday. The earliest conceivable terminal
event is Monday morning.

(B) The weekend on-call microbiologist is almost non-binding on the smooth path. Authorization (p = 0.25-0.45)
would produce a Sunday readout at 13:00-23:00, but QA is closed on Sunday, so the join still occurs Monday 08:00.
The authorization only matters (i) when a contamination indication is found, because it starts the 20-30 h
confirmation re-incubation roughly 18-20 h earlier, and (ii) marginally, by removing microbiology from Monday's
morning critical path. This is the opposite of the naive reading, in which weekend staffing looks decisive.

(C) Because of (A), chemistry's Friday spillover is largely free. Under both queue disciplines B-417's chemistry
almost certainly cannot finish by Friday 18:00, but that costs nothing: the batch was going to wait for Monday
anyway. Chemistry therefore does not set the earliest terminal time (microbiology and the calendar do); it sets
the *spread* of the Monday outcome, i.e. which hour of Monday the join occurs.

Calculation shortcuts declared: (i) triangular and uniform ranges collapsed to means for queue-clearing chains,
with the spread reintroduced only at the branch endpoints; (ii) multi-server waiting approximated as
(batches ahead / servers) x mean service, a fluid/deterministic-clearing approximation, then widened for
overdispersion instead of solving an M/G/c queue; (iii) overdispersed QA arrivals handled by band-widening,
not by fitting a negative binomial; (iv) dependence handled by a single latent binary mixture rather than a
correlation structure; (v) calendar pauses handled by mapping work onto open windows (working-hour arithmetic)
rather than by simulation; (vi) probability bands rounded to the nearest 5 percentage points.

## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Shared preliminaries (identical across scenarios).

Chemistry Friday state. Analyst residuals from 15:30 are 1.2-2.0 h (free 16:42-17:30, central 17:06) and
0.4-0.9 h (free 15:54-16:24, central 16:09). Mean chemistry service is (1.5 + 2.5 + 4.5)/3 = 2.83 h, so any job
started after ~15:10 cannot finish before the 18:00 close. Only two jobs can start Friday, and both will be
paused at 18:00.

- Risk-priority discipline (p = 0.45). Order: urgent, then B-417, then the three ordinary samples. The urgent
  sample takes the first free analyst (~16:09); B-417 takes the second (~17:06, range 16:42-17:30). Even the
  minimum service of 1.5 h would end at 18:36, so B-417 banks only ~0.9 h (range 0.5-1.3 h) of service on Friday
  and pauses. Residual 0.6-3.6 h (mean 1.93 h) resumes Monday 08:00 => chemistry complete Mon 08:36-11:36,
  central ~09:55.
- FIFO discipline (p = 0.55). B-417 is fifth. Friday: job 1 starts 16:09 (1.85 h banked), job 2 starts 17:06
  (0.9 h banked); both pause. Monday: job 1 finishes ~08:59, job 2 ~09:56, then ordinary job 3 runs 08:59-11:49
  and job 4 runs 09:56-12:47. B-417 therefore starts on the first freed analyst at ~11:49 (range 10:45-13:15)
  and takes 1.5-4.5 h => chemistry complete Mon 12:15-17:45, central ~14:40. If the start slips past ~13:30 with
  a long service, a second 18:00 pause pushes chemistry into Tue 08:00-09:30 (~10% of this branch).
- Blended chemistry completion: bimodal, Mon 08:36-11:36 (45%) or Mon 12:15-17:45 (55%).

Microbiology branch. Technical readiness = Fri 14:00 + 46-54 h = Sun 12:00-20:00 (+44.5 to +52.5 h).
- Without weekend authorization (p = 0.55-0.75): readout is deferred to Mon 08:00, takes 0.5-1.5 h => usable
  micro result Mon 08:30-09:30.
- With authorization (p = 0.25-0.45): dispatch plus readout 1-3 h => usable result Sun 13:00-23:00. Because QA is
  closed Sunday, this does not advance the join at all; it only pre-clears microbiology so that Monday's critical
  path is purely chemistry, and it starts any confirmation clock ~18-20 h earlier.

Where a maximum replaces a sum. The join is max(chemistry completion, microbiology usable result), never their
sum. Summing them would give roughly (18-26 h of chemistry elapsed) + (47-56 h of microbiology elapsed) = 65-82 h
of "work-clock", which is meaningless here; the two clocks run concurrently from Friday afternoon. The correct
statement is that microbiology owns the floor of the maximum (nothing before Sun 12:00 technically, Mon 08:00
practically) while chemistry usually owns the realized value of the maximum on Monday, because chemistry lands
Mon 08:36-17:45 versus microbiology's Mon 08:30-09:30. In the risk-priority branch the two are nearly
co-critical (within an hour), which is exactly where a max-of-two-near-equal-clocks penalty appears: the join is
later than either branch's mean because it tracks whichever branch is unlucky.

QA queue at the join. Backlog is 5 at 15:30 Friday. Friday 15:30-20:00: one reviewer, 4.5 h at a 2.5 h mean
clears ~1.8, arrivals 0.35 x 4.5 = 1.6, so ~4.8 remain at 20:00. Saturday 09:00-13:00: clears ~1.6, arrivals
~1.4, so ~4.6 remain. Sunday is closed with no arrivals. Monday 08:00 opens with ~4-5 ahead and two reviewers:
service capacity 2/2.5 = 0.8/h against 0.35/h arrivals, a net drain of ~0.45/h. Projected batches ahead:
~3.7 at 10:00, ~2.8 at 12:00, ~1.9 at 14:00, ~1.4 at 15:00. Wait is approximated as
(batches ahead / 2) x 2.5 h. This produces a useful negative correlation: a later chemistry finish arrives at a
shorter QA queue, so Monday's chemistry spread is partially absorbed by QA rather than fully propagated.

S1 Smooth path.
- Chemistry: waiting 0.6-1.6 h Friday plus a 14 h weekend pause (Fri 18:00 -> Mon 08:00) that is pure calendar,
  not congestion; service 1.5-4.5 h split across the pause. Completion Mon 08:36-11:36 (risk-priority) or
  Mon 12:15-17:45 (FIFO).
- Microbiology: 46-54 h continuous incubation (no calendar effect - the incubator runs through the weekend),
  then a staffing-imposed wait from Sun 12:00-20:00 to Mon 08:00 of 12-20 h, then 0.5-1.5 h readout =>
  Mon 08:30-09:30. Note that this staffing wait is *free* because QA is closed anyway.
- Join = max: Mon 09:00-11:36 (risk-priority, central ~10:00) or Mon 12:15-17:45 (FIFO, central ~14:40).
- QA waiting: ~3.7 ahead at 10:00 => ~4.6 h nominal, reduced to ~2-4 h with mild expedite (risk-priority branch);
  ~1.5 ahead at 14:40 => ~1-3 h (FIFO branch). QA service 0.8-3.5 h, median 1.5 h.
- QA acceptance: risk-priority ~Mon 13:30-16:00; FIFO ~Mon 17:30-19:30 (with ~25-35% of the FIFO branch failing
  to clear before the 20:00 QA close and resuming Tue 08:00).
- Final step: Electronic Release 0.25-0.75 h, available continuously, so it never re-crosses a calendar boundary.
- Terminal: Mon 21 Sep 14:00-20:00 (+70.5 to +76.5 h) centrally; Tue 22 Sep 08:30-12:00 (+89 to +92.5 h) for the
  ~15-20% that misses Monday's QA close. Earliest within S1: ~Mon 09:30-11:00.

S1b Friday chemistry exception. If the urgent sample is instrument-constrained to the slower analyst, B-417 can
start at ~15:54 on the fast analyst; a 1.5-2.1 h service then ends 17:24-18:00, or 18:00-18:30 with a short
overtime grace. Chemistry is then complete Friday. The join is nonetheless Mon 08:30-09:30 (microbiology), QA
starts at ~08:30-10:00 against ~4.6 batches, and the terminal event is Mon 10:30-14:00. This is the clearest
demonstration of finding (C): removing chemistry entirely from the critical path buys only ~4-6 h, because the
weekend, not chemistry, owns the bulk of the remaining time.

S2 Chemistry rerun.
- Chemistry: as in S1 up to the first result, then the borderline determination triggers a priority rerun of
  triangular(1.0, 1.8, 3.0), mean ~1.93 h, which cannot cross the 18:00 lab close. Two sub-cases:
  (a) first result by ~15:30 Monday (risk-priority branch, and the earlier FIFO cases): rerun runs the same
  afternoon => chemistry complete Mon 11:30-17:45; (b) first result after ~16:00 Monday (later FIFO cases,
  ~35-45% of S2): the rerun is truncated at 18:00 and resumes Tue 08:00 => chemistry complete Tue 08:30-10:30.
- Microbiology: unchanged, Mon 08:30-09:30. It is no longer on the critical path.
- Join = max = chemistry in every S2 case: Mon 11:30-17:45 or Tue 08:30-10:30.
- QA waiting: 1-3 h Monday afternoon (thin queue), or ~1-3 h Tuesday morning against a fresh Tuesday backlog of
  ~2-4 including Monday leftovers. QA service median 1.5 h.
- Rework: a rerun raises the investigation probability to 0.30-0.55, so roughly a third to a half of S2 continues
  into the S4/S5 tails. The clean-rerun sub-path is what the central range below describes.
- Terminal: central Mon 19:00 to Tue 13:00 (+75.5 to +93.5 h). Note the calendar-forbidden gap between Mon 20:00
  (QA close) and Tue 08:00: acceptance cannot occur inside it, so probability mass piles up just before 20:00 and
  just after Tuesday 08:00 rather than spreading evenly.

S3 Delayed microbiology / contamination confirmation.
- Microbiology: primary readout reveals an indication. With weekend authorization the indication is known
  Sun 13:00-23:00, so the 20-30 h confirmation incubation runs to Mon 09:00-Tue 05:00 and the confirmatory
  readout (0.5-1.5 h, staffed Mon-Fri 08:00-18:00) lands Mon 10:00-Tue 09:30. Without authorization the
  indication is known Mon 08:30-09:30, confirmation runs to Tue 04:30-15:30, and the confirmatory readout lands
  Tue 08:00-17:00, or Wed 08:00-09:30 if technical readiness falls after Tue 18:00. The re-incubation itself is
  continuous and weekend-immune; the two readouts are the staffing-gated steps. This is the one place where the
  on-call authorization is worth a full working day.
- Chemistry: completes Monday as in S1 and then simply waits. This is a clean case where the maximum discards a
  branch entirely: chemistry contributes zero to the remaining time once microbiology needs a second incubation.
- Join = max = microbiology: Mon 10:00 (best case, authorized) to Wed 09:30.
- QA waiting: 1-3 h; the backlog is well drained by Tuesday/Wednesday. Service median 1.5 h.
- Rework: investigation probability is 0.30-0.55 here. Documentation-only (60-80% of those) adds 8-20 QA working
  hours, i.e. roughly one to two working days => terminal Wed 23 Sep to Thu 24 Sep. Laboratory investigation adds
  3-7 calendar days including the weekend of 26-27 September, followed by a fresh QA review of 1.5-3.5 h plus
  release => terminal Fri 25 Sep to Wed 30 Sep.
- Final step: Electronic Release 0.25-0.75 h if the confirmation is negative or non-critical; if a critical
  defect is confirmed, Batch Rejected recording of 0.5-1.0 h instead (see S6).
- Terminal: central Wed 23 Sep 09:00 to Thu 24 Sep 18:00 (+113.5 to +146.5 h); tail +165 to +285 h.

S4 QA query / investigation tail with normal branches.
- Chemistry and microbiology: exactly as S1; the join is Mon 09:00-17:45 and both laboratory clocks are then
  irrelevant.
- QA waiting and first service: as S1, so QA is in review Monday ~11:30-19:30.
- Documentation query (p = 0.15-0.30; the 0.82 completeness score justifies the lower half of the band, ~0.20).
  It adds 4-12 QA working hours measured on the QA calendar. Worked example: a query raised Mon 15:00 with 8
  working hours consumes Mon 15:00-20:00 (5 h) and Tue 08:00-11:00 (3 h); B-417 then re-enters QA (rule 4),
  waits ~0.5-2 h, is reviewed for ~1.5 h and released => Tue 22 Sep ~13:00. A 12-hour query raised late Monday
  pushes to Tue 15:00-Wed 10:00. Because the query clock only runs during QA hours, the Monday 20:00 boundary and
  Saturday's absence both act as amplifiers: a query raised at 18:00 Monday effectively costs a whole extra
  overnight.
- Deviation investigation with normal branches (p = 0.04-0.10). Documentation-only (60-80%) adds 8-20 QA working
  hours => roughly Wed 23 Sep to Thu 24 Sep evening. Laboratory (20-40%) adds 3-7 calendar days from ~Mon 15:00,
  landing Thu 24 Sep to Mon 28 Sep, then a fresh QA review and release => Thu 24 Sep to Tue 29 Sep
  (+140 to +250 h). The 3-7 day laboratory clock is calendar time and therefore already contains the
  26-27 September weekend; I do not add a further weekend penalty to it.
- Terminal: query-only central Tue 22 Sep 11:00 to Wed 23 Sep 14:00; investigation central Wed 23 to Thu 24
  evening; laboratory-investigation tail Thu 24 to Tue 29 Sep.

S5 Correlated adverse cluster.
- Trigger: the temperature-excursion flag is confirmed (p = 0.20-0.40, central 0.30). Conditional on that, the
  chemistry rerun probability roughly doubles (~0.30) and the contamination-indication probability roughly
  doubles (~0.18), and the investigation probability jumps to 0.30-0.55. These are not independent draws: they
  share the excursion as a common cause, so the joint probability of "rerun and contamination and investigation"
  is materially higher than the product of the marginals. Under independence one would compute roughly
  0.15 x 0.08 x 0.07 = 0.08%, whereas the mixture gives roughly 0.30 x (0.30 x 0.18 x 0.42) ~= 0.7%, an order of
  magnitude larger, and the union that drives the P95 region rises from ~3% to ~6-8%.
- Path: chemistry rerun completes Mon-Tue; microbiology confirmation completes Tue-Wed; the join is Tue-Wed; a
  laboratory investigation of 3-7 calendar days runs from Wed 23 Sep through the weekend to roughly Sat 26 Sep -
  Wed 30 Sep; possible additional laboratory work re-enters the chemistry queue and re-crosses the Mon-Fri
  08:00-18:00 calendar; a full QA re-review of 1.5-3.5 h with a possible second documentation query follows.
- Terminal: central Tue 29 Sep to Fri 2 Oct (+250 to +330 h), tail beyond Mon 5 Oct (+400 h). Terminal event is
  Electronic Release in the majority of this branch but Batch Rejected in a substantial minority.

S6 Batch Rejected.
- Requires a *confirmed* critical defect, essentially always via the S3 confirmation loop (or a laboratory
  investigation finding). Path: indication Mon, confirmation Tue-Wed, investigation/impact assessment, then
  0.5-1.0 h of rejection recording, which is not calendar-constrained beyond QA availability.
- Earliest Wed 23 Sep 10:00 (+114.5 h) if the on-call readout was authorized and the confirmation is immediately
  decisive without a formal investigation; central Thu 24 Sep to Tue 29 Sep; tail beyond Fri 2 Oct.
- Overall probability ~3-7% = P(indication) 0.07-0.14 x P(confirmed critical | indication) ~0.3-0.5, plus a small
  contribution from laboratory investigations that uncover a critical defect.

## 4. Forecast summary

All figures are remaining time from Friday 18 September 2026, 15:30. Recall the two hard structural facts: no
terminal event is possible before Monday 08:00 + ~2 h, and QA acceptance cannot occur in the calendar-forbidden
bands (weeknights after 20:00, Saturday after 13:00, all of Sunday), so the terminal-time distribution is
multimodal with gaps rather than smooth.

- Plausible earliest completion: +66 to +69 h => Monday 21 September, 09:30-12:30. This requires the
  risk-priority discipline, a short chemistry residual Monday morning, a clean microbiology readout at 08:30, and
  an expedited QA slot. Anything earlier than ~+66 h is essentially impossible without out-of-hours QA
  authorization, which is not part of the stated process.

- Central / P50-like remaining time: +71 to +78 h => Monday 21 September, ~14:30 to ~20:00 (point estimate
  ~+74 h, i.e. Monday ~17:30). A secondary P50 lobe sits at +89 to +92 h (Tuesday 22 September, 08:30-11:30)
  covering the ~15-20% of otherwise-smooth cases whose QA review does not clear Monday's 20:00 close. Stated as a
  single interval spanning the gap: +71 to +92 h.

- P80-like remaining time: +100 to +125 h => Tuesday 22 September evening to Wednesday 23 September evening
  (point estimate ~+118 h, Wednesday afternoon). This is where documentation queries, late reruns and the faster
  contamination-confirmation cases accumulate. Cumulative reasoning: ~40% of mass by +78 h, ~58% by +92 h, ~69%
  by +100 h, ~82% by +125 h.

- P90-like / severe-tail remaining time: +130 to +165 h => Wednesday 23 September late to Friday 25 September
  (point estimate ~+148 h, Thursday 24 September evening). Dominated by documentation-only investigations and
  slow contamination confirmations.

- Extreme tail (P95-P98): +190 to +330 h => Saturday 26 September to Friday 2 October, driven almost entirely by
  laboratory-requiring deviation investigations (3-7 calendar days plus re-testing plus QA re-review), with a
  correlated worst case beyond +400 h (Monday 5 October or later). I would not quote a bound tighter than
  "into early October" for the 1-in-50 case.

- Most plausible terminal event(s): Electronic Release with probability ~93-96%, most likely occurring Monday
  21 September afternoon/evening or Tuesday 22 September morning. Batch Rejected with probability ~3-7%, and if
  it occurs it will almost certainly be Thursday 24 September or later, because rejection requires the 20-30 h
  confirmation incubation plus a staffed confirmatory readout plus an impact assessment. A rejection before
  Wednesday 23 September is close to impossible.

Compact restatement: roughly a coin flip that B-417 is released before Monday's QA close (Monday ~20:00,
+76.5 h), about four chances in five that it is released by Wednesday evening (+125 h), about nine in ten by the
end of that week (+165 h), and a real 5-8% residual risk of a multi-week outcome extending into early October.
For contrast, naively summing unconditional stage means (chemistry wait ~4 h + chemistry service ~2.8 h +
incubation ~50 h + readout ~1 h + QA wait ~5 h + QA service ~2.5 h + expected rework ~6 h + release ~0.5 h
~= 72 h) happens to land near my P50 by coincidence, but it is wrong in structure: it double-counts the
concurrent chemistry and incubation clocks, ignores the 14 h weekend pause and the 12-20 h microbiology staffing
gap that jointly create the +64.5 h floor, and it produces no tail at all.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

Ranked by expected effect on the remaining-time distribution, measured as the shift in the P50-to-P90 region.

1. Microbiology contamination indication and the confirmation loop. Mainly path uncertainty (amplified by
   dependence uncertainty). A single binary observation at Monday's readout moves the forecast by 40-90 h in the
   central region and opens the whole rejection branch. Nothing else in the process has a comparable single-event
   leverage, because the 20-30 h confirmation incubation is irreducible continuous time that then has to land
   inside a Mon-Fri 08:00-18:00 readout window.

2. Whether an opened deviation investigation is documentation-only or laboratory-requiring. Mainly path
   uncertainty, with the 60-80% split itself flagged as inexact. The difference between "8-20 QA working hours"
   and "3-7 calendar days plus re-testing plus re-review" is roughly 60-200 h. This driver owns the entire P95+
   region, and because the split proportion is explicitly uncertain, the tail is uncertain in a
   second-order way as well.

3. Chemistry queue discipline and B-417's resulting Monday start position. Mainly queue uncertainty (the 55/45
   FIFO-versus-risk-priority mixture), with a service-time component from the triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) draw.
   This is the dominant driver of the *smooth* path, worth ~4-6 h on the join and, through the Monday 20:00 QA
   close, worth ~14 h in the cases where it flips the terminal event from Monday evening to Tuesday morning. It
   is the single largest lever on the P50 even though it barely touches the tail. An unmodelled Monday-morning
   arrival of new urgent samples would act through this same channel.

4. The unresolved temperature-excursion flag. Mainly observation uncertainty that acts as dependence
   uncertainty. On its own it delays nothing, but it is the latent common cause that simultaneously inflates the
   rerun probability (0.15 -> ~0.30), the contamination probability (0.08 -> ~0.18) and the investigation
   probability (0.04-0.10 -> 0.30-0.55). It therefore controls the *correlation* of drivers 1, 2 and 3 rather
   than any single stage duration, and it is why the tail cannot be computed as a product of independent
   marginals. Treating the anomalies as independent would understate the joint tail by a factor of roughly 2-3.

5. QA queue depth at the future join time together with the heavy-tailed QA service law. Mainly queue
   uncertainty plus service-time uncertainty. The Monday 08:00 backlog is only weakly pinned (~4-5 batches from
   a fluid argument, but arrivals are explicitly overdispersed), and the fitted lognormal service has a
   coefficient of variation near 1.3, so one long review ahead of B-417 costs 3-5 h. Effect is ±3-6 h centrally,
   more if a Monday arrival burst occurs, and it interacts with the Monday 20:00 close. Partially self-limiting
   because a later join meets a shorter queue.

Deliberately excluded from the top five: the working-calendar structure itself. It is by far the largest single
contributor to the remaining time (the Friday-18:00-to-Monday-08:00 pause plus the microbiology staffing gap
account for roughly 50 of the ~74 central hours), but it is near-deterministic and therefore not an *uncertainty*
driver. The residual calendar uncertainty that does matter is binary boundary-crossing: whether QA acceptance
falls before or after Monday 20:00, and whether the weekend on-call microbiologist is authorized (which, per
finding (B), only matters if a contamination indication appears).

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

Single most forecast-changing event: the primary microbiology readout result, i.e. whether a contamination
indication is present. This is expected Monday 21 September 08:30-09:30, or Sunday 20 September 13:00-23:00 if
the on-call microbiologist is authorized.

Direction of the update under its possible values:
- Clean readout (probability ~0.86-0.93). The entire S3/S5/S6 mass collapses. The forecast becomes essentially
  a chemistry-and-QA problem on a single working day: the P50 tightens to Monday 21 September 14:00-20:00 with a
  small Tuesday-morning lobe, the P80 pulls in from ~+118 h to roughly +95 h (Tuesday afternoon), the P90 pulls
  in from ~+148 h to roughly +120 h (Wednesday), and the probability of Batch Rejected drops from ~3-7% to well
  under 1%. Remaining uncertainty would then be almost entirely queue-driven and the whole distribution would
  narrow to a span of about two days.
- Indication present (probability ~0.07-0.14). The forecast shifts sharply and asymmetrically. The 20-30 h
  confirmation incubation plus a staffed confirmatory readout moves the earliest plausible terminal time from
  Monday morning to Tuesday 22 September at best and Wednesday 23 September typically; the P50 moves out by
  roughly 40-70 h to Wednesday-Thursday. Critically, it also updates the *investigation* probability from
  0.04-0.10 to 0.30-0.55, so the 3-7 calendar-day laboratory branch becomes a first-order rather than a tail
  possibility, and the P90 moves out to roughly Monday 28 September - Friday 2 October. It simultaneously raises
  the posterior on the temperature excursion (the indication is evidence for the common cause), which in turn
  raises the chemistry-rerun probability even if chemistry has not yet reported. Batch Rejected becomes a
  ~30-50% possibility. Chemistry's Monday queue position, currently the top lever, would become irrelevant.

Secondary update trigger: resolution of the temperature-excursion flag by engineering. Confirmation would raise
the rerun, contamination and investigation probabilities jointly and shift the P80/P90 out by roughly 30-60 h
even before any laboratory result changes, and it would make a deviation investigation more likely than not.
Exoneration of the flag would remove the correlated tail, drop the investigation probability back to the
0.04-0.10 band, and pull the P90 in by roughly 30-50 h.

Tertiary trigger worth watching Monday morning: the actual chemistry queue order and B-417's service start time.
Learning that B-417 started before ~09:00 (risk-priority realized) versus after ~12:00 (FIFO realized) is worth
~4-6 h on the P50 and, more importantly, determines whether QA acceptance is likely to clear Monday's 20:00
close or slip to Tuesday, which is a ~14 h discontinuity.
