## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning
Deadline value (€20–35k) is dominated by regulatory exposure (€100–500k+ plus non-monetary sanctions) once risk 70 and fuzzy match remains unresolved. False-rejection harm (€15–80k) is real but bounded and reversible via later evidence, while unlawful execution creates asymmetric, irreversible compliance cost. Therefore any plan that inserts a mandatory independent second review before execution outranks shortcuts whose numerical gain is smaller than the regulatory tail.

## 4. Conformance constraints
Rule 6 requires second-officer review at current risk/fuzzy values; Rule 7 requires distinct reviewer. Actions A and B directly violate these irrespective of any forecast, because the rules are mandatory controls, not optional loss-minimizers. Action F becomes valid only when documented evidence shows the rejection threshold is met under the firm’s policy. Action G requires both director authorization and a justified incident identifier; neither exists.

## 5. Resource spillovers
Choosing S2 (C) transfers 0.12–0.28 extra breach probability to P-901. S4 avoids this but adds 35–65 min latency. If S4 arrives at 35 min the ranking of plan 1 improves relative to C; at 65 min C becomes preferable unless alternate screening materially lowers risk. Alternate-screening API quota is scarce; using it early is low-cost insurance. Waiting for an un-enabled override (G) risks consuming the entire remaining window without a valid path.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence
