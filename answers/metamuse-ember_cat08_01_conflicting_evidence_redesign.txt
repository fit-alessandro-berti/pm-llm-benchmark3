## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The evidence is contradictory because the process is not failing in one place: it is **front-loading speed and exporting work downstream**. The attractive metrics are concentrated in the first few activities; the expensive, slow and non-compliant consequences appear later as complaints, rework, abandonment and compliance exceptions.

Explicitly, the most frequent path is attractive on naive speed and cost: Intake  Auto eligibility screen (3,600 of 5,000 cases)  Fast approval (2,550 directly from Auto, plus 240 via Fraud/risk review)  Dispatch replacement (2,300 of the Fast approval cases). Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval has a median of only 0.4 hours, and Fast approval  Dispatch replacement has a median of 1.8 hours. Fast approval cases are also 31% cheaper before rework.

The same path is worse once the whole trace is observed. Customer complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases versus 3.3% of cases using Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device. The performance DFG shows why: Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen has mean 72 hours, median 50, p90 150; Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen has mean 96 hours, median 70, p90 180. Once accounting for complaints, reshipments and service credits, Fast approval is only 9% cheaper, not 31%. The performance problem is therefore not the 0.4-hour median edge; it is the long downstream tail on complaint edges.

**Performance.** The slowest system-level edges are not the auto edges. They are Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review (mean 34h, p90 88h), Manual eligibility review  Repair quote (mean 22h, p90 60h), Repair quote  Customer approval (mean 30h, p90 80h), Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review (mean 40h, p90 100h), and Customer complaint/reopen  Escalation board (mean 54h, p90 130h). The DFG shows a loop rather than a straight line: Request missing evidence is entered 1,430 times (650 from Auto, 780 from Manual) and returns to Manual eligibility review 890 times, while 320 cases are abandoned at that point. Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26% of cases entering it. This is a rework loop, not a one-time information request.

**Quality and rework.** Customer complaint/reopen is reported in 9.2% of all cases, with 510 complaint/reopen inbound edges in the DFG. A reopen is not a cheap administrative event: it sends 310 cases back to Manual eligibility review and 120 to the Escalation board, consuming scarce manual capacity. Repair quality is also segment-dependent: Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% of repaired low-risk devices, matching the 45 Quality check  Rework repair edges against 360 Repair device  Quality check edges. The wrong-part economics are sharply asymmetric: expected wrong replacement cost is EUR 95 for low-risk accessories but EUR 410 for high-value devices.

**Compliance.** Token-based replay gives fitness 0.91 but only 68% of traces fit the normative model. That combination matters: most activities can be explained, but almost one-third of cases deviate at least once. Alignment diagnostics identify exactly the deviations suggested by the DFG: Fast approval before Evidence completeness, Customer complaint/reopen after close, and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices. Under R1, a claim above EUR 400 must have Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval; the DFG alone cannot count violations because claim value is not an activity, but the Auto  Fast approval edge (2,550 cases) is the exposure point and alignment confirms high-value bypasses. Under R2, dispatch before evidence completeness is confirmed is prohibited except for Premium low-risk accessories; the large Fast approval  Dispatch replacement flow is therefore compliant only for that exception and for cases with confirmed completeness. Under R3, reopened claims must pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial; Escalation board  Dispatch replacement (65), Escalation board  Repair device (45) and Escalation board  Claim denied (10) create an ambiguity that must be audited rather than assumed compliant. Low precision of the discovered model also warns against treating every low-frequency auto/risk/repair combination as legitimate simply because it appears in the log.

**Customer experience is segment-specific, not average.** Premium low-risk accessories are 18% of cases: fast path median 8 hours with 6% complaint/reopen versus manual median 46 hours with 3%; service-credit exposure is high above 24 hours, so forcing this segment into the manual path destroys the main value proposition. Standard low-risk accessories are 25%: fast median 13 hours, 8% complaints, versus manual 52 hours, 4%; they tolerate up to 72 hours if status visibility is good. By contrast, Premium high-value devices are 14%: fast median 20 hours but 22% complaint/reopen versus reviewed/repair median 96 hours and 5%; compliance risk is high if R1/R2 are violated. Standard high-value devices are 21%: fast median 28 hours, 18% complaints, versus reviewed/repair 120 hours, 6%, with high wrong-replacement cost. Unclear defect / missing evidence cases are 22% with a median of 140 hours, the highest abandonment and repeated evidence requests. A single average cycle time would hide that the fast path is defensible for low-risk accessories, risky for high-value devices, and largely irrelevant for unclear-defect cases.

**Resource pressure.** Auto screen capacity is effectively unlimited but rules are outdated and not segment-aware. Manual eligibility review has only 7 reviewers at 36 productive hours per week and EUR 34 per labor hour, with queues growing sharply above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk review has only 2 specialists at EUR 52 per labor hour and is a Monday/campaign bottleneck. Repair quote has 3 technicians at EUR 45 per labor hour, delayed mainly by part-price confirmation. Quality check has 2 inspectors at EUR 38 per labor hour and is underused except after repair campaigns. The Escalation board has one daily 45-minute slot: valuable for satisfaction, but not scalable. The current design therefore pushes work into the scarcest resources (manual review, fraud specialists, escalation board) precisely when the fast path generates complaints.

Diagnosis in one sentence: **the process optimizes the decision token (Fast approval) while under-controlling evidence completeness, value/risk eligibility, and replacement correctness, so speed is borrowed from downstream quality, compliance and capacity.**

## 2. Redesign A: Ring-fenced fast path — segment- and risk-based routing with hard compliance gates

**Redesigned flow.** Intake  automated segment/risk triage (claim value, device class, defect type, fraud signals, evidence-completeness flag, Premium/Standard status)  two controlled branches. Branch 1, *eligible fast*: only low-risk accessories with confirmed evidence completeness, value at or below the EUR 400 R1 threshold, and no fraud signal proceed Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement, with the Premium low-risk R2 exception explicitly recorded. Branch 2, *reviewed decision*: high-value devices, claims above EUR 400, unclear defects, or fraud signals must complete Manual eligibility review and/or Fraud/risk review before any approval token; only then do they enter Reviewed approval  Repair quote or reviewed replacement. In system terms, the Fast approval token is blocked unless the R1/R2 conditions are logged, rather than relying on reviewer discipline.

**Targets.** This redesign targets the 35% high-value population (Premium 14% + Standard 21%), the Auto  Fast approval edge (2,550 cases), and the Fraud/risk review load (980 inbound edges). It deliberately protects Premium low-risk (18%) and, conditionally, Standard low-risk (25%) from being pushed into the 46–52-hour manual path.

**Mechanism of improvement.** It changes *who is allowed to be fast*. Instead of segment-blind auto rules, eligibility is decided by risk and value. That directly attacks R1/R2 non-conformance and the high-value complaint differential (22%/18% fast versus 5%/6% reviewed). It also protects the Premium low-risk 24-hour service-credit threshold by keeping that cohort out of the manual queue. For Standard low-risk cases, the eligibility gate is paired with status visibility so the tolerated 72-hour window is managed rather than wasted.

**Deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** High-value cycle time will increase from the fast medians of 20/28 hours toward the reviewed range unless the reviewed path itself is accelerated; this is accepted because the current speed is purchased with 18–22% reopen rates and high R1/R2 exposure. It increases manual/fraud inflow: the prompt warns that updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%, which can push Manual eligibility review beyond the 82% utilization cliff if launched without capacity protection. It does not fix the evidence ping-pong loop (Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review, mean 34h), the Repair quote  Customer approval wait (mean 30h), or wrong-part selection after dispatch. Low-risk fast-path complaints remain higher than manual (6–8% versus 3–4%); that is the explicit price for 8–13-hour medians versus 46–52 hours.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence once, properly — front-loaded completeness with a one-shot evidence service

**Redesigned flow.** Intake becomes a structured completeness stage *before* eligibility: Intake  Evidence completeness service  Eligibility  Technical decision  Fulfillment/repair  Quality/compliance close. The completeness service uses defect-specific checklists (serial/proof of purchase, photos, failure description, accessory/device class), validates submissions automatically, and issues **one consolidated request** listing every missing item with examples, a visible tracker, and a deadline. Only after completeness is confirmed does the case enter Auto or Manual eligibility. If new material evidence emerges later, the process uses a delta request tied to the specific decision element, not a full return to Manual eligibility review. Unclear-defect cases receive a diagnostic checklist and a named case owner. An assisted phone/retail channel is offered alongside the portal so digital literacy does not become an eligibility barrier.

**Targets.** This targets the 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence population, the 1,430 Request missing evidence entries, the 890 returns to Manual eligibility review, the 26% repeat-evidence rate, and the 320 abandonments at the evidence stage. It also targets the Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence edge (780 cases, mean 14h) and Auto eligibility screen  Request missing evidence edge (650 cases, mean 5.2h).

**Mechanism of improvement.** It changes the *order and structure* of evidence work. The current process discovers incompleteness repeatedly during eligibility, which creates the 34-hour mean / 88-hour p90 return loop. Front-loading completeness and consolidating requests should reduce repeat requests — the prompt indicates a plausible 30–45% reduction — and gives Standard customers the status visibility that makes a longer but predictable window tolerable. It also supports R2 by making “evidence completeness confirmed” a real state before dispatch, rather than an assumption.

**Deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** It adds upfront effort and may add time for cases that would otherwise have been complete and simple; therefore low-risk cases with complete evidence at intake should pass through the completeness check automatically without a human stage. It requires portal build, content design and assisted-channel staffing, and it carries a real abandonment risk for customers with low digital literacy if the portal is the only route. It does not by itself enforce R1 for high-value claims, does not prevent wrong replacement selection once dispatch is allowed, and does not shorten Repair quote or Fraud/risk queues. In short, B fixes information quality and loops; A is still needed for value/risk gating and C for fulfillment correctness.

## 4. Redesign C: Parallel fulfillment with a targeted pre-dispatch assurance gate

**Redesigned flow.** After the eligibility/technical decision, the downstream work is parallelized rather than strictly sequential. Decision  in parallel: Repair quote, part-price confirmation, final fraud/compliance check, Quality check slot reservation, and Customer approval request with status visibility and deadline. For replacements, a **pre-dispatch quality gate** verifies part compatibility, device class/value, evidence/R2 status and prior complaint history before Dispatch replacement. The gate is mandatory for high-value devices and uncertain part matches, but is a light automated check for Premium low-risk accessories to protect the 24-hour expectation. Post-repair Quality check is retained. Complaint/reopen handling changes from a full restart to a *focused delta review*: only the disputed element (eligibility, part selection, evidence sufficiency) is rechecked. The daily Escalation board is reserved for Premium, high-value, repeated or unresolved complaints rather than routine reopens.

**Targets.** This targets Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen (260 edges, mean 96h, p90 180h), wrong replacement cost (EUR 410 high-value versus EUR 95 low-risk), Repair quote  Customer approval (mean 30h, p90 80h), Customer approval  Repair device (mean 20h), Quality check  Rework repair (45 edges; 12.5% of high-value repairs), and the reopen load (310 to Manual eligibility review, 120 to Escalation board).

**Mechanism of improvement.** It changes the *temporal structure after decision*. Parallelizing quote, part-price confirmation, compliance check and approval removes part of the sequential sum that makes reviewed/repair paths reach 96–120 hours for high-value segments. The pre-dispatch gate catches wrong-part errors before the EUR 410 cost and before the 70–180-hour complaint tail, using Quality check capacity that is currently underused. Delta review prevents every complaint from consuming a full Manual eligibility review cycle (mean 40h) and reduces pressure on the single daily Escalation board.

**Deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** The gate adds the stated 6–18 hours for gated cases; that delay is deliberate, trading a bounded delay for avoidance of a much longer complaint/reshipment tail. It shifts pressure to Quality check (2 inspectors) and Repair quote technicians (3), so if applied indiscriminately it creates a new bottleneck. Parallel quotes can be wasted if Fraud/risk later denies the claim. Replacing routine escalation-board handling with async delta review may reduce the satisfaction benefit for some customers if the board’s human escalation effect is lost. It does not fix front-end evidence loops or R1 eligibility by itself; without A and B, faster fulfillment can simply fulfill the wrong decision more quickly.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A.**  
1. *Manual/fraud flood.* Tightening the auto screen can increase manual inflow by 15–35%. With 7 reviewers and a sharp queue effect above 82% utilization, the cure for high-value compliance can create a Premium queue that breaches the 24-hour service-credit threshold. Fraud/risk review (2 specialists) will also spike on Mondays and after campaigns.  
2. *Misclassification.* Auto rules are outdated and not segment-aware; value, defect class or evidence-completeness coding errors can send high-value cases through the fast branch or push low-risk cases into manual review. The gate is only as good as the triage attributes and audit logs.  
*Bottleneck migration:* the bottleneck moves from Auto screen/Fast approval to Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review queues; Monday/campaign peaks become the binding constraint.

**Redesign B.**  
1. *Digital exclusion/abandonment.* A portal-only design can increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy, worsening the already-high abandonment in the evidence stage (320 abandoned edges). The assisted channel is therefore not optional; without it, B can improve average completeness while losing a vulnerable minority.  
2. *Completeness perfectionism.* If “complete” is defined too strictly, simple cases are delayed and customers submit low-quality photos that still trigger repeat requests. A 30–45% reduction in repeats still leaves a residual repeat rate from the current 26%.  
*Bottleneck migration:* work moves from Manual eligibility review loops to portal support/assisted-channel queues and to the completeness triage function; if that team is understaffed, the 88-hour p90 evidence loop reappears in a new activity.

**Redesign C.**  
1. *Gate becomes the new queue.* Only 2 Quality check inspectors exist. Applying the 6–18-hour gate to all dispatches, or borrowing inspectors from post-repair Quality check, can delay Premium low-risk cases beyond 24 hours or increase post-repair rework (currently 12.5% for high-value). The gate must be risk-targeted.  
2. *Parallel work is wasted or conflicting.* Technicians may quote and reserve parts before Fraud/risk or eligibility is final, creating wasted quotes, part-price churn and customer confusion when approval terms change. Replacing the Escalation board with async review can also reduce satisfaction if customers perceive de-escalation.  
*Bottleneck migration:* pressure shifts from Repair quote waiting and the Escalation board to Quality check inspectors, the 3 Repair quote technicians, and the customer-approval wait itself, which remains an external delay.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Sequencing logic.** Start where compliance risk and segment size allow learning without flooding scarce capacity; then fix the evidence loop to release manual capacity; only then expand the hard gate to the larger high-value population; finally scale parallel fulfillment. This order respects three dependencies from the artifacts: rule tightening increases manual inflow, evidence-loop reduction can release manual capacity, and the pre-dispatch gate can be staffed from currently underused Quality check capacity.

**Phase 0 — instrument before changing.** For 2 weeks, add claim value, device class, defect type, Premium/Standard status and evidence-completeness state to conformance replay so R1/R2 violations can be counted rather than inferred from the DFG. Freeze outcome definitions: cycle time from Intake to END *plus* a post-close observation window of at least 14 days (complaint p90s reach 150–180 hours), complaint/reopen, reshipment, service credit, rework repair, abandonment, and fully loaded cost per closed case. Always report by segment; a pooled average can improve simply because mix shifts toward low-risk cases.

**First pilot — Redesign A-lite for Premium high-value devices only (14%).** Hard-block Fast approval for Premium high-value claims above EUR 400 or with incomplete evidence/fraud signals; route them to Manual/Fraud review with a priority slot, while Premium low-risk remains on the protected fast lane. Use Standard high-value as a delayed-rollout comparison group. This is the smallest cohort with the worst trade-off: 22% fast-path reopen versus 5% reviewed, EUR 410 wrong-part exposure, and high R1/R2 risk.

**Second pilot — Redesign B for unclear-defect cases (22%).** Launch the structured completeness service with one consolidated request, tracker and assisted channel for this cohort only. The goal is to reduce the 26% repeat-evidence rate and the 34h/88h Request  Manual loop before expanding A to Standard high-value, thereby releasing manual capacity.

**Third pilot — expand A to Standard high-value devices (21%).** Proceed only if manual utilization remains below the danger zone and B has released capacity or temporary Fraud/risk cover is arranged for Mondays/campaigns. Protect a Premium low-risk fast lane throughout so the 24-hour credit exposure is not created by queue spillover.

**Fourth pilot — Redesign C components.** First extend the pre-dispatch gate to Standard high-value replacements where catch rate justifies the 6–18-hour delay. Then pilot parallel quote/part-price/approval for the repair cohort. Finally, replace routine Escalation board routing with delta review for Standard reopens while retaining the board for Premium, high-value and repeated complaints.

**Metrics to monitor.** Leading: Auto  Manual inflow share, manual and fraud utilization/queue p90, evidence repeat rate, gate catch rate, parallel-quote cycle time, portal abandonment by channel. Lagging: R1/R2/R3 violation rate in replay, complaint/reopen within 14 days post-close, wrong-part reshipment, rework repair rate, abandonment, median *and p90* cycle time by segment, fully loaded cost per closed case including credits/reshipments.

**Stop/go thresholds (ranges, not exact ROI).**  
- Pilot 1 go: R1/R2 violations in the pilot cohort fall to near zero (for example <2–5% of audited cases), reopen rate moves from the 22% fast-path level toward the reviewed benchmark range (roughly 10–12% after the full post-close window), manual utilization stays below ~82%, and Premium low-risk p90 does not deteriorate. Stop/rollback if utilization persistently exceeds ~82% or queue p90 rises sharply.  
- Pilot 2 go: repeat evidence requests fall by at least ~20–30% relative (toward the plausible 30–45% range), unclear-defect median/p90 improve, and abandonment does not rise by more than a few percentage points in either portal or assisted channels. Stop if assisted-channel users are materially worse or portal support becomes the new 88-hour bottleneck.  
- Pilot 3/4 go: Standard high-value reopen moves from the 18% fast level toward the 6% reviewed level without p90 cycle exceeding the reviewed-path baseline by a wide margin; gate catch rate is high enough that avoided wrong-part cost plausibly offsets 6–18 hours of delay. Stop if QC utilization creates a new queue or if delta review increases repeat reopens.

**How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream.** Judge every pilot on intake-to-closed-plus-14/21-days outcomes, not on decision speed. Include reopen  Manual/Escalation work, reshipments, credits and rework in cost per closed case. Compare each pilot segment with its delayed-rollout control segment and with its own pre-pilot conformance replay. Audit a sample of “closed” cases for post-close complaints and R3 handling. If median cycle improves while p90, reopen rate, abandonment or manual utilization worsens, treat the result as bottleneck displacement, not improvement.
