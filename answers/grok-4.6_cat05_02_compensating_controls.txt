## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not “two activity names must differ.” It is a segregation-of-duties objective: no single human should be able to both initiate and authorize a completed transfer, because that capability is how an error or a fraud becomes money in motion. Shared or borrowed credentials are the same person. A compensating package is useful only if it restores *independent authorization before irrevocability* for the threats FEP-1 was meant to block.

| Objective / threat | What “good” looks like | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1. Honest solo error** (wrong vendor, amount, or bank typed by the same person who releases) | A second independent mind, or an automated check that actually covers the field that was wrong, stops the transfer before settlement | **Prevents** duplicates, basic PO mismatch, operator-limit breaches, and *recent* bank-account changes. **Does not** check commercial reasonableness or that goods/services were received | **Prevents** if the manager inspects invoice, bank details, and receipt *before release or before settlement*. Rubber-stamp is a miss | **Detects** after the fact. **Corrects** only while recall/recovery still works (typically ~24 hours) | Does not remove the error path; it *permits* same-user action inside a narrower envelope (amount, vendor age, no recent bank change, clean AV-2, pre-settlement manager, next-day audit) |
| **T2. Solo fraud / diversion** (one operator pays a related or substituted account) | The initiator cannot also be the effective authorizer; bank-master changes and ownership are challenged | **Partly prevents** (sanctions list, bank-ownership check, 7-day change window, limit). **Fails** if the account was planted earlier, the service is down, or the operator overrides the warning | **Prevents** only if the manager is independent *and* actually looks at bank details before settlement. A dependent supervisor, or a credential the operator can use, collapses this control | **Detects** pattern/beneficiary issues. **Corrects** only inside the recall window; after that, recovery is uncertain | Caps amount and forbids recent bank change and AV-2 override. **Does not** prove beneficiary legitimacy beyond those checks |
| **T3. Collusion** (preparer + releaser, or operator + manager) | A *third*, independent, substantive reviewer, plus detective review that can still reverse | **Fails to address** collusion. May still catch mechanical defects the colluders did not hide | **Prevents** only when the manager is outside the colluding set and not conflicted. **Fails** if the manager is the supervisor who shares a credential or rubber-stamps | **Detects** if the auditor is independent and looks at substance (receipt authenticity, related-party beneficiary). Weak after recall | **Fails to address** operator–manager collusion. The permission is not a collusion control |
| **T4. Compromised, shared, or service-account identity** | Attribution: the person at the keyboard is the named authorizer | **Does not** establish who controlled a compromised account (stated limitation) | Weak if approval is a reused credential or a click-through. Stronger if out-of-band and identity-assured | May **detect** anomalous reuse, shared workstations, or impossible timing after the fact | Explicitly rejects shared service accounts as “a second person.” Does not add identity assurance |
| **T5. Last-minute bank-account substitution / vendor-master fraud** | Release is blocked or independently re-approved after any recent account change | **Prevents/warns** on changes in the previous seven days. **Blind** after day seven, and **absent** when the service is unavailable | **Prevents** if bank details are reviewed before settlement | **Detects** later; **corrects** only if recall still works | Condition 4 *should* forbid the emergency path after a 7-day change. If that condition is ignored, ED-1 is being misused |
| **T6. Fictitious performance / false receipt** (invoice pays for work not done) | Someone independent challenges the commercial evidence | **Fails** by design: no goods-received or reasonableness test. A clean pass is not evidence of receipt | **Can prevent** if the manager inspects supporting receipt *and* can spot fabrication. A false document plus a shallow review defeats it | **Detects** if audit tests authenticity, not merely presence of a file. **Corrects** only while money is recallable | Adds no receipt test |
| **T7. Emergency-path abuse** (false or stale urgency used to drop four-eyes) | Same-user release only when *all seven* ED-1 conditions are true *and evidenced* | Must be fully up and a clean pass with no override — otherwise the emergency path is invalid | Independent L3+ approval **before settlement** is mandatory, not optional decoration | 100% audit within one business day is mandatory; it is detective, and only partly corrective inside 24 hours | This *is* the permission. Broken preconditions mean FEP-1 is still required. ED-1 never retires the four-eyes *objective* |

Across all seven threats, timing decides the verb: a control that fires before release is preventive of the authorization decision; one that fires after release but before irrevocable settlement can still be preventive of *loss*; one that fires after settlement is detective; it becomes corrective only if recall or recovery still works.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package is judged against FEP-1’s *objective*, not against activity counts.

**Fully equivalent.** Before the payment becomes irrevocable, an independent human (not the preparer/releaser, not a shared account, not a conflicted supervisor) has authorized the transfer; the checks that fire actually cover the threat that is live on that case; the operator cannot self-override those checks; evidence is contemporaneous; and any remaining same-user capability sits inside a *pre-declared* ED-1 envelope whose seven conditions are all evidenced. Equivalence is about restored independence and coverage, not about two activity labels appearing.

**Partially equivalent.** Independence of authorization is largely restored before settlement, and the main automated checks that apply did run clean, but something material is missing or late: no declared emergency when one was required, manager ratification after release rather than approval before release, audit after the 24-hour recall clock, or an ED-1 precondition that is only probably true. Residual risk is reduced and often tolerable; the designed control path was not fully followed.

**Merely mitigating.** Expected loss or some scenarios are reduced (clean AV-2, small amount, later audit) but a capable single actor, a dependent approver, or an overrideable warning remains. The package changes the odds; it does not restore the objective.

**Ineffective.** Wrong time (after settlement or after recall), wrong person (same user, direct supervisor, borrowed credential), wrong coverage (AV-2 down, warning overridden, receipt never tested), or detective-only review of an unrecoverable payment.

Sufficiency dimensions (all required; any one can sink the package):

| Dimension | Equivalent | Partial | Mitigating / ineffective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Independent authorization before release, or at latest before irrevocable settlement | After release, before settlement, and still able to stop the bank | After settlement; after the ~24h recall window |
| **Independence** | Different human, L3+, no reporting-line or family conflict, no shared credential | Different named user, but pair-capture or workstation-sharing not ruled out | Same user; supervisor who rates the operator; operator using the manager’s override |
| **Coverage** | Checks map to the live threat (bank change, duplicate, limit, receipt, related party) | Strong on AV-2’s listed tests, silent on receipt/collusion | Clean pass treated as “everything is fine,” or service unavailable |
| **Overrideability** | Operator cannot clear a block/warning with their own or a borrowed credential | Warning exists but escalation is independent | Self-override, especially with a manager credential |
| **Recoverability** | Defect would still be stoppable, recallable, or recoverable | Borderline 24h / next-morning audit | Audit days later; funds already gone |
| **Evidence quality** | Token time, AV-2 result, manager workpapers, bank-ownership snapshot at release | Controls logged, substance of review inferred | Unsigned checklist, missing token timestamp, no bank-ownership evidence |
| **Collusion resistance** | A third independent party, or structural separation plus monitoring of pairs/workstations | Two-person form only | Captured pair, shared workstation, operator+supervisor loop |

“Same expected loss” is the wrong sole standard. Controls also exist to remove *capability*, to keep tail risk inside a designed envelope, to preserve attribution for investigation, and to remain defensible when the payment happens to be honest. A €900k single-operator release that later proves legitimate can have the same realized loss as a clean four-eyes payment and still be a failed objective: the firm accepted an unrecoverable exposure it could not evidence. Conversely, a €1,800 convenience same-user payment with an independent pre-settlement manager can have tiny expected loss and still be only *partially* equivalent, because the firm used neither FEP-1 nor ED-1 and learned the wrong lesson if it scores that as “fine.” Mean loss also ignores recoverability, evidence quality, and deterrence. Judge capability, timing, independence, and tail exposure first; use amount as a severity scaler, not as the classification rule.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Intended type | When it is actually preventive | When it is detective | When it is corrective | Typical failure mode in this log |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FEP-1** (distinct preparer/releaser) | Preventive of single-actor initiate+authorize | Two *independent* humans, before release | Never, by itself | Never | Shared workstation / captured pair (AP-204, AP-209); not applicable once the same user already released |
| **AV-2** | Preventive / blocking | Full pass or hard block *before* release (AP-203, AP-205, AP-207) | A warning that is only logged | Only if a block stops completion (AP-205) | Warning self-overridden (AP-202); service down (AP-208); blind to receipt and collusion (AP-204) |
| **MG-4** | Preventive of loss if before settlement; strongest before release | `Manager_Approve` before release (AP-203, AP-206). `Manager_Ratify` before settlement *if* it can still stop the bank (AP-201, AP-207) | Ratify after release when settlement cannot be stopped; any after-the-fact “I have seen it” | Only if the manager stops or recalls before irrevocability | Conflicted supervisor (AP-202); after settlement (AP-208); absent (AP-204, AP-209) |
| **RA-7** | Detective; conditionally corrective | Almost never (too late for the release decision) | Always, if substantive | Only inside recall/recovery (roughly 24 hours; AP-203/206/207 still had a window; AP-202/204/208 did not) | After recall (AP-202, AP-204, AP-208); treated as if it replaced prevention |
| **ED-1** | Conditional *permission* that *bundles* the above | Only when all seven conditions are true before the same-user release is allowed to stand | Mandatory next-day audit is the detective half of the bundle | Next-day audit + 24-hour recall is the corrective half | Used as a slogan while conditions fail (AP-208); token time vs. prepare time (AP-206); invoked when no token exists (AP-201) |

**Semantics for manager action after `Release_Payment` but before settlement.** It is **hybrid**, and the two halves must be named separately:

- Relative to the *release decision*, it is **detective** (and sometimes remedial). The system has already accepted a same-user authorization.
- Relative to the *protected asset* (irrevocable cash outflow), it is still **preventive** if — and only if — ratification is a live gate on `Bank_Settlement` or can trigger a stop/recall before the bank treats the payment as gone.
- After `Bank_Settlement`, the same activity is **detective only**. Exceptional legal recovery does not make it a preventive control for internal-control purposes.

That is why AP-207 can still support ED-1 (ratify at 14:40, settle at 16:00) while AP-208’s next-day ratification cannot, and why AP-201’s m04 review is a real mitigator rather than theatre — provided settlement at 10:00 was still stoppable. Calling every `Manager_Ratify` “a control occurred, therefore sufficient” erases this distinction.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Collect evidence that lets a reviewer apply the sufficiency test, not just count activities.

**Evidence to retain with the case file**

- Token identifier, *create* timestamp, and the prepare timestamp on the same clock, plus who requested it.
- AV-2 mode (pass / warn / block / unavailable), rule-level hits, and the identity that cleared any warning — never accept “SYSTEM passed” as a receipt test.
- Manager action type (`Approve` vs `Ratify`), independence attestation (grade, reporting line, related-party check), and workpapers covering invoice, bank details, and receipt.
- Bank-ownership snapshot *at release time*, especially after any 7-day change.
- Settlement time, recall deadline, audit start, and whether a stop/recall was still technically possible.
- Workstation / session identifiers for prepare and release; repeated-pair statistics.

**Monitoring rules (alert ≠ proof)**

| Signal | Why it matters | Treat as |
|---|---|---|
| Same-user prepare+release rate, especially without a live pre-prepare token | Direct FEP-1 / ED-1 gate | **Alert** for 100% review. Proof of misconduct only after intent and loss are shown (AP-201 is discipline; AP-202 is fraud) |
| Override rate, and overrides by the *preparer* or with a *manager* credential | Independence destruction (AP-202) | **Alert**, immediate access lock on shared override IDs. Credential sharing is a finding; related-party cash is the misconduct |
| Manager after release, and especially after settlement | Hybrid vs detective-only (AP-201/207 vs AP-202/208) | **Alert** if after settlement or after recall; **queue** if after release but before settlement |
| Audit not started before the recall window / next business day | RA-7 loses corrective half | **Alert** for emergency and high-value same-user cases; late audit is a control defect, not proof the payment was bad |
| Repeated resource pairs above a peer baseline; 30-day joint volume | Cell vs capture (AP-204/209) | **Alert** for rotation, independent sampling, and receipt-authenticity tests. Pairing is not proof of collusion; org design may intend a two-person cell |
| Shared workstation / overlapping sessions on prepare and release | T4 identity threat | **Alert** for session forensics. Shared hardware is a risk factor, not by itself theft |
| Bank-account change inside 7 days, especially with new vendor or AV-2 warn/block | T5 | **Hard-stop candidate**, not a dashboard curiosity. Combine with amount and vendor age |
| AV-2 outage or unsigned manual checklist | Preventive layer gone (AP-208) | **Hard-stop** on release above a low threshold until the service returns. Outage is an incident, not evidence that any one payment was stolen |

Investigate clusters (same-user + override + bank change + dependent manager) rather than single flags. An alert selects cases for judgment using the section 2 test. It does not classify OBJECTIVE_FAILED by itself.

**PM4Py behavior that cannot be guaranteed across versions.** (1) `filter_four_eyes_principle` may keep satisfying cases (current docs) or, in older helpers, the complement — the script detects and flips. (2) Cases missing one of the two activities are usually dropped by the filter, not labeled violate/satisfy; AP-205 must be handled separately via `Attempt_Release`. (3) `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=)` is not universal; some builds only look at `org:resource` after rename. (4) DataFrame versus EventLog input, and whether the filter is case-level when an activity is repeated, differ; this log has one prepare and at most one release per case, so that ambiguity does not arise here. (5) Blank resources become `SYSTEM` by rule; `SYSTEM` is not an independent human and must never be treated as a second pair of eyes.
