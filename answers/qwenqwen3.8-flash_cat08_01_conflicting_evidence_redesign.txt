# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The contradiction in the evidence is not a data problem; it is a **routing and segmentation problem**. The process has a very fast, cheap front-end path that is over-applied to cases where evidence completeness, device risk, claim value, or part-selection quality make that path unsafe. The slower reviewed/repair paths often produce better quality, but they are also too slow for Premium low-risk cases and too burdensome for unclear-evidence cases.

## Performance problems

- The **frequency directly-follows graph** shows the dominant route is:
  - `START -> Intake claim: 5000`
  - `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen: 3600`
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval: 2550`
  - `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement: 2300`
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close: 2320`

  This makes the auto-screen/fast-dispatch route the process’s main engine.

- The **performance directly-follows graph** shows why this path is attractive:
  - `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen`: mean 0.8h, median 0.5h, p90 1.8h
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: mean 1.0h, median 0.4h, p90 4.0h
  - `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`: mean 3.0h, median 1.8h, p90 8.0h
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`: mean 5.0h, median 3.2h, p90 14.0h

  The fast-path edge medians are short, and the fast-path edge p90s remain modest.

- The manual and evidence-loop paths are much slower:
  - `Intake claim -> Manual eligibility review`: mean 7.5h, median 5.0h, p90 18.0h
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`: mean 14.0h, median 10.0h, p90 36.0h
  - `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: mean 34.0h, median 26.0h, p90 88.0h
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: mean 22.0h, median 15.0h, p90 60.0h
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`: mean 96.0h, median 70.0h, p90 180.0h

  The average is not the problem; the **tail** is the problem. The process creates long-tail complaint and evidence loops that consume time, service credits, and reshipment capacity.

## Quality and rework problems

The **rework statistics** make the contradiction explicit:

- `Customer complaint/reopen` occurs in **9.2% of all cases**, but:
  - **14.1%** of cases using `Fast approval`
  - only **3.3%** of cases using `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`

- `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in **26%** of cases that enter it.

- `Rework repair` occurs in:
  - **12.5%** of repaired high-value devices
  - **4.0%** of repaired low-risk devices

- Fast approval cases are **31% cheaper before rework**, but only **9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits**.

This means the fast path is not simply “better” because it is frequent. It is a **local optimum**: it reduces first-touch cycle time and front-end labor, but it shifts cost and delay into complaints, reshipments, service credits, and reopen work.

## Compliance problems

The normative rules are being violated in structurally predictable ways:

- **R1**: A claim above EUR 400 must have either `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` before `Fast approval`.
- **R2**: Replacement dispatch must not occur before evidence completeness is confirmed, except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.
- **R3**: Any reopened claim must pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial.

The **token-based replay summary** shows:

- log fitness: **0.91**
- percentage of fitting traces: **68%**

This means the normative model can explain most tokens, but **almost one-third of traces violate the policy model**.

The **alignment diagnostics** identify frequent move-on-log deviations:

- `Fast approval` before evidence completeness
- `Customer complaint/reopen` after close
- `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices

The **low precision** of the discovered model adds another warning: many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy. This indicates uncontrolled variation, not a clean, stable process.

## Customer-experience problems

The **customer segment outcomes** show that speed is not universally good:

- **Premium low-risk accessory**
  - 18% of cases
  - fast path median: 8h
  - manual path median: 46h
  - complaint/reopen: 6% fast vs 3% manual
  - service-credit exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24h

  For this segment, the fast path is mostly justified. The manual path is too slow relative to service expectations.

- **Standard low-risk accessory**
  - 25% of cases
  - fast path median: 13h
  - manual path median: 52h
  - complaint/reopen: 8% fast vs 4% manual
  - customers tolerate 72-hour completion if status visibility is good

  For this segment, speed matters less than visibility and avoiding unnecessary loops.

- **Premium high-value device**
  - 14% of cases
  - fast path median: 20h
  - reviewed/repair path median: 96h
  - complaint/reopen: 22% fast vs 5% reviewed/repair
  - compliance risk is high if R1 or R2 is violated

  Here the fast path is dangerous: it saves 76h median but creates 17 percentage points more complaints and major compliance exposure.

- **Standard high-value device**
  - 21% of cases
  - fast path median: 28h
  - reviewed/repair path median: 120h
  - complaint/reopen: 18% fast vs 6% reviewed/repair
  - cost of wrong replacement is high

  The reviewed path is better on quality but too slow. The redesign must make the reviewed path faster, not simply force every high-value case into the current slow path.

- **Unclear defect / missing evidence**
  - 22% of cases
  - median cycle time: 140h
  - highest abandonment rate and repeated evidence requests

  This is not primarily a fast-versus-slow eligibility problem. It is an evidence-collection and customer-support problem.

## Resource and cost problems

The **resource and cost information** explains why simple speed/cost optimization fails:

- `Auto screen` is cheap: EUR 0.20 per case and effectively unlimited capacity, but rules are outdated and not segment-aware.
- `Manual eligibility review` has only 7 reviewers × 36 productive hours = 252 hours/week, and queue growth becomes sharp above 82% utilization.
- `Fraud/risk review` has only 2 specialists and bottlenecks on Mondays and after marketing campaigns.
- `Repair quote` delays are mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation.
- `Quality check` has 2 inspectors and is underused except after repair campaigns.
- `Escalation board` has one daily 45-minute slot and poor scalability.
- Wrong replacement expected cost is:
  - EUR 95 for low-risk accessories
  - EUR 410 for high-value devices

Thus, the process is not resource-constrained everywhere. It is constrained in **manual eligibility**, **fraud/risk**, **escalation**, and **part-price confirmation**, while **quality inspection** is underused.

## The central contradiction

The most frequent path is fast and cheap because it uses the auto screen and avoids manual review. But the same path produces many downstream complaints and compliance exceptions because it is applied to cases that should not be eligible for fast approval:

- high-value devices,
- claims above EUR 400 without manual or fraud review,
- claims with incomplete evidence,
- defect types where replacement part selection is error-prone.

The slower reviewed/repair paths often have better quality because they include manual eligibility, fraud/risk review, repair quotes, customer approval, and quality checks. But they are not automatically better:

- Premium low-risk cases suffer if forced into slow manual paths.
- Unclear-evidence cases suffer if forced into repeated manual/evidence loops.
- Standard high-value cases may need reviewed quality but cannot tolerate 120-hour medians.

The redesign must therefore **segment the contradiction**, not choose one global path.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-segmented eligibility triage with policy-aware auto-screen and parallel evidence

## Redesigned process flow

The redesigned process replaces the one-size-fits-all auto screen with a **segment-aware eligibility decision layer**. The normal path becomes:

1. **Intake claim**
   - Capture:
     - customer segment: Premium / Standard
     - device risk: low-risk accessory / mid-risk device / high-value device
     - defect type
     - claim value
     - serial/model information
     - evidence completeness status
     - fraud/risk signal
     - digital literacy or assisted-channel preference, if available

2. **Segment-aware auto eligibility screen**
   - Updated rules route cases into controlled lanes instead of sending most cases directly to fast approval.

3. **Lane-specific eligibility decision**

   **Lane A1: Premium low-risk accessory fast lane**
   - Eligible for fast approval and dispatch under the R2 exception.
   - Evidence may be self-certified or lightly validated.
   - A post-dispatch audit sample is used instead of blocking dispatch.
   - Target: preserve the 8-hour median fast path and avoid the 46-hour manual path.

   **Lane A2: Standard low-risk accessory evidence-light lane**
   - Auto approval only if a lightweight evidence checklist is satisfied.
   - If evidence is incomplete, route to targeted evidence completion, not directly into manual review loops.
   - Status visibility is mandatory because Standard low-risk customers tolerate 72-hour completion if they can see progress.

   **Lane A3: Mid/high-value or above-EUR-400 controlled review lane**
   - No direct `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`.
   - Route to `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` based on risk signals.
   - Evidence request and manual review run in parallel:
     - customer receives a targeted evidence request,
     - reviewer begins eligibility assessment on available data,
     - dispatch is held until evidence completeness and risk clearance are confirmed.
   - This satisfies R1 and reduces R2 violations.

   **Lane A4: Unclear defect / missing evidence lane**
   - Do not loop through generic `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`.
   - Route to evidence co-production and technical defect triage, which can be combined with Redesign B.

4. **Fast approval only if policy guardrails are satisfied**
   - For claims above EUR 400: require `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` before fast approval.
   - For replacement dispatch: require evidence completeness, except Premium low-risk accessory claims.

5. **Dispatch replacement or repair quote**
   - Low-risk accessories can still be dispatched quickly.
   - High-value devices receive a more controlled dispatch or repair path, but with parallel evidence and risk handling to reduce the current 96–120-hour reviewed-path medians.

## Which segments, paths, or activities it targets

This redesign targets:

- the dominant frequent path:
  - `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`
- the high-value fast-path problem:
  - Premium high-value: 22% complaint/reopen on fast path vs 5% on reviewed path
  - Standard high-value: 18% complaint/reopen on fast path vs 6% on reviewed path
- compliance deviations:
  - `Fast approval` before evidence completeness
  - `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices
  - R1 violations for claims above EUR 400
  - R2 violations for non-Premium-low-risk dispatches
- the outdated auto-screen rule set:
  - auto screen is cheap and unlimited but not segment-aware.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **selective fast-lane protection plus policy-aware blocking**.

- It preserves the genuinely good fast path for Premium low-risk accessories, where median fast cycle time is 8h and service-credit exposure is high beyond 24h.
- It prevents the fast path from being used where it creates expensive downstream failures, especially high-value devices where wrong replacement cost is EUR 410 and complaint rates are 18–22%.
- It uses the cheap auto screen as a router, not as an approver.
- It reduces the current slow reviewed path by running evidence collection and manual review in parallel, attacking the `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` mean 34h / median 26h / p90 88h loop.
- It directly addresses the cost contradiction: fast approval is 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after rework. By reducing high-value complaints and reshipments, the redesign aims to improve total cost after rework rather than only front-end cost.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some speed for high-value and above-EUR-400 cases. It accepts that these cases may no longer receive the current 20–28-hour fast median if they require manual or fraud review.

It leaves unresolved:

- the evidence portal and customer-assistance problem for unclear defect / missing evidence cases;
- the wrong replacement part-selection problem addressed by `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`;
- the repair quote delay caused by part-price confirmation;
- the reopen and escalation-board scalability problem;
- the risk that updated auto-screen rules increase manual-review inflow by 15–35% if not capacity-managed.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence co-production and abandonment-rescue loop

## Redesigned process flow

This redesign changes the evidence problem from an internal back-and-forth loop into a **customer-supported evidence completion service**.

1. **Intake claim with defect taxonomy**
   - At intake, the system asks for defect type and captures structured evidence requirements:
     - photos,
     - serial/model,
     - purchase proof,
     - error code,
     - accessory compatibility,
     - device firmware or configuration information,
     - proof of physical damage, if relevant.

2. **Segment-aware auto screen identifies an evidence gap**
   - If evidence is incomplete, the system does not simply route the case to `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`.
   - It creates a specific evidence task with:
     - exact missing item,
     - reason,
     - SLA,
     - customer channel preference,
     - assisted-channel option if digital literacy is low.

3. **Evidence completion service**
   - Customer receives a one-shot evidence request through portal, SMS, email, or assisted phone/retail channel.
   - The request is targeted, not generic.
   - Status visibility is provided:
     - “Evidence needed,”
     - “Evidence received,”
     - “Eligibility in progress,”
     - “Decision expected.”

4. **Parallel eligibility work**
   - Manual eligibility review or risk screening can begin on available data while evidence is being collected.
   - The case is marked as “conditionally eligible” or “hold for evidence,” but dispatch is not allowed unless R2 permits it.

5. **Abandonment rescue**
   - If the customer does not respond within the SLA:
     - send a reminder,
     - offer phone or retail assisted evidence,
     - simplify the evidence request,
     - or, for low-risk Premium accessory claims, consider conditional dispatch with post-audit.
   - Cases at risk of abandonment are flagged before they become `Claim abandoned`.

6. **Repeated evidence prevention**
   - If a second evidence request is needed, the case triggers:
     - defect-type root cause,
     - specialist technical triage,
     - or a simplified alternative evidence path.
   - The goal is to stop the current pattern where `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it.

## Which segments, paths, or activities it targets

This redesign targets:

- the `Request missing evidence` loop:
  - incoming events: 650 from auto screen + 780 from manual review = 1430 evidence-request events
  - outgoing events: 890 to manual review, 320 to abandonment, 220 to fraud/risk review
- the slow evidence path:
  - `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: mean 34h, median 26h, p90 88h
- the unclear defect / missing evidence segment:
  - 22% of cases
  - median cycle time: 140h
  - highest abandonment and repeated evidence requests
- compliance deviations:
  - `Fast approval` before evidence completeness
  - `Dispatch replacement` without evidence completeness for non-Premium-low-risk claims
- customer-experience problems:
  - abandonment,
  - repeated requests,
  - lack of status visibility,
  - Standard low-risk tolerance for 72-hour completion if visibility is good.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **evidence co-production instead of evidence chasing**.

- The new evidence portal is expected to reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%, according to the provided resource information.
- Targeted evidence tasks reduce the chance that a customer submits irrelevant evidence and then receives another request.
- Parallel eligibility work reduces the current long wait after evidence is requested.
- Assisted channels protect against the known risk that a digital-only portal may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy.
- Status visibility improves the Standard low-risk experience, where customers tolerate 72-hour completion if they can see progress.
- Conditional dispatch is allowed only where the normative rule permits it: Premium low-risk accessory claims.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some immediate dispatch speed for cases with incomplete evidence. It accepts that high-value and non-Premium-low-risk cases should wait until evidence completeness is confirmed.

It leaves unresolved:

- the high-value fast-approval compliance problem if auto-screen rules are still not segment-aware;
- the wrong replacement part-selection problem;
- the `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` delay caused by part-price confirmation;
- the reopen and escalation-board scalability problem;
- the risk that assisted evidence channels add cost and require customer-service capacity.

# 4. Redesign C: Pre-dispatch fulfillment quality gate and closed-loop reopen control

## Redesigned process flow

This redesign focuses on the **fulfillment and reopen tail**: cases where the process closes quickly but later generates complaints, reshipments, rework, or escalation.

1. **Split fulfillment into two networks**

   **Network C1: Low-risk accessory fast fulfillment**
   - Premium and Standard low-risk accessories with complete evidence can still be dispatched quickly.
   - Use post-dispatch sampling and defect-code monitoring instead of blocking every case.
   - This avoids adding 6–18 hours to cases where the wrong-replacement cost is EUR 95 and service-credit exposure is high for Premium cases.

   **Network C2: High-value and error-prone fulfillment**
   - High-value devices, mid-risk devices, and defect types with known part-selection risk pass through a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate.
   - The gate checks:
     - serial/model compatibility,
     - defect code to part mapping,
     - inventory confirmation,
     - replacement part selection,
     - evidence completeness,
     - whether R1/R2/R3 requirements are satisfied.

2. **Use underused quality inspectors**
   - The current process has 2 quality inspectors at EUR 38/hour who are underused except after repair campaigns.
   - These inspectors can staff the pre-dispatch gate and reopen triage, reallocating capacity from underused quality check into a control point that prevents expensive dispatch complaints.

3. **Parallelize repair quote and part-price confirmation**
   - The performance DFG shows `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` is mean 22h, median 15h, p90 60h.
   - The resource information says repair quote delay is mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation.
   - The redesign starts part-price confirmation in parallel with eligibility review or customer approval, not after the claim has entered a slow quote queue.

4. **Closed-loop reopen control**
   - Any `Customer complaint/reopen` must pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, satisfying R3.
   - Reopen cases are root-coded:
     - wrong part,
     - missing evidence,
     - eligibility dispute,
     - shipping issue,
     - repair rework,
     - compliance exception.
   - Only high-value, repeated, or compliance-critical reopen cases go to the escalation board.
   - Routine reopen cases route directly to manual review, repair, or replacement with an SLA.

5. **Exception-based escalation**
   - The escalation board has one daily 45-minute slot and poor scalability.
   - It should be reserved for:
     - Premium high-value service failures,
     - repeated complaints after close,
     - R1/R2/R3 exceptions,
     - wrong-part clusters,
     - cases with high service-credit exposure.
   - Other cases are resolved through reopen triage.

## Which segments, paths, or activities it targets

This redesign targets:

- the long-tail fulfillment problem:
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`: mean 96h, median 70h, p90 180h
- the alignment finding that `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` is linked to incorrect replacement part selection;
- high-value repair rework:
  - 12.5% of repaired high-value devices
- the high cost of wrong replacement:
  - EUR 410 for high-value devices
- the repair quote delay:
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: mean 22h, median 15h, p90 60h
- the reopen compliance problem:
  - R3 requires manual eligibility review before final close or denial
  - alignment diagnostics show `Customer complaint/reopen` after close as a frequent deviation
- the escalation-board scalability problem:
  - one daily 45-minute slot
- the underused quality-check resource:
  - 2 inspectors, EUR 38/hour, underused except after repair campaigns.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **moving quality control earlier and making reopen work compliant and exception-based**.

- The lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate can catch wrong replacement selection before shipment.
- For high-value devices, the expected wrong-replacement cost is EUR 410, so adding 6–18 hours may be justified if it prevents reshipments, service credits, and customer complaints.
- For low-risk accessories, the expected wrong-replacement cost is EUR 95, so a full gate may not be justified; sampling and post-audit are better.
- Parallel part-price confirmation attacks the repair quote delay without forcing all Premium cases into the current 96–120-hour reviewed path.
- Reopen triage reduces the current pattern where complaints enter manual review or escalation in an uncontrolled way.
- Reserving escalation for exceptions protects the one daily 45-minute slot and makes the board more effective for the cases where it has a strong customer-satisfaction effect.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some dispatch speed for high-value and error-prone cases. It accepts a 6–18-hour gate delay where the alternative is a 70–180-hour complaint/reopen tail.

It leaves unresolved:

- the root cause of inappropriate fast approvals if the auto-screen rules are still not segment-aware;
- the evidence collection loop for unclear defect / missing evidence cases;
- the customer abandonment problem caused by repeated evidence requests;
- the risk that the quality gate becomes a new bottleneck if applied too broadly;
- the need for manual-review capacity if reopened claims are forced through R3-compliant review.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A: Risk-segmented eligibility triage

### Failure mode 1: Manual and fraud capacity is overwhelmed

Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%. Manual eligibility has only 7 reviewers and 36 productive hours per reviewer per week. Queue growth becomes sharp when utilization exceeds 82%. Fraud/risk review has only 2 specialists and already bottlenecks on Mondays and after marketing campaigns.

If the redesign routes too many high-value or above-EUR-400 cases into manual or fraud review, the process may become slower and more expensive even if compliance improves.

### Failure mode 2: Misclassification creates new compliance exceptions

If the segment-aware rules incorrectly classify a high-value device or unclear defect as low-risk, the process may continue to produce:

- `Fast approval` before evidence completeness,
- `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices,
- R1 violations for claims above EUR 400.

Alternatively, if the rules are too conservative, low-risk Premium accessories may be sent into manual review, destroying the fast path and triggering service-credit exposure beyond 24 hours.

### Likely bottleneck migration

The bottleneck may move from the auto screen to:

- `Manual eligibility review`,
- `Fraud/risk review`,
- or an evidence-hold queue for high-value cases.

This is especially likely if the redesign does not include parallel evidence handling and capacity guardrails.

---

## Redesign B: Evidence co-production and abandonment rescue

### Failure mode 1: Digital portal increases abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers

The resource information warns that a new evidence portal may reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45% but may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy. If the redesign is digital-only, the current abandonment problem may worsen rather than improve.

The DFG already shows `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned: 320`, and the unclear defect segment has the highest abandonment rate. A poorly designed portal could shift abandonment from manual loops into digital non-response.

### Failure mode 2: Parallel review creates premature conditional approvals

If manual reviewers work in parallel with evidence collection but dispatch is allowed before evidence completeness is confirmed, the redesign can violate R2 for non-Premium-low-risk cases. If the system treats “evidence requested” as “evidence complete,” the process may still produce `Fast approval` before evidence completeness.

### Likely bottleneck migration

The bottleneck may move from manual eligibility loops to:

- customer response time,
- assisted evidence channels,
- or a queue of cases waiting for evidence completion.

If assisted channels are under-resourced, the process may simply replace internal rework with external customer delay.

---

## Redesign C: Pre-dispatch quality gate and reopen control

### Failure mode 1: The quality gate becomes too broad and slow

The pre-dispatch quality gate adds 6–18 hours depending on resource design. If applied to all cases, including Premium low-risk accessories, it may breach the 24-hour service-credit threshold and turn a fast, acceptable path into a slow, expensive one.

The gate is most defensible for high-value and error-prone defect types, where wrong replacement cost is EUR 410 and complaint rates are high. It is less defensible for low-risk accessories, where wrong replacement cost is EUR 95 and speed is more important.

### Failure mode 2: Reopen triage still bypasses required review

R3 requires reopened claims to pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial. If the redesign routes complaints directly to escalation, dispatch, or repair without compliant review, the process may reduce escalation load but create more R3 exceptions and complaint-after-close deviations.

If part-compatibility rules are stale, the gate may also create false confidence: inspectors approve a part selection that is technically outdated for new SKUs or firmware-dependent devices.

### Likely bottleneck migration

The bottleneck may move from dispatch complaints to:

- quality inspectors,
- part-price confirmation,
- or manual eligibility review for reopened claims.

If quality inspectors become saturated, the process may trade a long complaint tail for a new pre-dispatch delay tail.

---

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

## Stage 0: Shadow instrumentation and baseline cohort design

Before changing routing, instrument the process with the attributes needed to evaluate the redesigns:

- customer segment,
- device risk,
- claim value,
- defect type,
- evidence completeness status,
- serial/model and part compatibility,
- reopen reason,
- complaint-after-close flag,
- service-credit exposure,
- manual/fraud utilization,
- abandonment risk.

Run Redesign A, B, and C logic in shadow mode:

- What would segment-aware auto-screen rules do?
- Which cases would require evidence completion?
- Which high-value cases would fail R1/R2?
- Which dispatches would the pre-dispatch gate have caught?
- Which complaints would be routed to reopen triage instead of escalation?

### Metrics to monitor

- expected manual-review inflow change,
- expected fraud-review inflow change,
- evidence-gap types,
- would-be R1/R2/R3 exceptions,
- would-be part-compatibility mismatches,
- complaint root causes,
- abandonment risk flags.

### Stop/go thresholds

Go to active pilot only if:

- data quality is sufficient to tag the major segments and risk attributes;
- shadow rules produce actionable volume without overwhelming manual or fraud capacity;
- shadow gate logic identifies a material share of known dispatch complaints or high-value deviations;
- the organization can define a holdout/control cohort.

---

## Stage 1: First pilot — Redesign A in two narrow cohorts

The first active pilot should be **Redesign A**, because the auto-screen rule update can be deployed in about two weeks and directly addresses the central contradiction: the fast path is frequent and cheap but creates compliance and quality exceptions.

### First pilot design

Pilot two cohorts:

1. **Premium low-risk accessory fast lane**
   - Preserve the current fast path under the R2 exception.
   - Use post-dispatch sampling instead of blocking dispatch.
   - Compare against current fast path and current manual path.

2. **Standard high-value devices above EUR 400**
   - No direct auto-screen-to-fast approval.
   - Require manual or fraud review before fast approval.
   - Run evidence request and eligibility review in parallel where evidence is incomplete.
   - Compare against current fast path and current reviewed path.

Use a randomized or matched holdout cohort.

### Metrics to monitor

- segment-specific median and p90 cycle time,
- complaint/reopen rate,
- R1/R2 exception count,
- manual utilization,
- fraud queue p90,
- abandonment rate,
- reshipment rate,
- service-credit exposure,
- total cost after rework, including labor, replacement errors, and service credits.

### Stop/go thresholds

Expand Redesign A if:

- Standard high-value complaint/reopen rate falls materially, for example by 5–10 percentage points or at least half the gap toward the reviewed-path rate;
- R1/R2 exceptions in the pilot approach zero;
- total cost after rework improves or at least does not worsen;
- manual utilization remains below the known danger zone of 82%;
- fraud p90 does not increase more than roughly 20%;
- abandonment does not increase more than 1–2 percentage points versus control.

Stop or redesign if:

- manual queue grows sharply above 82% utilization;
- fraud review becomes a Monday/campaign bottleneck;
- Premium low-risk median cycle time rises above the 24-hour service-credit threshold;
- high-value complaint rate does not improve;
- abandonment increases more than 3 percentage points;
- total cost after rework worsens without a clear quality benefit.

---

## Stage 2: Pilot Redesign B for unclear defect and missing evidence cases

After Redesign A establishes segment-aware routing, pilot Redesign B on cases that would enter `Request missing evidence`.

### Pilot design

Target the **unclear defect / missing evidence** segment and cases routed from Redesign A’s evidence-light lanes.

Test three variants:

1. **Digital evidence portal only**
2. **Portal plus assisted phone/retail fallback**
3. **Control: current request-missing-evidence loop**

The goal is to determine whether the portal reduces repeated evidence requests without increasing abandonment.

### Metrics to monitor

- repeated evidence request rate,
- time from first evidence request to evidence receipt,
- `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` cycle time,
- abandonment rate,
- digital vs assisted channel completion rate,
- complaint/reopen rate after evidence completion,
- R2 violations related to dispatch before evidence completeness,
- customer effort or contact rate.

### Stop/go thresholds

Expand Redesign B if:

- repeated evidence requests fall by 20–45%, consistent with the portal’s expected range;
- evidence-loop p90 time falls materially;
- unclear-evidence median cycle time improves from the current 140h;
- abandonment does not increase more than 1–2 percentage points;
- assisted-channel completion is strong enough to protect low-digital-literacy customers.

Stop or modify if:

- abandonment increases more than 3 percentage points;
- digital-only evidence requests create longer waits than the current loop;
- customers receive multiple targeted requests despite the one-shot design;
- manual reviewers begin approving or dispatching before evidence completeness is confirmed.

---

## Stage 3: Pilot Redesign C for high-value fulfillment and reopen control

After Redesign A and B reduce inappropriate fast approvals and improve evidence quality, pilot Redesign C on high-value devices and reopened claims.

### Pilot design

Apply the pre-dispatch quality gate only to:

- high-value devices,
- defect types with known wrong-part risk,
- cases where serial/model compatibility is complex,
- reopened claims.

Do not apply the full gate to Premium low-risk accessories. Use sampling there instead.

Test two reopen variants:

1. **All reopened claims routed to manual eligibility review**
2. **Reopen triage with exception-based escalation**

### Metrics to monitor

- `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` rate,
- median and p90 complaint resolution time,
- wrong-part reshipment rate,
- high-value repair rework rate,
- complaint-after-close rate,
- escalation-board utilization,
- quality inspector utilization,
- gate-added cycle time,
- service-credit exposure,
- total cost after rework.

### Stop/go thresholds

Expand Redesign C if:

- dispatch complaint rate falls materially, especially for high-value devices;
- wrong-part reshipments or complaint root causes decline;
- high-value repair rework falls from the current 12.5%;
- escalation-board load decreases without increasing R3 exceptions;
- gate-added delay remains within the expected 6–18h range for high-value cases;
- quality inspector utilization remains below a safe operating threshold, for example below 80–85%.

Stop or narrow if:

- Premium low-risk service credits increase;
- gate p90 delay exceeds the current complaint tail benefit;
- quality inspectors become a new bottleneck;
- reopen triage bypasses manual eligibility review;
- part-compatibility rules produce false positives or false confidence.

---

## Stage 4: Integrated optimization and policy tuning

Once the individual pilots show which redesigns work, combine them:

- Redesign A decides **which cases may be fast**.
- Redesign B decides **how evidence is completed without abandonment**.
- Redesign C decides **which fulfillments must be gated and how reopens are controlled**.

The combined process should look like:

1. Intake with segment, risk, defect, value, and evidence attributes.
2. Segment-aware auto screen routes to fast lane, controlled review lane, or evidence lane.
3. Evidence co-production prevents repeated requests.
4. R1/R2 guardrails prevent noncompliant fast approval and dispatch.
5. Pre-dispatch gate protects high-value and error-prone fulfillment.
6. Reopen triage enforces R3 and reserves escalation for exceptions.
7. Close only after quality/compliance confirmation, with post-close complaint monitoring.

---

## Sequencing logic

The sequence is deliberately ordered from **policy guardrails** to **evidence redesign** to **fulfillment quality control**:

1. **Start with Redesign A** because the auto-screen rules are cheap to update and directly address the most frequent noncompliant path: fast approval without appropriate review or evidence completeness.
2. **Then pilot Redesign B** because evidence loops are a major source of delay and abandonment, but they are easier to manage once the eligibility router knows which cases truly need evidence.
3. **Then pilot Redesign C** because the pre-dispatch gate adds time and should be applied only after eligibility and evidence quality have improved; otherwise the gate may simply slow down cases that were misrouted earlier.
4. **Finally integrate** the three redesigns so that each control point handles a different failure mode:
   - A: wrong routing,
   - B: wrong evidence collection,
   - C: wrong fulfillment and reopen control.

---

## Learning whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

The main risk is that a redesign makes `Quality/compliance close` faster while moving failure into:

- complaints after close,
- reshipments,
- service credits,
- abandonment,
- escalation,
- or repair rework.

To prevent this, use the following measurement discipline:

1. **Track cases for 60–90 days after close**
   - Do not count a case as successful when it reaches `Quality/compliance close`.
   - Monitor whether `Customer complaint/reopen` appears after close, as flagged in the alignment diagnostics.

2. **Measure total cost after rework, not front-end cost**
   - The current fast path is 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after rework.
   - Pilot success should be judged on labor, wrong replacement cost, reshipment cost, service credits, escalation time, and abandonment cost.

3. **Use segment-specific medians and p90s**
   - Average cycle time can hide the problem.
   - Premium low-risk cases need median control because of the 24-hour service-credit threshold.
   - Standard low-risk cases need visibility and p90 control because customers tolerate 72 hours if informed.
   - High-value cases need complaint and compliance control because fast-path complaint rates are 18–22%.

4. **Compare against a holdout cohort**
   - The holdout should match segment, defect type, claim value, and evidence status.
   - Without a holdout, apparent improvement may be caused by case-mix changes.

5. **Monitor displaced bottlenecks**
   - If manual utilization rises above 82%, the improvement is being paid for with queue delay.
   - If fraud p90 rises after campaigns, risk review has become the new bottleneck.
   - If evidence abandonment rises, the process has shifted delay from internal rework to customer loss.
   - If quality inspector utilization rises, the pre-dispatch gate may have created a new fulfillment bottleneck.

6. **Re-run conformance diagnostics after each pilot**
   - Track whether the percentage of fitting traces improves from the current 68%.
   - Track whether frequent deviations decrease:
     - fast approval before evidence completeness,
     - dispatch without risk review for high-value devices,
     - complaint/reopen after close.
   - Track whether low-frequency non-policy paths decrease, addressing the low precision problem.

The redesigns should not be judged by whether they make the most frequent path faster. They should be judged by whether they make the **right cases fast**, the **wrong cases controlled**, and the **downstream failures visible before they become expensive**.
