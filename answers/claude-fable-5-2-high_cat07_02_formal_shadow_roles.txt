# 1. Proposed role structure

The structure below has ten roles in three layers: a routine core (automation, front office, temps, technical, quality), specialist and authority roles (commercial, security, supervisor), and bridging roles (senior agent/commercial shadow, escalation broker). Membership is "primary plus secondary", not a hard partition. All shares are computed from the activity-resource matrix (10,534 events in 10 weeks) and the complexity table.

# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

I treat an activity as core for a role when it takes a large share of the member's own work, or the member holds a large share of that activity, and the member has the authority to do it at the complexity it normally occurs. Everything else is peripheral.

**Automated intake (R14_Nora).** A1 and A2 are her only activities. She never touches a high or critical case. Her A2 is a pre-check: in fragment 2, R01/R02 validate again for cases that go to diagnosis. She has volume but no authority.

**Front-office service agents (R01_Amira, R02_Ben).**
- **Frequency:** A1, A2, A4 and A12 are core. A3 (75/60) is triage before handing to Technical, as fragment 2 shows.
- **Complexity:** Amira handles 58 high and 9 critical cases against Ben's 31 and 3, so she is the front office's internal escalation step.
- **Substitution:** A13 rework is peripheral by count but is a real duty, because fragment 6 routes the temps' reopenings to them.
- **Authority:** their A7 events (18/5) and A8 events (42/25) are done without a pricing certification. These are probably preparation or customer contact around a pricing case, not pricing decisions.

**Seasonal capacity (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).**
- **Frequency and complexity:** core work is the same four activities, limited to low-complexity cases.
- **Authority:** they perform 520 A2 events (23% of all A2) without the entitlement certification. Validation is therefore core by frequency but not by authority.
- **Quality signal:** the 17 reopenings traced to their A2 notes are consistent with that gap.
- **Substitution:** they are a load substitute for the front office, not a competence substitute.

**Senior agent / commercial shadow (R07_Giorgio).** He has two cores.
- **Front-office core:** 68% of his events.
- **Commercial core:** 20% of his events, but 31% of all A7 and 26% of all A8.
- **Substitution:** 41 of his 72 A7 events and 70 of his 88 A8 events fall outside Fatima's absence, so this is standing practice and not only cover. The absence weeks show a limit: with him as primary reviewer, median pricing duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes.
- **Authority:** he is pricing-certified, so the work is legitimate. His 20 A9 events are peripheral and unclear, since he holds no approval certification.

**Technical resolution (R03_Chen, R04_Dina).**
- **Frequency:** A3, A5 and A10 are core for both, with almost equal counts.
- **Complexity:** Chen's work is concentrated in high and critical cases (222 vs 91) and in security-adjacent cases.
- **Authority:** Chen's 24 A6 events come without a security certification, so they are peripheral. He works late shift while Erik works days, so some may be after-hours cover or technical input to Erik's review.
- **Other peripheral work:** their A14 events (14/11) feed the knowledge base owned by Quality.

**Commercial specialist (R06_Fatima).**
- **Frequency:** A7 and A8 are core.
- **Complexity:** half her cases are high or critical.
- **Authority:** she owns the pricing decision. Her 16 A9 events may be delegated commercial sign-offs or logging under the wrong label.
- **Substitution:** the absence episode shows that the organisation has one full-speed pricing reviewer.

**Security specialist (R05_Erik).**
- **Frequency:** A6 is core although it is only 96 events in ten weeks, about ten a week.
- **Complexity:** 83% of his cases are high or critical.
- **Authority:** he holds the only security certification.
- **Peripheral work:** A10, A3 and A5 are follow-through on security cases, not general technical capacity.
- **Substitution:** nobody substitutes for him. In weeks 7–8 Mateo filtered his inflow, which is demand shaping and not a transfer of the review itself.

**Quality and knowledge gatekeeping (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).** A11 and A14 are core. Their A5 and A10 events (about 60 each) are most plausibly small corrections made at the gate. Their authority is the release decision. If they fix too much themselves, they end up checking their own work.

**Supervisory approval (R08_Hana).**
- **Frequency:** A9 is core.
- **Peripheral work:** A13 (24) and A8 (22) are escalation handling on difficult cases.
- **Authority:** her role is defined by decision right, not by volume. Mateo's week-5 approvals were preliminary, and she rechecked 13 of 21 (62%).

**Escalation broker (R13_Mateo).**
- **Frequency:** no activity exceeds 12.5% of his events, so frequency cannot define a core.
- **Function:** his core is routing between specialists, visible in fragments 3 and 5, and ownership of reopenings, where he holds the largest share of A13.
- **Complexity:** he touches more critical cases than anyone else.
- **Authority:** he can route and pre-approve, but his approvals and security filtering do not carry Hana's or Erik's decision rights.
- **Substitution:** he is the default stand-in for almost everyone, which is the main reason his profile is flat.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

These roles are useful to the organisation and are not signs of improper work. Each has benefits and risks.

**R07_Giorgio: commercial shadow.**
- **Why shadow:** his formal title is Senior Service Agent in Front Office, yet he does nearly a third of pricing reviews and matches Fatima's negotiation volume (88 vs 90). Fragment 4 shows him negotiating after Fatima's pricing review.
- **Benefits:** he is certified, and he gives the commercial function its only backup. Complex front-office cases with a pricing element can stay with one person instead of being handed over.
- **Risks:** as primary reviewer he was about twice as slow (38 to 74 minutes). The slowdown may also reflect his double load or harder cases, so it is not proof of lower skill. When he covers Fatima, the front office loses its most senior agent. If one person reviews the price and negotiates on the same case, segregation of duties weakens.

**R13_Mateo: cross-functional escalation broker.**
- **Why bridging:** he connects the front office, Technical, Risk, Commercial and Management in fragments 3, 5 and 6, and touches all 14 activities.
- **Benefits:** one owner for reopened and critical cases. His security filtering in weeks 7–8 reduced false-escalation complaints and protected Erik's time.
- **Risks:** two critical cases waited more than two days during the same filtering, so the filter can delay the cases that matter most. His preliminary approvals had a 62% recheck rate, so they added a step for most cases. He is on 76 critical cases, which makes him a bottleneck and a single point of failure for coordination.

**R05_Erik: rare but critical specialist.**
- **Why special:** he accounts for 1.6% of events but holds two thirds of security reviews and has the highest complexity profile.
- **Benefits:** the security decision is concentrated in one qualified person, which gives consistency.
- **Risks:** he has no certified backup. The other 50 A6 events are logged by Chen (24), Mateo (16), Dina (6) and Ivan (4), none of whom is security-certified. He works days, while Chen's late-shift A6 events suggest some security work happens when he is not there. This should be checked against timestamps.

**R14_Nora: automation boundary role.**
- **Benefits:** she absorbs 46% of registrations and 27% of validations and works around the clock.
- **Risks:** her pre-check can be mistaken for a completed validation. In fragment 1 the Nora-validated path continues directly to a standard response, sometimes by a temp, so no certified person validates those cases.

**R11_Kai and R12_Lina: temporary-workforce role.**
- **Benefits:** they give elastic capacity for simple requests and free certified agents for medium cases.
- **Risks:** the cost of their errors appears on other people. They log zero A13 events, while the reopenings are reworked by R01, R02 and R13. Lina works the late shift, where the only other front-office resource is the bot.

**R09_Ivan and R10_Julia: quality gatekeeping role.**
- **Benefits:** they are an independent last check before closure and they own the knowledge base.
- **Risks:** their A5/A10 fixes blur independence, and the gate did not catch the incomplete entitlement notes behind the 17 reopenings. Standard-response cases in fragments 1 and 6 do not pass through Quality Check at all.

**R03_Chen: possible informal security bridge.** He has 24 A6 events and more security-adjacent cases than Dina. This may be useful technical input to Erik, or uncertified cover on the late shift. The data cannot tell which.

Two general ambiguities also affect the reading:

- **The "Coordinate" step.** The fragments show a coordination step by Mateo that is not among A1–A14. He has 415 matrix events against 429 cases, and Hana has 245 against 274. Fewer events than cases suggests that part of their work is logged outside the matrix or not logged, or that the two tables are counted differently. Either way, the activity matrix understates both roles.
- **Shift cover.** All authority holders (Erik, Fatima, Hana, Mateo, Giorgio) work days, while Chen, Ivan and Lina work late. Some shadow work may therefore be shift cover and not a role choice. Only timestamps can separate the two.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The code is illustrative. File names, the complexity thresholds, the certification-to-activity mapping and the `start_timestamp` column are assumptions to confirm with the process owner.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
KW = dict(activity_key=ACT, resource_key=RES, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)

## ---- 1. Load and format --------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution.csv")
raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS], utc=True)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)

df["temporary_substitution"] = (
    df["temporary_substitution"].astype(str).str.lower().isin(["true", "1", "yes"])
)
df["case_complexity"] = df["case_complexity"].astype(float)
df["complexity_band"] = pd.cut(
    df["case_complexity"],
    bins=[-np.inf, 0.30, 0.55, 0.80, np.inf],          # assumed thresholds
    labels=["low", "medium", "high", "critical"],
)

## ---- 2. Stratified views, so one mixed log does not drive the result -----
df_regular = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "temporary_substitution", [True], level="event", retain=False
)
df_humans = df_regular[df_regular[RES] != "R14_Nora"]
df_complex = df[df["complexity_band"].isin(["high", "critical"])]
df_entsec = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "product_line", ["Enterprise Security"], level="case", retain=True
)

## ---- 3. Organizational roles (activity groups with resource weights) -----
def mined_roles(log, view):
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log, **KW)
    rows = [
        {"view": view, "mined_role": i,
         "role_activities": ", ".join(sorted(r.activities)),
         RES: res, "events": n}
        for i, r in enumerate(roles)
        for res, n in r.originator_importance.items()
    ]
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    out["share_of_resource"] = out["events"] / out.groupby(RES)["events"].transform("sum")
    out["share_of_role"] = out["events"] / out.groupby("mined_role")["events"].transform("sum")
    return out

roles_all = mined_roles(df, "all_events")
roles_regular = mined_roles(df_regular, "no_substitution")
roles_humans = mined_roles(df_humans, "no_substitution_no_bot")
roles_complex = mined_roles(df_complex, "high_and_critical_only")

## A resource appears in every mined role where it has at least one event.
## Keep the whole membership vector; do not reduce it to the largest count.
primary = (roles_regular.sort_values("share_of_resource", ascending=False)
           .drop_duplicates(RES)
           [[RES, "mined_role", "role_activities", "share_of_resource"]]
           .rename(columns={"share_of_resource": "primary_share"}))
material = (roles_regular[roles_regular["share_of_resource"] >= 0.10]
            .groupby(RES)["mined_role"].nunique()
            .rename("n_material_roles").reset_index())
## Membership that exists only because of substitution weeks:
subst_only = (roles_all.merge(roles_regular, on=[RES, "role_activities"],
                              how="left", suffixes=("", "_regular"))
              .query("events_regular.isna()", engine="python"))

## ---- 4. Activity-based similarity, then the same with complexity ---------
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df_regular, **KW)
sim_df = pd.DataFrame(
    [(a, b, w) for (a, b), w in sim.connections.items()],
    columns=["res_a", "res_b", "activity_similarity"],
)

def cosine_matrix(tab):
    m = tab.div(tab.sum(axis=1), axis=0).to_numpy(dtype=float)
    m = m / np.linalg.norm(m, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return (pd.DataFrame(m @ m.T, index=tab.index, columns=tab.index)
            .rename_axis(index="res_a", columns="res_b"))

cos_activity = cosine_matrix(pd.crosstab(df[RES], df[ACT]))
cos_activity_cx = cosine_matrix(pd.crosstab(df[RES], [df[ACT], df["complexity_band"]]))
## Pairs that look alike on activities but not on activity x complexity
## (expected: Chen-Dina, permanent agents vs temps).
similarity_gap = (cos_activity - cos_activity_cx).stack().sort_values(ascending=False)

## ---- 5. Handover, working-together and subcontracting networks -----------
hw = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df, beta=0, resource_key=RES,
                                             timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
wt = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df, resource_key=RES,
                                             timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
sub = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df, n=2, resource_key=RES,
                                            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
## pm4py.view_sna(hw)   # visual inspection

def to_graph(sna, directed):
    g = nx.DiGraph() if directed else nx.Graph()
    g.add_weighted_edges_from(
        (a, b, w) for (a, b), w in sna.connections.items() if a != b and w > 0
    )
    for _, _, d in g.edges(data=True):
        d["dist"] = 1.0 / d["weight"]
    return g

g_hw, g_wt, g_sub = to_graph(hw, True), to_graph(wt, False), to_graph(sub, True)
network = pd.DataFrame({
    "hw_betweenness": nx.betweenness_centrality(g_hw, weight="dist"),
    "hw_sources": dict(g_hw.in_degree()),
    "hw_targets": dict(g_hw.out_degree()),
    "wt_partners": dict(g_wt.degree()),
    "work_returned_to": dict(g_sub.in_degree(weight="weight")),
}).rename_axis(RES).reset_index()
## Brokers (R13) show high betweenness and many partners with a flat activity
## profile; rare specialists (R05) show few events but sit on critical paths.

## ---- 6. Join mined roles with complexity and substitution ----------------
res_case = df.drop_duplicates([RES, CASE])
complexity = (res_case.groupby(RES)
              .agg(cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
                   median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"))
              .reset_index())
band_share = (pd.crosstab(res_case[RES], res_case["complexity_band"], normalize="index")
              .add_prefix("share_").reset_index())
workload = (df.groupby(RES)
            .agg(events=(ACT, "size"),
                 n_activities=(ACT, "nunique"),
                 substitution_share=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
                 mean_escalation=("escalation_level", "mean"))
            .reset_index())
p = pd.crosstab(df[RES], df[ACT], normalize="index")
entropy = (-(p * np.log(p.where(p > 0, 1.0))).sum(axis=1)
           .rename("activity_entropy").reset_index())

profile = (primary.merge(material, on=RES, how="left")
           .merge(workload, on=RES).merge(entropy, on=RES)
           .merge(complexity, on=RES).merge(band_share, on=RES)
           .merge(network, on=RES, how="left"))

## Which activities depend on one person (finds Erik on A6, Hana on A9)?
q = pd.crosstab(df[ACT], df[RES], normalize="index")
concentration = pd.DataFrame({"top_resource": q.idxmax(axis=1), "top_share": q.max(axis=1)})

## Substitution detail: who did what as a substitute, at which complexity.
substitution_detail = (df[df["temporary_substitution"]]
                       .groupby([RES, ACT])
                       .agg(events=(ACT, "size"),
                            median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"))
                       .reset_index())

## ---- 7. Authority comes from outside the log -----------------------------
hr = pd.read_csv("resource_master.csv")   # org:resource, formal_title, contract, certifications
REQUIRED_CERT = {                          # assumed mapping, to be confirmed
    "Validate Entitlement": "entitlement",
    "Configure Solution": "configuration",
    "Security Review": "security",
    "Pricing Exception Review": "pricing",
    "Supervisor Approval": "approval",
    "Quality Check": "quality",
}
auth = df.merge(hr[[RES, "certifications", "contract"]], on=RES, how="left")
auth["required_cert"] = auth[ACT].map(REQUIRED_CERT)
auth["has_cert"] = [
    pd.isna(req) or req in str(certs)
    for req, certs in zip(auth["required_cert"], auth["certifications"])
]
uncertified_execution = (auth[~auth["has_cert"]]
                         .groupby([RES, ACT, "temporary_substitution"])
                         .size().rename("events").reset_index())

## Approvals later repeated by the formal authority (the week-5 pattern).
a9 = df[df[ACT] == "Supervisor Approval"].sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
a9["next_approver"] = a9.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift(-1)
rechecked = (a9[(a9[RES] != "R08_Hana") & (a9["next_approver"] == "R08_Hana")]
             .groupby(RES).size() / a9[a9[RES] != "R08_Hana"].groupby(RES).size())

## Reopen rate by the resource that performed the last entitlement validation.
reopened_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, ACT, ["Reopen Request"], level="case", retain=True)[CASE].unique()
a2 = df[df[ACT] == "Validate Entitlement"].drop_duplicates(CASE, keep="last")
reopen_rate = (a2.assign(reopened=a2[CASE].isin(reopened_cases))
               .groupby([RES, "complexity_band"], observed=True)["reopened"].mean())

## Performance of substitutes, only if start timestamps exist.
if "start_timestamp" in df.columns:
    df["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"], utc=True)
    service = pm4py.get_service_time(df, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
                                     aggregation_measure="median", activity_key=ACT,
                                     timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
```

How the output should be read so that frequency clusters are not mistaken for authority:

- **A mined role is a group of activities.** `discover_organizational_roles` gives each resource a weight in every group where it has events. Mateo will appear in all of them and Giorgio in at least two. Report the membership vector and the flags, not one label per person.
- **Run several views and compare.** A role that only appears in the all-events view is a substitution artefact. A resource whose role changes in the high-and-critical view, as Chen and Giorgio may, has a complexity-dependent role.
- **Similarity is a hypothesis about interchangeability.** Check it against complexity bands, certification, contract and outcomes (recheck rate, reopen rate, duration) before treating two people as substitutes.
- **Authority is a separate column.** It comes from the HR and certification master data, not from the log. The `uncertified_execution`, `rechecked` and `reopen_rate` tables show where behaviour and authority differ.
- **Low volume is not noise.** Do not filter resources by event count before role discovery; `concentration` and `flag_rare_critical` exist to keep Erik and Hana visible.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing**
- Plan front-office capacity in three tiers that are not interchangeable: the bot for intake, temps for low-complexity standard responses, and certified agents for validation, medium cases and rework.
- Count the temps' rework when judging their contribution. The 17 reopenings landed on Amira, Ben and Mateo.

**Training**
- Give the temps structured entitlement training and a checklist for validation notes, or restrict A2 on non-trivial cases to certified agents.
- Develop Dina on high-complexity and security-adjacent cases by pairing her with Chen.

**Risk control**

- Give the security triage written criteria, a rule that critical cases bypass the filter, and a time limit, so that fewer false escalations do not come at the price of delayed critical cases.

**Resilience**
- Three activities depend on one person each: Erik for A6 (66%), Hana for A9 (63%) and Fatima for A7 (52%, with one slower backup). Mateo is the single coordinator for critical and reopened cases.
- For each, name a deputy, define what the deputy may decide alone, and track recheck rate, duration and reopenings during cover periods.
- Ivan and Julia are a good model. They are a true pair with the same profile, similar complexity and complementary shifts.

**Cautions about changing staffing from mined roles alone**
1. **Similar profiles do not mean interchangeable people.** Kai and Lina look like Amira and Ben, and Dina looks like Chen, but certification, complexity and error exposure differ. Replacing the senior member with the cheaper one would move risk to reopenings and critical cases.
2. **A substitution episode is not proof of permanent competence.** Giorgio's cover doubled pricing duration and Mateo's approvals were mostly rechecked. Do not formalise either as a full replacement without outcome data and training.
3. **Low frequency does not mean low need.** Erik and Hana have the smallest workloads and the highest-complexity cases. Sizing them by event count would remove the organisation's main risk controls.
4. **A flat profile is not an inefficient generalist.** Assigning Mateo to one cluster, or removing the role because it has no dominant activity, would remove the link that carries reopened and critical cases between teams.
5. **The log is incomplete and covers only ten weeks.** It includes a seasonal peak, two absences and an incident surge, and the coordination work is not in the activity matrix. Validate the structure with the process owner and a longer period before changing headcount, titles or authorisations.
