## 2. Counterfactual continuations

**Rank 1 — D composite.**  
`Post-evidence Analyst Review (R17) ∥ alternate screen (15–30 min) → S4 Second Officer Review (start 35–65 min) → {Approve → Payment Executed | Reject | Hold + SAR}`

- Screen clears the name and a refreshed score falls below 70: the Rule 6 trigger for a mandatory second review drops. Auto-clear still does not open, because that path requires no alert and a score below 40. With €480,000 and a sanctioned-border corridor, S4 should still review if arrival is inside this window. Analyst-only approval becomes the conformant continuation only after both triggers are actually clear and S4 cannot start in time.
- Screen clears the name but the score stays ≥ 70, or the match stays unresolved at any score: Second Officer Review remains mandatory. S4 approves, rejects, or holds.
- Screen strengthens the match: S4 should lean to Reject or Hold + SAR. Payment Executed is not a continuation of this branch.
- Approve and the fast tail (S4 near 35 minutes, review-plus-recording near 40 minutes): execution can still make 90 minutes. Illustrative arithmetic only: if arrival is uniform on 35–65 and review-plus-recording is uniform on 40–90, the chance of finishing by 90 minutes is about 0.08. The usual continuation misses the deadline and then executes, rejects, or holds lawfully.
- Hold + SAR: no execution, and the report is not disclosed to the customer. Filing is not an approval.
- Main uncertainties: S4’s place inside 35–65 minutes; whether the screen moves a stale score of 72; transport of the 0.55–0.75 approval estimate once the screen is in the file.

**Rank 2 — C, only if S2 reviews independently.**  
`Interrupt P-901 → S2 Second Officer Review (35–75 min) → record (5–15 min) → {Approve → Payment Executed | Reject | Hold + SAR}`  
P-901’s breach probability rises 0.12–0.28 for a 45-minute interrupt.

- Approve inside 40–90 minutes: P-884 can make the deadline; P-901 carries the spillover even if P-884 is later rejected.
- Reject or SAR hold: P-884 still misses the operational upside, and P-901 has already paid the interrupt. A SAR hold does not authorize a later quiet execution.
- If the alternate screen is in flight, S2 signs after that result when it will arrive before an otherwise ready decision. Starting S2 at minute 0 and signing before a 15–30 minute screen wastes the information the second review is meant to use.
- Main uncertainties: the spillover figure is tied to 45 minutes and may not hold for a 75-minute review; interruption can worsen S2’s error rate on both files; the observational approval range need not survive a pressured review.

**Rank 3 — E.**  
`Evidence Requested → (6–24 h) → Evidence Received → Evidence Validated → Analyst Review → Second Officer Review if score ≥ 70 or the match is still unresolved → {Approve → Payment Executed | Reject | Hold + SAR}`

The 90-minute deadline is missed on essentially every branch. Customer documents may fail to resolve a sanctions-name mismatch. A later second review is still required while either trigger remains. The customer message stays factual and contains no SAR content. Main uncertainty: whether more customer evidence can move a list-match question at all.

**Rank 4 — F, only if the written rejection threshold is met.**  
`Documented reject (10–20 min) → case closed, no Payment Executed`

- Threshold truly met: conformant stop. Customer harm is the false-reject portion of €15,000–€80,000. With observational approval odds of 0.55–0.75, a reject entered before S4 or S2 looks at the file will often be that false reject.
- Threshold not met: F is not a valid terminal state. Continuation returns to the Rank 1 plan.
- Rule 6 names Second Officer Review before Approve or Payment Executed. It does not, by its own words, put it before Reject. If local policy still defines the second officer as part of the required review for a sanctions reject, F waits for S2 or S4.
- Main uncertainty: the prompt does not establish the threshold. A 0.46 unresolved match plus a score of 72 is the reason a second review exists, not by itself a demonstrated reject rule.

**Rank 5 — G, exception currently disabled.**

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The regulatory interval and the deadline interval do not overlap. Unlawful execution is €100,000–€500,000 plus license, correspondent, and possible criminal harm. Executing inside the deadline is worth €20,000–€35,000, and only if the payment is one that a conformant review would approve. Observational approval odds are 0.55–0.75, so the expected SLA stake of a fast lawful approval is about €11,000–€26,000, not €35,000 for sure. A material probability that the unresolved 0.46 match and a score of 72 reflect a real sanctions problem is enough for expected regulatory harm to exceed the entire SLA benefit. Non-monetary harm sits outside that euro comparison and makes the asymmetry larger.

Customer harm from a false rejection or a long hold is €15,000–€80,000. That band can exceed the SLA benefit, so a pure “reject to be safe” or “hold overnight” choice is expensive. It does not reach the regulatory band attached to an unlawful clear. The designed response at this state is an independent second review, which keeps both the approve branch and the reject or SAR branch.

D versus C is a trade inside the conformant set, not a compliance trade. D usually misses the deadline: under a uniform illustration (S4 arrival on 35–65 minutes, review-plus-recording on 40–90 minutes), roughly 0.08 of paths finish by 90 minutes. D buys a reviewer who sees the new screen, and it buys a zero interrupt of P-901. C can finish in 40–90 minutes and so can capture most of the €11,000–€26,000 expected SLA stake, at a spillover of +0.12–0.28 breach probability on P-901. That spillover is smaller than P-884’s SLA stake when P-901’s loss-if-breach is about €20,000–€35,000 (expected spillover roughly €2,000–€10,000), and it matches or exceeds P-884’s stake when P-901’s loss-if-breach is about €100,000 or more. P-901’s loss is unknown, its deadline is tighter, and the 0.12–0.28 figure is for 45 minutes, not for a review that runs to 75. Those three facts keep C behind D on current information.

E and F both give up a timely second review that is still staffable. E is clearly allowed and keeps the approval option, at a near-certain €20,000–€35,000 loss plus hold harm. F stops sanctions exposure in 10–20 minutes, but the threshold is unproven and the approval range says a pre-review reject is more often false than true. G’s upside exists only on a branch the facts do not support; its downside is a lost window.

The SLA-breach probability 0.48–0.64 and the approval probability 0.55–0.75 are observational under ordinary processing. They are not the probabilities under an S2 interrupt, a new screen, or a director chase. The screen should move the approval probability, in a direction that depends on the result. A pressured review can move it without adding information.

## 4. Conformance constraints

- **Rule 3 (auto-clear).** Auto-clear to Payment Executed requires no alert and a risk score below 40. P-884 has an unresolved fuzzy match and a score of 72. Amount, corridor, and deadline do not reopen auto-clear.
- **Rule 4 and Rule 5.** This file is past the auto-clear gate, so analyst review is mandatory. The prefix stops at Evidence Validated, so the cycle returns to Analyst Review before a final decision. R17’s packaging step in the Rank 1 plan is that return. Evidence may be requested again (E); that is allowed and slow.
- **Rule 6.** Score ≥ 70 or any unresolved fuzzy match requires Second Officer Review before Approve or Payment Executed. Both triggers are on. A new screen, a customer document, or a SAR does not replace that review while either trigger remains. If a refreshed score is clearly below 70 and the match is affirmatively resolved, the Rule 6 duty drops; auto-clear still does not open.
- **Rule 7.** The second officer must be a different person from R17. S2 and S4 qualify. R17 does not.
- **Rule 8 and Rule 11.** After the required review, the case may be Approved, Rejected, or held while a SAR is filed. A SAR does not authorize execution and must not be disclosed to the customer.
- **Rule 9.** Payment Executed is conformant only on a valid approval path: analyst path when Rule 6 is off, second-officer approval when Rule 6 is on, or a Rule 10 override after both enabling facts exist.
- **Rule 10.** Override needs a Compliance Director’s authorization and a valid incident identifier. Neither exists. Predicted SLA loss and a €480,000 amount are not an incident identifier.

**A** skips the second officer while the score is 72 and the fuzzy match is unresolved, then executes. That breaches Rules 6 and 9, and it is outside Rule 3. A forecast that deadline loss is likely, or that approval odds look favorable, leaves those missing controls missing. The control is a constraint on the action set. The euro comparison points the same way: the regulatory range sits above the SLA range.

**B** lets the first analyst produce the second signature. That breaches Rule 7, so the signature is not a Rule 6 review, and execution after it breaches Rule 9. Speed and a high observational approval probability do not create a second person. Independence is the control. A same-person signature also leaves a file that looks reviewed.

**C** is conformant when S2 is not R17, reviews the evidence independently, and execution waits for S2’s approval. A countersignature of R17’s conclusion fails the stated condition and falls back to a nonconformant file.

**D’s screen** is an extra check the model does not forbid. It is not a second officer. The composite is conformant because S4’s independent review, and execution only after approval, are part of the plan.

**F** becomes valid if the institution’s documented rejection threshold is met on this file and an authorized reviewer records it. What is required is that written threshold applied to these facts: unresolved fuzzy confidence 0.46, post-evidence score 72 (12 minutes stale), sanctioned-border destination, €480,000, evidence already validated. The case statement does not provide that threshold, so F stays conditional. If policy requires second-officer concurrence for sanctions rejections, F also waits for S2 or S4. A preference to avoid customer harm, or to avoid the deadline, is not that evidence.

**G** becomes valid only when two records exist: the Compliance Director’s authorization for this case, and a genuine incident identifier opened for a real incident. Both are required. A deadline, an unreachable director, or an identifier drafted to justify the deadline leaves the exception disabled.

## 5. Resource spillovers

S2 is free only by taking them off P-901. P-901 is a separate high-value payment with 70 minutes left. A 45-minute interrupt raises P-901’s breach probability by 0.12–0.28. A review that runs 75 minutes, or a context switch that degrades S2’s work on both files, can land above that band. S4 arrives in 35–65 minutes and leaves P-901’s officer in place. On current information the Rank 1 plan therefore waits for S4.

The alternate vendor has three expedited calls left. One call is warranted: the mandatory second review is being driven by an unresolved 0.46 name match on a €480,000 sanctioned-border payment, and the result will be back before S4 starts. Two calls remain. A second call on P-884 is justified only if the first result is inconclusive and a different data source would add an independent signal.

G on the critical path is a spend of 20–60 minutes with a high chance of zero authority. That interval overlaps both S4’s arrival and the time in which C could still finish. Director delay is not a partial credit toward Rule 10.

**If S4 is confirmed at 35 minutes.** Rank 1 is stronger. The screen (15–30 minutes) is in hand as S4 starts. Earliest lawful decision is about 75 minutes, inside the deadline only if the review itself is short. C’s spillover is not bought for that thin extra chance. S2 stays on P-901.

**If S4 is confirmed at 65 minutes.** D cannot make 90 minutes: earliest finish is about 105 minutes even with a 40-minute review. C, started now, still fits in 40–90 minutes. C moves to Rank 1 and the S4 plan moves to Rank 2 when three conditions hold: S2’s review remains a real independent review; P-901 is paused with its state saved; the operation accepts +0.12–0.28, knowing a long review may cost more. If P-901 is of equal or higher sanctions sensitivity, or its loss-if-breach makes 0.12–0.28 times that loss clearly larger than about €11,000–€26,000, D stays Rank 1 and P-884’s SLA value is the accepted cost.

If the screen and a refreshed score clear both Rule 6 triggers before either officer is committed, rerank at once. Second Officer Review is then no longer mandatory. Auto-clear remains closed unless the score is below 40 and no alert remains. For a near-70 score on this corridor, a waiting S4 is still the better conformant review while the clock allows it.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** Run the Rank 1 composite now. R17 finishes the post-evidence Analyst Review and packages the file. Send one expedited alternate sanctions screen in parallel. Do not interrupt P-901. When S4 is free, S4, who is not R17, performs an independent Second Officer Review on the validated evidence and the screen result. Approve and execute only on S4’s recorded approval; otherwise Reject or hold for a SAR, with no customer disclosure of any report. Accept that, under the stated time ranges, this path usually misses the 90-minute deadline. The €20,000–€35,000 SLA value is smaller than the regulatory asymmetry, and it is not worth a default interrupt of P-901 while S4 is expected inside the hour and the screen will be ready before that review.

**Fallback:** Switch to C if, within about 10 minutes, S4’s ETA is confirmed near 65 minutes and P-901’s incremental expected breach loss is clearly below P-884’s €11,000–€26,000 expected SLA stake. S2 must review independently and should take the screen result before sign-off when that result is imminent. If no second officer can review and the written rejection threshold is confirmed on this file, use F. If the officer cannot decide without customer documents, use E. A, B, and an unenabled G stay off the path.

**Confidence:** Medium. The ranking is stable on conformance and on the regulatory asymmetry. It is sensitive to S4’s ETA, to P-901’s unknown loss-if-breach, and to a risk score that is already 12 minutes old. The approval and SLA figures may not transport once the screen or an interrupt is applied.

**Rerank immediately on any of these:** a confirmed S4 ETA near 35 or near 65 minutes; the alternate-screen result; a refreshed risk score; resolution or strengthening of the fuzzy match; S2 becoming free because P-901 is completed or already breached; written confirmation that the rejection threshold is or is not met; or both Rule 10 artifacts actually existing.
