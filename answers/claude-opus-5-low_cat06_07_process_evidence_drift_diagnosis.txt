## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March — stable baseline.** AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier 0.130–0.132, ECE 0.022–0.025, mean predicted risk tracks observed breach rate within 0.01. Variant mix is steady at roughly 45/35/20. The parts edge sits at 28–35 h. Per-variant breach rates are flat (V1 ~0.16, V2 ~0.10, V3 ~0.31). This is the reference regime against which everything later is measured, and it establishes that a V3-heavy mix alone should raise the aggregate breach rate mechanically — V3 was always the risky variant.

**April — a schema and pipeline incident, not a drift event.** Every symptom is a data symptom. The activity rename broke prefix construction at the anchor event, so predictions were made from wrong or absent prefixes; the queue feature went missing for 14% of cases; 18% of cases fell into "unmapped/other" because their control flow no longer matched a known variant. Discrimination collapsed to 0.67 and calibration to ECE 0.110, and mean predicted risk inflated to 0.28 against an unchanged observed rate of 0.19. The decisive evidence that the *world* did not change is that matured per-variant breach rates in April (0.18 / 0.13 / 0.34) are within noise of March. The process was fine; the observation of the process was broken. Treating April as drift would have triggered a retrain on corrupted features — the single most expensive available mistake.

**May–June — partial recovery plus a genuine but benign routing change.** The 6 May patch restored the label mapping and backfilled most April records; unknown activity fell to 2.0%, unmapped variants to 1%, AUROC to 0.78. But recovery is incomplete: ECE is 0.050 in May and 0.060 in June against a 0.024 baseline, consistent with the un-backfilled cached "events before diagnosis" feature and with duplicate removal having been applied only to the new source after 20 April. Overlaid on this, the 1 June remote-routing policy moved V2 from 35% to 52% and V1 from 45% to 30%. This is real process drift in the control-flow sense, and it lowers the aggregate breach rate to 0.15 exactly as a mix shift toward the safest variant should. Crucially, the per-variant rates in June are at baseline and the diagnosis→`Remote Fix` edge is unchanged at ~4 h: nothing about the underlying outcome mechanism moved. June is a mix change that needs stratified verification and possibly a prior adjustment, not a retrain.

**July — the leading edge of a real performance shift.** The parts edge jumps from 42 h to 65 h following the 10 July supplier issue. V3's matured breach rate rises from 0.35 to 0.46 while V1 and V2 stay flat. Aggregate metrics barely move (AUROC 0.76) because V3 is still only 22% of volume and label maturity is still 98%. July is the month where an operations-side monitor on the performance DFG would have fired well before any predictive monitor did.

**August–September — three genuinely different things happening at once.** The parts edge reaches 130 h then 148 h; V3's matured breach rate reaches 0.64 then 0.61 — roughly double baseline for the same path. Simultaneously the variant mix tilts hard toward V3 (35%, then 42%). Then on 1 September Partner Z arrives at 24% of volume, routing 58% of its cases through V3, with rural travel distances and device ages the model has never seen as features. So the aggregate deterioration (AUROC 0.68 → 0.66, ECE 0.120 → 0.140, observed breach 0.28 → 0.31 against predicted ~0.20) decomposes into: a conditional outcome change inside V3 (concept drift, or omitted supplier covariate), a mix shift toward V3 (covariate/process drift), and a new subpopulation (population shift) — with 7% and 18% of labels respectively still immature, biasing even these numbers. The three have different remedies. Recalibration fixes the first partially; nothing needs fixing for the second beyond monitoring; the third needs new features. Collapsing them into "the model degraded in Q3" would produce a retrain that learns Partner Z's transient early mix as if it were permanent.

**October — mostly a measurement artifact.** Only 55% of labels are mature and median delay has stretched to 52 days, with the backlog biting hardest on long paths. The observed breach rate of 0.16 is not a recovery; it is the fast subset of a censored cohort. Note the internal contradiction that proves it: V3 is at 41% of volume and the parts edge is at its worst-ever 155 h, conditions under which the true breach rate must be at or above September's 0.31. October's AUROC of 0.64 and Brier of 0.205 are computed on a non-random subsample and are not comparable to mature months in either direction. October should be reported as "not assessable" and excluded from training and from any recalibration fit.

**Why these must not be merged.** April is a pipeline bug with fabricated drift signals. June is a real process change with no outcome change. August–September is a real outcome change compounded by real mix and population changes. October is largely unmeasured. A single "model has drifted since April" story would prescribe retraining on April's corrupted features, on June's benign mix shift, and on October's censored labels — three separate ways to make the model worse.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each start-month cohort, plot the fraction of labels resolved as a function of days since case start, stratified by variant and by source, and identify the horizon at which each cohort reaches ≥95%. Then recompute all monthly metrics at a *fixed vintage* — e.g. exactly 60 days after case start, using only the labels available at that point for every month including January — so that all months are measured under the same censoring regime. *Under the censoring hypothesis:* October's fixed-vintage breach rate rises substantially from 0.16 toward or above 0.31, and the January–March fixed-vintage metrics shift only trivially, confirming maturity as the driver. *Under a genuine-improvement hypothesis:* October's rate stays near 0.16 at fixed vintage and the maturity curve for October matches earlier months' shape, which would mean something really did improve — implausible given the 155 h parts edge, but this is the test that would falsify the censoring story.

**Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning.** Rebuild April–June from raw events with `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` mapped to `Initial Diagnosis Completed` before any prefix construction, with duplicate removal applied uniformly across both sources and across the whole period, and with the cached prefix-count feature recomputed rather than read from cache. Re-score April with the *original* model on the repaired features. *Under the pipeline-failure hypothesis:* April's AUROC recovers to roughly 0.80, ECE falls below 0.03, unknown-activity share drops to baseline, unmapped variants vanish, and mean predicted risk returns to ~0.19 — i.e. April was never a drift month. *Under an April-concept-drift hypothesis:* April's metrics stay degraded even on clean features, and April's per-variant matured breach rates would have to differ from March — which the given data already contradicts, making this hypothesis weak in advance.

**Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.** Compute AUROC, Brier, ECE, reliability curves, and mean-predicted-versus-observed within each (month × variant) and, from September, each (month × variant × source) cell, on matured labels only, with cell counts reported. *Under the mix-shift-only hypothesis:* within-cell calibration and discrimination stay at baseline in every cell, and the aggregate decline is fully reproduced by reweighting baseline within-cell rates to the new mix — this is what should hold for June. *Under the concept-drift hypothesis:* the V3 cells show systematic under-prediction from July onward with a widening predicted-minus-observed gap, while V1 and V2 cells stay calibrated — this is what should hold for August–September. The formal version is a decomposition of the aggregate breach-rate change into a mix component (Σ Δwᵥ · r̄ᵥ) and a within-variant component (Σ w̄ᵥ · Δrᵥ); June should be almost entirely mix, August–September substantially within-variant.

**Comparison of process variants and DFG / performance-DFG edges.** Discover the DFG and performance DFG per month and diff them: new or vanished edges, changed edge frequencies, changed median and p90 edge durations. Track `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Part Received -> Dispatch Technician`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`, and the remote branch separately. *Under the supplier-driven hypothesis:* the deterioration localizes almost entirely to `Order Part -> Part Received` with downstream edges roughly stable, and the V3 breach rise is quantitatively consistent with the added lead time relative to the promise horizon. *Under a broader-capacity hypothesis:* technician-side edges (dispatch → repair) also stretch from July, implicating field capacity rather than parts, which would change the remediation from supplier management to staffing.

**Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed.** Restrict to V3 cases only, and further match on observable pre-diagnosis covariates and on the realized `Order Part -> Part Received` duration; then compare P(breach) across months. *Under irreducible concept drift:* breach probability rises across months even after conditioning on the same path and the same parts lead time — the relationship between what the model can see and the outcome genuinely changed. *Under an omitted-covariate (pseudo-concept-drift) hypothesis:* once lead time is conditioned on, the month effect largely disappears — the "drift" was an unobserved input, and adding a lead-time feature restores stability without relearning the outcome function. A complementary check: fit the same model family on Jan–Jun V3 and on Jul–Sep V3 and compare coefficient/SHAP structure; stable structure with a shifted intercept favors recalibration, restructured effects favor retraining.

**Population reweighting / transport checks for Partner Z.** Reweight September–October non-Partner-Z cases to match Partner Z's covariate and variant distribution (propensity or density-ratio weights on the shared feature space), and compare predicted-versus-observed on matured labels between reweighted non-Z and actual Z. *Under a pure population-shift hypothesis:* after reweighting, Partner Z's residual error is indistinguishable from the reweighted comparison group — the model is not treating Partner Z differently, it is simply seeing a harder mix. *Under a genuine subgroup-failure hypothesis:* a residual gap survives reweighting, which localizes the problem to unmodeled Partner-Z-specific factors (travel distance, device age, rurality) and justifies feature extension or a segment model. Run this only on matured labels, and check the maturity profile of Z versus non-Z first — if Z's labels mature more slowly, the naive 0.45 versus 0.27 comparison is not like-for-like and must be redone at fixed vintage.

**Feature missingness and lineage audits.** Per month and per feature, report missingness rate, distributional summaries, and the provenance of each value (source system, transformation version, cache hit versus recompute, backfill timestamp). Specifically diff cached versus freshly recomputed values for "number of events before diagnosis" across April–June. *Under the residual-pipeline-bug hypothesis:* a nonzero fraction of April–June rows show cached-versus-recomputed mismatch, concentrated before 20 April for duplicate-affected records, and correcting them removes the May–June ECE excess. *Under a clean-pipeline hypothesis:* cached and recomputed agree everywhere, and the May–June calibration excess must instead be attributed to the June mix change — testable by checking whether the excess is concentrated in the reweighted V2 population.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0 — immediate containment (days 0–3).** Suppress October from all dashboards and replace it with an explicit "insufficient label maturity (55%)" state; do not report October AUROC, Brier, or breach rate as comparable figures. Re-publish August and September as fixed-vintage estimates with maturity-adjusted intervals. Flag V3 predictions as known-under-confident and, if the system drives dispatch prioritization, apply a temporary conservative uplift to V3 risk scores (a documented, reversible post-hoc adjustment, not a model change) so that operational triage is not systematically under-escalating parts-dependent cases. Notify operations of the `Order Part -> Part Received` regression with the edge-duration series — that is an actionable business finding independent of the model.

**Stage 1 — short-term recalibration (weeks 1–3).** Fit a variant-conditional recalibration map on matured labels from July through September only, using isotonic regression or Platt scaling per variant, with V3 fitted separately. Do not touch the ranking function: discrimination within V1 and V2 is intact, and the dominant defect in V3 is a level/calibration error (predicted ~0.20 against observed ~0.62). Validate the map on a held-out fixed-vintage slice and monitor its stability weekly — if the required V3 correction keeps growing, the supplier situation is still evolving and a static map will lag.

**Stage 2 — feature repair (weeks 2–6).** This is where the real fix lives, and it should precede retraining. Rebuild the pipeline so activity-label normalization happens before feature extraction, duplicate handling is uniform across sources and history, and no derived feature is served from a cache that can survive a schema change. Then add the features the current model is missing: part lead time and supplier identity (or a live parts-delay indicator available at diagnosis time), and Partner Z's travel distance, device age, and rurality. Adding parts lead time is likely to convert most of the apparent August–September concept drift into explicable covariate signal; adding the Partner Z features addresses population shift at its source. Recompute the full historical feature set under the repaired lineage.

**Stage 3 — full retraining (weeks 6–10, once labels mature).** Retrain only after the repaired features exist and the training window has matured.

*Month safety for training:*
- **January–March: safe.** Clean pipeline, mature labels, stable regime. Use as the stability anchor.
- **April: unsafe until recomputed; usable afterward.** Features are corrupted at source. After full recomputation from raw events with normalization, uniform duplicate removal, and cache invalidation, April becomes usable — but only after a verification pass shows April's recomputed metrics matching the March baseline. Never train on April as currently stored.
- **May: usable with caution.** Backfill was incomplete; verify the cached prefix-count feature before inclusion.
- **June–July: safe for labels, verify features.** Labels are 98–100% mature. The June mix shift is real signal that the model *should* learn, not contamination.
- **August: usable now (93% mature), preferred after full maturity.** Include with maturity weighting.
- **September: borderline (82%).** Include only at fixed vintage or after waiting for maturity; Partner Z's early months may also reflect onboarding transients rather than steady state.
- **October: unsafe.** 55% maturity with censoring correlated with the outcome and with path length. Training on it would teach the model that long parts-dependent paths do not breach — the exact inversion of the truth. Revisit no earlier than 60–90 days after month end.

*Segment- or variant-specific model?* A separate V3 model is **justified in the medium term**, conditionally. The case for it: V3 is now 41% of volume (up from 20%), its outcome mechanism is driven by a factor — parts lead time — that is irrelevant to V1 and V2, and its breach rate has moved to a different regime while the others stayed flat. A shared model must dilute a V3-specific supplier effect across a population where it does not apply. The case against: a single model with the parts lead-time feature and adequate interaction capacity may capture the same structure with more data and less maintenance burden; and V3's regime is currently non-stationary, so a V3 model fitted now would encode a supplier crisis that may resolve. **Recommendation:** first add the features and retrain a single model with variant-aware capacity; measure whether within-V3 calibration and discrimination reach baseline. Split into a V3-specific model only if a documented gap survives that test. A *Partner-Z-specific* model is not justified — Partner Z's difference appears to run through variant mix and unmodeled covariates, both of which are better handled by features than by segmentation, and a source-specific model on three months of onboarding data risks encoding transients and creating a fairness-relevant divergence that is harder to audit than a shared model with source-stratified monitoring.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Layered monitors.**

*Predictive monitor.* AUROC, AUPRC, and Brier per month, computed only on cohorts meeting a maturity threshold, and always reported alongside that cohort's maturity. Stratified by variant and source, never aggregate-only.

*Calibration monitor.* ECE, reliability curves, and mean-predicted-versus-observed, per variant and per source. Separate the *level* error (intercept shift → recalibration) from the *shape* error (rank degradation → retraining candidate); these are different alerts with different owners.

*Label-maturity monitor.* Maturity curve per start-month cohort, median and p90 label delay, and a maturity-weighted confidence band on every downstream metric. This monitor is a **gate**, not just a signal: metrics computed on cohorts below the maturity threshold are published as "provisional / not comparable" and are excluded from any automated decision.

*Data-quality monitor.* Unknown-activity share, unmapped-variant share, per-feature missingness, schema-hash of the source activity vocabulary, cache-versus-recompute agreement rate, and duplicate rate per source. A schema-hash change fires immediately and independently of any model metric — this is the monitor that would have caught 1 April on day one rather than at month end.

*Process-variant monitor.* Variant shares per month with a distributional distance from the reference window, plus detection of newly appearing or vanishing variants. Explicitly labeled as a *mix* monitor whose default interpretation is "verify stratified performance," not "degradation."

*Performance-DFG monitor.* Median and p90 duration per DFG edge against a reference window, with `Order Part -> Part Received` as a named business-critical edge routed to operations as well as to the ML team. This monitor is leading: it fired in July, a month before predictive metrics moved.

*Population monitor.* Source shares, covariate marginals, and density-ratio / propensity-based distance between the current and training populations, with per-source stratified performance reporting from a source's first day of volume.

*Intervention and deployment monitor.* A registry of known interventions — policy changes, partner onboardings, supplier incidents, pipeline deploys, model releases — timestamped and joined onto every alert, so that any alert is presented with the interventions active in its window. Also tracks whether the model's own predictions drive actions that alter the outcome (feedback loop).

**Alert dependency graph.** Alerts are evaluated in a fixed precedence order, and a higher-precedence firing *suppresses or downgrades* the lower ones rather than letting them fire independently:

1. **Data-quality / schema alert** fires → all predictive, calibration, and variant alerts for the affected window are downgraded to "unverified — pending recomputation." No retraining or recalibration may be triggered while this is open. *(April.)*
2. **Label-maturity alert** fires (maturity below threshold) → predictive and calibration alerts for that cohort are suppressed entirely and marked "not assessable." A label-delay alarm routes to data engineering and to the outcome-API owner; it **never** routes to the retraining queue. *(October.)*
3. **Variant-mix / population alert** fires with **stable within-stratum performance** → classified as "benign mix change," logged, prior/base-rate adjustment considered, no model action. *(June.)*
4. **Within-stratum calibration alert** fires on mature labels with clean data quality → recalibration workflow.
5. **Within-stratum discrimination alert** fires on mature labels with clean data quality, and persists across two consecutive mature cohorts → retraining candidate, with the intervention registry attached so a human decides whether the cause is an omitted feature (fix features first) or genuine relearning.

The essential rule: **retraining can only be triggered by a within-stratum, mature-label, clean-lineage, persistent degradation.** Everything else escalates to a human with a specific non-retraining hypothesis attached.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------- load & normalize ----------
df = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")

# The 2026-04-01 migration renamed the anchor activity. Normalize BEFORE any
# feature/prefix extraction, otherwise prefixes are built at the wrong event.
ACTIVITY_ALIASES = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
}
df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(ACTIVITY_ALIASES)

# UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is kept, not dropped: its share is itself a quality metric.
df["is_unknown"] = df["activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")

df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], utc=True)

# ---------- duplicate handling ----------
# Duplicate removal was historically applied only to the new source after
# 20 Apr. Apply one uniform rule to ALL sources and ALL history, and record
# how much it removes per month so the correction is auditable.
key = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
dup_mask = df.duplicated(subset=key, keep="first")
dup_report = (
    df.assign(month=df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str), dup=dup_mask)
      .groupby(["month", "source"], dropna=False)["dup"].mean()
      .rename("duplicate_rate").reset_index()
)
df = df.loc[~dup_mask].copy()

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

# ---------- two cohort definitions ----------
# Event-calendar cohort: events that OCCURRED in the month. Reflects what the
# process was doing that month; cases may span month boundaries.
# Case-start cohort: all events of cases STARTED in the month. This is the
# cohort predictions and labels are attached to, and the only one for which
# label maturity is well defined.
log["month_event"] = log["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
case_start = log.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
log["month_case_start"] = (
    log["case:concept:name"].map(case_start).dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
)

months = sorted(log["month_case_start"].unique())

summaries = []
for m in months:
    start, end = pd.Period(m).start_time, pd.Period(m).end_time

    # event-calendar slice: what happened inside the month window
    cal = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        log, str(start), str(end), mode="events"
    )
    # case-start slice: full traces of cases opened this month
    coh = log[log["month_case_start"] == m]

    dfg_cal, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cal)
    perf_cal, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cal)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(coh)

    n_cases = coh["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    var_share = {
        " -> ".join(v) if isinstance(v, tuple) else str(v): len(t) / max(n_cases, 1)
        for v, t in sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: -len(kv[1]))[:10]
    }

    parts_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
    summaries.append({
        "month": m,
        "cases_started": n_cases,
        "events_in_calendar_month": len(cal),
        "unknown_activity_share": coh["is_unknown"].mean(),
        # median seconds on the supplier-critical edge, calendar-month view
        "order_to_part_received_perf": perf_cal.get(parts_edge),
        "order_to_part_received_freq": dfg_cal.get(parts_edge, 0),
        "n_distinct_variants": len(variants),
        "top_variant_shares": var_share,
    })

process_summary = pd.DataFrame(summaries)

# ---------- temporal features, case-start cohorts ----------
# Descriptive throughput/timing features only. No model is fitted here; this
# script produces auditable evidence, and drift is judged from the month-over-
# month movement of these summaries plus the label-maturity table below.
# (PM4Py has no built-in concept-drift detector -- none is invoked.)
temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
temporal["month_case_start"] = (
    temporal.index.to_series().map(case_start).dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    if temporal.index.name == "case:concept:name" else None
)

# ---------- label maturity, reported next to every metric ----------
if {"label_resolved_at", "breach_label"}.issubset(df.columns):
    lab = df.drop_duplicates("case_id")[
        ["case_id", "label_resolved_at", "breach_label"]
    ].copy()
    lab["label_resolved_at"] = pd.to_datetime(lab["label_resolved_at"], utc=True)
    lab["month"] = lab["case_id"].map(case_start).dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    lab["delay_d"] = (
        lab["label_resolved_at"] - lab["case_id"].map(case_start)
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    maturity = lab.groupby("month").agg(
        maturity=("breach_label", lambda s: s.notna().mean()),
        median_label_delay_d=("delay_d", "median"),
        observed_breach_rate=("breach_label", "mean"),
    ).reset_index()
    process_summary = process_summary.merge(maturity, on="month", how="left")
    # Months below the maturity gate are provisional and must not be compared
    # to mature months or used for training.
    process_summary["comparable"] = process_summary["maturity"].ge(0.95)

print(dup_report)
print(process_summary)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**1. Retraining on immature labels (October).** If October is fed into training or recalibration, the model learns from a cohort in which resolved cases are systematically the fast ones and the censoring is correlated with both path length and the outcome. The model would learn that V3, parts-dependent, long-path, Partner Z cases *rarely* breach — the exact opposite of the truth, and precisely the cases where accurate escalation matters most. The damage compounds: the recalibrated model under-escalates, breaches rise, and the next cycle sees the degradation as further drift. *Mitigation:* a hard maturity gate (≥95%, or fixed-vintage evaluation) enforced in the training pipeline, not just in dashboards; label-maturity alerts routed exclusively to data engineering with no path to the retraining queue; every training run records the maturity of each month it consumed.

**2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April, and residually May–June).** April's inflated mean predicted risk (0.28 against 0.19 observed) is a tempting recalibration target. Fitting a corrective map to it would bake a compensation for broken prefix construction into the production scorer — and when the mapping patch landed on 6 May, that compensation would have become an active source of error in the opposite direction, invisible because the "fix" was already deployed. The same risk applies more subtly to the un-backfilled cache in May–June. *Mitigation:* data-quality and schema alerts hold absolute precedence and block all model-adjustment workflows for the affected window; require a clean recomputation-from-raw before any month is eligible for calibration fitting; always check whether a candidate recalibration month coincides with a deploy in the intervention registry.

**3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift by explaining it away as mix.** Because V3's *share* also rose in August–September, it is easy to attribute the whole aggregate deterioration to a mix shift and conclude no model action is needed. But V3's *within-variant* breach rate doubled from 0.32 to 0.64 — that is not mix. Dismissing it leaves the model under-predicting the highest-risk population by a factor of three during a live supplier crisis, causing systematic under-escalation exactly when proactive intervention has the most value, and leaving the operational root cause unaddressed. *Mitigation:* mandatory mix-versus-within-stratum decomposition on every aggregate metric movement, so a mix explanation must be quantitatively sufficient before it is accepted; a performance-DFG monitor on business-critical edges that routes to operations independently of the ML pipeline, so the supplier issue is escalated on its own merits (it was visible in July, a month before predictive metrics moved).

**4. Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence.** The headline 0.45 versus 0.27 breach gap invites a conclusion that the model discriminates against Partner Z. But Partner Z routes 58% of cases through V3 versus 36% for others, V3 was always the risky variant, and Partner Z's labels mature more slowly — so the comparison is not like-for-like on either variant mix or censoring. Acting on the unfairness reading could produce an unwarranted Partner-Z-specific model fitted on three months of onboarding transients, or a blanket risk adjustment that mis-serves both groups, while the actual fixable issue — missing travel-distance and device-age features — goes untouched. *Mitigation:* require matured-label, fixed-vintage, variant-stratified, reweighted comparisons before any fairness claim; run the transport-style check that asks whether reweighted non-Z cases show the same residual error; treat a surviving residual as a *feature-coverage* finding first and a fairness finding second, and state explicitly which it is.

**5. Treating June's routing change as model degradation.** The 1 June policy moved V2 from 35% to 52% and dropped the aggregate breach rate to 0.15 while every per-variant rate stayed at baseline. Reading the accompanying small metric movement as drift would trigger a retrain on a population whose composition is deliberately and permanently different — wasting a retrain cycle, resetting the model's stability baseline for no reason, and consuming the credibility needed for the genuine August intervention. *Mitigation:* classify mix-only changes as a distinct benign alert class whose default action is "verify stratified performance and consider a base-rate adjustment," with escalation to retraining only if within-stratum metrics also move.

**6. Merging all four episodes into one "model has drifted" narrative.** This is the meta-failure that produces all the others simultaneously: a single retrain spanning April (corrupted features), June (benign mix), August–September (real but partly feature-fixable drift), and October (censored labels) would learn corrupted inputs, an unnecessary adjustment, an under-specified drift, and an inverted risk relationship in one pass — and would almost certainly perform worse than the incumbent, while destroying the evidence trail needed to diagnose why. *Mitigation:* require every drift finding to name a month range, a mechanism, a falsifying test, and a confidence level before it can justify action; maintain the intervention registry so alerts arrive with their candidate causes attached; and stage remediation (contain → recalibrate → repair features → retrain) so that the cheapest reversible action is always tried and measured first.
