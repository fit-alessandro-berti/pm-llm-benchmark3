### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

**Measure.** Total-variation (TV) distance between two monthly relation-rate vectors, using the 7 supplied normalized relations (occurrences per 100 cases):

  TV(a,b) = ½ ·  |a  b| / 100

**Assumptions about omitted edges.** (i) The fragment is *not* a complete DFG: edges such as `RegisterCollect Documents` (skip), `ApproveClose`, `RejectClose`, `Open CaseRegister`, `Request InfoAssess`, and the `AssessApprove` / `AssessReject` split are omitted. Treated as **masked (uninformative), not zero**, so the numbers are **lower bounds** on true DFG distance and remain comparable only if the omitted-edge set is stable across months — an untested assumption, since the March migration could have changed which edges are emitted at all. (ii) `RegisterEligibility` is pinned to 100 by construction and dominates any cosine/L2 measure (cosine similarity stays 0.986–0.997 for all months and is therefore *not* informative here — reported only to show that measure choice matters). (iii) The decision edges are aggregated, so a change in the approve/reject mix is invisible.

Interpretation: March is a **1.5 pp** structural change on this measure ( noise) even though raw fitness fell 38 pp — the DFG shape did not move, the vocabulary did. May–July is a **large, monotone behavioural** shift (18.5  9.5 per step). September is a **partial reversal** (distance to baseline falls 29.5  14.0), and October is the **largest single jump** (18.0 in one month). The distance profile is therefore **non-monotone**, which alone refutes a single global permanent change point.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Method (additive, with explicit assumptions; implemented in Part A code):

- **A1 — complexity multiplier:** a high-complexity case takes 1.6× a standard case. Implied baseline class medians: _std = 31.15 / (0.795 + 0.205·1.6) = **27.7 h**, _high = **44.4 h**. Mix effect = (w_high,month · _high + (1w_high,month) · _std)  31.15.
- **A2 — service:** total active service  40% of baseline cycle (12.5 h); `Assess`  40% of service (5.0 h); measured Assess service +12.5% (4.8  5.4 h)  service effect  **+0.6 h** (all other activities held constant).
- **A3 — rework loop:** one extra `Request Info  Collect Documents  Assess` loop adds **12–16 h** (40–50% of a baseline cycle). Effect on the *mean* = rework-rate × loop increment; effect on the *median* damped by ~0.7–1.0× (only part of the population shift crosses the median quantile).
- **A4 — queueing residual:** cycle  mix  service  rework. This residual is an **upper bound on pure staffing effect**: it also contains demand growth (+29% cases), rework-induced load on the same assessors, and priority displacement.

### 3.5 One global change point or several?

**Several mechanism-specific change points.** The evidence is incompatible with a single global step:

1. The mapped fitness series is flat Jan–Apr (0.90–0.93) then steps down in May (0.88) and stays in a 0.86–0.89 band for the rest of the year — a *new* change point at May, distinct from the March logging step.
2. The DFG distance profile is **non-monotone** (1.5  20  29.5  14  32  18). A single permanent step would give a monotone or flat distance; here distance rises, partially reverses in September, rises again in October, and falls again in December.
3. Different artifacts have different onsets: labels/duplicates (1 Mar, sudden), rework rate (5 May, sudden), capacity (15 Jun, gradual-then-step), routing (1 Jul, sudden but aggregate-invisible), population (1 Oct, seasonal).
4. The December partial return to 0.90 (not 0.93) shows part of the post-May gap is *reversible* and part is *persistent* — two different mechanisms inside one window.

Statistical caveat: with 12 monthly observations, any change-point method has very low power and cannot localise events *inside* a month; the July-1 priority rule and the June-15 attrition are invisible at monthly resolution. All dates above are therefore **candidates to be tested at weekly/daily resolution on the event log** (e.g. PELT/Bai–Perron on weekly series), not established change points.

**Primary monitoring population.** The **stable reference cohort**: all cases with (a) complete resource attribution, (b) canonical activity labels after mapping, (c) **non-Supplier-Z** intake, (d) **standard complexity**, (e) **straight-through** (no `Request Info`), monitored on a rolling 28-day window. Rationale: it is the population least perturbed by the three identified mechanisms (supplier shock, seasonal complexity surge, priority displacement), so any statistically significant movement in its DFG distance, variant mix, wait or conformance is *diagnostic of new drift* rather than of known causes. Supplier Z, priority-class and renewal cohorts are monitored **in parallel with their own baselines** (they answer "is the known mechanism behaving as expected?"), and the reference cohort must never be used as the control group in a supplier difference-in-differences without also reporting the supplier cohort (the reference cohort excludes the treated unit). Caveats: the reference cohort shrinks to ~35–46% of volume by Oct–Nov and 12–20% in the highest-complexity months, so its statistical power must be tracked; if it drops below the support rule, widen the window rather than relax the composition.

| M14 Backlog & WIP | open cases by age bucket; WIP per assessor | daily | >15% of open cases older than 10 business days | capacity/prioritisation review | long-running complex cases legitimately | Ops manager |

**Seasonality.** No single reference window is used. Each quantitative monitor carries (i) a 12-month median and MAD for dispersion, (ii) a **month-of-year seasonal index** once 24 months of history exist; until then, October–November are treated as an *expected-band* window derived from the renewal-season profile documented by operations, and any alert inside that window is annotated "seasonally expected" and compared with the prior year's same-week value rather than with the annual median. Cycle-time, mix and volume monitors are never compared to a flat annual median; only structural monitors (labels, duplicates, ordering, resource completeness, conformance) use flat, non-seasonal thresholds.

**Multiple testing.** The 14 monitors are treated as one family. Each evaluation window produces up to 14 test statistics; p-values (from CUSUM/EWMA or permutation tests on weekly data) are adjusted with **Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.05** (Benjamini–Yekutieli if dependence is suspected). Sequential monitors use CUSUM with h/k set per monitor family: structural monitors (M1–M4) use zero-tolerance or 2-week rules; behavioural monitors use two-consecutive-window persistence; performance monitors require capacity corroboration (M10 + M11) before escalation. Concentration of several correlated monitors (M6, M7, M8, M10) is treated as *one* hypothesis rather than four independent confirmations, and the December-type confound (multiple simultaneous interventions) is handled by requiring a pre-registered control segment (the reference cohort) and a pre-registered comparison cohort.

def tv_distance(a, b) -> float:

def flag_material_changes(series: pd.Series, rel_thresh=0.10, persistence=0.25):
    """Explainable rule: a month is flagged if |MoM relative change| >= rel_thresh AND
    the level persists (next available month stays >= persistence of the move),
    otherwise it is reported as a short-lived excursion/reversal."""
    flags = []
    months = list(series.index)
    for i in range(1, len(months)):
        prev_v, cur_v = series[months[i-1]], series[months[i]]
        if prev_v == 0 or np.isnan(prev_v):
            continue
        rel = (cur_v - prev_v)/abs(prev_v)
        if abs(rel) < rel_thresh:
            continue
        nxt = series[months[i+1]] if i+1 < len(months) else None
        if nxt is None:
            status = "candidate (no persistence window)"
        elif (cur_v - prev_v) > 0:
            status = "step" if nxt >= prev_v + persistence*(cur_v-prev_v) else "excursion/reversal"
        else:
            status = "step-down" if nxt <= prev_v + persistence*(cur_v-prev_v) else "excursion/reversal"
        flags.append({"month": months[i], "rel_change_%": round(100*rel,1), "status": status,
                      "known_event_same_month": KNOWN_EVENTS.get(months[i], "-")})
    return pd.DataFrame(flags)

    "assess_service_aug_h": 5.4,
    "rework_loop_increment_h": (12.0, 16.0),
    "median_damping": (0.7, 1.0),   # rework effect at the median vs the mean
}
def decompose_cycle_time(m: pd.DataFrame, A=ASSUMPTIONS) -> pd.DataFrame:
    b = m[m["month"].isin(BASE_MONTHS)]
    w_high_b = b["high_complexity_pct"].mean()/100.0
    med_b = b["median_cycle_h"].mean()
    mu_std = med_b / (1 + (A["complexity_multiplier"]-1)*w_high_b)
    mu_high = A["complexity_multiplier"]*mu_std
    svc_base = A["service_share_of_cycle"]*med_b
    svc_delta = (A["assess_service_aug_h"]-A["assess_service_jan_h"])/A["assess_service_jan_h"] \
                * A["assess_share_of_service"] * svc_base
    rework_base = b["rework_variant_pct"].mean()
    out = []
    for _, r in m.iterrows():
        if r["month"] in BASE_MONTHS:
            continue
        w_high = r["high_complexity_pct"]/100.0
        mix_eff = (w_high*mu_high + (1-w_high)*mu_std) - med_b
        d_rate = max(0.0, (r["rework_variant_pct"] - rework_base))/100.0
        rw_lo = d_rate*A["rework_loop_increment_h"][0]*A["median_damping"][0]
        rw_hi = d_rate*A["rework_loop_increment_h"][1]*A["median_damping"][1]
        delta = r["median_cycle_h"] - med_b
        q_hi = delta - mix_eff - svc_delta - rw_lo
        q_lo = delta - mix_eff - svc_delta - rw_hi
        out.append({"month": r["month"], "delta_median_h": round(delta,2),
                    "case_mix_h": round(mix_eff,2), "service_h": round(svc_delta,2),
                    "rework_h": f"{rw_lo:.1f}-{rw_hi:.1f}",
                    "queueing_residual_h": f"{q_lo:.1f}-{q_hi:.1f}",
                    "queueing_share_%": f"{100*q_lo/delta:.0f}-{100*q_hi/delta:.0f}"})
    return pd.DataFrame(out).set_index("month")

# PART B — Event-level PM4Py pipeline (functions only).

# =============================================================================
from __future__ import annotations
import copy
from collections import Counter
import numpy as np

import pm4py

CANONICAL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility",
                 "Senior Review": "Senior Assessment"}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- B1. load ---
def load_event_dataframe(path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Read the raw event export. Required columns: case_id, activity, timestamp.
    Optional: resource, supplier, complexity, priority_class, team, region."""
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], errors="coerce")
    df = df.dropna(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]).copy()
    df["case_id"] = df["case_id"].astype(str)
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].astype(str)
    return df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)

# ------------------------------------------------------------ B2. format ---
def format_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame, case_id="case_id", activity="activity",
                     timestamp="timestamp", additional_case_attributes=None):
    """pm4py.format_dataframe -> an EventLog object."""
    kwargs = dict(case_id=case_id, activity=activity, timestamp=timestamp)
    if additional_case_attributes:
        kwargs["additional_case_attributes"] = additional_case_attributes
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(df, **kwargs)

# --------------------------------------- B3. normalisation (never mutates) --
def normalise_log(log, mapping=CANONICAL_MAP, drop_duplicate_events=TECHNICAL_EVENTS):
    """Return a NEW log with mapped labels and de-duplicated technical events.
    The input log is deep-copied first, so raw and normalised logs can be compared."""
    new_log = copy.deepcopy(log)                     # original untouched
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(new_log).copy()
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(mapping)  # canonical label mapping
    if drop_duplicate_events:
        df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
        dup = df.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity"], keep="first") & \
              df["activity"].isin(drop_duplicate_events)
        df = df[~dup]
    return format_event_log(df)

# ------------------------------------------------------- B4. time slicing --
def slice_log(log, start, end):
    """Window slice. Primary: pm4py.filter_time_range; fallback: dataframe filter."""
    try:
        return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end)
    except (AttributeError, TypeError):
        df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log).copy()
        df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"])
        df = df[(df["timestamp"] >= pd.Timestamp(start)) &
                (df["timestamp"] <= pd.Timestamp(end))]
        return format_event_log(df)

# -------------------------------------------------- B5. monthly DFG slice --
def monthly_dfgs(log, granularity="M"):
    """Per-month DFG via pm4py.discover_dfg on each monthly slice."""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log).copy()
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"])
    if granularity == "M":
        df["_month"] = df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    else:
        df["_month"] = df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("W").astype(str)
    out = {}
    for key, g in df.groupby("_month"):
        sub = format_event_log(g.drop(columns=["_month"]))
        d, counts = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)            # (dfg, activities_count)
        out[key] = {"dfg": d, "activities": counts, "n_cases": g["case_id"].nunique()}
    return out

def dfg_rate_vector(dfg_dict, relations):
    """Normalise a discovered DFG to occurrences per 100 cases for the given relations."""
    n = max(1, dfg_dict["n_cases"])
    return {r: 100.0 * dfg_dict["dfg"].get(r, 0) / n for r in relations}

def tv_distance_between_months(monthly_dfg, m1, m2, relations):
    """Same TV measure as Part A, applied to event-level DFGs."""
    v1 = np.array([dfg_rate_vector(monthly_dfg[m1], relations)[r] for r in relations])
    v2 = np.array([dfg_rate_vector(monthly_dfg[m2], relations)[r] for r in relations])
    return 0.5 * float(np.abs(v1 - v2).sum()) / 100.0

# ------------------------------------------------------- B6. variant model --
def variant_profile(log, top_n=25):
    """pm4py.get_variants -> variant frequencies. PM4Py >= 2.7 returns one tuple
    per case (duplicates included), so a Counter gives the distribution."""
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    counter = Counter(tuple(v) for v in variants)
    total = sum(counter.values()) or 1
    rows = [{"variant": " -> ".join(v), "cases": c, "share_%": round(100*c/total, 2)}
            for v, c in counter.most_common(top_n)]
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def variant_js_divergence(log_a, log_b):
    """Jensen-Shannon divergence between two variant distributions (0 = identical)."""
    ca = Counter(tuple(v) for v in pm4py.get_variants(log_a))
    cb = Counter(tuple(v) for v in pm4py.get_variants(log_b))
    keys = set(ca) | set(cb); na = sum(ca.values()) or 1; nb = sum(cb.values()) or 1
    pa = np.array([ca.get(k,0)/na for k in keys]); pb = np.array([cb.get(k,0)/nb for k in keys])
    m = 0.5*(pa+pb)
    def kl(p,q):
        mask = p>0
        return float(np.sum(p[mask]*np.log2(p[mask]/q[mask])))
    return 0.5*kl(pa,m) + 0.5*kl(pb,m)

def temporal_profile(log, activity, granularity="hour"):
    """pm4py.discover_temporal_profile for one activity (arrival/shape of work)."""
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(log, activity=activity, granularity=granularity)

# ------------------------------------------ B8. skeleton / DECLARE (opt.) --
def log_skeleton(log):
    """pm4py.discover_log_skeleton: the 'ideal' straight-through path."""
    return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)

def declare_model(log):
    """pm4py.discover_declare: DECLARE constraints with frequencies.
    Note: API is version-sensitive; fall back to the legacy module path if needed."""
    try:
        return pm4py.discover_declare(log)
    except AttributeError:
        from pm4py.declarative import discover_declare      # legacy layout
        return discover_declare(log)

def alignment_quality(log, petri_net):
    """Alignment-based fitness for a given model (complement to token replay)."""
    return pm4py.align_petri(log, petri_net, return_alignment=True)

# ------------------------------------------- B9. raw vs normalised compare --
def compare_raw_vs_normalised(log, mapping=CANONICAL_MAP, relations=None):
    """Run the same artefacts on the raw and mapped logs; the raw log is not modified."""
    norm = normalise_log(log, mapping)
    result = {}
    for name, lg in (("raw", log), ("mapped", norm)):
        dfg_m = pm4py.discover_dfg(lg)[0]
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(lg)
        result[name] = {"dfg": dfg_m,
                        "n_cases": len(set(c.get("concept:name") for c in lg)),
                        "n_variants": len(set(tuple(v) for v in variants)),
                        "variant_dist": Counter(tuple(v) for v in variants).most_common(10)}
    result["variant_js_divergence"] = variant_js_divergence(log, norm)
    return result

# ------------------------------------- B10. segmentation + waiting times --
def segment_log(log, attributes=("supplier","complexity","priority_class","team")):
    """Attach case attributes for stratified mining (case-level, from the log)."""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log).copy()
    cases = {c.get("concept:name"): {k: c.get(k) for k in attributes} for c in log}
    df["_case_attrs"] = df["case_id"].map(cases)
    for a in attributes:
        df[a] = df["_case_attrs"].map(lambda d: (d or {}).get(a))
    return df.drop(columns=["_case_attrs"])

def queue_wait_table(df, activity="Assess"):
    """Reconstructed waiting before an activity: (start of activity - case start)
    minus median active service of that activity. Approximation: documented as such."""
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id","timestamp"]).copy()
    first = df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")
    df = df.merge(first, on="case_id", how="left")
    df["_wait_before"] = (df["timestamp"] - df["case_start"]).dt.total_seconds()/3600.0
    acts = df[df["activity"] == activity]
    return acts.groupby("case_id")["_wait_before"].min().describe()

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions.** (i) The March 2026 fitness collapse is predominantly a **logging/provenance artifact**, not business drift (confidence  0.9): raw fitness falls 38 pp while mapped fitness falls only 2.5 pp; the mapping recovers ~93–96% of the loss, and the DFG distance in March is only 1.5 pp with cycle time flat. The label dictionary (911), resource missingness (1.04.8%) and duplicate `Open Case` events (04%) all move on 1 March. (ii) A **genuine, normalization-robust behavioural change begins on 5 May** (confidence  0.8): rework doubles (1224 per 100), the fast path falls 5945%, mapped fitness steps to 0.88 and never returns to baseline; these quantities are invariant to renaming and to missing resource ids. (iii) Cycle-time deterioration is **queueing-dominated, not service-dominated** (confidence  0.75): Assess service rose only 4.85.4 h while the queueing residual accounts for ~60% of the median and ~50%+ of the p90 deterioration at November.

**Only plausible, not established.** That the staffing loss of 15 June is the primary queueing driver (0.6): demand grew 29% by November and rework cases load the same assessors, so the residual cannot be apportioned without assessor-level arrival/capacity data. That the 1 July priority rule redistributed waiting (0.4): the aggregate series is structurally incapable of showing redistribution, and `Senior Assessment` was historically recorded inconsistently. That the October step is seasonal (0.7): the historical renewal profile is asserted, not measured, and only one year exists.

**Mediation and interaction prevent clean attribution.** Supplier Z's volume share (1843%) and Supplier Z's document quality changed in the same month, so the mix shock and the behavioural shock are collinear at monthly granularity; rework *causes* part of the queueing it is being blamed for alongside staffing; priority class is post-treatment; the December improvement coincides with seasonal decay, composition change and two interventions (overtime, quality campaign), and completion bias means the December gain may partly reflect backlog clearance.

**What would revise the account.** (a) A supplier-stratified event-level analysis showing the May rework surge is *not* concentrated in Supplier Z ( persistent behavioural adaptation, H6, displacing H2). (b) Stable supplier-stratified waits after 15 June ( demand growth and priority displacement, not staffing). (c) A pre-2026 log proving senior-assessment routing was under-recorded ( the October surge is semantic drift, H11, not routing change). (d) Recovery to baseline mapped fitness after the December campaign *at unchanged seasonality* ( campaign effect, and a larger December attribution). (e) A non-random-missingness analysis showing missing resource ids correlate with case outcomes ( H7 is a confounder of everything, not just an artifact). (f) Cohort-based (case-start-month) recomputation shifting the cycle-time onsets relative to completion-month onsets ( H10 completion bias is material).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** raw fitness falls by 5 pp in a month while mapped fitness stays within 1.5 pp of its 12-month baseline **THEN** classify the event as provenance/semantic drift — audit the label dictionary, mapping coverage, duplicate technical events and resource-field completeness — and do not open a process-improvement case **BECAUSE** the mapping recovers ~93% of the March-style collapse, demonstrating that the business sequence itself did not change and that re-baselining the process model is the correct response.

2. **IF** mapped fitness remains 3 pp below baseline for 2 consecutive months **AND** the `AssessRequestInfo` rate rises 5 pp above baseline in the same window **THEN** open a rework-reduction case targeted at the stratum with the largest gap (Supplier Z channel first, verified by stratified DFGs and variants) **BECAUSE** a normalization-robust conformance loss co-moving with rework is the signature of genuine behavioural change, whereas a raw-only fitness loss is a data-provenance signature.

3. **IF** the `AssessRequestInfo` rate rises 5 pp **AND** the supplier-stratified rate does not rise (the increase is concentrated in non-Supplier-Z cases) **THEN** reclassify the drift as persistent behavioural adaptation or queue-discipline change and investigate workarounds, documentation quality and queue rules rather than vendor performance **BECAUSE** a channel-concentrated rise implicates the supplier mechanism while a population-wide rise implicates an internal mechanism that supplier recovery cannot explain.

4. **IF** reconstructed waiting before `Assess` rises while the per-activity service-time median rises <5% **THEN** investigate capacity and queue discipline (arrival rate, available assessor hours, WIP, priority-rule displacement) rather than task effort or documentation quality **BECAUSE** the service-versus-waiting split separates queueing mechanisms from service degradation — as in July–August, where waiting grew far more than the 4.85.4 h Assess service increase.

5. **IF** standard-complexity queue waiting rises while high-complexity queue waiting falls by a comparable magnitude after a priority-rule date **THEN** log the event as delay *redistribution*, notify the service manager, and exclude it from "process degradation" reporting — and rebalance the priority rule or add capacity **BECAUSE** priority routing shifts waiting between classes, so an aggregate median and p90 can worsen while the prioritised segment improves, and misclassifying it would trigger unnecessary remediation.

6. **IF** high-complexity share, case volume and p90 cycle time simultaneously exceed their month-of-year expected band for 2 consecutive weeks **THEN** treat the event as seasonal/population drift, re-baseline only the affected segments for 60–90 days and adjust capacity plans rather than triggering a drift investigation **BECAUSE** renewal-season effects recur annually, would otherwise be re-detected every October, and any real drift inside the season will still breach the same month-of-year band once the seasonal index is applied.

7. **IF** a monitoring statistic breaches its segment-specific alert band for 2 consecutive evaluation windows after seasonal adjustment and FDR control, and at least two *independent* artifact families (e.g. DFG distance + variant mix + queue wait) breach simultaneously **THEN** escalate to a joint process-mining review with event-level re-extraction, a formal change-point test on weekly series, and a fresh alignment/model audit **BECAUSE** sustained, multi-artifact, normalisation-robust breaches are the only signal in this evidence base that reliably separates genuine process change from single-month noise, seasonal composition effects and logging artefacts.
