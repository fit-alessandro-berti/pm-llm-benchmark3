## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Decision-making here cannot be reduced to a single linear expected-value formula because **mandatory conformance controls act as hard feasibility boundaries**, and regulatory harms exhibit extreme tail asymmetry and non-monetary consequences.

1. **Conformance-First Lexicographic Dominance (Eliminating A and B):**
   - The maximum operational upside of beating P-884’s deadline is **€20,000–€35,000**.
   - Even setting aside mandatory process rules, the observational non-approval rate is **0.25–0.45**. If even a modest fraction of those non-approved cases involves a true sanctions breach on a €480,000 payment to a sanctioned-border region, the monetary exposure (**€100,000–€500,000+**) plus **non-monetary harms** (regulatory enforcement orders, loss of correspondent banking relationships, reputational damage, executive/criminal liability, and heightened supervisory scrutiny across all payments) overwhelms €20k–€35k.
   - More fundamentally, Actions A and B guarantee a **100% certainty of procedural nonconformance** regardless of whether the supplier turns out to be benign. Regulators penalize systemic control bypasses and four-eyes violations even in the absence of a confirmed sanctions hit. Thus, A and B are strictly dominated.

2. **Separating Execution Value from False-Rejection Harm (Why D, C, and E Dominate F):**
   - A key analytical trap is assuming fast Action F (10–20 min) avoids deadline loss. The €20,000–€35,000 benefit applies specifically to **executing within the deadline**, not merely closing the ticket.
