# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Preliminary note on evidence types used below:
- **Observation** = directly readable from the supplied rows.
- **Estimate/bound** = derived arithmetic (durations, lags, overlap counts).
- **Hypothesis** = physical/organisational story that would produce the observed pattern.
- **Recommendation** = action.

Two derived quantities drive most of the reasoning:
- *recording lag* = `recorded_at − complete`. Near-zero lag ⇒ the completion timestamp is generated at the moment of recording (device-generated); large or structurally identical lag ⇒ the timestamp is typed/derived, i.e. a *logging* event rather than an *execution* event.
- *interval geometry* = whether starts coincide, ends coincide, intervals abut (`start_i = complete_{i-1}`), or intervals overlap.

# 2. Expected temporal signatures

Legend: ✓ = expected, ✗ = not expected, ~ = possible/indifferent.

| hypothesis | start equality | end equality | adjacency (`start_i = comp_{i-1}`) | overlap | recording lag | source precision | resource identity reliability | queue-arrival pattern | behaviour under a smaller time tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine simultaneous batch | ✓ exact | ✓ exact | ✗ | ✓ total (100 % pairwise) | small and roughly equal per row; may differ by a few units | fine (device); equality survives fine precision | high (machine ID) | arrivals accumulate up to the cycle start, count = fixture capacity | **survives**: equality holds at 1 s, so batch persists as tolerance → 0 |
| Start batching / common setup | ✓ exact | ✗ monotone increasing | ~ (implicitly, ends chain) | ✓ nominal only (artefact of the shared start) | small, tied to each *completion* | any | high | arrivals precede the group start | start-batch **survives**, end-batch and "concurrent" readings vanish |
| End batching / bulk submission | ✗ monotone increasing | ✓ exact | ✗ | ✓ nominal (starts precede earlier ends) | identical for all rows (one write) | any; typically form-based | medium (self-reported) | arrivals spread over the whole folder period | end-batch **survives**; the apparent overlap is exposed as a submission artefact |
| Sequential batching | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ exact or near-exact | ✗ | small, per row, device-generated | fine | high | arrivals arbitrary; queue drains at constant takt | **dissolves** — with tolerance 0 no "batch" is detected at all, only a chain |
| Concurrent work / multitasking | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ partial, often *nested* (one interval inside another) | small, per row | any | high (single genuine worker) | arrivals arbitrary | overlap is tolerance-independent, so it **survives** any tolerance |
| Shared-account artefact | ~ | ~ | ✗ | ✓ partial, concurrency > 1 sustained; may exceed physical capacity | per row | any | **low** — this is the defining weakness | arrivals arbitrary | survives; only resource-level aggregation is affected |
| Timestamp coarsening | ✓ (bin-aligned) | ✓ (bin-aligned) | ~ | ✓ (spurious, bin-wide) | lag *increases monotonically across rows* while execution stamps are frozen | **coarse and declared**; all stamps are multiples of the grain | high | arrivals may fall *after* the recorded start — a logical impossibility that betrays rounding | equality is an artefact of the grain: at tolerance < grain the batch is undecidable, not confirmed; `recorded_at` ordering is the only fine signal |
| Queue accumulation after capacity loss | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | small, device-generated | fine | high | **cluster of arrivals well before the first start**, then a long dead interval matching an independent downtime record | dissolves into a chain; the diagnostic signal is the arrival-to-start gap, not any equality |
| End-of-shift bulk data entry (G6 variant of end batching) | ✓ | ✓ **and start = complete** (zero duration) | ✗ | ✓ degenerate | identical for all rows; **huge** relative to arrival | any | medium/low | arrivals spread over hours before the single stamp | survives trivially, but as a *logging* pattern: zero-length intervals are the tell |

Reading rule implied by the matrix: **equality of stamps at a resolution far finer than the pattern's spread (G1, G4-adjacency) is evidence about physics; equality at exactly the declared grain (G7) or at exactly one write instant (G3, G6) is evidence about the recording channel.**

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Convention: *waiting* = `start − queue_arrival`; *service* = `complete − start`; *utilisation* = busy time / elapsed time per resource; *bottleneck detection* = ranking activities by service time or queue length.

**G1 — genuine batch.** Waiting per case (6, 5, 4, 2 min) is real but *systematically shaped by the batch rule*: the first-arriving item waits for the fixture to fill, so waiting is a function of batch formation, not of resource scarcity. Service time is 3 min *per batch*, but a case-level aggregation reports 4 × 3 = 12 min of "work" against 3 min of elapsed time ⇒ **utilisation inflated ~4×** (can exceed 100 %, a useful red flag). Throughput is correct only if computed per batch cycle. Bottleneck detection: RBT-7 will look far busier than it is; the real constraint is fixture capacity and batch-fill delay.

**G2 — start batching.** Waiting is **understated** for cases 2–4 (each is credited with waiting only until 10:00, though it really waited until the previous case finished: S04 truly waited ~09:54→10:21, not →10:00). Service time is **overstated** for later cases (S04 shows 27 min instead of ~6). Utilisation appears >100 % (8+14+21+27 = 70 min of "service" in a 27-min window), again a diagnostic tell. Bottleneck detection: inspection looks like a severe bottleneck with rising service times, and one may wrongly infer fatigue or case-complexity effects. Conformance: any rule "start must follow the previous complete" is violated for all rows.

**G3 — end batching.** Waiting (22, 18, 19, 19 min) is plausible and probably the *least* damaged quantity here. Service time is **overstated for early cases** (E01 gets 28 min, E04 gets 6) and the artificial spread across cases invents variance that does not exist — a variance-driven bottleneck analysis will single out E01-like cases. Utilisation over 11:02–11:30 sums to 74 min in a 28-min window ⇒ inflated. Sojourn/throughput at the *folder* level is fine; per-case service is not. Conformance: instantaneous handovers to any downstream activity appear at 11:30 for four cases at once, creating fake downstream batching.

**G4 — serial chain.** All four metrics are essentially unbiased *if* starts are genuinely scanned. Waiting (7, 10, 13, 16, 19 min) is real and grows linearly — the canonical signature of an arrival rate above service rate. Utilisation = 20/20 = 100 %, correctly identifying a saturated single server. **This is the cluster that should anchor bottleneck conclusions.** Residual risk: if `start` is back-filled as the previous `complete`, service time absorbs all idle time and utilisation is inflated to 100 % by construction.

**G5 — shared account.** Waiting is fine (15, 15, 16, 15 min). Service times (25, 15, 20, 10 min) are per-case correct *if* one analyst worked one case; but under multitasking they include suspension time and are **overstated**. Utilisation is the badly broken metric: 70 min of service in a 30-min window ⇒ 233 % for a single "resource". Interpreted naively, `analyst_pool` becomes the top bottleneck resource in the whole log; interpreted as three analysts, utilisation is ~78 % and unremarkable. Social-network / handover mining is also corrupted: a shared ID collapses several people into one hub node.

**G6 — bulk entry.** This is the case the task singles out, and the mechanism is worth stating precisely: the *single* recorded timestamp 17:00 is simultaneously used as the end of waiting and the start of service. Therefore
- waiting = `17:00 − queue_arrival` = 175, 148, 122, 110, 76, 58, 35 min — **inflated**, because it silently includes all the time the ticket sat closed-but-unrecorded (and possibly time when the work was already done);
- service = `17:00 − 17:00` = **0** for every case — deflated to nothing, because the two lifecycle fields collapse onto the same recording instant.
The two biases are two halves of one accounting error: the entire case duration is pushed into the waiting bucket and none into the service bucket. Consequences: utilisation of T-F = 0 (the worker looks idle all afternoon), throughput looks like a spike of 7 closures in one minute (7/min instantaneous rate), and bottleneck detection will point at the *queue before* "Close ticket" while declaring the activity itself free — the exact inverse of a resource-capacity diagnosis. Conformance checkers with duration constraints ("close must take ≥ 1 min") flag all seven; timing-based drift detectors see a step change at 17:00 every day.

**G7 — coarsening.** Waiting for M02–M05 is **negative** (arrival 14:01–14:11 vs start 14:00) — an impossible value that many tools silently clamp to 0, hiding the defect. Service is a constant 15 min for all cases: variance is destroyed, so the activity looks perfectly deterministic and will be *excluded* from bottleneck suspicion even if it is variable. Utilisation = 5 × 15 = 75 min in a 15-min window ⇒ 500 %. Any regression of duration on case attributes returns noise. The only trustworthy fine-grained signal is `recorded_at`, which suggests ~1 min spacing between record writes and hence probably short, staggered measurements.

**G8 — downtime queue.** Waiting (38, 43, 48, 54, 58 min) is genuine and correctly attributed *if* the clocks agree; it is a mix of downtime (≈35 min) plus queueing (≈0–23 min). Service is a clean 7 min. Utilisation over 15:38–16:13 is 35/35 = 100 %, over 15:00–16:13 it is 35/73 = 48 % — the choice of window determines whether the station looks saturated or half-idle, so **the availability window must be modelled explicitly**. Bottleneck detection that ignores downtime will attribute the queue to insufficient service capacity and recommend adding a station, when the correct action concerns the safety check. Clock-offset risk: if the safety clock leads the repair clock by more than ~3 min the two intervals overlap and the downtime explanation weakens.

# granularity indicators: are the *execution* stamps coarser than the declared precision?
def granularity_indicator(series, grain_s):
    """Smallest grain (in s) from a candidate ladder that divides every stamp."""
    secs = [(t - t.normalize()).total_seconds() for t in series.dropna()]
    if not secs:
        return None
    for cand in (1, 60, 300, 600, 900, 1800, 3600):
        if all(s % cand == 0 for s in secs):
            best = cand
    # pick the LARGEST divisor that still divides everything
    divisors = [c for c in (1, 60, 300, 600, 900, 1800, 3600)
                if all(s % c == 0 for s in secs)]
    return max(divisors) if divisors else 1

# overlap rate per resource (pairwise, on half-open [start, complete) intervals)
def overlap_stats(sub):
    iv = sub.dropna(subset=["start_ts", "complete_ts"])
    pairs = list(itertools.combinations(iv.index, 2))
    if not pairs:
        return 0.0, 0
    ov = 0
    for i, j in pairs:
        a0, a1 = iv.at[i, "start_ts"], iv.at[i, "complete_ts"]
        b0, b1 = iv.at[j, "start_ts"], iv.at[j, "complete_ts"]
        if a0 < b1 and b0 < a1:          # strict overlap; zero-length intervals excluded
            ov += 1
    # max simultaneous concurrency via a sweep line
    events = []
    for i in iv.index:
        events.append((iv.at[i, "start_ts"], 1))
        events.append((iv.at[i, "complete_ts"], -1))
    events.sort()
    cur = mx = 0
    for _, d in events:
        cur += d
        mx = max(mx, cur)
    return ov / len(pairs), mx

    return "unclassified"

diag["rule_label"] = diag.apply(rule_label, axis=1)
# queue-accumulation flag is orthogonal to the batch label

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource IDs trusted vs. treated as shared accounts.**
- Trusted: G5 is a single analyst juggling four overlapping cases; utilisation 233 %, so `analyst_pool` becomes the log's dominant bottleneck and service times of 10–30 min include suspension. G2/G3/G6 are single humans, so their >100 % utilisations must be logging artefacts (which is exactly what the recording-lag evidence says).
- Shared-account reading: G5 becomes ~3 analysts at ~78 % utilisation — unremarkable, and the "bottleneck" disappears. Note this reading *still* fails to explain concurrency 4 at 13:12–13:20, so at least one of {pool size = 3, one-case-per-analyst, timestamp accuracy} must be wrong. Under the shared-account hypothesis one should also revisit T-B, T-C, T-F: nothing rules out that these are role accounts too, in which case *every* >100 % utilisation cluster admits a benign multi-worker explanation and the logging-artefact conclusions weaken (though G6's zero durations survive regardless).
- Practical consequence: resource-level utilisation, work-handover graphs, and "resource-based batching" detection should all be suspended for any ID not verified as a single natural person.

**Timestamps exact vs. interval-censored at the stated precision.**
- Exact reading: G7 gives five cases each taking exactly 15 min in parallel (500 % utilisation) and three cases with *negative* waiting — internally inconsistent, so the exact reading is refuted by the data themselves. G1 gives a clean 3-min batch cycle. G3/G6 give exact common completions.
- Censored reading: replace each stamp `t` with an interval. For G7 (grain 900 s, unknown rounding direction) `start ∈ [13:45, 14:15)` and `complete ∈ (14:00, 14:30]`, so service ∈ [0 s, 45 min] — an interval so wide that G7 contributes **no usable service-time information**, only order (from `recorded_at`) and the fact that all five fit inside a 45-min envelope. For 1-min sources (G2, G3, G5) censoring adds ±60 s, which changes nothing qualitatively: 6–28 min differences survive. For 1-s sources (G1, G4, G8) censoring is negligible, which is precisely why these clusters carry the most weight.
- Rule of thumb this yields: **weight each cluster's contribution to a duration estimate by (effect size ÷ declared grain).** G7 scores ~1 and must be censored; G4/G8 score in the hundreds.

**At least three patterns with genuinely multiple defensible physical readings.**
1. **G1 — identical start *and* end.** (a) One machine cycle processing four items on a fixture (favoured: capacity = 4 = batch size, device-generated lag). (b) Four independent single-item cycles whose true start/end differ by sub-second amounts, coarsened to whole minutes by an upstream aggregation layer — note the stamps are *minute*-round despite a 1 s declared precision, which is a real anomaly. (c) A PLC that writes a "batch complete" record replicated across the four member items, i.e. an end-batching *logging* pattern. Discriminator: fixture-cycle telemetry or per-slot sensor events.
2. **G2 — one start, staggered ends.** (a) A UI artefact: one "begin group" press for serially executed inspections (favoured by the documented button). (b) Genuinely parallel inspection of four panels laid out together, finishing at different times — physically plausible for visual surface inspection and equally consistent with the stamps. (c) Sequential work where the operator simply pressed begin once and the ~6–7 min ladder is the real takt. Readings (a) and (c) are the same physics; (b) is different physics with identical data. Discriminator: whether inspection is single-item-at-a-time at the station.
3. **G3 — staggered starts, one end.** (a) Serial reviews with a single folder submission (favoured). (b) Interleaved/multitasked reviews all genuinely finishing at the deadline 11:30. (c) Reviews performed at unknown times whose *starts* were also reconstructed on the form, making both endpoints fictional and the even ~7–8 min spacing an artefact of human estimation. Discriminator: form field-level audit trail (was the start typed or captured?).
4. **G5 — nested overlaps.** (a) Three or more analysts on a shared login. (b) One analyst multitasking with suspend/resume, where "service time" is wall-clock containment rather than effort. (c) A mixture. All three produce the same nesting; only badge data or keystroke/session telemetry separates them.
5. **G8 — long arrival-to-start gap then a clean chain.** (a) Capacity loss (safety check 15:00–15:35) followed by recovery at takt (favoured). (b) A scheduled shift/break start at 15:38 unrelated to the safety event. (c) A clock offset large enough that the safety check does not actually precede the work. Discriminator: a measured offset between the safety-system and repair-queue clocks.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritised, highest value first.

1. **Separate execution time from recording time everywhere (P0).** Persist `start`, `complete` *and* `recorded_at` as distinct mandatory fields for every source, and never derive one from another. G6 is unusable today precisely because one recording instant was written into both lifecycle fields; had `recorded_at = 17:01` been the only stamp with `start`/`complete` left null, the data would have been *honestly* missing instead of misleadingly zero-duration.
2. **Emit true lifecycle events, not summary rows (P0).** `start`, `suspend`, `resume`, `complete` as separate events with their own timestamps. This resolves G5 (is the 25-min interval effort or elapsed?) and G3 (is the gap review time or waiting-for-submission?), and it makes multitasking measurable instead of inferable.
3. **Explicit batch/group identifiers (P0).** A `group_id` plus `group_role` (`member` / `representative`) and `group_size` written by any UI that starts or submits several items at once — G1's fixture cycle, G2's "begin group" press, G3's folder, G6's bulk form. This converts the entire adjudication in Section 1 from inference to a lookup, and lets analysts choose batch-level or case-level aggregation deliberately.
4. **Machine-readable precision and rounding metadata (P1).** Ship `timestamp_precision_s`, `rounding_rule` (`floor`/`ceil`/`nearest`/`none`) and, critically, `is_derived` per timestamp field. G7's "undocumented rule" is the single cheapest fix in this list: documenting it converts a 45-min-wide censoring interval into a ~15-min one, and knowing the direction removes the impossible negative waiting times outright.
5. **Clock synchronisation and offset logging (P1).** NTP-discipline every source and record `clock_offset_ms` / `source_clock_id` per event. G8's whole downtime attribution rests on a 3-minute gap between systems with admittedly different clocks; without offsets that conclusion is untestable. Also add a periodic heartbeat per source so drift is observable retroactively.
6. **Per-person identity, always (P1).** Badge/SSO-level `actor_id` recorded even when work happens on a shared VDI or kiosk, with `account_id` kept separately as a secondary field. This resolves G5 and lets every >100 % utilisation figure be diagnosed as either real multi-worker parallelism or a logging artefact.
7. **Workstation capacity and resource model (P2).** A reference table of `resource_id → capacity` (RBT-7 = 4, T-D = 1, `analyst_pool` = 3) plus shift calendars and planned/unplanned downtime intervals. Capacity turns "utilisation 233 %" from an unexplained anomaly into an automated data-quality assertion (`concurrency ≤ capacity`), and the downtime calendar makes G8's availability window explicit rather than a modelling choice.
8. **Native sub-second stamps on manual sources (P2).** Tablets, desktop forms and MES screens should record at least second resolution at the moment of the user action; the 1-min and 15-min grains are almost always a storage-format choice, not a real measurement limit.
9. **Queue-side instrumentation (P2).** Keep `queue_arrival` (already present and valuable) and add `assigned_at` / `first_touch_at` so waiting can be decomposed into queueing, assignment delay and downtime rather than being one undifferentiated gap.
10. **Automated data-quality assertions in the pipeline (P2).** Reject or flag at ingestion: `start > complete`, `start < queue_arrival`, `service = 0` on activities with a positive expected duration, concurrency above declared capacity, and stamps aligning exactly on a grain coarser than declared precision. Every defect diagnosed in this exercise is detectable by one of these five rules.

# 7. Final adjudication

**High confidence, usable as-is.**
- **G4 (Pack item).** Second-resolution scanner stamps, device-generated lags of 3–6 s, a perfectly abutting serial chain and linearly growing waiting. This is the reference cluster: use it for service-time distributions, utilisation and bottleneck ranking. Single caveat to verify: whether `start` is scanned or back-filled.
- **G8 (Repair triage)** *for its service times* (a clean 7 min at second resolution). Its *waiting* times are also high-confidence in magnitude but their *attribution* to the safety check is only medium confidence until the clock offset is measured.
- **G6 (Close ticket)** — high confidence about the diagnosis, not about the process: this is unambiguously bulk data entry (zero-length intervals, one shared `recorded_at`, contiguous sequence numbers, a source named `manual_bulk_form`). We are confident the timestamps are worthless as execution times.
- **G1 (Apply label)** is high confidence as a genuine batch on the balance of evidence, with the minute-rounding anomaly noted as an open item.

**Exclude, or admit only as interval-censored, in queue and service analysis.**
- **G7 (Manual measure):** exclude from all duration analysis. Its grain (15 min) exceeds the effect being measured, it produces negative waiting times, and its rounding rule is undocumented. Admit only as `service ∈ [0, 45] min` and use `recorded_at` for ordering.
- **G6:** exclude from service-time and utilisation analysis entirely; retain arrivals for demand analysis, and treat the computed waiting times as **upper bounds** (`waiting ≤ 17:00 − queue_arrival`) since the true close may have happened earlier.
- **G3 (Release review):** interval-censor completions as `complete_i ∈ [start_i, 11:30]`. Only E04's 6-minute duration is defensible as a point estimate; the folder-level throughput (4 reviews in ≤ 28 min) is the honest aggregate.
- **G2 (Surface inspection):** interval-censor or reconstruct the starts. The completion times are trustworthy, so the *inter-completion* series (8, 6, 7, 6 min) is a sound service-time estimate; the recorded per-case durations (8/14/21/27) must not be used.
- **G5 (Root-cause analysis):** exclude from any resource-level utilisation or handover analysis until identities are resolved. Case-level durations may be retained with an explicit flag that they may include suspension.

**Requires operational validation before any performance conclusion.**
- **G5:** confirm how many analysts were logged into `analyst_pool` between 13:00 and 13:30, and reconcile the observed concurrency of 4 with the stated maximum of 3. Until then no statement about analyst capacity is defensible.
- **G8:** measure the offset between the safety-system clock and the repair-queue clock. The 35-minute downtime explanation for a 38-minute pre-start gap is attractive but rests on two unsynchronised sources and a 3-minute residual.
- **G1:** confirm with the cell owner that one PLC cycle labels four items, and explain why a 1 s source emits whole-minute execution stamps. If the minute-rounding indicates an aggregation layer, the "simultaneous" reading weakens toward coarsening.
- **G2:** confirm that inspection is physically one-item-at-a-time. If four panels can be inspected in parallel, the start-batching diagnosis is replaced by genuine concurrency and the service-time correction above becomes wrong.
- **G4:** confirm that pack starts are scanned rather than inherited from the previous completion — this is the only threat to the log's most reliable cluster.

Overarching verdict: the log contains exactly **two** clusters where identical timestamps plausibly reflect simultaneous physical work (G1, and G5 under the shared-account reading). In every other cluster, coincident stamps are a property of the recording channel — a button press (G2), a form submission (G3), an end-of-shift form (G6), or a rounding rule (G7). Bottleneck conclusions should therefore be drawn from G4 and G8, sanity-checked against arrivals elsewhere, and no capacity decision should be made from the >100 % utilisation figures that G2, G3, G5 and G7 produce.
