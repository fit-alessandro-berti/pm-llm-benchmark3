## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

Ranks are investigative priorities, not posterior probabilities.

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The aggregate Orion penalty is primarily case-mix confounding | The routing rule sends difficult, compliance-heavy, and high-risk cases to Orion; their intrinsic processing demands inflate its mean | Orion has 42.9% High complexity versus Nova’s 12.5%; the sign reverses in every complexity stratum; pooled-standardized contrast is 2.51 h | Complexity strata remain severely confounded with shift/load; Orion has only two Low cases; hidden risk is unmeasured | Use exact and propensity-score matching on pre-assignment complexity, shift, load, and a pre-routing risk score. Report common-support diagnostics and perform sensitivity bounds for unobserved risk. If support fails, do not estimate a matched effect | If case mix explains the gap, balanced estimates will be near zero or favor Orion, and sensitivity bounds will include that explanation. Persistent, robustly positive Orion estimates would weaken this hypothesis |
| 2 | Orion has a genuinely harmful congestion or capacity effect at comparable work | Night demand and reviewer availability create a longer queue around Orion’s initial review or compliance work | Orion carries most night/high-load work; waiting contributes 4.01 h of the crude gap; many Orion cases wait 9–21 h | Within every complexity stratum, sampled Orion waiting is actually shorter; Orion has more certified reviewers | Reconstruct queue states by timestamp and run a capacity-capped randomized routing pilot stratified by complexity, shift, and load. Compare first-review wait, service, and all assigned outcomes | A harmful Orion effect would show excess Orion wait and total time persisting under adequate balance or randomization. Vanishing or reversed contrasts would weaken it |
| 3 | Orion’s certified-reviewer capacity is beneficial but masked by case mix | More capable reviewers reduce review effort or errors on comparable cases, but difficult and night work obscure the benefit | Orion review service is 0.13–0.39 h shorter within each stratum; it has more certified reviewers | No reviewer identifiers or skill measures are available; the stratum cells are tiny; quality shows no discernible advantage | Link cases to reviewers, measure certification and experience, compare work handled by the same reviewer across routing arms, and estimate skill-adjusted and reviewer-fixed-effect contrasts | If skill is beneficial, Orion service should remain faster after balancing case risk and reviewer skill, with no quality deterioration. Equal service for the same reviewer would weaken the hypothesis |
| 4 | Shift and arrival load explain the apparent group effect through operating conditions | Night staffing, workload spikes, and queue dynamics determine wait; group is a proxy for exposure to those conditions | Orion is 92.9% Night versus Nova at 25.0%; overall Night cases are 7.76 h slower; 13/14 Orion cases are High-load | Within Nova, High-load cases are not slower than Normal-load cases; within-stratum Orion cases are faster | Use timestamped arrivals, staffing, and queue occupancy in time bins; exploit staggered capacity changes. Use pre-classification delay as a negative control for post-assignment load effects | If load drives waiting, occupancy and night coverage will predict queue wait after case-mix adjustment, while the negative-control pre-classification interval will not show the same pattern. No such relation would weaken it |
| 5 | Compliance and rework mediate part of any Orion effect | Compliance creates an additional queue; review findings trigger `Request Info` and another review, increasing service time | Compliance-required cases are much slower overall; Orion has higher compliance and rework percentages; wait includes compliance waiting and service includes repeated review | All High cases require compliance, preventing a High contrast; within Medium, required cases are not slower; rework is strongly mixed by complexity | Add event and resource timestamps, separate first and repeated review durations, and use a compliance-policy change for a front-door-like estimate of the compliance step. Do not use `compliance_required` as a front door for group because it is not shown to be caused by assignment | If mediation is important, compliance waiting will rise with compliance processing load and rework will explain a measurable share of service time. A group effect remaining unchanged after these mechanisms would weaken this explanation |
| 6 | Completed-case selection and metric construction create or exaggerate the contrast | Long cases may abandon differentially by group; waiting and service categories overlap with compliance and rework in ways not visible in the aggregates | Only completers are present; no censoring information; wait is not pure service; service bundles repeated reviews | The direction of selection bias is unknown; internal sums are largely coherent; no direct evidence of differential abandonment | Create an all-case census with status and observation-end dates, analyze time-to-completion with censoring, and bound results under differential non-completion. Reconstruct activity-level durations without fabricating timestamps | If selection is material, the all-case estimand and completer-only estimate will differ, with greater non-completion in the slower arm. Similar estimates across reasonable selection bounds would weaken this hypothesis |
| 7 | `resource_group` is mainly a proxy for a routing-policy bundle rather than an independent treatment | Relabeling cases without changing staffing, reviewer pool, shift demand, or workload rules may do little; routing policy creates the apparent group contrast | Assignment is automated; Orion bundles difficult work, night coverage, certification, and compliance exposure | Group assignment can in principle be manipulated prospectively, and staffing may differ materially by group | Use a small 2×2 pilot crossing routing label with shared staffing/shift capacity, or randomize eligible cases within a common qualified pool while independently changing load/capacity | If the label is only a proxy, changing staffing or workload rules will matter while changing the label alone will not. A persistent randomized label effect would weaken this hypothesis |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Measurement and baseline | Instrument the full opened-case population and reconstruct queue, compliance, review, rework, and completion states before changing routing | Selection, measurement, queue-state confounding, and missing completion outcomes | All newly opened cases; measurement covers the entire process | No change to staffing or routing; separate reviewer and case identifiers; protect sensitive case information | Event timestamps, queue entry/exit, compliance timestamps, reviewer IDs, shift roster, first versus repeated review, open/abandoned status, conformance and variant frequencies | Establishes the all-case estimand, permits censoring-aware analysis, and distinguishes waiting from service; creates a baseline for later mediation | Pause rollout if logging materially impairs operations or violates governance requirements; correct instrumentation before causal experiments |
| 1. Small randomized routing pilot | Randomize a small, capacity-capped fraction of eligible cases to Orion or Nova within complexity×shift×load blocks, including paired randomization where feasible | Total effect of assignment and heterogeneous effects of routing | Eligible case, blocked and pairwise randomized; use the same target population as the policy decision | Only cases for which both groups are qualified and staffed; no bypassing compliance checks; unchanged priority rules; monitor all assigned and open cases | Assignment and deviations, classify-to-close and register-to-close time, first-review wait, pure service, compliance wait, rework, completion, appeals, and queue occupancy | ITT estimates identify the total assignment effect without conditioning on post-treatment mediators; block interactions test whether effects differ by complexity or shift | Pre-agreed safety, compliance, completion, and backlog guardrails; rollback to the standard routing rule on breach. Estimate required sample size before starting rather than treating 30 historic cases as adequate |
| 2. Capacity-first, stepped-wedge intervention | Add a shared compliance cell and/or late-shift reviewer capacity, with randomized staggered activation by shift-week or team block; do not relabel the groups | Queueing and compliance-processing capacity | Shift-week block or team cluster, randomized or stepped-wedge | Compliance cannot be skipped; audit quality and errors; avoid simply transferring overload to the control period | Queue occupancy by activity and worker, compliance handling time, time to first review, reviewer utilization, rework, service distribution, completion, and quality | If added capacity reduces waiting without shifting work toward slower reviewers, it supports a queue mechanism. If waiting persists despite capacity, the group explanation is weakened | Stop or reduce capacity if queues migrate elsewhere, service quality declines, or the intervention creates persistent excess overtime/backlog |
| 3. Evidence-gated policy refinement | If the pilot identifies a harmful or beneficial group mechanism, alter only supported complexity/shift strata gradually and retain a comparison or holdout where feasible | Confirmed routing or capacity mechanism rather than an aggregate label | Case or route block, with pre-specified allocation rule and capacity limits | No blanket 50% transfer; preserve qualification and compliance safeguards; monitor distributional effects on Nova and night staffing | Full ITT outcomes, subgroup effects, queue and service pathways, completion, quality, and effects on displaced work | Confirms transportability and reveals whether the pilot effect persists under production volume | Revert the rule if benefit disappears, harms emerge, quality degrades, or operators cannot maintain the assumed capacity |

Do not approve the proposed 50% reassignment on the current evidence. The crude 6.72-hour Orion penalty reverses after complexity standardization, and exact support for comparable shift/load cases is absent; the most defensible forecast is no gain, with substantial risk of queue growth, work displacement, and quality deterioration. First instrument the full case census and resource-linked queues. Then run a capacity-capped, blocked randomized pilot among cases for which both groups are qualified, using all assigned cases—not completers—as the primary analysis. In parallel, add shared compliance/late-shift capacity in a stepped-wedge design. Pre-specify classify-to-close and register-to-close outcomes, queue and service pathways, non-completion, rework, and adjudicated quality.

Scale only if randomized ITT results show a practically meaningful improvement without worse completion, backlog, or quality, ideally replicated in the affected complexity/shift strata. If no group effect remains after balance, retain stable routing and use the capacity findings to redesign staffing and compliance flow. If a harmful Orion mechanism is confirmed, reallocate gradually in supported strata with capacity limits and a rollback trigger. A blanket transfer is neither necessary nor an adequate learning design.

def parse_group_log(frame):
    """Parse activities only; no timestamps are invented or used."""
    lines = ["case_id,concept:name"]
    for row in frame[["case_id", "trace"]].itertuples(index=False):
        for activity in row.trace.split(">"):
            lines.append(f"{row.case_id},{activity}")
    payload = "\n".join(lines)

    kwargs = {
        "variant": Variant.SINGLE,
        "activity_key": "concept:name",
        "case_id_key": "case_id",
    }
    signature = inspect.signature(pm4py.parse_event_log_string)
    if "timestamp_key" in signature.parameters:
        kwargs["timestamp_key"] = None

    return pm4py.parse_event_log_string(payload, **kwargs)


def dfg_to_edges(discovered):
    # Newer PM4Py versions may return (dfg, activities_count).
    dfg = discovered[0] if isinstance(discovered, tuple) else discovered
    if hasattr(dfg, "dfg"):
        dfg = dfg.dfg
    if not isinstance(dfg, pd.DataFrame):
        raise TypeError("Unexpected PM4Py DFG output type.")

    if isinstance(dfg.index, pd.MultiIndex):
        edges = dfg.reset_index()
    elif {"source", "target"}.issubset(dfg.columns):
        edges = dfg.copy()
    else:
        # Compatibility with adjacency-matrix-style DFG output.
        edges = (
            dfg.stack(dropna=True)
               .rename("count")
               .reset_index()
        )
        edges.columns = ["source", "target", "count"]

    if "source" not in edges.columns or "target" not in edges.columns:
        raise ValueError(
            "Could not identify source/target columns in PM4Py DFG output."
        )

    count_column = (
        "count" if "count" in edges.columns else edges.columns[-1]
    )
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "source": edges["source"].astype(str),
        "target": edges["target"].astype(str),
        "count": pd.to_numeric(edges[count_column], errors="coerce").fillna(0),
    })


edge_tables = []
print("\n=== Group-specific directly-follows relations ===")
for group in group_order:
    group_frame = df[df["group"] == group]
    log = parse_group_log(group_frame)
    discovered = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    edges = dfg_to_edges(discovered)
    edges = edges.sort_values(["source", "target"]).reset_index(drop=True)
    edges["group"] = group
    edges["per_case_count"] = edges["count"] / len(group_frame)
    edge_tables.append(edges)

    print(f"\nGroup: {group}; cases: {len(group_frame)}")
    print(edges[["source", "target", "count", "per_case_count"]].to_string(index=False))

relation_table = pd.concat(edge_tables, ignore_index=True)
per_case_wide = relation_table.pivot_table(
    index=["source", "target"],
    columns="group",
    values="per_case_count",
    aggfunc="sum",
    fill_value=0.0,
)
print("\nDirect-follows counts per case, side by side:")
print(per_case_wide.to_string())

for row in relation_table.itertuples(index=False):
    if row.count < 3:
        warnings.warn(
            f"Rare DFG relation {row.group}: {row.source} -> {row.target}; "
            f"count={row.count}.",
            RuntimeWarning,
            stacklevel=2,
        )

print(
    "\nInterpretation guardrail: these are descriptive diagnostics. "
    "Complexity standardization does not adjust shift, load, compliance, "
    "rework, reviewer skill, or selection into completed cases; the DFGs "
    "contain no event timestamps and do not identify causal effects."
)

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Selection into completed cases.** It is unknown whether Orion and Nova differ in abandonment or censoring, nor in which direction that biases cycle time. The single most valuable addition is a **case-census log covering every opened case, including current status, closure or abandonment timestamp, and observation-end date**.

2. **The temporal queue and compliance mechanism.** The supplied wait measure cannot distinguish capacity shortage from compliance waiting, and review service cannot separate repeated reviews. The single most valuable addition is a **resource-linked event log with arrival, queue entry/exit, compliance start/end, review start/end, staffing, and worker identifiers**.

3. **Hidden differences in case risk.** Observed complexity may inadequately capture why Orion was selected, and matching on it cannot remove unmeasured risk. The single most valuable addition is a **pre-routing case-risk score computed from a frozen snapshot before assignment**.

4. **The true routing-policy and staffing bundle.** It is unclear whether group labels themselves matter or merely proxy certification, shift allocation, reviewer skill, and eligibility rules. The single most valuable addition is a **routing-assignment audit containing the rule ID, pre-assignment eligibility, decision rationale, assigned reviewer, and reviewer certification/skill profile**.

5. **The meaning and timing of the 30-day quality outcome.** One appeal per group provides almost no information, and post-process factors may affect appeals. The single most valuable addition is an **adjudicated close-quality assessment linked to appeal reason and documented post-process factors at 30 days**.
