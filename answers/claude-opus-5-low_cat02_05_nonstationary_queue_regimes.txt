# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention.** The center runs 07:00–19:00 = 12 operating hours per day. All rates below are expressed *per operating hour*, never per calendar hour; dividing daily counts by 24 would understate intensity by a factor of 2. The system is a *closed-at-night* queue: no service occurs between 19:00 and 07:00, but cases persist, so the queue is a nightly-restarted-server / persistent-content system, not a continuously operating one.

**Estimands (definitions and units).**

| Symbol | Definition | Estimator available here | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ | arrival intensity during opening hours | `arrivals / 12` | cases/op-hour |
| μ_eff (X) | realized throughput (departure intensity) | `completed / 12` | cases/op-hour |
| L | time-average WIP in system (waiting + in service) | `mean_wip` from 15-min snapshots (48 snapshots/day if snapshots are taken only in opening hours) | cases |
| S̃₅₀, S̃₉₀ | median and 90th percentile of **active handling time** | given directly | minutes |
| W | mean **flow time** (arrival → completion, including waiting and overnight carry-over) | **not supplied**; only inferable via L/λ or L/X under strong assumptions | hours |
| ρ | capacity pressure (offered load / capacity) | `arrivals × E[S] / (60 × staff_fte × 12)` — but E[S] is unknown; median is a biased-low proxy | dimensionless |
| ΔB | daily backlog change | `arrivals − completed` | cases |
| B(t) | backlog level at 07:00 | **not supplied**; only cumulative ΔB relative to an unknown start |

**Observation vs estimate.** Everything in the table is an *observation* (a daily aggregate). λ, X, ρ, W are *estimates* built on top. Anything about waiting-time distributions, per-case flow time, or queue discipline is a *hypothesis*.

**Threats to naive inference (at least five).**

1. **Median ≠ mean service time.** The gap between S̃₅₀ and S̃₉₀ widens dramatically (24/44 = 1.83× early; 36/88 = 2.44× on 27 Apr). A right-skewing service distribution means E[S] > median, and the *degree* of understatement is itself regime-dependent. Any ρ computed from medians is a lower bound, and a lower bound that is loosest exactly in the regime where pressure matters most.
2. **Conservation is not closed within a day.** `completed` counts departures regardless of arrival day; 16 Apr shows 129 completions against 112 arrivals. Same-day "throughput/arrivals" ratios are therefore not conformance measures — they are backlog-drain measures.
3. **Unknown initial backlog.** Only cumulative ΔB is recoverable (from an arbitrary zero at 06 Apr). The cumulative net rises to +128 by 28 Apr. If the true 06 Apr backlog were, say, 20, all absolute WIP interpretations shift; but the *shape* (flat → step at the outage → linear ramp → drain) is robust.
4. **Snapshot WIP is a mixed quantity.** `mean_wip` mixes cases in service (≤ staff_fte) with cases waiting, and includes overnight carry-over cases counted again every morning. It is a *system* L, so pairing it with a *system* throughput is required; pairing it with service-time medians is a category error.
5. **Serial dependence and a weekly cycle.** Daily rows are strongly autocorrelated: lag-1 arrivals r ≈ 0.41, lag-7 r ≈ 0.99, weekday-only lag-1 r ≈ 0.54, WIP lag-1 r ≈ 0.61. Effective sample size is far below 28; naive standard errors and t-tests on 28 "independent" days are invalid.
6. **Confounded interventions.** The case-mix jump (complex_pct 25 → 41 on 20 Apr) is simultaneous with the peak-concentration jump (34 → 42) and follows the outage recovery by only two working days. The staffing intervention (staff_fte 6.0 → 7.0) starts *during* 29 Apr with unknown implementation time, so 29 Apr is a partially-treated day.
7. **`complex_pct` may be a measurement artifact.** A coding/classification change on 20 Apr would produce the same jump with no real mix shift. The corroborating evidence (median service +9 min, p90 +30 min, peak share +12 pp on the same day) argues for a real change or a *combined* change, but cannot separate the two.
8. **Staff FTE is a mean of concurrent availability**, not a schedule. Weekend 2.5 FTE and weekday 6.0 FTE differ by 2.4×, while arrivals differ by ~2.4× too — a coincidence that makes pooled utilization look stable while intraday alignment is very different.

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

**Primary axis: time-ordered operating regimes (demand/mix/capacity state), with weekday/weekend as a nested stratum inside every regime.** I do not treat weekend as a top-level regime because it recurs inside all periods; treating it as a separate top-level block would hide the level shifts.

Rates are per operating hour (÷12). "Wd" = weekday-only sub-stratum.

| # | Boundary | Label | Evidence | Arrival λ (cases/op-h) | Completion X (cases/op-h) | Service behavior (S̃₅₀ / S̃₉₀ min) | WIP & capacity | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| R3 | 16–17 Apr | **Recovery / catch-up** | X > λ (129 vs 112; 112 vs 99); ΔB = −17, −13; staff_fte lifted to 7.0 / 6.5; WIP 52 → 35 decaying | 8.8 | **10.0** (peak 16 Apr) | 33/72 then 29/60 — elevated but decaying | L falls 52 → 35 → back to ~20-ish level; the 10.0/h is a **drain rate, not sustainable capacity** | **High** on direction, **medium** on magnitude |
| R4 | 20 Apr – 28 Apr | **Post-mix-change, capacity-constrained** | complex_pct steps 25 → 41–47; peak_08_10 steps ~30 → 42–50; S̃₅₀ 34–36, S̃₉₀ 78–88; staff flat at 6.0/5.5 | Wd 9.5 (rising trend 108 → 121) | Wd 8.4 | 34–36 / 78–88; ratio ≈ 2.4 (heavier tail) | Wd L ≈ 44 and monotonically rising; ρ̂ ≈ 0.93 **on medians** → true ρ ≥ 1 → **unstable, not a steady state** | **High** |
| R5 | 29 Apr – 03 May | **Staffed intervention / partial drain** | staff_fte 7.0, 7.0, 6.5, 3.0, 3.0; X > λ on 4 of 5 days; ΔB = −18 over the block; S̃₅₀ eases 36 → 32–33, S̃₉₀ 88 → 72–74 | Wd 9.6 (demand *not* reduced) | Wd **10.1** | 32–33 / 72–78; still far above R1 | L falls 50 → 36 (Wd); ρ̂ ≈ 0.76 on medians | **Medium** — only 3 weekdays; 29 Apr is partially treated |

**Nested weekend stratum (applies within R1, R4, R5).** Weekends run λ ≈ 3.7/h, X ≈ 3.6/h, FTE 2.5–3.0, peak share 17–22% (flat intraday), L 12–23. Weekend service medians are *higher* than weekday medians in R1 (28–29 vs 24–26) despite far lower load — evidence that weekend service time reflects **staffing composition / case selection**, not congestion.

**Explicit outage and recovery treatment.**
- 15 Apr must be split into three sub-windows (07:00–11:00 normal, 11:00–14:00 zero-service, 14:00–19:00 constrained restart). Its daily row is an average over three incompatible states and should never enter a "typical weekday" pool.
- 16–17 Apr must be excluded from any capacity estimate. Their high throughput is *backlog-fed*: the queue was non-empty all day, so the server was never starved. This is the maximum-drain rate at 7.0 FTE, an upper bound on capacity — precisely the opposite of what a pooled average would infer.
- Symmetrically, R5's 121–126 completions/day are also partly backlog-fed. Sustainable capacity at 7.0 FTE is bounded above by ~10 cases/op-h and is probably lower.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative A — "Backlog-state segmentation" (regimes defined by the sign and level of cumulative net flow, not by calendar).**

| State | Days | Definition | Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Balanced** (\|ΔB\| ≤ 3/day, cum. flat) | 06–14 Apr, plus 18–19 Apr, 02–03 May | arrivals ≈ completions | queue empties or nearly empties daily; overnight carry-over minimal |
| **Shock accumulation** | 15 Apr | ΔB = +47 in one step | exogenous capacity loss |
| **Drain** | 16–17 Apr, 29 Apr–01 May | ΔB < 0, WIP falling | non-empty-server regime; throughput measures capacity, not demand |
| **Chronic accumulation** | 20–28 Apr | ΔB = +10…+16 every single day, cum. +5 → +128 | overloaded: ρ ≥ 1, queue growing linearly, WIP tracks cumulative net almost 1:1 |

**Operational story.** This segmentation says the relevant state variable is *whether the server is starved or saturated*, because that determines what `completed` measures. In balanced states, completions estimate **demand**; in drain/accumulation states, completions estimate **capacity**. That distinction is invisible in the calendar segmentation.

**Alternative B (sketch) — "Two-factor design": {weekday, weekend} × {pre-20 Apr mix, post-20 Apr mix}, treating the outage week as a censored block.** This is the cleanest for estimating the *mix effect* free of day-type confounding.

**Comparison.**

| | Primary (calendar regimes) | Alternative A (backlog state) | Alternative B (2-factor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | Aligns with known interventions (outage, mix change, staffing); communicable to management | Correctly separates capacity-revealing from demand-revealing days; explains why 16 Apr and 22 Apr must never be pooled | Cleanest causal contrast for the mix effect; balanced day-type composition |
| Weakness | Boundaries partly arbitrary (why 20 Apr and not 19?); 29 Apr is partly treated | Boundaries are *derived from the outcome* (ΔB), so it risks circularity — you cannot then "test" whether backlog drives WIP | Discards the outage week; only 3 post-intervention weekdays; assumes no time trend within blocks |
| Best used for | staffing decisions and narrative | capacity estimation and Little's-Law eligibility | effect-size estimation for complex-mix |

I would use the **primary** for reporting and **Alternative A** as the gate for every quantitative queueing claim.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

**Pooled baseline (all 28 days):** λ̄ = 7.5/op-h, X̄ = 7.2/op-h, L̄ = 29.9, S̃₅₀ = 31 min, S̃₉₀ = 67 min, FTE = 5.14, complex = 32.9%, ρ̂ = 0.75.

**(a) Weekday/weekend composition masks both level and load.**

| | λ (/op-h) | X (/op-h) | L | FTE | ρ̂ (median-based) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pooled | 7.50 | 7.17 | 29.9 | 5.14 | 0.75 |
| Weekday (n=20) | 9.00 | 8.60 | 34.8 | 6.15 | 0.75 |
| Weekend (n=8) | 3.74 | 3.60 | 17.4 | 2.62 | 0.76 |

The pooled ρ̂ (0.75) is *identical* to both strata — a coincidence of proportional staffing. But λ differs 2.4× and L differs 2.0×. Any capacity plan calibrated to the pooled λ of 7.5/op-h under-provisions every weekday by 17% and over-provisions every weekend by 100%. Pooled L̄ = 29.9 corresponds to **no actual day type**.

**(b) Complex-case mix: a Simpson-like reversal in the service-time/utilization relationship.**

Within R1 weekdays, complex_pct 18–24% ↔ S̃₅₀ 24–26. Within R4 weekdays, complex_pct 41–47% ↔ S̃₅₀ 34–36. Pooled across all 28 days, the correlation between complex_pct and median service time is strongly positive and one is tempted to read "mix drives service time" as a clean dose-response. But *within R4 alone*, the day-to-day variation of complex_pct (41→47) is essentially uncorrelated with S̃₅₀ (34→36) — the relationship is a **between-regime step, not a within-regime gradient**. Fitting a pooled regression would report a slope (≈ +0.4 min per pp) that has no within-regime support and would mispredict what a 5-pp mix reduction actually buys.

A sharper reversal: **weekend days have higher median service time than weekday days in every regime** (R1: 28.5 vs 25.0; R4: 35.5 vs 35.0) *despite* far lower load. Pooling days and regressing service time on WIP therefore mixes a positive congestion effect (across regimes) with a negative one (across day types within a regime). The pooled slope is uninterpretable.

**(c) The outage/recovery block inverts the throughput picture.**

| Window | λ | X | X/λ | ΔB | Correct reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 Apr | 8.75 | 4.83 | 0.55 | +47 | capacity loss |
| 16–17 Apr | 8.79 | 10.04 | 1.14 | −30 | backlog drain at elevated FTE |
| 15–17 Apr pooled | 8.78 | 8.31 | 0.95 | +17 | **"a slightly busy but basically fine 3 days"** — completely false |

Pooling the shock with its recovery nets out to a nearly balanced picture and erases both the 3-hour service failure and the 30-case catch-up effort. The pooled 3-day mean WIP of 44.7 also implies a chronic congestion that never existed in R1.

**(d) The staffing intervention looks small pooled, large segmented.** Pooled over 29 Apr–03 May, FTE = 5.3 vs the whole-period 5.14 — a 3% difference, apparently trivial. Weekday-only: 6.83 vs 6.0 in R4, i.e. **+14% capacity**, which flipped ΔB from +13.4/day to −6.0/day. The weekend days (3.0 FTE, 2 of 5 days) dilute the pooled figure until the intervention is invisible.

**Robustness note.** With n = 3 post-intervention weekdays and strong serial dependence, I report **ranges, not means with SEs**: post-intervention weekday completions 116–126 (median 121); R4 weekday completions 94–106 (median 100). The intervals do not overlap, which is suggestive but rests on 3 vs 7 dependent observations.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Intraday dispersion of the representative days.** Treating the 12 hourly arrival counts as a sample and computing a Fano-like index of dispersion (variance/mean of the hourly counts, i.e. dispersion *of the intraday profile*, which conflates deterministic time-of-day shape with stochastic burstiness):

| Day | Σ arrivals | Fano (arrivals) | CV (arrivals) | Fano (completions) | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 07 Apr (ordinary) | 101 | 3.23 | 0.62 | 1.70 | smooth unimodal morning peak, completions lag arrivals by ~1 h |
| 15 Apr (outage) | 105 | 4.14 | 0.69 | 2.25 | arrivals *normal*; completions bimodal with a zero-plateau — dispersion is entirely a **service-side** artifact |
| 21 Apr (post-mix) | 115 | **10.12** | **1.03** | 0.98 | three spikes (34 at 10–11, 24 at 13–14, 14 at 16–17); arrivals far more bursty than completions |
| 25 Apr (weekend) | 51 | 1.29 | 0.55 | 0.78 | nearly flat, close to Poisson-like |

**Interpretation.** A homogeneous Poisson process has Fano = 1. All weekdays exceed 1, but this is expected for any time-varying rate — the correct null is a *non-homogeneous* Poisson process, under which hourly counts are independent Poissons with different means and the *profile* variance is not a dispersion test. The informative comparison is therefore **21 Apr vs 07 Apr**: Fano triples (3.2 → 10.1) and CV rises to ≈ 1.0. This is not merely "the peak moved" — the 21 Apr profile has **multiple separated spikes**, consistent with batch/appointment-block arrivals or referral batching introduced with the mix change. The daily `peak_08_10_pct` metric (which rose 31 → 47) actually *understates* this, because the 21 Apr spikes fall at 10–11 and 13–14, outside the 08–10 window.

**Completion smoothing.** Completions are always less dispersed than arrivals on non-outage days (1.70 vs 3.23; 0.98 vs 10.12). The server acts as a low-pass filter, absorbing burstiness into queue length — which is exactly why WIP, not throughput, is the sensitive indicator here. On 15 Apr the ordering reverses (completions 2.25 > arrivals... no, arrivals 4.14 > 2.25, but completions carry a structural zero-run), confirming the disturbance is on the service side.

**Within-day carry-over.** Cumulative (arrivals − completions) within the day: 07 Apr ends at +2 (essentially balanced, small mid-morning bulge of +6); 25 Apr ends at +8 and never recovers; 15 Apr peaks at +55 around 14:00 and ends at +47; **21 Apr peaks at +27 and ends at +14** — i.e. even a normal post-mix day does not clear, confirming structural overnight carry-over in R4.

**Serial dependence (daily sequence).**
- Arrivals: lag-1 r ≈ +0.41, lag-2 r ≈ −0.32, **lag-7 r ≈ +0.99**. The lag-7 value is essentially a deterministic weekly cycle, not evidence of a stochastic AR structure; the negative lag-2 is a artifact of the weekend dip.
- Weekday-only series: lag-1 r ≈ 0.54, lag-2 r ≈ 0.50 — genuine persistence after removing the weekly cycle, consistent with a slow demand trend plus regime shifts rather than white noise around a constant mean.
- WIP: lag-1 r ≈ 0.61, and WIP tracks the cumulative-net series closely — i.e. WIP is near-integrated (a stock), so its autocorrelation reflects **backlog memory**, not day-to-day demand correlation.

**Change-point evidence.** A transparent heuristic (weekday-only series; scan every candidate split; score by the reduction in within-segment absolute deviation) flags:
1. **15 Apr** — outage, exogenous and documented (a level *spike*, not a level shift).
2. **20 Apr** — the strongest true level shift: complex_pct +16 pp, S̃₅₀ +5, S̃₉₀ +18, peak share +8 pp, and ΔB flips from ~0 to persistently positive. Multiple, mutually corroborating channels change on the same day.
3. **29 Apr** — staffing shift; visible in FTE, in the ΔB sign flip, and in the easing of S̃₉₀. Weaker: only 3 subsequent weekdays, and the implementation time within 29 Apr is unlogged, so 29 Apr is a **partially-treated boundary day** and should be either excluded or assigned a fractional weight.

I explicitly do **not** claim these are statistically established change points. With 20 weekday observations, strong autocorrelation, and change points selected by inspecting the same data, any p-value would be badly optimistic. The claim is: *the co-movement of independent channels (mix, service quantiles, peak share, backlog sign) on 20 Apr and 29 Apr makes those two dates the most defensible boundaries available.*

**Why four representative days cannot establish a stochastic model.** Each of the four days is a single realization of a 12-bin non-homogeneous process; the per-bin sampling noise on a count of 20 is already ±4.5 (Poisson sd). One cannot estimate an intraday rate function, an inter-arrival distribution, batch-size distribution, or the *dispersion relative to a time-varying mean* from one day per regime. Distinguishing "true batch arrivals" from "Poisson arrivals with a sharply peaked rate" requires the raw timestamps (inter-arrival gaps), which are not supplied. The four days are best read as **existence proofs of qualitatively different intraday shapes**, not as parameter estimates.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law L = λW requires a stationary window with (i) a well-defined system boundary, (ii) equal long-run arrival and departure rates, (iii) L, λ, W measured over the *same* window and consistent population. Two extra local hazards: the queue is **closed overnight** (so W measured in *operating* hours ignores closed time — a case arriving 18:00 and finished 08:00 next day has W_op ≈ 1 h but W_wall ≈ 14 h), and `mean_wip` is a snapshot average whose sampling window must match.

| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity | Implied flow time (if defensible) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 weekdays (06–14 Apr, 7 days)** | Stable; same day type, same FTE band | λ ≈ X (100.1 vs 99.6/day) → either works; use the average | L = 20.3, homogeneous across days | Low: ΔB ≈ 0, cum. drift +4 over 7 days | Acceptable — no trend in any channel | W_op = 20.3 / 8.35 ≈ **2.4 op-hours** (≈ 146 min). Note S̃₅₀ = 25 min, so ≈ 80–85% of flow time is waiting/idle-in-queue | **applicable** (approximate; overnight time excluded) |
| **R1 weekends (11–12, 18–19 Apr)** | Stable day type | λ ≈ X | L = 13–14 | Low | Acceptable | W_op = 13.5/3.4 ≈ **4.0 op-hours** — *longer* than weekdays despite lower load | **approximate only** (n = 4; the counter-intuitive result needs a mechanism check: thin staffing, part-time coverage, or carry-over of complex weekday cases) |
| **15 Apr (full day)** | Boundary intact but service capacity discontinuous | Neither: λ = 8.75 vs X = 4.83 | L = 47 is a mid-day artifact | ΔB = +47, extreme | Grossly non-stationary (3 distinct sub-states) | — | **not defensible** |
| **15 Apr, 07:00–11:00 sub-window** | Stable | X ≈ 7.3/h | Snapshot L not separately reported for the sub-window | Low | Plausible | — (L unavailable at this resolution) | **not defensible** (data resolution, not concept) |
| **16–17 Apr (recovery)** | Stable boundary, but system is draining by design | X = 10.04 ≫ λ = 8.79 | L falling 52 → 35 (not a time-average of a steady state) | High: −30 net | Explicitly transient | — | **not defensible** as a steady state. *If* one insists, L/X = 43.5/10.04 ≈ 4.3 op-h is a **flow-time estimate for the cases being cleared**, which is a different population from arrivals — a Little's-Law variant for a draining system, not a steady-state W |
| **20–28 Apr weekdays (R4)** | Stable boundary and staffing | λ = 9.5 vs X = 8.4 — they *disagree by 12%*, which is the whole point | L rising 37 → 50 monotonically; no time-average exists | **Very high**: +94 net over 7 weekdays | Non-stationary by construction (ρ ≥ 1) | Bracketed only: L/λ = 44.3/9.5 ≈ 4.7 op-h; L/X = 44.3/8.4 ≈ 5.3 op-h. These **bracket** an ill-defined quantity; the true W of arrivals in this window is *unbounded above* because some of them had not departed by 28 Apr | **approximate only**, and only as a lower-bound bracket. Report the range 4.7–5.3 op-h with an explicit "censored — true W is larger" caveat |
| **Single R4 day, e.g. 22 Apr** | Boundary fine | ΔB = +16 | L = 46 is one day's snapshot mean | High | No | — | **not defensible** |
| **29 Apr–01 May weekdays (R5)** | Boundary fine; 29 Apr partially treated | X = 10.08 > λ = 9.58 | L falling 43 → 32 | Draining, −18 | Transient | L/λ = 37/9.58 ≈ 3.9 op-h; L/X = 37/10.08 ≈ 3.7 op-h | **approximate only** — a *transitional* figure; not the post-intervention steady state, which is not yet observable |
| **Full 28 days pooled** | Boundary fine | λ = 7.50 vs X = 7.17; cumulative net +110 unexplained | L pools 12–52 | Extreme | Four regime shifts | L/λ = 29.9/7.50 ≈ 4.0 op-h — **numerically plausible, substantively meaningless** | **not defensible** |
| **Wall-clock (24 h) variant, any window** | — | — | WIP snapshots presumably only during opening hours, so L excludes the closed period in which cases still wait | — | — | Multiply W_op by up to 2 to approximate calendar flow time for carried-over cases | **approximate only** — flagged because customers experience wall-clock, not operating, time |

**Key discipline point.** Nowhere above do I substitute S̃₅₀ for W. The comparison W_op(R1) ≈ 2.4 h vs S̃₅₀ = 25 min tells us the *waiting* component dominates even in the balanced regime — the single most decision-relevant number in this analysis, and one that a median-service-time reading would have missed entirely.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.**
- The R5 evidence supports **7.0 FTE weekday as the drain configuration**, not the steady-state configuration. At R4 demand (λ ≈ 9.5/op-h) with post-mix service times, sustainable capacity at 6.0 FTE was ~8.4/op-h — a 1.1/op-h (≈ 13 cases/day) shortfall. Once the backlog is cleared, capacity must still be ≥ demand: plan for **≈ 6.7–7.0 weekday FTE indefinitely**, not a temporary 7.0 that reverts to 6.0.
- Track the *sign of ΔB* as the primary control signal. Reverting to 6.0 FTE will be tempting once WIP looks normal; the segmented view says demand and mix, not backlog, set the requirement.

**Backlog recovery.**
- The remaining cumulative net at 03 May is ≈ +110 above the 06 Apr baseline (level unknown). At the observed R5 drain of ~6 cases/weekday, full clearance takes **~18 more weekdays** — roughly 3.5 weeks. If the intervention is "temporary," it will end mid-recovery.
- Prioritize aged carry-over cases explicitly. Under an unmanaged discipline, a 5-op-hour mean flow time with a heavy tail means some cases sit for days; a FIFO-with-aging rule protects the tail without changing throughput.

**Peak smoothing.**
- `peak_08_10_pct` rose from ~30% to ~45%, but 21 Apr shows the real problem is **multi-spike burstiness** (10–11, 13–14, 16–17), not a single morning peak. Smoothing built around 08–10 alone will miss two of three spikes. Recommendation: instrument arrivals at 15-min resolution and target the *whole* profile — appointment slotting or staggered intake for the complex variant, which is the plausible source of the batches.
- Weekends are structurally under-utilized in peak terms (flat profile, 17–22%) and have long W_op (~4 op-h) with only 2.5 FTE. Shifting a modest amount of complex-variant work to weekends is cheap capacity — but only if weekend staff are qualified for the complex variant, which the data cannot confirm.

**Data collection (highest-leverage fixes).**
1. Log **case-level arrival and completion timestamps** — without them, no flow-time distribution, no queue discipline inference, no real Little's-Law validation.
2. Record **backlog at 07:00 and 19:00 daily**; this alone converts all cumulative-net statements from relative to absolute.
3. Record **intervention timestamps** (the 29 Apr staffing change is untimed, forcing an entire day to be discarded or fractionally weighted).
4. Publish **mean service time and the full quantile set**, not just median/p90 — E[S] is required for any honest ρ.
5. Version the **complex-variant coding rule**, with an effective date, so a mix shift can be distinguished from a reclassification.
6. Retain **15-min WIP snapshots with the sampling window documented** (opening hours only? overnight?).

**A policy that looks good pooled and is risky segmented.**
> *"Realized throughput averaged 10.0 cases/op-hour during 16–17 April and 10.1 during 29 April–1 May, versus a pooled demand of 7.5/op-hour. We have ~30% headroom — reduce weekday staffing back to 6.0 FTE and reallocate the surplus."*

Every number in that sentence is correct and the conclusion is dangerous. The 10/op-h figures are **backlog-fed drain rates** measured while the server was never starved; the 7.5/op-h demand is a **weekday/weekend blend** that no operating day resembles. The correct comparison is weekday demand (9.5–9.6/op-h) against sustainable weekday capacity at 6.0 FTE under the *new* mix (~8.4/op-h). Reverting to 6.0 FTE reinstates the R4 regime — ΔB ≈ +13/day, WIP growing linearly, ρ ≥ 1 — and, because the queue is unstable rather than merely busy, the damage compounds instead of settling at a worse-but-stable level.

A second, subtler one: **"Weekend utilization matches weekday utilization (ρ̂ = 0.76 vs 0.75), so weekend staffing is correctly sized."** Segmented, weekend W_op (~4.0 op-h) exceeds weekday W_op (~2.4 op-h) in the baseline regime. Equal utilization at low absolute staffing yields *worse* waiting, because with 2.5 servers there is no pooling benefit — the classic square-root-staffing effect. Utilization parity is the wrong target; a delay-based target is right.

Self-contained. Data are the DAILY AGGREGATES and REPRESENTATIVE HOURLY VECTORS
supplied in the prompt. NOTHING here is a PM4Py event log (see PM4Py notes at the end).

Conventions:
  - Opening hours = 07:00-19:00 => H = 12 operating hours/day.
  - All rates are per OPERATING hour.
  - median_service_min is ACTIVE HANDLING time; it is NEVER used as mean flow time.
"""

H = 12  # operating hours per day
pd.set_option("display.width", 200)

from io import StringIO
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(DAILY), parse_dates=["date"])
df["is_weekend"] = df["day"].isin(["Sat", "Sun"])

# Derived estimands (all per operating hour)
df["lam_h"]  = df["arrivals"] / H            # arrival intensity
df["x_h"]    = df["completed"] / H           # realized throughput (departure intensity)
df["net"]    = df["arrivals"] - df["completed"]   # daily backlog change
df["cumnet"] = df["net"].cumsum()            # RELATIVE backlog: 07:00 level on 2026-04-06 is UNKNOWN
# rho_hat uses the MEDIAN service time -> a LOWER BOUND on true offered load,
# because E[S] > median for the right-skewed service distribution implied by p90/p50 ~ 1.8-2.4.
df["rho_hat_lb"] = df["arrivals"] * df["median_service_min"] / 60.0 / (df["staff_fte"] * H)
df["skew_ratio"] = df["p90_service_min"] / df["median_service_min"]

HOURLY = {  # bins 07-08 ... 18-19
    "2026-04-07": dict(arr=[3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4],

    "2026-04-15": dict(arr=[4,6,13,22,17,11,9,7,6,4,4,2],
                       comp=[5,7,10,8,0,0,0,4,6,7,6,5],   note="outage ~11:00-14:00"),
    "2026-04-21": dict(arr=[2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4],

    "2026-04-25": dict(arr=[1,2,4,7,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,2],
                       comp=[1,2,3,5,6,6,5,4,4,3,2,2],     note="weekend"),
}

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Pooled and candidate-regime summaries
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
REGIMES = {  # primary segmentation (calendar / intervention based)
    "R1 baseline      (04-06..04-14)": ("2026-04-06", "2026-04-14"),
    "R2 outage day    (04-15)":        ("2026-04-15", "2026-04-15"),
    "R3 recovery      (04-16..04-17)": ("2026-04-16", "2026-04-17"),
    "R4 post-mix load (04-20..04-28)": ("2026-04-20", "2026-04-28"),
    "R5 intervention  (04-29..05-03)": ("2026-04-29", "2026-05-03"),
}

def summarise(sub, label):
    if len(sub) == 0:
        return None
    return dict(
        regime=label, n=len(sub),
        lam_h=round(sub["lam_h"].mean(), 2),
        x_h=round(sub["x_h"].mean(), 2),
        net_total=int(sub["net"].sum()),
        L_wip=round(sub["mean_wip"].mean(), 1),
        # medians (robust) rather than means for the skewed service quantiles
        s50_med=sub["median_service_min"].median(),
        s90_med=sub["p90_service_min"].median(),
        s50_rng=f'{sub["median_service_min"].min()}-{sub["median_service_min"].max()}',
        fte=round(sub["staff_fte"].mean(), 2),
        cpx=round(sub["complex_pct"].mean(), 1),
        rho_lb=round(sub["rho_hat_lb"].mean(), 2),
    )

rows = [summarise(df, "POOLED (all 28 days)")]
rows.append(summarise(df[~df.is_weekend], "POOLED weekdays"))
rows.append(summarise(df[df.is_weekend],  "POOLED weekends"))
for lab, (a, b) in REGIMES.items():
    sub = df[(df.date >= a) & (df.date <= b)]
    rows.append(summarise(sub, lab))
    wd = sub[~sub.is_weekend]
    if len(wd) and len(wd) != len(sub):
        rows.append(summarise(wd, "   |- weekday stratum"))
summary = pd.DataFrame([r for r in rows if r])
print("\n=== TABLE 1: pooled vs regime summaries (rates per OPERATING hour) ===")
print(summary.to_string(index=False))

# Simpson-style check: complex_pct vs median service time, pooled vs within-regime
print("\n=== TABLE 2: complex_pct -> median service, pooled vs within-regime slope ===")
def slope(sub):
    if sub["complex_pct"].nunique() < 2:
        return np.nan
    return np.polyfit(sub["complex_pct"], sub["median_service_min"], 1)[0]
slopes = [("POOLED all days", round(slope(df), 3), len(df)),
          ("POOLED weekdays", round(slope(df[~df.is_weekend]), 3), (~df.is_weekend).sum())]
for lab, (a, b) in REGIMES.items():
    sub = df[(df.date >= a) & (df.date <= b)]
    if len(sub) >= 3:
        slopes.append((lab, round(slope(sub), 3), len(sub)))
print(pd.DataFrame(slopes, columns=["window", "min_per_pp_complex", "n"]).to_string(index=False))
print("NOTE: the pooled slope is driven by a BETWEEN-regime step, not a within-regime gradient.")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Burstiness / dispersion indicators + serial dependence
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# (a) Fano-like index of dispersion of the intraday profile (var/mean of hourly counts).
#     CAVEAT: under a NON-homogeneous Poisson process this is inflated by the
#     deterministic time-of-day shape; it is a COMPARATIVE, not an absolute, test.
# (b) Coefficient of variation of hourly counts.
bl = []
for d, v in HOURLY.items():
    a = np.array(v["arr"], float); c = np.array(v["comp"], float)
    cum = np.cumsum(a - c)
    bl.append(dict(day=d, note=v["note"], sum_arr=int(a.sum()), sum_comp=int(c.sum()),
                   fano_arr=round(a.var(ddof=1) / a.mean(), 2),
                   cv_arr=round(a.std(ddof=1) / a.mean(), 2),
                   fano_comp=round(c.var(ddof=1) / c.mean(), 2),
                   cv_comp=round(c.std(ddof=1) / c.mean(), 2),
                   peak_hour=BINS[int(a.argmax())],
                   max_intraday_backlog=int(cum.max()), end_of_day_backlog=int(cum[-1])))
print("\n=== TABLE 3: intraday burstiness (Fano = var/mean of hourly counts; Poisson-homogeneous ref = 1) ===")
print(pd.DataFrame(bl).to_string(index=False))

# (c) Simple serial-dependence indicator: lag-k autocorrelation.
def acf(x, k):
    x = np.asarray(x, float)
    return float(np.corrcoef(x[:-k], x[k:])[0, 1])

print("\n=== TABLE 4: serial dependence (daily sequence) ===")
sd = []
for name, series in [("arrivals (all days)", df.arrivals.values),
                     ("mean_wip (all days)", df.mean_wip.values),
                     ("arrivals (weekdays only)", df.loc[~df.is_weekend, "arrivals"].values),
                     ("net backlog chg (weekdays)", df.loc[~df.is_weekend, "net"].values)]:
    row = dict(series=name, n=len(series))
    for k in (1, 2, 7):
        row[f"lag{k}"] = round(acf(series, k), 2) if len(series) > k + 2 else np.nan
    sd.append(row)
print(pd.DataFrame(sd).to_string(index=False))
print("lag7 ~ 0.99 on all days is the DETERMINISTIC weekly cycle, not stochastic AR structure.")
print("Effective sample size << 28; do not attach naive standard errors to daily means.")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Transparent change-point heuristic (EXPLORATORY, NOT ground truth)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def scan_single_changepoint(series, dates, min_seg=3):
    """Single-split scan minimising within-segment sum of absolute deviations from
    the segment MEDIAN (robust). Returns a ranked table.
    THIS IS A HEURISTIC: the split is chosen by looking at the same data used to
    score it, the series is autocorrelated, and no multiple-testing or
    autocorrelation correction is applied. No p-values are reported on purpose."""
    x = np.asarray(series, float)
    base = np.abs(x - np.median(x)).sum()
    out = []
    for i in range(min_seg, len(x) - min_seg + 1):
        cost = np.abs(x[:i] - np.median(x[:i])).sum() + np.abs(x[i:] - np.median(x[i:])).sum()
        out.append((str(pd.Timestamp(dates[i]).date()), round(1 - cost / base, 3)))
    return pd.DataFrame(out, columns=["split_at", "rel_cost_reduction"]).sort_values(
        "rel_cost_reduction", ascending=False)

wd = df[~df.is_weekend].reset_index(drop=True)
print("\n=== TABLE 5: exploratory change-point scan, WEEKDAYS ONLY (heuristic, not a test) ===")
for col in ["complex_pct", "median_service_min", "mean_wip", "net", "peak_08_10_pct"]:
    top = scan_single_changepoint(wd[col].values, wd["date"].values).head(2)
    print(f"  {col:20s} -> " + ", ".join(f"{r.split_at} (gain {r.rel_cost_reduction})"
                                          for r in top.itertuples()))
print("Multiple independent channels co-move at 2026-04-20 (mix/service/peak/backlog) and,")
print("more weakly, at 2026-04-29 (staffing). 2026-04-15 is a documented exogenous SPIKE, not a level shift.")
print("2026-04-29 is a PARTIALLY-TREATED day (intervention time unlogged) -> treat as a boundary, not a member.")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Little's Law, only for explicitly chosen windows
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# L = lambda * W. We report W in OPERATING hours. The queue is closed 19:00-07:00,
# so W_op EXCLUDES closed time; calendar flow time for carried-over cases is larger.
# We never substitute median service time for W.
LL_WINDOWS = [
    # (label, date_from, date_to, weekday_only, verdict, why)
    ("R1 weekdays",      "2026-04-06", "2026-04-14", True,  "applicable",
     "lambda~=X (100.1 vs 99.6/day), |cum net| small, no trend"),
    ("R1 weekends",      "2026-04-06", "2026-04-19", "wknd", "approximate only",
     "n=4, day type stable, but thin staffing regime"),
    ("R3 recovery",      "2026-04-16", "2026-04-17", True,  "not defensible (steady state)",
     "draining by design: X >> lambda, L falling 52->35"),
    ("R4 weekdays",      "2026-04-20", "2026-04-28", True,  "approximate only (bracket, censored)",
     "rho>=1, L rising monotonically, +94 net -> true W is censored above"),
    ("R5 weekdays",      "2026-04-29", "2026-05-03", True,  "approximate only (transitional)",
     "draining post-intervention; not yet a steady state"),
    ("FULL POOLED",      "2026-04-06", "2026-05-03", False, "not defensible",
     "4 regime shifts, +110 unexplained net, L pools 12..52"),
]
ll = []
for lab, a, b, wonly, verdict, why in LL_WINDOWS:
    sub = df[(df.date >= a) & (df.date <= b)]
    if wonly is True:
        sub = sub[~sub.is_weekend]
    elif wonly == "wknd":
        sub = sub[sub.is_weekend]
    lam, x, L = sub.lam_h.mean(), sub.x_h.mean(), sub.mean_wip.mean()
    ll.append(dict(window=lab, n=len(sub),
                   lam_h=round(lam, 2), X_h=round(x, 2), L=round(L, 1),
                   net=int(sub.net.sum()),
                   W_from_lambda_oph=round(L / lam, 2),   # arrivals as throughput proxy
                   W_from_X_oph=round(L / x, 2),          # completions as throughput proxy
                   s50_med_min=sub.median_service_min.median(),
                   verdict=verdict))
llt = pd.DataFrame(ll)
print("\n=== TABLE 6: Little's Law by window (W in OPERATING hours; closed time excluded) ===")
print(llt.to_string(index=False))
print("Throughput proxy: use lambda when lambda~=X (balanced windows). When they diverge,")
print("L/lambda and L/X BRACKET an ill-defined quantity -> report the range, never a point estimate.")
print("Compare W to median SERVICE time: R1 weekdays W~2.4 op-h vs s50=25 min => waiting dominates.")
for r in llt.itertuples():
    if "not defensible" in r.verdict:
        print(f"  SUPPRESSED as a steady-state claim: {r.window} ({r.verdict})")
