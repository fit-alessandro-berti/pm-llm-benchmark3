## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

**These are decision stress tests, not identified forecasts, confidence intervals, or statistical bounds.** Changes are intervention minus B baseline: negative values mean faster completion. Unless otherwise stated, median and p90 changes refer to **all Site B cases**, whose baseline values are 44 and 110 hours. 

### Structural anchors and eligibility bounds

For a hypothetical case with fixed task durations \(I\) and \(D\), no queues, and both inputs ready:

$$
T_{\text{serial}}=I+D,\qquad
T_{\text{parallel}}=\max(I,D),\qquad
\text{saving}=\min(I,D).
$$

Substituting B’s reported service medians **as illustrative fixed durations** gives:

$$
3.4+8.1-\max(3.4,8.1)=3.4\text{ h}.
$$

This is not a calculation of B’s median treatment effect: medians cannot generally be added, and actual branch waits and service distributions are missing.

For originals cases, let \(R=18\) h illustrate the receipt delay. If originals already proceed independently of identity, a current arrangement of \((I\parallel R)\rightarrow D\) takes:

$$
\max(3.4,18)+8.1=26.1\text{ h}.
$$

The candidate \(I\parallel(R\rightarrow D)\) also takes:

$$
\max(3.4,18+8.1)=26.1\text{ h}.
$$

Thus **the same candidate can produce no benefit** when identity already finishes during the receipt wait. Conversely, replacing a genuinely sequential \(I\rightarrow R\rightarrow D\) arrangement would save 3.4 hours in this illustration. Establishing actual release timing is therefore decision-critical.

The first proposed cohort requires no originals, no legal review, and ready digital documents. Its share \(q\) satisfies:

$$
0\le q\le 38\%.
$$

Even before adding digital readiness, the overlap of “no originals” (38%) and “no legal review” (60%) can range from 0% to 38%. Assuming independence and using \(0.38\times0.60=22.8\%\) would be unjustified. Moreover, 38% is **not** B’s digital share.

Confidence scores assess plausibility of the stated ranges **conditional on their assumptions**; they are not scenario probabilities.

**Mapping assumptions to the ranges.** S1 combines approximately 3 hours of illustrative critical-path savings with 0–4 hours of additional release/queue improvement near the median. Its p90 range assumes 4–15 hours of recoverable tail waiting. A’s observed six-hour waiting reduction and eighteen-hour p90 reduction motivate testing such mechanisms, but identify neither magnitude at B. 

S2 assumes 2–5 hours saved within the treated cohort, but allows almost no population movement because eligible cases may be few or already fast. S3 assumes comparable gains for typical cases but 8–20 hours of downstream congestion for tail cases. S4 assumes negligible execution overlap, with 0–3 hours of typical and 5–15 hours of tail coordination overhead. S5 assumes small upstream gains offset by up to three hours of net typical delay; tail congestion of 15–30 hours, less 0–5 hours of upstream savings, gives approximately 10–30 hours of tail harm. These congestion quantities are explicit stress inputs, not measurements.

For an example in which **average performance improves while the tail worsens**, assume 80% of cases save five hours and the slowest 20% incur twelve extra hours, without rank crossover:

$$
\Delta E[T]=0.8(-5)+0.2(12)=-1.6\text{ h},
$$

while p90 increases by twelve hours.

Population quantiles must ultimately be computed from counterfactual distributions:

$$
F_B^\pi(t)=\sum_x P_B(x)F_B^\pi(t\mid x),
$$

followed by inversion of the mixture CDF. **Multiplying a subgroup median gain by its population share does not produce the population median gain.**

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Eligibility and staging.** Begin with digitally complete claims requiring neither originals nor legal review, with verified input independence and low-to-moderate complexity. Record eligibility at registration, before assignment. Unknown legal requirements or document completeness trigger a conservative hold, not optimistic eligibility. Later stages separately test originals-required and legal-review pathways.

**Creation versus execution.** Following `Register`, create an identity task and a document-path work item. Administrative creation of a blocked document task is not itself unlawful, but it is not evidence of parallel verification. Execute `Document Check` only when its inputs are available and, where required, `Originals Receipt` is complete. For originals cases, allow identity work to proceed while originals are being obtained.

**Mandatory synchronization.** Assessment starts only when both verification results are complete, usable, and refer to the current evidence version. Public-sector or high-value cases must enter legal review before a final decision. These conditions preserve the supplied statutory ordering constraints. 

**Queues and responsibilities.** Retain separate identity, document, assessment, and legal accountability. Use priority classes with aging within each class; do not give digital claims unrestricted priority over older claims. Cap started-but-unfinished work separately by role, initially within an audited safe operating envelope. Keep externally blocked cases visible but distinguish them from active-service WIP. Throttle new verification releases when downstream ready-queue age or workload exceeds the agreed envelope. Do not copy A’s six-task limit without B-specific calibration.

**Legal and supplement loops.** After `Request Supplement`, await receipt before acting on new evidence. A recorded invalidation decision determines whether no earlier check, Assessment alone, or Document Check followed by Assessment must repeat. Every supplement cycle returns to Legal Review before exit. An identity-invalidating supplement goes to a separately controlled exception route; the compact model below assumes identity remains valid.

**Logging.** Capture eligibility and assignment; treatment version; task creation, readiness, start, suspension, resumption, and completion; actual receipt times; resource and role; lock waits; occurrence and recording timestamps; evidence versions; invalidation reasons; queue/WIP snapshots; and mature quality outcomes. `Supplement Received` is a **proposed additional event**, not an observation supplied in the brief.

A compact POWL-style specification is:

```text
R = Register
I = Identity Check
O = XOR(Silent, Originals Receipt)            [guarded by originals requirement]
D = Document Check
A = Assessment

Repeat = XOR(
    Silent,
    Assessment,
    SEQ(Document Check, Assessment)
)                                           [guarded by evidence invalidation]

Redo = SEQ(Request Supplement, Supplement Received, Repeat)
L = LOOP(Legal Review, Redo)                 # Legal Review (Redo Legal Review)*
G = XOR(Silent, L)                           [guarded by legal requirement]
Decision = XOR(Approve, Reject)

PO nodes = {R, I, O, D, A, G, Decision, Notify}
Covering precedence relations:
    R < I
    R < O
    O < D
    I < A
    D < A
    A < G
    G < Decision
    Decision < Notify

Take the transitive closure.
I is unordered with O and D.
```

The XORs describe an aggregate family of routes. Their choices must be constrained by case data; an unrestricted XOR is not a substitute for statutory enforcement.

**Support limitation.** Using 3,400 six-month completions only as a planning proxy, a 13-week window would yield about 1,700 cases. Even the 38% eligibility upper bound gives only 646 eligible cases, or 323 per arm under 1:1 assignment; actual digital/no-legal eligibility will be smaller. At 300 cases per arm, independent-case normal approximations give roughly ±6 percentage points uncertainty around a rework-rate difference near 17%, and ±3.5 points around an appeal-rate difference near 5%. Dependence can widen these intervals. Thus a modest pilot can test execution and speed, but **cannot automatically establish the proposed tight quality margins**.  

First test implementation: did the scheduler release each branch when legally ready, did task-level ownership work, and did actual timestamps agree with independent audit records? Compare assignment with executed policy without excluding blocked or nonadherent cases from the intention-to-treat analysis.

Then test the mechanism. For digitally ready claims, did independent release advance joint verification readiness? For originals cases, did identity finish during the originals wait? The latter can succeed without overlapping Identity Check and Document Check intervals, so a low overlap percentage alone is not implementation failure.

Finally test propagation: did earlier readiness reduce Notify time, or merely increase the assessment/legal queue? Earlier readiness with unchanged final duration indicates a downstream bottleneck; reliable execution with no readiness gain suggests redundant precedence or dependent inputs. Higher repeat workload indicates a quality/congestion countermechanism.

Use the randomized contrast for causal outcome claims. Fidelity and mediation analyses diagnose explanations but do not become causal merely by comparing adherent with nonadherent cases. Poor implementation calls for a separately versioned technical repair; high-fidelity harm calls for throttling, redesign, or stopping.

```python
    from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
except ImportError as exc:
    raise SystemExit("Install PM4Py: python -m pip install pm4py==2.7.17") from exc


def po(nodes: Sequence[POWL], edges: Sequence[Tuple[POWL, POWL]]) -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """Create the complete strict order, not merely its covering edges."""
    if len({id(n) for n in nodes}) != len(nodes):
        raise ValueError("A child node cannot occur twice in the same partial order.")
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for source, target in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(source, target)
    model.order.add_transitive_edges()
    if not model.order.is_strict_partial_order():
        raise ValueError("Order must be acyclic, irreflexive, and transitive.")
    return model


def seq(*nodes: POWL) -> StrictPartialOrder:
    return po(nodes, list(zip(nodes, nodes[1:])))


def guarded(node: POWL, required: Optional[bool]) -> POWL:
    """None is an unguarded aggregate choice; bool specializes a known stratum."""
    if required is True:
        return node
    if required is False:
        return SilentTransition()
    if required is not None:
        raise TypeError("Guard must be True, False, or None.")
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[node, SilentTransition()])


def build_site_b(originals_required: Optional[bool] = None,
                 legal_required: Optional[bool] = None) -> StrictPartialOrder:
    register = Transition(label="Register")
    identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
    originals = guarded(Transition(label="Originals Receipt"), originals_required)
    document = Transition(label="Document Check")
    assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")

    # Fresh objects for repeated labels: do not reuse initial-check nodes.
    # Runtime data guards must select which prior results were invalidated.
    repeat_checks = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[
        SilentTransition(),
        Transition(label="Assessment"),
        seq(Transition(label="Document Check"), Transition(label="Assessment")),
    ])
    redo = seq(
        Transition(label="Request Supplement"),
        Transition(label="Supplement Received"),  # Proposed additional logging event.
        repeat_checks,
    )
    # LOOP(do, redo) = do (redo do)*: every supplement returns to Legal Review.
    legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[
        Transition(label="Legal Review"), redo,
    ])
    legal = guarded(legal_loop, legal_required)
    decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[
        Transition(label="Approve"), Transition(label="Reject"),
    ])
    notify = Transition(label="Notify")

    model = po(
        [register, identity, originals, document, assessment, legal, decision, notify],
        [
            (register, identity), (register, originals),
            (originals, document),
            (identity, assessment),  # Explicit identity prerequisite.
            (document, assessment),  # Explicit document prerequisite / AND join.
            (assessment, legal), (legal, decision), (decision, notify),
        ],
    )
    # Identity is NOT ordered against either originals receipt or document checking.
    assert not model.order.is_edge(identity, document)
    assert not model.order.is_edge(document, identity)
    return model


def main() -> None:
    a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
        "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
    )
    a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', "
        "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
    )
    models = {
        "A_before": a_before,
        "A_after": a_after,
        "B_aggregate": build_site_b(),
        "B_first_pilot_stratum": build_site_b(False, False),
        "B_originals_and_legal_required": build_site_b(True, True),
    }
    accepting_nets = {}
    for name, model in models.items():
        net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
        accepting_nets[name] = (net, im, fm)
        if not pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net):
            raise RuntimeError(f"{name}: not a workflow net")
        # PM4Py 2.7.17 returns (bool, diagnostics); do not test tuple truthiness.
        sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
        print(f"{name}: workflow_net=True; sound={sound}")
        if not sound:
            raise RuntimeError(f"{name}: soundness failure: {diagnostics}")

    # Optional playout illustrates admissible activity order, not elapsed time.
    # It does not prove empirical overlap, statutory compliance, or performance.
    run_playout = False
    if run_playout:
        net, im, fm = accepting_nets["B_originals_and_legal_required"]
        log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
        for trace in list(log)[:3]:
            print([event["concept:name"] for event in trace])

    # LIMITATIONS:
    # Aggregate XORs do not enforce case-data guards. Use verified stratum flags
    # and external guards for document readiness, legal clearance, and redo scope.
    # The model cannot encode role capacities, case locks, WIP/queue discipline,
    # activity durations, originals/supplement arrival guarantees, or probabilities.
    # Untimed transitions express precedence, not start/complete interval overlap.
    # Identity is assumed to remain valid during supplement loops.


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

Structural soundness would establish a control-flow property, not correct legal routing for each case, sufficient staffing, or a deadline guarantee. A supplement loop can permit arbitrarily many repetitions even when completion remains possible. The aggregate model therefore needs case-data guards and operational controls in addition to net-level checks.

## 9. Recommendation

**conditional go**

Approve a gated, randomized pilot of a modified Site B intervention—not a universal copy of Site A.

Transfer the principle of removing unnecessary precedence between independently executable branches. For originals-required claims, the corresponding design is Identity Check in parallel with Originals Receipt followed by Document Check. Assessment must still wait for both usable verification results, and legal review and supplement obligations remain intact. 

Do not transfer the nine-hour reduction as a forecast, Site A’s staffing assumptions, its six-task limit, or its absence of mandatory legal review. The observed improvement combines treatment with API, staffing, case-mix, and queue changes, while rework and appeals moved slightly upward. Site B’s heavier observed tail and specialized queues make downstream congestion a credible countereffect.  

Start with digitally complete claims requiring neither originals nor legal review, with verified input independence and low-to-moderate complexity. Their population share is unknown and cannot exceed 38%; do not assume independent eligibility attributes. Keep contemporaneous controls and monitor the queues serving nonparticipants.

The initial benefit hypothesis is roughly two to five hours for eligible cases, not nine hours for every case. Overall improvement could be negligible if this subgroup is small or rarely determines the population median. Principal risks are apparent rather than real concurrency, assessment congestion, rework amplification, and delayed discovery of quality harm.

Prioritize information that changes the decision: an audited eligibility cross-tabulation, proof that concurrent execution is technically safe, and branch-to-join waiting decomposition. These have greater early decision value than an undifferentiated rollout. Monetary value-of-information cannot be estimated from the brief without implementation costs and valuations of delay and error.

Expand when randomized evidence supports the proposed meaningful median gain, excludes unacceptable tail harm, and satisfies agreed mature-quality safeguards. Modify release rates or staffing when readiness improves but downstream queues worsen, testing the revised package separately. Abandon parallelization where inputs are dependent, compliant concurrency cannot be implemented, or high-fidelity execution yields no worthwhile net benefit. Uncertain quality results justify continued bounded learning, not automatic expansion.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Occurrence timestamps can be made sufficiently reliable** — Needed to distinguish execution from recording artifacts — Audit lifecycle records against independent system evidence and back-entry logs — If false, overlap and waiting-mechanism estimates remain unreliable; pause causal interpretation of those metrics.

2. **An identifiable subgroup has independent verification inputs** — Needed for useful concurrent execution rather than speculative work — Review data dependencies and observe whether either check consumes the other’s output — If false, retain the necessary precedence for affected cases.

3. **Originals, legal-review, and digital-readiness eligibility can be determined before assignment** — Needed for lawful routing and unbiased strata — Audit intake decisions and later reclassifications — If false, use conservative gating and revise eligibility rather than excluding difficult cases retrospectively.

4. **The IT system can support safe task-level concurrency** — Needed to translate administrative release into actual execution — Test concurrent ownership, version conflicts, locking, and audited writes — If false, stop execution parallelization until a separately evaluated technical solution exists.

5. **The fixed-duration calculations and congestion ranges are useful planning approximations, not empirical effects** — Needed to construct quantitative scenarios from sparse summaries — Replace them with joint case-level timing distributions and workload-calibrated experiments — If false, revise the scenarios rather than defending their numerical ranges.

6. **Supplement handling can identify invalidated results, and identity usually remains valid during the modeled loop** — Needed for selective repetition and model fidelity — Audit supplement reasons, result versions, and identity changes — If false, add explicit invalidation and identity-reverification routes.

7. **Shared-pool exposure and major operational changes can be measured adequately** — Needed to interpret randomization under interference — Record saturation, carryover WIP, staffing, arrivals, and treatment versions — If false, limit conclusions to the observed operating regime; do not extrapolate to full-scale deployment.

8. **Complete follow-up or explicit censoring information is available for all assigned cases** — Needed to avoid completed-case selection and falsely reassuring quality results — Reconcile registration, Notify, open-case, rework, and mature appeal cohorts — If false, withhold expansion claims based on incomplete speed or quality evidence.
