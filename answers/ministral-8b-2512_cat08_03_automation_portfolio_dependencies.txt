# **7. Final Recommendation**

**Recommend the Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (A2, A3, A4, A6) for Year 1**, followed by compliance modules in Year 2.

### **Trade-Off Argument**:
- **Why not automate highest-frequency activities first (e.g., A1)?**
  - **Frequency  Impact**: A1 reduces back-office load but exposes downstream risks (e.g., misclassified cases cascade to KYC/committee bottlenecks). The 1% misclassification rate in A1 would create **120 rework cases/day**, overwhelming compliance and credit teams.
  - **Synchronization Delays Dominate**: The 31h wait for **Resolve entity mismatch** (1750 cases) and 20h wait for **Financial review** (5200 cases) are **critical-path bottlenecks** that no single high-frequency win can address. Automating these first would reduce end-to-end cycle time by **~40%** (from 60h to 36h).
  - **Resource Demand Shifts**: Automating A1/A9 would overload credit analysts if **A6** isn’t implemented to reduce manual normalization work. The bottleneck/synchronization portfolio **balances labor savings with control risk**.

- **Why not the Compliance-First Portfolio (A2, A3, A8, A12)?**
  - **Slower Cycle-Time Improvements**: While it reduces control violations, it delays labor savings and customer experience improvements. The bank’s management prioritizes **process-level performance**, not just compliance.
  - **Higher Implementation Risk**: **A8** requires deep integration and manual overrides, which may face resistance from relationship managers.

- **Why not the Quick-Win Portfolio (A1, A5, A9, A10)?**
  - **Unreliable Controls**: Without **A2/A3**, misclassified cases would trigger rework loops in **KYC, committee packaging, and contracts**, negating labor savings. The 5.8% four-eyes violations in High-exposure cases would persist.
  - **Customer Friction**: **A5** depends on RM behavior; without **A6**, document loops would remain a bottleneck.

### **Defensible Trade-Off**:
Automate **bottlenecks first** because:
1. **Process-level thinking**: The 31h KYC delay and 20h financial review wait are **synchronization points** that dominate end-to-end performance, not just frequency.
2. **Dependency management**: **A2/A3/A6** clean inputs for **A8/A9**, enabling scalable automation later.
3. **Risk mitigation**: Strengthens controls (A3/A4) while reducing rework (A6).
4. **Resource alignment**: Prepares credit analysts for higher-volume work by reducing manual normalization (A6).

**First-year outcome**:
- **Cycle time**: ~36h (from 60h) due to reduced KYC and financial review delays.
- **Rework**: Down by 30% (entity mismatch and document loops).
- **Control violations**: Stable or reduced (A3/A4 enforce compliance).
- **Labor savings**: Back-office and compliance teams see capacity gains, but credit analysts are shielded from overload.

This approach **avoids the pitfall of chasing frequency** while addressing the **hidden ground-truth**: synchronization delays and rework dominate performance.
