## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

The nominal four-eyes principle (FEP-1) aims to prevent a single human from independently preparing and releasing a payment. Breaking FEP-1 exposes the organization to distinct threat scenarios. Each must be evaluated against the operational boundaries of the compensating controls (AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1):

1. **Unilateral Fraud or Theft (Direct Embezzlement)**
   - *Threat:* A rogue operator creates a fraudulent payment to a personal, controlled, or co-conspirator account and completes both preparation and release.
   - *Control Performance:* AV-2 can catch duplicate invoices or blacklisted accounts, but cannot detect commercial unreasonableness or novel fake vendor setups. ED-1 requires independent manager pre-approval (MG-4) and prior emergency tokens, constraining unilateral execution. RA-7 provides retrospective detection, but if recall windows close, recovery fails. MG-4 prevents unilateral release if performed before settlement.

2. **Error and Accidental Misstatement**
   - *Threat:* An operator makes a typo in the amount, selects the wrong invoice, or duplicates a legitimate payout.
   - *Control Performance:* AV-2 strongly prevents duplicate invoices and amount anomalies (against operator limits). MG-4 catches errors if receipts/invoices are reviewed. RA-7 detects errors post-settlement, but correction depends entirely on bank recall windows. ED-1 does not prevent accidental inputs unless AV-2 or MG-4 covers them.

3. **Vendor Account Takeover / Business Email Compromise (BEC)**
   - *Threat:* An external attacker or compromised operator updates a vendor's bank account details shortly before payment release.
   - *Control Performance:* AV-2 specifically flags bank-account changes during the previous seven days, triggering a warning or block. MG-4 catches changes *only* if the manager independently verifies bank details against historical master data. RA-7 detects anomalies after settlement, but funds transferred to modified accounts are rarely recoverable. ED-1 forbids emergency release if a bank-account change occurred within seven days.

4. **Collusion and Fabrication**
   - *Threat:* Two distinct operators (or an operator and a manager) conspire to fabricate service receipts, bypass checks, or approve fictitious work.
   - *Control Performance:* AV-2 explicitly *cannot* detect collusion, validate commercial reasonableness, or verify receipt of goods/services. MG-4 can fail if the manager rubber-stamps approvals or participates in the collusion. RA-7 detects fabricated receipts only through deep sample audits or whistleblowers. Distinct user IDs satisfying nominal FEP-1 do *not* protect against structured collusion.

5. **Operational Circumvention / Emergency Abuse**
   - *Threat:* Operators invoke emergency permissions (ED-1) routinely or without valid operational justification to bypass segregation of duties.
   - *Control Performance:* ED-1 strictly bounds emergency usage to active emergency tokens, amounts under EUR 100,000, mature vendors (>180 days), stable bank accounts, and mandatory pre-approval/audit. RA-7 enforces 100% retrospective audit within one business day to verify emergency legitimacy. AV-2 must be fully operational without overrides.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating control package is deemed **fully equivalent**, **partially equivalent**, **merely mitigating**, or **ineffective** based on a structured evaluation across seven dimensions:

1. **Timing:** Preventive controls (blocking before release or bank settlement) outrank detective controls (post-settlement audit or recall). Once bank settlement occurs, the process moves from correction via recall to uncertain recovery.
2. **Independence:** The verifying actor (manager or auditor) must be organizationally and operationally independent of the preparer/releaser, with no direct reporting lines or shared credentials.
3. **Coverage:** The control must inspect the exact vector of risk (e.g., bank account modification, amount limits, receipt validity, vendor age). A clean pass on automated rules (AV-2) does *not* imply physical receipt of goods.
4. **Overrideability:** Controls that permit operator overrides (e.g., AV-2 warnings overridden by operators using manager credentials) require independent validation to avoid self-authorization.
5. **Recoverability:** The window between payment release, bank settlement, and audit discovery must fit within the bank recall window (typically 24 hours). Post-settlement discovery without recall capability shifts risk from operational control to legal loss.
6. **Evidence Quality:** Verifiable artifacts (signed receipts, immutable token logs, bank verification checks) must exist. Rubber-stamped manager approvals or missing audit trails invalidate equivalence.
7. **Collusion Resistance:** Distinct user IDs alone do not prove independence if operators share workstations, act as a closed payment cell, or consistently pair up.

*Why "same expected loss" is insufficient:* Expected loss ($Loss = Probability \times Impact$) aggregates rare catastrophic failures with frequent minor errors. A flawed control package might yield a low expected loss purely because fraud attempts were absent in the sample, while leaving the company structurally exposed to a single catastrophic BEC attack or collusion ring. Control evaluation must test structural integrity, not just historical luck.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control Dimension | AV-2 (Automated Validation) | MG-4 (Independent Manager Approval) | RA-7 (Retrospective Audit & Recall) | ED-1 (Emergency Single-Operator) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Nature** | Preventive (Blocks/Warns) | Hybrid (Preventive if pre-release/pre-settlement; Detective if post-settlement) | Detective (Becomes Corrective if within bank recall window) | Preventive framework with strict preconditions |
| **Timing relative to settlement** | Pre-release / Pre-settlement | Variable (Must be pre-release or pre-settlement to be effective) | Post-settlement (Within 24 hours for recall) | Pre-preparation (token active) to pre-settlement |
| **Independence** | Automated (Rule-based) | Human (Must be L3+, non-preparer, no conflict of interest) | Human (Internal Audit / Control team) | System + Human (Token + Manager + Auditor) |
| **Failure Modes** | Service unavailability (AP-208), unverified goods/receipts | Rubber-stamping, direct supervisor bias (AP-202), late sign-off | Audit delayed beyond recall window (>24h), superficial review | False emergencies, token creation timing anomalies |

### Semantics of Manager Actions After Release but Before Settlement (e.g., AP-201, AP-207)
A manager action occurring *after* `Release_Payment` but *before* `Bank_Settlement` functions as a **hybrid control**:
- **Preventive Effect:** Because bank settlement is the point of irrevocable financial transfer, a manager review during this window can actively abort, recall, or freeze the transaction before funds leave the company's control.
- **Detective/Corrective Limitation:** It does *not* prevent the operator from initiating and releasing the payment. It relies on the timing buffer between internal release and batch bank settlement. If bank settlement occurs immediately (as in AP-208), this control degrades entirely into a retrospective detective review.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

1. **Case AP-202 (EUR 480,000 | BEC / Fraud)**
   - *Most Plausible Loss Mechanism:* Operator u23 executed a fraudulent payment to a relative's bank account following an account change, overriding AV-2 warnings using stolen or shared supervisor credentials.
   - *Causal Chain Break:* Failed completely; no control broke the chain. AV-2 warned but was overridden; MG-4 was performed by the direct supervisor two days too late.
   - *Remaining Exposure:* Total financial loss (EUR 480k) with zero recovery.
   - *Under a Truly Independent Second Releaser:* A distinct, independent second releaser (not supervised by m09 and without shared credentials) would have been forced to verify the bank account change against master data, blocking the release and preventing the loss.

2. **Case AP-204 (EUR 220,000 | Fabricated Receipt / Collusion)**
   - *Most Plausible Loss Mechanism:* Operators u12 and u13 operated as a closed cell on a shared workstation, approving 37 payments together and fabricating service consulting receipts.
   - *Causal Chain Break:* Nominal four-eyes was technically met (different user IDs for prepare and release), but substantive verification failed due to collusion.
   - *Remaining Exposure:* Vulnerability to fictitious vendor billing and embezzlement via reciprocal rubber-stamping.
   - *Under a Truly Independent Second Releaser:* Mandating rotating workstation assignments, enforced segregation of duties (preventing fixed pairs), and sample-based physical receipt verification would disrupt the tacit agreement between u12 and u13.

3. **Case AP-208 (EUR 900,000 | System Outage & Bank Change Exposure)**
   - *Most Plausible Loss Mechanism:* A high-value payment to a recently modified bank account was rushed through during an AV-2 outage without pre-checks or manager approval.
   - *Causal Chain Break:* All preventive barriers collapsed (AV-2 unavailable, checklist unsigned, manager approval post-settlement).
   - *Remaining Exposure:* EUR 900k exposure to potential fraud or misdirection with no independent verification at release time.
   - *Under a Truly Independent Second Releaser:* Even with AV-2 down, a mandatory independent manager pre-approval and bank-change verification policy would have halted release until bank ownership was manually confirmed.

4. **Case AP-201 (EUR 1,800 | Convenience Same-User Violation)**
   - *Most Plausible Loss Mechanism:* Operator u17 performed both preparation and release for low-risk office supplies out of administrative convenience.
   - *Causal Chain Break:* AV-2 passed clean; manager m04 ratified pre-settlement; RA-7 audited at 25 hours.
   - *Remaining Exposure:* Minimal financial exposure; procedural non-conformance.
   - *Under a Truly Independent Second Releaser:* Identical outcome, but zero non-conformance flag. The cost of segregation exceeds the risk exposure in this specific tier.

5. **Case AP-206 (EUR 95,000 | Production Outage Emergency)**
   - *Most Plausible Loss Mechanism:* Urgent machine repair required rapid payment during a production outage; token creation lagged preparation by 2 minutes.
   - *Causal Chain Break:* ED-1 token framework combined with pre-settlement manager approval (m11) and full AV-2 pass.
   - *Remaining Exposure:* Low operational exposure, though logging synchronization uncertainty exists.
   - *Under a Truly Independent Second Releaser:* Strict enforcement of pre-preparation token activation would eliminate the timing ambiguity, though the actual risk remains negligible due to m11's pre-settlement approval.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

To transition from static compliance auditing to robust process governance, the industrial group should implement automated monitoring rules and telemetry collection:

1. **Same-User Rate:** Track the percentage of payments where `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` share the same `org:resource`. *Threshold:* >0% for amounts >EUR 10,000 triggers an automated review; >0% for amounts >EUR 100,000 triggers an immediate audit.
2. **Override Rate:** Monitor frequency of `Override_Warning` events per operator and per manager. *Threshold:* High override velocity by specific operators (e.g., u23 in AP-202) must flag for supervisory audit.
3. **Late Manager Approval:** Track time elapsed between `Release_Payment` and `Manager_Ratify` / `Manager_Approve`. *Threshold:* Any manager ratification occurring *after* `Bank_Settlement` must be flagged as a detective-only control, forfeiting preventive credit.
4. **Audit-Before-Recall-Window:** Measure time from payment release to `Audit_Review`. *Threshold:* Emergency or single-user payments must be audited within 12 hours to allow 24-hour bank recall if anomalies appear.
5. **Repeated Resource Pairs:** Analyze directed pairs of (Preparer, Releaser) over rolling 30-day windows (e.g., u12/u13 in AP-204 and AP-209). *Threshold:* Concentration of >20 shared transactions between the same two users on the same workstation must trigger rotation or mandatory supervisory review.
6. **Shared Workstation Telemetry:** Capture client IP addresses or hardware fingerprints alongside `org:resource`. *Threshold:* Multiple distinct user IDs executing transactions from identical hardware fingerprints within minutes indicates potential credential sharing.
7. **Bank-Change Proximity:** Cross-reference payment release timestamps against master data modification logs for bank accounts. *Threshold:* Any payment to a vendor whose bank account changed within 7 days must automatically block release pending dual-manager sign-off (addressing AP-202 and AP-208).
8. **Control Outages:** Monitor system availability events (`Validation_Service_Unavailable`). *Threshold:* If AV-2 or core controls fail, release permissions must default to closed (require manual secondary sign-off) rather than failing open.

*Distinction:* An automated alert (e.g., repeated pairing, warning override) is an **indicator of potential risk or anomaly**, not definitive proof of fraud or misconduct. Alerts must prompt targeted investigative reviews, not automatic disciplinary action.
