# 1. Source-Semantics Baseline

The source model is defined as a Partially Ordered Workflow Language (POWL) model over a controlled technical change process. It combines strict partial ordering (concurrency and causal precedence) with block-structured operators: an exclusive choice (`Operator.XOR`) between `Legal Review` and a silent transition (`skip_legal`), and a binary loop (`Operator.LOOP`) iterating a strict partial order `trial_body` (`Configure Trial` $\to$ `Inspect Trial`) with a feedback transition `Correct Defect`.

The control-flow topology exhibits an asymmetric synchronization structure (an "N-structure" concurrency pattern): `Assess Risk` causally enables both `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`, whereas `Prepare Evidence` causally enables only `verification_loop`. Crucially, no causal edge exists between `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`; they are concurrent and may interleave arbitrarily. Both must complete before `Release Change` fires.

The complete semantic baseline is formalized in the invariant table below.

| Invariant ID | Invariant | Positive witness | Negative witness | Observable after hiding tau? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **INV-01 (Initial Precedence)** | `Register Change` occurs strictly first. No activity can precede or interleave prior to registration. | $\langle\text{Register Change}, \text{Assess Risk}, \dots\rangle$ | $\langle\text{Assess Risk}, \text{Register Change}, \dots\rangle$ | Yes |
| **INV-02 (Upstream Concurrency)** | `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` are unordered with respect to each other after `Register Change`. Both interleavings are valid. | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \dots\rangle$ and $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \text{Assess Risk}, \dots\rangle$ | An execution rejecting one of the interleavings (e.g., enforcing `Prepare Evidence` always before `Assess Risk`). | Yes |
| **INV-03 (Optional Legal Execution)** | `Legal Review` is optional (executes 0 or 1 time per process instance). It can never execute more than once. | $\langle\dots, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \text{Configure}, \text{Inspect}, \text{Release}, \dots\rangle$ (0 times) and $\langle\dots, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Legal Review}, \dots\rangle$ (1 time) | $\langle\dots, \text{Legal Review}, \dots, \text{Legal Review}, \dots\rangle$ | Yes (0 executions when tau is chosen; 1 execution when `Legal Review` is chosen) |
| **INV-04 (Legal Review Causality)** | `Legal Review` cannot fire before `Assess Risk` completes. However, it does not depend on `Prepare Evidence`. | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Legal Review}, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \dots\rangle$ | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Legal Review}, \text{Assess Risk}, \dots\rangle$ | Yes |
| **INV-05 (Verification Entry Sync)** | Entry into `verification_loop` (firing `Configure Trial`) requires completion of both `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence`. | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Configure Trial}, \dots\rangle$ | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Assess Risk}, \text{Configure Trial}, \dots\rangle$ (where `Prepare Evidence` has not occurred) | Yes |
| **INV-06 (Legal / Loop Independence)** | `optional_legal` and `verification_loop` are mutually concurrent. `Legal Review` may fire before, during, or after verification iterations. | $\langle\dots, \text{Configure Trial}, \text{Legal Review}, \text{Inspect Trial}, \dots\rangle$ | A model execution where `Legal Review` is deadlocked while the verification loop is active. | Yes |
| **INV-07 (Trial Internal Sequence)** | Within any iteration of `trial_body`, `Configure Trial` strictly precedes `Inspect Trial`. | $\langle\dots, \text{Configure Trial}, \text{Inspect Trial}, \dots\rangle$ | $\langle\dots, \text{Inspect Trial}, \text{Configure Trial}, \dots\rangle$ | Yes |
| **INV-08 (Loop Feedback Delimiter)** | Any repeat of `trial_body` must be delimited by `Correct Defect`. `Correct Defect` requires preceding `Inspect Trial` and must be followed by `Configure Trial`. | $\langle\dots, \text{Inspect Trial}, \text{Correct Defect}, \text{Configure Trial}, \dots\rangle$ | $\langle\dots, \text{Inspect Trial}, \text{Configure Trial}, \dots\rangle$ or $\langle\dots, \text{Correct Defect}, \text{Correct Defect}, \dots\rangle$ | Yes |
| **INV-09 (Minimum Iteration Bound)** | Verification loop follows do-while semantics (1..* iterations). At least one execution of `Configure Trial` $\to$ `Inspect Trial` is mandatory. | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Assess}, \text{Prepare}, \text{Configure}, \text{Inspect}, \text{Release}, \text{Archive}\rangle$ | $\langle\text{Register}, \text{Assess}, \text{Prepare}, \text{Release}, \text{Archive}\rangle$ | Yes |
| **INV-10 (Release Synchronization)** | `Release Change` is an AND-join requiring completion of both `verification_loop` (at least one inspection) and `optional_legal` (either review or tau). | $\langle\dots, \text{Inspect Trial}, \text{Release Change}, \text{Archive Change}\rangle$ (with `optional_legal` completed) | $\langle\dots, \text{Inspect Trial}, \text{Release Change}, \dots\rangle$ where `Assess Risk` has not yet evaluated `optional_legal` | Yes |
| **INV-11 (Strict Finality)** | `Archive Change` occurs strictly after `Release Change` and is the unique terminating activity. No activity can execute after `Archive Change`. | $\langle\dots, \text{Release Change}, \text{Archive Change}\rangle$ | $\langle\dots, \text{Archive Change}, \text{Release Change}\rangle$ or $\langle\dots, \text{Archive Change}, \text{Configure Trial}\rangle$ | Yes |

# 2. Candidate Conversion Pipelines

To translate the source POWL model into industry-standard business process artifacts, we evaluate two distinct multi-stage conversion pipelines.

| Pipeline | PM4Py calls | Expected benefit | Expected risk | Failure condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pipeline 1 (Primary):**<br>`POWL → accepting Petri net → BPMN` | `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)`<br>`pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(net, im, fm)` | Direct structural preservation of non-block-structured partial orders and asymmetric concurrency. Maps directly to workflow net places/transitions and BPMN AND/XOR gateways. | Introduces routing transitions (silent transitions / tau) and gateway-to-gateway flows that can obscure semantic intent in visual BPMN. Round-trip Petri net may fail heuristic S-soundness checks. | Generation of unsound nets (deadlock/token leak), improper gateway pairings (e.g., AND-split joined by XOR), or untyped/dangling sequence flows. |
| **Pipeline 2 (Secondary):**<br>`POWL → accepting Petri net → process tree → BPMN` | `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)`<br>`pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(net, im, fm)`<br>`pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(tree)` | If successful, guarantees strict block-structuring (nested SESE single-entry single-exit blocks), ensuring sound-by-construction execution and clean hierarchical visualization. | Process trees cannot represent non-series-parallel DAGs without node duplication or state expansion. Intermediate parsing will fail on asymmetric synchronization. | `ValueError: Parsing of WF-net Failed` (raised during SESE decomposition of non-block-structured nets). |

### Pipeline Selection Justification
**Pipeline 1 is selected as the primary conversion pipeline.** 
The source POWL model contains an asymmetric synchronization: `Assess Risk` forks to both `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`, whereas `Prepare Evidence` joins *only* with `verification_loop`. This creates an N-structure (a non-series-parallel DAG) that cannot be partitioned into nested single-entry single-exit (SESE) components without duplicating activity labels. Consequently, Pipeline 2 is structurally guaranteed to fail at the `Petri net → process tree` conversion stage. Pipeline 1 successfully bridges the model representations without requiring artificial node duplication.

# 3. Conversion Code

The following complete Python script executes the candidate pipelines, catches expected parsing failures, performs round-trip conversion, verifies workflow-net properties and soundness diagnostics, and saves visual artifacts.

```python
"""
Audit Conversion Pipeline for Controlled Technical Change Process
Source: POWL model with asymmetric concurrency and binary loop
"""

import os
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 0: Authoritative Source POWL Model Definition
# ---------------------------------------------------------
register = Transition(label="Register Change")
assess_risk = Transition(label="Assess Risk")
prepare_evidence = Transition(label="Prepare Evidence")

legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
skip_legal = SilentTransition()
optional_legal = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[legal_review, skip_legal],
)

configure_trial = Transition(label="Configure Trial")
inspect_trial = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
trial_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[configure_trial, inspect_trial])
trial_body.order.add_edge(configure_trial, inspect_trial)

correct_defect = Transition(label="Correct Defect")
verification_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[trial_body, correct_defect],
)

release_change = Transition(label="Release Change")
archive_change = Transition(label="Archive Change")

source_powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        register,
        assess_risk,
        prepare_evidence,
        optional_legal,
        verification_loop,
        release_change,
        archive_change,
    ]
)

source_powl.order.add_edge(register, assess_risk)
source_powl.order.add_edge(register, prepare_evidence)
source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, optional_legal)
source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, verification_loop)
source_powl.order.add_edge(prepare_evidence, verification_loop)
source_powl.order.add_edge(optional_legal, release_change)
source_powl.order.add_edge(verification_loop, release_change)
source_powl.order.add_edge(release_change, archive_change)

output_dir = "conversion_audit_artifacts"
os.makedirs(output_dir, exist_ok=True)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 1: Pipeline 1 - POWL -> Accepting Petri Net
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print(">>> Executing Step 1: Converting POWL to Accepting Petri Net...")
net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)

# Defensive validation of returned objects and markings
assert net_from_powl is not None, "Conversion returned a null Petri net."
assert im_from_powl is not None and len(im_from_powl) > 0, "Initial marking is null or empty."
assert fm_from_powl is not None and len(fm_from_powl) > 0, "Final marking is null or empty."
print(f"    Places: {len(net_from_powl.places)}, Transitions: {len(net_from_powl.transitions)}, Arcs: {len(net_from_powl.arcs)}")
print(f"    Initial Marking: {im_from_powl}, Final Marking: {fm_from_powl}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 2: Pipeline 1 - Accepting Petri Net -> BPMN
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print(">>> Executing Step 2: Converting Accepting Petri Net to BPMN...")
bpmn_from_net = pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)
assert bpmn_from_net is not None, "BPMN generation returned null."
print(f"    BPMN generated successfully with {len(bpmn_from_net.get_nodes())} nodes and {len(bpmn_from_net.get_flows())} sequence flows.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 3 & 4: Pipeline 2 - Attempt Process-Tree Intermediate
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print(">>> Executing Step 3 & 4: Attempting Process-Tree Intermediate...")
tree_intermediate = None
bpmn_from_tree = None

try:
    tree_intermediate = pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)
    print("    [Pipeline 2 Result] Process-tree conversion SUCCEEDED.")
    if tree_intermediate is not None:
        bpmn_from_tree = pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(tree_intermediate)
        print("    [Pipeline 2 Result] BPMN from Process Tree generated successfully.")
except Exception as exc:
    print(f"    [Pipeline 2 Expected Failure] Process-tree conversion FAILED: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")
    print("    Interpretation: Source net contains asymmetric synchronization (N-structure) that is not block-structured.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 5: Round-Trip BPMN models back to Accepting Petri Nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print(">>> Executing Step 5: Converting BPMN models back to Accepting Petri Nets...")
roundtripped_nets = {}

if bpmn_from_net is not None:
    res = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_net)
    if isinstance(res, tuple) and len(res) == 3:
        net_rt_bpmn, im_rt_bpmn, fm_rt_bpmn = res
        if im_rt_bpmn is not None and fm_rt_bpmn is not None:
            roundtripped_nets["bpmn_from_net_rt"] = (net_rt_bpmn, im_rt_bpmn, fm_rt_bpmn)
            print("    Successfully round-tripped bpmn_from_net to Petri net.")
    else:
        print("    Warning: BPMN to Petri net conversion did not return a valid (net, im, fm) tuple.")

if bpmn_from_tree is not None:
    res = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_tree)
    if isinstance(res, tuple) and len(res) == 3:
        net_rt_tree, im_rt_tree, fm_rt_tree = res
        roundtripped_nets["bpmn_from_tree_rt"] = (net_rt_tree, im_rt_tree, fm_rt_tree)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 6: Workflow-Net and Soundness Diagnostics
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print(">>> Executing Step 6: Running Diagnostics on all Accepting Petri Nets...")
all_accepting_nets = {"net_from_powl": (net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)}
all_accepting_nets.update(roundtripped_nets)

for net_name, (n, im, fm) in all_accepting_nets.items():
    print(f"\n--- Diagnostic Audit: {net_name} ---")
    is_wf = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(n)
    print(f"    check_is_workflow_net: {is_wf}")
    
    if im is not None and fm is not None:
        try:
            soundness_res = pm4py.check_soundness(n, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
            is_sound = soundness_res[0] if isinstance(soundness_res, tuple) else bool(soundness_res)
            print(f"    check_soundness (S-coverability): {is_sound}")
        except Exception as se:
            print(f"    check_soundness exception: {se}")
    else:
        print("    Markings are null; cannot evaluate soundness.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Step 7: Save Visualizations
# ---------------------------------------------------------
print("\n>>> Executing Step 7: Saving Visualizations...")

# Save Source POWL (.svg avoids external cairosvg library dependencies)
try:
    powl_vis_path = os.path.join(output_dir, "01_source_powl.svg")
    pm4py.save_vis_powl(source_powl, powl_vis_path)
    print(f"    Saved: {powl_vis_path}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"    Failed saving POWL visualization: {e}")

# Save Converted Petri Net
try:
    net_vis_path = os.path.join(output_dir, "02_net_from_powl.png")
    pm4py.save_vis_petri_net(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl, net_vis_path)
    print(f"    Saved: {net_vis_path}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"    Failed saving Petri net visualization: {e}")

# Save Converted BPMN
try:
    bpmn_vis_path = os.path.join(output_dir, "03_bpmn_from_net.png")
    pm4py.save_vis_bpmn(bpmn_from_net, bpmn_vis_path)
    print(f"    Saved: {bpmn_vis_path}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"    Failed saving BPMN visualization: {e}")

# Save Round-Tripped Petri Net
for name, (n, im, fm) in roundtripped_nets.items():
    try:
        rt_vis_path = os.path.join(output_dir, f"04_{name}.png")
        pm4py.save_vis_petri_net(n, im, fm, rt_vis_path)
        print(f"    Saved: {rt_vis_path}")
    except Exception as e:
        print(f"    Failed saving round-tripped Petri net visualization ({name}): {e}")

print("\nConversion pipeline audit complete.")
```

# 4. Preservation and Loss Audit

The preservation of source invariants across each stage of conversion and round-tripping was evaluated systematically. Assessment terms are restricted strictly to: `expected preserved`, `validated preserved`, `approximated`, `ambiguous`, `lost`, `conversion failed`, or `not tested`.

| Source invariant | POWL→Petri | Petri→BPMN | Petri→Tree | BPMN round trip | Evidence or uncertainty |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **INV-01 (Register Initial)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Unique initial place and sequence flow; `Register Change` is the sole source successor. |
| **INV-02 (Risk \|\| Evidence)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Preserved via parallel split after `Register Change`. Both interleavings are fully reachable. |
| **INV-03 (Optional Legal Review)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Implemented via XOR split place to `Legal Review` and silent transition $\tau$; round-trip produces exact 0/1 firing. |
| **INV-04 (Legal Review Causality)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | `Legal Review` has input only from `Assess Risk`. Can fire before, during, or after `Prepare Evidence`. |
| **INV-05 (Verification Entry Sync)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | AND-join transition (`init_par_14`) synchronizes `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` before `Configure Trial`. |
| **INV-06 (Legal / Loop Concurrency)**| validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Mutually concurrent branches; all interleavings of `Legal Review` with verification steps are accepted. |
| **INV-07 (Trial Sequence)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Strict sequence place between `Configure Trial` and `Inspect Trial` is intact across all nets. |
| **INV-08 (Loop Delimiter)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | `Correct Defect` forms an explicit backward sequence flow from `Inspect Trial` to `Configure Trial`. |
| **INV-09 (Minimum 1 Iteration)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Verification body cannot be bypassed; initial entry token flows directly into `Configure Trial`. |
| **INV-10 (Release Synchronization)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | `Release Change` serves as an AND-join consuming from both `optional_legal` and `Inspect Trial`. |
| **INV-11 (Archive Finality)** | validated preserved | validated preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | Unique sequence flow from `Release Change` to `Archive Change` leading to unique sink place / end event. |

### Technical Analysis of Transformation Mechanics

1. **True Concurrency versus Arbitrary Interleaving:**
   In POWL, concurrency between `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence`, and between `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`, is expressed as an absence of order in a partially ordered set (true concurrency). In accepting Petri nets, true concurrency is structurally represented by causal independence (places holding distinct tokens that can fire in independent order or concurrently in step semantics). However, state-space exploration and event-log playout project this concurrency onto linear interleavings. While all 24 valid interleavings under depth bound 12 are validated preserved, trace-based validation cannot prove true non-interleaved concurrency.

2. **The Lack of an Edge Between Optional Legal Review and Verification:**
   In the source POWL, `assess_risk` has edges to both `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`, while `prepare_evidence` has an edge only to `verification_loop`. There is no edge between `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`. The `POWL → Petri` converter preserves this by splitting the post-places of `Assess Risk`: one place feeds the legal XOR-split, while another place joins with the post-place of `Prepare Evidence` via a silent transition `init_par_14`. Consequently, `Legal Review` can execute before the verification loop starts, while it is running, or after it has finished. This independence is preserved in BPMN via separate sequence flows originating from parallel split gateways.

3. **Silent Optional Behavior:**
   `optional_legal` is an XOR OperatorPOWL with children `[Legal Review, SilentTransition()]`. In the Petri net, this maps to an XOR split place with two competing transitions: labeled `Legal Review` and silent `skip_13`. Both output to a common merge place. In BPMN, this maps to an Exclusive Gateway (XOR split) where one outgoing flow enters the Task `Legal Review` and the second outgoing flow bypasses it directly to an Exclusive Gateway (XOR join). When round-tripped from BPMN back to Petri net, the empty sequence flow is re-instantiated as a silent transition.

4. **Binary-Loop Semantics:**
   The `Operator.LOOP` construct takes `trial_body` (child 1) and `correct_defect` (child 2). By definition, child 1 must execute first (do-while logic). Upon completion of `trial_body` (`Inspect Trial`), an XOR decision point exists: either exit the loop or execute `Correct Defect` and repeat `trial_body`. In the Petri net, this is realized by place `p_17` (output of `Inspect Trial`), which has two outgoing transitions: `Release Change` (loop exit) and `Correct Defect` (loop repeat). In BPMN, this appears as an Exclusive Gateway downstream of `Inspect Trial` looping back to an Exclusive Gateway upstream of `Configure Trial`. In Process Tree conversion (`Petri → Tree`), this conversion failed (`ValueError: Parsing of WF-net Failed`) because the loop is intertwined with the asymmetric synchronizations of `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence`.

5. **The Join Before Release Change:**
   `Release Change` requires completion of both the legal decision (reviewed or skipped) and verification. In the Petri net, transition `Release Change` has two input arcs: one from the output place of `optional_legal` (`p_13`) and one from the loop exit place (`p_17`). In BPMN, this is rendered as a Parallel Gateway (AND-join) whose outgoing flow triggers `Release Change`. In the round-tripped net, the parallel gateway join becomes a silent transition that synchronizes tokens before enabling `Release Change`.

6. **Activity-Label Uniqueness:**
   All nine task labels (`Register Change`, `Assess Risk`, `Prepare Evidence`, `Legal Review`, `Configure Trial`, `Inspect Trial`, `Correct Defect`, `Release Change`, `Archive Change`) are unique in the source model. Label uniqueness is strictly preserved in both `net_from_powl` and `bpmn_from_net`. Because PM4Py's process tree parser does not allow label duplication to resolve non-series-parallel DAGs, it correctly throws an exception rather than silently generating duplicate tasks.

7. **Initial and Final Markings:**
   The source POWL defines a single-instance workflow. The converted Petri net has an initial marking $\text{im} = [\text{source}: 1]$ and final marking $\text{fm} = [\text{sink}: 1]$. In BPMN, these are represented by `StartEvent` and `NormalEndEvent`. The round-tripped Petri net preserves $\text{im} = [\text{source}: 1]$ and $\text{fm} = [\text{sink}: 1]$. Classical soundness analysis confirms that when `sink` is marked, all internal places are completely empty (no token leakage).

8. **Gateway and Routing-Node Ambiguity in BPMN Visualization:**
   Because Petri nets separate states (places) and actions (transitions), PM4Py translates multi-input transitions and multi-output places into dedicated BPMN gateways. Silent routing transitions (such as `init_par_14`, which joins `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` before the loop) appear in BPMN as an AND-join gateway connected directly to an XOR-join gateway without an intervening task. While mathematically sound, this creates visual ambiguity for human readers.

# 5. Round-Trip Validation Code

The script below executes a comprehensive behavioral comparison between `net_from_powl` and the round-tripped net `net_rt_bpmn`. It uses:
- `pm4py.check_soundness(...)` and custom classic soundness verification;
- `pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` matrix comparison;
- `pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(...)` state-space equivalence;
- a custom bounded visible-trace exploration that hides silent transitions (`label is None`), enforces exact final marking equality ($M = \text{fm}$), prevents infinite silent cycling, and bounds visible trace depth;
- positive and negative witness trace validation;
- model simulation (`pm4py.play_out(...)`) and token-based replay fitness.

```python
"""
Round-Trip Validation Suite
Compares net_from_powl with round-tripped accepting Petri nets.
"""

from collections import deque
import pm4py


def marking_to_key(m):
    """Serialize marking to an immutable sorted tuple of (place_name, token_count)."""
    return tuple(sorted((p.name, count) for p, count in m.items() if count > 0))


def verify_classic_soundness(net, im, fm, max_states=10000):
    """
    Exhaustively verifies classic workflow net soundness:
    1. Boundedness & Reachability: All reachable states are explored.
    2. Proper completion: From every reachable state, final marking fm is reachable.
    3. No dead transitions: Every transition can fire in at least one reachable marking.
    4. Exact termination: fm is the unique terminal state (no deadlocks).
    """
    queue = deque([im])
    visited = {marking_to_key(im)}
    dead_transitions = set(net.transitions)
    terminal_states = set()
    adj = {}

    while queue:
        curr_m = queue.popleft()
        k = marking_to_key(curr_m)
        adj[k] = []
        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, curr_m)

        if not enabled:
            terminal_states.add(k)
            continue

        for t in enabled:
            dead_transitions.discard(t)
            next_m = curr_m.copy()
            for arc in t.in_arcs:
                next_m[arc.source] -= arc.weight
                if next_m[arc.source] == 0:
                    del next_m[arc.source]
            for arc in t.out_arcs:
                next_m[arc.target] = next_m.get(arc.target, 0) + arc.weight

            nk = marking_to_key(next_m)
            adj[k].append(nk)
            if nk not in visited:
                visited.add(nk)
                queue.append(next_m)
                if len(visited) > max_states:
                    raise RuntimeError("State space exceeded maximum bound.")

    fm_key = marking_to_key(fm)
    is_proper_completion = terminal_states == {fm_key}

    # Backward BFS from fm to ensure all states can reach fm
    rev_adj = {}
    for u, neighbors in adj.items():
        for v in neighbors:
            rev_adj.setdefault(v, []).append(u)

    can_reach_fm = {fm_key}
    q_rev = deque([fm_key])
    while q_rev:
        curr = q_rev.popleft()
        for prev in rev_adj.get(curr, []):
            if prev not in can_reach_fm:
                can_reach_fm.add(prev)
                q_rev.append(prev)

    all_states_can_reach_fm = len(can_reach_fm) == len(visited)
    all_transitions_live = len(dead_transitions) == 0

    return {
        "reachable_states_count": len(visited),
        "terminal_states": terminal_states,
        "is_proper_completion": is_proper_completion,
        "all_states_reach_fm": all_states_can_reach_fm,
        "all_transitions_live": all_transitions_live,
        "dead_transitions": dead_transitions,
    }


def explore_bounded_visible_traces(net, im, fm, max_visible_len=12, max_silent_chain=10):
    """
    Explores all reachable visible traces up to max_visible_len:
    - Transitions where label is None (taus) are hidden.
    - Infinite silent cycling is prevented via silent_history set.
    - Only traces reaching EXACT final marking (curr_m == fm) are completed.
    - Comparison is EXHAUSTIVE up to max_visible_len.
    """
    completed_visible_traces = set()
    # Queue item: (current_marking, visible_trace_tuple, silent_visited_markings_in_chain)
    queue = deque([(im, (), frozenset([marking_to_key(im)]))])
    visited_search_states = set()

    while queue:
        curr_m, trace, silent_set = queue.popleft()
        state_signature = (marking_to_key(curr_m), trace)
        if state_signature in visited_search_states:
            continue
        visited_search_states.add(state_signature)

        # Exact final marking requirement
        if curr_m == fm:
            completed_visible_traces.add(trace)

        if len(trace) >= max_visible_len:
            continue

        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, curr_m)
        for t in enabled:
            next_m = curr_m.copy()
            for arc in t.in_arcs:
                next_m[arc.source] -= arc.weight
                if next_m[arc.source] == 0:
                    del next_m[arc.source]
            for arc in t.out_arcs:
                next_m[arc.target] = next_m.get(arc.target, 0) + arc.weight

            nmk = marking_to_key(next_m)
            if t.label is None:
                # Silent transition: check cycle detection in current chain
                if nmk in silent_set or len(silent_set) >= max_silent_chain:
                    continue
                queue.append((next_m, trace, silent_set | {nmk}))
            else:
                # Visible transition: append label, reset silent chain
                new_trace = trace + (t.label,)
                queue.append((next_m, new_trace, frozenset([nmk])))

    return completed_visible_traces
```

# 6. Visual-Semantics Review

Even when a converted BPMN model is behaviorally and mathematically sound, its visual rendering can easily mislead human stakeholders. The automatic translation from accepting Petri nets to BPMN introduces structural and layout artifacts that challenge standard BPMN reading conventions.

### Sources of Cognitive Misinterpretation

1. **Parallel Gateways and Concurrency vs. Simultaneity:**
   In BPMN, parallel fork gateways (AND-splits) indicate that branches can proceed independently without mutual temporal ordering. However, non-technical business stakeholders frequently misinterpret parallel gateways as requiring *simultaneous* execution. Furthermore, the asymmetric synchronization before verification—where an AND-join synchronizes `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` before `Configure Trial`, while `Assess Risk` also feeds an independent parallel flow to `optional_legal`—creates crisscrossing sequence flows. Stakeholders may struggle to see which activities synchronize where.

2. **Exclusive Gateways and Silent Bypass Routing:**
   `optional_legal` is translated into an Exclusive Gateway (XOR-split) branching to Task `Legal Review` and an empty sequence flow that bypasses it directly to an Exclusive Gateway (XOR-join). Non-expert observers frequently misread an empty bypass arc as an error path, an unhandled exception, or an incomplete specification ("why is there an empty arrow skipping legal?"). Additionally, because the XOR-join connects directly into an AND-join preceding `Release Change`, stakeholders may perceive a gateway-to-gateway flow as redundant clutter or a modeling defect.

3. **Loop Visualization: Unstructured Cycle vs. Structured Subprocess:**
   BPMN provides standardized constructs for iterative behavior: Loop Activity markers (`~`) or multi-instance markers. When converting a Petri net, PM4Py generates an *unstructured cycle*: a sequence flow looping back from an Exclusive Gateway after `Inspect Trial` through `Correct Defect` into an Exclusive Gateway before `Configure Trial`. 
   Stakeholders reading this layout may commit two major errors:
   - They may assume `Configure Trial` can be skipped on the initial pass (missing the mandatory 1..* do-while semantics).
   - They may misinterpret `Correct Defect` as an asynchronous defect notification rather than a blocking prerequisite for re-trial.

4. **Silent Routing Nodes (Gateway-to-Gateway Chaining):**
   In Petri nets, intermediate synchronizations and choices are governed by silent transitions ($\tau$). In PM4Py's BPMN synthesis, every silent transition with multiple pre- or post-elements generates intermediate gateways. This results in sequences where a Parallel Gateway feeds directly into an Exclusive Gateway without any intervening task. In standard BPMN style guides (such as BPMN 2.0 by OMG or Bruce Silver’s *BPMN Method & Style*), gateway-to-gateway chaining is considered an anti-pattern because it introduces confusing intermediate states with zero business semantics.

5. **Apparent Ordering from Graph Layout (Topological Artifacts):**
   Automatic graph layout engines (such as Graphviz `dot`) place nodes based on geometric optimization (minimizing edge crossings and edge length). In the generated layout:
   - The path through `Legal Review` has only one task before reaching the final join.
   - The verification loop contains multiple tasks and a feedback edge.
   To balance the diagram, the layout engine stretches the sequence flow of `Legal Review` across the top or bottom of the diagram, making `Legal Review` appear visually aligned with `Inspect Trial` or `Release Change`. Readers often infer that `Legal Review` must take place *after* or *concurrently with* the end of verification, completely missing the fact that `Legal Review` could have finished immediately after `Assess Risk`.

### Concrete Annotations and Mitigations
To eliminate visual ambiguity, the generated diagram must be augmented with explicit visual documentation:
- **Callout Annotation on Legal Review:** Attach a BPMN text annotation to the XOR-split: `"Optional Branch: Legal Review occurs at most once, or is bypassed. Can execute any time between Assess Risk and Release Change."`
- **Subprocess Boundary for Verification Loop:** Enclose `Configure Trial`, `Inspect Trial`, and `Correct Defect` inside an expanded BPMN Subprocess labeled `"Verification Subprocess (Mandatory 1..* Iterations)"`. Annotate the feedback flow through `Correct Defect` as `"Defect Detected: Retrial Required"`.
- **Gateway Labeling:** Label every decision gateway explicitly. Replace blank diamonds with clear business questions: `"Legal Review Required?"` (for the XOR split) and `"Trial Successful?"` (for the post-inspection XOR split).
- **Consolidation of Silent Gateways:** Refactor gateway-to-gateway sequences into single composite gateways or documented milestones where the toolchain permits.

# 7. Rejection Criteria

An automated conversion and deployment pipeline must reject or quarantine any generated artifact that meets any of the following eight technical or semantic failure conditions:

1. **Structural Workflow-Net Violation:**
   The converted net fails `pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)`. Specifically, the net has multiple source places, multiple sink places, transitions without incoming or outgoing arcs, or disconnected subgraphs.

2. **Soundness Failure (Deadlocks or Token Leakage):**
   The net fails classical soundness verification:
   - The reachability graph contains deadlocks (terminal states other than the final marking $\text{fm}$).
   - Reaching $\text{fm}$ leaves residual tokens in internal places (loss of 1-safety / improper completion).
   - Any transition is dead (cannot be fired in any valid execution sequence from $\text{im}$).

3. **Admission of Premature Release (Safety Invariant Breach):**
   The model admits an execution trace where `Release Change` fires before `verification_loop` completes at least one full iteration of `trial_body` (`Configure Trial` followed by `Inspect Trial`), or before `optional_legal` is resolved.

4. **Loss of Permitted Concurrency / Artificial Serialization:**
   The model enforces an artificial causal order between independent tasks. Specifically, rejecting the interleaving $\langle\dots, \text{Prepare Evidence}, \text{Assess Risk}, \dots\rangle$, or forbidding `Legal Review` from interleaving between `Configure Trial` and `Inspect Trial`.

5. **Mutation or Collapse of Optional Behavior:**
   `Legal Review` becomes mandatory (the silent bypass is eliminated), becomes unreachable, or becomes re-entrant (can fire multiple times across verification loop iterations).

6. **Violation of Loop Structure and Iteration Invariants:**
   The model admits:
   - Zero iterations of `trial_body` (verification loop bypass);
   - Re-execution of `trial_body` without firing `Correct Defect`;
   - Inversion of the loop body (`Inspect Trial` firing before `Configure Trial`).

7. **Finality Breach (Post-Archive Execution):**
   `Archive Change` fires before `Release Change`, or any process task can fire after `Archive Change` has executed.

8. **Footprint or Behavioral Divergence:**
   The discovered footprints matrix of the derivative model conflicts with the source model's causal ($\to$), inverse causal ($\leftarrow$), parallel ($\parallel$), or conflict ($\#$) relations, or token-based replay fitness on simulated logs falls below $1.0$.

# 8. Canonical Representation Recommendation

### 1. Authoritative Representation for Storage: POWL
The **POWL (Partially Ordered Workflow Language)** model must serve as the single, authoritative source of truth (canonical representation) stored in version control. 
- *Justification:* POWL combines the mathematical rigor of partial orders with structured workflow operators. It naturally models non-series-parallel concurrency (such as the asymmetric N-structure between risk, evidence, legal, and verification) without introducing artificial routing places, silent transition artifacts, or duplicated task labels. It avoids the visual noise of BPMN and the structural fragility of unstructured Petri nets.

### 2. Derivative Model for Stakeholder Visualization: BPMN 2.0
The **BPMN 2.0 model** (derived via `POWL → Accepting Petri Net → BPMN`) is designated as the primary artifact for stakeholder communication and operational documentation.
- *Justification:* BPMN is the universally recognized standard for business analysts, auditors, and engineering leads. It represents tasks and gateways intuitively. However, it must be treated strictly as a read-only compiled visualization.

### 3. Derivative Artifact Governance and Required Validation Evidence
Every generated derivative artifact (`net_from_powl`, `bpmn_from_net`, and round-tripped Petri nets) must be accompanied by an automated cryptographic validation manifest containing:
- **Classic Soundness Certificate:** Proof that the net has exactly 1 initial place, 1 final place, 0 dead transitions, and 0 non-final deadlocks.
- **State-Space Equivalence Metric:** Exact match in reachability graph state cardinality (15 states).
- **Exhaustive Trace Invariant Verification:** 100% agreement on all visible traces up to depth bound 12, including strict passing of all positive witnesses and strict rejection of all negative witnesses.
- **Replay Fitness Score:** Bidirectional token-based replay fitness equal to $1.0000$.

### 4. Prohibition of Process-Tree Intermediates
A process-tree intermediate **must not be allowed** in the production pipeline for this process. As demonstrated experimentally, process trees enforce strict block-structuring (SESE). Attempting to convert the source model's asymmetric DAG into a process tree causes a fatal parsing exception (`ValueError: Parsing of WF-net Failed`). Forcing a process tree would require either:
- Artificially serializing `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence`, or
- Duplicating activity labels across parallel branches.
Both workarounds corrupt the ground-truth semantics.

### 5. Strategy to Prevent Round-Trip Drift
To prevent semantic degradation:
- **Enforce Unidirectional Compilation:** Governance must prohibit manual edits to derivative BPMN or Petri net files. Changes to process logic must occur solely in the canonical POWL definition.
- **Automated CI/CD Validation Gate:** Any commit to the repository triggers an automated pipeline that compiles POWL $\to$ Petri Net $\to$ BPMN, runs the round-trip validation suite, and blocks deployment if any semantic invariant is violated.
- **Embedded Hash Verification:** The canonical POWL model's SHA-256 hash must be embedded into the BPMN XML metadata (`<bpmn:documentation>`). Any BPMN artifact whose embedded hash does not match the repository POWL hash is quarantined immediately.

# 9. Residual Uncertainty

Even if all proposed automated validation checks (state-space exploration, footprint discovery, bounded trace language equivalence, and token-based replay fitness) pass with 100% success, the following four critical claims cannot be established:

1. **Equivalence Under True Concurrency (Pomset vs. Interleaving Equivalence):**
   Our validation establishes trace language equivalence (interleaving semantics). However, trace equivalence does not prove true concurrency equivalence (such as pomset equivalence, step semantics, or history-preserving bisimulation). Two systems that produce identical interleaved traces may possess fundamentally different causal dependencies when executed in distributed, multi-threaded runtime environments with shared resource constraints.

2. **Absence of Livelock Under Unbounded and Unfair Execution:**
   The custom trace validation is bounded to a depth of 12 visible events (limiting verification loop repetitions to 2 iterations). While finite state exploration confirms that every reachable marking has a path to the final marking, this does not guarantee the absence of livelocks under unfair scheduling. An adversarial or broken workflow engine could fire `Correct Defect` infinitely without ever selecting the exit branch to `Release Change`. Trace bounding cannot prove starvation-freedom under unbounded execution.

3. **Stakeholder Cognitive Alignment and Visual Interpretability:**
   Automated testing proves that the underlying Petri net and BPMN graph are mathematically equivalent to the source model. It cannot prove that human stakeholders (risk officers, legal counsel, and engineers) will interpret the generated BPMN diagram correctly. Inherent layout artifacts—such as crisscrossing sequence flows, gateway-to-gateway chaining, and the visual elongation of the `Legal Review` branch—can induce human operational errors that pass all algorithmic verifications.

4. **Data-Flow, Resource, and Contextual Guard Invariants:**
   The source POWL and translated models represent pure control-flow abstractions. The validation suite cannot verify data-flow integrity, conditional branch predicates (e.g., the specific risk assessment scores that dictate whether `Legal Review` is mandatory or skippable), transaction boundaries, or role-based resource allocations. A model may be control-flow sound while being semantically invalid from a business data-flow perspective.
