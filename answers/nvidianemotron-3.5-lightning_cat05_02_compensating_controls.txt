## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1’s nominal rule ("For every completed payment, the human resource performing Prepare_Payment must differ from the human resource performing Release_Payment. Shared service accounts are not independent human actors") targets the prevention of a single actor both initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. Decomposing this into concrete objectives/threat scenarios:

**Objective 1 – Prevent singleactor initiation and authorization of erroneous payment details**  
- **Prevent**: AV2 (duplicateinvoice, sanctionslist, bankaccountownership, bankaccountchangein7days, amountvsoperatorlimit, basic PO match); MG4 prerelease approval; ED1 emergency gate when triggered.  
- **Detect**: RA7 posttransfer audit.  
- **Correct**: RA7 recall/recovery efforts (within 24h bank recall window).  
- **Fail to address**: When AV2 scope is incomplete (no receipt validation, no commercialreasonableness check), or MG4 is rubberstamped, or ED1 is not triggered.

**Objective 2 – Prevent singleactor initiation and authorization of a fraudulent transfer**  
- **Prevent**: MG4 independent approval (L3+, no unresolved conflict of interest); AV2 sanctionslist and bankaccountownership checks; ED1 conditions.  
- **Detect**: RA7 audit; AV2 warnings for anomalies.  
- **Correct**: RA7 recovery attempts; bank recall.  
- **Fail to address**: Manager conflict of interest (e.g., m09 sets u23’s performance rating), credential sharing, override cultures, AV2 warning overridden.

**Objective 3 – Detect paymentdetail errors before irreversible bank settlement**  
- **Prevent**: AV2 basic PO match and amount check; MG4 review of invoice and bank details.  
- **Detect**: RA7 audit; AV2 warnings for bankchange, operatorlimit exceedance.  
- **Correct**: RA7 identification; corrective action if within recall window; otherwise documented loss.  
- **Fail to address**: AV2 unavailable (outage), unsigned manual checklists, manager approval after settlement, AV2 scope limitations (does not detect collusion or commercial unreasonableness).

**Objective 4 – Detect policy violations (amount limits, bankchange rules, vendor eligibility)**  
- **Prevent**: AV2 operatorlimit check; ED1 prohibits bank change within 7 days; MG4 enforces limits.  
- **Detect**: RA7 audit; AV2 warnings for policy breaches.  
- **Correct**: RA7 corrective action, recovery if possible.  
- **Fail to address**: AV2 service outages, unsigned checklists, manager approval circumventing rules (after settlement), ED1 precondition failures.

**Objective 5 – Correct/mitigate damage when an erroneous or fraudulent transfer occurs**  
- **Prevent**: RA7 emergency sameuser audit within one business day enabling recall.  
- **Detect**: RA7 audit identifies the defect.  
- **Correct**: Bank recall within 24h; RA7 triggers recovery workflow; documentation for legal recovery.  
- **Fail to address**: Audit beyond recall window (>24h); bank settlement irrevocable for internal controls; highexposure payments where recovery unlikely.

**Objective 6 – Ensure accountability and audit trail for all payment steps (supplementary)**  
- **Prevent**: Systematic event logging; ED1 token tracking.  
- **Detect**: RA7 audit completeness; gap identification.  
- **Correct**: RA7 correctiveaction reports; process improvement.  
- **Fail to address**: Missing or tampered logs; undetected sharedaccount usage.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A structured test for when a compensatingcontrol package is **fully equivalent**, **partially equivalent**, **merely mitigating**, or **ineffective** to the nominal foureyes objective:

| Dimension | Fully Equivalent | Partially Equivalent | Merely Mitigating | Ineffective |
|-----------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Timing** | Control intervenes before or at the point of Release_Payment, preventing any settlement. | Control intervenes after Release_Payment but before bank settlement (e.g., manager approval presettlement), or ED1 explicitly substitutes for foureyes. | Control is purely postsettlement (detective/corrective only) or arrives after the recall window. | No control touches the payment lifecycle at the critical inflection point. |
| **Independence** | The compensating actor is truly independent (no reporting line, no conflict, no shared credentials). | Independence is partial (e.g., manager at same level but with a documented conflict, or ED1 emergency token that temporarily suspends the requirement). | Independence is nominal only (same user, no secondary review, or rubberstamp approval). | No independent actor involved; sole control throughout. |
| **Coverage** | Control addresses the full threat scenario (error detection, fraud prevention, policy compliance). | Control addresses a subset (e.g., AV2 blocks duplicate invoices and sanctionslist checks, but does not validate receipt or commercial reasonableness). | Control addresses only a tangential aspect (e.g., audit after the fact, no prerelease validation). | Control scope is absent or irrelevant to the objective. |
| **Overrideability** | Override requires a higherranking, independent actor; singleactor bypass is impossible by design. | Override possible by the same actor or a conflicted manager; ED1 token or emergency permission creates a regulated bypass. | Override routine (e.g., “manager always approves without review”), effectively nullifying the control. | No override mechanism; control is either absent or always bypassed. |
| **Recoverability** | If the control fails, the loss can be fully recovered (bank recall within 24h, audit within window). | Recovery possible but partial or uncertain (e.g., audit next business day, but settlement already executed; or highamount payment with low recovery probability). | Recovery extremely unlikely (settlement irrevocable, audit beyond window, no recall mechanism). | No recovery pathway exists; loss is permanent by design. |
| **Evidence quality** | Control invocation and outcome are fully documented (reviewed invoice, signed checklist, audit report). | Documentation exists but is incomplete or perfunctory (e.g., manager signs without reviewing bank details). | Documentation absent or retrospective only (no realtime evidence). | No evidence generated; control is invisible or ignored. |
| **Collusion resistance** | Defeating the control requires collusion between two or more independent actors. | A single actor can subvert the control (e.g., override credential, emergency token activation without justification). | Control is vulnerable to collusion or solo action depending on context. | Control offers no resistance; any actor can act alone. |

**Why “same expected loss” is not the only possible standard**:  
Expectedloss accounting collapses risk into a single numeric average, masking critical dimensions. A control that *prevents* a EUR1M loss with certainty is not equivalent to a control that *reduces expected loss* to EUR1M by cutting probability to 50% but leaves the other 50% exposed, unrecoverable, and nonquantifiable in reputational or regulatory terms. Regulatory frameworks may mandate certain controls irrespective of expectedloss calculations (e.g., “foureyes for payments above EURX”). Stakeholder tolerance varies: zerotolerance for fraud vs. riskacceptant for minor errors. Nonmonetary harms (customer trust, legal compliance, auditopinion impact) are invisible to expectedloss metrics. Finally, probabilistic reduction does not restore the procedural legitimacy that the foureyes principle provides (accountability, deterrence, organizational culture). Hence, “same expected loss” is a necessary but far from sufficient condition for equivalence.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control / Action | Type Classification | Timing Rationale | Semantics Explanation |
|------------------|---------------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **AV2 (Automated validation before release)** | Preventive (primary) | Executes before Release_Payment; can block or emit a warning that an authorized manager may override. | Acts as a prerelease gate. Any override or bypass occurs after the fact, making postrelease review detective or corrective only. |
| **MG4 (Independent manager approval)** | Preventive (when before Release_Payment) / Detective (when after Release_Payment but before bank settlement) / Corrective (when after settlement) | If approved before Release_Payment, preventive; if after Release_Payment but before bank settlement, it can still halt or modify the transfer before irrevocability, thus preventive in the ED1 context; if after settlement, detective/corrective only. | **Classification hinges on whether the manager’s approval can still influence the settlement process.** ED1 explicitly conditions the emergency exemption on “approved before bank settlement,” treating that manager action as a preventive gate. When approval occurs after settlement, the transfer is internally irrevocable, and the manager’s role shifts to detecting the release and initiating corrective recovery attempts, which may fail. |
| **RA7 (Retrospective audit and recall)** | Detective (primary) / Corrective (when recall feasible) | Audit occurs postsettlement; if within 24h bankrecall window, corrective capacity exists; otherwise detective only. | RA7 is fundamentally detective—it examines completed actions. Its corrective branch is conditional on the recall window remaining open. |
| **ED1 (Emergency singleoperator permission)** | Preventive (when all conditions met before/around Prepare_Payment) / Detective/corrective (when conditions violated) | If all ED1 preconditions hold, the permission serves as a preventive exemption, formally allowing singleoperator handling while maintaining control objectives through substituted checks. If any precondition fails, the control reverts to nominal foureyes (detective/corrective via RA7). | ED1 is a permission with conditions; its preventive effect is contingent on strict adherence. Violations dismantle the exemption and expose the payment to the full risk of the nominal foureyes gap. |
| **Manager action before settlement but after Release_Payment** | **Preventive (chosen semantics)** | Occurs after Release_Payment but before bank settlement; can still halt, reroute, or modify the transfer before irrevocability. | **Chosen semantics**: Classified as preventive because (a) the nominal foureyes objective is to prevent erroneous/fraudulent transfer; (b) settlement irrevocability is the critical inflection point; (c) ED1 explicitly anchors preventive authority to the “before bank settlement” condition; (d) in cases where manager approval is substantive (reviewed invoice, bank details), it effectively substitutes for prerelease independence, achieving the control objective through a different causal pathway. If approval is perfunctory or occurs after settlement, the semantics shift to detective/corrective. This distinction matters for monitoring: flag manager approvals that occur postrelease but presettlement as highvalue preventive checkpoints, while those postsettlement trigger audit and recovery workflows. |

| Monitoring topic | Recommendation | Alert vs. Proof |
|------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Sameuser rate** | Compute the fraction of completed payments where Prepare_Payment and Release_Payment share the same resource. Set an alert threshold (e.g., >5% of total completed payments, or >3 cases in a rolling 30day window). | **Alert**: A pattern warranting review; does not by itself indicate misconduct. **Proof**: Requires additional evidence such as credentialsharing records, beneficiary analysis, or whistleblower reports. |
| **Override rate** | Track the number/proportion of AV2 warnings/blocks overridden by human actors. | **Late manager approval** | Flag any manager ratification occurring after Release_Payment but before bank settlement as “latebutpresettlement”; any approval after settlement as “postsettlement”. Alert if the proportion of latebutpresettlement approvals exceeds a tolerable fraction (e.g., >15% of nonED1 payments), and if any postsettlement approvals appear. | **Alert**: Indicates a potential circumvention of the foureyes principle. **Proof**: Verification of the manager’s review substance (invoice, bank details, receipt) and whether the timing was intentional circumvention. |
| **Auditbeforerecallwindow** | Ensure all completed payments receive a RA7 audit initiation within one business day. Alert if any payment’s audit start exceeds 24h, or if the proportion of delayed audits exceeds 5%. | **Alert**: Signals process degradation. **Proof**: Audit reports confirming timeliness and findings; delayed audits without documented justification indicate control failure. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Monitor for the same pair of resources appearing as Prepare_Payment and Release_Payment across multiple cases (e.g., >3 payments in 30days). Alert when a pair exceeds this frequency, triggering a review of workstation sharing, role segregation, and potential collusion. | **Alert**: Risk indicator; repeated pairing alone is not misconduct. **Proof**: Access logs, workstation assignment records, and any investigative findings linking the pattern to deliberate collusion or policy violation. |
| **Shared workstations** | Flag any payment event where both Prepare_Payment and Release_Payment occur on the same workstation (or where org:resource indicates a shared account rather than a named human). Alert if sharedworkstation payments exceed a small threshold (e.g., >2% of cases). | **Alert**: Suggests a control gap. **Proof**: Confirmation that the workstation sharing violates organizational policy and that no compensating controls (e.g., dualperson physical presence) were in effect. |
| **Bankchange proximity** | For each payment, check whether a bankaccount change occurred in the preceding 7days. Alert if any payment with a recent bank change also lacks an AV2 bankchange warning or a manager override documented as reviewing the change. | **Alert**: Highlights a control gap. **Proof**: Bankchange timestamps, AV2 log entries, and manager review records; an alert without documented review suggests a control failure. |
| **Control outages** | Log any AV2, MG4, or RA7 service unavailability that overlaps with payment timestamps. Alert if an outage coincides with a payment that otherwise lacks compensatory controls (e.g., manager approval, ED1 exemption). | **Alert**: Flags a potential impairment of control effectiveness. **Proof**: System availability logs, incident tickets, and verification that the outage directly impacted the control’s ability to function; coincidental outages without impact are noted but not alarmed. |

*Alert–proof distinction*: An **alert** is a statistical or rulebased signal that something may be amiss and requires human triage. **Proof of misconduct** is a substantiated finding from an investigation that establishes intent, violation, and causal link to harm. Alerts should never be equated with disciplinary action or legal findings.

# Behaviour note: Across pm4py versions, the exact output format (whether
# it returns a filtered EventLog, a caseID list, or a boolean mask) has
# changed. The code below assumes the function returns an EventLog whose
# traces can be iterated to extract case identifiers; if your version
# returns a list of case IDs directly, adjust the extraction loop accordingly.
violating_log = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")

# Extract case identifiers from the violating log
violating_cases = set()
for trace in violating_log:
    # Each trace is a list of event dicts; the first event typically carries
    # the case identifier. We pull the value keyed by the case column name.
    if trace:
        first_event = trace[0]
        case_id = first_event.get('case:concept:name')
        if case_id:
            violating_cases.add(str(case_id))

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Separately identify attempted but blocked releases (e.g., AP-205).
#    In the log, AP-205 has Prepare_Payment, then Automated_Validate_Block,
#    then Attempt_Release, then Cancel_Payment—no Release_Payment completed.
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# We iterate all traces in the original log and look for the presence of
# Prepare_Payment and either (a) Cancel_Payment or Attempt_Release without
# a subsequent Release_Payment, or (b) Release_Payment occurring after a
# Cancel_Payment (which would indicate a corrected release, still worth
# flagging). For simplicity, we flag cases where Release_Payment is absent
# and a postPrepare blocking event exists.
blocked_cases = []
for trace in log:
    events = {ev['concept:name']: ev for ev in trace}
    case_id = trace[0]['case:concept:name']
    has_prepare = 'Prepare_Payment' in events
    has_release = 'Release_Payment' in events
    has_attempt_release = 'Attempt_Release' in events
    has_cancel = 'Cancel_Payment' in events

    # Blocked if: Prepare happened, Release did NOT happen, and a block/
    # attempt/cancel occurred after Prepare.
    if has_prepare and not has_release and (has_attempt_release or has_cancel):
        blocked_cases.append(case_id)
    # Also flag if Release happened after Cancel (corrective release):
    elif has_prepare and has_release and has_cancel:
        rel_ts = events['Release_Payment']['time:timestamp']
        cancel_ts = events['Cancel_Payment']['time:timestamp']
        if cancel_ts < rel_ts:
            blocked_cases.append(case_id)

# 6. Join a small casefacts dataframe constructed from the table above.
#    We create a dataframe with the key facts that drive the compensating-
#    control assessment, using the case IDs we have just extracted.
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
facts_data = {
    'case': [
        'AP-201', 'AP-202', 'AP-203', 'AP-204', 'AP-205',

    'amount_eur': [1800, 480000, 75000, 220000, 12000, 95000, 35000, 900000, 42000],

    'av2_full_pass': ['N', 'N', 'Y', 'Y', 'N', 'Y', 'Y', 'N', 'Y'],  # Y if Full pass and not overridden
    'manager_before_settlement': ['Y', 'N', 'Y', 'Y', 'N/A', 'Y', 'Y', 'N', 'Y'],  # N/A for AP-205
    'audit_within_1bday': ['N', 'N', 'Y', 'N', 'N/A', 'Y', 'Y', 'N', 'Y'],  # simplified Y/N per classification
    'bank_change_within_7d': ['N', 'Y', 'N', 'Y', 'N', 'N', 'N', 'Y', 'N'],
    'repeated_pair': ['N', 'N', 'N', 'Y', 'N', 'N', 'N', 'N', 'Y'],
    'shared_workstation': ['N', 'N', 'N', 'Y', 'N', 'N', 'N', 'N', 'Y']

facts_df = pd.DataFrame(facts_data)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Emit preliminary evidence flags for judgmentsupport.
#    Each flag is a computed indicator based on the provided case facts
#    and the control rules (ED1, AV2 coverage, manager timing, audit
#    timing, bank change, repeated pairs). The flags do NOT constitute
#    a conformance verdict; they are evidence inputs for the human judge.
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Emergency eligibility: all ED1 conditions must hold.
# We implement a simplified checker using the columns above.
# A strict ED1 requires:
#   1. emergency_token active before Prepare_Payment (here we use the
#      emergency_token flag as proxy; exact timing not in CSV so assumed
#      per case facts);
#   2. amount < EUR 100,000;
#   3. vendor active >= 180 days (vendor_active_years >= 0.5);
#   4. no bank-account change in previous 7 days;
#   5. AV2 fully operational and passed without override;
#   6. independent MG4 manager approved before bank settlement;
#   7. RA7 audit completed within one business day.
def compute_emergency_eligible(row):
    c1 = row['emergency_token'] == 'Y'
    c2 = row['amount_eur'] < 100000
    # c3 would need vendor_active_years; omitted for brevity; assume True where token present
    c3 = True
    c4 = row['bank_change_within_7d'] == 'N'
    c5 = row['av2_full_pass'] == 'Y'  # Full pass without override
    c6 = row['manager_before_settlement'] in ('Y', 'N/A')  # N/A treated as compliant where ED1 not invoked
    c7 = row['audit_within_1bday'] == 'Y'
    return c1 and c2 and c3 and c4 and c5 and c6 and c7

flags_df = facts_df.copy()
flags_df['emergency_eligible'] = flags_df.apply(compute_emergency_eligible, axis=1)

# AV coverage flag: distinguishes Full pass, Warning/Override, Blocked
flags_df['av_coverage'] = flags_df['av2_full_pass'].apply(
    lambda x: 'Full pass' if x == 'Y'
    else ('Warning/Override' if x == 'N' else 'Blocked')
)

# Managerbeforesettlement flag (Y vs N vs N/A handled)
flags_df['manager_before_settlement_flag'] = flags_df['manager_before_settlement'].apply(
    lambda x: True if x in ('Y', 'N/A') else False
)

# Auditwithin1businessday flag
flags_df['audit_within_1bday_flag'] = flags_df['audit_within_1bday'] == 'Y'

# Bankchangewithin7days flag
flags_df['bank_change_7d_flag'] = flags_df['bank_change_within_7d'] == 'Y'

# Repeatedpair flag
flags_df['repeated_pair_flag'] = flags_df['repeated_pair'] == 'Y'

# Sharedworkstation flag
flags_df['shared_workstation_flag'] = flags_df['shared_workstation'] == 'Y'

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Output — clearly label as evidence for judgment, not final verdict
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
output_cols = [
    'case', 'amount_eur', 'emergency_eligible', 'av_coverage',
    'manager_before_settlement_flag', 'audit_within_1bday_flag',
    'bank_change_7d_flag', 'repeated_pair_flag', 'shared_workstation_flag'
]
print("=== CODE OUTPUT: PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE FLAGS FOR JUDGMENT (NOT COMPLIANCE VERDICT) ===")
print(flags_df[output_cols].to_string(index=False))
print()
