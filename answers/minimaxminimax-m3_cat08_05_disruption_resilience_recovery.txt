# 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 – Baseline (Day -30 to Day -1, 21 business days, 42,000 cases)**
- Operational state: stable, system-driven end-to-end process with high conformance (fitness 0.96, 91% traces fit the normative model).
- Dominant variant: linear Receive order  Validate authorization  Inventory reservation  Pick  Pack  Quality/release check  Dispatch  Proof of delivery  Invoice close.
- Risk posture: low. Traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain temperature doc missing 0.4%, dual-control missing 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellation 2.1%.
- Median/p90 cycle times show clear and reasonable service differentiation across customer segments (Hospital 8h/15h, Pharmacy 23h/49h, Wholesale 36h/72h).

**T1 – Incident onset (Day 0, morning)**
- Operational state: warehouse management system (WMS) unavailable. No scan, no electronic reservation, no system proof of delivery.
- Risk posture: high and rising. All electronic control points for inventory, authorization, and dispatch disappear simultaneously.

**T2 – Manual workaround (Day 0 afternoon  Day 2, 5,600 cases)**
- Operational state: improvised process. New dominant variants emerge: Emergency triage  Skip/Manual authorization  Manual inventory snapshot  Manual pick list  Manual pack  Dispatch without scan  Phone proof of delivery  Spreadsheet invoice hold.
- Performance: Hospital-critical fill rate held at 91% but cycle time degraded (median 10h, p90 21h). Pharmacy-standard fill rate collapsed to 61% with median 54h, p90 118h. Wholesale-bulk median 96h, p90 180h.
- Risk posture: severe. Traceability exceptions rose to 9.5% (12× baseline). Cold-chain temperature documentation missing 4.8% (12×). Controlled-substance dual-control missing 2.9% (15×). Rework 12.5% (3.6×), cancellation 7.8% (3.7×).
- Conformance: fitness 0.71; only 44% of traces fit the normative model. Move-on-log deviations: Skip system authorization, Dispatch without scan, Phone POD, Spreadsheet invoice hold. Move-on-model deviations: missing Quality/release check, missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature record.

**T3 – Partial recovery (Day 3  Day 6, 11,400 cases)**
- Operational state: order intake returns, but inventory sync and scanning are unreliable. A new “Reconcile manual reservation” node appears (1,400 cases) and “Proof-of-delivery remediation” (1,200) emerges.
- Performance: Hospital 9h/18h (near baseline). Pharmacy 38h/90h (still >1.5× baseline p90). Wholesale 84h/160h.
- Risk posture: elevated. Traceability 5.4%, cold-chain doc 2.3%, dual-control 1.4%, rework 9.0%, cancellation 5.2%. Conformance fitness 0.82; 59% fitting traces.

**T4 – Stabilization (Day 7  Day 21, 35,000 cases)**
- Operational state: systems mostly restored. Linear process re-dominates, but two new tails persist: Reconcile manual reservation (1,400) and Audit remediation (2,300 cases; 2,100 reaching END via that route).
- Performance: Hospital 8h/16h at baseline; Pharmacy 28h/74h (still elevated p90); Wholesale 58h/120h.
- Risk posture: superficially recovered. Headline metrics (median cycle time, fill rate) approach baseline, but exceptions (traceability 2.2%, cold-chain doc 0.9%, dual-control 0.5%, rework 5.8%, cancellation 3.0%) remain 2–4× baseline. The audit-remediation queue is a hidden compliance backlog. Conformance fitness 0.90; 76% fitting traces — the gap to baseline is the remediation tail, not the live process.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience, in this case, is the ability to preserve patient safety and product integrity under stress, not merely the ability to keep cases moving.** The T2 metrics show that throughput preservation and resilience can diverge sharply:

- **True resilience** is shown by patterns that protected *both* flow *and* control. A (when it stayed inside the authorization check) and B are the clearest examples: the Hospital-critical fast lane preserved 91% fill rate *because* it was supported by a manual authorization check, not by skipping it. F’s 100% controlled-substance check, D used as a short-lived first cut, and I/J’s explicit prioritization logic also qualify — they absorbed shock while keeping the protective logic of the process visible.

- **Risky shortcuts masquerading as resilience** are C, G, and parts of A and F. C shipped ambient product on customer history alone; this is throughput preservation, not resilience, because the authorization control simply disappeared for those 1,500 cases. G (Dispatch without scan) is the most consequential: it produced the single largest traceability gap and forced 1,200 proof-of-delivery remediations in T4. F reduced controlled-substance errors but allowed cold-chain temperature documentation to degrade by 12×.

- **Concealed noncompliance** is patterns K and L. They look like recovery — cases do eventually reach END — but they are after-the-fact corrections of evidence that should have existed in real time. Stabilization fitness of 0.90 (vs. 0.96 baseline) is not a sign of recovery; it is a sign of a hidden workload that does not appear in median cycle time. Treating K/L as equivalent to live compliance is the most dangerous interpretation of the data.

- **Ambiguous, depending on risk appetite**, are E, H, and I. E (manual pick list) is acceptable if and only if a contemporaneous paper/photo evidence trail exists. H (phone POD) is acceptable for ambient standard pharmacy under documented conditions, but not for controlled-substances. I (deferral) is acceptable if the deferral rule is explicit and time-bounded; otherwise it is rationing by discretion.

The discriminating question is: *“If this case were selected by an external regulator three months from now, could we reconstruct what happened, who decided it, and which control was applied?”* If the answer is yes, the deviation is resilience. If the answer is “we have a spreadsheet that says so,” it is concealed noncompliance.

# 4. Recovery process model

The redesign below is described in a process-tree/POWL-style notation ( = sequence, × = parallel/exclusive choice,  = concurrency,  = optional/repeatable, * = zero-or-more). It is a *target* recovery process activated only when a qualifying disruption trigger fires.

### 4.1 Trigger conditions (any one activates the BCP path)
- WMS unavailable > 30 minutes, OR
- Order intake or dispatch scanning unavailable > 30 minutes, OR
- Inventory synchronization lag > 4 hours for Cold-chain or Controlled-substance stock, OR
- Loss of proof-of-delivery capture for > 30 minutes.

### 4.2 Triage rules (priority matrix, explicit, not discretionary)
Priority is computed as **Customer tier × Product risk × Order age**:

| Customer tier  / Product risk  | Ambient | Cold-chain | Controlled-substance | Recall-sensitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hospital-critical | P1 | P1 | P1 | P1 |
| Pharmacy-standard | P2 | P1 | P1 | P1 |
| Wholesale-bulk | P3 | P2 | P1 | P1 |

A maximum deferral SLA applies: P1  4h, P2  24h, P3  72h. After SLA breach, the order is escalated or cancelled with customer notification.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product/customer risk (binding under BCP)
- **Hospital-critical + any product:** P1, 100% authorization, 100% dual signature at release, 100% POD evidence.
- **Controlled-substance (any customer):** 100% authorization, 100% dual-control at pick and release, 100% weighbridge/photo POD, never dispatch without scan-equivalent evidence.
- **Cold-chain (any customer):** 100% authorization, 100% temperature record at pack and at release, temperature logger serial captured, never dispatch without logged temperature evidence within range.
- **Ambient + Pharmacy-standard or Wholesale-bulk:** Sample-based (10%) authorization, single-signature release acceptable, phone POD with caller ID and customer name captured.
- **Recall-sensitive:** Same as Controlled-substance, plus lot-specific pick evidence.

### 4.4 The process (POWL-style)

```
START
   [Disruption trigger check]   false  NormalProcess
                             true  ActivateBCP
ActivateBCP
   ReceiveOrder
   ApplyPriorityMatrix(P1|P2|P3)
   (P3 within deferral SLA?   yes  DeferQueue(SLA-bound)  ReceiveOrder(when SLA expires))
                             no   Continue
   AuthorizationCheck
        × ManualAuthorizationCheck(trained staff, offline eligible list, recorded in EvidenceLog)
        × SystemAuthorizationCheck(if available)
        (Skip is NOT a legal branch under BCP.)
   InventoryCheck
        × LiveInventory(if available)
        × SnapshotInventory(printed  4h old, refreshed every shift)
        (For Cold-chain/Controlled: require second-source confirmation: physical count or second human.)
   ManualPickList
         Pick (with paper pick ticket: order id, product, lot, batch, expiry, picker signature, photo)
   Pack
         QualityReleaseCheck
             × FullCheck(Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive)
             × SampleCheck(10% of other P1; 5% of P2)
   ReleaseControl
        (Controlled: dual signature + photo + weight)
        (Cold-chain: temperature logger serial + reading within range)
   Dispatch
        (No bare Dispatch-without-scan branch. Dispatch requires one of:
           - SystemScan(if available)
           - CounterSignedManifest(paper, photographed, uploaded to EvidenceLog)
           - CourierAppPOD(with signature and timestamp)
         Controlled-substance may only use CounterSignedManifest or CourierAppPOD.)
   ProofOfDelivery
        (Acceptance per segment: CourierAppPOD > CounterSignedManifest > PhonePOD(ambient only))
   EvidenceLogConsolidation
        (Every paper/photo/phone record is uploaded, indexed by order id, within 2h of capture.)
   InvoiceHold
        (Cases with incomplete EvidenceLog do NOT proceed to InvoiceClose; they go to ReconciliationQueue.)
   ReconciliationQueue
        (Backed by independent QA, not the original operator; max age 10 business days.)
   AuditRemediationQueue
        (Time-boxed; reported weekly; SLA: 95% closed within 10 business days.)
   InvoiceClose
   END
```

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization and reconciliation flow

When systems return, the stabilization path is:

```
StabilizationEntry
   BacklogAgingReport
   PrioritizeByMatrix(P1|P2|P3, then by age)
   ReconcileManualReservation
        (Compare paper pick tickets, photos, and counter-signed manifests to recovered system data.)
         four-eyes sign-off
         if matches  ResumeNormal
         if mismatch  either Cancel(with reason) or Correct(with evidence)
   AuditRemediation
        (Time-boxed; independent reviewer; tracked separately from operational KPIs.)
   ResumeNormal
```

### 4.6 What this model changes vs. T2 practice
- “Skip system authorization check” is removed as a legal branch.
- “Dispatch without scan” is replaced by a tri-modal dispatch with evidence upload.
- “Phone proof of delivery” is restricted to ambient, non-controlled, non-cold-chain.
- “Spreadsheet invoice hold” is replaced by a structured EvidenceLog that gates InvoiceClose.
- “Backlog queue” gets an explicit SLA and age-based priority.
- “Reconcile manual reservation” and “Audit remediation” are moved to independent queues with their own SLAs and reporting lines.

# 5. Resilience indicators

### Leading indicators (predictive; measured during the incident)
- **Trigger-to-BCP-activation time** — should be  15 min from declared trigger.
- **Priority matrix coverage** — % of new orders assigned a P1/P2/P3 within 15 min of receipt.
- **Manual authorization throughput** — orders per trained-staff-hour on the offline list (early warning if it collapses).
- **Snapshot freshness** — age in hours of the printed inventory snapshot in use.
- **EvidenceLog capture rate** — % of dispatched cases with uploaded dispatch evidence within 2h of dispatch.
- **Deferred order SLA compliance** — % of P3 orders picked within 72h SLA.
- **Cold-chain temperature capture rate** — % of cold-chain releases with in-range logger reading at release.
- **Controlled-substance dual-control rate** — should remain at 100% even under BCP.

### Lagging indicators (confirm outcomes; measured after the incident)
- **Throughput by segment** — Hospital fill rate, Pharmacy fill rate, Wholesale fill rate; track per day.
- **p90 cycle time by segment** — divergence from baseline is the clearest signal that non-critical segments are absorbing the shock.
- **Traceability exception rate** — should not exceed 3× baseline; sustained >5× indicates systemic control loss.
- **Cold-chain temperature documentation completeness** — 100% target, 99% acceptable, <97% is an incident.
- **Controlled-substance dual-control completeness** — 100% target.
- **Backlog age p90 and p95** — by priority class.
- **Rework / correction case rate** — T2 hit 12.5%; recovery target is  2× baseline within 7 days of stabilization.
- **Cancellation rate by segment** — to detect unfair rationing.
- **Audit-remediation queue age p90** — should fall to 0 within 10 business days of operational close.
- **Customer fairness index** — variance in service level across customer tiers; an unfair BCP shows up as Pharmacy fill rate collapsing while Hospital holds.
- **Conformance fitness** — process-mining fitness should be reported weekly; recovery target is  0.93 within 14 days.

A composite “BCP health score” can be defined as the geometric mean of (Hospital fill rate, EvidenceLog capture rate, cold-chain temperature completeness, controlled-substance dual-control rate, audit-remediation queue age within SLA). A drop of more than 10% from baseline for two consecutive days is a re-escalation trigger.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept that, during a confirmed cyber disruption, throughput will degrade and that some Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk orders will be deferred or cancelled; this is the cost of protecting Hospital-critical supply and of preserving controlled-substance and cold-chain integrity. Management should **not** accept that authorization checks are skipped for ambient products, that controlled-substance or cold-chain orders are released without 100% of their mandatory evidence, or that dispatch occurs without any contemporaneous evidence capture. The T2 data shows that these “shortcuts” did not buy much extra throughput — Pharmacy-standard fill rate still fell to 61% — and they created a hidden compliance tail (1,400 reconciliations, 2,300 audit remediations) that the stabilization metrics hide. The single piece of evidence that would change this recommendation is a demonstrated, audited BCP exercise in which the tri-modal evidence capture, the binding priority matrix, and the independent reconciliation queue together hold the controlled-substance dual-control rate at 100% and the cold-chain temperature documentation at 100% across at least 1,000 simulated cases under stress; if that can be shown, the residual risk in recommendations 1–6 is acceptable. Until then, the asymmetric bet is: protect the controls that protect patients, accept the temporary service degradation in non-critical segments, and refuse to let an apparently recovered InvoiceClose mask an unclosed EvidenceLog.
