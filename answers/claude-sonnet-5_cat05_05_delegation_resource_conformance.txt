## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 (Draft/Approve separation).** `normalized_human(Draft_Change_Request, case)  normalized_human(Approve_Change, case)`. Unconditional — applies at every risk level, no delegation can waive it because it is an identity constraint, not an authority constraint.

**RR2 (Implement/Verify separation).** `normalized_human(Implement_Change, case)  normalized_human(Verify_Implementation, case)`. Same normalization rule; a service account must first be resolved to a human before this check can be evaluated meaningfully — an unresolved service account makes the check **indeterminate**, not automatically pass or fail.

**RR3 (Highrisk approval authority).** For risk = HIGH, the human approving must be:
`QM_standing(t)` **or** ` delegation D: D.principal is QM  D.start  t  D.end  D.scope  case.scope  condition(principal_absent, t) holds  no upstream link in D's provenance chain is a prohibited subdelegation`.
All four conjuncts must hold at the *event* timestamp, evaluated against the role/delegation state at that moment — never against current master data.

**RR4 (Emergency substitution).** Applies only when `risk=HIGH  emergency=true` and the emergency/safety rationale is credible. Approval may be by IC or valid IC delegate (same validity test as RR3, with principal = IC). Retrospective_QM_Review must then be performed by a QM or valid QM delegate **whose delegation scope covers the case's scope/system**, not merely "QMadjacent."

*Businessday counting for RR4:* count forward from the date of the implementation/emergency action, excluding that day itself; count only Mon–Fri; the site calendar shows no holidays in this window, so weekends are simply skipped. Two business days after a Tuesday implementation ends at close of business Thursday; a Friday completion is late.

*Action by a principal nominally on leave:* leave does not strip standing authority — a QM can, in principle, still act while on leave, and doing so does not itself violate any rule. But it does **not** get benefit-of-the-doubt either: when a principal's own credential is used during a declared absence, the record is treated as **contested identity evidence**. It can only be credited to the principal with independent corroboration (device fingerprint matching known equipment, geolocation, MFA/biometric log, or a corroborated business justification) — self-report ("I personally handled it") is relevant but not sufficient by itself, especially where the same absence is also the stated trigger for a parallel delegation.

*Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?* No. A delegation record's *creation timestamp* in a system of record is not evidence that the delegation existed at any earlier time, especially when created during a data migration (which can backdate/represent legacy data unreliably). Only independently and credibly timestamped evidence that predates the event (e.g., a signed document, dispatched email, notarized record) can support validity for events before the database entry existed. The database entry is corroborative for events *after* its creation, not proof for events before it.

**RR5 (Identity evidence).** A service-account event is provisionally "unattributed." It becomes usable evidence only when session evidence (badge + individual reauthentication, device/token binding) maps it to one specific human with reasonable confidence. Absent that, treat the actor as unknown for SoD purposes — report as DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM, not as pass or fail. Aliases of one human (e.g., u_bk/b.koch) must be merged **before** RR1/RR2 evaluation; failing to do so can hide real violations.

**RR6 (Suspicion vs. proof).** Repeated pairs, reciprocal drafting/approval, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are *inputs to further inquiry*, never a sufficient basis alone to conclude collusion, misconduct, or innocence. They must be paired with independent corroborating evidence (content review, financial/relationship data, forensic session data, admissions) before any adverse conclusion is drawn.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

**Repeated ClaraBen pair (18/23 HIGH-risk production changes).**
- Benign: Clara and Ben are assigned to the same production cell with no rotation policy; concentration is a natural artifact of team structure and specialization, not preference for each other specifically.
- Adverse: an informal, self-reinforcing arrangement in which Ben (under a legitimately-obtained but broadly-relied-upon delegation) essentially rubber-stamps Clara's drafts, undermining the intended independence of QM approval.
- Discriminator: compare approval latency (draftapprove elapsed time) and evidence of substantive review (comments, requested revisions, redlines) for this pair against other drafter/approver pairs; interview both; check for any personal/financial relationship; sample the technical content of a subset of these 18 approvals for genuine engagement.

**Reciprocal Ben-drafts/Clara-approves (4 LOW-risk training changes).**
- Benign: small team with overlapping duties on low-materiality documents; reciprocal roles are incidental to workload distribution.
- Adverse: a mutual "I'll sign yours, you sign mine" pattern that, while individually SoD-compliant (different people drafted/approved each time), signals a broader habit of low scrutiny that also produced the CHG508 alias-driven self-approval.
- Discriminator: examine whether these four approvals show independent substantive changes/comments, or are near-instantaneous "click-through" approvals; check timestamps for realistic review duration.

**Shared workstation (WSDM04, CHG503).**
- Benign: shared terminals are common on shop floors; badge presence shows Diego was in the room but not necessarily at the keyboard for the `svc_qms` session.
- Adverse: Diego used a shared/service login specifically to avoid individual attribution while self-verifying his own implementation.
- Discriminator: individual re-authentication or session-ownership logs (absent here), CCTV (unavailable here — a genuine evidentiary gap), or interview of co-located staff at that time.

**Absence conflicts (Asha's VPN during leave; Ben's account active during sick leave from an office IP).**
- Benign: legitimate remote emergency work by Asha during leave; Ben physically returned to the office despite being on sick leave.
- Adverse: credential sharing or unauthorized use — particularly concerning because Asha's declared absence is the *stated justification* for Ben's delegation; if Asha was actually available/working, the necessity condition for DQM01 becomes questionable for other cases too, and if it wasn't Asha at all, the approval chain integrity for CHG504 collapses.
- Discriminator: device/geolocation forensics on the VPN session, office badge-in records for Ben on 0813, comparison of behavioral patterns (typing cadence, typical session behavior) against known baselines, direct reconciliation interviews.

**General principle applied throughout:** none of the above indicators, singly or combined, is treated as proof of misconduct (RR6). Each is carried into the findings as a flagged uncertainty requiring further, specific corroborating evidence rather than resolved by pattern alone.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation:** require all delegations to be created and system-registered *before* their effective start, with cryptographic timestamping; treat DB entries created during migrations as unverified until reconciled against original signed instruments.
2. **Effective-time recording:** store delegation intervals and their evidentiary basis (document hash, signature timestamp) together, with automated alerts if an activity relying on a delegation occurs before its DB record exists.
3. **Principal availability:** require a formal, timestamped "unavailable" declaration (not just an HR leave calendar) before any delegation is triggered, and log any use of the principal's own credentials during declared absence for mandatory review.
4. **Alias management:** enforce a single authoritative identity per human at the identity-provider level; prohibit parallel login names (e.g., `b.koch` vs `u_bk`) or auto-merge them before any log reaches SoD tooling.
