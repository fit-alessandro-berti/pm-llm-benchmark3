# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 – Rounding / timestamp uncertainty**
Every displayed time *t* is rounded to the nearest 5 min; the true time lies in approximately **[t  2.5 min, t + 2.5 min]**. For ordering we use a *tight-sequence convention*: when reported times are separated by  5 min the true order is very likely preserved; when they are consecutive (e.g. 08:25  08:30) we treat them as sequential with a possible true overlap of at most ±2.5 min, but we do not force a false overlap unless resource constraints require it.

**A2 – Event-order convention (mandatory)**
For any case: `arrival_s1`  true S1 start  true S1 end; S1 end  true S2 entry; S2 start  true S2 end. A case enters the S2 queue exactly when its S1 activity completes (no bypass).

**A3 – Capacity and availability**
- S1: two single-task resources (I1, I2); total availability 2 × 360 = 720 min over 08:00–14:00.
- S2: two single-task resources (C1, C2); C2 unavailable for true maintenance in **[10:55, 11:10]**; reported times near 10:55/11:10 are interpreted accordingly.
- No intentional station bypass; a resource should not serve two cases in true time, but reported conflicts < 5 min apart are treated as possible rounding or logging artifacts, not true phantom concurrency.

**A4 – Status semantics**
- **MISSING start**: activity occurred; resource and completion are known; start was not recorded. We do *not* replace it with a single number.
- **OPEN**: start is known (or inferable) but completion has not occurred by 14:00.
- **Blank S2 start/end** with completed S1: case was waiting for S2 at 14:00.
- **Blank S1 start/end**: case was waiting for S1 at 14:00.

**A5 – Capacity convention for imputation**
For MISSING S1 starts we bound the start by:
- lower: previous known completion on the same resource (resource cannot serve two at once in true time);
- upper: `s1_end  s_min`, where `s_min` is a defensible minimum service time (~20 min, derived from shortest observed complete S1 interval: P01 = 20 min);
- also: start  `arrival_s1`.
For MISSING S2 starts we apply the analogous bound on the assigned S2 resource.

**A6 – Service-time reference range**
Fully observed S1 services range 20–30 min (mean  27 min); fully observed S2 services range 25–40 min (mean  30 min). We use **[20, 40]** as a generous but defensible true-service envelope for imputation, with **25–30 min** as a central estimate.

**A7 – Censorship at 14:00**
For OPEN or waiting cases all waiting/service times are *right-censored*. We report:
- lower bound = elapsed time observed (e.g. start to 14:00);
- central = elapsed + estimated remaining based on reference mean;
- upper = unbounded for service (but practically bounded by station capacity).

**A8 – Simultaneity / simultaneity convention for WIP**
If two events have the same reported timestamp (e.g. 11:00 end and 11:00 start), we apply the sequence rule in A2: the preceding case ends first, then the next begins, unless a resource conflict proves otherwise. For discrete-event WIP counts we treat changes as ordered at the reported minute, with arrival/increment before decrement when tied.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence vs. hypothesis**
- **Evidence:** S1 completes 20 cases over 6 h with ~70–78% utilization and sustained queue growth (waiting climbs from ~5 min early to >100 min late). S2 completes 14 cases with ~65–70% utilization and has visible idle capacity (C2 gap 10:55–11:10 does not cause a downstream failure; P08 starts at 11:15 without overwhelming delay).
- **Hypothesis (defensible):** S1 is the *primary* bottleneck for the entire window because its arrival rate (~3.7/hr) relative to effective service capacity (~4/hr with 2 single-task resources at 30 min) creates long transient queues that propagate to S2.
- **Time-varying aspect:** Early (08:00–10:00) both stations are busy but S2 has slack because S1 feeds it with delay; by 10:00 the S1 queue is deep (P09–P11 waiting 60–80 min). Mid-period (10:00–12:00) S2 utilization rises as S1 releases batches (P09 at 10:35, P10 at 10:55, P11 at 11:05). Late period (12:00–14:00) S1 arrivals slow (P16–P22 spread over 2+ hours), so S1 utilization falls while S2 is fed by the large backlog, leaving 4 cases waiting at 14:00 for S2.

**Maintenance interval (10:55–11:10)**
- C2 unavailable; P06 ends at 10:50, so no active case is interrupted. P08 starts at 11:15 (reported), which is 25 min after P06 completion; true gap could be 20–30 min. The maintenance explains part of the gap but does not cause a bottleneck shift because C1 continues (P07 10:20–11:00, P09 11:00–11:40). Hypothesis: if C2 had stayed available, P08 might have started 10–15 min earlier, reducing its S2 wait by a similar amount, but S1 queue would remain unchanged.

**Bottleneck conclusion (stable under sensitivity)**
Even if all MISSING S1 starts are at their latest possible bounds (shortest service), S1 still shows longer waiting times and higher utilization than S2. Even if C2 had zero downtime, S2 utilization would rise only to ~75%, still below S1. Thus: **S1 is the dominant bottleneck; S2 is a secondary, capacity-adequate station that accumulates queue due to upstream delay.**

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

We compare three scenarios for the four MISSING starts (P05, P07, P11, P15 S1; P05, P11 S2) and for the two OPEN cases (P15 S2, P17 S2, P21 S1).

| Scenario | Imputation rule | Impact on S1 wait | Impact on S2 wait | Stable conclusions? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic** | MISSING start = previous resource end + 0 min (instant handoff); service = 20 min (min observed); OPEN service = elapsed + 10 min (fast finish). | P05 wait  30, P07  40, P11  70, P15  90; mean S1 wait  50 min. | P05 S2 wait  20; P11 S2 wait  55; open service  40 min. | S1 bottleneck: **yes** (U  75%). Total sojourn estimates drop by ~10–15% for affected cases. WIP at 14:00 unchanged because censored cases still exist. |
| **Central** (reported above) | Start = midpoint of resource-window; service = 27–30 min; OPEN = elapsed + mean (S1=27, S2=30). | Mean S1 wait  60 min; P15 wait  100; P21 service  57 min. | Mean S2 wait  35 min; open services  35–60 min. | S1 bottleneck: **yes** (U  75%). Queue length trajectory stable. Little’s Law check meaningful in 08:30–10:00 but not in late interval due to censoring. |
| **Pessimistic** | MISSING start = `end  15 min` (short service) only if resource forces it; else start as late as resource permits with service = 40 min; OPEN = unbounded (assume 60+ min). | P05 wait  35, P07  55, P11  80, P15  110; P21  60 min elapsed + long tail. | P05 wait  30; P11 wait  60; open services 70 min. | S1 bottleneck: **yes** (U  82%). Censored waits at 14:00 become larger, reinforcing that S2 is starved, not overwhelmed. Relative ordering of cases by total sojourn is preserved. |

**Censorship impact**
- Cases P16, P18, P19, P20, P22 contribute to *lower-bound* WIP and queue length at 14:00 but no completed departure rate in the last interval. Any Little’s Law check that includes 12:00–14:00 is therefore invalid because exit rate is not fully observed.
- The 14:00 WIP count (6 in process: 2 open + 4 waiting S2) is a hard lower bound for total unfinished work; true unfinished work is at least that.

**Stable vs. unstable conclusions**
- **Stable:** S1 bottleneck; C2 maintenance does not reverse bottleneck; P01–P04 are fast and unremarkable; P15 is the most stressed case.
- **Unstable / range-sensitive:** Exact total sojourn of P05, P07, P11, P15 (±15–25 min); exact final S2 service of P15/P17; exact peak WIP minute (could be 09:55 or 10:05 due to rounding).
