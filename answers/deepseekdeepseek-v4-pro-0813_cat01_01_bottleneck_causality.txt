## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mainly confounding by complexity and routing | Orion receives harder cases due routing rule; complexity increases compliance, rework, service, and wait | Orion high-complexity share 42.9% vs 12.5%; within complexity Orion is faster | Small cells; no adjustment for shift/load/unobserved risk | Propensity-score matching or weighting on complexity, compliance, shift, arrival_load; overlap/positivity diagnostics | Adjusted Orion-Nova gap shrinks, null, or flips to Orion faster; poor overlap would warn against causal claims |
| 2 | Orion beneficial but masked by case mix | Orion’s certified reviewers reduce review service and rework for same complexity | Within complexity Orion wait and review service are lower/equal; Medium rework lower | Raw Orion review service is higher; high cell sizes small | Exact or propensity matching on complexity and compliance; within-route or within-reviewer comparisons | Matched Orion cases show equal or lower review_service and rework; otherwise beneficial-effect hypothesis weakened |
| 3 | Shift/arrival-load mechanism, not group label | Orion covers night high-load work; night shift increases queueing | Orion 92.9% night vs Nova 25.0%; Orion night 23.05 h vs Orion day 7.80 h | Within complexity Orion wait is lower; Nova day includes longer cases; no clean same-shift comparison | Compare same shift/complexity/arrival-load cells; reconstruct queue-state; use shift as treatment in sensitivity analysis | If same shift/load cells show no Orion excess, shift/load explanation is strengthened; if Orion remains slower, it is weakened |
| 4 | Compliance/rework mediation from case mix | Compliance check adds process steps/queueing; rework repeats review | Orion compliance 64.3% vs Nova 25.0%; rework 28.6% vs Nova 18.8%; required cases 26.78 h vs non-required 11.95 h | Within Medium, required cases are not longer; within complexity Orion rework not higher | Front-door-like mechanism analysis; compare non-compliance/no-rework pathways; conformance variant comparison | If removing compliance/rework pathway shrinks group gap, mediation is important; if not, mechanism is insufficient |
| 5 | Completed-case sample selection distortion | Longer or harder cases may be missing differentially; conditioning on completion creates collider bias | Only completed cases present; no abandoned/open cases; group case mix differs | No direct evidence of differential selection; does not explain stratified pattern alone | Audit open/abandoned cases; sensitivity analysis for censoring; negative controls | If missing Orion cases are high-risk and long, adding them would raise Orion mean; if not, selection hypothesis weakens |
| 6 | Genuine harmful Orion effect | Orion over-review or staffing/routing practices increase service time or queue wait | Raw Orion review_service 7.04 h vs Nova 5.04 h; raw rework higher; crude cycle higher | Within complexity Orion service/wait/rework not higher; High Nova n=2 | Matched comparison; small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot; within-reviewer analysis | If harmful, matched/pilot Orion cases should have longer service or wait; if not, harmful-effect hypothesis is rejected |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic baseline and overlap audit | Confounding and support for possible reallocation | Historical cases | No operational change | DFG variants, complexity-standardized cycle, wait/service decomposition, compliance and rework rates, outcome events | Identifies whether crude gap is confounded and which strata support causal comparison | Stop if overlap is poor or adjusted contrast is unstable |
| 2 | Small randomized/quasi-random routing pilot | Direct effect of resource_group in eligible strata | Case after Classify, randomized within Low/Medium day or safe Medium night buckets | Exclude High/compliance-heavy cases initially; cap pilot share; require certified Nova capacity; monitor appeals | cycle_time, wait_before_review_h, review_service_h, rework_count, outcome_30d, DFG conformance | Produces randomized evidence for group effect and mechanisms | Roll back if cycle-time non-inferiority fails or quality/rework worsens beyond threshold |
| 3 | Queue/compliance capacity intervention | Reduce wait around compliance and night queues without changing group label | Shift/case-type/queue unit | Do not reduce compliance; maintain group staffing | queue_wait, wait_before_review_h, compliance wait, review_service, backlog, DFG performance | Tests whether wait is bottleneck and whether capacity reduces group difference | Stop if wait does not fall or cycle time worsens |
| 4 | Phased routing-rule reallocation | Reallocate only supported case types | Routing-rule version or case-type bucket | Gradual rollout; monitor quality and cycle time | Same as pilot plus process conformance | Validates whether selected reallocation improves outcomes under real operations | Stop and revert if adjusted/randomized benefit disappears or quality declines |

Final recommendation: Do not blindly reassign 50% of Orion cases to Nova. The observed Orion excess is largely explained by case mix and routing: Orion handles more High/Medium, night, and compliance-heavy work, and within complexity Orion is not slower. A blunt reallocation would likely move complex or night compliance cases into a group with less certified staffing and day-heavy coverage, risking longer waits, more rework, and worse quality. Start with diagnostic phase 1, then run a small randomized pilot in Low/Medium day work where Nova has genuine capacity. Simultaneously target queueing and compliance capacity rather than changing the group label. Use process mining to monitor variants, waiting, service, rework, and quality. Reallocate only the supported case types, and keep high-complexity night/compliance work in Orion unless Nova capacity and certification are expanded.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual unobserved case risk and confounding.**  
   Most valuable addition: a pre-assignment case-risk score or granular claim characteristics such as injury severity, legal exposure, policy limits, and prior disputes.

2. **Selection into the completed-case sample.**  
   Most valuable addition: full case-status history including completed, open, abandoned, and withdrawn cases, with timestamps and reasons for non-completion.

3. **Queueing dynamics and capacity state over time.**  
   Most valuable addition: event timestamps for arrival, classification, assignment, queue entry, queue exit, compliance start/end, review start/end, and close.

4. **Reviewer skill, certification, and staffing capacity.**  
   Most valuable addition: anonymized reviewer identifier linked to certification level, tenure, shift, and daily staffing capacity.

5. **Quality outcome coarseness and post-process effects.**  
   Most valuable addition: richer longer-term outcomes such as 90-day appeal status, appeal cost/severity, complaint records, or audit quality scores.
