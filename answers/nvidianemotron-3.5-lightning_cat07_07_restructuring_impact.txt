- “Suggestion box” channel in processmining tool for adhoc notes; qualitative coding themed around “knowledge loss,” “bottleneck,” “fairness.”

**Rollback or adjustment triggers**  
- Any guardrail metric breached for **3 consecutive days**  immediate pause of new case routing into the shared service; revert affected cases to local handling.  
- Staff sentiment survey drop >15 points in any dimension  halt expansion, conduct rootcause workshop.  
- Rework rate increase >3 pp vs. baseline  independent quality audit; if linked to routing, adjust specialtymatching rules.  
- At pilot end, if guardrails consistently violated or fairness gaps widen (control teams outperform pilot on key metrics), **do not roll out** to North/South; redesign routing rules or retain local specialists for highcomplexity cases.

# 6. Recommendation

**Proceed with a pilotfirst approach, not a full rollout.**  

The restructuring promises measurable efficiencies (fewer localspecialist loops, higher specialist utilization) but introduces central queue formation, roleclarity diffusion, and fairness/learning risks that cannot be captured by average cycle time alone. The pilot design above provides a controlled environment to validate the management estimates against real processmining data, while protecting North/South teams from untimed exposure to unknown failure modes.

**What management should NOT infer from early averages:**  
- A stable or improved *average* cycle time does **not** mean all teams or customer segments are faring equally; taillatency and segmentspecific delays may have worsened.  
- An increase in *specialist utilization* does **not** guarantee higher quality; it may reflect forced routing of cases that were previously handled locally with lower rework.  
- Reduced *handover frequency* between local specialists and embedded tech does **not** equate to process efficiency if those handovers are simply deferred to longer central queues or result in later rework.  
- *Fairness* effects are invisible in aggregate metrics; a teamlevel or segmentlevel analysis is required before claiming the restructuring is equitable.  
- *Learning* and *expertise retention* decay will not show in shortterm averages but will manifest as rising rework, longer newhire rampup, and reduced resilience during absences.

**Conditional recommendation:**  
- **Proceed** with the East pilot as defined, using the guardrail metrics and feedback loops to guide realtime adjustments.  
- **Monitor** the leading indicators (handover patterns, queue utilization, variant shifts) weekly and the lagging indicators (cycletime percentiles, rework rates, fairness indices) biweekly.  
- **Modify** routing rules or reinstate localspecialist handling for highcomplexity/legalrisk cases if pilot guardrails are consistently breached or if control teams (North/South) show no degradation while East improves.  
- **Reject** fullorganization rollout until the pilot demonstrates sustained improvements across *all* guardrail metrics, fairness is confirmed across teams and customer segments, and roleclarity/accountability structures are codified and accepted by staff.
