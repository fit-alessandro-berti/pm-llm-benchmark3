## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

### 1.1 Rule catalog

**Hard invariants**

1. **Type compatibility**:  
   - E2O relations must link an event to an object whose type is semantically compatible with the activity (e.g., `Dispatch Package` ↔ `Package`/`Delivery`, `Record Payment` ↔ `Payment`/`Invoice`).  
   - No relations to non‑existent events or objects; no invented events/objects.

2. **Direction of O2O qualifiers** (from the problem statement):  
   - `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles` are directional (`Source --qualifier--> Target`).  
   - I never use the reverse as if it were present.

3. **Uniqueness of payment objects per event** (from rule 7):  
   - Each `Record Payment` event relates to exactly one `Payment` object.  
   - I treat this as already satisfied in the data and do not add or split payment objects.

4. **Temporal feasibility**:  
   - A relation may not contradict obvious time ordering (e.g., a delivery confirmation cannot precede its dispatch).  
   - Within this log, all candidates I propose respect the timestamps.

**Strong defaults**

5. **Packing default** (rule 3):  
   - `Pack Item` normally has exactly one Item and one Package.  
   - When I see an item‑only `Pack Item` row (ev08) plus an unused package object with matching hints (PK‑8), I treat “item ↔ package” as a strong default.

6. **Dispatch default** (rule 4):  
   - `Dispatch Package` normally has one Package and one Delivery.  
   - Bulk dispatches are possible, but a 1–1 pattern is preferred when there are matching IDs and routes.

7. **Invoice/order default** (rule 6):  
   - `Issue Invoice` normally links an Invoice to one or more Orders.  
   - Matching customer and compatible total amount provide strong but not decisive evidence that an order is covered by an invoice.

8. **Payment/invoice default** (rule 7):  
   - `Record Payment` normally links a Payment to one Invoice.  
   - Matching amount and bank reference patterns create strong but not decisive evidence that a payment settles (part of) a specific invoice.

9. **Lifecycle ordering**:  
   - For a given object, activities should follow a plausible lifecycle: create → add items → pack → dispatch → confirm for logistics; issue → pay for finance.  
   - When multiple repairs are possible, I favor those that respect this order with fewer anomalies.

**Weak heuristics**

10. **Identifier similarity** (heuristic, from rule 9):  
    - Shared prefixes (e.g., `N-420` vs `N-421` vs `N-42*`, or `PK8-1` vs `PK8-2`) are treated as weak heuristics unless combined with other evidence.  
    - Scanner references (`PK7-1`, `PK8-1`, `PK8-2`) are considered strong hints for package IDs but still defeasible.

11. **Amount arithmetic** (heuristic, constrained by rule 6 and the problem constraints):  
    - `declared_value(ORD-A) + declared_value(ORD-B) = 130 + 100 = 230 = total(INV-44)` is strong but not conclusive evidence that INV‑44 covers both orders.  
    - Likewise, `amount(PAY-1) + amount(PAY-2) = 100 + 130 = 230` is strong but not conclusive evidence that both payments settle INV‑44.

12. **Route and customer consistency** (heuristic):  
    - Matching `route_hint` on packages with `route` on deliveries (all `NORTH`) is weak evidence because the route is coarse.  
    - Matching `customer=C17` across orders, invoice, and confirm‑delivery portal attributes is supportive but not decisive, because a customer may have multiple concurrent orders.

13. **O2O/E2O pattern reuse** (heuristic):  
    - Observed patterns such as `Dispatch Package` E2O edges that always relate to a `Delivery` which in O2O `carries` a `Package` are used as templates for missing edges, but only when identifiers and times also line up.

**Evidence against a repair**

14. **Mismatched key attributes**:  
    - A relation that links different customers or obviously incompatible amounts (e.g., a 100‑unit payment applied to a 230‑unit standalone invoice with no other payments) is evidence against that repair.

15. **Over‑densification of lifecycles**:  
    - If a repair forces every object to have a fully populated lifecycle (e.g., every order must be fully invoiced and paid), I treat that as suspicious over‑repair and downgrade its plausibility.

16. **Asymmetric evidence**:  
    - When one side of a candidate relation has strong, independent evidence for a different lifecycle (e.g., a payment that clearly belongs to some other invoice in a larger dataset), that would be evidence against; in this small slice we only see the absence of such evidence, so I remain cautious.

### 1.2 Evidence combination

For each candidate relation I qualitatively combine:

- **Identifier similarity**:  
  - `PK8-1` / `PK8-2` strongly suggest PK‑8;  
  - `N-420`, `N-421`, `N-42*` link ev09, ev10, ev11 to deliveries DEL‑X and DEL‑Y.

- **Time proximity**:  
  - ev06–ev08 (`Pack Item` events) are within minutes; ev09–ev10 (`Dispatch Package`) share the same timestamp; ev11 (`Confirm Delivery`) follows dispatch events by four hours; payments follow the invoice.

- **Amounts and customers**:  
  - Values 130, 100, 230 across orders, invoice, and payments;  
  - `customer=C17` everywhere relevant.

- **Lifecycle order and cardinality**:  
  - Does the candidate edge turn obvious outliers (DEL‑Y, PK‑8, IT‑B1, PAY‑2) into lifecycles that look similar to the well‑supported ones (ORD‑A, PK‑7, DEL‑X, INV‑44, PAY‑1)?  
  - Does it violate “normally 1–1” defaults?

- **Existing O2O structure**:  
  - `PK-7 packs IT-A1` plus `DEL-X carries PK-7` already form a clean chain for ORD‑A’s `IT-A1`.  
  - I look for symmetric chains for `IT-A2` and `IT-B1` using the hints around PK‑8 and DEL‑Y.

I treat these as a qualitative scoring model: where multiple strong cues (identifier, time, lifecycle shape) align and nothing strongly contradicts, I assign higher confidence (≥0.8). Where cues conflict or are very indirect, I keep candidates unresolved even if they are tempting because they would “neatly complete” a lifecycle.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### 3.1 Bundle A: Conservative / minimum‑change repair

**Logistics (ev08 and PK‑8)**  
- Accept P1 and P2.  
- ev08 is treated as a `Pack Item` event for `IT-B1` into `PK-8`; PK‑8 now packs two items, `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`.  
- This turns `IT-B1` from an unpacked item into one packed into PK‑8, mirroring the structure seen for items A1 and A2 without introducing new objects.

**Dispatch (ev10 and DEL‑X/DEL‑Y)**  
- Accept P3 and P4.  
- ev10 is the dispatch of `PK-8` via `DEL-Y`, symmetric to ev09 dispatching `PK-7` via `DEL-X`.  
- DEL‑Y becomes the delivery object for PK‑8 and, by extension, for the items `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`.

**Confirm Delivery (ev11 and deliveries)**  
- Keep P5 and P6 unresolved: ev11 remains linked only to `ORD-A`, as in the observed data.  
- Semantics: the portal confirmation may be order‑level (rule 5), possibly referencing a delivery implicitly via `external_ref=N-42*`, but we do **not** commit to a specific Delivery object.  
- DEL‑X and DEL‑Y thus have dispatch but no explicit confirmation event in the data; this is acceptable given the possibility of portal data loss.

**Invoice INV‑44 and ORD‑B**  
- Keep P7 and P8 unresolved.  
- INV‑44 remains billed only to `ORD-A` both in O2O and E2O.  
- Arithmetic and customer matching are treated as strong hints but not decisive; ORD‑B could be billed separately or later.

**PAY‑2 and INV‑44**  
- Keep P9 and P10 unresolved; PAY‑2 is modeled as unapplied (or not yet matched) cash.  
- This aligns with rule 7 which allows unapplied cash and avoids forcing every payment to match an invoice.

**Coherence of lifecycles in Bundle A**

- **ORD-A**:  
  - Created (ev01) → items added (ev03, ev04) → items packed (ev06, ev07) → packages dispatched (via PK‑7/DEL‑X and PK‑8/DEL‑Y) → order‑level confirm delivery (ev11) → invoiced (ev12/INV‑44) → partially paid (ev13/PAY‑1); possible further payment (PAY‑2) left unresolved.  
  - This lifecycle is rich but still admits some uncertainty in the settlement stage.

- **ORD-B**:  
  - Created (ev02) → item added (ev05) → item packed (ev08 into PK‑8) → dispatched (PK‑8 via DEL‑Y) → no explicit confirm delivery, invoice, or payment.  
  - This creates a plausible but incomplete lifecycle: the order was shipped but may not yet have been invoiced or paid in this log slice.

Overall, Bundle A focuses on obvious logistics completions while explicitly **not** forcing financial edges that are only weakly indicated.

### 3.2 Bundle B: More aggressive but still plausible interpretation

Bundle B keeps the same logistics repair as Bundle A (P1–P4) but additionally accepts P7–P10 to create a more “complete” financial story.

**ev08 and PK‑8**  
- Same as Bundle A: IT‑B1 is packed into PK‑8 via ev08; PK‑8 packs both IT‑A2 and IT‑B1.

**ev10 and DEL‑X/DEL‑Y**  
- Same as Bundle A: ev10 dispatches PK‑8 via DEL‑Y; DEL‑Y carries PK‑8.

**ev11 and the delivery objects**  
- P5 and P6 remain unresolved.  
- In Bundle B I still **do not** assign ev11 to a specific delivery, to avoid violating the constraint against unique assignment without strong justification.  
- Interpretation: ev11 is an order‑level confirmation for ORD‑A (possibly summarizing multiple carrier confirmations), not a clean one‑to‑one mapping to DEL‑X or DEL‑Y.

**INV‑44 and ORD‑B**  
- Accept P7 and P8.  
- INV‑44 bills both ORD‑A and ORD‑B (O2O).  
- ev12 relates to both ORD‑A and ORD‑B (E2O).  
- Story: the customer C17 received one consolidated invoice for both orders whose declared values sum exactly to the invoice total.

**PAY‑2 and INV‑44**  
- Accept P9 and P10.  
- PAY‑1 and PAY‑2 both settle INV‑44 (O2O), covering 100 + 130 = 230.  
- ev13 and ev14 are both linked to INV‑44 (E2O).  
- Story: INV‑44 is fully settled via two payments with separate bank references.

**Coherence of lifecycles in Bundle B**

- **ORD-A**: lifecycle as in Bundle A, but invoice and payments now fully settle the associated invoice.  
- **ORD-B**: now shares the same invoice and payments as ORD‑A, leading to a fully observed financial lifecycle: created → item added → packed and dispatched → (implicitly) delivered with ORD‑A → invoiced on INV‑44 → fully paid via PAY‑1 and PAY‑2.  
- **INV‑44**: becomes a genuine multi‑order invoice, fully paid by two payments.  
- **PAY‑2**: no longer unapplied; participates in a standard invoice‑settlement process.

Bundle B is attractive for downstream analytics (no unapplied cash, both orders fully billed) but rests heavily on arithmetic and customer‑matching heuristics, so I treat it as plausible, not uniquely correct.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

### 5.1 Orders

**ORD-A**

- **Lifecycle in Bundle A**:  
  - `Create Order` ev01 → `Add Item` ev03, ev04 → `Pack Item` ev06, ev07 → `Dispatch Package` ev09 (via PK‑7/DEL‑X) and ev10 (indirectly via PK‑8/DEL‑Y because PK‑8 packs IT‑A2) → `Confirm Delivery` ev11 (order‑level) → `Issue Invoice` ev12 / INV‑44 → `Record Payment` ev13 / PAY‑1 (and possibly PAY‑2, unresolved).  
- **Cardinality**:  
  - One order, multiple items, multiple packages, potentially multiple deliveries and payments. This is realistic for an order that ships and is paid in partial installments.  
- **Orphans and anomalies**:  
  - None strictly orphaned; some uncertainty remains about which delivery objects and payments truly belong to ORD‑A.  
- **E2O vs O2O support**:  
  - The item relations ORD‑A contains IT‑A1/IT‑A2 and `Add Item` events provide independent support.  
  - The link from ORD‑A to deliveries is weaker: it is inferred via items → packages → deliveries plus ev11; I avoid treating that as independent confirmation to prevent circular reasoning.

**ORD-B**

- **Lifecycle in Bundle A**:  
  - `Create Order` ev02 → `Add Item` ev05 → `Pack Item` ev08 (via PK‑8) → `Dispatch Package` ev10 (via PK‑8/DEL‑Y).  
- **Cardinality**:  
  - Single item order with one package and one delivery; no invoice or payment observed.  
- **Orphans and anomalies**:  
  - Financially, ORD‑B is an orphan: no invoice or payment directly attached.  
  - This is acceptable and even expected in a slice; the order may not yet be invoiced or paid in this dataset.  
- **E2O vs O2O support**:  
  - `ORD-B contains IT-B1` plus `Add Item` ev05 and `Pack Item` ev08 provide consistent evidence.  
  - Any invoice/payment links would be purely inferential; by design, Bundle A does not add them.

### 5.2 Items and packages

- **Items IT-A1, IT-A2**:  
  - Clean lifecycles: added to ORD‑A, packed into PK‑7/PK‑8, dispatched via DEL‑X/DEL‑Y.  
  - Each item has one order and one package, matching rule 2 and rule 3’s defaults.

- **Item IT-B1**:  
  - In the original log, IT‑B1 is added to ORD‑B and appears in ev08 without a package; O2O has no `packs` edge.  
  - In Bundle A, IT‑B1 acquires a package PK‑8 via ev08 and P2, giving it the same pattern as IT‑A2.  
  - No suspicious one‑to‑many beyond “one item → one order, one package”.

- **Packages PK-7, PK-8**:  
  - PK‑7: packs IT‑A1, dispatched via DEL‑X.  
  - PK‑8 (after repair): packs IT‑A2 and IT‑B1, dispatched via DEL‑Y; a valid one‑to‑many relation (package with multiple items).  
  - No package has multiple deliveries; this respects a natural “one package → one delivery” assumption.

### 5.3 Deliveries

- **DEL-X**:  
  - Carries PK‑7; dispatched at ev09.  
  - No explicit confirmation event attached, but ev11 plausibly refers to it; I keep that as unresolved.  
- **DEL-Y** (Bundle A):  
  - Carries PK‑8; dispatched at ev10.  
  - Also lacks an explicit confirmation; again, ev11 might implicitly cover it but we do not enforce that.

**Suspicious relations or patterns**

- There is a potential many‑to‑many pattern at the conceptual level: one `Confirm Delivery` event might conceptually confirm multiple deliveries. However, since we do not create E2O links from ev11 to DEL‑X/DEL‑Y, this many‑to‑many does not appear in the explicit relation tables for Bundle A.

**Circular reasoning note**

- I avoid concluding “DEL‑Y is confirmed delivered” solely because I first hypothesize ev11 → DEL‑Y and then treat the resulting chain as evidence; instead I keep ev11 ↔ deliveries unresolved.  
- Similarly, `DEL-Y carries PK-8` (P4) is not treated as independent evidence for `ev10 → DEL-Y` (P3); both share the same underlying identifier and time heuristics.

### 5.4 Invoice and payments

- **INV-44**:  
  - Observed: bills ORD‑A and is linked to ev12; paid partially by PAY‑1.  
  - Bundle A: remains a single‑order invoice with partial payment; no forced match to ORD‑B or PAY‑2.  
  - This yields a common pattern: partly paid invoice with outstanding balance or later payments outside the slice.

- **PAY-1**:  
  - Clean lifecycle: `Record Payment` ev13 ↔ PAY‑1 ↔ INV‑44 via `settles`.  
  - Strongly supported by both E2O and O2O.

- **PAY-2**:  
  - Orphan payment in Bundle A: has its own `Record Payment` ev14 but no invoice.  
  - This is consistent with rule 7’s “unapplied cash is possible”.  
  - Any attempt to attach PAY‑2 to INV‑44 would rely mainly on amount arithmetic and timing, which is why P9/P10 stay unresolved.

**Suspicious cardinalities**

- Under Bundle B (not selected but important for comparison), INV‑44 would have a many‑to‑many relationship with orders (ORD‑A and ORD‑B) and multiple payments (PAY‑1, PAY‑2). This is semantically acceptable but denser and more assumption‑heavy than the conservative Bundle A.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Here I compare three datasets: **unrepaired log**, **Bundle A**, and **Bundle B**.

### 6.1 OC-DFG (object-centric directly-follows graph)

- **Unrepaired log**:  
  - IT‑B1 has a truncated lifecycle: `Add Item` → `Pack Item` (item‑only) with no subsequent dispatch; PK‑8 has packing for IT‑A2 only and dispatch without any connection to IT‑B1.  
  - DEL‑Y is not connected to any events; PAY‑2 is only a `Record Payment` → `Payment` fragment with no invoice context.

- **Bundle A**:  
  - New directly‑follows fragments appear for `IT-B1` and PK‑8:  
    - For `IT-B1`: `Add Item` ev05 → `Pack Item` ev08 → `Dispatch Package` ev10 (via PK‑8/DEL‑Y).  
    - For PK‑8: `Pack Item` ev07 → `Pack Item` ev08 → `Dispatch Package` ev10.  
  - DEL‑Y now participates in `Dispatch Package` ev10 → (implicit) `Confirm Delivery` not attached; still, in OC‑DFG per Delivery, DEL‑Y now has at least one event.  
  - No new DFG edges for financial activities relative to invoices and orders (those changes are only in Bundle B).

- **Bundle B**:  
  - In addition to Bundle A’s edges, order‑ and invoice‑centric DFGs gain:  
    - For ORD‑B: `Create Order` → `Add Item` → `Pack Item` → `Dispatch Package` → `Issue Invoice` → `Record Payment` (PAY‑1) → `Record Payment` (PAY‑2).  
    - For INV‑44: `Issue Invoice` → `Record Payment` (PAY‑1) → `Record Payment` (PAY‑2).  
  - These extra edges could bias analyses towards seeing fully settled, end‑to‑end lifecycles, potentially hiding partially paid or uninvoiced cases.

### 6.2 OTG (object type graph)

- **Unrepaired log**:  
  - Types and edges such as Order–Item (`contains`), Package–Item (`packs`), Delivery–Package (`carries`), Invoice–Order (`bills`), Payment–Invoice (`settles`) exist but PK‑8, DEL‑Y, and PAY‑2 are under‑connected instances of their types.

- **Bundle A**:  
  - No new *types*, but denser edges at instance level:  
    - The Item–Package and Package–Delivery patterns become more consistently instantiated: both PK‑7 and PK‑8 now have items and a delivery; IT‑B1 now participates in Item–Package.  
  - OTG edge multiplicities for `Package -> Delivery` and `Item -> Package` increase, reinforcing those as typical relations.

- **Bundle B**:  
  - Strengthens `Invoice -> Order` and `Payment -> Invoice` edges with more instances (multi‑order invoice, multi‑payment settlement).  
  - The OTG may now show stronger connectivity between financial and logistic types via shared orders than in Bundle A.

### 6.3 ET–OT graph (event type – object type)

- **Unrepaired log**:  
  - ev08 is only linked to `Item`; ev10 only to `Package`; ev11 only to `Order`; ev14 only to `Payment`.  
  - ET–OT shows a somewhat sparse mapping of event types to object types.

- **Bundle A**:  
  - `Pack Item` now consistently maps to both `Item` and `Package` (via ev08’s new link).  
  - `Dispatch Package` consistently maps to both `Package` and `Delivery` (via ev10’s new link).  
  - `Confirm Delivery` remains mapped only to `Order` (ev11), which is acceptable per rule 5.  
  - `Record Payment` for PAY‑2 still maps only to `Payment`, highlighting an unapplied cash pattern in ET–OT.

- **Bundle B**:  
  - `Record Payment` becomes consistently linked to both `Payment` and `Invoice` for *both* PAY‑1 and PAY‑2.  
  - `Issue Invoice` maps to both `Invoice` and two `Order` objects.  
  - This makes ET–OT denser for financial event types, but at the cost of more assumptions.

### 6.4 Flattenings on specific object types

**Order flattening**

- **Unrepaired**: ORD‑A and ORD‑B differ strongly; ORD‑B lacks dispatch for its item, and neither invoice nor payment.  
- **Bundle A**: ORD‑B gains a complete logistics path but still lacks finance; ORD‑A shows partial settlement. Equivalent‑execution clustering around orders will separate “fully shipped, partially paid” (ORD‑A) from “shipped, not yet billed/paid” (ORD‑B).  
- **Bundle B**: ORD‑A and ORD‑B become very similar when flattened: both show end‑to‑end from creation to full payment. Clustering will likely group them together, potentially hiding the fact that ORD‑B’s invoicing and payment links are less certain.

**Package flattening**

- **Unrepaired**: PK‑8’s flattened trace shows packing only IT‑A2 plus dispatch; IT‑B1 seems not shipped.  
- **Bundle A/B**: PK‑8’s flattened trace includes packing IT‑A2 and IT‑B1 and dispatch via DEL‑Y. This creates coherent package lifecycles and avoids false non‑shipments of IT‑B1.

**Delivery flattening**

- **Unrepaired**: DEL‑Y is almost an orphan: it appears only via attributes, not via events or O2O `carries`.  
- **Bundle A/B**: DEL‑Y’s flattening becomes mirror image of DEL‑X’s: a `Dispatch Package` event with a concrete package. Still no explicit confirmation events, which is realistic.

**Invoice flattening**

- **Unrepaired/Bundle A**: INV‑44 shows `Issue Invoice` and a single `Record Payment` (PAY‑1).  
- **Bundle B**: INV‑44’s flattened view includes both PAY‑1 and PAY‑2, and connects to both orders. This looks very “clean” but may give analysts false security about full settlement and multi‑order billing.

**Payment flattening**

- **Bundle B**: PAY‑2 flattening becomes analogous to PAY‑1’s, losing the orphan pattern and potentially hiding interesting exceptions.

### 6.5 Activity frequencies and clustering

- **Event counts** stay the same across all versions; only object–event associations change.  
- **Object‑based activity frequencies** (e.g., “how many orders see a `Record Payment`?”) change materially:  
  - Bundle A: only ORD‑A has a payment;  
  - Bundle B: both ORD‑A and ORD‑B have payments, and both see two payment events, increasing the apparent prevalence of split payments.

- **Equivalent-execution clustering around Order**:  
  - Unrepaired: ORD‑B looks like a short, possibly incomplete case; ORD‑A a richer case.  
  - Bundle A: clusters likely separate “logistics‑complete, finance‑partial” (ORD‑A) from “logistics‑complete, finance‑missing” (ORD‑B).  
  - Bundle B: both orders appear almost identical in behavior; clusters may collapse these into a single family, overstating process regularity.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

At least five ways in which an overly aggressive repair could create false process behavior in this dataset:

1. **Forcing INV‑44 to bill ORD‑B when it does not**:  
   - Would inflate the number of invoiced orders and make ORD‑B appear fully billable at 230’s arithmetic convenience, even if in reality it was on a separate invoice or still pending.

2. **Treating PAY‑2 as definitively settling INV‑44**:  
   - Could hide unapplied or misallocated cash, make INV‑44 look fully and promptly settled, and distort KPIs such as “days sales outstanding” and “percentage of invoices paid on time”.

3. **Attaching ev11 uniquely to DEL‑X or DEL‑Y**:  
   - Might mark one delivery as confirmed even if the portal confirmation was order‑level or multi‑delivery; this would misrepresent delivery reliability and on‑time performance per carrier or route.

4. **Reassigning IT‑B1 to a different package or order to “balance” weights or values**:  
   - For example, moving IT‑B1 to PK‑7 or even to ORD‑A solely to equalize values would break the already well‑supported `ORD-B contains IT-B1` relation and could corrupt analyses of order composition and picking accuracy.

5. **Creating synthetic O2O edges by symmetry alone**:  
   - Automatically adding `DEL-X carries PK-8` just because `DEL-X carries PK-7` exists (or vice versa) would fabricate extra logistics flows without event‑level support, overstating carrier workload and route utilization.

6. **Assuming every payment must be linked to some invoice**:  
   - Aggressively matching all standalone `Record Payment` events to invoices based on amount or timing would erase informative exception cases like unapplied cash, refunds, or prepayments.

7. **Inferring confirm‑delivery events for every delivered package**:  
   - Generating artificial `Confirm Delivery` relations or even new events to close every delivery lifecycle would bias conformance checking and falsely improve perceived service levels.

By keeping Bundle A conservative and explicitly labeling unresolved relations, the repaired log remains useful for analysis while preserving important uncertainties instead of turning them into misleading certainties.
