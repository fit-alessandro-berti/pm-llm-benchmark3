## Burden of Proof

**For declaring noncompliance (Prosecution):** The prosecution must show that a case deviates from the binding normative framework (CS-9 and M0 as formally controlled documents) and that no valid exception (formal amendment, controlled pilot, emergency directive within scope) applies. The prosecution need not prove intent or harm—conformance is the standard. However, the prosecution bears a **heightened burden** when the deviation is attributable to a defect in M0's encoding, log completeness, or scope definition rather than to an operational choice.

**For accepting an exception (Defense):** The defense must demonstrate either (a) a formally authorized exception that covered the case at the time of execution, or (b) that the alleged deviation is an artifact of model incompleteness, data quality, or scope misapplication—not a genuine behavioral nonconformance. The defense bears the burden of establishing that informal practice has been ratified by sufficiently authoritative action. Mere desirability or prevalence is insufficient.

**For scope, identity, and logging disputes:** When the scope document is ambiguous or when data quality makes conformance assessment unreliable, I apply a **precautionary approach**: the case is classified as DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE or MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT rather than compliant, and remediation is required regardless of which side's interpretation is correct.

# 2. Prosecution Brief

## 2.1 The Normative Framework Is Clear and Has Not Been Amended

CS-9 is the controlled, formally approved compliance standard. It has not been amended. M0 is the normative POWL model derived from CS-9. Neither PD-3 nor FD-7 nor stakeholder desire constitutes an amendment to either document. The steering committee that issued PD-3 did not follow the amendment process that produced CS-9. FD-7 was explicitly time-limited and self-expiring. Operations' desire for permanent auto-approval has "no permanent policy amendment exists"—their own source concedes this.

The prosecution does not argue that exceptions are impossible. It argues that exceptions require formal authorization within scope, and that the burden of demonstrating such authorization lies with the party invoking the exception.

## 2.2 PD-3: Authority Defect and Scope Overreach

PD-3 was a steering-committee decision "not incorporated into CS-9 or M0." The Compliance observer wrote that the "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception," but later stated she "had not approved weakening CS-9." This is not a formal CS-9 exception—it is a monitored experiment that the compliance function did not ratify as a standard deviation.

Even granting PD-3 maximum deference as a valid pilot:
- It applied only to **LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups**.
- **SUP-703** is a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier where an operator selected "pilot" despite ineligibility. This is not a pilot deviation—it is a compliance failure within the pilot's own terms. The EUR 180,000 first purchase amplifies risk. The prosecution notes that PD-3's eligibility fields were apparently not system-enforced, allowing manual override.
- **14 cases are clearly ineligible** per PD-3's own criteria and **9 have missing eligibility attributes**. The pilot's governance was insufficient to prevent misuse.
- PD-3 expired 2026-07-31. **SUP-714** (2026-08-02) used Auto_Approve two days after expiry with no policy in place. Operations' belief that "permanent approval was imminent" is not authorization.

## 2.3 FD-7: Strict Temporal Limits Were Breached

FD-7 is the strongest defense case because it was an emergency directive with explicit conditions. The prosecution acknowledges that **SUP-704** (2026-07-11, within FD-7's window, EUR 18,000, daily monitoring, retrospective quality/approval next business day) is a textbook justified emergency exception.

However, **SUP-705** (2026-07-13, one day after FD-7 expired) used the identical pattern with no written extension. Operations' claim that "the flood emergency still factually existed" is precisely the type of informal justification that emergency governance is designed to prevent. If factual persistence were sufficient, emergency directives would never constrain. The prosecution insists: expired authority is expired authority. If the emergency persisted, the remedy was to seek an extension, not to operate on assumption.

## 2.4 Activation Before Sanctions Clearance (SUP-708)

This is the most alarming case. A HIGH-risk supplier was activated **40 minutes before sanctions clearance** with no emergency or pilot justification. No transactions occurred before clearance, which is fortunate but irrelevant to conformance. CS-9's requirement that sanctions be checked before activation is not a guideline—it is a **compliance control**. The fact that no harm occurred is a matter of luck, not compliance. This case represents the precise risk that CS-9 exists to mitigate.

## 2.5 Same-Human Requester/Approver (SUP-712)

CS-9 explicitly requires different humans. SUP-712's requester and manual approver resolve to the same person through two aliases. The prosecution acknowledges that M0 does not encode this rule, but CS-9 is a compliance standard that exists independently of M0. The rule's absence from the model does not exempt it from enforcement. The system's failure to detect this is a **control gap** that must be remediated, but the case remains noncompliant.

## 2.6 Notification Absence

M0 requires Notify_Requester before Close_Case. For cases where the notification event is absent from the log (SUP-706) or only partially captured, the prosecution argues that:
- If the event is absent and CRM evidence is unreliable (mutable text fields), conformance **cannot be established**.
- 70% capture rate means ~30% of cases are in a compliance gray zone.
- This is not merely a logging problem—it is a **control evidence** problem. An auditor cannot verify what is not reliably recorded.

## 2.7 Desirable Practice Does Not Amend Policy

Operations commonly uses Request_More_Info cycles. Compliance agrees this is desirable. But desirability is not authority. CS-9 has not been amended to include Request_More_Info. M0 has not been revised. Until the normative framework is formally updated, the practice exists in a gap that the prosecution flags for remediation—but does not grant retroactive compliance.

## 2.8 Aggregate Evidence of Normalization

The prosecution observes a pattern:
1. PD-3 normalized Auto_Approve as a routine practice (312 cases in two months).
2. This normalization persisted beyond PD-3's expiry (6 cases in early August).
3. PD-3's eligibility controls were insufficiently enforced (14 clearly ineligible, 9 with missing attributes).
4. Operations now treats Auto_Approve as a permanent feature ("believed permanent approval was imminent").
5. This is **exception creep**: temporary exceptions become assumed entitlements.

The prosecution demands that CS-9 and M0 be enforced prospectively, that the 14 clearly-ineligible Auto_Approve cases be reviewed, and that the 6 post-pilot August cases be flagged as noncompliant pending review.

## 2.9 Prosecution Acknowledgment of Genuine Defects

The prosecution concedes:
- M0 is incomplete in not encoding Request_More_Info, the resource rule, or reactivation procedures.
- The CRM migration created genuine data-quality problems that affect notification conformance assessment.
- SUP-709's scope is genuinely ambiguous under the current documentation.
- SUP-711's duplicate is a migration artifact, not an operational failure.
- SUP-713's label equivalence has post-hoc audit support.
- M0's lack of encoding for the requester/approver resource rule makes system-level detection impossible without model or system enhancement.

These defects are **real** but do not excuse operational noncompliance—they explain detection failures and require model/data remediation alongside operational enforcement.

# 3. Defense Brief

## 3.1 M0 Is an Incomplete and Partially Obsolete Representation of the Process

The defense begins with a structural observation: M0 was designed to represent CS-9's control flow. It does so partially. It fails to represent:

1. **Request_More_Info loops**: 47 cases contain this activity. Compliance agrees it is desirable. M0 cannot express it. This is not a case of operational deviation—it is a case of model inadequacy. An incomplete model will inevitably generate "deviations" against behavior that the process legitimately performs. If M0 were updated tomorrow to include Request_More_Info, SUP-707 would instantly become compliant. The deviation is in the model, not the operation.

2. **The resource rule**: CS-9 requires different humans for requester and approver. M0 cannot encode actor assignments. SUP-712's same-human problem is a **system control gap**, not a process failure by the operators. The defense distinguishes between "operators violated a rule they could see" and "a rule existed only in a document that no automated system enforced." The latter is a governance failure, not an operational one.

3. **Reactivation procedures**: SUP-710 lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review because the supplier had been registered and quality-certified six months prior. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. The process owner's decision to reactivate rather than re-register is reasonable—the supplier's certifications were valid. The defense argues this is a **scope defect** in CS-9 and M0, not a compliance failure.

4. **Rejected leads**: SUP-709 follows a pre-screen rejection. The process owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance. The model owner says M0 governs the entire intake. The scope document is ambiguous. The defense argues that applying M0's full sequence to a lead that was rejected at intake is **over-inclusion**—Quality_Review for a supplier that failed sanctions is pointless overhead. M0's XOR node includes Reject_Supplier, but the positioning of rejection before quality review is operationally rational.

## 3.2 PD-3: A Valid Monitored Exception

PD-3 was authorized by a steering committee, the governance body responsible for operational decisions. The defense does not claim PD-3 amended CS-9. It claims PD-3 created a **monitored exception**—a controlled deviation tracked by the compliance function. The Compliance observer's initial statement ("pilot may proceed as a monitored exception") is the relevant authorization. Her later retraction ("had not approved weakening CS-9") reflects a change of opinion, not a retroactive nullification.

The defense acknowledges:
- **SUP-703 is genuinely noncompliant**: a HIGH-risk ineligible supplier was wrongly processed under the pilot. This is a case where operational error (or misconduct) caused harm regardless of model validity. The defense does not defend SUP-703.
- **14 clearly ineligible cases** using Auto_Approve are problematic and require review. The defense agrees this is a real risk.
- **9 cases with missing eligibility attributes** are DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE—insufficient evidence to classify.

However, for the **289 PD-3-eligible cases**, the defense argues: these cases were processed under a valid, monitored exception during its effective window, with compliance oversight. They are JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, not NONCOMPLIANT.

## 3.3 FD-7 and SUP-705: Emergency Reasonableness

The defense concedes that SUP-705 (2026-07-13) falls outside FD-7's explicit temporal scope. However, it argues:

1. FD-7's strict expiry was designed for a **planning horizon**, not as a declaration that flooding ceased on July 12.
2. SUP-705 involved EUR 12,000 in initial spend—well within FD-7's EUR 20,000 cap.
3. The operational need was real: continued flood recovery.
4. The defense distinguishes between "expired authority" and "illegitimate action." SUP-705's operators acted reasonably given the factual circumstances, even if they should have sought a written extension.

The defense classifies SUP-705 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION with a governance gap (missing extension document), not NONCOMPLIANT.

## 3.4 CRM Migration Is a Data Problem, Not a Process Problem

SUP-706 has no Notify_Requester event in the onboarding log, but CRM contains a message at +3 hours. The mutable text field is a logging defect, not evidence that notification didn't occur. The defense argues:

- 29% notification missingness post-migration is a **data quality artifact** of the CRM integration, not evidence that 29% of cases skipped notification.
- The root cause is the system migration, not operational noncompliance.
- Cases with CRM evidence of notification (even with weaker linkage) should be treated as DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, not NONCOMPLIANT.

## 3.5 Label Equivalence (SUP-713)

QA_Clearance is a renamed Quality_Review. The mapping was approved one week after the case, and audit documents show equivalent content. The defense argues this is a **label synchronization gap**, not a process deviation. Activity renaming is a normal system evolution. The defense classifies SUP-713 as COMPLIANT with a note about the timing of the mapping approval.

## 3.6 M0 Cannot Be the Sole Arbiter of Compliance

The defense's overarching argument: **M0 is a model, not reality**. It represents a partial, outdated snapshot of CS-9's control flow. It cannot express:
- Data-aware conditions (risk level, spend amounts, eligibility)
- Resource assignments
- Temporal constraints on pilots and emergencies
- Loops for information gathering
- Different case types (new onboarding, reactivation, rejection at intake)

Using M0 as the sole compliance oracle will produce false positives (SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-713) and miss genuine risks (SUP-703's eligibility misclassification, SUP-712's same-human problem, SUP-715's conflict of interest) because M0 does not encode the relevant rules.

## 3.7 Defense Acknowledgment of Genuine Risk

The defense concedes:
- **SUP-708 is genuinely noncompliant**: activation before sanctions clearance of a HIGH-risk supplier with no justification. This is a clear control failure.
- **SUP-703 is genuinely noncompliant**: ineligible use of Auto_Approve for a HIGH-risk supplier.
- **SUP-714 is noncompliant**: post-expiry use of Auto_Approve with no policy in place. Operations' normalization is understandable but not excusable.
- **14 clearly PD-3-ineligible Auto_Approve cases** represent real risk and require review.
- **SUP-715's conflict of interest** is a gray area: no explicit prohibition, but conflict-of-interest policy requires disclosure that was not recorded. This is at minimum a disclosure failure.
- The defense does **not** argue that prevalence creates validity. Widespread Auto_Approve use beyond PD-3's scope and expiry is a normalization risk that must be stopped.
- The defense acknowledges that **some** Request_More_Info cases, while desirable, may have been inserted in ways that delayed compliance-critical activities, and this should be monitored.

### Model Validity

M0 is **partially obsolete and incomplete**. It correctly encodes CS-9's core control flow for the standard happy path but fails to represent:
- The Request_More_Info loop (universally desired, non-harmful, 47 cases)
- The resource/segregation-of-duties rule (CS-9 requirement, unencoded, SUP-712)
- Reactivation procedures (no CS-9 provision, SUP-710)
- Scope for rejected leads (ambiguous, SUP-709)
- Any data-aware conditions (risk level, spend amounts, eligibility, temporal validity)
- Pilot and emergency overlay mechanisms

M0 serves as the control-flow backbone but cannot be the sole compliance oracle. It requires augmentation with data-aware rules, resource constraints, policy overlays, and explicit scope definitions.

### Where I Reject Each Side's Arguments

**Against the Prosecution:**
- The prosecution's insistence that M0 deviations constitute noncompliance without qualifying for model defects overstates the problem. SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, and SUP-711 are not operational failures—they are model/scope/data defects that require remediation of the framework, not punishment of the operation.
- The prosecution's position that "expired authority is expired authority" regarding SUP-705, while formally correct, understates the governance failure that caused the situation. Operations acted in good faith on a factual emergency; the failure was in not seeking a written extension. The remedy should address the governance gap, not merely the operational deviation.
- The prosecution's treatment of notification absence as a process failure (rather than a data quality issue) would create false positives. The CRM migration's data loss is a system problem requiring system remediation.

**Against the Defense:**
- The defense's argument that prevalence or desirability creates validity is rejected. 312 Auto_Approve cases do not create a permanent policy. 47 Request_More_Info cases do not amend M0. Compliance requires formal authorization, not popularity.
- The defense's treatment of PD-3 as a valid "monitored exception" is partially accepted but bounded: PD-3 was valid for its scope and duration (289 eligible cases), but its eligibility controls were insufficient (14 ineligible + 9 missing = 23 problematic cases out of 312). The pilot's governance was inadequate.
- The defense's argument for SUP-705 is sympathetic but rejected. Factual persistence of an emergency does not create authority. If it did, emergency directives would be meaningless. The defense acknowledges this is a "governance gap"—I agree and classify it as NONCOMPLIANT with the recommendation that emergency extension procedures be formalized.
- The defense's acceptance of SUP-714 as noncompliant is correct and I concur.
- The defense's argument that SUP-712 is a "system control gap, not an operational failure" is partially accepted but insufficient: the case remains noncompliant regardless of whether the system could detect the violation. The system must be fixed, and the case must be flagged.

# 6. Revised Normative Architecture

## 6.1 Architectural Overview

The revised architecture uses a **layered approach** where each layer has a clear purpose, encoding mechanism, and governance authority:

```
Layer 1: Control-Flow Model (POWL)
   Expresses sequencing, concurrency, loops, and choice
   Encoded in PM4Py POWL classes
  
Layer 2: Data-Aware Constraints (DECLARE/log-skeleton-style)
   Expresses eligibility, amount, temporal, and risk conditions
   Encoded as declarative constraints with pandas-style validation
  
Layer 3: Resource and Governance Rules (pandas/data checks + organizational controls)
   Expresses segregation of duties, conflict of interest, delegation
   Encoded as data validation + organizational policy
  
Layer 4: Policy Overlays (Organizational controls with system enforcement)
   Expresses pilot programs, emergency directives, and their scope/temporal limits
   Governed by formal amendment process; encoded as parameterized rule sets
  
Layer 5: Scope Rules (Documentation + model configuration)
   Defines case types (new onboarding, reactivation, rejection, withdrawal)
   Determines which model/layers apply to which case type
```

## 6.2 Layer 1: Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL)

The revised POWL model expands M0 to include:

1. **Concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review** for the standard route (retained from M0).
2. **Standard manual-approval route**: Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier (retained from M0).
3. **Low-risk auto-approval route**: Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier (new, subject to data-aware eligibility constraints in Layer 2).
4. **Emergency/provisional route**: Provisional_Activate  (retrospective quality + approval within 2 business days) (new, subject to emergency directive authority in Layer 4).
5. **Repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism**: Implemented as a loop in POWL, approximated by allowing Request_More_Info to repeat before the checks node.
6. **Rejection and closure**: Retained from M0.
7. **Notification before closure**: Retained from M0.

**POWL limitations acknowledged:**
- POWL cannot express that Auto_Approve requires LOW-risk + approved corporate group + active pilot.
- POWL cannot express that Provisional_Activate requires an active emergency directive + EUR 20K cap.
- POWL cannot express that Request_More_Info must stop before checks begin.
- POWL cannot express the resource rule (different humans) or conflict-of-interest constraints.
- These are handled by Layers 2–4.

## 6.3 Layer 2: Data-Aware Constraints

Encoded as declarative constraints validated against event/log data:

| Constraint | Rule | Encoding |
|---|---|---|
| C1: Sanctions before activation | `timestamp(Sanctions_Check) < timestamp(Activate_Supplier)` | DECLARE existence/precedence |
| C2: Quality before activation (standard) | `timestamp(Quality_Review) < timestamp(Activate_Supplier)` when `route = standard` | DECLARE with conditional |
| C3: Quality within 2 days post-activation (pilot/emergency) | `timestamp(Quality_Review) - timestamp(Activate_Supplier)  2 business days` when `route  {pilot, emergency}` | pandas timedelta check |
| C4: Manual approval before activation (standard) | `timestamp(Manual_Procurement_Approval) < timestamp(Activate_Supplier)` when `route = standard` | DECLARE precedence |
| C5: Auto_Approve eligibility | `risk_level = LOW AND corporate_group  approved_groups AND pilot_active = TRUE` | pandas boolean mask |
| C6: Emergency eligibility | `emergency_directive_id IS NOT NULL AND directive_active(case_date) = TRUE AND spend  EUR 20,000` | pandas conditional |
| C7: Notification before closure | `timestamp(Notify_Requester) < timestamp(Close_Case)` with stable `notification_id` | DECLARE precedence |
| C8: Request_More_Info before checks | All `Request_More_Info` events precede `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` | DECLARE precedence |
| C9: Retrospective approval (emergency) | `Manual_Procurement_Approval` exists within 2 business days of `Provisional_Activate` | pandas timedelta check |

## 6.4 Layer 3: Resource and Governance Rules

| Rule | Source | Encoding |
|---|---|---|
| R1: Requester  Manual Approver | CS-9 | pandas identity check on actor_id fields (after alias resolution) |
| R2: Conflict of interest disclosure | Conflict-of-interest policy | pandas check: `coi_disclosure_recorded = TRUE` when relationship_flag = TRUE |
| R3: Quality reviewer  Manual approver | Best practice (derived from CS-9 segregation intent) | pandas identity check |
| R4: Pilot eligibility operator cannot override system | PD-3 governance lesson | System-enforced eligibility check; manual override requires documented justification |

## 6.5 Layer 4: Policy Overlay Governance

Pilot programs and emergency directives are modeled as **parameterized rule sets** with explicit:

- **Authority source**: Formal document ID, authorizing body, approval chain.
- **Effective window**: Start datetime, end datetime, auto-expiry flag.
- **Scope conditions**: Risk level, supplier geography, category, spend cap.
- **Overridden rules**: Which base rules (Layers 1–3) are relaxed or replaced.
- **Monitoring requirements**: What oversight exists during the overlay.
- **Extension process**: How the overlay can be extended (cannot rely on "factual persistence").

Governance requirement: Policy overlays must be **registered in a policy overlay registry** before they take effect. The registry enforces that overlays have complete authority, scope, temporal, and monitoring specifications.

## 6.6 Layer 5: Scope Rules

| Case Type | Definition | Model/Layer Applicability |
|---|---|---|
| **New onboarding** | Supplier not previously registered; full due diligence required | All layers |
| **Reactivation** | Previously registered supplier with lapsed status; prior certifications may be valid | Modified Layer 1 (skip Register_Supplier if identity match confirmed; re-evaluate Quality_Review validity per CS-9 reactivation policy to be defined) |
| **Rejection at intake** | Pre-screen failure (e.g., sanctions); supplier never enters full onboarding | Abbreviated model: Register_Supplier  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case; Quality_Review not required |
| **Withdrawal** | Requester voluntarily withdraws before decision | Register_Supplier  (partial checks)  Withdraw_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case |
| **Post-decision amendment** | Material change after activation requiring re-evaluation | Trigger re-evaluation of affected checks; not a new onboarding |

# 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

## 7.1 Python Code: Revised POWL Model

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# === Activity Transitions ===
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
retro_approval = Transition(label="Retrospective_Approval")
retro_quality = Transition(label="Retrospective_Quality_Review")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
silent = SilentTransition()

# === Request_More_Info Loop ===
# Loop(L, B) executes L, then either exits or executes B then L again.
# Here: execute Request_More_Info, then either exit (proceed) or 
# execute a silent tau (re-gather), then Request_More_Info again.
# POWL Loop semantics: Loop(child1, child2) = execute child1, then
# choose to exit OR execute child2 then child1 again.
# 
# We want: optionally repeat Request_More_Info before checks begin.
# Approximation: Loop that allows 0..N Request_More_Info iterations.
# Loop(request_more_info, silent) means:
#   execute request_more_info, then XOR(exit, execute silent then request_more_info)
# This allows 1, 2, 3, ... iterations but not 0.
#
# To allow 0 iterations: XOR(silent, Loop(request_more_info, silent))
# But POWL Loop with a SilentTransition child2 is unusual.
# 
# SAFER APPROXIMATION: Use XOR to optionally include a bounded loop.
# We construct: XOR(silent, loop_body) where loop_body = Loop(request_more_info, silent)
# This means: either skip all Request_More_Info, or do at least one with optional repeats.
#
# NOTE: This is an approximation. A true "0 to N" loop would ideally use
# a different formalism. The silent transition in the loop's second child
# allows re-entry to request_more_info after each iteration.

loop_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_more_info])
# Loop with request_more_info as first child and silent as second child
# means: do request_more_info, then XOR(exit, do silent then request_more_info again)
info_gathering = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[request_more_info, silent])
optional_info = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[silent, info_gathering])

# === Concurrent Checks (Standard and Pilot routes) ===
# Sanctions_Check is always required before activation.
# Quality_Review timing depends on route:
#   - Standard/Manual route: concurrent with sanctions, both before approval
#   - Auto-approve route: quality may occur up to 2 days post-activation
#   - Emergency route: quality within 2 days post-provisional-activation
#
# We model the "checks" node as concurrent sanctions and quality for the 
# standard route. For pilot/emergency routes, quality is deferred (encoded
# in Layer 2 data constraints, not in POWL sequencing).

checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# No order edges between sanctions and quality = concurrency in POWL

# === Standard Approved Branch ===
# Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier
standard_approved = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
standard_approved.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# === Low-Risk Auto-Approve Branch ===
# Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier
# Quality_Review timing is deferred to Layer 2 (within 2 business days)
# POWL cannot express the temporal quality constraint, so we model the
# sequence and note the constraint externally.
auto_approved = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate])
auto_approved.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)

# === Emergency/Provisional Branch ===
# Provisional_Activate  Activate_Supplier (activate happens at provisional point)
# Retrospective approval and quality within 2 business days (Layer 2 constraint)
# We model: Provisional_Activate  Activate, then XOR(exit, retro_approval then retro_quality)
# Approximation: emergency path activates, then retrospective checks are required.
emergency_activate = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[provisional_activate, activate])
emergency_activate.order.add_edge(provisional_activate, activate)

# Retrospective checks after emergency activation
retro_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[retro_approval, retro_quality])
# Allow parallel retrospective checks or sequential; we'll make them concurrent
# but both must complete (enforced by Layer 2 timing constraint)

# Emergency full path: activate provisionally, then retrospective checks
emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[provisional_activate, activate, retro_checks]
)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(provisional_activate, activate)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate, retro_checks)

# === Decision Node ===
# XOR between:
#   1. Standard approved path (checks  manual approval  activate)
#   2. Auto-approve path (checks  auto_approve  activate) 
#   3. Emergency path (provisional_activate  activate  retro checks)
#   4. Reject
#
# But we need to model that checks precede the decision for standard and 
# auto-approve routes. For emergency, sanctions must precede activation
# but quality can be deferred.
#
# REVISED APPROACH: Build the decision as a choice among complete paths
# from checks to close.

decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_approved, auto_approved, emergency_branch, reject]
)

# === Full Model ===
# Register  optional Request_More_Info  checks  decision  notify  close
# 
# IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURAL NOTE:
# The checks node (concurrent sanctions + quality) applies to the standard 
# and auto-approve routes. For the emergency route, only sanctions is 
# required before activation. POWL cannot express conditional concurrency,
# so we model the standard path and note that the emergency route 
# requires only Sanctions_Check before Provisional_Activate (Layer 2).
#
# This is a known limitation: the POWL model over-constrains the emergency
# path by including Quality_Review in the concurrent checks node. The 
# emergency route's quality deferral is enforced by Layer 2 data-aware 
# constraints overriding the POWL sequencing for cases tagged as emergency.
#
# PRACTICAL COMPROMISE: We build the model to express the standard flow
# correctly and document the emergency deviation as a Layer 2 overlay.

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, optional_info, checks, decision, notify, close]
)
M1.order.add_edge(register, optional_info)
M1.order.add_edge(optional_info, checks)
M1.order.add_edge(checks, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# === Model Limitations (must be documented) ===
# 1. POWL cannot express that Auto_Approve requires data conditions
#    (LOW-risk, approved group, active pilot). Enforced by Layer 2.
# 2. POWL cannot express that emergency route requires only Sanctions_Check
#    before activation (not full checks). Enforced by Layer 2.
# 3. POWL cannot express the EUR 20,000 spend cap for emergencies.
#    Enforced by Layer 2.
# 4. POWL cannot express resource rules (requester  approver).
#    Enforced by Layer 3.
# 5. POWL cannot express 2-business-day retrospective quality/approval.
#    Enforced by Layer 2.
# 6. The emergency branch includes retrospective checks, but the POWL
#    model's checks node still precedes the decision. For emergency cases,
#    the quality component of checks may be skipped (Layer 2 override).
#    This creates a tension: the POWL says quality happens before the 
#    decision, but the emergency route says it happens after activation.
#    RESOLUTION: For emergency cases, the system should use the 
#    emergency_branch path which starts at decision, bypassing the 
#    checks-before-decision constraint. In a conformance checker, 
#    emergency-tagged cases should validate against the emergency 
#    branch template, not the standard checks-before-decision template.

# Print model structure for verification
print("=== Revised POWL Model M1 ===")
print(f"Nodes: {[n.label if hasattr(n, 'label') else str(n) for n in M1.nodes]}")
print(f"Edges: {[(e[0].label if hasattr(e[0], 'label') else str(e[0]), e[1].label if hasattr(e[1], 'label') else str(e[1])) for e in M1.order.graph.edges()]}")
print(f"Decision children: {[str(c) for c in decision.children]}")
print(f"Optional info children: {[str(c) for c in optional_info.children]}")
print(f"Loop structure: {info_gathering.operator} with children {info_gathering.children}")
```

## 7.2 POWL Limitations Statement

The revised POWL model **M1** is the best defensible approximation within POWL's expressiveness. The following behaviors cannot be safely or correctly encoded in a single POWL model and are handled by external layers:

1. **Conditional routing by risk level / eligibility**: POWL does not support data-aware guards. The XOR node includes Auto_Approve and Emergency branches, but the *activation* of these branches depends on data conditions enforced by Layer 2. Without Layer 2, the POWL model alone would allow any case to take any branch—this is **by design** in the layered architecture.

2. **Quality_Review timing divergence**: In the standard route, Quality_Review is concurrent with Sanctions_Check and precedes activation. In the pilot and emergency routes, Quality_Review may occur after activation. The POWL model includes Quality_Review in the `checks` node (concurrent with sanctions), which over-constrains the emergency path. The emergency branch's `retro_checks` node addresses this by including retrospective quality, but the model cannot prevent Quality_Review from also appearing in the checks node for emergency cases. **Resolution**: Conformance checking should apply route-specific templates, not a single monolithic trace alignment.

3. **2-business-day retrospective window**: POWL cannot express temporal constraints. Enforced by Layer 2 pandas timedelta checks.

4. **EUR 20,000 spend cap**: Data-aware constraint, Layer 2.

5. **Resource rules**: Actor assignment constraints, Layer 3.

6. **Conflict-of-interest disclosure**: Organizational control, Layer 3.

7. **Emergency directive validity**: Temporal and authority constraints, Layer 4.

## Summary of Final Judgment

**System-level verdict**: The operation exhibits **genuine noncompliance** in 5 of 15 cases (SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714), with SUP-715 borderline. **Recommended action**: Enforce CS-9 prospectively with the layered architecture above. Remediate the HIGH-severity cases immediately. Update M0 to M1 with the layered augmentation. Fix the CRM notification linkage. Formalize pilot and emergency governance. The prosecution and defense are each partially correct—enforcement without model reform will produce false positives, and model reform without enforcement will permit genuine risk.
