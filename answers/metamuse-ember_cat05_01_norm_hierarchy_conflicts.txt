## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts taken as given**

Amounts, supplier status, stated purposes, named actors, timestamps and the contents of Sources A to D are taken as stated. The ERP outage on 2026-07-14 08:30 to 13:00 CEST can add up to 12 minutes to ERP timestamps. The ECD-2 window is 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST. The Chief Risk Officer was unavailable and did not later ratify ECD-2. Incident Command members are not expressly stated to be budget owners. The CFO may interpret Finance controls, but no document states that the CFO may waive Compliance controls or GPIP-4 segregation of duties.

**Interpretive assumptions, not facts**

1. **No automatic trump.** Global scope, later issuance and strong wording are each only one factor. Weight is assessed by issuer authority for the specific issue, specificity to that issue, temporal validity at the moment of the act, fit with the control purpose, and whether a waiver is express or silent.
2. **Later-in-time is authority-bound.** ECD-2 is later than the Aachen SOP but is issued by the COO and Incident Commander, not the board. It therefore can displace ordinary local workflow, but later date alone does not displace a board integrity policy. The CFO email is later than ECD-2 but is an ordinary, uncontrolled email, so it is treated as an interpretation, not a board amendment.
3. **Specificity and silence.** ECD-2 expressly addresses PO timing and supplier-register use. Those local rules can be overridden within ECD scope and window. ECD-2 is silent on segregation, Finance approval and Compliance clearance. Silence is not read as a waiver of the specific GPIP-4 controls in A1 to A3, especially as A4 states that emergency status waives tendering only unless a written Chief Risk Officer exception is issued under A7.
4. **Primary treatment of ECD-2 as an A7 exception.** ECD-2 is not treated as an A7 exception. A7 reserves written, scoped, time-limited exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer. ECD-2 does not mention the Chief Risk Officer, GPIP-4 or A7, and was not ratified by the Chief Risk Officer. Prior organisational practice is noted, but it has not covered new-supplier Compliance clearance.
5. **Primary treatment of the CFO email.** Within the ECD window and up to EUR 150,000, the CFO email is given effect as a Finance-control interpretation that joint Incident Command authorisation is the operational approval for Finance and ordinary local approval purposes. It is not treated as waiving A1 segregation or A3 Compliance, because the CFO has no stated authority over those controls, and D4 preserves essential integrity controls. Above EUR 150,000 the email requires Legal clearance as well, not instead of Finance approval.
6. **Emergency necessity is not a freestanding authorisation.** A purchase must still meet the relevant definition of emergency or ECD scope, fall inside the time window, and satisfy the authorisation procedure for that regime.
7. **Critical scope.** “Critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event” is primarily read as parts required for the affected line within the next 24 hours to contain the event or restore that line. Backup-line parts with several days of stock are normally outside that scope unless documented imminent failure is shown. The Operations view is acknowledged as a credible alternative in Section 5.
8. **Retrospective evidence.** The real-world time of the act controls, not the later database entry. Contemporaneous supplier acknowledgements, telephone recordings, access logs and signed PDFs can prove that an act was timely even if the ERP timestamp is later. A late approval is different from late evidence of a timely act. Under A6, later approval mitigates but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
9. **Burden of proof.** The party invoking an exception must prove authority, scope, window, actor distinctness and timely recording. If a required record is absent, the exception is treated as not established. Missing non-required actor identity is treated as inability to prove an element, not automatically as the same substantive breach, but it prevents a COMPLIANT rating where that element is material.
10. **Thresholds are applied at commitment.** Amount and supplier status are assessed at Commit_to_Supplier. “Unapproved” is not automatically equated with “new supplier” for A3 unless evidence shows the supplier was new.

**Classification definitions**

COMPLIANT means no baseline deviation arises under the applicable rules. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION means a baseline rule is departed from but a valid exception or override in force at that moment covers the deviation. NONCOMPLIANT means a material deviation is not covered. INDETERMINATE is reserved for cases where a decisive fact cannot be established and the missing fact is not itself a required record. Severity describes residual control impact if the classification stands. Confidence describes confidence in the primary classification under the stated interpretation, not certainty that no alternative hierarchy could be defended.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501: alternative JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The classification would change if B2 were read as requiring Plant and Finance approval to exist before payment or PO finalisation rather than before an emergency commitment, and if SHIFT-1 were treated only as committer rather than as Plant substitute. That reading is credible because B2 lacks the express “before commitment” language found in A2 and B1, and emergency purchasing often requires a provisional commitment. It is weaker on process purpose because approval after commitment does not test the commitment itself, and B5 preserves Finance approval in emergencies. Actor evidence that Plant_Approve came from the Plant Manager would strengthen the alternative.

**P-502: alternative JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The classification would change if ECD-2 were treated as an A7 exception despite the absence of the Chief Risk Officer, and if the CFO phrase “all operational approvals” were read to include the new-supplier Compliance check. Prior treatment of signed incident directives as overlays gives this some credibility. It remains weak because A4 and A7 reserve exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer, the Chief Risk Officer did not ratify ECD-2, D3 limits the CFO to Finance controls, and prior practice never involved new-supplier Compliance clearance.

**P-503: alternative JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only on a stacked premise.** Operations would need to prevail on the meaning of critical to include backup-line pumps, ECD-2 would need to count as an A7 exception, the CFO email would need to cover Finance and Plant approvals at EUR 140,000, and A1 would need to be read as prohibiting only sole final approval rather than participation as one of two authorisers. Each premise is contestable. The alternative is therefore much weaker than the primary classification because segregation and Compliance clearance are integrity controls, not ordinary workflow steps.

**P-505: alternative NONCOMPLIANT.** The classification would change if the CFO email were treated as a non-binding ordinary email incapable of interpreting or satisfying GPIP-4 A2, and if the ERP timestamp were preferred over supplier evidence. The formalist premise is credible because A2 is a board policy and Incident Command members are not expressly budget owners. The timing part of the alternative is weak because the 17:59:31 acknowledgement and nine-minute clock correction strongly prove a pre-18:00 real commitment. The Finance-authority premise, not the timing premise, is the credible route to NONCOMPLIANT.

**P-507: alternative NONCOMPLIANT or logging-defect-only.** The classification would change if only ERP entry counted as recording under C3, if the CFO email could not satisfy Finance approval, or if the unnamed committer were in fact an authoriser. The ERP-only premise is credible only if the organisation defines recording as ERP posting rather than creation of a signed record. It is weakened by the signed PDF at 12:40, which is within four hours and is the kind of contemporaneous evidence the outage note directs attention to. Committer identity remains the more material missing proof for segregation.

**P-508: alternative JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The classification would change if “obtain Legal clearance as well” were read as a complete above-cap substitute regime in which Compliance, Legal and joint Incident Command authorisation functionally replace Finance and Plant approval, or if ECD-2 counted as an A7 exception. That reading is weak because “as well” ordinarily means in addition, Legal is not the Finance Controller or budget owner, the CFO cap is expressly EUR 150,000, and no text waives A2.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Policy breach** means a substantive pre-commitment control was missing or late. Examples are P-501 Plant and Finance timing, P-502 late Compliance, P-503 segregation and missing Compliance, P-504 unapproved supplier use, P-506 unauthorised post-expiry commitment and P-508 missing Finance approval. Retrospective approval can mitigate loss or supplier risk but does not rewrite the historical breach.

**Logging defect** means the act occurred on time but the system record was created late or incompletely. P-507 is primarily treated as late ERP logging of a timely signed PDF record, not as late authorisation. If the organisation adopts an ERP-only definition of recording, P-507 would contain a C3 logging defect even if the authorisation itself was timely.

**Late evidence of a timely act** means the act time is proved by stronger contemporaneous evidence despite a later system timestamp. P-505 is the clearest example. P-502 recording within four hours and P-501 form completion within 24 hours also should not be converted into breaches by outage lag alone.

**Inability to prove compliance** means a material element, especially actor distinctness or budget-owner status, cannot be established. This affects P-505 Create_PO and committer identity, P-507 committer identity and P-501 Plant_Approve actor identity. Inability to prove compliance should not be reported as proved substantive misconduct, but it also should not be upgraded to COMPLIANT.

**Recommendation: immediate case remediation**

- P-501: obtain a signed retrospective statement from the actual Plant and Finance approvers for mitigation, review why a reachable Plant Manager did not delegate, and record whether SHIFT-1 acted as substitute approver. Do not backdate approvals.
- P-502: complete a retrospective new-supplier Compliance review, verify supplier integrity and pricing, and place the supplier on hold for further commitment until Compliance clearance is documented for future orders.
- P-503: freeze further backup-line purchases under the same authorisation, investigate PROC-3 self-authorisation, review whether the EUR 140,000 commitment should be ratified, renegotiated or unwound by properly independent approvers, and retain the file as a segregation breach.
- P-504: qualify or remove the unapproved supplier, confirm freezer food-safety handling, and review whether any food was served after spoilage risk arose. The absence of a production effect does not cure the supplier-register breach.
- P-505: annotate the ERP record with the supplier acknowledgement, call record and clock-correction note; file the Create_PO evidence; confirm committer identity; obtain board or Chief Risk Officer confirmation of the CFO interpretation for future ECD use rather than relying on email alone.
- P-506: treat the verbal-extension claim as unproved, obtain retrospective budget-owner and Plant review for mitigation, and issue a clear instruction that verbal ECD extensions have no effect.
- P-507: link the signed PDF to the ERP purchase record, correct the authorisation timestamp to the PDF creation time with an audit note, and file committer and authoriser identities to close the segregation evidence gap.
- P-508: obtain retrospective Finance Controller and budget-owner review of price, need and supplier risk, but do not treat Legal clearance as a substitute. Consider whether contract ratification by authorised approvers is required before payment.

**Recommendation: monitoring**

Implement an ECD dashboard showing amount, supplier status, scope basis, authoriser identities, commitment time, recording deadline and expiry. Add ERP blocks for same-user request, commit and authorise; new-supplier commitment without Compliance; and commitment after ECD expiry. Require a four-hour recording timer and 24-hour emergency-form timer. Audit all ECD purchases within 10 days after the incident and report ECD spend, unapproved-supplier use and above-cap purchases to the executive committee.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Draft replacement hierarchy and exception clause**

1. **Hierarchy.** Board integrity controls, including segregation of duties and Compliance clearance for new suppliers and purchases above EUR 100,000, prevail over incident directives, local procedures and email. Only the Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped and time-limited exception. An incident directive may override local PO timing and supplier-register requirements only within its stated scope and window and only after two distinct Incident Command authorisations are recorded in the controlled system within four hours. A verbal extension of an incident directive is void.
2. **Scope.** “Critical” means a part required for the affected line within 24 hours to contain the event, prevent imminent safety harm or restore production. Stock covering more than 24 hours is not critical unless a documented imminent failure is recorded before commitment.
3. **Thresholds and approvals.** Up to EUR 150,000, joint Incident Command authorisation may substitute for Plant and Finance operational approval during a valid incident window, but the requester or committer must never be an authoriser. Above EUR 150,000, independent budget-owner approval, Finance Controller approval and Legal clearance are required before commitment. Legal clearance never replaces Finance approval.
4. **New suppliers.** Compliance clearance is required before commitment for every new supplier, including during an incident. Compliance must provide an expedited incident lane with a two-hour service standard. No incident directive or email may waive this requirement.
5. **Records and timestamps.** The system must record authoriser identities, amount, supplier status, scope basis and commitment time. If system time differs from real-world time, contemporaneous supplier acknowledgement, call recording or signed PDF prevails, provided a clock-correction note is attached. Late evidence of a timely act is distinguished from a late act. Later approval never cures a missing pre-commitment approval unless the Chief Risk Officer expressly ratifies it in writing.
6. **Expiry and anti-normalisation safeguard.** Incident directives expire automatically at the stated time and may be extended once only by written Chief Risk Officer approval. The Chief Risk Officer must ratify or revoke any incident overlay within 72 hours. All incident purchases must be audited within 10 days, counted on a rolling incident dashboard, and reported to the board if incident purchasing exceeds five days, EUR 500,000 or ten purchase orders. Unapproved suppliers used under an incident directive must be qualified or removed within 30 days. Repeated use for planned replenishment is prohibited and must be treated as a policy breach.
