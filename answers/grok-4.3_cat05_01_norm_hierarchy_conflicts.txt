## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method
Facts are limited to the stated documents, timestamps, and evidence notes. Interpretations include treating ECD-2 as a scoped overlay that can displace ordinary timing and supplier-register rules for items within its stated purpose, while GPIP-4 segregation-of-duties and pre-commitment Compliance_Clearance remain intact unless explicitly waived by the Chief Risk Officer. Later-in-time documents prevail only within their stated scope and only for the issues they address; an ordinary CFO email cannot waive GPIP-4 or Compliance controls. Emergency necessity is evaluated at the moment of Commit_to_Supplier, not at case completion. Retrospective evidence (recordings, acknowledgments, signed PDFs) can establish that an act occurred at a given time even when ERP timestamps are delayed, but cannot create a missing authorization that never existed. Burden of proof rests on the party asserting compliance or justified exception; absence of a required record at the time of commitment creates a prima-facie deviation unless contemporaneous non-ERP evidence directly supports the required act.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations
P-501: If B4 emergency definition is read broadly to include any production stoppage risk within six hours regardless of ECD-2 start time, classification could shift to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; premise change required is treating local SOP emergency as independent of ECD-2 effective time.  
P-503: If “critical contingency” label by Incident Command is accepted as conclusive on scope, and Incident Command members are treated as budget owners, classification could become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; premise change required is that operational labeling overrides audit view of 24-hour need.  
P-504: If cafeteria repair is recharacterized as site continuity under B7 corporate-directive language, classification could become INDETERMINATE; premise change required is that “ordinary workflow steps” includes supplier-register and authorization rules.  
P-506: If verbal overnight extension by Incident Command is accepted as sufficient under C3, classification could become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; premise change required is that oral extension need not be recorded within four hours when no written mechanism exists.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation
P-501, P-503, P-504, and P-506 show policy breaches (missing pre-commitment approvals or scope violations). P-505 and P-507 show logging defects (late ERP entry) but timely acts supported by contemporaneous evidence. P-502 shows late evidence of a timely act. Immediate remediation: obtain retroactive CRO ratification or documented post-incident review for P-501, P-503, P-504, P-506; require written confirmation that Incident Command members were budget owners for any future use. Monitoring: require dual real-time logging (ERP + shared incident log) for all emergency commitments and mandatory four-hour authorization recording regardless of system status.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification
During a declared incident, an ECD jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander may temporarily suspend ordinary timing and approved-supplier requirements for purchases within the explicitly defined critical scope and time window, provided (a) two distinct Incident Command members authorize before commitment, (b) GPIP-4 segregation of duties and pre-commitment Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers or €100k remain in force unless the Chief Risk Officer issues a written, time-limited exception, and (c) all verbal authorizations are recorded within four hours. No emergency measure may be extended beyond the stated expiry without a new written directive countersigned by the Chief Risk Officer. Post-hoc approvals cure only procedural defects, not substantive breaches of segregation or compliance clearance.
