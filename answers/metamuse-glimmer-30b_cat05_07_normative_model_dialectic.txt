M0 models Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review as concurrent, which is correct, but it has no Request_More_Info loop, no auto-approval branch, no emergency provisional branch, no reactivation, no rejected-lead scope, and no resource constraints. SUP-707’s Request_More_Info cycles are compliant in substance: no activation before evidence, and Compliance agrees the behaviour is desirable. The model defect is M0’s, not operations’.

Pilot and emergency overlays are real and bounded. PD-3 was issued by steering committee with compliance observation. For eligible cases such as SUP-702 on 2026-06-15, LOW-risk DE supplier in approved group, Sanctions_Check before activation, Auto_Approve used, Quality_Review next business day, and CRM notification with stable `notification_id` exists. The order matches PD-3. SUP-704 on 2026-07-11 matches FD-7: Sanctions_Check before activation, spend EUR 18,000 < 20,000, daily monitoring claimed, Quality_Review and manual approval next business day. The directive expressly permits provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval.

Data collection changes explain apparent deviations. Notification moved to CRM 2026-07-01. SUP-706 lacks Notify_Requester in onboarding log but CRM shows message at +3h. Missingness is a logging problem, not a process violation. Duplicate Register in SUP-711 is a migration artifact with same source transaction ID.

Scope and identity issues are model limits. SUP-709 rejected a lead after sanctions pre-screen without Quality_Review. CS-9 allows abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads and scope is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation with prior valid quality certification; CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. SUP-713 uses QA_Clearance; mapping was approved one week later and audit shows equivalent content. Label change is a data issue.

Resource rules are outside control-flow. SUP-712 and SUP-715 fit M0 perfectly. M0 was never intended to enforce requester  approver or conflict-of-interest disclosure. Declaring noncompliance on those bases confuses model semantics with organizational controls.

Normalization concern is overstated. 289 of 312 Auto_Approve cases appear PD-3-eligible. Only 14 are clearly ineligible and 9 lack attributes. Six post-pilot cases are concerning but represent a small tail, not systemic collapse, and reflect expectation of imminent permanent policy.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Layered governance:

* Control-flow POWL for mandatory orderings and allowed branches: standard manual route, low-risk auto-approval route, emergency provisional route, rejection route, and closure with notification before close.
* DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints for data-aware rules: risk tier, country, corporate group membership, spend cap, date windows, and temporal distance between Activate_Supplier and Quality_Review.
* Resource and conflict rules in organizational controls / pandas checks: requester  manual approver human identity, reviewer/approver conflict-of-interest disclosure, alias resolution.
* Policy-overlay governance: pilots and emergencies are explicit overlays with authority ID, effective/record timestamps, eligibility snapshot, and immutable versioning. Overlays cannot modify CS-9 without formal change control.
* Scope rules: define intake vs onboarding. Rejected leads after sanctions pre-screen are out of M0 scope or follow abbreviated process. Reactivations are a distinct case type with prior certification carry-over.

POWL encodes orderings and XOR choices. Amount, risk, date and identity conditions belong in policy-overlay tables and data checks, not in POWL.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Base activities
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")

# Standard checks concurrent
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])

# Standard manual route
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks, manual_approval, activate])
standard_route.order.add_edge(checks, manual_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# Low-risk auto route: sanctions before auto before activate, quality after activate
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto_approve, activate, quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)

# Emergency provisional route: sanctions before activate, quality and manual after activate
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, activate, quality, manual_approval])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, manual_approval)

# Request_More_Info approximation: loop before quality
# POWL Loop is used as an approximation for repeatable requests
info_loop_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_info, quality])
info_loop_body.order.add_edge(request_info, quality)
# In practice, a proper loop would be Operator.LOOP with request_info as iteration
# Here we approximate with a choice to include the request step before quality
quality_with_info = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[quality, info_loop_body])

# Replace quality in routes with quality_with_info for illustration
# Route choice
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[
    standard_route,
    auto_route,
    emergency_route,
    reject
])

# Decision and closure
decision_and_close = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[route_choice, notify, close])
decision_and_close.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
decision_and_close.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Full model
M_REVISED = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision_and_close])
M_REVISED.order.add_edge(register, decision_and_close)
```

Limitation note: POWL cannot enforce amount caps, risk tier, dates, identity inequality, or the two-business-day window. Those are enforced by a policy-overlay table and data checks.

**Normalized policy-overlay table**
`policy_overlay(case_id, overlay_type, authority_id, effective_from, effective_to, eligibility_snapshot_json, rule_version, actor_aliases, spend_cap_eur, max_quality_lag_days, immutable_recorded_at)`

Required event/data fields
* Stable `notification_id` for Notify_Requester, immutable link to CRM.
* `rule_version_id` and `overlay_id` on each event for pilot/emergency.
* Eligibility snapshot at decision time: risk, country, corporate_group_approved, reactivation_flag.
* Actor identity with alias resolution and delegation evidence.
* Immutable effective/record timestamps for all events and overlays.
* Spend amount and daily monitoring flag for emergency cases.

These controls separate control-flow conformance from data-aware eligibility and resource governance.
