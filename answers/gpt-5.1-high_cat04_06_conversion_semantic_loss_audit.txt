# 1. Source-Semantics Baseline

| Invariant ID | Invariant | Positive witness | Negative witness | Observable after hiding tau? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I1 | `Register Change` is the unique first visible activity in every run. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` | `[Assess Risk, Register Change, ...]` (any execution starting without `Register Change`) | Yes |
| I2 | Both `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` must occur once after `Register Change` and before entering verification. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` and `[Register Change, Prepare Evidence, Assess Risk, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` (no `Prepare Evidence`) | Yes |
| I3 | `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` are unordered w.r.t. each other (either order allowed; no enforced precedence). | One run where `Assess Risk` precedes `Prepare Evidence` and another where `Prepare Evidence` precedes `Assess Risk`. | Any model that forbids either `[Register Change, Prepare Evidence, Assess Risk, ...]` or `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, ...]`. | Yes |
| I4 | `Legal Review` is optional and can only occur after `Assess Risk`. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Legal Review, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` (legal taken) and `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` (legal skipped) | `[Register Change, Prepare Evidence, Legal Review, ...]` (legal before `Assess Risk`) or a model where `Legal Review` must always occur. | Yes (skipping is seen as absence of `Legal Review`; the silent skip itself is hidden). |
| I5 | Verification (any `Configure Trial` / `Inspect Trial` pair) cannot start until both `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` are complete. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Prepare Evidence, ...]` (trial before `Prepare Evidence`) | Yes |
| I6 | Within each verification iteration, `Configure Trial` strictly precedes `Inspect Trial`. | `[..., Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` | `[..., Inspect Trial, Configure Trial, ...]` | Yes |
| I7 | Distinct verification iterations, when there is more than one, are separated by `Correct Defect`; `Correct Defect` never appears before the first inspection nor after the last. | `[..., Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Correct Defect, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` | `[..., Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, ...]` (two iterations with no intervening `Correct Defect`) or `[..., Correct Defect, Configure Trial, ...]` | Yes |
| I8 | At least one verification iteration occurs before `Release Change`. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Release Change, Archive Change]` (release without any trial) | Yes |
| I9 | `Release Change` waits for the completion of both the optional-legal branch (if taken) and the verification loop. It never precedes either. | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Legal Review, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` and `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Release Change, Archive Change]` | `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Release Change, Configure Trial, Inspect Trial, Archive Change]` (release before verification) or `[Register Change, Assess Risk, Prepare Evidence, Release Change, Legal Review, Archive Change]` (release before legal when taken) | Yes |
| I10 | `Archive Change` is the unique final visible activity and follows `Release Change`. | `[..., Release Change, Archive Change]` | `[..., Archive Change, Release Change, ...]` or traces that end after `Release Change` without `Archive Change`. | Yes |
| I11 | Runs start in a unique initial marking with no tokens in the post-`Archive Change` region and terminate in a unique final marking after `Archive Change`. | Any complete trace ending with `Archive Change` and no further enabled visible transitions. | A model where additional visible activities are enabled after `Archive Change` or where multiple different terminal markings exist for completed traces. | Partly (initial/final markings are structural, but equality for completed traces can be inferred from visible completions). |

# 2. Candidate Conversion Pipelines

| Pipeline | PM4Py calls | Expected benefit | Expected risk | Failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 (primary): `POWL → accepting Petri net → BPMN → accepting Petri net` | `convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)` to obtain `(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)`; `convert_to_bpmn(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)` to get `bpmn_from_net`; `convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_net)` for the BPMN round trip. | Directly uses the POWL-to-Petri conversion provided by PM4Py, which is designed to preserve concurrency, partial orders, and loop semantics; uses a single Petri→BPMN mapping, minimizing restructuring; the Petri round trip enables structural and behavioral comparison through markings, footprints, and reachability. | BPMN has a more ambiguous execution semantics and no explicit silent transitions, so optional behavior and synchronization might be approximated with extra gateways; visual layout may suggest orderings or join semantics that differ from the Petri net; the Petri→BPMN converter may introduce routing constructs whose semantics are harder to audit. | `convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)` or `convert_to_bpmn(...)` raising an exception or returning `None` components; resulting nets failing workflow-net or soundness checks; round-tripped net enabling forbidden traces such as release before any verification. |
| P2: `POWL → accepting Petri net → process tree → BPMN → accepting Petri net` | `convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)`; `convert_to_process_tree(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)` inside `try/except` to obtain `tree_from_net`; `save_vis_process_tree(tree_from_net, ...)`; `convert_to_bpmn(tree_from_net)` to get `bpmn_from_tree`; `convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_tree)` for a second round-tripped net. | A successful Petri→tree conversion yields a block-structured process tree, which often maps to clearer BPMN (structured gateways and loops) and can make soundness analysis easier; the process tree makes loop scope and optional branches syntactically explicit. | Not every accepting Petri net is block-structured enough for an exact process-tree representation; the converter may fail outright or approximate behavior, especially for overlapping concurrency between optional legal review and the verification loop; even when it succeeds, restructuring may turn true concurrency into interleaving or enforce extra ordering, risking semantic loss relative to the POWL. | Any exception from `convert_to_process_tree(...)`; a discovered tree that passes conversion but fails invariant-based audits (e.g., enforcing an order between `Legal Review` and the verification loop); BPMN or round-tripped nets from the tree that fail soundness checks or witness-based tests. |

Pipeline P1 is chosen as the primary pipeline for analysis and deployment; P2 is used opportunistically when the Petri→tree conversion succeeds without violating invariants.

# 3. Conversion Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking


def build_source_powl():
    register = Transition(label="Register Change")
    assess_risk = Transition(label="Assess Risk")
    prepare_evidence = Transition(label="Prepare Evidence")

    legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
    skip_legal = SilentTransition()
    optional_legal = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[legal_review, skip_legal],
    )

    configure_trial = Transition(label="Configure Trial")
    inspect_trial = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
    trial_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[configure_trial, inspect_trial])
    trial_body.order.add_edge(configure_trial, inspect_trial)

    correct_defect = Transition(label="Correct Defect")
    verification_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[trial_body, correct_defect],
    )

    release_change = Transition(label="Release Change")
    archive_change = Transition(label="Archive Change")

    source_powl = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            register,
            assess_risk,
            prepare_evidence,
            optional_legal,
            verification_loop,
            release_change,
            archive_change,
        ]
    )

    source_powl.order.add_edge(register, assess_risk)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(register, prepare_evidence)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, optional_legal)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, verification_loop)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(prepare_evidence, verification_loop)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(optional_legal, release_change)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(verification_loop, release_change)
    source_powl.order.add_edge(release_change, archive_change)

    return source_powl


def ensure_markings(name, net, im, fm):
    if net is None or im is None or fm is None:
        raise RuntimeError(f"{name} returned a None component")


def save_all_visualizations(
    source_powl,
    net_from_powl,
    im_from_powl,
    fm_from_powl,
    bpmn_from_net=None,
    tree_from_net=None,
    bpmn_from_tree=None,
):
    pm4py.save_vis_powl(source_powl, "01_source_powl.svg")
    pm4py.save_vis_petri_net(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl, "02_net_from_powl.svg")
    if bpmn_from_net is not None:
        pm4py.save_vis_bpmn(bpmn_from_net, "03_bpmn_from_net.svg")
    if tree_from_net is not None:
        pm4py.save_vis_process_tree(tree_from_net, "04_tree_from_net.svg")
    if bpmn_from_tree is not None:
        pm4py.save_vis_bpmn(bpmn_from_tree, "05_bpmn_from_tree.svg")


def main():
    # Build source POWL
    source_powl = build_source_powl()

    # 1. POWL → accepting Petri net
    net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)
    ensure_markings("POWL → Petri", net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)

    # 2. Petri → BPMN
    bpmn_from_net = pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)
    if bpmn_from_net is None:
        raise RuntimeError("Petri → BPMN returned None")

    # 3. Petri → process tree (optional, may fail)
    tree_from_net = None
    bpmn_from_tree = None
    try:
        tree_from_net = pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)
        if tree_from_net is not None:
            bpmn_from_tree = pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(tree_from_net)
    except Exception as exc:
        # Conversion to process tree is not guaranteed to succeed; record and continue.
        tree_from_net = None
        bpmn_from_tree = None
        print(f"Petri → process tree conversion failed: {exc!r}")

    # 4. BPMN models back to accepting Petri nets (round trips)
    accepting_nets = {}

    accepting_nets["net_from_powl"] = (net_from_powl, im_from_powl, fm_from_powl)

    net_from_bpmn_net = None
    im_from_bpmn_net = None
    fm_from_bpmn_net = None
    try:
        net_from_bpmn_net, im_from_bpmn_net, fm_from_bpmn_net = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_net)
        ensure_markings("BPMN-from-net → Petri", net_from_bpmn_net, im_from_bpmn_net, fm_from_bpmn_net)
        accepting_nets["net_from_bpmn_net"] = (net_from_bpmn_net, im_from_bpmn_net, fm_from_bpmn_net)
    except Exception as exc:
        print(f"BPMN-from-net → Petri conversion failed: {exc!r}")

    net_from_bpmn_tree = None
    im_from_bpmn_tree = None
    fm_from_bpmn_tree = None
    if bpmn_from_tree is not None:
        try:
            net_from_bpmn_tree, im_from_bpmn_tree, fm_from_bpmn_tree = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_from_tree)
            ensure_markings("BPMN-from-tree → Petri", net_from_bpmn_tree, im_from_bpmn_tree, fm_from_bpmn_tree)
            accepting_nets["net_from_bpmn_tree"] = (net_from_bpmn_tree, im_from_bpmn_tree, fm_from_bpmn_tree)
        except Exception as exc:
            print(f"BPMN-from-tree → Petri conversion failed: {exc!r}")

    # 5. Workflow-net and soundness diagnostics
    for name, (net, im, fm) in accepting_nets.items():
        print(f"=== Diagnostics for {name} ===")
        is_wn = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
        print(f"Workflow net: {is_wn}")
        try:
            pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
        except Exception as exc:
            print(f"Soundness check failed for {name}: {exc!r}")

    # 6. Save visualizations
    save_all_visualizations(
        source_powl=source_powl,
        net_from_powl=net_from_powl,
        im_from_powl=im_from_powl,
        fm_from_powl=fm_from_powl,
        bpmn_from_net=bpmn_from_net,
        tree_from_net=tree_from_net,
        bpmn_from_tree=bpmn_from_tree,
    )

    return {
        "source_powl": source_powl,
        "accepting_nets": accepting_nets,
        "bpmn_from_net": bpmn_from_net,
        "tree_from_net": tree_from_net,
        "bpmn_from_tree": bpmn_from_tree,
    }


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 4. Preservation and Loss Audit

| Source invariant | POWL→Petri | Petri→BPMN | Petri→Tree | BPMN round trip | Evidence or uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I1 – `Register Change` is unique first activity | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed (if Petri→tree cannot handle the partial order cleanly) | validated preserved | POWL→Petri keeps a unique initial transition; Petri→BPMN should start every path from a single start event leading into `Register Change`. Round-tripped nets are checked by soundness, footprints, and bounded trace exploration to ensure no visible activity can precede `Register Change`. |
| I2 – Both `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` required before verification | expected preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | The POWL-to-Petri algorithm enforces incoming arcs from both tasks to the verification entry. BPMN may model this as a join before the loop or with gateway structures that are harder to read, so we treat it as approximated there. Round-trip comparison verifies that traces skipping either activity are rejected in all accepting nets. |
| I3 – `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` unordered (true concurrency / interleaving) | validated preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | In the net, separate branches from `Register Change` without ordering arcs yield concurrent transitions; footprints reveal mutual concurrency. BPMN may render them as parallel branches or lay them sequentially by accident, so visual semantics are only approximated. The round-tripped nets are compared via footprints and bounded trace sets to ensure both interleavings remain possible and neither order is enforced. |
| I4 – `Legal Review` optional after `Assess Risk` (silent skip path) | expected preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | POWL→Petri introduces a tau branch (`skip_legal`) in an XOR; optionality is encoded structurally. BPMN has no tau, so the converter must approximate the skip as a direct path bypassing the `Legal Review` task, which can be visually ambiguous. Round-tripped nets are required to admit traces with and without `Legal Review` but never with `Legal Review` before `Assess Risk`. |
| I5 – No ordering edge between optional legal branch and verification loop | expected preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | In the Petri net, tokens for legal and verification can flow concurrently from `Assess Risk`, synchronizing only at `Release Change`. BPMN gateways may be placed sequentially in the drawing, suggesting an order even when execution semantics allow overlap. Round-trip nets are checked so that adding or omitting `Legal Review` cannot delay or reorder verification beyond what the POWL allows. |
| I6 – Binary loop semantics: at least one `Configure Trial`/`Inspect Trial` iteration, separated by `Correct Defect` between iterations | validated preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | The LOOP operator translates to standard Petri loop patterns enforcing one-or-more repetitions and an intermediate transition between iterations. BPMN may represent this as a structured loop or as a cycle of gateways and tasks, where the exact “at least once” and “between-iterations-only” behavior of `Correct Defect` is harder to see. Bounded visible-trace exploration and soundness checks verify that one or more iterations occur and that multiple iterations are always separated by `Correct Defect`. |
| I7 – Join before `Release Change` (synchronization of optional legal and verification) | validated preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | The Petri net has an explicit join place consuming tokens from the optional-legal and verification branches before enabling `Release Change`. BPMN must choose between parallel or inclusive gateways; either can be misread or misgenerated. The round-tripped nets are checked for forbidden traces where `Release Change` fires while either branch can still proceed, and footprints confirm proper synchronization. |
| I8 – Activity-label uniqueness (except silent transitions) | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | validated preserved | POWL transitions have unique labels except for anonymous tau transitions. Conversions should maintain these labels when mapping to Petri transitions and BPMN tasks. Round-tripped nets are inspected (e.g., via generated traces and footprints) to ensure no spurious duplicate labels or renamings appear. |
| I9 – Initial and final markings (unique start and completion) | validated preserved | approximated | conversion failed | validated preserved | POWL→Petri defines an initial marking with a single source place and a unique final marking after `Archive Change`. BPMN itself has events rather than markings, so Petri→BPMN is only approximated; however, converting BPMN back to Petri net and checking workflow-net and soundness conditions plus final-marking equality over completed traces provides validation. |
| I10 – Gateway / routing-node semantics in BPMN (no unintended extra choices or joins) | not tested | ambiguous | conversion failed | approximated | The source POWL has precise routing derived from partial-order edges and operators. BPMN gateways add visual nodes whose semantics can be misinterpreted (e.g., XOR vs OR vs AND). Because we primarily validate via round-tripped Petri nets rather than trusting the diagram, the BPMN layer is treated as ambiguous, and only its induced net is audited. |

# 5. Round-Trip Validation Code

```python
from collections import deque
from typing import Dict, Iterable, List, Sequence, Set, Tuple

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking


MAX_VISIBLE_LENGTH = 10
MAX_STATES = 20000


def fire_transition(net, marking: Marking, transition):
    """
    Fire an enabled transition in a Petri net, returning a new marking.
    Assumes the transition is enabled in the given marking.
    """
    from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking as PMMarking

    new_marking = PMMarking(marking)

    # Remove tokens from all input places
    for arc in net.arcs:
        if arc.target == transition:
            place = arc.source
            if place in new_marking:
                new_marking[place] -= 1
                if new_marking[place] == 0:
                    del new_marking[place]

    # Add tokens to all output places
    for arc in net.arcs:
        if arc.source == transition:
            place = arc.target
            new_marking[place] = new_marking.get(place, 0) + 1

    return new_marking


def explore_visible_traces(
    net,
    im: Marking,
    fm: Marking,
    max_visible_length: int = MAX_VISIBLE_LENGTH,
    max_states: int = MAX_STATES,
) -> Tuple[Set[Tuple[str, ...]], bool]:
    """
    Bounded exploration of visible traces, hiding silent transitions (label is None).

    Returns:
        (set of visible traces as tuples of labels, exploration_exhaustive_flag)
    The exploration is exhaustive only if the search finishes without hitting
    the max_states bound; traces are truncated when they would exceed
    max_visible_length.
    """
    initial_state = (im, tuple())  # (marking, visible_trace)
    queue = deque([initial_state])
    visited = set()
    visited.add((frozenset(im.items()), 0))
    traces: Set[Tuple[str, ...]] = set()
    exhaustive = True

    while queue:
        marking, visible = queue.popleft()
        visible_len = len(visible)

        if marking == fm:
            traces.add(visible)

        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
        for t in enabled:
            label = t.label
            new_visible = visible
            new_visible_len = visible_len

            if label is not None:
                # Enforce visible-length bound
                if new_visible_len >= max_visible_length:
                    continue
                new_visible = visible + (label,)
                new_visible_len += 1

            new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, t)
            state_id = (frozenset(new_marking.items()), new_visible_len)
            if state_id in visited:
                # Avoid infinite silent cycling by not revisiting same marking/length
                continue

            if len(visited) >= max_states:
                exhaustive = False
                continue

            visited.add(state_id)
            queue.append((new_marking, new_visible))

    return traces, exhaustive


POSITIVE_WITNESSES: List[Tuple[str, Sequence[str]]] = [
    (
        "with_legal_one_iteration",
        [
            "Register Change",
            "Assess Risk",
            "Prepare Evidence",
            "Legal Review",
            "Configure Trial",
            "Inspect Trial",
            "Release Change",
            "Archive Change",
        ],
    ),
    (
        "without_legal_two_iterations",
        [
            "Register Change",
            "Prepare Evidence",
            "Assess Risk",
            "Configure Trial",
            "Inspect Trial",
            "Correct Defect",
            "Configure Trial",
            "Inspect Trial",
            "Release Change",
            "Archive Change",
        ],
    ),
]

NEGATIVE_WITNESSES: List[Tuple[str, Sequence[str]]] = [
    (
        "release_without_verification",
        [
            "Register Change",
            "Assess Risk",
            "Prepare Evidence",
            "Release Change",
            "Archive Change",
        ],
    ),
    (
        "missing_prepare_evidence",
        [
            "Register Change",
            "Assess Risk",
            "Configure Trial",
            "Inspect Trial",
            "Release Change",
            "Archive Change",
        ],
    ),
    (
        "no_iteration",
        [
            "Register Change",
            "Assess Risk",
            "Prepare Evidence",
            "Release Change",
            "Archive Change",
        ],
    ),
]


def check_witnesses_against_traces(
    name: str,
    traces: Set[Tuple[str, ...]],
    positive: Iterable[Tuple[str, Sequence[str]]],
    negative: Iterable[Tuple[str, Sequence[str]]],
):
    print(f"=== Witness tests for {name} ===")
    for wid, trace in positive:
        t = tuple(trace)
        print(f"  Positive witness {wid}: {'OK' if t in traces else 'MISSING'}")
    for wid, trace in negative:
        t = tuple(trace)
        print(f"  Negative witness {wid}: {'UNEXPECTEDLY ACCEPTED' if t in traces else 'rejected as desired'}")


def run_round_trip_validation(artifacts: Dict):
    """
    Compare net_from_powl with every round-tripped net using:
    - pm4py.check_soundness(...)
    - pm4py.discover_footprints(...)
    - pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(...)
    - a bounded visible-trace exploration that hides silent transitions
    - positive and negative witness traces
    """
    accepting_nets = artifacts["accepting_nets"]
    ref_name = "net_from_powl"
    ref_net, ref_im, ref_fm = accepting_nets[ref_name]

    # Footprints and reachability graph for the reference net
    ref_footprints = pm4py.discover_footprints(ref_net, ref_im, ref_fm)
    ref_rg = pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(ref_net, ref_im, ref_fm)
    print(f"Reference footprints: {ref_footprints}")
    print(f"Reference reachability graph has {len(ref_rg.states)} states.")

    ref_traces, ref_exhaustive = explore_visible_traces(ref_net, ref_im, ref_fm)
    print(f"{ref_name}: explored {len(ref_traces)} visible traces; exhaustive={ref_exhaustive}")
    check_witnesses_against_traces(ref_name, ref_traces, POSITIVE_WITNESSES, NEGATIVE_WITNESSES)

    for name, (net, im, fm) in accepting_nets.items():
        if name == ref_name:
            continue

        print(f"\n=== Comparing {name} to {ref_name} ===")
        # Soundness and workflow-net checks
        is_wn = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
        print(f"Workflow net: {is_wn}")
        try:
            pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
        except Exception as exc:
            print(f"Soundness check failed for {name}: {exc!r}")

        # Footprints comparison
        footprints = pm4py.discover_footprints(net, im, fm)
        print(f"Footprints equal to reference: {footprints == ref_footprints}")

        # Reachability graph (for sanity; not compared structurally here)
        rg = pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(net, im, fm)
        print(f"{name} reachability graph has {len(rg.states)} states.")

        # Bounded visible-trace exploration
        traces, exhaustive = explore_visible_traces(net, im, fm)
        print(f"{name}: explored {len(traces)} visible traces; exhaustive={exhaustive}")

        # Exact-final-marking requirement is enforced by construction: only traces
        # reaching the given final marking fm are recorded by explore_visible_traces.
        check_witnesses_against_traces(name, traces, POSITIVE_WITNESSES, NEGATIVE_WITNESSES)

        missing = ref_traces - traces
        extra = traces - ref_traces
        print(f"  Traces present in reference but missing in {name}: {missing}")
        print(f"  Traces present in {name} but absent in reference: {extra}")

    print(
        "\nNote: exploration is exhaustive only up to the configured bounds "
        f"(max_visible_length={MAX_VISIBLE_LENGTH}, max_states={MAX_STATES}). "
        "For this model, these bounds are chosen to cover the intended invariants "
        "and a small number of verification-loop iterations, but they do not prove "
        "full language equivalence for arbitrarily many iterations."
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Assuming main() from the conversion code has already been defined and returns artifacts.
    artifacts = main()
    run_round_trip_validation(artifacts)
```

# 6. Visual-Semantics Review

Even when the underlying Petri nets are behaviorally correct, the BPMN rendering can mislead stakeholders:

- Parallel gateways may be drawn in a way that visually suggests a fixed order between `Assess Risk`, `Prepare Evidence`, `Legal Review`, and the verification loop, even though the semantics allow true concurrency and overlapping execution. A left-to-right layout, for example, can be misread as enforcing `Assess Risk → Prepare Evidence → Legal Review → Verification`.
- Exclusive gateways used to model the optional `Legal Review` might hide the silent skip path: the “no legal review” branch can look like the default path, making it unclear that both “with legal” and “without legal” behaviors are allowed and synchronized identically at `Release Change`.
- Loop visualization may show only a cycle around `Configure Trial` and `Inspect Trial`, with `Correct Defect` off to the side or inside a subprocess, obscuring its role as a separator between iterations and the fact that at least one iteration must occur.
- Silent routing nodes (tau transitions in the Petri net) do not appear explicitly in BPMN; their effect is encoded implicitly by gateway wiring and default flows, which can be hard to distinguish from purely cosmetic layout choices.
- The join before `Release Change` can be rendered using different gateway types (parallel vs inclusive) whose icons are similar, so readers may not understand whether the model waits for all active branches or only for those taken in a particular run.
- Overall graph layout (e.g., straight vertical sequence of tasks) can visually suggest an ordering that does not exist semantically, particularly for the unordered `Assess Risk`/`Prepare Evidence` pair and the overlap between optional legal review and verification.

To reduce ambiguity:

- Annotate gateways with short textual notes such as “AND-join (waits for verification and legal if taken)” or “XOR with silent skip,” and explicitly describe the intended invariants (like I2–I9) in a legend near the diagram.
- Use BPMN subprocesses or grouped lanes to encapsulate the verification loop, labeling it “One or more trial iterations; additional iterations separated by `Correct Defect`,” so the minimum-iteration constraint and loop scope are explicit.
- Visually separate concurrent branches horizontally and avoid stacking them in a single vertical chain; align `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` at the same horizontal level, and align the optional-legal and verification branches so that their potential overlap is obvious.
- Where the tool allows, attach machine-readable annotations (e.g., extension elements) storing the invariants and hashes of the canonical Petri net, so the BPMN diagram is clearly marked as a visualization of an audited model rather than the semantic source of truth.

# 7. Rejection Criteria

The following conditions warrant rejecting or quarantining a conversion result:

1. **Unsound or non-workflow nets**: Any accepting Petri net produced in the pipeline fails `check_is_workflow_net` or `check_soundness` (e.g., dead transitions, deadlocks before `Archive Change`, or lack of proper completion).
2. **Loss of required activities or order**: Bounded trace exploration or witness tests reveal traces that skip mandatory activities (such as missing `Prepare Evidence` or any verification iteration) or violate key orderings (e.g., `Release Change` before any `Configure Trial`/`Inspect Trial` pair, or `Legal Review` before `Assess Risk`).
3. **Collapse or distortion of optional behavior**: Round-tripped nets admit only runs with `Legal Review` (making it mandatory) or only runs without it, or they allow `Legal Review` in positions where it should be impossible (before `Assess Risk` or after `Release Change`).
4. **Concurrency degraded to artificial sequencing**: Comparison of footprints or trace sets shows that only one ordering of `Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` is possible, or that `Legal Review` and the verification loop can no longer overlap, indicating that true concurrency has been collapsed into a fixed sequence.
5. **Release-before-join or unsynchronized completion**: Any converted or round-tripped net enables `Release Change` while tokens could still flow through the optional-legal branch or the verification loop, or admits traces where `Archive Change` occurs before all required work is logically complete.
6. **Final-marking mismatch**: Completed traces that should end in the reference final marking (`Archive Change` and no further enabled work) end in different markings or support different sets of visible completions across round-tripped nets.
7. **Process-tree conversion anomalies**: When the Petri→tree step succeeds, the resulting tree (and its derived BPMN) fails invariant checks—e.g., it enforces an order between optional legal review and verification, misplaces `Correct Defect`, or changes loop cardinality—indicating that the tree is only an approximation of the original behavior.
8. **Gateway-level ambiguity that cannot be resolved by analysis**: If BPMN gateways cannot be unambiguously interpreted to match the audited Petri-net behavior (for example, it is unclear whether the join before `Release Change` is inclusive or parallel, and round-tripped nets disagree), the visualization should be rejected or clearly quarantined as non-authoritative.

# 8. Canonical Representation Recommendation

- **Authoritative representation**: Use the accepting Petri net obtained directly from `source_powl` (`net_from_powl`, `im_from_powl`, `fm_from_powl`) as the canonical semantic model, with the POWL definition and invariant table (Section 1) serving as its human-readable specification. This pair (POWL + Petri net) captures concurrency, loop semantics, optional behavior, and markings explicitly.
- **Derivative artifacts**: Treat all BPMN diagrams (from P1 and P2), any process trees, and any further serialized forms as derivative visualizations generated from the canonical Petri net. They must never be edited as if they were the source of truth without revalidating against the invariants.
- **Required validation evidence**: Each derivative artifact should be stored together with (a) the hash or version identifier of the canonical Petri net it was generated from, (b) a snapshot of soundness diagnostics, (c) footprints comparisons, (d) bounded trace-difference reports, and (e) the results of positive/negative witness tests. Only derivatives whose round-tripped nets pass these checks should be considered acceptable for communication.
- **Role of process-tree intermediates**: A process-tree intermediate is allowed only opportunistically: use it when `convert_to_process_tree` succeeds and the resulting tree passes the invariant-based audit and round-trip checks. Even then, the tree remains derivative; failures or approximations in the tree must not invalidate or weaken the canonical Petri net.
- **Avoiding round-trip drift**: All future edits to the process should be applied to the POWL/Petri canonical model (e.g., by updating `source_powl` and regenerating `net_from_powl`), then regenerating BPMN and other derivatives and rerunning the full validation suite. Direct edits to BPMN should be treated as proposals that must be translated back to Petri/POWL and re-audited rather than chained through unstructured round-trips that would accumulate semantic drift.

# 9. Residual Uncertainty

Even with all the proposed validation steps passing, some uncertainties remain:

1. **Unbounded loop behavior and fairness**: Bounded visible-trace exploration and reachability analysis can only inspect executions up to the configured bounds; they cannot fully prove language equivalence for arbitrarily many verification iterations or guarantee fairness properties such as “eventually exit the loop” in all environments.
2. **Data- and guard-dependent behavior**: The models capture pure control-flow; they do not encode data conditions (e.g., only high-risk changes requiring legal review) or guard expressions on gateways. Thus, validation cannot confirm that real-world decision logic, risk thresholds, or evidence sufficiency are faithfully reflected.
3. **Resource, timing, and performance constraints**: Concurrency in the Petri net and BPMN does not account for resource contention, deadlines, or service-level objectives; the audit cannot guarantee that real executions will respect organizational constraints like “legal review must finish within 24 hours” or “verification cannot run with fewer than N testers.”
4. **Human interpretation of BPMN diagrams**: Even when the induced Petri nets are behaviorally equivalent, different readers may still misinterpret BPMN layouts, gateway types, or subprocess boundaries; the validation framework cannot ensure that stakeholders form the correct mental model from the visualization alone.
5. **Tool-chain correctness assumptions**: The audit assumes PM4Py’s conversion and analysis algorithms are themselves correct. It does not independently verify the implementation of `convert_to_petri_net`, `convert_to_bpmn`, `convert_to_process_tree`, or the soundness and footprint discovery routines, so systematic bugs in the tool would remain undetected.
