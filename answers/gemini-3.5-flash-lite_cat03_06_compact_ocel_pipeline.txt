## 1. Modeling assumptions

- **Object types and capitalization**: `Order` (`O100`, `O200`), `Item` (`I1`, `I2`, `I3`), `Package` (`P1`, `P2`), `Delivery` (`D1`, `D2`), `Invoice` (`V1`), `Payment` (`M1`, `M2`).
- **E2O qualifier vocabulary**: We use semantic qualifiers that describe the exact role of the object in the event: `target_order`, `involved_item`, `packed_item`, `packed_package`, `carried_package`, `delivery_vehicle` (or channel), `billed_order`, `payment_against_invoice`, `closed_order`.
- **O2O direction conventions**: Pointed from parent/higher-level entity to child/lower-level or settled entity (e.g., `Order` -> `contains` -> `Item`, `Invoice` -> `bills` -> `Order`, `Payment` -> `partially_settles` -> `Invoice`).
- **Logistics and financial events relation policy**: We adopt an **operational involvement** policy. Logistics events (Reserve Stock, Pack, Dispatch, Confirm Delivery) and financial events (Issue Invoice, Record Payment) are linked not just to their immediate physical carriers (Packages, Deliveries, Payments, Invoices), but also transitively/operationally to the impacted Orders and Items where business reality demands it (e.g., packing P1 with I1 and I3 relates to both O100 and O200, as P1 combines items from both).
- **Preventing confusion between propagation and source facts**: We explicitly partition relations into direct operational participation and analytically derived propagation. In this implementation, all relations represent the explicit operational scope stated in the narrative (including shared multi-order events).
- **PM4Py version-dependent assumptions**: We assume `pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL` is the standard constructor interface class. If a specific local installation wraps the OCEL constructor differently (e.g., via dictionary ingestion functions), we isolate that line with an explanatory comment.

## 4. Expected flattened behavior

- **Order**:
  * *Trace shape*: Linear lifecycle for `O100` (`Create Order` -> `Add Item` (x2) -> `Reserve Stock` -> `Pack Package` -> `Issue Invoice` -> `Close Order`) and `O200`.
  * *Shared events*: `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package` (P1), and `Issue Invoice` are duplicated across both traces because they touch both orders.
  * *Omitted aspects*: The internal breakdown of packages (`P1`, `P2`) and delivery transit times (`D1`, `D2`) are abstracted away.
- **Package**:
  * *Trace shape*: Two traces (`P1` and `P2`). `P1` goes: `Reserve Stock` -> `Pack Package` -> `Dispatch Package` -> `Confirm Delivery`.
  * *Shared events*: `Reserve Stock` and `Pack Package` are shared between `P1` and the items/orders.
  * *Omitted aspects*: Order creation and invoice billing steps are invisible.
- **Delivery**:
  * *Trace shape*: Two parallel traces (`D1`, `D2`). Each contains `Dispatch Package` -> `Confirm Delivery`.
  * *Shared events*: None heavily shared except via packages.
  * *Omitted aspects* Full upstream commercial history (order placement, item addition, payment settlement).
- **Invoice**:
  * *Trace shape*: One single trace (`V1`) containing `Issue Invoice` -> `Record Payment` (M1) -> `Record Payment` (M2).
  * *Shared events*: `Issue Invoice` is shared with both orders.
  * *Omitted aspects*: Complete operational fulfillment history (packing, shipping, delivery confirmation).

**Why Payment flattening is weak**: Flattening on `Payment` yields extremely short traces (`Record Payment` -> `Record Payment`), completely divorcing payments from the commercial orders (`O100`, `O200`) and the invoice (`V1`) they settle, rendering root-cause analysis of payment delays impossible.

---

## 5. Expected object-centric structures

- **OC-DFG**: Highlights parallel paths and explicit cross-object loops where packages (`P1`) merge execution threads from `O100` and `O200`, showing how a single dispatch event synchronizes two distinct order lifecycles.
- **OTG (Object Transition Graph)**: Quantifies the interaction density between `Order` and `Package`, revealing that `Order O100` interacts with both `P1` and `P2`, whereas `Order O200` interacts exclusively with `P1`.
- **ET–OT**: Maps events like `Pack Package` directly to the bipartite interplay between `Package`, `Item`, and `Order`, formalizing multi-object cardinality per event instance.
- **Object-centric Petri net**: Features duplicate transitions or place tokens representing `Order`, `Package`, and `Invoice` concurrently, demonstrating synchronization points (e.g., `Issue Invoice` requiring active token states in both `O100` and `O200`).
- **Object-interactions summary**: Confirms that `V1` heavily bridges `O100` and `O200`, while `P1` bridges `I1` and `I3`.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

1. **Source-minimal relations**:
   - *Policy*: Link each event *only* to its immediate physical actor (e.g., `Pack Package` linked only to `P1`).
   - *Discovery impact*: Yields clean, uncoupled object flows in flat logs, but hides the true multi-order dependency of batched packages, understating process complexity.
2. **Full operational involvement**:
   - *Policy*: (Our chosen approach) Link events to all business entities directly participating or immediately impacted (Orders, Items, Packages, Deliveries).
   - *Discovery impact*: Captures true cross-entity synchronization in OC-DFGs and object-centric Petri nets at the cost of denser event-to-object tables.
3. **O2O-propagated analytical relations**:
   - *Policy*: Link events only to primary objects (e.g., `Dispatch Package` to `P1`), and dynamically infer order/invoice involvement via the `o2o` graph during mining.
   - *Discovery impact*: Keeps raw event tables minimal, but shifts complexity into analytical runtime joins, risking false attribution if structural O2O relationships evolve over time.

---

## 7. Failure modes

1. **Relation inflation**: Multiplying E2O links carelessly for every indirect entity leads to combinatorial explosion in OCEL relation tables, degrading mining performance.
2. **Missing direct E2O links**: Forgetting to link `Pack Package` to `O100`/`O200` breaks order-centric KPIs and hides fulfillment bottlenecks from order-level analytics.
3. **Treating O2O propagation as observation**: Confusing statically derived O2O links with direct event execution timestamps distorts temporal causality.
4. **Duplicate shared events after flattening**: When flattening multi-object logs, shared events duplicate across traces, artificially inflating event occurrence counts in traditional DFGs.
5. **Ambiguous payment allocation**: Recording lump-sum payments (`M1`, `M2`) against invoice `V1` without item-level or order-level split amounts makes proportional revenue recognition impossible.
6. **Overly short payment traces**: Flattening on payment objects strips out all contextual order and fulfillment activities, leaving isolated payment events without operational context.
7. **Constructor/API version mismatch**: Incompatible PM4Py versions failing to parse standard keyword arguments in `OCEL(...)` initialization.
8. **False total order for same-time events**: Imposing artificial sequencing on events occurring at the exact same timestamp when source systems lack precise millisecond ordering.
