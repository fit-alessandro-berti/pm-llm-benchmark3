## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most forecast-changing next event is **confirmation or denial of the upstream temperature-excursion flag**.

- **If the excursion is confirmed:**  
  - The forecast should shift later.  
  - Chemistry rerun probability rises, microbiology contamination probability rises, and the deviation-investigation probability becomes 0.30–0.55.  
  - The central terminal time would move from roughly Monday 14:00–18:00 toward Wednesday 10:00–Thursday 12:00, and the severe tail would more plausibly extend to the following week if a laboratory investigation is required.  
  - The anomalies should be treated as correlated, not independent, because the same upstream excursion can plausibly worsen both chemistry and microbiology outcomes.

- **If the excursion is denied:**  
  - The forecast should shift earlier or at least narrow.  
  - Anomaly probabilities revert closer to the base ranges: chemistry rerun about 0.10–0.20, microbiology contamination about 0.05–0.12, and normal-branch investigation about 0.04–0.10.  
  - The smooth path becomes more likely, supporting a central terminal time of Monday 14:00–18:00 and a P80-like window of Tuesday 08:30–20:30.

A strong **secondary update trigger** is the **primary microbiology readout and whether it shows a contamination indication**. If no indication appears, the microbiology branch closes and the forecast is driven mainly by chemistry and QA. If a contamination indication appears, the forecast should add 20–30 h of confirmation incubation plus readout and increase the probability of a deviation investigation or rejection.
