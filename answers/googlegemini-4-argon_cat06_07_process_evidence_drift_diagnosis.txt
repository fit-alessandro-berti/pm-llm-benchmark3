## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

Treating April through October as a single monotonic model decay misdiagnoses four fundamentally distinct operational and technical phenomena:

*   **January – March (Baseline Stability):**
    *   *Observed:* Volume is steady (3,200–3,410 cases/month), 100% label maturity (32–34 d median delay). Variant mix is stable (44–45% V1, 35–36% V2, 20% V3). Observed breach rate (0.18–0.19) matches mean predicted risk (0.18–0.19). Discrimination (AUROC 0.81–0.82) and calibration (ECE 0.022–0.025, Brier 0.130–0.132) are strong.
    *   *Diagnosis:* Healthy baseline. V3 is inherently riskier (0.30–0.32 breach rate vs 0.10–0.11 for V2 and 0.15–0.16 for V1), and the model accurately separates prefixes associated with each downstream path.

*   **April – May (Schema Migration & Data-Pipeline Incident):**
    *   *Observed:* On April 1, the workflow migration renames `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. Unknown activities spike to 17.0%, unmapped variants hit 18%, and missing queue features jump to 14%. AUROC plunges to 0.67, Brier rises to 0.184, and ECE hits 0.110 because mean predicted risk inflates to 0.28 while actual breaches remain flat at 0.19. On May 6, a patch fixes the activity mapping, dropping unknown activities to 2.0% and restoring AUROC to 0.78 (ECE 0.050).
    *   *Why it is distinct:* The physical process did not change in April—mature variant breach rates (V1: 0.18, V2: 0.13, V3: 0.34) and part delivery times (34 h) remained near baseline. The model failed purely because prefixes were truncated at the wrong event, duplicated (pre-April 20), or fed corrupted features. May’s residual gap (AUROC 0.78 vs 0.81 baseline, mean risk 0.21 vs 0.18 observed) is a hangover from April/early-May unbackfilled cached features ("number of events before diagnosis").

*   **June (Policy-Driven Process & Benign Concept Shift):**
    *   *Observed:* The June 1 remote-routing policy shifts V2 share from 34% to 52% and drops V1 from 43% to 30%. Because V2 resolves fast (3.8–4.6 h) with a 0.09 breach rate, the overall observed breach rate drops from 0.18 to 0.15. AUROC recovers to 0.79, but ECE worsens slightly to 0.060 because mean predicted risk stays at 0.20.
    *   *Why it is distinct:* Discrimination is healthy (AUROC 0.79), proving the model still ranks case risk well. However, because predictions are made *at* diagnosis—before routing takes place—cases that historically went to V1 (0.16 breach) are now routed to V2 (0.09 breach). For those boundary cases, $P(\text{Breach} \mid X_{\text{pre-diag}})$ decreased, causing systematic **over-prediction** (predicted 0.20 vs observed 0.15).

*   **July – August (Supplier Bottleneck, Performance Drift & Severe Concept Drift):**
    *   *Observed:* On July 10, the parts supplier falters; `Order Part -> Part Received` median elapsed time jumps from 42 h (Jun) to 65 h (Jul) and 130 h (Aug). Simultaneously, V3 share climbs from 22% (Jul) to 35% (Aug) even before Partner Z arrives (indicating either seasonal fault shifts or technicians abandoning remote fixes for parts replacements). Consequently, V3's matured breach rate surges from 0.35 (Jun) to 0.46 (Jul) and 0.64 (Aug). Overall breach rate hits 0.19 (Jul) and 0.28 (Aug), while model predicted risk stays stuck at 0.18–0.19. AUROC drops to 0.76 (Jul) and 0.68 (Aug); ECE spikes to 0.120 in Aug.
    *   *Why it is distinct:* Unlike June (over-prediction from faster routing), August suffers from severe **under-prediction** (0.19 predicted vs 0.28 observed) and discrimination collapse. The model has no visibility into external supplier lead times (`Order Part -> Part Received` happens *after* the prediction point), so conditional breach probability for parts-bound cases doubled without any corresponding change in pre-diagnosis features. Furthermore, the 7% unmatured labels in August (93% maturity) are likely slow V3 cases, meaning the true August breach rate is even slightly above 0.28.

*   **September (Overlapping Population Shift & Supplier Bottleneck):**
    *   *Observed:* Partner Z is onboarded Sept 1 (24% of cases), pushing total volume from 4,180 to 5,100. Partner Z routes 58% of cases to V3 (vs 36% for legacy), pushing total V3 share to 42%. Meanwhile, supplier delays worsen to 148 h. Observed breach rate reaches 0.31 (Partner Z: 0.45, Legacy: 0.27), while predicted risk only nudges up to 0.21. AUROC falls to 0.66, Brier to 0.214, and ECE to 0.140. Label maturity drops to 82% (median delay 42 d).
    *   *Why it is distinct:* September compounds August’s supplier-driven concept drift with an external **population shift**. Partner Z brings unobserved confounders (rural travel distance, older device age) and a much higher V3 propensity. Even though mean predicted risk rose slightly (0.19 to 0.21), showing some observed covariates shifted, the missing rural/device features and ongoing supplier crisis cause massive under-prediction. Moreover, with 18% of labels still immature (disproportionately Partner Z and V3), the true September breach rate is likely >0.33.

*   **October (Severe Right-Censoring & Outcome API Backlog Masking High Risk):**
    *   *Observed:* Volume hits 5,250 (27% Partner Z, 41% V3), and supplier part delay reaches a worst-ever 155 h. Mean predicted risk rises to 0.23. Yet, the *observed* breach rate plummets from 0.31 to 0.16! Label maturity is only 55% and median label delay jumps to 52 d due to the outcome API backlog.
    *   *Why it is distinct:* The apparent drop in observed breaches (0.16) and reversal to "over-prediction" (0.23 predicted vs 0.16 observed, ECE 0.130) is a **censoring artifact**. Fast, non-breaching cases (like V2 remote fixes taking ~4.2 h) finish quickly and clear the API, whereas long-running V3 cases (155 h part wait + rural dispatch) breach their SLA and get stuck in the API backlog. Treating October as a genuine reduction in breach rate or recalibrating the model downward to 0.16 would be catastrophic.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Cohort Safety Assessment for Training & Calibration
*   **Jan – Mar:** **SAFE (Baseline regime).** 100% mature, clean pipeline, pre-policy/pre-supplier crisis. Useful as structural prior or base training data with sample down-weighting.
*   **Apr – May:** **UNSAFE until re-engineered.** Currently corrupted by unmapped diagnosis labels, pre-April 20 duplicates, and unbackfilled cached features. Becomes **SAFE** *only after* full offline raw-log deduplication and feature recomputation.
*   **Jun:** **SAFE (Post-routing policy regime).** 100% mature, clean pipeline, reflects the new permanent V2 remote-routing baseline (52% V2), prior to the supplier crisis.
*   **Jul – Aug:** **CONDITIONALLY SAFE (Supplier-crisis regime).** 98% and 93% mature. Must first refresh labels from the outcome store (since >60 days have elapsed by late Oct/Nov, Jul/Aug cohorts should now be near 100% mature once the API backlog is bypassed/cleared). Crucial for learning the impact of supplier delays, provided dynamic supplier lead-time features are added.
*   **Sep:** **UNSAFE for unweighted training right now (82% mature).** Contains the vital Partner Z population, but 18% of labels are censored (heavily skewed toward V3/Partner Z breaches). Can only be used *after* clearing the outcome API backlog or applying IPCW survival weights on cases with $\ge 45$ days elapsed.
*   **Oct:** **STRICTLY UNSAFE (55% mature).** Heavily right-censored by the API backlog (0.16 observed breach vs >0.30 expected). Must be completely excluded from both recalibration and retraining until label maturity exceeds 95%.

### Staged Remediation Plan
1.  **Stage 1: Immediate Containment (Days 1–3)**
    *   **Freeze automated retraining and October-based recalibration.** Prevent the model from calibrating down to October's artificial 0.16 breach rate.
    *   **Fix pipeline & outcome API:** Clear the October outcome API backlog (prioritizing open cases >30 days old). Run the deduplication and label-normalization script over April–May raw logs and recompute cached features.
    *   **Deploy a rule-based operational guardrail:** Since the model at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` severely under-predicts V3 cases during the supplier crisis, attach a post-prediction heuristic risk flag whenever triage indicates a parts replacement is likely *or* when the case originates from Partner Z.

2.  **Stage 2: Short-Term Recalibration (Week 1–2)**
    *   Once July–September labels are refreshed via the cleared API, do **not** apply a single global Platt/isotonic recalibration (a global multiplier would push V2 predictions way too high while still under-predicting V3).
    *   Instead, implement **stratified post-hoc recalibration (or contextual logistic offset)** conditioned on coarse information available at diagnosis: `(Source: Partner Z vs Legacy) x (Triage Route Intent: Remote vs Parts vs Standard)`. Use mature August–September cohorts (with IPCW for any remaining censored September tails) to align mean predicted risk with the ~0.31+ reality.

3.  **Stage 3: Feature Engineering & Pipeline Repair (Weeks 2–4)**
    *   **Predictive routing features:** Since the routing policy (Jun) and V3 surge (Aug–Oct) happen immediately *after* diagnosis, extract the diagnostic outcome codes, technician triage notes/flags, and fault categories generated *during* `Initial Diagnosis Completed` that determine whether the next step will be `Remote Fix`, `Dispatch Technician`, or `Order Part`.
    *   **Dynamic operational state features:** Inject rolling 7-day/14-day median `Order Part -> Part Received` elapsed time (overall and by part category) and current warehouse backorder counts into the feature vector at prediction time.
    *   **Population features:** Integrate `partner_id`, `rural_travel_distance_km`, and `device_age_months` into the feature pipeline.

4.  **Stage 4: Full Retraining & Architecture Decision (Month 2)**
    *   Retrain on **Jan–Sep (recomputed Apr–May, refreshed Aug–Sep)** using sample weights that emphasize Jun–Sep, and validate on a freshly matured early-October fixed-vintage cohort.
    *   **Model Architecture (Two-Stage / Hurdle vs Segment-Specific):**
        *   *Variant-specific models:* Because the true variant (V1, V2, V3) is only known *after* `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, you cannot route a case to a hard "V3-only model" at prediction time unless the next-activity routing decision is already deterministic at the end of diagnosis. Instead, a **multi-task or hierarchical two-stage architecture** is justified:
            $$\hat{P}(\text{Breach} \mid X) = \sum_{v \in \{\text{V1, V2, V3}\}} \hat{P}(\text{Variant}=v \mid X_{\text{diag}}) \cdot \hat{P}(\text{Breach} \mid X_{\text{diag}}, \text{Variant}=v, Z_{\text{env}})$$
            where $Z_{\text{env}}$ includes current supplier lead times and rural dispatch distance. This explicitly decouples **routing policy shifts** ($\hat{P}(\text{Variant}=v \mid X)$, like June's V2 policy) from **execution bottlenecks** ($\hat{P}(\text{Breach} \mid \text{Variant}=\text{V3}, Z_{\text{env}})$, like August's supplier crisis).
        *   *Source-specific (Partner Z) models:* A completely separate model for Partner Z is **not** recommended initially (only 1–2 months of partially matured data exist, risking severe overfitting). Instead, use a **unified model with explicit interaction terms / hierarchical embeddings** for `Partner Z`, `rural_travel_distance`, and `device_age`, combined with group-level calibration layers.

### Monthly Process-Evidence Extraction with PM4Py

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

def audit_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    # 1. Read raw events and standardize timestamps
    raw_df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    raw_df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw_df["timestamp"], utc=True)

    # 2. Normalize renamed diagnosis labels BEFORE deduplication and feature extraction
    activity_map = {
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    }
    raw_df["activity_normalized"] = (
        raw_df["activity"].replace(activity_map).fillna("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")
    )

    # 3. Duplicate-handling strategy:
    # Sort chronologically, remove exact duplicate (case_id, activity_normalized, timestamp)
    # and collapse back-to-back identical activity repetitions within a 5-minute window
    raw_df = raw_df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "activity_normalized"])
    raw_df = raw_df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity_normalized", "timestamp"], keep="first")
    time_diff = raw_df.groupby(["case_id", "activity_normalized"])["timestamp"].diff().dt.total_seconds()
    dedup_df = raw_df[(time_diff.isna()) | (time_diff > 300)].copy()

    # 4. Format as a PM4Py event log DataFrame
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        dedup_df,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity_normalized",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        timest_format=None
    )

    # Compute case start month for Case-Start Cohorting
    case_starts = log_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().dt.to_period("M")
    log_df["case_start_month"] = log_df["case:concept:name"].map(case_starts)
    months = sorted(log_df["case_start_month"].dropna().unique())

    known_activities = {
        "Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
        "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed",
        "Remote Fix", "Order Part", "Part Received"
    }

    records = []
    for m in months:
        m_start = m.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        m_end = m.end_time.tz_localize("UTC")

        # COHORT A: Event-calendar cohort (all events occurring within calendar month m).
        # Interpretation: Detects immediate operational/logging anomalies (e.g., April 1 migration)
        # and current in-flight bottleneck durations, regardless of when the case started.
        cal_events_df = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log_df, m_start.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), m_end.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), mode="events"
        )

        # COHORT B: Case-start cohort (full traces of cases that initiated in month m).
        # Interpretation: Aligns with predictive model evaluation cohorts and variant mix,
        # but later months (Sep/Oct) will have right-censored traces and immature labels.
        cohort_df = log_df[log_df["case_start_month"] == m].copy()

        # Discover frequency DFG and Performance-DFG on the Case-Start Cohort
        dfg_freq, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort_df)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort_df)

        # Extract variant distribution
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort_df)
        total_cases = cohort_df["case:concept:name"].nunique()

        def variant_share(target_tuple):
            # pm4py.get_variants keys may be tuples of activities or comma-joined strings depending on version
            count = sum(
                len(v) if isinstance(v, list) else v
                for k, v in variants.items()
                if (tuple(k.split(",")) if isinstance(k, str) else tuple(k)) == target_tuple
            )
            return count / max(total_cases, 1)

        v1_tuple = ("Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed")
        v2_tuple = ("Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix", "Case Closed")
        v3_tuple = ("Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Order Part", "Part Received", "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed")

        # Extract temporal features up to prediction point (Initial Diagnosis Completed)
        diag_times = (
            cohort_df[cohort_df["concept:name"] == "Initial Diagnosis Completed"]
            .groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].first()
        )
        prefix_df = cohort_df[
            cohort_df["time:timestamp"] <= cohort_df["case:concept:name"].map(diag_times)
        ]
        temporal_feats = (
            pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(prefix_df)
            if not prefix_df.empty else pd.DataFrame()
        )

        # Extract key Performance-DFG edge metrics (mean elapsed time in hours from perf_dfg)
        part_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        remote_edge = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
        part_wait_hrs = perf_dfg.get(part_edge, {}).get("mean", np.nan) / 3600.0
        remote_fix_hrs = perf_dfg.get(remote_edge, {}).get("mean", np.nan) / 3600.0

        unknown_share = (~cal_events_df["concept:name"].isin(known_activities)).mean()

        records.append({
            "month": str(m),
            "cases_started": total_cases,
            "calendar_events": len(cal_events_df),
            "unknown_activity_share_cal": round(float(unknown_share), 4),
            "v1_share": round(variant_share(v1_tuple), 3),
            "v2_share": round(variant_share(v2_tuple), 3),
            "v3_share": round(variant_share(v3_tuple), 3),
            "dfg_order_to_part_count": dfg_freq.get(part_edge, 0),
            "perf_order_to_part_mean_hrs": round(float(part_wait_hrs), 2) if pd.notna(part_wait_hrs) else None,
            "perf_diag_to_remote_mean_hrs": round(float(remote_fix_hrs), 2) if pd.notna(remote_fix_hrs) else None,
            "temporal_feature_rows": len(temporal_feats),
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(records)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1.  **Retraining or recalibrating on immature/right-censored labels (The October Trap)**
    *   *Failure Mode:* Taking October's observed breach rate (0.16) and mean predicted risk (0.23) at face value and recalibrating the model downward (or retraining on October data). Because fast V2 resolutions finish in ~4.2 hours while V3 breaches wait 155+ hours for parts and get stuck in the outcome API backlog, the 55% matured October cohort systematically excludes breaches.
    *   *Consequence:* Once the API backlog clears and October's true breach rate (>0.32) materializes, a downward-calibrated model will severely under-predict SLA breaches during the height of the supplier crisis, starving high-risk cases of proactive escalation.
    *   *Mitigation:* Enforce a hard **Label-Maturity Gate ($\ge 95\%$ completeness)** before any cohort enters calibration or training pipelines, use fixed-vintage comparisons for monitoring, and apply IPCW survival adjustments when estimating recent breach rates.

2.  **Calibrating or retraining around a data-pipeline bug (The April/May Trap)**
    *   *Failure Mode:* Treating April’s AUROC crash (0.67) and risk inflation (0.28 predicted vs 0.19 observed) as genuine concept drift, and retraining or fitting a calibration curve on corrupted April prefixes (where `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` was unmapped, events were duplicated, and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` was 17%).
    *   *Consequence:* The model learns spurious weights tied to broken prefix lengths and missing queue features. The moment the May 6 mapping patch restores normal feature distributions, the newly "recalibrated" model will systematically under-predict risk on clean data.
    *   *Mitigation:* Gate all drift detectors on **Layer 1 Data-Quality checks** (schema conformance, unknown activity rate <1%, missingness checks). When a schema migration occurs, backfill and recompute historical features from normalized raw logs rather than adapting model weights to corrupted inputs.

3.  **Ignoring supplier-driven downstream concept drift by relying purely on pre-diagnosis static features (The July–September Trap)**
    *   *Failure Mode:* Diagnosing the August–September degradation as simple covariate drift or tuning hyperparameters on existing pre-diagnosis features without incorporating external operational state (`Order Part -> Part Received` elapsed time jumping from 28 h to 155 h).
    *   *Consequence:* Because predictions are triggered at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`—*before* `Order Part` occurs—a static model has no mechanism to know that a case needing a part now faces a 6-day supplier delay instead of a 1-day wait. It will continue predicting ~0.32 risk for V3 cases when actual breach risk is >0.60.
    *   *Mitigation:* Augment case-level prefix features with **dynamic environment/context features** (rolling 7-day median duration of `Order Part -> Part Received` from the Performance-DFG and active backorder counts) and adopt a two-stage model that pairs routing propensity with dynamic path duration.

4.  **Attributing Partner Z’s higher breach rate to "unfair model bias" or applying blind demographic parity adjustments (The September Population Trap)**
    *   *Failure Mode:* Observing Partner Z’s 0.45 breach rate (vs 0.27 for Legacy) or higher risk scores after retraining, assuming the model is unfairly penalizing Partner Z, and enforcing equalized risk predictions across partners without stratified process evidence.
    *   *Consequence:* Partner Z genuinely has a higher structural risk because 58% of its cases require parts (V3) during a severe supplier shortage, its technicians travel longer rural distances, and its customers have older devices. Artificially suppressing Partner Z risk scores masks genuine operational bottlenecks and withholds necessary SLA buffer/expedited shipping from rural customers.
    *   *Mitigation:* Perform **variant-stratified and covariate-adjusted transportability audits** before making fairness or calibration interventions. Include explicit causal/operational covariates (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, `V3_routing_intent`) in the model so risk reflects physical repair complexity rather than an opaque partner ID proxy.

5.  **Triggering full model retraining for a benign post-diagnosis routing policy change (The June Trap)**
    *   *Failure Mode:* Seeing June’s ECE rise to 0.060 (0.20 predicted vs 0.15 observed) after the remote-routing policy increased V2 from 34% to 52%, and discarding historical training data to retrain from scratch on a tiny window.
    *   *Consequence:* The model's discrimination in June was still strong (AUROC 0.79), and within-variant breach rates (V1: 0.16, V2: 0.09, V3: 0.35) were completely stable. Dropping Jan–May training data throws away sample size and variance stability when only the post-diagnosis routing probability $P(\text{V2} \mid X_{\text{diag}})$ changed.
    *   *Mitigation:* Use a **decomposed (two-stage) prediction structure** or targeted **post-policy recalibration** when the Intervention Registry and Process-Variant Monitor show a clean routing shift with stable within-variant conditional breach rates.
