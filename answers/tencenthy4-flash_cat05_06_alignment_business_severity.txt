## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is the **business impact if the facts are confirmed**, not the edit distance. It should be judged against:

- **Control objective violated**: fraud screening, manual authorization, payment sequencing, notification SLA, evidence retention, model/data integrity.
- **Amount and exposure**: direct claim amount, high-risk status, bank-account change, and fraud indicators can matter more than amount alone.
- **Reversibility**: payments are hard to reverse; a post-payment approval failure is much worse than a logging defect.
- **Timing**: approvals must precede payment; claimant notification must be within 24 hours.
- **Customer and regulatory harm**: wrong recipient, unnotified claimant, unsupported high-risk payment, audit trail gaps.
- **Recurrence**: a repeated connector, mapping, or rules-engine defect raises systemic risk even when each case is small.
- **Evidence reliability**: signed, immutable, linked evidence is stronger than verbal statements or mutable text fields.
- **Model scope**: denied, withdrawn, or non-payable cases may be outside a payable-claim model; forcing them through the model creates artificial deviations.

Suggested scale:

| Severity | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No material control issue; harmless telemetry, fitting artifact, or properly handled case. |
| 2 | Low-impact data/model/scope issue; little or no financial exposure; no irreversible harmful action. |
| 3 | Moderate policy, evidence, or customer-communication gap; needs corrective action but not immediate containment. |
| 4 | Serious control or evidence failure involving high value, bank change, unreliable notification evidence, or material compliance exposure. |
| 5 | Severe control failure: payment before approval, high-risk/high-value payment without required fraud evidence, or other immediately material fraud/financial/regulatory exposure. |

**Confidence differs from severity.** Confidence is about how strongly the evidence supports the interpretation. A low-confidence issue can still be high severity if the potential harm is large; a high-confidence issue can be low severity if it is merely telemetry. A missing event is not proof the activity did not occur; it is proof that the event is absent from that log source. Confidence falls when evidence is verbal, external, mutable, or scope-disputed.

Key deviation categories used below:

- **Harmless technical noise**: UI telemetry, autosave, retry events not revealing a control issue.
- **Hidden model artifact**: `T(tau)` silent routing that is technically fitting.
- **Process-model mismatch / out-of-scope**: payable-claim model applied to denial or withdrawal.
- **Event-label mismatch**: same business activity recorded under a different label.
- **Missing evidence**: required activity may have happened outside the logged system, but retained evidence is absent or weak.
- **Actual operational deviation**: control was missing, late, or performed after payment.
- **Data-aware policy failure**: trace fits the process tree, but P1/P2 data conditions were violated.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is a chosen edit-distance metric. In this dataset every visible log/model move costs 1 uniformly, so the metric counts moves rather than business harm.

- **Cost-function design**: CL-602 has raw cost 1 because one model move, `M(Fraud Screen)`, is missing. That single move concerns a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change. CL-603 also has raw cost 1 because of a duplicate notification retry. Equal cost does not mean equal risk.
- **Irreversibility and sequencing**: CL-604 raw cost 3 includes a payment that occurred before manual approval. The financial and control failure is severe because the payment is hard to reverse and P3 is explicit. CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4 but are largely model-scope artifacts with no payment intended or executed. Raw cost would wrongly rank scope mismatches above a prepayment failure.
- **Hidden moves**: CL-605 includes `T(tau,c=0)`. Silent moves are technically fitting and cost 0. Here that is harmless, but zero from hidden routing should not be confused with proof that all business controls passed.
- **Data conditions absent from the model**: CL-609 and CL-610 have raw cost 0 because the process tree permits Auto Approve. The tree does not encode amount, risk, or bank-change conditions. CL-610 is especially dangerous: a recent beneficiary-bank change should have forced Fraud Screen and Manual Approve, yet the alignment is fully synchronous.
- **Activity granularity and labels**: CL-611 has cost 2 because `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` are treated as different activities. The business activity likely happened; the deviation is a canonical-label/mapping issue.
- **Duplicated telemetry**: CL-601 raw cost 3 comes entirely from workbench/autosave/close telemetry. CL-603 raw cost 1 comes from a retry duplicate with the same message ID. These are P5 technical events unless they reveal a control issue.
- **Missing-but-external evidence**: CL-606 has `M(Assess Loss)` in the core log, but an external licensed adjuster report exists. The business problem is evidence integration and reliability, not necessarily failure to assess.
- **Customer/regulatory timing and evidence quality**: CL-608 raw cost 1 reflects a missing core notification. The material issues are +3-hour timing against P4 and the mutable link used as evidence.
- **Scope**: CL-607 and CL-612 force denied and withdrawn cases through a payable-claim model. Their high raw costs are generated by model design, not by business noncompliance. P6 explicitly flags scope uncertainty.

Therefore, raw cost is useful as a diagnostic signal that something differs from the model, but business severity requires overlay policy, amount, temporal order, evidence reliability, customer impact, recurrence, and scope judgment.

        "risk_level": "LOW",
        "bank_change_recent": False,
        "policy_flags": ["withdrawal_not_modeled"],
        "scope_issue": True,
        "sla_issue": False,
        "confidence": "High",
        "business_severity": 2,
        "business_rationale": "Withdrawn claim forced through payable path.",
    },
]

supplied = pd.DataFrame(records)

def count_move_prefixes(alignment_string):
    parts = [p.strip() for p in alignment_string.split("")]
    counts = {"n_S": 0, "n_L": 0, "n_M": 0, "n_T": 0}
    for p in parts:
        if p.startswith("S("):
            counts["n_S"] += 1
        elif p.startswith("L("):
            counts["n_L"] += 1
        elif p.startswith("M("):
            counts["n_M"] += 1
        elif p.startswith("T("):
            counts["n_T"] += 1
    return pd.Series(counts)

supplied = pd.concat(
    [supplied, supplied["supplied_alignment"].apply(count_move_prefixes)],
    axis=1,
)

def flatten_unique(series):
    values = []
    for item in series:
        if isinstance(item, (list, tuple, set)):
            values.extend(item)
        elif isinstance(item, str) and item:
            values.append(item)
    return sorted(set(values))

pattern_summary = (
    supplied
    .groupby("pattern", as_index=False)
    .agg(
        cases=("case", lambda s: ", ".join(s)),
        n_cases=("case", "nunique"),
        n_S=("n_S", "sum"),
        n_L=("n_L", "sum"),
        n_M=("n_M", "sum"),
        n_T=("n_T", "sum"),
        technical_alignment_cost_sum=("technical_alignment_cost", "sum"),
        exposure_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        high_risk_cases=("risk_level", lambda s: int((s == "HIGH").sum())),
        max_business_severity=("business_severity", "max"),
        policy_flags=("policy_flags", flatten_unique),
    )
)

# 7. Evidence table for prioritization.
# This is deliberately an evidence table, not a formula replacing narrative judgment.
# Technical alignment cost and business severity remain separate columns.
risk_prioritization_evidence = (
    supplied[
        [
            "case",
            "pattern",
            "technical_alignment_cost",
            "business_severity",
            "confidence",
            "amount_eur",
            "risk_level",
            "bank_change_recent",
            "policy_flags",
            "scope_issue",
            "sla_issue",
            "business_rationale",
        ]
    ]
    .sort_values(
        by=["business_severity", "amount_eur", "technical_alignment_cost"],
        ascending=[False, False, False],
    )
    .reset_index(drop=True)
)

print("\nPattern aggregate evidence:")
print(pattern_summary.to_string(index=False))

print("\nRisk prioritization evidence table:")
print(risk_prioritization_evidence.to_string(index=False))
