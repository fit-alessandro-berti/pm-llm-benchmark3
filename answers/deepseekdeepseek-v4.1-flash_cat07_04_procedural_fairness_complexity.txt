# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

**Matching or stratification variables.** Stratify or match on risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month. Also include language_preference, disability_accommodation_requested, and applicant_group interactions. Run models both with and without risk_score_band and documentation_completeness because these may be legitimate controls or may encode historical bias/language barriers.

**Path/variant comparison.** Use V1 as the reference path. Compare variant shares by group after stratifying on case mix. Then compare outcomes within the same variant: e.g., V2 for B versus A, V4 for B versus A, V5 for C versus A, V3 for D versus A. A disparity within the same path is more procedurally concerning than a difference explained by different required paths.

**Resource-assignment comparison.** Compare caseworker mix, specialist queue assignment, handover counts, and resource roles. Check whether B’s bilingual routing creates extra generalistbilingualgeneralist handovers, and whether C’s accommodation routing creates generalistaccommodationsupervisor handovers. Adjust for region and path so resource effects are not confused with case mix.

**Waiting-time decomposition.** Split cycle time into active service time and waiting time. Decompose waits at Submit  Identity Verification, Completeness  Request Missing Documents, Language Support  Eligibility, Accommodation  Eligibility, Risk Screening  Fraud Review, Supervisor Review  Decision Draft, and Decision Draft  Notification. Compare medians and upper quantiles by group and variant.

**Sensitivity analysis for biased covariates.** Re-run adjusted models excluding risk_score_band and documentation_completeness. Test how much unmeasured bias would be needed to explain away residual group differences. Stratify documentation completeness by whether language support was provided before the completeness check. Treat risk_score_band as potentially discriminatory until audited.

**Subgroup checks by region and channel.** Check interactions by region and channel, especially in-person versus online. The notes say in-person applications wait longer and two regions have supervisor backlog. Group B in-person cases may be affected by bilingual staffing; Group C accommodation cases may be affected by regional specialist availability. Report subgroup estimates with uncertainty intervals, not just point estimates.

**Distinguishing disparity, discrimination, legitimate routing, and uncertainty.** Disparity is an observed difference. Legitimate routing is a policy-required path, such as fraud review for high risk or accommodation review for requested accommodation. Discrimination would require evidence that similarly situated applicants receive different treatment because of group membership or proxy variables. Uncertainty remains high because covariates are imperfect and resource workload is coarsely observed.

**Language support path (V4, Group B).** V4 is 24% for B versus 1% for A, with median cycle time 19.2 days and a 3.8-day wait from Language Support Contact to Eligibility. This is partly justified by need: 57% of Group B needs language support. But the concentration on only 7 bilingual caseworkers and extra handovers suggest a capacity and process-design problem. # 4. Plausible mechanisms

**Legitimate complexity and policy routing.** Higher risk scores trigger Fraud Review; disability accommodation requests trigger Accommodation Review; minority-language needs trigger Language Support Contact; incomplete documentation triggers Request Missing Documents. These are expected and not evidence of discrimination by themselves.

**Capacity bottlenecks in rights-preserving steps.** Few bilingual caseworkers and accommodation specialists create queues. If support steps are under-resourced, applicants using their rights wait longer. The process may be fair in policy but unfair in implementation.

**Documentation and language barriers.** Documentation completeness may reflect language access, notice clarity, digital access, or translation quality rather than applicant diligence. Request Missing Documents can create loops that disproportionately affect B/C even after controlling for recorded completeness.

**Differential scrutiny or risk aversion.** B/C have higher adjusted supervisor review and missing-document requests. D has higher fraud review and appeals. Risk scores, caseworker discretion, or regional cultures may lead to more escalation for some groups, especially if risk_score_band is historically biased.

**Resource assignment and handover design.** Generalistspecialistgeneralist and specialistsupervisor handovers create delays, information loss, and repeated contacts. Overloaded regional officers and supervisors who also do specialist reviews compound this.

**Appeal/rework driven by communication quality.** Higher appeals for B/D may reflect unclear denial reasons, lack of translated explanations, low trust, or correctable errors. The event log records appeal_flag but not appeal reason or quality of explanation.

**Monitoring gaps.** Service-level targets do not include separate timeliness thresholds for language support or accommodation review. Delays in these rights-preserving steps are therefore less visible and less accountable.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Measurement bias.** risk_score_band may encode historical over-policing, prior interactions, or socioeconomic proxies. documentation_completeness may be affected by language barriers, unclear forms, or lack of assistance. Neither is a neutral measure of applicant deservingness.

**Missing applicant needs.** The log lacks severity, urgency, health status, digital literacy, transportation, childcare, informal support, and specific accommodation needs. These affect cycle time and may differ by group.

**Unobserved resource workload.** Resource workload is observed only at daily granularity. Real-time queue length, caseload, experience, specialist availability, leave, and supervisor conflicts are missing.

**Appeal quality.** appeal_flag does not capture appeal reason, outcome, whether the applicant understood the decision, or whether the appeal revealed a process error. A higher appeal rate is not automatically bad, but it is a burden and a signal.

**Case outcome quality.** case_outcome does not capture dignity of treatment, explanation quality, consistency of communication, or whether the applicant felt heard.

**Historical bias in risk scores.** If risk_score_band is used to justify fraud review or supervisor review, existing disparities can be reproduced and amplified.

**Overlapping groups and selection effects.** Group C may also include B-language needs; Group D may overlap with other groups. Channel and region are not randomly assigned. These factors make causal claims weak.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

**Add capacity for language support or accommodation review.** Hire or train more bilingual caseworkers and accommodation specialists; use teleinterpretation, cross-training, and dedicated queues. Side effects: cost, recruitment time, possible overburdening of minority staff, increased demand, and need for quality control.

**Service-level targets for support steps.** Set separate timeliness thresholds for Language Support Contact  Eligibility and Accommodation Review  Eligibility, not only total cycle time. Side effects: resources may be diverted from other steps, staff may rush support, and targets can be gamed. Do not remove support steps to meet targets.

**Routing redesign to reduce handovers.** Co-locate specialists, use case-manager continuity, allow parallel specialist review, schedule joint reviews, and create warm handoffs. Side effects: loss of specialized expertise, role confusion, supervisor conflicts, and training needs.

**Decision-explanation improvements.** Use plain language, translated notices, reason codes, clear missing-document instructions, and explicit appeal rights. Side effects: increased workload, translation and legal review costs, and possible increase in appeals if applicants better understand their rights.

**Monitoring adjusted disparities.** Build dashboards by group, variant, region, channel, and resource, with alerts for adjusted residual disparities. Side effects: false positives from unmeasured confounding, privacy concerns, and need for statistical rigor.

**Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness.** Validate predictive fairness, recalibrate models, remove proxy variables, and manually review missing-document cases for language-access issues. Side effects: reduced predictive accuracy, governance burden, and difficulty removing historical bias.

**Additional options.** Use applicant navigators, proactively offer language support before completeness checks, allow flexible document upload, and prioritize support-step queues. Side effects: equity-efficiency trade-offs, possible gaming, and need for clear eligibility rules.
