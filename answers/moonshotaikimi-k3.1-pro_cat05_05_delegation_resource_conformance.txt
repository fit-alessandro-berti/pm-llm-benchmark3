## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let \(H(e)\) be the reliably attributed human for event \(e\), where such attribution exists, and \(t(e)\) its timestamp.

- **RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case, if \(d\) is Draft_Change_Request and \(a\) is Approve_Change, require \(H(d)\neq H(a)\). Aliases are normalized before this comparison. Emergency status does not waive RR1.
- **RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** For implementation \(i\) and verification \(v\), require \(H(i)\neq H(v)\). An unresolved service account is not automatically the implementer, but neither can it be used to claim that the control passed.
- **RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** For a non-emergency HIGH-risk approval, the attributed approver must either hold the QM role at \(t(a)\), or hold a valid QM delegation covering that event. A valid delegation requires:
  1. an authentic instrument;
  2. start \(\leq t(a)\leq\) end;
  3. coverage of the activity and change scope;
  4. satisfaction of any principal-absence/formal-unavailability condition at the event;
  5. attribution of the action to the named delegate; and
  6. no prohibited subdelegation.

  A delegation matching only the resource string is not enough if the account was actually used by someone else.

- **RR4 — Emergency substitution.** For a credible HIGH-risk emergency, a standing IC or valid IC delegate may provide the emergency approval, but the change must still have a QM or valid QM delegate’s retrospective review within two business days. RR4 does not waive RR1, RR2, delegation scope, or the prohibition on subdelegation. `emergency=true` alone is not proof of a credible safety rationale.

  I use the **implementation timestamp** as the review-clock trigger. The next site business day is day 1; weekends and site holidays are excluded; the deadline is the end of day 2. With UTC used for this analysis and no holidays in the period, an implementation on Tuesday 2026-08-11 has day 1 on Wednesday 2026-08-12 and day 2 on Thursday 2026-08-13. Thus:
  - CHG-505’s review on Friday 2026-08-14 at 16:00 was late.
  - CHG-510’s review on Wednesday 2026-08-12 at 10:00 was timely, but timeliness does not cure the reviewer’s lack of authority.

  Using an exact-hour rather than end-of-day deadline would not change either result.

- **RR5 — Identity evidence.** Service accounts have no human role. Reliable session evidence must connect the action to an authorized human before the event can support human-level conformance. Attribution strength and role authority are separate questions.
- **RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared devices, and dense networks are investigative indicators. They do not establish collusion without evidence such as coordinated account use, concealment, transaction-bound communications, or other corroboration.

**Principal and delegate leave.** Leave does not itself revoke a standing role or a delegation. Asha could validly perform an urgent QM approval while recorded on leave if she actually performed it. Conversely, her VPN session does not prove that she personally acted, and one urgent action does not automatically cancel D-QM-01 for later events. Ben’s sick leave likewise does not automatically terminate D-QM-01, but it materially conflicts with attribution when his account was used and he denies acting.

**Later database entries.** A QMS `created_at` value is not a delegation’s `valid_from` value. A later database entry cannot, by itself, prove that authority existed earlier. It can preserve or reference earlier evidence. Here, the signed PDF dated 2026-08-09 18:00 is the material support for D-QM-01; the 2026-08-12 migration record does not retroactively invalidate CHG-501.

**Technical versus substantive conformance.** A resource/delegation match can technically pass while the authorized-human objective remains unproven, as in CHG-506. Conversely, absence of a raw-resource overlap is not a substantive pass where the verifier is unattributed, as in CHG-503. Strong attribution in CHG-509 resolves identity much better, but not the missing principal-absence condition.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

The portfolio evidence supports monitoring and targeted investigation, **not a collusion finding**.

Two distinctions are important:

1. **The historical Clara–Ben concentration is also an authority-audit lead.** Ben has no standing QM approval authority. D-QM-01 cannot justify any of the 18 approvals falling outside 2026-08-10 through 2026-08-14, and each event inside that interval still requires its scope and principal-absence conditions. This question exists independently of collusion.
2. **PM4Py networks are descriptive.** A handover edge is not proof of a physical handoff or communication, and a working-together edge is not proof of coordination or simultaneous work. Aliases and unresolved service accounts can also distort the network.

At portfolio level, the pattern can reasonably be labelled **SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN** for monitoring purposes. It does not establish intent, collusion, or the actor in any disputed session.

def evaluate_delegation(delegation, event):
    timestamp = event[TS]

    if delegation["scope_type"] == "case_scope":
        scope_ok = bool(event["scope"] in delegation["scopes"])
    else:
        incident = incident_link.get(event[CASE])

            None if incident is None

        )

    if delegation["requires_absence"]:
        absence_ok, absence_note = principal_absence(
            delegation["principal"], timestamp
        )
    else:
        absence_ok, absence_note = True, "Not required"

    checks = {
        "time": bool(in_interval(
            timestamp, delegation["start"], delegation["end"]
        )),
        "activity": bool(event[ACT] in delegation["activities"]),
        "scope": scope_ok,
        "principal_absence": absence_ok,
        "instrument": bool(delegation["instrument_ok"]),
        "not_prohibited_subdelegation": bool(
            not delegation["prohibited_subdelegation"]
        ),
    }

    values = list(checks.values())
    if any(value is False for value in values):
        valid = False
    elif any(value is None for value in values):
        valid = None
    else:
        valid = True

    details = dict(checks)
    details["absence_note"] = absence_note
    details["evidence"] = delegation["evidence"]
    return valid, details

def role_or_delegation_authority(event, required_role):

        return "PASS", {"standing_role": required_role}

        delegations["delegate"].eq(human)
        & delegations["granted_role"].eq(required_role)
    ]

    evaluations = []
    for _, delegation in candidates.iterrows():
        valid, details = evaluate_delegation(delegation, event)
        evaluations.append({
            "delegation": delegation["id"],
            "valid": valid,
            "checks": details,
        })

    if not evaluations:
        return "FAIL", {"reason": f"No standing or delegated {required_role} authority"}

    if any(item["valid"] is True for item in evaluations):
        return "PASS", {"delegations": evaluations}
    if any(item["valid"] is None for item in evaluations):
        return "INDETERMINATE", {"delegations": evaluations}
    return "FAIL", {"delegations": evaluations}

def two_business_day_deadline(trigger):
    # Site calendar states no holiday in this period. UTC is used for analysis.
    day = trigger.normalize()
    count = 0
    while count < 2:
        day += pd.Timedelta(days=1)
        if day.weekday() < 5:  # Monday=0, Friday=4
            count += 1
    return day + pd.Timedelta(hours=23, minutes=59)

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** Require a controlled workflow before the effective time, with principal, canonical delegate ID, role, permitted activities, change/incident scope, inclusive start/end timestamps, subdelegation rule, revocation, and delegate acceptance. Block prohibited subdelegation technically rather than relying on email interpretation.
- **Effective-time recording:** Separate `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and migration timestamps. Preserve the original signed instrument and make approval services evaluate authority as of the event timestamp. Reconcile migrated records against signed source documents.
- **Principal availability:** Integrate leave, formal unavailability, and on-call records with delegation activation. Do not infer absence merely from a weekend. Provide an explicit way to record a principal temporarily working during leave and whether that suspends a delegation for a specified interval. Add a process for replacing or suspending a delegate who becomes unavailable.
- **Alias management:** Maintain an effective-dated canonical human directory across HR, IAM, QMS, and analytics. Prevent duplicate human accounts where possible, version the alias map, and normalize aliases before all SoD and network analyses. Never include a service account in a human alias mapping without session-specific evidence.
- **Individual authentication:** Remove approval and verification privileges from shared human-facing accounts. Require named authentication and transaction-bound FIDO/e-signature approval or verification, binding the person to case, activity, timestamp, and content hash. Where shared workstations remain, require individual reauthentication at each controlled action.
- **Emergency review:** Require a structured safety rationale and incident identifier before invoking RR4. Validate IC delegation scope, including CI-44 linkage. Calculate business-day deadlines automatically, escalate before expiry, and require the retrospective delegation to cover both the review activity and system scope. A late or unqualified review should remain visibly nonconforming even if a later review is completed.
- **Verification eligibility:** Maintain event-time verifier qualifications and independence requirements explicitly. The end of Eva’s Independent Verifier interval should trigger a procedure review rather than an assumption based on her current QE title.
- **Pair rotation and independent challenge:** Use risk-based rotation or independent sampling for concentrated drafter–approver pairs while preserving competent coverage. Assess review quality, rework, and exceptions rather than treating rotation itself as proof of control effectiveness.
- **Network monitoring:** Produce handover and working-together networks on canonical human identities, flag unresolved service nodes, and compare results with staffing, cell assignment, and expected workflows. Use thresholds to select cases for review, not to accuse users of collusion.
- **Credential-conflict response:** Preserve IdP, MFA, VPN, EDR, workstation, badge, and QMS audit records promptly. Distinguish local workstation use from remote office-hosted sessions, and investigate account use during leave without treating leave or denial alone as conclusive.
- **Evidential retention:** Retain signed delegations, migration lineage, role snapshots, safety rationales, incident links, session attribution, review content, and normalized-analysis versions. Apply appropriate legal-hold, privacy, and access controls so that evidence remains both reliable and lawfully usable.
