# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution
**Material Assumptions**

1. All activities are atomic and timestamped from the moment the onboarding request is received.  
2. Resources are uniquely identifiable by their names (e.g., Ana, Ben, ...).  
3. No events outside the listed activity labels occur in any trace.  
4. The **zeta** factor for temporal profile deviations is fixed at2.0.  
5. The event log does **not** contain an `emergency` attribute or jointauthorization flag, so any emergency activation must be inferred from absence of delay in `Obtain Legal Opinion`.

**Conflict R5 vs. R6**

- **R5 (Legal department)**: *After Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion must precede Activate Vendor under all circumstances.*  
- **R6 (Operations department)**: *Emergency activation may occur before Obtain Legal Opinion, but only when jointly authorized outside the event log; the opinion must then arrive within 24h.*

**Possible Resolutions**

1. **Prioritize R5**: Treat R5 as the authoritative rule. Any trace attempting emergency activation without explicit authorisation (i.e., `Obtain Legal Opinion` preceding `Activate Vendor`) is **invalid**. This eliminates R6’s allowance and enforces strict legal sequencing.  
   - *Compliance risk*: Minimal deviation from legal requirements; however, it may unnecessarily block legitimate emergency scenarios if such authorisation existed but was omitted from the log.

2. **Separate Emergency Path**: Augment the model with an **emergency** flag (not present in the log) and enforce that if the flag is set, R6 applies **only** when the `Obtain Legal Opinion` occurs within 24h after `Activate Vendor`. Otherwise, R5 applies.  
   - *Compliance risk*: Requires external metadata (emergency flag) not available; risk of false negatives if the flag is missing, leading to delayed release for genuine emergencies.

**Chosen Resolution**

We adopt **Resolution1** (prioritizing R5) because the event log explicitly lacks any emergency metadata, making the R6 condition unverifiable. R5 is the stricter, legally mandated ordering and guarantees that any attempt to activate before legal opinion results in a failure, thus protecting compliance. The tradeoff is that emergency cases must be handled via external authorization mechanisms outside the event log.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping
| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **R1** – Reject vs Activate | Log Skeleton (`never_together`) | `("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")` in `never_together` | Simple pairwise constraint, easy to enforce at runtime | None – not expressible in pure activity models |
| **R2** – Approval exclusivity | DECLARE (`noncoexistence`) | `noncoexistence: {("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")}`; with existence constraints for rejected cases | Guarantees at most one approval in accepted cases, none in rejected | None – captures both conditions |
| **R3** – Clarification loops | DECLARE (`response` / `altresponse`) | `response: {("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")}` with `support=1.0, confidence=0.95` | Models mandatory feedback loop; repeatable | Limited to linear respond pairs; loop depth not bounded |
| **R4** – Escalation  Legal Opinion | DECLARE (`mandatory_response` / `coexistence`) | `coexistence: {("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")}` with supporting rule | Ensures legal opinion if escalation occurs | Does not enforce strict order; order enforced by temporal profile |
| **R5** – Legal Opinion before Activation (chosen resolution) | Log Skeleton (`always_before`) | `("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` in `always_before` | Guarantees ordering irrespective of emergency flag | Cannot discriminate emergency cases without additional flag |
| **R6** – Emergency activation (not applicable in chosen resolution) | Separate resource/dataaware check | External validation: ignore if flag missing; otherwise enforce 24h window after activation | Handles emergency edge case when flag present | Not used in model; risk of false negatives when flag absent |
| **R7** – Sanctions Screening  Escalation | ProcessTree (`precedence`) or DECLARE (`precedence`) | `precedence: {("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")}` | Captures required sequencing | None |
| **R8** – No overlap of approvals | DECLARE (`noncoexistence`) | Duplicate of R2 for ManagerCompliance pair | Guarantees they never cooccur | Redundant but clarifies policy |
| **R9** – Resource segregation | Separate resource/dataaware validation | Python rule: ensure `resource_VerifyBank  resource_Approval` and `resource_Capture  resource_Activate` | Enforces SoD via activitylevel data | Not expressible in pure process models |
| **R10** – At most one activation / release | Log Skeleton (`activ_freq`) | `activ_freq = { "Activate Vendor": {1}, "Release First Payment": {1} }` | Caps occurrence count; easy to enforce | None |
| **R11** – Bank Details change  Suspend Vendor | ProcessTree (`ifthenelse` or DECLARE `altresponse`) | In ProcessTree, after `Activate Vendor`  `Change Bank Details` leads to mandatory `Suspend Vendor` branch, then re`Verify Bank Account` before any later `Release First Payment`. | Captures conditional postactivation behavior | Slight approximation if multiple changes possible |
| **R12** – Temporal expectations | Temporal Profile (dictionary) | See Section6 | Models normative timing; deviations flagged as warnings (zeta=2.0) | None |

# 3. Procedural Backbone
**Compact Formal Description**

```
Start  Onboard
Onboard  [Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account] (concurrent)
 Classify Risk (requires all three)
Classify Risk  (Low)  Manager Approval  Activate Vendor  Release First Payment  Close Onboarding
        (High)  Enhanced Due Diligence  Compliance Approval  Activate Vendor  Release First Payment  Close Onboarding
        (Reject)  Reject Vendor  Close Onboarding
After classification, any "Request Clarification" / "Receive Clarification" loops may interleave with the above branches, but must terminate before the final decision.
If `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` (R5). `Change Bank Details` after activation triggers `Suspend Vendor` and a new `Verify Bank Account` before any later `Release First Payment`.
```

**PM4Py Python Code (ProcessTree)**

```python
import pm4py

# Define activities as nodes
activities = {
    "Start": None,
    "Receive Onboarding Request": "Onboard",
    "Capture Master Data": "Concurrent",
    "Run Sanctions Screening": "Concurrent",
    "Verify Bank Account": "Concurrent",
    "Classify Risk": "Decision",
    "Request Clarification": "Loop",
    "Receive Clarification": "Loop_End",
    "Sanctions Escalation": "Conditional",
    "Obtain Legal Opinion": "Conditional",
    "Enhanced Due Diligence": "Conditional",
    "Manager Approval": "Decision",
    "Compliance Approval": "Decision",
    "Reject Vendor": "Terminate",
    "Activate Vendor": "Post_Activation",
    "Release First Payment": "Terminate",
    "Close Onboarding": "Terminate",
    "Change Bank Details": "Post_Activation_Change",
    "Suspend Vendor": "Post_Activation_Change",
    "Terminate": None
}

# Build ProcessTree (simplified for clarity)
procedural_model = pm4py.BuildProcessTree(
    activities,
    start_event="Receive Onboarding Request",
    decision_points={"Classify Risk": ["Low", "High", "Reject"]},
    loops={("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): True},
    precedence=[
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Verify Bank Account"),
        ("Capture Master Data", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Low_Route"),                # Low risk  Manager Approval
        ("Classify Risk", "High_Route"),              # High risk  Enhanced Due Diligence
        ("Classify Risk", "Reject_Route"),            # Reject
        ("Low_Route", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("High_Route", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Reject_Route", "Reject Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),
        # Escalation logic (R5 – enforced via Log Skeleton)
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor"),
        # Postactivation conditional
        ("Activate Vendor", "Change Bank Details"),
        ("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor"),
        ("Suspend Vendor", "Verify Bank Account"),   # reverification before next payment
        # Temporal ordering enforced in another layer
    ],
    parallel=["Capture Master Data", "Run Sanctions Screening", "Verify Bank Account"]
)

# The ProcessTree captures concurrency, optional clarification loops, and terminal outcomes.
print(pm4py.view_processtree(procedural_model))
```

**Explanation**

- **Concurrency**: Activities `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute in parallel after the request receipt, respecting the procedural backbone.  
- **Clarification Loop**: Pairs `Request Clarification`  `Receive Clarification` are modeled as a loop that can iterate any number of times before the classification decision.  
- **Terminal Outcomes**: Three exclusive paths (`Low_Route`, `High_Route`, `Reject_Route`) lead to distinct terminations, guaranteeing `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` never coexist (`R1`).  
- **PostActivation Behavior**: After `Activate Vendor`, if `Change Bank Details` occurs, the model forces `Suspend Vendor` followed by a fresh `Verify Bank Account` before any subsequent `Release First Payment`, captured as an “ifthen” branch.  
- **Exclusions**: Direct enforcement of `Sanctions Escalation`  `Obtain Legal Opinion` ordering is deferred to the **Log Skeleton** (`always_before`) because a ProcessTree alone cannot easily express “must occur before any later activation” without assuming a fixed sequence; Log Skeleton provides clearer, runtimefocused ordering checks.

```python
    # Noncoexistence of approvals in accepted cases
    "noncoexistence": {("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")},

    # Decision branches after classification
    "decision": {
        "Classify Risk": {
            "Low_Route": [{"activity": "Manager Approval", "probability": 1.0}],

            "Reject_Route": [{"activity": "Reject Vendor", "probability": 1.0}]
        }
    },
    # Optional loop for clarification (handled via response)
    # Additional precedence for postactivation change logic
    "succession": {
        ("Activate Vendor", "Change Bank Details"): 1.0,
        ("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor"): 1.0,
        ("Suspend Vendor", "Verify Bank Account"): 1.0,
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Release First Payment"): 1.0
    }
}
```
**Rule Table**

| Direction | Activity pair | Semantics |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Response** | (`Request Clarification`, `Receive Clarification`) | Every `Request Clarification` must be immediately followed by `Receive Clarification`. |
| **Precedence** | (`Capture Master Data`, `Classify Risk`) etc. | Classification cannot occur before all three checks are completed. |
| **Noncoexistence** | (`Manager Approval`, `Compliance Approval`) | Both cannot appear in the same accepted case. |
| **Coexistence** | (`Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`) | If one occurs, the other must also occur (enforces R4). |
| **Decision** | `Classify Risk`  `Low_Route`, `High_Route`, `Reject_Route` | Picks one exclusive branch based on risk outcome. |
| **Succession** | (`Activate Vendor`, `Change Bank Details`) … | Captures postactivation conditional flow. |

# 5. LogSkeleton Model
```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": set(),
    "always_before": {("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")},   # R5
    "always_after": set(),
    "never_together": {("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")},        # R1
    "activ_freq": {
        "Activate Vendor": {1},
        "Release First Payment": {1},
        "Request Clarification": None,   # allow any number, handled by response rule
        "Receive Clarification": None
    },
    "directly_follows": set()
}
```
**Explanation of Orienations**

- **`always_before`**: Guarantees legal opinion precedes activation at runtime.  
- **`never_together`**: Explicitly forbids simultaneous rejection and activation, aligning with R1.  
- **`activ_freq`**: Caps `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment` to at most one occurrence each (R10).  
- Overlap with DECLARE: `precedence` and `noncoexistence` are captured in DECLARE; Log Skeleton enforces these **operational** constraints at conformance checking time, providing a safety net against runtime slipthroughs (e.g., misordered events).  

# 6. Temporal Profile
```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),   # 48 hrs ± 24 hrs
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),       # 12 hrs ± 6 hrs
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600)         # 72 hrs ± 36 hrs
}
```
**Reasoning**

Temporal deviations are reported as **warnings** (using `zeta = 2.0`) because they indicate inefficient processing but do **not** constitute a violation of the business process itself. A deviation exceeding `mean ± zeta·std` flags potential delays for followup, not an outright rejection of the case.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots
- **Duplicated Enforcement**: Both `DECLARE` (`noncoexistence`) and Log Skeleton (`never_together`) cover the prohibition of simultaneous approvals. This redundancy provides a safety net but may cause duplicate alerts if both layers are independently evaluated.  
- **Loop Depth Limitation**: `DECLARE` `response` rule only models a single pair; while our traces show at most one clarification loop, the rule could be extended to allow multiple iterations without causing logical inconsistency.  
- **Temporal vs. ControlFlow**: Temporal deviations are warnings, not controlflow violations, potentially allowing inefficient but compliant cases to proceed. This design choice aligns with the policy that timing delays are *normative* but not *disqualifying*.  
- **Missing Emergency Flag**: R6 cannot be expressed without an external flag; the chosen resolution ignores it, possibly leading to false negatives for genuine emergency activations lacking the flag.  
- **Support/Confidence Metadata**: In DECLARE, `support` and `confidence` are statistical summaries; treating them as hard constraints could mislead analysts if the underlying log is small or biased. We deliberately keep them as **normative expectations**, not strict enforcement.  
- **Resource Attribute Reliance**: The resource checks assume every event carries a `resource` field; any trace lacking this attribute would be marked as `not evaluated`, risking silent compliance failures.  

# 10. Deployment Recommendation
1. **Primary Procedural Representation**: `procedural_model` (ProcessTree) defines the stable workflow, concurrency, and decision branches. It captures the highlevel logic while delegating strict ordering to Log Skeleton.  
2. **Authoritative Policy Layer**: DECLARE (`declare_model`) encodes all **mandatory presence/absence**, **coexistence**, **response**, and **decision** constraints. It is the central repository for policy rules that are explicitly enforced during conformance checking.  
3. **Runtime Conformance Checks Order**  
   a. **Temporal Profile** (`temporal_profile`) – compute zscore for each activity pair; flag warnings for deviations >zeta.  
   b. **Log Skeleton** – evaluate `always_before`, `never_together`, `activ_freq`; raise failures immediately (e.g., contradictory terminations).  
   c. **DECLARE** – run `pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_model)`. Capture any rule violations (e.g., missing clarifications, noncoexistence breaches).  
   d. **Resource/Data Validation** – execute `check_resource_segregation` and `check_temporal_profile` (if needed).  
4. **Handling Contradictory Findings**  
   - If **Procedural** and **DECLARE** both succeed but **Log Skeleton** reports a failure (e.g., `never_together` violation), surface the Log Skeleton failure as the decisive error.  
   - If **DECLARE** flags a rule (e.g., `response` missing) but the procedural model seems okay, present DECLARE’s specific rulelevel message.  
   - Warnings from the temporal profile are logged but **do not block** execution; they are surfaced for processimprovement teams.  
5. **User Interface**  
   - Consolidate verdicts into a unified dashboard: *Pass*, *Fail*, *Warning*, *Not Evaluated*.  
   - Provide drilldowns: for **Fail**, show the exact activity pair and timestamp; for **Warning**, include the statistical deviation.  
   - Highlight **policydependent** cases (e.g., emergency activation) with a note that external authorization is required.  

**Overall**, the hybrid model leverages the strengths of each formalism—Procedural for workflow clarity, DECLARE for exhaustive rule encoding, Log Skeleton for tight ordering checks, a temporal profile for timing health, and a separate validation layer for resource and datalevel SoD. This layered approach ensures comprehensive compliance while allowing flexible handling of emergent scenarios like emergencies.
