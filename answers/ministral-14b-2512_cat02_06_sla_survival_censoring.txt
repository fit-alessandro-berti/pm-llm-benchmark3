# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

## Time Definitions
- **Time origin (t)**: Case submission (`Submit` event).
- **Time horizon (T)**: 72 hours (SLA deadline).
- **Event time (t)**: Duration until terminal outcome or observation cutoff.
  - For terminal cases: `duration_or_age_h` (time to observed outcome).
  - For open cases: `duration_or_age_h` (age at cutoff) + `last_event_gap_h` (time since last event to cutoff).
  - For censored cases: `duration_or_age_h` (age at censoring) + `last_event_gap_h` (time since last event to censoring).

## Estimands
### **Primary Estimand: Cumulative Incidence of Resolution After 72 Hours**
- **Definition**: Probability that a case is resolved *after* the 72-hour SLA deadline, accounting for competing risks (withdrawal, rejection, transfer).
- **Interpretation**: Measures tail risk of unresolved cases beyond the SLA.
- **Contributions**:
  - **S03 (76h resolved)**: Contributes to cumulative incidence at 76h (breach).
  - **S09 (92h open)**: Contributes to risk set until 92h; if unresolved at 92h, censored (informative if `last_event_gap_h` is large).
  - **S06 (27h withdrawn)**: Competing event; does not contribute to resolution incidence.
  - **S08 (68h transferred)**: Competing event; treated as censored under Assumption 1 (informative censoring).

### **Secondary Estimand: Probability of Still Open at 72 Hours**
- **Definition**: Probability that a case has not reached any terminal state (resolved, withdrawn, rejected, transferred) by 72 hours.
- **Interpretation**: Measures active SLA breaches (cases still in process at deadline).
- **Contributions**:
  - **S03 (76h resolved)**: Resolved before 72h  does not contribute.
  - **S09 (92h open)**: Contributes to risk set until 72h; if unresolved at 72h, censored.
  - **S06 (27h withdrawn)**: Terminal before 72h  does not contribute.
  - **S08 (68h transferred)**: Terminal before 72h  does not contribute.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

| Observed Status | Event Indicator | Competing Risk Code | Censoring Rule                     | Sensitivity Alternatives                     |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Resolved        | 1 (Resolution)  | None                 | None                               | None                                          |
| Withdrawn       | 0 (Competing)   | Withdrawal           | None                               | Treat as censored (Assumption 2)              |
| Rejected        | 0 (Competing)   | Rejection            | None                               | Treat as censored (Assumption 2)              |
| Transferred     | 0 (Competing)   | Transfer             | **Assumption 1**: Censored (informative) | **Assumption 2**: Competing event (external failure) | **Assumption 3**: Ignore (drop from analysis) |
| Open            | 0 (Censored)    | None                 | Censored at `duration_or_age_h + last_event_gap_h` | Treat as competing (unobserved failure)       |

**Notes**:
- **Transfers** are treated as competing events under Assumption 2 (external resolution) or censored under Assumption 1 (informative censoring).
- **Withdrawal/Rejection** are competing events (internal failures).
- **Open cases** are right-censored at cutoff time (`duration_or_age_h + last_event_gap_h`).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

### **Survivor Bias**
- **Example**: Cases like **S01 (18h resolved)** and **S05 (9h rejected)** are overrepresented in completed-case analysis because they resolve/reject quickly. Cases like **S09 (92h open)** or **S11 (81h open)** are underrepresented because they are still active or censored.

### **Informative Censoring**
- **Example**: **S09 (92h open)** has a `last_event_gap_h = 31h`, meaning the last event occurred at 61h (92 - 31). If the process slows down after long gaps, censoring is informative (cases with large gaps are more likely to breach SLA).
- **Transfers**: **S08 (68h transferred)** is censored under Assumption 1, but transfer may correlate with unresolved cases (e.g., escalation due to complexity).

### **Competing Risks**
- **Example**: **S06 (27h withdrawn)** and **S14 (63h rejected)** are competing events. Ignoring them biases estimates of resolution risk.
- **Cohort Imbalance**: Cohort B has more **AutoCheck** paths (e.g., **S16, S17**), which may resolve faster, but **S25 (79h open)** shows escalation delays persist.

### **Active Open Cases Below vs. Above 72h**
- **Below 72h**: Cases like **S15 (35h open)** may resolve quickly (e.g., **S16 (16h resolved)**), but **S24 (67h open)** is at risk of breaching.
- **Above 72h**: Cases like **S09 (92h open)** are already breached, but **S26 (104h open)** may still resolve (if `last_event_gap_h` is small).

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

### **Primary Approach: Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF) with Competing Risks**
- **Method**: Aalen-Johansen estimator (non-parametric CIF) for resolution vs. competing events (withdrawal, rejection, transfer).
- **Justification**:
  - Directly estimates **probability of resolution after 72h** (primary estimand).
  - Handles competing risks explicitly (avoids bias from treating them as censored).
  - Robust to informative censoring (transfers/open cases).
- **Implementation**: `lifelines` (Python) or `cmprsk` (R) for CIF.

### **Secondary Approach: Multi-State Model**
- **Method**: Markov model with states:
  - **Active**  **Resolved/Withdrawn/Rejected/Transferred/Open**.
  - **Transfers** as absorbing states (Assumption 2) or competing risks.
- **Justification**:
  - Captures **process dynamics** (e.g., escalation  transfer).
  - Allows **stage-specific hazards** (e.g., risk after `Escalate`).
- **Implementation**: `msm` (R) or custom transition matrix in Python.

### **Sensitivity Analyses**
1. **Transfer Treatment**:
   - **Assumption 1**: Censored (informative).
   - **Assumption 2**: Competing event (external failure).
   - **Assumption 3**: Excluded (worst-case bias).
2. **Withdrawal/Rejection**:
   - Treated as censored vs. competing events.
3. **Cohort-Policy Effects**:
   - Stratified CIF by cohort (A vs. B) to test policy impact.
4. **Landmarking**:
   - Re-estimate risks at 48h and 72h to assess time-varying effects.

### **Excluded Methods**
- **Kaplan-Meier**: Biased by competing risks (e.g., transfers).
- **Cause-Specific Hazards**: Ignores competing risks (overestimates resolution risk).
- **Restricted Mean Time**: Less interpretable for tail risk.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

| Scenario                          | Transfer Treatment | Withdrawal/Rejection | Censoring Assumption | Cohort Effect | Qualitative Risk of Reversal |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Primary (CIF)**                | Competing Event    | Competing Event       | Informative           | Stratified     | None                          |
| **Assumption 1 (Censored)**      | Censored           | Competing Event       | Informative           | Stratified     | Underestimates tail risk      |
| **Assumption 2 (Excluded)**      | Excluded           | Competing Event       | None                  | Stratified     | Overestimates resolution risk  |
| **Naive KM**                     | Censored           | Censored              | Non-informative       | Unstratified   | Severe bias (ignores transfers) |

**Key Reversals**:
- If transfers are **censored** (Assumption 1), tail risk is underestimated.
- If transfers are **excluded** (Assumption 2), resolution risk appears artificially high.
- Cohort B’s **AutoCheck** path may mask true policy effects if not stratified.

# 7. Operational interpretation

### **Alerts and Workload Actions**
1. **SLA Breach Alerts**:
   - Flag cases **open at 72h** (e.g., **S09, S26**) with `last_event_gap_h > threshold` (e.g., 24h).
   - Prioritize **escalated cases** (e.g., **S11, S25**) for manual review.
2. **Workload Redistribution**:
   - **Cohort B** shows faster resolution via `AutoCheck` (e.g., **S16, S17**); replicate this path in Cohort A.
   - **Rework cases** (e.g., **S07, S27**) have higher resolution rates; assign more resources to similar paths.
3. **Policy Governance**:
   - Monitor **escalation timing**: Cases escalated after 48h (e.g., **S11**) have higher breach risk.
   - **Transfer rate**: High transfer rates (e.g., **S08, S21**) may indicate external dependency bottlenecks.

### **Calibration and Fairness Checks**
- **Priority Stratification**:
  - **P1 cases** (e.g., **S04, S18**) have higher escalation rates but mixed outcomes (e.g., **S21 transferred**).
  - **P3 cases** (e.g., **S05, S23**) resolve/reject quickly; ensure fairness in rejection rates.
- **Cohort Comparison**:
  - Cohort B’s **AutoCheck** path reduces resolution time (e.g., **S16 vs. S02**).
  - Ensure no **disparate impact** on minority cohorts if policy is rolled out unevenly.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

### **Critical Missing Data**
1. **Exact Event Timestamps**:
   - Current data uses `duration_or_age_h` and `last_event_gap_h`, but exact timestamps (e.g., `Submit` at 09:00 vs. 10:00) would improve time-varying analysis.
2. **External Outcomes After Transfer**:
   - **S08, S21, S32** are transferred; knowing if they resolve externally would clarify competing risks.
3. **Cutoff/Start Dates**:
   - Cohort B began after a policy change; exact dates would enable time-to-event alignment.
4. **Time-Varying Workload**:
   - Staffing levels or queue lengths during `Review`/`Escalate` stages could explain delays.
5. **Assignment Mechanism**:
   - Was escalation to `AutoCheck` randomized? Non-random assignment risks confounding.
6. **Policy Rollout Compliance**:
   - Was the new policy fully adopted in Cohort B? Partial compliance biases cohort comparisons.

### **Additional Process-Mining Features**
- **Activity Durations**: Time spent in each stage (e.g., `Review` duration).
- **Resource Attributes**: Assignee workload or skill level during `Escalate`.
- **External Dependencies**: Links to transferred cases’ external systems.

### **Statistical Limitations**
- Small sample size (n=32) limits precision in subgroup analyses (e.g., by priority).
- No validation set to test model generalizability.
- Assumptions about transfers (e.g., competing vs. censored) are unverifiable without external data.
