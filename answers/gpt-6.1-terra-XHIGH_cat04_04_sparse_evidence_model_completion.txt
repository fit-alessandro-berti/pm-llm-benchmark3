# 1. Evidence Ledger

The abbreviations below refer to completion events: **R** = Register Order, **C** = Credit Check, **E** = Engineering Review, **S** = Supplier Confirmation, **X** = Expedite Review, **A** = Approve Configuration, **P** = Release Production, **F** = Archive Case. A sequence such as `R C E A P F` is a complete trace. Counts refer to the supplied 30 cases, not to probabilities of future behavior.

| Evidence ID | What it supports | What it does not prove | Reliability | Modeling implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1: 12 × R C E S A P F | One common completion order. | C caused E or S, or that this order is required. | Direct log evidence; completion-only. | All candidates admit it. |
| V2: 7 × R E C S A P F | Reverse completion order for the two checks. | E started before C, or the checks overlapped. | Direct log evidence; completion-only. | Admit this completion order despite S1. |
| V3: 4 × R C E A P F | No S was recorded in this order. | S was unnecessary or actually skipped. | Direct log evidence, subject to S5. | Admit the recorded no-S trace; do not infer its business cause. |
| V4: 3 × R E C A P F | No S recorded with E completing first. | True absence of S or a different business rule. | Direct log evidence, subject to S5. | Admit it. |
| V5: 2 × R C S E A P F | S can complete before E in the recorded order. | S started after C, or C supplies all necessary information. | Direct log evidence; small count. | Avoid imposing E completion before every recorded S. |
| V6: 1 × R C E S X A P F | X can be recorded after S and both checks. | X is required in this situation. | Direct log evidence; singleton. | Admit S before X. |
| V7: 1 × R E C X S A P F | X can be recorded before S, after both checks. | X causes S, or S is required when X occurs. | Direct log evidence; singleton. | Admit X before S. |
| D1 | R starts and F ends the intended process. | Every other possible process step was exported. | High, domain rule. | Make R and F unique outer endpoints. |
| D2 | P requires A. | That A must immediately precede P in all real workflows. | High, domain rule. | Sequence A before P; immediate adjacency is an additional abstraction supported by this activity vocabulary and log. |
| D3 | C and E should complete before A. | Their relative order or concurrency. | Medium to high. | Require both once before A. |
| D4 | Required S precedes A. | Which cases require S or whether an absent event means no execution. | Medium. | A logged S precedes A; conditional obligation cannot be enforced without case data. |
| D5 | S may start once either check supplies enough information. | S always waits for a check to *finish*, or which check is sufficient. | Low to medium. | Balanced candidate uses completion after either as a proxy; permissive candidate relaxes that proxy. |
| D6 | X is optional, at most once, after both checks and before A. | Its selection criteria or dependence on S. | Medium. | Model optional X after both checks and before A. |
| D7 | Concurrency is unresolved. | Any specific execution or start-time relationship. | Explicitly unknown. | Interpret unordered model nodes as permitted completion orders, not established overlap. |
| S1 | A stakeholder asserts C must finish before E starts. | That the assertion applies to every case or that the log respects it. | Uncorroborated stakeholder rule; conflicts with E-before-C completions unless timestamps are inaccurate or cases have exceptions. | Keep as a competing compliance hypothesis, not a shared constraint. |
| S2 | Parallel checks are a plausible operating practice. | Actual overlap in individual cases. | Stakeholder report, supported but not established by completion orders. | Allow both completion orders; do not claim measured concurrency. |
| S3 | S is mandatory for each external component. | That every order has external components, or that one S event represents every required component. | Stakeholder domain statement; case attributes missing. | Treat conditional and component-level compliance as untestable here. |
| S4 | Stocked components can legitimately omit S. | That every logged omission is a stocked case. | Stakeholder domain statement, consistent with V3–V4. | Include an S-free visible branch. |
| S5 | Missing S events are plausible and the external/stocked attribute is absent. | Which omissions are errors, or the exact missing fraction in this sample. | Data-engineering estimate, 5–10% approximate. | Interpret the no-S branch as observationally ambiguous; separately investigate logging coverage. |
| S6 | X follows both checks and can be on either side of S. | That X appears in all cases or is independent of case attributes. | Compliance statement, consistent with both recorded X variants. | Preserve both X–S orders while requiring C and E before X. |
| S7 | Equal timestamps use ingestion order. | That displayed order is actual start order, strict completion order for ties, or causal order. | Logging-system note. | Obtain original timestamps and tie indicators; allow for serialization artifacts. |

The DFG records adjacent **completion** orders aggregated over cases. Both C→E and E→C can arise from parallel or overlapping work, from different sequential routing choices, from timestamp ties and ingestion ordering, or from errors. The DFG alone cannot distinguish these explanations, and adjacency neither establishes causation nor indicates start-time overlap. Its counts also discard case attributes and most long-range context; they must not be treated as independent transition probabilities.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

The models describe complete, recorded **completion-event sequences** over the eight given labels. Each case contains R, C, E, A, P and F exactly once, with R first and A → P → F as the final suffix. S and X appear at most once, and any recorded S and X precede A; X follows both C and E. These are explicit finite, loop-free abstractions, not claims that rework or other unexported work never occurs. Treat even tied timestamps as the supplied ingestion order. All models replay the given seven variants, but none can recover activity starts from completion events.

An omitted S is represented by an invisible control-flow alternative. It conflates a legitimate no-confirmation case with a required confirmation whose event was missing; it is not a separate representation of an executed but unlogged business activity. The external/stocked attribute, per-component requirements, and missingness indicators are unavailable, so the models cannot enforce S3 or tell the two causes apart. X's invisible alternative represents no recorded X; unlike S, an X logging-loss rate was not supplied. Optionality means a visible event can be absent in the model's trace language, not that every such absence is compliant. No branch probabilities or independence claims are implied.

For partial-order candidates, absence of a precedence edge permits either visible completion order in the trace model. It is a modeling device that also permits concurrent execution in its Petri-net representation; the evidence does not establish actual overlap. A sequence edge constrains the modeled completions and does not automatically establish a real start-time prerequisite.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

This candidate memorizes the seven observed complete variants exactly. It privileges evidence fidelity over extrapolation and makes no claim that repeated traces reveal the full business process. It treats even singleton variants as possible and does not discard E-first cases to enforce S1, because their recorded completion order conflicts with a universal C-finish-before-E-start rule under reliable timestamps. The result is a useful finite baseline but is highly sensitive to sampling and missing events.

Formal expression, where `|` is XOR and juxtaposition is sequence:

`(R C E S A P F) | (R E C S A P F) | (R C E A P F) | (R E C A P F) | (R C S E A P F) | (R C E S X A P F) | (R E C X S A P F)`.

Self-contained PM4Py ProcessTree construction:

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

R, C, E, S, X, A, P, F = (
    "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
    "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration",
    "Release Production", "Archive Case"
)
variants = [
    [R,C,E,S,A,P,F], [R,E,C,S,A,P,F], [R,C,E,A,P,F],
    [R,E,C,A,P,F], [R,C,S,E,A,P,F], [R,C,E,S,X,A,P,F],
    [R,E,C,X,S,A,P,F],
]
def node(op, children):
    result = ProcessTree(operator=op, children=children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = result
    return result
model_conservative = node(Operator.XOR, [
    node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [ProcessTree(label=a) for a in variant])
    for variant in variants
])
```

The enumeration duplicates each activity label across XOR branches. This is valid but makes diagrams and label-based alignment or token-replay diagnostics harder to interpret; the chosen branch can be ambiguous. Its size grows with the number and length of distinct variants, so it does not scale well. Counts are not encoded as branch weights.

Deliberately excluded: every complete sequence outside the seven listed variants, including any new interleaving, any X without recorded S, repetitions, or early approval. Known underfitting risks: rare legitimate combinations, new permutations consistent with the rules, and novel traces caused by missing S events are rejected; the finite sample is an inadequate inventory of all legitimate behavior.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

This candidate generalizes the observed orders while preserving the stronger stated constraints. It uses a partial order to admit either C/E completion order and allows S after either one has completed. That last condition is an operational proxy for D5, not a logical consequence of a start rule: an S that starts after one check has produced partial information could still finish first. X is optional and follows both completions, while S and X have no fixed relative order. The outer sequence keeps approval, release and archiving ordered. This candidate is a working hypothesis, not a concurrency finding.

Formal expression: `R ; (B_C | B_E) ; A ; P ; F`, where each branch contains C, E, `S?` and `X?`; `B_C` has edges `C < S?`, `C < X?`, `E < X?`, while `B_E` has edges `E < S?`, `C < X?`, `E < X?`. `?` denotes XOR with silent skip. Thus, if S is visible, at least one check precedes its completion; X, if visible, follows both. Silent skips have no visible effect. C and E are unordered, and S and X are unordered relative to each other except for these edges. The branch XOR expresses the disjunction of possible supplier prerequisites; both branches can produce the same visible trace.

Self-contained PM4Py POWL construction:

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

R, C, E, S, X, A, P, F = (
    "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
    "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration",
    "Release Production", "Archive Case"
)
def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label), SilentTransition()])
def partial(nodes, edges):
    reach = set(edges)
    for k in range(len(nodes)):
        for i in range(len(nodes)):
            for j in range(len(nodes)):
                if (i, k) in reach and (k, j) in reach:
                    reach.add((i, j))
    assert all(i != j for i, j in reach)
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)
    for i, j in reach:
        result.order.add_edge(nodes[i], nodes[j])
    return result
def branch(first):
    return partial([Transition(C), Transition(E), optional(S), optional(X)],
                   [(first, 2), (0, 3), (1, 3)])
inner = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [branch(0), branch(1)])
model_balanced = partial(
    [Transition(R), inner, Transition(A), Transition(P), Transition(F)],
    [(0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 4)]
)
```

Sequence versus concurrency versus partial order: a single C→E or E→C sequence would reject observed reverse completions; an XOR of sequential routes would also explain both orders but assume a route choice. The partial order permits both without claiming which of these explanations is true. S needs a disjunctive completion prerequisite in this candidate, so it uses two partial-order branches rather than an AND join requiring both checks. X has an AND prerequisite from C and E, but no S edge, matching D6 and S6. A waits for the whole inner block, including either optional visible event when selected. The two branch copies can complicate replay and precision diagnostics.

Deliberately excluded: S completing before both C and E, X completing before either check, approval before either check or a selected S/X, and any repeated activity. Known underfitting risks: D5 concerns when enough information exists and when S begins, not necessarily completion of a whole check; timestamp ties or missing completion records could also place a valid S first. The fixed activity set and no-rework assumption may reject genuine cases.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

This candidate removes the balanced model's uncertain supplier-completion prerequisite. It allows S to complete anywhere after registration and before approval, on the hypothesis that procurement might use information already available at registration or partial information before either check completes. It retains both checks before approval and before X, so its extra behavior is bounded and inspectable. This is a sensitivity hypothesis that weakens the completion-based interpretation of D5, not evidence that supplier work actually starts immediately.

Formal expression: `R ; PO{C, E, S?, X?; C < X?, E < X?} ; A ; P ; F`. The optional S has no inner precedence edge. A follows the completion or skip of all inner nodes.

Self-contained PM4Py POWL construction:

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

R, C, E, S, X, A, P, F = (
    "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
    "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration",
    "Release Production", "Archive Case"
)
def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label), SilentTransition()])
def partial(nodes, edges):
    reach = set(edges)
    for k in range(len(nodes)):
        for i in range(len(nodes)):
            for j in range(len(nodes)):
                if (i, k) in reach and (k, j) in reach:
                    reach.add((i, j))
    assert all(i != j for i, j in reach)
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)
    for i, j in reach:
        result.order.add_edge(nodes[i], nodes[j])
    return result
inner = partial([Transition(C), Transition(E), optional(S), optional(X)],
                [(0, 3), (1, 3)])
model_permissive = partial(
    [Transition(R), inner, Transition(A), Transition(P), Transition(F)],
    [(0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 4)]
)
```

Reasonable generalization here includes all completion interleavings respecting the specified prerequisite edges, including S first and optional S/X combinations; it does not assert any of them occurred. The boundary forbids approval without both checks, X before either check, S or X after approval, release before approval, repetition and altered endpoints. Deliberately excluded: those boundary violations and activities outside the vocabulary. Known underfitting risks: genuine exception handling, repeat confirmations for multiple components, rework, unlogged principal checks, or workflows without X's modeled prerequisite. Overgeneralization risk: S-first completion could violate an actual supplier information rule, and a missing S could hide a compliance breach.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

Abbreviations are defined in the evidence ledger. Every listed trace is complete. The conservative candidate admits exactly its seven enumerated traces at **all lengths**, so it admits no unobserved trace, including within the eight-event bound used below.

| Model | Unobserved trace | Why admitted | Plausible, risky, or clearly undesirable | Evidence used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | None at any length | XOR contains exactly seven fixed sequences. | N/A | V1–V7. |
| Balanced | R C E X A P F | X may occur after both checks without a recorded S. | Plausible but S omission is ambiguous. | D3, D6, S4–S6. |
| Balanced | R E C S X A P F | S follows a check; X follows both; S can precede X. | Plausible. | D5–D6, S6, V6–V7. |
| Balanced | R E S C A P F | S follows E and may precede C; no X. | Plausible but depends on supplier information sufficiency. | D5, V5, S4. |
| Permissive | R S C E A P F | S has no inner prerequisite; C and E precede A. | Risky: S completed before either check. | D4–D5, S7; D5 concerns starts and partial information. |
| Permissive | R S E C X A P F | S can finish first; X follows both checks. | Risky for supplier timing. | D3–D6, S6–S7. |
| Permissive | R E S C X A P F | S follows E; X follows both; both optional events are selected. | Plausible, but unobserved combination. | V5–V7, D5–D6, S6. |

Intentionally excluded complete traces, with the earliest point at which no compliant continuation remains:

- **Conservative:** `R C E X A P F` fails at X because no enumerated variant has prefix `R C E X`. `R E C S X A P F` fails at X because the only variant with prefix `R E C S` next has A.
- **Balanced:** `R S C E A P F` fails at S, before either check has completed. `R C X E A P F` fails at X because E has not completed.
- **Permissive:** `R C X E A P F` fails at X because E has not completed. `R E S A P F` fails at A because C has not completed. Both would also be rejected by the other candidates.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|

| Expected precision risk | Lowest visible-language overgeneralization, but severe memorization; measured precision is algorithm-dependent. | Moderate; inferred combinations and branch duplication affect diagnostics. | Highest of the three: permits additional supplier-first completions. |

All three have a substantial compliance limitation: they cannot identify required but missing S events, verify component-level obligations, or check S1's start-time constraint. Log fitness and conformance scores cannot resolve these missing facts. Precision, simplicity and generalization values also depend on metric implementation and model structure, not just intended business validity.

# 8. Validation Code

The following standalone script reconstructs the three models and 30 cases, converts them to accepting Petri nets, checks soundness, computes the named PM4Py metrics, samples playout, and checks the manual suite by reachability. It uses the PM4Py APIs in the question; exact output and metric conventions can vary by version. The parser supplies synthetic timestamps; they reconstruct order, not original time or ties. POWL conversion and soundness support require a compatible PM4Py installation. Some soundness implementations may warn about numerical solvers; inspect diagnostics and use a reliable solver if needed.

The exact search enumerates complete firing sequences up to eight visible events, including silent transitions. For these finite, acyclic, loop-free candidates, eight is also the maximum possible visible trace length; `seen` deduplicates identical marking/prefix pairs. This procedure is not a general exhaustive comparison of arbitrary infinite-language models, and can fail to terminate on nets with unbounded silent-state growth. Random playout is illustrative only: a sample does not prove language inclusion or exclusion. Fitness and precision are in-sample estimates, not evidence of causal correctness; the TBR generalization and structural simplicity metrics are implementation-specific proxies.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
from collections import deque

R, C, E, S, X, A, P, F = ('Register Order', 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review', 'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case')
observed = [
    (12, [R,C,E,S,A,P,F]), (7, [R,E,C,S,A,P,F]),
    (4, [R,C,E,A,P,F]), (3, [R,E,C,A,P,F]),
    (2, [R,C,S,E,A,P,F]), (1, [R,C,E,S,X,A,P,F]),
    (1, [R,E,C,X,S,A,P,F]),
]

def pt(op, children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=op, children=children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
    return node

def make_conservative():
    return pt(Operator.XOR, [pt(Operator.SEQUENCE, [ProcessTree(label=a) for a in trace]) for _, trace in observed])

def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label), SilentTransition()])

def partial(nodes, edges):
    # Close the relation transitively, as required of a strict partial order.
    reach = set(edges)
    for k in range(len(nodes)):
        for i in range(len(nodes)):
            for j in range(len(nodes)):
                if (i,k) in reach and (k,j) in reach:
                    reach.add((i,j))
    assert all(i != j for i, j in reach)
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)
    for i, j in reach:
        po.order.add_edge(nodes[i], nodes[j])
    return po

def wrap(inner):
    return partial([Transition(R), inner, Transition(A), Transition(P), Transition(F)], [(0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(3,4)])

def balanced_branch(first):
    # first = 0 for C or 1 for E as the supplier's prerequisite.
    return partial([Transition(C), Transition(E), optional(S), optional(X)], [(first,2), (0,3), (1,3)])

def make_balanced():
    return wrap(OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [balanced_branch(0), balanced_branch(1)]))

def make_permissive():
    return wrap(partial([Transition(C), Transition(E), optional(S), optional(X)], [(0,3),(1,3)]))

def language(net, im, fm, max_events=8):
    # Exhaust all reachable (marking, visible-prefix) pairs through the bound.
    todo = deque([(im, ())])
    seen = {(im, ())}
    completed = set()
    while todo:
        marking, prefix = todo.popleft()
        if marking == fm:
            completed.add(prefix)
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            new_prefix = prefix if t.label is None else prefix + (t.label,)
            if len(new_prefix) > max_events:
                continue
            new_marking = semantics.execute(t, net, marking)
            state = (new_marking, new_prefix)
            if state not in seen:
                seen.add(state)
                todo.append(state)
    return completed

models = {'conservative': make_conservative(), 'balanced': make_balanced(), 'permissive': make_permissive()}
log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([','.join(t) for n, t in observed for _ in range(n)])
assert len(log['case:concept:name'].unique()) == 30
observed_set = {tuple(t) for _, t in observed}
p1 = (R,C,E,X,A,P,F)
p2 = (R,E,C,S,X,A,P,F)
p3 = (R,E,S,C,A,P,F)
p4 = (R,S,C,E,A,P,F)
early_x = (R,C,X,E,A,P,F)
no_credit = (R,E,S,A,P,F)
repeat = (R,C,E,X,X,A,P,F)
suite = [
    ('observed', tuple(observed[0][1]), (True,True,True)),
    ('new expedite without supplier', p1, (False,True,True)),
    ('new supplier then expedite', p2, (False,True,True)),
    ('supplier after engineering first', p3, (False,True,True)),
    ('supplier before both checks', p4, (False,False,True)),
    ('expedite too early', early_x, (False,False,False)),
    ('missing credit', no_credit, (False,False,False)),
    ('repeat expedite', repeat, (False,False,False)),
]
for index, (name, model) in enumerate(models.items()):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    sound, details = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    print(name, 'sound:', sound)
    assert sound, (name, details)
    print('fitness:', pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
    print('precision:', pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
    print('generalization:', pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm))
    print('simplicity:', pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm))
    samples = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={'noTraces': 100})
    print('sampled cases:', len(samples))
    lang = language(net, im, fm)
    print('complete traces through 8 events:', len(lang), 'observed:', observed_set <= lang)
    assert observed_set <= lang
    for label, trace, expected in suite:
        actual = trace in lang
        print(label, actual)
        assert actual == expected[index], (name, label, actual, expected[index])
```

The local PM4Py 2.7.23.4 run returned soundness `True` for each candidate, admitted all seven observed variants and passed the suite. The bounded enumeration found 7, 14 and 18 distinct complete visible traces respectively. These results concern the constructed nets and supplied log, not real-world compliance; the local soundness checker also warned that its SciPy-based solution may be unstable without PuLP. The soundness API emitted a deprecation warning, so future PM4Py versions may require a replacement API.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use the **balanced candidate** immediately as an exploratory process and conformance-triage baseline, with supplier-first cases flagged for review rather than automatically treated as violations. Use the **permissive candidate** for sensitivity analysis: the difference isolates the uncertain supplier-completion prerequisite. Keep the conservative enumeration as a memorization baseline. Do not deploy any of these as an automatic compliance decision for external components without linking the component requirement and actual confirmation evidence.

1. Restore case and component identifiers, component source/type and external-versus-stocked status, whether confirmation was required, and the reason for any exemption. Link each required component to the actual supplier confirmation; establish whether one event represents one component or an order-level aggregate.
2. Capture lifecycle start and completion timestamps for C, E, S and X, with timezone, original timestamp precision, ingestion index, event IDs, source-system identifiers and clock-synchronization status. Retain ties rather than presenting arbitrary ingestion order as causal order. Capture intermediate information-availability milestones if supplier work can start before a full check finishes.
3. Reconcile exported events against procurement-system confirmations and case records, stratified by source and time, to estimate missingness and classify each no-S trace as true skip, missing event or unresolved. Log logging failures and recovery. Record X eligibility and exception or rework reasons as well.
4. Collect prospective cases across component classes and compare against predeclared hypotheses. An E completion before C completion challenges universal S1, but lifecycle data are needed to distinguish overlap from an E→C sequential route or clock/order artifacts. A case with overlapping C/E intervals supports concurrency; non-overlapping C→E and E→C cases, with route decisions, support an XOR of sequences. Repeated observations in both orders alone cannot distinguish those explanations.
5. Seek S completion before both checks and verify S's start and information source; that specifically separates the permissive completion-order language from the balanced one. Collect S between C and E in **both** orders to assess which check can enable it. For optionality, examine confirmed stocked cases with and without S and external cases with and without a matching source confirmation; capture X both with and without S and on each side of S. These combinations distinguish true skips from data loss and test whether options are coupled.
6. Reassess on a time-separated holdout and by component category, inspecting deviations and confidence intervals rather than only aggregate metrics. Review conflicting cases with Finance, Engineering, Procurement and Compliance before turning any inferred rule into an enforcement rule.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

- **Endpoints and A before P:** high confidence in D1–D2, although immediate A→P→F adjacency and exactly-once execution are stronger finite-log abstractions with lower confidence.
- **Both checks before A:** medium to high confidence from D3 and all observations; missing or misordered check events could alter observed conformance.
- **C/E ordering and concurrency:** low confidence in a causal or overlap claim. Both completion orders are well supported as recorded behavior; parallel execution, overlapping work, sequential routes and timestamp artifacts remain alternatives. S1 would require investigation if it is intended as a universal compliance obligation.
- **S optionality:** medium confidence that some cases legitimately skip it, given S4, but low confidence about any individual omitted event and the conditional requirement, given S3 and S5. The 5–10% missingness estimate is not a per-case probability.
- **S timing:** low to medium confidence in the balanced completion-order proxy; D5 is about starting with sufficient information, not whole-check completion. Supplier-first traces may be legitimate or erroneous.
- **X optional and after both checks:** medium confidence from D6 and S6, supported by two recorded X cases; its independence from S and eligibility conditions remain uncertain.
- **No repetitions or other activities:** low confidence as a real-world restriction; it is an explicit modeling boundary.

A materially different interpretation is that S1 is a mandatory, universally applicable start-time control and E-first completions result from timestamp or integration defects. If independently verified, a start/completion-aware compliance model should enforce C completion before E starts, and neither the balanced nor permissive completion-only candidate should be used as the primary compliance reference. Likewise, verified component-level requirements and confirmation records would justify replacing the unconditional visible S skip with data-conditioned logic and an explicit logging-error model.
