# 1. Fairness dimensions

**The central concern is whether applicants receive a proportionate, accessible, and timely procedure—not whether every group has the same approval rate or follows the same path.** A necessary support step can preserve a right while its staffing and implementation impose an avoidable disadvantage.

All numerical findings below come from the supplied summaries. The proposed analyses are tests to perform, not results already established from individual event records.

| Dimension                                | Appropriate fairness question                                                                     | Assessment from the supplied evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Outcome parity**                       | How do approval rates differ, and what warrants investigation?                                    | Approval rates are 78%, 63%, 69%, and 58% for A–D. These establish raw disparities, not discrimination: substantive eligibility and case mix may differ.                                                                              |
| **Adjusted outcome disparity**           | Among applicants comparable on defensible characteristics, do outcomes still differ?              | The preliminary approval differences remain −5.5, −2.2, and −3.1 percentage points for B, C, and D. These are unresolved associations; their uncertainty and sensitivity to adjustment choices are unknown.                           |
| **Procedural burden**                    | Are applicants asked to undertake unnecessary, repeated, or disproportionately difficult actions? | More document requests, rework, and handovers warrant examination. Count repeated submissions, contacts, visits, and time spent—not just activities, because useful support and burdensome repetition are not equivalent.             |
| **Timeliness**                           | Are necessary services delivered promptly, including for applicants with additional needs?        | B and C have substantial median and tail delays, with residual adjusted differences of +2.6 and +4.1 days. Missing support-specific timeliness targets are a governance gap, although no particular target breach can be established. |
| **Consistency of routing**               | Do equivalent, substantiated triggers produce equivalent requirements and opportunities?          | Additional fraud, language, and accommodation steps may be appropriate. Audit both unnecessary escalation and failure to provide required support; equal routing frequencies are not the objective.                                   |
| **Quality and accessibility of support** | Does support enable applicants to participate effectively?                                        | A recorded contact or review does not establish understandable communication, suitable accommodation, successful assistance, or dignified treatment. These dimensions remain largely unmeasured.                                      |
| **Appeal/rework burden**                 | Can applicants obtain correction without unnecessary effort or delay?                             | Appeals and rework are elevated outside A. However, more appeals can reflect more contestable decisions, better access to redress, or both. Lower appeal rates are not automatically better.                                          |
| **Resource-allocation fairness**         | Is qualified capacity allocated in proportion to demand, needs, and urgency?                      | B and C depend disproportionately on small specialist pools. Specialist assignment is potentially appropriate; systematically inadequate capacity would remain concerning even without unequal treatment by individual workers.       |

The relevant benchmark is therefore **equivalent procedural protection and a justified burden**, not identical treatment regardless of need.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

**Define the questions before choosing controls.** Keep three assessments separate:

* **Disparity:** an observed or standardized difference in outcomes, burdens, or timing.
* **Legitimate differentiation:** additional processing supported by a verified need or control requirement, implemented proportionately.
* **Discrimination:** disadvantage attributable to an impermissible distinction or mechanism. A regression coefficient alone does not establish this, and a coefficient that disappears after adjustment does not rule it out. Causal fairness conclusions depend on assumptions about how the variables relate, not merely on predictive model fit. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research][1])

## Cohorts and endpoints

Construct cohorts by **submission month**, using all submitted applications—not only approved, closed, or appealed cases. Check whether withdrawals, abandoned cases, and incomplete exports are represented.

Separate the following endpoints: time to the first notified decision; time from approval to benefit activation; and time through appeal, reopening, and final closure. Otherwise, a group with more appeals may appear slower partly because a different portion of the lifecycle is being measured.

Retain open cases in time-to-event analyses, with censoring at extraction. For activation analysis, denial is a competing outcome, not merely an unfinished approval. Compare appeal occurrence over a common post-decision follow-up period and distinguish appeals per application from appeals per appeal-eligible decision.

## Matching and stratification

First reproduce the preliminary model using **risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and submission month**. Then examine whether these values were actually known at intake or at the relevant routing decision. A final documentation status copied onto earlier events is not a valid baseline measure.

Use exact or coarsened matching on major categorical factors, supplemented where necessary by overlap weighting or flexible regression. Add verified language-support and accommodation needs for **need-specific service comparisons**. Do not infer language-support need solely from applicant group.

Publish covariate balance, missingness, sample sizes, effective sample sizes, and the fraction of each group outside common support. Group A may offer few comparable applicants for some support-intensive combinations. Do not extrapolate confidently or make unmatched applicants disappear from the fairness assessment.

For an outcome \(Y\), a standardized contrast could be:

$$
\Delta_g=\sum_x w(x)
\left[E(Y\mid G=g,X=x)-E(Y\mid G=A,X=x)\right],
$$

where \(w(x)\) describes an explicitly chosen common comparison population. For medians and percentiles, standardize the **outcome distributions** and then obtain their quantiles; do not average subgroup medians.

The preliminary table does not identify its standardized population, confidence intervals, or cycle-time estimand. Consequently, neither “78% minus 5.5 points” nor “8.1 days plus 2.6 days” is necessarily a valid adjusted prediction.

## Separate overall burden from conditional execution

Produce complementary analyses rather than one “fully controlled” result:

**Overall procedural disadvantage:** assess the applicant’s experience without controlling away realized document loops, supervisor reviews, variants, handovers, or assigned workers.

**Comparable-need execution:** among applicants with similar verified needs, assess whether support delivery, document requirements, and review timing differ.

**Mechanism diagnostics:** introduce route, resource, and workload variables to explore where disparities arise. A smaller group coefficient after these additions would not establish fairness: allocation and routing may be mechanisms producing the disadvantage.

In particular, do not define complexity by the eventual number of activities or rework cycles. Those may be consequences of the process being audited.

## Path and resource comparisons

At each routing gate, compare applicants using information available **before that gate**. Examine document-request probability after comparable completeness assessments, supervisor escalation after comparable assessments, and fraud referral under the same verified risk rule.

Compare route frequencies and then execution within comparable routes. Within-variant comparisons are useful descriptively, but conditioning on a realized path can select applicants differently across groups. Report them alongside—not instead of—the overall burden comparison.

For resources, distinguish assignment from treatment after assignment. Compare access to qualified staff, workload exposure at allocation, repeated transfers, and within-worker differences where workers serve multiple groups. Resource-adjusted models cannot recover within-worker comparisons when group assignments scarcely overlap.

Normalize demand by qualified staffed hours and task requirements, not simply by headcount. Daily workload measures should be aligned to the allocation time; end-of-day completed work is not the same as workload faced when the case arrived.

## Waiting-time decomposition

The supplied table labels intervals as waits, but the listed event attributes cannot independently reproduce a clean service/queue decomposition. One timestamp per event does not establish activity start, readiness, completion, or applicant response time.

Seek timestamps for referral/readiness, assignment, service start, service completion, document receipt, and resumption. Distinguish agency queueing, active work, applicant response intervals, and other holds. Keep applicant calendar time visible even when operational business-time measures are also reported.

Where activities overlap, calculate elapsed-time contributions without double-counting concurrent work. **Do not sum the supplied median waits:** they concern different case subsets and may describe overlapping intervals.

## Sensitivity and subgroup analysis

Re-estimate comparisons with risk and documentation variables included, excluded separately, excluded together, and replaced by independently reviewed measures where available. Also examine channel and region with and without adjustment: they may explain variation while also reflecting unequal access or geographically unequal provision.

Audit region × group and channel × group interactions, particularly in the two backlog regions. Check combined language and accommodation needs, risk bands, and changes over time.

Report uncertainty using methods that respect repeated events within cases and shared resource/queue conditions. Include model-specification and unmeasured-complexity sensitivity analyses. Without these checks, the adjusted differences should not be described as statistically significant or as causal effects.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

## Group B: document burden and language-support delivery

B’s raw median cycle time exceeds A’s by **5.6 days**, and its 90th percentile by **12.8 days**. Higher incompleteness—46% versus 21%—and greater in-person use plausibly explain some differences. Nevertheless, the preliminary adjustment leaves **+9.5 percentage points in document requests, +8.0 points in supervisor review, and +2.6 days in cycle time**.

B is less represented in V1, at 19% versus 42%, and more represented in V2, at 34% versus 24%. V4 accounts for 24% of B but only 1% of A.

The strongest operational concerns are the reported **1.6-day interval from completeness checking to requesting documents**, versus 0.4 days for A; the **3.8-day language-support-to-eligibility interval**; and repeated general-worker → bilingual-worker → general-worker transfers. Concentration on seven bilingual workers and three overloaded regional officers supplies a plausible allocation mechanism.

Language assistance itself is justified. The unresolved issue is whether applicants experience avoidable queueing, repeated explanations, or documentation demands because assistance is scarce or poorly integrated.

## Group C: accommodation and supervisory capacity

C has the largest raw median difference, **+7.8 days**, and tail difference, **+17.4 days**. Its adjusted cycle-time difference, **+4.1 days**, is also the largest.

Accommodation requests are much more common in C, so greater use of V5—31% versus A’s 2%—is not inherently problematic. The **4.7-day accommodation-to-eligibility interval**, dependence on five specialists, and shared specialist/supervisor duties warrant particular attention.

The two dual-role specialists could create competing capacity demands across stages. Their dual roles are not, by themselves, evidence of a prohibited conflict: no separation-of-duties rule is supplied.

C’s 24% rework rate, against A’s 11%, strengthens the case for investigating whether accommodation is effective early enough to prevent repeated processing.

## Group D: more plausible risk-based routing, unresolved appeal concerns

D’s higher V3 share—28% versus 9%—is consistent with its higher measured risk and prior-case counts. Its smaller adjusted cycle-time difference, **+0.9 days**, is compatible with recorded case mix accounting for part of the raw delay.

However, measured risk is not proof of actual fraud, nor proof that the score is neutral. The remaining **−3.1-point approval difference** and **+5.7-point appeal difference** require investigation into risk validity, decision reasons, and access to correction.

## Cross-cutting qualifications

The relatively small decision-to-notification differences suggest that the larger B/C timing concerns lie upstream, although notification accessibility remains unmeasured.

V1–V6 account for **82%, 99%, 98%, and 90%** of A–D respectively, as reported. The remaining shares and rounding must be reconciled. The paths are summaries, not necessarily complete traces: omitted activities cannot automatically be classified as missing mandatory steps.

Similarly, B’s 24% V4 share cannot be subtracted from its 57% language-support need to estimate unmet support. Other paths may contain support. The supplied variant medians are pooled, not group-specific, and do not establish the causal time cost of taking a support route.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Eligibility and needs are incompletely represented.** Household size, risk band, and documentation status do not fully describe substantive benefit eligibility, urgency, document complexity, functional accommodation needs, literacy, digital access, or availability of assistance. Differences in these factors could explain some associations—but their absence cannot be used as an untested blanket justification.

**Several variables may measure the process as much as the applicant.** Documentation completeness can depend on form design, translation, staff interpretation, and when it was assessed. An accommodation-request flag does not capture unmet or undisclosed needs. Language preference is not equivalent to a verified support request. Group definitions may also conceal important combinations of needs.

**Historical risk requires independent validation.** A score can predict historically recorded adverse findings while inheriting problems in how those findings were generated. “Prior cases” may represent materially different situations, including legitimate repeat applications or earlier administrative correction. More generally, fairness evaluations based on selectively observed or biased historical outcomes can retain the underlying unfairness. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research][2])

**Resource exposure is only partially observed.** Daily workload lacks within-day queue pressure, interruptions, staffed hours, absences, task difficulty, and work outside the log. The resource recorded on an event may be its performer rather than the person responsible for allocation or ownership.

**Outcome and appeal fields do not establish decision quality.** The data lack independent correctness judgments, decision reasons, appeal eligibility, appeal grounds, withdrawal reasons, overturns, and the distinction between new evidence and correction of an original error. An appeal flag cannot measure whether redress was understandable, accessible, or successful.

**Event quality and coverage remain uncertain.** Missing events, delayed recording, ambiguous timestamp semantics, changing attributes, and unlinked reopened episodes can distort comparisons. Applicants deterred before submission may be absent entirely.

Finally, the preliminary model supplies no standard errors, overlap diagnostics, missing-data strategy, or functional form. Its residual differences identify priorities for investigation, not fully identified quantities of unfair treatment.

Prioritize B/C support capacity and handover pilots while investigating B’s documentation/escalation patterns and D’s risk/appeal patterns.

Evaluate pilots at the **queue or team level**, measuring spillovers to every group, staff workload, decision quality, and support adequacy. A staggered rollout of additional resources or improved coordination can help distinguish mechanisms while preserving existing support rights. Success should mean less avoidable burden with maintained or improved protection—not merely shorter traces.

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

PM4Py can produce the process descriptions supporting this assessment, but it does not itself determine whether an adjustment set is normatively appropriate or whether a disparity is discriminatory.

**Preserve whole cases when selecting cohorts.** `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` retains cases containing matching values; event-level filtering can remove intervening activities and create misleading paths. Use audited case-level snapshots for cohort assignment when attributes change. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3])

**Distinguish observed transitions from causal or normative relations.** Variants, frequency DFGs, and performance DFGs describe recorded behavior. Use them within comparable cohorts, with rare paths retained for separate review, rather than treating the majority-group process as the standard. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][4])

The following skeleton assumes canonical labels, resolved event ordering, and timestamps already localized correctly and confirmed to represent completion events. `complete_ids` must identify complete, in-scope lifecycles independently; open cases remain necessary for the separate censored analyses.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CID = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"
DAY = 86400.0


def process_profile(
    events: pd.DataFrame,
    complete_ids: set,
    case_review_cutoff_days: float,
    edge_review_cutoff_days: float,
) -> dict:
    """Descriptive process views, not a causal fairness estimator."""

    required = {CID, ACT, TS, RES, "applicant_group"}
    if not required.issubset(events.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"Missing columns: {required - set(events.columns)}")
    if events[[CID, ACT, TS, "applicant_group"]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Resolve missing identifiers, labels, times, or groups.")

    df = events.copy()
    # Precondition: naive source times have already been correctly localized.
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True, errors="raise")

    if df.groupby(CID)["applicant_group"].nunique().gt(1).any():
        raise ValueError("Use an audited case-level group assignment.")

    # Resolve timestamp ties using reliable source ordering before this step.
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, case_id=CID, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
    )

    output = {}
    for group in log["applicant_group"].unique():
        sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log, "applicant_group", [group], level="case", retain=True
        ).copy()
        sub = sub.sort_values([CID, TS], kind="stable")
        done = sub[sub[CID].isin(complete_ids)].copy()

        view = {
            "all_case_count": sub[CID].nunique(),
            "complete_case_count": done[CID].nunique(),
            # All observed transitions; unfinished transitions are not zero waits.
            "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(sub),
            "performance_dfg": pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
                sub[[CID, ACT, TS]],  # Explicit completion-to-completion view
                business_hours=False,
                perf_aggregation_key="all",
            ),
        }

        # Keep case identity for later joins to baseline covariates and weights.
        previous = sub.groupby(CID)[[ACT, TS, RES]].shift()
        mask = previous[ACT].notna()
        edges = sub.loc[mask, [CID, ACT, TS, RES]].copy()
        edges["from_activity"] = previous.loc[mask, ACT]
        edges["from_resource"] = previous.loc[mask, RES]
        edges["elapsed_days"] = (
            edges[TS] - previous.loc[mask, TS]
        ).dt.total_seconds() / DAY

        known = edges[RES].notna() & edges["from_resource"].notna()
        edges["resource_changed"] = (
            edges[RES] != edges["from_resource"]
        ).where(known, pd.NA)
        view["edge_records"] = edges  # Elapsed gaps, NOT pure queue times.

        if not done.empty:
            view["variants"] = pm4py.get_variants(done)
            traces = done.groupby(CID)[ACT].agg(tuple)
            view["variant_shares"] = traces.value_counts(normalize=True)

            # Exact observed variant, not a shortened V1–V6 summary.
            exemplar = traces.iloc[0]
            view["variant_drilldown"] = pm4py.filter_variants(
                done, [exemplar]
            )
            view["complete_durations_seconds"] = (
                pm4py.get_all_case_durations(done)
            )
            view["long_case_drilldown"] = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
                done, case_review_cutoff_days * DAY, float("inf")
            )

        # Diagnostic cutoff only; do not interpret it as an established SLA.
        view["slow_supervisor_edge_cases"] = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
            sub,
            ("Supervisor Review", "Decision Draft"),
            edge_review_cutoff_days * DAY,
            float("inf"),
        )

        # Resource-complete cases: report coverage and selection sensitivity.
        # Never delete isolated events to "repair" a handover network.
        bad_resource_ids = sub.loc[sub[RES].isna(), CID].unique()
        net_log = sub[~sub[CID].isin(bad_resource_ids)]
        view["network_case_count"] = net_log[CID].nunique()

        if not net_log.empty:
            view["handover_network"] = (
                pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(net_log)
            )
            view["working_together_network"] = (
                pm4py.discover_working_together_network(net_log)
            )
            view["resource_similarity"] = (
                pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(net_log)
            )
            view["discovered_roles"] = (
                pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(net_log)
            )

        # Requires enriched, validated interval data—not supplied in this task.
        if "start_timestamp" in done.columns and not done.empty:
            interval_log = done.copy()
            interval_log["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(
                interval_log["start_timestamp"], utc=True, errors="raise"
            )
            valid = (
                interval_log["start_timestamp"].notna()
                & (interval_log["start_timestamp"] <= interval_log[TS])
            )
            if valid.all():
                view["interval_metrics_for_validation"] = (
                    pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
                        interval_log,
                        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
                    )
                )
                view["median_activity_service_seconds"] = (
                    pm4py.get_service_time(
                        interval_log,
                        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
                        aggregation_measure="median",
                    )
                )

        output[group] = view

    return output
```

Use common, predeclared drill-down thresholds across groups. Filtering long cases is for investigation, not for defining the primary fairness cohort.

Handover networks describe resource succession, and working-together networks describe shared case participation. Neither establishes unnecessary transfers, actual ownership, or service quality. Activity similarity and discovered roles also require validation against actual qualifications and responsibilities. Compare networks using case/transition exposure, not visual edge thickness alone. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][5])

The service-time functions accept a start-timestamp key, whose default can be the completion-timestamp column. Do not use that default to claim measured service time here. Even with genuine intervals, validate overlap handling and unrecorded work before interpreting inserted “waiting time” as agency queueing. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][6])

**Connect process evidence to adjustment.** Run the profile on the full cohort and prespecified matched/stratified cohorts. Join edge records and case-level burden measures to the audited baseline table. Apply adjustment weights explicitly: ordinary PM4Py outputs should not be assumed to incorporate them. Binary outcomes should contribute once per case; repeated transitions need clearly defined opportunity-level or case-level denominators.

The language-support-to-eligibility interval needs particular care. In V4, other activities intervene, so a direct-edge filter alone will miss it. Use explicitly paired stage or episode timestamps, retaining intervening work and handling repeated support/document cycles.

**Separate discovered regularities from policy constraints.** PM4Py supports log-skeleton discovery and conformance checking. A discovered constraint describes regularity in its source log; it does not become a fairness obligation merely because it is frequent. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][4])

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Overall procedural fairness is indeterminate, with substantial evidence of unequal procedural burden and credible indicators of structural unfairness—especially for Groups B and C.**

Their disadvantages persist under the preliminary recorded-case-mix adjustment and coincide with specialist dependence, additional handovers, and elevated rework. This supports targeted corrective action, but does not identify how much delay is avoidable or establish discriminatory decision-making.

Group D’s routing is more consistent with documented risk differences, yet historical risk validity and its residual appeal disparity remain unresolved.

Management should preserve required support while improving capacity, continuity, accessibility, and measurement. Neither equal approval rates nor the disappearance of a group coefficient after controlling for routes and resources would, by itself, demonstrate a fair process.
