# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Episode 1: Queue QB at 10:28 (Dispatch of B04 ahead of B06 and B07)
* **Observed Reality:** At 10:28, `B01` completes. Eligible jobs are `B04` (arr 09:35, P3, est 8m), `B06` (arr 10:05, P2, est 20m), and `B07` (arr 10:08, P3, est 6m, appt 10:30). Dispatcher selects `B04`.
* **Competing Explanations:**
  * *Explanation A (Two-Tier Hybrid - P1 Expedited / Standard FIFO):* Priority is strictly binary: P1 is an expedited class with preemption; P2 and P3 are administrative metadata treated as equal standard-tier jobs governed by FIFO. `B04` is selected because it arrived at 09:35, 30 minutes before `B06` (10:05).
  * *Explanation B (Non-Preemptive Priority with Dynamic Aging / Starvation Avoidance):* Standard 3-tier priority applies ($P1 > P2 > P3$), but jobs accumulate dynamic priority as waiting time increases (e.g., effective priority score $S_i = \text{priority}_i - \alpha \cdot \text{wait\_time}_i$). `B04` has waited 53 minutes (09:35 to 10:28), surpassing an aging threshold that elevates it above newly arrived P2 job `B06` (waited 23 minutes).
* **Counterfactual Prediction:**
  * *Scenario:* Suppose an identical job `B04_alt` arrived at 10:22 (waited only 6 minutes) with Priority 3, while `B06` (P2) arrived at 10:05.
  * *Under Explanation A (Two-Tier FIFO):* If `B04_alt` arrived at 10:22, `B06` (arr 10:05) arrived earlier, so `B06` would be selected at 10:28 under FIFO.
  * *Under Explanation B (Priority with Aging):* `B04_alt` has waited only 6 minutes (no aging promotion); `B06` (P2) would easily win on base priority and be selected at 10:28.
  * *Alternative Scenario:* Suppose job `B08` arrived at 10:00 with Priority 2 and est 20m. Under Explanation A, `B04` (arr 09:35) still beats `B08` on FIFO. Under pure Priority without aging, `B08` would beat `B04`.
* **Discriminating Observable:**
  * An audit log of the **dynamic dispatch ranking score** or an explicit queue configuration parameter indicating whether an aging promotion function $f(\text{wait\_time})$ exists, or recording whether Priority 2 jobs ever preempt/bypass older Priority 3 jobs when wait time is below 30 minutes.

### Episode 2: Queue QC at 10:32 (Dispatch of C04 ahead of C06)
* **Observed Reality:** At 10:32, `C03` completes. Eligible waiting jobs are `C01` (arr 10:00, est 36m), `C04` (arr 10:07, est 22m), `C05` (arr 10:11, est 40m, ddl 10:50), and `C06` (arr 10:25, est 6m, ddl 15:00). Dispatcher selects `C04` (22m), despite `C06` having est 6m.
* **Competing Explanations:**
  * *Explanation A (Static Batch SPT of Initial Backlog):* At 10:10, when `C1` finished its prior job, a batch dispatch list was compiled and locked for the initial backlog {C01, C02, C03, C04}. Sorted by SPT, the execution order is fixed: `C02` (8m) $\rightarrow$ `C03` (14m) $\rightarrow$ `C04` (22m) $\rightarrow$ `C01` (36m). Newly arriving walk-ins (`C06`) are placed into the next scheduling cycle and cannot disrupt an active batch unless an SLA emergency occurs.
  * *Explanation B (Rolling SPT with Physical Staging / Visibility Lag):* The queue follows dynamic, continuous rolling SPT, but arrival scans at 10:25 reflect gate entry. Physical unpacking, parts kitting, or staging to `C1`'s workbench takes 8–10 minutes. At 10:32, `C06` was not yet in the "ready-for-dispatch" buffer; among physically staged jobs {C01, C04, C05}, `C04` (22m) was the shortest job available.
* **Counterfactual Prediction:**
  * *Scenario:* Introduce job `C00` arriving at 10:06 (before the 10:10 batch creation) with est 25m, and job `C06_instant` arriving at 10:25 with zero staging delay (pre-staged electronic ticket, est 6m).

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

1. **Physical Staging Latency and Arrival Scan Decoupling (Data-Quality Exception)**
   * *Description:* The prompt notes that arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes, but in physical repair facilities, the gap between barcode scan at intake and physical placement on a technician's staging cart frequently spans 5 to 15 minutes due to unpacking, diagnostic triage, or parts kitting.
   * *Plausibility Ranking:* **Rank 1 (Highest)**. Universal physical bottleneck in depot repair operations.
   * *Diagnostic Impact:* Resolves the apparent anomaly in QC at 10:32: if `C06` (arrived 10:25) was not physically available at the workbench until 10:34, `C04` (est 22m) was genuinely the shortest available job at 10:32. This preserves a pure rolling SPT diagnosis for QC without requiring a frozen-batch assumption. It also explains why `B05` (arr 09:46) was not started at 09:48 in QB.

2. **Informal Two-Tier Priority Triage vs Documented 3-Level Scheme (Operational Rule Exception)**
   * *Description:* Management documentation defines three numeric priority tiers (1, 2, 3), but floor operators collapse tiers into binary buckets: "Emergency / Hot" (P1) vs "Routine Backlog" (P2 & P3). Floor dispatchers treat routine backlog as FIFO to minimize customer friction and complaints about perceived queue-jumping.
   * *Plausibility Ranking:* **Rank 2 (Very High)**. Operational staff routinely resist fine-grained priority distinctions unless an automated system enforces hard dispatch locking.
   * *Diagnostic Impact:* Reclassifies QB from an "inconsistent priority queue" into an elegant, intentional hybrid: strictly preemptive for P1, and strict FIFO for normal flow. This fully explains why `B04` (P3) preceded `B06` (P2) at 10:28.

3. **Batch Dispatch and Periodic Work-Order Release (Workflow Exception)**
   * *Description:* Dispatchers do not re-solve an optimization problem at every job completion. Instead, work is dispatched in periodic waves (e.g., at resource availability transitions or every 30–60 minutes), printing or staging a batch of traveler sheets.
   * *Plausibility Ranking:* **Rank 3 (Moderate to High)**. Very common in component and bench repair environments.
   * *Diagnostic Impact:* Directly accounts for the sequence in QC: the initial backlog {C01, C02, C03, C04} was sorted by SPT at 10:10 and executed as an indivisible sequence. Only an explicit SLA breach alarm (`C05` passing 10:50) can interrupt the execution of the active batch.

4. **Unrecorded Micro-Breaks, Tool Setup, and Pre-Meeting Buffers (Resource State Exception)**
   * *Description:* Technicians require switchover time between jobs for calibration, cleanup, tool retrieval, or walking to meetings. In QD, `D-F` completes `D03` at 11:36 and has a meeting at 12:00. The 24-minute window was not used for `D10` (est 12m), and the note explicitly states "D-F still unavailable until 12:20". In QA, `A1` exhibits a 2-minute gap between `A03` completion (08:30) and `A02` start (08:32).
   * *Plausibility Ranking:* **Rank 4 (Moderate)**. Standard human labor behavior that is rarely captured in high-level ERP event logs.
   * *Diagnostic Impact:* Eliminates false inferences of deliberate job withholding or artificial capacity restrictions. Demonstrates that `D-F` was unavailable starting at 11:36 (pre-meeting travel/prep), validating why electrical job `D10` had to wait for flexible resource `D-X` at 12:16.

# 7. Final synthesis

### Most Identifiable Queue: Queue QD
* **Assessment:** Queue **QD** is by far the most identifiable queue in the system (Confidence: 96%).
* **Justification:** Multi-resource routing creates strict, mathematically observable boundaries between skills and server capabilities. The skill requirements partition the work cleanly: firmware+electrical (`D05`) is structurally constrained to `D-F`, and mechanical+electrical (`D07`) is structurally constrained to `D-X`. Within each resource's feasible skill pool, secondary dispatch decisions unambiguously adhere to strict FIFO arrival order (e.g., `D-M` dispatches `D04` [P3] before `D08` [P1] at 11:25; `D-X` dispatches `D07` [P3] before `D10` [P1] at 11:48). Apparent idling episodes are completely explained by documented appointment times (`D-X` reserving 11:20–11:30 for `D06`) and calendar exceptions (`D-F` meeting).

### Least Identifiable Queue: Queue QB
* **Assessment:** Queue **QB** is the least identifiable queue (Confidence: 74%).
* **Justification:** QB exhibits multiple confounding mechanisms: work interruption and preemption, partial task resumption, explicit manual dispatcher overrides ("second stop: dispatcher reassignment"), and apparent contradictions in priority ordering. In particular, the selection of `B04` (Priority 3) ahead of `B06` (Priority 2) at 10:28 can be explained by three competing, observationally equivalent mechanisms:
  1. An undocumented two-tier hybrid policy where Priority 1 is the only expedited class, while Priorities 2 and 3 collapse into standard FIFO.
  2. A continuous dynamic priority aging mechanism where `B04`'s 53-minute wait promoted it above `B06`.
  3. Physical staging / scan latency masking `B06`'s visibility.
  Without decision snapshots and preemption logs, these cannot be separated from pure human dispatcher error.

### Most Dangerous Policy to Infer from Observations Alone: Pure Static Priority Scheduling
* **Risk Analysis:** The most dangerous inference management could make is assuming that any queue (especially **QA** or **QD**) operates under a **Priority Discipline** simply because high-priority jobs eventually get processed quickly or because Priority 1 jobs exist in the log.
* **Why it is Catastrophic:**
  * In **QA**, both Priority 1 jobs (`A04` and `A08`) suffered extended wait times behind Priority 3 jobs (`A02`, `A05`, `A07`). Every walk-in job was processed in strict FIFO sequence.
  * In **QD**, `D-M` explicitly bypassed a short Priority 1 job (`D08`, est 8m) to execute a Priority 3 job (`D04`, est 10m) solely because `D04` arrived 11 minutes earlier.
  * If business analysts infer a Priority discipline from management claims and build staffing models, customer SLAs, or automated dispatch software around priority weights, **critical SLA commitments will be systematically breached**. Priority 1 customers will experience unmitigated FIFO queueing delays, and capacity planning will fail to account for the real operational bottlenecks: appointment reservation windows, batch staging latencies, and skill fragmentation.
