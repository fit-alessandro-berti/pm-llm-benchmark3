## 1. What is observed
Availability of automation is a branch-month binary: Alpha and Beta 1 from April, Gamma 1 from July, Delta and Epsilon 0 through August. Actual use is continuous: `automated_fast_path_pct` shows low baseline ~4-6% before availability, rising gradually to ~48-50% in Alpha/Beta by August and 25-32% in Gamma, indicating staggered adoption with learning and incomplete fidelity. No branch reaches 100% automated cases; treatment intensity varies within treated months.

Branch selection into rollout is non-random and targeted: Alpha and Beta were chosen first for high arrivals and historically poor cycle times (~49h and ~46h median pre vs ~34h Delta, ~36h Gamma), Gamma later after pilot. Contemporaneous operational changes confound the period: centralized fraud desk from March raised escalation for all branches; routing campaign added ~10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta from April and ~5% to Gamma from July; staffing shifts (Alpha -2 FTE May, Beta +1 April, Gamma -1 June, Delta +1 June); product change in May raised `high_complexity_pct` by 5-8 points in Alpha, Delta, Epsilon and Beta/Gamma. Outcomes are branch-month aggregates: `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` summarize case-level cycle times, `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` summarize paths, `csat_1to5` is a monthly mean from surveys with 20-45% response but no branch-month response counts, and FTE is productive average, not headcount.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: Staggered event-study + stacked DiD / synthetic-control pooled with not-yet-treated, using relative-time indicators | Relative months k = -3..+4 around branch-specific adoption (Alpha/Beta k=0 April, Gamma k=0 July); binary availability + separate dose specification in second stage | Not-yet-treated branches in same calendar month (Gamma+Delta+Epsilon for April-June; Delta+Epsilon for July-August); synthetic control weights for Alpha/Beta from Delta/Gamma/Epsilon pre-trends | high_complexity_pct, arrivals/fte pressure, fte, calendar time effects; fraud-desk effect captured by time FE; exclude March transition | E1_ITT_median, E3_Tail, E4_Rework, E5_Escalation, E6_CSAT, E7_Het_Dynamic | Conditional parallel trends after adjusting for observed case-mix and staffing; no anticipatory changes before k=0; stable comparison group; limited spillover or bounded spillover; common shocks affect all branches similarly conditional on covariates | Pre-trend placebo: joint F-test on k<0 coefficients 0; leave-one-control-out; covariate balance; event-study plot; negative-control outcome (high_complexity_pct should not be caused by treatment); synthetic-control RMSPE ratio | Time-varying confounding exactly aligned with treatment timing (routing +10%, +5%, May complexity shock, staffing changes) breaks parallel trends; 5 clusters too few for cluster-robust inference; fraud-desk spillover biases toward zero/positive duration |
| Alternative A: Matched / synthetic control per adopter (interrupted time series with donor pool) | Branch-specific adoption date; single treated unit vs weighted donor pool | Donor: Delta, Epsilon, and pre-treated Gamma for Alpha/Beta; Delta+Epsilon for Gamma | Pre-treatment outcome path (Jan-Mar), high_complexity_pct, fte, arrival level | E1_ITT_median, E3_Tail | Donor pool can reproduce treated counterfactual had they not adopted; no spillover to donors; no unmeasured time-varying confounder after weighting | Pre-treatment fit RMSPE, placebo in-time and in-space, predictor balance, ratio of post/pre RMSPE | Donor pool small and structurally different (Delta low complexity/volume); product change May and routing not shared, poor fit |
| Alternative B: Dose-response panel with branch and month FE, instrumenting dose with availability | Continuous automated_fast_path_pct (intensity), instrumented by automation availability × elapsed time | Within-branch variation over time, between-branch variation in timing | branch FE, month FE, high_complexity_pct, arrivals/fte, fte, lagged dose to capture learning | E2_Dose_fastpath_median, E3/E4/E5 dose versions | Relevance (availability strongly predicts dose); exclusion: availability affects outcomes only via fast-path share; linear dose-response; no time-varying confounder correlated with dose | First-stage F, reduced form vs IV comparison, test of dose-outcome slope stability, residual plots vs complexity/shocks | Exclusion violated (availability changes practices beyond measured fast-path share, direct learning, fraud-desk threshold shift); dose endogenous to local effort and case selection |

Why primary is preferable for these data: Binary availability event-study using not-yet-treated comparisons most transparently leverages the only clean variation—staggered timing—while preserving the 1-5 month horizon leadership cares about. It does not require modeling the dose-response functional form as the sole identification, and it allows separate visualization of median vs tail dynamics and of learning (k=0,1 vs k=3,4). With Gamma as not-yet-treated for April-June, we have a contemporaneous control that experienced the same March fraud-desk shift and part of the May complexity shock but not the April routing shock, enabling a direct check of pre-trends. The stacked approach avoids the negative weighting problem of naive two-way fixed effects with heterogeneous timing. Month fixed effects partially absorb common shocks, while adjusting for high_complexity_pct and pressure (arrivals/fte) addresses the documented observable confounders without claiming to eliminate staffing and routing biases. Even so, inference must acknowledge five clusters make conventional asymptotics invalid, so the design is descriptive-synthetic rather than sharply identified, and the dose analysis is presented as corroboration, not proof. This balances operational interpretability, process awareness (median vs tail, escalation spillover), and honesty about small-N uncertainty better than a dose-only or single donor synthetic model.

3. Treatment intensity rather than binary flag

Within-branch dose slopes (visual and simple pooled FE described below): Pooled OLS of median_cycle_h on automated_fast_path_pct with branch and month FE and controls (high_complexity, arrivals/fte) yields approximately -0.12h per 1pp (SE cluster ~0.08, 5 clusters, unreliable). Unadjusted, higher dose months have lower median in later months but June contradicts (Alpha dose 39 median 53.5). Adjusting for complexity+staffing, the negative slope steepens, suggesting ~1.2h reduction per +10pp dose after netting May-Jun shock. For p90, slope is near zero to -0.15h per pp after adjustment, weaker than median. Rework vs dose: U-shaped – initial dose 25-27pp associated with -2pp rework drop April, further dose 40-50pp not associated with additional rework decline. Escalation vs dose: +0.06pp escalation per 1pp dose (adopters diverge from controls by ~1pp beyond common fraud-desk rise). CSAT vs dose: weak +0.002 per pp, no clear dose gradient.

Because dose is chosen by branches (effort, case selection) and learning is time-confounded, this is not causal.

4. Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Pre-trends Jan-Mar: Alpha median 50.446.949.4, Beta 45.445.946.4, Delta 34.432.836.2, Gamma 36.436.539.6, Epsilon 41.439.340.2. Common March jump +1 to +3h for all branches coincides with fraud desk start, suggesting shared shock. Prior to that, Alpha/Beta show no downward trend that would by itself explain later drop; if anything Beta trends up. However selection on historically poor levels raises regression-to-mean concern: Alpha/Beta baselines were 10-14h above Delta/Gamma. Random fluctuation could cause them to fall toward mean even without treatment, and Gamma also improved late without long exposure. The fact that Delta/Epsilon deteriorated when complexity rose, while adopters did not deteriorate as much, could be treatment benefit or mean-reversion masking complexity shock. No evidence of anticipation (dose stayed 4-6% until adoption month).

5. Sensitivity / bounded conclusions (at least two outcomes)

Median: Raw DiD -5 to -8h late vs controls. Bounding for worst-case bias: If routing +10% adds ~1.5h (queuing rule-of-thumb 0.15h per 1pp load) and staffing loss +2 FTE adds ~2h, and complexity +8pp adds ~3h (observed control slope ~0.4h per pp), then observed -3.3h late change for Alpha understates benefit by ~6.5h  upper bound ~-9h. Conversely, if regression-to-mean accounts for -4h, lower bound ~0h. Plausible interval for ITT availability effect on median in high-fidelity months Jul-Aug: 0 to -6h (-0% to -12%), center ~-3h. Per 10pp dose: 0 to -2h, sensitive to June outlier exclusion.

Tail p90: All branches worsened vs Jan-Mar; adopters worsened ~7-10h vs controls +15-17h late. Under same bias corrections, DiD -6 to -11h benefit but absolute levels still above baseline. If fraud-desk spillover harms controls equally, DiD overstates benefit; bound as low as 0. Interval for ITT on p90: -10 to +2h, with central tendency slightly negative but less robust than median; no evidence of tail reduction below baseline.

CSAT and rework/escalation intervals are narrow and near zero once adjusted for common shocks (see Section 7).

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

An immediate unbiased claim that automation reduces cycle time by X% is not defensible given targeted rollout, shared fraud-desk congestion, routing and staffing shocks, and small-N uncertainty. The most promising signal is the late-period median improvement once fidelity exceeded ~35-45% and after the May-June complexity and staffing peak, particularly Gamma's ~6.5h drop versus recent pre and Alpha's 10h drop from June peak. However tail performance did not improve below baseline and extra escalation suggests capacity pressure that would worsen if Delta and Epsilon add load without fraud-desk expansion. Therefore do not pause nor expand unconditionally; expand in a way that generates causal learning and guards the tail.

Next branches: Randomize Delta and Epsilon at case level within-branch if system allows, otherwise randomize phased branch-month staggered rollout with blocked randomization: assign one of Delta/Epsilon to early (e.g., September) and the other to delayed control (e.g., November), stratifying by complexity. If only one branch can be added at a time, prioritize Delta (lower complexity, more headroom) to test benefit in a historically better branch and avoid overgeneralizing from poor-baseline Alpha/Beta. Keep Gamma at support and Alpha/Beta at high-fidelity to continue learning curve.

Assignment: Use cluster-randomized encouragement at branch-month level with transparent lottery; if management refuses branch randomization, use randomized case-level routing to automated vs manual triage within the next branch (50/50) with identical fraud-desk queuing, preserving capacity measurement. Pre-register analysis.

Measurements: Minimum 3 months pre and 3 months post per branch at case level, daily aggregates at minimum, to establish parallel trends and detect learning. Collect process-mining outcomes: case-level path variant with timestamps for Register, Triage (manual/automated), Specialist Review, Rework loops, Resolve, Close; queue waiting time at fraud/specialist desk per case; triage model score and threshold; rework reason codes. Also collect staffing per shift, arrival rate, high_complexity class, and CSAT with response indicator per case.

Spillover monitoring: Real-time fraud-desk queue length and wait by originating branch; compare control branch waits before/after adopter escalation rises; cap total escalation rate and trigger specialist capacity surge if p90 exceeds guardrail.

Guardrails: Stop or throttle if p90 rises >10% above pre for two consecutive weeks, or rework rises >2pp, or CSAT drops >0.15 points with stable response rates, or fraud-desk wait exceeds SLA. Decision rule for scale-up: Expand to remaining branch only if event-study shows median improvement of at least 3h (6-8%) sustained over 2 late months with no tail harm, dose-response slope significantly negative after bias-corrected wild bootstrap, and fraud-desk capacity assessed adequate; otherwise redesign thresholds and retrain before scale.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Conditionally causal:** Automated triage at mature fidelity (~35-50% of cases on automated fast path) may reduce median cycle time by roughly 2-6 hours versus the counterfactual of no automation under similar load and complexity, conditional on adequate staffing and specialist capacity; effect is heterogeneous, emerging only after 2-3 months of learning and not guaranteed for historically low-cycle-time branches.
- **Suggestive:** Tail (p90) performance did not improve in absolute terms; the smaller deterioration of adopters relative to never-treated branches suggests a modest relative tail benefit but cannot be distinguished from differential exposure to the May complexity shock and shared fraud-desk congestion.
- **Observed:** Automation increased the share of cases completing via automated fast path from ~4% to 25-50%, was accompanied by a 1-3 percentage-point rise in specialist escalation beyond the March fraud-desk level, and by a transient 2-point fall in rework that returned to baseline.
- **Not supported:** A single defensible statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” or “automation improves customer satisfaction” for Delta/Epsilon or for the organization as a whole.
