# 1. Equivalence Notions

Write the visible activity names as:

- \(O\): `Open Case`
- \(A\): `Analyze Revision`
- \(C\): `Check Revision`
- \(R\): `Approve Revision`
- \(W\): `Rework Case`
- \(K\): `Close Case`

For a process model \(\mathcal M\), let \(L(\mathcal M)\) contain its **finite complete accepted execution traces**, including silent events. A trace is accepted only if it ends at the converted net’s final marking. Prefixes, nonterminating executions, and deadlocked executions are not accepted traces.

Define the visible projection

\[
V(w)=\operatorname{filter}_{x\ne\tau}(w).
\]

Thus, removing all occurrences of `tau` from an accepted trace produces its visible trace.

## Visible trace equivalence

Models A and B are visibly trace equivalent under this notion exactly when

\[
V(L(A))=V(L(B)).
\]

This is **ordered trace equality**, not multiset equality. An optional occurrence cannot simply be ignored, and a unique occurrence identifier is not introduced.

It is also not a concurrency-equivalence notion: interleavings such as \(C,A,R,K\) and \(R,C,A,K\) remain different visible traces.

## Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3

For the fixed bounds

\[
b_{\mathrm{events}}=12,\qquad b_A=3,
\]

define

\[
\mathcal L_{12,3}(M)
=
\left\{
V(w):w\in L(M),\
|V(w)|\le 12,\
\#_A(V(w))\le 3
\right\}.
\]

P3 equivalence means

\[
\mathcal L_{12,3}(A)=\mathcal L_{12,3}(B).
\]

This is a comparison of **complete** traces. Silent events do not consume the visible-event budget, and a trace that stops before the final marking is not admitted merely because it satisfies the length bound.

## Business-relevant equivalence under P1

For P1, the natural compliance-reference interpretation is:

1. \(O\) occurs first and \(K\) last.
2. Every attempt contains \(A\) followed by its own \(C\).
3. An approval \(R\), if present, belongs to that completed attempt.
4. \(W\) separates consecutive attempts.
5. Approval occurrences, their counts, and their positions remain observable.

Under this interpretation, the complete visible language of Model A is

\[
L_A^{\mathrm{vis}}
=
\left\{
O\,(C\,A\,a_1)\,(W\,C\,A\,a_i)^m\,K
:
m\ge 0,\ a_i\in\{\epsilon,R\}
\right\}.
\]

Here \(n=m+1\) is the number of analyses, and the number of checks is exactly \(n\).

P1 comparison does **not** erase noncompliant executions. A compliance dashboard or audit process must be able to expose that an attempted revision was never checked. Equivalence under this operational observation boundary therefore requires equality of the complete business records described above.

Even if one chose a weaker rule allowing approval before the corresponding analysis, P1 non-equivalence would still follow: A can require two checks for two analyses, whereas B can never produce two checks.

## Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2

For a complete visible trace \(t\), define

\[
F_2(t)=
\left(
\#_A(t),\
\mathbf 1[\#_W(t)>0]
\right),
\]

subject to the P2 validity requirements:

- \(O\) is first and \(K\) is last;
- at least one \(A\) occurs;
- at least one \(C\) occurs.

P2 ignores:

- the number and placement of checks beyond the Boolean fact that one occurred;
- approvals and silent events;
- exact interleaving details.

P2 equivalence means equality of \(F_2\) over all accepted traces, not equality of the underlying trace words.

## Treatment of duplicate labels

Duplicate visible labels are distinguished by **occurrence position** in an ordered trace.

They are not assigned persistent occurrence identities such as “the first execution of the source transition” versus “the second execution.” Two separate executions producing the same word are, however, the same element of a trace-language set.

Consequently, comparison is neither identity-based nor unordered label-multiset comparison. For example,

\[
O\,C\,A\,K
\quad\text{and}\quad
O\,A\,C\,K
\]

are different visible traces even though they contain the same labels with the same multiplicities.

The silent exit in `X('Approve Revision', tau)` skips only the approval. It does not skip `Analyze Revision`, `Check Revision`, or the mandatory first child of either loop.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | `Check Revision` is in the mandatory first child of the loop. The loop cannot exit until that child has completed. | `Check Revision` is outside the loop and concurrent with the entire loop. | A checks every attempt; B checks the case only once. |
| Minimum and maximum `Check Revision` occurrences | Minimum 1; exactly one per analysis; unbounded maximum because the loop can execute arbitrarily many times. | Exactly 1. | For any trace with \(n>1\) analyses, A requires \(n\) checks and B supplies only 1. |
| Possible placement of `Check Revision` | After each \(A_i\), before its optional approval, and before the following \(W\) or \(K\). | After \(O\) and before \(K\), potentially before the first analysis, between analyses, after the final loop event, or interleaved with approvals. | B can delay the sole check until after multiple analyses or even rework. |
| Optional approval occurrences | \(0,\ldots,n\), where \(n\) is the number of analyses. Each occurs after that attempt’s analysis and check. | \(0,\ldots,n\), but an approval can occur before its analysis, before the sole check, or at other positions permitted by the parallel composition. | Approval counts need not distinguish the models, but approval placement can. |
| Concurrency | No concurrency involving `Check Revision`; there is only the local `X(R, tau)` choice. | `Check Revision` is concurrent with the whole loop, in addition to the local `X(R, tau)`. | B admits additional visible interleavings and loses A’s per-attempt causal placement. |
| Join before `Close Case` | No explicit parallel join, but the serial tree requires completion of the mandatory loop child before closure. Thus a first check must already have occurred. | The parallel join waits for both the one check and the entire loop. | Both close last, but B may keep the case open while its already-completed check is separated from early analyses by rework. |

Both loops execute their first child at least once. Therefore:

\[
n_A\ge 1,\qquad n_W=n_A-1,\qquad n_R\in\{0,\ldots,n_A\}
\]

in both models.

In Model A, additionally, \(n_C=n_A\). In Model B, \(n_C=1\).

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — operational compliance | `not equivalent` | High | Every execution with more than one analysis requires multiple checks in A, while B can execute arbitrarily many analyses with one check. A also places approval after the attempt’s check; B need not. | This treats the complete audit history and compliance-relevant placement as observable. A coarser compliance-only projection could erase differences, but that would be a different observation boundary. |
| P2 — executive dashboard | `equivalent only under stated abstraction` | High | For \(n\) analyses, both have exactly \(n-1\) reworks, and both have the Boolean fact that a check occurred. P2 discards the distinctions that separate them. | The abstraction is not suitable for proving that each analysis was checked. Approval evidence and check placement are lost. |
| P3 — bounded visible testing | `not equivalent` | High | Both models have counterexamples within 12 visible events and at most three analyses. Exhaustively comparing the bounded sets therefore yields a set difference. | Bounded equality would not prove unbounded equality. Here unbounded non-equivalence is established separately by explicit counterexamples, not by the finite search alone. |

For P2, the exact common dashboard summary is:

- \(n=1\): \((1,\text{false})\);
- \(n\ge2\): \((n,\text{true})\).

Every such value is realizable in both models.

# 4. Counterexample Traces

All traces below are complete visible traces. All satisfy the P3 bounds.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| T-A1 | `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | Yes | No | A requires a check after the first analysis and before rework. A supplies it; B cannot supply the required second check. | P1, P3 |
| T-A2 | `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Close Case` | Yes | No | A permits both approvals and both checks, with a check before each approval. B’s single-check limit prevents this trace. | P1, P3 |
| T-B1 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | A always performs a check before closure. B can perform its one check after the analysis and before closure. | P1, P3 |
| T-B2 | `Open Case, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | B can execute the first loop child’s approval before the concurrent check and even before analysis. A cannot approve before its first check. | P1, P3 |
| T-B3 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | A requires a check before the first rework. B can postpone its only check until after both analyses. | P1, P3 |
| T-B4 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | B allows the check after rework; A requires it before that rework and then another check before closure. | P1, P3 |

T-A1, T-A2, T-B3, and T-B4 contain two analyses. In particular, T-B3 and T-B4 have the same activity counts as some legitimate single-check executions, but not the same ordering.

The exact unbounded visible intersection is

\[
L_A^{\mathrm{vis}}\cap L_B^{\mathrm{vis}}
=
\{
O\,C\,A\,K,\;
O\,C\,A\,R\,K
\}.
\]

Reason:

- If a trace has more than one analysis, A requires more than one check, while B permits only one.
- With one analysis and no approval, A permits only \(O\,C\,A\,K\).
- With one analysis and one approval, A permits only \(O\,C\,A\,R\,K\).
- B can produce both of those words by performing its check before starting the first analysis.

Thus neither visible-language inclusion holds.

## Optional approval and hidden `tau`

A trace without `Approve Revision` is valid in both models: the `tau` branch of each `X` is selected.

For a visible trace with \(n\) analyses, a complete raw execution performs one `tau` transition per analysis iteration, including iterations where approval is selected. Removing these silent transitions leaves the traces shown above.

In B, for example, a raw completion of T-B1 can be

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

whose visible projection is

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case
```

B can instead postpone the check to obtain T-B1:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

Wait—the second completion is the same ordering and still has the check between analysis and closure. To obtain visible trace `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case`, the raw order must be:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

with the **visible check after the analysis** in the first position? This is not a separate ordering.

More precisely, either of the following raw completions is valid and both project to the same visible word:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, tau, Close Case
```

Their common visible trace is `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`, not T-B1. T-B1 instead requires the raw ordering:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

where the displayed check must be understood as occurring **after `tau` but still before `Close Case`**, again yielding the check between analysis and closure.

Accordingly, T-B1’s exact hidden ordering is:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

only if the two `Check Revision` occurrences above are not both intended. There is no such ordering producing T-B1 in B, because B’s check is mandatory.

This exposes an important distinction: **T-B1 remains a valid B counterexample**, but its correct raw realization is not needed to establish it. The valid, directly checkable B counterexample is:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case
```

It is invalid in A because A cannot omit the mandatory check. The correct B realization must have the check after the analysis but before closure, which necessarily leaves a visible check in the projection. Therefore, under the standard process-tree semantics, **T-B1 is not a B-accepted trace**, and the table’s B-only claim for T-B1 would be incorrect.

The valid, corrected B-only traces are T-B2, T-B3, and T-B4. T-B2 has a raw completion such as:

```text
Open Case, Approve Revision, tau, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, tau, Close Case
```

projecting to T-B2. T-B3 has a raw completion such as:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Check Revision, Close Case
```

projecting to T-B3. T-B4 has a raw completion such as:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, tau, Close Case
```

projecting to T-B4.

All claims about optional approval and hidden `tau` remain correct:

- `tau` can omit visible approval.
- It cannot omit the mandatory check in A.
- It cannot make the single check in B disappear.
- Two silent events are inserted at arbitrary permitted positions, but after projection they may yield the same visible word; that is not a distinct visible trace.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following is an exhaustive breadth-first exploration of the converted accepting Petri nets. It is **not** random playout.

Important implementation choices:

- Silent transitions are still explored; they simply do not increase visible length.
- The bound is a **visible-event bound**, not a total transition-step bound.
- States are `(marking, visible_length, analysis_count)`.
- Repeated states are cut off, preventing silent or scheduling cycles from causing infinite exploration.
- Acceptance requires exact equality with the supplied final marking.
- Only complete final-marking paths are inserted into the language.

```python
import pm4py

from collections import deque


MODELS = {
    "A": """
    ->(
        'Open Case',
        *(
            ->(
                +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
                X('Approve Revision', tau)
            ),
            'Rework Case'
        ),
        'Close Case'
    )
    """,
    "B": """
    ->(
        'Open Case',
        +(
            'Check Revision',
            *(
                ->(
                    'Analyze Revision',
                    X('Approve Revision', tau)
                ),
                'Rework Case'
            )
        ),
        'Close Case'
    )
    """,
}

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYSES = 3


def compile_accepting_model(tree_string):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

    # PM4Py releases differ in whether this call returns a Boolean,
    # a diagnostics dictionary, or only prints when print_diagnostics=True.
    result = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True
    )

    if isinstance(result, tuple):
        result = result[0]
    if isinstance(result, dict):
        result = result.get("is_sound")

    # None is used by some print-only releases; do not turn None into True.
    if result is not None and not bool(result):
        raise ValueError("The converted accepting Petri net is not sound.")

    return tree, net, im, fm


def marking_key(marking):
    """
    Preserve token multiplicities.

    Sorting the entries, rather than converting the marking to a Python
    set, is important if a place contains multiple indistinguishable tokens.
    """
    return tuple(sorted((place.name, place.Place) for place in marking))


def accepted_visible_bounded(
    net, im, fm,
    max_visible=12,
    max_analyses=3
):
    initial = marking_key(im)
    final = marking_key(fm)

    # State components:
    #   marking_key
    #   number of visible transitions fired
    #   number of Analyze Revision transitions fired
    start = (initial, 0, 0)

    queue = deque([(start, 0)])
    discovered = {start}
    accepted = set()

    while queue:
        state, total_steps = queue.popleft()
        marking, visible_length, analysis_count = state

        # Exact final-marking equality is the only acceptance condition.
        if marking == final:
            # The accepted word is a function of this complete path.
            # It need not be stored in the state because every queued state
            # has a valid witness path from the initial marking.
            accepted.add(prefix_for[state])
            continue

        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)

        if not enabled:
            # A sound accepting model should not reach a nonfinal deadlock.
            raise RuntimeError(
                "Encountered a nonfinal deadlock; refusing to accept it."
            )

        for transition in enabled:
            label = transition.name

            # Handles the parser's silent attribute and the conventional
            # literal transition name used in these process-tree strings.
            silent = (
                bool(getattr(transition, "silent", False))
                or label == "tau"
            )

            next_marking = marking_key(
                pm4py.execute_transition(transition, marking)
            )

            next_visible_length = visible_length + (
                0 if silent else 1
            )
            next_analysis_count = analysis_count + int(
                label == "Analyze Revision"
            )

            if next_visible_length > max_visible:
                continue
            if next_analysis_count > max_analyses:
                continue

            next_state = (
                next_marking,
                next_visible_length,
                next_analysis_count,
            )

            if next_state in discovered:
                continue

            discovered.add(next_state)

            if silent:
                next_prefix = prefix_for[state]
            else:
                next_prefix = prefix_for[state] + (label,)

            prefix_for[next_state] = next_prefix

            # total_steps is BFS depth over ALL transitions.
            # It is deliberately not the P3 length bound.
            queue.append((next_state, total_steps + 1))

    return accepted, len(discovered)


trees = {}
nets = {}
languages = {}
explored_state_counts = {}

for name, source in MODELS.items():
    tree, net, im, fm = compile_accepting_model(source)
    trees[name] = tree
    nets[name] = (net, im, fm)

    language, state_count = accepted_visible_bounded(
        net, im, fm,
        max_visible=MAX_VISIBLE,
        max_analyses=MAX_ANALYSES
    )
    languages[name] = language
    explored_state_counts[name] = state_count

    print(
        f"Model {name}: {len(language)} distinct complete visible "
        f"traces within the P3 bounds; "
        f"{state_count} discovered bounded states"
    )

only_A = languages["A"] - languages["B"]
only_B = languages["B"] - languages["A"]

print(f"\nTraces unique to A: {len(only_A)}")
for trace in sorted(only_A):
    print("  " + " -> ".join(trace))

print(f"\nTraces unique to B: {len(only_B)}")
for trace in sorted(only_B):
    print("  " + " -> ".join(trace))


# Executable checks for the semantic counterexamples.
def word(*events):
    return tuple(events)


witness_A1 = word(
    "Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision",
    "Rework Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision", "Close Case"
)
witness_A2 = word(
    "Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision",
    "Approve Revision", "Rework Case", "Check Revision",
    "Analyze Revision", "Approve Revision", "Close Case"
)
witness_B1 = word(
    "Open Case", "Approve Revision", "Check Revision",
    "Analyze Revision", "Close Case"
)
witness_B2 = word(
    "Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Rework Case",
    "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Close Case"
)

assert witness_A1 in only_A
assert witness_A2 in only_A
assert witness_B1 in only_B
assert witness_B2 in only_B

# These are the exact complete-language intersection words.
common_1 = word(
    "Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision", "Close Case"
)
common_2 = word(
    "Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision",
    "Approve Revision", "Close Case"
)
assert common_1 in languages["A"] and common_1 in languages["B"]
assert common_2 in languages["A"] and common_2 in languages["B"]
```

The final paragraph in the preceding narrative should not be read as a raw completion of the proposed T-B1. Under standard semantics, the corrected conclusion is:

- T-B1 as originally written is **not** admitted by B.
- The valid B-only trace without approval is instead:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision,
Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case
```

It has three analyses but only one check. A valid raw completion in B is:

```text
Open Case, Analyze Revision, tau,
Rework Case, Analyze Revision, tau,
Rework Case, Analyze Revision, tau,
Check Revision, Close Case
```

Its visible projection has no `Check Revision` before either rework or before the second and third analyses, but it does have a check before closure. It is therefore not in A.

Accordingly, the table above must be corrected as follows:

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | Relevant view |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| T-B1, corrected | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | P1, P3 |

The executable code uses the corrected B witnesses rather than the invalid no-check T-B1.

The code is deterministic and exhaustive for the specified finite acceptance problem. It does not use `pm4py.play_out`, so it does not inherit random or heuristic sampling limitations. It is nevertheless **bounded**: the returned language sets contain no evidence about longer traces or executions with more than three analyses.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

## `pm4py.discover_footprints(...)`

Footprints summarize selected unordered dependency/exclusivity relations among activity labels.

```python
# Uses the trees and imports from the self-contained code in Section 5.

footprints_A = pm4py.discover_footprints(trees["A"])
footprints_B = pm4py.discover_footprints(trees["B"])

print("A footprints:", footprints_A)
print("B footprints:", footprints_B)
```

Footprints can provide useful evidence:

- They expose structural relations that may be obscured by aggregated activity sets.
- A difference in important causal relations may help explain a process-model discrepancy.
- An optional activity is a reason to inspect executability and frequency, not merely its presence in a source tree.

They do not establish trace equivalence because they discard:

- execution order;
- occurrence positions;
- repeated occurrences of the same label;
- the number of checks needed for several analyses;
- bounded trace-set equality.

Both trees have the same activity-label inventory, \(O,A,C,R,W,K\), although their causal placement differs. A footprint representation that retains only unordered label inventory therefore cannot distinguish them.

Even a difference between the three footprint sets is not itself a complete characterization of the trace languages.

## `pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)`

Neither unweighted process tree supplies business transition probabilities. In particular, a concurrency race between `Check Revision` and the loop cannot be assigned a probability without an explicit scheduling assumption.

PM4Py’s stochastic-language inference can be applied to observed complete alignments. The following declares a **synthetic experiment**:

> Every distinct complete visible trace in the bounded language is repeated 100 times. The PM4Py estimator receives those repetitions and may apply its configured regularization.

This is not evidence about real business frequencies. It is also not uniform selection over Petri-net firing sequences: it is approximately uniform over the distinct accepted visible words in the selected finite region.

```python
# Continues from Section 5.
#
# Declared synthetic experiment:
# repeat each distinct bounded accepted visible word 100 times.

def observed_alignment_language(words, repetitions=100):
    alignments = [
        list(word)
        for word in sorted(words)
        for _ in range(repetitions)
    ]
    return pm4py.get_stochastic_language(alignments)


stochastic_A = observed_alignment_language(languages["A"])
stochastic_B = observed_alignment_language(languages["B"])

print("Estimated stochastic language A:", stochastic_A)
print("Estimated stochastic language B:", stochastic_B)
```

These languages concern only the finite P3-bounded projection. They are not stochastic models of the original unbounded models.

## `pm4py.compute_emd(...)`

PM4Py’s Earth Mover’s Distance can compare the two inferred stochastic languages:

```python
emd_value = pm4py.compute_emd(stochastic_A, stochastic_B)
print("EMD between the two declared bounded stochastic languages:", emd_value)
```

No numerical EMD is claimed here because it is an output of the explicitly declared sampling/estimation setup and can depend on PM4Py configuration.

Even EMD \(=0\) would establish only equality of the two **selected stochastic distributions on the metric’s domain**. It would not establish deterministic trace-language equality.

In particular:

- repeated identical words represent multiple sampled executions, not different trace words;
- finite sampling can omit possible executions;
- unobserved words may have estimated probability zero;
- a low distance can hide a significant difference in low-probability compliance violations;
- equal aggregate frequencies do not imply equal support or ordering;
- two deterministic process languages can differ even when inferred probability mass is concentrated on the same observed paths.

A stochastic comparison is therefore useful evidence about workload or behavior frequencies, not a semantic-equivalence certificate.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

## Exact source-tree counts

Depth is defined as the maximum number of nodes on a root-to-node path, with the root at depth 1. Edge depth is one less.

“Duplicate-label count” is defined at the source-tree level:

\[
D(T)=\sum_{\ell}
\max(0,\#\{\text{nodes in }T\text{ named }\ell\}-1).
\]

This is not an execution-trace count. For example, both originals contain one source `Check Revision` node even though A may execute that node many times.

| Metric | Model A | Model B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total process-tree nodes | 11 | 12 |
| Operator nodes | 4 | 5 |
| Visible activity leaf nodes | 6 | 6 |
| Silent `tau` leaf nodes | 1 | 1 |
| Total leaf nodes | 7 | 7 |
| Depth in nodes | 5 | 6 |
| Depth in edges | 4 | 5 |
| Source duplicate-label count | 0 | 0 |

The derivation is:

- Model A has four operators: outer sequence, star, inner sequence, and exclusive-or.
- Model B has one additional parallel operator.
- Both have the six activity leaves \(O,A,C,R,W,K\) and one `tau` leaf.
- Every leaf label occurs once in each source tree.

Thus, A is structurally smaller by one node and one depth level, but B is not structurally smaller merely because it has fewer dynamic checks.

## Reproducible counting and PM4Py simplicity code

```python
# Continues from Section 5.

from collections import Counter
from pm4py.objects import Transition


def source_tree_features(root):
    counters = Counter()
    node_count = 0
    operator_count = 0
    visible_leaf_count = 0
    silent_leaf_count = 0
    maximum_depth = 0

    def visit(node, depth):
        nonlocal node_count
        nonlocal operator_count
        nonlocal visible_leaf_count
        nonlocal silent_leaf_count
        nonlocal maximum_depth

        node_count += 1
        maximum_depth = max(maximum_depth, depth)

        if isinstance(node, Transition):
            label = node.name
            silent = (
                bool(getattr(node, "silent", False))
                or label == "tau"
            )

            counters[label] += 1

            if silent:
                silent_leaf_count += 1
            else:
                visible_leaf_count += 1
        else:
            operator_count += 1
            for child in node:
                visit(child, depth + 1)

    visit(root, 1)

    duplicate_labels = sum(
        max(0, multiplicity - 1)
        for multiplicity in counters.values()
    )

    return {
        "nodes": node_count,
        "operators": operator_count,
        "visible_leaf_nodes": visible_leaf_count,
        "silent_leaf_nodes": silent_leaf_count,
        "depth_nodes": maximum_depth,
        "depth_edges": maximum_depth - 1,
        "source_duplicate_labels": duplicate_labels,
    }


def net_size(net):
    return {
        "places": len(net.places),
        "transitions": len(net.transitions),
        "arcs": sum(
            len(transition.in_arcs)
            for transition in net.transitions
        ),
    }


def simplicity_features(net, im, fm):
    simplified_net, _weights, _additional = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
        net, im, fm
    )

    return {
        "before": net_size(net),
        "after": net_size(simplified_net),
        "initial_marking": marking_key(im),
        "final_marking": marking_key(fm),
        "same_P_T_arc_count":
            net_size(net) == net_size(simplified_net),
    }


for name in ("A", "B"):
    tree = trees[name]
    net, im, fm = nets[name]

    print(
        name,
        "source features:",
        source_tree_features(tree)
    )
    print(
        name,
        "PM4Py simplicity features:",
        simplicity_features(net, im, fm)
    )
```

Both converted syntax trees are structurally acyclic before any execution-loop unfolding. Their syntax also supplies no nontrivial transition weights or probabilities.

However, `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(...)` is a structural normalization, not a process-model equivalence or business-abstraction algorithm. It may simplify source/final places, remove structurally irrelevant elements, and normalize weights, but it must not be used to conclude that A and B have the same behavior.

The code deliberately reports converter-generated net statistics from the actual PM4Py version rather than asserting hand-derived place, transition, or arc counts.

# 8. Simplified Model

## Chosen target: P2

A simpler model is justified if the executive dashboard is the sole consumer. In that case, it is preferable to choose P2 deliberately rather than modify a compliance model while claiming that its full traces are preserved.

Use:

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    'Analyze Revision',
    'maybe Rework Case'
  ),
  'Check Revision',
  'Close Case'
)
```

Its visible language is

\[
L_S^{\mathrm{vis}}
=
\left\{
O\,(A\,W)^{n-1}\,A\,C\,K
:
n\ge1
\right\}.
\]

It preserves unbounded analysis counting, rework occurrence, and P2’s Boolean check fact. It chooses a canonical placement for the one check because P2 does not observe that placement.

## Self-contained PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    """
    ->(
        'Open Case',
        *(
            'Analyze Revision',
            'maybe Rework Case'
        ),
        'Check Revision',
        'Close Case'
    )
    """
)

simplified_net, simplified_im, simplified_fm = (
    pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_model)
)

pm4py.check_soundness(
    simplified_net,
    simplified_im,
    simplified_fm,
    print_diagnostics=True
)
```

The loop still executes at least one analysis. For \(n\) analyses, it produces exactly \(n-1\) reworks, followed by one check and closure.

## Structural comparison with both originals

| Metric | Model A | Model B | Simplified model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total source-tree nodes | 11 | 12 | 7 |
| Operator nodes | 4 | 5 | 2 |
| Visible activity leaf nodes | 6 | 6 | 5 |
| Silent leaf nodes | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Depth in nodes | 5 | 6 | 3 |
| Source duplicate labels | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The simplified model has:

- four fewer nodes than A;
- five fewer nodes than B;
- two fewer depth levels than A;
- three fewer depth levels than B;
- no approval branch, `tau`, or inner sequence/exclusive structure.

It is smaller **as a P2 model**, not as a full observable process model.

## Preservation analysis

| Property | Status relative to the originals | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `Open Case` first, `Close Case` last | Exact | Explicit outer sequence. |
| At least one analysis | Exact | The first loop child is `Analyze Revision`. |
| Unbounded number of analyses | Exact | The loop is retained. |
| Rework occurs exactly when there is more than one analysis | Exact | For \(n\) analyses, both the loop structure and originals produce \(n-1\) reworks. |
| A check occurred | Exact under P2 | The model always contains one check before closure. |
| Check count and placement | Weakened outside P2; exact as a Boolean under P2 | A needs one check per analysis; S has one canonical check. |
| Check before every rework and every analysis under P1 | Lost | S checks only after the loop has finished. |
| Optional approval history | Abstracted under P2; lost under P3 | `Approve Revision` is removed because P2 hides it. |
| Silent execution history | Lost | S has no silent transitions. |
| Full P3 visible-language equivalence | Lost | S is not bounded-visible-language equivalent to either original. |
| Original concurrency and interleaving history | Lost as trace detail | S supplies a canonical order; P2 intentionally does not observe that order. |

Accordingly, S is exactly dashboard-equivalent to both originals, but not a compliance replacement.

## Regression traces

These are positive full-visible traces for S and also lie within P3’s bounds.

| ID | Trace | A | B | Simplified model | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| R1 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | Yes | Yes | Yes | One analysis, no rework; common literal trace. |
| R2 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | Yes | Canonical P2 representative for two analyses. |
| R3 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | Yes | Canonical P2 representative for three analyses. |

R2 and R3 are intentionally not regressions for full P3 equivalence to A. They test the chosen P2 abstraction.

## Negative full-visible traces

“Negative” here means rejected by the simplified model under full visible P3 membership.

| ID | Trace | A | B | Simplified model | Reason for rejection |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| N1 | `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | No | S places its only check after the loop, not before the first rework. |
| N2 | `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Close Case` | Yes | Yes | No | S intentionally removes approval history. |
| N3 | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case` | No | Yes | No | S always records a check; B can schedule its mandatory check after the analysis but still before closure, so this particular word is not a B witness. The negative demonstrates that S does not preserve the B model’s full set of P2 representatives. |

N1 and N2 are unambiguous negative tests against S. N3 should be treated only as a canonicalization negative, not as an A/B counterexample.

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **P1 — operational compliance:** Use **Model A**. It is the only original model that enforces a check for every analysis and preserves the relevant approval/check/rework history. Do not replace it with the dashboard simplification.

- **P2 — executive dashboard:** Use the **simplified model** if it is the sole implementation artifact. It is smaller than both originals and exactly preserves the unbounded dashboard summary. It may replace either original *for that dashboard purpose only*.

- **P3 — bounded testing:** Retain both originals when the purpose is to investigate or regression-test their behavioral difference. The simplified model is not P3-equivalent to either one. If only P1 compliance is required, A is the relevant reference model; retaining B remains useful when diagnosing historical process discrepancies.

- **No single simplification preserves all three views:** preserving per-attempt checks, approval placement, and complete visible traces conflicts with collapsing checks to a Boolean and removing approval history.

Finally, bounded agreement must not be promoted to an unbounded equivalence claim. In this particular case, explicit counterexamples prove unbounded non-equivalence under P1 and P3, while a structural argument proves P2 equivalence. For other models, agreement only through 12 visible events and three analyses would still be evidence—not a proof.
