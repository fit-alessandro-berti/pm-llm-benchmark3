# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

- **Supporting FIFO**:  
  A02 is served before A04 at 08:32 despite A04 having higher priority. A05  A06  A07  A08 from 09:05 onward is exactly arrival order, and A08, the only priority-1 job in that group, is served last.
- **Challenging FIFO**:  
  A03 is served at 08:20 before A02 and A04. This is an actual FIFO violation, but it is plausibly explained by the documented 08:20 appointment. A06’s 09:20 start is also appointment-adjacent, but it is already FIFO for that waiting set, so it does not require an appointment explanation.
- **Undocumented exception**:  
  The only unexplained anomaly is the 08:30–08:32 delay before A02. That looks like operational slack or scan/setup delay, not a queue-selection change.

## QB

- **Supporting FIFO / resume**:  
  B01 resumes at 09:31 and at 10:14 even though B03 and B06 have higher priority. After B01 is finally completed, B04, B06, and B07 run in arrival order. B07’s 10:30 appointment is ignored, which is strong evidence that appointments are not a real scheduling force in QB.
- **Challenging FIFO / resume**:  
  B02 preempts B01 at 09:16, and B05 overtakes B04 at 10:00. Both are priority-1 overrides. B03 is inserted at 09:48 under the label “dispatcher reassignment,” which is not a standard FIFO or priority decision.
- **Known constraint**:  
  Preemption/resumption costs and the “urgent arrival” reason explain B02. The dispatcher reassignment is partially documented and therefore should be treated as a real operational exception, but not as evidence for a pure priority rule.

## QC

- **Supporting SPT-like behavior**:  
  The initial backlog is processed C02(8), C03(14), C04(22) before C01(36), despite C01 being oldest. That is a textbook shortest-processing-time ordering for the initial backlog.
- **Supporting priority/deadline behavior**:  
  C05, the only priority-1 job and the only one with a very urgent deadline, is selected at 10:54. Since its deadline has already passed, both a priority rule and an EDF-type rule predict this choice.
- **Contradicting any single rule**:  
  C06(est 6) arrives at 10:25 and is still waiting at 10:32; SPT would strongly predict C06. At 10:54, SPT still predicts C06, but C05 is chosen. At 11:40, after C06, FIFO predicts C01 and both priority and SPT predict C07; the observed C01 is FIFO, not priority/SPT. No known constraint in the data explains all of these shifts.

## QD

- **Supporting skill-based routing**:  
  D05 requires firmware+electrical and is served by D-F, the only fully qualified resource. D07 requires mechanical+electrical and is served by D-X, the only resource with both of those skills. D-M handles only mechanical jobs.
- **Supporting FIFO per resource**:  
  D-M’s sequence D01  D04  D08  D09 is arrival order. D-F’s sequence D02  D03  D05 is arrival order. D-X’s sequence D06  D07  D10 is arrival order.
- **Contradicting pure global FIFO / priority**:  
  D08, a priority-1 mechanical job, waits behind D04 on D-M; this is FIFO-per-resource but not global priority. D-F is idle from 11:36 to 12:00 even though D10 is waiting and D-F can serve electrical jobs. This may be because D10 was preassigned to D-X or because D-F was blocked before the meeting, but that is not directly visible in the data.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Test 1: QC at 10:32 — was C06 really eligible?

- **Competing explanations**:
  - *A: Continuous SPT*: C06(est 6) should be next at 10:32.
  - *B: Hybrid SPT + priority/deadline/manual override*: C04 is chosen because C06 was not actually ready, or because another undocumented exception applied.
- **Counterfactual**:  
  If C06 had been in the ready queue at 10:32 with a remaining estimate of 6, SPT predicts C06, not C04. If C06 was not yet queue-eligible, the SPT story for the initial backlog remains viable.
- **Discriminating observable**:  
  Record the exact queue-entry / ready-to-start time for each job, not just the arrival scan time. If C06’s ready time is 10:25, SPT is refuted. If its ready time is later than 10:32, the SPT interpretation is rescued.

## Test 2: QD at 11:36 — why was D-F idle with D10 waiting?

- **Competing explanations**:
  - *A: Per-resource FIFO with D10 preassigned to D-X*: D-F’s queue contains only D05, and D05 is not started because it would cross the 12:00 meeting.
  - *B: Global skill-based priority*: D10 is p1, electrical, and D-F is qualified, so D10 should start on D-F immediately and finish before 12:00.
- **Counterfactual**:  
  Under B, D10 starts at 11:36 on D-F and D-X later handles D07 alone. Under A, D-F remains idle and D10 appears later on D-X.
- **Discriminating observable**:  
  Capture the “preferred resource” or “assigned resource” field for D10 at arrival, and a snapshot of D-F’s ready queue at 11:36. If D10 was never in D-F’s queue, A is supported. If D10 was in D-F’s queue but was deliberately deferred, then there is an undocumented priority/blocking rule.

## Test 3: QB after any future p1 interruption

- **Competing explanations**:
  - *A: FIFO/resume with priority-1-only preemption*: after the p1 job finishes, the previously interrupted p3 job resumes before p2 jobs.
  - *B: Full preemptive priority*: after the p1 job finishes, the highest-priority waiting job runs next.
- **Counterfactual**:  
  At 09:31 and 10:14, QD already favored A, but a cleaner test is a fresh p2 job waiting while a partially completed p3 job exists.
- **Discriminating observable**:  
  Log the triggering priority of every preemption and the priority of the job selected after the preempting job completes. If a p2 arrival never preempts a p3 job and p3 consistently resumes first, the rule is FIFO/resume with a narrow priority-1 override.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

These are ranked from most to least plausible as explanations of the observed anomalies.

1. **Unrecorded ready/eligibility times**  
   Jobs may have “arrived” but not been ready for service: missing parts, setup, documentation, or prior station work. This is the best explanation for C06 not being selected at 10:32 and for D10 not being picked up by D-F at 11:36.  
   *Effect on diagnosis*: QC could still be partly SPT, and QD could still be FIFO-per-resource, if the apparent waiting sets are wrong.

2. **Manual dispatcher overrides / undocumented hybrid rules**  
   QB’s 09:48 “dispatcher reassignment” is the clearest example. QC’s C04-at-10:32 choice may also be a manual override.  
   *Effect on diagnosis*: It would mean no stable documented rule can be inferred from these data alone; the correct label may be “hybrid with human judgment,” not a named queue discipline.

3. **Resource blocking around known meetings / breaks**  
   D-F’s idle period before 12:00 and D-X’s idle period before D06’s appointment suggest resources may be reserved or blocked in advance. Starting D05 at 11:36 would cross D-F’s meeting; starting another job on D-X at 11:20 might delay D06’s appointment.  
   *Effect on diagnosis*: These are availability constraints, not queue-ordering policies, so they should be modeled separately from the dispatch rule.

4. **Delayed arrival scans**  
   The prompt allows up to two minutes of scan delay. This can explain small timing ambiguities, such as whether B05 was visible at 09:48. It cannot explain the large QC anomaly, where C06 waits 69 minutes.  
   *Effect on diagnosis*: It shifts eligibility sets by at most two minutes; it is a data-quality limitation, but not a complete alternative explanation.

5. **Noisy or strategically inaccurate estimates**  
   If the dispatcher does not trust `est_min`, then apparent SPT patterns may be coincidental. All the observed service durations in QC happen to equal their estimates, but estimates are only claimed to be visible, not reliable.  
   *Effect on diagnosis*: SPT should be treated as weaker if estimates are known to be untrusted.

# 6. Additional event data needed

A prioritized collection plan should focus on the exact ambiguities: eligibility, decision-time snapshots, preemption semantics, and resource assignment.

**P1 — Highest priority: reconstruct eligibility sets and decision times**  
- `queue_entry_time`: the time a job actually entered the ready queue, separate from `arrival` scan time.  
- `ready_time`: when all preconditions for service were met.  
- `dispatch_decision_time`: the time the dispatcher/scheduler chose the next job.  
- `eligibility_snapshot`: the exact set of jobs considered at each decision.  
- `current_estimated_remaining_time`: updated at each service break, especially for B01.

**P2 — Preemption and appointments**  
- `preemption_trigger`: which event caused each stop, with the triggering job ID and priority.  
- `resume_policy`: whether an interrupted job is automatically placed at the head of the queue.  
- `appointment_window_start/end` and `appointment_hardness`: tolerance windows, not just nominal times.  
- `appointment_lateness_tolerance`: whether A06/B07 type late starts are accepted and how late is too late.

**P3 — Resource assignment and policy version**  
- `preferred_resource` or `assigned_resource` for each job.  
- `resource_state`: available, blocked, reserved, meeting, calibration, or idle-but-reserved.  
- `blocked_before_known_absence`: a flag to capture meeting/break avoidance.  
- `policy_version` or `rule_id`: the exact scheduler/dispatcher algorithm in use.  
- `manual_override_flag`: whether a human changed the scheduler choice, and the reason code.

These fields would directly discriminate between the competing explanations in Sections 4 and 5.

---

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue: nearly every contested decision is explained by FIFO, with appointments as a narrow overlay. QD is reasonably identifiable once skill-based routing is acknowledged, but the per-resource assignment logic remains partially hidden.

QC is the least identifiable queue. Its initial backlog has a strong SPT signature, but the C06 delay and the late C01-before-C07 choice prevent any single standard rule from fitting. The data are consistent with an undocumented hybrid or with unrecorded eligibility constraints, but not with a clean FIFO, priority, SPT, or deadline policy.

The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone is **SPT for QC**. The first few decisions make SPT look attractive, but the dynamic arrival of C06 and the later FIFO-like cleanup show that an SPT inference would mispredict too many future decisions. A close second is **priority scheduling for QB**, because the priority-1 preemptions are real but do not generalize to a full priority scheduler; the queue otherwise behaves as FIFO with resumption.
