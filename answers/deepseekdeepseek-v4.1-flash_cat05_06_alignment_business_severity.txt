## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is a business judgment about control objective, exposure, reversibility, timing, customer/regulatory harm, recurrence, evidence reliability, and model scope. Confidence is separate: it is how strongly the available evidence supports that judgment. A case can be severity 5 with medium confidence if the potential harm is huge but the missing event may reflect a logging gap; conversely, a case can be severity 1 with high confidence if the deviation is clearly harmless telemetry.

| Severity | Meaning | Typical indicators |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Harmless technical/model noise | UI telemetry, retry duplicate, hidden tau, label synonym, out-of-scope non-payment branch |
| 2 | Documentation/evidence or scope issue | Low financial exposure, activity likely occurred externally, weak linkage, model scope mismatch |
| 3 | Moderate control/evidence gap | Missing core evidence for required assessment/notification, high amount but other controls present |
| 4 | Serious control failure | Policy-overlay auto-approval violation, recent bank change, medium/high amount, possible fraud exposure |
| 5 | Severe control failure | Pay-before-approval, missing fraud screen for high-risk/high-value/bank-change claim, large irreversible payment, regulatory exposure |

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is edit distance under a chosen cost function. It is not compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or confidence in evidence. The supplied cost function charges 1 for every visible log/model move, which means a harmless UI event and a missing fraud screen can have similar cost.

- CL-601: raw cost 3 comes from `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, and `Close Workbench`, which P5 explicitly says are not business activities. The cost is technical noise.
- CL-602: raw cost 1 is only `M(Fraud Screen)`, but the business context is EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, and recent bank change. The missing fraud evidence is a severe control failure that cost 1 cannot express.
- CL-604: raw cost 3 includes move/order issues, but the business event is payment at 10:03 and approval at 10:20. Pay-before-approval is a severe P3 failure even if edit distance is moderate.
- CL-609 and CL-610: raw cost 0 because the model path is synchronous. But both violate P1/P2: CL-609 auto-approved EUR 18,000 MEDIUM risk, and CL-610 auto-approved after a bank change one hour earlier. Zero technical cost, high policy risk.
- CL-606: raw cost 1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, but an external adjuster report exists. Missing core event is not proof the activity did not occur. The risk is evidence-linkage quality, not necessarily missing assessment.
- CL-608: raw cost 1 for missing core Notify, but CRM/portal evidence exists at +3 hours. The real issue is whether the mutable link is reliable under P4.
- CL-607 and CL-612: raw cost 4 each because the model lacks denial/withdrawal branches. The missing payable activities are model-scope artifacts, not operational failures, because no payment was intended.
- CL-611: raw cost 2 because `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss` is a label mismatch. The business activity likely occurred; the cost reflects taxonomy, not control risk.
- CL-605: raw cost 0 includes a `T(tau)` silent move. Silent moves are fitting and should not be treated as deviations.
- CL-603: raw cost 1 is a retry duplicate with the same message ID. It is connector noise, not duplicate business notification.
- Irreversibility: pay-before-approval for CL-604 is harder to undo than a missing notification in CL-608, yet both can have low raw cost. Cost functions that weight all visible moves equally cannot capture this.
