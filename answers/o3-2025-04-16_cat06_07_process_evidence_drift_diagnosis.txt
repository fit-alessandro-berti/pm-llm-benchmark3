## 2. Competing explanations and timeline  
Jan-Mar – Baseline. Stable metrics, full label maturity, low unknown-activity share. Model well-calibrated and discriminative.

April – Single-month schema/pipeline incident. The workflow migration renames “Initial Diagnosis Completed” to “Diagnostic Assessment Completed”. Prefixes are constructed on the wrong event or omitted, many events labelled UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY, cached “events-before-diagnosis” feature stale. Prediction distribution shifts upward (mean 0.28), AUROC falls to 0.67 although process and breach rate unchanged. Variant mix shows 18 % “unmapped” – artefact, not real process change. Once patch deployed 6 May, metrics rebound (mean 0.21, AUROC 0.78). Therefore April deterioration  data-pipeline failure, not concept.

May – Recovery month. AUROC still 0.78 (slightly worse than pre-Apr) because missing cached feature not fully backfilled. Otherwise resembles Jan-Mar. Good reference month for re-training after repair.

June – Real routing/process change. New remote-routing policy jumps V2 share to 52 %. Observed breach rate drops to 0.15 because V2 path is fastest and low-risk. Model AUROC 0.79 (small drop) but Brier 0.139 > baseline; calibration worsens (ECE 0.06). These are classic covariate drift / process drift: P(X) changes, P(Y|X) largely unchanged, no evidence of concept drift. Important to recalibrate but not blame pipeline.

July – Supplier delay starts. Edge Order PartPart Received median 65 h (+55 %). V3 breach rate leaps to 0.46. Overall breach rate returns to 0.19 (label maturity 98 %). Model AUROC slides to 0.76. Early warning of V3-specific concept/covariate drift, still masked in aggregate by V2 volume.

August – Supplier crisis peaks, V3 share 35 %, P-edge 130 h. Overall breach 0.28, AUROC 0.68, calibration poor (ECE 0.12). Both covariate and concept drift: more cases in path with new causal mechanism (long part wait) plus inside-V3 breach probability higher than predicted. Data quality looks fine.

September – Partner Z arrives; label maturity 82 %. Population shift mixes with ongoing supplier issue (V3 42 %). AUROC tumbles to 0.66, ECE 0.14. Observed breach 0.31 but still immature. Attribution now triple-fold: (1) more V3, (2) longer delays, (3) new partner with rural logistics unseen by model. Must not conflate with concept drift until controlled for features.

October – Heavy censoring. Label maturity only 55 %, median delay 52 d. Observed breach falls to 0.16 (illusory improvement). AUROC 0.64. Cannot diagnose real performance until backlog cleared. Act only on data-quality front.

Hence, treating Apr, Jun, Aug-Sep, Oct as the same failure would mix: (1) pipeline bug; (2) intended process change; (3) exogenous supplier + population shifts driving covariate+concept drift; (4) label delay.

## 3. Diagnostic tests  
1. Label-maturity curves & fixed-vintage backtesting  
   • Plot cumulative label arrival for Jan-Oct cohorts.<br>   – If Oct weakness is due to censoring, AUROC and breach for Oct should converge to earlier months within 90 d.  
   – If true concept drift, poor metrics persist after labels mature.

2. Re-compute April features after activity-label normalisation + duplicate removal  
   – If metrics jump back to baseline, confirms pipeline bug.<br>   – If AUROC still low, then hidden concept drift existed.

3. Calibration & discrimination stratified by (variant, partner, month)  
   – Under pure covariate drift, AUROC within each variant remains high; global drop driven by mix.<br>   – Under concept drift, AUROC inside V3 will also deteriorate after Aug.

4. Compare DFG and performance-DFG edges month-over-month  
   – Hypothesis: supplier delay lengthens only V3 edge; other edges stable.<br>   – Falsified if non-part edges also lengthen.

5. Test P(Y|path) stability  
   – Condition on exact variant and discretised part-wait buckets; compute breach rate per month.<br>   – If still higher in Aug-Sep, concept drift; if equal, change fully explained by covariates.

6. Population transport check for Partner Z  
   – Importance-weighted AUROC: re-weight Sep data to Jan-Aug partner distribution.<br>   – If AUROC improves, performance loss due to population shift; else concept drift.

7. Feature missingness & lineage audits  
   – Compare “events-before-diagnosis” cached feature Apr vs May (backfilled vs not).<br>   – Expect spike in nulls April only; any other spike  wider pipeline issues.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision  
Immediate containment (next week)  
• Do not retrain yet; freeze training data at 31 Aug 2026 where labels 93 % mature and pipeline OK.  
• Patch online probability outputs with Platt/temperature scaling using Jun-Aug validated labels, stratified by variant (V1/V2/V3).

Short-term (when April back-fill and outcome backlog cleared)  
• Re-export April features and overwrite duplicate events; re-run training including April and May once clean.  
• Fit separate calibration layers per variant or add variant indicator + interaction.

Feature repair (parallel)  
• Add engineered features: part-supplier lead-time at prediction time, rural-distance, device-age, partner code.  
• Implement robust activity mapping to accept both old and new labels.

Full retraining (Q1-2027)  
• Eligible training window: Jan-Aug 2026 after re-processing; exclude Sep-Oct until labels final.  
• Use sample re-weighting so under-represented V3 July-Aug failures are adequately learnt.  
• Optionally deliver dual-model: generic model plus V3-specialist sub-model.

Segment-specific justification  
• V3 has fundamentally different causal chain and now dominates breaches; dedicated head or multi-task network advisable.  
• Partner-specific model not yet justified—sample size small and confounded with V3; wait for more data.

## 5. Monitoring architecture  
Layers  
1. Data-quality monitors  
   – Schema mapping validity, unknown-activity share, duplicate ratio, feature null rate.  
2. Prediction monitors  
   – Mean score, score quantiles, feature drift (e.g., Kolmogorov) versus training.  
3. Calibration monitors (requires mature labels)  
   – Rolling AUROC, Brier, ECE with 60-day lag; variant-stratified.  
4. Label-maturity monitor  
   – For each cohort compute % labels resolved over time; raises “yellow” flag that blocks automatic retraining triggers.  
5. Process-variant monitor  
   – Monthly variant distribution; alert on >5 pp delta.  
6. Performance-DFG monitor  
   – Median edge times per variant; alert when any 14-day moving median exceeds historic 95 th percentile.  
7. Population monitor  
   – Partner, geography, device-age distribution; covariate-shift detectors (e.g., PSI).  
8. Intervention monitor  
   – Track how many cases received escalations because of prediction; feed to cost-benefit.

Alert dependency graph  
• Data-quality red  suppress retraining & calibration alerts.  
• Label-maturity yellow  suppress concept-drift alarms but keep data-quality alarms.  
• Process-variant alert  triggers covariate-drift investigation, not automatic model rebuild.

Python sketch:

```python
import pandas as pd
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import factory as con_log_factory
from pm4py.algo.filtering.pandas.timestamp import timestamp_filter
from pm4py.statistics.variants.pandas import get as variants_get
from pm4py import discover_dfg, discover_performance_dfg, format_dataframe

def monthly_summaries(path_csv, start='2026-01-01', end='2026-10-31'):
    df = pd.read_csv(path_csv, parse_dates=['timestamp'])
    
    # Normalise renamed activity
    df['activity'] = df['activity'].replace({
        'Diagnostic Assessment Completed': 'Initial Diagnosis Completed'
    })
    
    # Drop duplicate events (same case, activity, timestamp)
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=['case_id', 'activity', 'timestamp'])
    
    # PM4Py formatting
    df = format_dataframe(df, case_id='case_id', activity_key='activity', timestamp_key='timestamp')
    
    months = pd.date_range(start, end, freq='MS')
    results = []
    
    for month_start in months:
        month_end = month_start + pd.offsets.MonthEnd()
        slice_df = timestamp_filter.filter_timestamp(df, month_start, month_end)
        
        if slice_df.empty:
            continue
        
        # Discover DFGs
        dfg = discover_dfg(slice_df)
        perf_dfg = discover_performance_dfg(slice_df)
        variants_count = variants_get.get_variants_df(slice_df)
        
        res = {
            'month': month_start.strftime('%Y-%m'),
            'cases_started': slice_df['case_id'].nunique(),
            'unknown_activity_pct': (slice_df['activity'] == 'UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY').mean()*100,
            'median_part_wait_h': perf_dfg.get(('Order Part', 'Part Received'), pd.Timedelta(0)).total_seconds()/3600 if ('Order Part', 'Part Received') in perf_dfg else None,
            'variant_top5': dict(list(variants_count.items())[:5])
        }
        results.append(res)
    
    return pd.DataFrame(results)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    summary = monthly_summaries('field_service_events.csv')
    print(summary)
```

The function creates calendar-month slices (event perspective) yet can be extended to case-start cohorts by filtering on each case’s first event date. It applies activity normalisation and duplicate removal before discovery, providing auditable KPI outputs.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis  
1. Retraining on immature labels (Sep-Oct).  
   • Failure mode: model learns that extremely long part waits seldom breach (due to censored outcomes).  
   • Mitigation: enforce label-maturity threshold; use only cohorts with 95 % maturity.

2. Calibrating on April pipeline bug.  
   • Failure mode: calibration layer will compress scores upward to match inflated April risks, harming later months.  
   • Mitigation: exclude months with known schema faults from calibration data; rerun after feature fix.

3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift.  
   • Failure mode: rely solely on covariate-shift reweighting; miss within-V3 risk rise; under-alert high-risk customers.  
   • Mitigation: perform conditional drift tests; add dynamic features (supplier lead time) and schedule focused retraining.

4. Attributing Partner Z issues to unfair model bias without evidence.  
   • Failure mode: create partner-specific override that masks systemic rural logistic delays, inflating cost.  
   • Mitigation: first test performance conditional on variant and distance; if calibrated, address operationally (add stock depots) instead of model patch.

5. Over-reacting to label backlog by switching off model.  
   • Mitigation: label-maturity monitor with graduated alerts; continue using predictions but flag uncertainty in dashboards.

By disentangling pipeline, process, population, covariate, and concept factors, the organisation can take proportionate, evidence-based actions rather than blanket model replacement.
