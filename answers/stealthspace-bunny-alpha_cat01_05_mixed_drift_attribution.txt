## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-fitness fall, from 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March, is largely a provenance and semantic break: mapping produces an immediate 35-percentage-point recovery, while March median cycle time remains near the January–February level. Across March–December, mapping adds 32–40 points each month, averaging 35.4 points, while mapped fitness remains 0.86–0.91. This strongly implicates migration in raw-model fitness; it does **not** prove that all subsequent differences are artificial.

A label crosswalk can restore conformance merely by restoring the expected activity name. It cannot establish that `Eligibility Screen` performed identical validation, that technical events represented business work, or that missing resources did not affect routing. Duplicated `Open Case` events can create spurious edges, inflate activity/edge counts, alter variant frequencies, and distort resource or waiting analyses. They need not change elapsed cycle time measured from stable business start/end timestamps. Missing resource IDs impair allocation, team, workload, and resource-aware analyses; their concentration in migrated activities makes missingness potentially informative rather than harmless random loss.

After May, normalized request-info rates rise from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases, while median and p90 durations increase. Those operational summaries are not explained by vocabulary replacement alone. Assuming valid business timestamps and the stated operational rework definition, supplier-related additional work and congestion are substantive. What remains uncertain is their separate causal shares.

- The largest **case-mix** change occurs in October–November, whereas the largest **supplier-exposure** change occurs in May–July.
- The largest **fragment distance** occurs in October, but that depends on the selected relations. Rework and senior-assessment relations need not move in the same direction.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

For the seven supplied relation rates, define:

\[
D_{\text{avg},m}
=\frac{1}{7}\sum_{j=1}^{7}|r_{mj}-r_{\text{Jan},j}|
\]

where each rate is occurrences per 100 cases. This is an **unweighted mean absolute difference across relation types**, not an edit distance or a complete-DFG divergence score.

As a distribution-sensitive companion, define:

\[
TV_{1/2,m}
=\frac{\sum_j|r_{mj}-r_{\text{Jan},j}|}{2\sum_j r_{\text{Jan},j}}
\]

This is total variation between two seven-component normalized rate vectors.

| month | absolute-rate sum | \(D_{\text{avg}}\), relation-rate points | \(TV_{1/2}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar | 3 | 0.43 | 0.0037 |
| May | 40 | 5.71 | 0.0480 |
| Jul | 59 | 8.43 | 0.0699 |
| Sep | 28 | 4.00 | 0.0339 |
| Oct | 64 | 9.14 | 0.0742 |
| Dec | 36 | 5.14 | 0.0428 |

Interpretation and assumptions:

- All monthly rates must use the same case denominator, event boundaries, and population.
- `Assess/SeniorAssessment->Decision` combines two source relations; its internal split cannot be analyzed.
- Omitted edges are **unknown, not zero**. A complete DFG could show a large change invisible in these seven relations.
- Equal weighting is convenient but not a process-frequency weighting. For example, a common `Register->Eligibility` edge receives the same weight as a rarer rework edge.
- These fragments cannot distinguish a new route from changed case composition unless rates are subsequently segmented by complexity, supplier, and priority.
- The March distance is small despite the raw-fitness collapse, illustrating why raw conformance and normalized control-flow distance answer different questions.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Use January–July/August as the clearest comparison for the queueing diagnosis. The comparison is partly hypothetical rather than an identified causal decomposition.

| component | Aggregate evidence | Defensible interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Case mix** | High-complexity share rose from 21% to 29% between January and August; monthly volume rose from 620 to 705. Supplier exposure changed substantially. | Important population pressure, especially alongside renewal, but these percentages cannot be converted into hours. “High complexity” and supplier share are incomplete descriptions of case mix. |
| **Control-flow/rework** | Operational rework rose from 12% to 27%; normalized `Assess->RequestInfo` rose from 12 to 29 per 100 cases by July. | Adds document collection, reassessment, and intervening queue exposure. A rate of 29 request edges per 100 cases is not necessarily 29% of cases if cases can repeat the relation. |
| **Service** | Median `Assess` duration changed only from about 4.8 to 5.4 hours, a 0.6-hour or 12.5% increase. | A real but comparatively small observed service change. It does not explain the 13.7-hour, 42.9% increase in total median cycle time between January and August. |
| **Queueing** | Reconstructed pre-assessment waiting increased much more than service time. Median/p90 total cycle time rose 42.9%/59.4% by August. | Strongest aggregate performance mechanism. It includes staffing capacity, arrival load, rework, and priority routing, but their separate shares are not identified. |

Under an explicitly illustrative **additive** assumption, the measured 0.6-hour service increase would leave 13.1 hours of the August median increase for other components. This is **not a statistical bound**: medians are not additive, and counterfactual service, waiting, and rework may change together. The p90 service change is also unknown and must not be assumed to be 0.6 hours.

A defensible event-level decomposition would use aligned case durations and fixed maturity/censoring rules, then standardize or replay cases over common:

- complexity and supplier strata;
- rework counts and routes;
- `Assess` service distributions;
- staffing/FTE availability and arrival load;
- priority treatment.

Report standardized median and p90 changes, with case-level bootstrap intervals and interaction terms or a Shapley-style allocation. Priority redistribution can lower high-complexity waiting while increasing standard-case waiting, so a single aggregate waiting residual is not itself a staffing effect.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

These are diagnostic candidates, not formally estimated change points. Monthly data generally identify a broad transition interval, not an exact day.

| artifact family | candidate boundary | evidence | interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|

| Behavioral/routing drift | **Jul 1** | Documented priority rule; senior-assessment routing later becomes prominent. | Strong event timing, but aggregate rework was already increasing before this date. |
| Performance drift | **May 5** | First conspicuous monthly increase in median and p90 duration. | Candidate onset of the performance deterioration, not proof of an instantaneous jump. |
| Performance drift | **Jun 15** | Staffing loss occurs during continued median/p90 deterioration; later evidence emphasizes waiting rather than service. | Plausible acceleration or change in the delay-generating mechanism. |
| Performance drift | **Oct 1** | Renewal-associated volume/complexity increase and further tail deterioration. | Strong seasonal candidate, confounded with existing backlog and queue policy. |

A transparent screening rule can compare the first two post-boundary months with preceding observations, using metric-specific floors. Consecutive flagged boundaries may represent one gradual transition rather than several independent causes.

The evidence does **not** support one global change point. March explains the raw-conformance discontinuity; May–June better explain rework and performance; July marks a routing-policy intervention; October marks seasonal population pressure; December marks partial recovery. These mechanisms should have separate monitored boundaries and may recur at different times.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary case cohort | Stable case ID; business start; close/administrative cutoff; admission cohort; completion status. | Maintain a representative denominator and handle open cases explicitly. | Retain every registered case, including rejects, incomplete bundles, and pending cases. | Data-quality provenance | Migration exposure, label version, duplicate-evidence status, timestamp completeness. | Separate analytical invalidity from operational drift. | Apply to the full cohort; report completeness by activity and segment rather than dropping cases. | Raw/mapped comparisons, complete-case sensitivity, and provenance alerts. | Excluding bad records without reporting them can turn measurement drift into apparent business improvement. |

The **primary monitoring population should be all registered business cases**, not only cases reaching `Assess`, completing review, or receiving an outcome. Use `Register` as the stable business start, remove only verified duplicate technical events from the analytical cycle definition, and retain rejects and incomplete cases.

Use a fixed outcome-maturity and censoring policy—for example, sufficiently mature admission cohorts for complete-cycle quantiles plus survival estimates for pending cases. Otherwise, December’s shorter observation exposure and unresolved backlog can look like improvement. Unknown complexity or supplier should be reported as a data-quality state, not silently imputed into a favorable category.

**Seasonality and multiple testing.** Do not learn a “normal” renewal pattern from a single year. Use October–November 2026 as a provisional seasonal reference, then replace it with same-calendar-period estimates from later years, preferably using robust medians/MADs or a hierarchical model. Freeze model and mapping versions before evaluation.

Apply hierarchical gates: first validate schema and provenance; then test process behavior conditional on case mix; then inspect operations and outcomes. Control false-discovery rate within related monitor families, require a minimum affected-case count, and use case-bootstrap intervals. Consecutive alerts often represent one transition, not several mechanisms. Compare an intervention with both its pre-period and a load-adjusted counterfactual so seasonal decline is not mislabeled as permanent process improvement.

def fragment_distance(rate_table, reference_month, relation_columns):
    indexed = rate_table.set_index("month")[relation_columns].astype(float)
    ref_date = pd.Timestamp(reference_month)
    if ref_date not in indexed.index:
        raise ValueError("Reference month is absent from the fragment table.")
    if indexed.isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Fragment distances do not accept missing relations.")

    reference = indexed.loc[ref_date]
    absolute = indexed.sub(reference, axis=1).abs()

    # These are occurrences per 100 cases, not a probability distribution.
    mean_absolute = absolute.mean(axis=1)
    total_variation = absolute.sum(axis=1) / (2.0 * reference.sum())
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "absolute_rate_sum": absolute.sum(axis=1),
        "mean_abs_relation_rate_points": mean_absolute,
        "normalized_l1_half": total_variation
    })

def candidate_boundaries(data, baseline_n=2, after_n=2):
    """
    Explainable screening rule, not a formal change-point estimator.

    For each boundary b:
      before = rows [baseline_n, ..., b-1]
      after  = rows [b, ..., b+after_n-1]

    Compare their means using a metric-specific absolute or relative floor.
    Multiple consecutive boundaries can describe one gradual transition.
    """
    specifications = {
        "cases": ("relative", 0.05),
        "high_complexity_pct": ("absolute", 3.0),
        "supplier_z_pct": ("absolute", 5.0),
        "fast_variant_pct": ("absolute", 3.0),
        "rework_variant_pct": ("absolute", 3.0),
        "median_cycle_h": ("absolute", 3.0),
        "p90_cycle_h": ("absolute", 5.0),
        "raw_model_fitness": ("absolute", 0.02),
        "mapped_model_fitness": ("absolute", 0.02),
        "missing_resource_pct": ("absolute", 1.0),
        "duplicate_open_case_pct": ("absolute", 1.0),
    }

    found = []
    for metric, (kind, floor) in specifications.items():
        for boundary in range(baseline_n, len(data) - after_n + 1):
            before = data[metric].iloc[:boundary]
            after = data[metric].iloc[boundary:boundary + after_n]

            before_mean = float(before.mean())
            after_mean = float(after.mean())
            if kind == "relative":
                if before_mean == 0:
                    continue
                difference = (after_mean - before_mean) / abs(before_mean)
            else:
                difference = after_mean - before_mean

            if abs(difference) >= floor:
                found.append({
                    "candidate_month": data["month"].iloc[boundary],
                    "metric": metric,
                    "before_mean": before_mean,
                    "after_mean": after_mean,
                    "relative_change": (
                        difference if kind == "relative" else np.nan
                    ),
                    "absolute_or_relative_floor": floor
                })
    return pd.DataFrame(found)

def _require_pm4py():

def _utc_timestamp(value):
    # Production must first decide the business timezone and DST policy.
    timestamp = pd.to_datetime(value, utc=True)
    if pd.isna(timestamp):
        raise ValueError("Invalid time-window boundary.")
    return pd.Timestamp(timestamp)

def format_event_log(
    events,
    label_map=None,
    verified_duplicate_row_ids=None
):
    """
    Return a NEW PM4Py EventLog. Never mutate the caller's dataframe.

    label_map:
        Explicit activity crosswalk, e.g.
        {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}.

    verified_duplicate_row_ids:
        Index values in the ORIGINAL dataframe that have already been
        verified as duplicate technical events. No label-only deletion occurs.
    """
    _require_pm4py()

    if not isinstance(events, pd.DataFrame):
        raise TypeError("events must be a pandas DataFrame.")
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
    missing = required.difference(events.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing event columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    if events.empty:
        raise ValueError("The event log is empty.")
    if events[list(required)].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Missing case ID, activity, or timestamp.")

    work = events.copy(deep=True)
    work["_source_row_id"] = list(work.index)
    work["activity"] = work["activity"].astype(str).str.strip()

    if label_map is not None:
        clean_map = {
            str(key).strip(): str(value).strip()
            for key, value in label_map.items()
        }
        # Unmapped activities remain visible rather than being silently dropped.
        work["activity"] = work["activity"].map(
            lambda x: clean_map.get(x, x)
        )

    if verified_duplicate_row_ids is not None:
        duplicate_ids = {
            str(x) for x in verified_duplicate_row_ids
        }
        before = len(work)
        work = work.loc[
            ~work["_source_row_id"].astype(str).isin(duplicate_ids)
        ].copy()
        if len(work) == before:
            raise ValueError("No supplied duplicate row IDs matched the log.")

    kwargs = {
        "case_id": "case_id",
        "activity": "activity",
        "timestamp": "timestamp",
        "concept": "activity",
    }
    if "resource" in work.columns:
        kwargs["resource"] = "resource"

    return pm4py.format_dataframe(work, **kwargs)

def compare_raw_and_normalized(
    events,
    label_map,
    verified_duplicate_row_ids=None
):
    """
    Build separate raw and normalized PM4Py logs.
    The original dataframe and the raw PM4Py log remain unchanged.
    """
    raw_log = format_event_log(events)
    normalized_log = format_event_log(
        events,
        label_map=label_map,
        verified_duplicate_row_ids=verified_duplicate_row_ids
    )
    return {"raw": raw_log, "normalized": normalized_log}

def slice_event_log(event_log, start, end):
    """
    Slice a PM4Py EventLog using a documented half-open interval:
        start <= timestamp < end
    """
    _require_pm4py()
    start_ts = _utc_timestamp(start)
    end_ts = _utc_timestamp(end)
    if start_ts >= end_ts:
        raise ValueError("start must be earlier than end.")
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        event_log, start=start_ts, end=end_ts
    )

def discover_monthly_artifacts(
    event_log,
    temporal_variant=None,
    include_skeleton=False
):
    """
    Compute DFGs, variants, variant frequencies, and temporal profiles
    for each calendar month present in event_log.

    temporal_variant:
        Optional activity sequence for a targeted temporal profile.
        None computes the month's overall temporal profile.
    """
    _require_pm4py()

    timestamps = pd.to_datetime(event_log["timestamp"], utc=True)
    periods = timestamps.dt.to_period("M")
    artifacts = {}

    for period in sorted(periods.unique()):
        start = pd.Timestamp(f"{period}-01", tz="UTC")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        month_log = slice_event_log(event_log, start, end)

        dfg, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(month_log)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(month_log)
        variant_counts = Counter(tuple(v) for v in variants)

        # PM4Py's str temporal-profile API returns the profile dictionary.
        temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
            month_log,
            variant=temporal_variant,
            format="str"
        )

        result = {
            "dfg": dfg,
            "variants": variants,
            "variant_counts": variant_counts,
            "temporal_profile": temporal_profile
        }

        if include_skeleton:
            # Optional log-skeleton discovery; do not overinterpret its
            # top-k relationships as strict control-flow constraints.
            result["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(month_log)

        artifacts[str(period)] = result

    return artifacts

def compare_monthly_raw_normalized(
    events,
    label_map,
    verified_duplicate_row_ids=None,
    temporal_variant=None
):
    """
    Convenience wrapper preserving both versions of every monthly artifact.
    No event-log overwrite occurs.
    """
    logs = compare_raw_and_normalized(
        events,
        label_map=label_map,
        verified_duplicate_row_ids=verified_duplicate_row_ids
    )
    return {
        "raw": discover_monthly_artifacts(
            logs["raw"], temporal_variant=temporal_variant
        ),
        "normalized": discover_monthly_artifacts(
            logs["normalized"], temporal_variant=temporal_variant
        )
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

**High confidence:** The March raw-fitness collapse is primarily logging and provenance drift. The documented migration, label replacement, duplicate technical events, missing identifiers, and immediate 35-point mapping recovery align closely. The absence of a corresponding March duration jump further supports this interpretation. This does not imply that the migrated process was identical, nor does it explain the later deterioration.

**Moderate-to-high confidence:** Supplier disruption substantially increased rework beginning in May. Supplier Z exposure rose abruptly, rework and normalized request-info edges increased, and incomplete bundles provide a direct mechanism. Supplier selection and pre-existing complexity prevent a clean supplier-only causal estimate. The May increase also precedes the staffing loss, so it cannot all be attributed to June departures.

**Moderate confidence:** Staff loss amplified queueing after June 15. The strongest evidence is the contrast between modestly increased `Assess` service duration and much larger reconstructed waiting. However, supplier-induced rework was already generating additional arrivals, and actual assessor hours and queue order are unavailable. Waiting is therefore a mediator of the broader disruption, not an independently measured staffing effect.

**High confidence, limited attribution:** July’s priority rule changed routing. It may have benefited high-complexity cases while delaying standard cases. Aggregate duration cannot reveal that redistribution. Likewise, October–November is strongly consistent with seasonal population pressure, but its effect on delay is confounded by backlog, complexity, and queue policy.

**Only plausible:** Some residual control-flow change is persistent. Normalized rework and route rates do not fully return to January levels after supplier and seasonal exposure recede. This could reflect real adaptation or incomplete normalization of senior-assessment and other paths. The supplied fragments cannot distinguish them.

December improvement has **moderate attribution confidence**: lower volume, lower complexity, and fewer supplier-affected cases coincide with overtime and the quality campaign. Because the month began before intervention, neither action receives a causal share.

I would revise this account with case-level, provenance-complete logs; actual priority and queue-order timestamps; assessor capacity calendars; bundle-defect measures; stable pre/post complexity definitions; and multiple renewal seasons. Supplier-stratified waiting, matched complexity comparisons, and intervention-versus-load counterfactuals could materially change the decomposition.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF mapped fitness remains within its reference band but raw fitness falls by at least 0.03 and an approved-label or duplicate-event discrepancy affects at least 20 cases, THEN quarantine the raw conformance result and repair the provenance crosswalk before interpreting process drift, BECAUSE semantic and technical-event changes can break token-based conformance without changing business work.`

2. `IF more than 1% of cases in a reporting window have invalid event ordering or lifecycle boundaries and at least 20 cases are affected, THEN suspend cycle-time and conformance alerts for the affected segment while investigating connectors, clocks, and lifecycle mappings, BECAUSE invalid temporal observations can create artificial performance and control-flow drift.`

3. `IF the request-info probability rises by at least 5 percentage points within a stable supplier-complexity stratum and at least 20 cases are affected, THEN inspect bundle completeness and repeated-assessment routes before attributing the increase to assessor behavior, BECAUSE incomplete documents directly generate document-recovery loops.`

4. `IF reconstructed assessment waiting increases by at least two hours or 25% for two consecutive weeks while service duration remains within its reference band, THEN investigate assessor capacity, arrivals, and backlog and rebalance work where operationally safe, BECAUSE the evidence points to queueing rather than slower assessment execution.`

5. `IF high-complexity waiting falls while standard-case waiting rises after the priority date without a corresponding increase in arrivals, THEN audit the prioritization rule and report both classes separately rather than declaring an overall improvement, BECAUSE priority routing can redistribute delay rather than remove it.`

6. `IF high-complexity share or arrivals move outside the expected seasonal band by at least 5 percentage points or 20%, respectively, THEN compare within-stratum process rates and reweight operational forecasts without automatically relabeling the change as process drift, BECAUSE renewal can change the monitored population while underlying behavior remains stable.`

7. `IF an outcome-rate change of at least 5 percentage points persists outside its risk-adjusted reference band and passes schema, maturity, and missingness checks, THEN investigate eligibility policy, document quality, and routing separately from conformance, BECAUSE a process can remain conformant while its decisions or case outcomes change for legitimate or unintended reasons.`
