Section 2: Equivalence and Sufficiency Test. Define structured test for fully equivalent, partially equivalent, merely mitigating, ineffective. Include timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance. Explain why “same expected loss” not only standard.
Need create criteria.
Maybe test:
- Trigger: FEP violation or risk event? For completed payment with same-user, compensating package must address root cause: one person can cause irrevocable loss without independent check.
- Timing: control must act before the point of no return (bank settlement) for preventive equivalence; after release but before settlement is hybrid; after settlement only detective/corrective if recall possible.
- Independence: human/system control must be independent of actor and free of COI; shared credentials/workstation/organizational cell undermine.
- Coverage: control must cover the specific threat: data validity, commercial legitimacy, bank ownership, collusion, override, emergency authorization.
- Overrideability: if control can be overridden by same actor or non-independent manager, equivalence reduced; override must be independently authorized and logged.
- Recoverability: if control is detective, must be within recall window and have ability to stop/recover; late audit reduces.
- Evidence quality: clean pass, reviewed documents, signed checklists, timestamps, token state. Unsigned/unavailable = weak.
- Collusion resistance: two-person nominal separation insufficient if pair is coordinated, shared workstation, repeated pair, no manager oversight; need independent third party or system checks.
- Proportionality: amount, vendor risk, bank change, emergency, loss exposure. But not sole.
Classification:
Fully equivalent: all critical risk dimensions addressed before settlement by independent, non-overrideable (or independently overridden) controls; evidence high; recall not required; collusion resistance adequate.
Partially equivalent: some critical dimensions addressed, but timing after release, weak independence, incomplete coverage, or reliance on late detective; risk reduced but residual material.
Merely mitigating: control reduces probability/impact but does not address core one-person authorization; e.g. low amount, AV pass, no independent pre-settlement approval, late audit.
Ineffective: control absent, overridden by same actor, after settlement, no recoverability, or evidence contradicts (fraud).
Why same expected loss not only: control objectives include assurance, segregation, deterrence, accountability, recovery, regulatory/audit, prevention of collusion, and timely stop. Two packages with same expected loss can differ in timing, independence, evidence, ability to stop, and organizational signal. Also expected loss uncertain; controls must be robust to tail risk and fraud.

## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not merely a “different user” rule. Its control objective is to prevent one person from being able to initiate and authorize an erroneous or fraudulent transfer without an effective independent check. The same nominal violation can therefore have very different control significance depending on timing, independence, coverage, recoverability, and case risk.

Legend: **P** = prevent, **D** = detect, **C** = correct, **F** = fail to address or insufficient by itself.

| Control objective / threat scenario | FEP-1 | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Single-operator data error: wrong vendor, wrong amount, duplicate invoice, invalid PO | **P** by separating preparation and release. | **P** for duplicate, sanctions, bank-account ownership, recent bank change, operator limit, basic PO match. **F** for all other data/commercial errors. | **P** if independent manager reviews invoice, bank details, receipt before settlement. **D** if after release but before settlement. | **D** after release; **C** only if recall/recovery is still possible. | **P** as a bounded permission only if all conditions hold; **F** if conditions are missing. |
| 2. Single-operator fraud: fake vendor, compromised account, manipulated bank details, unauthorized transfer | **P** by preventing one person from both preparing and releasing. | **P/D** for sanctions, bank change, ownership, limit, duplicate. **F** for commercial reasonableness, collusion, compromised-account control, or fabricated goods receipt. | **P** if independent, conflict-free, and genuinely reviews supporting evidence before settlement. **F** if rubber-stamped, conflicted, or after settlement. | **D** after the fact; **C** only within recall/recovery window. | Reduces risk but does **not** remove the objective. **F** if bank change, new vendor, AV override, late manager approval, or late audit exists. |
| 3. Collusion between preparer and releaser, or between preparer/releaser and manager | Nominal **P** only if the two humans are truly independent. **F** if they act as a collusive pair or share workstation/credential. | **F** for collusion; AV checks data fields, not intent or coordination. | **P** only if manager is a truly independent third party. **F** if manager is conflicted, colluding, or rubber-stamping. | **D** after settlement; may find pattern or defect, but does not prevent. | **F** if the required independent manager is not genuinely independent. |
| 4. Override or bypass of automated validation, including AV warning override or validation outage | **P** if an independent second person catches the bypass. | **P** if block; warning is weaker and can be overridden. **F** if unavailable or overridden by the same actor. | **P** if independent manager reviews the override/outage before settlement. **F** if approval is late, conflicted, or ceremonial. | **D** after the fact; may reveal override or outage. | **F** if AV-2 was not fully operational or passed without override. |
| 5. Bank-account change or account takeover | **P** by separating preparation and release. | **P/D** for bank changes in previous seven days and ownership checks. **F** if ownership evidence is stale or unavailable. | **P** if manager independently verifies bank details and supporting receipt before settlement. | **D** after release; **C** only if recall is possible. | **F** if a bank-account change occurred in the previous seven days. |
| 6. Emergency pressure leading to single-operator release and delayed independent approval | Nominal violation, but risk may be bounded if ED-1 conditions are met. | **P** for covered data risks; must be fully operational and passed without override. | **P** if independent manager approves before bank settlement. **D** if after release but before settlement. | **D/C** if audit is completed within one business day and recall/recovery is still possible. | **P** as a permission framework, not as elimination of the four-eyes objective. |
| 7. Settlement before detection or recovery | **F** as a corrective mechanism; FEP-1 is preventive. | **F** after settlement; only useful before release. | **P** if before settlement; **D/C** if after release but before settlement; **F** if after settlement. | **D** after settlement; **C** only if within recall/recovery window. | Requires timely audit and manager approval before settlement to be effective. |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should be tested against the specific threat and the point of no return, not by counting controls. A control that occurred is not automatically sufficient.

### Structured test dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Strong / sufficient | Weak / insufficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | When did the control act relative to release and bank settlement? | Before release, or at least before irrevocable bank settlement. | After bank settlement, especially after recall window. |
| Independence | Was the actor, manager, system, or evidence source independent of the person who prepared/released? | Independent human or system; no shared credential, no unresolved conflict, no shared workstation undermining identity. | Same person, direct supervisor with conflict, shared credential, shared workstation, or missing independence evidence. |
| Coverage | Did the control cover the actual risk in the case? | Covers bank change, new vendor, amount, duplicate, override, outage, commercial legitimacy as relevant. | Covers only some data fields while the actual risk is commercial fraud, collusion, bank takeover, or override. |
| Overrideability | Could the same actor or a conflicted actor override the control? | No override, or override independently authorized and documented. | Warning overridden by preparer/releaser, or by a conflicted manager, without independent review. |
| Recoverability | If the control is detective, can the loss still be stopped or recovered? | Audit within recall window; recall or recovery path available. | Audit after recall window; no reliable recovery path. |
| Evidence quality | Is the control evidenced in an auditable way? | Timestamped approval, reviewed documents, signed checklist, AV check results, token state, manager identity. | Unsigned checklist, unavailable AV, missing manager review evidence, ambiguous token timing. |
| Collusion resistance | Could two nominal actors operate as one economic actor? | Independent preparer/releaser, independent manager, distinct workstations/credentials, pair monitoring. | Repeated pair, shared workstation, missing organizational design, no independent manager. |

### Classification of compensating packages

- **Fully equivalent**: The package addresses the material threat before the point of no return, with independent coverage, low overrideability, high evidence quality, and adequate collusion resistance. Post-settlement detection is not required as the main safeguard.
- **Partially equivalent**: The package materially reduces risk but has one or more weak dimensions, such as manager approval after release but before settlement, uncertain ED-1 precondition, repeated pair risk, or reliance on timely audit. Residual risk may be tolerable if amount, vendor risk, and bank risk are low.
- **Merely mitigating**: The package lowers probability or impact but does not address the core single-actor authorization risk. Examples: low amount only, AV pass only, late manager approval, no independent pre-settlement approval, or no collusion resistance.
- **Ineffective**: The control is absent, overridden by the same actor, performed after settlement without recoverability, contradicted by evidence, or based on unreliable evidence such as unsigned manual checks during an AV outage.

### Why “same expected loss” is not enough

Two packages can have the same expected loss but differ materially in control quality. One may stop the payment before settlement; another may only detect it after funds are unrecoverable. One may have independent evidence; another may rely on a conflicted manager or an overridden warning. One may resist collusion; another may be defeated by a repeated pair or shared workstation. Control objectives include prevention, assurance, accountability, deterrence, recoverability, and timely stoppage, not only loss amount. Fraud and error are also tail risks, so expected-loss equivalence is an unstable standard for conformance.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

I treat **Bank_Settlement** as the point of no return for internal control. **Release_Payment** is an internal authorization to initiate transfer, but it is not necessarily irrevocable until bank settlement. Under that semantics, a manager action after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement is **hybrid**: it is detective relative to the release, but preventive/corrective relative to settlement because it can still stop the payment before irrevocable settlement. If Release_Payment immediately caused irrevocable settlement, the same manager action would be only detective/corrective.

| Control | Primary type | Effective point | Prevents | Detects | Corrects | Limitations | Effect on FEP-1 equivalence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FEP-1 | Preventive structural control | Before release | One person initiating and authorizing | None by itself | None | Nominal form can be satisfied by a collusive pair; shared accounts or shared workstations undermine independence | Baseline objective; violation does not automatically mean objective failure if compensating controls are strong |
| AV-2 | Preventive, with some detective warnings | Before release | Duplicate, sanctions, bank change, ownership issues, limit breach, basic PO mismatch | Warnings for bank change, limit, ownership, etc. | None | Does not establish goods received, detect collusion, validate commercial reasonableness, or identify compromised-account control; warnings can be overridden | Strong for covered data risks; weak for fraud, collusion, or commercial legitimacy |
| MG-4 | Hybrid: preventive before release; hybrid after release but before settlement; detective/corrective only if after settlement | Strongest before release or before bank settlement | Erroneous or fraudulent settlement if manager is independent and reviews evidence | Release defects, override risk, missing support | Can stop payment before settlement | Rubber-stamping, conflict of interest, late approval after settlement, or missing review evidence reduce value | Can compensate for same-user release if independent and before settlement; weak if after settlement or conflicted |
| RA-7 | Detective, partly corrective | After release/settlement | None | Errors, fraud, override, pattern anomalies | Only if recall/recovery is still possible | Late audit, missed recall window, or no recovery path reduces value | Backstop only; not equivalent to pre-settlement independent approval |
| ED-1 | Permission framework, not a standalone control | Before/during emergency | Unbounded single-operator release by imposing conditions | Through required AV, manager approval, and audit | Through timely audit and recall where possible | All conditions must hold; it does not make the four-eyes objective disappear | Can make same-user release acceptable if all conditions are met, especially independent manager approval before settlement |

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### Evidence to collect

- Immutable event logs with case ID, activity, resource, timestamp, workstation/IP, MFA identity, and credential identity.
- AV-2 check results, warnings, blocks, override reason codes, override approver, and override timestamp.
- Emergency token issuance, activation, scope, expiry, and linkage to the payment case.
- Manager approval artifacts: manager level, independence attestation, documents reviewed, invoice/bank/receipt evidence, and timestamp.
- Bank master change logs and bank-account ownership verification snapshots at release time.
- Audit workpapers, audit start/completion timestamps, recall request, and recovery outcome.
- Workstation/session logs to detect shared devices, shared credentials, or identity reuse.
- Vendor master data: vendor age, bank-change history, relationship flags, and receipt confirmation status.

### Monitoring rules

1. **Same-user completed payments**  
   Alert on any completed payment where Prepare_Payment and Release_Payment use the same human resource. Track same-user rate by user, team, and period. If emergency is claimed, automatically validate all ED-1 conditions.

2. **Override rate**  
   Alert on AV-2 warnings that are overridden, especially when the override is performed by the preparer/releaser or by a manager with a conflict of interest. Require independent override authorization, reason code, and manager review.

3. **Late manager approval**  
   Alert when Manager_Approve or Manager_Ratify occurs after Release_Payment. Escalate if it occurs after Bank_Settlement, or if the case has high amount, new vendor, recent bank change, or emergency status.

4. **Audit-before-recall-window**  
   Alert when audit does not start or complete within one business day for emergency same-user payments. Also alert when audit occurs after the 24-hour recall window for high-risk payments.

5. **Repeated resource pairs**  
   Flag pairs of preparer/releaser with high co-occurrence, for example top pairs, more than a defined number of payments in 30 days, or a high share of one user’s payments. Repeated pairing is an alert, not proof of misconduct.

6. **Shared workstations**  
   Flag payments where preparation, release, or manager approval uses the same workstation/IP/session or where identity evidence is weak. Require distinct MFA identities and investigate if the same physical workstation is used for nominally independent roles.

7. **Bank-change proximity**  
   Flag payments where the vendor bank account changed within the previous seven days, the vendor is new or less than 180 days old, or the amount is high. Require independent bank verification and manager review before settlement.

8. **Control outages**  
   Flag AV-2 unavailable, missing AV events, or unsigned manual checklists. During an outage, prohibit release unless an independent manual control is signed, manager approval occurs before settlement, and enhanced audit is scheduled.

### Alert versus proof

Monitoring alerts are risk indicators, not proof of misconduct. A repeated pair, shared workstation, override, or late manager approval may indicate weakness, but fraud or collusion requires corroboration such as credential sharing, communications, vendor relationships, forged documents, benefit evidence, inconsistent review behavior, or direct admission. The purpose of monitoring is to prioritize investigation and improve control design, not to automatically classify individuals as guilty.
