## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **A1  A2**: Registration data must be clean before product/exposure classification can be trusted. A1 creates the foundation for routing logic.
- **A2  A3/A5**: Classification decides whether a case goes through KYC-heavy or document-review-heavy paths. Misclassification damages downstream controls.
- **A3  A4**: KYC hit pre-classification can identify likely entity mismatches. A4 resolves those mismatches and must escalate to the two senior compliance specialists with an audit trail.
- **A5  A6**: Missing documents must be requested and collected before financial statements can be normalized. A5 also improves document quality for A6.
- **A6  credit analysis**: Normalized statements are needed before the credit model can run. A6 is the main protection against overloading the 9 credit analysts.
- **A7  committee packaging**: Collateral valuation, when required, is a long external wait. A7 triage/routing can parallelize and reduce that wait, but it does not eliminate external dependency.
- **A4 + A6 + A7  A8**: Committee pack auto-assembly needs KYC status, normalized financials, collateral valuation, and credit-analysis notes. A8 therefore depends on upstream data quality and exception handling.
- **A8  A9**: Contract generation needs a final committee decision and reconciled artifacts. If the committee pack is incomplete or inaccurate, contract remediation risk increases.
- **A9  A10  A11**: Contract generation must precede customer signing; signing must precede loan activation. Activation cannot safely run before KYC clearance and control completion.
- **A12**: Control remediation workbench sits in the loop between contract preparation and remediation, and also after signing if provisional signing occurred. It depends on control findings and policy clarity.

**Legal/compliance controls**  
- Any model suggestion, human override, and final decision must be recorded in an immutable audit trail.  
- The four-eyes principle must be preserved: credit analysis and committee packaging cannot be completed by the same person in High-exposure SME cases.  
- KYC clearance must precede customer signing; automation cannot silently allow provisional signing.  
- Contract terms must match committee decision; A9 control-rule checks should be versioned and legally reviewed.

**Human escalation capacity**  
- Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE. A3/A4 must include explicit escalation paths, not reduce human accountability.  
- Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central. A6 improves analyst throughput, but if document inflow increases too fast, credit analysts can still become the next bottleneck.  
- Legal ops has 4 FTE and month-end peaks. A9/A12 can smooth demand, but only if upstream KYC and committee data are reliable.

**External valuation dependency**  
- A7 can triage and route to external valuers in parallel, but the bank cannot directly control external valuer turnaround. Quality disputes and SLAs need governance.

**Core-banking integration constraint**  
- IT can deeply integrate only two modules in the first six months. In the recommended bottleneck portfolio, the two deep integrations should be **A4 entity-mismatch resolution** and **A6 financial-statement normalization**, because they are upstream to KYC and credit analysis. ## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Recommended first-year portfolio: **A4 + A6 + A7 + A8**

### Phase 0: Governance and data-quality baseline (Months 0–1)

- Establish audit logging, override codes, data-quality gates, and model-governance criteria.
- Build the **control shell for A8** before automation: completeness checks, four-eyes separation checks, and reconciliation rules.
- Do not yet deploy broad downstream automation.

### Phase 1: Deep integration for upstream bottlenecks (Months 1–4)

- Deploy **A4 entity-mismatch resolution assistant** and **A6 financial-statement extraction/normalization**. These are the two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months.
- Reason: A4 unblocks KYC and later contract paths; A6 protects credit analysts from increased clean inflow and normalizes statements for credit models.
- **Monitoring metrics**: median wait before entity-mismatch resolution; percentage of mismatch cases resolved by assistant; normalization exception rate; credit-analyst queue length; false-positive/false-negative logs.
- **Gate criteria**: If entity-mismatch wait is not reduced and analyst queue grows beyond capacity, tune or do not proceed to A7/A8.

### Phase 2: External and committee synchronization (Months 5–8)

- Deploy **A7 collateral valuation triage and valuer routing** using external APIs and panel management, not deep core integration.
- After the six-month core-banking integration limit has passed, activate **A8 committee pack auto-assembly** using the control shell built in Phase 0.
- **Monitoring metrics**: external valuation turnaround variance; committee-pack first-pass yield; committee decision backlog; four-eyes violation rate; completeness exceptions.
- **Gate criteria**: proceed only if A4/A6 are stable, external valuer SLAs are being met, and committee pack completeness is above baseline. If A8 generates too many incomplete packs, fix upstream data before expanding automation.

### Phase 3: Stabilization and second-year learning (Months 9–12)

- Do not add A1, A9, or A11 in the first year. They are not yet safe because upstream controls and data quality are still being stabilized.
- Use the remainder of the year to tune exception routing, valuer performance, committee cadence, and audit dashboards.
- **Monitoring metrics**: end-to-end cycle time by product and exposure segment; contract remediation rate; provisional signing rate; compliance violation rate; handover counts between resource pools.
- **Gate criteria for second-year funding**: all control violations below baseline, no unremediated audit exceptions, and no new bottleneck appearing in credit analysts or legal ops.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend the bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A6, A7, and A8.**

This portfolio deliberately avoids automating the highest-frequency activity first. **A1 auto-registration** has high direct labor savings, but it is an upstream, high-volume, low-variance step. Automating it first would only push more cases faster into KYC, document review, credit analysis, collateral valuation, and committee packaging — all of which already have long waits and high exception rates.

The bottleneck portfolio targets the activities that dominate end-to-end performance:

- **A4** attacks the 31-hour wait and 38% rework in entity-mismatch resolution, which is on the critical path in 82% of cases.
- **A6** reduces the 20-hour normalization wait and protects the 9 credit analysts from overload.
- **A7** reduces the 58-hour external valuation wait that is a synchronization point for Equipment finance.
- **A8** reduces the 26-hour committee packaging wait and strengthens controls by enforcing complete, reconciled, four-eyes-compliant packs.

This portfolio satisfies the compliance requirement because **A8 is a control-strengthening module**, and **A4/A6/A7** reduce reliance on scarce human specialists without removing compliance accountability. It also respects the two-deep-integration limit by placing A4 and A6 in the first six months and deferring A8 until after that window.

The highest-frequency win is politically attractive, but in this process it is not the highest-leverage investment. Automate the synchronization points first; high-frequency front-end automation can follow once the downstream system can absorb the increased flow without creating new risk.
