## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

Repairs to an incomplete OCEL 2.0 log must separate structural constraints from defeasible domain regularities. The evidence model below classifies each rule by epistemic strength and governs how competing hypotheses are weighed.

### Rule classification

**Hard invariants (never violated):**
- *Referential integrity:* Every E2O edge references an existing event ID and object ID; every O2O edge references two existing object IDs. No events or objects are invented.
- *O2O directionality and typing:* Observed O2O qualifiers are strictly directional and typed: `Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, and `Payment settles Invoice`. Reversed or cross-typed edges (e.g., `Package carries Delivery`, `Payment settles Order`) are invalid.
- *Tuple uniqueness:* Duplicate `(eid, oid, qualifier)` or `(oid, oid_2, qualifier)` rows are forbidden.
- *Causal-temporal precedence:* Under intact clocks, an item is packed only after being added, a package is dispatched only after packing begins, delivery confirmation follows dispatch, and invoice settlement follows invoicing.

**Strong defaults (defeasible by explicit exceptions):**
- *Activity–object signatures and baseline cardinalities:* `Create Order` relates to 1 Order; `Add Item` to 1 Order and 1 Item; `Pack Item` to 1 Item and 1 Package (scanner outages may omit the Package); `Dispatch Package` to 1 Package and 1 Delivery (bulk dispatches are possible); `Issue Invoice` to 1 Invoice and one or more Orders; `Record Payment` to 1 Payment and normally 1 Invoice (unapplied cash is possible).
- *E2O–O2O alignment:* Operational bindings (such as `Pack Item` linking an Item and Package) usually correspond to static O2O edges (`Package packs Item`), but an O2O edge inferred solely from a repaired E2O edge provides zero independent confirmation.
- *Entity-scope coherence:* Orders consolidated on one invoice normally share a customer (`C17`); packages loaded onto a delivery normally match its route (`NORTH`).

**Weak heuristics (informative, never decisive):**
- *Identifier similarity and sequencing:* Tokens such as `PK8-1`/`PK8-2`, `N-421?` vs. `N-421`, `N-42*` vs. `N-420`/`N-421`, and `B-771`/`B-772` raise prior plausibility, but uncertainty markers (`?`, `*`) and generic bank-feed numbering dilute specificity.
- *Temporal proximity:* Close or identical timestamps (`ev07`/`ev08` 1 minute apart; `ev09`/`ev10` both at 10:00; `ev13`/`ev14` 30 minutes apart) support co-processing but also occur across unrelated transactions.
- *Arithmetic sums:* Matching totals (`130 + 100 = 230` across orders/invoice and payments/invoice) provide suggestive evidence, not proof, because taxes, freight, handling fees, or discounts can explain identical differences.
- *Physical coherence:* Grouping two cables (`CABLE-2`, `CABLE-3`, 2.9 kg total) in `PK-8` while isolating fragile `GLASS-1` (3.1 kg) in `PK-7` is physically plausible.

**Evidence against a repair:**
- *Explicit uncertainty markers:* `?` in `N-421?` and `*` in `N-42*` signal unverified or truncated source records.
- *Missing join keys:* `PAY-2` lacks both customer and invoice attributes.
- *Licensed domain exceptions:* Portal carrier-ID loss (`Confirm Delivery`), surcharges/taxes (`Issue Invoice`), unapplied cash (`Record Payment`), and unused/planned objects (`DEL-Y`) explain apparent gaps without assuming missing relations.
- *Cross-boundary anomalies and duplication risks:* Cross-order packing (`PK-8` mixing `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`) raises the evidentiary bar; adding an object to an already-satisfied lifecycle event risks duplicate actions.

### Combining evidence

Confidence scores (0–1) reflect **convergent independent cues** penalized by **competing hypotheses** and **uncertainty markers**. Mechanical operational cues that align across identifier sequence, 1-minute lag, customer scope, SKU physics, and a documented scanner-outage mechanism (`ev08 -> PK-8`) earn high confidence (~0.8). - **`PAY-2` and `INV-44`:** Leave unresolved. Without a customer ID on `PAY-2`, unapplied cash or a different customer's bank entry cannot be ruled out.

*Lifecycle coherence:* Bundle A repairs only the concrete warehouse scan omission while preserving documented operational and financial uncertainties. Every repaired fragment is causally valid; incomplete lifecycles (`DEL-Y` untouched, `ORD-B` unbilled, `PAY-2` unapplied) are treated as honest reflections of partial observation or open business states rather than defects to be erased.

### Bundle B: Consolidated multi-order fulfillment and settlement
- **`ev08` and `PK-8`:** Include `P01` and `P02` (consolidated cable box for `C17`).
- **`ev10` and `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y`:** Include `P03` (`ev10 -> DEL-Y`) and `P04` (`DEL-Y carries PK-8`), interpreting `N-421?` as an unverified but accurate reference to `DEL-Y`.

Because `ORD-A` is split across `PK-7` (`DEL-X`) and `PK-8` (`DEL-Y`), assigning `ev11` uniquely to either delivery would be weaker than joint acknowledgement under `N-42*`; `ORD-B` rides in `PK-8` on `DEL-Y` for the same portal customer `C17`.
- **`INV-44` and `ORD-B`:** Include `P10` (`ev12 -> ORD-B`) and `P11` (`INV-44 bills ORD-B`), interpreting `INV-44` as a consolidated `C17` invoice (`130 + 100 = 230`).
- **`PAY-2` and `INV-44`:** Include `P12` (`ev14 -> INV-44`) and `P13` (`PAY-2 settles INV-44`), interpreting `PAY-1` and `PAY-2` as split settlements (`100 + 130 = 230`).

*Lifecycle coherence:* Bundle B yields a clean, end-to-end consolidated order-to-cash narrative for customer `C17` with zero orphan objects. Its weakness is **compounded inference**: `P08` and `P09` lean heavily on `P03` and `P01`, and the financial links lean on defeasible arithmetic and sequential bank references. It is a coherent hypothesis for sensitivity analysis, not an established fact.

### Bundle C: Bulk-dispatch single-delivery and decoupled finance
- **`ev08` and `PK-8`:** Include `P01` and `P02`.
- **`ev10` and `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y`:** Include `P05` (`ev10 -> DEL-X`) and `P06` (`DEL-X carries PK-8`), reading the identical 10:00 dispatch time and `NORTH` route as a single truck (`DEL-X`), with `N-421?` representing a canceled tentative manifest that leaves `DEL-Y` legitimately unused.
- **`ev11` and deliveries:** Assign `P07` (`DEL-X`) only, leaving `ORD-B` unlinked. Conditional on `P05` (only `DEL-X` dispatched), `N-42*` is best read as a portal-masked rendering of `N-420`, making `DEL-Y` or dual-delivery assignment weaker than `DEL-X` alone. Without `P05`, unique assignment to `DEL-X` would not be justified.
- **`INV-44` and `ORD-B`:** Keep unlinked; `INV-44` (230) bills `ORD-A` (130) plus 100 in taxes, freight, or fragile-glass handling, while `ORD-B` awaits invoicing.
- **`PAY-2` and `INV-44`:** Keep unresolved as unapplied cash (`B-772` lacks customer metadata).

*Lifecycle coherence:* Bundle C demonstrates that a fully coherent alternative exists without forcing `DEL-Y` into use or relying on arithmetic sums. Both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` ship on one north-route delivery (`DEL-X`), while billing and cash application remain legitimately partial.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Evaluating Bundle A across all six object types highlights both repaired consistency and deliberately preserved gaps:

- **Order (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`):** `ORD-A` exhibits a plausible five-event lifecycle (`Create Order  Add Item  Add Item  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice`). `ORD-B` ends at `Add Item`, which is incomplete but legitimate if `ORD-B` is not yet confirmed or billed. `Order 1:N Item` (`ORD-A` has 2 items; `ORD-B` has 1) is supported by both observed E2O (`ev03`–`ev05`) and observed O2O (`contains`).
- **Item (`IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, `IT-B1`):** All three items now follow `Add Item  Pack Item`, eliminating `IT-B1`'s pack-without-package defect. `IT-A1`/`PK-7` and `IT-A2`/`PK-8` have observed E2O and O2O support; `IT-B1`/`PK-8` relies solely on repaired `P01`/`P02`.
- **Package (`PK-7`, `PK-8`):** `PK-7` follows `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`; `PK-8` follows `Pack Item  Pack Item  Dispatch Package`. `PK-8` now participates in a suspicious-looking **many-to-many indirect relation** with `Order`: `ORD-A` spans two packages (`PK-7`, `PK-8`), while `PK-8` consolidates items from two orders (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`). Because both orders share customer `C17` and route `NORTH`, this M:N consolidation is operationally plausible, though it merits warehouse-policy verification. Event `ev10` remains delivery-less rather than orphaned (still linked to `PK-8`).
- **Delivery (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`):** `DEL-X` has a truncated single-event lifecycle (`Dispatch Package`), supported by observed `ev09` and `DEL-X carries PK-7`. `DEL-Y` is a true **orphan object** (zero E2O and zero O2O relations). Preserving `DEL-Y` as an orphan is preferable to guessing past `N-421?`; operationally it may represent a planned or canceled manifest. Event `ev11` is not an orphan (linked to `ORD-A`), though it lacks any Delivery link.
- **Invoice (`INV-44`):** Follows `Issue Invoice  Record Payment` (`ev12`, `ev13`), with `1:1` billing to `ORD-A` and `1:1` settlement from `PAY-1`, both present in raw E2O and O2O. The remaining unpaid balance (130) is an open business state, not a hard inconsistency.
- **Payment (`PAY-1`, `PAY-2`):** Both have `Record Payment`. `PAY-1` links to `INV-44` in both E2O and O2O; `PAY-2` is invoice-unlinked (valid unapplied cash under rule 7).

### Circular-reasoning audit

A major risk in OCEL repair is treating derived O2O relations as independent corroboration:
1. **E2OO2O circularity in Bundle A:** `P02` (`PK-8 packs IT-B1`) was inferred strictly from `P01` (`ev08 -> PK-8`). Citing `P02` as "independent O2O confirmation" of `P01` would be circular; both rest on the same scanner-token and timestamp evidence. Even in the unrepaired log, observed E2O/O2O agreement (e.g., `ev09` and `DEL-X carries PK-7`) is only truly independent if extracted from distinct source tables rather than generated by one ETL script.
2. **Chained circularity in Bundle B:** The pairs `(P03, P04)`, `(P10, P11)`, and `(P12, P13)` have the same E2OO2O non-independence. Worse, assuming `ev10 -> DEL-Y` makes `ev11 -> DEL-Y` appear consistent with `ORD-A`'s split shipment, and then citing `ev11` (`N-42*`) as proof that `DEL-Y` must have been dispatched closes a circular loop.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Comparing the **unrepaired log**, **Bundle A**, and **Bundle B** shows how seemingly small relation repairs substantially alter object-centric process discovery artifacts.

- **OC-DFG:**
  - *Unrepaired:* `Package` contains only `Pack Item  Dispatch Package` (weight 2); `Delivery` has an isolated `Dispatch Package` node (`DEL-X`) with zero edges; `Invoice` has `Issue Invoice  Record Payment` (1); `Order` has `Create Order  Add Item` (2), `Add Item  Add Item` (1), `Add Item  Confirm Delivery` (1), `Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice` (1).
  - *Bundle A:* Adds exactly one OC-DFG edge: the `Package` self-loop `Pack Item  Pack Item` (weight 1, `ev07  ev08` on `PK-8`). All other object-type edges stay unchanged.
  - *Bundle B:* Adds three major control-flow structures: `Delivery` gains `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` (weight 2: `ev09  ev11` on `DEL-X`, `ev10  ev11` on `DEL-Y`); `Order` increases `Add Item  Confirm Delivery` and `Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice` from weight 1 to 2 (via `ORD-B`); and `Invoice` gains a `Record Payment  Record Payment` self-loop (weight 1, `ev13  ev14` on `INV-44`), depicting installment settlement.

- **OTG (Object Type Graph):**
  - *Unrepaired* and *Bundle A:* Event co-occurrence connects `Order–Item`, `Item–Package`, `Package–Delivery`, `Invoice–Order`, and `Payment–Invoice`. There is **no direct `Order–Delivery` edge**. Bundle A increases `Item–Package` co-occurrence from 2 to 3 events and creates an indirect O2O path connecting `ORD-B` to `PK-8`.
  - *Bundle B:* Introduces a brand-new `Order–Delivery` edge via `ev11` (co-relating `ORD-A`, `ORD-B`, `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`), fundamentally altering the process's object-interaction topology, while strengthening `Package–Delivery`, `Invoice–Order`, and `Payment–Invoice`.

- **ET–OT Graph:**
  - *Unrepaired:* `Pack Item–Package` (2/3 events), `Dispatch Package–Delivery` (1/2), and `Record Payment–Invoice` (1/2) appear optional; `Confirm Delivery` connects solely to `Order`.
  - *Bundle A:* Makes `Pack Item–Package` mandatory (3/3 events, 1:1 per event) while keeping dispatch and payment links optional.
  - *Bundle B:* Adds the new edge `Confirm Delivery–Delivery` (multiplicity 2 on `ev11`), raises `Confirm Delivery–Order` and `Issue Invoice–Order` from 1:1 to 1:N (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`), and turns `Dispatch Package–Delivery` and `Record Payment–Invoice` into mandatory 1:1 bindings.

- **Flattenings:**
  - *Order:* Unrepaired and Bundle A produce identical traces (`ORD-A`: `ev01,03,04,11,12`; `ORD-B`: `ev02,05`). Bundle B extends `ORD-B` to `ev02,05,11,12`, **duplicating** shared events `ev11` and `ev12` across both cases (convergence).
  - *Package:* Unrepaired gives `PK-7` (`ev06,09`) and `PK-8` (`ev07,10`). Bundles A and B extend `PK-8` to `ev07,08,10`.
  - *Delivery:* Unrepaired and Bundle A include only `DEL-X` (`ev09`); `DEL-Y` vanishes from the flattened log. Bundle B materializes `DEL-Y` (`ev10,11`), extends `DEL-X` (`ev09,11`), and duplicates `ev11` across both traces.
  - *Invoice:* Unrepaired and Bundle A give `INV-44` (`ev12,13`); Bundle B adds `ev14` (`ev12,13,14`).
  - *Payment:* Identical across all three (`PAY-1`: `ev13`; `PAY-2`: `ev14`), illustrating that flattening on `Payment` completely hides `ev14 -> INV-44`.

- **Activity frequencies (events vs. unique objects vs. total object references):**
  - `Pack Item`: 3 events in all logs; total Package references rise from 2 (unrepaired) to 3 (Bundles A & B), while unique Packages remain 2 (`PK-7`, `PK-8`).
  - `Confirm Delivery`: 1 event in all logs; total object references jump from 1 (`ORD-A`) in unrepaired/Bundle A to 4 (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`, `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`) in Bundle B.
  - `Issue Invoice`: 1 event; Order references rise from 1 to 2 in Bundle B.
  - `Record Payment`: 2 events; Invoice references rise from 1 to 2 in Bundle B, while unique Invoices stay 1 (`INV-44`).

- **Equivalent-execution clustering around `Order`:**
  - *Unrepaired:* `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` occupy **two disjoint connected components** (`ORD-A` with `IT-A1, IT-A2, PK-7, PK-8, DEL-X, INV-44, PAY-1`; `ORD-B` with `IT-B1` only; `DEL-Y` and `PAY-2` isolated).
  - *Bundle A:* Adding `ev08 -> PK-8` immediately **merges `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` into one entangled execution component** via shared package `PK-8`, eliminating independent single-order clustering even under conservative repair.
  - *Bundle B:* Absorbs `DEL-Y` and `PAY-2` as well, collapsing the entire dataset into a single densely coupled component linked via `PK-8`, `DEL-Y`, `ev11`, and `INV-44`.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Aggressive repair aimed at achieving "complete lifecycles" can inject systematic bias into process mining and auditing:

1. **Hiding unbilled revenue through arithmetic billing repair (`ev12`/`INV-44 -> ORD-B`):** Treating `130 + 100 = 230` as proof of consolidated billing erases the possibility that `INV-44` includes 100 in freight, tax, or fragile-glass handling on `ORD-A` alone. Downstream conformance checks will then miss a genuinely unbilled order (`ORD-B`) and overstate consolidated-invoicing frequency.
2. **Masking unapplied cash and cross-customer collisions (`ev14`/`PAY-2 -> INV-44`):** Because `PAY-2` carries no customer or invoice identifier, forcing it onto `INV-44` based on `100 + 130 = 230` and sequential bank-feed numbers (`B-771`/`B-772`) conceals an accounts-receivable exception, fabricates a `Record Payment  Record Payment` installment loop on `INV-44`, and risks misallocating another customer's payment.
3. **Fabricating carrier execution for an unused manifest (`ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y`):** Overriding the `?` in `N-421?` and `*` in `N-42*` forces `DEL-Y` into active transit even if `DEL-Y` was a canceled plan and `PK-8` actually rode on `DEL-X` at 10:00. This inflates carrier utilization metrics, invents split-delivery behavior, and creates a false two-delivery confirmation pattern.
4. **Creating false multi-order delivery synchronization (`ev11 -> ORD-B, DEL-X, DEL-Y`):** Over-linking `ev11` adds an `Order–Delivery` OTG interaction and duplicates `Confirm Delivery` across flattened `Order` and `Delivery` logs, falsely implying synchronized multi-order/multi-delivery acknowledgement when the portal may have logged only an order-level confirmation for `ORD-A`.
5. **Over-entangling orders from a mis-buffered box scan (`ev08 -> PK-8` + `PK-8 packs IT-B1`):** Even for our highest-confidence repair, if `PK8-2` resulted from a scanner failing to clear `PK-8` before packing a new box, linking `IT-B1` to `PK-8` falsely merges `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` into one execution component and invents cross-order consolidation—compounded if `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is mistakenly treated as independent confirmation.
6. **Corrupting process schema with shortcut links (`ev14 -> ORD-A`, `ev08 -> ORD-B`, `ev09 -> PK-8`):** Exploiting `PAY-2` (130) = `ORD-A` (130) to link payments directly to orders, adding `Order` directly to `Pack Item`, or merging `PK-8` into `ev09` violates domain type signatures, pollutes the ET–OT graph, and introduces duplicate dispatches or spurious order-level events.
