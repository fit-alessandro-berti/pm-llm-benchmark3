## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The gap between raw and mapped fitness identifies the impact of system migration on process conformance reporting. By remapping `Eligibility Screen` events to `Validate Eligibility` and removing `Open Case` duplications, the mapped fitness recovers much of the raw data's apparent conformance loss. However, this does not prove the business process itself remained unchanged, as it reflects adjustments to labels and event handling, not to structural or behavioral consistency.

Duplicated technical events inflate activity counts, creating artifacts in sequence metrics and workflow visualizations. Missing resource identifiers limit alignment checks and resource-usage monitoring, potentially hiding unbalanced workloads.

Post-May changes, including rising rework and delays, cannot be attributed to logging drift alone. The increased `Assess` -> `Request Info` activities and elongated queues align more closely with supplier disruptions and capacity drops. Structural patterns also suggest actual disruption in resource allocation and process prioritization.

Fitness mapping is thus essential for diagnosing logging artifacts but must be combined with other analyses to differentiate real process drift from schema-level or data-quality artifacts.

import pandas as pd
from scipy.spatial.distance import jaccard
import numpy as np

# Embed input data

monthly_summary['cases_change'] = monthly_summary['cases'] - baseline['cases'].values[0]

# 3. Compute DFG distance (example: Jaccard for normalized relation rates)
edges = ['Register->Eligibility', 'Eligibility->CollectDocs'] # Example fragment

distances = {}
for _, row in normalized_dfg_rates.iterrows():
    if row['month'] != "2026-01":
        distances[row['month']] = np.mean([
            jaccard(
                baseline_rates[edge].values[0], row[edge]
            ) for edge in edges
        ])

# 4. Compute raw-to-mapped fitness recovery
monthly_summary['fitness_recovery'] = (
    monthly_summary['mapped_model_fitness'] - 
    monthly_summary['raw_model_fitness']
)

# 5. Flag change points
change_points = []
threshold = 0.1
for metric in ['mapped_model_fitness', 'cases_change']:
    shifts = monthly_summary[metric].diff().abs() > threshold
    change_points += monthly_summary['month'][shifts].tolist()

# 6. Print caveats

## 9. Provisional attribution

### Strongest conclusions
1. **March logging drift**: The fitness collapse and mapped recovery strongly confirm schema and technical issues caused by the migration. Label mismatches (`Eligibility Screen`) and duplicated `Open Case` events account for nearly all early misalignment.
2. **Supplier Z disruption**: The sudden rework and backlog rise in May are well explained by incomplete document bundles from Supplier Z, particularly impacting non-random, complex cases.

### Plausibly supported
1. **Staffing impact**: Gradual queueing delays align with workforce reductions, most visibly after June. Redistribution without sufficient replacement exacerbated delays.
2. **Priority-induced redistribution**: The July rule amplified delays for standard cases while benefiting high-complexity ones. Good evidence exists, but overlapping resource constraints add a confounding factor.

### Mediation and interaction
- **Seasonality**: October’s sharp high-complexity surge combines historical seasonal patterns with accumulated supply and resource disruptions, magnifying baseline trends.
- **December improvements**: Interventions reduced delays, though these effects interacted with reduced seasonal demand and backlog clearing.

### Revisions under better data
Better individual-level alignment and resource variability tracking could clarify supplier and priority-rule effects, particularly their interaction with team-specific bottlenecks.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF raw fitness drops but mapped fitness does not, THEN confirm new mapping is consistent, BECAUSE missing logs or schema drifts were likely adjusted.**
2. **IF Supplier Z rework rises >15% within a month, THEN trace document sources and escalate to supplier reviews, BECAUSE supplier errors drive case-level drift.**
3. **IF assess queue delays dramatically differ by complexity, THEN evaluate priority load distribution, BECAUSE new policy causes redistribution skew.**
4. **IF monthly high-complexity percentage exceeds historical seasonality by >5 points, THEN segregate for volume-induced high-congestion analysis, BECAUSE abnormal complexity shares indicate population drift.**
5. **IF resource missingness exceeds 5%, THEN audit event-record consistency for bias by team, BECAUSE hidden patterns distort conformance across resource pools.**
6. **IF rework paths reach historical peaks, THEN investigate drivers in supplier or staffing causes, BECAUSE rework variants often signal upstream bottlenecks.**
7. **IF queue waiting increases precede service events across segments, THEN reallocate staff to bottlenecked queues, BECAUSE pure queue delay is recoverable by flexible capacity.**
