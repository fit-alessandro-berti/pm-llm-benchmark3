# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining evidence shows a **segment- and risk-mismatched process**, not a simple case of “fast is bad” or “slow is good.” I explicitly use the following artifacts: the **frequency DFG**, the **performance DFG**, the **conformance/alignment findings**, the **rework statistics**, the **customer-segment outcomes**, and the **resource/cost information**.

## Main performance problem: a bimodal process

The **frequency DFG** shows that the dominant route is:

`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`

- 3,600 of 5,000 cases go to `Auto eligibility screen`.
- 2,550 cases move from `Auto eligibility screen` to `Fast approval`.
- 2,300 cases move from `Fast approval` to `Dispatch replacement`.

The **performance DFG** shows why this path is attractive:

- `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen`: median 0.5 hours.
- `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: median 0.4 hours.
- `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`: median 1.8 hours.
- `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`: median 3.2 hours.

By contrast, the slower reviewed and evidence-dependent paths are much slower:

- `Intake claim -> Manual eligibility review`: median 5.0 hours, p90 18.0 hours.
- `Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`: median 10.0 hours, p90 36.0 hours.
- `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: median 26.0 hours, p90 88.0 hours.
- `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: median 15.0 hours, p90 60.0 hours.
- `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review`: median 31.0 hours, p90 100.0 hours.

The process therefore has a **fast, high-volume lane** and a **slow, exception-heavy lane**. The slow lane is not merely “more thorough”; it is also often too slow for Premium expectations, especially because `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` is explicitly flagged as too slow for Premium cases.

## Main quality and rework problem: the fast path creates downstream cost

The **rework statistics** show the central contradiction:

- `Fast approval` cases are **31% cheaper before rework**, but only **9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits**.
- `Customer complaint/reopen` occurs in **9.2% of all cases**, but **14.1% of Fast approval cases**, versus only **3.3% of cases using `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`**.
- `Rework repair` occurs in **12.5% of repaired high-value devices**, versus **4.0% of repaired low-risk devices**.
- `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in **26% of cases that enter it**.

The performance DFG also shows that complaints are not immediate noise; they are long-tail downstream events:

- `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen`: mean 72 hours, p90 150 hours.
- `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`: mean 96 hours, p90 180 hours.

The alignment diagnostics explicitly link the `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` long tail to **incorrect replacement part selection**. This is important: the problem is not only that fast approval is fast, but that fast approval can be **under-informed** about evidence, risk, and part compatibility.

## Main compliance problem: frequent path conflicts with normative rules

The **conformance findings** show that the dominant behavior is not fully policy-compliant:

- Token-based replay fitness is **0.91**, but only **68% of traces fully fit** the normative Petri net.
- Frequent log deviations include:
  - `Fast approval` before evidence completeness.
  - `Customer complaint/reopen` after close.
  - `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices.
- The discovered model has **low precision**, meaning many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy.

These deviations matter because they map directly to the normative rules:

- **R1**: Claims above EUR 400 require `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` before `Fast approval`.
- **R2**: Replacement dispatch must not occur before evidence completeness, except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.
- **R3**: Any reopened claim must pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial.

The frequency DFG shows many `Fast approval` and `Dispatch replacement` cases, while the alignment diagnostics show that some of these occur without the required review or evidence state. That is a compliance risk, especially for high-value devices.

## Main customer-experience problem: outcomes differ sharply by segment

The **customer segment outcomes** show that one global policy would be wrong.

### Premium low-risk accessory

- Share: 18%.
- Fast path median cycle time: 8 hours.
- Manual path median cycle time: 46 hours.
- Complaint/reopen: 6% fast, 3% manual.
- Service-credit exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24 hours.

This segment needs speed. The fast path is attractive, but the 6% complaint rate still matters because the segment has strict service expectations.

### Standard low-risk accessory

- Share: 25%.
- Fast path median cycle time: 13 hours.
- Manual path median cycle time: 52 hours.
- Complaint/reopen: 8% fast, 4% manual.
- Customers tolerate 72-hour completion if status visibility is good.

This segment can accept slightly longer completion if communication is good. It does not need the same expensive manual treatment as Premium high-value cases.

### Premium high-value device

- Share: 14%.
- Fast path median cycle time: 20 hours.
- Reviewed/repair path median cycle time: 96 hours.
- Complaint/reopen: 22% fast, 5% reviewed/repair.
- Compliance risk is high if R1 or R2 is violated.

Here the fast path is dangerous. It is faster, but complaint/reopen is much higher and compliance risk is high.

### Standard high-value device

- Share: 21%.
- Fast path median cycle time: 28 hours.
- Reviewed/repair path median cycle time: 120 hours.
- Complaint/reopen: 18% fast, 6% reviewed/repair.
- Cost of wrong replacement is high.

Again, the reviewed path is slower but produces materially better outcomes.

### Unclear defect / missing evidence

- Share: 22%.
- Median cycle time: 140 hours.
- Highest abandonment rate and repeated evidence requests.

This is the worst customer-experience segment and a major source of process instability.

## Resource and cost constraints

The **resource and cost information** shows that the slower path cannot simply be scaled to all cases:

- `Auto eligibility screen` is cheap at EUR 0.20 per case and effectively unlimited, but its rules are outdated and not segment-aware.
- `Manual eligibility review` has only 7 reviewers, 36 productive hours each per week, at EUR 34 per labor hour; queue pressure rises sharply above 82% utilization.
- `Fraud/risk review` has only 2 specialists and bottlenecks on Mondays and after campaigns.
- `Repair quote` delays are driven by waiting for part-price confirmation.
- `Quality check` inspectors are underused except after repair campaigns.
- The escalation board has strong customer-satisfaction impact but poor scalability.

## The core contradiction

The most frequent fast path is attractive because it is fast and cheap before rework. But the same process-mining artifacts show that it produces more complaints, more compliance exceptions, and more hidden downstream cost. The slower reviewed/repair path has better quality and customer outcomes, but it is capacity-constrained, slower, and not always appropriate for low-risk or Premium low-risk accessory claims.

Therefore, the redesign should not choose “fast” or “slow” globally. It should **route cases by segment, value, risk, evidence completeness, and defect type**, while protecting compliance and controlling downstream rework.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware two-speed eligibility with risk-based routing and capacity guardrails

## Redesigned process flow

This redesign changes the **eligibility and routing logic** so that the process no longer treats all claims as if they belong to one generic fast lane.

### 1. Intake enrichment and classification

At `Intake claim`, the case is automatically classified by:

- Customer segment: Premium or Standard.
- Device risk class: low-risk accessory, mid-risk device, high-value device.
- Claim value: above or below EUR 400, because of rule R1.
- Defect type: clear replacement, likely repair, unclear defect.
- Evidence completeness flag.
- Fraud/risk signal.
- Service-level exposure: Premium 24-hour sensitivity, Standard 72-hour tolerance with visibility.

This uses the cheap, scalable `Auto eligibility screen`, but makes it **segment-aware** rather than outdated and generic.

### 2. Low-risk accessory straight-through lane

For Premium and Standard low-risk accessory claims:

- If evidence is sufficient and no fraud/risk signal exists, the case goes to `Fast approval`.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims receive priority dispatch because of service-credit exposure above 24 hours.
- Standard low-risk accessory claims may use the fast lane, but if the expected completion exceeds 24 hours, they receive proactive status visibility because they tolerate up to 72 hours when communication is good.
- These cases do not consume scarce manual reviewers unless an exception is detected.

This preserves the strength of the current fast path where it is most defensible.

### 3. High-value and mid-risk reviewed lane

For high-value devices and many mid-risk devices:

- If claim value is above EUR 400, the case must pass either `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` before any `Fast approval`, satisfying R1.
- Premium high-value claims receive priority in the manual review queue.
- Standard high-value claims are routed to manual review or `Repair quote`, depending on defect type and repairability.
- Cases with fraud indicators go to `Fraud/risk review`, but routing is smoothed to avoid overloading the two specialists on Mondays or after campaigns.
- Mid-risk devices receive manual review or repair quote if defect type or part compatibility is uncertain.

This reduces the current deviation where high-value devices receive dispatch without adequate risk review.

### 4. Repair-quote acceleration for reviewed paths

Because `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` is too slow for Premium cases, this redesign adds a repair-quote subflow:

- Common defect types receive standard part-price bands.
- Technicians can approve quotes within predefined value thresholds without waiting for external part-price confirmation.
- Premium high-value repair quotes receive a priority flag.
- Only unusual parts or high-uncertainty quotes require manual price confirmation.

This makes the slower, higher-quality path more viable for Premium customers.

### 5. Reopen and capacity guardrails

- Any `Customer complaint/reopen` is routed to `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, satisfying R3.
- Manual reviewer utilization is monitored against the 82% threshold.
- If manual utilization approaches the threshold, the system prioritizes Premium high-value, high-compliance-risk, and reopen cases.
- Low-risk exceptions may be handled by targeted post-audit rather than full manual review, provided no R1/R2 violation is created.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign primarily targets:

- Premium low-risk accessory claims that need speed.
- Standard low-risk accessory claims that can tolerate 72 hours with visibility.
- Premium and Standard high-value claims that currently receive too much fast approval.
- Mid-risk devices with uncertain repair/replacement decisions.
- `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` delays for Premium cases.
- Reopen compliance under R3.

## Mechanism of improvement

The improvement mechanism is **risk-based differentiation**.

- Low-risk accessory claims remain fast and cheap, preserving the benefit of the dominant path.
- High-value claims are prevented from using unreviewed fast approval, reducing R1/R2 violations and wrong-dispatch risk.
- Manual capacity is concentrated where it creates value: high-value, high-risk, and reopen cases.
- Repair-quote acceleration makes the slower path less damaging for Premium customers.
- The process reduces low-precision “hidden factory” variation by replacing many ad hoc combinations with explicit segment lanes.

This redesign does not assume the slow path is always better. It uses the slow path where the evidence shows better outcomes: high-value devices and complaint-prone cases. It keeps the fast path where the evidence shows acceptable outcomes: low-risk accessories.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some simplicity and may increase short-term cost:

- It may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35% if updated auto-screen rules become stricter.
- High-value claims may become slower than the current fast path, although they should avoid the long complaint tail.
- Fraud/risk review may become more pressured if risk flags are overused.
- It does not fully solve repeated evidence requests, unclear-defect cases, or wrong replacement part selection unless paired with Redesign B or C.
- It leaves some trade-off unresolved: better compliance and quality for high-value cases requires consuming scarce manual and fraud capacity.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first claim completion and guided customer intake

## Redesigned process flow

This redesign changes the **upstream evidence flow**. Instead of allowing eligibility and dispatch to run ahead of evidence completeness, it makes evidence completion an explicit, guided front-end process.

### 1. Guided evidence intake by defect type

At intake, the customer receives a defect-type-specific evidence checklist:

- Serial number or product identifier.
- Proof of purchase or warranty status.
- Photos or videos of the defect.
- Symptom description selected from structured choices.
- Accessories or components involved.
- Customer preference: replacement, repair, or diagnostic clarification.

The claim receives an **evidence completeness score**. The goal is not to ask for everything generically, but to ask for the minimum evidence needed for that defect type and risk class.

### 2. Evidence completeness before fast dispatch

For most claims:

- `Auto eligibility screen` is performed only after the evidence completeness score reaches the required threshold.
- `Dispatch replacement` is not triggered before evidence completeness, satisfying R2.
- For Premium low-risk accessory claims, the process uses the allowed R2 exception but still requires minimal identification and defect confirmation to avoid wrong dispatch.

This directly addresses the alignment deviation: `Fast approval` before evidence completeness.

### 3. One targeted evidence request, not repeated generic requests

If evidence is missing:

- The customer receives one targeted request specifying exactly what is missing.
- The request is delivered through the evidence portal, with SMS or email nudges.
- For customers with low digital literacy, an assisted channel is available: phone support, guided upload, or in-person/partner support where feasible.
- The process limits repeated evidence requests: after two failed attempts, the claim is paused or marked abandoned, but with an easy restart path if the customer returns.

This addresses the finding that `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it.

### 4. Unclear-defect diagnostic triage

For the 22% of cases with unclear defect or missing evidence:

- The case enters a diagnostic triage pod rather than bouncing between `Request missing evidence` and `Manual eligibility review`.
- The pod can combine customer service, technical support, and quality inspection input.
- The pod decides whether the claim should become:
  - A replacement claim,
  - A repair quote,
  - A further targeted evidence request,
  - Or a controlled denial/abandonment.

This reduces the 140-hour median cycle time in the unclear-defect segment.

### 5. Status visibility for Standard customers

For Standard customers:

- The claim status is visible throughout the evidence stage.
- Expected completion is shown, including whether the case is within the acceptable 72-hour window.
- If evidence is pending, the customer sees exactly what is needed and how to provide it.

This uses the segment outcome finding that Standard customers tolerate 72-hour completion if status visibility is good.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign primarily targets:

- Unclear defect / missing evidence cases, 22% of volume.
- Claims entering `Request missing evidence`.
- The 26% of evidence-request cases that receive repeated requests.
- The 320 observed abandonments after `Request missing evidence`.
- R2 violations involving dispatch before evidence completeness.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims where evidence exceptions are allowed but still need minimal control.
- Standard customers who can tolerate longer completion if visibility is good.

## Mechanism of improvement

The improvement mechanism is **upstream uncertainty reduction**.

- Better evidence reduces repeated loops between `Request missing evidence` and `Manual eligibility review`.
- Eligibility decisions become more accurate, reducing later complaints and reopens.
- Dispatch decisions are better grounded, reducing wrong replacement risk.
- Abandonment is reduced by making evidence requests specific, assisted, and visible.
- The process becomes more compliant with R2 because dispatch is not triggered before evidence completeness, except where explicitly allowed.
- The unclear-defect segment receives a dedicated diagnostic route instead of being pushed through a generic eligibility funnel.

This redesign is materially different from Redesign A because it does not primarily change eligibility routing. It changes the **precondition for eligibility**: the evidence state of the claim.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some immediate speed and may create new risks:

- Initial cycle time may increase for claims with incomplete evidence.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims could be delayed if the evidence requirements are too rigid, creating service-credit exposure.
- The evidence portal may increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy if assisted support is not adequately resourced.
- More complete evidence may reveal more fraud/risk signals, increasing pressure on `Fraud/risk review`.
- It does not fully solve high-value fast-approval compliance unless combined with Redesign A.
- It does not directly catch wrong replacement part selection after approval; that is addressed by Redesign C.

# 4. Redesign C: Closed-loop fulfillment quality gate and reopen governance

## Redesigned process flow

This redesign changes the **downstream fulfillment and compliance-close loop**. It introduces a risk-based quality gate before dispatch, strengthens repair quality feedback, and controls reopens before final closure.

### 1. Risk-based pre-dispatch quality gate

After a replacement decision but before `Dispatch replacement`, the case passes through a gate whose intensity depends on risk:

#### High-value devices

- 100% lightweight pre-dispatch quality check.
- Check includes:
  - Correct replacement part selection.
  - Serial/product compatibility.
  - Evidence completeness.
  - R1 compliance: manual or fraud review where claim value exceeds EUR 400.
  - R2 compliance: evidence completeness before dispatch, unless exception applies.
  - Customer address and entitlement validation.

This is justified because the expected cost of a wrong replacement is high: EUR 410 for high-value devices.

#### Mid-risk devices

- Either 100% check or high-rate sampling, depending on defect type and historical error rate.
- Cases with uncertain part compatibility receive full check.

#### Low-risk accessories

- Automated part-match validation.
- Low-rate sampling by quality inspectors.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims bypass manual gate delay where necessary to protect the 24-hour service expectation, but still receive automated validation.

This uses the finding that a lightweight pre-dispatch gate can catch wrong replacement selection but adds 6–18 hours. The gate is therefore applied selectively, not universally.

### 2. Use underused quality-check capacity

The two quality inspectors who are underused except after repair campaigns are redeployed to run the pre-dispatch gate during normal operations.

- During low volume, they perform deeper audits.
- During high volume, they focus only on high-value and high-risk cases.
- Automated checks handle low-risk accessory volume.

This avoids creating a large new fixed-cost team.

### 3. Repair quality feedback loop

For repair paths:

- `Repair device -> Quality check` remains mandatory for high-value devices.
- Rework repair is not treated as an isolated loop; each rework generates a root-cause code.
- Frequent rework triggers feedback to:
  - Repair instructions,
  - Part catalog,
  - Technician training,
  - Auto-screen rules,
  - Evidence requirements.

This addresses the 12.5% rework rate for repaired high-value devices.

### 4. Reopen governance before final close

The redesign enforces a strict rule:

- A case cannot reach `Quality/compliance close` if there is an unresolved `Customer complaint/reopen`.
- Any reopened claim must pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, satisfying R3.
- Reopens are categorized by cause:
  - Wrong replacement,
  - Missing evidence,
  - Incorrect eligibility,
  - Repair failure,
  - Customer communication failure.

This addresses the deviation where complaints/reopens occur after close.

### 5. Escalation board used as a targeted intervention

The escalation board remains available, but only for:

- Premium high-impact cases,
- Repeated failures,
- High-value disputes,
- Cases with regulatory exposure.

This preserves its strong customer-satisfaction effect while avoiding overuse of the one daily 45-minute slot.

## Targeted segments, paths, or activities

This redesign primarily targets:

- `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` long-tail pattern.
- Incorrect replacement part selection.
- High-value device claims where wrong replacement cost is high.
- High-value repair rework.
- Reopened claims and R3 compliance.
- Complaint-after-close deviations.
- Underused quality-check capacity.

## Mechanism of improvement

The improvement mechanism is **downstream error capture plus feedback**.

- The pre-dispatch gate catches wrong replacement decisions before the customer receives the wrong item.
- High-value cases receive stronger control because their error cost and complaint rate are higher.
- Low-risk cases retain speed through automated checks and sampling.
- Reopen governance prevents cases from being closed while unresolved.
- Root-cause feedback improves upstream auto-screen rules, evidence templates, and repair practices.
- Quality inspectors are used more continuously, reducing reliance on scarce manual reviewers for preventable quality issues.

This redesign is materially different from Redesign A and Redesign B because it focuses on **fulfillment quality and closed-loop governance**, not eligibility routing or evidence intake.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

This redesign deliberately sacrifices some dispatch speed:

- The gate adds 6–18 hours for checked cases.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims could be harmed if the gate is applied too broadly.
- Quality-check capacity may become a new bottleneck if high-value volume rises.
- The gate may catch symptoms without fixing root causes unless feedback loops are disciplined.
- It does not solve upstream evidence missing or manual eligibility capacity by itself.
- It increases process complexity and requires clear segmentation to avoid slowing low-risk claims unnecessarily.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

The experiment sequence should test the redesigns in a way that avoids mistaking local improvement for global improvement. The key is to monitor end-to-end outcomes, not only activity-level cycle time.

## Stage 0: Baseline instrumentation and guardrails

Before scaling any redesign, create a segment-level baseline:

- Cycle time p50 and p90 by segment and path.
- Complaint/reopen rate within 30 and 60 days after close.
- Wrong replacement and reshipment rate.
- Service-credit exposure for Premium low-risk accessories.
- Abandonment rate after evidence requests.
- Repeated evidence request rate.
- R1, R2, and R3 exceptions.
- Manual reviewer utilization, fraud-review backlog, repair-quote delay, and quality-check utilization.
- Cost after rework, including reshipments, credits, and complaint handling.

Also replay the log against the normative Petri net and track fitness, percentage of fitting traces, precision, and rule violations.

## First pilot: Redesign A on Premium low-risk accessory claims

### Pilot scope

Start with Premium low-risk accessory claims, which are 18% of volume and have strict 24-hour service expectations.

Pilot changes:

- Segment-aware auto-screen rules.
- Fast lane for complete, low-risk accessory claims.
- Automated replacement part validation.
- Exception routing to manual review only when risk or evidence flags appear.
- Service-credit monitoring.

This pilot is attractive because the auto-screen rule update can be deployed relatively quickly, the segment is high visibility, and the process can preserve speed while testing segment-aware routing.

### Metrics to monitor

- Median and p90 cycle time.
- Percentage of claims closed within 24 hours.
- Complaint/reopen rate compared with the current 6% fast-path rate.
- Service-credit incidence.
- R2 exceptions.
- Manual-review inflow and reviewer utilization.
- Wrong replacement or reshipment signals.

### Stop/go thresholds

Go if:

- Median cycle time remains well below 24 hours.
- p90 cycle time does not worsen by more than about 20–30%.
- Complaint/reopen rate is stable or decreases by around 1–2 percentage points.
- Service credits do not increase.
- Manual reviewer utilization remains below roughly 82–85%.

Stop or pause if:

- Manual inflow rises sharply and reviewer utilization persistently exceeds 82–85%.
- Premium low-risk cycle time frequently exceeds 24 hours.
- Complaints or service credits increase materially.
- R2 exceptions rise instead of falling.

## Stage 2: Expand Redesign A to Standard low-risk and high-value routing

### Scope

If the Premium low-risk pilot works:

- Extend the low-risk fast lane to Standard low-risk accessories, adding status visibility.
- Begin controlled routing for high-value claims:
  - No fast approval above EUR 400 without manual or fraud review.
  - Premium high-value claims receive priority manual review.
  - Standard high-value claims receive manual review or repair quote depending on defect type.
- Pilot repair-quote price bands for common defects.

### Metrics to monitor

- High-value complaint/reopen rate compared with current 18–22% fast-path rates.
- Movement toward the 5–6% reviewed-path complaint range.
- R1 and R2 exceptions for high-value claims.
- Manual and fraud utilization.
- Repair-quote delay.
- Premium high-value p90 cycle time.
- Standard high-value status visibility and customer satisfaction.

### Stop/go thresholds

Go if:

- High-value complaint/reopen moves meaningfully toward reviewed-path levels, for example below 10–12% in early pilots.
- R1/R2 exceptions fall.
- Premium high-value p90 cycle time does not explode beyond the current reviewed-path range by a large margin.
- Manual and fraud utilization remains manageable.

Stop or pause if:

- Manual queues become persistently overloaded.
- Fraud review backlog becomes chronic, especially on Mondays or after campaigns.
- Premium high-value claims experience unacceptable delays.
- The process simply shifts high-value claims into repair quote without improving quote turnaround.

## Stage 3: Pilot Redesign B on unclear defect and missing-evidence cases

### Scope

Target the 22% unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment and claims that would otherwise enter `Request missing evidence`.

Pilot changes:

- Defect-type-specific evidence templates.
- Evidence completeness score.
- One targeted evidence request.
- Assisted channel for low digital literacy.
- Status visibility for Standard customers.
- Diagnostic triage pod for unclear defects.

### Metrics to monitor

- Repeated evidence request rate.
- Median cycle time for unclear-defect cases.
- Abandonment rate.
- Digital-assisted channel usage.
- Manual-review time spent on evidence loops.
- R2 exceptions.
- Customer complaints related to evidence requests.

### Stop/go thresholds

Go if:

- Repeated evidence requests fall toward the expected 30–45% reduction range.
- Unclear-defect median cycle time falls materially from 140 hours, for example toward an 80–110 hour range in early pilots.
- Abandonment does not increase by more than about 1–2 percentage points.
- Manual reviewers spend less time on repeated evidence loops.

Stop or pause if:

- Abandonment rises sharply, especially among low-digital customers.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims are delayed beyond 24 hours because of rigid evidence demands.
- The evidence portal creates new support queues without reducing manual evidence work.

## Stage 4: Pilot Redesign C on high-value dispatches and reopens

### Scope

Apply the closed-loop quality gate to:

- High-value replacement dispatches.
- Mid-risk dispatches with uncertain part compatibility.
- Reopened claims.
- Repair rework cases.

Pilot changes:

- 100% lightweight pre-dispatch gate for high-value cases.
- Automated part-match and sampling for low-risk accessories.
- Reopen must pass manual eligibility review before final close or denial.
- Root-cause feedback to auto-screen rules, evidence templates, and repair practices.
- Use underused quality inspectors to staff the gate.

### Metrics to monitor

- Wrong replacement rate.
- Reshipment rate.
- Dispatch-to-complaint p90.
- High-value complaint/reopen rate.
- High-value repair rework rate compared with the current 12.5%.
- Gate delay p50 and p90.
- Quality-check utilization.
- R3 compliance for reopened claims.
- Complaint-after-close deviations.

### Stop/go thresholds

Go if:

- Wrong replacement and reshipment rates fall.
- Dispatch-to-complaint p90 falls from the current long tail.
- High-value rework decreases.
- Gate delay remains mostly within the expected 6–18 hour range, with p90 not exceeding roughly 18–24 hours for high-value cases.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims are not materially delayed.

Stop or pause if:

- The quality gate becomes a chronic queue.
- Gate delay p90 exceeds acceptable service levels, especially for Premium cases.
- Complaints do not fall after controlling for case mix.
- Quality-check capacity is consumed without measurable downstream improvement.

## Sequencing logic

The sequence is deliberate:

1. **Redesign A first** because it uses the cheap, scalable auto screen and protects the most service-sensitive low-risk Premium segment. It also creates the segment lanes needed for later changes.
2. **Redesign B second** because evidence problems are upstream. If evidence loops are not fixed, manual reviewers will be overwhelmed when high-value routing becomes stricter.
3. **Redesign C third** because downstream quality gates work best once routing and evidence completeness are more stable. Otherwise the gate will simply catch the same upstream mistakes repeatedly.

If compliance pressure is urgent, a limited version of Redesign A for high-value claims and Redesign C for high-value dispatches can be accelerated in parallel, but only with explicit manual surge capacity and quality-check staffing.

## How to know whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

Improvements should be judged at the **end-to-end case level**, not at a single activity.

An improvement is not real if:

- Fast approval cycle time improves, but complaints, reopens, or reshipments increase later.
- Evidence requests decrease, but abandonment increases.
- Dispatch speed improves, but wrong replacement or service credits increase.
- Manual review time decreases, but fraud review, repair quote, or quality gate becomes overloaded.
- Fitting traces improve, but low-frequency noncompliant paths simply move to new variants.
- Cost before rework improves, but cost after rework does not.

To detect this, each experiment should compare:

- Pre-rework cost versus post-rework cost.
- Activity cycle time versus end-to-end p50 and p90 cycle time.
- Immediate completion versus 30-day and 60-day complaint/reopen rates.
- Compliance fitness versus actual R1, R2, and R3 exceptions.
- Segment-level outcomes rather than global averages.
- Resource utilization across manual review, fraud review, repair quote, quality check, evidence support, and escalation board.

The goal is not to make the average case faster. The goal is to make the process **fast where speed is safe, controlled where risk is high, evidence-complete where uncertainty is high, and closed-loop where quality failures would otherwise reappear downstream**.
