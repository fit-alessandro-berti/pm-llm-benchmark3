## 1. Target definition

**Primary target:** For an application that reaches its first Credit Review and is eventually activated under the historical operating policy, predict whether the activated account defaults within 90 days after its Account Activated event. The landmark prediction time is the timestamp of the first Credit Review; only events and values observable by that timestamp may be used. The unit is the original application, with linked transfer identities consolidated when linkage is reliable. The prediction horizon is 90 days from activation, not 90 days from application start. The population and estimand are therefore the historical-policy, eventually activated applications reaching the landmark, with a matured and ascertainable outcome: the conditional predictive risk P(default within 90 days after activation | prefix at first Credit Review, eventual activation under the observed policy). This is a selected-population predictive estimand, not a causal or all-applicant risk. Report its activation and label ascertainment coverage alongside every performance estimate. Cases without a first review are outside this landmark model and must be counted and described, not silently dropped from the overall application funnel.

This is a defensible primary target because it preserves the warehouse outcome's actual meaning and permits evaluation against observed labels. It can support risk ordering among accounts selected for activation by the current process, with the clear limitation that eventual activation is not known at scoring time. For application-time decisioning it is at most one input to a policy that also predicts or specifies activation; it cannot alone answer the lender's request to predict default for every application. Rejected and withdrawn applications have no observed activated-account default outcome.

A credible alternative is a **multi-state / competing-outcome model for all applications**. It would estimate transitions from application/review to rejection, withdrawal, transfer, activation, and then to default or survival through activation +90 days. This answers “what pathway and observed outcome is this application likely to reach under the current process?” It does not turn rejection into non-default and requires explicit treatment of unlinked transfers and right censoring. A policy-specific counterfactual such as “would this applicant default if approved and activated?” is a different target: it needs outcomes under interventions not observed for rejected cases and stronger identification assumptions (consistency, exchangeability, positivity, and a specified activation policy).

## 2. Cohort construction

Use the application as the unit, with a stable cross-platform entity key where available. Deduplicate only migration duplicates confirmed by event identifiers or an audited exact-duplicate rule; preserve legitimate repeated document requests and reviews. Link transferred cases before determining activation or outcome. Do not treat an unlinked transfer as a negative. Keep the original source application date for temporal splitting.

For the primary landmark analysis, include starts in the stated observation window that reach a first Credit Review, have a valid as-of prefix, are eventually activated under the historical process, and have an ascertainable label by the analysis cutoff. Include only events with timestamps no later than the configured cutoff and only feature values available by the landmark. A conservative operational maturity rule is activation timestamp +90 days +35 days for feed delay, and the label must also have label_available_at no later than the cutoff. A label missing after this buffer is “feed missing / status unknown,” not a negative. Cases with an activation after the cutoff or before the 125-day buffer expires are pending, not labeled. The extraction counts below are the supplied disjoint funnel; further losses due to absent first-review landmarks or bad records must be reported from the raw event data.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

- **Ordinary right censoring:** Open applications have not yet reached a terminal process state by extraction. For time-to-event analyses, censor process follow-up at the last reliable observation / extraction time. For the 90-day binary target, they are not negative examples. Activated accounts with fewer than 90 days of follow-up are also right-censored for the default horizon.
- **Delayed labels:** The 90-day outcome clock begins at activation. Add the observed 7–35 day feed latency to the 90-day horizon. Use a fixed 35-day maturity buffer for a conservative complete-label snapshot, preserve label_available_at, and replay historical backtests using only labels actually available by each simulated cutoff. The buffer reduces premature “no default” assignments; it does not prove that every absent feed is merely delayed.
- **Selection through activation:** Activation is an outcome of the application process, not ordinary censoring. The primary model is explicitly conditional on eventual activation under the observed policy. Do not apply censoring weights to pretend rejected cases had a default outcome. If estimating application-wide pathway probabilities, model activation and competing process outcomes separately. Inverse-probability-of-activation weighting would still require measured predictors of selection, positivity, and exchangeability; it cannot recover an undefined observed outcome for rejected applications without a counterfactual target and assumptions.
- **Transfer:** Treat transfer as a competing process event if the target is the original platform's path. If the estimand follows the applicant across platforms, linked transfer cases can continue follow-up under the resolved entity ID. Unlinked transfers are loss to follow-up / selectively unobserved, not non-default. With linkage for only about 60%, report linked versus unlinked transfer rates and outcomes, and bound or sensitivity-test the remaining cases.
- **Permanently missing feeds:** A missing feed beyond activation +125 days is a suspected missing outcome, not automatically a censored non-event. Compare missingness by source system, partner origin, channel, region, activation date, and prefix risk. A complete-case primary estimate is transparent but may be biased if missingness depends on risk after conditioning on recorded data.

Primary analysis: train and evaluate the binary predictor on activated cases with mature, available labels, stratified/reported by policy epoch and source. For survival-oriented analysis, use activated accounts with activation as time origin and default as the event, administratively censor at known follow-up, and use IPCW or a survival estimator only when censoring is conditionally independent given available covariates. For the current fixed-horizon classification, keep only fully matured outcomes and do not treat pending cases as controls.

Sensitivity analyses should include (1) inverse-probability-of-label-observation weighting among activated cases using pre-landmark and source variables, with weight truncation and overlap diagnostics; (2) best/worst-case or tipping-point bounds that vary the default rate among missing-feed and unlinked-transfer accounts; and (3) a linked-transfer-only versus all-source analysis. Weighting addresses missingness only under a conditional-MAR/independent-censoring assumption and adequate positivity; it does not solve MNAR feed loss. Calibration and performance should be shown both weighted and unweighted when weights are defensible.

Observable and partially testable assumptions include timestamp order, label delay distributions among observed feeds, linkage rates, duplicate frequency, covariate overlap, and whether missingness is associated with recorded fields. Conditional independence of label loss/censoring can be probed but never established from observed data alone. The outcomes rejected applicants would have under hypothetical activation, selection exchangeability, and the missing outcomes of unlinked transfers are fundamentally untestable here. They require external follow-up, randomized/policy variation, or explicit sensitivity assumptions.

## 4. Prefix extraction

The primary prefix ends at the **first arrival at Credit Review**. Sort by event timestamp and a stable event sequence key; include events at or before the landmark, never events after it. This is an operationally recognizable early point and occurs before the final accept/reject result in the stated process. Verify this ordering empirically and quarantine cases where decision events precede the landmark due to migration or bad timestamps.

Also run a fixed event-count diagnostic at lengths such as 2, 3, and 5. Define each such prediction time as the timestamp of the kth event; if a case closes before k events, mark it “short prefix / terminal before k” and do not pad it with future or synthetic events. Compare only cohorts eligible at each k, since event-count cohorts differ. For an elapsed-time diagnostic, use a fixed window such as events in the first 24 hours after Application Started, with the prediction timestamp fixed at start +24 hours; cases closing sooner remain competing terminal pathways, not artificially extended prefixes. A variant with “up to 24 hours, score at last observed event” has a variable prediction time and must be reported separately.

Repeated document loops are valid behavior: retain each request/return iteration, derive loop counts and elapsed times, and guard against migration duplicates using audited identifiers. Create a versioned activity mapping for the 15 Oct 2025 rename, retaining both original and canonical activity names for audit. Do not infer missing events or collapse repeated reviews merely to make traces look regular.

## 5. Process-aware features

- **Static case attributes:** case:channel, case:region, and requested amount as first known at/before the landmark; preserve missingness and units. Do not use a later corrected value unless its effective timestamp shows it was known by prediction time.
- **Prefix control flow:** canonical activity sequence/variant, counts by activity, current activity, transitions/DFG edges observed within the prefix, time since start, time since previous event, number of review returns, document-request count, and whether the process is in a rework loop. Keep sequence-derived features within each case prefix. Discover reference process models or common variants on training data only, then apply the frozen mapping to later periods.
- **Timing and workload:** inter-event gaps, time spent between review and document loops, business-calendar indicators, event hour/day, queue or workload estimates built from historical event arrival/closure data available before the landmark, and resource handoff counts. Queue features must be computed point-in-time, not from final-period aggregates.
- **Resource:** resource ID or role/team at events in the prefix, handoff count, and historical experience calculated from events preceding the prediction time. The stored extraction-time experience is not valid as-is. Consider privacy, sparse resources, and changing team composition; unknown/new resource categories need explicit handling.
- **Score updates:** latest risk_score whose update timestamp is no later than the landmark, optionally with prior value and change computed from prefix updates. Preserve its timestamp and update process. Never take the final non-null score from the complete case.
- **Calendar/cohort:** application start week/month, policy epoch (pre/post 1 Jul 2025), days since policy change, migration epoch (pre/post 15 Oct 2025), and source system. These help diagnose and calibrate shifts; they do not remove confounding or guarantee future stability. Avoid post-landmark aggregates and target encodings unless computed with strictly earlier training data.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| final_limit | Populated after final decision; directly reflects approval and policy | None for first-Credit-Review scoring unless a separately timestamped limit truly existed by then | Drop | Post-landmark decision information leaks process selection and decision policy |
| decision_reason | Populated at/after Accept or Reject and can encode terminal decision | Only a reason already explicitly recorded by the landmark, verified by event-time provenance | Drop for primary prefix | Usually direct information about future process outcome |
| collection_status | Updated after activation and may directly reveal default | None before activation/landmark | Drop | Near-direct target leakage |
| Future events (Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded) | Reveal approval, activation, or outcome | Event timestamp <= prediction timestamp and event genuinely observed then | Drop future rows; retain only prefix rows | Full-trace features make the score retrospective |
| documents_total nightly snapshot | May include documents uploaded later on the same calendar day | Use an event-time document count reconstructed strictly through landmark timestamp, or lag snapshot until its actual availability | Repair or drop | Calendar-day snapshot is not necessarily point-in-time safe |
| risk_score | Updated multiple times; final value may reflect later documents/decisions | Latest version with an auditable update timestamp <= landmark | Repair | A final case-level value leaks updates after scoring time |
| Resource experience | Stored at extraction, so includes future tenure/work | Recompute from events/tenure known as of each event or landmark | Repair; otherwise drop | Extraction-time experience carries future information and time drift |
| label_available_at | Can expose whether/when outcome feed arrived and correlate with outcome/source | Use only for label maturity, censoring, and audit; never as predictor | Drop from features | Post-outcome metadata creates ascertainment leakage |
| default_90d | Repeated on events when known; direct target | Use as y only after entity-level consistency and maturity checks | Target only | Never include in feature matrix, including on early events copied from the case |
| Source system / migration aliases | Can identify era and duplicate/renamed event artifacts | Source and event name known at the landmark; version mapping frozen and audited | Keep with epoch checks | Useful for shift monitoring but may encode migration-specific artifacts |

## 7. Validation design

Use case start time for all partitions and never split events from one linked entity across partitions. A practical initial rolling-origin design is train on starts from 1 Jan 2024 through 31 Mar 2025, validate on 1 Apr–30 Jun 2025, and use 1 Jul–14 Oct 2025 as a post-policy-change validation window. Reserve 15 Oct 2025 onward as a migration-era temporal test, but score only cases whose outcomes satisfy the 90+35-day maturity rule by the analysis cutoff. The latest cohorts will have incomplete outcome ascertainment; report their eligible-label fraction, source mix, and effective sample size. Repeat as rolling quarterly backtests, with a label cutoff fixed at each historical scoring date and an embargo sufficient for the 125-day outcome/feed window. Do not train on future outcomes merely because they are available at today's extraction when simulating an earlier deployment.

Keep policy epoch and migration epoch separate: the 1 July change may alter both activation selection and risk relationships, while the 15 October migration may alter event semantics and duplication. Report results by epoch, channel, region, source/partner status, and relevant operational groups, with uncertainty intervals. Check feature and prefix distribution shift, activation rates, label rates, missing-feed rates, and linkage rates. Compare no-update, intercept/calibration-only, and refit strategies as separate backtests. Fit calibration only on a prior matured calibration window; never calibrate against the final test window.

For the matured activated binary target, report ROC AUC and PR AUC with the base rate, Brier score/log loss, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/reliability plot, and decision-threshold metrics tied to an explicitly stated capacity or cost scenario. Include confidence intervals (case-level bootstrap respecting time/entity clusters) and subgroup calibration, not only pooled discrimination. For survival models, add time-dependent AUC, calibration at 90 days, and IPCW Brier score; for competing-risk pathway estimates, evaluate cumulative incidence with appropriate competing-risk methods. Do not report accuracy alone. Random case splits mix pre/post-policy and pre/post-migration behavior, can leak repeated entities, overrepresent the selected matured activated cohort, and hide prospective degradation.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The study can estimate predictive association for matured, observed outcomes among accounts activated under the historical process, subject to selection and feed ascertainment. It cannot estimate default risk for rejected or withdrawn applications as if they had activated, establish what those cases would have done under approval, or claim a causal effect of approving/declining. It also cannot guarantee calibration under a changed activation policy, a partner pathway with systematically missing labels, a changed collection practice, or an altered applicant mix.

Deployment can change the data-generating process in several ways: (1) using the score to approve/decline changes who is activated and therefore which labels become observable; (2) staff may request different documents or route high scores to extra review, changing prefixes, timing, and outcomes; (3) applicants or brokers may alter behavior in response to decisions or score-related friction; (4) collections teams may intervene earlier on high-risk accounts, changing realized default rates; and (5) a system/partner migration may change event capture and outcome-feed completeness. Monitor selection, maturity, linkage, and calibration after launch, and version every policy and activity mapping. Any application-wide claim requires either a separately defined competing-outcome target or a justified counterfactual design.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

    import pandas as pd
    import numpy as np
    import pm4py
    
    DATA = "applications_events.csv"
    AS_OF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15", tz="UTC")
    CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
    
    # Parse times as UTC and retain raw activity names for migration audit.
    df = pd.read_csv(DATA)
    for col in [TS, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
        if col in df:
            df[col] = pd.to_datetime(df[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    df = df.loc[df[TS].notna() & (df[TS] <= AS_OF)].copy()
    df["activity_raw"] = df[ACT].astype("string")
    
    # Replace with the audited, versioned rename table from the migration record.
    ALIASES = {"Old KYC Name": "Identity/KYC Check",
               "Old Review Name": "Credit Review"}
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].replace(ALIASES)
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
    # Apply only after confirming these are migration-generated copies.
    dup_key = [CASE, ACT, TS, "source_system"]
    df = df.drop_duplicates(dup_key, keep="first")
    
    # Standard PM4Py dataframe format; preserve business columns alongside it.
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df.copy(), case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
    )
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(log_df)
    # Fixed-k diagnostic; the primary landmark below is first Credit Review.
    three_event_prefixes = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, length=3)
    
    ACTIVATION = "Account Activated"
    REJECT, WITHDRAW, TRANSFER = "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"
    REVIEW = "Credit Review"
    TERMINALS = {REJECT, WITHDRAW, TRANSFER}
    
    # Resolve linked cross-platform identities before this step where possible.
    rows = []
    for cid, g in df.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        g = g.sort_values(TS, kind="stable")
        starts = g.loc[g[ACT].eq("Application Started"), TS]
        start = starts.iloc[0] if len(starts) else g[TS].iloc[0]
        activated = g.loc[g[ACT].eq(ACTIVATION), TS]
        at = activated.iloc[0] if len(activated) else pd.NaT
        term = g.loc[g[ACT].isin(TERMINALS), [ACT, TS]]
        terminal = term.iloc[0][ACT] if len(term) else None
        terminal_time = term.iloc[0][TS] if len(term) else pd.NaT
        labels = pd.to_numeric(g["default_90d"], errors="coerce").dropna()
        label_times = g.loc[g["default_90d"].notna(),
                            "label_available_at"].dropna()
        label = labels.iloc[-1] if len(labels) else np.nan
        label_at = label_times.max() if len(label_times) else pd.NaT
        conflict = labels.nunique() > 1
        mature_by = at + pd.Timedelta(days=125) if pd.notna(at) else pd.NaT
        if pd.notna(at):
            if (not conflict and pd.notna(label) and pd.notna(label_at)
                    and mature_by <= AS_OF and label_at <= AS_OF):
                status = "activated_mature_labeled"
            elif mature_by > AS_OF:
                status = "activated_pending_maturity"
            else:
                status = "activated_feed_missing_or_conflict"
        elif terminal == TRANSFER:
            status = "transferred_unresolved_or_competing"
        elif terminal == REJECT:
            status = "rejected_competing"
        elif terminal == WITHDRAW:
            status = "withdrawn_competing"
        else:
            status = "open_right_censored"
        reviews = g.loc[g[ACT].eq(REVIEW), TS]
        rows.append({CASE: cid, "case_start": start, "activation_time": at,
                     "terminal": terminal, "terminal_time": terminal_time,
                     "label": label, "label_at": label_at,
                     "label_conflict": conflict, "status": status,
                     "landmark": reviews.iloc[0] if len(reviews) else pd.NaT})
    cases = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    print(cases["status"].value_counts(dropna=False))
    assert len(cases) == df[CASE].nunique()
    
    # Reconstruct every feature using events no later than first Credit Review.
    static_cols = ["case:channel", "case:region", "case:requested_amount"]
    feature_rows = []
    for cid, landmark in cases.set_index(CASE)["landmark"].dropna().items():
        g = df.loc[df[CASE].eq(cid)].sort_values(TS, kind="stable")
        p = g.loc[g[TS] <= landmark].copy()
        if p.empty or p[ACT].iloc[-1] != REVIEW:
            continue
        f = {CASE: cid, "prefix_len": len(p),
             "elapsed_hours": (landmark - p[TS].iloc[0]).total_seconds() / 3600,
             "doc_requests": int(p[ACT].str.contains(
                 "Document", case=False, na=False).sum()),
             "review_visits": int(p[ACT].eq(REVIEW).sum()),
             "handoffs": int(p["org:resource"].nunique())
                         if "org:resource" in p else 0,
             "activity_sequence": tuple(p[ACT].astype(str))}
        for c in static_cols:
            if c in p:
                values = p[c].dropna()
                f[c] = values.iloc[0] if len(values) else np.nan
        if "risk_score" in p:
            scored = p.loc[p["risk_score"].notna(), [TS, "risk_score"]]
            f["risk_score_asof"] = (
                scored.iloc[-1]["risk_score"] if len(scored) else np.nan)
        feature_rows.append(f)
    features = pd.DataFrame(feature_rows)
    model_table = features.merge(cases, on=CASE, validate="one_to_one")
    model_table = model_table.loc[
        model_table["status"].eq("activated_mature_labeled")
        & model_table["label_conflict"].eq(False)
    ].copy()
    
    # Hard audit: labels, final-decision fields, and post-activation fields stay out.
    forbidden = {"default_90d", "label", "label_at", "label_available_at",
                 "final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status"}
    assert forbidden.isdisjoint(features.columns)
    assert model_table["landmark"].notna().all()
    assert (model_table["landmark"] <= AS_OF).all()
    
    # Temporal partitions use original case start, not a random case split.
    train = model_table.loc[model_table["case_start"] < "2025-04-01"]
    valid = model_table.loc[model_table["case_start"].between(
        "2025-04-01", "2025-06-30", inclusive="both")]
    post_policy = model_table.loc[model_table["case_start"].between(
        "2025-07-01", "2025-10-14", inclusive="both")]
    migration_test = model_table.loc[model_table["case_start"] >= "2025-10-15"]
    print({"train": len(train), "valid": len(valid),
           "post_policy": len(post_policy), "migration_test": len(migration_test)})
