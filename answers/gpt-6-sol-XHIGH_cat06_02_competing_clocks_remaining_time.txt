## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Shared branch arithmetic.** From Friday 15:30 to Monday 08:00 is 64.5 calendar hours. The earliest risk-priority chemistry start is about Friday 16:42: one analyst frees at 15:54 at the earliest and takes the urgent sample; the other frees at 16:42. Even a minimum 1.5 h B-417 service then has 12 minutes left at Friday 18:00, so routine chemistry cannot finish before Monday 08:12. A typical risk-priority chemistry result is around Monday 09:00–12:00. Under FIFO, four queued samples precede B-417; using their service times as the proxy above gives a typical result around Monday 13:00–18:00, with a late-service Tuesday spill possible. These ranges include queue waiting, service, and the Friday-night/weekend pause, rather than treating all of that time as service.

Primary microbiology incubation ends Sunday 12:00–20:00. With on-call authorization, the first usable result can be Sunday about 13:00–23:00; without it, about Monday 08:30–09:30, subject to a small Monday staffing queue. QA's start is **max(usable chemistry-result time, usable microbiology-result time)**. Thus Sunday microbiology cannot make this batch ready for QA on Sunday: chemistry still waits for Monday. In the ordinary path, the branches are often close under risk priority, while FIFO chemistry generally dominates. QA can process some of its five current cases before Monday, but new arrivals and variable service leave its join-time queue uncertain. Once assigned, review is typically about 1–3.5 h; ordinary Monday waiting is roughly 0–4 h, with a longer congestion tail. Saturday QA hours help clear other cases but are not an opportunity to review B-417 before its Monday join.

- **Smooth path:** Chemistry waits for its place and finishes mainly Monday 09:00–18:00, with risk priority earlier than FIFO. Incubation runs continuously until Sunday; first readout is Sunday with on-call staffing or Monday morning without it. The maximum of those result times gives a mainly Monday 09:00–18:00 join. Add Monday QA waiting of about 0–4 h, roughly 1–3.5 h of review, no optional rework, and 0.25–0.75 h for continuous Electronic Release. A late join or QA queue can push acceptance past Monday 20:00 and resume it Tuesday 08:00.

- **Routine Monday microbiology readout:** Chemistry waiting/service is as on the smooth path. Incubation is technically finished Sunday, but the staffed readout waits through Sunday night to Monday 08:00–09:30. Join is the later of that readout and chemistry, commonly Monday morning for risk priority or Monday afternoon for FIFO. QA waiting/service and final release follow the smooth path, with no inherent rework. The deferred readout adds little terminal delay when chemistry is already later.

- **Chemistry rerun:** Chemistry first waits and completes as above; only then does a borderline result start a priority rerun of 1–3 laboratory hours. An early Monday result can yield a Monday rerun result, while a result near 18:00 pauses the rerun to Tuesday 08:00. Microbiology follows its ordinary Sunday/Monday route unless it too is abnormal. The join is the maximum of the final chemistry result and microbiology result, usually set by the rerun. QA then waits roughly 0–4 h ordinarily, reviews for roughly 1–3.5 h, and may investigate because of the rerun; without that extra path, release follows in 0.25–0.75 h. With a confirmed critical defect, record rejection in 0.5–1 h instead.

- **Microbiology confirmation:** Chemistry retains its Monday result range (or later if it separately reruns). Primary incubation runs to Sunday, but the indication is identified at the first staffed readout: Sunday about 13:00–23:00 with on-call, or Monday about 08:30–09:30 without it. Add 20–30 continuous hours **from that readout**, then another 0.5–1.5 h staffed readout; a technical finish after Monday 18:00 waits for Tuesday staffing. With no weekend on-call, the confirmed result is commonly Tuesday 08:30–17:00; Sunday on-call can allow a Monday result, otherwise a Tuesday one. Join is max(chemistry, confirmed microbiology), now usually microbiology. QA waiting and 1–3.5 h review follow at the applicable weekday opening, often Tuesday. An indication also raises investigation risk. Release takes 0.25–0.75 h after acceptance, or rejection recording takes 0.5–1 h if a critical defect is established.

- **QA query or documentation investigation:** Chemistry and microbiology first follow whichever branch clocks apply; their maximum establishes the join. After ordinary QA queue waiting and an initial review, a query adds 4–12 QA working hours; a documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours. A Monday late-afternoon query can consume the hours until 20:00, pause overnight, and resume Tuesday 08:00; 20 working hours can reach Wednesday. Allow a further short resumed review and possible queue wait before acceptance. Then allow 0.25–0.75 h for release, or 0.5–1 h for recording a proven critical-defect rejection. A query and investigation may both occur, so their combined tail is longer.

- **Laboratory investigation:** Chemistry waiting/service and microbiology incubation/readout still determine the initial join by their maximum, possibly after rerun or confirmation. QA first waits and reviews during open hours, then the laboratory investigation adds 3–7 **calendar days** rather than 3–7 QA working days. A Monday/Tuesday opening therefore points to Thursday through the following Tuesday for its result. QA's final disposition must again occur in QA hours, with possible re-queue and review; continuous electronic release then takes 0.25–0.75 h, or critical-defect rejection recording 0.5–1 h. This path can overlap a query, rerun, contamination indication, and the excursion flag.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **QA investigation path — path uncertainty:** Whether review opens no investigation, a documentation-only investigation, or a 3–7-calendar-day laboratory investigation has the largest effect on the upper tail.
2. **Chemistry queue order and Friday spillover — queue and calendar uncertainty:** FIFO versus risk priority changes how many of the four waiting samples precede B-417; Friday's 18:00 closure converts a small service difference into an overnight/weekend wait and sometimes a Tuesday result.
3. **Temperature-excursion flag — dependence and observation uncertainty:** A confirmed flag raises both branches' anomaly risks and QA investigation risk together, making combined delays more likely than independent-event arithmetic suggests.
4. **Microbiology indication and staffed readout — path and calendar uncertainty:** The first readout occurs Sunday only with authorization; an indication starts a new 20–30 h incubation clock and may require a Tuesday readout.
5. **QA queue at the future join — queue uncertainty:** Five cases now do not determine the Monday queue. Overdispersed arrivals, Friday/Saturday completions, two Monday reviewers, and variable service determine whether review starts promptly or waits many hours.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The **first microbiology readout and its recorded result** is the single most useful update. A normal result fixes the microbiology branch at Sunday with on-call or Monday morning without it, removes the confirmation clock, and leaves chemistry plus QA as the main remaining constraints. A contamination indication starts 20–30 h of new continuous incubation **after** that readout, then another staffed readout; it usually moves the join toward Tuesday and increases investigation and possible rejection risk. If the expected Monday readout itself does not appear, extend the staffed-readout/queue delay rather than treating Sunday technical readiness as a usable result.

The secondary trigger is resolution of the temperature-excursion flag: confirmation raises the joint rerun, contamination, and investigation tails; clearance lowers them.
