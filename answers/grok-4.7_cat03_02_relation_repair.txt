## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

Observed E2O and O2O rows are kept in every bundle. No new events or objects are introduced. A hole is a candidate for repair only when a documented default fails and an existing identifier still points at an existing object. Completion of a lifecycle is not a goal.

Hard invariants. Relation endpoints must be existing event and object identifiers. Qualifiers stay directional in the observed vocabulary: `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`, with the container or commercial actor as source. `Create Order` keeps exactly one order. `Record Payment` keeps exactly one payment. Time must remain feasible: create, then add, then pack, then dispatch, then any confirm, then invoice, then payment. An inferred O2O edge is not a second observation of the E2O edge that motivated it.

Strong defaults. `Add Item` expects one order and one item; the three add events already meet that and match `contains`. `Pack Item` expects one item and one package; an item-only row is legal during a scanner outage and may be reconciled from the scanner reference. `Dispatch Package` expects one package and one delivery; a bulk event may cover several packages, but only when the carrier reference agrees. `Issue Invoice` expects one invoice and one or more orders. `Record Payment` normally cites one invoice, while unapplied cash remains legal. `Confirm Delivery` may cite one delivery, several deliveries, or only an order when the carrier id is degraded.

Weak heuristics. Identifier quality is ranked as exact match, then a structured prefix already used by reconciled rows, then a trailing `?`, then a `*` wildcard. `PK7-1` with PK-7 and `PK8-1` with PK-8 make `PK8-2` evidence for PK-8. `N-420` equals DEL-X. `N-421?` is close to DEL-Y and is penalized for the uncertainty mark. `N-42*` matches both carrier refs and therefore identifies neither. Shared customer C17 and route NORTH support compatibility only; they do not choose among ORD-A, ORD-B, DEL-X, and DEL-Y. Equal timestamps make two dispatches simultaneous, which is compatible with two shipments or with a split recording. Amount alignment is a hypothesis generator: declared values 130 and 100, invoice total 230, and payments 100 and 130 line up. That alignment does not prove that INV-44 bills ORD-B or that PAY-2 settles INV-44, because fees, discounts, taxes, partial payment, and unapplied cash can reproduce the same figures.

Evidence against a repair. A candidate loses when it breaks an exact cardinality, contradicts an exact reference, or stacks two uncertain inferences and then treats the second as confirmation. Near-tied candidates, especially on ev11, stay unresolved. Local scores are rechecked at bundle level, because putting IT-B1 into PK-8 changes whether DEL-Y still carries only ORD-A goods and therefore changes what a joint confirmation would mean.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

Bundle A is the conservative, minimum-change repair and the one selected below. It adds P1–P4 only. ev08 is reconciled to PK-8, and PK-8 then packs both IT-A2 and IT-B1. ev10 is provisionally bound to DEL-Y, and DEL-Y carries PK-8. ev11 stays an order-level portal row for ORD-A; DEL-X, DEL-Y, and ORD-B remain unresolved. INV-44 continues to bill only the observed order, ORD-A. PAY-2 stays unapplied. The physical story is coherent enough to use and still incomplete on purpose: ORD-A is a split shipment whose cable half shares a package with ORD-B; ORD-B is packed and dispatched only through that shared package; neither delivery is confirmed; the invoice may be only partly paid. Those open ends are legal under the scanner, portal, and unapplied-cash defaults.

Bundle B is the consolidated commercial reading. It keeps P1–P4, then provisionally adds P8, P9, and P10 together, plus P11–P14. Both deliveries are acknowledged by the `N-42*` portal row, and ORD-B is included because its only item rides the shared package. INV-44 is treated as a joint bill for the two declared values, and PAY-2 closes the residual after PAY-1. The full lifecycle is then create and add for each order, pack into PK-7 or the shared PK-8, dispatch on DEL-X and DEL-Y, one joint confirm, one joint invoice, and two payments. That lifecycle is internally consistent and still hypothetical at every commercial join. If P1 is wrong, ORD-B is not on DEL-Y, and P10 becomes much weaker while a two-delivery confirm of ORD-A can still stand. If the amount alignment is coincidental, Bundle B manufactures a settled joint invoice.

Bundle C separates those layers. It keeps P1–P4 and adds only P8 and P9, leaving P10–P14 unresolved. Deliveries both move from dispatch to confirm, which matches a carrier-batch reading of `N-42*` once ORD-A’s items sit on both packages. ORD-B still has no confirm, no invoice, and no payment, so a delivered package is not rewritten as an acknowledged and billed order. This bundle is the right comparison when the operational wildcard is believable and the monetary arithmetic is not allowed to decide.

Across A and B, the fragile point is the same: P2 and P4 repeat P1 and P3. They should move together. Dropping P1 while keeping P3 leaves DEL-Y carrying only IT-A2, so a later joint confirm is an ORD-A split shipment and is not evidence for ORD-B.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Order. Under Bundle A, ORD-A still runs Create Order, two Add Item events, Confirm Delivery, and Issue Invoice. ORD-B runs Create Order and Add Item, and touches logistics only through IT-B1. The missing confirm and invoice on ORD-B are open tails, which is acceptable. No order-level cardinality is expanded. Confirm and invoice remain supported by observed E2O and, for the invoice, by observed `bills` on ORD-A. Nothing in A adds a second order onto those events.

Item. IT-A1 and IT-A2 already have add and pack events aligned with `contains` and `packs`. IT-B1 already has add and pack; P1 gives the pack event a package. The item OC sequence does not gain an event. Support is one-sided for the new package link: the item was observed on ev08, while the package was inferred. Each add event remains one order and one item.

Package. PK-7’s lifecycle is the observed pack of IT-A1 and dispatch on DEL-X, with E2O and O2O agreeing. PK-8 already packs IT-A2 and is dispatched by ev10; P1 adds a second pack event one minute later. Two items in one package respect a normal one-to-many pack. The suspicious part is cross-order contents, not the count. Pack→Pack on PK-8 is a second scan, and it will look like repetition in a package directly-follows graph. P2 does not independently confirm P1.

Delivery. DEL-X is a clean one-package delivery: ev09, exact `N-420`, and `carries` agree. DEL-Y is eventless before repair. P3 gives it one dispatch and no confirm. That orphan tail is intentional. A delivery with two packages is not created. P4 repeats P3 and must not be counted as separate evidence.

Invoice. INV-44 is issued for ORD-A and receives PAY-1. Total 230 against declared value 130, and payment 100 against total 230, are both already in the observed log and can be legitimate fees or a partial payment. Bundle A leaves that unexplained on purpose. Adding ORD-B would be a new many-order bill, which rule 6 allows and the arithmetic does not establish. Observed E2O and `bills` support only ORD-A.

Payment. PAY-1 is a one-event payment tied to INV-44 by ev13 and `settles`. PAY-2 is a one-event orphan under A. That matches unapplied cash. Two payments on one invoice would be a normal many-to-one if P13 were accepted later; it is not accepted here. ev14 still has exactly one payment.

The circular pair is P1 with P2, and P3 with P4. The scanner string and the carrier string are the evidence. The O2O rows are a second encoding for the object graph, useful so static structure matches the repaired events, and they are withdrawn if the corresponding E2O row is withdrawn. Customer, route, and the sum 230 were not used to justify Bundle A.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Event counts never change, because no bundle adds events. What changes is object incidence, directly-follows structure, and which objects are eligible for flattening.

Unrepaired, the order OC-DFG is Create Order → Add Item → Add Item → Confirm Delivery → Issue Invoice on ORD-A, and Create Order → Add Item on ORD-B. The package graph has Pack Item → Dispatch Package for PK-7 and for PK-8. The delivery graph contains only DEL-X at Dispatch Package. DEL-Y contributes no event. Confirm Delivery touches Order only, so there is no Dispatch → Confirm edge. Invoice runs Issue Invoice → Record Payment once. PAY-2 never meets the invoice. At type level, Pack Item already links Item and Package through ev06 and ev07, and Dispatch Package already links Package and Delivery through ev09, so the ET–OT skeleton is largely present while DEL-Y and PAY-2 are isolated objects. The OTG follows the observed pairs: order–item, item–package, package–delivery, invoice–order, payment–invoice.

Bundle A leaves every order trace unchanged, so equivalent-execution clustering around Order still separates the long ORD-A trace from the short ORD-B trace. The package OC-DFG gains Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package on PK-8. That self-succession is the main new fragment and can be misread as rework. Unique packages at Pack Item stay {PK-7, PK-8}; total package incidences at that activity rise by one. Dispatch Package’s unique deliveries rise from {DEL-X} to {DEL-X, DEL-Y}, and delivery flattening changes from a single one-event trace to two identical one-event traces. Confirm Delivery is still absent from both. Type-level OC-DFG and ET–OT edges barely move; object-level support on Package–Delivery rises from one dispatch event to two. Invoice, payment, and order flattenings match the unrepaired log. Clustering of orders is therefore stable, while clustering of deliveries becomes “two dispatched, none confirmed.”

Bundle B keeps that package self-succession and the second delivery, and it adds a large commercial fragment. Confirm Delivery grows the edges Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery on both DEL-X and DEL-Y, and it co-relates Order with Delivery, a new ET–OT interaction. ORD-B’s flattened trace becomes Create Order, Add Item, Confirm Delivery, Issue Invoice, close to ORD-A except for one fewer Add Item, so near-equivalent order clustering starts to merge them. Issue Invoice’s order incidence rises from one to two without a new event. Record Payment → Record Payment appears on INV-44, and the invoice looks settled because 100+130 equals 230, an appearance the arithmetic does not prove. Payment flattening itself stays one event per payment object; the damage is on the invoice graph and the OTG bill and settle links. If an analyst then walks O2O from the mixed package up to both orders, ev10 is copied onto ORD-A and ORD-B and one physical dispatch is double-counted in object-weighted frequencies.

Bundle C sits between them: delivery flattening gains Dispatch → Confirm twice, order clustering stays separated, and the invoice remains a partial-payment object. The edge that appears only in B, and disappears again if the commercial hypothesis is dropped, is Issue Invoice and Confirm Delivery on ORD-B together with the second payment on INV-44.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Expected columns after any rename from the source extract:
# events: ocel:eid, ocel:activity, ocel:timestamp
# objects: ocel:oid, ocel:type
# relations: ocel:eid, ocel:oid, ocel:qualifier
# o2o: ocel:oid, ocel:oid_2, ocel:qualifier

events_raw = events.copy()
objects_raw = objects.copy()
relations_raw = relations.copy()
o2o_raw = o2o.copy()

e2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},
])
o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

relations_repaired = pd.concat([relations_raw, e2o_additions], ignore_index=True)
o2o_repaired = pd.concat([o2o_raw, o2o_additions], ignore_index=True)

assert set(relations_repaired["ocel:eid"]) <= set(events_raw["ocel:eid"])
assert set(relations_repaired["ocel:oid"]) <= set(objects_raw["ocel:oid"])
assert set(o2o_repaired["ocel:oid"]) <= set(objects_raw["ocel:oid"])
assert set(o2o_repaired["ocel:oid_2"]) <= set(objects_raw["ocel:oid"])

e2o_key = ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
o2o_key = ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
assert not relations_repaired.duplicated(e2o_key).any()
assert not o2o_repaired.duplicated(o2o_key).any()

# Unresolved candidates must stay out of the selected repair.
assert not ((relations_repaired["ocel:eid"] == "ev11") & relations_repaired["ocel:oid"].isin(["DEL-X", "DEL-Y", "ORD-B"])).any()
assert not ((relations_repaired["ocel:eid"] == "ev12") & (relations_repaired["ocel:oid"] == "ORD-B")).any()
assert not ((relations_repaired["ocel:eid"] == "ev14") & (relations_repaired["ocel:oid"] == "INV-44")).any()

# VERSION-DEPENDENT: PM4Py OCEL constructor signature and required columns
# differ by release. PM4Py 2.2/2.3 style is shown. Do not treat a successful
# constructor call as semantic validation.
ocel = pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL(
    events=events_raw,
    objects=objects_raw,
    relations=relations_repaired,
    o2o=o2o_repaired,
)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")

# Deliberately not called: pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment.
# Propagating pack/dispatch/confirm across contains/packs/carries would invent
# order-level logistics and double-count the shared package PK-8.
```

Compare `ocdfg` with a twin built from `relations_raw` and `o2o_raw`. The expected A-versus-raw difference is a second pack incidence on PK-8 and a dispatch incidence on DEL-Y. Confirm, invoice, and payment graphs should match the raw log. Repeating the script with Bundle B’s extra rows is the check that those commercial edges appear only when that hypothesis is explicitly switched on.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Forcing INV-44 to bill ORD-B because 130+100 equals 230, and then hanging both payments on that invoice, welds two orders into one settled case. Fees on ORD-A, or a genuine open balance plus unapplied cash, produce the same numbers and a different process.

Choosing a single delivery for ev11 writes Dispatch → Confirm on that delivery alone. The other delivery becomes a permanent non-confirmation in the OC-DFG, even though `N-42*` cannot separate `N-420` from `N-421`.

Adding both deliveries and ORD-B to ev11 makes ORD-B look delivered from ORD-A’s portal row and doubles the new confirm edge. Models induced from that log will treat joint northbound confirmation as normal behavior.

Enriching along O2O after P1 copies ev10, and any later confirm of DEL-Y, onto both ORD-A and ORD-B. One package movement is then counted twice in object-weighted frequencies, and order clustering collapses for a logistical accident.

Binding ev10 to DEL-X because the timestamps match loads the cable package onto the glass delivery and leaves DEL-Y with an empty lifecycle that looks like missing master data. The differing carrier refs are the reason that merge was rejected.

Linking PAY-2 to ORD-A because both say 130 puts Record Payment on the order flattening and invents a payment-to-order path. The activity’s normal object, when it has one, is the invoice, and unapplied cash needs no order.

Inventing a package for the outage, or moving IT-B1 onto PK-7 to avoid a mixed carton, either creates an object the extract does not contain or contradicts `PK8-2`. Completing every tail also erases the diagnostic holes: unconfirmed deliveries, a short ORD-B trace, and a payment with no invoice.
