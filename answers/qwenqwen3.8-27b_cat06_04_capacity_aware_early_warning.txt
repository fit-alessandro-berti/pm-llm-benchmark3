## 1. Decision rule

Use a gated, score-based rule rather than a single breach-probability threshold. The rule has five parts: eligibility, alert persistence, calibration, prioritization, and capacity reservation.

### 1.1 Stage and feasibility eligibility

A case is eligible for a Senior Diagnostic Swarm only if:

1. **Stage gate:** current stage is before `Diagnosis Complete`  
   - Eligible: `Triage`, `Diagnosis Started`
   - Not eligible: `Diagnosis Complete`, `Resolution Work`, `Customer Validation`, `Closed`
   - `I-03` is therefore not eligible for a swarm despite its high `p_now`.

2. **Time-to-SLA feasibility gate:**  
   - Require at least **75 minutes** to SLA deadline: 45-minute swarm + 30-minute buffer for diagnosis redirection.
   - If time to SLA is below 75 minutes, allow intervention only with human override and only if the swarm can plausibly change the diagnosis path before the deadline.

3. **Repeat-intervention gate:**  
   - If the case has already received a swarm, do not repeat unless a **materially new event** has occurred.
   - Materially new event means: new diagnostic log, failed diagnosis, stage regression, corrected missing data, new error, customer escalation, or new evidence that changes the diagnostic hypothesis.
   - Model refresh alone is not a materially new event.

4. **Data-quality gate:**  
   - If a key live feature is missing and imputed, the case is not automatically eligible for a scarce slot.
   - Require rapid data validation or a human check before spending a slot, unless the case is otherwise urgent and the imputation is not central to the alert reason.

### 1.2 Alert persistence and minimum evidence change

Define:

\[
\Delta p = p_{now} - p_{prev}
\]

Use the following alert logic:

- **First alert:** enter scoring if at least one is true:
  - \(\Delta p \ge 0.10\), or
  - calibration-adjusted \(p_{cal}\) midpoint \(\ge 0.55\), or
  - value of avoiding breach \(\ge €50{,}000\) and \(\Delta p \ge 0.05\), or
  - a materially new event has occurred.

- **Repeated alert with no prior swarm:** enter scoring only if at least one is true:
  - a materially new event has occurred, or
  - \(|\Delta p| \ge 0.05\), or
  - data quality materially changed, e.g., imputation replaced by live data.

- **Repeated alert with prior swarm:** re-enter only if a materially new event has occurred and either:
  - time to SLA is below 2 hours, or
  - \(p_{cal}\) midpoint is above 0.60, or
  - diagnosis has failed or stalled.

This explicitly treats **I-05** and **I-07** differently: I-05 has repeated alerts but no new event and only \(\Delta p = 0.02\), so it is suppressed from automatic intervention. I-07 has a new customer log, which is a materially new event, so it is eligible for human review even though \(p_{now}\) is slightly lower than \(p_{prev}\).

### 1.3 Calibration adjustment

Treat the supplied predicted probability as observational, not as the probability under intervention. Adjust for known segment calibration issues:

| Segment | Calibration adjustment | Use in rule |
|---|---:|---|
| SME | Underpredicts by 0.04–0.09 | Use \(p_{cal} = p_{now} + 0.065\), interval \([p_{now}+0.04, p_{now}+0.09]\) |
| Strategic Enterprise | Overpredicts by 0.05–0.12 | Use \(p_{cal} = p_{now} - 0.085\), interval \([p_{now}-0.12, p_{now}-0.05]\) |
| Public Sector | Broadly acceptable, moderate sample | Use \(p_{cal} = p_{now}\), interval \(\pm 0.03\) |
| Non-Native-Language Support | Small sample, uncertain calibration and effect | Use \(p_{cal} = p_{now}\), interval \(\pm 0.10\), require human review |

Clip all probabilities to \([0.01, 0.99]\).

For Strategic Enterprise cases with \(p_{cal} < 0.35\), require stronger evidence before intervention: either \(\Delta p \ge 0.10\), a materially new event, or a very high value of avoiding breach with a positive robust lower-bound score.

### 1.4 Prioritization score

Do not rank by \(p_{now}\) alone. Rank by expected net benefit with explicit uncertainty penalties.

Use:

\[
S_{mid} = \Delta p_{mid} \times V - 450 - B - S_{spill}
\]

where:

- \(\Delta p_{mid}\) is the midpoint of the estimated absolute risk-reduction range.
- \(V\) is the value of avoiding breach.
- \(B\) is the false-alarm burden.
- \(S_{spill}\) is the marginal expected spillover harm from using one slot.

For today’s base spillover assumption:

\[
S_{spill, mid} = 6 \times 0.02 \times €15{,}000 = €1{,}800
\]

The €15,000 is a planning average for the six untreated diagnosis-stage cases in the spillover model; it is not a supplied exact value.

Also compute a robust lower-bound score:

\[
S_{low} = \Delta p_{low} \times V - 450 - B - S_{spill, high} - U
\]

where:

- \(\Delta p_{low}\) is the lower bound of the effect range.
- \(S_{spill, high} = 6 \times 0.03 \times €15{,}000 = €2{,}700\).
- \(U\) is an uncertainty penalty:
  - €500 if key data is imputed or missing,
  - €300 if the alert is repeated with no materially new event,
  - €400 if Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty applies,
  - €500 if Strategic Enterprise overprediction applies and \(p_{cal} < 0.40\).

Tiering:

- **Tier 1:** \(S_{mid} > €1{,}500\) and \(S_{low} > -€500\), or an urgency exception applies.
- **Urgency exception:** time to SLA < 3 hours, \(p_{cal, mid} > 0.55\), and \(\Delta p_{mid} \times V > €2{,}000\).
- **Tier 2:** \(S_{mid} > -€200\) but not Tier 1, or the case is conditional on data validation / new event.
- **Tier 3:** otherwise; monitor or suppress.

This rule avoids using a single probability threshold. A high-probability case with low effect, high false-alarm burden, poor calibration, or late stage can be deprioritized. A lower-probability case with high value, strong evidence change, and good effect estimate can be prioritized.

1. Use at most four slots.
2. Reserve one slot if:
   - a plausible high-priority unlisted incident may arrive before 15:00, and
   - the marginal fourth current case has weak robust net benefit, and
   - spillover from a fourth slot is likely to be material.

Today, reserve one slot. The marginal fourth current case is not robustly attractive enough to override the regulatory optionality and spillover concern.

Release the reserved slot only if:

  - I-06 is verified as real rather than an imputation artifact,
  - I-05 has a materially new event,
  - I-07’s new log is confirmed by human review to change the diagnosis path,
  - I-01 or I-10 shows a material risk increase.

I-04 has a 10-hour runway, so it can be scheduled after the reserved block without immediate SLA risk. If the regulatory incident uses the reserved slot, I-04 shifts to the next available slot before its 22:00 deadline.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

### 4.1 Cool-down

- After any alert, impose a **60-minute cool-down** before an automatic re-alert for the same case.
- The model refreshes every 30 minutes, so duplicate refreshes within the cool-down are suppressed unless there is a materially new event or \(|\Delta p| \ge 0.05\).
- The cool-down prevents 30-minute model refreshes from consuming scarce intervention capacity.

### 4.2 Suppression

A repeated alert is suppressed from the intervention queue if:

- no materially new event has occurred, and
- \(|\Delta p| < 0.05\), and
- data quality has not materially changed.

Suppressed cases remain in a watchlist. Suppression does not mean the case is ignored; it means the alert is not treated as new evidence requiring a scarce slot.

**I-05:** third alert, no new event, \(\Delta p = +0.02\).  
Decision: suppress automatic intervention. The high estimated effect is noted, but the alert itself is not new information.

### 4.3 Escalation

Escalate to human review if any of the following occur:

- \(p_{cal} \ge 0.75\) and time to SLA is below 2 hours;
- a materially new event occurs and time to SLA is below 2 hours;
- a key data field is missing or imputed and \(p_{cal} \ge 0.60\);
- stage regresses or diagnosis fails;
- customer escalation or regulatory relevance appears.

**I-07:** new customer log, 1.5 hours to SLA, SME underprediction implies \(p_{cal} \approx 0.76\)–0.81.  
Decision: escalate for manual review. The new log may contain genuinely new information, but the low value and small expected net benefit mean the case should not automatically consume a Tier 1 slot.

**I-02:** second alert, 3.0 hours to SLA, \(\Delta p = +0.08\).  
Decision: not an escalation case, but Tier 1 because of urgency, value, and positive midpoint effect.

### 4.4 Re-entry

Re-entry after a prior swarm requires a materially new event. Examples:

- new diagnostic log,
- failed diagnosis,
- stage regression,
- corrected missing data,
- new error or customer escalation.

For cases with no prior swarm, repeated alerts require either a materially new event or \(|\Delta p| \ge 0.05\).

### 4.5 Explicit comparison: I-05 vs I-07

| Case | Alert pattern | Evidence change | Rule consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| I-05 | Third alert, no new event | \(\Delta p = +0.02\), no materially new event | Suppress automatic intervention. Treat as alert persistence, not new causal evidence. Re-enter only if a new event occurs or \(\Delta p \ge 0.05\). |
| I-07 | Third alert, new customer log | \(\Delta p = -0.03\), but new log is materially new | Allow re-entry and human review. The lower \(p_{now}\) does not block review because the log may reduce uncertainty or reveal a new diagnostic path. Use reserve only if review confirms the log changes the diagnosis path and the swarm can start before 13:15. |

The distinction is important: repeated alerts are not automatically meaningful, but repeated alerts with new evidence can be.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

### 5.1 Value-based prioritization may starve lower-value cases

Risk: high-value Strategic Enterprise cases may repeatedly consume slots, while lower-value SME cases such as I-05, I-07, and I-10 are deprioritized even when their predicted breach risk or effect estimate is high.

Concrete safeguards:

- Cap the influence of value in the score. For example, no case should receive a slot solely because of value if its robust lower-bound score is negative.
- Add a **low-value urgency floor**: at least one of four daily slots, or at least 25% of weekly slots, should be available to cases with:
  - \(p_{cal} \ge 0.65\), and
  - time to SLA below 3 hours,
  - regardless of value tier.
- Monitor intervention rate, breach rate, and false-alarm burden by value tier.
- Trigger a policy review if lower-value cases with similar \(p_{cal}\) and effect estimates receive fewer than 70% of the intervention rate of higher-value cases for four consecutive weeks.

### 5.2 Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support

Risk: I-09 and similar cases have sparse calibration and uncertain intervention-effect estimates. The model may either under- or overstate risk, and the swarm effect may be less reliable.

Concrete safeguards:

- Require human review before intervening on Non-Native-Language Support cases.
- Use wider confidence intervals, e.g., \(\pm 0.10\) on \(p_{cal}\).
- Apply a 20% effect discount in the score for this segment until causal evidence improves.
- Track Non-Native-Language outcomes separately: breach rate, diagnosis time, swarm benefit, false-alarm burden.
- Do not use Non-Native-Language cases as the default control group for effect estimation because their calibration is less reliable.
- When capacity allows, reserve a small number of eligible Non-Native-Language cases for controlled no-intervention comparison to learn the effect.

### 5.3 Differential calibration across segments

Risk: SME cases are underpredicted, Strategic Enterprise cases are overpredicted, and Public Sector calibration is only moderately supported. Using raw \(p_{now}\) would systematically over-intervene on Strategic cases and under-intervene on SME cases.

Concrete safeguards:

- Use segment-specific calibration offsets in all scoring and alert gates.
- Update offsets weekly using recent backtests and live outcomes.
- Report calibration by segment:
  - expected calibration error,
  - Brier score,
  - false-positive rate,
  - false-negative rate.
- If a segment’s calibration offset changes by more than 0.03 in two consecutive weeks, freeze automatic threshold changes for that segment and trigger recalibration.
- Use calibration-adjusted intervals, not point probabilities, for high-stakes decisions.

### 5.4 Selective intervention changes future training labels

Risk: intervened cases have different outcomes than they would have had without intervention. If the model is later trained on post-intervention outcomes without accounting for treatment, it may learn that alerts or interventions reduce breach risk, biasing future predictions.

Concrete safeguards:

- Log treatment status for every case: whether a swarm was used, when, and why.
- Store pre-intervention features separately from post-intervention outcomes.
- Maintain a control or holdout group when capacity allows:
  - use the reserved slot as an exogenous source of variation,
  - or randomly withhold intervention from 10–20% of eligible Tier 2 cases when at least one slot remains unused.
- Train or validate the model using counterfactual methods:
  - propensity-score weighting,
  - doubly robust estimation,
  - difference-in-differences around alert time,
  - or instrumental-variable approaches where valid.
- Version the policy and model together so that policy-induced label changes can be audited.

### 5.5 Spillover harms may fall systematically on untreated groups

Risk: each swarm delays other diagnosis-stage cases. If the untreated cases are systematically lower-value, Public Sector, SME, Non-Native-Language, or otherwise vulnerable, the policy may shift harm from selected cases to unselected groups.

Concrete safeguards:

- Include expected spillover harm in the score, as done above.
- Monitor queue delay for non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases after each slot.
- Track spillover by segment:
  - average added delay,
  - breach rate among delayed cases,
  - SLA breach rate,
  - customer disruption reports.
- Cap slots per hour if queue congestion is high.
- Stagger swarms to avoid overlapping with peak diagnosis periods.
- If average added delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot, or if one segment experiences more than 20% higher spillover harm than the portfolio average for four weeks, reduce slot usage or compensate by expediting affected cases.
- Report “spillover fairness” as a first-class metric, not just total portfolio benefit.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

### 6.1 Operational monitoring

Track daily:

- number of slots used, reserved, and released;
- slot start and end times;
- specialist interruption time;
- cases waiting in diagnosis queue;
- queue length before and after each swarm;
- SLA breaches by stage and segment;
- time to diagnosis completion;
- data-quality failures, especially imputed live queue features;
- alert-to-intervention latency;
- number of alerts suppressed due to repeated-alert policy;
- number of materially new events classified by humans;
- regulatory incident arrivals and reserve usage.

Trigger for review:

- if average queue delay after a swarm exceeds 20 minutes,
- if more than 20% of slots are used on cases later classified as data artifacts,
- if reserve is repeatedly released late enough to miss urgent cases.

### 6.2 Predictive monitoring

Monitor model calibration continuously by segment:

- reliability diagrams,
- expected calibration error,
- Brier score,
- log loss,
- false-positive rate,
- false-negative rate,
- drift in feature distributions,
- drift in \(p_{now}\) and \(\Delta p\),
- imputation rate for key features.

Segment-specific triggers:

- recalibrate if expected calibration error exceeds 0.05 for two consecutive weeks;
- recalibrate if a segment’s calibration offset changes by more than 0.03 in two consecutive weeks;
- investigate if imputed-feature alerts cause more than 20% of false alarms.

For Non-Native-Language Support, monitor separately and avoid making policy changes based on small samples without Bayesian shrinkage or pooled priors.

### 6.3 Causal-effect monitoring

The supplied effect ranges are not known causal effects. They should be treated as priors and updated.

Estimate intervention effects using:

1. **Controlled holdout when capacity allows**
   - If at least one slot remains unused after Tier 1 cases, randomly withhold intervention from 10–20% of eligible Tier 2 cases.
   - Use the reserved slot as a natural experiment when it is released or not released.

2. **Observational causal estimation**
   - Propensity-score matching or doubly robust estimation.
   - Difference-in-differences around alert time.
   - Instrumental-variable methods if reserve release is sufficiently exogenous.

Track effect heterogeneity by:

- segment,
- stage,
- time to SLA,
- \(p_{now}\),
- \(\Delta p\),
- value tier,
- language support,
- data-quality status,
- alert history.

Update the effect ranges used in scoring when the posterior or confidence interval changes materially.

Triggers:

- stop assuming a positive effect for a segment if the lower 90% confidence bound is below zero.
- tighten evidence gates if more than 20% of interventions are later classified as “no benefit because model reason was wrong.”
- expand effect estimates only with causal or quasi-causal evidence, not with post-intervention observational outcomes alone.

### 6.4 Capacity and spillover monitoring

Monitor queue externalities explicitly:

- aggregate delay to non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases per slot;
- number of cases delayed by each swarm;
- breach probability or realized breach rate among delayed cases;
- specialist utilization;
- congestion level before each slot;
- reserve availability relative to urgent case arrivals.

Compare observed spillover to the base model:

- expected delay: 8–20 minutes per slot;
- expected breach-probability increase: 0.01–0.03 per untreated diagnosis-stage case.

Triggers:

- if observed delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot on average, reduce slots or stagger interventions.
- if observed breach-probability increase among untreated cases exceeds 0.03 per slot, increase the spillover penalty in the score.
- if spillover harm exceeds the marginal direct benefit of the last used slot in a given day, pause further non-Tier 1 interventions.

### 6.5 Fairness monitoring

Maintain a fairness dashboard with at least the following metrics by segment, language, and value tier:

- intervention rate,
- breach rate,
- false-alarm burden,
- average spillover delay,
- time to diagnosis,
- customer disruption reports,
- repeated-alert suppression rate,
- reserve release rate.

Concrete disparity triggers:

- lower-value segment intervention rate is below 70% of higher-value segment rate at similar \(p_{cal}\) and effect estimate for four weeks;
- one segment experiences more than 20% higher spillover delay than the portfolio average for four weeks;
- Non-Native-Language cases are systematically excluded from intervention despite comparable \(p_{cal}\) and urgency;
- Strategic Enterprise cases receive more than 60% of slots while contributing less than 40% of estimated net benefit for two consecutive weeks.

When a trigger fires, do not immediately change the live rule. First run a shadow evaluation, then adjust the rule in a phased rollout.

### 6.6 Detecting policy-induced drift

The policy itself can change the data-generating process. Monitor for:

- declining breach rates among intervened cases that may reflect treatment rather than model improvement;
- rising \(p_{now}\) among non-intervened cases after repeated spillover;
- changes in alert frequency after cool-down or suppression rules;
- changes in data quality after human review;
- model performance degradation on cases with different treatment exposure.

Methods:

- compare model performance on treated and untreated cases separately;
- use counterfactual evaluation to estimate what would have happened without intervention;
- run shadow mode for proposed threshold or allocation changes before deployment;
- maintain a small randomized holdout to provide an unbiased estimate of policy effect.

### 6.7 Decision rule for changing the policy

Change the threshold or allocation rule only when at least one of the following is true:

1. Calibration drift exceeds predefined limits.
2. Estimated intervention effect for a segment has a lower confidence bound below zero.
3. Spillover harm exceeds the marginal benefit of the last slot used.
4. False-alarm burden exceeds 30% of estimated direct benefit for a segment.
5. A fairness trigger has persisted for four weeks.
6. Reserve usage shows that the current reserve policy is systematically too large or too small.
7. More than 20% of interventions are classified as no-benefit due to wrong model reason or data artifact.

Change process:

1. Freeze the affected rule component.
2. Run a shadow evaluation for at least one week.
3. Compare old and new rules on:
   - net benefit,
   - spillover,
   - fairness,
   - calibration,
   - reserve adequacy.
4. Roll out in phases, starting with Tier 2 cases.
5. Re-evaluate after two weeks and after the next weekly calibration cycle.
