- **Inability to prove compliance:** P-506 lacks a written extension, so the claimed verbal extension cannot be proven. The burden of proof is on the party relying on the exception.

**Immediate case-level remediation:**  
- P-501: Reconstruct the approval timeline; require pre-commitment approvals in any future emergency; provide targeted training.  
- P-502: Obtain late Compliance and Finance approvals, but document that they are post-commitment; perform a supplier risk review.  
- P-503: Segregate roles immediately; require independent budget owner and Finance approval for any future commitment; review PROC-3’s authority.  
- P-504: Stop using the unapproved supplier; assess whether goods already received meet quality standards.  
- P-505: Preserve telephone and supplier acknowledgment evidence; determine actual commitment time; if after expiry, treat as non-ECD-2.  
- P-506: Require written extensions for any future directive; no verbal extensions.  
- P-507: Preserve the signed PDF and outage logs; correct ERP timestamps where possible.  
- P-508: Obtain Finance approval and document that it is post-commitment; review whether the incident command authorization was within charter.

**Monitoring actions:**  
- Conduct an internal audit of all ECD-2 purchases, focusing on pre-commitment approvals and segregation.  
- Implement a control that blocks supplier commitment if Finance approval for  EUR 50,000 is missing.  
- Add a rule that any emergency directive must be ratified by the Chief Risk Officer within 48 hours.  
- Review all emergency declarations monthly to detect “emergency normalization.”

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed clause to replace ECD-2 and clarify GPIP-4:**

> Only the Chief Risk Officer, or an explicit delegate with written authority, may issue a scoped, time-limited exception to GPIP-4 integrity controls. Incident directives may suspend operational timing and supplier-register requirements, but they may not suspend segregation of duties, Finance approval, Compliance clearance, or threshold-based approvals.
>
> During any declared emergency, the following minimum controls apply:  
> 1. Two distinct Incident Command members, neither of whom is the requester or committer, must authorize the purchase before commitment.  
> 2. Finance approval must be obtained before commitment for all purchases  EUR 50,000.  
> 3. Compliance clearance must be obtained before commitment for any purchase from a new supplier or any purchase > EUR 100,000.  
> 4. All verbal authorizations must be recorded within four hours and supported by contemporaneous evidence.  
> 5. After an incident directive expires, no verbal extension is valid. Any extension must be in writing, signed by the Chief Risk Officer, and time-limited.  
>
> To prevent emergency normalization, any use of emergency procedures for the same category of goods more than twice in a calendar quarter must be reviewed by Internal Audit, and any permanent change must be made through a formal policy amendment rather than repeated emergency declarations.

This preserves the purpose of emergency speed while keeping essential integrity controls intact and making the hierarchy of authority explicit.
