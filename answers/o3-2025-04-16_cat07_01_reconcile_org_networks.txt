# 1. Network-by-network interpretation  

### • handover-of-work network  
What it implies:  
– Out-degree 0.82 and very high betweenness (0.77) show that Mira frequently receives cases from Bot_Rules and then redistributes them to four adjusters and two specialists.  
– The A  B pattern with short 18–19 min elapsed times is typical for a dispatcher or triage gate.  
What it does **not** imply:  
– It does not prove she works long on the content; it only shows she touches many cases briefly.  
– Centrality here is compatible both with “helpful broker” and “single-point failure.”

### • working-together network  
What it implies:  
– Mira’s weighted degree is only 0.16; co-case rates with adjusters are  8 %.  
– Low co-occurrence means that once she releases a case she rarely re-enters while the adjuster is still active.  
What it does **not** imply:  
– Low working-together does not mean unimportant; dispatchers naturally disappear from this network.

### • subcontracting network  
What it implies:  
– High subcontracting betweenness (0.69) and A-B-A patterns with Omar and Priya show “Mira  specialist  Mira” loops.  
– This pattern fits an escalation desk that follows up after specialist input.  
What it does **not** imply:  
– It cannot tell whether she adds value or merely relays information.

### • performance/time statistics  
– Median service time is only 4.2 min, but absence raised 90th-percentile cycle time for exceptions from 8.4  13.9 days, confirming she is a throughput lever for difficult cases.  

### • role-discovery / activity-profile similarity  
– Activity vector (Request MI, Reopen, Escalate, Assign) is dissimilar to adjusters and specialists, grouping her into a separate “coordination” role in pm4py.discover_organizational_roles.  
– Similarity analysis supports the idea that her work is routing-centric, not claim-processing-centric.

# 3. Risk assessment  

• Bottleneck & key-person risk: High. Betweenness 0.77 and backlog during absence indicate low redundancy.  
• Coordination risk: If Mira mis-routes, wrong specialists or vendors are engaged, increasing rework.  
• Compliance/control risk: Same person both detects problems and decides routing; may bypass the “four-eyes” principle.  
• Specialist overload risk: Removing or limiting Mira without substitute could flood Omar/Priya with unfiltered work.  
• Workload fairness: 48 % of her cases are exceptions vs 30 % for Bot_Rules; load may be cognitively heavier though numerically lighter.  
• Data-quality risk: If multiple clerks share this login, audit trail lacks accountability.

# 6. Short executive summary  

Event-log analysis shows that “Mira_Coordinator” is the main gateway for complicated or disputed claims. Although she touches only 22 % of cases, her handovers link almost every specialist and adjuster, and when she was absent for six days the slowest exception cases became 66 % longer. Her interactions are short, so she barely appears in the “working-together” network—typical for a dispatcher rather than a case worker.  

Whether Mira is a formal exception-desk, an informal bottleneck, or both, the organisation relies heavily on a single person. That creates key-person and compliance risks: if she mis-routes or is unavailable, cases stall; if she both detects and decides on exceptions, four-eyes controls may be bypassed. Management should immediately appoint and train a backup coordinator and log a distinct “Exception Reviewed” step for audit clarity, while monitoring cycle-time and network centrality to judge whether further automation or rule changes can safely absorb part of the triage work.
