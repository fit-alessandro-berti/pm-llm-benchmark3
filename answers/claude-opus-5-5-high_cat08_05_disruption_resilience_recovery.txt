## 1.2 Phase narrative

**T0: Baseline (Day -30 to Day -1).**
- *Operational state:* The process is stable and system-supported, at about 2,000 orders per business day.
- *Dominant variants:* The normative happy path accounts for 36,800 of 42,000 cases reaching Invoice close. The minor loops are Compliance hold (700, of which 520 returned to validation), Backorder wait (900, of which 300 were cancelled) and Repack/recheck (450).
- *Risk posture:* Controls are embedded in the system, and control gaps are rare (0.2% to 0.8%). About 4,300 cases have no END in the window. That is normal right-censoring at the window boundary, and it matters later because incident-period DFGs also contain "open" cases that should not all be read as lost.

**T1: Onset (Day 0 morning).**
- *Operational state:* The warehouse management system (WMS) is unavailable. This is a cyber incident, not just an outage, so the integrity of the last-known state (inventory, authorization master data, recall and quarantine status) is uncertain as well as its availability.
- *Critical temporal anchor:* The printed inventory snapshot comes from "the morning of the incident". Every downstream inventory decision in T2 is conditioned on it, and its reliability decays with every hour of unrecorded picks, receipts, returns and quarantine changes. It is also unclear whether it was taken before or after in-flight transactions at T1 were interrupted.

**T2: Manual workaround (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2).**
- *Operational state:* The process runs offline, on phone, paper and spreadsheets. Intake continued at roughly baseline or higher daily volume (5,600 cases in about 2.5 days), but only about 2,530 cases reached END in the phase (2,100 via Spreadsheet invoice hold and 430 cancelled). WIP therefore accumulated sharply.
- *Dominant variants (from the DFG):*
  - Triage split 5,100 cases three ways: Manual authorization check 2,500 (49%), Skip system authorization check 1,600 (31%) and Hospital-critical fast lane 1,000 (20%). The fast-lane path goes straight to Manual inventory snapshot check with **no authorization activity at all**. So about 2,600 cases (46% of the phase) have no recorded authorization.
  - Of 3,600 manually packed cases, only 1,650 (46%) passed Temporary release control. 1,800 (50%) went **Pack → Dispatch without scan with no release step**.
  - 100% of dispatches (3,250) were unscanned. Only 2,600 (80%) have a Phone proof of delivery (POD) in-phase, which leaves 650 dispatched cases with no delivery evidence.
  - There were 1,080 deferrals (500 at intake and 580 after the snapshot check). The backlog queue of 960 produced 420 picks, 320 cancellations (33%) and about 220 carried forward.
  - There is visible leakage at nearly every node: 740 pick-listed cases never reached pack, 200 manual-authorization cases never reached the snapshot check, and 200 cases entered release control but not dispatch. This is consistent with a mix of stock-outs found at the shelf (a stale snapshot), rejections, and unlogged work.
- *Risk posture:* Service was protected for hospitals (median +2h, fill rate 91%), while pharmacies (fill 61%, median 2.3x) and wholesale absorbed the loss. Control completeness collapsed by 12x to 15x.
- *Measurement caveat:* T2 cycle times are survivorship-biased. They count only cases that finished, and many slow cases were still open. The end activity also shifted from Invoice close to Spreadsheet invoice hold, so T2 cycle times probably understate the true delay.

**T3: Partial recovery (Day 3 to Day 6).**
- *Operational state:* This is a hybrid phase. Order intake is back, but inventory synchronization and scanning are unreliable. No DFG is given, but fitness of 0.82 with 59% fitting traces implies that about 4 in 10 cases still mixed system events with manual ones.
- *Volume:* Intake surged to 11,400 cases in 4 days (about 2,850 per day). That is consistent with backlog re-entry, catch-up demand, and possibly **duplicate orders** re-keyed after earlier phone orders.
- *Risk posture:* This phase is arguably the most dangerous for data integrity. People assume "the system is back", but records are partly system-captured and partly reconstructed. Any continued use of the Day 0 snapshot would be 3 to 6 days stale. Control gaps halved but were still about 6x to 7x baseline.

**T4: Stabilization (Day 7 to Day 21).**
- *Operational state:* Hospital service is fully recovered (8h / 16h). Pharmacy (p90 +51%) and wholesale (p90 +67%) are still degraded at Day 21.
- *Dominant variants:* The happy path is back, but it is wrapped in recovery loops:
  - Backlog prioritization: 1,500 cases, of which 450 (30%) were cancelled.
  - Compliance hold: 1,300. That is 3.9% of validated cases, against 1.7% at baseline.
  - Reconcile manual reservation: 1,400 cases, of which 300 (21%) were cancelled.
  - Repack/recheck: 900 cases (3.0% against 1.2% at baseline). There is no return edge in the DFG, so these are probably still open.
  - Proof-of-delivery remediation: 1,200.
  - Invoice close → Audit remediation: 2,300 cases (8.5% of invoice closes).
- *Cross-period work:* POD edges (27,900 + 1,200) exceed dispatches (28,800), and audit remediation (2,300) far exceeds T4's own traceability exceptions (2.2% ≈ 770). A plausible explanation is that T4 is processing **incident debt from T2 and T3**. Summing the T2, T3 and T4 traceability exceptions (about 532 + 616 + 770 ≈ 1,920), plus temperature and authorization gaps, gives roughly the 2,300 remediation cases.
- *Risk posture:* This is operational recovery without compliance recovery. Assuming the rates share a comparable denominator, T4 produces as many or more *absolute* control gaps as T2, because volume is 6x larger:
  - Controlled dual-control missing: about 175 case-equivalents in T4 against about 162 in T2.
  - Excess rework over the baseline rate: about 800 cases in T4 against about 500 in T2.
- *Hidden work:* Extra recovery-activity instances in T4 total about 7,000 to 8,500 (backlog prioritization, excess compliance holds, reconciliation, excess repacks, POD remediation and audit remediation). That is roughly a quarter of T4 case volume, and none of it shows in the headline cycle times, which are measured to invoice close.

## 1.3 Three recovery clocks

The case shows three different recovery curves:
1. **Critical-service clock:** hospital cycle time. It is V-shaped and recovered by Day 7.
2. **Flow clock:** pharmacy and wholesale cycle time, fill rate and cancellations. It is U-shaped and not yet recovered at Day 21.
3. **Compliance clock:** traceability, temperature and dual-control completeness, plus the remediation queue. It is L-shaped with a long tail, and persisted beyond operational recovery.

Declaring recovery on the first clock is the core measurement error management must avoid.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

## 3.1 The distinction

In this case, **resilience** means maintaining the critical function (safe, authorized, traceable supply of medicines to those who need them most) while preserving each control objective. Where the normal mechanism fails, the objective is kept either by a substitute of comparable strength or by an explicit, bounded and recorded exception decided by an accountable role.

**Concealed noncompliance** means maintaining *throughput* by dropping a control objective in a way the organization cannot see in time. It is invisible in operational metrics, absent from the log, or later masked by reconstruction that makes the log look conformant.

The deviations from the baseline process are not the problem in themselves. Manual authorization check (B) deviates from the process but not from the control objective. Dispatch without scan (G) deviates from both.

Five tests separate the two:

| Test | Question | Resilient if... |
|---|---|---|
| Objective preservation | Is the purpose of the control (right customer, right product, product integrity, security, traceability, delivery evidence) still achieved? | A substitute of comparable strength exists (B, F for controlled) |
| Visibility | Is the deviation recorded *contemporaneously* as a deviation? | Yes, with reason, decision-maker and time |
| Boundedness | Is it restricted by product risk, customer, quantity and time? | Explicit limits exist (C would qualify only with them) |
| Recoverability | Can the missing evidence be recovered from an independent source? | Yes: courier records, customer signature, temperature logger |
| Authorization of the exception | Did an accountable role (Responsible Person, QA, compliance) decide it? | Yes, not individual teams improvising |

## 3.2 How concealment happened in this case

Concealment was not necessarily intentional. It arose through four mechanisms:
1. **Metric concealment:** Hospital cycle time (10h) and fill rate (91%) looked excellent while 46% of T2 cases had no recorded authorization and 50% had no release check.
2. **Log concealment:** Scans were "later reconstructed" (E) and phone PODs "entered later" (H). Without provenance, a remediated case is indistinguishable from a compliant one. This is also why rising fitness (0.71 → 0.82 → 0.90) partly overstates improvement: some of it is backfilled events.
3. **Temporal concealment:** Invoice close → Audit remediation pushes the compliance debt beyond the point where the case counts as "done". The 2,300-case remediation tail therefore never appears in cycle time.
4. **Aggregation concealment:** An overall fitness of 0.90 and "normal systems mostly returned" hide the fact that controlled-substance dual-control gaps are still 2.5x baseline, and in absolute terms are as high in T4 as in T2.

## 3.3 Classification

**True resilience:**
- **B (Manual authorization check):** an equivalent substitute control, capacity-limited.
- **F for controlled substances:** 100% pharmacist check that reduced errors, although it needs a second signature to meet dual control.
- **D on Day 0 only:** a reasonable, fresh proxy for inventory.
- **I (Defer noncritical order) in principle:** explicit capacity protection for critical supply.
- **J (Backlog prioritization), if the rules are explicit:** risk-based sequencing.
- **K (Reconcile manual reservation):** restoring inventory truth is necessary. The fault is timing, not concept.
- **Spreadsheet invoice hold:** a conservative financial control.

**Risky shortcuts (control objective dropped):**
- **C (Skip system authorization check) as practised:** authorization assumed, not verified, with no bound and no evidence.
- **G (Dispatch without scan) for controlled, cold-chain and recall-sensitive items:** chain of custody lost, and the largest traceability gap.
- **F's unreleased path:** 1,800 cases went Pack → Dispatch with no release step and an undefined sample.
- **D after about 24h:** allocation against stale and possibly corrupted data, blind to recalls and quarantine.
- **H for controlled and cold-chain:** phone confirmation is not an acceptable receipt for these products.
- **E and L when reconstruction lacks provenance:** this turns a documentation gap into a data-integrity problem. It is the most dangerous form of concealment because it defeats future audits.

**Ambiguous (depends on risk appetite and on facts not in the aggregate log):**
- **A (Hospital-critical fast lane):** acceptable if the hospitals were already known and licensed and an accountable person approved emergency supply. It is unacceptable if it also skipped controlled or cold-chain checks. The DFG shows no authorization activity on this path, so this must be checked case by case.
- **G and H for ambient, non-restricted products:** tolerable with a manifest and a structured call record, especially for hospitals. Without them, they become traceability gaps.
- **I and J for pharmacies:** defensible as triage, but potentially unfair and clinically risky if "standard" pharmacies had patient-critical lines. The 61% fill rate and the persistent p90 gap need a line-level review.
- **L:** resilient as a recovery mechanism, but not a substitute for real-time control. Acceptable for documentation gaps; not acceptable as a way to "close" controls that were never performed.

# 4. Recovery process model

## 4.1 Operating modes and explicit trigger conditions

The redesigned process has four declared modes. The mode is declared by an incident lead together with the Responsible Person / QA lead, is time-stamped, and is itself logged as an event, so every case carries its mode as a case attribute.

| Mode | Resembles | Entry trigger (any one) | Exit criteria (all) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N: Normal** | T0 | n/a | n/a |
| **D1: Degraded, partial systems** | T3 | Scan success rate < 98% over 1h at any dock; inventory synchronization lag > 2h; WMS read-only or integrity suspect; POD system down > 2h | Scan success ≥ 99.5% for 24h; synchronization lag < 15 min; cycle-count variance 0 for controlled and < 0.5% for cold-chain and recall-sensitive locations; evidence backlog below threshold (for example < 1 day of volume) |
| **D2: Manual / offline** | T2 | WMS unavailable > 30 min; a security team declares systems untrusted (cyber); order intake down > 1h | Order intake restored and trusted; manual reservation ledger loaded and reconciled for high-risk products |
| **R: Reconciliation and remediation** (runs in parallel from the first hour of D1 or D2, not after) | T4 | Automatically opened with D1 or D2 | Remediation queue at baseline level; zero open controlled-substance items; every unrecoverable gap has a deviation report and CAPA; compliance-close rate ≥ 99% of incident cases |

**In-mode escalation triggers (continuous checks):**
- Snapshot age > 24h: stop allocation from the snapshot and allocate only from physically counted locations.
- Wait for compliance review > 2h for a Hospital-critical order: activate the reserve pool of cross-trained compliance staff.
- Any controlled-substance order at the dispatch dock without two signatures: **stop that order**. No throughput override.
- Share of cases without delivery evidence 24h after dispatch > 5%: escalate to courier-evidence retrieval.
- Pharmacy fill rate < 80% or pharmacy backlog age p90 > 2x baseline: trigger the fairness review.
- Intake/completion ratio > 1.5 for 12h: activate deferral rules and customer communication.

## 4.2 Process structure (process-tree notation)

Notation, following process trees and POWL:
- `→(a, b, …)` is a sequence.
- `×(a, b, …)` is an exclusive choice, with the guard in [brackets].
- `∧(a, b, …)` means the activities run in parallel.
- `⟲(body, redo)` is a loop.
- Activities written in CAPITALS are hard gates: the case cannot proceed until the gate is satisfied.

```
IncidentResponse = →(
    DeclareMode[trigger, time, RP sign-off],
    ∧(
        ⟲(CaseFlow, next case),
        ⟲(EvidenceDigitization, next form batch),     # from hour 1, not after recovery
        ⟲(InventoryIntegrity, next shift),            # rolling cycle counts, recall/quarantine push
        ⟲(RemediationFlow, next exception)            # compliance lane starts in D1/D2
    ),
    ExitReview[exit criteria met, lookback report]
)

CaseFlow = →(
    ReceiveAndDedupe,                                  # match phone, e-mail and re-keyed orders
    RiskClassify[customer tier × product class × clinical-need flag],
    ×( PriorityLane   [Hospital-critical OR patient-critical line],
       StandardLane   [Pharmacy-standard, non-deferrable lines],
       DeferLane      [Wholesale-bulk or deferrable lines] → BacklogQueue ),
    AUTHORIZE = ×( OnlineValidate          [system trusted],
                   OfflineListCheck        [B; licence list ≤ 24h old],
                   KnownCustomerRule       [C'; ambient-unrestricted, qty ≤ history band, retro-verify ≤ 24h],
                   BreakGlassEmergency     [A'; hospital only, RP named sign-off, retro-verify ≤ 24h],
                   ComplianceHold          [otherwise] ),
    RESERVE  = ×( SystemReservation, ManualLedgerReservation[snapshot age ≤ TTL], CountedLocationReservation ),
    ∧( PickWithEvidence, CaptureLotExpiryQty ),        # controlled: ∧(picker, witness)
    ∧( Pack, CapturePackOut ),                          # cold-chain: logger ID, shipper type, pack-out time
    RELEASE  = ×( FullRelease[controlled, cold-chain, recall-sensitive, break-glass],
                  SampledRelease[ambient; documented sample plan] ),
    HANDOFF  = →( ManifestSignedByCourier, SealAndPhoto[controlled] ),
    POD      = ×( SignedReceipt[controlled, cold-chain + logger readout],
                  StructuredPhonePOD[ambient] ),
    ×( →(EVIDENCE_COMPLETE, ∧(InvoiceClose, ComplianceClose)),
       →(EvidenceGap, ∧(InvoiceHoldOrClose[risk-based], RaiseException → RemediationFlow)) )
)
```

Key design choices:
- Authorization, release and evidence are **gates with tiered alternatives**, not optional steps. The fast lane changes *order*, not *control content*.
- **Invoice close and compliance close are separate states.** A case is "done" only when both are done.
- Remediation is a **parallel lane from hour one**, so that the T4 tail becomes a T2/T3 activity with a much shorter lag.

## 4.3 Triage rules (ordered, deterministic)

1. **Recall, quarantine or integrity concern:** any line whose lot or product is on the current recall or quarantine push list is blocked, regardless of customer.
2. **Clinical criticality at line level:**
   - Tier 1: Hospital-critical orders, and patient-critical lines for any customer (a maintained list, for example insulin, anti-epileptics, oncology and emergency drugs).
   - Tier 2: remaining Pharmacy-standard lines.
   - Tier 3: Wholesale-bulk and stock-replenishment orders.
3. **Product risk determines the control set, not the priority.** Controlled and cold-chain items keep full controls even in Tier 1. If the controls cannot be met in time, escalate to the Responsible Person for an alternative-source decision (for example redirecting the hospital to another depot) rather than shipping uncontrolled.
4. **Capacity allocation:** reserve capacity per tier, for example Tier 1 up to 50%, Tier 2 at least 30% (a protected floor), and Tier 3 the remainder. Unused Tier 1 capacity flows to Tier 2 first.
5. **Deferral:** only Tier 3 orders and explicitly deferrable Tier 2 lines may be deferred. Every deferral carries a reason code, a promised re-evaluation time (at most 24h) and customer notification.
6. **Compliance capacity:** when authorization-review queue wait exceeds 2h, route ambient orders to the Known-customer rule (if eligible) and non-eligible orders to hold. Controlled and cold-chain orders always get compliance capacity first.

## 4.4 Minimum controls by product and customer risk

| Control step | Ambient | Cold-chain | Controlled substance | Recall-sensitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authorization | Offline licence list, or a bounded known-customer rule with retro-verification ≤ 24h | Offline licence list; no known-customer rule | Full check of registration and licence by compliance staff, recorded; no known-customer rule, no break-glass without Responsible Person | Offline licence list |
| Inventory | Snapshot within TTL or counted location | Counted location; check expiry and stability budget | Vault count at start and end of each shift; running manual ledger | Lot-level location confirmed; checked against recall push |
| Pick | Form: SKU, quantity, lot, picker | Plus pack-out time, shipper type, logger ID | Two-person pick (picker and witness signatures) | Lot and expiry mandatory; no lot means no pick |
| Release | Documented sample (for example 10% or AQL-based) | 100% release including a temperature record | 100% pharmacist release plus a second signature (dual control) | 100% lot check against the recall list |
| Handoff | Courier-signed manifest | Manifest plus logger ID; dispatch within validated pack-out window | Sealed tamper-evident tote, seal number, two-person handoff, courier identity verified | Manifest at lot level |
| POD | Structured phone POD within 4h, or courier POD | Signed receipt plus logger readout within 24h | Signed receipt (paper, photo or e-signature) only; phone contact is interim with 24h escalation | Signed or structured POD, lot recorded |
| If a control cannot be met | Defer or hold | **Do not ship**; source alternative | **Do not ship**; source alternative | **Do not ship** |

**Customer overlays:**
- **Hospital-critical:** priority lane; break-glass allowed only for ambient and for already-licensed hospitals, with named Responsible Person sign-off and retro-verification within 24h.
- **Pharmacy-standard:** protected capacity floor; patient-critical lines are promoted to Tier 1.
- **Wholesale-bulk:** first deferral pool; quantity caps during D2 to prevent panic buying and stockpiling.

## 4.5 Manual evidence capture

- **Pre-staged degraded-mode kit:**
  - numbered, pre-printed forms (pick, release, manifest, POD);
  - offline licence, recall and quarantine lists (daily encrypted export stored on hardened offline devices, plus a print set);
  - store-and-forward handheld scanners that buffer scans locally;
  - a camera or phone for photos of labels, seals and signatures.
- **Mandatory fields:** order ID, line, SKU, lot, expiry, quantity, location, actor IDs (picker, checker, witness), occurred-at time, logger ID, seal number, courier identity, receiver identity.
- **Bitemporal recording:** every event carries both *occurred_at* and *recorded_at*. Records are never backdated.
- **Provenance code on every event:**
  - `SYS`: system-captured in real time;
  - `MAN-C`: manual, contemporaneous, on a signed form;
  - `MAN-T`: transcribed from a form, four-eyes verified;
  - `3P`: third-party evidence (courier, customer, temperature logger);
  - `REC-INF`: reconstructed or inferred;
  - `NP`: control not performed.
- **Digitization SLA:** controlled and cold-chain forms within 12h; others within 24h. Digitization starts during D2, as a parallel lane, not after recovery.
- **Four-eyes transcription** for controlled and cold-chain records. Original forms are retained as primary records.
- **Conformance is computed separately** on `SYS`/`MAN-C` events (real-time compliance) and on all events (evidence completeness). The difference between the two is the measured concealment risk.

## 4.6 Backlog prioritization

- **Deduplication first:** match re-keyed orders against phone and e-mail orders taken in D2, by customer, SKU, quantity and time window. This addresses the T3 intake surge.
- **Priority score** per backlog line:

```
Score = 40·ClinicalCriticality + 20·ProductRiskUrgency (expiry, stability budget)
      + 25·AgeFactor (hours waiting ÷ segment baseline p90, uncapped)
      + 15·CustomerStockoutRisk (days of cover, if known)
```

- **Aging guarantee:** any line older than 2x the segment baseline p90 is promoted one tier. This prevents the starvation visible in pharmacy p90 of 74h in T4.
- **Fairness floor:** at least 30% of backlog-release capacity goes to Pharmacy-standard until the pharmacy backlog age is below 1.5x baseline.
- **Cancellation governance:** cancel only with customer contact and a reason code (customer-sourced elsewhere, phantom stock, customer request, authorization failure). The 30% (J) and 21% (K) cancellation rates must be explainable by reason.
- **Transparency:** the rules are published internally and to key customers, and every override is logged with its decision-maker.

## 4.7 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

```
RemediationFlow = →(
    DetectGap[missing auth | scan | release | temp record | dual control | POD | lot],
    ClassifyGap = ×(
        EvidenceExistsOffline      → Digitize[MAN-T] → Verify,
        EvidenceRecoverable3P      → Retrieve[courier scan, customer signature, logger download] → Verify,
        EvidenceNotRecoverable     → DocumentGap[NP] → ImpactAssessment ),
    ImpactAssessment = ∧(
        ProductQualityAssessment   [cold-chain: stability data vs logger/trip time],
        PatientSafetyAssessment    [need for customer notification or recall],
        RegulatoryAssessment       [controlled-substance loss/discrepancy reporting, licence authority],
        FinancialAssessment        [invoice validity, credit notes] ),
    ×( Close[evidence verified], →(DeviationReport, CAPA, Notify[if required], Close) ),
    ComplianceClose
)
```

- **Risk-ordered SLAs:**
  - controlled-substance gaps: triaged within 24h, closed or reported within 72h;
  - cold-chain gaps: triaged within 24h (product-quality decisions are time-critical while stock is at the customer);
  - recall-sensitive lot gaps: within 5 days;
  - ambient documentation gaps: within 10 days.
- **Dedicated capacity:** the remediation lane is staffed separately from the operational flow, so the hidden workload is planned and not borrowed from normal operations.
- **Inventory reconciliation** (K) starts in D1:
  - rolling counts of high-risk locations;
  - phantom stock separated from true backorders;
  - controlled-substance discrepancies reported as discrepancies, never netted away.
- **Invoice and compliance status:**
  - Controlled and cold-chain invoices stay on hold until compliance close.
  - Ambient invoices may close first, but compliance close remains open and visible.
- **Lookback report:** the incident closes only when management receives a count of cases by provenance class, the number of `NP` gaps, notifications made, and the final remediation queue age.

# 5. Resilience indicators

Leading indicators are monitored in real time during an incident and trigger escalation. Lagging indicators judge the outcome after the incident. The reference values come from this incident.

| Dimension | Leading indicator (during incident) | Lagging indicator (after incident) | Target / threshold | This incident (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Throughput** | Intake/completion ratio per 4h; WIP count and growth rate | Days until daily completions ≥ intake and WIP back to baseline | Ratio ≤ 1.2 in D1; WIP normalized within 7 days | T2: about 5,600 received vs about 2,530 ended; WIP still elevated in T4 |
| **Service protection** | Hospital-critical p90 cycle time; fill rate by tier; patient-critical line fill | Hospital stock-out incidents; critical-line fill over the full incident | Hospital p90 ≤ 1.5x baseline; critical-line fill ≥ 95% | Hospital p90 1.4x in T2, fill 91% (good) |
| **Traceability** | Share of dispatches with a manifest and lot capture (real time); POD evidence lag p90 | Traceability exceptions by phase; recall mock-trace success on incident cases | Controlled, cold-chain and recall-sensitive: 100% at dispatch; overall ≤ 2x baseline in D2 | 100% unscanned dispatch in T2; 9.5% (11.9x) exceptions; still 2.75x at Day 7 to 21 |
| **Control completeness** | Control completeness *at time of dispatch* by product class (authorization, release, dual control, temperature), from `SYS`/`MAN-C` events only | Real-time conformance vs remediated conformance (gap = concealment risk); number of `NP` gaps | Controlled dual control 100% contemporaneous; cold-chain temperature record 100% | Dual control missing 2.9% (14.5x); about 46% of T2 cases with no recorded authorization; 50% with no release |
| **Backlog age** | Backlog age p50/p90 by segment; snapshot age; compliance-queue wait | Time to backlog clearance per segment; remediation-queue age at Day 21 | Backlog p90 ≤ 2x segment baseline p90; snapshot ≤ TTL | Backlog → pick exceeded baseline p95; audit queue persisted after recovery |
| **Rework** | Rework inflow rate per day; pick-list-to-pack loss rate; repack rate | Total excess rework cases; rework hours; reconciliation cancellations | Rework ≤ 2x baseline in D1 | T2 12.5%; about 1,900 excess rework cases across T2 to T4, with the largest excess in T4 (about 800) |
| **Fairness across customer groups** | Service degradation ratio per segment (current p90 ÷ baseline p90) and its spread; fill-rate gap between tiers | Recovery time per segment; cancellation rate by segment; complaints and churn | Pharmacy degradation ratio ≤ 1.5x hospital's; fill-rate gap ≤ 15 points | T2 ratio: hospital 1.4, pharmacy 2.4, wholesale 2.5; fill gap 30 points; T4 pharmacy p90 still 1.51x |
| **Hidden workload / compliance recovery** | Remediation inflow vs burn-down per day; evidence-digitization lag | **Time to compliance recovery** (distinct from time to operational recovery); remediation hours; cases closed after invoice close | Compliance recovery ≤ operational recovery + 7 days | About 7,000 to 8,500 extra recovery activities in T4; 2,300 post-invoice remediations, 200 unresolved |
| **Data integrity** | Share of events with provenance tag; share of events with recorded_at − occurred_at > 24h | Audit findings on backfilled or backdated records | 100% tagged; no untagged reconstructed events | Unknown: reconstructed scans had no provenance, which is itself the finding |
| **Cancellation** | Cancellations per day by reason code | Cancellation rate by segment and reason | ≤ 2x baseline, 100% with reason code | T2 7.8% (3.7x); backlog cancellations 33% (T2) and 30% (T4) |

Two composite indicators are recommended for management reporting:
- **Resilience area (service):** the sum over the incident of (baseline p90 ÷ actual p90 − 1) × volume, per segment. It shows *who* paid for the disruption.
- **Compliance debt:** the open count of incident cases without compliance close, weighted by product risk (controlled ×5, cold-chain ×3, recall-sensitive ×3, ambient ×1). The incident is not over until this reaches its baseline level.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Implementation effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hard-stop rules for high-risk products:** no dispatch of controlled, cold-chain or recall-sensitive items without dual control, temperature record and lot capture; if not possible, source an alternative | Low (policy and training) | Eliminates the most severe concealed noncompliance (G and the unreleased F path for these products); removes regulatory and patient-safety exposure | Lower throughput for these products during D2, so some hospitals must be served from an alternative depot or partner. Accept this explicitly |
| 2 | **Pre-approved degraded-mode playbook** with declared modes, triggers, exit criteria and the tiered minimum-control matrix (Section 4) | Medium (4 to 8 weeks of design, QA and regulator-aware review) | Converts improvisation into governed, bounded adaptation; separates resilience from shortcuts before the next incident | Stress-induced deviation from the playbook; mitigated by drills |
| 3 | **Offline evidence kit** (numbered forms, store-and-forward scanners, cameras) plus mandatory **provenance and bitemporal tagging** of all manual and reconstructed events | Medium to high (hardware, integration of buffered scans, data model change) | Largely closes the traceability gap; makes remediation honest; enables real-time vs remediated conformance measurement | Device failure or loss; transcription errors (mitigated by four-eyes checks) |
| 4 | **Daily offline export of authorization, recall and quarantine master data** (encrypted, integrity-hashed, stored off-network) | Low to medium | Makes B fast and scalable; makes C unnecessary or safely bounded; blocks recalled lots | Up to 24h staleness; the export itself could be compromised if the cyber incident predates it (verify hash chain) |
| 5 | **Start reconciliation and remediation in D1/D2 as a parallel, separately staffed lane;** separate compliance close from invoice close | Low to medium (roles, workflow states, reporting) | Shortens the compliance tail; removes the illusion of recovery; makes hidden workload visible and planned | Requires reserving staff during peak disruption, which slightly reduces flow capacity |
| 6 | **Formal backlog scoring with aging and a pharmacy capacity floor;** line-level clinical criticality list; deduplication of re-keyed orders | Low | Fairer, auditable prioritization; protects patient-critical pharmacy lines; reduces the 30% backlog cancellations and duplicate shipments | Hospital p90 may rise slightly; the criticality list needs maintenance |
| 7 | **Cross-trained compliance reserve pool** (for example 2 to 3x normal authorization-review capacity on call) | Medium (training and certification) | Removes the B bottleneck that likely drove C; supports faster pharmacy service | Reserve staff are less experienced (mitigated by checklists and sampling review) |
| 8 | **Inventory freshness policy:** snapshot TTL, shift-based cycle counts of high-risk and fast-moving locations, single manual reservation ledger | Medium | Prevents phantom-stock promises (740 pick-list losses, 300 reconciliation cancellations) and double allocation | Counting consumes labour during a disruption |
| 9 | **Courier and customer evidence integration** (courier tracking and POD feeds, e-signature link for customers) as independent secondary evidence | Medium | Strengthens H and G for ambient; recoverable evidence for remediation | Dependence on third-party systems, which may also be affected by the incident |
| 10 | **Incident process-mining review:** mode-attributed conformance against an emergency reference model, fairness and compliance-debt dashboard, and a lookback report to management and QA | Low to medium | Institutional learning; detects normalization of deviance (T4 control gaps still 2.5x) | Findings depend on the quality of the provenance data (see recommendation 3) |
| 11 | **Twice-yearly degraded-mode drills** (tabletop plus a live half-shift exercise), including a cyber-integrity scenario | Low to medium | Faster, more disciplined switching into D2; tests the kit and the exports | Drills never fully replicate real stress |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** a temporary loss of flow and fairness for lower-priority segments: longer pharmacy and wholesale cycle times, deferral of bulk orders, and a bounded rise in documentation effort. Those are the price of protecting hospital supply. It should also accept **governed, recorded substitutes**: manual authorization from offline lists, pharmacist release gates, manual pick forms with lot capture, and structured phone POD for ambient products.

Management should **not accept** four things:
1. Throughput preserved by removing critical controls: assumed authorization, dispatch without release or scan for controlled, cold-chain or recall-sensitive items, and phone-only POD for controlled substances.
2. Reconstructed evidence that is indistinguishable from real-time evidence.
3. Declaring recovery when cycle time returns while compliance debt and remediation workload remain open.
4. Informal prioritization that silently shifts the burden to pharmacies.

The recommendation would change under these conditions:
- **Looser controls:** case-level review could show that the C, fast-lane and unscanned-dispatch cases were almost exclusively ambient, licence-valid and fully recoverable from courier or customer records. In that case, a more permissive known-customer and ambient-dispatch rule is justified.
- **Tighter controls:** the review could find `NP` dual-control gaps, shipments of quarantined or recalled lots, or unauthorized recipients. The same applies if the T4 gaps prove to be new cases rather than legacy ones, which would indicate normalization of deviance. In that case, controls must be stricter and D2 capacity for high-risk products should be deliberately reduced in favour of alternative sourcing.
- **Pharmacy priority:** evidence that deferred pharmacy orders contained patient-critical lines would move pharmacies up the protection floor.
