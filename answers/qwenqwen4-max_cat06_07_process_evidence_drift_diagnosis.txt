| Jan–Mar | Clean, 100% mature, old regime | Safe: base model, baseline standardisation, control edges. Not representative of current V3 risk |
| Apr | **Unsafe** until T2 replay passes and cache is recomputed | Quarantine; after certified replay, usable with an incident flag; never usable if UNKNOWN-affected prefixes cannot be reconstructed |
| May | Conditionally safe | Only after confirming no April contamination in features/fits and residual ECE explained; otherwise treat as suspect |
| Jun | Clean + mature, new mix | Safe and valuable (teaches the new routing mix); flag routing-selection shift |
| Jul | 98% mature | Safe with IPCW or by excluding the last days of the cohort; first month of the new V3 regime |
| Aug | 93% mature, new regime | High-value but **wait for full maturity** (p95 label delay) or use IPCW; essential for learning supplier-driven risk |
| Sep | 82% mature, differential maturity by source | Not yet for calibration/training; usable now for covariate/population monitoring and unlabeled drift tests |
| Oct | **55% mature, informative censoring** | **Unsafe** for any label-dependent fitting or metric certification. Usable for DQ, process, population and score-distribution monitoring only |

**Segment- or variant-specific model?** Prefer **one model with variant, partner and lead-time features plus interactions and hierarchical offsets** over a fleet of models. Justification: V1/V2 conditionals are stable and share structure; V3's problem is a missing mediator, best fixed by features, not partitioning. A dedicated V3 model becomes defensible only if T5 shows surviving conditional divergence *after* lead-time features, and mature V3 volume supports it (Jul–Oct V3 6.6k cases, but only the mature fraction is trainable). A dedicated **Partner Z** model is *not* justified now: Z V3 in September is 0.24×5,100×0.58  710 cases, mostly immature — a recipe for overfitting and for encoding a maturity artefact as "Z risk." Revisit after T6.

# 5. Monitoring architecture

**Layer 0 — Change/intervention ledger (the spine).** Every deployment and business intervention is a first-class annotated event: 1 Apr rename, 6 May mapping patch, 20 Apr dedup boundary, 1 Jun routing policy, 10 Jul supplier degradation, 1 Sep Partner Z onboarding, Oct outcome-API backlog, plus model/calibrator versions and feature-schema versions. All charts overlay these markers; no change point is investigated without consulting the ledger.

**Layer 1 — Data quality & lineage (highest alert precedence).** Alias-registry hit rate; `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share (warn >1%, page >3%); exact/near-duplicate rates *by source*; missingness per feature × source × variant (page >5%); PSI per feature; cache-version freshness and backfill completeness; **prefix-integrity assertions** (ends at normalised diagnosis; zero post-diagnosis leakage); prediction coverage (scored/started).

**Layer 2 — Label maturity & outcome availability.** Maturity curve by start month × variant × source; median and p90 label delay; outstanding-label count; outcome-API queue depth/oldest item; IPCW/KM eventual-breach estimate with bounds. Emits a machine-readable `maturity_ok(month, segment)` flag and a `censoring_informative` flag (tests whether outstandingness correlates with variant/path length).

**Layer 3 — Predictive performance.** AUROC/AUPRC/recall at fixed alert budget, per variant × source × month, computed **only** where `maturity_ok` and DQ green; otherwise a fixed-vintage or IPCW variant clearly labelled *provisional*.

**Layer 4 — Calibration.** Calibration slope/intercept, ECE, observed/expected ratio, Brier decomposition, split into mix vs within-stratum components. Gated on Layers 1–2.

**Layer 5 — Process variant & performance DFG.** Variant frequency JS divergence vs baseline; unmapped/new-variant share; rework-loop rate; median/p90 per critical edge with EWMA/CUSUM, always paired with a **negative-control edge** (diagnosis`Remote Fix`) to separate real slowdown from clock/logging faults.

**Layer 6 — Population.** Partner/source mix; rural-distance and device-age distributions; density-ratio overlap and out-of-support fraction; per-stratum maturity differential (so a new stratum cannot silently bias aggregate metrics).

**Alert dependency / routing policy (prevents unnecessary retraining)**

| Trigger | Suppressed by | Routed to | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| DQ red (UNKNOWN/dup/missingness/prefix assertion) | — (highest precedence) | Data platform | Incident + replay; **suppresses all calibration/drift/retrain alerts downstream** |
| Label-maturity red or informative censoring | DQ red | Outcome-API owner | Switch to fixed-vintage/IPCW; mark provisional; **suppress recalibration & retraining triggers** |
| Variant-mix / population shift, within-stratum metrics stable | DQ or maturity red | Ops + modelling | Mix-standardised reporting; recalibration review; **no retraining** |
| Performance-DFG edge shift, control edge flat | DQ red | Supplier/procurement + modelling | Operational escalation; feature-engineering ticket |
| Within-stratum calibration drift on mature data | DQ or maturity red | Modelling | Segment-conditional recalibration |
| Within-stratum discrimination drift, persisting after reweighting **and** after lead-time features | DQ or maturity red | Modelling | Retraining candidate  shadow  challenger |

Retraining requires a conjunction: DQ green **AND** maturity 95% (or IPCW-certified) **AND** drift survives mix reweighting **AND** within-stratum degradation confirmed **AND** minimum mature sample met **AND** human approval. A label-delay alarm alone can never satisfy it.

```python
# Monthly process-evidence extraction for audit. No model fitting, no invented drift detector.
# Pin versions: pm4py>=2.7, pandas>=2.0. Outputs auditable JSON/CSV per month.
import json
import numpy as np, pandas as pd, pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

ALIAS = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}  # eff. 2026-04-01, map v2
DIAG, EDGE, CTRL = "Initial Diagnosis Completed", ("Order Part", "Part Received"), (DIAG, "Remote Fix")
BASE_MIX = {"V1": 0.45, "V2": 0.35, "V3": 0.20}

def build_log(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "ingested_at"])
    audit = {"rows_raw": len(df)}
    df["activity_raw"] = df["activity"]
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(ALIAS)                    # normalize BEFORE features/variants
    audit["renamed"] = int((df.activity_raw != df.activity).sum())
    audit["unknown_share"] = float(df.activity.eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean())  # quarantine, never guess
    dup = df.duplicated(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first")     # uniform across ALL sources
    audit["dups_dropped_by_source"] = df[dup].groupby("source").size().to_dict()
    df = df.loc[~dup].copy()
    gap = df.groupby(["case_id", "activity"])["timestamp"].diff().dt.total_seconds()
    df["near_dup_flag"] = gap.le(60)                                  # flag only: may be genuine rework
    audit["near_dup_flagged"] = int(df.near_dup_flag.sum())
    df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp")
    return df, audit

def to_log(frame):
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(frame)

def prefix_frame(df):                                                  # prediction-time view: cut at diagnosis
    d = df[df.activity.eq(DIAG)].groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("t_diag")
    j = df.join(d, on="case_id")
    return j[j.t_diag.notna() & j.timestamp.le(j.t_diag)].drop(columns="t_diag"), int(d.size)

def edge_stats(df, edge):                                              # pm4py perf-DFG defaults to mean; audit tails too
    s = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
    nxt = s.groupby("case_id")["activity"].shift(-1)
    dt = (s.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].shift(-1) - s["timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    m = s.activity.eq(edge[0]) & nxt.eq(edge[1])
    v = dt[m].dropna()
    return {"n": int(v.size), "median_h": float(v.median()) if len(v) else None,
            "p90_h": float(v.quantile(0.9)) if len(v) else None}

def monthly(df, month):
    s = pd.Timestamp(f"{month}-01", tz="UTC"); e = s + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    full = to_log(df)
    ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(full, s, e, mode="events")            # event-calendar cohort: workload view,
    starts = df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()                  #   mixes start cohorts, favours long cases
    ids = starts[starts.ge(s) & starts.lt(e)].index
    cs_df = df[df.case_id.isin(ids)]                                   # case-start cohort: aligns to the model's
    cs = to_log(cs_df)                                                 #   scoring population; right-censored tail
    pre, n_scored = prefix_frame(cs_df)
    dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(cs)
    perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cs)                          # mean seconds per edge (pm4py convention)
    var = pm4py.get_variants(cs)
    counts = {(k if isinstance(k, str) else "|".join(k)): len(v) for k, v in var.items()}
    shares = {k: v / max(sum(counts.values()), 1) for k, v in counts.items()}
    p = np.array(list(shares.values())); q = np.array([BASE_MIX.get(k, 0.0) for k in shares])
    q = q / q.sum() if q.sum() else q
    m = (p + q) / 2
    js = float(0.5 * np.sum(p[p > 0] * np.log2(p[p > 0] / m[p > 0])) +
               0.5 * np.sum(q[q > 0] * np.log2(q[q > 0] / m[q > 0])))   # computed here, not a pm4py API
    try:
        tf = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(to_log(pre))    # audit distributions only, never fit
        tf_summary = tf.describe().loc[["mean", "50%", "max"]].to_dict()
    except Exception as exc:
        tf_summary = {"error": str(exc)}
    return {"month": month, "n_cases_started": int(len(ids)), "n_cases_with_diagnosis_prefix": n_scored,
            "prefix_coverage": n_scored / max(len(ids), 1),
            "event_calendar_events": len(ev), "variant_counts": counts, "variant_shares": shares,
            "variant_js_vs_baseline": js, "dfg_edges": {f"{a}->{b}": int(c) for (a, b), c in dfg.items()},
            "perf_dfg_pm4py_mean_s": {f"{a}->{b}": v for (a, b), v in perf.items()},
            "parts_edge": edge_stats(cs_df, EDGE), "control_edge": edge_stats(cs_df, CTRL),
            "prefix_temporal_features": tf_summary,
            "note": "labels intentionally absent; join maturity/IPCW estimates downstream"}

if __name__ == "__main__":
    frame, dq = build_log()
    out = {"data_quality_audit": dq,
           "months": [monthly(frame, f"2026-{m:02d}") for m in range(1, 11)]}
    with open("process_evidence_2026.json", "w") as f:
        json.dump(out, f, indent=2, default=str)
```

The two cohorts answer different questions and must never be pooled: the **event-calendar** cohort describes what the organisation *did* in a month (throughput, supplier load) and over-represents long-running cases; the **case-start** cohort is the predictive denominator and is right-censored at the reporting cutoff, which is exactly why its label-dependent metrics must be maturity-gated.

# 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**R1 — Retraining or recalibrating on October's immature labels.** With 55% maturity and censoring concentrated on long V3 paths, the training signal says "breach 0.16, model over-predicts." A downward intercept correction would be fitted at precisely the moment the true rate is plausibly ~0.36, systematically under-triaging the highest-risk cases and converting a measurement artefact into an SLA failure. *Mitigation:* hard maturity gate on every label-dependent fit; IPCW/KM or fixed-vintage targets; publish worst-case bounds; require the sign of the calibration gap to be stable across vintages before acting; shadow-mode any candidate.

**R2 — Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.** Fitting a calibrator on April's +0.09 intercept gap "fixes" ECE while cementing a wrong-prefix feature representation, hiding the incident, and poisoning the training set with corrupted cached counts and asymmetrically deduplicated events. It also destroys the ability to detect the *next* schema change, because the monitor now expects inflated risk. *Mitigation:* DQ precedence over performance alerts; replay-before-recalibrate policy; quarantine April until certified; version the alias registry and assert prefix integrity in CI; never let a calibrator absorb a shift that co-occurs with a DQ spike.

**R3 — Reading Aug–Sep as pure mix/covariate drift and only reweighting.** Reweighting to the January mix leaves the +8-pt within-V3 deterioration untouched; the model keeps pricing V3 at ~0.33 when reality is 0.61–0.64. Recall on breaching parts-dependent cases collapses while aggregate AUROC looks "only mildly down." *Mitigation:* mandatory within-variant conditional tests (T5) before choosing recalibration; add lead-time-at-diagnosis features (ETA, not realised receipt, to avoid leakage); monitor V3 recall at a fixed alert budget; pair the statistical fix with supplier escalation, since the cause is operational.

**R4 — Attributing Partner Z's 0.45 vs 0.27 gap to model unfairness without stratification.** Most of that gap is variant composition (0.42 vs 0.31 from mature variant rates) and Z's slower maturity biases its observed rate *downward*. Lowering Z's threshold would over-alert on rural cases and could entrench a stereotype; conversely, declaring "no fairness issue" ignores that rural distance and device age are legitimate, *unmeasured* risk drivers whose omission systematically under-serves Z. *Mitigation:* stratify by variant and maturity before any fairness claim; audit whether Z's promised resolution dates differ contractually (label definition, not behaviour); run the transport/reweighting test; add the missing features; evaluate fairness conditional on legitimate risk factors; avoid partner ID as a bare proxy and avoid a small-sample Z-only model.

**R5 — Treating June's breach-rate drop as improvement.** 0.180.15 is composition (fixed-mix 0.174  baseline 0.171). Reading it as capability gain invites threshold relaxation, capacity cuts, or premature model promotion — just before the supplier shock. *Mitigation:* always publish mix-standardised rates alongside raw rates; decompose every aggregate movement into mix vs within-stratum terms; annotate the routing-policy intervention on all charts.

**R6 — Over-aggressive "cleaning" that deletes real process signal.** Deduplicating on (case, activity) alone, or within-60s windows, removes genuine rework and repeat `Order Part` events — precisely the events most predictive of breach. Similarly, force-mapping `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` to diagnosis fabricates prefixes. *Mitigation:* dedup only on deterministic keys including source event ID; flag near-duplicates for review rather than deleting; quarantine UNKNOWN and report its share; keep a removal ledger and re-run metrics pre/post cleaning to quantify impact.

**R7 — Auto-retraining loops and alert fatigue.** Censoring, DQ and population alarms firing together produce oscillating models and unreviewable churn. *Mitigation:* the dependency-gated conjunction in §5, a monthly change budget, human approval for weight changes, and a champion/challenger shadow period with pre-registered acceptance criteria on mature, clean, mix-standardised cohorts.
