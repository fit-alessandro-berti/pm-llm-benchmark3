**1. Current-process diagnosis**

The dominant performance contradiction is that the highest-frequency path (Intake  Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement, 2550 cases) shows the lowest pre-rework cost and shortest cycle times (median 0.4 h Auto-to-approval, 1.8 h to dispatch) yet produces materially worse downstream outcomes than slower paths. Specific artifacts confirm this: (a) frequency DFG shows 2550 fast approvals versus only 390 repair paths; (b) performance DFG records Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen at mean 72 h (p90 150 h) and Dispatch  complaint at mean 96 h (p90 180 h); (c) conformance diagnostics show frequent move-on-log violations of R1 (high-value >EUR 400 without Manual/Fraud review) and R2 (dispatch before evidence completeness except Premium low-risk); (d) rework statistics indicate 14.1 % complaint/reopen rate on fast-approval cases versus 3.3 % on Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device; (e) segment outcomes reveal Premium high-value fast-path complaints at 22 % and Standard high-value at 18 %, with only 9 % net cost advantage after reshipments and credits. Additional problems include repeated evidence loops (26 % of cases entering Request missing evidence), low model precision from non-normative auto/risk/repair combinations, and Manual eligibility review queue pressure above 82 % utilization. The 22 % unclear-defect segment drives 140 h median cycle time and highest abandonment.

**2. Redesign A: Segment-aware auto-screen with high-value pre-dispatch gate**

The flow becomes: Intake  updated Auto eligibility screen (segment + value rules)  conditional Fast approval only for Premium/Standard low-risk accessories or low-value devices; all high-value and unclear-defect cases route to Fraud/risk review or Manual eligibility review, followed by a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate (6–18 h) before Dispatch replacement; repair paths remain unchanged for mid/high-value. It targets Premium and Standard high-value segments (35 % of volume) plus the 22 % unclear-defect group. Improvement occurs by enforcing R1/R2 at the auto layer and catching wrong-part selection before dispatch, directly cutting the 14.1 % fast-path complaint rate observed in the performance DFG. It deliberately sacrifices Auto-to-dispatch speed for high-value claims and accepts a 15–35 % increase in manual-review inflow.

**3. Redesign B: Evidence-portal-first for unclear and repeated-evidence cases**

The flow inserts a customer-facing evidence portal immediately after Intake claim for all unclear-defect or missing-evidence cases (22 % + 26 % repeated-evidence subset); only cases that remain incomplete after one portal cycle enter Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review; fast approval is restricted to cases with complete evidence at first screen; repair quote and quality-check paths unchanged. It targets the unclear-defect segment and the Request missing evidence  Manual loop (890 cases). Improvement is achieved by reducing repeated evidence requests 30–45 % and lowering the 140 h median cycle time for that segment, which also reduces abandonment. It deliberately leaves high-value compliance risk (R1) and Premium service-credit exposure unresolved and may increase abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers.

**4. Redesign C: Premium fast-lane plus mandatory repair quote for Standard high-value**

The flow creates two parallel tracks after Intake: Premium cases (32 % share) receive expedited Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch with dedicated escalation-board priority; Standard high-value cases are forced through Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review  Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device, bypassing fast approval. Low-risk accessories retain fast approval. It targets Premium service-level expectations and Standard high-value cost-of-wrong-replacement exposure. Improvement is realized by protecting Premium cycle time (currently 20 h fast-path median) while shifting Standard high-value volume to the observed 3.3 % complaint repair path, cutting reshipment cost (EUR 410 expected for wrong high-value replacement). It deliberately sacrifices fast-path cost advantage for Standard high-value and increases pressure on the three repair-quote technicians.

**6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks**

Redesign A could fail if the pre-dispatch gate is under-resourced, creating new 18–30 h queues, or if auto-rule updates misclassify 10–15 % of borderline high-value claims. Bottleneck migration likely moves from Fraud/risk review to the new gate inspectors.  
Redesign B could fail if portal adoption is below 60 % among low-digital customers, increasing abandonment, or if evidence quality remains insufficient, merely shifting loops. Bottleneck migration moves from Manual eligibility review to portal support staff.  
Redesign C could fail if repair technicians cannot absorb the added Standard high-value volume (capacity already constrained) or if Premium fast-lane creates perceived inequity. Bottleneck migration moves from manual review to repair-quote technicians and escalation-board slots.

**7. Recommended experiment sequence**

First pilot: Redesign A restricted to Premium high-value devices for six weeks, using existing Fraud/risk reviewers plus one new gate inspector. Metrics: R1/R2 violation rate, complaint/reopen %, cycle time p90, service-credit exposure, manual-queue utilization. Stop/go: continue if violation rate drops 40 % and complaint rate 12 % with p90 cycle time 48 h; stop if utilization exceeds 90 % or abandonment rises >5 %.  
Second pilot: Redesign B on the unclear-defect segment only, measuring evidence-loop count and abandonment.  
Sequencing logic: run A first because it directly addresses the highest-volume compliance and complaint deviations visible in conformance diagnostics; proceed to B only if A shows stable resource pressure.  
Learning whether gains are real: track end-to-end cost per claim (including 90-day post-close credits) and segment-specific complaint rates rather than cycle time alone; compare against a hold-out group of identical segments to detect downstream cost shifting.
