## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is partial, so an observation is classified by the *status* of the relation it touches, not by whether it appears in the normative edge set.

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation whose status is `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10) and whose supporting events are believed real. Closed-world applies, so one occurrence is a candidate violation; the only escape is showing the event/object link is an artefact.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: an in-scope object or event lacks a `mandatory` relation (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Closed-world applies only inside the stated scope ("externally shipped Packages", "newly created standard Orders", "every Add Item event"), so scope must be confirmed before a gap counts.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: a relation covered by no forbidden or mandatory rule (Validate Order -> Issue Invoice, MCR–Customer, Package codeath Delivery, N6/T4/T5/E9). Open-world: absence from the norm is not evidence against it.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: an in-scope object lacks a `typical` relation (N2, N5, T3). A follow-up signal, never a violation; the norm names legitimate exceptions.
- **Likely data-quality error**: the relation is probably an extraction fact (bad join, dropped reference, unlabeled event type) rather than a process fact, and a concrete mechanism is known (E10 reused identifier, Delivery present only as a carrier attribute).
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the rule says "eventually" but the summary measures directly-follows (N1), or the rule is event-scoped but the summary is object-level (T2 vs E6). Behavior may conform under the intended abstraction and fail under the measured one.
- **Insufficient evidence**: the summary cannot separate alternatives (14 open invoices could be credit terms or right-censoring). Classification is deferred to trace inspection.

Open-world vs closed-world cuts both ways. A fully closed-world reading turns 72 Validate -> Issue Invoice transitions and 100 Order–Customer interactions into false violations; an open-world reading of the forbidden rows would wave through two Cancel -> Dispatch cases as "rare behavior". Closed-world is therefore applied only to forbidden rows and to in-scope mandatory rows; everything else is open-world with a watch list for typical patterns.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG establishes, per object type, which activity pairs occur in sequence within a lifecycle and how often. It shows Cancel Order followed by Dispatch Package for 2 Orders, exactly what N3 forbids, and 198 of 200 external packages going Pack -> Dispatch.

It cannot establish causality or intent, and cannot distinguish "never happened" from "not yet happened" (right-censoring) or "happened but unrecorded". The 14 open invoices and the 2 orders without Create -> Validate are indistinguishable from censoring here.

Counts and scope drive interpretation. N4 is already scoped to external packages (200), so the 2 gaps are in-scope; but the ET–OT table shows Pack–Package = 200 and Dispatch–Package = 200, so both events exist for every package and the gaps must be ordering inversions or intervening events, a sharper hypothesis than "packing skipped". The counts are also inconsistent with strict directly-follows semantics: 98 Create -> Validate plus 3 Create -> Manual Compliance Review exceeds 100 orders, and 83 Validate -> Close plus 72 Validate -> Issue Invoice exceeds 100. Either the report counts eventually-follows pairs or Validate Order repeats. Under eventually-follows, the 2 orders never validated at all, which is more serious; under directly-follows, the 3 compliance-review orders should already have produced 3 gaps, not 2. The metric decides whether finding 8 is noise or a validation bypass.

This is the danger of reading an "eventually expected" rule (N1's scope text) as directly-follows: every legitimate intermediate event (review, hold, enrichment) becomes a false negative, while the genuine case (validation never occurred) hides inside the same count. The correct N1 check is eventually-follows with a censoring cut-off; the directly-follows figure is a proxy.

A relation can conform at one abstraction and be suspicious at another: Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package is an ordinary Package lifecycle step (Pack -> Dispatch holds) but a forbidden Order lifecycle step, visible only in the Order-flattened view.

### OTG

The OTG establishes which object types share events (interaction), are born together (cobirth) or die together (codeath), with aggregate weights. It confirms T1 (Order–Item 1450), T2 at object level (Package–Delivery 200), T3 (Invoice–Payment 92 of 93 payments), and shows one forbidden T6 cobirth.

It cannot identify the producing event, count per-event coverage, or separate a real relationship from a spurious join. Cobirth is defined on each object's first event, so one mis-attributed Payment (a prepayment record whose earliest event is Create Order) yields a T6 hit without any payment being created with the order.

T1 and T2 are event-scoped ("for an Add Item interaction", "for a Dispatch Package interaction") but the OTG aggregates over all events. Package–Delivery = 200 therefore looks fully conforming while two Dispatch Package events lack a Delivery; the 200 is satisfied by the later confirmation event that also yields the 7 codeath edges. Conforming in OTG, non-conforming in ET–OT, and ET–OT is the view matching the norm's scope. Codeath and Order–Customer edges are open-world; nothing in the OTG can make them violations.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT graph establishes, per activity, which object types are attached and how often. It is the right abstraction for E1–E10 because those rules are event-scoped. It shows E1, E4, E5, E7 fully satisfied (100, 200, 200, 93), E2 short by 2 (1448 vs 1450, assuming one Item per Add Item), E6 short by 2 (198 vs 200), E8 short by 1 (92 vs 93), and one E10 hit.

It cannot show order or timing, nor whether a missing reference means the object does not exist or was not extracted. The Delivery gap shows a business fact (carrier attribute) the model omits; the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge shows the model containing a relation the business lacks. The supplied ET–OT table is also visibly partial (no rows for Validate, Cancel, Close, Issue Invoice, Write Off), so a missing row is not a missing relation, one more reason for the open-world default.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**
- N1 Create -> Validate: 98/100; the 2 gaps are abstraction mismatch if an intermediate event exists, censoring if the orders are recent, violations if eventually-follows also fails.
- N4 Pack -> Dispatch: 198/200; benign if an intermediate re-pack/inspection exists, violation if Dispatch precedes Pack.
- E2/T1 Add Item–Order: 1448/1450; violation if real orphan lines, DQ if the order key was dropped.
- E6/T2 Dispatch–Delivery: 198/200; model incompleteness if the carrier attribute maps to a Delivery, violation if no delivery exists.
- E8 Record Payment–Invoice: 92/93; labeling violation now; conforming only if re-typed as unapplied cash.
- E1, E4, E5, E7: satisfied.

**Forbidden**
- N3 Cancel -> Dispatch (2): violation unless timestamps or linkage prove otherwise.
- T6 Order cobirth Payment (1): violation unless the payment's first event is mis-attributed.
- E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice (1): formally forbidden; becomes a data-quality error once the reused-identifier join is confirmed.

**Typical**
- N2 Validate -> Close: 83 direct; others route via invoicing or remain open.
- N5 Issue Invoice -> Record Payment: 92/110; 4 write-offs are named exceptions; 14 open become a collections finding only if past due.
- T3 Invoice–Payment: 92/93; the gap is the E8 case.

**Unregulated**
- Validate -> Issue Invoice (72), Create -> MCR (3), Reopen -> Close (1), Order–Customer (100), Order–RiskCase (3), MCR–RiskCase (3), MCR–Customer (3), Package codeath Delivery (7). None can become violations without norm revision; Reopen -> Close changes bucket only if N6 is later governed.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 "eventually" vs directly-follows.** Risk: false negatives for every legitimate intermediate event, and masking of genuinely unvalidated orders. Clarification: define N1 as eventually-follows within a horizon (e.g., 48 h) with censoring rules near the snapshot end. Status: mandatory.
2. **Cancellation semantics for N3.** Risk: partial cancellations, returns after dispatch and timestamp ties are all counted alike. Clarification: N3 applies to full cancellations whose timestamp strictly precedes Dispatch Package; partial cancellation gets its own activity. Status: forbidden (as clarified).
3. **Unapplied cash labeling (E8).** Risk: finance says unapplied cash exists but nothing is typed, so every case is a formal violation and real misapplications hide among them. Clarification: an explicit "Record Unapplied Cash" activity or attribute with a later reconciliation requirement. Status: mandatory labeling; the labeled case explicitly unspecified for E8.
4. **Delivery materialisation (E6/T2).** Risk: carrier references stored only as attributes create phantom violations and break traceability. Clarification: every Dispatch Package must reference a Delivery object; carrier attributes are mapped to Delivery ids at extraction. Status: mandatory.
5. **Snapshot and open-item handling (N2, N5).** Risk: open invoices and unclosed orders are misread as absences; no tolerance bands exist. Clarification: exclude objects younger than the maximum expected cycle time; define an aging threshold after which absence becomes a collections finding. Status: typical, with the threshold specified.
6. **Reopen governance (N6).** Risk: reopened orders can be closed or dispatched without re-validation. Clarification: either declare Reopen -> Validate typical or keep N6 explicitly unspecified with a pointer to the governing policy. Status: explicitly unspecified unless the business chooses to govern.
7. **Identifier typing behind E10.** Risk: reused numeric identifiers keep producing forbidden edges. Clarification: require typed, namespaced object identifiers at extraction. Status: mandatory (data governance).

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust: each forbidden row is touched at least once (N3 twice, T6 once, E10 once); the ET–OT view shows real coverage gaps for E2, E6 and E8; the unregulated relations (Validate -> Issue Invoice, compliance review, reopen, codeath, Order–Customer) are not violations under any reading of the norm.

Dependent on the norm's incompleteness: whether the 2 N1 and 2 N4 gaps are violations hinges on the undefined eventually-vs-directly semantics, intermediate events and snapshot horizon; whether the E8 case is a violation hinges on the absent unapplied-cash label.

Dependent on suspected data quality: E10 is almost certainly a join artefact; T6 and the E2/E6 gaps may be extraction defects; the reused-identifier problem could affect edges not yet noticed.

Not decidable from summaries alone: event order and timing for N3 and N4, whether the same orders, packages or payment recur across findings, the true metric behind the counts, and whether the 14 open invoices and 2 unvalidated orders are censored or stalled. These require trace-level inspection of the OCEL.
