
The shared mechanism is the critical-path effect. When two independent checks can both start at registration, running them together removes the shorter branch (identity at both sites) from the time to Assessment. Both sites share the route true_overlap → sync_wait → waiting_before_assessment → cycle_time, and the idea that rework rises when a check loses information it used to get from the other one.

The site-specific mechanisms are the statutory originals prerequisite and the variability of originals delay, the legal-review stage and its supplement loop, the case lock and back-entry of events, specialized roles, and staffing levels. At Site A, the API upgrade, added staff and huddles act on queues and service times directly, not through overlap, so Site A's before/after change mixes paths that Site B will not reproduce.

The effect modifiers are originals_required and digital_documents, which determine whether overlap is feasible at all; IT_case_lock and role_specialization, which determine whether it happens in practice; assessment_capacity, which determines whether gains survive or merely move the queue downstream; and case_complexity and legal_review_required, which determine the tail and the rework risk.

A similar control-flow diagram does not guarantee transportability because a process tree records only ordering. It does not record data guards (when originals are required), resource bindings, locks, capacity or delay distributions. The same `+` operator yields true concurrency at Site A and, at Site B, blocked tasks, lock-serialized execution or tasks that merely look concurrent through back-entry. Transport requires that the mechanism, not the diagram, is invariant: P_B(y | do(x), z) = P_A(y | do(x), z) only within strata z where the S-nodes do not point into the path.

## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

Inputs common to all scenarios:

- **Site A prior.** Of the 9 h median reduction, roughly 1–5 h is plausibly attributable to parallelization. The rest is explained by mix (1.5–3.5 h), document capacity (1–2.5 h), API and service changes (0.3–1 h), huddles (0.5–2 h) and measurement (0–1 h). This is a prior, not an identified effect.
- **Mechanistic ceiling at Site B.** For a case where both checks can start at registration, the saving is about min(identity sojourn, document sojourn). The document branch is the longer one (8.1 h service against 3.4 h, and about 1.5 × the per-head load), so the saving is about the identity sojourn: 3.4 h + identity queue (assume 1–4 h) = 4.4–7.4 h. Subtracting handoff and synchronization overhead between specialized pools (0.5–2 h) gives a realistic 2.5–6 h.
- **Eligible cases.** Cases without originals are 38% (about 1,292). Cases without originals and without legal review are about 23% if the two traits are independent (15–30% if public-sector cases need originals more often).
- **Median arithmetic.** The site median (44 h) moves only through cases crossing it. Cases without originals avoid the 18 h wait and mostly lie below 44 h, so perhaps 20–40% of the density at the median comes from eligible cases. The site median change is therefore about 0.2–0.4 × the segment saving.
- **Tail arithmetic.** Supplement-loop cases are 10.6% of volume, about the top decile. The p90 of 110 h is dominated by loops, legal review and late originals, where parallelization has no lever. p90 moves mainly through the harmful mechanisms.

How each range was derived:

- **S1** multiplies Site A's relative changes by Site B's baseline (44 × 0.22 ≈ 9.7 h; 110 × 0.19 ≈ 20.6 h). It is included only to show what naive transport implies. It double-counts co-interventions and ignores the originals rule, and is legally infeasible for 62% of cases.
- **S2.** Segment saving 3–6 h (the ceiling after overhead) × 0.3–0.5 median sensitivity, plus a small gain in fast-originals cases, gives −1.5 to −3 h. Some tail cases have an identity branch that was late (slow external identity sources), which gives the p90 reduction of 2–5 h.
- **S3.** Segment saving 3–5 h, bounded by the lower half of Site A's attributable 1–5 h combined with Site B's identity sojourn. Site effect = 0.1–0.3 × segment saving. The tail is untouched because few eligible cases are in the top decile.
- **S4.** Assessor load is about 3,600 assessments per 26 weeks (46 per assessor per week), and the current 20 h wait before Assessment suggests queueing. If 50–100% of the verification gain is absorbed by the assessor queue and service order favours ready simple cases, the median still improves slightly. Complex and originals cases, which fill the tail, wait longer, so p90 rises by 3–8 h.
- **S5.** Blocked tasks in the system ≈ originals arrival rate × mean delay ≈ 0.48 cases/h × about 25 h (mean > 18 h median) ≈ 12 tasks, about 1.7 per document reviewer. Under a Site A-style cap of 6, that ties up about 28% of WIP slots. Premature checks on copies generate redo work, and cases waiting for originals are the tail cases, so p90 rises by 6–15 h while digital cases still improve the median.
- **S6.** Each lock conflict adds a handoff of 0.5–2 h. The ideal saving is cancelled for serialized cases, and conflict and data-quality issues add rework, which lands in the tail.
- **S7.** 1.5–3.5 pp more loop cases (about 50–120 cases) at 25–60 h each, entering the top decile, gives p90 +4 to +12 h. The median is barely affected because loop cases lie above it.
- **S8** combines S2's mechanism with the mitigations that remove S4–S7's harmful paths. Confidence is capped because the mitigations are themselves untested.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Name:** Eligibility-gated concurrent verification with originals-triggered document checking (EGCV).

**Eligibility rules (evaluated at `Register`, stored as case attributes):**
- **E1, wave 1:** `originals_required = false` AND all documents digital at registration AND NOT (public-sector OR high-value) AND no identity-document dependency flag (for example, power of attorney, name or ID mismatch, or identity documents that are also evidence). Complexity is recorded as a stratum and not used to exclude.
- **E2, wave 2 (after E1 guardrails pass):** as E1, but legal-review cases are allowed.
- **E3, originals cases:** no concurrent `Document Check`. `Identity Check` starts at registration, and the `Document Check` task is created by the `Originals Receipt` event. An optional separate treatment adds proactive originals requests and chasing.
- **Default (all others):** current sequential practice, which is a linear extension of the same partial order.
- **Re-evaluation:** if eligibility attributes change (for example, originals later found to be required), the case reverts to E3 and any started `Document Check` is voided and logged as an eligibility error.

**Concurrency permitted:**
- E1 and E2: `Identity Check ‖ Document Check`, both created on completion of `Register`.
- All cases: `Identity Check ‖ Originals Receipt`, and `Identity Check ‖ Document Check` once originals are received.
- Not permitted: `Assessment` concurrent with any verification; `Legal Review` concurrent with `Assessment`; rechecking the same document by two owners at once.

**Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory:**
- `Register` comes first.
- `start(Document Check) ≥ complete(Originals Receipt)` whenever originals are legally required.
- `Assessment` starts only after both verifications are complete.
- `Legal Review` happens before the decision for public-sector and high-value cases.
- After `Request Supplement`, the affected `Document Check` (and `Originals Receipt` if new originals are needed) and `Assessment` are repeated before `Legal Review` resumes.
- `Notify` comes after `Approve` or `Reject`.

**Queue and WIP controls:**
- Tasks are created only when they can start; blocked tasks never enter work queues.
- Per-reviewer cap of 5 open verification tasks.
- Priority in each verification pool: case age, plus a boost when the partner branch has completed, plus a boost for originals cases on arrival of originals.
- Assessment queue is FIFO by registration time, with no cherry-picking of ready simple cases.
- Trigger: if the Assessment queue exceeds 1.5 days of assessor capacity, or assessor utilization exceeds 85% for a week, pause new E2 admissions and escalate.
- Legal queue monitored weekly.

**Resource responsibilities:**
- Identity reviewers own `Identity Check`; document reviewers own `Document Check` and rechecks after supplements, returned to the original reviewer when available.
- Assessors own `Assessment` and reconciliation of both findings.
- Legal reviewers own `Legal Review` and `Request Supplement`, with specific document references.
- A rotating verification coordinator (team lead) watches synchronization waits and lock incidents.
- No cross-training is assumed; cross-training would be a separate intervention.

**Legal review and supplement loops:**
- A supplement request lists the affected documents and whether originals are required.
- Only the affected documents are rechecked; `Identity Check` is repeated only if the supplement touches identity.
- Supplement cases get priority on return, and re-review goes to the same legal reviewer.
- The supplement rate is a pilot guardrail.

**Logging requirements:**
- Start and complete events for every task, with resource and role.
- Lock acquire and release events.
- Claimed timestamp and system entry timestamp, with a manual-entry flag.
- Eligibility attributes and eligibility version; treatment arm and block.
- `originals_required`, originals request, receipt and channel.
- Legal flag; supplement reason and affected documents; rework reason codes.
- Daily snapshots of queue length and WIP per pool.
- Object links (case, documents, originals) suitable for OCEL export.

The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by measuring the chain from treatment to outcome link by link, instead of looking only at cycle time.

- **Implementation failure** means the treatment did not happen as designed: low lock-verified concurrent ownership, high back-entry, partner tasks started late because priority rules were ignored, or document checks created for blocked cases. The fidelity metrics from the run-in and each phase detect this directly. Effects are then estimated both as intention-to-treat and within high-fidelity weeks, and treated cases with no real overlap are identified from lock and lifecycle events.
- **Mechanism failure** means that real, verified overlap did not shorten the time to Assessment readiness in the proximal outcome. It would show that identity is already off the critical path, or that the synchronization wait is not what drives the pre-assessment interval.
- **Downstream failure** means the proximal gain appears but is absorbed by the assessment or legal queues. Phase B's system-level contrasts and the split of waiting into synchronization and assessor queue reveal this.

Comparing T1 with T2 shows whether the implementation bundle, rather than concurrency itself, delivers the benefit. The negative controls guard against measurement artefacts like Site A's timestamp change being read as an effect.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1) Site A before/after as process trees
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SITE_A_BEFORE = (
    "->( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)
SITE_A_AFTER = (
    "->( 'Register', +( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', "
    "X( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

tree_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SITE_A_BEFORE)
tree_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SITE_A_AFTER)


def describe_net(name, net, im, fm):
    is_wf = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
    soundness = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    is_sound = soundness[0] if isinstance(soundness, tuple) else bool(soundness)
    print(f"{name}: places={len(net.places)} transitions={len(net.transitions)} "
          f"workflow_net={is_wf} sound={is_sound}")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2) Convert Site A trees to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
for name, tree in [("Site A before", tree_a_before), ("Site A after", tree_a_after)]:
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    print(name, "tree:", tree)
    describe_net(name, net, im, fm)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3) Site B candidate as POWL
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def close_transitively(spo):
    """StrictPartialOrder must be transitive; add implied edges explicitly."""
    nodes = spo.children
    changed = True
    while changed:
        changed = False
        for a in nodes:
            for b in nodes:
                if a is b or not spo.order.is_edge(a, b):
                    continue
                for c in nodes:
                    if c is not a and spo.order.is_edge(b, c) and not spo.order.is_edge(a, c):
                        spo.order.add_edge(a, c)
                        changed = True
    return spo


def partial_order(nodes, cover_edges):
    spo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in cover_edges:
        spo.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return close_transitively(spo)


register = Transition("Register")
identity_check = Transition("Identity Check")
# Guard (data, not encodable): Originals Receipt iff originals legally required.
originals_gate = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition("Originals Receipt"), SilentTransition()])
document_check = Transition("Document Check")
assessment = Transition("Assessment")

# Supplement redo branch: Request Supplement -> [originals if needed] -> Document Check -> Assessment
request_supplement = Transition("Request Supplement")
supplement_originals = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition("Originals Receipt"), SilentTransition()])
document_recheck = Transition("Document Check")
reassessment = Transition("Assessment")
supplement_redo = partial_order(
    [request_supplement, supplement_originals, document_recheck, reassessment],
    [(request_supplement, supplement_originals),
     (supplement_originals, document_recheck),
     (document_recheck, reassessment)],
)

# *(Legal Review, redo): Legal Review, then zero or more (redo, Legal Review) iterations.
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [Transition("Legal Review"), supplement_redo])
# Guard (data, not encodable): legal branch iff public-sector or high-value.
legal_branch = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [legal_loop, SilentTransition()])
decision = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition("Approve"), Transition("Reject")])
notify = Transition("Notify")

site_b_nodes = [register, identity_check, originals_gate, document_check,
                assessment, legal_branch, decision, notify]
site_b = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=site_b_nodes)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4) Explicit order edges. Identity Check is unordered w.r.t. Originals Receipt
#    and Document Check (true concurrency); Assessment waits for BOTH branches.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
site_b.order.add_edge(register, identity_check)
site_b.order.add_edge(register, originals_gate)
site_b.order.add_edge(originals_gate, document_check)   # statutory prerequisite
site_b.order.add_edge(identity_check, assessment)       # AND-join, branch 1
site_b.order.add_edge(document_check, assessment)       # AND-join, branch 2
site_b.order.add_edge(assessment, legal_branch)
site_b.order.add_edge(legal_branch, decision)
site_b.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
close_transitively(site_b)

assert site_b.order.is_strict_partial_order()
assert supplement_redo.order.is_strict_partial_order()
assert not site_b.order.is_edge(identity_check, document_check)
assert not site_b.order.is_edge(document_check, identity_check)
assert site_b.order.is_edge(identity_check, assessment) and site_b.order.is_edge(document_check, assessment)
print("Site B POWL:", site_b)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5) + 6) Convert POWL to an accepting Petri net and check properties
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_b)
describe_net("Site B candidate", net_b, im_b, fm_b)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7) Playout: sample traces (control-flow only; frequencies are NOT calibrated)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sim_log = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
for trace in list(sim_log)[:10]:
    print(" -> ".join(event["concept:name"] for event in trace))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8) What this model cannot encode
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# - Data guards: the XOR choices for Originals Receipt (62% legal requirement) and
#   Legal Review (public-sector/high-value, 40%) are unguarded; playout picks
#   branches arbitrarily, so it can generate a legal-review-free public-sector case.
#   The statutory rules need data-aware conformance (guards on case attributes).
# - Eligibility of the concurrent variant (digital, no originals, no legal review):
#   the partial order permits concurrency for all cases; the policy restricts it.
# - Resource roles and capacity (4 identity, 7 document, 3 assessors, 2 legal),
#   WIP caps, queue priorities and assessor saturation: no resources in POWL.
# - Case locks: "concurrent" in the model does not mean executable concurrently in
#   the legacy system; lock-serialized execution fits the same model.
# - Durations, originals-arrival variability, tails and queueing delay: none; use a
#   stochastic/simulation model calibrated on Site B event data for that.
# - Partial rework semantics (which documents are rechecked after a supplement) and
#   bounded loop counts; the loop admits unbounded iterations.
# - Timestamp reliability: back-entered events can fit the model perfectly while
#   misrepresenting true overlap.
```

When run, both Site A nets and the Site B net are workflow nets and sound. Sample Site B traces include `Register -> Document Check -> Identity Check -> Assessment -> Approve -> Notify` and `Register -> Identity Check -> Originals Receipt -> Document Check -> Assessment -> Legal Review -> Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment -> Legal Review -> Reject -> Notify`. No trace has `Document Check` before `Originals Receipt` on the originals branch, and `Assessment` never precedes either verification.

## 9. Recommendation

**conditional go**

Site B should adopt the mechanism behind Site A's change, not its diagram or its headline number.

**What to transfer:**
- Concurrent identity and document checking where both can legally start at registration: no originals required, digital documents, no identity-document dependency.
- Identity checking started at registration for every case.
- Site A's WIP discipline and real lifecycle timestamps.

**What not to copy:**
- Creating both tasks at registration for every case. For 62% of cases, `Document Check` cannot legally start until originals arrive; pre-created tasks clog queues and invite premature checks, statutory breaches, rework and a longer tail.
- Site A's 9-hour gain as a target. It is confounded by a simpler mix, two extra reviewers, an API upgrade, huddles and changed timestamp recording; only about 1–5 h is plausibly due to parallelization.
- Lock workarounds or back-entered events presented as parallelism.

**Who enters first:** cases with no originals, no legal review and digital documents (roughly 20–30% of volume), stratified by complexity. Legal-review cases without originals follow once guardrails hold. Originals cases get identity at registration, an originals-triggered document task and originals chasing.

**Expected benefits:** 3–5 h shorter time to Assessment readiness in the eligible segment, but only about −0.5 to −3 h on the site median, because eligible cases mostly lie below 44 h. Little p90 benefit, since the tail comes from originals delay, legal review and supplement loops.

**Risks:** the bottleneck moves to the three assessors (average improves, p90 worsens); the case lock turns concurrency into serialization; parallel checks lose context and raise supplements and appeals, as Site A's rising rework and appeals hint.

**Conditions for go:** a working task-level lock, validated logging, and evidence that a material part of the 20 h pre-Assessment wait is synchronization wait.

**Triggers:**
- **Expand** if the primary outcome improves by at least 15% with fidelity above 80%, rework and supplements within +1 pp, no rise in waits for non-eligible cases, and site p90 no worse in Phase B.
- **Modify** (assessor capacity, priority rules) if the gain is absorbed downstream.
- **Abandon concurrency** in favour of originals handling and assessor capacity if verified overlap yields under 1 h, the lock cannot be fixed, or a statutory or quality stop rule fires.

## 10. Assumption register

1. Identity and document checks are informationally independent for eligible Site B cases — needed so that parallel execution does not degrade decision quality — test by auditing a sample of sequential cases for document checks that used identity findings, and by comparing rework, supplement and assessor-return rates between T1 and T0 — if false, rework and appeals rise; narrow eligibility with dependency flags or add a shared-findings step before `Assessment`.
2. The originals requirement and the legal-review flag can be determined correctly at `Register` — needed for eligibility routing and statutory compliance — test by comparing registration-time flags with later determinations during the run-in (target misclassification under 2%) — if false, misrouted cases start `Document Check` illegally or lose the benefit; add a legal pre-screen step before task creation.
3. The case-management system can be changed to task-level (or child-object) locking without data-integrity loss — needed for true overlap — test in an IT sandbox plus run-in lock-conflict and lost-update counts — if false, concurrency is only nominal (scenario S6); no-go for concurrency, and focus on originals handling and queue controls.
4. A material part of Site B's 20 h median wait before Assessment is synchronization wait for the partner branch rather than assessor queueing — needed for parallelization to have a lever — test by splitting lifecycle timestamps into (later − earlier verification completion) and (`Assessment` start − later completion) — if false, the bottleneck is the three assessors, parallelization mostly shifts the queue (S4), and assessor capacity should be addressed first.
5. Site B's event data, after the logging fix, are reliable, and "service" medians are elapsed open-task durations rather than effort — needed because the current DFG is internally inconsistent (3,910 `Register` out-arcs for 3,400 cases) and elapsed hours imply more than 100% utilization (3,400 × 3.4 h ≈ 11,560 h for 4 identity reviewers) — test with a timestamp audit, DFG consistency checks and effort sampling — if false, baselines, the capacity model and overlap estimates are biased; rebuild the baseline before any decision.
6. Site A's parallelization-attributable median effect is roughly 1–5 h — needed as the prior for Site B's scenario ranges — test by re-analysing Site A's case-level log (mix-standardized interrupted time series, dose-response by lock-verified overlap, negative-control activities) — if false (near zero), the mechanism may not work even at Site A and the pilot's prior should be pessimistic; if larger, scenario S2 becomes more likely.
7. Case mix, legislation, staffing and other practices stay stable during the pilot, apart from the assigned treatments — needed so that switchback and case-level contrasts are not confounded by concurrent changes, which was Site A's main flaw — test with weekly control charts of mix, arrivals and staffing, plus negative-control outcomes — if false, rely on within-block randomized contrasts, extend the duration, or re-stratify.
8. Staff follow the queue discipline, and treated cases do not take capacity from control or non-eligible cases (limited interference) — needed for unbiased case-level estimates in Phase A — test by comparing pick-order conformance and the waiting of non-eligible cases between treatment and control weeks in Phase B — if false, Phase A overstates the benefit; use Phase B system-level estimates and strengthen the priority rules before rollout.
