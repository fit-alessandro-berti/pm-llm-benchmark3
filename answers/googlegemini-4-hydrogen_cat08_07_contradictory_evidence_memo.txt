# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team  
**From:** Process Strategy & Operations Analytics  
**Subject:** Decision Recommendation on Fiber-Installation Redesign R  

## Recommendation
Adopt **Alternative E (Targeted Hybrid Deployment with Controls and Measurement)**, reinforced with immediate operational guardrails. Do **not** scale Redesign R nationally (Alternative A), and do **not** roll it back completely (Alternative B). Instead:
1. **Scale** the merged validation/scheduling workflow and coordinator ownership **only for simple, low-risk houses** (34% of pilot volume), while transitioning coordinators to a **shared-pod backup model** to eliminate single-point-of-failure stalls.
2. **Immediately suspend** predictive field-survey skipping for medium-risk/older buildings and **halt** post-billing activation checks across all segments.
3. **Measure and re-test** full 60-day downstream costs, risk-rule accuracy, and structured rework causes before considering broader expansion.

## The Strategic Trade-Off
Pilot R (9,200 cases across 4 regions) proved that Redesign R is not a uniform success or failure—it is a **bimodal intervention** that trades faster happy-path throughput and lower upfront cost for severe tail delays, hidden rework, and compliance-like billing defects in complex cases.

On one hand, Redesign R delivered genuine value for straightforward installations:
- **Simple, low-risk houses** saw median duration drop by **6.0 days**, p90 duration drop by **8.0 days**, first-time-right (FTR) rise by **+3 pp**, and complaints fall by **1 pp**.
- Overall cases with 3+ handovers fell from **34% to 12%**, and median duration improved from **14.2 to 10.8 days**.

On the other hand, scaling Redesign R indiscriminately would destroy value and overload field and support teams:
- Tail duration deteriorated sharply (**p90: 31.0  38.5 days; p95: 45.0  62.0 days**).
- Rework and customer friction spiked: FTR dropped (**82%  76%**), second visits rose (**11%  18%**), manual activations doubled (**7%  15%**), and 30-day complaints jumped (**6.1%  9.8%**), shifting toward *"installed but not working"* and *"billing before activation"*.
- **Apartments and old, permit-sensitive buildings** (47% of pilot cases) suffered **+12 to +25 days** in p90 duration and **-8 pp to -11 pp** in FTR.
- Visible upfront cost fell (**EUR 184  161**), but incomplete downstream estimates already turn net-negative (**EUR 198  207**), excluding post-week-4 complaint costs.

## Why Incomplete Evidence Demands Selective Action Now
Two measurement blind spots prevent an immediate binary go/no-go decision on the full redesign: pilot regions over-represented older apartments (with incomplete segment/risk tags before week 3), and downstream complaint costs after week 4 were omitted. However, segment-level data proves that complex buildings deteriorated *within* their own strata—this is not merely a regional mix artifact. Waiting months for pristine data before acting would perpetuate avoidable customer harm and 92% second-visit team saturation; rolling back entirely would forfeit proven gains on simple houses.

By deploying R selectively where it works (simple houses), stopping the two mechanisms driving rework and billing disputes (unvalidated survey skipping and post-billing activation), capping coordinator load via pod backup, and closing the cost and risk-rule measurement gaps over the next 6–8 weeks, management protects customers today while preserving the option to scale safely later.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

### 1. High fitness (0.94) but low precision (0.51)
In process mining, **fitness** measures whether the discovered model can replay the observed event log, while **precision** measures how tightly the model restricts behavior to what actually occurs (penalizing "underfitting" models that allow unseen paths). Pilot R created a **bimodal log**: ~34% of cases (simple houses) followed a clean, compressed path via *Combined validate/schedule*, boosting replayability, while complex cases generated sprawling combinations of skipped surveys, failed visits, second visits, manual activations, and out-of-order billing/complaints. To achieve 0.94 fitness across both the streamlined path and these chaotic exception loops, the discovery algorithm produced an overly permissive ("flower-like") structure allowing arbitrary interleaving of rework and billing steps—collapsing precision from 0.79 to 0.51 and generalization from 0.70 to 0.56.

### 2. Improved median (14.2  10.8 days) but worse tail duration (p90: 31.0  38.5d; p95: 45.0  62.0d)
The median reflects the 50th percentile—dominated by simple houses (34%) and routine installs that benefited from merged validation/scheduling, fewer handovers, and skipped surveys. However, when a skipped survey caused an unflagged technical issue or missed permit (especially in apartments and old buildings), the case entered compound delay loops: *Failed visit* (13%19%)  *Second visit* (10%17%, constrained by 92% second-visit team utilization and 93% coordinator load)  *Manual activation* (7%15%)  *Complaint escalation* (3%8%). Because failures multiply waiting and rescheduling cycles, the upper percentiles (p90, p95) stretched dramatically even as the typical case got faster.

### 3. Fewer handovers (3+ handovers: 34%  12%) but lower resilience
Eliminating handovers reduced coordination friction on standard cases, but it also eliminated **shared situational awareness**. The handover-of-work network became simpler, yet the working-together network became heavily centralized around coordinators and activation specialists. With coordinators operating at **93% utilization**—near the queueing-theory threshold where wait times spike nonlinearly—any coordinator absence left cases stranded because context was held by a single individual rather than a shared team. Efficiency came at the expense of redundancy and load-sharing.

### 4. Lower visible cost (EUR 184  161) but higher rework/downstream cost (EUR 198  207, incomplete)
Redesign R removed upfront, easily tracked activities: merging validation/scheduling and cutting field surveys (31%18%, dropping survey-team utilization from 78% to 52%). That created immediate, visible cost savings of EUR 23 per case. However, those savings merely **shifted cost downstream** into less visible buckets: second-visit field dispatches (+7pp), labor-intensive manual activations (+8pp), activation-specialist escalations, and customer-service complaint handling (+3.7pp). Because cost accounting excluded complaint handling after week 4, the EUR 207 estimate understates true lifecycle cost.

### 5. Improvement for simple houses vs. deterioration for apartments/old buildings
Redesign R’s two core shortcuts—skipping field surveys via a predictive rule and combining validation/scheduling—assume standardized site access, predictable building infrastructure, and rare permit needs. Those assumptions hold for **simple, low-risk houses** (34% share: -6.0d median, -8.0d p90, +3pp FTR). In **apartments (29%)** and **old, permit-sensitive buildings (18%)**, hidden physical constraints (risers, shared ducts, landlord/municipal permits) are common. Skipping surveys suppressed *Permit check* steps (confirmed by move-on-model alignment deviations), converting a fast upfront check into a failed installation visit, a stalled second visit, and failed activation.

# 6. Final recommendation

### Recommended Decision
Execute **Alternative E (Segmented Hybrid Deployment with Immediate Safeguards)**, incorporating the operational guardrails of Alternative C.

### 1. Immediate Action (Weeks 1–2)
- **Enforce pre-billing activation gate:** Restore the rule that *Billing start* cannot trigger until *Activation test* passes (automated or verified manual), eliminating *"billing before activation"* disputes.
- **Mandate intake risk & permit screening:** Require complete segment and building-risk tagging at order intake (fixing the pre-week-3 blind spot) and enforce mandatory *Permit check* routing for all old/permit-sensitive and multi-dwelling buildings.
- **De-risk coordinator load:** Rebalance coordinator queues below 80–85% utilization and institute **coordinator pods (shared-team backup)** so absences do not stall active cases.

### 2. What to Pause
- **Pause predictive survey skipping** for all apartments, old/permit-sensitive buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance installs—returning them to standard *Field survey* routing until the risk model is validated.
- **Pause post-billing activation checks** across **all** segments (including simple houses and business accounts).
- **Pause any national rollout** of Redesign R to non-simple segments.

### 3. What to Scale (Controlled Scope)
- **Deploy the streamlined path only for verified Simple, Low-Risk Houses:** Keep *Combined validate/schedule* and pod-backed *coordinator ownership* for low-risk single-family houses, where pilot data showed consistent improvements across duration (-6.0d median, -8.0d p90), FTR (+3pp), SLA breaches, and complaints (-1pp). Monitor conformance weekly to confirm precision and billing compliance remain tight.

### 4. What to Measure Next (6–8 Week Learning Cycle)
Execute the top VOI measurements immediately:
1. **M4 (60-day full cost tracking):** Capture complete post-billing complaint, credit, manual activation, and revisit costs by segment.
2. **M6 + M3 (Risk-rule back-testing & structured reason codes):** Validate the predictive survey-skip rule against actual survey/permit findings and code every failed visit, second visit, manual activation, and complaint.
3. **M7 + M5 (Conformance monitoring & pod-ownership trial):** Track zero-tolerance billing/permit conformance and compare solo vs. pod coordinator resilience.
4. **M1 (Targeted RCT, Phase 2):** Only after M6 shows a recalibrated rule with high precision, run a stratified randomized test on medium-risk apartments.

### 5. Thresholds / Evidence Required to Justify Broader Rollout
Management should expand Redesign R beyond simple, low-risk houses **only if all four gate criteria are met** over two consecutive 4-week measurement cycles:
- **Economics (M4):** Full 60-day unit cost (including revisit, manual activation, and complaint handling) is ** Baseline (EUR 198)** and shows positive net savings in the target segment.
- **Quality & Tail Control:** First-time-right meets or exceeds baseline (** 82%** overall, no segment degrading >1pp), second visits return to ** 11%**, and segment p90 cycle time improves or holds flat against baseline.
- **Customer & Billing Integrity (M7):** 30-day complaints return to ** 6.1%**, and premature *Billing start before Activation test* drops to **< 0.5%** of cases.
- **Process Conformance & Resilience (M5):** Discovered process-model precision rises to ** 0.72**, coordinator utilization stays ** 85%** with verified pod coverage, and second-visit team utilization falls below **75%**.
