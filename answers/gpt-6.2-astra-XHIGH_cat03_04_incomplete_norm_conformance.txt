## 1. Conformance semantics

The unit of judgment is the Order, Package, Invoice, or event to which a rule applies. A positive graph weight shows that a relation occurred somewhere. A mandatory rule asks whether it occurred for **each eligible instance**.

- **Forbidden behavior** requires a positive witness of the prohibited relation, with the identities, timing, and business meaning specified by the rule. A forbidden link in the log warrants investigation even when its source is suspect.
- **Missing mandatory behavior** requires an applicable obligation and evidence of its omission. A missing event–object link can establish a recording defect while leaving the underlying transaction unresolved.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is an observed relation with no governing obligation or prohibition here. Both explicitly unspecified and unlisted relations receive this treatment.
- **Typical-pattern absence** is a departure from a preferred path. A legitimate exception, an unfinished lifecycle, or a recording gap may explain it.
- **Likely data-quality error** describes the probable origin of a finding. A faulty join can produce a genuine breach of a log-integrity rule.
- **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the graph tests a different proposition from the rule, such as adjacency in place of eventual occurrence.
- **Insufficient evidence** applies when scope, instance identity, ordering, observation window, or link cardinality prevents a decision.

Closed-world reasoning applies to the named prohibitions and the specified mandatory obligations within scope. Elsewhere the norm is open-world. It defines neither minimum prevalence nor an allowance for occasional mandatory or forbidden breaches.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

A directly-follows edge witnesses neighboring activities in an object's recorded history under the chosen ordering. For N1, validation anywhere later on the same standard Order satisfies the business requirement. An observed review between creation and validation breaks adjacency. A wholly unrecorded review could explain the business steps but cannot, by itself, split two adjacent *logged* events. Inspect what was actually recorded next and whether validation arrived before the extract ended. The 3 review Orders cannot simply be assigned to the 2 gaps. If the 98 and 3 came from one cohort with one creation and one unique successor per Order, the counts would exceed 100. Check cohort membership, repeated events, and tie handling.

For N3, adjacency gives two candidate witnesses, but the prohibition extends across intervening events. Search all later dispatches for each cancelled Order. N6's silence on reopening supplies no exception to N3. For N4, the denominator already establishes external shipment for 200 Packages. Two lack the specified direct edge. If adjacency is required, an intervening scan still breaks that requirement. If ordered milestones suffice, the same scan can coexist with proper packing and dispatch. Positive E4/E5 weights of 200 each cannot pair the milestones by Package ID.

The 83 and 72 N2 observations count Orders on particular paths. Their sum supplies no denominator for a closure rate. For N5, the 4 write-offs and 14 without successors account for the 18 outside the typical payment edge. A missing successor says nothing conclusive about whether a balance is overdue.

### OTG

An OTG distinguishes interaction, cobirth, and codeath by object type. The 200 Package–Delivery interactions demonstrate association somewhere in the log. They cannot certify that each Dispatch Package event supplied both participants. The same limitation applies to the 1,450 T1 interactions. Recover the contributing event and object IDs before interpreting a weight as event coverage. T3's 92 Invoice–Payment interactions are compatible with one unapplied receipt, but cannot grant the event-level E8 exception.

For T6, establish that the shared event created both objects at source. Sharing an ordinary event would be permissible under this rule. First appearance in a limited extract may suggest a birth in error. Conversely, the seven shared ends concern different object types and a different relation. T6 provides no grounds to investigate them as prohibited births.

### ET–OT

This graph identifies which object types participate in an activity. It supports a direct E10 finding even at weight one, because any such link is forbidden. Mandatory coverage requires checking distinct event IDs, including events with zero links, then testing presence of each required type. Even equal weights could arise from a missing participant on one event and several on another. Here the additional evidence specifically identifies two deficient dispatches and one deficient payment. The Add Item evidence gives only aggregate weights. A carrier attribute helps recover a Delivery, and staff knowledge helps investigate cash status. Neither supplies the missing typed evidence on the recorded event.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory.** N1 has 98 direct witnesses and two eventual-validation questions, conditional on standard Order scope. N4 has two direct gaps in scope. E1/E3/E4/E5/E7 have positive support. E2/T1 need an Add Item event-by-event check. E6/T2 lack the required participation on two dispatches. E8 lacks one required Invoice unless source evidence establishes the explicit exception and the typing is corrected.
- **Forbidden.** N3 has two recorded sequences, T6 one recorded cobirth, and E10 one recorded link. Confirm effective chronology and true birth for the first two. Correcting a false join would resolve the third in a repaired log.
- **Typical.** N2 has 83 direct closures, N5 has 92 payment successors and 18 alternatives or open histories, and T3 has Invoice–Payment interaction weight 92. Typicality supplies no numeric acceptance threshold.
- **Unregulated.** The 72 invoice successors, 3 review successors, 4 write-off edges, review links to RiskCase and Customer, T4/T5 interactions, N6 reclosure, and 7 codeaths carry no adverse finding under these edge definitions. N1 can still apply to a reviewed Order, and the separate Customer policy may have its own requirements.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
from inspect import signature
import pm4py

observed = {
    "dfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}

def observed_input(check, model):
    # Supply the observed operand declared by the installed comparison API.
    first = next(iter(signature(check).parameters)).lower()
    return ocel if "ocel" in first or "log" in first else model

raw = {
    "dfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(
        observed_input(pm4py.conformance_ocdfg, observed["dfg"]), norm_ocdfg),
    "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(
        observed_input(pm4py.conformance_otg, observed["otg"]), norm_otg),
    "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(
        observed_input(pm4py.conformance_etot, observed["etot"]), norm_etot),
}

# Maintain rule IDs, status, scope, and exception predicates in a versioned
# policy alongside the graphs. Bare edges and weights cannot encode them.
status = {
    **dict.fromkeys("N1 N4 T1 T2 E1 E2 E3 E4 E5 E6 E7 E8".split(), "mandatory"),
    **dict.fromkeys("N3 T6 E10".split(), "forbidden"),
    **dict.fromkeys("N2 N5 T3".split(), "typical"),
    **dict.fromkeys("N6 T4 T5 E9".split(), "unspecified"),
}
event_rules = {
    "Create Order": {"Order": "E1"},
    "Add Item": {"Order": "E2", "Item": "E3"},
    "Pack Package": {"Package": "E4"},
    "Dispatch Package": {"Package": "E5", "Delivery": "E6"},
    "Record Payment": {"Payment": "E7", "Invoice": "E8"},
    "Manual Compliance Review": {"RiskCase": "E9"},
    "Sensor Heartbeat": {"Invoice": "E10"},
}
interaction_rules = {
    "Add Item": ("T1", {"Order", "Item"}),
    "Dispatch Package": ("T2", {"Package", "Delivery"}),
}
explicit_unapplied_ids = set()  # From explicit event typing. Empty in this snapshot.
eid, oid = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.object_id_column
activity, otype = ocel.event_activity, ocel.object_type_column
links = ocel.relations[[eid, oid]].drop_duplicates().merge(
    ocel.objects[[oid, otype]], on=oid, how="left", validate="many_to_one")
by_event = links.groupby(eid)[otype].agg(lambda types: set(types.dropna()))

semantic = {
    "event_findings": [],
    # These cohort counts come from the supplied summary. The denominators
    # count objects in the reported cohorts.
    "cohort_findings": [],
    "unresolved_object_checks": {
        "N1": "Test eventual validation on each eligible Order with a direct gap.",
        "N3": "Correlate same-Order dispatch and effective cancellation times.",
        "N4": "Inspect direct gaps on externally shipped Package traces.",
        "T6": "Compare the candidate pair's true creation events at source.",
    },
}
# The N1 cohort counts newly created Orders; standard status still needs checking.
for rule, with_edge, cohort in [("N1", 98, 100), ("N4", 198, 200), ("N5", 92, 110)]:
    label = ("typical path absent" if status[rule] == "typical"
             else "mandatory direct witness absent; review scope and trace")
    semantic["cohort_findings"].append((rule, cohort - with_edge, label))

# Start with the event population so that events with no relations are included.
for event_id, name in ocel.events[[eid, activity]].itertuples(index=False, name=None):
    present = by_event.get(event_id, set())
    rules = event_rules.get(name, {})
    findings = semantic["event_findings"]
    for typ, rule in rules.items():
        if rule == "E8" and event_id in explicit_unapplied_ids:
            continue
        if status[rule] == "mandatory" and typ not in present:
            findings.append((event_id, rule, typ, "missing recorded participant"))
        elif status[rule] == "forbidden" and typ in present:
            findings.append((event_id, rule, typ, "forbidden recorded link"))
        elif status[rule] == "unspecified" and typ in present:
            findings.append((event_id, rule, typ, "unregulated"))
    for typ in present.difference(rules):
        findings.append((event_id, None, typ, "unregulated by ET–OT policy"))
    if name in interaction_rules:
        rule, needed = interaction_rules[name]
        if not needed.issubset(present):
            findings.append((event_id, rule, sorted(needed - present),
                             "scoped interaction missing an endpoint"))

result = {"observed": observed, "raw_diagnostics": raw, "semantic": semantic}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

These are proposed clarifications for adoption, with the proposed status of each.

| Gap | Operational risk | Clarification | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 lacks a standard-Order classifier and validation deadline | Review detours trigger false alarms and abandoned Orders stay pending indefinitely. | Name the source classification and the milestone by which later validation must occur. Mark still-eligible open cases pending until then. | Mandatory |
| N3 lacks an authoritative event clock and package attribution rule | Late messages can implicate the wrong shipment or hide a real one. | Define effective cancellation time, physical handover time, and the source Order–Package link. Preserve the prohibition across intermediate activities. | Forbidden |
| N4 leaves adjacency and shipment intent operationally ambiguous | Routine scans can look like skipped packing. A missing dispatch can disappear from a denominator based only on completed shipments. | Define intent at release and require packing and later dispatch on the same eligible Package by the shipment deadline, allowing intermediate scans. | Mandatory |
| T6 lacks a business-birth test | Batch imports can create false financial exceptions. | Define birth using authoritative creation events and stable source identities. Forbid a shared creation event under that definition. | Forbidden |
| E6 lacks a carrier-reference mapping procedure | Delivery evidence can remain stranded in text attributes. | Require a verified Delivery object link per dispatch and specify the source mapping for carrier references. | Mandatory |
| E8 leaves unapplied-cash typing underspecified | Legitimate receipts may be misclassified, or allocations may be excused after the fact. | Identify the authoritative posting code and require explicit exception typing whenever that exception is invoked. | Mandatory |
| N5 lacks a review horizon | Long credit terms and stale receivables can look identical. | Assess expected settlement against contract due dates and recognized write-offs, retaining exceptions. | Typical |
| Shared confirmation has no OTG treatment | Graph comparisons may repeatedly flag ordinary codeaths. | Document Package–Delivery codeath at confirmation as outside the policy. | Explicitly unspecified |

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

- **Immediate containment.** Contact fulfilment about the two N3 Orders while a stop may still be possible. Preserve cancellation and handover evidence. In parallel, identify the T6 pair and hold automated decisions that rely on its disputed creation history. Quarantine the E10 link from financial reporting.
- **Data repair.** Replay the E10 join with type-qualified source identifiers and check its other matches. Restore the two Delivery links only after unique carrier-reference resolution. Repair payment typing or allocation from the ledger. Preserve the original snapshot and record each correction.
- **Process investigation.** Reconstruct N3 chronology and T6 source births first. Assign fulfilment or financial-control follow-up if verified. Then inspect the two N4 traces, the two N1 candidates, and distinct Add Item events. Match findings by event ID to avoid counting E6 and T2 twice as separate incidents.
- **Norm revision.** Have process and data owners agree on the definitions in section 6. Version the rules with their scope predicates, evidence sources, and permitted exceptions.
- **Monitoring.** Alert on each forbidden witness, track mandatory coverage per eligible instance, and keep pending cases visible. Report typical outcomes by age and disposition. Retain rare reviews, codeaths, and reclosures as descriptive counts.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The recorded N3, T6, and E10 relations and the event-level E6 and E8 gaps are established in this snapshot. Effective dispatch and true cobirth still require source evidence. E10 has a specific suspected join cause. N1 and N4 depend on trace detail and temporal semantics. Unregulated edges and typical alternatives provide no independent grounds for a breach. Aggregate graphs alone cannot determine exact Add Item omissions, overdue balances, or whether a missing link originated in operations or extraction.
