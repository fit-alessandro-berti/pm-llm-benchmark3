- **FD-7 was an emergency directive**, effective 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, allowing provisional activation before Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval under safeguards.
- **CRM migration caused notification logging loss.** Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%.
- **Request_More_Info is operationally common and desirable**, but not modeled in M0.
- The case-level traces and aggregate evidence are accepted as observed data for this review.

### Disputed propositions

- Whether PD-3 was valid as an exception to CS-9 despite not being incorporated into CS-9 and despite the compliance observer’s later statement.
- Whether FD-7’s factual emergency continued after its automatic expiry such that SUP-705 can be treated as justified.
- Whether M0 covers rejected pre-screen leads such as SUP-709.
- Whether M0 covers reactivations such as SUP-710.
- Whether missing or mutable notification evidence means the notification did not occur or is only a logging defect.
- Whether `QA_Clearance` is equivalent to `Quality_Review` for conformance.
- Whether spousal conflict-of-interest disclosure failure is a control violation or only an organizational policy gap.

### Burden of proof

- **For declaring noncompliance:** there must be case-specific evidence that a mandatory control was missed, an unauthorized route was used, or an exception was exceeded. Raw M0 trace mismatch is not automatically noncompliance.
- **For accepting an exception:** the party relying on the exception must show a valid, time-effective, scoped authority; that the case met eligibility; and that compensating controls were executed. Informal belief, post-hoc rationale, or expired directives are insufficient.
- **For model/data defect:** there must be enough evidence that the observed mismatch is better explained by model incompleteness, scope ambiguity, label equivalence, or logging failure than by actual business misconduct.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The prosecution should not defend every weakness of M0. It can concede that M0 is incomplete on loops, resource rules, and overlays. But the formal control baseline remains CS-9, and several cases show real material noncompliance.

**Mere model obsolescence does not legalize serious failures.**

- **SUP-703** is severe: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier was routed through the pilot Auto_Approve path despite pilot ineligibility. First purchase was EUR 180,000. No emergency, no pilot eligibility, no manual approval. This is exactly the kind of activation that the standard route exists to prevent.
- **SUP-708** is severe: a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check. No pilot or emergency applied. The fact that no transaction occurred before sanctions clearance mitigates impact, but the control violation remains.
- **SUP-712** only appears M0-compliant. In fact, the requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through two aliases. CS-9’s segregation-of-duties rule is violated. This shows that M0 conformance is not sufficient.
- **SUP-715** is also a governance failure: quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line, and no conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded.

**Expired exceptions were normalized beyond their authority.**

- **SUP-705** followed the FD-7 emergency pattern on 2026-07-13, after FD-7 had expired. Operations may have believed the emergency was still factually present, but there was no written extension. If an expired directive can be extended informally, emergency authorities are uncontrollable.
- **SUP-714** used Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 expired. Operations had normalized the pilot flow, but no permanent amendment existed.
- Aggregate evidence reinforces this: **six post-pilot cases** used Auto_Approve in the first week of August. **14 cases** were clearly ineligible for PD-3, and **9 had missing eligibility attributes**.

**Desirable outcomes are not retroactive amendments.**

- The 47 Request_More_Info cases may reflect good practice, but M0’s lack of a loop is a model defect, not a reason to ignore control failures.
- The CRM migration explains missing notification events in some cases, but it does not explain unauthorized activation, missing manual approval, or missing sanctions clearance before activation.

**The prosecution acknowledges real model/data defects.**

- M0 should not be used as the only conformance artifact.
- SUP-702 may be defensible if PD-3 applies and if CRM notification is reliable.
- SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 have plausible model/data explanations.
- But these defects do not erase SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, or SUP-715.

## 3. Defense Brief

The defense must concede that some cases are genuine control failures. But it should argue that raw M0 conformance is the wrong measure and that the current model is obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, and applied to inadequate data.

**M0 is not a valid normative control-flow model for actual operations.**

- M0 has no **Request_More_Info loop**. SUP-707 shows repeated requests before approval; no activation occurred before requested evidence arrived. This is good behavior that M0 falsely treats as deviation.
- M0 has no **pilot Auto_Approve route**. PD-3 created one for a timeboxed class of eligible suppliers. SUP-702 was eligible: LOW-risk DE supplier, approved corporate group, quality review next business day, and CRM notification with stable ID. The onboarding log missing Notify_Requester is explained by CRM migration, not by a missing business event.
- M0 has no **emergency/provisional route**. SUP-704 followed FD-7: sanctions before activation, spend below EUR 20,000, daily monitoring, and next-business-day quality and approval. The M0 deviation is real, but the operational risk was controlled.
- M0 has no **rejected-lead pre-screen scope**. SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen and rejected. Whether that case is governed by M0 at all is ambiguous.
- M0 has no **reactivation scope**. SUP-710 concerns a supplier closed six months earlier with a prior valid quality certification. CS-9 does not address reactivation, and M0 cannot distinguish it from a new case.
- M0 has no **label equivalence**. SUP-713 used `QA_Clearance`, the procedure/UI rename of `Quality_Review`. Audit documents show equivalent content.

**Data quality is not the same as noncompliance.**

- SUP-711 contains a duplicate Register event due to migration duplication. The source transaction ID is identical. This is a data quality issue.
- SUP-706 likely has a CRM notification at +3 hours, but the case link is mutable. The missing onboarding-log notification is a logging defect, not proof of missed control.

**M0 conformance misses actual violations.**

- M0 would deem SUP-712 conforming because trace order fits. But M0 does not encode the resource rule. This is not a defense of SUP-712; it is evidence that M0 is insufficient as a sole compliance instrument.
- Similarly, M0 would miss SUP-715’s conflict-of-interest disclosure issue.

**The defense must acknowledge limits.**

- The defense cannot defend SUP-703: HIGH-risk, ineligible pilot use, EUR 180,000 first purchase.
- The defense cannot defend SUP-708: HIGH-risk activation before sanctions with no emergency or pilot.
- The defense cannot defend SUP-705 and SUP-714 on formal authority grounds. Operations’ belief that a factual emergency continued, or that permanent approval was imminent, is not the same as having effective authority.
- The defense cannot claim that the aggregate Auto_Approve evidence is entirely innocent: 14 clearly ineligible cases and 6 post-pilot cases indicate normalization of an expired exception.

### System-level judgment

**The operation is not fully compliant with the formal normative baseline.** Real material violations exist in high-risk cases and in the normalization of expired or ineligible exceptions. The most serious are SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-705, and SUP-714. These cannot be excused as model/data defects.

**M0 is nevertheless obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, and applied to inadequate data.** It would falsely penalize request-for-information cycles, rejections after pre-screen, reactivations, label equivalences, and CRM notification gaps. It also misses resource and conflict-of-interest violations.

Therefore:

- **Enforce CS-9 as the formal baseline**, but not M0 as the sole conformance model.
- **Replace M0** with a layered architecture that includes control-flow routes, data-aware eligibility/time/amount checks, resource rules, policy overlays, and explicit scope rules.
- **Treat raw M0 mismatch as a screening indicator**, not as a final legal or compliance conclusion.

### Where each side is rejected

- **Prosecution overreach rejected:** It treats too many M0 mismatches as noncompliance. SUP-702, SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are better explained by model incompleteness, scope ambiguity, label equivalence, or data quality defects.
- **Defense overreach rejected:** It uses M0 obsolescence as a shield for SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715. Those cases involve unauthorized activation, expired authority, segregation-of-duties failure, or disclosure failure, not merely model absence.
- **Defense minimization of SUP-708 rejected:** The fact that no transaction occurred before sanctions clearance reduces impact but does not remove the control failure.
- **Prosecution assumption that M0 conformance is sufficient rejected:** SUP-712 and SUP-715 show that a trace can satisfy M0 and still violate CS-9 or organizational controls.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layer 1: Control flow — revised POWL

- Standard route: Register  Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrently  Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier.
- Low-risk auto-approval route: Register  Sanctions_Check and Master_Data_Validation  Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review.
- Emergency/provisional route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval before close.
- Rejection route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier.
- Repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism before approval/activation.
- Notify_Requester before Close_Case.

### Layer 2: Data-aware rules — DECLARE/log-skeleton and pandas/data checks

Belongs in **DECLARE/log-skeleton** or **pandas data checks**, not POWL:

- Sanctions_Check must precede activation in all routes.
- Auto_Approve only when risk=LOW, country=DE, corporate_group=approved, and effective_rule_id=PD-3 or later permanent policy.
- Quality_Review within two business days after provisional activation for pilot/emergency routes.
- FD-7 spend cap EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval.
- Daily transaction monitoring for emergency cases.
- No activation before requested evidence for Request_More_Info cycles.
- Notification_id stable and linked before case closure.
- Duplicate event detection using source_transaction_id.

### Layer 3: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

Belongs in **organizational controls and data checks**, not control flow:

- Requester and manual approver must resolve to different humans through alias resolution.
- Quality reviewer and manual approver must not have undisclosed close-relationship conflicts.
- Delegation evidence must be recorded when authority is delegated.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure must be immutable.

### Layer 4: Policy-overlay governance

Each exception must carry a rule/version ID, effective/sunset timestamp, authority source, eligibility snapshot, and compensating controls.

Examples:

| Rule/version | Effective | Expiry/Sunset | Authority | Scope | Flow/resource change | Data constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9 v1 | 2025-01-01 | Ongoing | Compliance standard | All onboarding cases | Sanctions+Quality before activation; Manual before Activate | Requester  approver |
| PD-3 v1 | 2026-06-01 | 2026-07-31 | Steering committee pilot | LOW-risk DE supplier in approved corporate group | Auto_Approve instead of Manual; Quality after provisional activation up to 2 business days | Eligibility snapshot required |
| FD-7 v1 | 2026-07-10 | 2026-07-12 | Emergency authority | Critical relief suppliers | Activation before Quality/Manual; sanctions before activation | Spend  EUR 20k until retrospective approval; daily monitoring |
| COI/Resource v1 | Ongoing | Ongoing | Organizational policy | All actors | Requester  approver; COI disclosure | Alias resolution, disclosure ID |
| Request_More_Info practice | Operational | Not yet formal | Operations/Compliance agreement | Pre-decision | Repeatable Request_More_Info; no activation before evidence | Evidence ID |

### Layer 5: Explicit scope rules

- **Rejected leads:** define whether pre-screen rejections are full cases or intake cases. If full cases, they may use an abbreviated route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject.
- **Reactivations:** define whether Register and Quality_Review can be skipped when prior certification remains valid.
- **Withdrawals:** define whether a withdrawal event replaces rejection or closure.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The revised POWL below encodes four main routes and an approximate repeatable Request_More_Info loop. POWL cannot enforce risk, time windows, spend caps, actor separation, or authority snapshots; those belong in the data/rule overlay.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def Tx(label):
    return Transition(label=label)

# Common start/end
register = Tx("Register_Supplier")
notify = Tx("Notify_Requester")
close = Tx("Close_Case")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard route:
# Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are concurrent because neither
# edge imposes order between them. Both must precede Manual_Approval.
# Request_More_Info may repeat after Sanctions and before Manual_Approval.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_std = Tx("Sanctions_Check")
quality_std = Tx("Quality_Review")
manual_std = Tx("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = Tx("Activate_Supplier")
req_info_std = Tx("Request_More_Info")

info_loop_std = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.LOOP,
    children=[SilentTransition(label=None), req_info_std]
)

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_std, quality_std, info_loop_std, manual_std, activate_std]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_std, info_loop_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(info_loop_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(quality_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_std, activate_std)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route:
# Sanctions and master-data validation occur before Auto_Approve.
# Quality may occur after provisional activation.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_auto = Tx("Sanctions_Check")
mdv_auto = Tx("Master_Data_Validation")
req_info_auto = Tx("Request_More_Info")
auto_appr = Tx("Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = Tx("Activate_Supplier")
quality_auto = Tx("Quality_Review")

info_loop_auto = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.LOOP,
    children=[SilentTransition(label=None), req_info_auto]
)

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_auto, mdv_auto, info_loop_auto, auto_appr, activate_auto, quality_auto]
)
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_auto, info_loop_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(mdv_auto, auto_appr)
auto_route.order.add_edge(info_loop_auto, auto_appr)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_appr, activate_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate_auto, quality_auto)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency/provisional route:
# Sanctions before activation; Quality and Manual approval after.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_emg = Tx("Sanctions_Check")
activate_emg = Tx("Activate_Supplier")
quality_emg = Tx("Quality_Review")
manual_emg = Tx("Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_emg, activate_emg, quality_emg, manual_emg]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_emg, activate_emg)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_emg, quality_emg)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_emg, manual_emg)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Rejection route:
# Sanctions pre-screen may reject without Quality_Review.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_rej = Tx("Sanctions_Check")
reject = Tx("Reject_Supplier")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_rej, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_rej, reject)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Top-level XOR choice from Register to one of the routes,
# followed by Notify_Requester and Close_Case.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route]
)

revised_powl = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, notify, close])
revised_powl.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
revised_powl.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
revised_powl.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Limitation.** The `Operator.LOOP` construction is an approximation intended to allow zero or more Request_More_Info cycles by making the silent transition the loop body and Request_More_Info the redo branch. If a particular PM4Py version gives different LOOP semantics, replace it with an XOR skip and enforce cardinality using DECLARE: `Request_More_Info` may repeat, but `Activate_Supplier` cannot occur until requested evidence is recorded.

### Event and data-collection proposal

Each event and case should capture:

- `event_id`: immutable, unique.
- `case_id`: stable case identifier.
- `activity_observed`: raw label from source system.
- `activity_normalized`: canonical label, e.g., `QA_Clearance`  `Quality_Review`.
- `timestamp_effective`: actual business time.
- `timestamp_recorded_immutable`: audit timestamp.
- `source_system`: onboarding log, CRM, ERP, etc.
- `source_transaction_id`: for duplicate detection.
- `actor_id`: stable actor identity.
- `actor_alias_key`: resolves aliases to same human.
- `delegation_evidence_id`: where delegation is claimed.
- `coi_disclosure_id`: for conflict-of-interest review.
- `notification_id`: stable UUID for CRM notifications, not mutable text.
- `mirror_status`: whether notification was mirrored into onboarding log.
- `rule_id_version`: active CS-9, PD-3, FD-7, or other authority.
- `eligibility_snapshot`: risk class, country, corporate group, supplier type.
- `transaction_amount_eur`: for spend-cap checks.
- `exception_authority`: specific rule ID and version.
- `case_scope`: standard, rejected_lead, reactivation, withdrawal.
- `event_object_snapshot`: relevant entity state before/after event.

This allows conformance checking to distinguish genuine control violations from logging failures, label drift, duplicate events, scope exclusions, and timeboxed policy overlays.
