## 1. Decision rule

At each 30-minute refresh, make a dispatch decision, not just an alert decision. A case must still be before Diagnosis Complete, have a credible path from a 45-minute swarm to resolution before its SLA, and have a current, actionable diagnostic question. Check that both specialists and a slot are actually available. Diagnosis Complete is a hard exclusion for this intervention, regardless of predicted breach risk.

Use a calibrated *no-swarm* risk interval for urgency and for the chance of an unnecessary customer disruption. As provisional segment corrections, add 0.04–0.09 to SME predictions, subtract 0.05–0.12 from Strategic Enterprise predictions, and leave Public Sector centered on the supplied prediction while retaining uncertainty from its moderate sample. Widen the interval for Non-Native-Language Support rather than inventing a point correction. For example, I-07's 0.72 becomes approximately 0.76–0.81, while I-04's 0.48 becomes approximately 0.36–0.43. Recalibrate these corrections regularly. Do **not** apply them to the supplied effect ranges: observational risk, untreated risk, and causal risk reduction are different quantities.

For eligible cases, calculate a conservative direct margin, `value × plausible lower effect − €450 − false-alarm burden`, and a midpoint margin for ordering. Charging the entire listed false-alarm burden is a conservative screening convention; realized burden occurs only when the swarm was unnecessary. Subtract a *marginal queue shadow cost*: the estimated extra breach loss, delay, and contractual or customer harm among the six exposed untreated cases. The effect ranges are uncertain planning inputs, not established causal effects. A slot is dispatched only after a quick queue check and specialist review confirm that its expected benefit and any service obligation justify that shadow cost. Prefer a case whose treatment opportunity will expire soon over an equally attractive case that can safely be reconsidered. Allow a documented urgent-service exception for a lower-value case, so euro value does not become an absolute exclusion rule.

An alert qualifies for review if it persists at the next refresh, follows a verified material event, or has a large credible change (provisionally at least 0.10 in predicted risk) with too little time to wait. A material event means new diagnostic evidence, a stage transition, a newly endangered SLA, or a verified queue change; another timer-driven model refresh alone is not one. Failed or imputed key inputs require source verification before an irreversible dispatch. Keep one of today's four slots provisionally reserved for the possible regulatory incident. Release it before 15:00 only if an expiring opportunity has a stronger documented claim than the reserve's option value; release it after 15:00 if no such incident arrives. Recompute priorities after each swarm because congestion, stages, and remaining capacity change.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Maintain one episode per case. After an alert review, suppress identical notifications for at least two refreshes (one hour); keep the case visible in the portfolio so a newly urgent deadline or freed capacity can still change its decision. After an actual swarm, suppress further swarms for the rest of that diagnostic episode. Re-entry requires a documented material event, an ongoing pre-Diagnosis-Complete stage, and explicit specialist approval that a second swarm has a distinct task and enough incremental benefit to pay its new cost and spillover. Clear the episode at closure or a genuine new incident.

Escalate from watch to dispatch review when a verified new log, a material stage or queue change, a persistent calibrated-risk rise, or a shrinking effective window changes the decision. A mere reissued score is not escalation. Thus I-05's third alert has no new evidence: suppress its duplicate notification, while reconsidering its standing candidacy if its deadline or capacity position changes. I-07's third alert includes a new customer diagnostic log: read the log, treat it as a new evidence event, and act now despite its slightly lower `p_now`. For I-06, the first escalation is to repair or verify the missing queue feature, not to assume the jump is real.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Value-weighted ranking can systematically defer SMEs or cases with smaller contractual penalties. Set a minimum critical-incident service floor: track deadline proximity and clinical/operational severity independently of euro value, permit a documented urgent-service override such as I-07, and review unmet eligible demand by segment. Do not use the override to hide unlimited queue harm; record both the beneficiary and the untreated cases exposed.

For Non-Native-Language Support, I-09's sparse validation makes both risk and effect uncertain. Offer language-capable review, record whether communication delays or missing translations caused the alert, and use wider uncertainty bands until enough cases accrue. Do not interpret low sample size as evidence of low priority. Audit calibration and false-alert burden separately for SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and language-support intersections. Correct the known SME underprediction and Enterprise overprediction, but monitor whether the correction itself changes access to swarms.

Selective swarming changes observed breach labels: treated high-risk cases may no longer breach, while untreated cases may be delayed by the specialists' absence. A model trained naively on those labels can learn that high-risk cases are safe or that untreated groups are inherently risky. Log treatment assignment, eligibility, scores, stage, capacity, propensity to treat, reasons for override, and queue exposure; retain a no-swarm counterfactual estimate for evaluation. Report spillover by segment, customer tier, language-support status, and specialist queue. If a group repeatedly absorbs delays or added breaches, lower the congestion cap, alter scheduling, or give that group explicit protection in the shadow cost.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

On every refresh, log stage and deadline, raw and calibrated scores, evidence changes, feature missingness, specialist availability, queue depth, planned versus actual dispatch, and reasons for waiting or overriding. Operational dashboards should show alert volume, duplicate suppression, time from alert to swarm, swarms completed before Diagnosis Complete, labor cost, customer disruption, SLA outcomes, and delay imposed on untreated cases. Capacity dashboards should show slots used and reserved, simultaneous specialist demand, overtime, and the opportunity cost of an unused reserve.

Weekly or after enough outcomes accumulate, measure calibration, Brier/log loss, and lead-time-specific error overall and by segment; report intervals rather than unstable point estimates for small groups. Check whether the SME and Strategic Enterprise adjustments still hold and whether I-06-type imputation produces false jumps. Track calibration separately in treated and untreated cases, but do not mistake treated outcomes for untreated-risk labels.

Estimate the swarm's causal effect prospectively. Where ethically and operationally acceptable, randomize among similarly eligible cases when demand exceeds slots, with a protected urgent or regulatory exception; record the assignment probability. Compare breach, diagnosis time, and customer burden using intention-to-treat and treatment-received analyses. Model interference explicitly at the queue and shift level: measure untreated cases' waiting time and breaches versus contemporaneous queue load, and consider queue-level randomized scheduling or phased policy changes to identify spillovers. Use doubly robust or propensity-weighted observational analysis as a supplement, with sensitivity bounds for unmeasured reasons specialists chose a case. Examine effect heterogeneity by stage, time to SLA, segment, and new-evidence type.

Review the allocation rule when observed lower-bound benefit falls below measured marginal queue harm, the reserve is regularly unused while eligible cases expire, untreated breach or delay limits are exceeded, calibration drifts, or a segment's service floor is repeatedly missed. Tighten dispatch during congestion, relax reserve use when its option value is low, and change the alert-evidence or score cutoffs only after replaying recent decisions with measured capacity and spillovers. A sudden workflow or queue change triggers immediate recalibration and a temporary wider uncertainty margin; it does not justify treating today's effect ranges as proven causal facts.
