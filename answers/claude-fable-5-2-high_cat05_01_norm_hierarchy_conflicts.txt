## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts taken as given (F)**

- F1. GPIP-4 is board-approved, covers all entities and purchases, and has been in force since 2026-01-01. It routes exceptions through a written, scoped, time-limited exception by the Chief Risk Officer (A4, A7). No such exception was issued, and the CRO did not later ratify ECD-2.
- F2. SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 rev. 6 is a controlled local procedure for Aachen maintenance and production-support purchases, in force since 2025-03-01.
- F3. ECD-2 was signed by the COO and the Incident Commander. Its window is 07-14 09:00 to 07-18 18:00 CEST. It is silent on segregation of duties, Finance approval, Compliance clearance, the EUR 100,000 threshold and the CRO (C5).
- F4. The CFO email dates from 07-15 07:20 and is not a controlled document. The CFO may interpret Finance controls; no source gives the CFO power over Compliance controls or segregation of duties (D3). The email itself preserves "essential integrity controls" (D4).
- F5. ON-44 concerns only the ERP on 07-14, 08:30–13:00, with stamps up to 12 minutes late. It changes evidential weight and is not an exception. The possibly fast ERP clock in P-505 and the document-system outage in P-507 are separate, case-specific facts.

**Interpretations I adopt (I)**

- I1. Authority is competence-based and issue-specific. A source prevails on an issue only if its author is competent for that issue and its text actually addresses it. Rank, recency and strength of wording are indicators, not trump cards.
- I2. Silence is not waiver. This follows from A4, from A5 ("do not silently weaken") and from C5.
- I3. ECD-2 is not an A7 exception. A7 reserves the power to the CRO, the CRO did not sign or ratify, and past practice of treating directives as "policy overlays" never reached new-supplier Compliance clearance. ECD-2 is a valid overlay on the SOP (through B7 and the COO's operational authority) for the two things it expressly addresses: PO sequencing (C1) and the supplier register (C2, C4). That overlay is conditional on C3.
- I4. Specificity works only inside the author's competence. ECD-2 beats the SOP on the issues it names. It does not beat GPIP-4.
- I5. Later-in-time matters only between sources of comparable authority on the same issue. The CFO email has force only through the CFO's competence over Finance controls. I read D1/D2 as an instruction by the control owner on how Finance approval is discharged for ECD-2 purchases, from 07-15 07:20 until the directive expires. It is not retroactive, it does not widen ECD-2's scope or window, and it does not touch A1 or A3. Because it adjusts a GPIP-4 control (A2) without using the A7 channel, reliance on it can produce at most JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, never COMPLIANT.
- I6. Joint Incident Command authorization (C3) can stand in for Plant_Approve and for the budget-owner limb of A2 only if both authorizers are independent of the requester and the committer. I read A1 into C3.
- I7. Emergency necessity is not a free-standing override. It matters in three ways: as a trigger condition (B4, ECD-2 scope), as mitigation of severity, and as justification only where the omitted control could not feasibly have been performed in the available time.
- I8. Rules are applied at the time of the act. The reference act is Commit_to_Supplier, the point of legal exposure. ECD-2 does not reach back before 07-14 09:00, the CFO email does not reach back before 07-15 07:20, and a signed time-limited directive cannot be extended orally.
- I9. Post-hoc approval mitigates severity and does not change the classification (A6). I apply the same principle by analogy to SOP approvals.
- I10. Timestamps are defeasible claims. My evidence ranking is: independent or third-party contemporaneous artifacts (supplier acknowledgment, call recording, mail headers); then signed documents with verifiable metadata; then ERP stamps in normal operation; then ERP stamps in degraded periods; then uncorroborated later statements.
- I11. Burden of proof. Baseline rules apply by default. Whoever invokes an exception must show authority, scope, time and fulfilment of the exception's own conditions, on the balance of probabilities and with at least one contemporaneous or independent artifact. Equally, a breach finding that rests only on a timestamp requires that timestamp to be reliable.
- I12. Scope of "critical sterilization-line lots". I read it purposively: an item for a sterilization line, needed to contain the event, with a need-by time shorter than the time ordinary controls would take. I adopt neither Internal Audit's 24-hour line (it is not in the text) nor Operations' label-based reading (it amounts to self-certification). An Incident Command label is evidence, not proof.

**Assumptions (AS), each reversible by evidence**

- AS1. P-502 occurred on 07-14. No date is stated; I infer it from the times and the outage reference. If it occurred later, the result is unchanged because A3 still fails.
- AS2. Plant_Approve is the budget-owner approval of A2, and Finance_Approve is the Finance Controller approval.
- AS3. An "unapproved" supplier (B3) is not necessarily a "new" supplier (A3). New-supplier status is unknown for P-504, P-507 and P-508.
- AS4. B2 approvals are normally due before commitment. The text is silent; I infer this from B1 and from the substitution mechanism in B4.
- AS5. The seals in P-507 are for a sterilization line involved in the event. The case does not say so.
- AS6. Facility repair (P-504) falls under "maintenance" in the SOP scope.
- AS7. Where actor identities are not given, I do not presume a segregation-of-duties breach, but I list the identities as missing evidence.

**Decision method**

1. Fix the best-supported time of commitment.
2. Determine which sources were in force and in scope at that time.
3. Resolve conflicts issue by issue (Section 2).
4. Test each exception's own conditions.
5. Classify:
   - COMPLIANT: all applicable rules are met as written.
   - JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: every deviation is covered by an exception from an authority competent for that issue, and the exception's conditions are met and evidenced.
   - NONCOMPLIANT: at least one deviation is uncovered.
   - INDETERMINATE: a decisive fact cannot be resolved either way.
6. Grade severity and confidence separately from the classification.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The premise to change is AS4: B2 fixes no timing, and B4/B5 preserve the approvals without requiring them before commitment. Same-day approvals plus the timely B6 form would then complete a valid B4 emergency. This is moderately credible, because the text is silent and the ERP was down. Its weakness is that it makes the B4 substitution clause pointless and lets any employee commit the company first.

**P-502 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The premise to change is I3: either ECD-2 is functionally an A7 exception (the CRO was unavailable and directives are treated as overlays), or "supplier-onboarding requirements" in C4 includes pre-commitment Compliance clearance. This is weak to moderate. A7 names the CRO, the CRO declined to ratify, the precedent expressly never covered this control, and C5 confirms the silence. The A2 timing breach would also need separate cover, since the CFO email did not yet exist.

**P-505 → NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE.**
- Factual route: treat the ERP stamp of 18:06 as the commitment time, or treat commitment as formed only by a later written order. That places the act after expiry, and B1 and A2 then fail. This is weak, because the acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is independent of the ERP. It becomes INDETERMINATE only if the mail timestamp cannot be verified.
- Normative route: the strict A4 reading set out under P-507/P-508 below. Finance approval at 19:00 would then be late.

**P-506 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The premise to change is R18: Incident Command has implied authority to continue its own regime while recovery is ongoing, and corroboration of the verbal extension turns up. This is weak. The directive carries its own end time, and even an extended ECD-2 would require a C3 authorization that does not exist. A narrower alternative is that B4 facts are proven. That would excuse the PO timing, but the late budget-owner approval at EUR 51,000 remains.

**P-507 and P-508 (and P-505) → NONCOMPLIANT.** The premise to change is I5. Under a strict A4 reading, only the CRO can relax A2. A CFO "interpretation" that removes case-specific Finance Controller review is a waiver in substance, sits in an uncontrolled email, and is limited by D4. For P-508 there is a further point: "as well" in D2 may mean Legal clearance in addition to the ordinary approvals. This alternative is credible and is the most serious challenge to my hierarchy.

I do not adopt it for four reasons:
- The CFO is the documented owner of the control.
- The instruction is explicit, capped and time-bound rather than a silent weakening.
- Two independent authorizers preserve the four-eyes purpose.
- I label the result an exception that needs ratification, not compliance.

If the strict reading prevails, these cases become noncompliant on A2 only, with low culpability because staff relied in good faith on apparent authority.

**P-503 and P-504** are robust. No reasonable change of premise removes the A1/A3 breaches in P-503 or brings P-504 within any emergency regime.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**GPIP-4 clause A7 (replacement) — Emergency Exceptions and Incident Directives**

1. **Hierarchy.** GPIP-4 prevails over local procedures, incident directives and management communications. Local procedures may be stricter. An incident directive may modify local workflow steps only (PO sequencing, supplier-register use, local approval routing), and only for its stated scope and window. A GPIP-4 control may be modified only by an Emergency Exception under paragraph 2. Emails and oral statements are neither.
2. **Authority.** An Emergency Exception is issued in writing by the CRO. If the CRO is unreachable for two hours, it is issued by the designated deputy (Head of Compliance or General Counsel), never by a person in the incident's operational line. It must list each control modified, and it is entered in the controlled repository within 24 hours. The CFO may define an alternative Finance approval route only through such an exception.
3. **Non-waivable controls.** Segregation of duties (A1) is never waived; an authorizer may not be the requester or committer. Compliance clearance for new suppliers and for purchases above EUR 100,000 may be expedited (two-hour on-call screening) but not waived.
4. **Scope.** An exception or directive must name the lines, assets and material categories it covers and a need-by horizon (default: required within 48 hours to prevent or end a stoppage or safety harm). Contingency or backup stock is excluded unless individually approved.
5. **Thresholds during an exception.**
   - Below EUR 50,000: two independent Incident Command authorizers.
   - EUR 50,000 to 150,000: in addition, the on-call Finance Controller (verbal approval permitted).
   - Above EUR 150,000: in addition, Legal clearance and the CFO or a delegate.
   - All approvals precede the supplier commitment.
6. **Records.** A verbal approval is recorded within four hours in a signed record stating the actual time, the persons and the purchase. During system outages an offline record with a trusted timestamp suffices; a later system entry is flagged as late evidence and linked to the original. An approval given after commitment is recorded as a ratification and never counts as a pre-commitment approval.
7. **Expiry.** Applicability is determined by the time of supplier commitment, established by the best contemporaneous evidence. An extension requires a written instrument from the issuing authority before expiry, for at most 72 hours each time. There is no oral or implied extension.
8. **Safeguards against emergency normalization.** Cumulative duration beyond ten days requires Audit Committee approval. Every purchase under an exception is reviewed within 15 working days and reported to the CRO. A supplier first used under an exception may not be reused until regularly onboarded. Repeated emergency use by the same requester or for the same category triggers a root-cause review.
