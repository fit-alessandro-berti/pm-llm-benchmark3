## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded.** Confidence: **68/100**.

Crude means show Orion slower (21.96h vs Nova 15.24h). Within every observed complexity stratum the direction reverses: Orion is faster on Low (7.50 vs 9.64), Medium (17.17 vs 19.38), and High (31.57 vs 35.00). Orion is also heavily selected into high-complexity work (42.9% vs 12.5%), night shift (92.9% vs 25%), and compliance-required paths (64.3% vs 25%). That pattern is the strongest evidence that longer Orion cycle times are largely case-mix and operating-condition association, not a harmful total effect of the Orion label.

Evidence against a pure confounding story: strata are tiny (e.g., High–Nova n=2, Low–Orion n=2); shift, load, compliance, and routing are entangled with group; only completed cases are observed; and Orion’s “more certified reviewers” skill bundle is unmeasured. Residual direct effects or mechanism effects (queue discipline at night, compliance handling) remain possible, so the estimand is not cleanly identified.

The proposed 50% reassignment of future Orion cases to Nova is likely to **fail or backfire**. It moves work off the group that appears faster conditional on complexity and that holds certified capacity for difficult cases, while dumping complex/night/compliance work onto Nova without adding night or compliance capacity. Any apparent “win” from diluting Orion’s difficult mix would be compositional, not causal improvement, and quality/rework risk could rise on high-complexity paths.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Case-mix confounding (Orion not harmful) | Routing sends difficult/compliance cases to Orion; crude mean rises | Stratum reversal; higher high% and compliance% on Orion; facts on routing | Tiny opposite-group cells; possible residual “difficulty” | Exact/propensity matching on complexity+compliance+load; overlap diagnostics; sensitivity for unobserved risk | Matched (cycle) near 0 or favors Orion; effect appearance collapses when mix balanced |
| 2 | Orion beneficial but masked (certified skill) | Higher skill shortens service and rework on hard cases | Within-stratum faster cycle and shorter waits; more certified reviewers | Cannot see reviewer IDs; wait not only skill | Within-complexity reviewer-level or group service-time models; front-door split wait vs service | Orion review_service lower holding complexity and rework; moving hard cases to Nova increases service and rework |
| 3 | Shift/load queue mechanism | Night + high arrival_load inflates wait_before_review | Orion 93% night; high load frequent; wait is large share of long cycles | Within strata Orion still lower wait despite more night | Queue-state reconstruction by shift; compare Nova night vs Orion night on same complexity; negative control: day-only subset | If shift drives harm, night coefficient large and group effect shrinks when shift conditioned; day-only Orion vs Nova gap near 0 |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 – Observe & stabilize | No routing change; instrument queue timestamps, reviewer ID, open cases, compliance timestamps | Measurement gaps; selection | System-wide | Do not cut Orion capacity | DFG/variant rates; wait vs service split; completion hazard by group | Establishes baselines and positivity map | If data quality SLAs missed for 2 weeks, delay pilots |
| 1 – Capacity, not label | Add night/compliance review capacity (shared pool) without renaming groups; optional compliance pre-queue | queue_wait and compliance bottleneck | Shift slots / compliance desk | Cap utilization; protect SLA on High | wait_before_review by hour; compliance sojourn; DFG edge waits | Tests whether mechanism is capacity vs Orion identity | Rollback if High-case wait rises >20% or appeal rate doubles over 4 weeks |
| 2 – Small randomized routing pilot | Randomize eligible Medium cases (common support) 50:50 Orion vs Nova after Classify; hold High on current certified path initially | Total effect of group under overlap | Case-level Bernoulli on eligible set | Exclude High until Phase 1 stable; monitor quality; informed ops override logged as noncompliance | Cycle, wait, service, rework, variant, outcome_30d by assigned and as-treated | Identifies assignable group effect where positivity holds | Stop if pre-specified harm boundary hit (e.g., +15% mean cycle or quality signal) on 1-week looks |
| 3 – Policy shadow then scale | Shadow-route difficult cases under new rule (skill-based + load-aware) before binding cutover; never blind 50% OrionNova | routing_rule redesign | Case segments by complexity×shift×load | Keep certified reviewers on High; gradual ramp 10%30% | Conformance to new rule; balanced mix metrics; standardized cycle | Separates policy effect from brand label | Rollback to pre-pilot routing if standardized cycle or appeals worsen vs control band |

**Final recommendation.** Do not execute the proposed 50% OrionNova reassignment. The crude Orion penalty is the wrong causal target: within complexity strata Orion is faster, and Orion currently absorbs night, compliance, and high-complexity mass with more certified reviewers. Blind transfer would likely move congestion and skill mismatch to Nova and can worsen total system cycle time and quality. Prefer (1) fixing measurement (open cases, reviewer ID, atomic timestamps), (2) intervening on queue/compliance capacity, then (3) a restricted randomized routing pilot on medium-complexity cases with overlap, while keeping high-complexity work on certified capacity. Re-estimate total and mechanism effects (wait vs service vs rework paths) before any large reallocation. Treat “Orion” as a routing-and-capacity bundle, not a toxic label to dilate by half.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual difficulty within coded complexity (unobserved case risk driving routing to Orion).** Most valuable addition: pre-routing risk score or classifier features used by the engine (or expert difficulty rating) at Classify time.

2. **Whether completion/abandonment differs by group (selection into the completed-case sample).** Most valuable addition: full case census including open and abandoned cases with censoring times and last activity.

3. **Reviewer-level skill vs group-label effects.** Most valuable addition: reviewer ID, certification flag, and time-stamped activity-level resource assignment for each Review segment.

4. **Atomic timing of compliance vs pure queue (composite `wait_before_review_h`).** Most valuable addition: separate timestamps for Classify end, compliance start/end, queue entry for Review, and Review start/end (including second Review after Request Info).

5. **Quality signal too coarse and possibly post-process contaminated (`outcome_30d`).** Most valuable addition: nearer-term process quality events (rework reason codes, supervisor overrides, customer contacts) plus a design that randomizes routing on a restricted eligible set to identify assignable effects.
