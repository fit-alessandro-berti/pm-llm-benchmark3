## 1. Feasibility model

```text
For every case:
Register  Triage
Triage  Identity Check
Triage  Document Check, subject to originals rule
Identity Check  Assessment
Document Check  Assessment
Final Assessment  Decision
Decision  Notify

When originals are required:
Triage  Originals Receipt  Document Check

When high-risk or high-value:
Latest relevant Assessment  Final Compliance Review  Decision

When Request More Info occurs:
Request More Info  New Document Check  New Assessment  Decision
For high-risk/high-value:
New Assessment  New or explicitly reaffirmed Final Compliance Review  Decision

Permitted concurrency:
Identity Check || Document Check, if originals are not required
Preliminary Compliance Work || Identity Check || Document Check
Escalation Advice may overlap other checks
Final Compliance Review cannot precede final Assessment
```

```text
Conditional constraints:
- Originals required => no Document Check before Originals Receipt.
- High-risk OR high-value => final Compliance Review is mandatory.
- Rework request => both a new Document Check and a new Assessment are mandatory.
- Rework in high-risk/high-value case => compliance must be performed anew or explicitly reaffirmed.
- Final assessor.resource != final compliance reviewer.resource.
- Preliminary compliance work cannot substitute for final Compliance Review.
- Suppression of a Request More Info must produce an explicit evidence-based decision:
  approve with disclosed conditions, or reject; unresolved evidence cannot be ignored.

Declarative/resource constraints:
- A resource cannot overlap its own service intervals.
- C1 is the only certified final compliance reviewer in the window.
- M1 performs Decision before Notify.
- Queue estimates describe availability forecasts, not appointments.
- Recorded concurrency is valid only when real service timestamps and distinct ownership support it.
- A provisional reservation may be released, consumed, or displaced under an explicit priority rule.
```

K-104 mainly alters timing and early routing to S1/C1 preparation, while preserving final review order. K-219 alters document-resource routing from D1 to D2. K-411 alters task creation and timing, enabling lawful concurrency rather than changing prerequisites. K-337 alters the allowed decision policy: it removes an otherwise available information-gathering loop and forces a transparent decision under unresolved uncertainty.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage already contained the sanctions signal, but no escalation artifact was created. Consequently, A1 rediscovered it after both verification tasks, S1 advised only on Tue evening, and C1 entered the case with little reusable preparation. The dominant delay was not just C1 service time but late readiness against a scarce, backlogged resource.

**Minimal intervention:** Preserve all factual verification and assessment routing, but at Mon 08:50 send the triage evidence to S1, request a preliminary C1 screen, and create a releasable provisional slot. No final compliance conclusion is recorded before Assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** S1 can classify the issue while I1 and D1 process the case. C1 may pre-read identifiers, sanctions references, and corporate structure during otherwise available capacity. Document completion and A1 availability still govern Assessment, but the post-assessment handoff becomes shorter: C1 needs to reconcile A1’s findings rather than start from zero. Earlier readiness helps only if a suitable C1 slot exists; it does not guarantee earlier completion.

**Process-feasibility check:** Identity and documents both precede Assessment; final review follows A1’s final Assessment; C1 and A1 are distinct, satisfying four-eyes. The provisional screen is explicitly preliminary.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: useful pre-reading and a retained slot yield 8–12 h. Base: partial reuse saves 2–8 h. Worst: stale/incomplete material causes rereading, or reservation displacement yields no gain and up to 3 h delay.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 logs showing no idle fragments suitable for preliminary work, a high rate of packet invalidation, or urgent-case delays greater than target-case savings would make early reservation unattractive.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** D1’s specialist routing protected accuracy but imposed a long wait after originals arrived. D2 had earlier forecast availability, although handwritten evidence increases its service and clarification risk.

**Minimal intervention:** On Mon 17:30, release the document task to D2 rather than retain it for D1. Identity remains complete; no document work occurs before originals receipt.

**Temporal propagation:** D2 might start Mon evening or early Tue and finish before D1’s factual Tue 13:30 completion despite slower service. That advances Assessment readiness, but A2’s queue may consume some gain. If D2 asks for clarification, external response time dominates; the returned evidence must pass a new document check and assessment, potentially making the intervention slower than factual.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals precede D2’s check, and both Identity and Document Check precede Assessment. A clarification is modeled as `Request More Info`, not informal off-path correction, so mandatory rework is retained. No compliance review is required.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: legible material, prompt D2 start, and an earlier A2 slot save 8–12 h. Base: 5–9 h saved, partly absorbed downstream. Worst: clarification adds 8–36 h externally plus repeat queues, producing a 6–30 h net delay.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Historical handwriting data showing D2 clarification/error rates materially above D1’s, or D2 occupancy preventing starts before Tue morning, would favor factual specialist routing.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The second unresolved discrepancy caused a Friday information request; slow supplier behavior and the weekend postponed the response until Monday, after which three mandatory internal stages remained. This loop improved evidence completeness and supported the eventual approval.

**Minimal intervention:** After Fri 10:30 Assessment 2, freeze the evidentiary record. Record the discrepancy explicitly, send that record to C1, and require M1 to choose rejection or approval with enforceable conditions.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing the external response eliminates the weekend wait and prevents Document Check 3 and Assessment 3. C1 can queue from Friday rather than Tuesday. The actual gain depends on Friday/Monday C1 and M1 capacity; deletion of activities does not imply immediate signing.

**Process-feasibility check:** Assessment 2 becomes the final Assessment. C1 then performs final Compliance Review, followed by M1’s Decision and Notify. A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes. No discrepancy is silently waived; it is incorporated into the rationale and conditions or rejection.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: C1 reviews Friday and M1 signs the same day, saving nearly five calendar days. Base: Monday completion, saving about three days. Worst: modest time saving but a materially worse decision, later appeal, or reopened case that exceeds the original duration and cost.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Data showing residual discrepancies are usually immaterial and conditions are effective would support the intervention; evidence of high losses, overturns, or legal requirements for complete confirmation would rule it out.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The case lock created only the identity task and delayed document-task ownership until identity completion. This technical serialization was not required by process rules. The delayed readiness then encountered a larger Wednesday assessment queue.

**Minimal intervention:** At Tue 09:35 create separately owned I2 and D2 tasks under a controlled lock override, with immutable start/stop timestamps captured at service time.

**Temporal propagation:** D2 can wait in its own queue while I2 waits in the identity queue. Their intervals may overlap, and Assessment becomes ready at the later completion. Earlier readiness may capture a Tue slot, but if A2 remains congested, the case simply spends the saved verification time waiting for Assessment.

**Process-feasibility check:** Both checks still complete before Assessment, and no originals are required. Different resources permit genuine overlap. Timestamp validation must establish actual service intervals rather than simultaneous back-entry.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 finishes near I2 and A2 accepts the case Tue, saving 8–10 h. Base: 2–7 h saved. Worst: no material completion gain, lock errors, or 1–3 h delay from duplicate handling.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Work-sampling that disproves true overlap, frequent override failures, or near-certain overnight assessment waiting would eliminate most expected benefit.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early escalation/C1 preparation | Adds early S1/C1 touches; may reduce later C1 service but introduces reservations and duplicate reading. | Post-assessment C1 handling if preparation is reusable | Preliminary/reserved C1 capacity; possibly urgent compliance queue | Urgent high-risk cases competing for C1 | Ambiguous; positive only with high packet utility and disciplined release | Near-saturation reservation can create idle fragments while urgent cases wait |
| Route originals to D2 | Transfers checks from D1 to D2; may add clarification rework | D1 | D2, external clarification, and Assessment after rework | D2’s standard cases and D1’s complex cases | Positive under low clarification rates; negative above a rework threshold | Small error-rate changes trigger full external and internal loops |
| Suppress second rework | Removes external response, repeat document/assessment work; may add appeals or loss management | D1, A1, and external-response backlog | C1/Decision earlier; later appeal or exception queues | Cases moved forward by freed D1/A1 capacity; customers exposed to stricter decisions | Fast operationally but quality-adjusted effect is uncertain or negative | Weekend avoidance produces large apparent gains while tail risk moves outside the measured process |
| Parallel verification | Earlier simultaneous I2/D2 demand; same nominal checks; earlier releases to Assessment | End-to-end verification waiting/lock queue | Assessment, and possibly D2 at peak release times | Cases already waiting for A1/A2; concurrent verification workload | Generally positive if Assessment has headroom | Once Assessment saturates, verification gains become queue displacement rather than throughput gains |

At 100 similar cases per week, local gains no longer add independently. Reserving provisional C1 slots for many K-104-like cases could consume most of the only certified reviewer’s schedule. Even if preliminary reading shortens some final reviews, incomplete packets, no-shows, and fragmented reservations can delay urgent non-target cases. A triage rule, short reservation expiry, and preemptible slots are therefore essential.

Routing K-219-like work to D2 substitutes one queue for another. D1’s complex-document queue may shrink, which has value because D1 has comparative advantage there, but D2 can become saturated. If even a modest share of handwritten cases triggers clarification, the policy creates external requests, repeat document checks, and repeat assessments. The added workload is nonlinear because clarification invokes an entire mandatory loop rather than a small service-time increment.

Suppressing second rework frees D1 and A1 capacity and avoids weekend delays for slow suppliers. Yet it advances high-risk cases to C1 and M1, and may move costs into appeals, losses, or exception handling not visible in ordinary cycle time. External-response savings will vary sharply by weekday: a Friday request avoided appears much more beneficial than a Tuesday request avoided.

Parallel task creation for 100 K-411-like cases increases true simultaneous demand on identity and documents and releases more cases earlier to A1/A2. Given the snapshots, Assessment is a plausible migrating bottleneck; median verification time may improve while total completion barely changes and incumbent assessment cases are displaced. Real-time timestamps are crucial: identical or overlapping database timestamps do not establish true concurrency. Audit samples should compare task ownership, workstation/session evidence, and service intervals. Overall throughput improves only where downstream capacity and priority controls prevent saved upstream time from becoming another queue.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411-style standard digital cases, no originals, no risk/value trigger, and distinct available identity/document resources | K-219-style standard originals with handwriting scored as legible or moderately difficult; exclude complex and suspected fraud cases |
| assignment rule | Randomize eligible cases at Triage to controlled dual-task creation versus legacy serialization, stratified by hour and predicted Assessment load | Randomize after Originals Receipt to D2-first versus D1 routing, stratified by legibility, weekday, and D2 queue |
| sample/support logic | Staged pilot: first 20 instrumented cases for safety, then enough cases to estimate median and upper-tail time; preserve an A2 congestion stratum | Begin with a supported cohort and double reading for the first 20; expand only if clarification/error limits hold |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify time, analyzed with queue-load adjustment | Originals Receipt-to-Notify time, including all rework |
| process-path outcome | Share with verified `Identity || Document` overlap; time from later verification completion to Assessment start | D2 start advantage, first-pass completion, and frequency/duration of mandatory clarification loops |
| quality outcome | Identity/document defect rate, duplicate-task rate, and audit exceptions | Blinded document-accuracy review, missed ambiguity rate, downstream decision corrections |
| queue-spillover outcome | A1/A2 waiting and completion time for non-pilot cases; I2/D2 peak utilization | D1 and D2 waiting for pilot and non-pilot cases; added Assessment arrivals from rework |
| logging validation | Compare event timestamps with ownership, session, and workstation logs; reject batch-entered overlap as concurrency | Capture originals timestamp, actual service intervals, clarification reason codes, and version lineage |
| stopping rule | Stop for lock corruption, duplicate processing above threshold, unverifiable overlap, or material worsening of non-target Assessment tails | Stop for excess material-error or clarification rates, D2 tail inflation, or quality-adjusted cycle time worse than control |

## 7. Final synthesis

Controlled parallel verification in K-411 appears most robust. It removes a legacy serialization that is not required by the normative process, preserves both verification checks, and is readily reversible. Its likely weakness is not case-level feasibility but bottleneck migration: earlier verification readiness may merely create a longer Assessment wait. A pilot should therefore measure total cycle time and non-target Assessment delays, not just the time at which both checks finish.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite offering the largest apparent speed improvement. The intervention does not merely accelerate work; it prohibits further evidence gathering and changes the decision frontier. It can turn an eventual approval into rejection or conditional approval and can move cost into losses, appeals, reopened cases, and regulatory challenge. Avoiding a Friday request also exaggerates the visible gain because much of it is weekend calendar time.

K-104 is the least transportable from a favorable local counterfactual to a broad policy. One case may benefit from C1 pre-reading and a provisional slot, but applying reservations to 100 cases competes directly for the sole certified reviewer. Fragmentation, incomplete packets, and urgent-case displacement can reverse the sign of the intervention. K-219 is more scalable only if routing uses observed legibility and D2’s clarification rate remains below the point where repeat checks and assessments erase the waiting advantage.

The most valuable additional data are actual service intervals rather than entry timestamps; queue-entry, assignment, release, preemption, and abandonment events; C1 reservation utilization and displaced-case priority; document legibility and clarification reason codes by reviewer; assessment readiness-to-start times; supplier response distributions by weekday; and post-decision outcomes such as appeals, losses, condition breaches, and reversals. These data would distinguish real capacity savings from shifted waiting and hidden quality costs.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** The stated prerequisites and rework rules fully describe the mandatory control-flow relevant to these cases.
2. **temporal:** Queue snapshots approximate relative availability but do not guarantee any proposed start time.
3. **resource:** S1 preliminary advice does not consume C1 capacity, while any C1 pre-reading does.
4. **behavioral:** Staff follow formal rework logging rather than resolving clarification informally off-path.
5. **quality:** D2’s handwriting disadvantage raises uncertainty but does not make all K-219-like cases unsuitable.
6. **interference:** Advancing cases does not create new capacity; it can displace non-target cases under existing priority rules.
7. **structural:** Conditional approval or rejection is legally available for K-337, while silently disregarding the discrepancy is not.
8. **temporal:** Weekend and supplier-response delays are calendar-dependent and may differ materially across otherwise similar cases.
