## 1. Feasibility model

The four cases do not share one linear sequence: K-104 has an escalation branch and no rework; K-219 and K-337 have an originals gate; K-337 has repeated rework loops; K-411 has no compliance branch. The partial order below is the union of the per-case constraints, with conditional guards making some edges active only for specific attribute profiles.

```
PARTIAL-ORDER RELATION SET (≺ means "must complete before")

Global spine (all cases):
  Register ≺ Triage
  Triage   ≺ Identity_Check
  Triage   ≺ Document_Check           (Doc task creation; see originals guard)
  Identity_Check ≺ Assessment
  Document_Check ≺ Assessment
  Assessment ≺ Decision
  Decision   ≺ Notify

Originals guard (K-219, K-337; originals required = yes):
  Triage           ≺ Originals_Receipt
  Originals_Receipt ≺ Document_Check
  (Identity_Check is NOT ordered after Originals_Receipt — may proceed earlier)

Concurrency permission (K-104, K-411; originals required = no):
  Identity_Check ∥ Document_Check      (no precedence between them)

High-risk / high-value compliance branch (K-104, K-337):
  Assessment_final       ≺ Compliance_Review_final
  Compliance_Review_final ≺ Decision
  (Escalation_Advice, if present, is advisory; it may overlap other checks)

Rework loop (any case that issues Request_More_Info; realized in K-337):
  Assessment_k ≺ Request_More_Info_k
  Request_More_Info_k ≺ Document_Check_{k+1}
  Document_Check_{k+1} ≺ Assessment_{k+1}
  (high-risk/value) Assessment_final ≺ new-or-reaffirmed Compliance_Review ≺ Decision

Preliminary screen (optional, K-104 under intervention):
  Triage ≺ Compliance_Screen_prelim   (does NOT satisfy Compliance_Review_final)
```

```
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS

C1 (init):     Register is the unique first event.
C2 (originals):(originals_required) ⇒ Originals_Receipt ≺ Document_Check.
C3 (parallel): (¬originals_required) ⇒ Identity_Check and Document_Check may be concurrent.
C4 (compliance): (high_risk ∨ high_value) ⇒ ∃ Compliance_Review after the LATEST relevant
                 Assessment and before Decision.
C5 (rework):   Request_More_Info ⇒ (new Document_Check) ∧ (new Assessment) before Decision;
                 if high_risk∨high_value ⇒ new-or-reaffirmed Compliance_Review too.
C6 (prelim):   A preliminary compliance screen never discharges C4's final review.
C7 (4-eyes):   resource(Assessment_final) ≠ resource(Compliance_Review_final).
               With pool Compliance = {C1} only, this forces Assessment_final ∉ {C1};
               it also forbids C1 from being the final assessor.
C8 (no-overlap): ∀ resource r, no two service intervals of r overlap in time.
C9 (capacity):  C1 is the sole certified reviewer in-window ⇒ compliance is a hard single-server queue.
C10 (forecast): queue figures are forecasts; realized starts may drift ±.
C11 (decision): Decision must precede Notify; M1 signs all Decisions (single-server for sign-off).
```

Which lever each intervention pulls:
- **K-104 — timing/readiness only (with a routing-of-attention nuance).** It does not change the activity set required for a valid path (final Compliance_Review after final Assessment is preserved). It front-loads *preparation* so the final review is faster, and reserves scarce C1 capacity. It alters *when things become ready*, not the allowed decision policy.
- **K-219 — routing only.** Same activities, same order; only the *resource* for Document_Check changes (D1→D2). No policy or precedence change.
- **K-337 — decision-policy change.** Prohibiting the second Request_More_Info removes a rework loop and forces a decision on incomplete evidence. This is not a timing edit: it changes the *set of allowed terminal decisions* and the evidence standard, not merely the clock.
- **K-411 — routing/structure (concurrency enablement).** It changes task-creation and locking so Identity_Check ∥ Document_Check is actually realized. It alters routing/structure and *timestamp fidelity*, not the required order.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** The sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage (Mon 08:50) but not acted on until Assessment (Tue 19:10) rediscovered it, then S1 advised, then C1 reviewed on Wed 13:10–17:40 with "most evidence read from scratch." Two things sat on the critical path: the ~18–20 h C1 backlog and a cold-start review of a complex, high-risk/high-value file. Compliance is a single-server (C1) bottleneck, so both wait and service time hurt.
**Minimal intervention:** Keep every mandatory activity and its order (final Compliance_Review still follows final Assessment; A1≠C1 preserved). Only add a Triage-time preliminary escalation packet to S1 and a provisional C1 reservation, both overlapping the ID/Doc checks. Nothing about the decision policy or required path changes.
**Temporal propagation:** Earlier *readiness* is the mechanism. The preliminary screen lets C1/S1 pre-read sanctions context while D1 processes complex documents (Mon 21:40→Tue 05:40) and A1 assesses (Tue 15:10–19:10). When final Assessment finishes, the *final* review is a warm-start delta (verify against the completed Assessment) rather than a 4.5 h cold read—guidance says preliminary work can save 1–3 h. The reservation also removes part of the 18–20 h C1 queue wait for this case. Net final-review completion moves earlier by roughly 4–12 h. Crucially, earlier *preparation* ≠ earlier *final completion*: the final review still cannot start before Assessment ends, so the saving is bounded by service-time reduction plus reservation, not by the full backlog.
**Process-feasibility check:** C4 satisfied (final Compliance_Review after final Assessment, before Decision). C6 satisfied (prelim screen does not replace final review). C7/4-eyes satisfied (A1 assesses, C1 reviews). C8/C9: the C1 reservation is a deliberate scheduling choice, consistent with single-server capacity but consuming it.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: packet complete, C1 pre-reads, final review ~2 h, cycle −12 h, same decision. Base: partial pre-read, review ~3–4 h, cycle −4 to −7 h. Worst: packet incomplete/premature (the flagged complication) → C1 re-reads anyway, saving ≈0, and the reservation has idled scarce capacity that another urgent case needed.
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If C1's review time is dominated by reading the *final* Assessment (not background context), pre-reading saves little. If C1 is saturated by genuinely urgent peers, the reservation's opportunity cost exceeds this case's gain. Logs showing prelim packets are usually incomplete would flip the sign.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** Handwritten-but-standard docs were routed to D1 for accuracy, but D1's ~11–13 h queue meant Document_Check waited until Tue 09:30 even though originals arrived Mon 17:30 and Identity finished Mon 16:20. Idle waiting on a busy specialist, not service time, was the delay.
**Minimal intervention:** Reroute Document_Check to D2 as soon as originals arrive; identical order and activity set, only the resource changes (routing lever).
**Temporal propagation:** With originals ready Mon 17:30 and D2's queue ~2–5 h in the relevant snapshots, D2 could plausibly start Mon evening or early Tue rather than Tue 09:30. D2 is slower (standard 4–8 h vs D1 3–6 h), so service lengthens, but the earlier *start* usually dominates the *slower service*, pulling Assessment (A2) and Decision earlier by ~4–10 h. The offsetting risk: D2 is a generalist with higher clarification probability on difficult handwriting; a clarification triggers a rework loop (new Document_Check + new Assessment) whose external response can take 8–36 h, swamping the saving.
**Process-feasibility check:** C2 satisfied (Originals_Receipt ≺ Document_Check). Low-risk/low-value ⇒ no compliance branch, so C4/C7 not triggered. C8 requires D2 actually free when scheduled.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 free Mon evening, no clarification, cycle −8 to −10 h. Base: D2 starts early Tue, minor slowdown, cycle −4 to −6 h. Worst: D2 requests clarification → rework returns to Document_Check and Assessment → net +8 to +30 h and possible late decision.
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Historical D2 clarification/rework rate on handwriting above ~30–40%, or D2 not actually free when originals land, would erase or reverse the benefit. If D1's queue was shorter than posted, the counterfactual gain shrinks.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** The cycle was dominated by two external rework loops. RMI1 ran Wed 11:30→Thu 08:00; RMI2 ran Fri 10:30→Mon 09:00 (weekend delay), each followed by another Document_Check and Assessment. The supplier, not internal capacity, drove duration.
**Minimal intervention — but note it is a policy change, not a timing edit:** After Assessment 2 (one inconsistency remaining), prohibit RMI2 and force a decision on available evidence. This deletes the DocCheck3/Assess3 loop and the ~Fri→Mon external wait, but it changes the *allowed terminal decision set* and evidence standard—so it is not merely "subtract hours."
**Temporal propagation:** Removing RMI2 eliminates the Fri 10:30→Mon 09:00 external wait plus DocCheck3 (Mon 13:00–16:30) and Assess3 (Tue 08:00–10:30). Compliance still must occur (high-risk/high-value), now on the record as of Assessment 2 with a documented residual discrepancy; C1 reviews (A1≠C1 preserved), then M1 decides. End-to-end contracts by roughly 24–40 h, largely by removing a weekend-straddling external dependency.
**Process-feasibility check:** C5's normal expansion (RMI ⇒ new DocCheck+Assess+reaffirmed compliance) is intentionally *not* invoked because the intervention forbids the loop; the path remains valid because it terminates in Assessment_final=Assessment2 → Compliance_Review_final → Decision. C4 and C7 hold. The rule that the discrepancy "may not be silently ignored" is honored by forcing an explicit approve-with-conditions or reject.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: discrepancy was immaterial; approve-with-conditions is correct; cycle −40 h with no quality loss. Base: approve-with-conditions with residual risk priced in; cycle −24 to −35 h, modest added appeal/loss exposure. Worst: the unresolved inconsistency was material; the forced decision is wrong (bad approval or an unjustified reject), creating loss, appeal, or compliance-defensibility problems that dwarf the time saved.
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Data showing second-loop supplier corrections frequently flip decisions (materiality high) would argue for keeping the loop. Evidence that residual inconsistencies are usually cosmetic would strengthen the intervention. Regulatory/audit standards requiring full resolution before approval would forbid it outright.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** A legacy case lock created only the identity task, so the Document_Check task was not spawned until Identity completed (Tue 13:20), starting D2 at Tue 15:00—two logically independent checks were serialized by tooling, not by process rules. The case then hit a grown Assessment queue (Wed 09:30).
**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create both verification tasks and apply a controlled, auditable workaround so I2 and D2 run concurrently; require real-time timestamps (routing/structure + logging fidelity lever).
**Temporal propagation:** With both tasks live at Triage (Tue ~09:35) and no originals gate (originals not required, C3 permits concurrency), D2 can start when free (~Tue 11:30 alongside I2) instead of Tue 15:00. The serialized ~1.5–3.5 h gap collapses, moving Assessment eligibility earlier by ~3–5 h. But the saving is *capped by the Assessment queue*: if A2's queue is the binding constraint, earlier readiness only means the case waits earlier, and much of the local gain is absorbed.
**Process-feasibility check:** C3 concurrency is legitimate (no originals). C1 init and Register≺Triage hold. The key risk is C10/audit: if the "workaround" is batch-entered later, timestamps show false concurrency—feasible on paper but non-compliant in evidence. Real-time timestamps are required precisely to keep the log truthful.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: both resources free, Assessment queue absorbs the case immediately, cycle −5 h. Base: partial overlap, Assessment queue eats part of the gain, cycle −3 h. Worst: earlier completion just deepens the Assessment backlog (bottleneck migration) and poor logging creates an audit finding—near-zero net benefit plus governance risk.
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If Assessment is the true bottleneck (queues consistently long), earlier verification yields little end-to-end. If the lock exists for a real data-integrity reason, the workaround could corrupt ownership. Audit evidence of batch timestamp entry would negate the "true concurrency" claim.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 prelim escalation + C1 reservation | +S1 advisory time; C1 demand shifts earlier and is partly pre-consumed (reservation); final review shorter | Compliance wait for prepared cases; per-case final-review service time | Compliance queue for *non-reserved* urgent cases (reservation blocks slots) | Other high-risk/high-value cases needing C1; S1's other advisory work | Net negative-to-neutral if applied broadly (C1 is the scarce single server) | C1 is a single server: reserving capacity is zero-sum; benefit is convex only while C1 utilization < ~1 |
| K-219 route to D2 | Shifts load D1→D2; +D2 service time; +expected rework probability | D1 queue; per-case wait for early-ready cases | D2 queue; possible rework (Doc+Assessment) volume | Standard cases relying on D2; complex-corporate cases needing D1 | Positive if D2 slack exists and rework rate low; negative once D2 saturates | Rework is a step-function: one clarification adds 8–36 h, dwarfing the linear routing saving |
| K-337 suppress 2nd RMI | −1 Doc Check, −1 Assessment, −1 external wait; +compliance scrutiny per case | Assessment queue (fewer rework passes); external-wait time | Compliance defensibility / appeal-handling load; possible rework *after* appeal | Cases sharing A1/D1 freed capacity benefit; appeals unit loaded | Positive on time, ambiguous on quality/risk system-wide | Decision-quality risk is non-monotone in evidence completeness; small evidence gaps can cause large loss tail |
| K-411 parallelize + real-time logs | Verification demand overlaps (same total work, earlier); +logging discipline | Verification-phase idle gap; Doc-Check start latency | Assessment queue (cases arrive earlier/together) | Cases already queued at A1/A2; auditors reviewing logs | Positive locally, but bottleneck migrates to Assessment | Little gain until Assessment capacity added; congestion is nonlinear near saturation |

**100-case weekly rollout.** Applying each policy to ~100 similar cases in one week exposes interference that single-case reasoning hides.

*Reservation of scarce compliance capacity (K-104).* C1 is the only certified reviewer in-window—a hard single server. Reserving provisional C1 slots for many cases converts a shared queue into pre-committed blocks. If even a fraction of packets are incomplete (the flagged complication), reserved time is idled while genuinely-ready urgent cases wait, *raising* mean compliance wait system-wide even as prepared cases individually improve. The policy is beneficial only while C1 utilization stays below saturation; near ρ→1 it is roughly zero-sum and can be net negative.

*Substitution between D1 and D2 (K-219).* Diverting handwritten/standard work from D1 to D2 relieves D1's chronic backlog but loads D2. At scale, D2 becomes the new bottleneck, and its higher clarification rate multiplies rework across many cases—each clarification injecting an 8–36 h external loop and re-queuing at both Document_Check and Assessment. The linear per-case saving can be overwhelmed by a step-function increase in rework volume.

*Additional or avoided rework (K-337).* Suppressing second loops removes large external waits and frees A1/D1 capacity, shrinking the Assessment queue for everyone. But avoided rework is partly *displaced* into appeals, conditional-approval monitoring, and compliance defensibility. Across 100 cases, a small increase in wrong or contested decisions creates a heavy loss/appeal tail that time-savings do not offset.

*Assessment-queue bottleneck & bottleneck migration (K-411).* Parallelizing verification makes many cases *ready* earlier and simultaneously, pushing them into an already-grown Assessment queue. The system-wide effect is bottleneck migration: verification stops being binding, Assessment becomes binding, and end-to-end cycle time barely moves unless Assessment capacity (A1/A2) scales too.

*Weekend and external-response effects.* K-337's biggest factual delay was a weekend-straddling RMI; at scale, avoiding weekend-crossing loops yields outsized calendar savings, but only for the subset whose loops would have straddled the weekend—an uneven, arrival-timing-dependent benefit.

*Timestamps vs true concurrency.* K-411's benefit is real only if I2 and D2 genuinely overlap. If the workaround is batch-logged, the event log shows false concurrency: metrics improve on paper while true cycle time and audit integrity do not. At 100 cases this becomes a systemic data-quality and compliance liability.

## 5. Robustness comparison

| rank | intervention | robustness across plausible assumptions | expected benefit | principal risk | reversibility | information value of a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | K-104 prelim escalation + provisional C1 (as *readiness*, not blanket reservation) | High: preserves all mandatory activities and 4-eyes; benefit degrades gracefully to ~0 if packet weak | −4 to −12 h/case + smoother compliance flow | Blanket C1 reservation starves other urgent cases | High (stop reserving; keep prep) | High: measures warm-start saving and packet-completeness distribution |
| 2 | K-411 parallelize verification + real-time logs | Medium-high locally; bounded by Assessment queue; logging fix is pure upside | −3 to −5 h/case + better data quality | Bottleneck migration to Assessment; false-concurrency if mis-logged | High (revert task creation) | High: reveals whether Assessment is the true binding constraint |
| 3 | K-219 route handwritten to D2 | Medium: hinges on D2 slack and handwriting clarification rate | −4 to −10 h if clean; can flip negative | Rework step-function (8–36 h) + D2 saturation | High (route back to D1) | High: directly estimates D2 clarification/rework rate |
| 4 | K-337 suppress 2nd RMI loop | Low: changes decision policy and evidence standard, not just time | −24 to −40 h but quality-contingent | Wrong-decision, appeal, loss, compliance-defensibility tail | Low (a wrong decision, once notified, is hard to unwind) | High but ethically/legally sensitive; needs guarded design |

Ranking rationale: K-104 and K-411 change *readiness/routing* while preserving every compliance and four-eyes constraint and are easily reversed, so their downside is bounded. K-219 is genuinely useful but exposed to a heavy-tailed rework risk and resource saturation. K-337 offers the largest raw time saving yet is ranked last because it alters the *decision policy*—its speed is purchased with decision quality, appeal risk, and low reversibility, exactly the trade the evaluation warns against treating as "just time."

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1: K-104 prelim escalation + provisional C1 | intervention 2: K-411 parallelize verification + real-time logs |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | High-risk or high-value cases with a compliance flag raised at/near Triage | Low-risk/low-value cases with no originals required and digital/standard docs |
| assignment rule | Randomize flagged cases to (prep+reservation) vs standard path; block-randomize by day to balance C1 load | Randomize eligible cases to (both tasks at Triage + concurrency workaround) vs legacy serial path |
| sample/support logic | Enough flagged cases to detect a ≥2 h final-review reduction with adequate power; cap reservations/day to protect C1 | Enough cases to detect a ≥3 h verification-phase reduction and to observe Assessment-queue response under load |
| primary outcome | End-to-end cycle time; secondary: final Compliance_Review service time | End-to-end cycle time; secondary: verification-phase (Triage→both-checks-done) duration |
| process-path outcome | Confirm path still Assessment_final ≺ Compliance_Review_final ≺ Decision with A1/C1 four-eyes intact | Confirm Identity_Check ∥ Document_Check actually overlapped and order/precedence preserved |
| quality outcome | Decision-change rate and post-hoc compliance-defensibility review vs control | Assessment defect/rework rate; downstream decision-reversal rate vs control |
| queue-spillover outcome | Compliance wait for *non-pilot* urgent cases; C1 idle-due-to-reservation time | Assessment queue length/wait for non-pilot cases (bottleneck-migration check) |
| logging validation | Audit that prelim screen is timestamped and distinct from final review (C6 not violated) | Audit real-time vs batch entry; flag any zero-gap timestamps indicating false concurrency |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-104's preliminary escalation with a *disciplined* provisional C1 slot**, implemented as earlier readiness rather than blanket capacity hoarding. It keeps every mandatory activity, the final-review-after-final-assessment ordering, and the four-eyes separation (A1 assesses, C1 reviews), so it cannot corrupt the decision. Its benefit—a warm-start final review and reduced compliance wait—degrades gracefully to roughly zero when a packet is weak, and it is trivially reversible. **K-411** is a close second: it fixes a tooling-induced serialization and, importantly, hardens logging, though its end-to-end payoff is capped by the Assessment queue.

The most **dangerous despite its speed is K-337**. Deleting the second rework loop can cut 24–40 hours, but it is a *decision-policy* change: it forces a verdict on an unresolved discrepancy, shifting risk from cycle time into wrong-decision, appeal, loss, and compliance-defensibility tails that are hard to reverse once a Notify has gone out. Raw speed here masks a quality trade the organization may not want.

The counterfactual **least transportable to a system-wide policy is K-104's C1 reservation** (and secondarily K-219's D2 substitution). C1 is a single certified server, so what helps one case is zero-sum for the next; a locally-beneficial reservation, scaled to 100 cases with imperfect packets, can raise mean compliance wait and starve genuinely urgent cases—pure interference invisible in single-case analysis. K-219 similarly transports poorly because it moves the bottleneck to D2 and amplifies a heavy-tailed rework risk.

The most valuable additional data are: C1's service-time decomposition (background reading vs final-Assessment reading) to size the warm-start benefit; D2's clarification/rework rate on handwriting; the Assessment-queue arrival/service distribution to test bottleneck migration; supplier second-loop *materiality* (how often corrections flip decisions); and truthful, real-time verification timestamps to distinguish genuine concurrency from batch-entered artifacts.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The originals gate (Originals_Receipt ≺ Document_Check) applies only where originals are required (K-219, K-337) and does not constrain Identity_Check.
2. `resource` — C1 is the sole certified compliance reviewer in-window, making compliance a hard single-server queue and reservations zero-sum.
3. `temporal` — Posted queue figures are forecasts; realized start times may drift, so all cycle-time deltas are ranges, not point estimates.
4. `behavioral` — A preliminary escalation packet at Triage is usually complete enough to give C1 a genuine warm start (assumption that can fail, per K-104's worst case).
5. `quality` — D2 (generalist) has a materially higher clarification/rework probability on difficult handwriting than D1, and rework injects an 8–36 h external loop.
6. `structural` — Suppressing K-337's second RMI produces a *valid* terminal path (Assessment2 → final Compliance_Review → Decision) only because the residual discrepancy is explicitly adjudicated, not ignored.
7. `interference` — At 100-case scale, K-411's earlier verification pushes cases into the Assessment queue faster than it is served, migrating the bottleneck rather than removing it.
8. `interference` — Reserving C1 capacity across many cases, combined with any incomplete packets, raises mean compliance wait for non-target urgent cases even while target cases improve.
