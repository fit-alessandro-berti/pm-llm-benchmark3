## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts:**
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved global policy.
- SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled local operating procedure.
- ECD-2 is a written incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander.
- The CFO email is an ordinary communication, not entered into the controlled policy repository.
- System Outage ON-44 caused ERP unavailability and potential clock skews, affecting digital timestamps.
- Past organizational practice treats signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays, but not for new-supplier Compliance clearance.

**Assumptions:**
- I assume the CFO has delegated executive authority to interpret Finance controls (as supported by D3), making the CFO email a valid interpretive overlay for Finance approvals during the ECD-2 window.
- I assume ECD-2’s waiver of "supplier-onboarding requirements" (C4) applies to local SOP B3 (approved-supplier register) but does not waive the global GPIP-4 A3 Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers, as past practice explicitly excludes this.
- I assume "critical sterilization-line lots" (ECD-2 scope) must be interpreted functionally as items required for immediate containment and restoration of the affected line, excluding long-term contingency stock for backup lines.

**Decision Method:**
- **Authority & Specificity:** Specific, time-bound operational directives (ECD-2) override general local SOPs (B). However, core integrity controls (GPIP-4 A1, A3) cannot be overridden by operational directives unless explicitly stated by the Chief Risk Officer (A7). 
- **Time & Evidence:** Compliance is assessed at the exact moment of `Commit_to_Supplier`. Contemporaneous external evidence (supplier emails, signed PDFs, phone logs) supersedes ERP timestamps during the known outage or suspected clock skew. The burden of proof for an exception lies with the actor.
- **Retrospective Approvals:** Post-hoc approvals mitigate consequences but do not cure missing pre-commitment requirements (A6).

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

1. **P-501:** If we interpret SOP B4 "absent Plant Manager" functionally to mean "unable to approve in the system due to the ongoing ERP outage" (outage started 08:30, commit 08:50), SHIFT-1's action could be a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. This is credible because system unavailability mimics absence, but weak because the manager was reachable (e.g., via phone) and could have verbally delegated.
2. **P-502:** If we interpret ECD-2's C4 ("takes precedence over... supplier-onboarding requirements") as implicitly waiving the GPIP-4 A3 Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers, P-502 could be a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. This is credible given the severe emergency, but weak because past organizational practice explicitly excludes waiving new-supplier Compliance clearance via operational directives.
3. **P-503:** If we accept Operations' broad definition of "critical sterilization-line lots" to include backup contingency parts, and assume the CFO's email implicitly covers all non-legal controls up to 150k, the scope and threshold deviations might be justified. However, the Segregation of Duties violation (PROC-3 acting as requester, committer, and authorizer) remains a fatal flaw, making this alternative interpretation insufficient to change the primary classification.
4. **P-505:** If we rigidly enforce the ERP timestamp as the sole legal record of commitment, the commitment occurred at 18:06, outside the ECD-2 window. This would make P-505 NONCOMPLIANT. This is weak because the system clock skew and contemporaneous supplier acknowledgment email (17:59:31) provide stronger, real-world evidence of the true time of action.
5. **P-506:** If we accept that Incident Command has inherent authority to verbally extend written directives during ongoing recovery operations, P-506 could be a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. This is weak because GPIP-4 and standard compliance frameworks require written, scoped exceptions (A7), and ECD-2 was a formal written document.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

- **Policy breach:** P-501 (unauthorized delegate, missing approvals), P-502 (missing pre-commit Compliance), P-503 (SoD violation), P-504 (non-emergency unapproved supplier), P-506 (expired directive).
- **Logging defect / late evidence:** P-505 (ERP clock skew/late entry), P-507 (PDF queued due to outage).
- **Inability to prove compliance:** P-506 (no written record of ECD-2 extension).
- **Remediation:**
  - **Immediate:** Conduct retrospective compliance and legal reviews for all new/unapproved suppliers used during the incident (P-502, P-504). Issue disciplinary warnings for the Segregation of Duties violation in P-503 and non-emergency abuse in P-504.
  - **Monitoring:** Implement hard ERP blocks preventing the same user from being requester, committer, and authorizer. Formalize an ERP clock synchronization protocol and an offline emergency approval procedure (e.g., standardized PDF forms) to prevent evidence gaps during outages.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Global Policy Exception Clause (Replaces A7):**
In the event of a declared operational emergency, the Chief Risk Officer (or, if unavailable, the COO and CFO jointly) may issue a written, time-bound Emergency Directive. This Directive may waive local SOPs, competitive tendering, and standard financial approval thresholds up to EUR 150,000.

**Safeguards against normalization:**
1. No Emergency Directive may waive Segregation of Duties (the committer cannot be the sole approver).
2. Compliance Clearance for new suppliers or purchases >EUR 100,000, and Legal Clearance for purchases >EUR 150,000, remain mandatory before supplier commitment.
3. All emergency authorizations must be recorded in writing (or signed PDF) within 4 hours. Verbal extensions of Directives are strictly prohibited.
4. Retrospective system entry is permitted only if supported by contemporaneous external evidence (e.g., supplier emails, phone logs).
