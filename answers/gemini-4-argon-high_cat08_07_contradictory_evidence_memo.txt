# 1. Executive memo

**TO:** Executive Leadership Team  
**FROM:** Process Excellence & Analytics Lead  
**DATE:** November 12, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Decision Memo: Unbundling Fiber-Installation Redesign R—Scale Simple-House Fast Track, Suspend High-Risk Shortcuts, and Re-balance Capacity  

### Decision Recommendation
Management should **adopt Alternative E (Segmented Hybrid Rollout) combined with targeted operational guardrails from Alternative C and controlled testing from Alternative D**. Specifically, we must **unbundle Redesign R** immediately rather than scaling it uniformly (Alternative A) or rolling it back wholesale (Alternative B).

Redesign R is not a single intervention; it is a bundle of four distinct process changes whose effects diverge sharply by customer and building segment. When applied to simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot volume), the redesign is an unambiguous win: median cycle time drops by 6.0 days, p90 drops by 8.0 days, first-time-right (FTR) installation improves by 3 percentage points (pp), and complaints fall by 1pp. However, applying predictive survey skipping and post-billing activation to medium-risk apartments (29%), permit-sensitive old buildings (18%), and business accounts (8%) shifted verification failures downstream into expensive physical rework and customer-facing billing defects.

### The Core Strategic Trade-Off
Management faces a trade-off between **front-loaded verification vs. downstream exception recovery**:
1. **Front-end speed vs. tail reliability:** Merging validation/scheduling and skipping field surveys cut overall median cycle time from 14.2 to 10.8 days (-24%) and reduced visible pre-complaint unit cost from EUR 184 to EUR 161. However, false negatives in the survey-skip rule caused failed visits to rise (DFG share 13% to 19%) and second visits to jump from 11% to 18%. Once a case fails its first visit, it enters a congested rework loop where second-visit team utilization is 92% and coordinator utilization is 93%, blowing out p90 cycle time from 31.0 to 38.5 days and p95 from 45.0 to 62.0 days.
2. **Apparent cost savings vs. hidden total cost:** Saving a field survey reduces upfront cost by EUR 23 per case, leaving the field-survey team underutilized at 52% (down from 78%). Yet fully loaded cost—even with complaint data truncated after week 4—rose from EUR 198 to EUR 207 (+EUR 9/case) due to repeat truck rolls, manual activations (up from 7% to 15%), and 30-day complaints rising from 6.1% to 9.8%.
3. **Single-owner accountability vs. operational resilience:** Assigning a single installation coordinator cut 3+ handover cases from 34% to 12%, simplifying the handover-of-work network. However, at 93% utilization, coordinators became a centralized bottleneck; without shared team backup during absences, exception cases stall.

### Immediate Action Plan (Next 30 Days)
1. **Keep and scale for Simple, Low-Risk Houses:** Retain merged `Combined validate/schedule` and coordinator ownership for verified low-risk single-family homes.
2. **Pause harmful shortcuts across all segments:**
   - **Halt post-billing activation immediately** (`Billing start` before verified `Activation test`). It drives billing disputes in business accounts (+4pp complaints), obscures ownership for activation specialists, and creates a new long-tail `Billing start -> Complaint escalation` pathway (up from 3% to 8%).
   - **Suspend the predictive field-survey skip rule** for apartments, old buildings, and rural installs, redeploying idle field-survey capacity (currently at 52% utilization) to restore FTR and relieve the 92%-utilized second-visit team.
   - **Enforce mandatory system gates for Permit Checks** on older/apartment buildings before scheduling installation visits.
3. **De-bottleneck coordinators:** Implement pod-based backup coverage for coordinator absences and cap coordinator utilization at <=80–85%.
4. **Execute high-value measurements (M7, M3, M6, M4, M1):** Instrument conformance gates, failure reason codes, and full 60-day cost accounting before deciding on wider national rollout in 8 weeks.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

Process-mining evidence often appears contradictory when an intervention alters both the central tendency of the happy path and the branching probability and queueing dynamics of exception paths. Below is the technical reconciliation of the five apparent contradictions.

### 3.1 High Fitness (0.94 vs. 0.87) but Low Precision (0.51 vs. 0.79)
* **Mechanism:** In process discovery, **fitness** measures recall (the proportion of observed log traces that the discovered model can replay without penalty), whereas **precision** measures exactness (how tightly the model restricts behavior to *only* what is observed in the log, penalizing extra "escaping edges" enabled by the model).
* **Why they diverge in Pilot R:** A large share of cases (especially simple houses using `Combined validate/schedule`) followed a shorter, highly regular backbone path, allowing the discovery algorithm to capture the main flow cleanly and boost fitness to 0.94. Simultaneously, however, the relaxed business rules (ad-hoc survey skipping, post-billing activation, repeat visits without surveys, and post-billing escalations) introduced loosely ordered exception events (`Manual activation`, `Failed visit`, `Second visit`, `Billing start`, `Complaint escalation`) occurring in varied orders. To replay all these conflicting ordering variants without lowering fitness, the discovery miner introduced concurrent constructs and silent transitions (a local "flower model" in the back-end subsystem). Because those concurrent loops allow exponentially many interleavings that never occur together in a single trace, model precision collapsed from 0.79 to 0.51 and generalization fell from 0.70 to 0.56.

### 3.2 Improved Median (10.8d vs. 14.2d) but Worse Tail Duration (p90: 38.5d vs. 31.0d; p95: 62.0d vs. 45.0d)
* **Mechanism:** The overall cycle-time distribution is a **two-component mixture distribution**:
  \[
  f(t) = (1 - p_{\text{exc}}) \cdot f_{\text{fast}}(t) + p_{\text{exc}} \cdot f_{\text{rework}}(t)
  \]
  where \(p_{\text{exc}}\) is the probability of falling into an exception/rework loop (1 - FTR = 24% in Pilot vs. 18% in Baseline).
* **Why they diverge in Pilot R:** Because 76% of cases still complete on the first visit—and simple cases save days via merged `Combined validate/schedule` (38% DFG share) and skipped surveys (`Appointment scheduling -> Installation visit` up from 54% to 64%)—the 50th percentile (median) sits comfortably inside \(f_{\text{fast}}(t)\) and improves by 3.4 days. Conversely, the 90th and 95th percentiles sit completely inside \(f_{\text{rework}}(t)\). Not only did \(p_{\text{exc}}\) grow from 18% to 24% (pushing more cases into the tail), but the duration of \(f_{\text{rework}}(t)\) stretched dramatically because second-visit utilization hit 92% and coordinator utilization hit 93%. By Kingman's queueing approximation, mean waiting time scales proportional to \(\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\); moving utilization \(\rho\) from 66% to 92% multiplies queueing delay by more than **\(5.8\times\)** (\(\frac{0.92/0.08}{0.66/0.34} = \frac{11.5}{1.94} \approx 5.9\)).

### 3.3 Fewer Handovers (3+ handovers: 12% vs. 34%) but Lower Resilience
* **Mechanism:** In organizational mining, the **handover-of-work network** measures sequential transfers between distinct individuals, whereas **system resilience** depends on resource pool fungibility and structural redundancy in the **working-together network**.
* **Why they diverge in Pilot R:** Assigning a dedicated coordinator after address validation replaced multi-role sequential chains with a hub-and-spoke topology. While this reduced transfer friction and coordination overhead during normal operations (cutting 3+ handovers by nearly two-thirds), it created extreme **betweenness centrality** around individual coordinators and activation specialists. In queueing theory, splitting a pooled server queue into dedicated single-server queues eliminates pooling efficiency when a server goes offline (`coordinator absence`) or hits saturation (93% utilization). Because case context was locked with the individual coordinator rather than standardized in shared workflow states, absences caused immediate case stalls.

### 3.4 Lower Visible Cost (EUR 161 vs. EUR 184) but Higher Rework / Downstream Cost (EUR 207 vs. EUR 198+)
* **Mechanism:** **Cost-horizon boundary misalignment** and **prevention-versus-failure economics**.
* **Why they diverge in Pilot R:** Visible cost before complaint handling tracks only initial fulfillment activities (`Address validation`, `Field survey`, initial `Installation visit`). Merging validation/scheduling and cutting field surveys from 31% to 18% of scheduled cases directly eliminated EUR 23/case in upfront labor and truck-roll cost. However, field surveys act as an upstream quality gate. Removing that gate increased failed first visits (+6pp), second visits (+7pp), manual activations (+8pp), and 30-day complaints (+3.7pp). A second physical installation visit plus manual engineering troubleshooting and billing dispute resolution costs far more than a preventative survey. Worse still, because Pilot R cost tracking cuts off downstream complaint handling after week 4—and `Billing start -> Complaint escalation` is a long-tail relation—the reported EUR 207 is right-censored and understates the true total cost.

### 3.5 Improvement for Simple Houses vs. Deterioration for Apartments and Old Buildings
* **Mechanism:** **Heterogeneous treatment effects (feature-segment interaction)** compounded by **Simpson's paradox / compositional bias**.
* **Why they diverge in Pilot R:**
  - *Simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot):* Have standard drop-cable routing, no multi-dwelling riser complexities, and rarely require municipal or landlord permits. For them, field surveys were redundant overhead and merging validation/scheduling removed pure waiting time—yielding -6.0d median, -8.0d p90, +3pp FTR, and -1pp complaints.
  - *Medium-risk apartments (29%) & permit-sensitive old buildings (18%):* Require internal conduit tracing, building-management access, complex signal splicing, and formal permits. Skipping surveys (`Appointment scheduling -> Field survey` down 13pp) and bypassing `Permit check` (observed as frequent move-on-model alignments) meant technicians arrived unable to install (FTR -8pp and -11pp; p90 +12d and +25d). Furthermore, apartments experienced degraded `Installation visit -> Activation test` times due to shared distribution box issues, which triggered premature post-billing manual activations.
  - *Compositional confounding:* Because the pilot regions contained a higher proportion of apartments and old buildings than the baseline average, aggregate metrics heavily weight these negatively affected segments, partially masking the strong success in simple houses.

# 6. Final recommendation

Management should execute a **Phased Hybrid Strategy (Alternative E enhanced with C's process guardrails and D's high-VoI measurements)**. This captures proven cycle-time and cost improvements in simple buildings while immediately amputating the defect-generating shortcuts in complex segments.

### 1. Immediate Action (Week 1)
* **Mandate upfront segment & building-risk classification** at `Order received / Address validation` (enforcing 100% completeness on building type, age/permit sensitivity, and customer tier to fix the pre-week-3 data gap).
* **Reallocate capacity across teams:** Shift excess capacity from the underutilized field-survey team (52% utilization) to handle restored pre-installation surveys for apartments/old buildings and to cross-support the saturated second-visit team (92% utilization).
* **Implement "Coordinator Pods" (3–4 coordinators per pod):** Retain primary single-coordinator accountability per case, but configure automatic queue failover to pod peers whenever a coordinator is absent or individual utilization exceeds 85%.

### 2. What to Pause Immediately
* **Pause post-billing activation across ALL segments:** Restore the hard workflow gate requiring a passed `Activation test` (or verified `Manual activation` completion) *before* `Billing start` can execute.
* **Pause predictive field-survey skipping for all non-simple segments:** Suspend the skip rule for medium-risk apartments (29%), permit-sensitive old buildings (18%), business customers (8%), and rural long-distance installs (11%).
* **Pause merged scheduling without permit verification for older/apartment buildings:** Enforce a mandatory `Permit check` milestone in the workflow engine before `Appointment scheduling` can book an installation crew for permit-sensitive addresses.

### 3. What to Scale Now
* **Scale Redesign R's Fast-Track (`Combined validate/schedule` + Coordinator Ownership + Survey Bypass) for verified `Simple house, low building risk` orders** across pilot regions and prepare national deployment for this segment (which demonstrated -6.0d median, -8.0d p90, +3pp FTR, and -1pp complaints).
* **Scale pod-backed Coordinator Ownership** across segments *with* standard pre-install survey and pre-billing activation gates intact.

### 4. What to Measure Next (8-Week Measurement Sprint)
1. **Deploy M7 (Conformance monitoring)** and **M3 (Structured reason codes)** in Week 1 to verify zero unapproved `Billing start` before `Activation test`, zero skipped `Permit checks` on old buildings, and categorized root causes for every failed visit and manual activation.
2. **Execute M6 (Offline validation of the building-risk predictive rule)** in Weeks 1–3 against historical field-survey outcomes to rebuild the classifier before any future live testing (M1).
3. **Run M5 (Coordinator vs. Pod backup trial)** and **M2 (Start-timestamp instrumentation)** in Weeks 2–6 across the four pilot regions to verify queue-delay reduction.
4. **Track M4 (Full 60-day downstream cost per order)** stratified by customer segment.

### 5. Quantitative Thresholds to Justify Broader National Rollout
Management should authorize full national rollout of the Hybrid Redesign only when an 8-week post-guardrail evaluation cohort demonstrates all six gates:
1. **Tail Cycle Time Recovery:** Overall p90 cycle time **<= 31.0 days** and p95 **<= 45.0 days** (with old-building and apartment p90 returning within +/- 1.0 day of baseline, and simple houses maintaining >= 5.0 days p90 reduction).
2. **Quality & Rework Restoration:** Overall First-Time-Right (FTR) installation **>= 82%** (and **>= 85%** for simple houses), with `Second visit` rate **<= 11%** and `Manual activation` rate **<= 8%**.
3. **Conformance & Process Precision:** Zero (<0.5%) move-on-log violations for `Billing start` before `Activation test`, <1% missing `Permit check` alignments on permit-sensitive buildings, and discovered model precision recovering to **>= 0.75**.
4. **Customer & Complaint Performance:** 30-day complaint rate **<= 6.0%** overall (and business billing-dispute complaints eliminated), with post-close NPS **>= +21**.
5. **Resource Resilience:** Coordinator and second-visit team utilization stabilized inside the **75%–85%** operating band, with no spike in `Failed visit -> Second visit` temporal delay during staff absences.
6. **True 60-Day Unit Economics (M4):** Fully loaded 60-day cost (including all revisits and complaint handling) **<= EUR 190 per case** overall (at least EUR 8 below the EUR 198 baseline).
