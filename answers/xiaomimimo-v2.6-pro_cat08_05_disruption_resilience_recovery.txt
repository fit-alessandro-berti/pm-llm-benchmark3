Service metrics (median cycle time) were essentially back to baseline by T4 (Hospital 8h = baseline), yet every control-completeness metric remained 1.4–2.75× baseline after three weeks.

**(ii) A "dual clock" opened at T2 and never closed inside the window.** `Invoice close -> Audit remediation: 2,300` is the smoking gun: 2,300 cases were *commercially closed before their compliance evidence existed*. On top of that, `Audit remediation: 2,300 in / 2,100 out` leaves **200 cases still open when the log ends**, and `Reconcile manual reservation: 1,400 in / 1,200 out` leaves **200 cases with no recorded successor at all**. The operational clock said "recovered"; the compliance clock was still running.

**(iii) The manual log cannot account for its own cases.** In the T2 DFG, 12 of 14 manual-variant nodes show inflow exceeding outflow — the largest gaps being Manual pick list (4,340 in / 3,600 out = 740 unrecorded), Dispatch without scan (3,250 in / 2,600 out = 650 unrecorded), Phone POD (2,600 in / 2,200 out = 400 unrecorded), Manual inventory snapshot (4,720 in / 4,500 out = 220 unrecorded), Hospital fast lane (1,000 in / 920 out = 80 unrecorded). Conversely `Cancel order` shows 430 exits but only 320 recorded entrances — **110 cancellations with no recorded origin**. Roughly 3,000+ transitions are unmatched. Even conservatively interpreted, hundreds to low-thousands of cases passed through states that the log cannot reconstruct — which is precisely the definition of a traceability exception, expressed structurally rather than as a percentage.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

## The operative distinction

The difference is **not** "compliant vs. non-compliant" and it is **not** "throughput saved vs. throughput lost." A deviation from the baseline model is not automatically a defect (the constraints require this), and preserving throughput is not automatically resilience. The test has three parts:

1. **Is the control objective preserved, or merely the output?** A substitutive control that re-implements *authorization*, *release*, *identity verification* or *segregation* by another mechanism is resilience. A mechanism that keeps goods moving while the control objective is dropped is not.
2. **Is the exception visible at the time of the act?** Resilient improvisation leaves a contemporaneous, signed, timestamped record of the deviation and of the compensating control. Concealed noncompliance leaves either no record, or a record created later by reconstruction.
3. **Where does the residual risk land — and is it bounded and known?** Resilience transfers risk to a *named, measured, time-bounded* place. Concealed noncompliance transfers risk to an *unknown* place (a gap in the log, an unrecorded successor, a customer who may not have been authorized) or to a point in time where the evidence can no longer be generated at all.

## The decisive technical line: perishable vs. reconstructible evidence

This case turns on a distinction that the metrics alone obscure:

| Evidence type | Can it be recreated after the fact? | Examples in this case | What post-hoc remediation is worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reconstructible** | Yes, with some loss of assurance | Pick list content, invoice close, scan identity, order–customer linkage, back-order status | Substantial. `Reconcile manual reservation` and the restorable half of `Audit remediation` are genuine recovery. |
| **Perishable** | **No** | Cold-chain temperature *at the time of handling/dispatch/delivery*; dual control *at the moment of pick/pack*; chain of custody *at courier handoff*; authorization *at the time of sale*; lot/expiry *at the moment of physical selection* | Approximately zero. A missing temperature record is not an administrative gap — the fact itself is gone. A phone POD cannot retrospectively prove who took custody of a controlled substance. |

**Concealed noncompliance, in this case, is precisely the reporting of perishable-evidence failures as though they were reconstructible ones.** 638 excess cold-chain temperature gaps and 393 excess dual-control gaps cannot be "remediated"; they can only be *disclosed and risk-assessed*. Yet the process created an `Audit remediation -> END` path of 2,100 cases and the summary reports cycle times back at baseline — the visible metric says "recovered" while the true state includes cases whose compliance record is permanently deficient.

## Classification

**True resilience (control objective preserved, contemporaneous, bounded, visible):**
- **B (Manual authorization check)** — the canonical example. Authorization was not waived; it was re-performed by trained staff using offline evidence and phone confirmation, at the time. Cost: capacity strain (a *sustainability* problem, not a *compliance* problem).
- **F (Temporary release control)** for its *covered* scope — pharmacist judgement is an equivalent-or-stronger release control, contemporaneous, and explicitly risk-weighted toward controlled substances. That is textbook resilient design.
- **I (Defer noncritical order)** and **J (Backlog prioritization)** *as intentions* — deliberate, risk-ordered rationing of a scarce resource is exactly what resilience is. They are resilience **conditional on being rule-based and recorded**; see below.

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance:**
- **C (Skip system authorization check)** — the clearest case. The control objective was deleted and replaced by an *assumption* ("customer history", "standing authorization") that was never validated at the time of shipment. 1,600 cases. Nothing downstream can detect an unauthorized product–customer pairing; it only surfaces (if ever) as a recall-notification failure or a licensing finding. This is not "loose compliance"; it is the absence of the control, with no exception record.
- **G (Dispatch without scan)** — the largest volume of lost perishable evidence (3,250 handoffs, 650 unrecorded successors). Chain of custody is gone for every one of those cases. For controlled substances this is a diversion risk that no later reconciliation can close.
- **D (Manual inventory snapshot) once stale** — at hour 0 it is resilience; by hour 6 it is a *silent* degradation of the accuracy control, with no staleness flag. Silent staleness is the archetypal concealed risk: the process looks identical on day 1 and day 2 while its assurance decays.
- **L (Audit remediation) where it "closes" perishable gaps** — the queue is honest work, but its *closure semantics* are false for temperature and dual-control findings. 2,300 cases commercialized before evidence, 200 still open, and an unknown share permanently deficient.

**Ambiguous — the classification depends on stated risk appetite and on the product/customer class:**
- **A (Hospital-critical fast lane)** — genuinely mission-aligned (91% fill vs 61%), and for ambient hospital supplies with pre-verified accounts, deferring authorization evidence by hours is an acceptable, bounded residual. For controlled substances shipped to a hospital, deferring authorization to after dispatch is not: sale to an unauthorized party is irreversible. Same behaviour, opposite verdicts, depending on the risk class. It is also a fairness question: the fast lane was funded by pharmacy service.
- **E (Manual pick list with later scan reconstruction)** — physical picking under human attention is a real compensating control; but "reconstructed" scans are assertions, not observations. Acceptable for ambient standard; materially deficient for recall-sensitive (lot/expiry) and controlled (quantity/segregation).
- **H (Phone proof of delivery)** — a legitimate substitute for ambient/wholesale delivery evidence, and an inadequate one for controlled substances. The pattern itself is neutral; its permissibility is class-conditional.
- **D at T2 start vs. T2 end**, and **K (Reconcile manual reservation)** — legitimate repair, but only meaningful while its residual (200 unrecorded cases) is disclosed rather than absorbed.

**One further nuance the metrics hide:** even "true resilience" was not free. `Temporary release control` covered only 46% of manual packs and the `Quality/release check` node disappears from the T2 graph entirely. The *description* of adaptation F ("all controlled-substance orders checked") is reassuring; the *event data* supports a much weaker claim. When a workaround is documented in narrative but not instrumented in the event stream, the organization cannot know its own coverage rate — and that is itself a concealed noncompliance risk, because management will believe the stronger statement.

# 4. Recovery process model

## Notation

Structured text in **BPMN-style form**: `->` sequence, `AND` parallel branches, `XOR` exclusive choice, `[guard]` conditional, `||> ` parallel gateway, `{...}` repeating loop. Swimlanes are shown as `LANE:` blocks. Two notational additions are used and defined here:

- **CONTROL GATE** `<<gate>>` — a task that cannot be waived at the operator's discretion; waiving it requires a named approver and creates a record in the **Waiver Register**.
- **COMPLIANCE DEBT REGISTER** `<<register>>` — a ledger of evidence that is missing or restorable-but-not-yet-restored, with age and owner. It is a first-class process object, not a report.

## 4.1 Trigger conditions (entry to *Controlled Continuity Mode*, CCM)

```
TRIGGER T-DEGRADE (any one, sustained >= 15 min):
  [WMS / scanning / validation / reservation service unavailable at >= 1 site]
  XOR [data integrity SUSPECT (cyber event) -- integrity doubt overrides availability]
  XOR [system returns data FAILING plausibility checks (negative stock, impossible timestamps)]
  -> Declare CCM-LEVEL by XOR:
       L1 "Data trusted, system down"      -> manual authorization permitted (Pattern B)
       L2 "Data stale/unverified"          -> stale-snapshot TTL enforced (Pattern D+), no assumed authorization
       L3 "Integrity suspect / confirmed breach" -> 100% manual dual control; Controlled & Cold-chain
                                                    dispatch only under pharmacist + named security officer;
                                                    cold-chain only with re-issued logger evidence
TRIGGER T-CAPACITY (any one):
  [Hospital-critical backlog age p90 > 12h] XOR [fill rate < 95% for any class] 
  XOR [compliance staff coverage ratio < 0.5 of required] 
  -> Activate triage/deferral policy (4.2) and notify account managers within 1h
TRIGGER T-CONTROL-DECAY (any one, measured on a rolling 4h window):
  [traceability exception rate > 2%] XOR [any missing dual-control event] 
  XOR [cold-chain documentation completeness < 98%] XOR [stale-snapshot usage beyond TTL > 0]
  -> FREEZE the affected lane (XOR controlled-substance lane / cold-chain lane) until the control is restored
  -> NOTE: T-CONTROL-DECAY is a STOP trigger, not a report. This is what was missing at T2.
```

## 4.2 Triage rules

```
LANE: Incident Command
  DECLARE priority score per open case:
      score = customer_criticality(Hospital=3, Pharmacy=2, Wholesale=1)
            x product_risk(Controlled=4, Cold-chain=3, Recall-sensitive=3, Ambient=1)
            x delay_fragility(perishable/short shelf-life/urgent therapy = 1.5, else 1.0)
  -> BANDS:
       P1 score >= 27   e.g. Hospital x Controlled/Cold/Recall   SLA 24h
       P2 score 12-26   e.g. Hospital x Ambient, Pharmacy x Controlled/Cold/Recall  SLA 48h
       P3 score 4-11    Pharmacy x Ambient                        SLA 72h
       P4 score < 4     Wholesale-bulk                            SLA 120h
  -> WITHIN band: FIFO by order age (deterministic, auditable)  <<no discretionary re-ordering>>
  -> DEFERRAL (XOR) allowed only for P3/P4, max 48h, 
       REQUIRES: customer notification + deferral register entry + named approver
  -> CANCELLATION REQUIRES: named approver (rank >= shift manager) + customer notification + reason code
  -> PUBLICATION: the scoring rule, band SLAs and current fill rate by class are published to customers
       at CCM declaration and every 24h thereafter (this is the fairness control, see §5)
```

## 4.3 Minimum control matrix (what may **never** be waived)

```
                    | Hospital-critical | Pharmacy-standard | Wholesale-bulk
--------------------+-------------------+-------------------+----------------
Controlled-substance| VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*
                    | DUAL CTRL pick+   | DUAL CTRL pick+   | DUAL CTRL pick+
                    |   pack            |   pack            |   pack
                    | SECURE MANIFEST   | SECURE MANIFEST   | SECURE MANIFEST
                    | POD named receiver| POD named receiver| POD named receiver
                    |   + signature/ID  |   + signature/ID  |   + signature/ID
                    | SCAN waivable     | SCAN waivable     | SCAN waivable
--------------------+-------------------+-------------------+----------------
Cold-chain          | VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*
                    | LOGGER EVIDENCE** | LOGGER EVIDENCE** | LOGGER EVIDENCE**
                    | PACK-OUT TIME     | PACK-OUT TIME     | PACK-OUT TIME
                    | HANDOVER TIME     | HANDOVER TIME     | HANDOVER TIME
                    | POD + delivery    | POD + delivery    | POD + delivery
                    |   temp reading    |   temp reading    |   temp reading
                    | SCAN waivable     | SCAN waivable     | SCAN waivable
--------------------+-------------------+-------------------+----------------
Recall-sensitive    | VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*   | VALIDATED AUTH*
                    | LOT/BATCH/EXPIRY  | LOT/BATCH/EXPIRY  | LOT/BATCH/EXPIRY
                    |   capture (photo) |   capture (photo) |   capture (photo)
                    | QUARANTINE SYNC***| QUARANTINE SYNC***| QUARANTINE SYNC***
                    | RELEASE CHECK 100%| RELEASE CHECK 100%| RELEASE CHECK 100%
--------------------+-------------------+-------------------+----------------
Ambient standard    | AUTH backfill<=4h | AUTH backfill<=24h| AUTH backfill<=24h
                    | RELEASE sample    | RELEASE sample    | RELEASE sample

    against an OFFLINE AUTHORIZATION REGISTER that is re-issued every 4h and carries a version ID.
    **Pattern C is abolished**: assumed standing authorization is only lawful if it appears in the
    pre-authorized Standing Order Register (named SKU x named customer x validity window x approver).
 ** LOGGER EVIDENCE = calibrated logger ID + photograph of reading at pack-out, dispatch and delivery.
    Zero waivers. A missing logger reading is an immediate T-CONTROL-DECAY freeze of the cold-chain lane.
*** QUARANTINE SYNC = re-issued quarantine/recall addendum every 4h, checked at pick. This is the control
    that pattern D silently lost.
```

## 4.4 Manual evidence capture — the *Continuity Packet*

```
LANE: Warehouse / Compliance
  ON case entry to CCM flow, XOR by risk band ->
    P1/P2 -> PRE-NUMBERED CONTINUITY PACKET (carbonless, 3 copies: case / manifest / register)
    P3/P4 -> PRE-NUMBERED CONTINUITY MANIFEST LINE (batch form, one line per case)
  PACKET FIELDS (all mandatory unless marked waivable):
    case ID (hand-assigned, prefix "CCM-<site>-<shift>-<seq>")
    customer ID + authorization evidence ref (register version ID + checker initials)
    product SKU + LOT + BATCH + EXPIRY (photo attached)
    risk flags (Controlled / Cold / Recall) -> drives the 4.3 matrix
    pick: picker initials + CHECKER initials  (dual control where required)
    pack-out time + logger ID + reading photo (cold-chain)
    release: pharmacist initials + scope statement ("full" / "controlled-only" / "sample n=")
    dispatch: courier, manifest #, driver signature + ID, case count, load photo, time
    POD: receiver name + role + signature/photo + delivery time (+ delivery temp if cold)
    EXCEPTION FLAG field: any waived or substituted control MUST be ticked here with approver ID
  END-OF-SHIFT: physical reconciliation count (packets issued = picked = dispatched + in-process)
  T+24h: packets scanned into the case record, tagged CAPTURE_MODE = "MANUAL-CONTEMPORANEOUS"
    or, if reconstructed later, "RETROSPECTIVE-RECONSTRUCTED" (never indistinguishable from live scans)
  <<register>> any unmet item from 4.3 -> COMPLIANCE DEBT REGISTER with owner + due date + evidence class
       (RESTORABLE / PERISHABLE-NONRESTORABLE)
```

## 4.5 Backlog prioritization (T3/T4 re-entry)

```
LANE: Backlog Control
  RE-OPEN backlog under the 4.2 score (published, versioned)
  -> AGING CAPS: P1 24h / P2 48h / P3 72h / P4 120h
  -> breach of a cap => AUTO-ESCALATION to Incident Command + customer notification + reason code
  -> CASES THAT CANNOT MEET 4.3 CONTROLS => do not ship; route to XOR:
        (a) HOLD with customer consent and a substitute product, OR
        (b) CANCEL with named approver and customer notification
     ** "Ship without the control" is NOT a branch in this model. That branch is what patterns C and G added. **
  -> DAILY published dashboard: fill rate and cancellation rate BY CLASS (fairness transparency)
```

## 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow (runs **in parallel from day 0**, not after close)

```
LANE: Reconciliation (AND-parallel to fulfilment, starts at CCM declaration, daily cadence)
  ||> AND
    [R1 Inventory match: paper pick/pack counts vs reservation vs physical count]
    [R2 Authorization match: every shipped case has a VALIDATED AUTH ref of the right version]
    [R3 Evidence match: every case in 4.3 matrix has its mandatory fields present]
  -> each break => <<register>> COMPLIANCE DEBT REGISTER entry (RESTORABLE or PERISHABLE-NONRESTORABLE)
  -> RESTORABLE breaks => remediation task, SLA 5 business days, named owner
  -> PERISHABLE-NONRESTORABLE breaks => NOT "remediated". Route to:
        documented residual finding + risk assessment + quality/compliance escalation
        + (Controlled) possible regulatory notification decision
        + (Cold-chain) product quality decision (release / quarantine / destroy)

LANE: Case closure -- REORDERED relative to the incident behaviour
  P1/P2 with Controlled, Cold-chain or Recall-sensitive:
      Quality/release check <<gate>> -> Dispatch -> POD <<gate>> -> COMPLIANCE CLOSE <<gate>>
        -> INVOICE CLOSE        # compliance BEFORE money
  P3/P4 and Ambient:
      ... -> INVOICE CLOSE AND<parallel> COMPLIANCE CLOSE (SLA: compliance within 5 bd)
        # but the case is REPORTED as "open - compliance" until R1-R3 pass
  STOP RULE (exit from CCM):
      >= 5 consecutive business days with
        traceability exceptions <= 1.5x baseline  AND cold-chain gaps = 0  AND dual-control gaps = 0
        AND conformance fitness >= 0.94 AND % fitting traces >= 85%
        AND COMPLIANCE DEBT: count trending down 3 days, age p90 <= 5 bd, PERISHABLE findings dispositioned
      => declare "operational recovery complete, compliance recovery complete" as SEPARATE statements
      => if operational criteria met but compliance criteria not: declare "OPERATIONS RESTORED / 
         COMPLIANCE DEBT OUTSTANDING = n cases, m perishable" in the management report. Never merge the two.
```

**Why this model is different from what happened.** In the observed process, `Invoice close -> Audit remediation` was the *only* path to evidence recovery, and it fired 2,300 times in T4 with a tail that outlived the log. In the redesigned model, reconciliation starts at T2 on a daily cadence, compliance close is a gate in front of invoice close for the risk-bearing classes, and perishable evidence failures are structurally prevented from ever being reported as "closed."

# 5. Resilience indicators

Leading indicators are measured **during** the incident on short windows (4h/shift) and are designed to trigger action; lagging indicators are measured per phase and are designed to prevent self-congratulation.

## Leading indicators (with triggers)

| # | Indicator | Definition | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Time to declare CCM** | Minutes from first service degradation to governed degraded mode | > 30 min = fail |
| L2 | **Control coverage rate** | % of packs passing a release control; % of dispatches with a manifest attestation; % of picks with dual-control initials where required | < 100% for Controlled/Cold/Recall = freeze lane. (Would have caught the F-vs-DDF gap: only 46% of packs touched a release control.) |
| L3 | **Contemporaneous evidence completeness** | % of Continuity Packet mandatory fields filled *at the time of the act*, measured T+4h | < 98% overall, < 100% for P1/P2 |
| L4 | **Stale-source exposure** | # of decisions made on data older than its TTL (snapshot age at use) | > 0 beyond TTL = mandatory exception record; > 5% = re-issue source |
| L5 | **Real-time exception rates** | Traceability / cold-chain / dual-control gaps per 4h window, by class | > 2%, > 0%, > 0% respectively = T-CONTROL-DECAY |
| L6 | **Unrecorded-flow rate** | Node inflow  outflow in the live event feed (the structural gap seen in the T2 DFG) | > 2% of cases with an unexplained state = logging integrity event. This is the early-warning version of "3,000 unmatched transitions." |
| L7 | **Compliance capacity coverage ratio** | Compliance/pharmacy FTE hours available ÷ required by the 4.3 matrix for projected volume | < 0.5 = trigger volume reduction/deferral *before* controls are silently dropped (Pattern B's real cost) |
| L8 | **Backlog age p90 by band** | Age of oldest open case per P1–P4 | Band SLA breach = auto-escalation + notification |
| L9 | **Triage rule adherence** | % of dispatches consistent with the published score ordering | < 95% = prioritization integrity review (the J fairness risk) |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** a controlled reduction in throughput and an explicit, time-bounded deferral of low-criticality orders as the price of keeping non-waivable controls intact: the fast lane, the manual authorization desk, the pharmacist release check, the deferral of bulk orders and the risk-ordered backlog were genuine resilience — they preserved the hospital mission (91% fill) and they preserved the *control objective*, not merely the case count. Management should **accept** slower cycle times for wholesale-bulk (which in fact never returned to 36h and sat at 58h/120h at T4) and should accept that some paper-era evidence is only *restorable* rather than *contemporaneous*. Management should **not accept** shipping on assumed authorization (1,600 cases), dispatching without handoff attestation (3,250 events), letting inventory snapshots silently decay, or — most importantly — reporting a case as recovered when a perishable fact (temperature at handling, dual control at pick, chain of custody at handoff) was never captured and can never be created: the ~638 cold-chain and ~393 dual-control gaps are residual findings to be dispositioned and, where required, disclosed, not items to be "closed" in a queue that reached 2,300 cases and still held 200 when the log ended. Management should also **not accept** a single recovery number: operational recovery (achieved ~day 7–10) and compliance recovery (not achieved by day 21; fitness 0.90 and 76% fitting traces) must be reported separately, with the compliance debt published as a standing KPI. The specific evidence that would change this recommendation is: (a) instrumented proof that the temporary release control actually covered ~100% of controlled and cold-chain packs rather than the 46% the event data implies — if so, Pattern F moves from "modify" to "keep as-is"; (b) discovery that the fast-lane and skipped-authorization cases all had valid, dated standing orders in a register — which would move Pattern C from "retire" to "keep under register control" and shrink the residual finding materially; (c) a demonstration that temperature and custody facts for the affected cold-chain and controlled cases *are* recoverable from independent sources (courier telematics, in-box loggers already deployed, customer systems) — which would convert a perishable gap into a restorable one; and (d) evidence that the pharmacy fill-rate collapse (61%) was contractually permitted and customer-agreed rather than an unmanaged transfer of cost — which would change the fairness finding from "risk" to "accepted, documented trade-off". Absent that evidence, the correct posture is: restore the *controls* first, the *volume* second, and never count restored volume as restored compliance.
