## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

Source keys: N1–N5 are the normative and operational materials; A1–A5 are the aggregate bullets in their listed order (A1 = 312 Auto_Approve cases, A2 = 47 Request_More_Info cases, A3 = notification missingness, A4 = six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases, A5 = Internal Audit flags and the Operations rebuttal).

### 1.1 What M0 actually says (not in dispute)

M0 is a closed-world model with no silent transitions, no loop and one XOR. Its language is exactly four traces:

- Register_Supplier, then Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review in either order, then Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier, Notify_Requester, Close_Case (two traces);
- Register_Supplier, then both checks in either order, then Reject_Supplier, Notify_Requester, Close_Case (two traces).

Three consequences matter for everything that follows.

1. **M0 is stricter than CS-9 in places.** It requires Quality_Review before a rejection, Register_Supplier in every case, and a logged Notify_Requester. CS-9 as quoted demands the two checks and manual approval only "before activation" and says nothing about notification.
2. **M0 is looser than CS-9 in places.** It is blind to the requester/approver rule (N1), to identities generally, and to every date, amount, risk class and deadline.
3. **M0 is silent on everything else.** Auto_Approve, Request_More_Info, QA_Clearance, reactivation and provisional activation are not in its alphabet.

Alignment replay of the fifteen raw traces on M0 gives three fitting cases (SUP-701, 712, 715) and twelve deviating ones. SUP-704, SUP-705 and SUP-708 produce the same alignment signature (one Activate_Supplier in the wrong position), although one is an authorised emergency, one an expired emergency and one a sanctions breach. For SUP-703 and SUP-714 the cheapest alignment explains the trace as a rejected supplier with a spurious activation. M0 deviations are therefore flags that need interpretation, not findings.

### 1.5 Burden-of-proof approach

**Hierarchy.** CS-9 is the norm. M0 is a representation of it. Where M0 is stricter or looser than CS-9, CS-9 governs and the gap is a model defect. Stakeholder practice (N5) has no normative force until adopted.

**Declaring noncompliance.** The party asserting it must show, on the balance of probabilities:

1. a binding rule that was in force at the event's effective time and whose scope covers the case type;
2. reliable evidence of the conduct (existence, order and actor identity) from a source whose completeness for that activity is known;
3. the absence of a valid exception.

For High or Critical findings, or attribution to a named person, I require clear and convincing evidence. Materiality is assessed separately from the existence of a breach.

**Effect of an M0 deviation.** It shifts only the burden of explanation to Operations. Conversely, fitting M0 creates no presumption of compliance with rules M0 does not encode.

**Accepting an exception.** This is an affirmative defence. Its proponent must show five things: a written instrument; issued or countersigned by someone with authority over the derogated rule; in force at the relevant time; eligibility as recorded at decision time; and satisfaction of every compensating condition. Exceptions are read narrowly. Factual necessity, analogy, good outcomes and expected future approval do not extend them. Missing eligibility data defeats the exception until it is reconstructed.

**Disputed instruments.** Where the instrument's authority is itself contested (PD-3), I separate individual reliance from institutional validity. An operator who followed a recorded, time-boxed decision within its terms is not at fault. The organisation may still carry a governance nonconformity.

**Missing data.** Absence of an event from a log with known incompleteness is not evidence that the act did not happen. For gate controls (sanctions, approval) the operation must be able to evidence performance, and failure to do so after a reasonable search counts against it. For post-migration notifications, absence is indeterminate unless independent, immutable evidence resolves it.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Thesis.** CS-9 is the only approved norm. It uses "every" twice. The operation has repeatedly activated suppliers without the controls CS-9 demands, has allowed time-boxed exceptions to become habit, and benefits from a model so narrow that its worst resource violations replay as perfect.

**Formal authority.**

- PD-3 is meeting minutes and an email. It was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0 (N2). A controlled standard is amended through its own change process, not by a committee that the standard does not name.
- The only Compliance voice was an observer, and she denies having approved any weakening. On a strict reading every one of the 312 Auto_Approve cases (A1) breached "every activation requires Manual_Procurement_Approval", and every post-activation review breached "Quality_Review before activation".
- Even if PD-3 is accepted, the defence carries the burden and fails for 23 cases: 14 clearly ineligible and 9 unverifiable. A "monitored" pilot let an operator tick "pilot" for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier (SUP-703).
- PD-3's own safeguard, master-data validation before activation, is not evidenced in any pilot trace shown.

**Expiry.**

- A sunset clause is a control. FD-7 "expired automatically" at 23:59 on 2026-07-12. SUP-705 was provisionally activated the next day with no written extension.
- That day was a Monday. If SUP-704's retrospective review and approval were completed then, the people whose absence justified the emergency route were at their desks.
- PD-3 ended 2026-07-31. SUP-714 and five other cases used Auto_Approve the following week (A4). "We believed permanent approval was imminent" is an admission that the actors knew no approval existed.

**Deviations hidden by M0.**

- SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly while breaching the one resource rule CS-9 states, through two aliases of the same person.
- SUP-715 fits while two controls meant to be independent were performed by spouses in one reporting line, with no recorded disclosure.
- M0 checks no deadline and no amount. Nobody has shown that the 289 "eligible" pilot cases received Quality_Review within two business days.
- The true violation rate is therefore higher than conformance checking suggests, not lower.

**Normalisation of exceptions.**

- The sequence is visible in the record: a pilot is tolerated as an exception, operators can self-select into it (SUP-703), it continues after expiry (SUP-714, A4), and Operations now asks for permanence (N5).
- The emergency route shows the same drift within 24 hours (SUP-705).
- Operations is using the volume of deviations it created as evidence that the model is obsolete (A5).

**High-risk cases.**

- SUP-703: no manual approval ever, review only after activation, and EUR 180,000 of spend. That is nine times the cap that FD-7 imposed on provisionally activated suppliers in a genuine emergency.
- SUP-708: activation before the sanctions check, the single control that CS-9, PD-3 and FD-7 all keep in front of activation (E4), and before manual approval as well. "No transactions occurred" describes luck. The system permitted a sanctioned-party exposure window of 40 minutes for a HIGH-risk supplier.
- Internal Audit independently flagged both (A5).

**Desirable outcomes do not amend policy.**

- Policy is changed prospectively by its owner. If good outcomes ratified deviations, every control would be optional for anyone confident of the result, and only the cases that went wrong would count as violations. That is survivorship reasoning.
- The same applies to Request_More_Info. However sensible, it should be added to the norm by its owner, not waved through by an auditor.

**Concessions the prosecution must make.**

- M0 over-reaches for SUP-709. CS-9 requires the checks before activation, not before rejection, and expressly contemplates an abbreviated pre-screen.
- Request_More_Info (SUP-707, A2) breaches no CS-9 rule. The missing loop is a model defect.
- SUP-711 is a migration duplicate and SUP-713 a rename. Neither is misconduct.
- After 2026-07-01 a missing Notify_Requester proves almost nothing (A3). SUP-706 cannot be charged on the log alone.
- CS-9 does not define reactivation. SUP-710 cannot be proved a breach without a scope ruling.
- M0 does not encode the resource rule the prosecution relies on for SUP-712. The model is defective in both directions.

The prosecution does not need the conceded cases. Its case rests on SUP-703, 708, 712, 705 and 714, the 14 ineligible pilot cases and the six post-expiry cases.

## 3. Defense Brief

**Thesis.** M0 accepts four traces. It is being used as a compliance oracle for a process that has acquired a pilot, an emergency directive, a renamed activity, a migrated notification channel and legitimate rework. Most of what it reports is its own obsolescence. A trace deviation is not business misconduct.

**Concurrency.**

- M0 correctly leaves Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review unordered. It then makes both a mandatory join before any decision, including rejection. CS-9 conditions the checks on activation only. M0 therefore demands a quality review of a lead that has already failed sanctions (SUP-709).
- Across N1–N3 the only ordering common to all instruments is sanctions before activation. Where Quality_Review sits relative to activation depends on the policy in force. A single fixed partial order cannot be the norm for June and July 2026.

**Missing loops.**

- Forty-seven cases contain Request_More_Info and none activated before the evidence arrived (A2). Compliance agrees the behaviour is desirable (N5).
- M0 charges each request as a deviation. SUP-707 is penalised for obtaining missing certificates before approval. A model that scores diligence as nonconformance is wrong.

**Pilot and emergency overlays.**

- PD-3 is a real, recorded, scoped and time-boxed decision of the steering committee. Compliance wrote at the time that the pilot "may proceed as a monitored exception". Her later statement that she did not approve weakening CS-9 is consistent with that: an exception does not amend the standard.
- 92.6% of pilot uses appear eligible (A1). SUP-702 is exactly what PD-3 describes: eligible, in window, sanctions first, review next business day.
- FD-7 is explicit, and SUP-704 met every condition: sanctions first, EUR 18,000 against a EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, review and approval the next business day.
- M0 was never updated for either instrument, so it mislabels more than 300 authorised cases.

**CRM migration.**

- About 70% of notifications are mirrored, so about 30% are expected to be missing. The observed figure is 29%, against 2% before (A3). Post-migration absence from the onboarding log carries almost no information about whether the requester was notified.
- For SUP-702 the CRM holds a stable notification ID. The better evidence contradicts the log.

**Reactivation.**

- CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. Requiring Register_Supplier for a supplier that already exists is meaningless.
- In SUP-710 the time-sensitive controls were repeated (fresh sanctions check, manual approval) and the quality certification was still valid. The gap is in the norm, not the conduct.

**Rejected leads.**

- CS-9 itself allows an abbreviated pre-screen. If the model owner is right, SUP-709 is out of scope. If the process owner is right, M0 over-constrains CS-9. Either way there is no breach.

**Label equivalence.**

- SUP-713's QA_Clearance is Quality_Review under its new UI name. Audit documents show equivalent content and the mapping is now approved. The deviation is purely lexical.

**Trace deviation versus business misconduct.**

- Of twelve M0-deviating cases, realised exposure is shown in one (SUP-703). SUP-706, 707, 709, 711 and 713 are artefacts of the model or the log. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are authorised. SUP-710 is unregulated.
- SUP-705 stayed inside every substantive FD-7 safeguard: sanctions first, EUR 12,000 of spend, review and approval afterwards. It is at most a lapse of paperwork during a continuing flood and should be ratified.
- SUP-714 is a low-risk, eligible supplier handled by a route that the organisation left switched on. That is an organisational failure to disable a route, not operator misconduct.

**Concessions the defence must make.**

- SUP-703 and SUP-708 are indefensible under any instrument or any plausible revised model. Sanctions before activation is non-negotiable, and SUP-703 was never eligible.
- SUP-712 is a breach of a rule CS-9 states outright. M0's blindness to it is a reason to extend the model, not to excuse the case.
- Fourteen clearly ineligible pilot cases and an operator-selectable "pilot" flag show the pilot's controls were weak. The nine unverifiable cases are the defence's burden.
- Informal practice has limits. Belief that approval is imminent (SUP-714) and the factual persistence of an emergency (SUP-705) are not authority. A permanent auto-approval branch requires an amendment that does not exist (N5).
- Request_More_Info should be formally adopted, not merely tolerated. PD-3's standing should be regularised, not assumed.

Explanations for the cases where another label was arguable:

- **SUP-702** is not MIXED. Its dominant character is an exception properly used by the people handling the case. The doubt about PD-3's authority belongs to the organisation and is recorded at system level. If the CS-9 owner formally rules PD-3 void, this case becomes an organisational nonconformity of low severity, not individual misconduct.
- **SUP-705** is not a justified exception. FD-7 shows the organisation knows how to authorise an emergency in writing, and nobody with authority extended it. The finding is curable by ratification, but ratification should be recorded as such and not relabelled as compliance.
- **SUP-711** is COMPLIANT and not DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE because the data problem is fully resolved by the shared transaction ID. **SUP-706** is the opposite: the data problem cannot be resolved on present evidence.
- **SUP-713** is classified by the cause of the alarm (the model's vocabulary). The conduct was compliant. The late mapping approval is a change-control observation, not a case violation.
- **SUP-710** is not NONCOMPLIANT because element 1 of the burden (an applicable rule) is unproved. It is not COMPLIANT either, since no rule authorises reusing a certificate. An interim reactivation rule is needed immediately.
- **SUP-715** is NONCOMPLIANT only against the disclosure requirement. The relationship is not prohibited and the approval is not void. The remedy is disclosure and an independent re-review.

### 5.2 Aggregate populations

- **289 apparently eligible pilot cases:** presumptively justified exceptions, subject to a batch test of PD-3's conditions (sanctions before activation, review within two business days, validation evidence) and to resolution of D1.
- **14 clearly ineligible pilot cases:** noncompliant. Each needs individual review with spend, starting with SUP-703.
- **9 pilot cases with missing attributes:** exception not established. They are provisionally noncompliant and reversible if eligibility is reconstructed from immutable sources.
- **6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases:** noncompliant. The root cause is a route that was not switched off at expiry.
- **47 Request_More_Info cases:** model defect, no finding.
- **Post-migration missing notifications:** indeterminate. They should not be counted as deviations until reconciled against the CRM.

### 5.3 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance.** The operation is materially noncompliant in a bounded set of respects, not across the board.

- Proven: activation before sanctions clearance (SUP-708); use of exception routes outside eligibility and outside their windows (SUP-703, 705, 714, plus 14 and 6 cases in aggregate); a defeated segregation of duties (SUP-712).
- The common root cause is that controls are advisory, not enforced. The system allows activation without sanctions clearance, lets an operator assert pilot eligibility, leaves expired routes enabled, and resolves identity by login, not by person.
- Materiality comes from severity, not frequency. One EUR 180,000 high-risk bypass and one sanctions-order breach are material on their own.
- Not proven: that the operation is generally noncompliant. Most deviations by count are authorised overlays, benign rework or logging loss.

**Governance.** The pilot derogated from a controlled standard without the documented approval of that standard's owner. That is an organisational nonconformity owned by the steering committee and Compliance. It should be closed by formal ratification or a formal finding. It should not be left ambiguous, and it should not be charged to operators.

**Model validity.** M0 is valid as a statement of the default route and remains binding for it. It is invalid as a compliance classifier.

- On these fifteen cases, twelve M0 alarms contain four real violations (SUP-703, 705, 708, 714), and the three passes contain two violations (SUP-712, 715). The cases are illustrative, not a random sample, so these are not population rates.
- M0 is over-strict (review before rejection, no information loop, no label versioning, registration for reactivations) and under-inclusive (resources, time, amounts, authority).
- It is being replayed on a log that has lost about 30% of one of its mandatory activities.

### 5.4 Where I reject each side

**Against the prosecution:**

- M0 fitness is not a measure of compliance. Six of the twelve deviations are model or data artefacts (SUP-706, 707, 709, 710, 711, 713).
- Missing notifications after the migration cannot be charged as noncompliance.
- Charging all 312 pilot cases to the people who handled them, because PD-3 was never incorporated, fails. The decision was recorded, scoped, time-boxed and contemporaneously acknowledged by Compliance. The point survives only as a governance finding.
- SUP-709 and SUP-710 are not proven violations. CS-9 does not say what the prosecution needs it to say.
- "Enforce M0" as the remedy is wrong. Enforcing M0 literally would forbid information requests and force quality reviews on sanctioned leads, while still passing SUP-712.

**Against the defence:**

- Obsolescence does not excuse SUP-703, 708 or 712. No instrument and no plausible revised model permits them.
- A continuing emergency does not extend an expired directive (SUP-705). Expected approval does not authorise SUP-714.
- "No transactions occurred" (SUP-708) and "low risk" (SUP-714) go to severity, not to whether a breach occurred.
- "Mostly obsolescence" is true by count and false by materiality (D8). The minority of real violations includes the most serious cases in the file.
- The desirability of Request_More_Info does not generalise. It is accepted because it breaches no CS-9 rule, not because stakeholders like it. Permanent auto-approval does breach CS-9 and needs an amendment.
- The 92.6% eligibility figure shows entry criteria were mostly met. It does not show PD-3's conditions were met (U1, U5), and a 4.5–7.4% ineligible or unverifiable rate in a monitored pilot is itself a finding.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The principle is to stop asking one control-flow model to carry authority, time, money and identity. Each rule goes to the layer that can express and test it.

### 6.1 Layers

| Layer | Content | Formalism | Why there |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Scope | Case type (new onboarding, pre-screen lead, reactivation), case boundaries, withdrawal | Case attributes plus a scope rule table | Decides which model applies before any replay |
| L1 Control flow | Routes, mandatory activities, concurrency, choice, repetition | POWL (M1, section 7) | Structural behaviour is what POWL expresses well |
| L2 Invariants | Rules that hold on every route and must survive model edits | DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints | Route-independent, individually diagnosable, cheap to check |
| L3 Data rules | Eligibility, dates, amounts, deadlines, evidence grading | pandas checks over events and reference tables | POWL has no data perspective |
| L4 Resource rules | Segregation of duties, conflict of interest, delegation | pandas checks over a resolved identity table | Needs person-level identity, not labels |
| L5 Overlay governance | Who may derogate, for how long, on what conditions | Overlay registry plus organisational controls | Authority is not a property of a trace |
| L6 Preventive controls | System gates, identity management, change control | Organisational and technical controls | Detective checks cannot stop a SUP-708 |

### 6.2 Revised control-flow model (L1, POWL)

- **Standard route:** Register_Supplier, then Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review and zero or more Request_More_Info concurrently, then manual approval and activation, or rejection, or withdrawal, then notification.
- **Early exit:** rejection after a failed sanctions pre-screen without Quality_Review, or withdrawal before checks.
- **Auto-approval route (overlay-gated):** sanctions and master-data validation before Auto_Approve and activation; Quality_Review unordered relative to activation; confirmation or revocation afterwards.
- **Emergency route (overlay-gated):** sanctions before activation; Quality_Review and manual approval may follow; confirmation or revocation afterwards.
- **Reactivation route (draft):** Reactivation_Request, fresh sanctions check, Quality_Review or Verify_Quality_Certificate, manual approval.
- Notification is inside every route and Close_Case follows all of them.

A route's presence in M1 is not authorisation. The model applied to a case is built from the overlays in force for that case.

### 6.3 Invariants (L2, DECLARE or log skeleton)

- Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier) on every route, with a clear outcome. No overlay may waive it.
- Responded existence(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review or Verify_Quality_Certificate).
- Responded existence(Activate_Supplier, Manual_Procurement_Approval or Auto_Approve), and precedence of that approval before activation unless an emergency overlay is recorded on the case.
- Not co-existence(Reject_Supplier, Activate_Supplier). Not succession(Withdraw_Application, Activate_Supplier).
- Exactly one of Register_Supplier or Reactivation_Request; at most one Activate_Supplier; exactly one Close_Case, as the last event.
- Response(Activate_Supplier / Reject_Supplier / Withdraw_Application / Revoke_Activation, Notify_Requester) and precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case).
- Alternate response(Request_More_Info, Info_Received), with no Activate_Supplier while a request is open.

These duplicate part of M1 on purpose. Alignments can explain SUP-703 as a rejection or a withdrawal. A violated precedence constraint cannot be explained away.

### 6.4 Data-aware eligibility, amount and time rules (L3, pandas)

- **Overlay validity:** the activation's effective timestamp lies within the overlay window, in the overlay's stated time zone.
- **Eligibility:** evaluated against a snapshot frozen at decision time, computed by the system, never asserted by an operator.
- **Deadlines:** Quality_Review, and retrospective approval where applicable, within two business days of provisional activation, using a maintained business calendar.
- **Amounts:** cumulative spend from provisional activation to retrospective approval stays within the overlay cap.
- **Loop bound:** number and duration of Request_More_Info cycles within a set limit, with escalation beyond it.
- **Pre-screen:** early rejection without Quality_Review is allowed only when the recorded pre-screen outcome is a failure.
- **Evidence grading for notification:** grade A is a CRM record with an immutable ID created at send time; grade B is a mirrored log event; grade C is a mutable or late link. Only A and B satisfy the rule. C is indeterminate.
- **Repairs before replay:** de-duplicate on source system and transaction ID; canonicalise labels with an effective-dated mapping, flagging any mapping approved after the event.

### 6.5 Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (L4)

- Requester and manual approver must resolve to different persons, using an alias table valid at event time (SUP-712).
- On the auto route there is no human approver, so the four-eyes rule is empty. Replace it with a rule that the requester may not set eligibility attributes or select the route (SUP-703).
- A delegated approval needs a delegation record valid at the time, and the delegate must not be the requester.
- Related persons (family or same reporting line) performing two control roles on one case need a disclosure recorded before the second action and an independent second approver (SUP-715). The second approver is a proposal for the policy owner. Only disclosure is currently required.
- Whether quality reviewer and approver must always differ is not stated in CS-9. I flag it for the owner and do not assume it.

### 6.6 Policy-overlay governance (L5, organisational)

1. An overlay exists only as a row in the registry. No row means no route.
2. It must be issued or countersigned by the owner of every clause it derogates. Observer remarks and meeting minutes do not qualify.
3. Mandatory content: scope predicate, effective window with time zone, the timestamp that anchors applicability, clauses derogated, clauses that may never be waived, compensating conditions with deadlines, monitoring owner and exit criteria.
4. Routes are enabled by a rule engine from the registry and disabled automatically at expiry (SUP-714, A4).
5. Extensions must be written and recorded before expiry. For emergencies, a named duty officer may extend for a short fixed period, with ratification on the next business day (SUP-705).
6. Pilots report ineligible uses daily and stop automatically above a threshold (the 14 cases).
7. A pilot ends in a formal amendment or in switch-off. Operational habit is not a third outcome.
8. Retrospective ratification is recorded as a ratified nonconformity, with the original classification preserved.

### 6.7 Scope rules (L0)

- **Rejected leads.** A case starts when a lead is registered in the onboarding system. A lead that fails pre-screen ends through the early-exit route and never needs Quality_Review. This follows the process owner's reading and makes the dispute harmless, but the CS-9 owner must confirm it. A rejected lead may be reconsidered only as a new case.
- **Reactivations.** A reactivation is a request to activate a supplier previously activated and closed. It always needs a fresh sanctions check and manual approval. Quality may rest on an existing certificate only if it is valid on the activation date, covers the same scope, and the inactivity period is below a limit the CS-9 owner must set. Overlay routes are not available. Until CS-9 is amended this is an interim rule.
- **Withdrawals.** The requester or supplier may withdraw before the decision. No activation may follow. A withdrawal after provisional activation requires Revoke_Activation.
- **Out of scope.** Master-data changes to active suppliers and CRM-only correspondence are not onboarding cases.

### 6.8 Preventive controls (L6)

- Activation is technically blocked without a clear sanctions result on the same case.
- Label changes go through change control before the UI changes (SUP-713).
- Identity is managed at person level, with alias creation logged.
- Event stores are append-only, with separate effective and record timestamps.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The block below runs under PM4Py 2.7.x and reads no external files. It builds M1, a baseline variant without overlay routes, and an optional self-check that replays the fifteen observed traces on the variant the overlay registry authorises for each case.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def act(label):
    # POWL is a tree: every leaf object may have exactly one parent, so each
    # occurrence of a label is a fresh Transition (duplicate labels are legal).
    return Transition(label=label)


def tau():
    return SilentTransition()


def po(nodes, edges=()):
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for source, target in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(source, target)
    model.order.add_transitive_edges()          # keep the relation a strict partial order
    assert model.order.is_strict_partial_order()
    return model


def xor(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))


def info_loop():
    # do = tau, redo = Request_More_Info  ->  zero or more requests.
    # Approximation: POWL cannot bound the repetitions or pair each request with the
    # receipt of the evidence; both are enforced in the declarative/data layer.
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[tau(), act("Request_More_Info")])


def approve_then_activate():
    manual, activate = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), act("Activate_Supplier")
    return po([manual, activate], [(manual, activate)])


def provisional_outcome():
    # tau = provisional activation confirmed; otherwise revoke and notify again.
    revoke, renotify = act("Revoke_Activation"), act("Notify_Requester")
    return xor(tau(), po([revoke, renotify], [(revoke, renotify)]))


def standard_route():
    # CS-9 default: Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review (|| optional info requests),
    # then manual approval -> activation, or rejection, or withdrawal; then notify.
    sanctions, quality, more_info = act("Sanctions_Check"), act("Quality_Review"), info_loop()
    checks = po([sanctions, quality, more_info])                    # no edges = concurrent
    decision = xor(approve_then_activate(), act("Reject_Supplier"), act("Withdraw_Application"))
    notify = act("Notify_Requester")
    return po([checks, decision, notify], [(checks, decision), (decision, notify)])


def auto_route():
    # OVERLAY-GATED (e.g. PIL-PD3). Structural presence is NOT authorisation.
    # Sanctions and master-data validation precede Auto_Approve; Quality_Review is
    # unordered w.r.t. activation (may be before, or after within the overlay deadline).
    sanctions, mdv = act("Sanctions_Check"), act("Master_Data_Validation")
    auto, activate = act("Auto_Approve"), act("Activate_Supplier")
    quality, notify, outcome = act("Quality_Review"), act("Notify_Requester"), provisional_outcome()
    return po(
        [sanctions, mdv, auto, activate, quality, notify, outcome],
        [(sanctions, auto), (mdv, auto), (auto, activate), (activate, notify),
         (quality, outcome), (notify, outcome)],
    )


def emergency_route():
    # OVERLAY-GATED (e.g. EMG-FD7). Sanctions is never waived. Quality review and
    # manual approval may be retrospective; approval needs the quality result.
    sanctions, activate = act("Sanctions_Check"), act("Activate_Supplier")
    quality, manual = act("Quality_Review"), act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    notify, outcome = act("Notify_Requester"), provisional_outcome()
    return po(
        [sanctions, activate, quality, manual, notify, outcome],
        [(sanctions, activate), (activate, manual), (quality, manual),
         (activate, notify), (manual, outcome), (notify, outcome)],
    )


def early_exit_route():
    # Pre-screen exit: rejection on a failed sanctions pre-screen without Quality_Review
    # (CS-9 "abbreviated pre-screen"), or withdrawal before any check.
    sanctions, reject = act("Sanctions_Check"), act("Reject_Supplier")
    exit_choice = xor(po([sanctions, reject], [(sanctions, reject)]), act("Withdraw_Application"))
    notify = act("Notify_Requester")
    return po([exit_choice, notify], [(exit_choice, notify)])


def new_supplier_case(overlay_routes):
    register = act("Register_Supplier")
    routes = [standard_route(), early_exit_route()]
    if "auto" in overlay_routes:
        routes.append(auto_route())
    if "emergency" in overlay_routes:
        routes.append(emergency_route())
    choice = xor(*routes)
    return po([register, choice], [(register, choice)])


def reactivation_case():
    # DRAFT route (CS-9 is silent): fresh sanctions check, quality assurance either by a
    # new review or by recorded verification of a still-valid certificate, and always
    # manual approval. No auto or emergency reactivation until a policy says so.
    request, sanctions = act("Reactivation_Request"), act("Sanctions_Check")
    quality = xor(act("Quality_Review"), act("Verify_Quality_Certificate"))
    more_info = info_loop()
    decision = xor(approve_then_activate(), act("Reject_Supplier"), act("Withdraw_Application"))
    notify = act("Notify_Requester")
    return po(
        [request, sanctions, quality, more_info, decision, notify],
        [(request, sanctions), (request, quality), (request, more_info),
         (sanctions, decision), (quality, decision), (more_info, decision), (decision, notify)],
    )


def build_m1(overlay_routes=("auto", "emergency")):
    # overlay_routes = routes switched on by a registered overlay in force for the case.
    # build_m1(()) is the CS-9 baseline; the default is the full structural superset.
    cases = xor(new_supplier_case(overlay_routes), reactivation_case())
    close = act("Close_Case")
    return po([cases, close], [(cases, close)])


M1 = build_m1()            # structural superset of all routes
M1_BASE = build_m1(())     # what applies when no overlay is in force

# Miniature of the policy-overlay registry (full normalized table follows the code).
OVERLAYS = [
    {"overlay_id": "PIL-PD3", "route": "auto", "from": "2026-06-01", "to": "2026-07-31",
     "eligible": lambda c: c.get("risk") == "LOW" and c.get("country") == "DE" and c.get("approved_group") is True},
    {"overlay_id": "EMG-FD7", "route": "emergency", "from": "2026-07-10", "to": "2026-07-12",
     "eligible": lambda c: c.get("critical_relief") is True},
]


def routes_in_force(case_data):
    # Authority test: overlay in force on the activation date AND eligibility snapshot satisfied.
    day = case_data.get("activated", "")
    return tuple(o["route"] for o in OVERLAYS if o["from"] <= day <= o["to"] and o["eligible"](case_data))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Optional self-check on in-memory data only (no external files): each observed trace
    # is replayed on the model variant that the overlay registry authorises for that case.
    import pandas as pd
    import pm4py

    R, S, Q, M, A = ("Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Quality_Review",
                     "Manual_Procurement_Approval", "Activate_Supplier")
    AA, N, C, RMI, REJ = "Auto_Approve", "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case", "Request_More_Info", "Reject_Supplier"
    traces = {
        "SUP-701": [R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-702 raw": [R, S, AA, A, Q, C],
        "SUP-702 + CRM notification": [R, S, AA, A, N, Q, C],
        "SUP-703": [R, S, AA, A, Q, N, C],
        "SUP-704": [R, S, A, Q, M, N, C],
        "SUP-705": [R, S, A, Q, M, N, C],
        "SUP-706 raw": [R, S, Q, M, A, C],
        "SUP-707": [R, S, RMI, RMI, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-708": [R, Q, A, S, M, N, C],
        "SUP-709": [R, S, REJ, N, C],
        "SUP-710": [S, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-711 raw": [R, R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-711 deduplicated": [R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-712": [R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-713 raw": [R, S, "QA_Clearance", M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-713 label-mapped": [R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
        "SUP-714": [R, S, AA, A, Q, N, C],
        "SUP-715": [R, S, Q, M, A, N, C],
    }
    case_data = {
        "SUP-702": {"activated": "2026-06-15", "risk": "LOW", "country": "DE", "approved_group": True},
        "SUP-703": {"activated": "2026-06-20", "risk": "HIGH", "country": "non-DE", "approved_group": False},
        "SUP-704": {"activated": "2026-07-11", "critical_relief": True},
        "SUP-705": {"activated": "2026-07-13", "critical_relief": True},
        "SUP-714": {"activated": "2026-08-02", "risk": "LOW", "country": "DE", "approved_group": True},
    }
    for name, trace in traces.items():
        routes = routes_in_force(case_data.get(name.split()[0], {}))
        net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(build_m1(routes))
        frame = pd.DataFrame({
            "case:concept:name": name,
            "concept:name": trace,
            "time:timestamp": [pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01", tz="UTC") + pd.Timedelta(minutes=i) for i in range(len(trace))],
        })
        alignment = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(frame, net, initial_marking, final_marking)[0]["alignment"]
        # keep log-only moves (x, '>>') and visible model-only moves ('>>', label)
        deviations = [m for m in alignment if (m[0] == ">>") != (m[1] == ">>") and m[1] is not None]
        print(f"{name:28s} overlay routes={routes or '-'}  control-flow deviations={deviations or 'none'}")
```

### 7.2 Limitations and approximations

- **Authorisation is outside POWL.** The superset M1 admits the emergency route for any case. Only the registry-driven variant, or the data rules, separates SUP-704 from SUP-705. Putting exception routes into one permanent model without that gate would legitimise them.
- **Master_Data_Validation is mandatory in the auto route.** Historic pilot traces therefore show it as missing. I did not hide this behind a silent skip, because it is a real evidence gap in a PD-3 condition (U1). If the validation is embedded in Auto_Approve, the fix is to emit the event.
- **The information loop is unbounded and unpaired.** POWL cannot count repetitions or tie a request to its answer. Both are handled by the constraint and data layers.
- **Withdrawal is approximated.** POWL has no cancellation. Withdrawal is allowed before checks and at the decision point. A withdrawal part-way through checks will show as a deviation and should be dispositioned by rule.
- **Activate_Supplier is one label for full and provisional activation,** to stay compatible with the existing log. The mode is carried as an attribute. A distinct label would be cleaner later.
- **Duplicate labels across routes** mean an alignment may pick the cheapest route, not the true one. The route must be confirmed from the recorded overlay ID.
- **A failed auto-validation falling back to the standard route** is not modelled. The validation event would appear as an extra log move.
- **The reactivation route is a draft** and has no normative force until the CS-9 owner adopts it.
- **Dates, amounts, risk, identity and authority** are not expressible in POWL and are handled in the tables below.

### 7.4 Event and data-collection proposal

**Event table (append-only), one row per event:**

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| event_id, case_id, case_type | Identity and scope (new, pre-screen lead, reactivation) |
| activity, source_label, label_map_version | Canonical label plus the label the system emitted (SUP-713) |
| effective_ts, record_ts, time zone | When it happened versus when it was written; both immutable (SUP-708, overlay windows) |
| source_system, source_txn_id | De-duplication key (SUP-711) |
| actor_login, actor_person_id, role | Person-level identity (SUP-712) |
| delegation_id | Evidence of acting on another's behalf |
| rule_version_id, overlay_id | Which version of CS-9 and which overlay the actor relied on |
| eligibility_snapshot_id | Hash-referenced snapshot used for the decision |
| outcome | Pass, fail, hit, approved, denied |
| activation_mode | Full or provisional |
| notification_id | Immutable ID minted by the CRM at send time |
| related_event_id | Links a Request_More_Info to its Info_Received |

**Reference tables:**

- **Overlay registry:** the table in 7.3, versioned, with authority evidence.
- **Eligibility snapshot:** risk class, country, corporate-group status, data source and computation time; frozen, never overwritten.
- **Identity alias map:** login to person, with validity dates and creation audit.
- **Delegation register:** delegator, delegate, scope, validity window, approving manager.
- **Conflict-of-interest register:** disclosed relationships, disclosure time, reviewer.
- **Notification ledger:** notification ID, case ID set at send time, recipient, channel, send time, content hash. Mutable free-text links are retired. Legacy links are graded C.
- **Label map:** source label, canonical label, effective-from, approval reference and approval time.
- **Spend ledger:** supplier, amount, time, for cap checks.
- **Business calendar:** for deadline computation.
- **Certificate register:** quality certificate scope and validity, for reactivations.
