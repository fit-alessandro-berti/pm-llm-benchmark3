## 1. Interaction-graph description

The object-centric graph is a typed graph whose nodes are not only jobs and machines but also components, component lots, batches, tools, and shipments. The important node types are:

- **Job**: J1–J6.
- **Component**: C1–C6.
- **ComponentLot**: L7 (C2,C5), L8 (C1,C3).
- **Batch**: B1 (J1,J2,J3), B2 (J4,J5).
- **Machine**: CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1.
- **Tool**: FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.
- **Shipment**: S1 (J1,J4), S2 (J2,J3), S3 (J5,J6).

Important edge types and qualifiers include:

- `Job --requires--> Component`
- `ComponentLot --contains--> Component`
- `Batch --batches--> Job`
- `Shipment --consolidates--> Job`
- `Batch --on--> Machine`, `Batch --using--> Tool`
- `Job --processed_on--> Machine`, `Job --uses/acquires/releases--> Tool`
- `ComponentLot --quarantine/release--> Job` or `Component`, with inconsistent linkage.

Compact edge list:

```text
J1 requires C1; J2 requires C2; J3 requires C3; J4 requires C4; J5 requires C5; J6 requires C6.
L7 contains C2, C5. L8 contains C1, C3.
B1 batches J1,J2,J3. B2 batches J4,J5.
S1 consolidates J1,J4. S2 consolidates J2,J3. S3 consolidates J5,J6.
B1 on OVEN-1 using RACK-7. B2 on OVEN-1 using RACK-7.
J3 acquires/releases FIX-A; J4 Machine Process relates to CNC-2 and FIX-A.
J5/J6 Final Test on TEST-1 requires CAL-3.
Quarantine/Release L7 relates to C2,C5,J2,J5.
```

Higher-order motifs involving more than two object types:

1. **B1 synchronization motif**: B1 batches J1,J2,J3; B1 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7; J1/J2/J3 require C1/C2/C3; L7 and L8 contain those components. This couples jobs, components, component lots, batch, machine, and tool.
2. **FIX-A contention motif**: J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts Machine Process at 11:45 with FIX-A and CNC-2; J4 belongs to B2. This couples job, tool, machine, component C4, and batch.
3. **CAL-3 calibration motif**: J5 and J6 are ready for final test at 16:00 and 16:05; TEST-1 requires CAL-3; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 consolidates J5,J6. This couples job, machine, tool, and shipment.

5. **S1 consolidation motif**: S1 consolidates J1 and J4; J1 is in B1 and packed 13:30; J4 is in B2 and packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25. This couples shipment, job, and batch.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All calculations are counterfactual decompositions, not causal proofs.

1. **J1 in B1 synchronization.** J1 ready 08:10, B1 starts 12:05. Waiting = 12:05  08:10 = **3 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: if B1 did not wait for J3 ready at 11:55, or if J1 could cure separately, J1’s wait would be much smaller. If B1 started when J1 and J2 were ready, J1 would still wait 08:25  08:10 = 15 min.

2. **J2 in B1 synchronization.** J2 ready 08:25, B1 starts 12:05. Waiting = 12:05  08:25 = **3 h 40 min**. Counterfactual: if B1 could start at max(J1,J2) readiness, J2 wait = 0. The large residual is tied to J3 readiness and batch policy.

3. **FIX-A for J4.** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; FIX-A is held by J3 until 11:40; J4 Machine Process starts 11:45. Strict delay relative to J4 arrival = 11:45  09:50 = **1 h 55 min**. Conservative delay, if CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 is unavoidable, is 11:45  10:47 = **58 min**. Counterfactual: FIX-A available at 09:50 or not held by J3 until 11:40.

4. **J1 from S1 shipment consolidation.** J1 packed 13:30; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25. J1 waits for its consolidation partner = 19:10  13:30 = **5 h 40 min**. Pack-to-dispatch for J1 = 19:25  13:30 = **5 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: J1 dispatched separately, or S1 not forced to wait for J4.

5. **CAL-3 for J6.** J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50. TEST-1 is otherwise idle 16:35–17:25. Delay after TEST-1 availability = 17:25  16:35 = **50 min**. If J5 occupancy is ignored and J6 could test at readiness, delay = 17:25  16:05 = **1 h 20 min**. Counterfactual: CAL-3 ready at 16:35 or recalibration done earlier.

6. **L7 quarantine coupling.** Quarantine 10:20 to Release 13:50 = **3 h 30 min**. J2 had a supervisor override into B1 before release; J5 did not proceed until after release. J5 is ready for B2 only at 14:40, so L7 release is not the direct cause of B2 starting at 14:50, but it is a plausible earlier coupling. Counterfactual: L7 not quarantined or C2/C5 released separately.

7. **B2 synchronization.** B2 starts 14:50. J4 ready 13:10  wait = **1 h 40 min**. J5 ready 14:40  wait = **10 min**. If B2 did not wait for J5, J4 could start after RACK-7 is available at 14:35, so J4’s RACK-related wait would be 14:35  13:10 = **1 h 25 min**. Counterfactual: dynamic batching or alternate rack.

8. **OVEN-1/RACK-7 in B2.** OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 = **1 h 55 min**. RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine ends 14:35, but J5 is not ready until 14:40, and B2 starts 14:50. Thus RACK-7 is not the sole visible blocker; after J5 and RACK are ready, there is still **10 min** before B2 starts.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job viewpoint.** A Job-flattened DFG makes visible Release Job  Stage Component  Machine Process  Inspect  Pack  Dispatch, plus rework. It hides that J1’s 08:10 readiness is followed by a 12:05 Batch Cure, and that J1 is packed at 13:30 but not dispatched until 19:25. Misleading conclusion: “No individual job has total duration more than 1.8 SD above mean,” even though J1 accumulates large waits in B1 and S1.

**Machine viewpoint.** Machine flattening shows CNC-2 54%, OVEN-1 44%, TEST-1 58%, with gaps. It hides why CNC-2 is idle while J4 waits for FIX-A, and why TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25 while J6 waits for CAL-3. Misleading conclusion: no machine bottleneck because utilization is moderate.

**Batch viewpoint.** Batch flattening shows B1 Batch Cure at 12:05 and B2 at 14:50. It hides individual readiness: J1 08:10, J2 08:25, J3 11:55. Misleading conclusion: the batch event is efficient, when it actually synchronizes J1/J2 behind J3.

**Tool viewpoint.** Tool flattening shows FIX-A 09:00–11:40, RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, CAL-3 recal 16:35–17:20. It hides queueing because there is no `Wait for Tool` activity. Misleading conclusion: tool utilization is low, so tools are not a problem.

**ComponentLot viewpoint.** Lot flattening would show L7 quarantine 10:20–13:50 and its C2/C5 membership. The Job DFG omits it. Misleading conclusion: J2 and J5 are independent jobs, when L7 and the supervisor override couple them.

**Shipment viewpoint.** Shipment flattening shows Create Shipment  Load  Dispatch in 0.8 h and 0.3 h. It hides J1 packed at 13:30 waiting until S1 dispatch 19:25. Misleading conclusion: shipment is fast, when consolidation imposes 5 h 40 min on J1.

Low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting because machines are only one resource in the object graph. Jobs can be ready but infeasible because their batch partner is not ready, FIX-A or CAL-3 is held, a lot is quarantined, or a shipment partner is late. The machine is idle while the job is not actually available to run.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. If J1 or J2 could not be processed before 12:05 because C1/C2 were unavailable, then their waiting is component shortage, not batch policy.
2. If OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable despite B1 starting at 12:05, then batch timing may be resource-forced.
3. If J3’s 11:55 readiness was fixed by an external customer due date, then waiting for J3 may be unavoidable policy.
4. If B1 had to start at 12:05 to avoid a later OVEN-1 conflict not shown, then synchronization is not the primary cause.
5. If J1’s 13:30–19:25 shipment wait was already on a non-critical path, then S1 consolidation may not affect the true delivery constraint.
6. If all jobs in B1 had identical priority and batching rules required full-batch cure, then the “avoidable” label is weaker.
7. If CNC-2 or TEST-1 was actually busy during the stated idle intervals, then machine capacity becomes more plausible.
8. If the L7 quarantine was optional and J5’s 14:40 readiness was independent of it, then component-lot coupling is less important.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool` and `Wait for Batch` events with start/end timestamps and resource type.
2. Reliable `Job–ComponentLot` links for quarantine, release, and override events.
3. Batch readiness timestamps for each job, including whether readiness can be preempted.
4. Tool acquire/release queues with requested timestamp, granted timestamp, and holder.
5. Shipment packed-to-dispatch timestamps per job and consolidation cut-off policy.
6. Machine setup/changeover times separate from process time and idle time.
7. Resource calendars, shifts, maintenance, cleaning, and quarantine schedules.
8. Operator override reason codes and authorization timestamps.
9. Job priority, due date, dispatch policy, and expedite flags.
10. Component inventory availability and inspection release status by component.
11. Rework cause and whether rework consumes the same tool/machine.
12. Quality calibration intervals and whether CAL-3 recalibration is mandatory after every Final Test.
