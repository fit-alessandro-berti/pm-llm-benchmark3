## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Core chain (the process as a dependency graph):**

Register (A1)  Classify (A2)  {KYC (A3, A4 downstream)  Financial review (A5, A6)}  Credit analysis  {Collateral (A7)}  Committee packaging (A8)  Committee decision  Contract prep (A9, A12 loops)  Customer signing (A10 gate)  Activation (A11)

The main dependencies:

**a. Upstream data quality**
- **Classification (A2)** determines which control path a case follows. Misroutes (8%) mean a case may enter the wrong KYC or review path, and every downstream control then depends on a wrong premise. A2 therefore has to be reliable before any module that assumes the path is correct (A3, A9, A11).
- **Financial document review (A5, A6)**: missing documents drive a 52% repeat rate. Normalization (A6) on incomplete or poor-quality inputs produces low-confidence extractions that flow into credit analysis. A5 should precede or accompany A6 so that extraction is not asked to recover missing content.
- **Committee packaging (A8)** consumes four artifacts: KYC status, normalized statements, collateral valuation (if required), and credit notes. If any of these is inconsistent, auto-assembly only produces a polished inconsistency.
- **Contract generation (A9)** requires that the committee decision and KYC state are final. Automating it before those states are reliable would encode the 8.5% (14% for credit-line renewal) remediation loop into code.

**b. Legal and compliance controls**
- **KYC must precede signing.** Provisional signing (6.5%) must be prevented by a system gate, not by policy alone. This gate is a precondition for A10 and A11. Even without A10, the existing signing workflow should be configured with a hard stop.
- **Four-eyes principle** (credit analysis and committee packaging by different people in High-exposure SME). Violations occur in 5.8% of High-exposure cases at month-end. Any module that changes task assignment (A6 freeing analyst time, A8 automating packaging, A2 routing) must carry explicit separation-of-duties rules.
- **Audit trail.** Every module must record model suggestion, human override, and final decision. A4 and A3 require this most strongly because they touch KYC decisions. A12 is effectively the audit tool for the rest of the portfolio.
- **Automation does not transfer compliance responsibility.** Compliance remains accountable for KYC outcomes and control design, whichever modules are chosen.

**c. Human escalation capacity**
- **Compliance specialists (3 FTE)**, with KYC ops dependent on two senior specialists. A4 and A3 both route exceptions here. If A3 is deployed before escalation capacity is planned, false-negative review and escalation volume will land on the same two people. This is the strongest reason to sequence A4 before A3.
- **Credit analysts (9 FTE)** are central in the working-together network. A6 increases clean inflow; without a work-in-progress cap, the analysts become the next constraint. This is why A6 should be piloted, not scaled, until the inflow is shaped by A5.
- **Legal Ops (4 FTE)** is underutilized when KYC is slow and overloaded at month-end. Faster KYC or mismatch resolution will concentrate contract volume unless it is levelled; A9 needs capacity smoothing.
- **Back office (12 FTE)** falls below 65% utilization after 15:00. This is slack that can absorb exception queues, but only if scheduling is changed. The "saving" from A1 is largely a peak-time relief, not a headcount reduction.

**d. External valuation dependency**
- The 58 h wait for external valuation is outside direct control. Only triage and parallelization are within reach: requesting valuation earlier (at the start of credit analysis for Equipment finance, where 71% need it) and routing by complexity. A7 cannot remove the external wait, so its benefit is bounded.
- Packaging receives 1,980 cases from valuation and 2,700 directly from credit analysis, so the synchronization point affects a subset of cases. Packaging should not block on valuation when valuation is not required, and the process should make that explicit.

**e. Core-banking integration constraints**
- IT can deeply integrate two modules in the first six months. Choose modules that touch core KYC and registration data (A2, A4). Modules that work at the portal or document-pipeline layer (A5, A6 pilot) can run with light integration and be deepened later.
- A3 and A10 depend on KYC and signing states in core systems; deep integration of either should wait until the first two are stable.

**f. Data-consistency issues to resolve before modeling**
- Packaging inflows (1,980 from valuation + 2,700 from credit analysis = 4,680) exceed recorded packaging frequency (3,900).
- Contract-prep outflows (650 + 7,300 = 7,950) exceed recorded frequency (7,600).
- Mismatch returns to KYC (1,160) and the other mismatch outflows total 1,540 of 1,750, so about 210 cases are unexplained.
- These usually indicate repeated activity instances (loops) or duplicated events in the log. They must be cleaned before cycle-time or bottleneck estimates are trusted.
- **Concurrency:** KYC and financial review run concurrently in practice but are recorded sequentially. Sequential timestamps can inflate end-to-end time and misattribute waiting to the second-recorded activity. The 18 h financial-review wait and the 4 h KYC wait should be re-measured with interval-based (concurrency-aware) timestamps before final priorities are locked.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each portfolio contains up to four modules.

### Portfolio Q: Quick-win (labor and frequency)
**Modules:** A1 (auto-registration and duplicate detection), A2 (classification recommender), A5 (missing-document nudging), A9 (contract generation, **check mode only**)

- **Optimizes:** visible throughput at high-frequency steps (registration, classification, document requests, contract drafting). Produces early labor savings and a politically visible result.
- **Sacrifices:** the critical path. It leaves the mismatch loop (31 h), the valuation wait (58 h), and the signing reminder loop (44 h) untouched. A9 depends on clean decision data that does not yet exist, so in check mode it mostly flags problems that people must still fix. It also accelerates arrival into queues that are already the constraint, so the work-in-progress before KYC and review grows.

### Portfolio B: Bottleneck and synchronization
**Modules:** A4 (mismatch assistant), A3 (KYC hit pre-classifier, human escalation), A6 (normalization), A7 (valuation triage and routing)

- **Optimizes:** the largest waits and loops: the mismatch critical path, KYC hit resolution, the analyst-facing normalization step, and the valuation synchronization point.
- **Sacrifices:** low direct labor savings, and it adds risk on the highest-stakes control (A3) in year one. A7's benefit is limited by the external valuer. It also puts the most load on the two senior compliance specialists and the analyst pool without first shaping inflow (no A5), so the bottleneck may move to analysts or to month-end.

### Portfolio C: Compliance-first
**Modules:** A2 (classification), A3 (KYC pre-classifier), A4 (mismatch assistant), A12 (control remediation workbench)

- **Optimizes:** control integrity: correct routing, KYC decisions with audit trails, mismatch decisions with audit trails, and visibility of contract and signing remediation.
- **Sacrifices:** almost all direct labor relief, and it does nothing for analyst capacity or the valuation wait. Customer-side friction is unchanged, and the signing gate is not built. A12 is useful for control evidence but adds no throughput. This portfolio is defensible for regulators but weak as an operating improvement.

### Portfolio D (recommended): Sequenced bottleneck-plus-control foundation
**Modules:** A2 (classification), A4 (mismatch assistant), A5 (missing-document nudging), A6 (normalization, piloted then scaled)

- **Optimizes:** the critical-path and loop structure in order: correct routing first (A2), then the largest single wait (A4), then the repeat-request loop that feeds analysts (A5), then analyst throughput (A6) once inflow is cleaner. Two of the four modules are control-strengthening.
- **Sacrifices:** no KYC hit automation in year one (A3 is deferred to year two), no valuation module (handled by policy change), no direct contract or signing automation, and a slower headline labor saving. It also demands the most governance discipline, because A6 introduces analyst-facing extraction risk.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Where it arises | Compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Auditability** | Any module that suggests and a human decides; A3 and A4 most of all | Immutable logs of suggestion, confidence, override, reason code, and final decision; periodic audit sampling; the log is a required output of every module, not a feature |
| **False negatives (A3, A4, A2)** | KYC hit auto-clearance; missed mismatch; misrouting to lighter path | Asymmetric thresholds that favour escalation; **no auto-clearance** in year one; 100% human review of High-exposure outcomes; retrospective QA sample of auto-handled cases each month; misroute rate tracked against the 8% baseline |
| **False positives** | Over-flagging creates extra work on 3 compliance FTE | Monitor escalation rate and time per escalation; set a capacity ceiling so the queue cannot exceed specialist capacity; tune rules on a labeled set |
| **Model drift** | Changing customer mix, document formats, product rules | Monthly drift monitoring on input distributions and error rates; champion–challenger for A2 and A6; revalidation trigger when error rate exceeds gate threshold; documented owner for each model |
| **Exception-handling overload** | Escalation queues created by A4, A3, A6 extraction errors | Explicit exception SLAs; queue-level capacity planning; use back-office off-peak slack (after 15:00) for exception triage; escalation paths to a named senior reviewer |
| **Four-eyes compliance** | Automation changes task allocation; month-end overload | System-enforced separation of duties: a case cannot be assigned to the same user for credit analysis and packaging; monthly monitoring of violations (baseline 5.8% in High-exposure cases); month-end capacity plan; SoD tests before go-live of any module that changes assignment |
| **Provisional signing** | Signing before KYC clearance (6.5%) | Hard system gate on KYC clearance before signing is possible; alerts on any signing without KYC flag; remediation log; this is a precondition for A10 and A11 |
| **Customer and RM friction** | A5 nudging, A10 reminders | Checklist rather than rigid rules; frequency caps on nudges; RM-facing instruction templates; track RM-triggered incomplete instructions and customer abandonment as guardrail metrics |
| **Garbage-in / garbage-out** | A6 extraction; A8 packaging | Extraction confidence thresholds; mandatory human validation on low-confidence fields; reconciliation checks before any artifact reaches committee |
| **Legal rule currency (A9)** | Contract generation using outdated terms | Versioned rule library with named owner; legal sign-off per version; check mode before generation; monthly reconciliation to committee decisions |
| **Data inconsistency in the log** | Count mismatches in DFG (packaging, contract prep); concurrency misrecorded | Clean the log before gates are set; concurrency-aware timestamps for KYC and FDR; re-baseline KPIs after cleaning |
| **Vendor/external dependency** | Valuer delays and quality disputes (A7) | Valuer SLAs; early requests; tracking of late-rate (19%) as a gate metric for any future A7 module |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Recommended portfolio: **A2  A4  A5  A6**. Two modules (A2, A4) are deeply integrated with core banking in months 0–6; A5 runs at the portal layer; A6 pilots through the document pipeline and is integrated deeply after month 6.

### Phase 0: Foundation (months 0–2)
- Clean the event log: resolve the count inconsistencies (packaging, contract prep, unexplained mismatch outflows), and add interval-based timestamps for concurrent KYC and financial review.
- Establish baseline KPIs, including the 82% critical-path share for mismatch, the 8% misroute rate, the 52% repeat-request rate, and the 5.8% four-eyes violation rate.
- Configure the system gate so that signing cannot proceed without KYC clearance (no module needed; a configuration and control change).
- Process-only changes that cost nothing in the module budget: RM instruction templates; early valuation request for Equipment finance; packaging not blocked by valuation when it is not required.
- Define audit-log schema and separation-of-duties rules.

### Phase 1: Routing and mismatch (months 2–6)
- **A2 (deep integration), first.** Advisory mode with human confirmation for High-exposure cases; full routing once misroute rate and recall gate are met.
- **A4 (deep integration), second.** Assist mode for the mismatch queue; the specialist makes every decision; the assistant pre-fills evidence and flags likely matches.
- Both modules write to the same audit log.

### Phase 2: Repeat-request loop (months 4–8, portal layer)
- **A5**: document checklists by product and exposure tier; portal nudges with frequency caps; RM instruction templates.
- Begins in month 4 because it needs no core integration; it is measured against the baseline from Phase 0.

### Phase 3: Analyst throughput (months 6–12)
- **A6 pilot**: one product (Working-capital is the simpler starting point), with human validation on low-confidence fields. Analyst WIP cap at credit analysis.
- Scale to Equipment finance and credit-line renewal once extraction accuracy and analyst time gates are met.
- Deep integration to the credit model begins after month 6, once the two-deep integration limit no longer applies to the pilot.

### Monitoring metrics
- Mismatch: wait time, share of cases on the critical path, loops back to KYC (1,160 baseline).
- Routing: misroute rate (8% baseline); path recall for High-exposure cases.
- Document loop: repeat-request rate (52% baseline); FDRRequestFDR count (2,800 baseline).
- Analyst: normalization time per statement (75 min), normalization rework (16%), analyst WIP and queue age.
- Control: four-eyes violations (5.8% in High-exposure at month-end); provisional signing (6.5% baseline); audit-log completeness (target 100%).
- Customer/RM: incomplete-instruction rate, abandonment, nudge complaints.

### Gate criteria
- **Gate 1 (end of Phase 0):** log cleaned and reconciled; baseline KPIs agreed; signing gate configured; SoD rules specified.
- **Gate 2 (A2 to full routing):** misroute rate falls clearly below 8% with no drop in High-exposure recall; 100% audit-log completeness; no increase in KYC rework.
- **Gate 3 (A4 to autonomous pre-fill mode):** critical-path mismatch wait falls materially from 31 h; loops back to KYC fall; no increase in false matches in monthly QA; specialist load per case does not rise.
- **Gate 4 (A5 to deep integration):** repeat-request rate falls from 52%; RM complaint rate stable; no measurable abandonment increase.
- **Gate 5 (A6 scale-up):** extraction error rate below threshold on sampled documents; analyst rework on normalization falls; WIP queue stays within cap at month-end; four-eyes violations do not increase.

### Year-two sequence (contingent)
A3 (after A4 audit trail is proven and escalation capacity is planned), A7 (valuation triage), A10 with the signing gate (after KYC gate is verified), A8 and A9 (after upstream artifacts are reliable), A11 last. A1 is handled as hygiene before then.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio D: A2 (classification), A4 (mismatch assistant), A5 (missing-document nudging), A6 (normalization, piloted then scaled), with the explicit process-level sequence above.**

**Why this portfolio:**
- **It targets the constraints the event data actually shows.** The largest waits are mismatch (31 h), valuation (58 h), signing (44 h), financial review (18 h), and packaging (26 h). Mismatch sits on the critical path in 82% of its cases and blocks both KYC and signing. A4 is the single intervention that touches the most critical-path time.
- **It fixes inputs before outputs.** A2 corrects routing, which determines control path. A5 reduces the repeat-request loop that feeds analysts. Only then does A6 change analyst throughput. This respects the dependency structure rather than automating the most visible step first.
- **It satisfies the control requirement.** Two of four modules are control-strengthening, and every module writes to the audit log. A4 and A2 are compliance-visible wins, which the compliance mandate requires.
- **It respects capacity.** Routing and mismatch decisions stay with compliance and senior reviewers, and the two-deep integration limit is met in months 0–6. A6 is piloted so that analyst overload does not move the bottleneck. A5 and A6 use lighter integration until IT capacity frees up.

**Why not automate the highest-frequency activity first (A1 registration):**
- Registration is 11,880 cases, but its waiting time is 1.5 h, against 4 h for KYC, 18 h for financial review, 31 h for mismatch, and 58 h for valuation. Saving time at registration does not shorten the case if the case then waits for days downstream. Automating A1 first would accelerate arrival into queues that already limit throughput, growing work-in-progress before the constraints.
- The labor saving is also partly illusory in the short run: the back office is below 65% utilized after 15:00. The benefit is peak-time relief and redeployment, not headcount reduction.
- Registration is still worth automating, and duplicate detection should be added as hygiene. It belongs after the constraints are addressed, when faster arrival translates into faster completion.
- The same logic applies to A2, which is also high-frequency (11,820 cases). It is prioritized here because misrouting determines which control path a case follows. Its priority comes from its control role, not its frequency.

**Trade-offs accepted:**
- Year-one labor savings are smaller and less visible than a registration bot would give. Management should be told this explicitly, with the expected labor benefit from A5 and A6 back-loaded.
- KYC hit automation (A3) is deferred. This leaves the 4 h KYC wait partly unaddressed in year one. The mitigation is A4, which relieves specialist capacity, and the concurrency-aware re-measurement, which may show the KYC wait is smaller than the sequential log implies.
- Valuation (A7) is handled by policy change rather than automation. The external 58 h wait cannot be automated away, and the benefit depends on valuer cooperation.
- Contract generation (A9) and signing automation (A10) wait until the KYC and decision states are reliable. Without that, they would automate the 8.5%/14% contract loops and the provisional-signing exceptions.

**Conditions that would change the recommendation:**
- If the cleaned log shows that financial review, not mismatch, is the dominant critical-path wait once concurrency is correctly represented, A6 and A5 move ahead of A4.
- If compliance judges that A2 misroutes are rare or low-impact on control path, A2 could be deferred behind A4.
- If analyst capacity is already spare at month-end, A6 can be scaled faster.
