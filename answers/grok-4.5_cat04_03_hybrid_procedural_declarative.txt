# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

1. **Accepted vs rejected case.** A case is *rejected* if `Reject Vendor` occurs and *accepted* if `Activate Vendor` occurs. R1 forbids both in one case. Cases with neither are incomplete and fail the procedural backbone.
2. **Primary route choice is exclusive.** After `Classify Risk`, exactly one route applies: reject, low-risk (`Manager Approval`), or high-risk (`Enhanced Due Diligence` then `Compliance Approval`). R2 and R8 reinforce that the two approval types do not mix.
3. **Clarification is interruptive but not route-defining.** `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` pairs may repeat; they do not themselves choose reject vs accept. They must be answered before another request and before any terminal outcome (`Reject Vendor` or `Activate Vendor`).
4. **Sanctions escalation is optional and orthogonal.** `Sanctions Escalation` may occur only after screening (R7). It can appear on otherwise high-risk or low-risk paths; the backbone does not force a dedicated escalation branch.
5. **Post-activation maintenance is out-of-band relative to the happy path.** `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and a re-`Verify Bank Account` before a later payment are real obligations (R11) but are not part of the initial onboarding spine that ends at first `Close Onboarding`.
6. **Event log attributes.** Activities and timestamps are always present. Resources are present in the tests; an “emergency” flag and joint-authorization evidence are *not* in the log (R6).
7. **Temporal profile (R12) is diagnostic**, not a hard gate: deviations produce warnings, not automatic control-flow failure (`zeta = 2.0`).
8. **At-most-once** for `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, and `Release First Payment` (R10) is treated as a cardinality constraint, not as “exactly once in every case.”

## R5 / R6 conflict

| | Resolution A — Strict Legal (R5) | Resolution B — Operations emergency (R6) |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | If `Sanctions Escalation` occurred, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` in every logged case. | Activation before opinion is allowed; opinion must follow within 24 hours; joint authorization is required outside the log. |
| Log-checkable? | Yes (order of activities). | Partially (order + 24h bound). Authorization is **not** log-checkable. |
| Model effect | DECLARE/external: forbid `Activate` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` when escalation happened. | Control-flow allows early activate; resource/data layer checks t  24h and demands external evidence. |

**Further possible resolution C (hybrid governance):** default enforce R5 in automated conformance; allow R6 only when an external GRC ticket ID is joined to the case. Without that attribute, fall back to R5.

### Choice for the implemented model

**Resolution A (Strict Legal / R5)** is authoritative inside the activity-only and DECLARE layers. R6 is implemented only as an *optional diagnostic* in the resource/data layer that labels a trace `policy-dependent` when activation precedes opinion after escalation, and additionally checks the 24h bound—but it does **not** override R5 in the overall verdict unless an operator explicitly switches policy pack.

### Compliance risk of the choice

- **Risk of A:** Blocks genuine emergency activations that operations believes are valid; may encourage off-log workarounds if emergencies are frequent.
- **Risk of B (if chosen):** Silently accepts activations that Legal considers void; the missing authorization attribute makes “compliant emergency” indistinguishable from policy breach in the log alone.
- **Risk of mixing without disclosure:** The worst outcome—encoding both “opinion must precede activate” and “activate may precede opinion” in one DECLARE set—produces contradictory or vacuous enforcement. This design keeps them in separate policy packs.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description

Process tree (block-structured approximation):

\[
\begin{align*}
S &= \rightarrow\big( \\
&\quad \mathsf{Receive\ Onboarding\ Request}, \\
&\quad \wedge(\mathsf{Capture\ Master\ Data},\ \mathsf{Run\ Sanctions\ Screening},\ \mathsf{Verify\ Bank\ Account}), \\
&\quad \mathsf{Classify\ Risk}, \\
&\quad \times\big( \\
&\quad\quad \rightarrow(\mathsf{Reject\ Vendor},\ \mathsf{Close\ Onboarding}), \\
&\quad\quad \rightarrow(\mathsf{Manager\ Approval},\ \mathsf{Activate\ Vendor},\ \mathsf{Release\ First\ Payment},\ \mathsf{Close\ Onboarding}), \\
&\quad\quad \rightarrow(\mathsf{Enhanced\ Due\ Diligence},\ \mathsf{Compliance\ Approval},\ \mathsf{Activate\ Vendor},\ \mathsf{Release\ First\ Payment},\ \mathsf{Close\ Onboarding}) \\
&\quad \big) \\
&\big)
\end{align*}
\]

**Deliberately excluded from the tree (handled elsewhere):** clarification cycles; `Sanctions Escalation` / `Obtain Legal Opinion`; `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor`; re-verification after bank change.

**Approximation disclosure:** Clarifications in the tests interleave before `Classify Risk` (and could theoretically appear elsewhere). A block tree cannot freely interleave unanswered-request constraints without exploding structure; those are authoritative in DECLARE. Escalation/opinion are optional sidepaths, not exclusive branches—encoding them as a third parallel optional arm would allow opinion-free activation even under R5; they stay out of the tree.

## 3.2 PM4Py code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def leaf(name: str) -> ProcessTree:
    t = ProcessTree()
    t.label = name
    return t

def node(op: Operator, children: list) -> ProcessTree:
    t = ProcessTree(operator=op)
    t.children = children
    for c in children:
        c.parent = t
    return t

recv = leaf("Receive Onboarding Request")
cmd = leaf("Capture Master Data")
rss = leaf("Run Sanctions Screening")
vba = leaf("Verify Bank Account")
classify = leaf("Classify Risk")

reject = leaf("Reject Vendor")
mgr = leaf("Manager Approval")
edd = leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence")
comp = leaf("Compliance Approval")
activate = leaf("Activate Vendor")
pay = leaf("Release First Payment")
close = leaf("Close Onboarding")

parallel_checks = node(Operator.PARALLEL, [cmd, rss, vba])

route_reject = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [reject, close])
route_low = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [mgr, activate, pay, close])
route_high = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, [edd, comp, activate, pay, close])
xor_routes = node(Operator.XOR, [route_reject, route_low, route_high])

procedural_model = node(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [recv, parallel_checks, classify, xor_routes],
)
```

## 3.3 Explanation

- **Concurrency:** The three checks are a true AND-split/join after receipt; any interleaving is allowed; `Classify Risk` joins all three.
- **Choices:** Exclusive choice among reject / low-risk / high-risk after classification. This encodes R2/R8 at the spine level for the main happy/reject paths.
- **Clarification loops:** Not in the tree (see §1–2). Including a `` around an optional request/receive before classify would still miss “must receive before terminal” if terminals are outside the loop body.
- **Terminal outcomes:** `Close Onboarding` ends every complete spine path; reject path skips activation and payment; accept paths require activation then first payment then close.
- **Post-activation:** `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` excluded so the spine stays an onboarding net, not a full vendor lifecycle. R11 lives in DECLARE + scripts.
- **Escalation/legal:** Excluded so the tree does not take a side on R5/R6 ordering beyond what the high-risk route already requires (EDD  Compliance  Activate).

### Rule semantics table

| Rule | Template | Direction / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Start | `init` | First event is `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| Core milestones | `exactly_one` | Receipt, classify, close occur once (complete cases). |
| R3 pair | `response` + `altresponse` | Every request is eventually followed by a receive; **alternate** response forbids a second request until the previous is answered. **Not** chain response (other events may interleave). |
| R4 | `response(SE, OLO)` | After escalation, opinion must eventually occur. Does **not** say opinion before activate. |
| R7 | `precedence(RSS, SE)` | If escalation occurs, screening already occurred. **Precedence  response:** screening need not be followed by escalation. |
| Checks before classify | `precedence` | Each check before classify if classify occurs. |
| EDD before Compliance | `precedence` | High-risk internal order. |
| Classify before route activities | `precedence` | Route activities not before classification. |
| R1 | `noncoexistence` + bidirectional `nonsuccession` | Never together in the same case; neither ordering is allowed. Noncoexistence is multiset-level; nonsuccession is ordered. |
| R8 / R2 fragments | `noncoexistence` | Manager vs Compliance; EDD vs Manager; approvals vs reject. |
| Payment after activate | `succession` | Response + precedence: if either occurs, activate before payment and payment eventually after activate. |
| R11 fragment | `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` | Suspend eventually after bank change. Re-verify-before-pay needs script. |

**Not used:** `chainresponse` / `chainprecedence` (would force adjacency; clarification and legal steps allow interleaving). **Vacuous cases:** `response(SE, OLO)` is vacuously true when SE never occurs (T1–T5, T8).

### Orientation and overlap notes

- **`always_after(A, B)`** means B occurs after A when A occurs (skeleton “after” semantics aligned with response-style obligations).
- **`always_before(A, B)`** means A occurs before B when B occurs (aligned with precedence).
- **Clarification pair:** `always_after(Request, Receive)` mirrors DECLARE response. The pair `always_before(Receive Clarification, Request Clarification)` is **only sound for the 2nd+ request** (alternate). Putting it globally is a known over-approximation if interpreted as “every request has a receive before it,” which would ban the first request. **Correction for deployment:** remove `(Receive Clarification, Request Clarification)` from `always_before` and rely on DECLARE `altresponse` for multi-cycle discipline. Safer skeleton:

```python
# Safer clarification handling:
# drop (Receive Clarification, Request Clarification) from always_before
```

Below, test evaluation uses the **safer** interpretation (no `always_before(Receive, Request)`).

- **Disagreement with procedural layer:** Skeleton allows `Verify Bank Account` frequency 2 for R11; tree allows only 1 on the spine. Skeleton is looser on lifecycle, tighter on cardinality of terminals.
- **`directly_follows` empty:** Parallel checks and optional legal/clarification steps would create false DF violations (e.g., T1 vs T2 check order).
- **Equivalence empty:** No two activities are required to share identical frequencies in all cases (requests can differ from receives only mid-violation; we enforce via DECLARE instead).

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

Deviation test for a pair instance with elapsed \(t\): flag **warning** if \(|t - \mu| > \zeta \cdot \sigma\).

### Why warnings, not control-flow failures

R12 is explicitly “normative enough to flag but not automatically reject.” Temporal spreads model SLO-style expectations (vendor response latency, legal turnaround, finance payment cycle). A slow clarification is still a valid clarification under R3; a slow legal opinion may still satisfy R4/R5 order. Hard-failing on time would conflate service-level management with admissibility and would double-punish T7-style cases that are already borderline under legal policy. The temporal layer therefore never alone sets `Overall verdict = fail`.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required attributes: `case_id`, `activity`, `timestamp`, `resource`. Optional: `emergency_auth_id` (absent in current log).

```python
import pandas as pd
from datetime import timedelta

# Expected columns: case_id, activity, timestamp, resource

APPROVALS = ["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"]

def missing_attr_action(df, cols):
    """If required attributes missing  fail-closed for SoD; policy-dependent for emergency."""
    for c in cols:
        if c not in df.columns:
            raise ValueError(f"required attribute {c} missing — cannot evaluate check")

def check_r9_four_eyes(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """SoD: Verify Bank Account resource  applicable approval resource;
       Capture Master Data resource  Activate Vendor resource.
    """
    missing_attr_action(df, ["case_id", "activity", "resource"])
    violations = []
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("timestamp").groupby("case_id"):
        res = dict(zip(g["activity"], g["resource"]))  # last occurrence wins
        # Bank verify vs whichever approval occurred
        if "Verify Bank Account" in res:
            for ap in APPROVALS:
                if ap in res and res[ap] == res["Verify Bank Account"]:
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": cid, "rule": "R9",
                        "detail": f"same resource {res[ap]} on Verify Bank Account and {ap}",
                    })
        if "Capture Master Data" in res and "Activate Vendor" in res:
            if res["Capture Master Data"] == res["Activate Vendor"]:
                violations.append({
                    "case_id": cid, "rule": "R9",
                    "detail": "same resource on Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor",
                })
    return pd.DataFrame(violations)

def check_r9_pm4py(log):
    """Optional PM4Py filters (activity-pair SoD). Apply per approval type."""
    import pm4py
    # Keep cases that VIOLATE four eyes for investigation:
    v1 = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log, activity1="Verify Bank Account", activity2="Manager Approval"
    )
    v2 = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log, activity1="Verify Bank Account", activity2="Compliance Approval"
    )
    v3 = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log, activity1="Capture Master Data", activity2="Activate Vendor"
    )
    return v1, v2, v3

def check_r5_conditional_precedence(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Pack A: if Sanctions Escalation and Activate Vendor, then Obtain Legal Opinion
    must occur at an earlier timestamp than Activate Vendor.
    """
    missing_attr_action(df, ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    viol = []
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("timestamp").groupby("case_id"):
        acts = list(g["activity"])
        times = dict(zip(g["activity"], g["timestamp"]))  # last wins; prefer first_occurrence in prod
        if "Sanctions Escalation" in acts and "Activate Vendor" in acts:
            if "Obtain Legal Opinion" not in acts:
                viol.append({"case_id": cid, "rule": "R5", "detail": "missing legal opinion"})
            else:
                # use first occurrence timestamps
                t_olo = g.loc[g.activity == "Obtain Legal Opinion", "timestamp"].min()
                t_av = g.loc[g.activity == "Activate Vendor", "timestamp"].min()
                if not (t_olo < t_av):
                    viol.append({
                        "case_id": cid, "rule": "R5",
                        "detail": "Obtain Legal Opinion does not precede Activate Vendor",
                    })
    return pd.DataFrame(viol)

def check_r6_emergency_pack_b(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Pack B diagnostic: early activation after SE allowed only if
    opinion within 24h AND emergency_auth_id present and non-null.
    Without emergency_auth_id column  all early activations are policy-dependent/fail-closed.
    """
    missing_attr_action(df, ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    has_auth = "emergency_auth_id" in df.columns
    rows = []
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("timestamp").groupby("case_id"):
        if "Sanctions Escalation" not in set(g.activity):
            continue
        if "Activate Vendor" not in set(g.activity):
            continue
        t_se = g.loc[g.activity == "Sanctions Escalation", "timestamp"].min()
        t_av = g.loc[g.activity == "Activate Vendor", "timestamp"].min()
        olo = g.loc[g.activity == "Obtain Legal Opinion", "timestamp"]
        if len(olo) == 0:
            rows.append({"case_id": cid, "status": "fail", "detail": "no opinion after emergency path"})
            continue
        t_olo = olo.min()
        if t_av < t_olo:
            delta = t_olo - t_av
            auth_ok = False
            if has_auth:
                auth_ok = g["emergency_auth_id"].notna().any() and (g["emergency_auth_id"] != "").any()
            else:
                auth_ok = False  # missing attribute
            if delta <= timedelta(hours=24) and auth_ok:
                rows.append({"case_id": cid, "status": "pass", "detail": "R6 emergency satisfied"})
            elif delta <= timedelta(hours=24) and not auth_ok:
                rows.append({
                    "case_id": cid, "status": "policy-dependent",
                    "detail": "opinion within 24h but joint authorization not in log",
                })
            else:
                rows.append({"case_id": cid, "status": "fail", "detail": "opinion later than 24h after activate"})
        else:
            rows.append({"case_id": cid, "status": "pass", "detail": "opinion before activate (R5-compatible)"})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def check_r3_before_terminal(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Every Request Clarification answered before next request and before Reject/Activate."""
    missing_attr_action(df, ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    viol = []
    terminals = {"Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"}
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("timestamp").groupby("case_id"):
        pending = 0
        for _, row in g.iterrows():
            a = row["activity"]
            if a == "Request Clarification":
                if pending > 0:
                    viol.append({"case_id": cid, "rule": "R3", "detail": "request while prior unanswered"})
                pending += 1
            elif a == "Receive Clarification":
                pending = max(0, pending - 1)
            elif a in terminals and pending > 0:
                viol.append({"case_id": cid, "rule": "R3", "detail": f"terminal {a} with unanswered clarification"})
        if pending > 0:
            viol.append({"case_id": cid, "rule": "R3", "detail": "case ended with unanswered clarification"})
    return pd.DataFrame(viol)

def check_r11_bank_change(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """After Activate, if Change Bank Details then eventually Suspend;
    any Release First Payment after that change must have Verify Bank Account in between.
    """
    missing_attr_action(df, ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    viol = []
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("timestamp").groupby("case_id"):
        acts = list(g["activity"])
        if "Activate Vendor" not in acts:
            continue
        i_av = acts.index("Activate Vendor")
        suffix = acts[i_av + 1 :]
        for i, a in enumerate(suffix):
            if a == "Change Bank Details":
                if "Suspend Vendor" not in suffix[i:]:
                    viol.append({"case_id": cid, "rule": "R11", "detail": "bank change without suspend"})
                # payments after this change
                for j in range(i + 1, len(suffix)):
                    if suffix[j] == "Release First Payment":
                        window = suffix[i + 1 : j]
                        if "Verify Bank Account" not in window:
                            viol.append({
                                "case_id": cid, "rule": "R11",
                                "detail": "payment after bank change without re-verify",
                            })
    return pd.DataFrame(viol)
```

**Missing attributes:** SoD and R3/R5/R11 **fail closed** (cannot claim pass). R6 without `emergency_auth_id` yields `policy-dependent` even if the 24h bound holds.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement.** R1/R2/R8 appear in the process-tree XOR, DECLARE `noncoexistence`, and skeleton `never_together`. This is intentional defense in depth: tree catches structured deviations; DECLARE catches the same on free-form logs; skeleton catches cardinality co-occurrence. Authoritative conflict resolution when tools disagree: **DECLARE + R1 script** for exclusion; **tree** for ordering of the happy path.

**Loops and post-activation inconsistency.** `activ_freq` for `Verify Bank Account` allows 2 for R11, but the tree allows 1. A second verify after close is outside the spine language. `exactly_one(Close Onboarding)` fights long-running vendor lifecycle if suspend/re-activate continues in the same case id. Recommendation: close the onboarding case at first `Close Onboarding` and open a child “vendor maintenance” case—or drop `exactly_one(Close)` for multi-episode logs.

**Vacuity.** `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is true on all non-escalation cases. High support/confidence in discovery would not mean the rule was stress-tested. Similarly `precedence(RSS, SE)` is vacuous without SE. Compliance dashboards should report **activation counts**, not only confidence.

**Missing case attributes.** R6 joint authorization and any “risk tier” data attribute are absent; risk route is inferred only from which approval path was taken (circular if auditing whether classification matched the route). SoD fails open if `resource` is missing—we choose fail closed.

**Timestamp-only limitations.** Temporal profile cannot distinguish business-hour delays from idle cases; concurrent sub-clocks (vendor vs internal legal) share one case clock. R6’s 24h bound is a hard data check, not part of the profile means.

**Support/confidence metadata.** Values are fixed at `1.0` as *normative prescriptions*, not mined statistics. Treating them as empirical truth would hide that they were authored. Downstream tools that filter rules by discovered support must not drop mandatory R1-level rules.

**R5/R6 contradiction** remains the central policy conflict; encoding both as hard DECLARE response/precedence simultaneously is inconsistent for T6. Separate policy packs are mandatory.

**DECLARE gap on conditional precedence.** “If SE then OLO before AV” is not a standard template; external check is required. Using bare `precedence(OLO, AV)` would false-fail all activations without legal opinion even when SE never occurred.

**Clarification vs classify order.** T5 shows classify can still be projected as legal spine while R3 fails—layers must not be collapsed into a single pass/fail without explanation.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

1. **Canonical procedural representation:** the ProcessTree in §3 (`procedural_model`) as the onboarding spine for simulation, token-based replay education, and happy-path KPI projection. Do not overload it with escalation or clarification.

2. **Authoritative policy layer:**  
   - **Structural exclusions and obligations (R1, R2 fragments, R3, R4, R7, R8, R11-response):** DECLARE.  
   - **Cardinality (R10, optional frequencies):** Log Skeleton `activ_freq`.  
   - **Conditional legal order (R5) and emergency (R6):** resource/data pack, with **pack A default**.  
   - **SoD (R9):** resource layer / `filter_four_eyes_principle`.  
   - **SLO latency (R12):** temporal profile warnings only.

3. **Conformance check order:**  
   (1) Schema/attribute presence   
   (2) Procedural spine projection/replay   
   (3) DECLARE   
   (4) Log Skeleton   
   (5) Resource/data scripts (R3 terminal guard, R5/R6, R9, R11)   
   (6) Temporal profile (`zeta=2.0`).  

   Fail-closed rules short-circuit the **overall** verdict; temporal runs always for diagnostics.

4. **Surfacing contradictions:** Return a **layered report**, not a boolean alone: each layer’s verdict, earliest decisive fail, and explicit `policy-dependent` when pack A vs B disagree (template text: “Legal policy R5 violated; Operations R6 may apply if authorization ticket X is attached”). Never average layers into a silent score. UI should show T6-style cases under both packs side by side when escalation and early activation co-occur.

5. **Operations follow-up:** Add optional attributes `risk_tier`, `emergency_auth_id`, `authorization_timestamp` to retire R6 ambiguity; until then, default deny early activation in automated gates.
