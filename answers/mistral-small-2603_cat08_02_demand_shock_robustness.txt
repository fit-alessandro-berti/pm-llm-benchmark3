# **Redesigning the Diagnostic Lab Process for Robustness Under Disruption**

---

## **1. Stress Scenarios**

We define four plausible disruption scenarios that vary by **demand mix**, **resource degradation**, and **external disruptions**, each stressing different parts of the process:

### **Scenario A: Winter Infection Wave (Demand Mix Shift + Specialist Absence)**
- **Demand mix**: Urgent cases rise to **45%**, Complex-panel to **25%**, Routine drops to **20%** (similar to peak-period mix but sustained).
- **Resource disruption**:
  - **Medical validators**: 30% absenteeism (flu season)  validator utilization spikes to **120%**.
  - **Specialist review**: One of two specialists unavailable for 2 weeks.
- **Impact**:
  - Medical validation becomes the primary bottleneck.
  - Complex-panel cases (which require medical validation) accumulate in queues.
  - Urgent cases may still meet 18-hour targets, but Routine cases face severe delays.

### **Scenario B: Assay Machine M2 Outage (Equipment Failure)**
- **Demand mix**: Normal mix (61% Routine, 18% Urgent, etc.).
- **Resource disruption**:
  - **M2 outage** for 48 hours (M1 remains at 98% utilization).
  - Repeat assays (1140 normal-period cases) must reroute to M1, increasing its failure rate.
- **Impact**:
  - Assay run queues grow rapidly; **p90 cycle time for Urgent cases exceeds 18 hours**.
  - Technical validation queues back up due to delayed assays.
  - More recollection loops (pre-analytical failures) as M2’s faster runtime is unavailable.

### **Scenario C: Courier Arrival Compression (External Logistics Disruption)**
- **Demand mix**: Normal mix.
- **Resource disruption**:
  - Courier delays cause **70% of samples to arrive in two 2-hour windows** (10:00–12:00 and 17:00–19:00) instead of spread out.
  - Pre-analytical check utilization spikes to **110%** during peaks.
- **Impact**:
  - Pre-analytical queues grow (**peak median queue jumps to 20h**).
  - Manual accession corrections (500 normal-period cases) surge, overwhelming the 2 specialists.
  - Urgent cases are delayed because samples arrive late.

### **Scenario D: Specialist Review Blackout (Peak Demand + Absence)**
- **Demand mix**: Peak-period mix (42% Routine, 29% Urgent, 20% Complex-panel).
- **Resource disruption**:
  - **Specialist review unavailable for 5 days** (e.g., due to outbreak).
  - Medical validators are cross-trained but can only handle **50% of specialist review load**.
- **Impact**:
  - **Hold for specialist review** cases (290 normal-period) back up, creating a **36h median queue** in peak.
  - Medical validation queues grow (**p90 cycle time for Complex-panel cases exceeds 240h**).
  - Urgent cases may still pass through if medical validators prioritize them, but Routine cases are severely delayed.

## **2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis**

For each scenario, we trace how bottlenecks **migrate** across the process and how relieving one station overloads another.

| **Scenario** | **Primary Bottleneck** | **Secondary Bottleneck** | **Tertiary Bottleneck** | **Why?** |
|-------------|----------------------|------------------------|------------------------|----------|
| **A: Winter Wave** | Medical validation (120% utilization) | Specialist review (87%  100%) | Assay run (98%  100% due to Complex-panel repeats) | Complex-panel cases require medical validation; absenteeism starves this step. Assay machines get overloaded as repeats accumulate. |
| **B: M2 Outage** | Assay run (M1 at 100%) | Technical validation (backlog from delayed assays) | Pre-analytical check (repeats from failed assays) | M1 cannot handle the volume; assay failures increase pre-analytical workload. |
| **C: Courier Compression** | Pre-analytical check (110% utilization) | Manual accession correction (91% utilization) | Registration clerks (85% utilization) | Late-arriving samples create a surge; corrections and registration become bottlenecks. |
| **D: Specialist Blackout** | Hold for specialist review (queue grows to 36h) | Medical validation (backlog from unprocessed holds) | Assay run (Complex-panel repeats) | Specialist absence forces medical validators to handle specialist-review cases, creating a new bottleneck. |

**Key Insight**: Relieving one bottleneck **shifts pressure downstream**. For example:
- In **Scenario A**, adding medical validators (I5) helps, but if **specialist review** is also constrained, the benefit is limited.
- In **Scenario B**, dedicating M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel (I3) helps, but if **technical validation** is already at 101% utilization, the assay bottleneck resolves into a validation bottleneck.
- In **Scenario C**, smoothing courier arrivals (I7) reduces pre-analytical pressure, but if **manual accession corrections** are already a bottleneck, the system still fails.

## **3. Robust Redesign Package**

We propose a **modular redesign** that combines **structural changes** (process redesign) with **adaptive policies** (scenario-specific triggers). The goal is to **reduce queue variability** and **prevent bottleneck migration**.

### **Core Interventions (Always On)**
| **Intervention** | **Rationale** | **Assumptions** | **Failure Mode** |
|-----------------|--------------|----------------|------------------|
| **I8: Automate registration for digital orders** | Reduces registration queue (currently 85% peak utilization). | 40% of orders have complete digital metadata. | If metadata quality drops, automation fails. |
| **I6: Early pre-analytical defect triage** | Rejects poor samples within 2 hours, reducing recollection loops. | Pre-analytical failures are identifiable early. | May increase false rejections if triage is too strict. |
| **I4: Cross-train 2 technical validators for accession correction** | Reduces accession correction queue (91% peak utilization). | Validators can be trained in 4 weeks. | If validators are needed for technical validation, this creates a new bottleneck. |
| **I7: Smooth courier arrivals (staggered pickups)** | Reduces pre-analytical surge (110% peak utilization). | Couriers can adjust schedules. | If physicians insist on fixed pickup times, this fails. |

**Why these are robust**:
- They **reduce variability** (e.g., automation smooths registration, triage reduces rework).
- They **prevent bottleneck migration** (e.g., cross-training prevents accession correction from starving technical validation).
- They **do not assume constant capacity** (e.g., smoothing courier arrivals is cheaper than adding couriers).

---

### **Adaptive Policies (Scenario-Triggered)**
| **Policy** | **Trigger** | **Action** | **Exit Condition** |
|------------|------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Urgent-first dispatch with dynamic batching** | Urgent cases queue > 4h OR p90 Urgent cycle time > 18h | Bypass batch wait for Urgent; limit Routine batch size to 12. | When Urgent queue < 2h for 24h. |
| **Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel** | M1 utilization > 95% OR assay p90 > 18h | Route all Urgent/Complex-panel to M1; Routine to M2. | When M1 utilization < 80% for 48h. |
| **Medical validation shift activation** | Medical validator queue > 12h OR Complex-panel p90 > 120h | Activate evening shift (2 extra validators). | When queue < 6h for 24h. |
| **Partner lab overflow for Complex-panel** | Complex-panel queue > 24h OR specialist review queue > 36h | Send 30% of Complex-panel cases to partner lab. | When queue < 12h for 48h. |

**Why these are robust**:
- They **activate only when needed**, preventing over-optimization for averages.
- They **target the right case types** (e.g., Urgent-first dispatch doesn’t starve Complex-panel).
- They **include exit conditions** to avoid over-correction.

## **4. Scenario-Specific Policies**

| **Scenario** | **Policy Bundle** | **Trigger** | **Exit Condition** |
|-------------|------------------|------------|-------------------|
| **A: Winter Wave** | - Medical validation shift (I5) <br> - Partner lab overflow (I10) <br> - Urgent-first dispatch (I1) | Medical validator queue > 12h OR Complex-panel p90 > 120h | When validator queue < 6h for 24h. |
| **B: M2 Outage** | - Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel (I3) <br> - Reduce Routine batch size to 12 | Assay p90 > 18h OR Urgent queue > 4h | When M1 utilization < 80% for 48h. |
| **C: Courier Compression** | - Smooth courier arrivals (I7) <br> - Early triage (I6) <br> - Cross-train validators (I4) | Pre-analytical queue > 12h OR accession correction queue > 14h | When queues stabilize for 72h. |
| **D: Specialist Blackout** | - Partner lab overflow (I10) <br> - Medical validation shift (I5) | Specialist review queue > 36h OR Hold for specialist review queue > 24h | When queue < 12h for 48h. |

**Key Insight**: No single policy works for all scenarios. The **adaptive policies** ensure the system **self-corrects** when conditions change.

## **5. Trade-off Table**

| **Intervention/Policy** | **Scenario Coverage** | **Effect on Urgent** | **Effect on Routine** | **Effect on Complex-panel** | **Impact on Rework/Repeats** | **Next Bottleneck Risk** | **Implementation Burden** | **Monitoring Signal** |
|-----------------------|----------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|----------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------|
| **I8: Automate registration** | A, B, C, D | Neutral | Neutral | Neutral | Reduces rework (fewer accession corrections) | Registration clerks underutilized | Medium (IT integration) | Registration queue time > 3h |
| **I6: Early triage** | A, B, C, D | Neutral | Neutral | Neutral | Reduces recollection loops by 30% | Pre-analytical check may reject too many | Low (process change) | Pre-analytical defect rate > 5% |
| **I4: Cross-train validators** | A, C | Neutral | Neutral | Neutral | Reduces accession correction queue | Technical validation may become bottleneck | Medium (training) | Accession correction queue > 10h |
| **I7: Smooth courier arrivals** | C, D | Neutral | Neutral | Neutral | Reduces pre-analytical surge | Physician pushback on pickup times | Low (logistics change) | Courier arrival spikes > 50% in 2h windows |
| **I1: Urgent-first dispatch** | A, B | Improves p90 to <18h | May delay Routine | May delay Complex-panel | Neutral | Assay run may fragment | Low (policy change) | Urgent queue > 4h OR Routine queue > 24h |
| **I3: Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel** | B | Improves p90 to <18h | May delay Routine | Improves p90 | Increases repeats if M2 fails more | Technical validation backlog | Medium (machine routing) | M1 utilization > 95% OR M2 failure rate > 10% |
| **I5: Medical validation shift** | A, D | Neutral | Neutral | Improves Complex-panel p90 | Neutral | Specialist review may still be bottleneck | High (staffing) | Medical validator queue > 12h |

**Key Trade-offs**:
- **I1 (Urgent-first dispatch)** helps Urgent cases but **may starve Routine/Complex-panel** if not monitored.
- **I3 (M1 dedication)** helps Urgent/Complex-panel but **increases Routine delays** and **M2 failure risk**.
- **I10 (Partner lab)** relieves specialist review but **adds 12h transport delay** and **traceability complexity**.

## **6. Residual Vulnerabilities**

Even with the redesign, **three key vulnerabilities remain**:

1. **Medical Validator Absenteeism During Winter Waves (Scenario A)**
   - **Cause**: Even with the evening shift (I5), a **30% absenteeism** may overwhelm the system.
   - **Why it persists**: Medical validators are a **single-point bottleneck** for Complex-panel cases.
   - **Mitigation**: Pre-emptively **reserve 20% of Complex-panel capacity for partner lab overflow (I10)** during flu season.

2. **M2 Failure During Assay Surges (Scenario B)**
   - **Cause**: Routine cases routed to M2 during M1 dedication **increase failure rate** (M2 has higher failure rate).
   - **Why it persists**: M2’s **faster runtime** makes it attractive for Routine cases, but it’s less reliable.
   - **Mitigation**: **Cap Routine batch size to 12** when M2 utilization > 90%.

3. **Courier Pushback on Staggered Pickups (Scenario C)**
   - **Cause**: Physicians may **insist on fixed pickup times**, making I7 unenforceable.
   - **Why it persists**: External stakeholder resistance.
   - **Mitigation**: **Offer incentives** (e.g., faster turnaround for staggered pickups) or **contractually enforce pickup windows**.

---

### **Final Notes**
- **Do not optimize for averages**: The redesign **prioritizes variability reduction** (e.g., smoothing courier arrivals, early triage).
- **Avoid over-optimizing one case type**: Policies like **Urgent-first dispatch** are **scenario-triggered** to prevent starvation.
- **Monitor downstream effects**: Every intervention is checked for **bottleneck migration** (e.g., cross-training validators may starve technical validation).

This approach ensures the lab remains **resilient** under **multiple plausible disruptions** without relying on **historical averages** or **unlimited capacity**.
