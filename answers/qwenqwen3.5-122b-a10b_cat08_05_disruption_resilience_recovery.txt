# 1. Disruption timeline

**T0: Baseline Period (Day -30 to Day -1)**
*   **Operational State:** Fully automated WMS-driven workflow. Stable throughput with low variance.
*   **Dominant Variants:** Standard receive-to-invoice flow with minimal holds (Compliance hold <2% of intake).
*   **Risk Posture:** Low. Traceability exceptions at 0.8%; Controlled-substance dual-control missing at 0.2%. Cycle times stable (Hospital-critical median 8h).

**T1: Incident Onset (Day 0 Morning)**
*   **Operational State:** WMS unavailable. Immediate halt to automated reservation and scanning.
*   **Dominant Variants:** Interruption of normal flow; transition to emergency protocols.
*   **Risk Posture:** High uncertainty. Immediate loss of visibility into inventory and authorization status.

**T2: Manual Workaround Phase (Day 0 Afternoon to Day 2)**
*   **Operational State:** Decentralized manual operations using printed snapshots, spreadsheets, and phone calls.
*   **Dominant Variants:** Significant deviation from baseline (Fitness 0.71). Emergence of "Emergency triage," "Skip system authorization," and "Dispatch without scan."
*   **Risk Posture:** Critical degradation. Traceability exceptions spiked to 9.5%; Controlled-substance missing controls rose to 2.9% (14x baseline). Hospital fill rate held at 91% (Resilience), but Pharmacy standard fill rate collapsed to 61%. High rework rate (12.5%).

**T3: Partial Recovery (Day 3 to Day 6)**
*   **Operational State:** Order intake restored; inventory synchronization unreliable. Hybrid manual/automated flow.
*   **Dominant Variants:** Reintegration of validation steps but persistent manual reconciliation ("Reconcile manual reservation"). Fitness improved to 0.82.
*   **Risk Posture:** Moderate. Exceptions decreasing but remained elevated (Traceability 5.4%). Cycle times better than T2 but slower than baseline.

**T4: Stabilization (Day 7 to Day 21)**
*   **Operational State:** Systems functional; backlog clearing; audit remediation active.
*   **Dominant Variants:** Return to near-normal flow but with added "Backlog prioritization" and "Audit remediation" paths. Fitness 0.90 (still below baseline 0.96).
*   **Risk Posture:** Residual risk persists. Traceability exceptions (2.2%) and Controlled-substance missing controls (0.5%) remain above baseline. A significant "hidden workload" exists (2,300 cases routed to Audit remediation after Invoice close), indicating deferred compliance rather than real-time adherence.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this incident, **resilience** is defined as the ability to maintain critical service levels (specifically Hospital-critical fill rate) while adhering to minimum safety and legal constraints. **Concealed noncompliance** occurs when operational throughput is preserved by bypassing controls, creating a false sense of normalcy that is only revealed through delayed audit trails or increased rework.

*   **True Resilience:** Adaptations **A (modified)**, **B**, **I**, and **J** demonstrate resilience. They prioritized hospital supply (mission critical) and managed capacity (I) without abandoning core verification (B). The maintenance of a 91% Hospital fill rate against a 61% Pharmacy rate indicates successful triage under pressure.
*   **Risky Shortcuts (Concealed Noncompliance):** Adaptations **C**, **G**, and parts of **D** represent concealed noncompliance. "Skip system authorization" (C) and "Dispatch without scan" (G) removed essential regulatory gates. While they kept trucks moving, they broke the chain of custody and authorization audit trail. The fact that Traceability exceptions remained at 2.2% in T4 (vs 0.8% baseline) proves these gaps were not fully resolved operationally.
*   **The Hidden Tail:** Adaptation **L (Audit remediation)** is the clearest indicator of concealed noncompliance becoming visible. The Stabilization DFG shows a distinct path `Invoice close -> Audit remediation` affecting 2,300 cases. This indicates that compliance was treated as a post-processing cost rather than a real-time requirement. Real-time compliance would have captured this evidence during T2/T3, preventing the backlog.
*   **Ambiguous Cases:** Adaptation **H (Phone proof of delivery)** is ambiguous. It recovered evidence (resilience) but lacked the rigor of a digital scan (compliance risk). Its classification depends on whether the phone record was legally sufficient for the specific product class (e.g., acceptable for ambient, insufficient for controlled substances).

# 4. Recovery process model

**Model Name:** Resilient Manual Continuity Protocol (RMCP)
**Notation:** Structured Process Tree (Root -> Conditions -> Actions -> Constraints)

**1. Trigger Conditions (Enter RMCP Mode)**
*   **Condition:** WMS Availability < 15 mins OR Cyber Incident Declared Level 2+.
*   **Action:** Activate Offline Playbook v1.0; Notify Compliance Officer.
*   **Constraint:** Do not enter RMCP for < 30 mins downtime (overhead exceeds benefit).

**2. Triage Rules (Order Intake)**
*   **Input:** Customer Type + Product Risk Class.
*   **Logic:**
    *   IF Hospital-critical AND (Controlled OR Cold-chain) -> **Route: Priority Red** (Requires Dual Control + Temp Log).
    *   IF Hospital-critical AND Ambient -> **Route: Priority Yellow** (Requires Single Auth + Snapshot).
    *   IF Pharmacy-standard -> **Route: Priority Green** (Deferred if Capacity < Threshold).
    *   IF Wholesale-bulk -> **Route: Queue** (Suspend unless Contractual SLA Breach imminent).
*   **Constraint:** No order leaves Warehouse without a unique Paper Case ID linked to Customer ID.

**3. Minimum Controls by Segment (Non-Negotiables)**
*   **Authorization:** Must verify Customer/Product Eligibility via Offline List or Phone Confirmation (Pattern B). *Never skip (Pattern C).*
*   **Inventory:** Snapshot refresh every 4 hours. Manual decrement counter required per pick location.
*   **Temperature:** Digital thermometer reading + Signature on paper manifest for Cold-chain. *Never omit.*
*   **Controlled Substances:** Dual-signature required on Pick & Pack sheets. Video recording of handoff encouraged.
*   **Traceability:** Scan barcode onto local device or write Batch/Lot # on Paper Manifest. *No "Dispatch without scan" (Pattern G).*

**4. Manual Evidence Capture (Real-Time Logging)**
*   **Tool:** Hardcopy "Continuity Ledger" or Offline Tablet App.
*   **Fields:** Order ID, Time, Picker ID, Checker ID, Temp Value, Batch #, Courier ID.
*   **Handoff:** Courier must sign Ledger copy. One copy stays in Warehouse, one travels with shipment.
*   **Upload:** Upon system restore, Ledger entries must be uploaded within 4 hours.

**5. Backlog Prioritization (System Restore)**
*   **Sequence:** 1. Pending Critical Orders (Red/Yellow), 2. Pending Standard Orders (Green), 3. Deferred Bulk (Queue).
*   **Conflict Resolution:** If inventory conflict arises (Manual vs System), System wins. Manual orders flagged for "Reconcile" (Pattern K).

**6. Reconciliation and Audit-Remediation Flow**
*   **Step 1:** Auto-match Paper Manifest IDs to System Orders.
*   **Step 2:** Flag mismatches (Missing Temp, Missing Sig, Inventory Diff).
*   **Step 3:** **Immediate Remediation:** Assign to Compliance Team within 24h of restore.
*   **Step 4:** **Limit:** Maximum 5% of total volume allowed to enter "Audit Remediation" queue. If exceeded, trigger Executive Review (indicates protocol failure).
*   **Constraint:** No Invoice Close allowed until Proof of Delivery (Digital or Signed Ledger) is attached.

# 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading Indicators (During Incident)**
*   **Manual Control Completion Rate:** % of manual orders with all required fields (Auth, Temp, Dual-Sig) filled on paper ledger. Target: >95%.
*   **Critical Fill Rate Variance:** Difference between Hospital-critical fill rate and Baseline. Target: <10% drop.
*   **Offline Data Latency:** Time between manual event occurrence and upload to system. Target: <4 hours post-restoration.
*   **Capacity Utilization:** % of warehouse labor allocated to manual processes vs. backlog. Target: <80% to prevent burnout/errors.

**Lagging Indicators (Post-Incident)**
*   **Traceability Exception Rate:** % of orders with missing chain-of-custody data after reconciliation. Target: <1.0% (vs 2.2% observed).
*   **Audit Remediation Volume:** Absolute count of cases requiring post-close audit fix. Target: <1% of total volume.
*   **Rework Rate:** % of orders requiring correction after initial dispatch. Target: <5% (vs 12.5% observed).
*   **Fairness Index:** Ratio of Cancellation Rates (Pharmacy vs. Hospital). Target: <3:1 ratio (Prevents excessive deferral of non-critical).
*   **Hidden Workload Hours:** Total hours spent on Pattern K (Reconcile) and L (Audit). Target: <20% of incident duration.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management must accept a temporary increase in cycle time and operational cost to protect the integrity of the supply chain; specifically, we must allow for slower processing of Hospital-critical orders if it means verifying authorization and temperature manually. However, management **must not accept** the deletion of controls (such as skipping authorization or scanning) even for short durations, as evidenced by the 2.2% residual traceability exception rate in T4 which implies lasting liability. We should also reject the normalization of the "Audit Remediation queue"; any recovery plan that pushes compliance work to post-invoice close is a failure of resilience design. Evidence that would change this recommendation includes regulatory waivers allowing purely verbal confirmations for controlled substances or the deployment of blockchain-based offline verification that guarantees immutability without central system connectivity.
