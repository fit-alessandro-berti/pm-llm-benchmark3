## 1. Materiality Framework

Alignment cost measures the cheapest configured repair to reconcile a trace with the control-flow model. Here every visible log or model move costs 1, regardless of claim value, timing, policy significance, or whether money has already left the insurer. I use the following business-severity scale:

| Severity | Business meaning |
|---:|---|
| 5 — Critical | Payment occurred without a mandatory control, especially where exposure is high, risk is HIGH, a bank account recently changed, or approval followed payment. Potential fraud, recovery, regulatory, or material-loss implications. |
| 4 — High | Material policy violation or materially unreliable evidence over a key control; significant exposure, recurrence, or customer/regulatory impact. Payment may have occurred, but facts may require confirmation. |
| 3 — Moderate | Control or evidence weakness that is material but does not presently demonstrate an improper payment; or a customer obligation was met with weak/late evidence. |
| 2 — Low | Localized data-quality, labeling, duplicate-message, or model-coverage problem with little direct financial risk, but some audit or operational impact. |
| 1 — Informational | Conforming behavior, silent routing, or harmless technical noise with no meaningful control failure. |

Each rating considers:

- **Control objective:** Was coverage, loss assessment, fraud review, approval, payment sequencing, or customer notification affected?
- **Amount/exposure:** Higher claim value increases potential loss, but does not alone prove a failure.
- **Reversibility:** A pre-payment evidence gap is less consequential than an externally accepted payment. Recovery from a changed beneficiary account may be difficult.
- **Timing:** Payment before approval is materially different from a late log entry. Notification after 24 hours breaches P4 even if eventually sent.
- **Customer/regulatory harm:** Includes missed notices, wrongful payment or denial, fraud exposure, and inability to demonstrate compliance.
- **Recurrence:** Systematic failures deserve higher portfolio priority than isolated harmless artifacts.
- **Evidence reliability:** Stable signed and case-linked evidence can rebut a missing core event; mutable or weakly linked evidence gives less assurance.
- **Model scope:** Denials and withdrawals may be legitimate behavior omitted from a payable-claims model rather than operational noncompliance.

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Severity asks how serious the consequence would be under the best-supported interpretation. Confidence asks how strongly the evidence supports that interpretation. For example, CL-602 is high severity and high confidence because mandatory fraud evidence is absent on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk, recently changed-bank claim. CL-608 is moderate severity but only medium confidence because notification probably occurred, although its linkage is mutable. A missing event is evidence of an evidence gap—not proof that the activity did not occur.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

1. **The cost function has no business weighting.** Every visible log or model move costs 1. Thus CL-602’s missing fraud-screen event costs only 1 despite EUR 480,000 exposure, HIGH risk, and a recent bank change. By contrast, CL-601 costs 3 merely because three UI telemetry events are present.

2. **Control-flow fitness cannot enforce data conditions.** CL-609 and CL-610 both have cost 0 because Auto Approve is structurally allowed by the exclusive-choice branch. Yet CL-609 exceeds the EUR 5,000 and LOW-risk limits, while CL-610 has a recent beneficiary-bank change requiring fraud screening and manual approval. These are zero-cost policy failures.

3. **Scope mismatch creates large but nonmaterial repair costs.** CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 because the payable-claims model has no denial or withdrawal terminal branch. The alignment inserts approval, payment, and notification to finish the model even though payment should not occur. The high cost is diagnostic of model coverage, not proof of business misconduct.

4. **Hidden moves are normal fitting behavior.** CL-605 includes `T(tau,c=0)`. It is a silent routing transition produced by the Petri-net representation, not a hidden employee action or missing control. The trace is technically and substantively conforming.

5. **Activity granularity and labels can double-count one semantic issue.** In CL-611, `Assess Damage` is log-only and `Assess Loss` is model-only, generating cost 2. If they are governed synonyms, one completed assessment is represented as both an extra event and a missing event.

6. **Duplicated technical records can look like extra business work.** CL-603 costs 1 for a second notification, but the same message ID and retry telemetry indicate one business message represented twice. The 74-case pattern confirms a connector issue rather than 74 repeated customer-notification failures.

7. **A missing core event is not proof that work did not happen.** CL-606 has cost 1 for missing Assess Loss, while a signed external-adjuster report supports performance before later controls. The real issue is whether that evidence is authentic, immutable, and stably correlated. Conversely, CL-602’s unsupported verbal assertion provides much weaker assurance even though its cost is also 1.

8. **Alignment repair does not reverse irreversible actions.** In CL-604, the alignment inserts a model payment after manual approval, but the actual bank accepted funds at 10:03, before the 10:20 approval. A cheap edit can produce a legal model path; it cannot recall funds, restore segregation of duties, or eliminate fraud exposure.

9. **Notification evidence depends on channel and correlation.** CL-608’s cost 1 could mean a missed notification or merely that the event lives in CRM. The +3-hour portal message likely satisfies P4, but the mutable link lowers confidence. Alignment alone cannot evaluate evidence integrity.

Accordingly, raw cost is useful for locating structural disagreement and recurring extraction patterns. It should remain a separate diagnostic field, supplemented—not replaced—by contextual severity, confidence, policy tests, timing analysis, evidence quality, and scope assessment.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Expand and aggregate supplied move patterns
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
move_rows = []
move_re = re.compile(r"([LMT])\(([^)]+)\)")

for record in case_summaries:
    matches = move_re.findall(record["move_summary"])
    if not matches:
        # Represent a fully synchronous trace without inventing a costed move.
        move_rows.append({
            "case": record["case"],
            "move_type": "SYNC_ONLY",
            "activity": "No visible alignment deviation",
            "raw_cost": record["raw_cost"],
            "amount_eur": record["amount_eur"],
            "risk": record["risk"],
            "policy_overlay_flag": record["policy_overlay_flag"],
            "business_severity": record["business_severity"],
        })
    else:
        for move_type, activity in matches:
            move_rows.append({
                "case": record["case"],
                "move_type": move_type,
                "activity": activity,
                "raw_cost": record["raw_cost"],
                "amount_eur": record["amount_eur"],
                "risk": record["risk"],
                "policy_overlay_flag": record["policy_overlay_flag"],
                "business_severity": record["business_severity"],
            })

moves_df = pd.DataFrame(move_rows)

move_pattern_agg = (
    moves_df.groupby(["move_type", "activity"], dropna=False)
    .agg(
        case_count=("case", "nunique"),
        cases=("case", lambda s: ", ".join(sorted(set(s)))),
        total_case_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
        total_known_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        policy_flagged_cases=("policy_overlay_flag", "sum"),
        max_business_severity=("business_severity", "max"),
    )
    .reset_index()
    .sort_values(
        ["policy_flagged_cases", "max_business_severity", "case_count"],
        ascending=[False, False, False],
    )
)

risk_amount_agg = (
    summary_df.groupby(
        ["risk", "policy_overlay_flag"], dropna=False
    )
    .agg(
        case_count=("case", "count"),
        total_known_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        mean_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "mean"),
        max_business_severity=("business_severity", "max"),
    )
    .reset_index()
)

#    This sorts evidence for review but does not calculate or replace
#    narrative judgment with a composite risk formula.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
evidence_table = summary_df[
    [
        "case", "amount_eur", "risk", "recent_bank_change",
        "raw_cost",                 # technical diagnostic
        "business_severity",         # separately assigned business judgment
        "confidence", "policy_overlay_flag", "policy_reason",
        "move_summary",
    ]
].copy()

    evidence_table["business_severity"],
    categories=[5, 4, 3, 2, 1],
    ordered=True,
)
evidence_table = (
    evidence_table.assign(_severity_order=severity_order)
    .sort_values(
        ["_severity_order", "policy_overlay_flag", "amount_eur"],
        ascending=[True, False, False],
        na_position="last",
    )
    .drop(columns="_severity_order")
)

print("\nAuthoritative case evidence table:")
print(evidence_table.to_string(index=False))

print("\nAggregated supplied move patterns:")
print(move_pattern_agg.to_string(index=False))

print("\nRisk/amount aggregation:")
print(risk_amount_agg.to_string(index=False))

print(
    "\nInterpretation note: raw_cost remains a technical alignment measure. "
    "business_severity and confidence are contextual narrative judgments; "
    "they are intentionally not derived from a single arithmetic score."
