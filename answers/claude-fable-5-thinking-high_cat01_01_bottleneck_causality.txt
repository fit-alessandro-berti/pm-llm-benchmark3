## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded**. Confidence: **60/100**.

The crude comparison (Orion 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h, +6.7h) reverses inside every complexity stratum: Orion is faster by 2.1h (Low: 7.50 vs 9.64), 2.2h (Medium: 17.17 vs 19.38) and 3.4h (High: 31.57 vs 35.00). Standardizing both groups to the pooled complexity mix (40% Low, 33% Medium, 27% High) gives Orion 17.1h vs Nova 19.7h — a −2.5h contrast. The reversal is fully consistent with the stated routing policy: Orion receives 42.9% high-complexity cases (vs 12.5%), 64.3% compliance-required cases (vs 25.0%) and 92.9% night/high-load work (vs 25.0%). Within strata, review service times are nearly identical (e.g., High: 9.17 vs 9.30h), so the crude gap lives almost entirely in case mix and waiting.

The strongest evidence against declaring this settled: cells are tiny (Orion-Low n=2, Nova-High n=2), shift and group are almost perfectly collinear (only one Orion day case), complexity is a coarse label the routing engine may select within (residual severity), and only completed cases are observed. "Not identifiable" is a defensible fallback; I choose "mainly confounded" because every observable adjustment moves the estimate toward Orion-neutral-or-better, and the confounding direction implied by the routing rule biases against Orion.

The 50% reassignment may therefore fail or backfire: it would push difficult, compliance-bound, night-arriving cases to a group with fewer certified reviewers and no night coverage, likely lengthening those cases and congesting Nova's currently fast low-complexity queue. It could only succeed if Orion's queue itself is the binding constraint and Nova has genuine slack — which the data neither shows nor rules out.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Instrument (weeks 0–4) | No routing change; add event timestamps for compliance start/complete, queue entry/exit, reviewer id; snapshot open cases | Measurement: separate queue_wait from compliance wait; expose censoring | None (logging only) | Read-only; privacy review of reviewer ids | Full event-log rebuild; PM4Py performance DFG per group; open-case age distributions; negative control (Register→Classify) by group | Converts wait_before_review_h into attributable components; tests H5 (selection/measurement) | If logging degrades systems, revert; otherwise no risk |
| 2. Capacity fix, not label change (weeks 2–8) | Add compliance-check capacity/fast-lane at night and a WIP cap on the pre-review queue; keep routing rule intact | Queueing and compliance waiting — where all stratum gaps live | Compliance work-slots and queue policy (not cases, not groups) | Cap applies to both groups equally; policy-required checks never skipped; monitor appeal rate | Before/after wait decomposition within complexity strata; queue-state reconstruction; conformance check that required compliance still occurs | Tests H2/H4: if waits fall for both groups proportionally, mechanism is capacity, not group; a group-specific drop implicates group organization | Roll back if compliance backlog age > threshold or appeals rise above baseline + 2pp |
| 3. Randomized routing pilot (weeks 6–14) | Among the overlap region only (low/medium-complexity cases arriving in day or early-evening windows where both groups are staffed and qualified), randomize 10–20% of eligible cases 50/50 at the routing engine, stratified by complexity and compliance flag | Isolates the resource_group effect from routing_rule selection | Individual case, stratified randomization | Exclude cases requiring certifications only Orion holds; night cases stay with Orion; committee review at week 10 | Per-arm cycle time, wait, service, rework, variant mix and DFGs; CATE by stratum; positivity check before launch | The only design here that identifies a group effect: replaces the confounded routing rule with a coin flip inside the region of common support | Stop if pilot-arm mean cycle time exceeds control by >20% for 2 consecutive weeks, or any compliance breach |
| 4. Staged rebalancing (weeks 14+) | If and only if the pilot shows Nova non-inferior for a stratum, shift 10–15% of that stratum's volume per month, re-estimating each month | Applies learned stratum-specific effects; protects against congestion spillover | Complexity × shift stratum | Night coverage guaranteed; Nova utilization capped at 85%; certified-reviewer ratio floor | Rolling DFG performance comparison; queue-length monitoring in both groups; appeal tracking at 30/90 days | Confirms transportability of pilot estimates under increasing load (effects may not scale linearly with queueing) | Revert the last increment if either group's median wait rises >15% or quality signal deteriorates |

**Final recommendation.** Do not reassign 50% of Orion's cases. The 6.7-hour crude gap is an artifact of a routing policy that sends difficult, compliance-bound, night-arriving work to Orion; within every observed complexity stratum Orion is as fast or faster, and essentially all remaining differences sit in waiting time, not reviewer service. Moving half of Orion's volume would transplant exactly the hard cases into a group with fewer certified reviewers and no night coverage, while congesting Nova's currently fast low-complexity flow — a realistic risk of making both groups slower. Instead: first instrument the process so queueing and compliance delay are separately measurable and censoring is visible (phase 1); attack the actual bottleneck — compliance and pre-review queueing capacity — symmetrically (phase 2); and run a small stratified randomized routing pilot inside the region where both groups genuinely overlap (phase 3). Rebalance volume only in strata where the pilot demonstrates Nova non-inferiority, in 10–15% increments with explicit rollback triggers. This learns the causal quantity management actually needs — the effect of reassignment itself — at a fraction of the operational risk.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Within-label case severity.** The routing engine selects "difficult" cases, and the 3-level complexity label almost certainly hides residual severity that stratification cannot remove. Most valuable addition: a granular pre-routing risk score (e.g., claim amount, policy type, fraud-flag) captured at `Classify` time.

2. **Shift–group collinearity.** With only one Orion day case and four Nova night cases, shift effects and group effects are not separable. Most valuable change: a design change — schedule a small Nova night pod (or occasional Orion day rotation) to create genuine overlap, or at minimum record which groups were eligible for each case.

3. **Survivorship in the completed-case sample.** Long-running open or abandoned cases are invisible and may differ by group, biasing every mean. Most valuable addition: a snapshot table of open/abandoned cases with case age, group, complexity, and current activity.

4. **Composition of `wait_before_review_h`.** It mixes pure queueing with compliance-step waiting, so the dominant driver of all group gaps cannot be attributed to capacity versus policy steps. Most valuable addition: event-level timestamps for compliance start/complete and queue entry/exit per case.

5. **Quality signal weakness.** Two appeal events (one per group) and a 30-day horizon contaminated by post-process factors make the speed–quality trade-off unmeasurable; faster routing could silently degrade outcomes. Most valuable addition: case-linked longer-horizon quality fields (90-day appeal/overturn outcome and payment-accuracy audit result).
