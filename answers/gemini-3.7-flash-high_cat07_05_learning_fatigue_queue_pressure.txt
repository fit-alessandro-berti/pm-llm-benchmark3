# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance patterns in the claims triage and resolution center cannot be explained by any single operational or psychological mechanism. Instead, the empirical data reveals a dynamic multi-phase system where **case-mix selection, cognitive warm-up, queue-induced speed-accuracy tradeoffs, worker experience stratification, and task batching interact across the shift cycle**.

```
Shift Progression & Mechanism Interaction:
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Phase 1: Shift Start (Events 1-15, 0-90 min)                                                      |
|  - High Complexity (0.72 -> 0.61) & Priority (41% -> 34%) from Night/Early Backlog                 |
|  - Senior Resource Selection Bias + Cognitive Orientation/Warm-Up                                   |
|  -> Result: Long Service Times (18.8 -> 15.2 min), Moderate Errors (3.4% -> 2.8%)                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Phase 2: Mid-Shift Steady State (Events 16-50, 90-270 min)                                        |
|  - Stabilized Queue (31-33), Normalized Complexity (0.54 -> 0.50)                                  |
|  - Peak Cognitive Fluency + Stable Processing Cadence                                              |
|  -> Result: Optimal Performance (12.9-13.6 min Service Time, 2.5-2.7% Error, 4.8-5.0% Rework)       |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Phase 3: Late-Shift Congestion & Fatigue (Events 51-76+, 270-480 min)                             |
|  - Queue Pressure Surge (WIP 62 -> 88, especially Day/Late Shifts)                                 |
|  - Speed-Quality Tradeoff + Junior Batching Shortcuts + Cognitive Fatigue                          |
|  - Deferred Quality Check Bursts + Bot_Risk Rushed Overrides                                       |
|  -> Result: Depressed Service Times (11.4 -> 10.8 min), Surging Errors (4.6% -> 7.9%), High Rework |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Phase 1: Shift Opening (Events 1–15, 0–90 Minutes) — Case Mix, Triage Selection, and Warm-up
At the beginning of each shift, mean service times are at their peak (18.8 minutes for events 1–5; 15.2 minutes for events 6–15). A naive operational reading might attribute this solely to worker sluggishness or "warm-up" friction. However, the data demonstrates that this phase is heavily confounded by **case-mix and selection bias**:
1. **Backlog Carryover**: Opening queues are loaded with unresolved complex cases from preceding shifts (Early shift starts with 48% complex cases clearing night carryover; Night shift handles 56% complex urgent exceptions; events 1–5 average a complexity score of 0.72 and 41% high-priority share).
2. **Senior Triage Bias**: Senior resources deliberately select or are assigned these high-complexity, high-priority backlog cases during the first hour. Because high-complexity cases require 22.4 minutes even under baseline conditions, the elevated early service time is predominantly a structural consequence of case mix.
3. **Cognitive Re-orientation**: In conjunction with case difficulty, workers experience genuine warm-up effects as they re-establish situational awareness on open claims, review previous case notes, and re-engage with decision rules. This is evidenced by the drop in error rate from 3.4% (events 1–5) to 2.8% (events 6–15) as workflows stabilize.

### Phase 2: Mid-Shift Equilibrium (Events 16–50, 90–270 Minutes) — True Operational Steady State
Between the second and fourth hours of the shift, the process achieves its operational sweet spot:
- Case complexity normalizes to standard intake levels (0.54 down to 0.50).
- Queue lengths stabilize at moderate, manageable levels (31 to 33 cases).
- Workers have completed warm-up and are operating with high cognitive fluency.
- Service times stabilize between 12.9 and 13.6 minutes, while error rates reach their nadir (2.5%–2.7%) and 7-day rework drops to 4.8%–5.0%. Under these balanced conditions, workers do not experience queue panic, nor have they accumulated debilitating fatigue.

### Phase 3: Late-Shift Congestion (Events 51–76+, 270–480 Minutes) — The Queue Pressure Paradox
In the final hours of the shift, an apparent paradox emerges: **service times fall to their lowest levels (11.4 down to 10.8 minutes), yet error rates and 7-day rework surge exponentially (errors rise to 4.6% and 7.9%; rework reaches 8.2% and 13.7%)**. 

This phenomenon is explained by the convergence of four distinct drivers:
1. **Queue-Pressure-Induced Speedup (The Dominant Driver)**: 
   - Demand accumulates substantially over the day (Day shift queue swells from 38 to 75; Late shift inherits a queue of 70). As mean queue lengths hit 62 (events 51–75) and 88 (events 76+), workers perceive severe backlog pressure.
   - Workers adapt by adopting heuristic processing: truncating validation steps, rushing document verifications, and skipping sanity checks. When WIP > 70, service time on low-complexity cases drops from 12.7 to 9.4 minutes, but errors jump from 2.1% to 5.8%. On high-complexity cases, service time is compressed from 22.4 to 16.8 minutes, causing error rates to spike to 10.9%.
2. **Experience-Differentiated Vulnerability**:
   - *Seniors* possess robust cognitive schema and defensive routines; under high WIP, their error rate rises only modestly from 2.1% to 4.9%.
   - *Juniors*, lacking deep procedural automaticity, suffer severe quality breakdown under pressure: their error rate escalates from 4.4% (low WIP) to 12.4% (high WIP).
3. **Batching Workarounds and Heuristics**:
   - Junior staff (e.g., Junior_H, Junior_I) cope with high WIP by executing sequential batching on repetitive tasks (`Document Review`), running 9–18 consecutive items in rapid succession without switching contexts. While this slashes local service time, it introduces systematic oversight errors across entire batches.
   - When queue pressure exceeds 70, workers interacting with `Bot_Risk` rush their evaluations, leading to erroneous overrides or uncritical acceptances of automated classifications.
4. **End-of-Shift Measurement and Fatigue Artifacts**:
   - Prolonged time-on-task degrades sustained attention, impairing workers' ability to catch subtle anomalies.
   - Crucially, `Quality Check` tasks are routinely postponed until the final 45 minutes of the shift. This structural delay produces an artificial concentration of recorded errors and rework flags at shift end, making late-shift performance appear even more error-prone than it is in real-time.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To rigorously isolate and quantify these competing mechanisms, we structure an end-to-end analytical pipeline combining process mining (PM4Py) and econometrics.

```
Analytical Pipeline Architecture:
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Data Enrichment & Temporal Feature Extraction                                                  |
|    - Compute case-level service, waiting, WIP, and arrival rates via PM4Py                        |
|    - Extract diurnal, shift-relative, and sequence metrics                                         |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. Process Mining & Pattern Discovery                                                             |
|    - Automated Batch Discovery (discover_batches)                                                 |
|    - Performance DFG & Bottleneck Routing (discover_performance_dfg)                               |
|    - Dotted Chart Temporal Dispersion (view_dotted_chart)                                         |
|    - Rework & Loop Attribution (get_rework_cases_per_activity)                                    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. Econometric & Statistical Disentanglement                                                      |
|    - Two-Way Fixed Effects Regression (Resource & Shift FE)                                       |
|    - Propensity Score Matching on Case Complexity Strata                                          |
|    - Queue Threshold Non-Linear Spline Estimation                                                 |
|    - Moderation Models (Experience x WIP x Time-on-Task)                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Statistical & Process Mining Methodologies
1. **Within-Resource Fixed-Effects Models**: Include worker fixed effects ($\alpha_i$) to control for time-invariant individual capabilities, isolating within-worker variation across shift sequence and queue levels.
2. **Complexity-Matched Stratification**: Employ Propensity Score Matching (PSM) on `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `activity_family` to compare service times and error rates across identical case profiles processed at different shift positions.
3. **Queue-Pressure Spline Regressions**: Model `queue_length_at_start` and `wip_at_start` as restricted cubic splines to detect non-linear tipping points (e.g., WIP > 70) where error rates accelerate.
4. **Batching & Sequence Clustering**: Use PM4Py batch discovery algorithms to detect activity clustering, quantifying batch size, inter-event gap, and batch-associated error propagation.
5. **Downstream Defect Propagation**: Trace cases from initial execution through `Quality Check` and `Rework Correction` to measure the true temporal latency between error occurrence and error detection.

### Python / PM4Py Implementation Script

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

def run_comprehensive_claims_analysis(log_csv_path: str):
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Load and Format Event Log
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    df = pd.read_csv(log_csv_path)
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    df['start_timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['start_timestamp'])
    df['shift_start_time'] = pd.to_datetime(df['shift_start_time'])
    
    # Format for PM4Py compliance
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
        start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp'
    )
    
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. PM4Py Feature Enrichments
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # Calculate case-level service time and waiting time
    df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        df,
        case_id_column='case:concept:name',
        timestamp_column='time:timestamp',
        start_timestamp_column='start_timestamp'
    )
    
    # Calculate arrival and finish rates across log
    df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(
        df,
        case_id_column='case:concept:name',
        timestamp_column='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # Extract temporal features (hour of day, day of week, elapsed seconds)
    df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df,
        timestamp_column='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # Calculate precise event service duration in minutes
    df['service_time_min'] = (df['time:timestamp'] - df['start_timestamp']).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Process Mining Discovery & Diagnostics
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # Discover sequential and concurrent batching behaviors
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
        df,
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
        start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp',
        resource_key='org:resource',
        merge_distance=180, # 3-minute threshold
        min_batch_size=4
    )
    print(f"[PM4Py] Discovered {len(batches)} activity batches across resources.")
    
    # Discover Performance Directly-Follows Graph (DFG)
    perf_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)
    
    # Rework analysis per activity
    rework_per_activity = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
    print("[PM4Py] Rework occurrences per activity:\n", rework_per_activity)
    
    # Visualizations for operational distributions
    pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(df, "events_distribution.png", distribution_type="days_weeks")
    pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(df, "dotted_chart_shift.png", attributes=['org:resource', 'activity_family'])
    
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. Stratified Filtering & Cohort Comparisons
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # Filter by WIP regimes to test queue pressure directly
    low_wip_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, 'wip_at_start', [w for w in df['wip_at_start'].unique() if w < 30])
    high_wip_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, 'wip_at_start', [w for w in df['wip_at_start'].unique() if w > 70])
    
    # Filter paths to evaluate Bot_Risk handovers
    bot_risk_events = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, 'org:resource', ['Bot_Risk'])
    
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. Econometric Modeling & Disentanglement
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # Categorize experience groups
    def assign_tier(resource):
        if 'Senior' in resource: return 'Senior'
        if 'Mid' in resource: return 'Mid'
        if 'Junior' in resource: return 'Junior'
        if 'Night' in resource: return 'Night'
        return 'Bot'
        
    df['experience_group'] = df['org:resource'].apply(assign_tier)
    df['high_wip_flag'] = (df['wip_at_start'] > 70).astype(int)
    df['late_shift_flag'] = (df['minutes_since_shift_start'] > 300).astype(int)
    
    # Model A: Service Time Determinants (Log-Linear Fixed Effects)
    # Tests whether speedup is driven by complexity, sequence (warmup), queue, or late-shift rush
    model_service = smf.ols(
        "np.log(service_time_min + 0.1) ~ case_complexity_score + priority + "
        "sequence_position_within_shift + minutes_since_shift_start + "
        "wip_at_start + C(shift_id) + C(activity_family) + C(org:resource)",
        data=df[df['experience_group'] != 'Bot']
    ).fit(cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df.loc[df['experience_group'] != 'Bot', 'shift_id']})
    
    print("\n================== SERVICE TIME DETERMINANTS ==================")
    print(model_service.summary().tables[1])
    
    # Model B: Error Rate Determinants (Logistic Regression with Interactions)
    # Disentangles fatigue vs queue pressure vs experience moderation
    model_error = smf.logit(
        "error_flag ~ case_complexity_score + minutes_since_shift_start + "
        "wip_at_start + wip_at_start:C(experience_group) + "
        "minutes_since_shift_start:wip_at_start + C(activity_family) + C(shift_id)",
        data=df[df['experience_group'] != 'Bot']
    ).fit(disp=False)
    
    print("\n================== ERROR RATE DETERMINANTS (LOGIT) ==================")
    print(model_error.summary().tables[1])
    
    # Model C: Downstream 7-Day Rework Probability
    model_rework = smf.logit(
        "rework_flag ~ error_flag + wip_at_start + case_complexity_score + "
        "sequence_position_within_shift + C(experience_group)",
        data=df[df['experience_group'] != 'Bot']
    ).fit(disp=False)
    
    print("\n================== REWORK PROBABILITY MODEL ==================")
    print(model_rework.summary().tables[1])
    
    return {
        "model_service": model_service,
        "model_error": model_error,
        "model_rework": model_rework,
        "batches": batches,
        "rework_per_activity": rework_per_activity
    }
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Evaluating human performance in high-pressure operational environments requires extreme methodological and managerial caution. Naive interpretations of event logs frequently lead to invalid, unfair, and counterproductive conclusions:

```
Sources of Performance Distortion & Systemic Traps:
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Selection Bias & Task Allocation Inequity                                                      |
|     - Seniors handle disproportionate early complex backlog; Juniors handle queue-spillover.      |
|     - Unadjusted comparisons penalize seniors for complexity and juniors for volume pressure.      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. Measurement Artifacts & Temporal Displacement                                                  |
|     - Quality Checks batched at shift end log defects hours after their actual occurrence.          |
|     - Latent errors falsely attributed to late-shift fatigue rather than mid-shift root causes.    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3. Management-Induced Systemic Queue Pressure                                                     |
|     - High WIP (>70) is a structural capacity failure, not an individual behavioral flaw.          |
|     - Speed-accuracy tradeoffs represent rational adaptation to unmanaged intake spikes.           |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                               |
                                               v
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  4. Automation Misattribution & Goodhart's Law                                                     |
|     - Flawed Bot_Risk classifications force human rework under extreme queue pressure.             |
|     - Punitive error metrics induce gaming, hidden defects, data falsification, and attrition.     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Selection Bias and Task Allocation Inequity
Comparing raw service times or error rates across workers without rigorous risk-adjustment is methodologically flawed:
- **Senior Resource Load**: Senior staff systematically absorb the initial shock of highly complex carryover cases (complexity 0.72, 41% high priority). Evaluating them against unadjusted speed metrics penalizes them for solving the organization's hardest problems.
- **Junior Overflow Trapping**: Junior staff are disproportionately exposed to late-day queue surges where WIP exceeds 70. Attributing their elevated error rates (12.4%) entirely to individual incompetence ignores the reality that the operational system placed them in a high-risk failure regime without structural safeguards.

### 2. Measurement Artifacts and Temporal Displacement
Event timestamps in process logs record when an activity was logged, which often diverges from when a defect was created:
- **Batched Quality Checks**: Because `Quality Check` tasks are routinely postponed to the final 45 minutes of a shift, error flags and rework triggers are clustered at shift end. Attributing these errors to "late-shift worker exhaustion" confuses the **time of detection** with the **time of occurrence**.
- **Downstream Rework Latency**: 7-day rework events (`Rework within 7 days`) reflect systemic downstream failures that may stem from incomplete documentation during intake, ambiguous triage rules, or client miscommunication, rather than simple operator error at the immediate event step.

### 3. Management-Induced Systemic Queue Pressure
When WIP exceeds 70, workers face an impossible double bind: maintain thoroughness and watch the queue spiral out of control, or accelerate throughput to prevent operational collapse and suffer elevated errors. 
- High queue pressure is an **architectural capacity and scheduling failure**, not an individual disciplinary issue.
- Penalizing workers for speed-accuracy tradeoffs that are directly induced by under-staffed shifts constitutes a severe management failure that misdiagnoses systemic friction as personal negligence.

### 4. Automation Quality and Human-in-the-Loop Friction
- `Bot_Risk` executes instantaneously, but its automated classifications exhibit higher defect rates during high-volume spikes.
- When human workers are overwhelmed by queues, their capacity to critically audit bot recommendations collapses. The resulting errors represent a failure of the **human-automation interface and handoff protocol**, yet traditional logging frameworks attribute the rework flag entirely to the human resource who signed off on the case.

### 5. Goodhart's Law and the Dangers of Punitive Error Metrics
Instituting punitive measures based on raw late-shift error rates (Intervention 8) inevitably triggers **Goodhart's Law** ("When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"):
- **Gaming and Hidden Queues**: Workers will avoid logging `Close Case` or `Quality Check` events, park difficult cases in unmonitored statuses, or trade cases informally to manipulate their individual scorecards.
- **Defect Concealment**: Workers will stop flagging rework internally, allowing defective claims to escape to downstream customers rather than reporting corrections.
- **Attrition and Demoralization**: Punishing front-line staff for workload-induced stress accelerates burnout, drives turnover among promising junior workers, and destroys institutional trust.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

To enable real-time operational control and fair, root-cause-oriented process management, we specify an enterprise process-mining dashboard comprising **nine distinct indicators**.

```
Process-Mining Operational Dashboard Layout:
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PANEL 1: FLOW VELOCITY & CONGESTION                                                                |
| [1. Complexity-Adjusted Service Time (CAST)]       [2. Queue-to-Throughput Pressure Index]         |
| Log-linear standardized duration vs. expected      Active WIP & queue length with >70 alert zone   |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PANEL 2: QUALITY & DEFECT ESCAPE                                                                   |
| [3. Immediate Error vs. 7-Day Rework Latency]      [4. End-of-Shift Defect Concentration Ratio]    |
| In-line error flags vs. downstream escapes         Detection timing vs. creation time lag          |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PANEL 3: CASE-MIX & FAIRNESS ALLOCATION                                                            |
| [5. Case-Mix & Complexity Allocation Index]        [6. Worker Load & Complexity Equity Ratio]      |
| Inflow complexity distribution across shifts       Gini coefficient of difficulty across tiers     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PANEL 4: WORKAROUNDS & AUTOMATION GOVERNANCE                                                       |
| [7. Batching Intensity & Cluster Index]            [8. Automation Override & Reversion Rate]       |
| Sequential repetition size and cadence             Bot_Risk classification override accuracy       |
| [9. Shift-Boundary Handover Leakage Rate]                                                          |
| Unresolved carryover aging & queue transfer                                                        |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Indicator Specifications

#### 1. Complexity-Adjusted Service Time (CAST)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{CAST}_{i,j} = \text{Actual Service Time}_{i,j} - \mathbb{E}[\text{Service Time} \mid \text{Activity}_j, \text{Complexity}_i, \text{Priority}_i]$$
  Calculated using baseline expected duration derived from low-WIP, experienced-worker benchmarks.
- **Visualization Type**: Control Chart (Shewhart $X$-bar chart) plotting moving average of residuals across shift hours.
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{CAST} < -3.5\text{ min}$ (indicates severe rushing/cutting corners) or $\text{CAST} > +5.0\text{ min}$ (indicates operational blockage).
- **Operational Action**: If CAST falls below threshold under high WIP, trigger real-time quality sampling on affected cases.

#### 2. Queue-to-Throughput Pressure Index (QTPI)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{QTPI}_t = \frac{\text{WIP}_t + \text{Queue Length}_t}{\text{Active FTE Staffing}_t \times \text{Standard Hourly Completion Rate}}$$
- **Visualization Type**: Real-time Gauge and Time-Series Heatmap color-coded Green ($<1.0$), Amber ($1.0–1.5$), Red ($>1.5$).
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{QTPI} > 1.4$ or absolute $\text{WIP} > 70$ for $>20$ consecutive minutes.
- **Operational Action**: Automatically trigger dynamic load-balancing: divert non-urgent intake to holding queues, activate senior on-call support, and notify team leads.

#### 3. Immediate Error vs. 7-Day Downstream Rework Gap
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{Escape Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cases with Rework within 7 Days but No In-Shift Error Flag}}{\text{Total Cases with Rework within 7 Days}}$$
- **Visualization Type**: Dual-axis Trend Line comparing in-shift `error_flag` rate against lagged 7-day `rework_flag` rate across sequence bins.
- **Alert Trigger**: Escape Ratio $> 0.65$ or 7-day rework $> 8.0\%$ in any sequence position bin.
- **Operational Action**: Initiate root-cause review of upstream verification checklists; audit quality of initial triage decisions.

#### 4. End-of-Shift Defect Discovery Concentration Ratio (DDCR)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{DDCR} = \frac{\text{Defects Detected in Final 45 Minutes of Shift}}{\text{Total Defects Detected Across Full 8-Hour Shift}}$$
- **Visualization Type**: Hourly Stacked Bar Chart showing defect discovery time vs. original event processing timestamp.
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{DDCR} > 0.40$ (indicates systematic postponement of `Quality Check` activities).
- **Operational Action**: Mandate distributed intra-shift quality verification cadences (e.g., automated sampling after every 15 completed cases).

#### 5. Case-Mix Inflow Index (CMI)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{CMI}_{\text{shift, hour}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^N \text{case\_complexity\_score}_k}{N}$$
- **Visualization Type**: Area Chart displaying incoming and processed complexity profiles by shift hour (Early, Day, Late, Night).
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{CMI} > 0.65$ during hours with $<25\%$ senior staffing coverage.
- **Operational Action**: Re-route high-complexity claims to designated senior queues or adjust shift staffing overlays to cover peak complexity windows.

#### 6. Worker-Load & Complexity Equity Ratio (Equity Gini)
- **Calculation Logic**: Gini coefficient of the cumulative complexity points handled per active resource within each shift:
  $$G = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n |c_i - c_j|}{2n^2 \bar{c}}$$
  where $c_i$ is the total complexity handled by resource $i$.
- **Visualization Type**: Lorenz Curve and Resource Radar Chart showing complexity share vs. volume share.
- **Alert Trigger**: $G > 0.35$ within any single shift team.
- **Operational Action**: Recalibrate automated dispatching rules to ensure equitable distribution of difficult cases across available qualified staff.

#### 7. Batching Intensity & Consecutive Cluster Index (BICI)
- **Calculation Logic**: Number of consecutive identical activities executed by a single resource with inter-event gap $<180\text{ seconds}$ and identical queue batch IDs:
  $$\text{BICI} = \text{Length of Monotonous Activity Sequence}$$
- **Visualization Type**: PM4Py Dotted Chart highlighting repetitive single-activity streaks in bright red.
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{BICI} \ge 8$ consecutive events for non-triage activities (`Document Review`, `Eligibility Decision`).
- **Operational Action**: Prompt user in the workflow UI to alternate tasks; inject a mandatory 60-second cognitive reset after batch completion.

#### 8. Automation Override & Reversion Rate (AORR)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{AORR} = \frac{\text{Human Overrides of Bot\_Risk Decisions Requiring Subsequent Rework}}{\text{Total Human Overrides of Bot\_Risk Decisions}}$$
- **Visualization Type**: 2x2 Confusion Matrix and Trend Line tracking override accuracy stratified by WIP regime ($<30$ vs. $>70$).
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{AORR} > 15.0\%$ when $\text{WIP} > 70$.
- **Operational Action**: Restrict manual overrides of automated risk classifications during high-WIP conditions unless accompanied by mandatory senior co-signature.

#### 9. Shift-Boundary Handover Leakage Rate (SHLR)
- **Calculation Logic**: 
  $$\text{SHLR} = \frac{\text{Open Cases Active in Final 30 Minutes Carried to Next Shift}}{\text{Total Case Intake of Shift}}$$
- **Visualization Type**: Sankey Diagram showing case flows and carryover volume across shift boundaries (Early $\rightarrow$ Day $\rightarrow$ Late $\rightarrow$ Night $\rightarrow$ Early).
- **Alert Trigger**: $\text{SHLR} > 0.30$ or carryover backlog aging $> 120\text{ minutes}$.
- **Operational Action**: Convene a structured 10-minute shift handover huddle between outgoing and incoming leads to prioritize aged carryover before new intake is opened.
