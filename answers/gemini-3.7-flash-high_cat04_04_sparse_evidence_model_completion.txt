### Why Bidirectional DFG Edges Do Not Prove Concurrency

In Directly-Follows Graphs (DFGs), the existence of bidirectional edges between two activities—such as $(\text{Credit Check}, \text{Engineering Review})$ with frequency 17 and $(\text{Engineering Review}, \text{Credit Check})$ with frequency 11—is frequently misinterpreted as conclusive proof of concurrent execution. However, an event log recording only point completion timestamps produces observational equivalence across multiple distinct underlying behavioral phenomena:

1. **True Concurrency**: Activities $A$ and $B$ are executed independently in parallel. Because their duration distributions overlap, whichever finishes first is recorded first, producing both $(A, B)$ and $(B, A)$ completions.
2. **Mutually Exclusive Sequential Routing (XOR Choice)**: A hidden context attribute (e.g., Order Type, Customer Tier, or Value) routes cases sequentially: Type 1 cases execute $A \to B$, while Type 2 cases execute $B \to A$. Neither individual case involves concurrency.
3. **Looping and Rework**: A single case executes $A \to B \to A$. This generates transitions in both directions within the exact same process instance.
4. **Ingestion and Quantization Jitter**: Two activities completing almost simultaneously receive identical system timestamps and are serialized arbitrarily based on ingestion queue index (as explicitly noted in **S7**).

Therefore, bidirectional DFG transitions provide an inductive hint, but domain rules (**D3**, **D6**), stakeholder insights (**S2**), and data engineering constraints (**S7**) must be triangulated to establish defensible causal models.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

Across all three candidate models, the following structural, semantic, and data-quality assumptions are held constant:

1. **Event-Completion Semantics**: As established by **S7** and **D7**, logged events represent atomic, point-in-time completions rather than interval durations. The observed sequence reflects the completion order, not the start or execution span of tasks.
2. **Missing Attribute Abstraction**: The business condition determining whether an order uses stocked vs. external components (**S3**, **S4**) was omitted during export (**S5**). Consequently, data-driven guards cannot be modeled. All candidates abstract this domain condition as a non-deterministic control-flow routing choice.
3. **Data Loss and Optionality Unification**: Because 5–10% of `Supplier Confirmation` events were dropped due to an integration glitch (**S5**) alongside legitimate bypasses for stocked components (**S4**), `Supplier Confirmation` is represented across all models as an optional construct (an `XOR` branch with a silent transition $\tau$).
4. **Expedite Review Optionality**: `Expedite Review` is an infrequent, optional activity (**D6**, **S6**) that occurs at most once per case, modeled via a bypassable silent branch.
5. **Strict Process Bookends**: In accordance with high-confidence rule **D1**, `Register Order` is the unique entry point, and `Archive Case` is the unique terminal activity for every case.
6. **Immutable Terminal Pipeline**: In accordance with high-confidence rule **D2**, once configuration is approved, manufacturing release and archival proceed strictly sequentially: `Approve Configuration` $\to$ `Release Production` $\to$ `Archive Case`.
7. **Single-Instance Execution (Acyclic Workflow)**: In accordance with domain rules and empirical log evidence (no activity occurs more than once in any of the 30 traces), every activity executes at most once per case instance.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### Inductive Bias Statement
The conservative model adopts a strict closed-world empirical bias. It posits that only the exact trace variants observed in the historical event log reflect authorized, executable process behavior. It rejects inductive generalization across concurrent permutations, treating unobserved interleavings as potentially unsafe or non-compliant anomalies.

### Compact Formal Expression
$$\text{model\_conservative} = \text{Register Order} \to \left( \bigoplus_{k=1}^{7} \sigma_k \right) \to \text{Approve Configuration} \to \text{Release Production} \to \text{Archive Case}$$

where the 7 mutually exclusive sub-sequences correspond to the observed pre-approval variants:
- $\sigma_1 = \langle \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC} \rangle$
- $\sigma_2 = \langle \text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{SC} \rangle$
- $\sigma_3 = \langle \text{CC}, \text{ER} \rangle$
- $\sigma_4 = \langle \text{ER}, \text{CC} \rangle$
- $\sigma_5 = \langle \text{CC}, \text{SC}, \text{ER} \rangle$
- $\sigma_6 = \langle \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}, \text{ExR} \rangle$
- $\sigma_7 = \langle \text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{ExR}, \text{SC} \rangle$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree

conservative_tree_str = """
->( 'Register Order',
    X(
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )
    ),
    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case'
)
"""
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(conservative_tree_str)
```

### Deliberately Excluded Behavior
1. **Symmetric Expedite Interleavings**: Excludes `RO -> ER -> CC -> SC -> ExR -> AC -> RP -> ArC` (the direct concurrency symmetry of V6).
2. **Stocked Expedited Orders**: Excludes `RO -> CC -> ER -> ExR -> AC -> RP -> ArC` and `RO -> ER -> CC -> ExR -> AC -> RP -> ArC` (orders skipping `SC` due to stocked parts but requiring expedite).
3. **Symmetric Pre-Review Supplier Confirmations**: Excludes `RO -> ER -> SC -> CC -> AC -> RP -> ArC` (the symmetry of V5 where `SC` completes before `CC`).

### Known Underfitting Risks
- **Extreme Overfitting to Sample Artifacts**: Fails to generalize to legitimate operational permutations arising from concurrent work.
- **Structural Redundancy**: Duplicates task labels across 7 discrete branches (25 Petri net transitions for 8 distinct activities), artificially inflating model size and lowering generalization metrics ($\approx 0.315$).
- **High False-Rejection Rate**: Rejects future valid cases simply because their specific interleaving was not among the 30 sampled instances.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### Inductive Bias Statement
The balanced candidate adopts an abductive, partial-order inductive bias. It balances empirical observations with domain rules by modeling `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` as concurrent principal checks, allowing `Supplier Confirmation` to proceed once `Credit Check` completes (concurrently with `Engineering Review`), and constraining `Expedite Review` to require completion of both principal checks.

### Decision Rationale: Sequence, Concurrency, and Partial Order
1. **Credit Check and Engineering Review ($\text{AND}$ Concurrency)**: S1's assertion of strict sequence is disproven by 11 cases where `ER` finishes before `CC`. S2 and S7 explain that both checks run in parallel, and point-logging serializes completion. D3 requires both to finish before `AC`. Hence, `CC` and `ER` are concurrent branches spawned by `RO`.
2. **Supplier Confirmation (Partial Order downstream of CC)**: In V5 (2 cases), `SC` completes before `ER` finishes (`CC -> SC -> ER`), validating D5 that `SC` does not need to wait for both checks. However, in all observed cases with `SC`, `CC` completed before `SC`. Thus, `SC` is modeled as an optional branch conditionally enabled by `CC`, executing concurrently with `ER`.
3. **Expedite Review (Partial Order downstream of AND(CC, ER))**: D6 and S6 explicitly require completion of both principal checks before `ExR` is permitted. Observed traces V6 and V7 show `ExR` can precede or follow `SC`. Thus, `ExR` depends on both `CC` and `ER`, but remains unordered relative to `SC`.
4. **Approve Configuration (Synchronization Join)**: `AC` acts as the join milestone, synchronizing `ER`, `opt(SC)`, and `opt(ExR)`.

### Compact Formal Expression
$$\text{POWL} = \langle N, < \rangle$$
where $N = \{ \text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{opt\_SC}, \text{opt\_ExR}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{ArC} \}$ with $\text{opt\_SC} = \text{SC} \oplus \tau$, $\text{opt\_ExR} = \text{ExR} \oplus \tau$, and the strict partial order $<$ is the transitive closure of:
$$\{ (\text{RO}, \text{CC}), (\text{RO}, \text{ER}), (\text{CC}, \text{opt\_SC}), (\text{CC}, \text{opt\_ExR}), (\text{ER}, \text{opt\_ExR}), (\text{ER}, \text{AC}), (\text{opt\_SC}, \text{AC}), (\text{opt\_ExR}, \text{AC}), (\text{AC}, \text{RP}), (\text{RP}, \text{ArC}) \}$$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Define activity transitions
t_ro = Transition(label='Register Order')
t_cc = Transition(label='Credit Check')
t_er = Transition(label='Engineering Review')
t_sc = Transition(label='Supplier Confirmation')
tau_sc = SilentTransition()
opt_sc = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_sc, tau_sc])

t_ex = Transition(label='Expedite Review')
tau_ex = SilentTransition()
opt_ex = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_ex, tau_ex])

t_ac = Transition(label='Approve Configuration')
t_rp = Transition(label='Release Production')
t_ar = Transition(label='Archive Case')

# Construct StrictPartialOrder POWL model
model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_ro, t_cc, t_er, opt_sc, opt_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar])
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, opt_ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_sc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_ex, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)
```

### Deliberately Excluded Behavior
1. **Premature Expedite Review**: Excludes traces where `ExR` executes before `CC` or `ER` completes (enforcing compliance rule **S6** / **D6**).
2. **Early Supplier Confirmation without Credit Check**: Excludes `RO -> SC -> CC -> ...` where procurement commits before credit clearance.
3. **Bypassing Core Checks**: Excludes cases reaching `AC` without both `CC` and `ER` completing.

### Known Underfitting Risks
- **Engineering-Triggered Supplier Confirmation**: If domain rule D5 was intended to allow `SC` to be triggered solely by `ER` (e.g., `RO -> ER -> SC -> CC -> ...`), the balanced model excludes this trace because `SC` is strictly ordered after `CC`.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### Inductive Bias Statement
The permissive candidate adopts a maximum-entropy, unconstrained concurrency bias within macro milestones. It assumes that all pre-approval validations (`Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, optional `Supplier Confirmation`, and optional `Expedite Review`) can execute in arbitrary parallel interleavings once an order is registered, joining strictly before configuration approval.

### Boundary: Reasonable Generalization vs. Forbidden Behavior
- **Admitted (Reasonable Generalization)**: Any interleaving of $\{ \text{CC}, \text{ER}, [\text{SC}], [\text{ExR}] \}$ between `Register Order` and `Approve Configuration`. This admits cases where `SC` starts immediately upon registration or after `ER`, and cases where `ExR` runs concurrently with principal checks.
- **Forbidden (Strict Inviolable Invariants)**:
  1. `Approve Configuration` cannot execute until `CC` and `ER` (and `SC`/`ExR` if initiated) have completed (**D3**, **D4**).
  2. `Release Production` cannot precede `Approve Configuration` (**D2**).
  3. `Archive Case` cannot precede `Release Production` (**D1**, **D2**).
  4. Mandatory activities (`RO`, `CC`, `ER`, `AC`, `RP`, `ArC`) cannot be omitted.
  5. No activity can execute more than once (no loops/rework).

### Compact Formal Expression
$$\text{model\_permissive} = \text{Register Order} \to \Big( \text{Credit Check} \parallel \text{Engineering Review} \parallel (\text{Supplier Confirmation} \oplus \tau) \parallel (\text{Expedite Review} \oplus \tau) \Big) \to \text{Approve Configuration} \to \text{Release Production} \to \text{Archive Case}$$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree

permissive_tree_str = """
->( 'Register Order',
    +( 'Credit Check',
       'Engineering Review',
       X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ),
       X( 'Expedite Review', tau )
    ),
    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case'
)
"""
model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(permissive_tree_str)
```

### Deliberately Excluded Behavior
1. **Post-Approval Inversions**: Strictly excludes `RO -> ... -> AC -> ArC -> RP` or `RO -> ... -> RP -> AC`.
2. **Missing Core Checks**: Strictly excludes `RO -> CC -> AC -> RP -> ArC` (omitting mandatory `ER`).
3. **Pre-Registration Execution**: Strictly excludes any task firing before `Register Order`.

### Known Underfitting and Precision Risks
- **Underfitting**: Zero underfitting risk; admits all 7 observed variants and all theoretical concurrent permutations.
- **Severe Precision Risk (Over-Generalization)**: Admits traces violating compliance rule **S6** (e.g., `ExR` firing before `CC` or `ER` has even started), reducing precision to $\approx 0.702$.

# 8. Validation Code

The following self-contained Python script reconstructs the event log, converts all three models to accepting Petri nets, verifies structural workflow soundness, computes fitness, precision, generalization, and simplicity, conducts bounded playout, and tests a positive and negative trace suite.

> **API Note**: Playout on finite/bounded acyclic models generates the reachable trace language; however, for models with unbounded loops, sampled playout does not prove formal language inclusion. Metrics are computed using standard PM4Py alignment and token-based replay algorithms.

```python

    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,

from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Suppress deprecation warnings for cleaner benchmark output
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

# ==============================================================================

v1 = "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Supplier Confirmation,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"
v2 = "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"
v3 = "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"
v4 = "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"
v5 = "Register Order,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,Engineering Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"

v7 = "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Expedite Review,Supplier Confirmation,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"

trace_collection = (
    [v1] * 12 + [v2] * 7 + [v3] * 4 + [v4] * 3 + [v5] * 2 + [v6] * 1 + [v7] * 1

log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_collection)

# ==============================================================================
# 2. DEFINE CANDIDATE MODELS
# ==============================================================================

# Candidate 1: Conservative (ProcessTree XOR Enumeration)
conservative_tree_str = """
->( 'Register Order',
    X(
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' ),
       ->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' ),
       ->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )
    ),
    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case'
)
"""
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(conservative_tree_str)

# Candidate 2: Balanced (POWL Partial Order)
t_ro = Transition(label='Register Order')
t_cc = Transition(label='Credit Check')
t_er = Transition(label='Engineering Review')
t_sc = Transition(label='Supplier Confirmation')
tau_sc = SilentTransition()
opt_sc = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_sc, tau_sc])
t_ex = Transition(label='Expedite Review')
tau_ex = SilentTransition()
opt_ex = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_ex, tau_ex])
t_ac = Transition(label='Approve Configuration')
t_rp = Transition(label='Release Production')
t_ar = Transition(label='Archive Case')

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t_ro, t_cc, t_er, opt_sc, opt_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar]
)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, opt_ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_sc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_ex, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)

# Candidate 3: Permissive (ProcessTree Parallel Block)
permissive_tree_str = """
->( 'Register Order',
    +( 'Credit Check',
       'Engineering Review',
       X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ),
       X( 'Expedite Review', tau )
    ),
    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case'
)
"""
model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(permissive_tree_str)

candidates = {
    'Conservative': model_conservative,
    'Balanced': model_balanced,
    'Permissive': model_permissive,
}

# ==============================================================================
# 3. DEFINE POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE TEST SUITES
# ==============================================================================
positive_test_cases = [
    # Observed V1
    "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Supplier Confirmation,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
    # Observed V5
    "Register Order,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,Engineering Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
    # Unobserved Plausible 1: Stocked component order requesting Expedite Review
    "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Expedite Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
    # Unobserved Plausible 2: ER then CC, SC then ExR
    "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,Expedite Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
]

negative_test_cases = [
    # Violates D1: Process starts with Credit Check before Register Order
    "Credit Check,Register Order,Engineering Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
    # Violates D2: Release Production executes prior to Approve Configuration
    "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Release Production,Approve Configuration,Archive Case",
    # Violates D3: Mandatory Engineering Review omitted entirely
    "Register Order,Credit Check,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
    # Violates S6/D6: Expedite Review executes prior to Credit Check and Engineering Review
    "Register Order,Expedite Review,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case",
]

pos_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(positive_test_cases)
neg_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(negative_test_cases)

# ==============================================================================
# 4. EXECUTE VALIDATION AND BENCHMARKING
# ==============================================================================
evaluation_results = []

for name, model in candidates.items():
    # Convert to accepting Petri Net
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    
    # Soundness verification
    is_sound, _ = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    
    # Core conformance metrics
    fitness_dict = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    precision_val = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    generalization_val = pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)
    simplicity_val = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    
    # Bounded playout analysis
    playout_traces = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
    distinct_playout_count = len(
        set(tuple(x['concept:name'] for x in tr) for tr in playout_traces)
    )
    
    # Test suite conformance
    pos_res = pm4py.fitness_alignments(pos_log, net, im, fm)
    neg_res = pm4py.fitness_alignments(neg_log, net, im, fm)
    
    evaluation_results.append({
        'Model': name,
        'Sound': is_sound,
        'Places': len(net.places),
        'Transitions': len(net.transitions),
        'Fitness': fitness_dict['log_fitness'],
        'Precision': precision_val,
        'Generalization': generalization_val,
        'Simplicity': simplicity_val,
        'Playout Language Size': distinct_playout_count,
        'Positive Suite Fit %': pos_res['percentage_of_fitting_traces'],
        'Negative Suite Fit %': neg_res['percentage_of_fitting_traces'],
    })

# Render validation results table
df_eval = pd.DataFrame(evaluation_results)
print(df_eval.to_string(index=False))
```

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

### Calibrated Confidence Assessment

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------+
| Modeling Decision                                                 | Calibrated Conf.   |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------+
| 1. Register Order as unique start / Archive Case as unique end    | High (>98%)        |
| 2. Terminal sequence: Approve Config -> Release Prod -> Archive   | High (>98%)        |
| 3. Mandatory participation of both Credit Check and Eng Review    | High (95%)         |
| 4. Concurrency between Credit Check and Engineering Review        | Medium-High (85%)  |
| 5. Expedite Review requiring completion of both CC and ER         | Medium-High (85%)  |
| 6. Supplier Confirmation optionality (stocked vs external)        | Medium-High (80%)  |
| 7. Supplier Confirmation dependency strictly tied to Credit Check | Medium-Low (55%)   |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------+
```

### Plausible Alternative Interpretations

1. **Alternative 1: Data-Driven Sequential Routing (Hidden Sub-Processes)**
   - *Hypothesis*: Finance Manager **S1** was correct regarding a subset of orders. High-credit-risk orders strictly require `CC` before `ER` can be dispatched to prevent wasted engineering labor; conversely, complex custom engineering orders strictly require `ER` before `CC` to calculate accurate pricing.
   - *Material Impact*: If verified, the observed bidirectional transitions do not represent concurrency at all, but rather two mutually exclusive, data-dependent sequential pathways ($\text{XOR}(\text{CC} \to \text{ER}, \text{ER} \to \text{CC})$). Modeling this as concurrency over-generalizes by allowing interleaved states that never physically occur.

2. **Alternative 2: Engineering BOM Trigger for Supplier Confirmation**
   - *Hypothesis*: Procurement cannot request supplier confirmation without engineering specifications. Under this interpretation, `ER` produces a preliminary Bill of Materials (BOM) early in its lifecycle. In Variant 5 (`CC -> SC -> ER`), `ER` had already started and yielded BOM data before `CC` finished, but `ER`'s formal sign-off was logged last.
   - *Material Impact*: `Supplier Confirmation` causally depends on `Engineering Review` rather than `Credit Check`. This would require shifting the enabling edge in the partial order from $(\text{CC}, \text{opt\_SC})$ to $(\text{ER}, \text{opt\_SC})$.
