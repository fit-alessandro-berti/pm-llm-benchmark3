def add_business_days(ts, n=2):
    day = ts.normalize()
    added=0
    while added < n:
       day = day + pd.Timedelta(days=1)
       if day.dayofweek < 5:
          added += 1
    return day + (ts - ts.normalize())

## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation**  
For each case:  
`human(Draft_Change_Request) != human(Approve_Change)` after alias normalization.  
If the actor is a service account and cannot be reliably attributed to an authorized human, the check is not conformed and is marked indeterminate or a data/identity problem.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation**  
For each case:  
`human(Implement_Change) != human(Verify_Implementation)` after alias normalization and service-account attribution.  
A service account may satisfy this only if reliable session evidence maps it to a human who is not the implementer.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority**  
For non-emergency HIGH-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by:
1. a human with standing QM role at the event timestamp, or  
2. a valid QM delegate at the event timestamp.

A QM delegation is valid only if all of the following are true at the approval timestamp:
- the effective interval covers the event;
- the scope covers the case scope;
- the principal is absent or formally unavailable if the delegation requires absence;
- no prohibited subdelegation occurred;
- the evidence supports the delegation as of the event time.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution**  
For HIGH-risk emergency cases, a justified exception exists only when:
- the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible;
- `Approve_Change` is performed by the IC or a valid IC delegate;
- implementation occurs after approval;
- `Retrospective_QM_Review` is performed by the QM or a valid QM delegate within two business days.

Business-day counting: business days are Monday through Friday. The site calendar has no holidays in the period. The deadline is the second business day after the approval date. For conservative testing, I use the same clock time on the second subsequent business day; if the policy allows end-of-day, the conclusion for the cases here is unchanged.  
Example: approval on Tuesday 2026-08-11 has a two-business-day deadline on Thursday 2026-08-13. Friday 2026-08-14 is late. Wednesday 2026-08-12 is timely.

If the emergency flag or safety rationale is not credible, RR4 does not apply and RR3 governs approval.

**RR5 — Identity evidence**  
A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance only if reliable session evidence maps the action to an authorized human. Aliases belonging to the same human must be normalized before SoD checks.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof**  
Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, concentrated handovers, and unusual network patterns are indicators. They are not proof of collusion without supporting evidence such as credential sharing, falsified records, financial benefit, quality degradation, or deliberate bypass.

**Action by a principal while on leave**  
A principal’s own authenticated action while on leave is not automatically unauthorized. Leave is an availability control, not necessarily a role revocation. However:
- if the action is performed by the principal’s account and availability cannot be established, the identity is indeterminate;
- if a delegation requires the principal to be absent, a live authenticated principal session can undermine the “absent” condition for that time window;
- if the principal’s account is used by another person, the action is an identity/credential problem and potentially unauthorized.

**Later database entry as proof of earlier delegation**  
A database record created after the event, especially during migration, cannot by itself prove that a delegation was valid at the earlier event time. It may corroborate a delegation if it is tied to independent timestamped evidence that predates the event, such as a signed PDF or controlled record created before the effective interval.

    if pd.isna(ts):

    return day + (ts - ts.normalize())

## 6. Control Improvements

**Delegation creation**
- Require delegations to be created in a controlled QMS record before the effective start time.
- Capture principal, delegate, role, scope, incident ID, effective interval, no-subdelegation flag, and evidence link.
- Prohibit retroactive delegation records unless supported by independent timestamped evidence.
- Block approval actions outside delegation scope or interval at the system level.

**Effective-time recording**
- Synchronize QMS delegation records with identity, leave, and availability systems.
- During migrations, preserve original creation timestamps and source evidence.
- Treat DB creation time during migration as administrative time, not necessarily effective time.

**Principal availability**
- Integrate annual leave, sick leave, and formal unavailability into delegation validation.
- If a delegation requires principal absence, automatically invalidate or pause the delegation when the principal has an authenticated active session, unless the absence record is formally corrected.
- Require a formal “unavailable” state for weekend or off-site unavailability if delegations depend on it.

**Alias management**
- Maintain a canonical identity registry mapping all user IDs, aliases, and legacy accounts to one human.
- Normalize aliases in the QMS, SoD engine, audit logs, and network discovery.
- Alert when the same human uses multiple aliases in the same case or short time window.

**Individual authentication**
- Require MFA or FIDO for all human approval, verification, and retrospective actions.
- Eliminate shared service accounts for human decision points.
- If service accounts are required for automation, require session binding, individual re-authentication, and an audit assertion identifying the responsible human.
- For shared workstations, require individual login or biometric/FIDO confirmation before privileged actions.

**Emergency review**
- Require emergency changes to include incident ID, safety rationale, IC authority basis, and expected retrospective owner.
- Automatically create a QM retrospective task with a two-business-day deadline.
- Validate retrospective reviewer scope at event time, not from current master data.
- Escalate automatically if retrospective is late, out of scope, or performed by an unavailable delegate.

**Pair rotation**
- Define a drafter/approver rotation policy for HIGH-risk changes.
- Monitor pair concentration and reciprocal approvals.
- Require independent QM sampling for high-frequency pairs.
- Avoid assigning the same drafter/approver pair as the default path for HIGH-risk production changes.

**Network monitoring**
- Use PM4Py handover and working-together networks as monitoring dashboards, not automatic proof of misconduct.
- Alert on unusual reciprocity, concentrated handovers, service-account verification, and approvals during absence.
- Combine network alerts with authentication, leave, delegation, and quality evidence before investigation.

**Evidential retention**
- Retain delegation PDFs, QMS records, leave records, badge logs, MFA/FIDO logs, IP/device logs, workstation logs, CCTV where available, and session attribution evidence.
- Ensure timestamps are trusted and migration events are auditable.
- Preserve evidence for a period sufficient to support audits, investigations, and regulatory review.
