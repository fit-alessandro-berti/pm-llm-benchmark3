## 1. Target definition

### Primary target formulation
**Target:** Predict the probability of default (`default_90d`) for activated credit accounts within a 90-day window after account activation.
**Prediction time:** 90 days after the `Account Activated` event.
**Horizon:** 90 days post-activation.
**Unit of analysis:** Individual credit applications (cases).
**Population:** All credit applications started between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025.
**Estimand:** The average probability that an application will default within 90 days of being activated, conditional on the application's observed process history.

### Alternative target formulation
**Alternative target:** Predict default for all applications, regardless of activation status.
**Rationale:** This would provide a broader view of default risk across the entire applicant pool but would not directly inform credit decisions for activated accounts.

### Why primary target is operationally and statistically defensible
- **Operational relevance:** The lender's immediate concern is the risk associated with activated accounts, as they are the ones that have entered into contractual obligations.
- **Statistical defensibility:** The target formulation aligns with available labeled data and avoids confusing non-defaulted applications that were never activated. It respects the inherent selection bias by focusing on a subset where default outcomes are observable.

## 2. Cohort construction

### Inclusion rules
- Applications that have reached a `Contract Signed` state.
- Applications with a `Default Recorded` event within 90 days of activation.
- Applications flagged as `Withdrawn` or `Transferred` (but not labeled for default) are included as censored cases.
- Applications terminated before activation are excluded.

### Exclusion rules
- Applications omitted from the initial labeled export (rejected, withdrawn, transferred, open, and activated but unlabeled).
- Applications where default outcome is permanently missing (partner-originated accounts).

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

### Primary analysis strategy
- **Right censoring:** For applications that have not defaulted within 90 days, censor the outcome. This reflects the uncertainty about the default status beyond the observation horizon.
- **Delayed labels:** Handle the case where the default label is only available 7–35 days after the 90-day horizon. Use the available label for analysis.
- **Selection through activation:** Focus on activated accounts only, acknowledging that rejected or withdrawn applications were never exposed to the risk of default within the 90-day window.

### Sensitivity analyses
- **Model with full population:** Include rejected, withdrawn, and transferred cases as a separate group with a known default risk (e.g., 0 for rejected, missing for withdrawn/transferred).
- **Policy-specific counterfactual target:** Model default risk assuming all applications would have been activated under the same policy conditions, but this requires additional assumptions about policy impact.

### Testable vs. untestable assumptions
- **Testable:** Right censoring under the observed policy, delayed label impact when labels are present, and the relationship between activation and default.
- **Partially testable:** The effect of policy changes and system migrations can be partially tested by comparing outcomes before and after these events.
- **Fundamentally untestable:** The default risk for applications never activated under current policies (rejected, withdrawn, transferred).

## 4. Prefix extraction

### Fixed event-count prefixes
- Generate prefixes by selecting the last *n* events based on a fixed time window (e.g., last 30 days before activation).

### Elapsed-time prefixes
- Generate prefixes based on the time elapsed since the `Credit Review` event, ensuring all events within this window are included.

### Handling short cases and repeated loops
- **Short cases:** Discard cases with fewer than a predefined number of events (e.g., less than 5).
- **Repeated document loops:** Aggregate repeated events into a single event with cumulative values (e.g., `documents_total`).

### System migration handling
- Create a mapping table for renamed activities and apply it to unify event names across the dataset.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/Repair/Drop | Rationale |
|--------------------------|----------------------|------------------------|------------------|-----------|
| `final_limit`            | High: May reflect future risk | Only use values from before `Account Activated` | Drop | Final limit may change after activation, introducing bias |
| `decision_reason`        | Medium: May encode policy changes | Only use decision reasons from the `Credit Review` event | Keep | Relevant to the decision-making process before activation |
| `collection_status`      | High: May reflect future data | Only use `collection_status` from before `Account Activated` | Drop | Collection status may change after activation |
| `documents_total`        | Low: May be accurate but outdated | Use `documents_total` only up to the `Account Activated` event | Keep | Captures relevant documentation without bias |
| Updated `risk_score`     | Medium: May reflect model updates | Use only the `risk_score` from the last event before prediction | Keep | Ensures consistency in risk assessment |
| `resource_experience`    | Medium: May change over time | Use `resource_experience` as of `Account Activated` | Keep | Reflects agent experience at the time of decision |
| `label_available_at`     | High: Potential leakage into training | Use `label_available_at` to ensure temporal separation | Keep | Ensures temporal separation between feature generation and target |

## 7. Validation design

### Temporal validation and backtesting scheme
- **Temporal train/validation/test split:** Split the data by the `time:timestamp` of the first event, ensuring that training data precedes the validation set, which in turn precedes the test set.
- **Policy change handling:** Create separate validation windows before and after the policy change (July 2025) to assess model stability.
- **System migration handling:** Validate the model's performance after accounting for the activity renaming and duplicate events.
- **Label maturity:** Ensure that the validation data includes cases where the default label is available within the prediction window.
- **Calibration:** Evaluate model calibration across different risk bins to assess predictive performance.
- **Subgroup performance:** Analyze performance across different regions, channels, and risk scores to detect potential biases.
- **Distribution shift:** Monitor changes in the distribution of the data over time (e.g., changes in applicant demographics) to detect potential shifts in the data-generating process.

### Evaluation metrics
- **Cohen's kappa** for binary classification performance.
- **AUC-ROC** to assess the model's ability to distinguish between default and non-default cases.
- **Time-dependent ROC curves** to account for right censoring and delayed labels.

### Why random case split can be misleading
A random split across policy and migration periods can introduce temporal leakage, as the validation set may contain cases that are influenced by events that occur after the training period. This can lead to overly optimistic performance estimates.

## 7. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### Study limitations
- **Selection bias:** The target is defined only for activated accounts, potentially omitting important information about applications that were not activated.
- **Missing outcome feeds:** Partner-originated accounts with missing outcome feeds reduce the size of the labeled dataset.
- **Counterfactual assumptions:** The primary target assumes that default risk is only observable for activated accounts, which may not capture the risk for other application statuses.

### Deployment considerations
- **Policy changes:** Future policy changes could alter the relationship between process variables and default risk, necessitating model updates.
- **System migrations:** Ongoing system migrations could introduce new events or rename existing ones, requiring periodic model retraining or feature updates.
- **Label availability:** As the data extraction date approaches, the availability of default labels may decrease, impacting the model's predictive power.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.util import exec_utils
from pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg import discover_dfg
from pm4py.algo.discovery.variants import get_variants
from pm4py.algo.process_models.temporal_features import extract_temporal_features_dataframe
from pm4py.algo.process_models.prefixes import get_prefixes_from_log

# Read the CSV file
events_df = pd.read_csv('applications_events.csv', parse_dates=['time:timestamp'])

# Discover Process Definition Graph (PDG)
pdg = discover_dfg.discover_dfg(events_df, case_id='case:concept:name',
                                activity_id='concept:name', timestamp='time:timestamp')

# Discover variants of the PDG
variants = get_variants.get_variants(pdg, case_id='case:concept:name')

# Extract temporal features
temporal_features = extract_temporal_features_dataframe.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    pdg, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_id='concept:name', timestamp='time:timestamp',
    case_service_waiting_time=True)

# Build case-level information
case_info = temporal_features.groupby('case:concept:name').agg({
    'default_90d': 'first',
    'documents_total': 'max',
    'time_to_decision': 'max',
    'time_to_activation': 'max',
    'time_to_default': 'max'
})

# Create cohort table
cohort_table = pd.DataFrame({
    'case': case_info.index,
    'default_90d': case_info['default_90d'].values,
    'documents_total': case_info['documents_total'].values,
    'time_to_decision': case_info['time_to_decision'].values,
    'time_to_activation': case_info['time_to_activation'].values,
    'time_to_default': case_info['time_to_default'].values
})

# Leakage audit table
leakage_audit = pd.DataFrame({
    'Feature': ['final_limit', 'decision_reason', 'collection_status', 'documents_total', 'risk_score'],
    'Leakage Risk': ['High', 'Medium', 'High', 'Low', 'Medium'],
    'Availability Rule': ['Only before activation', 'Only before activation', 'Only before activation', 'Up to activation', 'Up to activation'],
    'Keep/Repair/Drop': ['Drop', 'Keep', 'Drop', 'Keep', 'Keep'],
    'Rationale': ['Changes after activation', 'May change after activation', 'May change after activation', 'Captures relevant documentation', 'Risk may change after activation']
})

# PM4Py training split
train_split, valid_split, test_split = pm4py.split_train_test(events_df, 
                                                              case_id='case:concept:name',
                                                              activity_id='concept:name', 
                                                              timestamp='time:timestamp', 
                                                              train_size=0.7, 
                                                              random_state=42)

# Demonstrate prefix extraction
```
