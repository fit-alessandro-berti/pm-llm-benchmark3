# Warranty Process Redesign: Reconciling Speed, Cost, Quality, Compliance, and Customer Experience

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The core contradiction is real but explainable: the high-frequency fast lane is locally cheap and fast, yet it externalizes cost and risk into downstream rework, complaints, and compliance exposure. Speed and unit cost are being optimized at the point of decision while quality and compliance costs land later and off the same ledger.

**Performance and speed.** The dominant flow is `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`. On the frequency DFG this path carries the bulk of volume (`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen: 3600`; `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval: 2550`; `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement: 2300`; `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close: 2320`). The performance DFG confirms it is genuinely fast in the happy case (`Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` median 0.4h; `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` median 1.8h). The problem is the tail, not the median: `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` runs mean 96h / p90 180h, and `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` mean 72h / p90 150h. These long-tail edges are where the "fast" path silently converts into the slowest experiences a customer sees.

**Quality and rework.** The rework statistics quantify the hidden cost. Complaint/reopen is 9.2% overall, but 14.1% on Fast approval versus only 3.3% on the `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device` route — the fast path roughly quadruples reopen rate. The decisive artifact: Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. That collapse from 31% to 9% is the empirical statement of the contradiction. The `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` tail is explicitly linked to incorrect replacement-part selection, and wrong replacements cost EUR 410 for high-value devices versus EUR 95 for accessories, so the fast-path damage is concentrated exactly where it is most expensive.

**Compliance.** Token-based replay shows fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces, and precision is low because many low-frequency paths mix auto-approval, risk review, and repair in policy-violating ways. The alignment diagnostics name the offenders: *Fast approval before Evidence completeness* (breaches R2), *Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices* (breaches R1 for claims above EUR 400), and *Customer complaint/reopen after close* combined with reopened claims that skip Manual eligibility review (breaches R3). So the fast path is not just quality-risky — it is the primary generator of conformance violations, and it violates most where value and compliance stakes are highest (high-value devices).

**Customer experience by segment.** The segment table shows the fast path is not uniformly bad. Premium low-risk accessories complete in 8h fast vs 46h manual with only 6% vs 3% reopen, and carry high service-credit exposure above 24h — for them the fast path is *correct* and the manual path actively harms outcomes and cost. In sharp contrast, Premium high-value devices show 22% reopen on the fast path vs 5% on the reviewed/repair path, and Standard high-value 18% vs 6%. The "unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases, median 140h, highest abandonment) is a third, distinct pathology unrelated to the fast/slow debate.

**Bottlenecks and rework loops.** Manual eligibility review is the fragile resource: 7 reviewers, queues blow up above 82% utilization, and it sits on the reopen loop (`Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review: 310`) and the evidence loop (`Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review: 890`). Evidence completion is itself a loop trap — `Request missing evidence` recurs at least twice in 26% of cases entering it, and `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned: 320`. Fraud/risk review (2 specialists) spikes on Mondays and after campaigns.

**Synthesis of the contradiction.** The fast path is not globally good or bad; it is *mis-targeted*. It is applied uniformly across segments where its expected downstream damage varies by an order of magnitude. Any redesign that keeps or removes it wholesale is wrong. The design lever is *conditional routing by segment, risk, and defect certainty*, plus attacking the two independent pathologies (evidence loops; wrong-part dispatch) that the fast/slow framing obscures.

At least five artifacts used above: the frequency DFG fast-path edges; the performance DFG complaint tails (72h/96h); the 31%→9% cost-after-rework statistic; the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split; replay fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting with named alignment deviations; and the per-segment reopen contrasts.

## 2. Redesign A: Risk-and-value gated routing (segment-aware triage at intake)

**Redesigned flow.** Introduce a deterministic router immediately after Intake that reads segment, defect-type, and claim value, and assigns each claim to a lane instead of defaulting everyone into Auto screen:
- **Green lane (keep fast path):** Premium and Standard low-risk accessories with clear defect and value under EUR 400 -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch -> Quality/compliance close. R2 is satisfied for Premium low-risk accessories by explicit exception; Standard low-risk accessories retain a lightweight evidence check.
- **Amber lane (mandatory review before approval):** any claim above EUR 400 or any high-value device is forced through Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before approval, hard-wiring R1 and R2 into the control flow so `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices` becomes structurally impossible.
- **Red lane (unclear defect / missing evidence):** routed straight into a structured evidence-completion sub-process (see the portal in Redesign B is optional here) rather than bouncing through Auto screen first.

**Targets.** The compliance-critical high-value segments (Premium 14%, Standard 21%) and the fast-path reopen concentration; preserves the genuinely-good fast path for the 43% accessory volume.

**Mechanism.** It converts the discovered low-precision model into a prescriptive one. The 31%→9% erosion is driven mostly by high-value wrong-dispatch (EUR 410) and high-value reopen (22%/18%); routing those cases to the reviewed/repair path (5%/6% reopen) recovers most of the lost margin while *not* penalizing accessories that already do well fast. It directly removes the three named alignment deviations for the high-value segment and should lift fitting-trace percentage well above 68%.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** High-value cycle time increases (toward the 96h/120h reviewed profile), which risks Premium service-credit exposure — Redesign A does not solve slow review, it just aims it correctly. It also depends on segment/value/defect attributes being trustworthy at intake; mis-tagging silently misroutes. Manual-review inflow rises (the prompt warns of +15–35% from rule updates), and A does nothing to expand that constrained resource. The evidence-loop and abandonment pathology (Red lane) is only parked, not fixed.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first front door with digital portal and abandonment safety net

**Redesigned flow.** Attack the process at Intake and Evidence completion rather than at the approval decision. Before eligibility branching, run an **automated evidence-completeness gate**: the claim is checked for required artifacts up front, and missing items are requested once, in a batch, through a new **evidence portal** with proactive status visibility. Only evidence-complete claims proceed to eligibility (auto or manual). Customers flagged as low digital literacy or who stall in the portal are diverted to an **assisted intake** channel (short callback / concierge) instead of being left to abandon.

**Targets.** The "unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22%, median 140h, highest abandonment) and the evidence loop generally: `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review: 890`, recurrence ≥2× in 26% of entrants, and `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned: 320`. It also benefits Standard accessory customers who "tolerate 72h if status visibility is good."

**Mechanism.** The portal is stated to cut repeated evidence requests 30–45%, which collapses the most expensive loop and shortens the 140h median for the worst segment. Front-loading completeness also structurally supports R2 (evidence confirmed before dispatch) for the non-exception segments and reduces spurious Manual-review inflow caused by incomplete claims bouncing back. Status visibility converts tolerated waiting into acceptable experience rather than complaints.

**Materially different from A.** A re-routes *decisions* by risk/value and leaves evidence untouched; B re-engineers the *evidence and intake* stage and is largely orthogonal to the fast/slow approval debate. They compose but neither subsumes the other.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** The portal may *increase* abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers — the assisted channel mitigates but adds cost and headcount not budgeted here. B does nothing about R1 or wrong-part dispatch on high-value devices, so compliance and reshipment cost on those segments remain. Portal build is heavier and slower to deploy than a rules change, and benefits are a range (30–45%), so payback is uncertain.

## 4. Redesign C: Pre-dispatch quality gate plus tiered reconciliation of fast decisions

**Redesigned flow.** Keep the fast approval lane broadly in place (protecting median speed and cost) but insert a **lightweight, risk-weighted pre-dispatch quality gate** specifically on the `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` edge for mid- and high-value devices: an automated part-selection/BOM check plus, for high-value only, a brief inspector confirmation before the replacement ships. Complaint-heavy patterns feed a closed loop: reopened claims are routed through Manual eligibility review (enforcing R3), and recurring wrong-part signatures update the gate rules. Accessories bypass the gate entirely.

**Targets.** The wrong-replacement tail — `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` (mean 96h, linked to incorrect part selection), the EUR 410 high-value error cost, Rework repair in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices, and the 22%/18% high-value fast-path reopen rates.

**Mechanism.** The prompt states a pre-dispatch gate can catch wrong-selection at a cost of +6–18h. Spending 6–18h up front to avoid a 96h/180h complaint tail plus a EUR 410 reshipment is a favorable trade *only* where error cost and reopen rate are high — hence gate high-value, skip accessories. Quality-check inspectors are underused except after campaigns, so there is latent capacity to staff the gate (2 inspectors, EUR 38/h) without new hires. This is the redesign most directly aimed at converting the 31%→9% cost erosion back toward real savings without slowing the eligibility decision at all.

**Materially different from A and B.** A moves the *routing* decision; B fixes the *evidence* front door; C leaves both decisions alone and inserts a *verification checkpoint just before the physical action*, plus a reopen-conformance loop. It is a control on execution error rather than on decision routing or intake.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** The gate adds 6–18h to every gated dispatch, eroding Premium high-value cycle-time expectations and risking service credits; a poorly designed gate becomes a new queue in front of Dispatch. It does not address R1/R2 approval-order violations (it acts after approval), nor the evidence loop or abandonment. If gate rules are weak, it adds delay without catching errors — pure waste.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (routing).**
- *Failure 1 — attribute quality:* if value/segment/defect tags are wrong or gameable, high-value claims leak into the Green lane and reproduce the exact violations A was meant to stop, while accessories misrouted to Amber inflate cost and Premium credit exposure.
- *Failure 2 — capacity denial:* the router assumes the reviewed path has headcount. Rule updates raise manual inflow +15–35%; without staffing, A converts a fast-path quality problem into a review-queue delay problem.
- *Bottleneck migration:* pressure shifts from downstream complaints to **Manual eligibility review / Fraud-risk review** (2 specialists, campaign spikes), pushing utilization past the 82% cliff.

**Redesign B (evidence portal).**
- *Failure 1 — digital exclusion:* portal raises abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers; if the assisted channel is under-resourced, B trades evidence-loop cost for lost claims and CSAT damage.
- *Failure 2 — completeness theater:* an over-strict gate rejects adequate evidence, adding front-door friction and re-creating a loop before intake even completes.
- *Bottleneck migration:* load shifts to the **assisted intake / concierge channel** and to portal support; the constraint moves from Manual review to human-assisted onboarding.

**Redesign C (pre-dispatch gate).**
- *Failure 1 — gate becomes a queue:* if inspector capacity is misjudged (spare "except after campaigns"), the gate stacks in front of Dispatch, and the +6–18h becomes +days for Premium high-value, triggering service credits.
- *Failure 2 — weak detection:* if the part-check rules don't actually catch wrong selection, C adds latency with no quality gain — cost up, quality flat.
- *Bottleneck migration:* the bottleneck moves from post-dispatch complaint handling to the **Quality-check / inspector** step and the physical Dispatch handoff.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Sequencing logic.** Start with the change that has the best ratio of downstream-damage-avoided to new-resource-risk and is reversible. That is **Redesign C on high-value devices**, because it targets the single most expensive, best-attributed pathology (EUR 410 wrong-part reshipment, 12.5% high-value rework) using latent inspector capacity, and it can be turned off instantly. Then add **Redesign A routing** (compliance + correct fast/slow assignment), which needs C's data to calibrate gate rules and needs capacity planning. Run **Redesign B** in parallel but independently, since it addresses a disjoint segment (unclear/missing evidence) and has the longest build.

**Stage 1 — First pilot: C on high-value devices only (4–6 weeks).**
- *Metrics:* wrong-part / `Dispatch -> Customer complaint/reopen` rate on gated vs control; reshipment count and EUR; added dispatch latency (target within the stated 6–18h); high-value rework rate; inspector utilization; Premium service-credit incidence.
- *Stop/go:* GO if reopen on gated high-value drops materially (e.g. toward accessory-like single digits) **and** added latency stays inside ~6–18h **and** Premium credits do not rise. STOP/redesign if the gate adds delay without a clear reopen drop, or inspector utilization saturates.

**Stage 2 — A routing, shadow then live (start in shadow mode).**
- Run the router in shadow first: log where it *would* send each claim and compare predicted vs actual outcomes and R1/R2 conformance, without changing flow. Go live for high-value once shadow shows correct classification.
- *Metrics:* fitting-trace % (baseline 68%) and R1/R2/R3 alignment deviations; high-value reopen (22%/18% baselines); Manual-review utilization vs the 82% cliff and queue length; accessory cycle time (must stay flat); net cost after rework (watch the 9% figure move upward).
- *Stop/go:* GO to broaden if fitting % rises and high-value reopen falls **without** Manual-review utilization breaching ~82%. HOLD/staff-up if the review queue grows sharply before quality gains materialize.

**Stage 3 — B evidence portal, phased with assisted channel from day one.**
- *Metrics:* repeat evidence-request rate (target 30–45% reduction), abandonment overall and specifically for low-digital-literacy cohort, median cycle for unclear/missing (from 140h), Manual-review inflow from incomplete claims, CSAT/status-visibility signal for Standard accessories.
- *Stop/go:* GO if repeat requests fall in range **and** abandonment does not rise for the assisted cohort. STOP/adjust if portal deflects but abandonment climbs, indicating the assisted channel is undersized.

**Distinguishing real improvement from displaced downstream cost.** For every stage, evaluate on **net cost after complaints, reshipments, and service credits** (mirroring the 31%→9% correction), not on unit cost or median cycle time — the prompt's constraint against average-only optimization. Track outcomes over a full claim horizon that spans the complaint tails (p90 up to 180h), and monitor the specific downstream edges each redesign could push load onto (Manual review queue for A, assisted channel for B, inspector/dispatch for C). An improvement is "real" only if the targeted metric improves *and* the named bottleneck-migration metric and net-after-rework cost do not worsen by an offsetting amount. Use holdout/control cohorts per stage and per segment so gains are attributable rather than seasonal (campaign and Monday effects are known confounders).
