## 1. Conformance semantics

I distinguish **normative behavior**, **data quality**, **model incompleteness**, and **insufficient evidence** before assigning blame to a process actor or control.

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation matches a relation explicitly marked `forbidden`, and the stated scope plausibly applies. Forbidden rules are closed-world: if the norm says “never,” an observed instance is a high-priority finding unless there is strong evidence that the observation is spurious.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: a mandatory relation is absent for an object or event that falls inside the norm’s scope. This is not automatically a violation if the scope is uncertain, the abstraction is too strict, or a legitimate exception exists. Mandatory gaps require evidence that the expected object, event, or link should have existed.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: an observed relation is not covered by the norm. Because the norm is open-world except for forbidden rules and mandatory scoped columns, such behavior is not noncompliance. It may be benign, rare, or a candidate for future governance.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: a `typical` relation is missing. This may indicate delay, open work, credit terms, write-offs, or incomplete logging, but it is not a norm breach by itself.
- **Likely data-quality error**: the observed relation is implausible operationally, contradicts known system behavior, or is explained by identifier reuse, missing labels, duplicate events, or join errors.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the observed model uses a stricter or different representation than the norm. The clearest example is treating an “eventually happens” expectation as a directly-follows relation.
- **Insufficient evidence**: the graph summary does not contain enough timestamp, identity, scope, or attribute information to decide between violation, exception, and data error.

The norm is **closed-world only** for explicitly forbidden relations and for mandatory relations inside their stated scope. It is otherwise **open-world**. Therefore, absence from the normative edge set does not imply noncompliance. Rare but unregulated behavior should not be escalated merely because it is rare.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG summary can establish **temporally adjacent or frequently following event-type relations** on an object type. It is useful for detecting forbidden sequences such as `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` and for approximating common paths such as `Validate Order -> Close Order`.

It cannot establish, by itself, whether a rule is truly **eventual**, whether intermediate events are missing, whether the same business object identity is preserved, or whether an exception is legitimate. Counts and scopes matter: N1 applies to newly created standard Orders, N4 applies only to externally shipped Packages, and N5 allows legitimate exceptions. A directly-follows count of 98/100 for `Create Order -> Validate Order` may understate conformance if validation occurs after an unrecorded review. Likewise, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` may be perfectly normal even though it is not in the normative OC-DFG.

The main danger is interpreting an **eventually expected business rule** as a directly-follows rule. If the business rule is “every created order should eventually be validated,” then a directly-follows model will flag orders with intermediate review, risk, or amendment events as missing, even though the eventual obligation may be satisfied. This creates false positives and can distract from genuine forbidden behavior.

### OTG

The OTG can establish **object-object relation existence and weight**, such as Order–Item, Package–Delivery, Invoice–Payment, Order–Customer, and Order–RiskCase. It is useful for checking whether expected object interactions exist in the object graph.

It cannot establish event sequencing, causality, or whether a mandatory relation is present for every event instance. For example, `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight 200, while ET–OT shows only 198 `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links. The OTG may be conforming at aggregate object level while the ET–OT model reveals two dispatch events lacking a Delivery object. Similarly, `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` has weight 1450, but `Add Item -> Order` has only 1448 links, suggesting possible event-level orphan items.

A relation can be conforming at OTG and suspicious at ET–OT when the object graph contains an aggregate or inferred edge, but the specific event type required by the norm does not consistently link the object. Conversely, an OTG edge may be absent simply because the norm does not govern that object relation.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT model can establish **whether specific event types are linked to required object types**. It is the most direct family for checking mandatory event-object participation: every `Add Item` should link Order and Item; every `Dispatch Package` should link Package and Delivery; every `Record Payment` should link Payment and, unless explicitly unapplied, Invoice.

It cannot establish temporal order, business intent, or whether a missing link reflects a legitimate exception. The one `Record Payment` without Invoice may be unapplied cash, but because the event is not labeled as such, E8 treats it as a mandatory gap pending clarification. The two missing `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links may be data-quality gaps or legitimate carrier-reference exceptions, depending on the norm’s intended scope.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction and suspicious at another. `Package-Delivery` appears complete in OTG, but two dispatch events lack Delivery in ET–OT. `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` is a forbidden ET–OT relation, but the suspected reused identifier makes data quality the leading explanation. PM4Py diagnostics can locate these mismatches, but they do not provide the final business or legal interpretation.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 eventual validation is expressed through directly-follows data**  
   Operational risk: false violations for orders with intermediate review events.  
   Clarification: state that `Create Order` must eventually be followed by `Validate Order`, allowing specified intermediate events.  
   Status: mandatory eventual relation.

2. **E8 unapplied-cash exception is not event-typed**  
   Operational risk: legitimate unapplied cash appears as mandatory noncompliance.  
   Clarification: require an explicit `unapplied_cash` attribute or event subtype for payments without Invoice.  
   Status: mandatory labeling; Invoice link mandatory otherwise.

3. **E6/T2 Delivery object versus carrier reference**  
   Operational risk: dispatches with carrier references but no Delivery object are ambiguous.  
   Clarification: require a Delivery object for externally dispatched packages; carrier reference may be supplemental but not a substitute unless explicitly approved.  
   Status: mandatory Delivery object; carrier reference unspecified or typical supplemental.

4. **N3 cancellation effectiveness and reversal**  
   Operational risk: forbidden findings may be wrong if cancellation is reversed or timestamped incorrectly.  
   Clarification: define effective cancellation time and state that dispatch after effective cancellation is forbidden unless cancellation is formally reversed.  
   Status: forbidden after effective cancellation.

5. **N5 write-offs, credit notes, and open credit terms are only informally mentioned**  
   Operational risk: invoice-payment absence is hard to triage.  
   Clarification: model `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` and `Issue Invoice -> Credit Note` as legitimate exceptions, with approval requirements.  
   Status: typical exceptions; approval metadata mandatory.

6. **N6 reopening is unspecified**  
   Operational risk: reopen-close cycles could hide fraud or control bypass without governance.  
   Clarification: require a reopen reason and optional risk review; make `Reopen Order -> Close Order` a monitored typical path rather than forbidden.  
   Status: typical relation with mandatory reason attribute.

7. **T6 cobirth inference is undefined**  
   Operational risk: forbidden cobirth findings may arise from shared timestamps or reused identifiers rather than genuine simultaneous creation.  
   Clarification: define cobirth as same business-event identifier creating both objects; prohibit only genuine same-event creation.  
   Status: forbidden relation, mandatory event-identity definition.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**

Priority 1: the two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` cases under N3. Suspend or hold related shipments if still possible, identify whether dispatch occurred after effective cancellation, and determine whether goods can be recalled or payment blocked.

Priority 2: the single `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge under T6. Freeze downstream processing of the affected payment until the creation event is verified. Determine whether the payment was genuinely born in the same business event as the order or whether the edge is an inference artifact.

The suspected `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` link should also be quarantined from analytical and compliance views, but it should not be treated as intentional process misuse until the join error hypothesis is rejected.

**Data repair**

- Remove or correct the suspected sensor-invoice join using reliable source keys.
- Backfill or correct missing Order links for the two Add Item events.
- Create or link missing Delivery objects for the two Dispatch Package events, or record an approved carrier-reference exception.
- Add explicit unapplied-cash labels for payment events that legitimately lack an Invoice.
- Add missing intermediate review or validation events if they occurred but were not logged.

**Process investigation**

For N3, investigate warehouse timing, cancellation propagation, and carrier handoff. Determine whether cancellation failed to reach fulfillment in time or whether the event order was recorded incorrectly.

For T6, investigate the order-payment creation transaction. Check whether payment was taken before authorization, whether a system integration created both objects, or whether the cobirth edge is a graph-inference error.

Do not escalate benign rare behavior such as `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, or `Package object_codeath Delivery` unless further evidence shows control abuse.

**Norm revision**

Revise the norm to distinguish direct-follows and eventual relations, define unapplied cash typing, define Delivery object requirements, define effective cancellation, model write-offs and credit notes, and specify reopen governance. Also define cobirth inference rules and prohibited cross-domain joins.

**Monitoring**

Monitor mandatory gaps at event level, forbidden relations with high precision, and typical absences through operational dashboards rather than compliance alarms. Use eventually-follows conformance for N1, exception labels for E8, and carrier/Delivery reconciliation for dispatch.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are limited: the two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` observations and the single `Order object_cobirth Payment` edge are the strongest normative concerns; the mandatory event-object gaps are concrete data-completeness issues; the unregulated relations are not violations merely because rare or absent from the norm.

Several conclusions depend on norm incompleteness: whether validation is eventual, whether unapplied cash is properly typed, whether carrier references can replace Delivery objects, how write-offs and reopenings should be governed, and whether codeath or customer/risk interactions should be controlled.

Other conclusions depend on suspected data-quality issues: the sensor-invoice link, missing Order links in Add Item, missing Delivery links in Dispatch, and the unlabeled payment without Invoice.

Graph summaries alone cannot decide object identity, true causal order, effective cancellation time, business intent, exception legitimacy, or whether missing events occurred outside the observed snapshot.
