# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin.** Elapsed clock time starts at `Submit`. That is the SLA clock given in the scenario; no calendar start dates are supplied, so analyses are in hours since submit, not in calendar time.

**Horizon.** \(L = 72\) hours. A case can be classified at \(L\) only if it has either a terminal event at time \(\le L\) or follow-up age \(\ge L\). Five cases are still open with age \(< L\) (S10 47h, S15 35h, S24 67h, S29 51h, S31 23h). For those cases the 72-hour outcome is not yet observed.

**Event vocabulary.** Let \(T_R\) be time to internal `Resolve`, \(T_W\) to `Withdraw`, \(T_J\) to `Reject`, \(T_X\) to `Transfer`, and \(T_\emptyset\) the observation age for `open` rows. Let \(T_{\mathrm{term}} = \min(T_R, T_W, T_J, T_X)\) when a terminal is observed, and \(T_{\mathrm{term}} > T_\emptyset\) when the case is still open. Internal operational success is reaching `Resolve`. `Withdraw` and `Reject` are terminal internal non-successes. `Transfer` ends internal observation; the external outcome is unknown.

**Already-observed 72-hour facts versus censoring.** Right censoring applies only when the 72-hour question is still unanswered. It does **not** apply to a case that has already crossed 72 hours without `Resolve` (S09 92h open, S11 81h open, S25 79h open, S26 104h open, S32 96h transferred, and the four late resolutions S03 76h, S13 110h, S20 74h, S28 88h). Those rows are observed breaches of on-time internal resolution, or at least observed non-resolution by 72, depending on the estimand. Competing exits before 72 (S05, S06, S08, S12, S14, S21, S22, S23, S30) are also not 72-hour censors for “was this internally resolved by 72?”: that answer is already no.

Two estimands are defined for decisions; a third is the competing-risk decomposition that makes them jointly readable.

**Estimand A — internal on-time resolution (and its complement, SLA non-resolution).**
\[
\theta_A = \Pr(T_R \le 72) = F_R(72),
\]
the cumulative incidence of internal `Resolve` by 72 hours in the presence of `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and (in the primary encoding) `Transfer`. The complement \(1-\theta_A = \Pr(T_R > 72)\) is the probability of **not being internally resolved by 72 hours**. This includes late resolves, still-open cases, and cases that left via a competing terminal without ever resolving. This is the customer/SLA success estimand: “what fraction of submitted cases receive an internal resolve inside the SLA?” It is **not** the net Kaplan–Meier probability of resolution in a hypothetical world without withdrawal, rejection, or transfer.

**Estimand B — residual internal occupancy at the SLA horizon.**
\[
\theta_B = \Pr(T_{\mathrm{term}} > 72),
\]
the probability of still having **no terminal disposition** at 72 hours (still in the internal system). This is the workload/WIP estimand: “how much of the intake will still be sitting in queue or review when the SLA clock expires?” Transfer is a terminal disposition for this estimand, because the internal system stops observing the case.

**Estimand C (supporting) — cause-specific incidence profile.**
\[
\big(F_R(72),\; F_W(72),\; F_J(72),\; F_X(72),\; \theta_B\big),
\]
with \(F_R+F_W+F_J+F_X+\theta_B = 1\) once follow-up is complete. This is the governance estimand: it separates on-time success, benign/negative internal exits, leakage to another organization, and leftover tail WIP.

**How four illustrative cases contribute**

| Case | Facts | Estimand A (\(\theta_A\) / \(1-\theta_A\)) | Estimand B (\(\theta_B\)) | Already-observed 72h fact? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resolved at 76h (S03) | Internal resolve after \(L\) | Counts toward \(1-\theta_A\). CIF of resolve jumps at 76, not at 72. | In-system at 72 (\(T_{\mathrm{term}}=76>72\)). Then leaves at 76. | Yes: not resolved by 72; still open at 72. |
| Open at 92h (S09) | Still open, age \(>L\), last gap 31h | Counts toward \(1-\theta_A\). Censored only for *eventual* resolution after 92, not for the 72-hour question. | In-system at 72. Censored at 92 for eventual terminal. | Yes: not resolved by 72; still open at 72. |
| Withdrawn at 27h (S06) | Competing internal exit before \(L\) | Known never-resolved. Leaves the resolve risk set at 27. Contributes to \(F_W\), hence to \(1-\theta_A\). **Not** a non-informative censor for A. | Terminal at 27, so **not** in-system at 72. | Yes for A (never resolved by 72); not an occupancy case for B. |

**Assumption A1.** Priority is known at submit and is a baseline covariate. **Assumption A2.** `duration_or_age_h` is elapsed time from submit on a comparable clock for every row. **Assumption A3 (primary).** After `Transfer`, internal `Resolve` cannot occur; \(T_R=\infty\) internally. External resolution is out of scope for \(\theta_A\) and \(\theta_B\).

**Three transfer assumptions (S08 68h P1 escalated; S21 26h P1 escalated; S32 96h P1 escalated)**

All three transferred cases are P1 and escalated. That is already evidence against “transfer is like a random dropout.”

1. **T-compete (primary, internal competing exit).** Code 4 at the transfer time. Internal \(T_R=\infty\). S08 and S21 contribute to \(F_X(72)\) and therefore to \(1-\theta_A\). S32 is still internally open at 72, so it contributes to \(\theta_B\) and to \(1-\theta_A\), and only later to \(F_X\). Matches Estimand A as *internal* resolution and Estimand B as *internal* occupancy. **Hypothesis:** transfers are the hard P1 tail being exported.

2. **T-censor (unknown external outcome).** Recode transfer as `0` and censor at the transfer time. S08 and S21 leave the 72-hour risk set with the 72-hour customer outcome unknown (external org might still resolve by 72). S32 is *not* unknown for internal on-time resolution: the case was still inside this system until 96h, so it was not internally resolved by 72 under any reading. T-censor is the only encoding that can make S08/S21 look like ordinary incomplete follow-up. It is optimistic if transferred cases are systematically harder.

3. **T-fail (composite SLA failure).** Transfer is a failure mark (composite “not successfully resolved internally,” possibly lumped with late resolve and maybe with reject). S08 and S21 fail at 68 and 26. S32 has already failed on-time resolution at 72 and then transfers. Appropriate if governance treats hand-off as missing the internal SLA. Inappropriate if transfer before 72 is an accepted on-time disposition.

A fourth, non-primary reading exists and should stay in sensitivity only: **T-success-if-before-72**, i.e. a timely hand-off meets an internal handling SLA. That would credit S08 and S21 as successes and would make P1 look much better. It answers a different question (“did we finish *our* handling in time?”) than Estimand A.

**Open-case gap note, not a fourth status.** `last_event_gap_h` is not an outcome code, but it changes how credible independent censoring is. S15, S24, S29, S31 have gaps of 2–6h and look recently active. S11, S25, S26 look stalled. Treating all nine opens as equivalent independent censors is an assumption, not a fact.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A completed-case analysis that keeps only the 13 `resolved` rows and computes “late / resolved” gets \(4/13 \approx 31\%\) (S03, S13, S20, S28). That number is not \(\theta_A\), not \(\theta_B\), and not a 72-hour breach probability for submitted work.

**Survivor / completion bias.** The 13 resolved cases are those that both stayed inside the system and reached `Resolve`. They exclude four already-late opens (S09, S11, S25, S26), one transfer after 72 (S32), two transfers before 72 (S08, S21), four withdrawals, and three rejects. Among the 27 cases with a known 72-hour internal-resolution bit, 9 are on-time resolves and 18 are not, so \(18/27 \approx 67\%\) are not internally resolved by 72. The completed-case 31% and the complete-follow-up 67% are not two estimates of one thing; the first conditions on eventual internal resolution. S13 (resolved at 110 after rework and escalate) enters the naive denominator; S11 (P1, escalated, open at 81 with a 55h gap) does not, even though S11 has already missed on-time resolution more clearly than a case resolved at 76.

**Active opens below versus above 72 hours.** These are different statistical objects. S10, S15, S24, S29, S31 are genuine right-censored observations for both estimands: they have not yet hit 72. S24 is 5 hours from the horizon with a 4h gap at `Review` and is the most decision-relevant near-horizon case. S09, S11, S25, S26 are **already-observed** non-resolutions and in-system occupancies at 72. Folding them into “incomplete / drop / ignore” with the under-horizon opens understates tail risk. S26 is the extreme: age 104h, last event 72h ago, prefix still `Submit>Screen>Review` — the last recorded activity was at hour \(104-72=32\), so by the SLA instant the case had already been inactive for 40 hours.

**Competing risks.** S05 (reject 9h) and S23 (reject 11h) are fast screen-outs. S06 and S12 are early withdrawals. If they are dropped, the remaining sample is enriched for cases that were allowed to continue. If they are KM-censored as if they might still resolve, the estimator invents a fictional continuation after a terminal activity. S14 (reject at 63h after `Review`) consumed almost a full SLA budget and then failed; dropping it hides SLA-consuming non-success. S30 (withdraw at 70h) is a near-horizon competing exit, not an on-time resolve and not an incomplete observation.

**Informative censoring, transfers, and long gaps.** Independent censoring says “had we kept watching, the case would have behaved like those still in the risk set.” That is implausible for transfers: S08, S21, S32 are all P1 and all escalated, and both complete transfer prefixes end `Escalate>Transfer`. Transfer is a selected path for the hardest (or most externally owned) work. It is also implausible for long-gap opens sitting on `Escalate` (S11, S25) or abandoned after `Review` (S26). Censoring them as if they were like S15 (open 35h, gap 2h, recently in `Review`) moves apparent risk off the tail and onto whichever cases happen to resolve in view.

**Cohort imbalance and non-random policy.** Cohort B is later, after automated screening and escalation-policy change, but assignment was not randomized and compliance was incomplete. B has more P1 cases (S18, S21, S25, S29, S32 vs A’s S04, S08, S11) and more `AutoCheck`-first prefixes, but four B cases never show `AutoCheck` at all (S20, S23, S26, S30). Those four include a late resolve (S20 74h), a fully stalled open (S26), a near-horizon withdraw (S30), and a fast reject (S23). A naive B-versus-A comparison of resolved-only durations therefore mixes policy, mix of priorities, incomplete rollout, and different competing-exit patterns. B’s raw on-time resolve count is 5/17 versus A’s 4/15; that is not a causal policy effect.

**Immortal time preview, with case IDs.** Escalated resolved cases include both the fastest P1 success (S18, 12h) and late tails (S03 76h, S13 110h, S28 88h). Non-escalated resolved cases include the other fast successes (S01 18h, S16 16h). A completed-case regression of duration on `escalated` compares cases that *needed and survived long enough to receive* escalation with cases that often finished before escalation was relevant. That is selection plus immortal time, not a baseline effect of the policy.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

The sample is 32 cases. The plan must stay nonparametric and stratified. Covariate-rich hazard models are secondary illustrations, not primary inference.

**Kaplan–Meier of time to resolve, competing exits censored.** Estimates a net survival curve: probability of still not being resolved in a world where withdraw/reject/transfer cannot happen. Useful only as a diagnostic contrast. On these data it will look more optimistic than Estimand A because S06, S08, S12, S21, S22, S30 and the rejects are removed from the denominator instead of being counted as never-resolved. **Do not use as the primary SLA number.**

**Kaplan–Meier of time to any terminal (Estimand B).** Here KM is the right nonparametric tool: the event is “internal observation ended,” and the only censors are the nine opens. \(\widehat S(72)=\hat\theta_B\). This is a recommended **co-primary** curve for occupancy.

**Aalen–Johansen / cumulative incidence (Estimand A and C).** AJ estimates \(F_k(t)\) for resolve, withdraw, reject, and transfer without forcing competing exits to be censors. \(\hat F_R(72)=\hat\theta_A\) and \(1-\hat F_R(72)=\widehat{\Pr}(T_R>72)\). With \(n=32\), report point estimates plus the identified bounds that do not depend on a hazard model: 9 on-time resolves are known, 18 cases are known not internally resolved by 72, 5 are unknown, so
\[
\theta_A \in [9/32,\; 14/32] = [0.281,\; 0.438]
\]
if every submit is in the population. AJ interpolates inside that interval by using the timing of exits and the under-horizon risk sets. **This is the primary estimator** for SLA success and for the cause profile.

**Cause-specific hazards.** Instantaneous rate of each exit among cases still internally active. Right tool for *mechanism*: e.g. high transfer hazard after `Escalate` on P1 (S08, S21, S32) versus high early reject hazard from `Screen` (S05, S23). Not the SLA probability itself. Fitting a cause-specific Cox model with several covariates on 32 rows would be theater; report stratified Nelson–Aalen / empirical rates, not a 6-parameter Cox table, as the primary cause-specific view.

**Subdistribution (Fine–Gray) hazards.** Directly target \(F_R(t)\), keeping competing cases in a modified risk set. Conceptually aligned with Estimand A, but with \(n=32\) and three competing causes the subdistribution model is unstable and harder to explain to operations than AJ curves. Use Fine–Gray only if a single covariate effect on CIF is required, and even then only for `cohort` or `priority` separately, never for post-baseline `escalated`.

**Restricted mean time.** Restricted mean time to resolve, or to any terminal, truncated at 72, is the expected SLA-budget hours consumed. Complementary to \(\theta_A\): S18 (12h) and S05 (9h) both consume little budget; S14 (reject 63h) and S08 (transfer 68h) consume almost the whole budget without success. Recommend as a **secondary operational metric**, computed from the same AJ/KM curves, not as a new model.

**Landmarking.** Required if any post-submit state is used as a predictor. A 48-hour landmark is natural: it is two-thirds of the SLA, and several cases are still open then (including S07, S08, S14, S19, S24, S29, S30 and all later tails). The landmark estimand is
\[
\Pr(T_R \le 72 \mid T_{\mathrm{term}} > 48,\; \text{history up to 48}).
\]
History allowed at the landmark: current last activity, whether `AutoCheck` has already occurred, whether `Escalate` or `Rework` has already occurred, gap if the last event is known to predate 48. **This is the secondary inferential approach** for process-aware risk, because it respects time order.

**Multi-state model.** Best *representation* of the prefixes: transient states such as Screen/AutoCheck, Review, Rework, Escalated; absorbing Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, Transfer. Intensities out of `Escalate` are the scientifically interesting ones (resolve vs transfer vs stall). With 32 cases and no intermediate timestamps, holding times between activities are **not identified**. The multi-state model should therefore be used as a state occupancy / next-activity competing-risk model at observed visits, or as a schematic for landmark states, not as a continuous-time fit with invented sojourns.

**Primary recommendation.** Nonparametric Aalen–Johansen cause-specific CIFs with transfer as a competing exit (T-compete), evaluated at 72 hours, overall and stratified separately by cohort and by priority; report \(\hat\theta_A\), \(1-\hat\theta_A\), the four cause CIFs, and the identified bounds. Co-primary: KM / all-cause survival at 72 for \(\theta_B\).

**Secondary recommendation.** (i) 48-hour landmark using current stage and pre-landmark escalation/rework/AutoCheck only; (ii) qualitative multi-state occupancy from prefixes; (iii) the sensitivity suite in Section 6. Do not lead with completed-case rates, do not lead with KM of resolve that censors transfers, and do not put `escalated` or `rework_count` into a from-submit regression as if they were baseline.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

Prefixes are the process evidence. Intermediate event times are not supplied, so control-flow features are identified; sojourn features are not.

**Baseline (safe at \(t=0+\))**

- `cohort` (A/B): policy period, not a randomized arm.
- `priority` (P1 > P2 > P3): assumed known at submit.
- Optional: whether the designed B path is `AutoCheck`-first. That is a *policy* attribute of B, not an observed case feature, because four B cases never execute it.

**Time-varying / history-up-to-\(t\) (safe only in landmark, TVC, or multi-state form)**

- Current stage = last non-terminal activity in the prefix: `Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Rework`, `Escalate`. Open-case stages: S31 `Screen`; S10/S11/S25 `Escalate`; S09/S15/S24/S26/S29 `Review`.
- `autocheck_occurred` and `autocheck_first` (first activity after Submit is `AutoCheck`). In B this is the compliance marker: 13/17 B cases have `AutoCheck`; S20, S23, S26, S30 do not.
- `review_count` and `rework_count` (already tabulated; prefixes confirm S07, S13, S27).
- `escalated_by_t` once `Escalate` has appeared. Observed escalations: A = S03, S04, S08, S10, S11, S13; B = S18, S21, S25, S28, S29, S32.
- Inactivity: `last_event_gap_h` for opens; for terminals it is 0 by construction and is not a contrast. Gaps of 48–72h (S11, S25, S26) are stall markers.
- Path pattern / variant: AutoCheck-first short success (`Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve`, S16; `Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve`, S18) versus Screen-Review-Escalate-Transfer (S08, S21, S32) versus Screen-Review stall (S09, S26).

**Post-treatment / leakage if used as baseline predictors of time-from-submit**

- `escalated` as a from-submit covariate: immortal time plus selection. S18 cannot be compared with S01 as “escalation effects” without time-aware encoding. Escalation is an intermediate treatment/state.
- `rework_count` as baseline: rework happens after at least one `Review` (S07, S13, S27). Conditioning on it from \(t=0\) requires surviving to that review loop.
- Full prefix including `Resolve`/`Transfer`/`Reject`/`Withdraw`: using the terminal activity or “path ends in Transfer” to predict duration is outcome leakage.
- Any feature that needs the eventual duration to know that escalation “was late.”

**Stage-hazard hypotheses (labeled as hypotheses)**

- H1. From `Escalate`, the next identified exits in these data are fast resolve (S04, S18), late resolve (S03, S13, S28), transfer (S08, S21, S32), or long-gap open (S10, S11, S25, S29). Transfer and stall dominate P1.
- H2. From `Screen` without `AutoCheck`, early reject is common (S05, S23) and so is early withdraw (S12).
- H3. B cases that skip `AutoCheck` (S20, S26, S30) look like A’s long Review paths; incomplete compliance may reconstruct the pre-change tail.
- H4. Long `last_event_gap_h` on open escalations (S11, S25) is closer to a hidden absorbing “stalled/waiting” state than to independent censoring.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

Three fully specified analyses, plus what could reverse.

**Analysis S1 — primary (T-compete, WD/RJ competing, opens independently censored, ITT cohorts).** AJ with causes `{resolve, withdraw, reject, transfer}`. Cohort is a stratification factor, not a causal contrast. Unknown 72-hour mass is only the five under-horizon opens. Qualitative reading: on-time internal resolution is a minority outcome (\(\theta_A\) cannot exceed \(14/32\)); a large share of remaining mass is competing exits plus already-late WIP; P1 is transfer- and stall-heavy (S08, S11, S21, S25, S32) rather than fast-success-heavy (only S04, S18 on-time).

**Analysis S2 — customer-unknown transfers and informative opens (T-censor + stall recode).** Transfers S08 and S21 become censors at 68 and 26 (S32 still known-unresolved at 72). Opens with gap \(\ge 48\)h (S11, S25, S26) recoded as a competing “stall/hidden withdraw” at last-event time, or at age, as a pair of bounds. This *raises* apparent net resolution among cases still believed active (hard P1 exports and abandoned files leave the risk set) and *lowers* \(\theta_B\) if stalls are treated as already disposed. It can make Cohort B look better if S26 is removed from B’s tail occupancy.

**Analysis S3 — policy-compliance and composite failure (T-fail + B restricted to AutoCheck-observed).** Transfer is a failure. Cohort B is split into executed-AutoCheck (13 cases) versus non-compliant prefixes S20, S23, S26, S30. This asks whether the visible B improvement, if any, lives only on the new path (S16, S18, S19, S27) while the old path still produces S20/S26-like tails. Because rollout was incomplete and non-random, S3 is descriptive of *executed* process, not of the intention-to-treat policy.

**Additional one-at-a-time checks.** (i) Collapse withdraw+reject into one competing cause. (ii) Landmark at 48h instead of from submit. (iii) Bounds only, ignoring AJ interpolation: \(\theta_A\in[0.281,0.438]\), \(\theta_B\in[9/32,14/32]=[0.281,0.438]\) because nine cases are known still internal at 72 (four late resolves, four late opens, S32) and five under-horizon opens may or may not remain.

**Which qualitative conclusions could reverse**

- “P1 is the worst SLA stratum.” Holds under T-compete and T-fail (3/8 transferred, 2/8 late-open, only 2/8 on-time resolve, 1 still unknown). **Reverses** under T-success-if-before-72, which would credit S08 and S21 as on-time handling successes and leave P1 looking like a fast-resolve-or-fast-hand-off lane.
- “About one-third of work breaches.” True only in the completed-resolved slice (\(4/13\)). **Reverses** as soon as late opens and competing non-resolves are included (\(1-\theta_A\) is at least \(18/32\) and at most \(23/32\)).
- “Cohort B improved tail risk.” Possible if analysis is restricted to AutoCheck-executed B and T-censor drops B’s P1 transfers (S21, S32). **Reverses or vanishes** in ITT S1, because B still has S20 (late), S26 (stall), S25 (late open after escalate), S28 (late resolve), S30 (withdraw at 70), and more P1 volume.
- “Escalation helps” or “escalation hurts.” Either can be produced by a leaked baseline `escalated` flag. Time-aware landmarking can go either way: S18 vs S11/S25 are opposite destinies after the same state. Small \(n\) means this conclusion is not identified.
- “Open cases are still healthy WIP.” True for S15/S31/S29. **Reverses** for S11/S25/S26 if long gaps are stalls. Occupancy \(\theta_B\) then overstates actionable workload.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Convert curves into actions without treating scores as causal effects of priority, cohort, or escalation.

**Alerts (case level, time-aware).** Do not alert on a static “P(breach)” trained with future `Escalate`/`Rework`. Alert on landmark state and gap:

- Near-horizon active review: S24 (67h, gap 4h) is an SLA countdown alert — still salvageable.
- Escalated and silent: S11 (gap 55h) and S25 (gap 48h) are stall/exception alerts, not “more of the same queue.”
- Abandoned review: S26 (gap 72h) is a data-quality or dead-case alert.
- Recently touched under-horizon work (S15, S29, S31) is ordinary watch, not a breach siren.

**Workload actions.** \(\hat\theta_B\) and the AJ occupancy of `Review` vs `Escalate` at 48–72h are staffing objects. Late resolves (S03, S13, S20, S28) show that some tail cases *do* finish after 72; they are overtime/backlog, not automatic loss. Transfers (S08, S21, S32) are *exported* workload: internal headcount drops but customer outcome is unknown — do not congratulate a team for a falling WIP that is actually \(F_X\).

**SLA governance.** Report the Section 4 primary bundle, not a single “breach rate”:

- On-time internal resolve \(\hat\theta_A\) (credit).
- Cause split at 72: late-still-open, withdraw, reject, transfer (explanation).
- Residual occupancy \(\hat\theta_B\) (capacity).
- Bounds, because five cases are still open under 72.

A target such as “80% resolved by 72” is not currently attainable on the identified interval even in the best case (\(14/32\approx 44\%\)) unless the population is redefined to exclude withdraw/reject/transfer. If the business truly wants “80% of *workable* cases,” it must pre-specify that population (Estimand A among cases that do not withdraw or reject) and accept that this is no longer an intake-level SLA.

**Not causal.** P1 cases are routed to `Escalate` and `Transfer` by design. Cohort B is a time period with incomplete compliance. Differences are mixtures of mix, policy, and selection. Do not use a higher P1 non-resolution rate to argue that high priority “causes” delay, and do not use B’s AutoCheck-path successes (S16, S18) as proof the rollout worked for all of B.

**Calibration.** With 32 rows, do not deploy a parametric risk score. If a landmark score is trialed, check calibration in the only slices that matter: predicted vs observed on-time resolve among cases that reach 48h still open, overall and inside P2 (the only priority with enough volume). Report Brier or observed-vs-expected in two or three score bins, not AUC theater. Recalibrate when the five under-horizon cases close.

**Fairness / equity checks (descriptive).**

- By priority: P1 has the highest transfer and post-escalate stall share; P3 has the early reject/withdraw share (S05, S12, S23) and two long Review tails (S09, S26). Equalizing \(\theta_A\) across priorities is not fairness if P3 includes appropriate fast rejects.
- By cohort: publish ITT A vs B *and* B-compliant vs B-noncompliant. If only the compliant slice looks better, the fairness issue is uneven rollout, not a uniformly improved process.
- Do not let a model that uses `escalated` or `rework_count` as raw features auto-deprioritize cases that already received extra handling.

def encode_transfer(cause: pd.Series, t: pd.Series, scenario: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Three transfer treatments. Open rows always remain event=0 (never dropped)."""
    out = pd.DataFrame({"t": t.astype(float), "cause": cause.astype(int)})
    if scenario == "T-compete":
        return out
    if scenario == "T-censor":
        out.loc[out["cause"] == 4, "cause"] = 0
        return out
    if scenario == "T-fail":
        # composite failure: late resolve, any transfer, treated as fail=5
        # on-time resolve stays 1; WD/RJ stay competing 2/3; open stays 0
        late_resolve = (out["cause"] == 1) & (out["t"] > L)
        transferred = out["cause"] == 4
        out.loc[late_resolve | transferred, "cause"] = 5
        return out
    raise ValueError(scenario)


def aalen_johansen(time: np.ndarray, cause: np.ndarray, times=(72.0,)):
    """Nonparametric AJ CIFs + all-cause KM. Independent of lifelines."""
    time = np.asarray(time, float)
    cause = np.asarray(cause, int)
    order = np.argsort(time, kind="mergesort")
    time, cause = time[order], cause[order]
    unique_t = np.unique(time)
    causes = sorted(set(cause) - {0})
    cif = {k: 0.0 for k in causes}
    surv = 1.0
    hist = []
    want = set(float(x) for x in times)
    snap = {}
    for ut in unique_t:
        at = time == ut
        n_risk = int(np.sum(time >= ut))
        d_all = int(np.sum(at & (cause != 0)))
        d_k = {k: int(np.sum(at & (cause == k))) for k in causes}
        if n_risk == 0:
            continue
        cif_step = {k: surv * (d_k[k] / n_risk) for k in causes}
        for k in causes:
            cif[k] += cif_step[k]
        surv *= 1.0 - d_all / n_risk
        hist.append((ut, n_risk, d_k, dict(cif), surv))
        if ut in want or ut >= max(want):
            # snapshot immediately after this time if it is a requested grid point
            pass
    for grid in times:
        # last history row with ut <= grid
        prev = [h for h in hist if h[0] <= grid]
        if prev:
            _, n_risk, _, cif_g, surv_g = prev[-1]
            snap[grid] = {"S_all": surv_g, "cif": dict(cif_g), "n_after_last_event": n_risk}
        else:
            snap[grid] = {"S_all": 1.0, "cif": {k: 0.0 for k in causes}, "n_after_last_event": len(time)}
    return snap, hist


def summarize_block(label: str, part: pd.DataFrame, scenario: str) -> dict:
    enc = encode_transfer(part["cause_raw"], part["t"], scenario)
    snap, _ = aalen_johansen(enc["t"].values, enc["cause"].values, times=(L,))
    s = snap[L]
    cif = s["cif"]
    f_resolve = cif.get(1, 0.0)
    # Estimand A: CIF of (on-time) resolve. For T-fail, cause 1 is only on-time resolve.
    # Complement 1 - F_R includes competing exits, late work, and residual survival.
    theta_A = f_resolve
    theta_B = s["S_all"]  # residual no-terminal if transfer is an event; if T-censor, transfer does not end T_term
    n = len(part)
    n_on_time = int(((part["observed_status"] == "resolved") & (part["t"] <= L)).sum())
    n_unknown = int(((part["observed_status"] == "open") & (part["t"] < L)).sum())
    n_known_fail = n - n_on_time - n_unknown
    return {
        "stratum": label,
        "scenario": scenario,
        "n": n,
        "theta_A_CIF_resolve": round(theta_A, 3),
        "one_minus_theta_A": round(1.0 - theta_A, 3),
        "theta_B_S_all": round(theta_B, 3),
        "CIF_withdraw": round(cif.get(2, 0.0), 3),
        "CIF_reject": round(cif.get(3, 0.0), 3),
        "CIF_transfer": round(cif.get(4, 0.0), 3),
        "CIF_composite_fail": round(cif.get(5, 0.0), 3),
        "bound_theta_A_low": round(n_on_time / n, 3) if n else np.nan,
        "bound_theta_A_high": round((n_on_time + n_unknown) / n, 3) if n else np.nan,
        "naive_late_among_resolved": (
            round(
                ((part["observed_status"] == "resolved") & (part["t"] > L)).sum()
                / max((part["observed_status"] == "resolved").sum(), 1),
                3,
            )
        ),
        "n_open_kept": int((part["observed_status"] == "open").sum()),
    }

# 9. Limitations and additional data

The extract can bound 72-hour internal non-resolution and can show that completed-resolved rates are the wrong object. It cannot support a causal claim about Cohort B, a calibrated individual risk score, or a continuous-time multi-state fit.

**Highest-value missing data**

1. **Exact event timestamps** for every activity, not only duration/age and last-event gap. Without them, escalation time, sojourn in `Review`, and whether `Escalate` preceded hour 48 are unidentified. Landmark and multi-state models are forced to use coarse rules. Immortal-time bias cannot be fully removed, only avoided by refusing leaked covariates.
2. **External outcomes after `Transfer`.** S08, S21, S32 stop the internal film. Customer-centric SLA (did the *case* resolve by 72, anywhere?) is unidentified. T-compete, T-censor, and T-fail will keep disagreeing until external resolve/reject times exist.
3. **Observation cutoff and case start dates.** `duration_or_age_h` is a frozen age, not a calendar. Administrative censoring may cluster (end-of-extract). Entry-cohort effects are entangled with policy Cohort B. Delayed entry and calendar workload shocks cannot be modeled.
4. **Time-varying workload and queue state.** Tail risk after `Review` or `Escalate` may be congestion, not case mix. S24 versus S26 cannot be separated into “hard case” versus “unstaffed queue” with the current columns.
5. **Assignment mechanism and priority rules.** P1 is both a covariate and a treatment that triggers `Escalate`/`Transfer`. Without the routing rule (and whether S18-style fast escalate is automatic), P1 strata are descriptive.
6. **Policy rollout compliance.** Four B cases lack `AutoCheck` (S20, S23, S26, S30). We need the intended rule, the reason for skip, and whether skip was allowed. Incomplete compliance means B is a mixture, not a treated arm.
7. **Meaning of long gaps.** S11, S25, S26 may be waiting on customer evidence, sitting with an external party without a `Transfer` event, abandoned, or logging failures. Independent censoring stands or falls on that distinction.
8. **Richer terminals and SLA clock rules.** Whether a fast `Reject` (S05, S23) counts as SLA success, whether the clock pauses, and whether `Transfer` before 72 is an accepted disposition are definitional, not statistical. They must be fixed before any single “breach probability” is published.
9. **Sample size and follow-up of the five under-horizon opens.** S10, S15, S24, S29, S31 still move \(\theta_A\) and \(\theta_B\) across a 15-point range. Closing those five cases is worth more than adding covariates.

Until those items exist, the defensible statements are the ones that stay close to the table: on-time internal resolution is at most 14 of 32 submits; at least 18 of 32 are already known not internally resolved by 72; naive 4/13 late-among-resolved is survivor-biased; transfers are selected P1 escalations, not ordinary censors; `Escalate` and `Rework` are intermediate states; and Cohort B cannot be read as a randomized policy effect.
