## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (taken as given by the record).** Timestamps, amounts, roles, the ECD-2 window (2026-07-14 09:00 – 07-18 18:00 CEST), the ON-44 outage window (07-14 08:30–13:00) and its one-directional skew (ERP stamp  real time), the signatories of each source, the CFO's documented competence (Finance framework only), the CRO's unavailability and non-ratification, and the organizational precedent that signed incident directives have been treated as temporary policy overlays (but never on new-supplier Compliance clearance).

**Interpretations (my reading of the sources, argued not asserted).**
- *Hierarchy by subject matter, not by document.* GPIP-4 controls (A1–A3) rank above everything for integrity, financial dual-control, and Compliance-gate issues, because of board status, later-in-time effect over the SOP, and A5's express anti-weakening clause. ECD-2 is a *time-and-scope-limited operational overlay*: it validly displaces local workflow steps (B1, B3, the Plant leg of B2) inside its window and scope — supported by B7 and by C4 — but it is **not**, on the primary reading, a valid A7 exception, because A7 reserves written exceptions to the CRO, the CRO neither issued nor ratified ECD-2, and A4 confines emergency relief to tendering. A7's silence about COO/IC directives is treated as unresolved ambiguity, resolved **against** waiver power (see burden rule below).
- *The CFO email (D)* is treated as an authentic executive direction with intermediate weight: the CFO may **interpret** the Finance control (so joint pre-commitment IC authorization can satisfy the *Finance-Controller leg* of A2 and the Finance leg of B2, within the window and  EUR 150k). It cannot satisfy the *independent budget-owner leg* (a GPIP-4 requirement outside the CFO's stated competence; IC members are not designated budget owners), cannot waive A1 or A3, and cannot operate outside the window or above EUR 150k. D2 (Legal clearance > 150k) binds as management direction. D4 is read as confirming that D1 was never meant to displace integrity controls. D's uncontrolled status lowers its weight but does not nullify it, since the addressed actors acted on it.
- *Time-of-act discipline.* Every rule is tested at the **commitment** (the moment the company becomes bound), using window, scope, and approval status at that moment. Later approvals mitigate (A6) but do not cure.
- *Scope of ECD-2 (C6).* Primary reading: "required to contain the current contamination event" is necessity-anchored — parts needed within roughly 24 hours for the affected line. Backup/contingency stock (P-503) fails this test; same-day and 8-hour needs (P-502, P-505, P-507, P-508) pass it. The Operations reading is acknowledged as the strongest alternative.
- *"New supplier" in A3* is read to include any supplier not in the approved register at commitment time (matters only for P-504; P-502 is expressly "new"; P-507/P-508 cleared Compliance anyway).
- *A2's "independent budget owner" leg* is operationally instantiated by Plant_Approve where the approver is independent of requester/committer; IC joint authorization does not satisfy it (charter silence). Note A2's independence qualifier textually attaches to the budget owner, not the Finance Controller — which is what makes the CFO's D1 substitution of the finance leg defensible.
- *A1's "final business approver"* includes any person whose authorization is the operative pre-commitment business approval under the applicable regime, including IC joint authorizers.

**Assumptions (flagged, would change results if false).** Plant approvers are site budget owners; requester/committer identities do not overlap with approvers except where stated (P-503, P-508); unrecorded events (forms, POs, recordings) were not performed unless corroboration exists; verbal statements recorded in the case table (e.g., IC authorizations) occurred as described.

**Burden of proof and evidential discipline.** The proponent of an exception, of a waiver, or of compliance credit for a contested act bears the burden; unresolved waiver authority resolves against override (A4's "unless," A5's anti-weakening clause). ON-44 makes contemporaneous evidence (telephony, signed PDFs, supplier acknowledgments, access logs) superior to ERP timestamps during disturbances; the skew is one-directional, so a real act can be *earlier* than its ERP stamp, never later. Where the occurrence of a required act is unproven but plausible, the gap is logged as a recording defect and does not by itself drive the classification; where the classification depends on an unproven act, the case trends to INDETERMINATE. No case in this set ultimately required INDETERMINATE, but two (P-505, P-506) come close on specific sub-issues.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501  COMPLIANT / JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: read B4 purposively so that a Shift Lead may commit whenever the Plant Manager cannot act within the emergency horizon, treating the reachable manager's non-objection plus same-day formal approvals (15:10/15:12) as ratification, with A6 mitigation. Credibility: moderate on purpose (a 4-hour stoppage is exactly the B4 scenario), weak on text — B4 says "absent … actively delegated," and a reachable manager is not absent; A6 expressly denies automatic cure. This alternative would also have to explain why the SOP's own safeguard was designed around delegation if reachability sufficed.

**P-502  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (or COMPLIANT).** Premise change: ECD-2 is an *implied A7 exception* — the COO holds executive authority, the organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays, and A7's silence is tolerance rather than reservation. Then C4 waives the new-supplier Compliance pre-commitment; with D1 covering the finance leg and IC treated as de facto budget owners, only documentation lapses remain. Credibility: this is the pivotal question of the entire set and is genuinely arguable; it is weakened by A4's express reservation ("other controls remain unless … CRO"), A5's anti-weakening clause, the CRO's non-ratification, and the stated fact that the precedent never covered new-supplier Compliance clearance. If accepted, P-502, P-507 (fully), P-508 (as to A2), and P-505 (as to A2) all shift toward COMPLIANT/JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION — which is precisely why the primary reading must be stated, not assumed.

**P-505  COMPLIANT.** Premise change (two parts): (a) D1's "all operational approvals" is read to include the budget-owner function during the window — supported by the CFO's executive-committee standing and the IC's de facto decision authority, and by the argument that A2's independence concern is attenuated when two distinct IC members authorize; (b) the supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is the binding commitment. Credibility: (a) is moderate — it requires reading a Finance-framework interpretation as reaching a GPIP-4 control, against D3/D4; (b) is genuinely fragile: a reviewer could equally find the commitment completed only at the ERP entry (18:06), post-expiry, which would additionally breach A2-F and B2. The case's outcome is the most time-sensitive in the set.

**P-506  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: the alleged verbal overnight extension is valid (incident practice; IC as operational continuators of the signatories), and Plant_Approve at 09:10 is a prompt budget-owner ratification under a lenient reading of A6 ("not automatically"  "never"). Credibility: weak — a directive whose own regime demands 4-hour recording cannot be extended by unrecorded verbal assertion; A7's writing requirement applies by analogy to any waiver of A-controls; and no joint authorization record exists. Even under this alternative the classification improves only to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, not COMPLIANT.

**P-508  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: ECD-2-as-A7 overrides A2 entirely within window  scope, treating C5's silence as deliberate and the Compliance + Legal + joint-IC package as an equivalent safeguard mix. Credibility: moderate — P-508 has the strongest necessity facts ("only available pump") and the best pre-commitment control package among the large purchases; but D2 cuts against it, because the CFO's own directive demanded *more* (Legal clearance) above €150k, implying IC authorization alone was understood to be insufficient at that level.

**P-503 — robustness note (no classification-changing alternative found).** No reasonable premise reaches COMPLIANT: A1 self-approval survives every source (nothing waives A1; D4 preserves integrity controls; even ECD-2-as-A7 would require a CRO writing to displace SoD). The scope dispute changes only the *set* of additional breaches (B1/B2/A2 in full under strict scope; A2-B plus tainted approvals under lenient scope) and the severity, not the primary classification. Where a case's classification is invariant across the plausible interpretive range, that robustness is itself the strongest defensible finding.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Category distinctions applied:**

- **Policy breach (substantive rule violated at the time of the act):** P-501 (unauthorized commitment under B4's own conditions); P-502 (A3 timing; A2-B); P-503 (A1; A2; plus B1/B2 under strict scope); P-504 (B1, B3, probable A3, unauthorized commitment); P-505 and P-507 (A2-B); P-506 (B1; A2-B timing); P-508 (A2 both legs).
- **Logging defect (act occurred; record defective):** P-501 (ON-44 entries); P-502 (verbal auth recorded 2h40m later — within rule but late relative to act); P-505 (ERP 18:06 vs real ~17:57–17:59:31; Create_PO never recorded); P-507 (ERP 14:30 vs PDF 12:40).
- **Late evidence of a timely act (corroboration accepted over database):** P-505 supplier acknowledgment; P-507 queued signed PDF; P-502 telephone recording. These were credited because ON-44-type disturbances make contemporaneous sources superior; the crediting is one-directional (it can establish earliness, never lateness).
- **Inability to prove compliance:** P-506's alleged verbal extension (unprovable  treated as absent); P-503's necessity/scope and PROC-3's IC membership; P-504's B6 timing and the "new vs unregistered" supplier status; budget-owner independence in P-502/P-505/P-507/P-508; C3 recording timing in P-508.

1. Route P-502, P-503, P-505, P-506, P-507, P-508 to the CRO (or delegated successor) for documented post-hoc risk acceptance or rejection — explicitly framed as mitigation under A6, not cure; where rejected, escalate for commercial unwinding assessment.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Draft — GPIP-4 §A8: Incident Procurement Overlay and Exception Regime (replacement clause)**

1. **Order of precedence.** During a declared incident window, a signed incident directive displaces *local workflow steps* (sequencing, PO timing, supplier-register use, Plant-approval substitution) **within its certified scope and window only**. It does not displace GPIP-4 §A1–A3 unless a valid §A7 exception exists.
2. **Non-waivable core.** Segregation of duties (A1), dual pre-commitment approval  €50k (A2), and the Compliance gate for new suppliers or > €100k (A3) may be waived only by a written, scoped, time-limited exception signed by the Chief Risk Officer. If the CRO is unreachable, a named deputy chain (CRO  General Counsel  CFO, in that order) applies; no other officer or body, including joint Incident Command action, may waive the core.
3. **Scope certification.** A directive must define "critical" by an operational test — goods required within 24 hours to prevent or contain the declared event — and each purchase under it requires an Incident Commander certification with reason codes. Backup, contingency, and out-of-scope needs are excluded unless separately certified; an incorrect certification is a reportable control breach.
4. **Unified thresholds.** During a declared window, a single incident matrix replaces the 25k/50k/100k/150k stack: joint IC authorization satisfies the Finance-approval leg  €150k; above €150k, both the ordinary Finance approval and Legal clearance are required; the independent budget-owner leg is never satisfied by IC authorization.
5. **New suppliers.** A pre-commitment expedited Compliance check (target SLA: 2 hours) is mandatory for any unregistered supplier; if unachievable before commitment, a §A7 exception is required — silence or urgency is not a waiver.
6. **Records and evidence.** Verbal authorizations must be recorded within 4 hours by signed PDF or system entry; where systems are impaired, contemporaneous evidence (telephony, e-signature queues, acknowledgments, access logs) prevails over ERP timestamps, and known clock skew must be documented and corrected one-directionally (real time  ERP stamp). Missing records are logging defects and are monitored separately from policy breaches.
7. **Expiry and renewal.** Directives lapse automatically at the stated time. No verbal extension is valid. Renewal must be written and signed by the original signatories (or the deputy chain) **before** expiry; commitments perfected before expiry remain governed by the directive (60-minute in-flight grace for completion of the commitment act).
8. **Ratification limits.** Post-hoc approval mitigates and may support documented CRO risk acceptance for defined minor breaches; it never retroactively validates a breach of §A1 or an uncertified scope expansion.
9. **Anti-normalization safeguards.** (a) Every purchase in an incident window is auto-flagged for Internal Audit review within 5 business days; (b) any emergency purchase above a de minimis threshold requires CRO countersignature within 10 business days; (c) Internal Audit reports quarterly on emergency-purchase frequency, value, per-requester repeats, and out-of-scope labeling, with a mandatory incident review triggered by any out-of-scope "emergency" label; (d) repeated use of emergency pathways by the same requester within 90 days triggers automatic escalation to the CRO.
