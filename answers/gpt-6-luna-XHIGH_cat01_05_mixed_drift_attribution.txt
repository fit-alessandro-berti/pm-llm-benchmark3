## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March gap is diagnostic: raw fitness drops from about 0.925 in January-February to 0.55, while mapped fitness is 0.90. Replacing Eligibility Screen with Validate Eligibility and removing duplicated technical Open Case events recovers 0.35 fitness points, close to the pre-migration level. This strongly supports a migration-related label/provenance effect in the raw conformance result. It does not show that all recorded events kept the same meaning, that event ordering is correct, or that behavior was unchanged. A mapping asserts equivalence for analysis; it cannot establish that the screen implemented the same eligibility check or scope.

Duplicate Open Case events can inflate event counts, create false starts/loops in raw directly-follows graphs and variants, and distort case-duration or ordering calculations if timestamps are included. Removing known duplicates is useful only if the deduplication rule is validated. Missing resource IDs mainly weaken resource/team attribution, handover and workload analyses, and resource-stratified performance comparisons; they need not directly depress control-flow fitness. Their concentration in migrated activities means complete-case resource analysis can be biased.

After May, mapped fitness also declines (0.88-0.86 in May-July), while the normalized graph shows Assess->Request Info rising 12 to 29 per 100 and Request Info->Collect Documents 11 to 28. Rework variants rise 11-13% to 24-29%, and cycle time increases. These concordant, operational-table and normalized signals are not explained by the March label mapping alone. Supplier disruption is a plausible source of rework; staffing and priority changes plausibly affect queueing. Aggregation and incomplete edge coverage prevent assigning exact causal shares.

### Normalized DFG-fragment distance

Let each month be a seven-dimensional vector of the supplied per-100-case relation rates, in the listed order. Use mean absolute difference (MAD) from January: sum of the seven absolute edge-rate differences divided by seven. This transparent distance is on the scale of relation occurrences per 100 cases; it is not a probability distance, since edges can co-occur and the fragment omits edges. January-to-March MAD is 0.43; May 5.71; July 8.43; September 4.00; October 9.14; December 5.14. For example, July's L1 difference is 59 rate points: lower CollectDocs->Assess (-6) and decision-edge rate (-11), and higher RequestInfo-related rates (+17 and +17) and Senior Assessment (+5). January-to-March is small after normalization despite the large raw fitness collapse. October's rise is driven particularly by Assess->Senior Assessment, 4 to 22 per 100, alongside rework and reduced decision-edge rate. Since these are fragments, case completion, omitted transitions, and case mix can change observed rates; the distance summarizes a directional signature, not whole-log graph distance or causal effect.

### Cycle-time deterioration decomposition

From the Jan-Feb median baseline (31.15 h) to July (44.2 h), the median rises 13.05 h; to November (49.0 h), it rises 17.85 h. The available data support this qualitative decomposition, but not a uniquely identified additive split:

| component | evidence and plausible contribution to observed deterioration |
|---|---|
| Case mix | High complexity rises from 20.5% baseline to 28% in July and 45% in November; October-November volume is 24-29% above baseline. This can increase expected work and queue load. Its direct median contribution is unidentifiable without within-complexity cycle times and fixed-mix standardization. |
| Control-flow/rework | Rework variants increase 17.5 pp by July; Request Info and return-to-Collect Documents edges rise by 17 and 17 per 100. Extra collection/assessment rounds plausibly add service and waiting time, but variants and edge occurrences are not a count of hours. |
| Service | Assess median service time increases only about 0.6 h (4.8 to 5.4) by July-August. That is modest relative to a 13.05 h total-median increase. More repeat visits can raise total service effort even if per-visit service time is stable. |
| Queueing | Reconstructed pre-Assess queue waiting increased much more than service time; assessor exits and rising demand provide a mechanism. It is the strongest candidate for the largest share of deterioration, especially the tail, but exact hours by mechanism are not supplied. Priority can move delay between complexity groups. |

The components mediate one another: rework and case mix raise demand, which raises queues; staffing reduces capacity; priority reallocates the queue. Therefore, assigning percentages that sum to the observed change would manufacture precision.

### Candidate change points and global-change assessment

- **Logging/provenance:** March 1 is the primary candidate for labels, duplicates, and missing-resource discontinuity. March-April is a secondary candidate window for persistence/settling of the migrated capture (not a separately established intervention); resource missingness rises from 4.8% to 5.2%. A further persistent capture-quality level is evident from May onward, but needs event-level/schema evidence before being called a new change point.
- **Behavior/control flow:** May is the clearest candidate (supplier disruption aligns with rework and Request Info jumps); July 1 is a second candidate for priority-related routing/ordering. October is a further candidate for seasonal complexity mix and recorded Senior Assessment, whose behavioral versus recording meaning is unresolved.
- **Performance:** May is a candidate as median/p90 rise with supplier-related rework; June-July is another as staffing/priority changes coincide with queue growth and worsening cycle times. October is a seasonal workload candidate, and December a joint recovery/intervention candidate.

There is no support for one global change point. The strong March break is largely semantic/provenance in fitness and labels; real rework/performance changes start around May, capacity and priority mechanisms enter June-July, and seasonal mix rises in October. Different artifacts have different onset, persistence, and reversal patterns. Formal change-point testing should use event-level data, account for serial dependence and seasonality, and report uncertainty rather than force a single date.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0: all registered cases | Intake month/week, region, supplier, pre-case complexity; retain unknown values | Operational volume, total delay, and outcome surveillance | Report cohort counts; suppress unstable quantiles below 100 completed cases and pool adjacent weeks | Overall capacity and seasonal trend | Aggregate confounding by changing mix; do not interpret as causal |
| 1: fixed baseline-supported strata | Pre-case complexity x broad region x supplier class, with stable category definitions | Primary case-mix-standardized monitoring population | Keep only strata represented in baseline and current period; merge sparse regions; require at least 30 cases per comparison cell and 100 for tail quantiles | Like-for-like DFG, rework, conformance, and delay comparison | Conditioning on supplier/region may select customers; baseline support limits generalization |
| 2: operational mechanism strata | Priority eligibility (pre-rule definition), assigned priority, assessor/team, staffing exposure band | Separate queue redistribution and capacity effects | Require 30 per cell; aggregate team-months to quarter or service line if below threshold; keep unknown resource explicit | High vs standard queue, staffing exposure, within-team patterns | Assigned priority/team can be post-treatment mediators or colliders; use eligibility/exposure for primary contrasts |

| 4: temporal/event-level slices | Migration version, pre/post dates, activity, source system, event completeness | Logging, semantic, and ordering audits | Require enough cases/events for stable rate intervals; otherwise qualitative audit | Raw versus normalized path, duplicate and missingness concentration | Filtering on observed events can select better-logged cases |

The primary monitoring population should be registered cases in the Jan-Feb-supported strata, reweighted to fixed Jan-Feb case-mix weights for complexity, broad region, and supplier class. Keep unknown/missing categories visible and publish the unstandardized all-intake metrics beside it. This provides a stable like-for-like process signal without hiding actual volume or supplier exposure; it also avoids defining the population by downstream events such as rework, completion, or assigned priority.

baseline = monthly[monthly["month"].dt.month.isin([1, 2])].mean(numeric_only=True)
tracked = ["cases", "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "rework_variant_pct",
           "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "mapped_model_fitness", "missing_resource_pct"]
changes = monthly[["month"] + tracked].copy()
for col in tracked:
    changes[col + "_delta"] = changes[col] - baseline[col]
    if col == "cases":
        changes[col + "_pct_change"] = 100 * changes[col + "_delta"] / baseline[col]
print("Jan-Feb baseline:")
print(baseline[tracked].round(3).to_string())
print("Baseline-relative values and changes:")
print(changes.round(3).to_string(index=False))

# Transparent mean absolute difference from January across the seven supplied
# edge rates. This is not a probability distance: edges may co-occur and other
# edges are omitted from the supplied fragment.
edge_matrix = dfg_rates.set_index("month")[edge_cols]
reference = edge_matrix.loc[pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01")]
dfg_distance = edge_matrix.sub(reference, axis="columns").abs().mean(axis=1)
print("DFG fragment MAD from January (rate occurrences per 100 cases):")
print(dfg_distance.round(3).to_string())

# Fitness recovery is the raw-to-mapped gap divided by the raw loss from the
# Jan-Feb raw baseline; it is descriptive and not proof of equivalence.
raw_baseline = baseline["raw_model_fitness"]
monthly["fitness_gap"] = monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
monthly["raw_loss_recovered_fraction"] = (
    monthly["fitness_gap"] / (raw_baseline - monthly["raw_model_fitness"])
).where(monthly["raw_model_fitness"] < raw_baseline)
print("Raw-to-mapped fitness gap and fraction of baseline raw loss recovered:")
print(monthly[["month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness", "fitness_gap",
               "raw_loss_recovered_fraction"]].round(3).to_string(index=False))

# Simple, auditable candidate flags compare each month with its predecessor.
# These are screening rules, not formal change-point tests.
monthly = monthly.sort_values("month").reset_index(drop=True)
monthly["raw_fitness_drop"] = -monthly["raw_model_fitness"].diff()
monthly["rework_rise_pp"] = monthly["rework_variant_pct"].diff()
monthly["median_rise_h"] = monthly["median_cycle_h"].diff()
monthly["logging_flag"] = (
    (monthly["distinct_activity_labels"] >= 2 + 9) &
    (monthly["raw_fitness_drop"] >= 0.20)
) | (monthly["duplicate_open_case_pct"] >= 4)
monthly["behavior_flag"] = monthly["rework_rise_pp"] >= 8
monthly["performance_flag"] = monthly["median_rise_h"] >= 3
print("Candidate months under simple rules:")
for flag in ["logging_flag", "behavior_flag", "performance_flag"]:
    print(flag, monthly.loc[monthly[flag], "month"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").tolist())

import copy

LABEL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

def format_event_dataframe(raw_df):
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted copy using standard case/activity/time keys."""
    df = raw_df.copy(deep=True)
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="raise")
    if "start_timestamp" in df.columns:
        df["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp" if "start_timestamp" in df.columns else None
    )

def normalized_copy(raw_df, label_map=None, drop_known_duplicate_open=False):
    """Map labels on a copy; drop only explicitly identified technical duplicates."""
    mapped = raw_df.copy(deep=True)
    mapped["activity"] = mapped["activity"].replace(label_map or LABEL_MAP)
    if drop_known_duplicate_open:
        if "technical_duplicate" not in mapped.columns:
            raise ValueError("Need an explicit technical_duplicate flag to deduplicate safely")
        mapped = mapped.loc[~(
            mapped["technical_duplicate"].fillna(False).astype(bool) &
            mapped["activity"].eq("Open Case")
        )].copy()
    return mapped

def slice_time_window(formatted_log, start, end, mode="events"):
    """Filter a PM4Py dataframe/EventLog to [start, end] using event timestamps."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_log, start, end, case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp", mode=mode
    )

def monthly_dfgs(formatted_log):
    """Return month -> directly-follows frequency map for the events in each month."""
    df = formatted_log.copy()
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    results = {}
    for month in sorted(df["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str).unique()):
        start = pd.Timestamp(month + "-01", tz="UTC")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        window = slice_time_window(df, start.to_pydatetime(), end.to_pydatetime(), mode="events")
        results[month] = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
    return results

def variant_distribution(formatted_log):
    """Return PM4Py's variant-to-cases mapping for an already formatted log."""
    return pm4py.get_variants(formatted_log)

def temporal_profile(formatted_log):
    """Return PM4Py temporal profiles for directly-follows activity pairs."""
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(formatted_log)

def optional_constraints(formatted_log):
    """Discover optional log-skeleton/DECLARE artifacts when exposed by the install."""
    found = {}
    if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_log_skeleton"):
        found["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(formatted_log)
    if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare"):
        found["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(formatted_log)
    return found

def compare_raw_and_normalized(raw_df):
    """Format separate raw/mapped copies and compare labels, DFGs, and variants."""
    raw_log = format_event_dataframe(raw_df)
    mapped_df = normalized_copy(raw_df, label_map=LABEL_MAP,
                                drop_known_duplicate_open=True)

    return {
        "raw_log": raw_log,
        "mapped_log": mapped_log,
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(raw_log),
        "mapped_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(mapped_log),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw_log),

        "raw_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(raw_log),
        "mapped_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(mapped_log),
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is the March discontinuity to migration-related logging and provenance changes (confidence: high, about 90-95%). The raw fitness collapse is large and abrupt, mapped fitness recovers nearly all of it, labels change exactly as described, and technical duplicates appear at the same time. Normalized January-March DFG rates are also very similar. This supports treating the raw fitness fall as mostly a measurement/semantic artifact. It does not establish that the mapped label has identical business meaning, and the resource-ID loss makes team-level conclusions less reliable throughout the remaining months.

A second, likely real process change starts around May (confidence: moderate-high, 75-85%). Supplier Z exposure, rework variants, Request Info/return edges, and cycle times rise together. Supplier Z's incomplete bundles are a credible mechanism, but its customer and regional mix was nonrandom and somewhat more complex even before May. The observed aggregate change therefore combines supplier-driven behavior with population composition. The September-to-December easing is consistent with supplier recovery and lower Z share, but backlog and evolving mix make the lag and effect size uncertain.

Queueing is the most plausible main source of the large cycle-time deterioration after May, with staffing loss and rising workload interacting (confidence: moderate, 65-80%). Recorded Assess service time increases by only about 0.6 h, while reconstructed waiting rises much more. Four assessor departures are a dated capacity shock, but supplier loops and high-complexity cases also consume capacity. The July priority rule plausibly redistributes waits between high-complexity and standard cases; the aggregate median/p90 cannot reveal who benefited. A within-priority queue comparison is needed before assigning direction or magnitude.

October-November show a strong seasonal mix/load shift (high confidence, 85% that it occurred; moderate confidence that it explains the performance change). Renewal timing, volume, and complexity move together. The Senior Assessment increase may be a true rise in senior routing, better recording of a historically inconsistent activity, or both (low-to-moderate confidence). December improvement cannot be assigned to overtime or the quality campaign separately from lower seasonal load, supplier recovery, and backlog clearance (low confidence in separate effects).

I would revise this account if fixed-mix, within-supplier and within-complexity comparisons eliminated the May rework shift; if event audits showed the mapped March activity changed definition; or if priority-stratified waits failed to show redistribution after July. Event-level queue intervals, staffing exposure, stable activity definitions, multi-year seasonality, and mature outcomes are the key missing evidence. These mechanisms can mediate one another, so the confidence levels concern the plausibility of mechanisms, not identified causal shares.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw conformance falls while a validated normalized score, stable-field ordering, and case outcomes remain within their expected bands, THEN investigate schema aliases and duplicate-event capture before escalating a process nonconformance, BECAUSE semantic/provenance drift can create a raw-only break.
2. IF a new unmapped label or impossible event order crosses its source-specific integrity limit, THEN quarantine the affected metric version and audit representative traces and producer changes, BECAUSE a corrupted event vocabulary or ordering can contaminate every downstream process artifact.
3. IF Request Info loops and rework rise within Supplier Z and comparable complexity/region strata, THEN start a supplier document-quality review and track bundle defects and backlog, BECAUSE the supplier disruption predicts incomplete bundles and repeat collection.
4. IF queue waiting and aged work rise while within-activity service time remains stable, THEN review capacity, assessor availability, and arrival-to-completion balance, BECAUSE queueing pressure is more consistent with demand/capacity imbalance than slower handling alone.
5. IF high-complexity waits fall while standard waits rise after the priority rule, THEN review priority allocation and publish the group-specific service gap, BECAUSE priority can redistribute delay while leaving the monthly aggregate ambiguous.
6. IF raw performance worsens during the renewal season but fixed-mix performance remains near its season-matched band, THEN adjust seasonal capacity plans and continue monitoring standardized quality, BECAUSE population and workload drift can raise aggregate delay without a within-stratum process failure.
7. IF performance improves after an intervention while seasonal load, supplier exposure, or case mix also falls, THEN treat the improvement as joint and evaluate intervention effects against a matched or controlled comparison, BECAUSE concurrent changes prevent attributing recovery to one cause.
