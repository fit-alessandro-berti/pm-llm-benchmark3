Not-yet-treated placebo: In Mar, before any adoption, Alpha/Beta/Gamma and Delta/Epsilon all moved together: median +2.2-3.1h, escalation +2.0pp. No obvious differential pre-trend Jan-FebMar supports parallel trends short-term, but May divergence breaks it.

**5.3 Treatment intensity analysis (descriptive, branch-FE):**

Within-adopter months Apr-Aug: regress median on intensity with branch FE:

- Alpha slope  -0.22h per 1pp (25%48% intensity, median 48.343.3). 
- Beta slope  -0.10h per pp (27%50%, 47.345.0)
- Gamma slope  -0.34h per pp (25%32%, 38.436.0)
- Pooled OLS with branch + month FE: coefficient  **-0.08 to -0.15h per pp** ( -0.8 to -1.5h per +10pp). For median ~45h, that is ~2-3% per 10pp.

p90 vs intensity: slope near zero or slightly positive early (Apr-Jun intensity up with p90 up), then negative late — no stable dose-response, consistent with queue congestion masking.

With complexity and arrivals/FTE controls, intensity coefficient shrinks toward zero and SE blows up (clusters=5), indicating confounding: May intensity rise coincides with complexity rise.

**5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean:**

*Fact:* Alpha/Beta selected for poor historical cycle times (48.9h/45.9h Jan-Mar vs 34.5h Delta, 40.3h Epsilon, 37.5h Gamma). Selection on high pre-values creates mean reversion risk: even without intervention they might improve. However pre-period JanFebMar shows no reversion: Alpha 50.446.949.4, Beta flat 45.445.946.4, parallel to controls. If mean reversion dominated, we would expect Jan-Mar already trending down; it does not.

*Counterpoint:* Short 3-month pre-window cannot rule out longer mean reversion; leadership explicitly targeted weak branches, so upward bias in estimated benefit expected. The observed *absolute* medians did not revert downward in early post months (Alpha/Beta medians rose to 49-53h in May-Jun) until late maturity, inconsistent with strong natural reversion.

**5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions:**

Median: 
- Lower bound (most optimistic vs controls, late window, Jan-Mar baseline) = -8.5h DiD (~-17% relative to 49h). 
- Upper bound (absolute vs own pre, no control, early window) = +1.9h Beta increase. 
- *Range we would report:* **-6 to 0h relative to deteriorating counterfactual; 0 to -7% absolute late improvement.** Any claim of >10% median reduction is not robust to window/baseline choice.

p90: 
- Absolute vs Jan-Mar: +7 to +10h worse (tail degraded everywhere). 
- Relative vs controls: -6 to -2h better than counterfactual. 
- Late vs Jan-Jun for Gamma: -0.9h flat. 
- *Bounded:* **No robust absolute p90 benefit; relative benefit -5h ± large uncertainty depends entirely on assuming controls are valid counterfactual for May complexity.** If controls had different complexity exposure, tail effect is not identified.

Rework and csat sensitivity similarly bounded near zero (see §7).

## 8. Rollout recommendation
**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Immediate broad expansion to claim “X% faster” is indefensible: effects are modest, relative to a deteriorating counterfactual, mixed median vs tail, and confounded by routing, staffing, and complexity. Pausing entirely forfeits plausible relative benefit and learning. The optimal policy maximizes both operational benefit and causal learning via a staged, randomized expansion.

Next branch: Delta alone for the next quarter, holding Epsilon as pure never-treated control. Delta is preferred to Epsilon because its volume, complexity trajectory and FTE gain (23) mirror Gamma’s profile, giving better overlap, and Epsilon’s high-complexity (38%) and distinct history would be a poor early test of generalizability.

Assignment: Within Delta, randomize at team-week or incoming-case level (e.g., 50% of triage shifts or arrival hours use automation) for 12 weeks after a 4-week warm-up, then cross over if guardrails pass. This creates exogenous variation in intensity uncorrelated with staffing/routing and doubles effective clusters versus branch-level rollout. Epsilon remains without automation for at least 12 weeks as external control for fraud-desk spillover measurement. Pre-period must be at least 12 weeks (Jan-Mar equivalent) of case-level event logs to establish stable baselines; post-period 12-16 weeks to capture learning (months 0-1 vs 3-4). Collect process-mining outcomes: full event log with Register, Triage mode (manual/auto + bypass reason), Specialist Review, fraud-desk referral, Rework loops, Resolve, Close timestamps; hourly queue lengths per station; staffing productive hours per shift; complexity at registration and triage; arrivals per FTE; first-pass resolution rate. Monitor spillover: fraud-desk queue time by origin branch and total referrals per day. Guardrails: pause if Delta p90 rises >10% vs synthetic control for 2 consecutive weeks, csat drops >0.12 points, rework exceeds 15% or fraud queue >2× baseline. Decision rule for scale to Epsilon: Bayesian hierarchical model pooling branch-month medians/p90 with complexity and load covariates; require posterior >80% probability of median benefit 2h with no tail harm (p90 increase <5%) and rework non-inferior, evaluated at mature months 3-4 only.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- Observed: Branch-month availability rose from 4-6% to 25-50% fast-path use over 4-5 months, while adopters’ median cycle times were flat to modestly improved late (-3.3h Alpha, -3.4h Gamma vs relevant pre, +1.9h Beta) and p90 worsened absolutely (+7-10h), whereas never-treated controls deteriorated more in median (+4.4h to +6.0h) and p90 (+14-18h) after March fraud desk and May complexity shocks, with escalation +2.5-3.1pp in adopters vs +1.8pp in controls.
- Suggestive: Within-adopter months, a 10pp higher automated_fast_path_pct is associatively linked to ~0.8-1.5h lower median (2-3%) when branch and month effects are held constant, but the association shrinks toward zero once high_complexity_pct and arrivals_per_FTE are considered and is indistinguishable from staffing/routing confounds with only 5 branches.
- Conditionally causal: If parallel trends held conditional on branch and month effects, and fraud-desk spillovers, staffing changes, and May complexity affected adopters and controls equally, then making automation available mitigated about 3-6h of median deterioration relative to counterfactual (0-12% relative benefit) with no clear absolute p90 improvement; these conditions are not credibly met in the 8-month window.
- Not supported: A single generalizable statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” for Delta/Epsilon, or that it improves tail SLA, reduces rework, or raises customer satisfaction, is not supported by the aggregated, confounded, small-N data and would ignore treatment-version variability, shared-queue interference, and survey nonresponse.
