## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts relied on
- GPIP-4 is board-approved, globally scoped, and effective throughout July 2026.
- SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled Aachen local procedure, effective throughout July 2026.
- ECD-2 was effective only from 2026-07-14 09:00 CEST to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST and only for “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.”
- The CFO email is an ordinary email, not a controlled policy document.
- The ERP outage and document-system delays are evidential facts, not normative exceptions.
- No Chief Risk Officer (CRO) written exception under GPIP-4 A7 was issued or later ratified.

### Interpretive assumptions, separated from facts
These are assumptions used to make the assessment operable, not established facts:

1. **Commitment point**: `Commit_to_Supplier` is treated as the moment the company becomes commercially bound or the supplier acknowledges commitment. Where ERP timestamps conflict with contemporaneous supplier recordings, acknowledgments, signed PDFs, or access logs, the contemporaneous evidence is preferred.
2. **Incident Command authorization as business approval**: When ECD-2 joint Incident Command authorization is used as the operative approval mechanism, it is treated as a “final business approval” for GPIP-4 A1 segregation-of-duties analysis.
3. **Role evidence**: Incident Command membership alone is not assumed to make a person an independent budget owner or Finance Controller. Where role identity matters and is not stated, the case is treated as having missing evidence.
4. **Unapproved versus new supplier**: “Unapproved supplier” is treated as a local approved-register issue under SOP B3. For GPIP-4 A3, a supplier is treated as “new” only when the case says so or when no existing registered status is evidenced; however, purchases above EUR 100,000 trigger Compliance_Clearance regardless.
5. **B2 timing**: SOP B2 requires Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve but does not expressly say “before commitment.” For purchases below the GPIP-4 A2 threshold, I do not automatically treat B2 as a pre-commitment condition unless the local emergency exception clearly requires it. This is a contestable interpretation and is made visible in the case assessments.
6. **ECD-2 scope**: “Critical sterilization-line lots” is interpreted narrowly but not mechanically. Parts needed for the affected line within hours are presumptively covered. Backup-line or contingency items require contemporaneous justification. A three-day stock position, without more, is weak evidence of ECD-2 scope.
7. **CFO email authority**: The CFO email is treated as interpretive guidance for Finance operational controls, not as a controlled waiver of GPIP-4 integrity controls. I accept it as a temporary, rebuttable basis for substituting joint Incident Command authorization for ordinary Plant/Finance operational approvals only up to EUR 150,000, and only where Compliance and segregation-of-duties controls are not impaired. This is the most contestable interpretive choice in this assessment.
8. **Emergency necessity**: Operational urgency explains timing and evidential gaps but does not itself create an unwritten exception to controlled integrity requirements.

### Decision method
1. Identify the relevant act, usually `Commit_to_Supplier`, and apply the rules in force at that time.
2. Determine whether ECD-2 applied by time and scope.
3. Apply a dimension-specific hierarchy rather than a single total ranking:
   - integrity controls: GPIP-4 dominates;
   - local PO/register/workflow timing: ECD-2 can dominate SOP within its window and scope;
   - Finance operational approvals: GPIP-4 presumptively dominates, with a limited, uncertain accommodation for the CFO email up to EUR 150,000;
   - evidential timing: contemporaneous non-ERP evidence can rebut late ERP timestamps.
4. Classify each case under the stated interpretation, while identifying evidence or premises that would reverse the classification.
5. Distinguish:
   - substantive policy breach;
   - logging or timestamp defect;
   - late evidence of a timely act;
   - inability to prove compliance.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### P-501 — Alternative: COMPLIANT
**Premise that must change:** SOP B2 is interpreted as not requiring pre-commitment approval for local-only purchases below EUR 50,000, and SHIFT-1 had ordinary procurement authority independent of the B4 Shift Lead substitution clause.

**Why credible:** B2 does not expressly say “before commitment,” while B1 expressly uses “before” for PO. Plant and Finance approvals occurred the same day, the supplier was approved, the emergency was real, and the emergency form was timely.

**Why weak:** The fact that the Plant Manager was reachable and did not formally delegate SHIFT-1 suggests possible unauthorized emergency action. If B2 is read by control purpose as pre-commitment, or if SHIFT-1 needed delegation, the case becomes NONCOMPLIANT.

### P-502 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** ECD-2 C4’s precedence over “supplier-onboarding requirements” is interpreted to include Compliance_Clearance, or ECD-2 is treated as a valid temporary A7-style exception despite not being signed by the CRO.

**Why credible:** ECD-2 was an incident directive signed by senior operational leaders, the purchase was needed the same day for the affected line, two Incident Command authorizers acted before commitment, and the authorization was recorded within four hours.

**Why weak:** GPIP-4 A3 expressly requires Compliance_Clearance before commitment for new suppliers. GPIP-4 A4 limits emergency waiver to competitive tendering unless the CRO issues a written exception. The CFO email explicitly lacks authority over Compliance controls. This alternative is therefore legally and procedurally weaker.

### P-505 — Alternative: NONCOMPLIANT
**Premise that must change:** Either the ERP timestamp 18:06 is treated as the actual commitment time after ECD-2 expiry, or GPIP-4 A2 is held to be unaffected by the CFO email, making the post-commitment Finance_Approve insufficient.

**Why credible:** GPIP-4 is board-approved and A2 expressly requires Finance Controller approval before commitment. The CFO email is not a controlled policy and says it does not remove essential integrity controls.

**Why weak:** The supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is strong contemporaneous evidence of commitment before 18:00. The ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast. If the CFO email is accepted as a temporary Finance-control interpretation for  EUR 150k, the classification remains JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

### P-507 — Alternative: NONCOMPLIANT
**Premise that must change:** C3 is interpreted to require entry in the official ERP or controlled system within four hours, or the CFO email is held unable to substitute for GPIP-4 A2 Finance/budget approval.

**Why credible:** The ERP authorization appears at 14:30, more than four hours after commitment. There is no separate Finance event. GPIP-4 A2 is explicit for EUR 110,000.

**Why weak:** The evidence note says ERP timestamps may be misleading during outages. The signed PDF at 12:40 was created within four hours and is contemporaneous. Compliance_Clearance occurred before commitment, which preserves the key integrity control for a > EUR 100k unapproved-supplier purchase.

### P-508 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** D1 and D2 are read together to mean that during ECD-2, joint Incident Command authorization plus Legal_Clearance is sufficient for all operational approvals above EUR 150,000, and the CFO has authority to interpret Finance controls that way.

**Why credible:** The purchase was within ECD-2 scope, Compliance and Legal were obtained before commitment, two independent Incident Command members authorized, and the requester/committer was not an authorizer. It was also described as the only available pump capable of restoring the affected line.

**Why weak:** D1 expressly limits sufficiency to EUR 150,000. D2 only says Legal clearance is also required; it does not clearly replace GPIP-4 A2’s independent budget owner and Finance Controller pre-commitment approval. At EUR 155,000, the burden of proving an exception should be higher, not lower.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

### Replacement emergency-procurement exception clause

**Emergency Procurement Exception Framework (EPEF)**

1. **Authority and hierarchy**
   - GPIP-4 integrity controls remain in force during emergencies.
   - Only a written, scoped, time-limited exception signed by the Chief Risk Officer may waive GPIP-4 segregation of duties, Compliance_Clearance, or pre-commitment Finance/budget controls.
   - An incident directive issued by the COO and Incident Commander may override only local operational workflow controls, including PO-before-commitment, approved-supplier-register use, competitive tendering, and local Plant/Finance approval timing.
   - The CFO may issue a controlled, published Finance-control protocol for emergency Finance approvals. Ordinary email is not sufficient to waive board-policy controls.

2. **Scope of emergency purchases**
   - “Critical emergency lot” means an item or service required within 24 hours to prevent safety harm, regulatory breach, or production stoppage directly related to the incident.
   - Backup-line or contingency purchases are covered only if a contemporaneous risk assessment shows failure or need within 24 hours, or if no reasonable alternative exists.
   - Incident Command labels are evidence but not conclusive without documented rationale.

3. **Thresholds and approvals**
   - All emergency purchases require two distinct Incident Command authorizers before commitment.
   - The requester or committer may not be an Incident Command authorizer or final business approver.
   - For purchases < EUR 50k: joint Incident Command authorization may substitute for local Plant/Finance operational approval.
   - For purchases  EUR 50k: Finance Controller or a named pre-delegated Finance deputy must approve before commitment, unless a CRO exception is issued.
   - For purchases > EUR 100k or from new suppliers: Compliance_Clearance is required before commitment and cannot be waived by incident directive or CFO email.
   - For purchases > EUR 150k: Legal_Clearance and Finance Controller approval are required before commitment, unless a CRO exception is issued.

4. **Retrospective records and evidence**
   - Verbal emergency authorization is allowed only if recorded in a durable medium within four hours.
   - If ERP or document systems are unavailable, a signed PDF, telephone recording, supplier acknowledgment, or access log created within the deadline is sufficient, provided it is entered into the official system within 24 hours of recovery.
   - Late ERP entries must be flagged as recovery entries and cannot override stronger contemporaneous evidence.
   - Post-hoc approval may mitigate consequences but does not cure a missing pre-commitment control unless the CRO expressly ratifies the exception.

5. **Expiry and anti-normalization safeguard**
   - Every emergency directive automatically expires at a stated date and time.
   - Extensions must be written, signed by the COO and CRO or CRO delegate, and published to affected functions.
   - Emergency procurement authority may not be used for routine maintenance, non-incident facilities work, or purchases unrelated to the incident scope.
   - If emergency spend exceeds a predefined percentage of site monthly spend, or if an extension exceeds 48 hours, the CRO must conduct a mandatory review and may suspend emergency powers.
