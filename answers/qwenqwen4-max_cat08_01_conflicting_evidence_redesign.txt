# 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware lanes with preventive guards and a pre-dispatch fit gate

## Redesigned flow
Structured intake captures segment, value band, defect-type code, model/serial and evidence artifacts, then a rewritten (segment-aware) rules engine assigns one of four lanes:

- **Lane G — Premium low-risk accessory, clear defect:** auto screen  auto approval  **automated part-fit/compatibility match**  dispatch (uses the R2 exemption; no evidence gate, no human gate)  close. Design target: p90 comfortably inside the 24h credit cliff.
- **Lane S — Standard low-risk, clear defect:** auto screen  evidence-complete flag from intake capture  auto approval  automated fit match; **human inspector gate only on mismatch/ambiguity**  dispatch  close inside 72h with proactive status pushes.
- **Lane H — high-value, both segments:** **`Fast approval` is disabled for this band.** Flow becomes auto screen  **technical decision by a repair technician** (repair vs configured replacement, using a published part-price catalog)  evidence completeness enforced  **mandatory fit gate on idle inspectors**  dispatch or repair  quality check  close. Fraud/risk review is invoked only on risk signals, in a reserved batch slot.
- **Lane U — unclear defect / missing evidence:** one consolidated, defect-specific evidence request with a named case owner and an assisted channel; no auto approval.

Two **hard guards in the workflow engine**, not in a report: dispatch blocked without an evidence-complete token unless Premium low-risk; close/denial of a reopened case blocked without a Manual eligibility review token (R3), executed asynchronously so the escalation board keeps decision authority without becoming the compliance step.

## Targets
The 35% high-value band (R1/R2 deviation surface, €410 error cost), the 43% low-risk band (protect its speed), and all 510 reopen events.

## Mechanism
It removes the violation *surface* rather than auditing it: R1 constrains Fast approval, so eliminating Fast approval for high-value satisfies R1 without buying 1,750 reviewer touches — the scarce resource is deliberately bypassed by changing **decision rights**, moving the eligibility question into a technical decision. Error detection migrates from a €410, 96h-mean post-dispatch complaint loop to a pre-dispatch compatibility check, mostly automated, with human effort absorbed by underused inspectors. Low precision is attacked at source by making the case system a state machine instead of a permissive workflow.

## Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved
High-value **mean** cycle time rises versus today's fast path (it should still land below the 96–120h reviewed median); the 31% pre-rework cost advantage is given up on purpose; the gate adds **6–18h** wherever automated matching fails. It does **not** fix evidence-loop abandonment beyond consolidation, nor the Monday fraud peak, nor the 30h quote-price wait. It requires explicit compliance sign-off that "no Fast approval for high-value" meets policy *intent* (an auditor could read it as rule avoidance), and the normative net must be extended to log the evidence flag and fit gate or measured fitness will fall for purely instrumental reasons.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first intake, single-request discipline, asynchronous risk and price transparency

## Redesigned flow
Routing logic is left largely intact; the **information flow is re-sequenced**. Intake becomes defect-type-specific evidence acquisition: a required-artifact checklist per defect family, captured at first contact through a **dual channel** — the evidence portal for digitally capable customers, and agent-completed capture (phone walkthrough, retail/partner drop-off) for the rest, selected by a digital-literacy proxy, not by customer choice alone. Evidence completeness is resolved **before** eligibility assessment, so `Request missing evidence` becomes an exception path with a **one-shot consolidated request**, a named owner and a 48h nudge, instead of a 26%-repeat loop. `Fraud/risk review` becomes **batched and asynchronous** with pre-computed risk signals and sampling for low-risk, freeing the 2 specialists for Monday/campaign peaks. A **published part-price catalog plus standing repair authority below a value threshold** removes the price-confirmation wait inside `Repair quote`, and quotes are issued with an all-in price and a pre-authorised repair-vs-replace recommendation. Premium gets a 24h clock with proactive updates; Standard gets visibility against its 72h tolerance.

## Targets
The **22% unclear-defect segment** (140h median, highest abandonment), the 1,430 evidence-request cases (26% repeats, 320 abandonments), the 570 repair quotes (80 complaints, 70 abandonments), and the fraud Monday bottleneck.

## Mechanism
It deletes loop **iterations** instead of accelerating them. Each avoided repeat request removes roughly a `Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence` (14h mean) + `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` (34h mean) cycle — on the order of ~48h of mean loop time, though edge means are not additive to case medians. It also cuts the 46% of reviewer load that is loop-generated, which is the cheapest possible capacity release, and it converts quote-stage abandonment by removing price surprise rather than by chasing customers faster.

## Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved
Work is shifted **to the customer and to intake agents**: intake handling time and front-line cost rise, and the portal's **30–45% repeat reduction is a vendor range, not a measurement**, with an explicit abandonment hazard for low-digital-literacy customers. Abandonment must never be booked as a saving — it is suppressed demand and consumer-rights exposure. B does **nothing** about wrong-part selection or high-value R1/R2 exposure, so 18–22% high-value complaint rates would persist; sampling-based risk review needs compliance agreement; and evidence-first must not be applied to Premium low-risk accessories, where it would destroy the exemption's whole purpose.

# 4. Redesign C: Decoupled restoration — advance exchange, detective controls, delegated authority

## Redesigned flow
Customer-visible restoration is **decoupled from internal decisioning and made reversible**. At first contact, remote diagnostics/telemetry triage; if a hardware fault is likely on a high-value device, ship a **certified refurbished advance exchange with a return label immediately**. In parallel, and joined before close: evidence completeness, risk review, and a technical decision on the **returned** unit (repair / refurbish / scrap), with `Quality check` output feeding the refurb pool. A **48h outbound verification** (call or telemetry confirmation of correct model/part) replaces waiting for the customer to complain. Control philosophy shifts from universal preventive gates to **detective controls**: statistical sampling audit of auto-approved/dispatched cases by inspectors, with automatic lane suspension when a sample fails. The escalation board is replaced by a **delegated authority matrix** (senior reviewer decides within limits; the 45-min board handles only novel cases), and reopened cases still acquire a Manual eligibility review token before close.

## Targets
Premium high-value first (14%, 22% fast-path complaints, 96h reviewed path), then Standard high-value; the 120 escalation-routed reopens; the reactive 96h complaint lag.

## Mechanism
Three distinct levers, none of them a gate: **parallelism** (compliance work no longer sits on the customer's critical path), **inventory buffering + reversibility** (a wrong or fraudulent dispatch is recoverable, so the cost of being early is bounded), and **detective instead of preventive assurance** (sampling cost scales with risk, not volume). Proactive verification converts an uncontrolled 96h-mean/180h-p90 tail into a scheduled 48h check, and delegated authority removes the hard 45-min/day ceiling that no routing redesign can fix.

## Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved
Working capital and reverse-logistics cost for the refurb pool; fraud leakage on unreturned units; weaker per-case assurance than a gate. Critically, **R1/R2 as written probably forbid dispatch before review/evidence for high-value** — C is only legal for that band after a formal policy amendment agreed with compliance/audit; if refused, C collapses to low-risk and Premium accessories. It also demands trace-level join semantics the current log cannot demonstrate (27.5% of cases never reach END), so instrumentation is a precondition, not a nice-to-have. Pool sizing, refurb yield and return-compliance rates are entirely unmeasured in the prompt.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
1. *Gate inflation:* automated matching covers less than hoped, so most high-value dispatches need the human gate at the upper **18h** end; two inspectors saturate and the gate becomes the new queue, pushing Premium high-value past expectations while low-risk waits behind it.
2. *Lane leakage / rule over-correction:* the segment-aware rules add more than the stated **15–35%** manual inflow, or defect-type coding at intake is wrong, so high-value cases leak into Lane S/G and reproduce exactly today's R1/R2 deviations — now with a false compliance signal from the guards.
3. *Policy rejection:* compliance rules that disabling Fast approval for high-value is intent-compliant in form but not substance, forcing 1,750 cases into review/fraud and breaking the 82% cliff.
- **Bottleneck migration:** reviewers  **quality inspectors and repair technicians** (technical decision + fit gate), with the price catalog needed to keep technicians from stalling.

**Redesign B**
1. *Abandonment inversion:* the portal reduces repeats but pushes low-digital-literacy customers out; evidence-stage abandonment rises above today's 22.4% of entrants, and cycle-time metrics "improve" because hard cases disappear from the log.
2. *Front-load without payoff:* defect families are heterogeneous, so intake checklists cannot be specified crisply; a second request is still needed, intake cost is paid twice, and the 34h handback returns.
3. *Async risk becomes late risk:* batched fraud review clears cases after dispatch, converting a preventive control into an unrecoverable loss; sampling is rejected by compliance.
- **Bottleneck migration:** reviewers/fraud  **intake agents and the assisted channel**, plus the **part-price catalog governance** (who maintains prices) as the new single point of delay.

**Redesign C**
1. *Pool starvation / wrong-unit spiral:* refurb inventory is thin or yields are poor, advance exchange waits on stock, and the promised decoupling silently reverts to the old sequential path — with extra reverse-logistics cost.
2. *Leakage and audit blindness:* unreturned units, fraud on advance exchange, or a sampling plan too small to detect a wrong-part regression; the 48h verification reaches customers who already complained, so the "proactive" control is decorative.
3. *Join failure:* close fires before parallel tokens complete — reproducing the observed "complaint after close" deviation in a new architecture, and R3 remains unprovable.
- **Bottleneck migration:** eligibility/fraud/board  **reverse logistics, refurb refurbishment and Quality check**, which becomes the pool's throughput limiter; inspectors shift from idle to structurally scarce.
