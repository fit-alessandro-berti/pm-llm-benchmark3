## 1. Decision rule

To transform raw observational breach predictions into an operationally robust, capacity-aware early-warning policy, the system evaluates cases through a five-stage decision filter:

```
[Incoming Case Alert] 
       │
       ▼
1. Stage Eligibility Gate ──(Stage ≥ Diagnosis Complete)──► [Reject Swarm / Standard Resolution]
       │ (Eligible: Triage or Diagnosis Started)
       ▼
2. Calibration & Data Sanity Adjustment ──(Imputed/Corrupt Telemetry)──► [Hold for Fast Verification]
       │ (Calibrated Probability & Uncertainty Bounds)
       ▼
3. Alert Persistence & Evidence Gate ──(Repeat Alert & ΔEvent = ∅)──► [Suppress / Maintain Cool-Down]
       │ (First Alert OR Valid Material Evidence ΔEvent)
       ▼
4. Temporal Urgency Window ──(t_SLA > 6.0h & Triage)──► [Monitor / Queue Progression]
       │ (Urgent Window: t_SLA ≤ 4.0h or Diagnosis Started)
       ▼
5. Capacity-Constrained Net Benefit Ranking ──► [Allocate Top Slots + Dynamic Reserve]
```

### 1.1. Stage Eligibility Gate
* **Hard Rule:** A case is eligible for a 45-minute *Senior Diagnostic Swarm* if and only if its current stage is strictly prior to `Diagnosis Complete` (`Current Stage ∈ {Triage, Diagnosis Started}`).
* **Mechanism:** Once `Diagnosis Complete` is reached, root-cause identification is finished; diagnostic swarms have negligible causal impact ($\Delta p \in [0.00, 0.03]$). Cases at or beyond `Diagnosis Complete` are immediately routed to standard resolution workflows.

### 1.2. Calibration and Robustification Adjustments
Observational model outputs ($p_{\text{now}}$) are transformed into calibrated operational risk intervals ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = [\hat{p}_{\text{low}}, \hat{p}_{\text{high}}]$) by segment:
* **Strategic Enterprise:** Apply downward correction for recent workflow overprediction: $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = p_{\text{now}} - \delta_{\text{SE}}$, with $\delta_{\text{SE}} \in [0.05, 0.12]$ (point adjustment: $-0.085$).
* **SME:** Apply upward correction for systematic underprediction: $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = p_{\text{now}} + \delta_{\text{SME}}$, with $\delta_{\text{SME}} \in [0.04, 0.09]$ (point adjustment: $+0.065$).
* **Public Sector:** Maintain baseline calibration ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \approx p_{\text{now}}$) with standard sampling error intervals ($\pm 0.04$).
* **Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS):** Apply robust epistemic bounds $[\hat{p}_{\text{now}} - 0.05, \hat{p}_{\text{now}} + 0.10]$ and shrink causal effect estimates toward the conservative lower bound to hedge against small-sample estimation variance.
* **Data Quality Gate:** If an alert spike is driven by feature failure or default imputation (e.g., missing live queue features), the alert is suspended for a mandatory 10-minute automated telemetry check before committing specialist capacity.

### 1.3. Alert Persistence and Minimum Evidence Change Rule
* **Suppression of Static Alerts:** If a case generates a repeat alert solely due to the 30-minute periodic model refresh without an intervening process event ($\Delta \mathcal{E} = \emptyset$), the alert is automatically suppressed and logged as static background risk.
* **Material Evidence Requirement ($\Delta \mathcal{E} \neq \emptyset$):** Re-entry into active intervention candidacy after a previous alert requires a certified state transition:
  1. *Artifact Injection:* Customer uploads new logs, error traces, or diagnostic dumps.
  2. *Workflow Milestone:* Case transitions from `Triage` to `Diagnosis Started`.
  3. *Telemetry Shock:* Verified state change causing $|\Delta p_{\text{cal}}| \ge +0.15$ with intact data pipelines.

### 1.4. Temporal Urgency Filtering
* **Runway Rule:** Early-stage cases (`Triage`) with long time to SLA ($t_{\text{SLA}} > 6.0\text{ h}$) are placed in `Wait / Monitor`. Swarming early in triage when standard diagnostic queues have not yet engaged introduces unnecessary false-alarm disruption and occupies capacity needed for immediate bottlenecks.
* **Critical Intervention Window:** Highest priority is given to cases with $t_{\text{SLA}} \le 4.0\text{ h}$ in `Diagnosis Started` or late `Triage`, where failing to accelerate diagnosis now creates an irreversible deadline breach.

### 1.5. Decision-Theoretic Prioritization Metric
Eligible candidates are ranked by Net Expected Value of Intervention per Slot ($\text{NEV}_i$):

$$\text{NEV}_i = \mathbb{E}[\Delta p_{\text{swarm}, i}] \cdot V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - (1 - \hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i}) \cdot C_{\text{fa}, i} - \text{SpilloverHarm}(\text{Queue})$$

Where:
* $\mathbb{E}[\Delta p_{\text{swarm}, i}]$ is the case-specific expected causal risk reduction (not observational risk).
* $V_i$ is the contractual value of avoiding a breach.
* $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$ (labor and coordination cost).
* $C_{\text{fa}, i}$ is the case-specific customer disruption burden, incurred if the case would have succeeded naturally without intervention (probability $1 - \hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i}$).
* $\text{SpilloverHarm}(\text{Queue}) = \sum_{j \in \text{Untreated Queue}} \Delta p_{\text{spillover}, j} \cdot \bar{V}_j \approx 6 \times \Delta p_{\text{spillover}} \times \bar{V}_{\text{queue}}$, accounting for the negative externality imposed on 6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases by interrupting senior specialists (8–20 aggregate minutes of delay per slot).
* **Capacity Constraint:** At most 4 slots available. Exactly 1 slot is dynamically reserved for unlisted high-severity regulatory arrivals before 15:00, leaving 3 slots for immediate allocation at 12:00.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Early-warning systems in continuous-time business processes are vulnerable to "alert fatigue" and repetitive triggering from static state features. The following temporal policy governs lifecycle progression:

```
[Incoming Prediction at 30-min Refresh]
                   │
                   ▼
       Is Case currently in Cool-Down?
         ├── YES ──► Did Material Evidence (ΔE) occur?
         │             ├── NO  ──► [SUPPRESS ALERT] (e.g., I-05)
         │             └── YES ──► [BREAK COOL-DOWN & RE-ENTER] (e.g., I-07)
         └── NO  ──► Is it a First Alert?
                       ├── YES ──► [EVALUATE CANDIDACY] (e.g., I-01, I-02, I-09)
                       └── NO  ──► Apply Evidence & Urgency Rules
```

### 4.1. Cool-Down, Suppression, and Re-Entry Logic
* **Mandatory Cool-Down Window:** When an alert is evaluated and not swarmed (or swarmed once), it enters a 120-minute cool-down window. During this window, periodic score refreshes (which run every 30 minutes) cannot re-alert unless a **Material Evidence Change ($\Delta \mathcal{E}$)** is detected.
* **Suppression Logic:** Repeat alerts with $\Delta \mathcal{E} = \emptyset$ are suppressed from specialist queues. They are logged as background telemetry to prevent specialist interruption.
* **Re-Entry & Escalation Logic:** Re-entry into active intervention candidacy requires at least one of:
  1. *New Artifact:* Upload of customer diagnostic logs, crash reports, or environment traces.
  2. *Stage Progression:* Transition from `Triage` to `Diagnosis Started`.
  3. *Critical Escalation:* Breach of an intermediate process milestone with $t_{\text{SLA}} \le 3.0\text{ h}$.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Deploying early-warning intervention policies creates substantial risks of disparate impact, algorithmic bias, and closed-loop data contamination. The following safeguards are established:

### 5.1. Value-Based Starvation of Lower-Tier Clients
* **Risk:** Prioritizing solely by $\mathbb{E}[\Delta p] \times V$ would cause high-value Strategic Enterprise cases (€60k–€100k, e.g., I-04, I-08) to capture all capacity, even when their breach risk is low, runway is long, or causal benefit is marginal. SME clients (€5k–€11k) with massive treatment efficacy ($\Delta p = 0.25–0.40$) and imminent deadlines would be starved.
* **Concrete Safeguards:**
  1. *Lexicographic Efficiency Hurdles:* Enforce a minimum causal efficacy hurdle ($\Delta p \ge 0.15$) and an urgency constraint ($t_{\text{SLA}} \le 4.0\text{ h}$) before value ranking is computed.
  2. *Effective Value Saturation:* Cap the contractual value used in optimization at $V_{\text{cap}} = €30,000$ ($\tilde{V}_i = \min(V_i, €30,000)$). This prevents enterprise cases from outbidding urgent SME cases on sheer balance-sheet weight while preserving strong commercial incentives.
  3. *Segment Capacity Floors:* Guarantee a minimum allocation floor of 25% of weekly swarm slots to the SME/Public Sector segments.

### 5.2. Epistemic Uncertainty in Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS)
* **Risk:** Because validation data for NNLS is sparse, confidence intervals around predicted risk and intervention efficacy are wide. Algorithmic uncertainty aversion might systematically withhold swarms from non-native language cases, despite communication hurdles making senior diagnostic swarming uniquely beneficial.
* **Concrete Safeguards:**
  1. *Optimism Under Uncertainty (UCB) Allocation:* Score sparse segments using upper-confidence-bound heuristics for treatment efficacy rather than conservative shrinkage, actively testing candidate cases.
  2. *Dedicated Multilingual Swarm Protocols:* Pre-assign diagnostic specialists with language-assistive tooling or regional specialists to NNLS swarms, directly attacking the root cause of language-induced diagnostic delays.

### 5.3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
* **Risk:** The raw model underpredicts SME breach risk by 0.04–0.09 and overpredicts Strategic Enterprise risk by 0.05–0.12 (following a workflow redesign). Uncorrected, the policy over-allocates to enterprise cases (incurring severe false-alarm disruption, such as €5,000 on I-08) and neglects genuinely failing SME cases.
* **Concrete Safeguards:**
  1. *Dynamic Isotonic Recalibration:* Apply running segment-level post-hoc calibration offsets updated weekly.
  2. *Segment-Specific False-Alarm Weighting:* Explicitly penalize interventions by $(1 - \hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i}) \cdot C_{\text{fa}, i}$, which naturally penalizes overpredicted enterprise cases with large disruption costs.

### 5.4. Feedback Loops and Training Label Contamination (Confounding)
* **Risk (Survivor Bias):** When high-risk cases are successfully swarmed, they do not breach ($Y=0$). Future supervised models trained on raw operational logs will observe high-risk features associated with successful outcomes, mistakenly learning that these critical states are benign and lowering future risk scores (treatment-induced label corruption).
* **Concrete Safeguards:**
  1. *Explicit Treatment Logging & Causal Counterfactual Modeling:* Log an immutable boolean flag $\text{SwarmAdministered} \in \{0, 1\}$ in the process event log. Retrain future predictive models using causal inference frameworks (Inverse Probability Weighting / Double Machine Learning) estimating potential outcomes $Y(0)$ and $Y(1)$ rather than naive supervised learning.
  2. *Epsilon-Exploration Holdout:* Reserve a tiny, randomized holdout fraction (3–5% of non-critical borderline alerts) as an untreated control group to continuously benchmark the un-swarmed baseline hazard rate.

### 5.5. Asymmetric Queue Spillover Externalities
* **Risk:** Senior specialists interrupted for swarms abandon the general diagnosis queue, adding 8–20 minutes of aggregate delay per slot. Untreated cases (frequently lower-priority SME cases) are pushed into SLA breaches, meaning one high-value rescue causes multiple unobserved collateral failures.
* **Concrete Safeguards:**
  1. *Dedicated Swarm Rotation Model:* Decouple swarms from the live diagnosis queue by establishing dedicated on-call senior swarm shifts (specialists on pure swarm/escalation duty) rather than interrupting specialists working active queue tickets.
  2. *Congestion-Dependent Throttling:* If diagnosis queue wait times exceed 45 minutes, automatically suspend non-critical swarms until queue health is restored.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

To ensure sustained decision-theoretic coherence and operational stability, the early-warning policy is governed by a multi-tier monitoring and recalibration framework:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        MONITORING CADENCE                              │
├───────────────────┬─────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ Real-Time / Daily │ Weekly              │ Monthly / Quarterly          │
│ • Queue Congestion│ • Segment ECE & Brier│ • Causal Efficacy (DML/RDD) │
│ • Swarm Durations │ • Prediction Drift  │ • Disparate Impact Audits    │
│ • Telemetry Health│ • Spillover Delays  │ • Capacity & Policy Triggers │
└───────────────────┴─────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6.1. Multi-Dimensional Monitoring Telemetry
1. **Operational Metrics (Continuous Real-Time):**
   * *Swarm Execution Duration:* Monitored against the 45-minute SLA (alert threshold: $> 50\text{ min}$).
   * *Queue Congestion Tracking:* Average wait time and backlog size in `Diagnosis Started`.
   * *Data Quality / Imputation Watchdog:* Automated circuit-breaker alerting when missing feature rates exceed 1% (preventing phantom alert spikes as seen in I-06).
2. **Predictive & Calibration Metrics (Weekly):**
   * *Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier Score:* Computed per segment.
   * *Prediction Drift:* Tracking divergence between $p_{\text{prev}}$ and $p_{\text{now}}$ across process stages.
3. **Causal-Effect Metrics (Bi-Weekly / Monthly):**
   * *Observed vs. Counterfactual Breach Rates:* Estimated causal risk reduction $\hat{\Delta p}$ achieved by swarms using Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) on slot cutoff thresholds.
   * *Observational-to-Causal Ratio:* Tracking whether real-world risk reduction aligns with expected case bounds ($0.08–0.35$).
4. **Capacity & Spillover Metrics (Daily):**
   * *Empirical Spillover Delay:* Measuring actual queue wait time increases for non-swarmed cases during and immediately following swarm events.
   * *Untreated Cohort Breach Rate:* Monitoring SLA performance of cases sharing the queue with active swarms.
5. **Fairness & Parity Audits (Monthly):**
   * *Disparate Allocation Ratio:* Ratio of swarm allocation rate to SLA breach hazard across segments.
   * *False-Alarm Disruption Index:* Cumulative false-alarm burden imposed per client segment.

### 6.2. Causal Effect Estimation and Policy-Induced Drift Detection
* **Quasi-Experimental Evaluation (RDD & Instrumental Variables):** On days with constrained capacity (e.g., 4 slots for 8 candidates), evaluate treatment effects by comparing borderline cases that narrowly received a slot versus those that narrowly missed it due to queue arrival timing.
* **Detection of Policy-Induced Drift:** Track the covariate distribution $P(X \mid \text{Stage})$ and base breach rates $P(Y=1 \mid X, W=0)$. If standard feature weights decay because swarmed cases consistently succeed, trigger an immediate retraining run using counterfactual IPW weights.

### 6.3. Policy Recalibration and Threshold Governance Triggers
The allocation rule and decision parameters are dynamically retuned upon hitting pre-defined operational triggers:

| Trigger Condition | Diagnostic Root Cause | Automated Governance Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Segment ECE $> 0.08$** for 2 consecutive weeks | Workflow redesign or client mix shift | Automatically update empirical segment calibration offset ($\delta_{\text{segment}}$); schedule model retraining. |
| **Untreated Queue Delay $> 25\text{ min}$ per slot** | Severe queue congestion / understaffing | Increase minimum net-EV threshold ($\text{NEV}_{\text{threshold}}$) by 50% for Triage cases; restrict swarms to `Diagnosis Started`. |
| **Telemetry Imputation Rate $> 5\%$** | Pipeline / feature service failure | Revert affected segment to rule-based fallback (stage duration + static SLA milestones) until data integrity is restored. |
| **Reserved Regulatory Slot Unused $> 85\%$ over 30 days** | Over-conservative reservation policy | Transition regulatory reservation from static hold to dynamic Bayesian arrival probability threshold ($P(\text{Arrival} \le 3\text{h}) > 0.40$). |
| **Disparate Impact Ratio $< 0.70$ on SME/NNLS** | Commercial value metric bias | Lower the value cap ($V_{\text{cap}}$) from €30,000 to €20,000 to rebalance capacity toward operational rescue potential. |
