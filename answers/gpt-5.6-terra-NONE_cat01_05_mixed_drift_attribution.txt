## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-to-mapped fitness gap is strong evidence of a semantic and technical logging break. Raw fitness falls from approximately 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March, whereas mapped fitness remains near 0.90. The label substitution from `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen`, plus duplicated `Open Case` events, therefore explains most of the apparent conformance collapse against the old normative model. It does not prove that the business process was unchanged: mapping can make two labels equivalent by analyst choice even if the new screen changed decision rules, timing, user behavior, or eligibility outcomes.

Duplicated technical events primarily distort sequence-based artifacts: DFGs, variants, alignments, event counts, and raw conformance. They can create spurious starts, paths, or deviations. Missing resource identifiers affect organizational mining more directly: workload allocation, handover networks, team performance comparisons, and resource-conditioned queue analyses may become biased, especially because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and is not plausibly random.

Post-May deterioration cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. The normalized DFG shows substantive increases in `Assess -> Request Info` and `Request Info -> CollectDocs` from roughly 12/11 per 100 cases in January to 24/23 in May and 29/28 in July. These are business-loop relations after the supplied migration normalization. In parallel, supplier mix changes sharply, cycle times worsen, and the p90 rises far more than median service time at Assess. Those patterns are consistent with document incompleteness, repeated work, and queue accumulation. Logging quality remains a confounder, but it is insufficient to explain the coherent rework, supplier, and delay pattern.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

Use a transparent normalized L1 distance over the seven supplied relation rates:

\[
D(m,b)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e \in E}\left|r_{m,e}-r_{b,e}\right|/100
\]

where \(r_{m,e}\) is occurrences per 100 cases for edge \(e\), \(b\) is January, and the factor one-half keeps the scale interpretable as a distribution-like difference, although these relation rates are not a true probability distribution because multiple edges can occur in one case.

| month | normalized DFG distance from Jan | dominant changes |
|---|---:|---|
| Mar | 0.025 | Minor normalized change: Senior Assessment +1, decision relation 1 |
| May | 0.105 | Rework edges roughly double; CollectDocsAssess and decision relation decline |
| Jul | 0.180 | Rework peaks; downstream completion/decision path declines; Senior Assessment rises |
| Sep | 0.090 | Partial rework recovery and partial restoration of decision relation |
| Oct | 0.185 | Rework remains elevated; Senior Assessment rises sharply to 22 per 100 |
| Dec | 0.095 | Rework and Senior Assessment remain above baseline but improve from peaks |

This is a fragment distance, not whole-process drift. Omitted edges, repeated loops, end states, and event durations are not represented. It is therefore suitable for screening and temporal comparison, not for a precise estimate of total behavioral divergence.

For the July-August increase of about 13–14 median hours over baseline, queueing is plausibly the largest component, followed by rework/control-flow, with case mix and service-time changes smaller. For October-November, seasonal complexity and volume become more important, but queueing likely remains substantial.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level 0: all eligible cases | Month, case identifier | Stable enterprise-level sentinel and volume denominator | All cases; report counts and coverage | Overall trend, seasonality, raw versus mapped quality | Can conceal supplier, complexity, and priority heterogeneity |
| Level 1: core business strata | Supplier group, complexity band, renewal/non-renewal, month | Separate composition shifts from within-stratum process change | At least 100 completed cases per month/stratum; otherwise pool adjacent months | Supplier and complexity-adjusted DFG, rework, cycle comparisons | Conditioning on complexity can be biased if complexity is revised downstream |
| Level 2: operational strata | Priority class, team/queue, supplier group, complexity band | Test priority redistribution and local capacity effects | At least 50 cases per cell per reporting period; otherwise quarterly rolling window | Waiting-time, WIP, queue and team routing analysis | Priority/team assignment may be endogenous to expected difficulty |
| Level 3: data-quality strata | Migrated/non-migrated activities, complete/missing resource, source system/version | Identify semantic/provenance drift and assess selection effects | At least 200 events per activity-source cell | Schema completeness, timestamp/order, resource capture comparisons | Complete-case filtering can induce strong selection bias |
| Level 4: exception cohorts | Rework variants, senior-assessment cases, long-tail cases | Investigate drivers of costly deviations | At least 30 cases for descriptive review; no automated causal claims below 100 | Root-cause review, trace inspection, alignment diagnostics | Selecting on rework or long duration can condition on outcomes caused by exposure |
| Level 5: matched analytic cohorts | Predefined supplier, region, complexity, priority, calendar cohort | Attribution-oriented comparisons | Balance diagnostics required; standardized mean differences below 0.1 where feasible | Difference-in-differences and adjusted temporal comparisons | Unobserved confounding remains; matching may discard important cases |

The primary monitoring population should be all completed eligible cases, reported monthly and in rolling 4-week windows, with mandatory stratification by supplier group, complexity band, and renewal status. This preserves a stable denominator while preventing the enterprise aggregate from being interpreted as a behavioral change when it is actually composition drift. Priority and team should be diagnostic strata rather than the only headline population because their assignment may be endogenous.

Apply false-discovery-rate control or alert budgets across sibling subgroup tests, and require persistence across two reporting windows for non-critical operational alerts.

# Part B — Reusable event-level PM4Py functions for future logs
# ============================================================
# Expected input columns:
#   case_id, activity, timestamp
# Optional:
#   resource, supplier, complexity, priority, source_system, event_id
#
# PM4Py API availability can vary by installed version. The core calls below
# follow the current high-level PM4Py style; keep package version pinned and
# test against the local PM4Py manual/environment.

import copy
import pm4py

def prepare_event_log(
    df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity="activity",
    timestamp="timestamp",
    resource="resource",
):
    """
    Preserve source columns, parse timestamps, and format an event dataframe.
    PM4Py formatting returns a dataframe suitable for discovery functions.
    """
    work = df.copy(deep=True)
    work[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    if work[timestamp].isna().any():
        raise ValueError("Unparseable timestamps found.")

    required = [case_id, activity, timestamp]
    missing = [c for c in required if c not in work.columns]
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {missing}")

    kwargs = {
        "case_id": case_id,
        "activity_key": activity,
        "timestamp_key": timestamp,
    }
    if resource in work.columns:
        kwargs["resource_key"] = resource

    return pm4py.format_dataframe(work, **kwargs)


def slice_time_window(formatted_df, start, end, timestamp_key="timestamp"):
    """
    Slice an already formatted dataframe. Boundary semantics may differ by
    PM4Py version; record the installed version and test inclusivity.
    """
    start = pd.Timestamp(start)
    end = pd.Timestamp(end)
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df,
        start_timestamp=start,
        end_timestamp=end,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )


def normalize_activity_labels(
    raw_df,
    activity="activity",
    label_mapping=None,
    duplicate_event_rule=None,
):
    """
    Return a normalized copy without overwriting raw evidence.
    duplicate_event_rule should be a callable receiving a dataframe and
    returning a filtered dataframe. It must be versioned and audited.
    """
    normalized = raw_df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized["activity_raw"] = normalized[activity]

    label_mapping = label_mapping or {
        "Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"
    }
    normalized[activity] = normalized[activity].replace(label_mapping)

    if duplicate_event_rule is not None:
        normalized = duplicate_event_rule(normalized.copy(deep=True))

    return normalized


def monthly_dfgs(
    formatted_df,
    timestamp="timestamp",
    start=None,
    end=None,
    freq="MS",
):
    """
    Compute a DFG per calendar month. Returns a dictionary keyed by Period.
    """
    data = formatted_df.copy()
    data[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(data[timestamp], utc=True)

    if start is not None and end is not None:
        data = slice_time_window(data, start, end, timestamp_key=timestamp)

    out = {}
    for period, part in data.groupby(data[timestamp].dt.to_period(freq)):
        if len(part) == 0:
            continue
        out[str(period)] = pm4py.discover_dfg(part)
    return out


def monthly_variants(formatted_df, timestamp="timestamp", freq="MS"):
    """
    Compute variants per month. Variant return structures vary by PM4Py version;
    persist both PM4Py version and a serialized summary.
    """
    data = formatted_df.copy()
    data[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(data[timestamp], utc=True)
    out = {}
    for period, part in data.groupby(data[timestamp].dt.to_period(freq)):
        out[str(period)] = pm4py.get_variants(part)
    return out


def monthly_temporal_profiles(formatted_df, timestamp="timestamp", freq="MS"):
    """
    Temporal profiles characterize time between activities.
    """
    data = formatted_df.copy()
    data[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(data[timestamp], utc=True)
    out = {}
    for period, part in data.groupby(data[timestamp].dt.to_period(freq)):
        out[str(period)] = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(part)
    return out


def optional_constraint_models(formatted_df):
    """
    Optional discovery. Availability and return type depend on PM4Py version.
    Log skeleton is useful for order/co-existence constraints; DECLARE is useful
    for declarative behavioral constraints.
    """
    results = {}
    if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_log_skeleton"):
        results["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(formatted_df)
    if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare"):
        results["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(formatted_df)
    return results


def compare_raw_and_normalized(
    raw_df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity="activity",
    timestamp="timestamp",
    resource="resource",
    label_mapping=None,
    duplicate_event_rule=None,
):
    """
    Keep raw and normalized logs separate, then produce comparable discovery
    artifacts. This avoids silently replacing original evidence.
    """
    raw_copy = raw_df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_copy = normalize_activity_labels(
        raw_copy,
        activity=activity,
        label_mapping=label_mapping,
        duplicate_event_rule=duplicate_event_rule,
    )

    raw_formatted = prepare_event_log(
        raw_copy, case_id=case_id, activity=activity,
        timestamp=timestamp, resource=resource
    )
    normalized_formatted = prepare_event_log(
        normalized_copy, case_id=case_id, activity=activity,
        timestamp=timestamp, resource=resource
    )

    return {
        "raw_log": raw_formatted,
        "normalized_log": normalized_formatted,
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(raw_formatted),
        "normalized_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(normalized_formatted),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw_formatted),
        "normalized_variants": pm4py.get_variants(normalized_formatted),
        "raw_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(raw_formatted),
        "normalized_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(normalized_formatted),
        "raw_constraints": optional_constraint_models(raw_formatted),
        "normalized_constraints": optional_constraint_models(normalized_formatted),
    }


# Example duplicate rule, to be validated against technical event semantics:
def remove_exact_duplicate_open_case_events(
    df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity="activity",
    timestamp="timestamp",
):
    """
    Conservative example: remove exact duplicate Open Case rows only.
    Do not use this to remove legitimate reopenings without an event identifier
    and a documented source-system deduplication specification.
    """
    is_open = df[activity].eq("Open Case")
    duplicates = df.loc[is_open].duplicated(
        subset=[case_id, activity, timestamp],
        keep="first",
    )
    drop_index = df.loc[is_open].index[duplicates]
    return df.drop(index=drop_index).copy()

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw-fitness collapse is predominantly logging-semantic and technical drift. The timing is exact, raw fitness falls by roughly 0.38–0.42, mapped fitness remains near baseline, label cardinality increases from 9 to 11, duplicated `Open Case` events appear, and resource missingness rises sharply. Confidence is high, approximately 90–95%, that migration artifacts explain most of the raw conformance break. This conclusion does not establish that every migrated business action was behaviorally identical; it only establishes that the old raw-label model became inappropriate.

The strongest substantive behavioral conclusion is that rework increased after the Supplier Z disruption. From May, both operational rework variants and normalized rework-loop edges rise sharply, while Supplier Z representation rises from about 15–18% to more than 40%. Confidence is moderately high, around 75–85%, that incomplete bundles materially contributed. However, Supplier Z cases were already more complex and serve non-random regions, so the aggregate cannot separate supplier quality from compositional effects cleanly.

Performance deterioration is best attributed to interacting demand and capacity mechanisms. Supplier-induced rework likely increased workload beginning in May. The June assessor departures then plausibly reduced available assessment capacity, producing queue accumulation by July-August. The modest increase in recorded Assess service time, compared with much larger reconstructed waiting increases and p90 deterioration, supports queueing as the dominant explanation for late-summer delay. Confidence is about 75–85% for a material queueing/capacity mechanism, but its independent effect cannot be isolated from rework demand.

The July priority rule is plausible as a redistribution mechanism rather than a proven cause of total delay. Confidence is only moderate, about 50–65%, because aggregate data do not show waiting separately for high-priority and standard cases. October-November seasonal population drift is strongly supported by volume and complexity increases and the known renewal season. It likely amplified pre-existing backlog and capacity stress rather than replacing those explanations.

December improvement is real descriptively but weakly attributable. Overtime, the document-quality campaign, lower volume, and lower complexity all coincide. I would revise the account with event-level supplier-quality fields, case-level queue timestamps, staffing rosters, priority assignments, prior-year seasonal data, and audits confirming whether Senior Assessment was newly recorded or genuinely more frequent.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops sharply while mapped fitness remains within its historical band and new labels or duplicate technical events increase, THEN classify the first response as a schema/provenance investigation and freeze raw-model escalation, BECAUSE semantic migration can create apparent conformance failure without equivalent business-process deviation.

2. IF mapped rework-loop rates rise materially within Supplier Z cases relative to matched non-Z cases after controlling for complexity and renewal status, THEN initiate supplier document-quality remediation and payload validation, BECAUSE incomplete bundles should create repeated Request Info and Collect Documents behavior.

3. IF waiting before Assess rises while Assess active service time remains stable within team and complexity strata, THEN trigger queue-capacity and WIP review rather than assessor productivity escalation, BECAUSE the dominant mechanism is likely congestion rather than slower service execution.

4. IF high-priority or high-complexity cases show reduced waiting while standard cases show increased waiting after the priority-rule date, THEN review priority thresholds and reserve capacity for standard work, BECAUSE prioritization may be redistributing delay rather than reducing total queue pressure.

5. IF DFG or variant drift persists after supplier recovery within matched supplier-complexity cohorts, THEN investigate process adaptation, policy change, or unresolved backlog behavior, BECAUSE persistent within-cohort changes cannot be explained solely by a temporary supplier mix shift.

6. IF resource missingness changes differentially by activity, team, or source system, THEN suspend complete-case organizational conclusions and run missingness sensitivity analysis, BECAUSE non-random missing identifiers can bias workload, handover, and team-performance findings.

7. IF renewal-season volume and complexity exceed the seasonal forecast and queue p90 breaches persist for two reporting windows, THEN activate seasonal staffing and overtime capacity plans while stratifying alerts by complexity, BECAUSE recurring population pressure can amplify existing rework and capacity constraints rather than represent novel behavioral drift.
