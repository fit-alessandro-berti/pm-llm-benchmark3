Section 2: Equivalence & Sufficiency Test — structured test with dimensions:

Define tiers:
- Fully equivalent: achieves each of FEP-1's operative objectives with (a) an independent human authorization decision made before irrevocability by someone neither preparer nor releaser nor conflicted, who actually examined evidence (not rubber stamp), OR (b) an ED-1-compliant emergency package satisfying all seven conditions with clean evidence; plus recoverability still open at decision time; plus no override of automated checks; plus collusion resistance proportional to case risk (e.g., high-value/new-vendor demands more than two collusible actors).
- Partially equivalent: covers most objectives; deficiencies confined to timing slippage that preserves veto before settlement, or evidence-quality gaps on a low-risk case.
- Merely mitigating: reduces expected loss but leaves material attack surface (e.g., override warnings, post-release ratifications, audits outside recall window).
- Ineffective/no equivalence: safeguards absent, overridden by conflicted parties, after irrevocability, or inapplicable due to broken preconditions (e.g., ED-1 token without conditions).

Dimensions checklist:
1. Timing: control events vs settlement (point of irrevocability), not merely vs log order. Before-release > before-settlement-but-after-release (hybrid) > after-settlement (detective only).
2. Independence: human distinctness, no reporting line/conflict, credential attribution (no shared credentials/accounts), workspace separation (shared workstation weakens).
3. Coverage: what threat scenarios does the control actually test? AV-2 list  receipt validation  commercial reasonableness.
4. Overrideability: block vs warn; who holds override credential; can preparer hold/inherit it (AP-202)?
5. Recoverability: recall window; audit latency vs 24h; "detective-only after settlement."
6. Evidence quality: signed manual checklists, immutable timestamps, token provenance (pre-condition), review artifacts vs ratify-clicks.
7. Collusion resistance: does the package require N independent corruptions? Two-person cells defeat vanilla four-eyes; pattern analytics needed.

Why "same expected loss" isn't the only standard: controls differ in (a) variance/tail risk (deterministic blocks vs probabilistic deterrents), (b) distribution of losses across threat types, (c) deterrence effects and process learning, (d) verifiability/evidentiary value (ex-post assurance to auditors/regulators), (e) gaming asymmetry — a control that works only when everyone behaves is worth less; robustness across adversary models matters; (f) epistemic difference: expected-loss parity computed under uncertainty can mask nonzero probability of catastrophic unrecoverable loss, and firms often apply a safety-margin standard (tolerance thresholds, qualitative gating) rather than pure expected-value neutrality. Also equity/compliance duties: some standards are deontic/procedural (regulators demand demonstrable independent authorization irrespective of realized loss frequency).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

### 2.1 Structured test (dimensions applied per case)

| Dimension | Question | Discriminating examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Did the binding human decision precede **settlement**, not merely the release event? | m04 at 08:30 vs settlement 10:00 (AP-201) = protected; m21 at T+24h post-settlement (AP-208) = detective only |
| **Independence** | Is the authorizer a distinct human, without reporting-line conflict, using their own credential, physically/session-separated? | m09 supervises u23 and the override credential bore m09's name (AP-202); u12/u13 share a workstation (AP-204) |
| **Coverage** | Which of O1–O3 did the control actually test? Does "full AV-2 pass" tell us anything about goods received? (No.) | AP-204: perfect AV-2 pass, fabricated receipt |
| **Overrideability** | Was the preventive layer a block or a warning, and who held the override capability? | AP-205 block  full protection; AP-202 warning  zero protection once overridden |
| **Recoverability** | At detection time, was the recall window still open? | 17–23h (AP-206/201) inside; 5–8 days (AP-208/202) outside |
| **Evidence quality** | Signed artifacts? Immutable token lifecycle? Review provenance, or a bare "Ratify" click? | Unsigned manual checklist (AP-208); token born after Prepare (AP-206) |
| **Collusion resistance** | How many independent people must conspire for loss? One (single-user paths), two (nominal four-eyes), or more? | AP-202 needs ~2 corruptibles; AP-204/209's pattern shows the two-actor budget can be spent |

### 2.2 Equivalence tiers

- **Fully equivalent:** reaches O1–O3 *and* preserves O6 with (a) an independent, substantive human authorization before irrevocability (pre-release being the strongest form; post-release/pre-settlement acceptable where the statute, i.e., ED-1 condition 6, allows it), (b) no overridden automated objections, (c) auditable evidence chains, (d) collusion resistance proportionate to case risk (amount × novelty × bank-change proximity).
- **Partially equivalent:** most objectives covered; deficits confined to *provability* (e.g., a retracing-timestamp token) or to margins so thin that corrective capacity is theoretical (1h recall headroom), on otherwise low-risk transactions.
- **Merely mitigating:** reduces expected loss but leaves the architecture intact for an adaptive adversary (warning-type AV-2 with overridable bank-change flags; post-settlement ratifications).
- **Ineffective:** a layer that was absent, unavailable, overridden by a conflicted borrower, timed after irrevocability, or inapplicable because its own preconditions were blown.

### 2.3 Why "same expected loss" is not the sole admissible standard

Expected-value parity collapses several distinctions regulators and prudent controllers should keep:
1. **Tail/irreversibility weighting:** two packages with equal mean loss differ sharply in CVaR where one settles first and asks later. Irrecoverable states deserve option-preserving weights, not linear pricing.
2. **Binding vs statistical compensation:** "we lose the same amount on average, but only if nobody abuses the shortcut" is not equivalent to "the shortcut cannot be abused." Expected loss computed under cooperative behavior silently prices away the adversary.
3. **Adaptive adversaries:** a static expectation is stale the moment insiders learn which reviewer is lazy or whose credential is borrowable. Robustness-across-strategies, not point expectation, is the right measure.
4. **Who bears the loss and who can know:** identical EV can hide transfers of risk to counterparties, and identical protection can differ hugely in *demonstrability* — a control you can prove ran has audit value beyond loss avoidance.
5. **Precedent dynamics:** tolerated exceptions shift the behavioral baseline; the shadow price of AP-208 is paid in future AP-208s.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Primary modality | Preventive when… | Detective when… | Corrective when… | Characteristic failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | Gate (block) or sensor (warning) | Emits **block** and nobody circumvents it | Emits warning that is logged but overridable | Never, intrinsically | Overridability converts prevention into telemetry (AP-202); downtime opens a clean window (AP-208) |
| MG-4 | Human judgment checkpoint | Approved **pre-release** with substantive artifact review | Ratify/review occurs post-settlement, informing future cases only | Indirect: a timely rejection triggers stop/recall machinery it does not itself operate | Rubber-stamping; conflicted ratifiers; borrowed credentials (O5 hole) |
| RA-7 | Retrospective examination | — | Always | Only when detection lands **inside the ~24h recall envelope** and recovery is realistic | Lateness obliterates the "corrective" half; audit-start vs audit-completion ambiguity |
| ED-1 | Meta-permission / governance | Enforces timing, caps, and audit discipline that keep the envelope intact | Provides the ledger by which exceptions can be reconstructed | — | Late-issued tokens (AP-206); application as super-waiver above its caps (AP-208) |

**Semantics chosen.** I anchor "preventive" to the causal chain of the *loss* (economic irrevocability at settlement), not to the log position of Release_Payment. Hence:

- A manager action **after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement** (m04 in AP-201, m15 in AP-207) is a **hybrid, best described as "preventive-through-detection within the containment window."** In kind it is detective — it evaluates an already-executed transaction; in effect it is preventive, because at that instant the firm still holds a live veto over irrevocability. Whether that potentiality is *realized* depends on headroom: m15 had 80 minutes; m04 had 90; but recall execution itself consumes part of any remaining window, so I grade such capacity "conditional corrective."
- The same act **after settlement** (m09 in AP-202, m21 in AP-208) is pure detective theater with respect to that case: it cannot change the loss, only the lessons. Calling it "approval" is the precise vocabulary failure this framework exists to correct.
- Boundary exemplars: AP-207 defines the outer edge of hybrid; AP-208 defines what happens one minute past that edge.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Format per case: most plausible loss mechanism  which control breaks the causal chain  what remains exposed  effect of a truly independent second releaser.

- **AP-201** — Mechanism: ordinary execution error (miskeyed amount/payee) by a lone operator. Breaker: m04's pre-settlement review intercepts before irrevocability; RA-7 could not (audit  recall-window edge). Exposed: the branch where m04 rubber-stamps, capped by operator limits; also reputational erosion via tolerated shortcut. Independent second releaser: materially changes little for this €1.8k low-risk case; systemically it removes the "convenience corridor" and forces contemporaneous challenge instead of a 24-minute echo.
- **AP-202** — Mechanism: vendor-fraud/BEC redirection exploiting *capture*: preparer initiates, borrows supervisor's override credential to silence the bank-change warning, supervisor-conflict neutralizes later review, audit lands after recall. Breaker: any one of — block-class treatment of bank-change warnings; credential-custody enforcement (MFA-bound, non-transferable); an independent L3 reviewer pre-settlement; or same-week audit exercising recall. Exposed: even with an honest second releaser, a rehearsed pair presenting coherent documents on a fresh vendor remains dangerous (needs call-back-to-known-contact verification, which no listed control mandates). Independent releaser effect: high — a disinterested reviewer confronting a 2-hour-old bank change on a €480k first-payment vendor plausibly halts the chain; expected change in that scenario: from full-loss toward blocked-or-recalled.
- **AP-204** — Mechanism: fabricated acceptance evidence siphoning consulting fees, executed by two mutually trusting operators whose IDs differ but whose independence does not. Breaker: none installed, until RA-7's (day-4) discovery. Exposed: all four-eyes-shaped transactions processed by that pair, plus the untranslated lesson for every other pair. Truly independent second releaser *with genuine artifact inspection* (delivery-system cross-reference) plausibly surfaces the fabrication ex ante; a passive approver changes nothing — this is why the fix is rotation + verification depth, not an additional signature.
- **AP-206** — Mechanism (harm path, not observed outcome): "production-down, pay-now" pretext laundering a coercive or fraudulent push through the emergency channel, laundered further by a post-hoc token. Breakers that held: m11's pre-release independent review; audit inside the recall window. Exposed: the *governance* layer — if timestamps can be explained rather than corrected, future attackers need only a plausible outage story. Independent releaser: adds little; the case already featured a real pre-release second brain. The remedial lever is token-ledger immutability, not staffing.
- **AP-208** — Mechanism: the classic coincident-failure exploit: validation outage + recent bank change + oversized amount, signed off by whoever was reachable after the money was gone. Breaker: only a fail-safe default ("validation unavailable  no settlement above threshold, escalate instead"), and crucially the amount/limit checks would bite immediately upon an honest pre-settlement MG-4. Independent releaser: relevant only if independence is real and informed; a pressured duo signing jointly recreates AP-204 in miniature at 12× the stakes. Residual exposure even after fixes: senior-management override under production pressure — mitigable by board-visible logging, not eliminable.
- **AP-209** — Mechanism: salami-style skimming across many mid-size legitimate-looking invoices, invisible per case. Breaker today: none specific; the proposed workstations-session correlation, pair-rotation analytics, and relationship-targeted audit sampling. Exposed: however this resolves, the same-trust extends beyond AP-204. An "independent second releaser" would be a contradiction if the pattern reflects a fixed cell; there the intervention is rotation and separation of duties redesign, with monitoring rather than transaction-level controls doing the initial work.

(Illustrative only, clearly labeled: if one assigned coarse orders of magnitude — prevented-in-full cases approaching zero incremental loss, partial-equivalence cases bounded by their caps, and the AP-202/AP-208 class carrying the dominant share of aggregate modelled exposure — the concentration of tail risk in the "ineffective-tier" cases would dominate several-fold over all convenience-tier deviations combined.)

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Each rule lists the **alert** (a question-raiser) and what would constitute **proof** (never implied by the alert alone).

1. **Same-user rate** (share of completed payments where Prepare=Release, weekly, by team, by amount band, split by token-present/absent). *Alert:* rate drift or clusters without tokens (AP-201-style). *Proof:* none by itself — AP-203/207 show conforming same-user cases exist; proof requires absence of ED-1 conditions **and** corroborating motive/control-pressure indicators.
2. **Override rate**, by warning type and by holder of the override credential. *Alert:* any override of bank-change warnings; any override where holderpreparer's manager lineage. *Proof:* borrowed-credential use is near-proof of an *identity-integrity* control breach (AP-202) even before any fraud inference; link fraud intent via financial tracing and communications.
3. **Late manager approval:** distribution of (Approval  Release) and (Approval  Settlement). *Alert:* any post-settlement ratification (tolerance  0; both AP-202 and AP-208 sit here). *Proof:* lateness proves a timing-policy breach, not wrongdoing; the AP-207/201 hybrids prove the reverse — lateness is judged against **settlement**, never against log adjacency.
4. **Audit-before-recall-window KPI:** % of relevant cases with audit *completed* within 24h of settlement; publish margin-to-window for near-misses (AP-201's ~1h; contrast 17–20h elsewhere). *Alert:* completion (not start!) beyond window. *Proof:* measures process health; defects found late still matter civilly/criminally, so lateness  exoneration or conviction either way.
5. **Repeated resource pairs** (rolling 30/90-day Prepare×Release co-occurrence; thresholds trigger review of the whole joint book). *Alert:* u12/u13-pattern densities. *Proof:* density legitimizes deep-dive, sampled, relationship-focused audits; conclusive misconduct requires document/financial corroboration — statistical pattern alone must never discharge anyone, and must equally never convict.
6. **Shared workstations/devices** (session-ID  resource-ID inconsistency mining). *Alert:* frequent swaps by constant pairs. *Proof:* proves session-management control weakness outright; collusion remains inferential until access logs show one human driving both identities or benefits flow.
7. **Bank-change proximity:** enhanced verification (call-back to previously known contact, dual control) mandatory whenever payment follows a beneficiary bank change within N days; hard combination: new/high amount + recent change + override = auto-hold plus same-day independent review. *Alert:* the combination fires. *Proof:* the alert indicates elevated fraud posterior; proof of fraud lives in beneficiary ownership tracing.
8. **Control-outage ledger:** every `Validation_Service_Unavailable` logged with duration, requester traffic, and settlements cleared during the outage; correlation scan of outage windows vs subsequent dispute/fraud events. *Alert:* outage coinciding with anomalous payments (AP-208). *Proof:* coincidence alone proves fragility, not conspiracy; repeated coincidence with the same actors approaches conspiratorial weight only alongside documentary corroboration.
9. **Token lifecycle governance:** issueactivateuseretire with reason codes, requester, and system-clock provenance; reconciliation of token time vs Prepare time with flagged discrepancies (captures AP-206 exactly). *Alert:* post-Prepare issuance. *Proof:* proves ED-1 condition failure as a documentation matter; whether the outage was real needs the incident ticket, not the token.
10. **Evidence-retention & attestation cadence:** managers retain what they inspected (invoice, bank-detail comparison, receipt); quarterly independence attestations including supervisor relationships and credential custody; monthly review of the four-eyes-violation register with dispositions matching this framework's taxonomy rather than binary pass/fail.

**PM4Py behaviors I cannot guarantee across versions**
- `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps cases where the two activities are executed by *different* resources; how it treats traces **missing** one of the activities has varied across releases, so the script derives the violation set twice (manual overlap test vs filter-minus-all) and prints any disagreement instead of silently trusting either. Cases with no `Release_Payment` (AP-205) are excluded by construction from the completed-payment violation set.
- `format_dataframe` expects naive datetimes in most versions (tz-aware input has raised errors in older builds); the script strips UTC-offsets to naive UTC wall-clock. Where format_dataframe simply passes canonical columns through, behavior is inert.
- DataFrame vs EventLog returns differ by version; both are handled defensively (`hasattr` branch) and everything sits behind a guarded import so the script still runs, with pm4py-based results replaced by the manual set.
- `numpy.busday` assumes Monday–Friday and ignores local holidays, so "one business day" is an approximation to be reconciled with the plant calendar.
- Resource equality is plain string equality: if shared-service or pooled accounts ever carried identical IDs, the manual check would false-negative; organizational resource resolution belongs upstream of any such checker.
