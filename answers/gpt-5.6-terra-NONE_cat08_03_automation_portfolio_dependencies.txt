# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

## Core process dependencies

The process should be treated as a set of synchronized workstreams rather than a strictly linear sequence:

- After **Classify product and exposure**, KYC and financial document review frequently proceed in parallel, even though the event log often records them sequentially.
- **Committee packaging** is a synchronization point. It requires:
  - KYC status,
  - normalized financial statements,
  - credit-analysis notes, and
  - collateral valuation where required.
- **Customer signing** is legally dependent on KYC clearance, regardless of provisional-signing behavior visible in the log.
- **Loan activation** depends on completed KYC, valid signing, decision-consistent contract terms, and operational controls.

Thus, speeding a single activity is valuable only if it improves the relevant synchronization path or avoids creating a larger downstream queue.

## Upstream data-quality dependencies

1. **A1 auto-registration** and **A2 classification** influence nearly every downstream path. Incorrect registration, duplicate resolution, or product/exposure classification can:
   - route a case to the wrong KYC or collateral path;
   - produce an incorrect missing-document checklist;
   - omit High-exposure controls; and
   - affect committee and contract rules.

2. **A5 missing-document automation** is a practical prerequisite for **A6 financial-statement normalization**.  
   Financial review has a 24% exception rate, while missing-document requests repeat in 52% of relevant cases. Improving completeness before analyst work prevents automation from merely processing defective inputs faster.

3. **A6 normalization** is a material prerequisite for **A8 committee-pack assembly**. Pack automation should not assemble a formally complete-looking package from incomplete, unnormalized, or inconsistent financial data.

4. **A3 KYC pre-classification** and **A4 entity-mismatch assistance** depend on accurate identity data, explainable matching evidence, and a conservative route to human review.

## Legal and compliance control dependencies

- **A9 contract generation** requires a controlled rule library mapping committee decisions to contract terms. It should also enforce:
  - KYC-clearance confirmation;
  - required approvals;
  - product-specific conditions;
  - decision-to-contract consistency; and
  - evidence retention.

- **A10 e-signature automation** must consume a trusted KYC-clearance status and must not permit signing solely because an event appears later in the log. The 6.5% provisional-signing rate is a control issue, not a valid variant to automate.

- **A11 activation** is last in the dependency chain. It should not be prioritized before preventive controls at KYC, committee-to-contract reconciliation, and signing. At most, it can be designed as a “readiness checker” during the first year.

- **A8 committee-pack automation** must enforce the four-eyes rule in High-exposure SME cases. The same person must not complete both credit analysis and committee packaging.

## Human escalation capacity

Automation changes, rather than eliminates, human work.

- **A3** may reduce routine KYC effort but will send uncertain hits to compliance specialists. There are only three specialists, with dependence concentrated on two senior people. Thresholds must be set to protect the specialist queue, and the design should reduce repetitive evidence gathering rather than simply create more referrals.
- **A4** should assist specialists with evidence retrieval, comparison, and case summarization, but final matching decisions should remain human-controlled for uncertain/high-risk matches.
- **A6** needs analyst exception handling for poor PDFs, non-standard statements, and extraction discrepancies. Without an exception workbench and capacity plan, it may shift work from document review into a larger analyst validation queue.
- **A5** must improve RM behavior, because relationship managers currently trigger many missing-document loops through incomplete instructions. Automation should give RMs guided templates and visibility, not merely automate reminders after an incomplete request was sent.

## External valuation dependency

**A7** cannot eliminate the 58-hour median external wait, but it can improve the controllable portion:

- identify valuation requirement earlier from product/collateral data;
- submit complete requests the first time;
- route to the appropriate valuer based on geography, asset type, service-level performance, and capacity;
- trigger late-valuation escalation before committee packaging is blocked;
- allow internal preparation to proceed in parallel while valuation is pending.

It is especially relevant for Equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases. The benefit is lower for Working-capital cases, where only 18% require collateral.

## Core-banking integration constraints

Only two modules can be deeply integrated in the first six months. Deep integration should be reserved for controls or major flow dependencies, not merely for the most visible manual task.

Recommended first-six-month deep integrations:

1. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks**  
   It needs authoritative KYC, committee decision, contract, and signing data to prevent downstream legal/control failure.

2. **A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization**  
   It should write validated structured financial data into the credit-analysis workflow and make exception status visible.

Modules that can begin with lighter integration, workflow orchestration, APIs, or pilot workbenches include A4, A5, A7, A10, and A12. A1 can also be deployed with less than full core-banking integration if it initially creates draft records subject to validation.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

## Quick-win portfolio

**Modules:** A1 Auto-registration, A5 Missing-document checklist/nudging, A9 Contract generation/control checks, A10 E-signature/reminders.

### What it optimizes
- Visible labor savings in back office and legal operations.
- Faster intake, cleaner customer interactions, reduced contract drafting effort, and lower signing delay.
- Political momentum through demonstrable automation across the customer journey.
- At least one strong control module: A9.

### What it sacrifices
- It does not directly relieve the credit-analyst bottleneck or the entity-mismatch delay.
- Faster registration and better document intake may increase throughput into KYC and credit analysis before those queues are ready.
- E-signature gains are constrained by the legal KYC-before-signing gate.
- It may improve local activity performance more than end-to-end cycle time for High-exposure and collateral-dependent cases.

## Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

**Modules:** A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant, A5 Missing-document checklist/nudging, A6 Financial-statement extraction/normalization, A7 Collateral valuation triage/routing.

### What it optimizes
- End-to-end flow through the most material waits, loops, and synchronization points.
- KYC blockage caused by entity mismatch: 31-hour median wait and critical-path relevance in 82% of cases where it appears.
- Financial-document rework and analyst capacity.
- Collateral-dependent variants, particularly Equipment finance, where external valuation is a major source of delay.
- Quality of inputs to committee packaging.

### What it sacrifices
- It delivers less immediate visible automation at intake and contract drafting.
- It does not by itself prevent contract/committee mismatch or provisional signing; compensating operational controls are required until a later A9 deployment.
- A6 can create a surge in analyst-ready work, so analyst capacity management is essential.

## Compliance-first portfolio

**Modules:** A3 KYC hit pre-classifier with escalation, A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant, A9 Contract generation/control-rule checks, A12 Control remediation workbench.

### What it optimizes
- Prevention and recovery of the bank’s most consequential control failures:
  - KYC clearance before signing;
  - decision-to-contract consistency;
  - auditable identity resolution;
  - structured remediation;
  - visibility of policy ambiguity.
- Better use of the small compliance-specialist pool through triage and evidence preparation.
- Stronger audit trail for model suggestion, override, and final decision.

### What it sacrifices
- Less reduction in customer document delays and analyst manual work.
- Limited direct relief for collateral synchronization and external valuer delay.
- Potentially slower initial rollout because model governance, validation, and legal rule ownership are substantial.
- If the KYC pre-classifier is too aggressive, it could create unacceptable false-negative risk; if too conservative, it could add specialist workload.

A10 can reduce reminder-loop delays, not the entire customer-signing wait. |
| **Rework** | A5 should reduce the 52% repeated missing-document cycle. A9 can reduce the 8.5% contract-remediation loop, particularly the 14% rate in Credit-line renewal, if the rule library is accurate. |
| **Handovers** | A1 reduces clerk/back-office touches. A5 can reduce RM-to-back-office clarification handovers by standardizing requests. A9 reduces legal drafting handoffs but may add an exception handoff to legal for non-standard clauses. |

Specific DFG implications:

- The large `Receive application -> Register application` volume makes A1 attractive locally, but it does not address the longer waits later in the path.

- A9 should use `Committee decision -> Contract preparation` as a controlled data handoff, rather than treating contract preparation as a free-text drafting task.
- A10 should only allow `Contract preparation -> Customer signing` if a trusted KYC-clearance event exists, not merely because the process sequence appears complete.

- A5 and A6 improve the path from financial review through normalization to credit analysis: `Financial document review -> Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis`.
- Committee packaging waits for normalized financials, KYC, credit notes, and collateral where required. Therefore, improving only one path is insufficient; the portfolio deliberately improves multiple inputs to that synchronization point.
- A7 is most valuable in Equipment finance, rather than uniformly across all products.

| **Rework** | A9 reduces contract-remediation loops. A12 makes `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` more structured and less repetitive. A4 reduces KYC re-entry loops. |
| **Handovers** | A3 and A4 can standardize KYC-ops-to-specialist handovers with evidence packages. A12 clarifies ownership across legal, compliance, relationship management, and operations. A9 reduces manual legal drafting handovers for standard cases. |

- The 650 contract-to-remediation cases become a source of structured learning through A12; repeated reasons should feed changes to the contract-rule library and upstream decision templates.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Automation risk | Where it arises | Compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| **False negatives in KYC screening** | A3 | Use conservative thresholds; do not auto-clear uncertain or high-risk cases; require human approval for final KYC disposition; independently test recall on adverse cases; maintain immutable evidence and decision logs. |
| **False positives creating specialist overload** | A3, A4 | Capacity-based routing; tiered escalation; evidence pre-packaging; queue ageing alerts; monitor volume and age of escalations to the three-person specialist pool. |
| **Incorrect entity match or duplicate merge** | A1, A4 | No automatic merge for low-confidence or regulated entities; dual review for high-risk matches; retain source documents and match rationale; allow reversible actions; periodic sample review. |
| **Model drift and changing input quality** | A2, A3, A4, A6, A7 | Version models and rules; track accuracy by applicant segment, product, document type, and risk class; challenger testing; drift thresholds; formal change approval; rollback capability. |
| **Incorrect extraction from financial statements** | A6 | Field-level confidence scores; reconciliation checks; analyst validation for material fields; mandatory manual review for low-quality PDFs and High-exposure SME cases; retain original document and extracted values. |
| **Customer friction or exclusion** | A5, A10 | Use adaptive rather than rigid checklists; support assisted channels; permit RM-guided exceptions; test language and accessibility; measure abandonment, repeat contact, complaint rates, and document-request cycle rate. |
| **Misclassification routes cases to wrong controls** | A2 | Advisory-first deployment; confidence thresholds; mandatory human confirmation for High-exposure SME, new products, and ambiguous cases; periodic confusion-matrix review by product/risk type. |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out committee pack** | A8 | Do not auto-finalize packs; validate artifact completeness and timestamps; show source lineage; require approver attestation; block pack completion if KYC, financial, collateral, or role-separation checks fail. |
| **Outdated contract rule library** | A9 | Legal-owned rule governance; effective dates; dual approval for rule changes; regression testing against historical decisions; exception workflow; daily reconciliation of committee decisions and generated terms. |
| **KYC-before-signing violation** | A9, A10 | Hard technical gate using authoritative KYC status; no override except documented compliance-approved emergency process; alert and case freeze if signing event occurs without clearance. |
| **Four-eyes violation for High-exposure SME** | A8/A9 and workflow configuration | Enforce role-based segregation; compare user IDs for credit analysis and committee packaging; block completion where same person is assigned; require manager-approved reassignment; report violations by month-end period. |
| **Automation hides rather than resolves remediation causes** | A12 | Root-cause taxonomy; mandatory causal classification; periodic review of recurring causes; link remediation outcomes to rule-library, training, and upstream process changes. |
| **External valuer dependency remains unresolved** | A7 | Partner SLA dashboards; alternate-valuer routing; incomplete-request prevention; ageing escalation; distinct reporting of bank-controlled versus partner-controlled delay. |
| **Uncontrolled shifted workload** | All, especially A5/A6/A3 | Capacity plan by queue; monitor arrivals, work-in-progress, queue age, exception rate, and utilization; do not measure success only by automated-task volume or local service time. |
| **Insufficient auditability** | All model- or rule-based modules | Retain input data, model/rule version, recommendation, confidence, human override, final decision, user, timestamp, and reason code; make logs immutable and reviewable by compliance/internal audit. |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

## Phase 0: Process and control baseline — Months 0–2

### Objectives
- Confirm the true concurrency of KYC and financial review rather than relying on sequential timestamps.
- Establish segment-level baselines for Micro, SME, High-exposure SME, Working-capital, Equipment finance, and Credit-line renewal.
- Define authoritative control statuses and ownership.

### Deliverables
- A process-mining dashboard showing:
  - end-to-end cycle time and waiting time;
  - document-loop frequency and recurrence;
  - KYC hit/mismatch ageing;
  - analyst queue age and utilization;
  - valuation turnaround by product, valuer, and asset type;
  - committee-pack completeness;
  - signing-before-KYC events;
  - contract-remediation reasons;
  - four-eyes violations.
- A control data model defining authoritative fields for:
  - KYC clearance;
  - committee decision and conditions;
  - contract-version and term mapping;
  - signer identity/consent;
  - credit analyst and committee-pack preparer identities.
- Human exception-routing design and service levels for compliance specialists, analysts, legal operations, relationship managers, and collateral teams.

### Gate criteria
- Clear data lineage for KYC, decision, contract, and signing statuses.
- Named business owners for KYC rules, financial-data validation, contract-rule library, and exception queues.
- Agreement that no automation may bypass an existing legal or four-eyes control.

## Phase 1: Flow-quality pilots — Months 2–4

### Modules
1. **A5 Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging**
2. **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**

These can begin as workflow-support tools with limited core-banking dependence.

### Why this order
- A5 reduces the major rework loop before analyst work.
- A4 targets a lower-frequency but critical-path delay and supports scarce compliance specialists.
- Both improve input quality and reduce avoidable waiting before more complex automation.

### Monitoring metrics
- Repeat missing-document rate and number of request cycles per case.
- Time from financial review to receipt of complete documents.
- RM adoption of standardized request templates.
- Entity-mismatch queue age, resolution time, re-entry rate to KYC, and specialist workload.
- Match-assistant precision, override rate, and false-match findings.
- Customer abandonment, complaints, and channel-switching rate.

### Gate criteria
- Material reduction in repeat document loops without increased complaints or abandonment.
- Entity-mismatch assistant meets pre-agreed conservative quality thresholds; no material compliance incident.
- Specialist escalation backlog remains within service-level limits.

## Phase 2: Deep integrations and analyst-capacity protection — Months 4–6

### Modules
3. **A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization** — deep integration 1  
4. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks** — deep integration 2

### Why these are the two deep integrations
- A6 affects the central analyst network and feeds credit analysis and committee packaging.
- A9 is the strongest direct preventive control module and must use authoritative core-banking data.

### Deployment design
- A6 begins with standard financial formats and low-to-medium complexity cases. High-exposure SME and low-confidence documents remain analyst-validated.
- A9 starts with standard products and standard contract templates, then expands by product after legal regression testing. It must hard-gate signing on KYC clearance and decision/contract reconciliation.

### Monitoring metrics
- Extraction accuracy and analyst correction rate by document type.
- Normalization turnaround time and exception-queue age.
- Credit-analysis queue length, ageing, and analyst overtime.
- Contract term mismatch rate, contract-remediation rate, and time to remediation.
- Signing attempts blocked for missing KYC clearance.
- Four-eyes violations in High-exposure SME cases.
- Rule-library change failures, override frequency, and legal exception volume.

### Gate criteria
- No increase in material credit-analysis errors.
- Analyst queue age does not deteriorate beyond agreed limits despite increased clean inflow.
- A9 demonstrates reliable KYC gate and decision-to-contract reconciliation.
- Four-eyes blocks operate correctly in simulated and live High-exposure cases.
- Legal approves rule-governance operating model.

## Phase 3: Controlled scale and synchronization improvement — Months 7–9

### Module
5. **A7 Collateral valuation triage and valuer routing**  
This is a second-wave candidate if budget permits a module beyond the first four only through replacement, re-scoping, or separately approved funding. If the first-year cap is strict, it should be prepared in this phase but deployed after year one.

### Parallel non-module actions
- Use the improved process data to redesign analyst staffing/routing and month-end workload management.
- Establish partner performance reviews for external valuers.
- Implement manual workflow controls for committee packaging and e-signing until A8/A10 are funded.

### Monitoring metrics
- Valuation request completeness.
- Submission-to-valuation turnaround by valuer and asset type.
- Late valuation rate.
- Committee-pack waiting attributable to valuation.
- Equipment-finance cycle time relative to Working-capital cases.

### Gate criteria
- Evidence that routing can change controllable delay, not merely reclassify external delay.
- No increase in valuation disputes or revaluation requests.
- Clear escalation process for overdue valuations.

## Phase 4: Year-end review and next-wave selection — Months 10–12

### Review decisions
Choose the next module based on residual constraint:

- If signing wait remains dominant after KYC gating is stable: **A10 e-signature adoption/reminders**.
- If committee preparation remains a major internal wait after data quality improves: **A8 committee-pack auto-assembly** with four-eyes enforcement.
- If control exceptions remain substantial: **A12 remediation workbench**.
- If KYC routine volume remains high and specialist queue is stable: **A3 KYC pre-classifier**.
- If management still needs a visible productivity win and downstream queues are stable: **A1 auto-registration**.

### Portfolio monitoring scorecard
Use a balanced scorecard, not local automation counts:

- End-to-end cycle time by segment and product.
- Median and tail waiting time at KYC, financial review, normalization, credit analysis, valuation, packaging, contract, and signing.
- Rework-loop frequency and recurrence.
- Queue size and age by resource pool.
- Analyst, compliance-specialist, legal, RM, and collateral workload.
- Customer effort and abandonment.
- KYC-before-signing violations.
- Four-eyes violations.
- Contract/committee mismatch and remediation rates.
- Override rates, model/rule accuracy, and audit-log completeness.

# 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio**:

1. **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**
2. **A5 Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging**
3. **A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization**
4. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks**

This is a slightly modified bottleneck portfolio: A9 replaces A7 in the first-year four-module cap because it provides the required first-wave control strengthening and directly addresses material legal/control failures. A7 should be the leading year-two candidate, or a year-one replacement if valuation delay proves to be the dominant residual constraint for Equipment finance.

The trade-off is deliberate:

- **A4** addresses a low-frequency activity with disproportionate critical-path impact: 31 hours of median waiting and critical-path status for 82% of affected cases.
- **A5** attacks a high-volume rework loop, not merely a high-frequency activity. The 3,300 missing-document requests and 52% repeat rate create avoidable delay and poor-quality analyst inflow.
- **A6** relieves a central resource constraint. Credit analysts are central in the working-together network, and normalization consumes substantial analyst time while waiting before normalization is already 20 hours.
- **A9** prevents automation from accelerating defective downstream processing. It directly addresses the 8.5% contract-remediation rate, the 14% rate for Credit-line renewals, KYC-before-signing violations, and the need for an auditable decision-to-contract chain.

**A1 auto-registration should not be the first strategic module**, despite its very high frequency and feasibility. Registration takes only eight minutes of mean service time and the back-office pool is under 65% utilized after 15:00. Automating it first would produce visible local labor savings, but it would mainly accelerate cases into KYC, financial review, and analyst queues where waits and exception handling are much larger. It risks making downstream bottlenecks more visible without improving end-to-end performance.

A1 remains a valid later quick win once the bank has stabilized downstream capacity, exception routing, and controls. In the first year, however, the bank should prioritize automation that improves the quality and synchronization of prerequisite artifacts, protects compliance obligations, and avoids shifting workload into already constrained specialist and analyst pools.
