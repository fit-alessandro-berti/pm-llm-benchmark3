## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I organise the repair logic around four tiers of rule strength, each playing a distinct role in evaluating plausible additions or rejections of E2O and O2O edges.

- **Hard invariant**: *O2O qualifiers are directional* (domain rule8). Reversing a qualifier (e.g., storing `PK7 carries DELX` instead of `DELX carries PK7`) produces a malformed OCEL and is always rejected. This is a syntactic constraint; it does not speak to business semantics but must be enforced in any repair.

- **Strong defaults** (defeasible but frequently reliable):  
  1. *Create Order* normally relates to exactly one Order.  
  2. *Add Item* normally relates to one Order and one Item.  
  3. *Pack Item* normally relates to one Item and one Package; a scanner outage can create an itemonly row reconciled later.  
  4. *Dispatch Package* normally relates to one Package and one Delivery; a bulk dispatch may cover multiple packages.  
  5. *Confirm Delivery* may refer to one delivery, several deliveries acknowledged together, or only a customer/order if the portal loses the carrier identifier.  
  6. *Issue Invoice* relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders; arithmetic equality is evidence, not proof.  
  7. *Record Payment* relates to exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice; unapplied cash is possible.  

  These defaults gatekeep the most common lifecycle patterns. When an event or O2O violates a strong default, the repair must either provide compelling contrary evidence or treat the relation as provisional/unresolved.

- **Weak heuristics** (informative but not decisive):  
  - *Matching timestamps and identifier prefixes are informative but not decisive* (rule9).  
  - *Arithmetic equality is evidence, not proof* (rule6 second sentence).  
  - *Matching customer, route, or attribute values across objects is supportive but not determinative*.  

  Weak heuristics raise the plausibility of a repair but never alone justify acceptance; they must be corroborated by stronger evidence or by internal consistency across the whole log.

- **Evidence against a repair**: A repair is downgraded or rejected if it (a) violates cardinality (e.g., an *Add Item* event linking to two orders), (b) contradicts the directional O2O structure, (c) relies on arithmetic matching as if it were deterministic proof, (d) creates an orphan object/event without compensating reconciling links, or (e) introduces circular reasoning (inferring an O2O edge because an E2O edge was first assumed, then treating the inferred O2O as independent confirmation).

**How I combine evidence**: I weight strands by lifecycle stage (earlier events constrain later ones), by attribute congruence (customer, route, amount, SKU), and by rule strength. Identifierprefix matching (e.g., `N420` vs `N421`) receives moderate weight because prefixes can be reused or typoed, but combined with route and timestamp they become more suggestive. Amount congruence (100+130=230) receives strong weight as *supportive* evidence but is explicitly not proof. I also check whether a repair yields a coherent object lifecycle or introduces unresolvable orphans; if it does the latter without a compensating benefit, it is downgraded. The final confidence score in [0,1] reflects the cumulative evidential strength, not a probability.

- ITB1: created ev05, packed ev08 (unresolved package in BundleA), dispatched ev10 (PK8  DELY added), but no delivery confirmation. Lifecycle incomplete under minimal repair; would require O2O `PK8 packs ITB1` (explicitly rejected in BundleA).

**Package**  
- PK7: packed ITA1 (O2O), dispatched DELX (O2O E2O ev09). Lifecycle complete.  
- PK8: packed ITA2 (O2O). In BundleA, dispatched DELY (addition). No pack ITB1, so PK8’s weight2.9 may reflect underutilisation or unmodelled items. Lifecycle plausible but slightly “light” on items; acceptable under uncertainty preservation.

**Delivery**  
- DELX: carries PK7 (O2O), dispatched ev09 (E2O), confirmed delivery ev11  ORDA (BundleA E2O addition). Lifecycle complete under BundleA.  
- DELY: exists, carrier_refN421, route NORTH. In BundleA, receives PK8 via addition, dispatched via ev10 addition. No confirmdelivery event for ORDB; delivery confirmation orphaned for ORDB. If BundleB, confirmation added via ev11  DELY.

**Invoice**  
- INV44: total230, customerC17. E2O ev12 issue invoice ORDA, ev13 record payment PAY1 INV44. BundleA adds PAY2  INV44 settles. O2O `INV44 bills ORDA` observed; `PAY1 settles INV44` observed; `PAY2 settles INV44` added. Cardinality: 1 invoice, 2 payments (1:2). Lifecycle plausible; the invoice’s order scope remains ORDAonly per observed, but the total suggests two orders—a tension kept unresolved.

**Payment**  
- PAY1: amount100, settles INV44 (E2O ev13, O2O). Complete.  
- PAY2: amount130, no target originally. BundleA adds settles INV44. Lifecycle complete under BundleA.  

**Orphan events/objects**: ev08 (itempack without package resolution), ev10 (dispatch without delivery link in original), ev11 (delivery confirmation with ambiguous delivery), ev14 (payment record without invoice link originally, added in BundleA). No objects are strictly orphaned, but DELY’s confirmation status for ORDB is orphaned under BundleA.

**Suspicious cardinality**: INV44 billing ORDA only while total230 implies two orders is a manytoone (two orders  one invoice) that rule6 permits, but without O2O for ORDB it is a onesided inference. PAY1 and PAY2 both settling INV44 creates a manytoone paymentinvoice link, also permitted. These are not violations per se, but they are *suspicious* in the sense that they rely on the minimumchange assumption rather than explicit evidence.

**Circular reasoning identified**: In any bundle that adds `INV44 bills ORDB` (BundleB), one must check whether that assumption was subsequently used to justify `PAY2  ORDB` (amount130 vs declared100). If the invoiceexpansion drives the payment allocation, the inference is circular: the invoice scope is enlarged *because* the total matches, and the payment allocation is then *because* the invoice scope is enlarged. BundleA avoids this by keeping the invoice scoped to ORDA only and treating PAY2 as a residual settlement independent of ORDB’s order status.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

I compare the unrepaired log, BundleA, and BundleB across several mining artifacts.

**OCDFG (Observed Causality-Driven Flow Graph)**  
- *Unrepaired*: Two disconnected order branches. ORDA has a connected path create  add  pack (PK7)  dispatch (PK7)  delivery (DELX)  confirm (ev11)  invoice (INV44)  payment (PAY1). ORDB halts after pack/dispatch; no delivery confirmation, no invoice link, PAY2 isolated. The DFG shows a “dead end” for ORDB and a spurious isolated payment node.  
- *BundleA*: ORDA’s path becomes a full loop (adds delivery confirmation via ev11  DELX, payment settlement via PAY2  INV44). ORDB’s branch now reaches dispatch  delivery (PK8  DELY) but stops before confirmation; the DFG shows ORDA as a complete cycle and ORDB as a halfcycle. The isolated PAY2 node is resolved, linking to INV44.  
- *BundleB*: Both orders achieve nearparallel full cycles (create  add  pack  dispatch  confirm delivery  invoice  payment). The DFG exhibits two symmetric tracks that may merge at the Invoice node if the model treats INV44 as consolidating both orders. This can reduce the apparent number of distinct controlflow variants, potentially masking genuine process divergence.

**OTG (ObjectTask Graph)**  
- *Unrepaired*: ORDA and ORDB are topologically distinct; ORDB lacks a deliveryconfirmation node, so the OTG reflects an asymmetric dependency. PAY2 has no outgoing edge, making it a sink.  
- *BundleA*: ORDA gains a confirmdelivery edge to DELX, creating a cycle (Order  …  Delivery  Confirm  Invoice  Payment  INV44). ORDB gains a dispatchdelivery edge (PK8  DELY) but no confirmdelivery edge, so the OTG shows ORDA with a full cycle and ORDB with a dangling delivery node. PAY2 now has an outgoing `settles INV44` edge, eliminating the sink.  
- *BundleB*: Both orders gain confirmdelivery edges (ORDA  DELX, ORDB  DELY). If INV44 bills both orders, the OTG may introduce a “shared invoice” node with two incoming Order edges. PAY2’s edge may split: `settles INV44` (as in BundleA) *and* an optional `allocated to ORDB` edge, depending on the subinterpretation. The OTG becomes more homogeneous, potentially inflating equivalence between ORDA and ORDB.

**ET–OT graph (EventtoObject & ObjecttoTask)**  
- *Unrepaired*: PAY2 is an isolated event with no O2O target; ev08 has no package target; ev11 has no clear delivery object. The ETOT graph contains several unattached leaves.  
- *BundleA*: PAY2 attaches to INV44 (settles), resolving its leaf status. ev08 remains unresolved (intentionally), so its event node stays partially attached (scanner_ref PK82 visible but no O2O target). ev11 attaches to DELX  ORDA, reducing unattached nodes.  
- *BundleB*: PAY2 may attach to ORDB (allocation) or stay on INV44; ev08 gains O2O `PK8 packs ITB1`, linking the event to both ITB1 and PK8; ev11 gains a second attachment (DELY  ORDB). The graph becomes denser, which can improve connectivity metrics but risks overconnection if the added edges are weakly supported.

**Flattenings** (pm4py.ocel_flattening)  
- *Order flattening*:  
  - Unrepaired: ORDA flattening includes full lifecycle; ORDB flattening stops at dispatch/pack, missing delivery, invoice, payment activities.  
  - BundleA: ORDA flattening unchanged (full); ORDB flattening gains a “dispatch” activity but still lacks confirmdelivery, invoice, and payment rows.  
  - BundleB: Both ORDA and ORDB flattenings include confirmdelivery and invoicebilling rows, making them fully symmetric. If INV44 bills both, the flattening may duplicate the invoice row for each order, or create a single invoice row with two order attachments, depending on pm4py’s handling of multiorder invoices.  
- *Delivery flattening*:  
  - Unrepaired: DELX flattening includes ORDA confirmation; DELY flattening has no order association.  
  - BundleA: DELX flattening adds ORDA confirmation (already present); DELY flattening gains a “dispatch PK8” row but no confirmdelivery row for ORDB.  
  - BundleB: Both DELX and DELY flattenings include their respective order confirmations, yielding two parallel delivery logs.  
- *Invoice flattening*:  
  - Unrepaired: INV44 flattening links to ORDA only; PAY1 linked, PAY2 isolated.  
  - BundleA: INV44 flattening links to ORDA; both PAY1 and PAY2 linked as settling rows; ORDB not explicit.  
  - BundleB: INV44 flattening explicitly links to both ORDA and ORDB; payments may be split perorder or kept as residual, altering the flattening’s orderinvoice mapping.  
- *Payment flattening*:  
  - Unrepaired: PAY1 linked to INV44 and ORDA (via invoice); PAY2 isolated.  
  - BundleA: PAY2 linked to INV44, implicitly to ORDA (as residual).  
  - BundleB: If PAY2 allocated to ORDB, the flattening shows two separate paymentinvoiceorder chains (PAY1  ORDA, PAY2  ORDB) converging on the same INV44 node; if PAY2 stays on INV44 as residual, the pattern resembles BundleA but with the invoice now known to bill both orders.  

**Activity frequencies** (events vs. unique objects vs. total objects)  
- *Unrepaired*: Create Order2, Add Item3, Pack Item4 events, Dispatch Package2 events, Confirm Delivery1, Issue Invoice1, Record Payment2. Unique objects: 2 orders, 3 items, 2 packages, 2 deliveries, 1 invoice, 2 payments =12. Total objects same (no duplicates).  
- *BundleA*: No new events or objects added; the same 17 events and 12 objects, but now PAY2 has a target, DELY has a dispatch link, ev11 has a delivery object. Activity frequencies unchanged, but “effective” frequencies (events with complete lifecycles) shift: ORDA’s fullcycle events increase from 1 to 7 (create, 2 adds, pack, dispatch, confirm, invoice, pay); ORDB’s fullcycle events remain at 3 (create, add, dispatch).  
- *BundleB*: Same raw counts, but the *perceived* activity distribution changes because both orders now show full cycles. If the miner counts unique object lifecycles, ORDA and ORDB each gain 4 new activity slots (confirm delivery, invoice billing, payment allocation), raising the average activitiesperorder from ~3 to ~7.

**Equivalentexecution clustering around Order**  
- *Unrepaired*: ORDA and ORDB are not equivalent; ORDB missing delivery/payment breaks topological similarity. Clustering would likely place them in different variants or mark ORDB as an outlier.  
- *BundleA*: ORDA becomes a “completecycle” cluster; ORDB remains a “partialcycle” cluster. The two orders may still be grouped together in a “parallel orders” metacluster due to shared customer, route, and item SKUs, but their internal lifecycle lengths differ.  
- *BundleB*: ORDA and ORDB become topologically equivalent (both have create  add  pack  dispatch  confirm  invoice  pay). A clustering algorithm would likely group them as a single execution pattern with two order tokens, potentially identifying them as the dominant variant. This could lead to erroneous conclusions that the process *always* handles two orders together, when in reality the equivalence is repairinduced.

## 8. Overrepair warnings

1. **Crossorder package assignment (PK8 packs ITA2+ITB1)** creates a singlepackage multiorder path in the OCDFG. Order tokens for ORDA and ORDB merge prematurely, hiding the true separation of the two orders and leading to false conclusions about process variants (e.g., suggesting a singleorder flow where two distinct orders are interleaved).

2. **Forcing ev11 uniquely to DELX when the ext_ref is ambiguous** reroutes ORDA’s delivery confirmation to the wrong carrier. In the OTG, the routelabeled path may flip from NORTH to an undefined or SOUTH branch, and routebased filters (common in downstream dashboards) could exclude the event entirely, distorting deliverytime analytics.

3. **Adding INV44 bills ORDB without explicit E2O order linkage** inflates the invoice’s order scope. The OTG then shows a single Invoice node feeding two Order tokens that were originally separate, artificially reducing the number of identified order variants and masking the true billing structure (perorder vs. consolidated).

4. **Assigning PAY2  ORDB based solely on amount congruence (130 vs declared100)** introduces a paymentorder association that does not reflect the actual business process. Downstream chargeback or allocation reports may incorrectly attribute the 130€ payment to ORDB, creating phantom reconciliation entries and skewing financialprocess metrics.

5. **Eliminating all unresolved relations to force complete lifecycles** introduces phantom events (e.g., a Confirm Delivery for ORDB that never occurred). When such edges are added, the OCDFG may develop spurious selfloops or parallel splits, inflating fitness scores in processmining evaluation while sacrificing precision; the resulting model appears to fit the log perfectly but captures none of the original uncertainty, leading to overconfident, misleading process analyses.
