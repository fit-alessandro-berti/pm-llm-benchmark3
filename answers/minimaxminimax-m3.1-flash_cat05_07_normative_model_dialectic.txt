### 1.4 Burden of proof

**For declaring NONCOMPLIANT against a case (or an operator):** the reviewer must establish **all five** elements, and failure of any one mandates a different classification:

1. **Instrument and authority** — a rule in force at the moment of the act, with an identified issuer competent to bind the relevant population (CS-9 for the base rules; PD-3/FD-7 only for their own scoped populations and windows).
2. **Applicability** — the case's facts satisfy the instrument's scope predicates (risk class, jurisdiction, group membership, criticality, lifecycle scope, amount).
3. **Breach** — the event order or omission is established from evidence whose completeness and integrity are established (T1, or T2 with corroboration).
4. **Identity and quantification** — actors resolve to canonical persons; amounts and business days are computed deterministically.
5. **Absence of valid exception** — no overlay was in force and applicable at the act's timestamp.

If (3) or (4) fails on evidence quality, the correct output is **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, not a lenient pass. If (1) or (2) fails because the rule itself does not reach the case, the correct output is **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**.

**For accepting an exception:** the exception must be (a) identified by instrument and version; (b) **in force at the timestamp of the act**; (c) applicable by its own predicates; (d) have **all** its conditions satisfied with contemporaneous evidence; (e) traceable to a named accountable authority; (f) discharged (retroactive obligations completed on time). **Factual persistence, operational belief, normalization, or good outcomes do not satisfy (b).** This asymmetry is deliberate: authority is a documentary fact, and T3 assertions cannot manufacture it.

**Standard of proof:** preponderance for case classification; a documented-evidence threshold (the instrument's own text plus T1 records) for any enforcement or severity escalation; a presumption of regularity for controlled documents and system-generated records, rebuttable by T1 contrary evidence (e.g., the alias resolution in SUP-712 rebuts the presumption of separation of duties).

- **Every pilot and emergency case is invisible to M0.** A conformance metric keyed to M0 would score 312 + emergency + 6 August cases as "not in model" rather than as "breach," and would report a *stable* deviation rate while the risk profile of the operation was changing materially. This is why the deviation rate the operation cites is not a compliance rate in either direction.
- **M0 masks the case that matters most for integrity.** SUP-712 conforms to M0 *exactly* and violates CS-9's segregation-of-duties rule: requester and approver resolve to the same human through two aliases. No amount of M0 conformance could have detected it. SUP-715 likewise conforms to M0 while an undisclosed close-relationship sits in the approval role. If the control framework's only assurance mechanism is structural conformance, then structural conformance is measuring the wrong thing.
- **The pilot's own conditions were never audited against.** There is no evidence in the file that "automated master-data validation before activation" (PD-3) was executed for any case, and no evidence of daily monitoring under PD-3. A pilot whose conditions cannot be evidenced is, as a control, an assertion.

### 2.4 Two cases are genuine control defeat, not logging noise

- **SUP-708**: HIGH-risk supplier activated **40 minutes before** the sanctions check completed, with no pilot and no emergency available. Sanctions-before-activation is the one rule that *every* instrument in the file preserves (CS-9, PD-3, FD-7). It is the rule whose failure allows an unscreened party to transact. Internal Audit independently flagged it. No transactions occurred before clearance — this is a genuine mitigant and the prosecution concedes it — but the gate was open.
- **SUP-703**: as above. Activation without manual approval and without pre-activation quality review for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, under an authority that did not apply to it.

These two are qualitatively different from SUP-706 (a missing notification row in a migrated pipeline), SUP-711 (a duplicated register row), or SUP-713 (a renamed label). The operation's framing — "the deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope" — is true of the denominator and false of the numerator.

### 2.5 Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy

- **SUP-707** (Request_More_Info ×2, no activation before evidence arrived) is good practice. It is also, in M0, an **unmodelled deviation**. Compliance itself agrees the behaviour is desirable (F-06) — which proves the point in the prosecution's favour: the correct institutional response is a *model and policy change*, and it has not been made. A control framework that is not maintained does not thereby lose the ability to enforce.
- **SUP-713**: the content was equivalent, but the mapping was approved **one week after** the case. Retroactive mapping approval is a governance weakness and a precedent — it makes post-hoc taxonomy repair a normal way of manufacturing conformance. It cannot be allowed to become the standing method.
- **SUP-714** is the clearest illustration of the prosecution's structural point: the pilot **normalized into routine practice** (six cases in the first week of August), and normalization converted a monitored exception into de facto policy. If the absence of an exception instrument does not stop the practice, the exception regime has no content. This is the well-documented failure mode of time-boxed pilots, and it is what Section 6's governance layer must prevent.

## 3. Defense Brief

### 3.1 The measurement claim: "M0 conformance" was never a compliance test, so its failure rate proves little

M0 is a **single-route skeleton**. It has no representation of the pilot, the emergency, the rework loop, the pre-screen path, reactivation, segregation of duties, or amounts. A reviewer who computes "M0 deviation rate" is computing a **category-mixed statistic**: authorized overlays (SUP-702, SUP-704), scope artifacts (SUP-709, SUP-710), instrumentation artifacts (SUP-706, SUP-711), taxonomy drift (SUP-713), control-enhancing practices (SUP-707), and true breaches (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-714, SUP-712) all collapse into one number. The operation's argument is not that the number is small; it is that **the number is not a measure of the thing the prosecution wants it to measure.** Attributing a 27-point "deviation" to operators on the basis of a model that could not represent their lawful work is not enforcement, it is category error.

### 3.2 On the pilot: a real governance act with tight, monitored conditions

PD-3 was recorded (minutes + email), dated, time-boxed to exactly two months, population-bounded (LOW risk, DE, approved corporate group), conditioned on *additional* controls (master-data validation before activation; quality within two business days), and monitored by a named Compliance observer who recorded her position contemporaneously. The observer's later statement ("I had not approved weakening CS-9") is best read as a **permanent-amendment** disclaimer, not a contemporaneous denial of pilot authority: the pilot was a *monitored exception*, which is precisely the instrument a mature control framework needs to test a change before codifying it. A framework that forbids testing is a framework that freezes. The defense further notes that the observer's note is a **T1/T2 document created during the pilot window**, whereas her later statement is a retrospective clarification (T3) with no date attached in the file — the prosecution cannot have the contemporaneous document establish authority and the undated recollection defeat it.

Substantively, the eligible cases show the pilot's risk objective was **achieved**: sanctions before activation, quality review the next business day, low-risk approved-group counterparties, 289/312 = **92.6% apparently eligible**. A regime that delivered its controls for 92.6% of its users and produced a 4.5% ineligible rate is a *successful controlled experiment with a residual defect*, not a bypass program. It is also inconsistent with any theory of deliberate circumvention: a management team intent on evasion would not produce (and publish) an eligibility breakdown that identifies its own violators.

### 3.3 On the emergency: FD-7 is a model compensating-control design

FD-7 is precisely the artifact a control framework should contain in the face of a flood: it **preserves** the sanctions gate before activation (the irreducible risk control), **bounds** the deviation (provisional activation allowed before quality and approval), **caps** exposure (EUR 20,000), **monitors** (daily transaction monitoring), and **discharges retroactively** (quality review within two business days). SUP-704 satisfies every one of those conditions: sanctions first, EUR 18,000 initial spend below the cap, daily monitoring present, quality and retrospective approval the next business day. It is also worth noting a legal point the prosecution's framing obscures: **obligations triggered inside FD-7's window survive its expiry**. FD-7's retrospective-review duty was triggered by a 2026-07-11 activation and was properly discharged on Monday 2026-07-13 — after the directive expired, but lawfully and on time. **That is the precise line on which SUP-704 and SUP-705 differ**, and the defense accepts the line is a real one (see §5).

### 3.4 The logging facts are migration facts, and the defense asks the prosecution to apply its own standard consistently

- **SUP-706**: Notify_Requester moved to a CRM service on 2026-07-01 (F-05). Missingness moved 2%  29% across exactly that boundary. SUP-706's CRM message exists at **+3 hours** — consistent with the *control* being performed (SUP-702 shows +2 hours with a stable `notification_id`). What is missing is a **link**, not a notification. The prosecution's implicit inference — that a missing notification event means the requester was not notified — assumes the pre-migration log is the correct instrument of proof after the migration, which is exactly the assumption the documentation change falsifies.
- **SUP-711**: the same source transaction ID on two rows is a *proof* of duplication, not a proof of double registration. Concluding otherwise would treat an idempotency-key collision as a control breach.
- **SUP-713**: the process did the same work under a renamed label, and the audit documents confirm equivalent content. A conformance checker keyed on a hard-coded string would flag this as a false positive — a defect in the *checker*, not the process. The absence of a taxonomy version field or synonym registry is a **model defect** the prosecution should be pressing the model owner to fix, not a ground for enforcement against an operator.
- **SUP-710**: CS-9 has **no reactivation procedure**. The substantive controls that *do* exist were performed (sanctions before activation, approval before activation, notification, closure), and the prior certification remained valid. Holding a case to a Quality_Review in a context where the standard neither requires a reactivation event nor states that certification may not be reused is to invent a rule and apply it retrospectively.

### 3.5 Scope: the two cases the prosecution needs are the two cases CS-9 does not reach

- **SUP-709**: CS-9 *permits* an abbreviated pre-screen process for rejected leads and then is silent on whether such leads are M0 cases (F-02, D-05). The observed behaviour — sanctions screen fails, case rejected, requester notified, closed — is the *economical* reading of the standard: refusing to spend a quality review on a party that failed sanctions is risk *reduction*, not risk creation. It is the mirror image of SUP-708. To convict SUP-709, the prosecution must first resolve D-05 against the plaintiff.
- **SUP-712** is the strongest demonstration that M0's silence is not neutrality: the case conforms to M0 perfectly and breaches CS-9. The prosecution wants to convict; but the *system* that failed here is the model's inability to encode a resource rule and the absence of an actor-identity model (D-10). Blaming the operator for a rule the framework never operationalized is not deterrence, it is scapegoating a surface.
- **SUP-715**: the reviewer and approver are **two different humans**; CS-9's stated requirement is satisfied. The failure is the absence of a *disclosure* record under a CoI policy — an organizational control that appears in no instrument and in no model. The gap is a rule-making gap.

### 3.6 Trace deviation is not business misconduct, and the two sides must be drawn at the right line

The file contains **zero** evidence of concealment, falsification, or benefit-taking. Two cases (703, 708) show real control defeat; the remainder are either authorized overlays, artifacts, or improvements. The defense does not ask the reviewer to dismiss 703 and 708 — it asks that they not be used to characterize a population in which 47 rework cases are *stricter* than the model, 289 pilot cases are eligible, and 97% of pilot uses are within design. A finding of "materially noncompliant" would require either a pattern of intentional circumvention (not evidenced) or a failure rate that survives stratification (not demonstrated). A defensible review must, at minimum, stratify before it generalizes.

**Where MIXED is used, it is not a hedge.** In SUP-705 the two limbs are different in kind: a documentary failure (no instrument) and a factual-mitigation (the emergency plausibly persisted). The classification is driven by the documentary limb; the factual limb moves severity only. In SUP-715 the two limbs are different in kind: a satisfied rule and an unsatisfied unstated duty. The classification is driven by the rule gap; the disclosure failure is recorded as the substantive concern.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance — partial compliance with a governance defect, not "materially noncompliant."**

Across the fifteen sampled cases, the irreducible gate (sanctions before activation) held in 14 of 15 and failed once (SUP-708). The approval-before-activation gate failed in five cases: two authorized (SUP-702, SUP-704), two unauthorized (SUP-703 ineligible; SUP-714 post-expiry), and one authority-lapsed (SUP-705). Three cases (SUP-706, SUP-711, SUP-713) are artifacts of the data pipeline and taxonomy, not control failures, and one (SUP-707) is a control improvement the model cannot represent. The aggregate is consistent with this reading: 289/312 (92.6%) of pilot Auto_Approve uses appear eligible; 14 clearly ineligible; 9 unevaluable; 6 post-expiry uses. So the operation ran a bounded exception regime with a **4.5% misuse rate and a 2.9% unverifiable rate**, and then let the expired regime survive into August.

Three system-level findings are nonetheless adverse to the operation, and they are the reason the aggregate verdict is not "compliant":

1. **Exception self-certification.** Eligibility was asserted by the actor, not enforced by the system. 23/312 cases are the direct evidence. This is a design defect with predictable recurrence.
2. **Expiry non-enforcement.** Both overlays expired by their own terms and usage continued (SUP-705, SUP-714, six August cases). Time-boxed exceptions without machine-enforced stop conditions decay into policy.
3. **Non-control-flow rules are ungoverned.** Segregation of duties (SUP-712) is stated in CS-9 but unmodelled and unenforced; CoI disclosure (SUP-715) exists only as a policy expectation; pilot conditions (master-data validation, D-03) are unevidenced.

**Model validity — M0 is invalid as a compliance specification and valid as a standard-route skeleton.**

M0 cannot express four regimes that were legally operative (standard, pilot, emergency, and the lead/reactivation lifecycle variants), one required control mechanism (rework), and four rule classes (resource identity, amount caps, time windows, authority). Any conformance statistic computed against M0 across 2026-06-01  2026-08-31 is therefore uninterpretable. M0's own merits are real and should be preserved: its parallel checks block correctly enforces *both* checks before activation, and its single XOR correctly models approve-and-activate versus reject. The correct remedy is **not** to enforce M0 harder but to supersede it with M1 plus an overlay/data layer, and to re-baseline metrics with a disclosed stratification (standard route / overlay-authorized / scope-defined / data-artifact / breach). Historic conformance rates must not be "repaired" retrospectively — SUP-713 shows exactly how retroactive repair manufactures false assurance.

### 5.3 Where I reject each side

**Rejected prosecution arguments:**

- **That the M0 deviation rate evidences materiality.** Rejected. M0 cannot represent four operative regimes, so its deviation rate is a category-mixed statistic. Materiality must be assessed on a stratified basis.
- **That PD-3 was void ab initio.** Rejected. A time-boxed, population-bounded, monitored exception recorded by a steering committee with contemporaneous observer attestation is a legitimate governance instrument; the observer's disclaimer is best read as declining to endorse *permanent* amendment. If the rule were that only CS-9 amendments can suspend CS-9, then PD-3 could never have run lawfully and the organization would have had no lawful route to test the change it wanted — the prosecution's construction produces an unintended and undesirable consequence.
- **That the 29% notification-missingness indicates notification failure.** Rejected. The missingness change coincides exactly with a documented migration; SUP-702 demonstrates a contemporaneous CRM notification with a stable ID. Charging 27 percentage points of control failure against a pipeline migration is the same species of error as charging SUP-711's duplicate row as a control breach.
- **That SUP-706 and SUP-711 are operator defects.** Rejected; both are provable or highly probable data artifacts.
- **That SUP-709 and SUP-710 are compliance cases at all.** Rejected as a matter of text: CS-9 permits an abbreviated pre-screen and defines no reactivation procedure. These are drafting defects in the standard.

**Rejected defense arguments:**

- **That factual persistence of an emergency substitutes for authority.** Rejected. SUP-705 activated on 2026-07-13 with no live instrument. A directive that the drafters gave an expiry date to was designed to bound itself; "the situation was still bad" is the classic argument for renewal, and the organization chose not to renew.
- **That expected or imminent approval legitimizes post-expiry conduct.** Rejected. SUP-714 and the six August cases had no instrument; if belief could authorize, no exception could ever expire, and the exception-regime concept collapses.
- **That SUP-703 is "only a trace deviation."** Rejected. The ineligibility is a matter of the case's own facts; the activation occurred without approval and without pre-activation quality review; this is control defeat regardless of the label the operator selected.
- **That SUP-712 is excusable because M0 cannot encode it.** Rejected as an excuse (it remains a CS-9 breach) and accepted as an explanation of *why* the breach survived undetected — which is a system finding, not a defense to the case.
- **That M0's blind spots excuse the operation generally.** Rejected. Two models can both be wrong: M0 can be inadequate *and* the operation can be non-compliant. Section 6 accepts the model's defects; Section 5.1 refuses to let them cancel the case findings.
- **That "good outcomes" and Compliance's own agreement with rework practice settle the matter.** Rejected as a legal argument (outcomes are evidence of value, not of authority), while *accepted* as the strongest justification for the model revision in Section 6.

**What would change the system-level verdict:** evidence that master-data validation never executed for any pilot case (would move the 289 eligible cases to indeterminate); evidence that the 9 missing-attribute cases are predominantly ineligible; discovery of Auto_Approve usage after 2026-08-02 beyond the six cases; evidence of concealment or retrospective editing of pilot classification fields; or a server-side timestamp showing SUP-708's sanctions check completed before activation.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 6.1 Layer map — what belongs where, and why

| Layer | Content | Enforced by | Why not the layer above/below |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 — Policy hierarchy & authority** | CS-9 as the single controlled standard; numbered overlays (pilots, emergencies) with issuer, scope predicate, effective window, conditions, expiry behaviour, owner; explicit amendment protocol; "no tacit continuation" rule | Organizational controls (change control board, issue registers, quarterly attestation of expired-overlay usage = 0) | POWL cannot express *authority*; a chart cannot create it |
| **L1 — Control-flow (POWL M1)** | Four scope paths (onboarding, emergency, lead, reactivation); parallel sanctions  quality; approval gate with manual / auto / reject; rework gate; notification before closure | PM4Py POWL (Section 7) + conformance checking on a rework-insensitive projection | Data conditions (risk, amount, date) are **not** representable in POWL; they belong to L3 |
| **L2 — Global log constraints (DECLARE-style)** | Existence, uniqueness, mutual exclusion, coexistence, response, and *global ordering* constraints over a single case trace — the things local POWL matching misses | Log-skeleton/DECLARE evaluator over the event table | DECLARE works per trace; cross-case aggregates (e.g., "zero Auto_Approve after expiry") need L3/L4 |
| **L3 — Data-aware rules (pandas/SQL)** | Eligibility joins (risk class, jurisdiction, approved-group membership + validity dates, master-data validation result), amount caps, business-day arithmetic, authority-window tests, alias resolution, duplication detection, completeness/mirroring checks, evidence-link quality scoring | pandas/SQL rule engine, versioned, producing per-case × per-rule verdicts | These are predicates over records, not over control flow |
| **L4 — Resource, identity & conflict of interest** | Canonical actor identity graph with alias table; requester  approver; delegation register with scope/expiry; relationship/CoI declarations; reporting-line adjacency | L3 enforcement over L4 reference data + organizational attestation and training | Cannot be encoded in POWL; requires an authoritative actor master |
| **L5 — Monitoring, analytics & organizational controls** | Exception register; pilot/emergency review cadence; stratifed conformance dashboard; escalation paths; RACI; training; immutable audit storage; independent audit sampling | Organizational | — |

### 6.2 Revised control-flow model (M1) — narrative

**Scope paths (L1).** The top level is an exclusive choice among four paths, so that *scope becomes visible in the log* rather than inferred from a missing step: `Register_Supplier` (onboarding), `Register_Lead` (abbreviated pre-screen, ending in `Reject_Lead_Pre_Screen`), `Reactivate_Supplier` (with `Certification_Currency_Check`), and the standard onboarding path. This directly cures the SUP-709/SUP-710 ambiguity: a case that *lacks* Quality_Review is not "non-compliant," it is on a *different, explicitly named path*.

**Standard route.** `Register_Supplier  (Sanctions_Check  Quality_Review)  [rework gate: 0–2 × Request_More_Info]  XOR{Manual_Procurement_ApprovalActivate_Supplier | Auto_ApproveActivate_Supplier | Reject_Supplier}  Notify_Requester  Close_Case`. Concurrency of the two checks is preserved; the approval gate carries both the manual and the low-risk auto branches as *distinct activity labels*, so the exception is visible in the trace rather than inferred from a branch identity.

**Emergency route.** `Register_Supplier  Sanctions_Check  Emergency_Authority_Verified  Provisional_Activate_Supplier  (Emergency_Quality_Review  Retrospective_Procurement_Approval)  XOR{Notify_RequesterClose_Case | Revoke_ActivationNotify_RequesterClose_Case}`. Sanctions remain a hard pre-activation gate; quality and retrospective approval are post-activation but must complete within two business days (L3); the EUR 20,000 cap and daily monitoring are L3 obligations, with `Emergency_Authority_Verified` recording the authority ID and the monitoring attestation.

**Rework.** Two chained optional gates give a bounded 0–2 episode approximation of a zero-or-more loop. **Limitation:** PM4Py's POWL operator set as deployed does not support a guaranteed-iteration construct, and StrictPartialOrder is acyclic, so true unbounded repetition cannot be expressed in a single POWL. The defensible compensation is (i) evaluating conformance on the **rework-insensitive projection** () =  \ {Request_More_Info}, and (ii) enforcing closure-before-approval in L3 (PROW-06), which is the invariant that actually matters — SUP-707 shows no activation preceded evidence.

### 6.4 Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (L4)

- A **canonical actor registry** is authoritative: `actor_id` (HR/payroll key), `aliases[]` (former names, initials, email aliases, delegated identities), `employer_id`, `reporting_line_id`. All events carry `actor_id` **and** the alias used, so SUP-712 is caught at ingest by PROW-08 rather than at audit.
- **Segregation of duties** (CS-9) is encoded as a rule with a delegation carve-out that *requires* a delegation record (PROW-09) — the carve-out must be evidenced, never assumed.
- **Conflict of interest** is promoted from an unwritten expectation to a rule: declaration thresholds (same reporting line, close relationship, common vendor interest), a **CoI register**, disclosure recorded **before** the approval event, and an attestation that a reviewing officer is not conflicted.
- **Delegation** is a first-class object: scope, expiry, delegator/delegated canonical ids, evidence, and revocation.

### 6.5 Policy-overlay governance (L0)

**Normalized overlay table (authoritative form):** `overlay_id | instrument_type | issuer | authority_evidence | scope_predicate | effective_from | effective_to | conditions | monitoring | expiry_behavior | renewal_rule | evidence_required | owner | status`.

**Governance rules the file's failures point directly at:**

1. **Machine-enforced stop.** An overlay's `effective_to` must be a hard gate in the rule engine (PROW-13), not a date in a document. SUP-705 and SUP-714 are what happens without it.
2. **No tacit continuation / explicit renewal only.** Renewal requires a **new numbered instrument**; it is never effected by operational belief (SUP-714) or factual persistence (SUP-705). The fact that FD-7 was drafted to expire is evidence that this rule was intended.
3. **Population boundedness is machine-checked.** Pilot eligibility is evaluated by the engine, not asserted by the operator (PROW-04). Self-certification is the root cause of SUP-703 and of the 9 unverifiable cases.
4. **Conditions must be auditable.** Every overlay condition must name the log or ledger that evidences it. "Master-data validation" must have a run record; "daily monitoring" must have dated records. An unevidenced condition is a drafting defect.
5. **Conditions must be *tested*, not merely *stated*.** PD-3's two-business-day quality allowance worked (SUP-702: next business day). FD-7's monitoring and cap worked (SUP-704). The controls that were measurable were respected.
6. **Amendment linkage.** Any permanent low-risk auto-approval branch requires a **CS-9 amendment with a version and effective date** before use; until then, Auto_Approve usage outside a live overlay is a rule breach (PROW-13/PROW-04) — exactly SUP-714 and the six August cases.
7. **Retrospective ratification is prohibited as a conformance method.** SUP-713's mapping was approved a week late; the fix is a **forward-dated taxonomy version** plus an evidence-based mapping record, not retroactive approval of history.

### 6.6 Explicit scope rules

- **Rejected leads (SUP-709):** CS-9 §Pre-screen — "leads registered under `Register_Lead` and failing sanctions pre-screen follow the abbreviated path (Sanctions pre-screen  Reject_Lead_Pre_Screen  Notify_Requester  Close_Case); such cases are **out of scope** of the onboarding control set. A case that has passed pre-screen but not yet been registered as an onboarding is **in scope**, and the transition must be an explicit, logged `Register_Supplier`." Every case must carry exactly one scope tag (PROW-10).
- **Reactivations (SUP-710):** CS-9 §Reactivation — "reactivation is governed by the reactivation path: sanctions check and certification-currency check before activation; certification in force at activation date is required; reactivation does not require a new quality review **iff** the certification is current and the prior scope is unchanged; otherwise a full quality review is required."
- **Withdrawals:** currently absent from both CS-9 and M0. Add `Withdraw_Request  Withdraw_Decision  Close_Case` (with requester notification), since a withdrawn case must not be classified as either activated or rejected, and must be excluded from activation metrics while included in cycle-time metrics.

### 6.7 Event and data-collection proposal

**Event table (append-only; no updates, no deletes).** Required fields: `event_id`, `case_id`, `activity_canonical`, `activity_raw`, `taxonomy_version`, `event_ts_utc` (server-side, immutable), `recorded_ts_utc` (append timestamp, immutable), `source_system`, `source_transaction_id` (idempotency key), `actor_id` (canonical), `actor_alias_used`, `actor_role`, `delegation_id`, `authority_id` (overlay/rule), `rule_version`, `scope_tag`, `eligibility_snapshot_id` (immutable JSON: risk class, jurisdiction, group id + validity, master-data validation result at activation), `amount_value`, `amount_currency`, `cap_applied`, `notification_id`, `rework_episode_id`, `evidence_refs[]`, `prev_hash`/`event_hash` (tamper-evident chain).

**Supporting tables.** `authority_register` (overlay_id, type, issuer, issue_ts, expiry_ts, scope_predicate, conditions, status, version); `actor_registry` + `actor_alias` + `delegation_register` + `coi_register`; `eligibility_snapshot`; `notification_outbox` (stable `notification_id`, case linkage enforced by a foreign key rather than free text, mirroring status, delivery receipt); `spend_ledger`; `monitoring_log` (daily rows); `rule_evaluation` (case_id × rule_id × rule_version  verdict, evidence pointers, evaluator version, timestamp, reviewer); `exception_register`.

**Two corrections to current practice, both driven by this file:** (i) notification linkage must be a **foreign key to a stable `notification_id`**, never a mutable text field (SUP-706); (ii) **immutable effective and recorded timestamps** are mandatory, so that expiry, business-day arithmetic, and "which rule version was in force" are answerable from records rather than from recollection (SUP-705, SUP-714).

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Limitations stated explicitly (not hidden in the code)

- **L1 — Data conditions are not expressible in POWL.** The `Auto_Approve` branch is *unconditional* in the model; its guard (PROW-04) lives in the rule engine and the authority register. A POWL-conformant trace with `Auto_Approve` is **not** evidence of authorized auto-approval.
- **L2 — Scope is expressed as disjoint branches.** The top-level XOR admits onboarding, emergency, lead and reactivation paths; which one applies is determined by the entry activity label plus the scope tag (PROW-10), not by the model.
- **L3 — Unbounded loops are not expressible.** The rework gate is a **bounded 0–2 episode approximation** using XOR + SilentTransition (semantics unambiguous across PM4Py versions). True zero-or-more repetition would require an iteration operator not reliably supported by PM4Py's POWL planner/playout, or a cyclic structure that `StrictPartialOrder` forbids. Conformance is therefore evaluated on the rework-insensitive projection, with closure enforced by PROW-06.
- **L4 — Label identity, not object identity, defines conformance.** The same label appears in different branches via distinct objects; `SilentTransition` instances are invisible.
- **L5 — Model choice cannot express resource, amount, date, identity or authority rules.** Those require L2/L3 of Section 6.

### 7.2 Executable model + normalized overlay table (single block; no external file reads)

M1 — Revised supplier-onboarding normative model + normalized policy-overlay table.
PM4Py >= 2.7 (pm4py.objects.powl). No external file I/O.

Design notes (see prose for full rationale):
  L1 Data conditions (risk, amount, date, identity) are NOT expressible in POWL.
     The Auto_Approve branch is unconditional; its guard is rule PROW-04.
  L2 Scope is made visible as four disjoint paths in the top-level XOR.
  L3 Unbounded rework is approximated by a bounded 0..2 episode XOR/Silent gate.
     True iteration is unavailable in PM4Py's POWL operator set as deployed, and
     StrictPartialOrder is acyclic. Compensations: conformance is evaluated on the
     rework-insensitive projection (drop Request_More_Info) and closure-before-
     approval is enforced by rule PROW-06 in the data layer.
  L4 Conformance is keyed on activity LABELS, not on object identity.
  L5 SilentTransition instances are invisible in the log.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition

L_ACT       = "Activate_Supplier"

L_NOTIFY    = "Notify_Requester"
L_CLOSE     = "Close_Case"

def T(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def SIL():
    return SilentTransition()


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- rework
def rework_gate(episodes=2):
    """Optional rework episodes before the approval gate.

    Bounded approximation (L3): each gate is XOR{ Request_More_Info -> silent ,
    silent }. Chaining `episodes` gates yields 0..episodes rework episodes,
    all of which must precede approval. Repeatability beyond the bound is
    enforced by PROW-06 (no approval while a rework episode is open).
    """
    gates = []
    for _ in range(episodes):
        act = T(L_RMI)
        end = SIL()
        body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[act, end])
        body.order.add_edge(act, end)
        gates.append(OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[body, SIL()]))
    g = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=gates)
    for a, b in zip(gates, gates[1:]):
        g.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return g

emg_post = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[branch_ok, branch_revoke])

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reg_e, sanc_e, auth_e, prov_e, retro, emg_post]
)
for a, b in [(reg_e, sanc_e), (sanc_e, auth_e), (auth_e, prov_e),
             (prov_e, retro), (retro, emg_post)]:
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(a, b)


# ---------------------------------------------------------- lead (pre-screen)
reg_l  = T(L_REG_LEAD)                # distinct label makes scope visible (L2)
scr_l  = T(L_LEAD_SCR)
rej_l  = T(L_REJ_LEAD)
not_l, clo_l = T(L_NOTIFY), T(L_CLOSE)
lead_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg_l, scr_l, rej_l, not_l, clo_l])
for a, b in [(reg_l, scr_l), (scr_l, rej_l), (rej_l, not_l), (not_l, clo_l)]:
    lead_route.order.add_edge(a, b)


# ---------------------------------------------------------- reactivation
rea   = T(L_REACT)
sanc_r = T(L_SANC)

man_r = T(L_MANUAL)
act_r = T(L_ACT)
branch_react = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[man_r, act_r])

reactivation_route = StrictPartialOrder(

)

    reactivation_route.order.add_edge(a, b)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------- M1

# --------------------------------------------------- inspection utilities
def leaves(node, acc=None):
    """Collect activity labels (silent transitions shown as <silent>)."""
    if acc is None:
        acc = []
    if isinstance(node, Transition):                    # SilentTransition is a Transition
        acc.append(node.label if node.label else "<silent>")
    elif isinstance(node, StrictPartialOrder):
        for n in node.nodes:
            leaves(n, acc)
    elif isinstance(node, OperatorPOWL):
        for c in node.children:
            leaves(c, acc)
    return acc

### 7.3 What the proposal changes, and what it does not

**It does not weaken any substantive control.** Sanctions-before-activation remains a hard gate on every path, including the emergency path. Quality-before-activation and approval-before-activation remain the default, with deferral permitted only under a numbered, dated, machine-stopped authority. Notification-before-closure is preserved on all four paths, and a revocation path is added to the emergency route.

**It converts three assertion-based controls into evidence-based controls:** who may use auto-approval (PROW-04 + authority register instead of operator selection); when an overlay stops (PROW-13 auto-stop instead of a date in a PDF); and who may approve (canonical actor identity instead of alias-ambiguous names).

**It separates the three things the current file conflates:** *what happened* (L1/L2 control flow), *whether it was permitted* (L3 rules + L0 authority), and *who did it and under what conflicts* (L4). The current single M0 conformance rate cannot distinguish them, which is why both sides in this dispute could cite the same statistics and reach opposite conclusions.

**It leaves two genuine breaches unexcused** — SUP-703 and SUP-708 would fail under the revised architecture exactly as they fail under CS-9 — while correctly excusing the four artifact cases (SUP-706, SUP-711, SUP-713, and the pilot-usage element of SUP-702/714 timing) and correctly reclassifying the three scope/loop cases (SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710) as model obligations rather than operator obligations. That asymmetry is the test of whether a revised architecture is an honest improvement or a device for excusing everything: it must still convict the cases that deserve conviction, and it must change the outcome for those that do not.
