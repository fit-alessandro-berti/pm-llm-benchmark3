Closed 19:00-07:00 is a deterministic server vacation: no arrivals counted, no completions counted, no service occurs, but uncompleted cases **carry over overnight**. `mean_wip` is therefore a time-average of `L(t)` = cases in system sampled every 15 min (48 snapshots per open day). It includes both queued and in-service cases.

**Estimands:**

* Demand intensity $\lambda$ [cases h$^{-1}$ open]: `arrivals / 12`. Counts are per open window, not calendar 24h.
* Departure/throughput intensity $X$ [cases h$^{-1}$ open]: `completed / 12`. `completed` need not have arrived same day; $X\neq\lambda$ momentarily if boundaries move.
* Capacity `c(t)` [FTE]: `staff_fte` = mean concurrent servers proxy. Service rate per server $\mu = 1/E[S]$ unknown.
* In-service time $S$ [min active handling, excluding waiting]: only `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` observed, not $E[S]$. **Do not substitute median for mean.** Distribution shape and right-tail matter for load.
* Flow/sojourn time $W$ [open-hours + wait]: including queue wait + $S$ + any overnight carry-over. Not observed. Inferable only via Little's Law under strict conditions.
* Backlog/state: opening backlog at 07:00 not supplied for every day.
* Mix: `complex_pct` = % arrivals classified as multi-step complex variant. Change may be real mix shift, coding change, or both.
* Intraday concentration: `peak_08_10_pct` = % arrivals 08:00-09:59. Hourly bins 07-08 ... 18-19 for representative vectors.
* Outage: `outage_min` = recorded downtime inside open window.

**Assumptions required:**

* **A1 Open-window normalization:** Arrival/completion intensities divided by 12h assumes uniform exposure within window unless using hourly vectors. Closed periods contribute zero throughput but increase calendar $W$.
* **A2 Snapshot proxy:** `mean_wip`  $\bar L$ over open interval, assuming 15-min snapshots unbiased for $L(t)$. Misses intra-interval transients <15 min.
* **A3 Classification stability:** Treating `complex_pct` as behavioral driver is hypothesis H-complex-real vs H-coding-artifact.
* **A4 No abandonment/loss:** Not logged; assumed negligible or folded into throughput gap. If abandonment exists, $L=\lambda_{eff} W$ needed.

**Limitations of aggregates:**

1. No event-level inter-arrival / service-start / service-end timestamps. Cannot estimate $C_a^2$, $C_s^2$, queue discipline, re-entrancy.
2. $E[S]$ and $Var(S)$ not identified from median+p90 alone.
3. Backlog stock at each morning/evening not given  $L$ endpoints unknown  Little's Law boundary condition unverifiable.
4. Daily row is already time-averaged; intraday burstiness, autocorrelated arrivals, and time-varying $c(t)$ collapsed.
5. `staff_fte` is mean concurrent; variance of staffing within day (breaks, shifts) invisible.

## Threats to naive inference (5)

* **T1 Boundary & carry-over:** $A(t)-D(t)=B(t)$. With overnight storage, daily `arrivalscompleted` does not imply $W$ small. Comparing daily rates ignores $B$. Pooled $L/\lambda$ conflates open-hours and calendar time.
* **T2 Non-stationarity & structural breaks:** Sequence from 96/d to 121/d arrivals, `complex_pct` 18%47%, service median 2436 min. Daily points are serially dependent; i.i.d. tests, simple means, pooled regression invalid. Simulated standard errors under independence are understated.
* **T3 Aggregation & loss of burstiness:** Daily sums hide hourly over-dispersion. Two days with 100 arrivals can have Fano=3 vs Fano=9 hourly, implying vastly different peak staffing needs. Fluid daily model underestimates queue.
* **T4 Service vs sojourn confusion:** `median_service_min` is active processing only. Using it as $\hat W$ underestimates $W$ by waiting + overnight holds (often 3-5× larger). p90 drift signals tail inflation not captured by median.
* **T5 Endogeneity / reverse causality:** `staff_fte` raised to 7.0 on 04-16 and 04-29 *because* WIP exploded to 47-52. Correlation staffWIP positive pooled reverses within-regime meaning.
* **T6 Composition/Simpson:** Pooling weekday (108/d) and weekend (45/d) creates a 90/d phantom average representing neither. Pooling pre- and post-mix periods masks that higher `staff_fte` coincides with higher WIP after 04-20.
* **T7 Deterministic shock misread as productivity:** Outage 180 min on 04-15 forces $X$=58 (< 105) then rebound $X$=129 (> 112). Averaging treats recovery catch-up as sustainable high throughput regime.
* **T8 Internal inconsistency / reporting change:** Representative hourly vectors imply 08:00-09:59 share 16.8% on 04-07 and 9.6% on 04-21, vs table `peak_08_10_pct` 31% and 47% same dates. Indicates candidate vectors are illustrative or coding definition shifted; blind modeling of either as exact contaminates intensity estimates.

> Distinction: **Observations** = rows, bins supplied. **Estimates** = $\lambda,X,\bar L$, Fano/CV with sampling variation. **Bounds** = interval for $W$ given alternative throughput proxies. **Hypotheses** = mix-shift real vs artifact, intervention timing. **Recommendations** = staffing/data actions.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law: $\bar L = \bar\lambda_{eff}\times \bar W$ (and $L_q = \lambda W_q$) holds for *any* system if and only if observation window $[0,T]$ is long enough that boundary stocks are equal (or $T\to\infty$ with ergodic limits) and averages are taken over comparable time bases, regardless of distributions, disciplines (with conservation). Variants require throughput proxy equals long-run input rate ($\lambda_{eff}$) and $W$ counts same population over same clock as $\bar L$.

*Opening-hours subtlety:* $\bar L$ sampled only 07-19, $\lambda$ counted per open window. Then $\bar W_{little}= \bar L /\bar\lambda_{open}$ estimates **open-hours time in system**, excluding closed-night suspension. Calendar $\bar W_{cal}= \bar W_{open}+k\times12h$ where $k$=average number of overnights case spans. Without open/close backlog per-day we underestimate calendar SLA.

# 7. Operational implications

### Staffing

1. **Schedule to peaks, not daily mean.** Daily  9.5/h post-mix average masks 10-11 spike 34/h (Fano 9.28) and afternoon echo 24/h. Recommendation: split weekday FTE into core 5.5 covering 07-19 plus surge +1.5 FTE 09:30-11:00 & 13:00-14:30 overlap, or smooth upstream batch release causing 08-10 46% concentration. Pooled 5.1 FTE average leaves morning 1.6.

2. **Maintain reinforced weekday FTE 7.0 until WIP 22-24** (return to baseline upper quartile) not merely until daily $X>\lambda$ turns positive. Current 37 still 80% above normal; withdrawing 04-29 boost after 5-d trend risks immediate rebound (+13/d deficit resumes). Estimate need 7-8 additional days at 121/d to burn +90 excess cases (observed accumulation 13.4/d×794). Model as fluid drain: dB/dt = c  6/d under 7.0 FTE, so target 2 weeks.

3. **Separate weekend staffing rule.** Weekend demand 45/d vs weekday 108/d requires distinct standard (2.53.0 FTE already uplifted 05-02). Weekend $W$3.9h open longer than weekdays signaling lower priority or skill shortage.

### Backlog recovery triage

4. **Prioritize complex/long-tail cases differently during drain.** p90/median 2.37 tail indicates blocking. Use shortest-processing-time or split complex track to parallel team to reduce $C_s^2$ impact per Kingman. Record re-entrancy loops for complex variant to validate $S$ inflation.

5. **Declare formal recovery protocol after >60 min outage.** Recovery days showed effective throughput 10/h achievable (+22% vs normal) via overtime/extended focus. Codify not as permanent expectation but emergency mode with burnout guard; pair with interim arrival throttling (defer non-urgent).

### Peak smoothing / demand shaping

6. **Address 08-10 surge (30%46%)** via appointment windows, staggered recalls, or upstream system batch staggering seen 10-11 spike 34 vs 12 expected. Even smoothing 46%35% (redistribute ~12 cases over afternoon) cuts peak  from ~2.0 to ~1.3 at same headcount.

### Data-collection upgrades (process-mining readiness)

7. **Log backlog at 07:00 and 19:00 daily** by case type; needed for boundary correction of Little's Law and fluid models. Currently T1 threat blocks inference.

8. **Capture event log at case-event granularity** (case_id, variant, arrival_timestamp, queue_entry, service_start, service_end, staff_id, rework flag) instead of daily aggregates. This unlocks PM4Py discovery: variant mining to confirm `complex_pct` coding vs reality, dotted charts for burst visualization, performance spectrum.

9. **Store intraday staffing schedule per hour** not daily mean FTE; capacity pressure is peak-sensitive.

10. **Instrument abandonment/balk/loss** if exists; reconciles persistent arrivals>completions gap (net +110 over 28d vs flat closing WIP suggests 70-80 cases missing outcome – either hidden queue or losses).

### Policy that looks good pooled but risky segmented

* **“Cut 0.5 FTE weekday (6.05.5) saves cost, pooled utilization still 85% and average WIP only 30, within SLA.”** Pooled 7.50/h ÷ (5.5 FTE × assumed 1.5/h) 0.91 sustainable. Segmented reveals baseline already 8.35/h / (5.93×)  balanced but high-mix pushes service time 2535 min reducing effective  ~29%; new 1.13 at 5.5 FTE  backlog +13/d, $W$ +130 min, breach within 4 weekdays. Cost saving rapidly offset by overtime and SLA penalties.

* **“Incentivize maximizing $X$/FTE based on 04-16/17 and 04-29/30 benchmarks (18-20 cases/FTE/d) as KPI.”** Pooled view suggests those days are best practice to emulate. Segmented shows high $X$/FTE was catch-up consuming pre-built inventory, not sustainable creation; permanent chase raises error rate, tail p90 already +77% indicates quality tradeoff. KPI would reward under-capacitation then heroics.

**Decision framing:** Report KPIs regime-conditional (e.g., median $W_{open}$ by variant, peak , drain rate) with control charts stratified by weekday/weekend and pre/post-04-20, not single pooled SPC chart whose limits triggered only after 2-week delay.

def change_score(series):
    n=len(series); pooled_sd=series.std(ddof=0)
