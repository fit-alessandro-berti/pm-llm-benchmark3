# 1. Stress scenarios

Historical averages conceal the operational dynamics of this laboratory because volume, case mix, and resource-specific failure modes interact nonlinearly. Comparing the 8-week normal baseline (**18,400 cases; 2,300 cases/week**) against the 3-week peak log (**9,900 cases; 3,300 cases/week**) reveals a critical insight: **weekly Routine volume does not actually increase during peaks** (1,403 Routine cases/week in normal vs. 1,386/week in peak). Instead, the entire **+43.5% weekly volume surge (+1,000 cases/week)** is driven by a structural case-mix shift:
- **Urgent orders** surge **+131%** (from 414/week to 957/week; 18% to 29% of mix).
- **Complex-panel orders** surge **+121%** (from 299/week to 660/week; 13% to 20% of mix).
- **Repeat-after-failure orders** surge **+61%** (from 184/week to 297/week; 8% to 9% of mix).

Because Urgent cases fragment preparation and assay schedules, Complex-panel cases multiply downstream clinical validation load (3.1x Medical validation likelihood, 2.4x Specialist review likelihood), and Repeat-after-failure cases consume assay capacity twice, average-based capacity planning fails. Below are five plausible stress scenarios covering distinct demand-mix and disruption vectors.

---

### Scenario S1: Winter Infection-Wave Mix Shift (High Urgent & Complex-Panel Surge)
- **Demand & Mix Profile:** Total arrivals rise from 2,300/week to 3,300–3,500/week. Routine volume remains flat (~1,380–1,400/week), while respiratory/infection panels drive Urgent cases to 29–32% (~1,000/week) and Complex-panel orders to 20–22% (~700/week).
- **Disruption Mechanism:** The mix shift simultaneously attacks both ends of the schedule. Upstream, Urgent orders demand immediate `Batch wait` bypass at Preparation benches, fragmenting run setups. Downstream, the +121% to +135% surge in Complex-panel and Urgent orders causes weekly entries into `Medical validation` to jump **2.58x** (from 530/week [4,240/8 wks] to 1,367/week [4,100/3 wks]) and weekly entries into `Hold for specialist review` to jump **5.6x** (from 36/week [290/8 wks] to 203/week [610/3 wks]).
- **Primary SLA Threat:** Urgent median cycle time reaches 17h (p90 = 33h), meaning **nearly 50% of Urgent orders breach the 18h contractual penalty threshold**. Routine p90 reaches 120h (breaching the 72h target), and Complex-panel p90 reaches 210h.

### Scenario S2: Assay-Machine Degradation or Partial Outage (M1 Outage / M2 Reliability Drift)
- **Demand & Mix Profile:** Normal-to-moderate volume (2,400–2,800 cases/week) with standard or mildly elevated case mix (Urgent 20%, Complex-panel 15%), decoupling the shock from pure volume growth.
- **Disruption Mechanism:** Assay machine **M1** suffers an unplanned 24–48h outage (or calibration lock), or **M2** suffers optical/reagent degradation during night shifts. Because M2 has a faster runtime, operators route greater share of volume to M2 and extend night-shift runs to catch up. However, M2 has a much higher failure rate, and Repeat-after-failure cases cluster after night shifts and M2 usage.
- **Primary SLA Threat:** The per-run failure rate (`Assay run -> Repeat assay`) spikes from the normal **6.7%** (1,140 / 17,030 assay exits) to or beyond the peak **14.8%** (1,260 / 8,500 assay exits), and potentially **20–25%** under full M2 dependence. Each repeat loop adds **13h (normal) to 34h (peak)** (`Assay run -> Repeat assay` [3–8h] + `Repeat assay -> Assay run` [10–26h]) and consumes assay and preparation capacity a second time, creating a self-reinforcing capacity spiral that pushes Repeat-after-failure p90 toward 240h and starves Urgent runs.

### Scenario S3: Downstream Clinical Validation Shock (Alternate-Friday Specialist Absence + Medical Validator Shortage)
- **Demand & Mix Profile:** Moderate-to-high Complex-panel demand (16–20% of mix) coinciding with the scheduled **every-second-Friday absence** of one Specialist reviewer and an unplanned 2–3 day illness or leave in the Medical validator pool.
- **Disruption Mechanism:** Effective capacity drops by 33–50% at `Specialist review` and 25–35% at `Medical validation`. Complex-panel orders (which are 2.4x more likely to enter `Hold for specialist review` and 3.1x more likely to require `Medical validation`) and Repeat-assay escalations (`Repeat assay -> Medical validation`: 80 in normal, 120 in peak) accumulate in front of the depleted clinical pools.
- **Primary SLA Threat:** `Specialist review` median queue explodes from 5.0h toward or above the 36.0h peak level (with `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` taking **48h** median), while `Medical validation` queue balloons from 2.0h to >22.0h (`Technical validation -> Medical validation` edge = **24h**). Because Urgent orders also require Medical validation, Urgent orders that cleared Assay in 10h still breach the 18h penalty limit while sitting in the 22h Medical validation queue.

### Scenario S4: Courier Arrival Compression & Front-End Defect Cascade
- **Demand & Mix Profile:** Total daily volume is near normal (~330–380 cases/day), but winter weather, traffic disruptions, or clinic batching compress the standard **10:00 and 17:00 courier arrival spikes** into a single late-afternoon dump (e.g., 60% of daily tubes arriving between 15:30 and 18:00), accompanied by longer transit times that degrade sample stability.
- **Disruption Mechanism:** Compressed arrivals overwhelm `Courier intake` (queue jumps from 1.0h to >7.5h; `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` jumps from 2h to 9h). To clear the evening backlog, `Registration clerks` work **>9-hour overtime shifts**, where their error rate rises sharply. Simultaneously, delayed transit increases pre-analytical sample degradation.
- **Primary SLA Threat:** Pre-analytical defect rates double from **6.95%** in normal ((760 + 500) / 18,120) to **12.33%+** in peak ((780 + 420) / 9,730). Specifically:
  1. Clerk overtime errors flood the **2-person `Accession correction specialists` pool** (queue jumps from 3.0h to 14.0h; `Manual accession correction -> Test preparation` jumps from 5h to 16h), and cases failing correction (`Manual accession correction -> Request recollection`) double from **14.0%** (70/500) to **26.2%** (110/420).
  2. `Request recollection` loops (830 normal; 890 peak) impose a **42h–60h delay** (`Request recollection -> Receive sample`) or result in order abandonment (`Request recollection -> Cancel order` rises from 34.9% [290/830] to 41.6% [370/890]).
  3. Recollection phone calls to physicians consume Registration clerk capacity during the next day's 10:00 arrival spike, locking in a multi-day front-end backlog.

### Scenario S5: Compound Winter Crisis (Mix Shift + Courier Delay + Alternate-Friday Specialist Absence)
- **Demand & Mix Profile:** Peak infection wave (3,300 cases/week; 29% Urgent, 20% Complex-panel) occurring on an alternate Friday with weather-delayed couriers.
- **Disruption Mechanism:** Late courier arrivals compress front-end intake, triggering clerk overtime errors and night-shift M2 reliance. The resulting surge of Urgent, Complex-panel, and Repeat-after-failure cases hits a 101%-utilized `Technical validation` pool and a depleted Friday `Specialist review` / `Medical validation` pool.
- **Primary SLA Threat:** System-wide gridlock where >54% of cases experience at least one queue >12h, overall rework exceeds 11.8%, and WIP accumulation drops period completion rate below 86.7% (8,580 completed out of 9,900 started in peak).

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

A single bottleneck never remains static in this lab. Below is the trace of how bottlenecks migrate naturally across stations and how naive managerial interventions (**I1–I10**) shift overload downstream or trigger feedback loops.

### Scenario S1 Migration: From Preparation Fragmentation to Technical & Medical Validation Gridlock
1. **Stage 1 — Preparation & Batch-Wait Contention:** As Urgent volume surges +131% (to 957/week), operators attempt to bypass `Batch wait`. However, because `Preparation benches` reach 89% utilization (11.0h median queue), the Urgent bypass success rate drops from **74% (normal) to 58% (peak)**. Consequently, `Test preparation -> Batch wait` triples in share from **4.4%** (760/17,310) to **12.7%** (1,180/9,320), where cases suffer a **36h combined delay** (`Test preparation -> Batch wait` [14h] + `Batch wait -> Assay run` [22h]).
2. **Stage 2 — Assay Schedule Fragmentation & M2 Repeat Loop:** The 58% of Urgent cases that *do* bypass `Batch wait` break batch continuity on `Assay machines M1/M2`, pushing Assay utilization from 71% to **98%** (18.0h median queue). To clear queues, Routine and spillover panels are pushed onto faster **M2** and night shifts, causing `Assay run -> Repeat assay` to jump from **6.7% to 14.8%** (1,260 repeats). Each repeat re-enters `Assay run` after a **26h median wait**, consuming assay capacity twice.
3. **Stage 3 — Technical Validation Over-Saturation (101% Utilization):** Assay eventually discharges cases into `Technical validation`, which crosses critical threshold from 76% to **101% utilization** (16.0h queue; `Assay run -> Technical validation` = 15h). Under extreme cognitive load and Complex-panel volume, technical validators escalate **7.5%** of cases to `Hold for specialist review` (610/8,080 vs. 1.8% [290/15,890] in normal) and **42.3%** to `Medical validation` (3,420/8,080 vs. 24.5% [3,900/15,890] in normal).
4. **Stage 4 — Terminal Bottleneck at Medical Validation & Specialist Review:** The bottleneck locks at `Medical validation` (93% utilization, **22.0h queue**, `Technical validation -> Medical validation` = 24h) and `Specialist review` (87% utilization, **36.0h queue**, `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` = 48h).
5. **Intervention-Induced Migration Risk:**
   - Applying **I1 (Strict Urgent-first dispatch at prep and assay)** forces Urgent bypass toward 100%, starving Routine and Complex-panel orders in `Batch wait` (driving Routine p90 >120h) and further fragmenting M1/M2 setups. Worse, Urgent cases accelerated through Assay simply hit the **16.0h Technical validation queue** and **22.0h Medical validation queue**, so Urgent orders *still* breach the 18h SLA while destroying total system throughput.
   - Applying **I2 (Increasing Routine batch sizes from 12 to 24)** improves preparation setup efficiency, but doubles batch accumulation wait (`Test preparation -> Batch wait -> Assay run` >36h), traps any misclassified Urgent sample, and dumps 24-sample slugs into `Assay` and `Technical validation` (101% utilization), increasing downstream queue variance.

### Scenario S2 Migration: From Assay M2 Dependence to Self-Feeding Rework & Validation Overload
1. **Stage 1 — Assay Queue Spike:** An M1 outage or M2 degradation immediately cuts effective reliable assay capacity, spiking `Assay machines` queue from 4.0h to >18.0h.
2. **Stage 2 — Parasitic Rework Loop (`Repeat assay`):** Routing runs to faster M2 or night shifts increases `Assay run -> Repeat assay` from 6.7% to $\ge$14.8%. Because `Repeat-after-failure` cases consume assay capacity twice and take **34h per loop in peak** (`Assay run -> Repeat assay` [8h] + `Repeat assay -> Assay run` [26h]), parasitic rework consumes ~15–25% of remaining machine slots, starving fresh arrivals in `Test preparation` and `Batch wait`.
3. **Stage 3 — Downstream Migration to Technical & Medical Validation:** Repeat runs generate dual technical validation work and direct medical escalations (`Repeat assay -> Medical validation`: 120 cases in peak), overloading `Technical validators` (101% util) and `Medical validators` (93% util).
4. **Intervention-Induced Migration Risk:**
   - Applying **I3 (Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel; route all Routine to M2)** protects Urgent/Complex cases on M1 initially, but forces **100% of Routine volume (~1,400 cases/week)** onto higher-failure M2. During high load, M2 fails on Routine batches at elevated rates, converting Routine cases (which normally have a 38h median cycle time and 73.6% direct release after Technical validation) into **Repeat-after-failure cases** (145h peak median, 240h p90) that subsequently demand M1/M2 re-runs and Medical validation!

### Scenario S3 Migration: From Specialist & Medical Validation Back-Pressure to Technical Validation Stall
1. **Stage 1 — Specialist Review Freeze:** On the alternate Friday when one specialist is absent, `Specialist review` queue surges from 5.0h to **36.0h+** and the `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` transition reaches **48h** median.
2. **Stage 2 — Medical Validation Saturation:** As Friday's held cases release on Monday alongside fresh Urgent (29%) and Complex-panel (20%) arrivals, `Medical validation` hits 93–100% utilization (**22.0h+ queue**; `Technical validation -> Medical validation` = 24h).
3. **Stage 3 — Upstream Contagion to Technical Validators:** When Medical validators and Specialists are unavailable for rapid consults, `Technical validators` (already at 101% peak utilization) spend extra time documenting borderline panels and placing cases into `Hold for specialist review` (which rises 4.1x from 1.8% to 7.5% of exits), slowing Technical validation and backing up `Assay run -> Technical validation` (15h edge).
4. **Intervention-Induced Migration Risk:**
   - Applying **I10 (Outsource Complex-panel overflow to partner lab)** blindly to all Complex-panel cases relieves `Specialist review` and `Medical validation`, but adds a guaranteed **12h transport delay**, increases pre-analytical splitting/packaging work at `Pre-analytical check` (already at **96% peak utilization** and 9.5h queue), and creates manual result-reconciliation load at `Registration/Technical validation`.
   - Applying **I9 (Hold Routine releases until batch report is ready)** frees minor release clicks, but adds artificial waiting to Routine cases (pushing p90 closer to/above the 72h limit) without relieving `Medical validation` (since Routine cases primarily take `Technical validation -> Release result` directly: 11,700/15,890 in normal).

### Scenario S4 Migration: From Courier Spikes to Pre-Analytical Collapse, Accession Starvation, and Technical Validation Deficit
1. **Stage 1 — Courier Intake & Registration Overtime:** Compressed 10:00/17:00 courier drops push `Courier intake` to 88% utilization (7.5h queue; `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` = 9h). `Registration clerks` work >9h overtime shifts, pushing utilization to 85% (2.8h queue) and triggering an error spike.
2. **Stage 2 — Pre-Analytical Check & 2-Specialist Accession Correction Bottleneck:** Delayed tubes and clerk entry errors hit `Pre-analytical check` (**96% peak utilization**, 9.5h queue; `Pre-analytical check -> Test preparation` = 13h). Defects jump from 6.95% to **12.33%**:
   - **420 cases** enter `Manual accession correction`, overwhelming the **2 specialists** (91% utilization, **14.0h queue**, `Manual accession correction -> Test preparation` = 16h).
   - **890 total cases** (780 direct + 110 from correction) enter `Request recollection`, triggering a **60h return loop** (`Request recollection -> Receive sample`) and 370 cancellations.
3. **Stage 3 — Feedback Loop into Registration Intake:** The 890 recollection loops require `Registration clerks` to phone physicians to explain errors, stealing front-end capacity during the next day's courier arrivals and perpetuating overtime (>9h shifts).
4. **Stage 4 — Unblocking Surge into Prep, Assay, and Technical Validation:** Once the 2 accession specialists clear a backlog wave (`Manual accession correction -> Test preparation`), a synchronized burst of delayed orders hits `Preparation benches` (89% util) and `Assay` (98% util).
5. **Intervention-Induced Migration Risk:**
   - Applying **I8 (Automate registration for complete digital metadata)** and **I7 (Shift courier slots)** without downstream throttling eliminates the 2.8h registration queue, but immediately dumps the full unbuffered arrival wave onto **`Pre-analytical check` (96% util, 9.5h queue)** and **`Assay machines` (98% util, 18.0h queue)**.
   - Applying **I4 (Cross-train two technical validators to do accession correction)** during a peak wave relieves the 2 accession specialists (91% util, 14.0h queue), unblocking hundreds of cases into Prep and Assay—while **simultaneously stripping 2 validators from `Technical validation`, which is already at 101% utilization (16.0h queue)**! When the unblocked cases finish Assay 15–20 hours later, they hit a crippled Technical validation pool, causing an even larger downstream pileup.

# 3. Robust redesign package

Rather than adding permanent headcount everywhere or tuning for average cycle time, the robust redesign package consists of **four permanent structural pillars** that eliminate parasitic rework loops, decouple case types with conflicting physics, and enforce cross-station pacing (`CONWIP` throttling).

### Pillar R1: Smoothed Intake, Digital Registration, and Hard Shift Caps (Integrated I7 + I8 + Clerk Overtime Cap + Intake Throttle)
- **Design:**
  1. **Stagger Courier Slots (I7):** Re-contract courier routes from two sharp spikes (10:00 and 17:00) into four staggered arrival waves (08:30, 11:30, 14:30, 17:30), prioritizing clinics with high Urgent/hospital volume on the earlier runs.
  2. **Automated Digital Registration (I8) + Hard 9-Hour Clerk Shift Cap:** Enable automated registration for orders with complete digital metadata. Use the freed clerk capacity to enforce a strict **$\le$9-hour shift limit** for Registration clerks, eliminating the fatigue-induced accession error spike that feeds `Manual accession correction` (420 peak cases) and `Request recollection` (110 secondary escalations).
  3. **Downstream Protection (Virtual Release Buffer):** To prevent I7+I8 from immediately flooding `Pre-analytical check` (96% peak util), automated registration logs Routine orders instantly but releases physical tube-check batches to `Pre-analytical check` under a **pull cap** (releasing Routine trays only when Pre-analytical active queue is $<4.0\text{h}$; Urgent and Complex-panel orders pass immediately).
- **Why It Is Robust:** Works across S1, S4, and S5 by attacking both arrival variance (reducing `Courier intake` queue from 7.5h toward 1–2h) and root-cause human error generation, cutting inflow to the fragile 2-person `Accession correction` pool by ~35–50%.
- **Key Assumptions & Failure Modes:** Assumes $\ge$50% of clinics submit clean digital metadata and courier providers accept 4-wave scheduling without prohibitive fees. *Failure mode:* If clinic EHR systems transmit systematically corrupted metadata (e.g., mismatched tube/test codes), I8 will ingest defects rapidly without a clerk noticing until `Pre-analytical check`.

### Pillar R2: Rapid 2-Hour Defect Triage & Automated Physician Recollection Loop (Integrated I6 + Digital Error Notification)
- **Design:**
  1. **Front-Gate 2-Hour Defect Triage (I6):** Split `Pre-analytical check` into a rapid 2-minute visual/barcode front-screen at sample receipt (checking tube integrity, fill level, clot/hemolysis, and label match within 2h of arrival) before full pre-analytical sorting.
  2. **Automated Digital Recollection & Accession Query Workflow:** Replace manual phone calls by Registration clerks to physicians with automated EHR/SMS/portal recollection alerts containing structured defect reason codes and one-click physician confirmation.
- **Why It Is Robust:** In peak, defective samples wait 9h at `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` + 7h at `Pre-analytical check -> Request recollection` (or 16h in `Manual accession correction`) before the **60h recollection clock** even starts (total 76h–85h lost). Catching defects within 2h saves **12–22h per defective case**, prevents compromised specimens from entering `Assay run` (where they cause `Repeat assay` loops), and **severs the feedback loop** where 890 peak recollection phone calls steal Registration clerk capacity.
- **Downstream Overload Protection:** Rejecting unviable samples at the gate *reduces* load on downstream `Pre-analytical check`, `Preparation benches`, and `Assay machines`.
- **Key Assumptions & Failure Modes:** Assumes physical defects (clotting, underfilling, barcode mismatch) are detectable via rapid intake inspection. *Failure mode:* Under extreme time pressure to meet the 2h triage target, technicians may over-reject borderline samples, artificially inflating `Request recollection -> Cancel order` (already 41.6% [370/890] in peak).

### Pillar R3: Segmented Preparation Cadence & Risk-Adjusted M1/M2 Assay Routing (Modified I1 + Modified I2 + Modified I3)
- **Design:**
  1. **Fixed Urgent Micro-Windows at Preparation (Controlled I1 + I2):** Instead of uncontrolled Urgent preemption (I1, which fragments setups) or unbuffered 24-sample batching (I2, which traps Urgent cases in a 36h `Batch wait` path), establish **scheduled Urgent fast-track releases every 60 minutes** at `Preparation benches`. Routine samples batch at 12 (normal) to 16 (moderate load) between fast-track windows. This restores Urgent `Batch wait` bypass from 58% back above **80–85%** while keeping preparation setup efficiency predictable.
  2. **Reliability-Matched M1/M2 Routing & Night-Shift Safeguards (Modified I3):**
     - **M1 (High Reliability, Standard Speed):** Dedicated primary engine for **Urgent**, **Complex-panel**, and **all `Repeat-after-failure` / `Repeat assay` orders**. Because Repeat-after-failure cases have already failed once (median cycle time 70h normal / 145h peak) and Complex-panel orders require multi-assay consistency, they must never be exposed to M2's higher failure rate.
     - **M2 (Faster Runtime, Higher Failure Rate):** Assigned to **first-pass Routine orders** (up to an 80% utilization cap), with mandatory **mid-shift and pre-night-shift QC calibration** (since repeats cluster after night shifts and M2 usage). Once M2 hits 80% utilization or fails QC drift thresholds, excess Routine stays in `Batch wait` rather than running uncalibrated on M2 at night. Conversely, when M1 queue is $<2\text{h}$, M1 pulls Routine batches from M2 to prevent M2 overload.
  3. **Downstream Protection (Assay-to-Technical Validation CONWIP Pacing):** Assay batch releases for Routine orders are paced by `Technical validation` queue depth so Assay does not flood the 101%-utilized Technical validation pool.
- **Why It Is Robust:** Directly breaks the **Assay repeat-loop death spiral** (which doubled from 6.7% to 14.8% in peak and consumes 34h per repeat). Cutting peak repeat assays from 14.8% (1,260 runs) back to ~7.5% (~640 runs) eliminates **~620 redundant assay runs and ~620 redundant technical validations** over 3 weeks—effectively creating ~7–8% net new capacity at both `Assay` and `Technical validation` without buying hardware.
- **Key Assumptions & Failure Modes:** Assumes M2's failure rate can be contained via scheduled QC calibration and avoiding <80% utilization overload. *Failure mode:* If M1 suffers a full mechanical breakdown (Scenario S2), M2 must temporarily carry Urgent and Complex-panel volume, requiring immediate activation of Scenario Policy P2.

### Pillar R4: Inverted Cross-Training Matrix & Rule-Based Validation Filtering (Inverted I4 + Auto-Verification Rules)
- **Design:**
  1. **Invert and Broaden Cross-Training (Fixing the Flaw in I4):** Manager proposal **I4** (cross-training 2 Technical validators to do Accession correction) pulls staff *from* the 101%-utilized bottleneck (`Technical validation`) to help a 91%-utilized station (`Accession correction`). Instead, implement a **three-tier asymmetric cross-training structure** (completed during the 4-week cross-training window in normal periods):
     - **Tier A (Clerk $\rightarrow$ Accession Correction):** Cross-train **3 senior Registration clerks** (whose time is freed by Pillar R1's digital registration I8 and Pillar R2's automated physician alerts) to handle standard `Manual accession correction`, expanding the 2-specialist pool to 5 capable staff without touching Technical validators.
     - **Tier B (Accession Specialists & Senior Prep Techs $\rightarrow$ Technical Validation):** Cross-train the **2 Accession correction specialists and 2 senior Preparation technicians** over 4 weeks to perform Routine `Technical validation` when Technical validation utilization exceeds 85%.
     - **Tier C (Technical Validators $\rightarrow$ Accession Correction Backup Only):** Cross-train 2 Technical validators on complex Accession correction *strictly as absentee backup or for 90-minute early-morning intake swarming* before assay runs finish (see Policy P4).
  2. **Structured Escalation Protocol at Technical Validation:** Implement explicit quantitative decision trees for `Technical validation -> Hold for specialist review` and `Technical validation -> Medical validation`. In peak, defensive escalations by overloaded technical validators drive Specialist holds up **4.1x** (1.8% to 7.5%). Rule-based filtering restricts `Hold for specialist review` strictly to true discordances/critical complex panels, keeping the referral rate near 2.5–3.5%.
- **Why It Is Robust:** Relieves the single highest utilization bottleneck in the entire lab (`Technical validators` at **101% peak utilization**, 16.0h queue) while simultaneously eliminating the single-point-of-failure risk of having only 2 `Accession correction specialists`.
- **Key Assumptions & Failure Modes:** Assumes regulatory/accreditation rules permit cross-trained prep/accession staff (after 4-week training) to sign off on standard Routine technical validations, and senior clerks to resolve accession discrepancies. *Failure mode:* Insufficiently supervised cross-trained validators could either miss subtle analytical drift or over-escalate to `Medical validation`.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

Permanent structural changes cannot economically size the lab for every simultaneous extreme shock. The following four contingent policies remain **dormant during normal periods** (to avoid unnecessary cost, transport delay, or Routine slowdown) and activate/deactivate via strict **hysteresis triggers** (separate ON and OFF thresholds to prevent policy thrashing).

### Policy P1: Infection-Wave Surge & Mix-Shift Protocol (Targets Scenario S1 & S5)
- **Actions When Active:**
  1. **Activate Evening Medical Validation Shift (I5):** Add a 4–6 hour temporary evening `Medical validation` shift (18:00–23:00) focused exclusively on clearing Urgent orders (to beat the 18h penalty) and ready Complex-panel orders.
  2. **Gated Routine Batch Expansion (Modified I2):** Step Routine preparation batch size from **12 up to 20–24** at `Preparation benches` to reduce setup overhead, *strictly conditioned* on maintaining the 60-minute Urgent fast-track bypass window (Pillar R3).
  3. **Deploy Tier-B Cross-Trained Validators to Technical Validation:** Shift 2 cross-trained Prep/Accession staff into `Technical validation` for 4 hours/shift.
  4. **Controlled Routine Batch Release (Modified I9):** Group non-critical Routine result releases into 2–3 scheduled automated release windows per day *only* if manual release handling is slowing Technical validators, provided Routine elapsed cycle time is $<56\text{h}$ (protecting the 72h target).
- **Trigger ON (any condition met for 2 consecutive shifts):**
  - Rolling 24h arrival mix shows **Urgent $>23\%$** OR **Complex-panel $>16\%$** (or combined $>38\%$ vs. 31% normal baseline); OR
  - `Technical validation` median queue $>6.0\text{h}$ (normal: 3.5h) AND `Medical validation` median queue $>6.0\text{h}$ (normal: 2.0h); OR
  - Urgent p90 cycle time exceeds **16.5h** (approaching the 18h penalty cliff).
- **Trigger OFF (all conditions met for 48 hours):**
  - Combined Urgent + Complex-panel share drops below **33%**; AND
  - `Technical validation` queue $<4.5\text{h}$ AND `Medical validation` queue $<3.5\text{h}$; AND
  - Urgent p90 cycle time $<14.0\text{h}$.

### Policy P2: Assay Degradation & M2 High-Failure Containment Protocol (Targets Scenario S2)
- **Actions When Active:**
  1. **Suspend Normal M1/M2 Split & Throttle M2 Night Runs:** If M1 is degraded/down, place M2 under **mandatory QC verification every 4 hours** and reduce M2 speed/batch density to stabilize failure rates. If M2's failure rate spikes, **halt Routine night-shift runs on M2** (holding Routine safely in `Batch wait` rather than generating 34h `Repeat assay` loops).
  2. **Strict SLA Triage at Assay:** Prioritize M1 (or remaining healthy machine capacity) strictly by **Remaining SLA Slack Time**: (1) Urgent cases $<8\text{h}$ from the 18h limit, (2) `Repeat-after-failure` orders (to prevent third-pass failures), (3) Complex-panel orders, and (4) Routine orders $<24\text{h}$ from the 72h limit.
  3. **Assay-Overflow Partner Routing (Modified I10 for Assay Outage):** If M1 outage is projected $>12\text{h}$, route newly arrived Complex-panel and stable Routine orders directly after `Pre-analytical check` to the partner lab (absorbing the 12h transport delay in exchange for avoiding an 18h+ local assay queue + 34h repeat loop).
- **Trigger ON (any condition met):**
  - M1 or M2 unplanned downtime $>3\text{h}$; OR
  - Rolling 12h `Assay run -> Repeat assay` transition rate exceeds **9.5%** (normal: 6.7%, peak: 14.8%); OR
  - `Assay machines` median queue exceeds **8.0h** (normal: 4.0h).
- **Trigger OFF (all conditions met for 24 hours):**
  - Both M1 and M2 operational with 2 consecutive clean QC cycles; AND
  - Rolling 12h `Assay run -> Repeat assay` rate $<7.5\%$; AND
  - `Assay machines` median queue $<5.0\text{h}$.

### Policy P3: Clinical Validation & Alternate-Friday Specialist Relief Protocol (Targets Scenario S3 & S5)
- **Actions When Active:**
  1. **Targeted Complex-Panel Overflow Outsourcing (Conditional I10):** Route **new Complex-panel arrivals** exceeding local specialist capacity to the partner lab immediately after `Pre-analytical check`.
     - *Quantitative justification:* In peak/absence conditions, local `Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation` takes **48h** (median queue 36.0h) plus `Technical validation -> Hold for specialist review` (12h) and `Medical validation -> Release result` (5h) = **65h+ in validation alone**. The partner lab's **12h transport delay** + lower specialist queue cuts 25–40 hours off Complex-panel cycle time while preventing local `Medical validation` (22.0h peak queue) from stalling Urgent orders.
  2. **Pre-Friday Specialist Sweep & Split-Panel Reporting:** On the Thursday before the alternate-Friday specialist absence, prioritize Complex-panel batches through `Assay` and `Technical validation` by 14:00 so the specialist clears them before Friday. On Friday, enable **partial/preliminary release** of normal sub-assays within Complex-panels so only abnormal sub-components wait in `Hold for specialist review`.
  3. **Activate Evening/Locum Medical Validation (I5):** Backfill Medical validator absence immediately with evening shift coverage.
- **Trigger ON (any condition met):**
  - **24 hours prior (Thursday 08:00)** to scheduled alternate-Friday specialist absence whenever weekly Complex-panel arrivals exceed **350/week** (normal: 299/wk); OR
  - Unplanned absence of $\ge$1 `Medical validator` or `Specialist reviewer`; OR
  - `Specialist review` median queue $>12.0\text{h}$ (normal: 5.0h) OR pending WIP in `Hold for specialist review` $>35$ cases; OR
  - `Medical validation` median queue $>8.0\text{h}$ (normal: 2.0h).
- **Trigger OFF (all conditions met):**
  - Full Specialist and Medical validator staffing restored for $\ge$24h; AND
  - `Specialist review` median queue $<6.5\text{h}$ AND `Medical validation` median queue $<3.0\text{h}$.

### Policy P4: Courier Compression & Front-End Swarming Protocol (Targets Scenario S4 & S5)
- **Actions When Active:**
  1. **Time-Boxed 2-Hour Front-End Swarming (Time-Shifted I4 + Tier-A Clerks):** When a delayed courier wave dumps tubes simultaneously, deploy the **3 Tier-A cross-trained Registration clerks** and (if needed) **2 Tier-C cross-trained Technical validators** to `Pre-analytical check` (I6 triage) and `Manual accession correction` for a **strictly capped 2-hour window** immediately following courier arrival.
     - *Why this avoids the I4 trap:* Because a courier wave takes ~10–15 hours to travel through `Pre-analytical check` -> `Test preparation` -> `Assay run`, borrowing 2 Technical validators for 2 hours right at courier arrival clears the front-end bottleneck *before* that wave ever reaches `Technical validation`, and returns the validators to their benches before the assay wave finishes!
  2. **Strict Urgent Extraction at Courier Intake:** Physically color-code Urgent and Complex-panel transport bags so `Courier intake` extracts them in $<15\text{ minutes}$, bypassing the 7.5h peak intake queue while Routine bags are metered into `Pre-analytical check`.
- **Trigger ON (any condition met):**
  - Courier route delayed $>90\text{ minutes}$ OR $>35\%$ of daily tube volume arrives within a single 2-hour window; OR
  - `Courier intake` queue $>2.5\text{h}$ (normal: 1.0h) OR `Pre-analytical check` queue $>4.0\text{h}$ (normal: 1.8h); OR
  - `Accession correction` queue $>6.0\text{h}$ (normal: 3.0h) or $>25$ orders waiting.
- **Trigger OFF (all conditions met):**
  - `Courier intake` queue $<1.5\text{h}$, `Pre-analytical check` queue $<2.5\text{h}$, and `Accession correction` queue $<3.5\text{h}$.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with the robust redesign package and scenario-triggered policies in place, five vulnerabilities remain—including **three introduced directly by the redesign itself**:

1. **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability — Automated Registration (I8) "Garbage-In at Speed" Risk:**
   - *Mechanism:* Automating registration for orders with complete digital metadata eliminates manual clerk entry errors and prevents $>9\text{h}$ overtime shifts. However, digital completeness does not guarantee clinical or physical accuracy. If an external clinic's EHR update maps the wrong tube preservative or panel code to a valid digital schema, I8 will ingest hundreds of flawed orders instantaneously without a human clerk noticing the discrepancy, shifting discovery to `Test preparation` or `Technical validation` where remediation is much costlier.
   - *Mitigation:* Pair I8 with automated plausibility rules (e.g., cross-checking ordered panel vs. scanned tube barcode prefix at Pillar R2's 2-hour triage) and sample-audit 2% of auto-registered orders daily.

2. **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability — Early 2-Hour Defect Triage (I6) Over-Rejection & Patient Abandonment:**
   - *Mechanism:* Enforcing a strict $<2\text{h}$ pre-analytical defect rejection target creates an incentive for front-end staff under peak pressure to summarily reject borderline samples (e.g., slight underfill or minor label smudge that could have been salvaged via `Manual accession correction`) rather than investigating them. In the peak log, **41.6% of recollection requests end in cancellation** (`Request recollection -> Cancel order`: 370 / 890). Over-triggering I6 could silently increase total cancelled orders (`Cancel order -> END`), delaying patient care outside the lab's cycle-time metrics.
   - *Mitigation:* Require a dual-check (or 30-second digital photo log) before issuing a `Request recollection` on Complex-panel or Urgent tubes, and track the `Request recollection -> Cancel order` conversion rate as a guardrail.

3. **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability — Policy-Switching Thrashing & Split-Specimen Traceability (I10 / P1–P4):**
   - *Mechanism:* Operating conditional policies (batch size 12 vs. 20–24 in P1, partner-lab outsourcing in P3, 2-hour swarming in P4) introduces coordination complexity. If hysteresis bands are violated, an Urgent sample could be trapped in a 24-sample Routine batch, or a Complex-panel order split between in-house Urgent sub-tests and partner-lab outsourced sub-tests (I10) could suffer accession mismatches, lost transport manifests during the 12h transit, or conflicting reference intervals during `Medical validation`.
   - *Mitigation:* Enforce minimum 24–48h lock-in windows (hysteresis) on policy switches, and only outsource whole Complex-panel orders (or use automated barcode child-aliasing) rather than manually splitting single tubes.

4. **Structural Vulnerability — Simultaneous M1 + M2 Hardware/Reagent Failure:**
   - *Mechanism:* With peak assay utilization at **98%** across only two machines (M1/M2), the lab has zero hardware redundancy during peak waves if *both* M1 and M2 experience simultaneous failure or a shared reagent-lot defect. Even partner-lab overflow (I10) cannot absorb 3,300 cases/week without breaching the 18h Urgent SLA (since partner transport alone adds 12h).
   - *Mitigation:* Maintain an emergency reciprocal SLA with a nearby hospital lab (<2h courier radius) strictly for Urgent orders during dual-machine outages.

5. **Structural Vulnerability — 4-Week Cross-Training Lag vs. Sudden Staff Attrition:**
   - *Mechanism:* Pillar R4 relies on cross-trained staff (4-week training lead time). If peak infection waves coincide with staff illness across both `Technical validators` and cross-trained `Accession/Prep` staff, human validation capacity cannot be expanded inside a 3-week peak window.

### Part B: Lagging Outcome Metrics (Verification & Post-Mortem — Monitored Daily / Weekly)

These metrics confirm whether the redesign and leading-indicator triggers succeeded, but **must never be used as the sole trigger to start interventions** because they only register after SLA penalties and rework have already occurred:

1. **Case-Type Cycle Time Distributions (Median & p90 vs. SLAs):**
   - *Urgent:* Target median $\le 11\text{h}$, **p90 $<18.0\text{h}$** (contractual penalty cliff; normal p90 = 19h, peak p90 = 33h).
   - *Routine:* Target median $\le 42\text{h}$, **p90 $<72.0\text{h}$** (target SLA; normal p90 = 68h, peak p90 = 120h).
   - *Complex-panel & Repeat-after-failure:* Target p90 $<115\text{h}$ and $<130\text{h}$ respectively (preventing the peak blowouts to 210h and 240h).
2. **Percentage of Completed Cases with Any Queue $>12\text{h}$:**
   - Lagging measure of systemic congestion (normal = 22%, peak = 54%; target under redesign during peak $\le 28\%$).
3. **Total End-to-End Rework Percentage:**
   - Lagging aggregate of recollection, accession correction, and repeat assay loops (normal = 7.5%, peak = 11.8%; target under redesign during peak $\le 8.0\%$).
4. **Order Abandonment / Cancellation Rate (`Request recollection -> Cancel order` and Total `Cancel order -> END`):**
   - Lagging quality-of-service guardrail ensuring that faster triage (Pillar R2 / I6) and partner outsourcing (Policy P3 / I10) do not increase lost orders above the **3.1% normal baseline** (570 / 18,400) toward the **5.5% peak rate** (540 / 9,900).
