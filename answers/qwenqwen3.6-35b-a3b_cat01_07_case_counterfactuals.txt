**100-case weekly rollout analysis (288 words):**
Rolling out these interventions across 100 similar cases in one week exposes systemic interdependencies. Reserving C1 capacity for K-104-style cases creates a hard floor on compliance throughput. Since C1 is the sole certified reviewer, 100 reservations will displace standard compliance work, pushing non-target cases into Wed/Thu backlogs and potentially violating SLA thresholds. The system exhibits a non-linear compliance bottleneck: each reserved slot reduces effective C1 capacity by ~5%, but queue wait times accelerate exponentially once utilization exceeds 85%. Substituting D1 with D2 for K-219 cases shifts document load to a slower, less specialized resource. With 100 cases, D2’s queue will likely swell past 8 h, increasing rework rates for handwritten documents. This displaces complex corporate cases from D1, degrading overall document throughput. K-337’s rework suppression saves external-response time but shifts risk to the decision layer. At scale, conditional approvals accumulate appeal risk and may trigger audit flags, increasing post-decision rework that offsets cycle-time gains. K-411’s concurrency push will flood the assessment queue. With 100 cases ready simultaneously, A1/A2 queues will exceed 12 h, causing assessment delays to cascade into decision and notify stages. Weekend and external-response effects remain unmitigated at scale; supplier delays and batch timestamp logging will continue to create false concurrency or audit holds. True concurrency requires system-level task routing fixes, not workarounds. The net system-wide effect is likely negative without capacity expansion or queue-smoothing policies.
