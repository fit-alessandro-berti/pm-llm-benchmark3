# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Assumptions**
1.  **Service Time Distributions**: Service time ranges (e.g., 5-8 minutes) are modeled as Uniform distributions for simulation/approximation purposes. The "complex-tail" is modeled as a mixture distribution: with probability $p_{tail}$, draw from the complex range; otherwise, draw from the ordinary range.
2.  **Parallelism Decision**:
    *   **P1 Cases**: Always execute ME/ES branches in **parallel** to minimize elapsed time, accepting the 8-minute coordination penalty.
    *   **P2/P3 Cases**: Execute ME/ES branches **sequentially** on a single cross-skilled resource if available, or parallel if single-resource queuing delay exceeds 15 minutes. For base analysis, we assume Sequential to conserve capacity.
3.  **Preemption Modeling**: Preemption is approximated as a 15% effective service time inflation on P2/P3 diagnostic tasks during blocks where P1 load exceeds 20% of capacity, representing the restart penalty and context switching.
4.  **Handover**: A handover occurs if the resource changes between Intake, Diagnostic, and Validation. We assume Policy 1 (Specialization) incurs high handover; Policy 2 (Pooling) incurs moderate; Policy 3 (Hybrid) aims to minimize.
5.  **Arrival Process**: Expected arrivals per block are treated as deterministic for capacity planning but Poisson-distributed for delay estimation (variance = mean).

**Objectives**
1.  **SLA Risk Minimization**: Minimize the weighted violation rate of priority SLAs.
    $$ \text{Cost}_1 = w_1 \cdot (1 - \text{P1\_SLA}) + w_2 \cdot (1 - \text{P2\_SLA}) + w_3 \cdot (1 - \text{P3\_SLA}) $$
    Where $w_1 \gg w_2 \gg w_3$.
2.  **Robustness & Fairness**: Minimize the maximum resource utilization variance and ensure no single resource exceeds 90% utilization in the base case to prevent fragility.
    $$ \text{Cost}_2 = \max(\text{Utilization}) + \lambda \cdot \text{StdDev}(\text{Utilization}) $$

**Decision Variables**
*   **Routing Logic**: Static (Skill-based) vs. Dynamic (Load-based).
*   **Reservation**: Percentage of capacity reserved for P1 (0% vs 20%).
*   **Parallelization Threshold**: Elapsed time trigger for splitting ME/ES branches.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Policy 1: Strict Specialization (Static)**
*   **Eligibility**: Cases routed to queues based on primary skill (M, E, S). ME/ES split automatically.
*   **Priority**: Strict Priority Queuing (P1 > P2 > P3) within each skill queue.
*   **Preemption**: Allowed within skill queues.
*   **Handover**: High. Intake (General) -> Diagnostic (Specialist) -> Validation (General) implies at least 2 handovers.
*   **Overflow**: None. If Mechanical queue is full, Mechanical cases wait even if Electrical resources are idle.

**Policy 2: Full Pooling (Dynamic)**
*   **Eligibility**: Any resource qualified for the *next* activity can pull from a global queue.
*   **Priority**: Global Priority Score = $1000 \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{P1}) + 100 \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{P2}) + \text{WaitTime}$.
*   **Preemption**: P1 preempts any diagnostic activity globally.
*   **Handover**: Minimized. Resource tries to retain case from Intake through Validation if skilled ("Case Ownership").
*   **Overflow**: Implicit.

**Policy 3: Hybrid (Reserved Capacity + Controlled Overflow)**
*   **Eligibility**: 20% of each resource's capacity is logically reserved for P1 cases.
*   **Priority**: P1 accesses reserved + general pool. P2/P3 access general pool only.
*   **Parallelization**: P1 ME/ES always parallel. P2/P3 sequential unless queue > 3 cases.
*   **Overflow**: If P1 queue > 2, P2/P3 work is paused (preempted) to clear P1.
*   **Handover**: "Skill-Adjacent" routing preferred (e.g., Mechanical resource takes ME case to avoid handover to Electrical).

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary Recommendation: Policy 3 (Hybrid with Reserved Capacity)**

**Rationale**:
1.  **SLA Protection**: Policy 1 fails P1 SLAs in the Base scenario due to specialist bottlenecks (Electrical). Policy 3 guarantees capacity for P1 via reservation.
2.  **Robustness**: Under U1 (Surge) and U3 (Absence), pooling (Policy 2) tends to starve P3 completely as resources chase P1/P2. Policy 3's reservation ensures P1 moves, while the "Controlled Overflow" prevents total P3 starvation by limiting preemption frequency.
3.  **Handover Efficiency**: By encouraging "Skill-Adjacent" routing (e.g., a Mechanical resource doing an ME case), we reduce the 7-minute handover penalty compared to Strict Specialization.

**Fallback Mode**:
*   **Trigger**: If P1 Queue Length > 5 *and* Average Wait > 60 mins.
*   **Action**: Switch to **Policy 2 (Full Pooling)** temporarily. Suspend P2/P3 intake if necessary to clear the P1 backlog.
*   **Reversion**: When P1 Queue < 2.

**When Policy 1 is Preferable**:
*   If audit requirements demand strict segregation of duties (e.g., Security incidents) which is not the case here (Technical Incidents).

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

1.  **Gaming of Estimates**: Resources might classify complex P2 cases as P1 to access reserved capacity. *Mitigation*: Automated P1 classification based on keywords, manual override requires manager ID.
2.  **Starvation of P3**: In U5 (Combined Stress), P3 cases may wait >24 hours. *Mitigation*: Escalation rule: If P3 wait > 20 hours, promote to P2.
3.  **Skill Erosion**: Hybrid policy encourages cross-skilling. However, if specialists always take complex work, generalists may not learn. *Mitigation*: Rotate complex cases to junior staff during low-load blocks.
4.  **Excessive Preemption**: The 10% restart penalty adds up. *Mitigation*: "Non-preemptive zone" – if a task is >90% complete, do not preempt.
5.  **Validation Bottleneck**: All policies funnel to Validation. If Diagnostic speeds up (Pooling), Validation becomes the constraint. *Mitigation*: Ensure Intake resources (Ada, Ben) are cross-trained for Validation (they are).
6.  **Human Interpretability**: Dynamic routing can confuse staff ("Why did I get this case?"). *Mitigation*: Dashboard showing "Next Best Case" with reason code (e.g., "P1 Reservation").

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**Limits of Current Analysis**
1.  **Variance Ignorance**: The capacity analysis uses mean service times. It does not fully capture the "Complex Tail" variance which drives queue instability (Kingman's Formula).
2.  **Correlation**: It assumes arrival spikes are independent of service complexity. In reality, surges (U1) might correlate with complex incidents (e.g., system-wide outage).
3.  **Human Behavior**: The model assumes resources work at the defined multiplier constantly. Fatigue, learning curves, and morale during U5 (Combined Stress) are not modeled.
4.  **Preemption Overhead**: The 10% penalty is a static estimate. Real context switching might cost more if documentation is poor.

**Validation Plan (Pilot)**
1.  **A/B Test**: Run Policy 1 (Status Quo) on Morning Shift and Policy 3 (Hybrid) on Afternoon Shift for 2 weeks.
2.  **Safety Guardrails**:
    *   **P1 Protection**: If P1 SLA drops below 85% in the Pilot, revert to Policy 1 immediately.
    *   **Work-in-Progress (WIP) Limits**: Cap the number of active cases per resource to 3 to prevent multitasking overload.
3.  **Data Collection**: Log `policy_version` and `handover_flag` for every case. Use PM4Py drift detection weekly to ensure service times haven't degraded due to the new policy.
4.  **Success Criteria**: Policy 3 is adopted if P1 SLA > 90% AND P3 Mean Delay does not increase by more than 15% compared to baseline.
