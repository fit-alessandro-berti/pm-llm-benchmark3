## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining evidence describes a classic **local speed/cost optimum that is globally expensive and risky**. The dominant directly-follows chain is:

`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`

with edge counts `3,600 -> 2,550 -> 2,300 -> 2,320` out of 5,000 cases. That path is genuinely fast: the performance DFG shows `Intake -> Auto eligibility screen` at mean 0.8 h, `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` at mean 1.0 h, `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` at mean 3.0 h, and `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close` at mean 5.0 h. It is also cheap before rework: auto screening costs EUR 0.20 per case, and Fast approval cases are reported as 31% cheaper before rework.

But the same evidence shows why this fast path is not a safe default:

- **Compliance:** Token-based replay gives fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces. Alignment diagnostics show frequent deviations: `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Customer complaint/reopen after close`, and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. These are direct violations of R1, R2, and R3. The discovered model also has low precision, meaning many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways policy does not allow.
- **Rework and quality:** Customer complaint/reopen occurs in 9.2% of all cases, but 14.1% of cases using Fast approval versus only 3.3% of cases using `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`. Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% of repaired low-risk devices. The fast path’s cost advantage shrinks from 31% cheaper before rework to only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits.
- **Long-tail customer damage:** The performance DFG shows `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` at mean 96 h, median 70 h, p90 180 h. Complaints then loop into `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` at mean 40 h or `Escalation board` at mean 54 h. So the fast path creates slow, expensive downstream recovery.
- **Segment mismatch:** Premium high-value devices have 22% complaint/reopen on the fast path versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path, with high compliance risk if R1/R2 are violated. Standard high-value devices show 18% fast-path complaints versus 6% reviewed/repair. By contrast, Premium low-risk accessories are 8 h median on the fast path versus 46 h manual, with only 6% versus 3% complaints, but high service-credit exposure if cycle time exceeds 24 h. Standard low-risk accessories tolerate 72 h if status visibility is good. Unclear defect/missing evidence cases are 22% of volume, have median 140 h, the highest abandonment, and repeated evidence requests.
- **Resource constraints:** Manual eligibility review has only 7 reviewers at EUR 34/h and queue grows sharply above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk review has 2 specialists at EUR 52/h and is already a Monday/post-campaign bottleneck. Repair quote delay is mostly waiting for part-price confirmation. Quality check has 2 inspectors at EUR 38/h and is underused except after repair campaigns. The escalation board has one daily 45-minute slot: strong satisfaction effect but poor scalability.

**The contradiction, explicitly:** the most frequent path is attractive on first-pass speed and direct cost, but it externalizes cost into complaints, rework, compliance exceptions, and customer-experience damage. The slower reviewed/repair path is better for high-value devices and unclear cases, but it is not universally better: for low-risk accessories it is much slower with only modest complaint reduction, and for Premium low-risk accessories it risks service credits. The redesign problem is therefore not “fast versus slow”; it is **segment-aware routing, evidence completeness, and downstream quality control**.

## 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware gated fast lane

**Redesigned process flow**

`Intake claim -> Segment/risk classifier ->`  
- **Premium low-risk accessory, complete evidence, no risk flags** -> `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`  
- **Standard low-risk accessory, complete evidence, no risk flags** -> `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close` with proactive status visibility  
- **High-value device above EUR 400, unclear defect, missing evidence, or any risk flag** -> `Manual eligibility review` and, where indicated, `Fraud/risk review` before any `Fast approval`  
- **Reopened claim** -> `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, with complaint reason captured  

Hard gates: no `Fast approval` for above-EUR-400 claims without Manual or Fraud/risk review; no `Dispatch replacement` before evidence completeness is confirmed, except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the dominant fast path, the 43% low-risk accessory segment, the 35% high-value device segment, and the compliance deviations around R1/R2/R3. It also targets the 310 `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` loops by reducing the upstream causes of reopen.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The current auto-screen rules are outdated and not segment-aware. Redesign A makes the auto screen segment-aware and defect-aware, so low-risk accessories with complete evidence can remain fast, while high-value and unclear cases are forced into reviewed paths. This should reduce high-value compliance violations and high-value fast-path complaints, while preserving the 8–13 h fast lane where it is economically and experientially justified. It also makes R3 systematic for reopened claims.

**Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues**

It deliberately accepts that low-risk accessory fast paths will still have higher complaint rates than manual paths: 6–8% fast versus 3–4% manual. It does not solve repeated evidence requests or the 140 h unclear-defect segment. Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%, which could overwhelm the 7 reviewers if not managed. It also does not directly fix wrong replacement part selection; that is left to Redesign C.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first intake and customer co-production

**Redesigned process flow**

`Intake claim -> Evidence checklist + document upload portal -> Automated completeness check ->`  
- **Complete evidence, low-risk** -> segment-aware eligibility routing as in Redesign A  
- **Complete evidence, high-value** -> `Manual eligibility review` / `Fraud/risk review`  
- **Incomplete evidence** -> dedicated `Evidence completion track` with SLA, proactive reminders, one-click upload, and assisted phone/chat fallback for low digital literacy  
- **No response after defined SLA** -> graceful abandonment with re-entry path, not silent loss  
- After evidence completion -> `Technical decision -> Fulfillment or repair -> Quality/compliance close`  
- Reopened claims receive an evidence re-check before manual review.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the 22% unclear defect/missing evidence segment, the 26% of evidence-request cases that repeat, the 890 `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` edges with mean 34 h and p90 88 h, and the 390 abandoned claims. It also supports Standard low-risk customers, who tolerate 72 h if status visibility is good.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The current process lets incomplete claims enter eligibility review, then bounces them back for evidence, creating a slow loop: `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` at mean 34 h. Redesign B moves evidence completion upstream, before eligibility assessment. The prompt states a new evidence portal could reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%. Completeness before decision should improve decision quality, reduce manual-review rework, reduce abandonment caused by repeated chasing, and shorten the 140 h median for unclear cases.

**Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues**

It deliberately trades some customer effort for speed and quality. The prompt warns the portal may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy, so an assisted fallback is mandatory, not optional. It does not by itself enforce R1 or R2 for high-value claims. It also does not fix wrong replacement part selection or repair rework. If the portal merely deflects incomplete cases to human support, labor cost may shift rather than disappear.

## 4. Redesign C: Post-decision fulfillment quality and recovery

**Redesigned process flow**

After technical decision:

- **Replacement path:** `Part-selection verification / pre-dispatch quality gate` for high-value devices and, where economically justified, mid-risk devices -> `Dispatch replacement -> Proactive delivery tracking -> Quality/compliance close`
- **Repair path:** `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device -> Quality check` with first-time-right repair checklist, technician certification for high-value devices, and a part-price confirmation SLA to reduce repair-quote delay
- **Complaint/reopen path:** `Complaint triage within 24 h -> Frontline recovery with authority for low-risk cases -> Specialist recovery for high-value/complex cases -> Escalation board reserved for systemic cases`, with asynchronous case review augmenting the daily 45-minute board
- Reopened claims must pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, satisfying R3.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the 14.1% complaint rate on Fast approval cases, the 18–22% complaint rate for high-value devices on fast paths, the `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` long tail of mean 96 h and p90 180 h, the 12.5% rework repair rate for high-value devices, and the expected wrong-replacement cost of EUR 410 for high-value devices versus EUR 95 for low-risk accessories.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The temporal profile links `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` to incorrect replacement part selection. A lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate can catch wrong replacement selection but adds 6–18 h. Redesign C spends that 6–18 h where the expected loss is large: high-value devices. It also uses the currently underused quality-check capacity to improve repair quality and replaces the poorly scalable escalation board with tiered recovery. The goal is to reduce downstream complaints, reshipments, service credits, and rework rather than merely accelerating the first dispatch.

**Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues**

It deliberately adds 6–18 h to some replacement paths. If applied to Premium low-risk accessories, it could push the 8 h fast path toward or beyond the 24 h service-credit threshold. It does not reduce upstream eligibility delay or evidence-loop delay. It may not pay for low-risk accessories, where wrong replacement costs only EUR 95. Repair-quote delay may persist if part-price confirmation is not addressed. It also risks over-crediting if frontline recovery authority is too broad.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Segment-aware gated fast lane

**Failure modes**

1. The auto-screen update increases manual-review inflow by 15–35%. With only 7 reviewers, 36 productive hours per week, and queue growth above 82% utilization, manual eligibility becomes a severe bottleneck. High-value and unclear cases wait longer, worsening Premium high-value cycle time and service-credit exposure.
2. Conservative rules push borderline low-risk accessories into manual review, eroding the 8–13 h fast path. Premium low-risk accessories may exceed the 24 h service-credit threshold, while Standard low-risk customers lose speed they did not need to lose.
3. Misconfigured thresholds create new R1/R2 exceptions, for example if claim value is missing or defect type is miscoded. Compliance risk shifts into new low-frequency paths rather than disappearing, especially because the discovered model already has low precision.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

From auto/manual eligibility into `Fraud/risk review`, where only 2 specialists already face Monday and post-campaign peaks, and into `Manual eligibility review` for reopened claims. If evidence loops remain, `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` at mean 34 h persists.

### Redesign B: Evidence-first intake and customer co-production

**Failure modes**

1. The portal increases abandonment among customers with low digital literacy. The unclear-defect segment, already 22% of cases with the highest abandonment, may abandon more. A cycle-time improvement could be partly an artifact of losing customers.
2. The automated completeness check produces false positives or false negatives. Customers submit documents that pass the portal but are insufficient for technical decision, pushing rework to manual reviewers. Others bypass the portal through assisted channels, creating labor cost.
3. The portal reduces repeated evidence requests but does not resolve unclear defects. High-value and complex cases still require manual and fraud review, so the 2 fraud/risk specialists become the next constraint.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

From the `Request missing evidence` loop into `Fraud/risk review` and `Manual eligibility review` for high-value cases, into portal support and assisted-channel staffing, and potentially into `Repair quote` part-price confirmation as more complete cases reach repair.

### Redesign C: Post-decision fulfillment quality and recovery

**Failure modes**

1. The pre-dispatch quality gate adds 6–18 h. If applied to Premium low-risk accessories, it can push the median 8 h path toward or beyond the 24 h service-credit threshold. If applied broadly, the 2 quality-check inspectors, currently underused, can become a queue.
2. The gate catches wrong part selection but not all failure modes. Low-risk wrong replacements cost EUR 95 and may not justify the gate, while high-value wrong replacements cost EUR 410 but may still slip through if the defect is intermittent or the part database is incomplete.
3. Frontline complaint recovery with broad authority may over-credit or over-refund, increasing service-credit cost. Replacing the daily escalation board with asynchronous review may lose the strong satisfaction effect for complex cases.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

From the dispatch/complaint long tail into `Quality check` inspectors and `Repair device` technicians, and into a new specialist recovery team. Upstream `Manual eligibility review` remains pressured for reopened claims unless Redesigns A and B reduce complaint volume.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0: Instrumentation and baseline, before any pilot**

Add missing event attributes: evidence-completeness timestamp, segment, defect type, claim value, rule outcomes for R1/R2/R3, complaint reason, part-selection correctness, abandonment reason, and digital-literacy flag. Re-run process mining on the baseline, stratified by segment. This is necessary because the current discovered model has low precision and the performance DFG is only a selected edge set.

**Stage 1: First pilot — Redesign A narrow, low-risk accessories only**

Pilot segment-aware auto-screen rules for Premium and Standard low-risk accessories with complete evidence. Keep high-value devices, unclear defects, and incomplete evidence on the current manual path. Use one region or channel as pilot and another as control.

Metrics to monitor:

- Median and p90 cycle time by segment
- Complaint/reopen rate
- R1/R2/R3 violations
- Manual-review inflow increase
- Reviewer utilization and queue
- Service-credit exposure, especially Premium low-risk accessories above 24 h
- Direct cost and downstream rework cost

Stop/go thresholds:

- Go if manual-review inflow increases by no more than about 15%, reviewer utilization stays at or below 82%, there are zero high-value R1/R2 violations, low-risk complaint rate stays at or below about 8%, and Premium low-risk median cycle time stays at or below 24 h.
- Stop if manual inflow exceeds about 35%, reviewer utilization exceeds 82%, any high-value R1/R2 violation occurs, or Premium low-risk service credits increase materially.

**Stage 2: Pilot Redesign B for unclear evidence and incomplete low-risk cases**

Pilot the evidence portal with an assisted fallback for low digital literacy. Use a stepped-wedge or randomized encouragement design.

Metrics to monitor:

- Repeated evidence requests, target reduction 30–45%
- Abandonment rate overall and by digital-literacy stratum
- Median cycle time for the unclear-defect segment, baseline 140 h
- Downstream complaint rate
- Portal completion versus assisted-channel use
- Manual-review and fraud/risk queue

Stop/go thresholds:

- Go if repeated evidence requests fall by at least 30%, abandonment does not increase by more than about 2–3 percentage points overall, abandonment among low digital literacy does not increase when the assisted fallback is available, and unclear-segment median cycle time falls by at least 30% without increasing complaints.
- Stop if abandonment rises materially among low digital literacy customers or assisted-channel labor cost exceeds the value of reduced manual loops.

**Stage 3: Pilot Redesign C for high-value devices and repair**

Pilot the pre-dispatch quality gate for high-value replacements, first-time-right repair practices, part-price confirmation SLA, and tiered complaint recovery.

Metrics to monitor:

- Wrong replacement rate
- High-value complaint/reopen rate, baseline 18–22% on fast path
- Rework repair rate, baseline 12.5% for high-value devices
- `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` p90, baseline 180 h
- Added cycle time from the gate, expected 6–18 h
- Quality-check utilization
- Service credits, refunds, reshipments

Stop/go thresholds:

- Go if high-value complaint/reopen falls toward 8–10%, rework repair falls toward 6–8%, added cycle time stays at or below 18 h, quality-check utilization stays at or below 82%, and Premium low-risk accessories are not pushed above 24 h if included.
- Stop if the quality-check queue grows sharply, added delay exceeds 18 h, or complaint reduction is not observed after 4–6 weeks.

**Stage 4: Integrated scale-up and displacement check**

Combine A, B, and C by segment: low-risk complete evidence uses the gated fast lane; unclear or incomplete evidence uses the evidence-first track; high-value devices use manual/fraud review plus pre-dispatch quality and repair quality. Use control regions and difference-in-differences or interrupted time-series comparison.

Metrics to monitor:

- End-to-end median and p90 cycle time by segment
- Cost per case including complaints, reshipments, service credits, and rework
- Complaint/reopen after close
- Abandonment
- Compliance exceptions R1/R2/R3
- Resource utilization across manual review, fraud/risk, repair quote, quality check, and recovery

Stop/go thresholds:

- Scale if improvements persist in pilot versus control and no downstream displacement appears.
- Roll back or redesign if `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` or `Escalation board` volume increases, if `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` tail does not shrink, if abandonment increases, or if any bottleneck exceeds 82% utilization.

**How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream**

Do not judge success by first-pass cycle time alone. Track downstream displacement indicators: `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` baseline 310 edges, `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` baseline 120 edges, `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` baseline 260 edges with mean 96 h, `Repair device -> Customer complaint/reopen` baseline 30 edges, `Quality check -> Rework repair` baseline 45 edges, repeated `Request missing evidence`, and abandonment. If a redesign reduces first-pass cycle time but increases these downstream flows, the improvement is shifted, not real. Re-run process mining on pilot data and check whether fitness, precision, and conformance deviations improve. Stratify by segment and use p90, not only mean. Finally, compare cost after rework, because the prompt shows the fast path is only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits, not 31%.
