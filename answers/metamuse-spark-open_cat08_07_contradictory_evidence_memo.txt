## 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Committee - Fiber Operations & Customer Operations
**From:** Process Intelligence & Operations Design Office
**Re:** Pilot Redesign R Decision - Scale Selectively, Do Not Scale Nationally

**Recommendation: Do not scale Redesign R nationally. Adopt Hybrid Option E with embedded controlled measurement (Option D).**

Redesign R delivers a real but narrow win and creates systemic tail risk. For one-third of cases - simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot) - median duration improved by 6 days, p90 improved by 8 days, and first-time-right improved by +3pp. Handovers dropped sharply and the merged validation/scheduling step worked.

The same redesign failed for the other two-thirds. Overall median improved (14.2d -> 10.8d) but p90 worsened (31d -> 38.5d) and p95 worsened severely (45d -> 62d). First-time-right fell 82% -> 76%, complaints 6.1% -> 9.8%, second visits 11% -> 18%, and billing-before-activation became a new failure path. The trade-off is not random noise: it is a heterogeneity effect.

The choice is therefore not scale vs. rollback. It is speed-to-appointment vs. install-right-and-bill-right.

National scaling (A) would institutionalize three unresolved risks: 1) a long-tail dominated by rework, with Billing Start before successful Activation Test in frequent alignment deviations, 2) missing Permit Checks for older apartments that create compliance stoppages later, and 3) a coordinator bottleneck (93% utilization) that has already made the process less resilient to absence.

Full rollback (B) would waste a proven gain for simple houses and the accountability benefit of coordinator ownership.

We propose:

**Scale now:** Coordinator ownership + merged validation/scheduling **only for Simple house / low building risk** with two hard guardrails: no survey skipping, no billing before activation test passes.

**Pause nationally:** Predictive survey skipping for medium-risk apartments and old buildings; post-billing activation; and any expansion until downstream cost and permit compliance are measured.

**Run for 8 weeks:** A controlled regional experiment in two pilot and two baseline regions with full cost, reason codes, and conformance monitoring. The investment in measurement is cheaper than locking in a +60% complaint-tail process.

Success is scale conditional on proof, not hope.

## 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness (0.94) but low precision (0.51):** Fitness measures "can the model replay the log?" - yes, because most pilot cases follow the new coordinator path. Precision measures "does the model allow only the log?" - no. The pilot model allows almost any combination of *skip survey, manual activation, second visit, bill early* because those variations were observed. Discovery therefore over-generalized from observed non-conformance. High fitness here is not quality proof; low precision flags loss of process control. Generalization drop 0.70->0.56 reinforces that the model will likely fit future cases worse despite fitting pilot cases well.

**Improved median but worse p90/p95:** No contradiction when effect is heterogeneous and rework is tail-driven. 34% simple houses gained ~6d with no rework, pulling median down. The remaining ~48% apartment/old-building cases where the survey-skip or permit-skip was wrong incurred a second-visit or complaint loop that added 12-25d at p90. Median reflects the winner majority; p95 reflects the failure loop. Improved Installation->Activation for no one cannot be seen in median; Billing->Complaint long tail dominates percentile tails.

**Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Handover-of-work counts formal transfers. Working-together network captures who must collaborate when exceptions occur. Ownership reduced routine transfers (34%->12% with 3+ handovers) but centralized tacit knowledge and decision authority in one coordinator (utilization 93%). When that coordinator is absent, there is no shared context to pick up the case, so cases stall. Efficiency under normal conditions traded for brittleness under variation. Field-survey 52% idle vs second-visit 92% busy shows capacity imbalance, not net saving.

**Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:** Visible cost (EUR 161 vs 184) counts steps before complaint, i.e., saved survey labor. Full estimated cost (EUR 207 vs 198) adds revisits/complaints. The redesign shifts cost downstream past the measurement boundary (excluded after week 4). It is classic local optimization: front-end saving that creates downstream failure demand. Without 60-day full cost, we report a false saving.

**Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings:** The building-risk predictive rule has asymmetric error cost. For low-risk houses, survey adds waiting with little information value -> skipping helps. For medium-risk apartments and old permit-sensitive buildings, survey/permit provides high information value -> skipping produces Failed visits (13%->19%), Manual activations (8%->19%), and Missing permit deviations that later block activation. Segment-level DFG and alignment findings isolate where the rule systematically fails.

## 5. Value-of-information analysis

Ranked by ability to change the A/B/C/E choice now, especially to prevent tail/compliance regret and to quantify true economics.

**1. M4 - Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start**
*Uncertainty:* Is EUR 161 vs 184 real saving or accounting illusion vs EUR 207 vs 198 loss? *Decision change:* If full cost remains lower for simple houses but higher overall, it justifies E over A and quantifies budget for rework reduction. If full cost still higher even for simple houses, pause that scale. Without M4 any financial decision is blind.

**2. M6 - Validate building-risk predictive rule vs field-survey outcomes (tie with M1)**
*Uncertainty:* How often is "skip survey" prediction wrong for medium-risk apartments/old buildings? *Decision change:* Directly separates winner vs loser segments. A low positive predictive value kills A and justifies pausing skipping. High accuracy for low-risk only would confirm E segmentation.

**3. M1 - Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum**
*Uncertainty:* Causal vs selection bias due to pilot building mix and incomplete week 1-3 data. *Decision change:* Only randomization proves that skipping *causes* p90 +12-25d and FTR -8 to -11pp. Would convert plausible interpretation into evidence for rule retuning or abandonment. Higher cost to run, so just below M6.

**4. M3 - Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint, Second visit**
*Uncertainty:* Why did failures spike? Rule error, skill, parts, customer not ready? *Decision change:* Distinguishes fixable process (retrain, checklist) vs. design (rule) vs. external. Low cost/high speed, enables targeted fix instead of wholesale rollback. Essential for DFG/temporal diagnosis.

**5. M7 - Conformance monitoring for Billing before Activation and Permit absence**
*Uncertainty:* How prevalent are true compliance breaches vs. logging artifacts? *Decision change:* Immediate risk control. If violation rate >2-3%, national scale politically untenable. Can be deployed in days as hard system block, preventing further harm while measuring.

**6. M2 - Instrument start timestamps for Installation, Activation Test, Manual activation**
*Uncertainty:* Is duration change waiting time (queue) vs. processing/rework? *Decision change:* Clarifies whether coordinator bottleneck (queueing) vs. rework loop drives tail, informing staffing vs. rule fix. Less decision-critical short-term than cost/compliance.

**7. M8 - Split customer experience: speed-to-appointment vs. working-service-after-install**
*Uncertainty:* Does NPS +21->+17 average hide divergent experiences? *Decision change:* If simple houses rate speed high and apartment failures rate "billed but not working" extremely low, it strengthens segment-specific E and counters median-only narratives. Informative but does not alter financial/compliance decision alone.

**8. M5 - Compare coordinator vs shared-team ownership under coordinator absence**
*Uncertainty:* Is resilience loss causal from single ownership? *Decision change:* Informs organizational design (backup ownership, shared context). Important for scaling ownership safely, but after guardrails for tail/cost are settled.

M4, M6, M3, M7 can be started in parallel within 2 weeks at low cost; they deliver >80% of decision value before any expansion.

## 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended Decision: Hybrid E + Embedded Controlled Experiment D.**

**Immediate action (Week 0-2):**
1. Enforce two non-negotiable guardrails in workflow system: (a) Billing Start blocked until Activation Test = PASS or documented manual override with reason code; (b) Permit check mandatory for Old building / permit-sensitive flag.
2. Keep Redesign R running only in pilot regions but with guardrails on. Do not expand nationally.
3. Start measurement M4, M6, M3, M7 immediately; instrument M2 in pilot regions.

**What to PAUSE nationally:**
- Predictive survey skipping for Apartment medium-risk (29% of pilot) and Old building permit-sensitive (18%).
- Post-billing activation path (Manual activation -> Billing start).
- Any hiring/freeze based on visible cost EUR 161 saving.

**What to SCALE now (controlled):**
- Single coordinator ownership + Combined validation/scheduling for **Simple house, low building risk only** (34% share) in all regions, with workload cap coordinator utilization <85% and documented backup owner to restore resilience. **What to MEASURE next (8-week window):**
- Full 60-day cost, stratified by segment (M4).
- Rule validation against survey outcomes (M6) and randomized skip test in one region per stratum (M1).
- Reason-coded failures and conformance dashboards (M3/M7) reviewed weekly.
- Temporal profiles with start timestamps (M2) to separate queue vs rework delay.

**Thresholds to justify broader rollout beyond simple houses:**
- For expansion segment: first-time-right >= baseline (>=81-82%), complaint within 30d <=7%, second visit <=12%, manual activation <9%, billing-before-activation violations =0%, permit compliance >=98%.
- p90 not worse than baseline +3 days and p95 not worse than baseline +5 days for that segment.
- Full downstream cost per case <= baseline EUR 198 with statistical confidence, and coordinator / second-visit utilization <85% sustained.
- Predictive rule precision >=90% for medium-risk before any skip permission.

If thresholds not met in 8 weeks, default to Option C (coordinator ownership only, no predictive skipping) and retune rule. National scaling without meeting tail, rework, and compliance thresholds would be indefensible.
