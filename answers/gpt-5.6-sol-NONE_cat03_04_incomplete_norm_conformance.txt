## 1. Conformance semantics

I apply the norm relation-by-relation, not as a globally closed list.

- **Forbidden behavior:** An observed relation explicitly prohibited by an applicable norm. A genuine instance is noncompliant even if rare. Examples are N3, T6, and E10.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** Required behavior is absent within the stated scope. This requires confirming both scope and measurement validity. A missing directly-follows edge may not prove that an “eventually” requirement failed.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** An observed relation is absent from the norm or explicitly unspecified, but not forbidden. Under the open-world assumption it is not a violation; it may still merit operational review.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** A desirable/common relation is absent. This is a soft exception, not noncompliance, unless another mandatory rule applies. Legitimate alternatives must be considered.
- **Likely data-quality error:** Evidence indicates the graph relation was created by incorrect linkage, missing references, extraction defects, or event typing. It should be repaired and should not automatically be attributed to business conduct.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** The business rule and measured relation use different semantics—for example, “eventually validate” versus “directly follows validation.” The observed graph can flag cases but cannot establish a breach.
- **Insufficient evidence:** Aggregated counts do not expose object identities, event order, event attributes, scope eligibility, or alternative paths needed to decide compliance.

The norm is **closed-world only** for explicit prohibitions and applicable mandatory requirements. Thus, an observed forbidden edge is presumptively noncompliant, while an absent mandatory edge is presumptively a gap subject to scope and evidence checks. For typical and unspecified areas, and for relations omitted from the norm, the interpretation is open-world: novelty alone is not a violation.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can show directly-follows relations along object lifecycles and their frequencies. It supports strong identification of the two observed N3 candidates because cancellation is immediately followed by dispatch for the same Order. Nevertheless, the underlying timelines must establish that “dispatch” was effective, occurred after a valid cancellation, and was not reversed or incorrectly linked.

It cannot, from summary counts alone, prove eventuality, explain intermediate events, establish observation-window completeness, or determine why a path differs. This is especially important for N1: its prose says every newly created standard Order should **eventually** enter validation, while the summary approximates that rule with a direct-follow relation. If an Order follows `Create -> Review -> Validate`, it satisfies eventual validation but fails the direct-edge proxy. Treating an eventually rule as directly-follows systematically creates false positives. The two N1 cases therefore require reachability analysis over full histories, not merely edge comparison.

N4 similarly applies only to Packages intended for external shipment. The denominator states that all 200 are externally shipped, so scope appears applicable. Yet the two absent direct edges might reflect a genuine packing omission or an intermediate scan/repack event. The mandatory requirement’s wording and its representation should be clarified.

For N5, 92 payment paths support the typical pattern; four write-offs are expressly legitimate exceptions, and 14 open-ended cases are not automatically deviations. Their interpretation depends on invoice age, credit terms, and log horizon. `Validate -> Issue Invoice`, `Reopen -> Close`, and compliance-review paths are not violations merely because they are unlisted or unspecified.

Counts indicate prevalence, not severity. Two forbidden shipments can be more important than 14 missing typical successors.

### OTG

An OTG establishes that object types interact, co-occur at birth/death, or otherwise relate, generally with aggregate weights. It can reveal the prohibited Order–Payment cobirth under T6 and show that Package–Delivery and Order–Item interactions exist.

It cannot normally establish that **every relevant event** has the required objects. For example, Package–Delivery OTG weight 200 coexists with only 198 Dispatch–Delivery ET–OT links. The OTG edge may include Delivery interactions from other events and therefore cannot cure the event-level E6 gap. Conversely, an OTG count can look normal while individual required events are malformed.

T1 is scoped to Add Item interactions. The Order–Item OTG weight 1450 suggests broad interaction, but ET–OT reports only 1448 Add Item–Order links. Event-level analysis is therefore more probative for the two suspected omissions.

The seven Package–Delivery codeath edges are unregulated and have a plausible construction: shared lifecycle-ending confirmation. Their rarity is irrelevant because no minimum frequency or prohibition exists. Order–Customer and Order–RiskCase edges are explicitly unspecified. Invoice–Payment weight 92 realizes a typical relation but does not require all invoices to have payments.

A relation can thus be acceptable at OTG abstraction yet suspicious at ET–OT abstraction: the Package and Delivery types interact overall, while two specific Dispatch events lack the mandatory Delivery participant.

### ET–OT

ET–OT associations are best suited to event-scoped participation requirements. E1, E3–E5, and E7 appear fully represented by the supplied counts. The 1448 Add Item–Order links against 1450 Add Item–Item links identify two E2 candidates, while 198 Dispatch–Delivery links against 200 Dispatch–Package links identify two E6 candidates.

Still, aggregate weights do not prove one-to-one event coverage. One event associated with two objects could inflate a count and mask another event with none. Compliance should be calculated per event ID and with cardinality rules.

The 93 Record Payment–Payment versus 92 Record Payment–Invoice links expose one E8 gap. Unapplied cash is operationally legitimate, but E8’s exception requires explicit typing, and none is present. It is therefore a current mandatory metadata/association gap, not a conclusively improper payment.

The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge directly matches forbidden E10. At the graph level it is a forbidden observation; at the source-system level, the supplied join explanation makes a data-quality defect the most likely cause. This illustrates dual classification: formally nonconforming extracted data, probably not forbidden business behavior.

Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase is allowed but unspecified, and its Customer association is also unregulated under this norm. Neither becomes a violation through absence from the normative ET–OT graph.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory**
  - Satisfied evidence: E1 100/100; E3 1450; E4 200; E5 200; E7 93; broadly T1 and T2.
  - Candidate gaps: E2 has 2 Add Item events without Orders; E6/T2 has 2 Dispatch events without Deliveries; E8 has 1 unlabeled payment without Invoice.
  - N1 has 2 direct-edge gaps, but only violates the business requirement if eventual validation is absent.
  - N4 has 2 direct-edge gaps among externally shipped Packages, unless intervening events expose a proxy mismatch.

- **Forbidden**
  - N3: 2 cancellation-to-dispatch cases, if the same Orders truly remained cancelled when dispatch occurred.
  - T6: 1 Order–Payment cobirth, if genuine rather than inference/linkage error.
  - E10: 1 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge; forbidden in extracted data but probably caused by the stated faulty join.

- **Typical**
  - N2: 83 direct Validate-to-Close paths conform to a typical pattern; the 72 invoice intermediates are not violations.
  - N5/T3: 92 payment paths/interactions are typical; four write-offs are legitimate exceptions.
  - Fourteen no-successor invoices are typical-pattern absences only; aging, credit terms, and horizon determine concern.

- **Unregulated or explicitly unspecified**
  - `Validate -> Issue Invoice`, Order–Customer, Order–RiskCase, review–Customer, `Reopen -> Close`, and Package–Delivery codeath.
  - Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase is legitimate but unspecified.
  - These change classification only if another applicable policy imposes a mandatory or forbidden rule, or source review reveals incorrect linkage.

def semantic_layer(raw, metadata):
    """Annotate, but never overwrite, PM4Py diagnostics."""
    assessments = []
    for rule_id, meta in metadata.items():
        status = meta["status"]
        if status == "forbidden":
            interpretation = "Observed match is presumptively noncompliant."

            interpretation = (

            "scope": meta.get("scope"),
            "exception": meta.get("exception"),
            "semantic": meta.get("semantic"),
            "interpretation": interpretation,
            # Raw family result is referenced, not modified.
            "raw_family_diagnostic": raw[meta["family"]],

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 mixes eventuality with direct-follow approximation.**  
   Risk: false violations when reviews intervene.  
   Clarification: require eventual reachability to validation within a defined period; permit listed intermediates.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

2. **“Standard Order” is undefined.**  
   Risk: exceptional, test, imported, or cancelled-at-creation Orders may be wrongly included.  
   Clarification: define eligibility attributes and exclusions.  
   Status: **mandatory** scope definition.

3. **N4’s “intended for external shipment” is not operationalized.**  
   Risk: incorrect denominators and inconsistent treatment of pickup/internal transfer Packages.  
   Clarification: define intent by shipment mode/status and when it is evaluated.  
   Status: **mandatory** scope definition.

4. **N4 does not say whether packing must directly precede dispatch.**  
   Risk: scans or repacking create false gaps, while old packing may be incorrectly accepted.  
   Clarification: require a prior successful pack, with allowed intermediates and repack rules.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

5. **N3 lacks cancellation-state and reversal semantics.**  
   Risk: reinstated Orders or corrected cancellations may appear forbidden.  
   Clarification: forbid effective dispatch while cancellation remains effective; define reversal and timestamp precedence.  
   Status: **forbidden**.

6. **T2/E6 do not define whether a carrier-reference attribute can substitute for a Delivery object.**  
   Risk: systems disagree on conformance and lose traceability.  
   Clarification: require a Delivery object by dispatch, or specify a temporary attribute exception and conversion deadline.  
   Status: preferably **mandatory**.

7. **E8’s unapplied-cash typing is underspecified.**  
   Risk: legitimate cash is flagged or missing invoice links are excused informally.  
   Clarification: define event type/flag, authority, lifecycle, and reconciliation deadline.  
   Status: **mandatory**, with an explicit exception.

8. **Typical invoice timing and acceptable outcomes are undefined.**  
   Risk: 14 open invoices cannot be distinguished from overdue failures.  
   Clarification: define aging bands and legitimate successors such as write-off, credit note, void, or open terms.  
   Status: **typical**.

9. **T6 “same business event” and cobirth inference are undefined.**  
   Risk: timestamp coarseness creates false prohibited cobirths.  
   Clarification: base cobirth on common source transaction/event ID, not timestamp equality alone.  
   Status: **forbidden**.

10. **Codeath has no declared treatment.**  
    Risk: tools or analysts may inconsistently flag benign lifecycle co-termination.  
    Clarification: state that Package–Delivery codeath is outside this norm unless separately governed.  
    Status: explicitly **unspecified**.

11. **Observation-window and late-arriving-event rules are absent.**  
    Risk: open invoices and missing successors are overstated.  
    Clarification: define completeness lag and censoring treatment.  
    Status: **mandatory** for conformance measurement.

12. **No cardinality rules are stated.**  
    Risk: aggregate edge counts can hide events with zero or multiple required objects.  
    Clarification: define minimum/exact cardinality per E-rule and event.  
    Status: **mandatory**.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

### Immediate containment

1. Prioritize the two N3 cases: suspend any still-actionable shipment, notify fulfilment control, and verify cancellation effectiveness, package identity, and reversals.
2. Quarantine the T6 cobirth case from automated compliance conclusions and inspect whether an Order and Payment were genuinely created by one business event.
3. Block or filter Sensor Heartbeat joins to financial objects while preserving evidence of the faulty extraction.
4. Flag the two Dispatch events without Delivery objects for operational reconciliation.

### Data repair

- Replace reused numeric joins with typed composite keys and provenance checks; remove the false E10 edge after audit logging.
- Materialize or link Delivery objects from the two carrier references where source evidence supports them.
- Repair the two missing Add Item–Order associations.
- For the unmatched payment, either link the correct Invoice or explicitly classify it as unapplied cash—never infer the exception merely from absence.
- Recompute all three graphs after repairs and retain before/after diagnostics.

### Process investigation

- Reconstruct object-level histories for the two N3 cases, the N1 direct-edge gaps, and the N4 gaps.
- Determine whether the N1 Orders were eventually validated after an unrecorded/intermediate review.
- Verify whether the two Packages were packed before dispatch with intervening scans.
- Age the 14 invoices and distinguish open terms, missing data, write-offs, credit notes, and overdue receivables.
- Validate the T6 source transaction and cobirth algorithm.

### Norm revision

- Separate eventual-reachability rules from direct-follow proxies.
- Operationalize scope, exception typing, cardinality, observation horizons, cancellation reversals, and carrier-reference treatment.
- Keep review–Customer, RiskCase relations, reopening, and codeath explicitly unspecified unless the business chooses to govern them.

### Monitoring

- Create per-event mandatory coverage metrics for E1–E8 rather than relying only on weights.
- Alert immediately on N3, T6, and E10 observations, with a data-quality triage branch.
- Monitor N1 and N4 using reachability plus direct-follow diagnostics separately.
- Age typical N5 exceptions instead of treating low frequency as noncompliance.
- Track join-integrity failures, orphan objects, late events, and repaired-link rates.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The most robust conclusions are that the extracted graph contains two N3 forbidden patterns, one T6 forbidden cobirth, one E10 forbidden association, and concrete aggregate gaps for E2, E6, and E8. The severity of the N3 cases depends on confirming object identity and effective cancellation state.

Interpretations of `Validate -> Issue Invoice`, compliance-review relations, reopening, codeath, and customer interactions depend on the norm’s intentional incompleteness and are not violations under this norm. The E10 finding likely depends on a known data-quality error; the Delivery, Add Item, payment, and cobirth findings may also involve linkage or typing defects. Whether N1 and N4 were truly breached, whether invoices are improperly unresolved, and whether aggregate counts conceal cardinality defects cannot be decided from graph summaries alone; object-level events, scope attributes, timestamps, exceptions, and observation-window evidence are required.
