## 2. Rule Interpretation

1. **RR1 — Drafter/approver separation**
   - For each case, identify the human who performed `Draft_Change_Request` and the human who performed `Approve_Change`.
   - Normalize aliases first.
   - The same human violates RR1 regardless of risk level. No emergency exception to RR1 is stated.
   - A delegated approval role does not change the identity of the approver: if Ben drafts under one identity and approves under another, both resolve to Ben.

2. **RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation**
   - For each case, identify the human who performed `Implement_Change` and the human who performed `Verify_Implementation`.
   - If verification used a service account, the human must first be established through reliable session evidence.
   - If the verified human is also the implementer, RR2 fails.
   - RR2’s literal same-human prohibition does not independently establish whether the verifier otherwise held an authorized verification role. That broader qualification remains a separate event-time control judgment.

3. **RR3 — High-risk approval authority**
   - For a non-emergency HIGH-risk case, the approver must be the standing QM or a valid QM delegate.
   - A delegation is valid only when:
     1. its effective interval covers the event;
     2. its scope covers the case;
     3. any principal-absence condition is satisfied;
     4. the delegation was supported by evidence existing at the time of use; and
     5. no prohibited subdelegation occurred.
   - A standing role is evaluated from the role interval effective on the event date, not from a later master-data snapshot.
   - For an emergency change, RR4 supplies the IC/valid-IC-delegate approval path. It does not waive RR1, RR2, delegation-chain restrictions, or the retrospective-review requirement.

4. **RR4 — Emergency substitution**
   - The emergency flag must be credible and supported by a safety rationale; a flag alone is not conclusive proof.
   - The IC or valid IC delegate must approve before implementation.
   - A QM or valid QM delegate must perform the retrospective review within two business days.
   - **Business-day counting:** the implementation day is day zero. The next site-business day is business day one, and the end of the second site-business day is the deadline. Weekends and site holidays are excluded. For the supplied August dates, 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 are non-business days and there are no listed holidays.
   - For a Tuesday, 2026-08-11 implementation, the deadline under this convention is end of Thursday, 2026-08-13. Friday, 2026-08-14 is late. The conclusion is unchanged under an event-day-inclusive convention.

5. **Principal acting while on leave**
   - Leave is evidence of unavailability and can satisfy a delegation’s principal-absence condition.
   - Leave does **not automatically erase** a standing role unless a policy or access rule says that it does. Thus, Asha’s CHG-504 approval is technically role-conformant under the literal rules, but it is a governance exception if she lacked formal recall authority.
   - For Ben’s CHG-506 approval, his sick leave does not invalidate D-QM-01 by its terms, but his denial and the office-endpoint evidence create a serious attribution problem.
   - Whether leave was formally overridden, recalled, or simply ignored is a separate judgment.

6. **Later database entry as delegation evidence**
   - A QMS record created after the delegated action cannot, by itself, prove that a delegation was valid at the earlier event time.
   - D-QM-01 is supportable because a signed PDF existed on 2026-08-09, before both its effective interval and the approvals. The later database row is corroborative or migration evidence, not the sole proof.
   - D-QM-02 and D-IC-03 have controlled or registered evidence predating use.
   - D-IC-04 fails because of the prohibited-subdelegation condition, irrespective of when the email was sent.

7. **RR5 — Identity evidence**
   - Aliases are normalized to a stable person identity before segregation checks.
   - Service-account actions require reliable, case-specific mapping to an authorized human.
   - Identity mapping and authorization are separate: CHG-509’s reliable mapping to Eva establishes who acted, but it also reveals that she was no longer an Independent Verifier.

8. **RR6 — Suspicion versus proof**
   - Repeated pairs, reciprocal workflows, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are risk indicators.
   - They are not proof of collusion. A finding of collusion would require evidence of coordination, intentional circumvention, and often communication or benefit.

def assess_delegation(d, case_id, event_scope, event_time):
    checks = {
        "effective_interval": bool(d["start"] <= event_time <= d["end"]),
        "scope_category_match": bool(d["scope"] == event_scope),
        "scope_identifier_match": True,
        "principal_absence": True,
        "controlling_evidence_pre_event": bool(
            d["controlling_evidence_pre_event"]
        ),
        "no_prohibited_subdelegation": bool(
            not d["prohibited_subdelegation"]
        ),
    }

    if pd.notna(d["scope_detail"]):
        checks["scope_identifier_match"] = (
            case_incident.get(case_id) == d["scope_detail"]
        )
    checks["scope"] = (
        checks["scope_category_match"]
        and checks["scope_identifier_match"]
    )

    if bool(d["absence_required"]):
        checks["principal_absence"] = bool(
            pd.notna(d["absence_start"])
            and pd.notna(d["absence_end"])
            and d["absence_start"] <= event_time <= d["absence_end"]
        )

    registry_after_event = bool(
        pd.notna(d["registry_created"])
        and d["registry_created"] > event_time
    )
    strict_valid = all(checks.values())
    provisional_valid = (
        all(v for k, v in checks.items() if k != "scope")
        and checks["scope_category_match"]
    )

    notes = []
    if not checks["scope_identifier_match"]:
        notes.append("Formal incident/scope identifier not established")
    if registry_after_event:
        notes.append(
            "Registry entry is later than event; controlling evidence must "
            "independently predate use"
        )
    notes.append(d["evidence_note"])

    return {
        "delegation_id": d["delegation_id"],
        "delegated_role": d["delegated_role"],
        "valid": strict_valid,
        "provisional_scope_only": provisional_valid and not strict_valid,
        "checks": checks,
        "registry_after_event": registry_after_event,
        "notes": "; ".join(notes),
    }

def graph_evidence_rows(graph_object):
    if graph_object is None:
        return []

    if isinstance(graph_object, tuple):
        graph_object = graph_object[0]

    graph = graph_object
    if not hasattr(graph, "nodes") and hasattr(graph_object, "graph"):
        graph = graph_object.graph

    rows = []
    try:
        nodes = list(graph.nodes(data=True))
    except TypeError:
        nodes = [(node, {}) for node in graph.nodes()]

    for node, data in nodes:
        name = data.get("name", str(node))
        rows.append({
            "source": str(name),
            "target": "",
            "weight": data.get("weight", data.get("frequency", 1)),
            "relation": "node",
        })

    try:
        edges = list(graph.edges(data=True))
    except TypeError:
        edges = []
        for edge in graph.edges():
            if len(edge) >= 3:
                edges.append((edge[0], edge[1], edge[2]))
            else:
                edges.append((edge[0], edge[1], {}))

    for source, target, data in edges:
        weight = data.get(
            "weight",
            data.get("frequency", data.get("count", 1)),
        )
        rows.append({
            "source": str(source),
            "target": str(target),
            "weight": weight,
            "relation": "edge",
        })
    return rows

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation**
   - Require a controlled delegation form with principal, delegate, role, case/incident scope, start/end, absence condition, and prohibited-subdelegation flag.
   - Require independent approval of the delegation and prohibit creation through ordinary email.
   - Enforce subdelegation prohibition in the authorization service, not only in documentation. D-IC-04 should have been technically rejected.

2. **Effective-time recording**
   - Store separate fields for instrument creation, registration, effective start, effective end, signature time, and source-system import time.
   - Prevent backdated or retroactive registration.
   - Treat a post-event registry insert as migration evidence unless backed by independently timestamped authorization.
   - Preserve delegation versions and supersession history.

3. **Principal availability**
   - Integrate leave, on-call, and absence calendars with delegation eligibility.
   - Require a documented recall or temporary availability override if a principal on leave must act.
   - Alert the principal, delegate, and quality system when both principal and delegate act during the same absence period.

4. **Alias management**
   - Maintain a stable immutable `person_id` and map every login, email, badge, and service-session identity to it.
   - Apply segregation checks to `person_id`, not resource strings.
   - Test and approve alias merges; require retrospective recomputation of prior cases when aliases are consolidated.
   - Display alias relationships in the workflow so CHG-508 cannot appear to involve two people.

5. **Individual authentication**
   - Require FIDO or equivalent phishing-resistant authentication for approval and verification.
   - Prohibit shared human accounts and shared terminal sessions for privileged actions.
   - Replace human-operated `svc_qms` transactions with workload identities plus a cryptographically bound named-human session.
   - Bind the action to the individual challenge, device, case, role, and purpose.
   - Automatically suspend or challenge accounts during certified leave where operationally appropriate.

6. **Emergency review**
   - Require the CI/incident identifier, safety rationale, IC/delegate authority, and implementation authorization before emergency implementation.
   - Start the two-business-day retrospective SLA from implementation and send alerts before the deadline.
   - Prevent close or release until the retrospective review is valid and timely.
   - Require retrospective reviewers to be independent of the emergency implementation where feasible.

7. **Pair rotation**
   - Establish a formal rotation or independent-review threshold for high-risk drafters and approvers.
   - Use soft alerts for concentration, with manual review and documented risk acceptance.
   - Track repeated combinations by case category, scope, shift, and production cell so legitimate specialization is not treated as collusion.

8. **Network monitoring**
   - Normalize identities before calculating handover and working-together networks.
   - Separate trusted human attribution from unresolved service sessions.
   - Trend centrality, reciprocal activity, unusual after-hours use, and concentration over time.
   - Use network evidence to select audits or interviews, never as an automatic adverse conclusion.

9. **Evidential retention**
   - Retain signed delegation instruments, registry import logs, role versions, leave records, FIDO challenges, VPN/device certificates, badge logs, SSO and endpoint telemetry, email headers, emergency rationales, and immutable event-log snapshots.
   - Store evidence with chain-of-custody metadata and a site-specific retention schedule.
   - Record why an action was performed, not only which account performed it.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Excluding CHG-501, which appears to be a valid substitution, there are nine cases with an apparent deviation:

- **Six are strong rule-based control failures**: CHG-502, CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-508, CHG-509, and CHG-510.
- **Two are primarily identity/data-quality failures**: CHG-503 and CHG-506. Either could become an unauthorized-action finding if forensic evidence confirms the suspected person or impersonation.
- **One is primarily an availability/governance concern despite literal role conformance**: CHG-504.

Thus, roughly **two-thirds of the apparent deviations are likely genuine authority or control failures**, around **one-quarter are identity/data-quality problems**, and the remainder are policy-dependent governance or organizational-pattern risks. These proportions are directional rather than statistical, and CHG-505 and CHG-509 retain scope/role-matrix uncertainties.

The evidence does **not** presently prove collusion. It does establish explicit segregation failures, at least one unequivocally invalid delegation chain, a late emergency review, an unauthorized high-risk approval, and serious attribution weaknesses around service and delegated accounts.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **CHG-510 and the cooling-emergency chain:** preserve D-IC-04, workflow override, CI-44, safety-rationale, implementation, and Ben review evidence; determine operational impact. CHG-505 should be assessed in the same emergency-change safety review.
2. **CHG-503’s WS-DM-04 service session:** perform endpoint, token, badge, and neighboring-session forensics to determine whether the implementer self-verified or another person used the shared terminal.
3. **CHG-506’s `u_bk` sick-leave session:** establish the actual user of the office endpoint and perform an account-level sweep for other approvals made under Ben’s delegated QM authority, including the alias behavior in CHG-508.
