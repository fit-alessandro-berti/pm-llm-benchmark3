## 1. Interaction-graph description

The six jobs look independent in a Job-flattened log because each requires a distinct component — J1C1 through J6C6 — and no job precedes another. The object-centric graph shows they are coupled *laterally*, through shared Batch, Tool, Machine, ComponentLot, and Shipment objects. Delays propagate not along a job's own lifecycle but sideways, from one job to another via a shared object.

**Node types:** Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3).

**Edge list (qualifier in brackets).** Direct observations = stated O2O relations or events relating the objects; inferred = deduced from timing/co-location, not an explicit event.

```
Job        --[requires]--> Component        J1-C1 ... J6-C6        (direct)
ComponentLot--[contains]--> Component       L7:{C2,C5}, L8:{C1,C3} (direct)
Batch      --[batches]--> Job               B1:{J1,J2,J3}, B2:{J4,J5} (direct)
Shipment   --[consolidates]--> Job          S1:{J1,J4}, S2:{J2,J3}, S3:{J5,J6} (direct)
Batch Cure event: B1-OVEN-1-RACK-7-{J1,J2,J3}-{C1,C2,C3}            (direct, one event, 9 objects)
J3 --[acquires/holds 09:00-11:40]--> FIX-A ; J4 Machine Process relates {J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A} (direct event; hold partly inferred)
J5,J6 Final Test --[requires]--> CAL-3 on TEST-1                    (direct)
Quarantine/Release Lot events relate {L7,C2,C5,J2,J5}               (direct in OCEL; absent in Job flattening)
RACK-7 "cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35 gates B2                   (direct state, causal role inferred)
J4 waits for FIX-A while CNC-2 idle 10:05-10:47                     (inferred - no Wait for Tool event exists)
J2 entered B1 despite L7 quarantine (supervisor override, separate system) (inferred from cross-system record)
```

**Higher-order motifs (>2 types):**

1. **Batch synchronization star:** Job–Component pairs {J1/C1, J2/C2, J3/C3} converge on Batch B1, executed on Machine OVEN-1 with Tool RACK-7, in a *single* `Batch Cure` event. One event binds 4 object types; the earliest jobs cannot proceed until the latest arrives. B2 repeats the motif with J4/J5.
2. **Shared-lot coupling across batches and shipments:** L7 contains C2 (for J2, in B1/S2) and C5 (for J5, in B2/S3). A lot-level event therefore couples two jobs that share no batch, machine slot, or shipment. L8 similarly links J1 and J3.
3. **Tool-mediated machine blocking:** J3–FIX-A–CNC-2–J4: J4's Machine Process needs CNC-2 *and* FIX-A simultaneously; the machine is free but the fixture is not, so capacity exists yet is unusable. A parallel motif is J5–CAL-3–TEST-1–J6, and B1/B2–RACK-7–OVEN-1 (both batches share one rack).
4. **Consolidation fan-in:** S1 joins J1 (B1) and J4 (B2) — jobs from *different* batches whose completion times differ by hours — so batch/tool delays upstream in J4's chain are exported to J1 at dispatch.

Directly observed edges are the O2O table and multi-object events. Inferred edges are precisely the *waiting* relations: no event records "J4 waits for FIX-A" or "J1 waits for J4"; these are reconstructed from timestamps, idle intervals, and release times, and are therefore the most contestable part of the graph.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are counterfactual comparisons, not causal proof. Each states what would have had to be different.

**(a) B1 synchronization — J1 and J2.** Batch starts 12:05 for all. J1 ready 08:10  waits **3h55m**; J2 ready 08:25  **3h40m**; J3 ready 11:55  10m. *Counterfactual:* if the batch could start when the last member is ready and J3's readiness is exogenous, then only ~10m of each wait is inherent to batching; the remaining **3h45m (J1) and 3h30m (J2)** is synchronization-induced — avoidable only by splitting the batch, starting a partial batch, or delaying J1/J2's upstream work. Note the circularity risk: if J3 was itself delayed by FIX-A (see b), the "last arrival" is not exogenous, and part of this wait is imported from the fixture.

**(b) FIX-A contention — J4.** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; `Machine Process` starts 11:45  **1h55m** gap. J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 starts 5 minutes after release. CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47 (42m), so the machine was not the binding constraint for at least that window. *Counterfactual:* with a second fixture or no contention, processing could plausibly have begun near 09:50–10:05, implying **~1h40m–1h55m** of fixture-associated delay. The 5-minute release-to-start lag is consistent with, but does not prove, fixture gating; an unrecorded setup or operator absence could coincide.

**(c) S1 consolidation — J1.** J1 packed 13:30; S1 dispatches 19:25  **5h55m** packed-and-waiting. Benchmarks: S2 dispatches 25m and S3 35m after their last job is packed. *Counterfactual:* if J1 had shipped with S2/S3-like latency after its *own* packing (~25–35m), dispatch would be ~14:00; the excess attributable to waiting for J4 (packed 19:10, 15m before dispatch) is **~5h20m–5h30m**. This is policy-induced, not capacity-induced: nothing was being processed during most of it.

**(d) CAL-3 recalibration — J6.** J6 ready 16:05, tests 17:25–17:50  **80m** wait. J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20 (45m); TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 (50m). *Counterfactual:* absent the recalibration (or with a second calibrated tool), J6 could start ~16:35–16:40, so **~45–50m** is plausibly CAL-3-associated; the other ~30m is queueing behind J5's test itself. Whether recalibration was mandatory after J5, scheduled, or triggered by a failed check is not recorded — this decides if the delay is policy or quality-driven.

**(e) B2 / RACK-7 — J4 again.** J4 ready 13:10, B2 starts 14:50  **100m** wait; J5 ready 14:40 (10m wait). OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50 (115m), but RACK-7 is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, and B2 starts 15m later. *Counterfactual decomposition:* of J4's 100m, at most ~85m overlaps the rack's unavailability and ~90m overlaps waiting for J5; the two explanations overlap almost completely, so the wait cannot be uniquely apportioned — a second rack would not have helped before ~14:40 unless partial batches were also allowed, and vice versa. This non-identifiability is itself a finding.

**(f) Lot L7 quarantine.** Quarantine 10:20  Release 13:50 = **3h30m** lot-level block affecting J2 and J5. J5 did not proceed until after 13:50; J2 entered B1 (started 12:05) anyway via supervisor override. *Counterfactual:* without quarantine, J5's downstream path could have started up to 3h30m earlier at that gate — but J5's B2 readiness was 14:40, only 50m after release, so the lot gate may not have been on J5's critical path at all. The measurable effect may be near zero for J5 and exactly zero (by override) for J2, despite the dramatic 3h30m window.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job flattening.** *Visible:* clean per-job DFG, plausible medians (ReleaseStage 0.4h … PackDispatch 1.1h), no job >1.8 SD — hence "broadly normal." *Hidden/duplicated:* every convergence event (Batch Cure relating 9 objects) is either dropped or duplicated per job; lot events vanish by extraction rule; waiting is absorbed into innocent-looking edges (StageMachine median 1.1h conceals J1's 3h55m; PackDispatch median 1.1h conceals J1's 5h55m). *Misleading conclusion:* "process is stable, delays are random variation" — in fact the tails are structured, caused by other jobs.

**Machine flattening.** *Visible:* utilization 44–58%, normal sequences, short gaps. *Hidden:* *why* the machine is idle — OVEN-1's 115m idle stretch reads as slack, not as "blocked by RACK-7 plus batch partner." *Misleading conclusion:* "spare capacity exists, add work" — adding jobs would worsen batch/tool contention. This explains the utilization paradox: a job waits for a *combination* (machine  tool  batch partners  lot release). Each resource individually is often free; the joint conjunction is rarely satisfied, so waiting accumulates while every single-resource utilization stays moderate. Utilization measures busy time, not the probability that all required complements align.

**Batch flattening.** *Visible:* LoadCureUnload timing, batch duration. *Hidden:* member-level readiness dispersion (J1 ready 3h45m before J3) — the batch looks punctual from its own start. *Misleading conclusion:* "batches run on schedule," when the schedule is defined by the batch start itself.

**Tool flattening.** *Visible:* FIX-A hold 09:00–11:40, RACK-7 quarantine, CAL-3 recalibration windows. *Hidden:* which jobs were *blocked* (no queue events); tool log shows occupancy, not demand. *Misleading conclusion:* "tools are utilized, no queue observed" — queues are invisible without Wait-for-Tool events.

**ComponentLot flattening.** *Visible:* quarantine 10:20–13:50 as a lot lifecycle. *Hidden/duplicated:* downstream job impact depends entirely on whether events are related to Jobs; two integration pipelines give different Job logs from the same reality. *Misleading conclusion:* either "lot caused 3h30m delay" (over-attribution; J2 overrode, J5 possibly not critical-path) or "lot irrelevant" (Job view).

**Shipment flattening.** *Visible:* CreateLoadDispatch in ~1.1h total. *Hidden:* the pre-creation wait of packed jobs — J1's 5h55m sits *before* the shipment's measured lifecycle effectively begins. *Misleading conclusion:* "logistics is fast" while customers experience J1 dispatched 5h55m after packing.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — J4's compounded day (Job–Component–Tool–Machine–Batch–Tool–Machine–Shipment).** *Temporal precedence:* J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50  CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47  FIX-A released by J3 11:40  J4 processes 11:45  ready for B2 13:10  RACK-7 quarantined until 14:35, J5 ready 14:40  B2 starts 14:50  J4 packed 19:10  S1 dispatches 19:25. *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 was available before FIX-A was; OVEN-1 was available before RACK-7 and J5 were. At each gate a *different* complement bound. *Policy constraints:* batch all-or-nothing start; shipment consolidation with J1. *Causal speculation:* that FIX-A, then rack/partner, then consolidation *caused* the chain — plausible and consistent, but unrecorded operator availability or setup could substitute at the first gate.

**Narrative B — J1, the innocent bystander (Job–Batch–Machine–Tool … Job–Shipment).** *Precedence:* J1 ready 08:10, waits for B1 until 12:05 (with J2, J3; C1–C3; OVEN-1; RACK-7), packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25 with S1 partner J4. *Feasibility:* J1's own component (C1, lot L8) shows no block; every delay is imported — first from J3's readiness, then from J4's entire upstream chain (Narrative A). *Policy:* batching and consolidation rules convert other jobs' variance into J1's latency twice. *Speculation:* that J3's 11:55 readiness was itself FIX-A-driven (J3 held FIX-A until 11:40, ready 11:55 — a 15m gap consistent with this) would make J1's morning wait a *second-order* fixture effect; the timestamps invite but do not establish this.

**Narrative C — L7 couples strangers (ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch–Shipment ×2).** *Precedence:* Quarantine Lot 10:20 on {L7,C2,C5,J2,J5}  J2 enters B1 at 12:05 anyway (override, separate system)  Release Lot 13:50  J5 proceeds, ready for B2 14:40. *Feasibility:* the lot gate was physically binding only where not overridden. *Policy:* quarantine hold vs supervisor override authority — the coupling is policy-mediated and inconsistently enforced. *Speculation:* that J5's 14:40 readiness was set by the 13:50 release (50m later) rather than by its own processing; and that, had the override not existed, J2 would have delayed B1 further, exporting lot delay to J1 and J3. The inconsistent event-to-Job linkage means the Job log can neither confirm nor refute this.

**Narrative D — CAL-3 serializes the tail (Job–Machine–Tool–Job–Shipment).** J5 and J6 ready 16:00/16:05  J5 tests to 16:35  recalibration to 17:20  J6 tests to 17:50, with TEST-1 idle meanwhile; both then feed S3, which dispatches 35m after last pack. Precedence and feasibility are clear (single tool, single tester); whether recalibration is a fixed policy or triggered by J5's test outcome is unknown, and that unknown decides whether J6's ~45–50m is necessary quality time or schedulable overhead.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked (batch synchronization) explanation:**

1. Readiness timestamps for J1/J2 (08:10/08:25) prove to be planned, not actual, times — actual readiness near 11:55 would erase the sync wait.
2. Records showing the oven could not have started earlier anyway (e.g., RACK-7 or OVEN-1 unavailable 08:00–12:00 for B1 too), making the batch start resource-bound, not partner-bound.
3. Evidence J3's 11:55 readiness was set by an unavoidable external delivery, and J1/J2 were deliberately released early for unrelated reasons (e.g., material perishability windows) — synchronization then being intentional buffering, not waste.
4. Historical batches with similar readiness dispersion but no early-job waiting (because partial starts occurred), showing dispersion alone doesn't bind.
5. Cure-cycle documentation proving batch composition/thermal mass required all three jobs, so the counterfactual "start earlier" was physically invalid.
6. Data showing early jobs' post-batch steps were occupied until ~12:05 anyway (downstream blocking), so earlier cure would only relocate the queue.

**Additional fields/event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool / Batch / Partner / Lot` start–end events with blocking-object reference.
2. Tool state-change log (acquire, release, cleaning start/end, quarantine reason) as first-class events for FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.
3. Machine state log distinguishing idle-starved, idle-blocked, setup, and down, plus operator/shift assignment per event.
4. Override records integrated into the OCEL: who overrode L7 quarantine for J2, when, and authorization.
5. Readiness definition audit: what event marks "ready for batch," and per-job ready timestamps for *every* gate (tool, lot, test), not just batch.
6. Batch formation policy parameters and the decision timestamp: when B1's membership was fixed, and whether partial start was permitted.
7. Shipment creation trigger and planned dispatch windows, plus per-job packload times, to separate consolidation wait from handling.
8. Calibration trigger for CAL-3 (scheduled interval, post-test failure, drift reading) to classify J6's 45m as policy or quality response.
9. Lot genealogy with per-component release status, so lot holds can be scoped to C2 vs C5 independently.

Until such data exist, the honest conclusion is structural, not singular: these six jobs are coupled through batch barriers, single-instance tools, a shared lot, and consolidation policy, and each mechanism is sufficient to produce multi-hour waits while every conventional, single-object view reports a healthy process.
