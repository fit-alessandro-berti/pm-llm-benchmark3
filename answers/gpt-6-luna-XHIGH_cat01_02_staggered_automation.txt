## 1. What is observed

`automation=1` marks when a branch could use the system; it does not indicate that any particular case used it. Actual automated-fast-path share rose gradually: Alpha and Beta went from 4% and 6% in March to 25–27% in April and 46–50% by August; Gamma remained at 4% through June, then reached 25% in July and 32% in August. Adoption was selective: Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical cycle times, while Gamma followed a readiness pilot. The never-adopting Delta and Epsilon are not automatically exchangeable controls.

Several changes coincide with rollout. A fraud desk began in March; from April, routing increased arrivals to Alpha and Beta, and later to Gamma. FTE changed in three branches, and May's product change raised recorded high-complexity shares in several branches. Learning means availability and treatment intensity diverged over time. The outcomes are branch-month summaries, not case records: no individual times, denominators, queue waits, or sampling variances are supplied. Median and p90 can conceal within-branch differences. CSAT is a monthly mean with response rates of 20–45% and no response counts or respondent composition. Thus the observed survey movement may reflect who replied as well as experience.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: cohort-specific group-time ATT / event study as a cautious retrospective design** | Separate April 2026 Alpha/Beta cohort and July Gamma cohort; event time from first availability; estimate each cohort and horizon rather than a single pooled post coefficient | For April, Gamma before July plus Delta/Epsilon; for July, Delta/Epsilon; use only valid not-yet-treated periods | Branch and calendar-month effects; pretreatment outcome levels/trends and baseline risk; sensitivity models for incoming load, productive FTE, and registration risk mix, with explicit distinction between confounders and post-treatment mediators | Cohort-specific effect of availability on branch-month outcomes | Conditional parallel untreated trends, no anticipation, stable measurement, sufficiently comparable controls, and no consequential spillovers; consistency across versions must be addressed | Plot leads/outcomes, placebo dates, cohort-specific pre-trends, leave-one-control-out results, and adjusted/unadjusted estimates; show raw paths | Five branches and short pre-period make assumptions weakly testable; readiness/selection, fraud-desk interference, and time-varying local practices remain. TWFE pooling could mix heterogeneous effects, so do not interpret one pooled coefficient as the ATT. |
| Randomized phased activation in eligible service teams/queues | Randomize stable teams/queues within Delta and Epsilon to early vs delayed access; retain assigned phase as ITT and record actual use | Randomized delayed-phase teams/queues in the same branch and calendar months | Branch strata, prespecified baseline risk and queue measures; adjust only for prespecified precision variables | Causal availability effect; randomized assignment can instrument actual uptake for a complier effect if assumptions hold | Random assignment, limited cross-team contamination, stable assignment, and no differential outcome capture | Assignment balance, adherence, contamination, attrition, event-time plots, and queue-level randomization inference | Requires enough independent teams/queues and operationally separable queues. Shared staffing or fraud-desk capacity can transmit effects and reduce effective sample size. |
| Comparative interrupted time series | Separate branch-specific level/slope changes at April or July, plus common calendar shocks | Never-treated branches and not-yet-treated branch before July | Pre-event level/slope, calendar shocks, registered case mix, arrivals and FTE in sensitivity analyses | Local change in branch trend attributable to activation | Untreated trend would continue absent activation; no simultaneous branch-specific shock and stable measurement | Longer pre-trends, placebo breakpoints, alternative trend forms, residual autocorrelation checks | Only three pre-months for April adopters and short post-period; May product/routing/staff changes can mimic a break. |
| Synthetic-control-style comparisons | Cohort-specific activation date; construct a weighted donor from untreated branches | Weighted Delta/Epsilon and, before July, Gamma | Pretreatment outcome paths and baseline branch characteristics | Branch-specific availability effect | Donors reproduce untreated outcome path and are unaffected by treatment spillovers | Pretreatment fit, placebo branches/dates, donor-weight sensitivity | Only two never-treated donors and three treated branches; good fit is unlikely to imply valid counterfactual trends. |

The primary strategy is preferable for describing the historical rollout because it respects the two distinct start dates and avoids treating already-treated branches as clean controls for later cohorts. It can show whether outcome paths diverged before activation, estimate early and later windows separately, and compare each cohort only with branches that had not yet adopted at that date. It also makes the key assumptions visible. I would report cohort-specific raw and adjusted contrasts, event-time plots, and leave-one-control-out sensitivity rather than a single pooled “automation coefficient.”

This is a framework for disciplined estimation, not a claim that the supplied panel identifies a causal effect. There are only five branches, just three adopting branches, and at most three pre-months for Alpha and Beta. Branch-clustered standard errors with five clusters are unreliable, while branch-month medians have no supplied sampling uncertainty. Parallel trends and absence of spillovers cannot be established from these data. In particular, controlling mechanically for arrivals, staffing, or case mix can remove part of the total policy effect if rollout changed them; show such models as sensitivity analyses and state the estimand they imply. A prospective randomized queue/team phase-in is needed for stronger identification.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

**Transparent branch-specific contrasts.** Pre/post windows are Jan–Mar vs Apr–Jun for Alpha and Beta, and Apr–Jun vs Jul–Aug for Gamma. These are equal-weight averages of branch-month summaries. Changes are post minus pre; percentage changes for time outcomes use the pre-window branch mean. They are arithmetic summaries, not causal estimates.

| branch | pre → post window | fast-path share, pre → post | median change (h; %) | p90 change (h; %) | rework change (pp) | escalation change (pp) | CSAT change |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alpha | Jan–Mar → Apr–Jun | 4% → 32% | +1.53 (+3.1%) | +11.47 (+11.7%) | +0.03 | +2.77 | −0.01 |
| Beta | Jan–Mar → Apr–Jun | 6% → 34% | +2.53 (+5.5%) | +10.03 (+10.8%) | −0.87 | +2.47 | −0.03 |
| Gamma | Apr–Jun → Jul–Aug | 4% → 28.5% | −6.47 (−14.8%) | −9.27 (−9.8%) | −1.23 | +1.17 | +0.14 |

**Comparison with not-yet/never-treated branches.** For Alpha/Beta, their equal-branch mean median rose 2.03 hours from Jan–Mar to Apr–Jun; the mean change in Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon was +6.23 hours. The simple difference-in-differences is −4.20 hours. For p90, the corresponding changes were +10.75 vs +16.86 hours, a −6.11-hour difference. For Gamma, July–August vs April–June median changed −6.47 hours, compared with −2.63 in Delta/Epsilon, or −3.84 hours difference-in-differences; p90 changed −9.27 vs −2.22, or −7.05 hours. These comparisons are sensitive to the control mix, trends, and time-varying shocks. They do not overcome selection or identify causal effects.

**Treatment intensity.** The early adopters' first three available months average 32% and 34% automated-fast-path share, up 28 percentage points from their March levels; Gamma's July–August average is 28.5%, up 24.5 points from 4%. Early adopters nonetheless have higher early post medians and p90s, whereas Gamma improves after its uptake rises. Across all branches, May/June deterioration and later improvement are also seen among nonadopters. Thus there is no simple observed monotone dose-response. The branch-month intensity is endogenous to eligibility, learning, case mix, and routing; a coefficient per 10-point increase is descriptive only.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** Alpha moves 50.4, 46.9, 49.4 hours in Jan–Mar and Beta 45.4, 45.9, 46.4: neither gives a reliable parallel-trend check, and the series are short. Gamma improves before its later rollout from a May/June shock only after May, while controls also deteriorate with the product change. Alpha/Beta were explicitly selected for poor historical performance, so unobserved earlier extremes could naturally regress toward the mean. The supplied months alone cannot quantify that selection or rule out regression to the mean.

**Sensitivity / scenario bounds.** For median cycle time, the raw early-adopter change is +1.5 to +2.5 hours, while the two simple trend-adjusted cohort contrasts are about −3.8 to −4.2 hours. These support a scenario span of roughly −4 to +3 hours for the short-run branch-level change under alternative trend assumptions, not a confidence interval. For p90, early adopters worsen by about 10–11 hours raw, while the two difference-in-differences are −6 to −7 hours; a reasonable evidence span is −7 to +11 hours. For rework, raw changes range from +0.03 to −1.23 pp, while the two comparisons are around −1.7 to −2.2 pp; a conditional scenario is 0 to −2.2 pp. These broad ranges show model dependence and cannot be interpreted as identified causal bounds.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Include both Delta and Epsilon in the next rollout, but phase access across stable service teams or queues in each branch. Randomize the activation order within branch and readiness strata, with a delayed-access group maintained long enough to provide contemporaneous controls. This reaches both branches while preserving a comparison. Before committing, verify that teams can be assigned separately and that shared specialist staffing will not erase the contrast; if routing cannot be contained, randomize at the larger desk-connected service unit and analyze spillovers as part of the policy effect. Keep assignment fixed for analysis, record availability and actual fast-path use separately, and do not use adoption-selected uptake as if randomized.

Collect at least six months of pre-activation and six months after each phase for case-level timestamps and denominators. The supplied January–August branch summaries are not a substitute for queue-level baseline distributions. Log registration, triage start/end and version, eligibility, actual path, manual overrides, specialist arrival/queue/service times, rework loops, resolve/close, reopen, repeat contact, and case risk. Capture queue backlog and aging, arrival rates by routing source, productive hours/FTE and skills, training, and fraud-desk load by originating branch. Link survey invitations, replies, response rate, case attributes, and resolution timing; audit registration-risk calibration.

Use median and p90 separately, plus rework, escalation appropriateness, resolution, reopen, and respondent CSAT. Monitor shared-desk waits and control-queue outcomes weekly. Set guardrails before activation: pause a phase for material increases in p90 or aged backlog, rework/reopen, routing errors, or specialist waits; investigate any CSAT deterioration while tracking response composition. A practical scale-up rule is to expand the next randomized wave only after at least three post months show a pre-specified operational improvement (for example, a 5% median reduction) without p90 worsening by more than 5%, rework rising by more than 1 percentage point, or CSAT falling by more than 0.1 among comparable respondents. Review the six-month estimates and uncertainty before full scale. Treat that rule as a policy guardrail, not a substitute for adequate cluster-level inference.

```python
    "Beta":  ("2026-01", "2026-03", "2026-04", "2026-06"),
    "Gamma": ("2026-04", "2026-06", "2026-07", "2026-08"),
}
metrics = outcomes + ["automated_fast_path_pct"]
print("\nAdopter pre/post contrasts (post minus pre):")
for branch, (pre0, pre1, post0, post1) in windows.items():
    b = df[df.branch.eq(branch)].copy()
    pre = b[b.month.between(pre0, pre1)].mean(numeric_only=True)
    post = b[b.month.between(post0, post1)].mean(numeric_only=True)
    print(branch, "pre", pre0, "to", pre1, "post", post0, "to", post1)
    print(pd.DataFrame({"pre": pre[metrics], "post": post[metrics], "change": post[metrics] - pre[metrics]}).round(3).to_string())

# Simple cohort comparisons against branches untreated during those windows.
def mean_for(branches, start, end, outcome):
    x = df[df.branch.isin(branches) & df.month.between(start, end)]
    return x.groupby("branch")[outcome].mean().mean()
print("\nDescriptive difference-in-differences for median and p90:")
for outcome in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
    early_treat = mean_for(["Alpha", "Beta"], "2026-04", "2026-06", outcome) - mean_for(["Alpha", "Beta"], "2026-01", "2026-03", outcome)
    early_ctrl = mean_for(["Gamma", "Delta", "Epsilon"], "2026-04", "2026-06", outcome) - mean_for(["Gamma", "Delta", "Epsilon"], "2026-01", "2026-03", outcome)
    late_treat = mean_for(["Gamma"], "2026-07", "2026-08", outcome) - mean_for(["Gamma"], "2026-04", "2026-06", outcome)
    late_ctrl = mean_for(["Delta", "Epsilon"], "2026-07", "2026-08", outcome) - mean_for(["Delta", "Epsilon"], "2026-04", "2026-06", outcome)
    print(outcome, "Alpha/Beta DiD", round(early_treat - early_ctrl, 3), "Gamma DiD", round(late_treat - late_ctrl, 3))

# Event time is relative to each adopting branch's first available month.
first_treat = {"Alpha": "2026-04", "Beta": "2026-04", "Gamma": "2026-07"}
df["month_index"] = df.month.dt.year * 12 + df.month.dt.month
df["adopt_index"] = df.branch.map(first_treat).map(
    lambda x: pd.Period(x, freq="M").year * 12 + pd.Period(x, freq="M").month if pd.notna(x) else np.nan
)
df["event_time"] = df["month_index"] - df["adopt_index"]
for k in [-3, -2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4]:  # -1 is the omitted reference month
    df[f"rel_{'m' + str(abs(k)) if k < 0 else 'p' + str(k)}"] = (df.event_time.eq(k)).astype(int)
print("\nRelative-time support counts (reference event time -1 omitted from model):")
print(df.loc[df.automation.eq(1)].groupby(["branch", "event_time"]).size().to_string())

# These fixed-effects regressions are descriptive associations only. Branch FE and
# calendar-month FE do not remove endogenous rollout, case selection, or interference.
# Do not adjust the total availability effect for mediators without changing its estimand.
cluster_count = df.branch.nunique()
print(f"\nWARNING: only {cluster_count} branch clusters; cluster-robust standard errors are unreliable with so few clusters.")
print("WARNING: outcomes are aggregated branch-month medians/percentiles, not raw case times; model coefficients are not case-level effects.")

availability_model = smf.ols(
    "median_cycle_h ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)", data=df
).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"], "use_correction": True})
print("\nDescriptive panel model: median hours ~ availability + branch FE + month FE")
print(availability_model.summary().tables[1])

intensity_model = smf.ols(
    "median_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + C(branch) + C(month)", data=df
).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"], "use_correction": True})
print("\nDescriptive treatment-intensity model: median hours ~ fast-path share + branch FE + month FE")
print(intensity_model.summary().tables[1])

# Exploratory event-study panel. Cohort effects are pooled by relative month here;
# heterogeneous event-time effects and five-cluster inference make coefficients fragile.
rel_terms = [f"rel_{'m' + str(abs(k)) if k < 0 else 'p' + str(k)}" for k in [-3, -2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4]]
event_formula = "median_cycle_h ~ " + " + ".join(rel_terms) + " + C(branch) + C(month)"
event_model = smf.ols(event_formula, data=df).fit(
    cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"], "use_correction": True}
)
print("\nExploratory descriptive event-time model; event month -1 is reference")
print(event_model.params[rel_terms].to_string())
print(event_model.bse[rel_terms].rename("clustered_SE_unreliable_with_5_branches").to_string())

# Causal assumptions remain explicit: conditional parallel untreated trends, no
# anticipation, consistent measurement/treatment versions, and no consequential
# spillovers are not verified here. Higher fast-path share is not randomized.
```

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Automation availability was followed by rising use, but Alpha/Beta's early median and p90 times worsened while Gamma's later times improved; p90 behavior is distinct from median behavior.
- Suggestive: Rework shares fell in Beta and Gamma, and simple trend-adjusted comparisons are favorable for time outcomes, but these are fragile branch-level contrasts.
- Conditionally causal: A randomized, queue-level phased rollout with measured spillovers could estimate the effect of offering automation and, under additional assumptions, the effect of actual use.
- Not supported: “Automation reduces cycle time by X%” as a general causal statement, or a claim that customer satisfaction improved for all customers.
