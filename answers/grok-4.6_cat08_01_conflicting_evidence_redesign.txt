# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The modal spine is `Intake claim → Auto eligibility screen (3600/5000) → Fast approval (2550) → Dispatch replacement (2300) → Quality/compliance close (2320)`. That path is short on the performance DFG (about 0.8h + 1.0h + 3.0h + 5.0h on the successive means) and cheap at the gate (`EUR 0.20` auto screen, unlimited capacity). It is also the organization’s quality and compliance problem, not its solution.

**Performance.** Speed is polarized, not uniformly good. Auto routing is sub-hour to a few hours; `Intake → Manual eligibility review` is already 7.5h mean / 18h p90, and the evidence loop is the long pole (`Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` 34h mean / 88h p90). `Manual eligibility review → Repair quote` is 22h mean / 60h p90, which the temporal-profile diagnostic flags as too slow for Premium. The 22% unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment sits at 140h median with the highest abandonment. End-state counts show 390 abandonments versus 220 denials: the process loses more cases to friction than to a clean no.

**Quality and rework.** Customer complaint/reopen is 9.2% overall, 14.1% after Fast approval, and only 3.3% on `Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device`. The frequency DFG locates the leak: 260 `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` versus 140 from Fast approval, 80 from Repair quote, and 30 from Repair device. That dispatch-to-complaint edge is a long-tail failure (96h mean / 180h p90) and is explicitly linked to incorrect replacement-part selection. Repair is not innocent either: rework-repair is 12.5% of repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% of low-risk. Evidence is a ping-pong, not a single touch—26% of cases that enter `Request missing evidence` do so at least twice, consistent with 890 returns from evidence to manual review and 320 evidence-path abandonments.

**Compliance.** Token replay is deceptively healthy (fitness 0.91) while only 68% of traces fit. Alignment deviations match the frequent path: Fast approval before evidence completeness (R2), dispatch without risk review on high-value devices (R1), and complaint/reopen after close (and R3 requires reopened claims to pass Manual eligibility before final close or denial). Precision is low because auto approval, risk review, and repair are combined in policy-illegal ways. The discovered “happy path” is partly a policy violation, not a capable process.

**Customer experience.** Outcomes are segment-specific, which is why a single KPI is misleading. Premium low-risk accessories (18%) get 8h median on the fast path and blow service-credit exposure after 24h, so slowing them is expensive. Premium high-value (14%) get 20h on the fast path but a 22% complaint/reopen rate, versus 96h and 5% on the reviewed/repair path; Standard high-value (21%) is 18% versus 6%, and a wrong replacement costs about `EUR 410`. Standard low-risk (25%) will wait 72h if status is visible. The escalation board (120 cases, one 45-minute daily slot) repairs some of this with high CSAT and no scale.

**The contradiction.** Fast approval is 31% cheaper *before* rework and only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits. The most frequent path therefore looks dominant on first-pass cycle time and unit cost and is dominated on fully loaded cost, complaints, and R1/R2. The slow path is not “always better”: it is better for high-value and compliance-sensitive work, and it is the wrong default for Premium accessories whose credit risk starts at 24h. Any redesign that only chases average cycle time will re-create the current illusion.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-stratified eligibility (stop the wrong cases from using the fast path)

**Redesigned flow.** Intake must capture segment (Premium/Standard), defect type, estimated value, and an evidence-completeness score before routing. Replace the current non-segment-aware auto screen with explicit route classes:

- Low-risk accessory + evidence complete → Auto screen → Fast approval → Dispatch → Close (Premium stays inside a 24h design envelope).
- Value above `EUR 400` or high-value device → Auto may only send to Fraud/risk review or Manual eligibility; Fast approval from auto alone is blocked (R1).
- Incomplete evidence → one structured evidence request, then *back to auto* if the case is now low-risk and complete; otherwise Manual. Unclear defect cannot receive Fast approval.
- After Fast approval, high-value still cannot dispatch unless a review token exists (R1/R2). Reopens follow R3 through Manual eligibility, not a silent re-close.

**Targets.** The 2550 `Auto → Fast approval` edges; R1/R2 alignment deviations; Premium/Standard high-value (14% + 21%); the outdated auto rules that can be redeployed in two weeks.

**Mechanism.** The frequent path is attractive because the *mix* on it is wrong. High-value and incomplete-evidence cases are borrowing a lane designed for accessories. Removing them from auto-fast should cut the 18–22% high-value fast-path complaint rates and the illegal dispatch-without-review pattern, while protecting the 8–13h accessory medians that actually need speed. This is a classification redesign, not a new factory of activities.

**Deliberately sacrificed / left open.** High-value cycle time will move away from the current 20–28h fast medians toward the reviewed world unless later work speeds repair (Redesign C). A rule update can raise manual inflow 15–35% and push the 7 reviewers through the 82% utilization cliff, turning a compliance win into a queue failure. This design does not fix wrong-SKU selection on the remaining dispatches, the 34h evidence wait, the 26% repeat-evidence rate, or `Manual → Repair quote` slowness. Mid-risk devices remain a residual judgment call; the log does not isolate them cleanly.

# 3. Redesign B: Fast-path hardening (evidence token + pre-dispatch part gate)

**Redesigned flow.** Keep volume on `Intake → Auto → Fast approval → Dispatch`. Change the *conditions of passage*, not the default treatment:

1. Dispatch is forbidden without an evidence-completeness token, except the policy exception already in R2 (Premium low-risk accessory).
2. Insert a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate before ship: automated SKU/model/configuration match, with human inspect only on mismatch, high-value, or known-confusion parts. Staff it from the two Quality check inspectors, who are underused except after repair campaigns.
3. Offer an evidence portal aimed at first-touch completeness (the prompt’s 30–45% reduction in repeat requests), with a non-digital assisted channel so low-literacy customers are not forced into self-service.
4. Do **not** apply the 6–18h gate to Premium low-risk accessories; that segment’s credit trigger is 24h and their error cost is `EUR 95`, not `EUR 410`.

**Targets.** The 2300-wide dispatch spine; the 260 `Dispatch → complaint` edges (96h / 180h p90, wrong part); Fast-approval-before-evidence move-on-log deviations; 26% repeat evidence; the 350 `Fast approval → Close` cases that already skip ship stay ungated.

**Mechanism.** Frequency and the temporal profile say a large share of downstream pain is a fulfillment defect on the cheap path, not a need to send thousands of cases to seven reviewers and two fraud specialists. Hardening dispatch attacks the largest complaint inflow and the fully loaded cost gap (31% → 9%) without detonating manual utilization. The portal attacks the 890 evidence-to-manual returns and 320 abandonments at the source of incompleteness rather than with another generic chase.

**Deliberately sacrificed / left open.** R1 can remain broken: a complete-evidence high-value case can still Fast approve if A is not also in force. Added 6–18h will hurt Standard customers less than Premium accessories (hence the exemption), but any leakage of the gate onto the 18% Premium accessory stream creates credit exposure. The portal can raise abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers if the assisted channel is weak. Two inspectors cannot deep-inspect ~2300 replacements; if the gate is implemented as a full human queue it becomes Redesign A in disguise. Diagnosis errors that are not SKU-visible will pass. Repair-path slowness and Premium `Manual → Repair quote` breaches are untouched.

# 4. Redesign C: Repair-preferred high-value lane and single-owner evidence concierge

**Redesigned flow.** Change *what work is done* for the expensive and the stuck segments; leave capable accessory fast-path volume alone.

- **High-value devices (Premium 14% + Standard 21%).** Default off Fast approval / replacement. After a thin eligibility screen, cases go to technical triage → Repair quote against a pre-fetched part-price catalog (the current quote delay is wait-for-price, not touch time) → time-boxed customer approval with a clear replace-if-uneconomic escape → Repair device → Quality check. Premium high-value gets an express repair / loaner promise so the alternative to the unsafe 20h fast path is not the current 96h reviewed median.
- **Unclear defect / missing evidence (22%).** A named concierge owns the case across one structured evidence cycle (portal *plus* phone/chat). After one cycle: complete and re-route, inspect-to-repair, deny, or close-abandon. A second generic `Request missing evidence` is not allowed. That directly targets the 26% double-request statistic and the 140h median.
- Reopens on this lane still satisfy R3 via Manual eligibility; the escalation board is reserved for true deadlocks, not as a overflow of preventable dispatch errors.

**Targets.** 18–22% high-value fast-path complaint rates; `EUR 410` wrong-replacement cost; 12.5% high-value repair rework; `Repair quote → Customer approval` 30h mean and 70 post-approval abandonments; the evidence swamp (890 returns, 320 abandons); Premium temporal breaches on `Manual → Repair quote`.

**Mechanism.** For high-value, the repair path’s 3.3% complaint rate is the quality benchmark the frequent path cannot reach by going faster. Making that path *usable* (price catalog, time-boxed approval, Premium loaner/express) is what makes “stop replacing the wrong device” commercially viable rather than a cycle-time regression. For unclear claims, ownership beats another system bounce: the current loop (`evidence → manual → evidence`) manufactures the 140h tail. This is a product/operating-model change, not a routing tweak (A) or a shipping checkpoint (B).

**Deliberately sacrificed / left open.** First-pass cost and labor will rise; 3 quote technicians and 2 inspectors cannot absorb anything like 35% of 5,000 cases without capacity or a strict “repair only when diagnosis is clear / replacement still allowed when quote exceeds X” rule. Some customers already reject the repair offer (80 `Repair quote → complaint`, 70 `Customer approval → abandoned`). Loaners cost money that the log does not price. Accessory fast-path issues and residual R2 breaches on low-risk Standard replacements are out of scope. If diagnosis at quote is weak, the 12.5% high-value rework rate can worsen under volume.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- *Failure 1:* Updated rules over-capture. Manual inflow rises 15–35% (or more if mid-risk is lumped with HV). Utilization exceeds 82%, and `Intake → Manual` (already 7.5h / 18h p90) becomes the new cycle-time crisis.
- *Failure 2:* Rules under-capture or game on estimated value. High-value still reaches Fast approval; fitness stays ~0.91 while the 68% fitting-trace problem remains. Alternatively, Premium accessories are trapped in review and generate 24h credit events.
- *Bottleneck migration:* Manual eligibility (7 reviewers) and Fraud/risk (2 specialists, already weak on Mondays and after campaigns). Work does not disappear; it leaves the unlimited auto resource.

**Redesign B**
- *Failure 1:* The gate is implemented as a human hold on all dispatches. The +6–18h lands on Premium low-risk (18% of volume), service credits spike, and the “fast path” dies without a quality gain proportional to `EUR 95` error cost.
- *Failure 2:* Portal-only evidence completion raises abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, shifting 320 abandons upward while “repeat request %” looks better. Or the gate only catches catalog mismatches and the 14.1% Fast approval reopen rate barely moves because the defect was eligibility/diagnosis.
- *Bottleneck migration:* Pre-dispatch / Quality check (2 inspectors) and a new portal-support desk. Complaint volume may also shift later in time rather than disappear if bad cases still ship after a superficial checklist.

**Redesign C**
- *Failure 1:* Repair/quote capacity cannot take HV share. The 96–120h reviewed medians get worse; Premium HV defects against an implicit replacement expectation; 70 approval-abandonments grow.
- *Failure 2:* Concierge becomes a heroic queue. Single-owner evidence without a hard one-cycle stop rule recreates the 26% double-request loop with a friendlier name. Weak diagnosis at quote inflates the 12.5% HV rework-repair rate.
- *Bottleneck migration:* Repair quote (3 technicians, part-price wait), the repair bench, Quality check (now loaded from repair *and* any leftover gates), and the concierge roster. Escalation-board pressure may fall even while total WIP rises in the workshop.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

Do not deploy A+B+C. The open causal question is whether the frequent path fails because **the wrong cases are on it (A)**, because **shipping is uncontrolled (B)**, or because **replacement is the wrong treatment for HV (C)**. Average cycle time cannot answer that; lagged complaints can.

**First pilot (learn causality, small irreversible footprint).** Run a **high-value-only pre-dispatch part gate** (a thin slice of B) in parallel with **shadow scoring of segment-aware R1/R2 rules** (A in observe-only). Do not change accessory routing. Do not move HV defaults to repair yet. This uses idle inspection capacity, prices the `EUR 410` error, and tests the log’s own claim that `Dispatch → complaint` is a wrong-part long tail.

**Metrics (segment × path, not portfolio averages).**
- Reopen/complaint rate and *count* by segment and by last gateway (Fast approval vs Dispatch vs Repair).
- Time-to-complaint (current means 72h from Fast approval, 96h from Dispatch—need a several-week observation window).
- Gate catch rate (wrong SKU / config), added latency p50/p90, Premium-accessory 24h breach and credit events (should be ~zero if exemption holds).
- R1/R2/R3 violation counts and % fitting traces *by segment* (not global fitness 0.91).
- Fully loaded cost proxies: reships, credits, second dispatches—not first-pass labor only.
- Resource: inspector utilization; shadow estimate of manual inflow if A were enforced; abandonment; repeat-evidence rate; escalation-board take-up.

**Stop/go (ranges, not fake ROI).**
- *Expand B (gate) if* HV dispatch-reopen falls materially toward the mid-single-digit repair benchmark **and** added latency stays in the lower half of 6–18h for Standard HV **and** accessory SLAs are untouched. If catch rate is high, part error was causal.
- *Stop expanding B if* catch rate is low and HV reopens stay near 18–22%. The problem is upstream mix/diagnosis → enforce A next, do not staff a larger gate.
- *Enforce A (narrowly) if* shadow scores show HV/value>`EUR 400`/incomplete-evidence cases are enriched in the 2550 auto-fast edges and in alignment deviations. Go-live only on R1 (block auto-fast above `EUR 400`) plus incomplete-evidence block. **Stop or throttle A if** projected or actual manual inflow approaches the 15–35% band *and* reviewer utilization heads through ~82% with rising `Intake → Manual` waits.
- *Open a C pilot only after* A/B have isolated residual HV that are *correctly* eligible yet still fail as replacements, or the 22% unclear segment is still at extreme cycle time/abandonment. Pilot C on one high-value defect family plus a concierge cell for unclear claims, with a written replace-if-uneconomic escape.

**Sequencing logic.** B-scoped-to-HV first (cheap falsification of the part-error story, no reviewer shock). A second, but only the compliance-critical predicates, because a full rule rewrite can bury the 7-reviewer pool. C last, because it is an operating-model change that needs the repair path to be faster than today’s 96–120h before it is a fair alternative to a 20h unsafe replace.

**Real improvement versus downstream shift.** A win that only shortens time-to-close will look like today’s 31%-cheaper illusion. Declare success only if, after a lag covering the 72–180h complaint tails: (1) segment-specific reopen rates fall, (2) credits/reships fall, (3) R3 reopens still pass Manual eligibility rather than silent re-close, (4) no new queue’s p90 rose by more than the hours saved upstream, and (5) abandonment did not absorb cases that would have become complaints. Watch Fraud Mondays, inspector WIP, quote wait, and the escalation slot specifically—those are the predicted landing sites for displaced work.
