Actual use is separate: `automated_fast_path_pct` was only 4–6% before availability and rose to roughly 25–50% after availability, differing by branch and month. Rollout was not random: Alpha and Beta were selected because of high volume and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot. Several contemporaneous changes occurred: a centralized fraud desk began in March, routing increased arrivals to Alpha/Beta by about 10% and Gamma by about 5%, FTE counts changed in several branches, and a May product change raised `high_complexity_pct` in multiple branches. The primary strategy is preferable because the rollout is staggered and treatment intensity changes over time. A simple treated-vs-untreated contrast would confound adoption timing with May complexity changes, routing, and staffing. The event-study structure uses not-yet-treated Gamma as a short-term control for Alpha/Beta and keeps Delta/Epsilon as never-treated controls for the full window. It also separates availability from actual use: `automation=1` is an ITT-like contrast, while `automated_fast_path_pct` describes dose-response within treated months. This matters because teams learned gradually and use rose from about 25% to 50%. However, the design cannot eliminate selection into adoption or shared fraud-desk spillovers. Therefore diagnostics must focus on pre-trends, placebo dates, and sensitivity to excluding May–August or specific branches. The treatment-intensity model is descriptive unless case-level eligibility and use are measured prospectively. The strategy should be treated as the best available observational approximation, not as proof of a single causal effect.

| `median_cycle_h` | No clear absolute improvement; relative to controls, less worsening | 6 to +2 h vs control-adjusted; absolute 3.4 to +1.9 h | 25 | Alpha/Beta DID negative vs controls; Gamma fell 3.4 h | Alpha/Beta absolute median flat/slightly up; product-change confounding | Do not claim automation reduces median cycle time; at most it may have attenuated deterioration |
| `p90_cycle_h` | No evidence of tail improvement; Alpha/Beta tail worsened absolutely | Relative 10 to +5 h; absolute likely +0 to +12 h in Alpha/Beta | 20 | DID suggests less worsening than controls | Absolute p90 rose ~10 h in Alpha/Beta; controls also worsened | No safe claim of tail improvement; monitor p90 as harm guardrail |
| `rework_pct` | Mixed; initial drop then rebound | 2 to +1 pp | 25 | Some treated branches lower than controls | Alpha/Beta rework returned to or above pre levels | No reliable reduction in rework |
| `escalation_pct` | Increased in all branches; automation did not reduce escalation | 0 to +3 pp increase | 60 | All branches rose after March; automated triage may send borderline cases onward | Fraud desk and case mix confound | Expect escalation to remain high or increase; do not claim reduction |
| `csat_1to5` | Small, inconsistent, nonidentified | 0.05 to +0.10 | 15 | Gamma/Alpha small increases | Beta small decrease; response counts missing | No defensible CSAT improvement claim; monitor with response rates |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Do not expand to Delta and Epsilon simultaneously without evaluation. Next, randomize Delta and Epsilon to immediate versus delayed adoption, or if only one can go first, choose Delta because it has stable volume, no concurrent FTE loss, and moderate complexity, while holding Epsilon as a not-yet-treated control for at least four months. Better still, add case-level randomized encouragement within the adopting branch: eligible cases are randomly prompted toward automated triage versus usual manual triage, while branch availability is phased. This separates the availability effect from the actual-use effect.

Minimum pre-period: three prospective months with case-level event logs, plus existing six months as historical baseline. Minimum post-period: six months, not two, because learning and use intensity change over time. Collect process-mining outcomes: timestamps for Register, Manual/Automated Triage, Specialist Review, Rework, Resolve, Close; loop counts; fast-path eligibility; override reasons; and specialist/fraud-desk queue waits. Monitor spillovers through the centralized fraud desk by recording sending branch, wait time, and queue length. Track arrivals, FTE, arrivals per FTE, and `high_complexity_pct` monthly.

Guardrails: pause expansion if p90 cycle time exceeds control-adjusted baseline by more than 10%, if rework or escalation rises by more than 2 percentage points, if CSAT falls by more than 0.1 with adequate response, or if fraud-desk waits exceed the pre-specified threshold. Scale-up decision rule: proceed only if after six months the ITT median effect is non-positive with upper confidence bound below +3%, p90 is not worse than control by more than 5%, escalation/rework do not worsen, CSAT is non-inferior, and automated fast-path use exceeds 50% of eligible cases. Otherwise pause and redesign.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation availability was staggered and targeted; actual automated fast-path use rose gradually; all branches experienced concurrent changes in complexity, staffing, routing, and fraud-desk operations.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to never-treated Delta/Epsilon, adopting branches show lower median and p90 cycle times after adjustment, but absolute tail performance often worsened and the estimates are fragile to nonparallel trends.
- `Conditionally causal:` Under a prospective randomized phase-in with case-level event logs, queue-state controls, and spillover monitoring, automation availability may modestly reduce median cycle time and may increase specialist escalation, while effects on p90 and CSAT remain uncertain.
- `Not supported:` A single statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” across branches, or that it improves tail performance, rework, escalation, and customer satisfaction, is not defensible from the supplied data.
