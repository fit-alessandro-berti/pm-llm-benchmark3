## 1. Drift diagnosis table

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January:** Stable baseline. Labels are mature, breach rate is 0.18, mean risk is 0.19, AUROC is 0.81, and quality defects are negligible. Variant mix and variant breach rates are stable.

- **February:** Stable baseline continues. AUROC improves slightly to 0.82, calibration remains good, and there is no meaningful process, population, or data-quality signal.

- **March:** Stable baseline continues. The model remains well discriminating and calibrated. V1/V2/V3 mix and variant breach rates are consistent with January–February.

- **April:** This is primarily a **schema and pipeline incident**, not credible evidence of a single operational deterioration. The migration changed the diagnosis event name, causing prediction prefixes to be built at the wrong point or not built at all. This aligns with the discontinuity in `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share (17%), missing queue feature (14%), unmapped variants (18%), AUROC (0.67), Brier score (0.184), ECE (0.110), and excessive mean risk (0.28 versus 0.19 observed breach rate). Labels are fully mature, so label delay is not the explanation. April should be repaired and recomputed rather than used as-is to justify retraining.

- **May:** The mapping patch largely restores data quality: unknown activity drops to 2%, missing queue feature to 3%, and AUROC rebounds to 0.78. However, the cached pre-diagnosis event-count feature was not fully backfilled for April, and May performance remains weaker than the Jan–Mar baseline. This is consistent with residual pipeline effects and incomplete repair, not proof that concept drift began in May.

- **June:** This is mainly a **real routing/process-mix change**. The remote-routing policy increases V2 from 34% to 52%; V2 has the lowest breach rate, 0.09, and the weighted variant mix helps explain the lower aggregate observed breach rate of 0.15. Remote-fix timing remains stable. AUROC remains relatively strong at 0.79, although mean predicted risk of 0.20 exceeds observed breach rate. The aggregate calibration change may reflect changed mix, routing selection, or insufficient variant-specific calibration. It does not by itself establish concept drift.

- **July:** Early supplier deterioration becomes visible. The `Order Part -> Part Received` median increases to 65 h and V3 breach rate rises to 0.46, compared with 0.35 in June. Label maturity remains high at 98%, so this is unlikely to be chiefly censoring. This is evidence of real process deterioration and a plausible beginning of V3-specific concept drift relative to the old model.

- **August:** This is the clearest month of **overlapping operational/process and concept change**. The parts edge increases to 130 h, V3 reaches 35% of cases, and V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. Aggregate breach rate rises to 0.28 while mean predicted risk remains only 0.19; AUROC, Brier, and ECE all deteriorate. With 93% label maturity, incomplete labels may slightly bias estimates but cannot plausibly explain the main signal. A model built before supplier degradation is underestimating current parts-related breach risk.

- **September:** Several effects overlap. Supplier performance remains poor (`Order Part -> Part Received` 148 h), V3 mix reaches 42%, and Partner Z joins at 24% of volume. Partner Z has more rural and older-device cases and routes 58% through V3. The observed Partner Z breach rate is higher, but its labels mature more slowly. Thus September contains real parts-related process/concept effects, population shift, and meaningful label-delay confounding. A single aggregate deterioration statistic cannot distinguish them.

- **October:** October should not be interpreted as a recovery simply because currently observed breach rate falls to 0.16. Only 55% of labels have matured, median label delay is 52 days, and the outcome API backlog disproportionately delays longer-resolution paths. The continued 155 h parts edge, 41% V3 share, and 27% Partner Z share indicate that operational risk may still be elevated. October predictive metrics are selected-label metrics, not stable estimates of full-cohort model performance.

April, June, August–September, and October are therefore different failure modes: April is mainly data representation failure; June is routing and mix change; August–September combine supplier-driven process deterioration, likely V3 concept drift, and September population shift; October is heavily censored by label maturity.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test | Concrete implementation | Expected result if explanation A is true | Expected result if competing explanation B is true |
|---|---|---|---|
| Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting | For every case-start cohort, estimate the probability that an outcome is available by case age, month, variant, source, and predicted-risk decile. Re-evaluate historical predictions at fixed ages, such as 45, 60, and 90 days, using only labels that would have been available at those historical cutoffs. Report confidence intervals and label-completeness thresholds. | **Outcome backlog/censoring:** October’s maturity curve is materially below historical curves, especially for V3 and long-path cases. As labels arrive, observed breach rate, calibration, and possibly AUROC change materially. | **Real degradation:** At a common mature vintage, July–September still show high V3 breach and poor calibration/discrimination. August remains poor after 100% or near-100% maturity. |
| Recompute features after activity normalization and duplicate cleaning | Rebuild April–May prefixes from raw events after mapping both diagnosis labels to one canonical activity, retaining only exact or stable-ID duplicates, recomputing cached event-count and queue features, and scoring from the proper post-diagnosis timestamp. Compare original versus repaired predictions case by case. | **Pipeline failure:** Unknown activity, missing queue features, unmapped variants, score disagreement, and April calibration error decline substantially after reconstruction; prediction coverage rises and April AUROC may recover. | **Real process/concept deterioration:** Cleaned April still has materially poor within-segment calibration/discrimination despite correct prefix construction and feature lineage. |
| Stratified calibration and discrimination | On mature or fixed-vintage cohorts, calculate AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier, ECE, calibration intercept/slope, and reliability curves by month × V1/V2/V3 × source. Use bootstrap intervals and minimum sample thresholds. | **Mix/covariate shift:** Aggregate degradation shrinks after stratifying; within V1, V2, and V3 the model remains similarly calibrated and discriminating. | **Concept drift:** Within V3, particularly from Jul onward, calibration intercept/slope and AUROC worsen even after conditioning on variant and source. |
| Compare process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges | Build monthly DFGs and performance DFGs. Track path frequencies, `Order Part -> Part Received` median and upper-tail duration, diagnosis-to-remote-fix time, and breach rates by path. | **Supplier/process deterioration:** V3 breach rises in tandem with the parts edge, while remote-fix timing and V2 breach remain comparatively stable. | **Pure data-pipeline issue:** Operational timing and mature variant outcomes are stable, but observed DFGs contain artificial unknown activities, duplicate transitions, or broken diagnosis anchors. |
| Test whether outcome relationships changed conditional on the same path | Using mature labels, fit a forensic outcome model containing old-model score, normalized realized path/variant, source, and month interactions. Test whether month effects remain after conditioning on the same path and available-at-diagnosis covariates. Do not use post-diagnosis elapsed time as a deployable predictor. | **Concept drift:** July/August month effects or score-calibration intercept shifts remain within the same normalized V3 path; old scores systematically underpredict later V3 cases. | **Process-mix drift only:** Month effects largely disappear once path/variant composition is controlled; deterioration is mainly due to more cases entering historically high-risk paths. |
| Partner Z transport and reweighting checks | Compare Partner Z and non-Z after standardizing both groups to the same V1/V2/V3 mix, geography, device age, queue state, and prediction age. Fit a domain classifier for Partner Z membership and use inverse-odds or direct-standardization weights. Repeat only at common label maturity. | **Population/composition shift:** The Partner Z versus non-Z performance gap narrows after reweighting to common covariates and variants. | **Source-specific residual risk or concept drift:** A substantial Partner Z calibration or breach gap remains after standardization and common-vintage evaluation. |
| Feature missingness and lineage audit | Maintain raw-to-feature lineage for event source, schema version, activity mapping version, dedupe rule, prefix timestamp, feature value, cache version, and model version. Compare raw and recomputed feature distributions, missingness, and prediction coverage by source and day. | **Pipeline failure:** Defects cluster around the migration date/source version; cached and recomputed features disagree; missingness and unknown activities spike sharply. | **Authentic covariate shift:** Feature distributions change but raw events, recomputed features, timestamps, and lineage remain internally consistent. |
| Outcome-definition and API audit | Reconcile outcome API records against case-close records and promised-date logic. Compare API arrival time, resolution time, breach status, and missingness by path and source. | **Censoring/backlog:** Missing labels are concentrated in long-path, unresolved, or recently closed cases and are late in the API but recover later without definition changes. | **Definition problem:** The same completed cases receive different breach labels before/after a date or source change, indicating a target-definition issue rather than ordinary delay. |
| Routing and intervention audit | Join prediction time, score, intervention decision, routing policy, technician/partner assignment, and later outcome. Compare pre/post-policy cohorts while conditioning on pre-diagnosis characteristics. | **Policy-induced mix shift:** V2 increase is explained by routing policy and its within-variant outcome relationship remains stable. | **Feedback or hidden selection:** High-risk cases are preferentially routed or escalated in ways that alter observed outcomes, requiring causal caution when interpreting model-performance changes. |

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

| Stage | Decision and actions |
|---|---|
| Immediate containment | Do not use April raw metrics to trigger retraining or threshold changes. Reconstruct April–May predictions from normalized, deduplicated events and recomputed features. Mark Sep–Oct performance as provisional because labels are incomplete. For current operations, use a temporary business escalation rule for known parts-dependent/supplier-exposed cases rather than pretending the old model score is calibrated for V3. This is an operational safeguard, not a substitute for model validation. |
| Short-term recalibration | Consider a temporary variant-aware calibration layer only after using clean, fixed-vintage labels. A V3-specific intercept or monotonic calibration adjustment may be justified if July–August mature data show stable discrimination but a persistent calibration shift. Do not globally recalibrate with October or raw April data. V1 and V2 should be assessed separately because their observed breach rates remain much lower and more stable than V3. |
| Feature repair | Repair diagnosis-event mapping, duplicate handling, cached features, queue-feature lineage, and prediction-prefix anchoring. Enforce as-of prediction-time checks so no post-diagnosis information leaks into features. Add operationally available features that represent the changed environment, such as supplier/part availability state, expected parts lead-time, current queue load, rural travel distance, and device age, subject to data-governance and fairness review. Do not use actual `Part Received` time as a prediction-time feature because it occurs after the prediction point. |
| Full retraining | Full retraining is justified if, after clean labels mature, V3 or source-stratified discrimination remains degraded or if conditional month effects persist after path and population standardization. The post-July supplier disruption and September Partner Z introduction create a new operating regime that may require recency weighting, temporal validation, and richer features. Retraining should be evaluated on a later fixed-vintage holdout, not a random split. |
| Model segmentation | Variant-specific treatment is justified only when the variant is known or reliably inferable immediately after diagnosis. A shared model with variant interactions and hierarchical/variant-specific calibration is usually safer than entirely separate models, particularly for V3 sample size and changing supplier conditions. A separate Partner Z model is not yet justified solely because Partner Z is new or has higher observed breach. First test whether the excess risk remains after maturity adjustment and transport to a common covariate/path distribution. |

**Training-data disposition until remediation**

- **January–March:** Safe for baseline training and benchmarking. They are fully mature and have low data-quality defects, but they are insufficient by themselves for the current supplier regime.
- **April:** Unsafe in raw form. It may become usable only after raw-event reconstruction, feature recomputation, duplicate audit, and validation that repaired records match the intended post-diagnosis prediction point.
- **May:** Potentially usable after lineage validation. Labels are mature and quality largely recovered, but it should be checked for residual mapping/cache effects.
- **June:** Potentially usable after validation. It represents a legitimate routing-policy regime and should not be discarded merely because mix changed.
- **July:** Do not use as a final supervised-training cohort until remaining labels mature and the supplier-change period is explicitly represented. It is valuable for monitoring and later training.
- **August:** Not yet safe for final training at the reported cutoff despite 93% maturity. It should become a high-value recent training cohort once labels mature and outcome completeness is confirmed.
- **September:** Unsafe now because of 82% maturity, Partner Z population shift, and slower Partner Z label arrival.
- **October:** Unsafe for training, recalibration, or comparative performance claims. It is heavily confounded by the outcome API backlog.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

A useful architecture should maintain separate time axes: event time, prediction time, case-start cohort, outcome availability time, and reporting cutoff. It should not allow a late-label problem to masquerade as a model-learning problem.

| Monitor family | What to monitor | Cadence and segmentation | Alert interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predictive monitor | Prediction coverage, mean/median risk, risk-decile counts, score distribution drift, AUROC, PR-AUC, recall/precision at operating thresholds. | Daily score coverage; monthly fixed-vintage evaluation. Segment by variant, source, geography, supplier exposure, and model version. | A score-distribution shift alone is a covariate/process alert, not proof of model failure. |
| Calibration monitor | Brier score, ECE, calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves, predicted versus observed breach rate. | Fixed label age and mature cohorts; variant/source/month slices. | Trigger recalibration investigation only when data-quality checks pass and label maturity is adequate. |
| Label-maturity monitor | Fraction of outcomes available by cohort age, median/p90 label delay, API backlog size, missing-label composition by variant/source/risk decile/path length. | Daily operational view and monthly cohort view. | A low-maturity alarm suppresses definitive performance alarms and blocks automatic retraining; it does not itself change the model. |
| Data-quality and lineage monitor | Activity-label frequencies, renamed-label mapping coverage, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, duplicate rates, timestamp ordering, feature missingness, stale cache use, prediction-prefix location, schema version, feature lineage, model version. | Near-real-time with daily audit summaries. | A red data-quality alarm invalidates affected metric windows and triggers repair/recomputation before performance interpretation. |
| Process-variant monitor | Variant shares, unmapped paths, rate of diagnosis-to-dispatch/order/remote-fix transitions, route-policy adherence, case-volume changes. | Daily/weekly by source and region. | A V2 or V3 mix change is an operations and covariate-shift alert; evaluate conditional model performance before retraining. |
| Performance-DFG monitor | DFG edge counts and performance-DFG latency quantiles, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis-to-remote-fix, dispatch-to-repair, and tails rather than only medians. | Daily/weekly, supplier and partner stratified. | A worsening parts edge is an operational escalation even before labels mature. It becomes model-drift evidence only if later mature outcomes show conditional predictive failure. |
| Population monitor | Source share, Partner Z share, rurality, travel distance, device age, geography, device mix, case type, supplier exposure, and domain-classifier separability. | Weekly/monthly. | Population alerts require stratification and transport checks, not automatic source-specific penalties or a global retrain. |
| Intervention and deployment monitor | Model/feature/mapping version, routing-policy version, thresholds, manual overrides, supplier interventions, routing decisions, model-action rates, and canary-versus-control behavior. | Every deployment and daily thereafter. | Prevents attributing a policy or feature release effect to spontaneous concept drift. |
| Fairness and service-equity monitor | Error rates, calibration, review/escalation rates, and service outcomes by source, rurality, geography, and other governed groups where appropriate. | Mature, common-vintage cohorts. | A Partner Z gap should be decomposed into maturity, path mix, geography, and residual error before claiming unfairness or changing treatment. |

**Alert dependencies**

1. **Label-maturity alert first:** if cohort maturity is below a predefined threshold, for example 95% overall and adequate within important segments, label performance metrics as provisional. Do not automatically retrain.
2. **Data-quality gate second:** if schema, mapping, duplicate, timestamp, or missingness checks fail, freeze affected evaluation windows and recompute features before acting on AUROC/ECE changes.
3. **Process alert independent of model alert:** a severe parts-edge increase should trigger supplier and operations action even if labels are immature.
4. **Model action gate:** only when labels are sufficiently mature, data quality is green, and deterioration persists within relevant variants/sources should the system choose between recalibration and retraining.
5. **Population-shift gate:** if Partner Z or another source changes the mix, perform standardization and segment evaluation before changing global thresholds or asserting source-specific model failure.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# Expected CSV columns: case_id, activity, timestamp, source, and preferably event_id.
# This is an audit extraction sketch: it creates process evidence, not a predictive model.
CID, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAG_OLD = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
DIAG_NEW = "Diagnostic Assessment Completed"

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
missing = required - set(raw.columns)
if missing:
    raise ValueError(f"Missing required event columns: {missing}")

raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
raw = raw.dropna(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]).copy()
raw["source"] = raw.get("source", "unknown_source")
raw["_ingest_row"] = np.arange(len(raw))

# Normalize before any variant, feature, or prefix extraction.
# UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is deliberately retained for auditing, not silently converted.
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({DIAG_NEW: DIAG_OLD})

# Conservative duplicate strategy:
# - Prefer a contractually stable cross-system event_id when available.
# - Otherwise remove only exact same-case/activity/time/source duplicates.
# - Do not remove repeated activities at different timestamps: they may be real rework.
before_dedup = len(raw)
if "event_id" in raw.columns:
    has_id = raw["event_id"].notna()
    with_id = (
        raw.loc[has_id]
        .sort_values(["event_id", "timestamp", "_ingest_row"])
        .drop_duplicates(subset=["event_id"], keep="first")
    )
    without_id = (
        raw.loc[~has_id]
        .sort_values(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "source", "_ingest_row"])
        .drop_duplicates(
            subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "source"], keep="first"
        )
    )
    clean = pd.concat([with_id, without_id], ignore_index=True)
else:
    clean = (
        raw.sort_values(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "source", "_ingest_row"])
        .drop_duplicates(
            subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "source"], keep="first"
        )
        .copy()
    )

duplicate_audit = {
    "rows_before": before_dedup,
    "rows_after": len(clean),
    "rows_removed": before_dedup - len(clean),
    "normalization": {DIAG_NEW: DIAG_OLD},
}

# PM4Py formatting is done after normalization and conservative duplicate handling.
events = clean.rename(
    columns={"case_id": CID, "activity": ACT, "timestamp": TS}
).copy()
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events, case_id=CID, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
)

case_start = events.groupby(CID)[TS].min()

def consecutive_edge_median_hours(log, left, right):
    """Median elapsed time for immediately consecutive left -> right events."""
    x = log.sort_values([CID, TS, "_ingest_row"]).copy()
    x["_next_activity"] = x.groupby(CID)[ACT].shift(-1)
    x["_next_time"] = x.groupby(CID)[TS].shift(-1)
    edge = x[(x[ACT] == left) & (x["_next_activity"] == right)].copy()
    hours = (edge["_next_time"] - edge[TS]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    return float(hours.median()) if len(hours) else np.nan

def top_variant_counts(variants, n=5):
    # pm4py.get_variants returns a mapping from variant representation to cases/traces.
    pairs = [(str(variant), len(cases)) for variant, cases in variants.items()]
    return dict(sorted(pairs, key=lambda z: z[1], reverse=True)[:n])

def summarize_process_log(log, month, cohort_type):
    if len(log) == 0:
        return {
            "month": str(month), "cohort_type": cohort_type, "events": 0, "cases": 0
        }, {}

    # PM4Py discovery functions provide auditable frequency and performance DFGs.
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CID
    )
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CID
    )
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CID
    )

    # Temporal features are extracted for audit/profiling only; no model is fitted.
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        log, case_id_key=CID, timestamp_key=TS, activity_key=ACT
    )

    case_source = (
        log.sort_values([CID, TS, "_ingest_row"])
        .groupby(CID)["source"]
        .first()
    )

    summary = {
        "month": str(month),
        "cohort_type": cohort_type,
        "events": int(len(log)),
        "cases": int(log[CID].nunique()),
        "unknown_activity_share": float((log[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
        "diagnosis_case_coverage": float(
            log.loc[log[ACT] == DIAG_OLD, CID].nunique() / log[CID].nunique()
        ),
        "order_part_to_received_median_hours": consecutive_edge_median_hours(
            log, "Order Part", "Part Received"
        ),
        # The PM4Py performance DFG aggregation is retained in details below.
        "order_part_to_received_perf_dfg_value": perf_dfg.get(
            ("Order Part", "Part Received"), np.nan
        ),
        "temporal_feature_rows": int(len(temporal)),
        "source_case_share": case_source.value_counts(normalize=True).round(4).to_dict(),
        "top_variants": top_variant_counts(variants),
    }
    details = {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "start_activities": start_acts,
        "end_activities": end_acts,
        "variant_counts": top_variant_counts(variants, n=20),
        "temporal_feature_columns": list(temporal.columns),
    }
    return summary, details

monthly_rows, monthly_details = [], {}
months = pd.period_range(
    case_start.min().to_period("M"), case_start.max().to_period("M"), freq="M"
)

for month in months:
    start = month.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
    end = (month + 1).start_time.tz_localize("UTC")

    # Event-calendar cohort: events that occurred in the month.
    # Useful for operational DFG/edge monitoring, but paths can be truncated at boundaries.
    event_calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        events,
        start,
        end - pd.Timedelta(nanoseconds=1),
        mode="events",
        timestamp_key=TS,
        case_id_key=CID,
    )

    # Case-start cohort: all events for cases whose first event was in the month.
    # This is the natural cohort for prediction-at-diagnosis and later label-maturity analysis.
    started_ids = case_start[(case_start >= start) & (case_start < end)].index
    case_start_cohort = events[events[CID].isin(started_ids)].copy()

    for cohort_type, log in [
        ("event_calendar", event_calendar),
        ("case_start", case_start_cohort),
    ]:
        summary, details = summarize_process_log(log, month, cohort_type)
        monthly_rows.append(summary)
        monthly_details[(str(month), cohort_type)] = details

monthly_process_summary = pd.DataFrame(monthly_rows)
# Auditable outputs: monthly_process_summary, monthly_details, duplicate_audit
```

The code intentionally uses PM4Py for formatting, filtering, DFG discovery, performance-DFG discovery, variants, and temporal feature extraction. It does not claim that PM4Py supplies a built-in drift detector; drift conclusions require the cohort comparisons, uncertainty estimates, and label-maturity controls described above.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Why it is harmful | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining on immature September–October labels | The training set would overrepresent shorter, faster, more readily labeled paths and may underlearn breach risk for long V3 cases. It could make the model look better on selected labels while worsening production performance. | Require cohort-specific maturity thresholds and fixed-vintage backtests before supervised retraining. Keep October out of training until backlog recovery and label-completeness verification. |
| Calibrating around the April pipeline bug | A calibration layer fit to wrong prefixes, missing queue values, duplicate events, or unknown activities would encode an artifact as if it were real risk.
