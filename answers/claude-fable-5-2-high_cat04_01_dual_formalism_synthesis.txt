# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

Abbreviations used in tables and traces (the code always uses the full labels): RD = Register Deviation, CM = Contain Material, ARC = Analyze Root Cause, PV = Plan Verification, DCA = Design Corrective Action, SR = Supervisor Review, QR = Quality Review, RR = Regulatory Review, CS = Consult Supplier, ET = Execute Trial, IT = Inspect Trial, AP = Adjust Parameters, RM = Release Material, AC = Archive Case.

1. **Events and atomicity.** One event per activity instance, recorded at completion (rule 11). Activities are therefore atomic in both models. "X may start only after Y is complete" is enforced as "complete(Y) precedes complete(X)". This is necessary for the narrative's start-based rule but not sufficient: a start that happened too early cannot be seen in completion-only data.
2. **Meaning of "may overlap".** It is interpreted as the absence of an order constraint between completion events (interleaving semantics), not as observable true concurrency. Duration overlap is not expressible in the event alphabet. The POWL model still keeps such activities as genuinely unordered nodes (compatible with a true-concurrency reading); the process tree does not everywhere (see point 7).
3. **Risk route.** The low/high decision is an unguarded exclusive choice `X(SR, {QR, RR})`, enabled once ARC is complete. The decision is latent and becomes visible with the first review event. High risk means QR and RR each exactly once, unordered. Routes cannot be mixed, reviews cannot be repeated, and no "review rejected" outcome exists because the narrative names none.
4. **Optional supplier consultation.** `X(CS, tau)`: a choice between the visible activity and a silent skip, enabled after ARC and required to be resolved before RM. This gives "at most once". The model does not know *when* the supplier must be consulted; a case that needed the supplier but skipped it is accepted.
5. **Passing inspection.** Inspection outcomes are not labels. "Fail" is abstracted as "IT is followed by AP", "pass" as "the loop is left after IT". The trial is the loop `*( ->(ET, IT), AP )`, whose language is `ET IT (AP ET IT)*`. The exit is only possible directly after an IT, so AP can never follow the passing inspection, and RM can only fire after the loop has exited. The number of iterations is unbounded and at least one trial is mandatory.
6. **Multiplicities.** RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, RM, AC occur exactly once per case; CS at most once; ET/IT at least once; AP zero or more times. There is no cancel/abort path. "Archive Case occurs after Release Material" is read as RM strictly before AC, with AC last.
7. **Duplicate visible labels.** Not permitted in the POWL model (every label is exactly one `Transition`). Permitted, deliberately and with full disclosure, in the process tree: 11 of 14 labels are duplicated (CM twice; ARC, PV, DCA, SR, QR, RR, CS, ET, IT, AP three times). Same-label leaves always sit in mutually exclusive branches.
8. **Approximation policy for the process tree.** The dependency order contains an "N" (PV < Trial, ARC < Trial, ARC < Approval, with PV unordered to ARC and to Approval, and Trial unordered to Approval), so it is not series-parallel and no process tree with unique labels can be exact. I use a **duplication-based, trace-language-preserving approximation**: a three-way case split on where ARC completes relative to the chain CM < PV. It neither removes intended completion-event traces nor adds unintended ones; the loss is structural (concurrency encoded as choice, artificial decision points, duplicated subtrees). Rejected policies, measured on the bounded language with at most one AP (7,896 intended traces):
   - Label-unique conservative tree with a milestone after both initial activities, `->(RD, +(CM, ARC), +( ->( +(PV, DCA), Trial ), Approval, Supplier ), RM, AC)`: 4,588 traces (58.1%), none unintended. It adds the false constraints ARC < PV and CM < {DCA, Approval, Supplier}. This is the fallback I would use if duplicates were prohibited, because it keeps every overlap that rules 2, 6 and 7 name explicitly.
   - Label-unique conservative tree forcing ARC before CM: 4,815 traces (61.0%), none unintended. Slightly larger, but it removes the rule-2 concurrency and makes containment wait for root-cause analysis.
   - Label-unique conservative tree forcing approvals and supplier before the trial: 1,408 traces (17.8%), none unintended.
   - Label-unique permissive tree dropping PV → Trial: all 7,896 intended traces plus 37,367 unintended ones (trials before the verification plan exists). Unacceptable for a release-control process.
9. **Data conditions.** Risk level, supplier involvement and inspection result are not encoded (rule 11). All three are free control-flow choices, so both models are permissive with respect to data: they cannot detect a high-risk case that took the supervisor route.
10. **Silent behavior.** The only modeled silent step is the supplier skip. Converted Petri nets contain further silent transitions (splits, joins, loop exit) that are artifacts of conversion and must not create extra visible behavior; this is tested in section 7.

# 2. Intended Dependency Specification

"Trial" denotes the loop block, "Approval" the risk-route block, "Supplier" the optional block. Precedence between blocks means: every event of the source block precedes every event of the target block.

| ID | Source activity or submodel | Target activity or submodel | Relation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | RD | CM | Precedence | Rule 1/2: registration starts every case |
| D2 | RD | ARC | Precedence | Rule 1/2 |
| D3 | CM | ARC | Concurrency (no order) | Rule 2: independent, may overlap |
| D4 | CM | PV | Precedence | Rule 3 |
| D5 | ARC | DCA | Precedence | Rule 4 |
| D6 | ARC | Approval | Precedence | Rule 5: route selected after ARC |
| D7 | SR | {QR, RR} | Exclusive choice | Rule 5: low risk vs high risk |
| D8 | QR | RR | Concurrency (no order) | Rule 5: either completion order |
| D9 | ARC | Supplier | Precedence | Rule 6 |
| D10 | CS | silent skip | Optionality (exclusive choice, at most once) | Rule 6 |
| D11 | PV | Trial | Precedence (AND-join with D12) | Rule 7 |
| D12 | DCA | Trial | Precedence (AND-join with D11) | Rule 7 |
| D13 | ET | IT | Precedence inside one cycle | Rule 8 |
| D14 | ->(ET, IT) | AP | Loop: do = cycle, redo = AP, exit only after IT | Rule 8: repeat until pass, no AP after pass |
| D15 | Trial | RM | Precedence (synchronization) | Rule 9: passing inspection |
| D16 | Approval | RM | Precedence (synchronization) | Rule 9: selected route complete |
| D17 | Supplier | RM | Precedence (synchronization) | Rule 9: CS complete if selected |
| D18 | RM | AC | Precedence | Rule 10 |
| D19 | {CM, PV} | {ARC, DCA, Approval, Supplier} | Concurrency (no order) | Rules 2-6: containment stream joins the analysis stream only at Trial |
| D20 | Approval | {DCA, PV, Trial, Supplier} | Concurrency (no order) | Rules 6, 7: approvals overlap design, planning, trial, supplier |
| D21 | Supplier | {DCA, PV, Trial, Approval} | Concurrency (no order) | Rule 6 |
| D22 | RD / AC | all others | Unique start / unique end | Rule 1; follows from D1, D2, D15-D18 by transitivity |

D19-D21 are stated explicitly because the absence of an order is a requirement here: a model that adds an order between these pairs is an under-approximation.

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

**3.1 Compact expression**

```text
W(P) := +( ->( P, *( ->( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ) ),
           X( 'Supervisor Review', +( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ),
           X( 'Consult Supplier', tau ) )

->( 'Register Deviation',
    X( ->( 'Analyze Root Cause',                                   # B1: ARC before CM
           W( +( ->( 'Contain Material', 'Plan Verification' ), 'Design Corrective Action' ) ) ),
       ->( 'Contain Material',
           X( ->( 'Analyze Root Cause',                            # B2: CM before ARC before PV
                  W( +( 'Plan Verification', 'Design Corrective Action' ) ) ),
              ->( 'Plan Verification', 'Analyze Root Cause',       # B3: PV before ARC
                  W( 'Design Corrective Action' ) ) ) ) ),
    'Release Material',
    'Archive Case' )
```

`W(P)` is a macro, not a pm4py operator: "prerequisites P, then the trial loop, all concurrent with the approval route and the supplier option". It is instantiated three times with fresh leaves. The fully expanded string printed by `str(tree)` is accepted by `pm4py.parse_process_tree`.

**3.2 Code**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

SEQ, XOR, AND, LOOP = (Operator.SEQUENCE, Operator.XOR,
                       Operator.PARALLEL, Operator.LOOP)


def leaf(label=None):
    """Visible leaf, or silent leaf (tau) when label is None."""
    return ProcessTree(label=label)


def node(operator, *children):
    """Operator node with explicit parent/child wiring."""
    parent = ProcessTree(operator=operator)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = parent
        parent.children.append(child)
    return parent


# Each factory returns a FRESH subtree: a ProcessTree node has one parent,
# so a duplicated subtree must be a separate object, never a shared one.
def approval():
    return node(XOR,
                leaf("Supervisor Review"),
                node(AND, leaf("Quality Review"), leaf("Regulatory Review")))


def supplier():
    return node(XOR, leaf("Consult Supplier"), leaf())


def trial():
    return node(LOOP,
                node(SEQ, leaf("Execute Trial"), leaf("Inspect Trial")),
                leaf("Adjust Parameters"))


def downstream(prerequisites):
    """prerequisites -> trial loop, concurrent with approval and supplier."""
    return node(AND, node(SEQ, prerequisites, trial()), approval(), supplier())


# B1: root-cause analysis completes before containment.
b1 = node(SEQ,
          leaf("Analyze Root Cause"),
          downstream(node(AND,
                          node(SEQ, leaf("Contain Material"),
                               leaf("Plan Verification")),
                          leaf("Design Corrective Action"))))

# B2: containment, then root-cause analysis, then verification planning.
b2 = node(SEQ,
          leaf("Analyze Root Cause"),
          downstream(node(AND,
                          leaf("Plan Verification"),
                          leaf("Design Corrective Action"))))

# B3: containment and verification planning both complete before analysis.
b3 = node(SEQ,
          leaf("Plan Verification"),
          leaf("Analyze Root Cause"),
          downstream(leaf("Design Corrective Action")))

containment_first = node(SEQ, leaf("Contain Material"), node(XOR, b2, b3))

tree = node(SEQ,
            leaf("Register Deviation"),
            node(XOR, b1, containment_first),
            leaf("Release Material"),
            leaf("Archive Case"))
```

**3.3 Why the three branches cover exactly the intended traces**

CM < PV holds in every case, and ARC is one atomic event, so ARC completes in exactly one of three positions: before CM (B1), between CM and PV (B2), after PV (B3). Adding the case condition to the order of section 2 and closing transitively gives a series-parallel order in each case:

- B1: ARC precedes everything in the middle region; what remains is `{CM -> PV, DCA} -> Trial`, unordered to Approval and Supplier.
- B2: CM, then ARC, precede everything else; what remains is `{PV, DCA} -> Trial`, unordered to Approval and Supplier.
- B3: CM, PV, ARC in sequence; what remains is `DCA -> Trial`, unordered to Approval and Supplier.

Each branch is exactly "intended behavior restricted to that case", the cases are disjoint and exhaustive, and the Trial/Approval/Supplier blocks are identical in all three, so the argument does not depend on a loop bound. Each case-splitting XOR is resolved by the next visible label (ARC vs CM, then ARC vs PV), so the duplicates introduce no hidden nondeterminism.

**3.4 What the tree cannot represent exactly**

The tree is exact only at the level of completion-event traces; the following requirements are represented through approximation. (a) Rule 2: the concurrency of CM and ARC is not a parallel operator but an exclusive choice between the orders ARC-then-CM and CM-then-ARC, so the tree never has a state where both are independently in progress, and any duration-aware or partial-order reading of the tree understates overlap. (b) Rules 3 and 4, the split dependencies (PV needs only CM; DCA, approvals and supplier need only ARC): these are not one dependency each but are enumerated over the three positions of ARC; in B2 the tree literally says "PV after ARC" and in B1 "CM after ARC", which is true only inside that branch. (c) Rule 7, the AND-join of PV and DCA before Execute Trial, is built three different ways (`+(->(CM,PV),DCA)`, `+(PV,DCA)`, and DCA alone because PV is already done). (d) Rules 5, 6, 8 and 9 (one risk decision, at most one supplier consultation, one trial loop, one release synchronization) hold per case only because the three copies are mutually exclusive, not because there is a single node; a later edit to one copy silently breaks the equivalence. (e) The two case-splitting XORs are not business decisions; they appear nowhere in the narrative and have no data condition. (f) As in the POWL model, risk level, supplier need and inspection result (rule 11) are not encoded. Rules 1 and 10 and the loop shape of rule 8 are represented directly.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

**4.1 Compact description**

```text
Approval := X( SR, PO(nodes={QR, RR}, order={}) )
Supplier := X( CS, tau )
Trial    := *( PO(nodes={ET, IT}, order={ET-->IT}), AP )

powl := PO(
  nodes = { RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, Approval, Supplier, Trial, RM, AC },
  order = { RD-->CM,        RD-->ARC,
            CM-->PV,        ARC-->DCA,
            ARC-->Approval, ARC-->Supplier,
            PV-->Trial,     DCA-->Trial,
            Trial-->RM,     Approval-->RM,   Supplier-->RM,
            RM-->AC } )
```

**4.2 Code**

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)

rd = Transition(label="Register Deviation")
cm = Transition(label="Contain Material")
arc = Transition(label="Analyze Root Cause")
pv = Transition(label="Plan Verification")
dca = Transition(label="Design Corrective Action")
sr = Transition(label="Supervisor Review")
qr = Transition(label="Quality Review")
rr = Transition(label="Regulatory Review")
cs = Transition(label="Consult Supplier")
et = Transition(label="Execute Trial")
it = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
ap = Transition(label="Adjust Parameters")
rm = Transition(label="Release Material")
ac = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# High-risk route: both reviews, no order between them.
high_risk = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[qr, rr])

# Risk route: supervisor review XOR (quality review and regulatory review).
approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[sr, high_risk])

# Optional supplier consultation: visible activity XOR silent skip.
supplier = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[cs, SilentTransition()])

# Trial: do = (Execute Trial -> Inspect Trial), redo = Adjust Parameters.
cycle = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[et, it])
cycle.order.add_edge(et, it)
trial = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[cycle, ap])

powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rd, cm, arc, pv, dca, approval, supplier, trial, rm, ac]
)

powl.order.add_edge(rd, cm)            # D1
powl.order.add_edge(rd, arc)           # D2
powl.order.add_edge(cm, pv)            # D4
powl.order.add_edge(arc, dca)          # D5
powl.order.add_edge(arc, approval)     # D6
powl.order.add_edge(arc, supplier)     # D9
powl.order.add_edge(pv, trial)         # D11
powl.order.add_edge(dca, trial)        # D12
powl.order.add_edge(trial, rm)         # D15
powl.order.add_edge(approval, rm)      # D16
powl.order.add_edge(supplier, rm)      # D17
powl.order.add_edge(rm, ac)            # D18
```

Only the twelve covering edges are added; implied precedences such as RD before RM follow by transitivity. In pm4py 2.7.23.4 the converted net has the same bounded language with or without an additional `powl.order.add_transitive_edges()` call; I checked both.

**4.3 Why unconnected nodes may remain concurrent**

Of the 45 node pairs in the root order, 32 are ordered (directly or transitively) and 13 are intentionally unordered:

- **CM / ARC**: rule 2, the two initial streams are independent.
- **CM / DCA, CM / Approval, CM / Supplier**: design, approvals and supplier need only ARC (rules 4, 5, 6), never containment.
- **PV / ARC, PV / DCA, PV / Approval, PV / Supplier**: planning needs only CM (rule 3); rule 6 explicitly lets the supplier overlap planning, and nothing ties planning to the analysis stream before the trial.
- **DCA / Approval, DCA / Supplier, Approval / Supplier**: all three are enabled by ARC alone and none waits for another (rules 5, 6).
- **Trial / Approval**: rule 7, the approval route may overlap the whole trial cycle.
- **Trial / Supplier**: rule 6, the supplier may overlap trial work.

Inside `high_risk`, QR / RR is unordered by rule 5. Children of the XOR and LOOP operators are related by the operator, not by the order. The edge ARC --> Supplier also places the silent skip after ARC, which has no visible effect.

# 6. Semantic Comparison

| Requirement | Intended semantics | Process tree | POWL | Overconstraint or underconstraint | Business consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two initial work streams | CM and ARC unordered after RD | XOR between "ARC first" (B1) and "CM first" (B2/B3) | CM and ARC are unordered nodes | Tree: none on traces; concurrency is replaced by a choice of orders. POWL: none | Tree-based simulation or resource analysis never shows containment and analysis in progress together |
| Split dependencies into planning and design | PV needs CM only; DCA needs ARC only | Enumerated over three positions of ARC; each branch states a stronger, branch-local order | Two independent edges CM-->PV and ARC-->DCA | Tree: none on traces, but each branch read alone is overconstrained. POWL: none | A reader of one tree branch may wrongly conclude that planning must wait for root-cause analysis |
| Risk route | Exclusive low/high choice after ARC; QR and RR unordered | `X(SR, +(QR, RR))`, three copies | One `X(SR, PO{QR, RR})` node after ARC | Both underconstrain data: the route is not tied to actual risk | A high-risk case with only SR is accepted by both; needs a data-aware check |
| Overlap of approval and trial | Approval unordered to PV, DCA and the whole trial loop | Parallel to `->(prerequisites, trial)` in every branch | No edge between Approval and PV, DCA, Trial | None in either model | Late approvals do not block trials, only release |
| Optional supplier consultation | At most once, after ARC, before RM, unordered to the rest | `X(CS, tau)`, three copies, parallel to trial and approval | One `X(CS, tau)` node with ARC-->Supplier-->RM | Both underconstrain data: skipping is always allowed | A case that required the supplier but skipped it is not detected |
| Trial start synchronization | ET needs PV and DCA | Three different join constructions (see 3.4) | Edges PV-->Trial and DCA-->Trial | None on traces | Tree: three places to maintain for one rule |
| Repeat-until behavior | `ET IT (AP ET IT)*`, no AP after the pass | `*( ->(ET, IT), AP )`, three copies | One `*( PO{ET-->IT}, AP )` node | Both: unbounded iterations; "pass" is only "loop exit" | A release after a failed but unadjusted inspection is indistinguishable from a pass |
| Release synchronization | RM after Trial, Approval and Supplier | RM follows the whole XOR block in sequence | Three edges into RM | None | Release cannot overtake any obligation in either model |
| Start and end | RD first, AC last | First and last child of the root sequence | Unique minimal and maximal nodes | None | None |
| Duplicate-label risk | One node per activity | 35 visible leaves for 14 labels; duplicates only in exclusive branches, choices resolved by the next label | No duplicates | Tree: no language effect; structural redundancy | Per-activity statistics, decision mining and diagnostics are split over copies; edits must be made three times or the branches drift apart |
| Silent behavior | Only the supplier skip | Three tau leaves, plus conversion silents | One `SilentTransition`, plus conversion silents | None found (section 7) | A silent bypass of a mandatory activity would be a release-control defect, hence tested explicitly |

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

**Canonical representation: the POWL model.** It states each requirement once: twelve edges correspond one-to-one to the precedence rows of section 2, the thirteen unordered pairs correspond to the concurrency rows, and no label is duplicated. The process tree has the same completion-trace language but pays for it with three copies of the trial, approval and supplier blocks and two artificial choices. It should be treated as a derived artifact for tools that require block structure, regenerated from the POWL model rather than edited by hand.

**What I ran (pm4py 2.7.23.4) and what it showed.**

- Conversion: the tree gives a net with 50 places, 49 transitions (35 visible, 14 silent) and 120 arcs; the POWL model gives 20 places, 18 transitions (14 visible, 4 silent) and 44 arcs.
- Soundness: `pm4py.check_soundness` reports both nets sound.
- Bounded exhaustive exploration: with at most one AP both nets have 7,896 distinct visible traces; with at most two AP both have 20,952. The two sets are identical, and identical to the output of an independent enumerator of linear extensions written from section 2. Shortest trace: 10 events.
- All eight witnesses of section 5 produce the stated verdicts on both nets.

This is evidence for the stated loop bounds and my reading of the narrative, not a proof of fidelity.

**Plan as code** (run after the code of sections 3 and 4; it expects `tree` and `powl`):

```python
import itertools
from collections import Counter

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

FULL = {
    "RD": "Register Deviation", "CM": "Contain Material",
    "ARC": "Analyze Root Cause", "PV": "Plan Verification",
    "DCA": "Design Corrective Action", "SR": "Supervisor Review",
    "QR": "Quality Review", "RR": "Regulatory Review",
    "CS": "Consult Supplier", "ET": "Execute Trial", "IT": "Inspect Trial",
    "AP": "Adjust Parameters", "RM": "Release Material", "AC": "Archive Case",
}
ABBR = {v: k for k, v in FULL.items()}

# Check A: conversion to accepting Petri nets.
nets = {
    "tree": pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree),
    "powl": pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(powl),
}

# Check B: soundness of the converted nets.
for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    result = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    is_sound = result[0] if isinstance(result, tuple) else bool(result)
    assert is_sound, f"{name} net is not sound"

# Check C: label and silent-transition audit.
for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    counts = Counter(t.label for t in net.transitions)
    duplicates = {ABBR[l]: c for l, c in counts.items() if l and c > 1}
    print(name, "silent:", counts[None], "duplicates:", duplicates)
    assert set(l for l in counts if l) == set(ABBR), "label set mismatch"
    if name == "powl":
        assert not duplicates, "POWL must not duplicate labels"


# Check D: duplicate-specific check on the tree: same-label leaves must be
#    mutually exclusive, i.e. their lowest common ancestor is an XOR.
def leaves(n, path=()):
    if not n.children:
        return [(n.label, path + (n,))] if n.label is not None else []
    return [x for c in n.children for x in leaves(c, path + (n,))]


for (l1, p1), (l2, p2) in itertools.combinations(leaves(tree), 2):
    if l1 == l2:
        common = [a for a, b in zip(p1, p2) if a is b]
        assert common[-1].operator == Operator.XOR, f"{l1} duplicated concurrently"


# Check E: exact replay (token game with silent moves); nets are safe, so finite.
def accepts(net, im, fm, trace):
    stack, seen = [(im, 0)], set()
    while stack:
        marking, i = stack.pop()
        key = (frozenset(marking.items()), i)
        if key in seen:
            continue
        seen.add(key)
        if i == len(trace) and marking == fm:
            return True
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            if t.label is None:
                stack.append((semantics.execute(t, net, marking), i))
            elif i < len(trace) and t.label == trace[i]:
                stack.append((semantics.execute(t, net, marking), i + 1))
    return False


# Check F: explicit positive and negative trace tests (section 5).
witnesses = {
    "W1": ("RD CM ARC PV DCA SR ET IT RM AC", True),
    "W2": ("RD ARC CM DCA RR PV ET QR IT RM AC", True),
    "W3": ("RD CM ARC DCA PV SR ET IT CS RM AC", True),
    "W4": ("RD ARC CM PV DCA ET QR IT AP CS ET RR IT RM AC", True),
    "W5": ("RD CM PV ARC DCA SR ET IT RM AC", True),
    "W6": ("RD CM ARC DCA ET PV SR IT RM AC", False),
    "W7": ("RD CM ARC PV DCA SR ET IT AP RM AC", False),
    "W8": ("RD CM ARC PV DCA QR ET IT RM RR AC", False),
    # Silent-behavior probes: skip must not bypass anything else,
    # and the supplier must not occur twice.
    "S1": ("RD CM ARC PV DCA ET IT RM AC", False),
    "S2": ("RD CM ARC CS PV DCA SR CS ET IT RM AC", False),
    "S3": ("RD CM ARC PV DCA SR RM AC", False),
}
for wid, (text, expected) in witnesses.items():
    trace = [FULL[a] for a in text.split()]
    for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
        assert accepts(net, im, fm, trace) == expected, (wid, name)


# Check G: independent oracle written from section 2 (not from either model).
def intended(trace):
    c = Counter(trace)
    once = ["RD", "CM", "ARC", "PV", "DCA", "RM", "AC"]
    if any(c[a] != 1 for a in once) or c["CS"] > 1:
        return False
    low = (c["SR"], c["QR"], c["RR"]) == (1, 0, 0)
    high = (c["SR"], c["QR"], c["RR"]) == (0, 1, 1)
    trial = [a for a in trace if a in ("ET", "IT", "AP")]
    k, rest = divmod(len(trial) - 2, 3)
    if not (low or high) or k < 0 or rest:
        return False
    if trial != ["ET", "IT"] + ["AP", "ET", "IT"] * k:
        return False
    pos = {a: i for i, a in enumerate(trace)}      # last occurrence
    first_et = trace.index("ET")
    ok = (trace[0] == "RD" and trace[-1] == "AC"
          and pos["CM"] < pos["PV"] < first_et
          and pos["ARC"] < pos["DCA"] < first_et
          and pos["IT"] < pos["RM"] < pos["AC"])
    for a in ("SR", "QR", "RR", "CS"):
        if c[a]:
            ok = ok and pos["ARC"] < pos[a] < pos["RM"]
    return ok


# Check H: bounded exhaustive playout, all visible traces with <= max_adjust AP.
def bounded_language(net, im, fm, max_adjust):
    done, seen, stack = set(), set(), [(im, ())]
    while stack:
        marking, trace = stack.pop()
        key = (frozenset(marking.items()), trace)
        if key in seen:
            continue
        seen.add(key)
        if marking == fm:
            done.add(trace)
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            if t.label is None:
                stack.append((semantics.execute(t, net, marking), trace))
            elif t.label != FULL["AP"] or trace.count("AP") < max_adjust:
                stack.append((semantics.execute(t, net, marking),
                              trace + (ABBR[t.label],)))
    return done


lang = {name: bounded_language(*n, max_adjust=2) for name, n in nets.items()}
assert lang["tree"] == lang["powl"], "tree and POWL differ on bounded language"
assert all(intended(list(t)) for t in lang["powl"]), "unintended trace admitted"
assert min(len(t) for t in lang["powl"]) == 10, "silent bypass shortens a case"
print("bounded traces:", len(lang["powl"]))

# Check I: sampled playout with cross-replay (unbounded loop depth, random).
sample_tree = pm4py.play_out(tree)
sample_powl = pm4py.play_out(*nets["powl"])
for log, other in ((sample_tree, "powl"), (sample_powl, "tree")):
    for case in log:
        trace = [e["concept:name"] for e in case]
        assert intended([ABBR[a] for a in trace])
        assert accepts(*nets[other], trace)
```

The plan covers the required items: conversion (check A), soundness (B), bounded and sampled playout (H, I), positive and negative traces (F), and checks aimed at duplicated labels (C, D) and at silent behavior (probes S1-S3 in F, the minimum-length assertion in H).

**Limitations.**

- The bounded exploration is exhaustive only up to two adjustments. Completeness in the other direction (every intended trace is accepted) was checked at the same bounds with a separate linear-extension enumerator that is not reproduced here; the sampled playout only samples deeper loops.
- The soundness check printed a warning that its invariant computation through scipy may be unstable without PuLP installed, so its "sound" verdict is weaker evidence than the exhaustive exploration.
- The oracle and both models were written by the same author from the same reading of the narrative, so a shared misinterpretation would pass every test.
- Soundness says nothing about fidelity; a sound net can encode the wrong process. `pm4py.check_soundness` is deprecated in recent pm4py versions and may move.
- `pm4py.play_out(powl)` raised a `TypeError` in the version I used, so POWL playout goes through the converted net; the conversion itself is therefore part of what is trusted.
- Nothing here validates data conditions, start times or real overlap; that needs lifecycle events and case attributes.
- Results were obtained on one pm4py version only.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **Semantic: trace equivalence is not concurrency equivalence.** My claim that the process tree is "exact" holds only for sequences of completion events. The tree turns the concurrency between the containment stream and root-cause analysis into an exclusive choice among three orderings, so its partial-order runs differ from those of the POWL model. Anyone who uses the tree for duration-aware simulation, partial-order conformance or decision mining gets a distorted picture, and the two case-splitting XORs will look like business decisions that do not exist. In addition, "may start only after" is checked on completion order only, which is weaker than the narrative.
2. **Semantic: all data-dependent rules are unguarded.** Both models accept a high-risk case approved by a supervisor alone, a case that skipped a required supplier consultation, and a release after an inspection that actually failed but was not followed by an adjustment. "Passing inspection" is nothing more than "the loop was left". I also assumed, without support in the text beyond silence, that reviews cannot be rejected or repeated and that there is no abort path.
3. **Evidence and maintainability.** Fidelity rests on a hand argument (section 3.3) plus exploration bounded at two adjustments on a single pm4py version, with an oracle that shares my interpretation. The tree code triplicates the trial, approval and supplier blocks through factory functions; this keeps the copies identical today, but the model object itself has 35 visible leaves for 14 activities, and any direct edit of one branch breaks the equivalence without any tool noticing unless the bounded comparison of section 7 is rerun.
