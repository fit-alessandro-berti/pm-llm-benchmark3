## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1 and RR2:** For each case compare the sets of human drafters and approvers, and separately implementers and verifiers. Any intersection is a violation, even if aliases differ or other people also participated. An unresolved identity can leave the result indeterminate. These are separate controls; authority does not cure a same-person violation.
- **RR3:** Every HIGH-risk approval needs an event-time QM or valid QM delegate, except an eligible emergency approval under RR4. A delegation must cover the time, activity and subject scope, meet any stated principal-absence or unavailability condition, and have an unbroken permitted chain. Treat the emergency pathway as a conditional exception, not as ordinary QM authority.
- **RR4:** For an emergency, establish a credible emergency flag and safety rationale, approval by the IC or valid IC delegate before implementation, and a retrospective review by the QM or a valid delegate within two business days. The review requires authority for that review and scope; an approval-only delegation does not automatically confer it. For this assessment, exclude the event date, count Monday–Friday excluding site holidays, and use 23:59:59 UTC on the second business day as the deadline. The stated calendar has no holidays in this period. Thus implementation on Tuesday August 11 has a deadline of Thursday August 13 at day end; Friday August 14 is late. If the site's controlled procedure instead uses business hours or a different start event, recalculate under that procedure.
- **RR5:** Normalize aliases to humans and use reliable session attribution for service accounts. A role attached to a shared account cannot establish human authority or separation. Account attribution can also be challenged by evidence of credential misuse.
- **RR6:** Networks and repeated pairs are risk indicators, not findings of collusion. Case-level rule violations and the effectiveness of the broader control objective are distinct.

A principal who personally acts during recorded leave retains their standing authority; leave alone does not invalidate the action. Their presence or formal availability can, however, affect a delegate's absence condition at the relevant time. A VPN login alone proves neither the user's identity nor their availability for every case. A database entry made after an event cannot by itself prove that authority existed at the event. A contemporaneous, authentic signed instrument can establish earlier effective authority despite later migration, subject to its terms and audit trail.

## 5. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

The script uses the supplied log only. It retains raw account IDs, resolves known aliases, treats CHG-509's service action as provisionally Eva, and marks CHG-503 as an unknown service actor. PM4Py's four-eyes filter is run on cases with both relevant activities and fully resolved, non-disputed identities; the explicit pandas results also report unknown cases. Network calculations use an explicit unknown node, so the unresolved event is not silently removed and its neighbors are not falsely joined. Network edges involving that node are account-level evidence, not human relationships. Ordinary account identities are provisional, and the script separately marks disputed CHG-506 and conditional CHG-504. The code assumes the installed PM4Py API exposing `keep_violations`.

Role/delegation interval results are **candidate authority**, not a final conclusion: manual assessment is needed for authenticity, absence and availability, actor attribution, CI-44 linkage, credible safety rationale, complete delegation records, and the retrospective review deadline and authority. A delegation with an unknown condition is not treated as valid. The specific D-QM-01 record grants approval only; it cannot authorize reviews merely because Ben appears in the log.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. Create controlled delegations before use with explicit principal, delegate, activities, scope, time zone, effective interval, absence conditions, chain limits and revocation. Preserve signed originals and migration provenance; validate rules at event time while retaining both effective and record-creation timestamps.
2. Record principal absence and formal availability at sufficient resolution, including exceptions during leave. Escalate uncertain availability instead of silently assuming a weekend or a VPN login settles it.
3. Maintain an effective-dated human identity registry linking aliases and historical roles; reconcile QMS and HR changes and flag ambiguous or contested attribution.
4. Require individual authentication and action-level reauthentication for shared terminals and service-account transactions. Retain token, session, device and badge evidence; restrict account sharing and investigate disputed credentials promptly.
5. Define the emergency pathway and business-day clock in the controlled procedure.
