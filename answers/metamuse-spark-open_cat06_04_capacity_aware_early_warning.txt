## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
**Why needed:** Model refreshes every 30 min; without persistence rule every case generates ~16 alerts/day. Re-intervening on same case without new information wastes capacity, creates disruption, and induces training-label feedback.

**Definitions:**
* **Material new event:** stage transition, new customer log/message, reassignment, SLA clock change, explicit queue-feature recovery, or manually confirmed missing-info arrival. Not: time passage alone, imputed feature refresh.
* **Cool-down window:** 4 hours after an alert where no swarm delivered, extended to 6h if prior alert count 3. Within window, repeat alerts are **suppressed** (logged, not scored, not counting toward priority).
* **Suppression:** No swarm, no slot consumption, no increase in priority. Counter increments. After 2 consecutive suppressions, trigger **escalation:** 5-min triage review (not swarm) to ensure case not neglected due to starvation rule.
* **Re-entry:** Allowed during cool-down only if (a) material new event + calibrated p (absolute after offset) 0.05 OR (b) stage change OR (c) time-to-deadline crosses <4h critical threshold with p_adj still >0.50. Re-entry resets cool-down and counts as new alert for scoring.
* **Repeated swarm on same case:** Forbidden unless material new event **and** senior lead approves, documenting new mechanism (e.g., logs reveal different root cause). Prevents double-dipping on same diagnosis trajectory.
* **Alert persistence requirement:** For non-urgent cases (deadline >6h), require `p_adj` > threshold on 2 consecutive prefixes or corroborating risk factor (e.g., missing logs + customer escalation) before Tier upgrade. Prevents single-prefix spike (I-06) driving swarm.

**I-05 vs I-07 comparison:**

* **I-05:** `Triage, 5.0h, p 0.550.57, third alert, no new event, no prior swarm, complete data, SME.` p = +0.02 noise. Meets suppression criteria: third alert within 4h window, no material event. Despite SME underprediction (true risk ~0.61-0.66) and attractive ARR 0.25-0.40, policy **suppresses**. Action: no swarm, log suppression, schedule next scoring after cool-down expires or event occurs. Escalation after 2 suppressions  light review at next triage huddle, not swarm. Rationale: treating refresh artifact as evidence wastes slot that could help I-04/I-09 and adds spillover harm. If SME segment systematically generates more refresh alerts due to data sparsity, suppressing prevents alert-fatigue starvation paradox.

* **I-07:** `Triage, 1.5h, p 0.750.72, third alert, new customer log uploaded, complete, SME.` Qualifies for **re-entry** despite slight p drop: new log is material event. Policy allows scoring, but does not auto-grant swarm. Required steps: (1) human reads new log (does it contain diagnostics or just acknowledgment?); (2) if log is informative, update risk qualitatively; p drop suggests log may be reassuring despite model not fully ingesting text. (3) Given 1.5h = swarm duration, assign to **light-touch** not full swarm: senior reads log + calls customer (15 min) vs 45-min swarm that blocks queue. Re-entry preserves information value without over-consuming capacity. Contrast with I-05: same alert count, opposite handling due to evidence change, not count alone.

**Other illustrations:** I-02 and I-09 second alert no prior swarm with p +0.08/+0.07 and no reported new event  not suppressed on second alert (allow one free re-score) but flag as lower confidence than I-07; if they alert third time without event, they move to suppression like I-05. I-06 sharp +0.32 jump with imputed feature  enters VERIFY, not persistence: must not count as two consecutive high readings until feature restored.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
**1. Value-based starvation of lower-value cases.** Ranking strictly by `ARR × value` means I-08 (€100k) always beats I-01 (€9k) even if I-08 ARR_low is -€450 and I-01 is +€920. Over weeks SMEs/Public Sector never receive swarms, breach rates diverge, and training data becomes enriched with untreated low-value breaches.

*Safeguards:* (a) Tier + floor: reserve 1 slot per 4 for non-Strategic or lottery among Tier2; (b) equity weight: multiply value by 1.3 for Public/SME in scoring (transparent, auditable, not hidden); (c) report **opportunity cost**: publish weekly % swarms by segment vs breach rate by segment; if SME breach rate > Strategic by >5pp, trigger floor increase; (d) benefit-cost ratio alternative: prioritize `ARR / (cost+burden)` for marginal cases to surface low-burden SME where false-alarm cost €50-100 vs Strategic €5k.

**2. Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (I-09 sparse segment).** Small n  both calibration and ARR estimates high variance. Naive use of point `0.69` and ARR 0.24 may systematically over- or under-serve this group; errors concentrate harm on customers already facing language barriers, and false-alarm burden (€800) may include interpreter disruption not captured.

*Safeguards:* (a) Wider interval decision: require human-in-loop senior approval for this segment, present interval not point; (b) conservative shrinkage: use ARR_low for ranking, not mid; (c) oversample for monitoring: enrich validation set with all Non-Native cases for 4 weeks to improve calibration; (d) track **separate precision/recall and benefit** for this segment; if observed breach rate diverges >0.10 from predicted, suspend automated scoring for segment until recalibrated.

**3. Differential calibration across segments.** SME underprediction (+0.04-0.09) means model systematically understates risk for I-05/I-07/I-10; Strategic overprediction (-0.05-0.12) overstates for I-02/I-04/I-08. Using raw `p_now` for threshold would over-allocate to Strategic, under-allocate to SMEs, amplifying inequity and misestimating benefit of avoiding breach (since true `p` bounds ARR).

*Safeguards:* (a) Segment-specific offsets applied as intervals in Step 1, with dashboard showing `p_now` vs `p_adj`; (b) do not use `p` alone for prioritization – use benefit after calibration; (c) monthly reliability diagrams by segment; if gap persists >0.05 for 2 weeks, recalibrate or add segment interaction feature; (d) audit decisions: what % of Tier1 cases are SMEs after adjustment vs before – ensures correction is working.

**4. Selective intervention changing future training labels (feedback loop).** If we swarm high-risk cases, they breach less often  future training labels show “high `p` + swarm  no breach”  model learns *intervention as feature* and deflates risk for similar cases  next iteration underpredicts where swarms are effective, creating perverse incentive to swarm less where most effective. Also I-03-like post-diagnosis cases remain high-risk in training, reinforcing spurious stage effect.

*Safeguards:* (a) **Intervention flag in training data:** every training row marks `swarm_delivered (0/1)`, time, and stage; never train on post-intervention outcome without adjustment; (b) **Hold-out causal estimation cohort:** within Tier2 near-threshold, randomize tie-breaker (e.g., 20% of equal-score cases randomized to wait) to preserve unbiased comparison – this is the only reliable ARR estimate; (c) evaluate model on **intention-to-treat** (eligible at scoring time) vs per-protocol; (d) causal modeling: use IPW / difference-in-differences by stage, not naive observed breach rate; (e) policy-induced drift detector: if breach rate among high-`p` cases drops faster than spillover-adjusted expectation, flag as intervention effect, not model improvement.

**5. Spillover harms systematically falling on untreated groups.** Spillover affects six non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases sharing the queue. If we consistently swarm Strategic high-value Triage cases, the diagnosis queue (often staffed for SME routine cases) bears delay. SME diagnosis cases may be lower priority in queue discipline  they absorb disproportionate 8-20 min delay  breach uplift concentrates on SMEs, invisible in direct benefit accounting.

*Safeguards:* (a) **Spillover audit:** track diagnosis-queue breach rate on swarm-heavy days vs swarm-light days, stratified by value/segment of victim cases; (b) **Congestion cap:** if diagnosis queue length >6 or avg wait >30 min, block new swarms unless net benefit >€5k and Tier1; (c) **Attribution accounting:** portfolio net impact table (§3) must be shown to decision-maker before approving 3rd/4th slot – make spillover cost explicit, not external; (d) rotate specialists: swarm duty not always taken from same diagnosis team handling SMEs to distribute load; (e) quarterly fairness review: compare breach trends for never-intervened diagnosis cases vs intervened Triage cases.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
**Operational (hourly/daily):**
* Swarm slot utilization, adherence to eligibility gate (% decisions overriding rule, with reason code), time from alert to swarm start, specialist interruption time, aggregate queue delay minutes added (target <80 min/day), standby slot usage (reserve hit rate). Alert suppression rate by case and reason (e.g., I-05 type suppressions) to detect alert fatigue.
* Queue health: diagnosis queue length, wait time distribution, breach rate among the six spillover-exposed cases within 4h of swarm.

**Predictive (daily/weekly):**
* Calibration: reliability bins by segment (SME, Strategic, Public, Non-Native). Track observed breach rate vs `p_adj` interval. Alert if SME underprediction exceeds 0.09 or Strategic overprediction exceeds 0.12 for 2 consecutive weeks  trigger recalibration.
* Discrimination stability: AUROC, alert volume per 100 cases, % cases with 3 alerts (like I-05/I-07), feature missing rate (live-queue imputation rate – I-06 root cause).
* Drift detection: PSI on `p_now` distribution, on value mix; if PSI >0.2, investigate workflow change (as with recent Strategic redesign).

**Causal-effect (weekly):**
* **Gold standard:** randomized tie-breaker cohort among Tier2 borderline cases (equal score ±€500). Estimate ARR via ITT difference: breach rate swarm vs wait. Target 50 randomized decisions/month to shrink uncertainty from 0.18-0.35 ranges to ±0.05.
* Observational backup: IPW by propensity (stage, time-to-deadline, segment, `p_adj`), DoD comparing breach trends for eligible vs ineligible stages (e.g., Triage vs Diagnosis Complete – I-03 as natural control where effect should be ~0). If observational ARR systematically below supplied ranges, shrink ranges by 30-50% in next scoring cycle.
* Heterogeneity: ARR by stage (must be ~0 post-Diagnosis Complete – validates gate; if I-03-like swarmed, ARR should remain low) and by segment; if Non-Native ARR confidence interval includes 0, downgrade automatic eligibility.

**Capacity (per swarm):**
* Expected vs actual spillover: regression of breach uplift among non-intervened diagnosis cases on number of concurrent swarms, controlling for congestion. If estimated per-slot spillover >0.025 (above base 0.02) for 2 weeks, raise spillover cost in decision rule and reduce max slots from 3 to 2 even if reserve unused.
* Breach value-weighted capacity model: update avg exposed-case value monthly from actual breach costs; if exposed mix shifts to high-value, net benefit threshold rises.

**Fairness (weekly/monthly):**
* Intervention rate, false-alarm rate, direct benefit, net benefit (including spillover attribution) stratified by segment (SME/Strategic/Public/Non-Native) and value decile. Publish disparity metrics: if top-value decile gets >60% of swarms while bottom decile breach rate rises >5pp, trigger floor increase.
* Non-Native-language subgroup: separate calibration, override rate, and outcome audit; small-n uncertainty requires manual review of all decisions for this segment for 8 weeks.
* Spillover equity: % of spillover breaches falling on SME vs Strategic – if >70% on SMEs, implement specialist rotation and congestion cap.

**Recalibration and policy-change triggers:**
* **Threshold/allocation change rule:** Decision rule is not re-tuned daily. Change only at weekly governance review if (i) net portfolio benefit under base scenario negative for 2 weeks running, (ii) fairness floor violated (SME intervention share <20% while SME breach share >40%), or (iii) calibration drift >0.05 in any segment, or (iv) spillover uplift >0.03 confirmed.
* **Model recalibration:** Full recalibration after workflow redesign events (like Strategic change) using post-change data only; interim use segment offset intervals. Log all swarms as intervention features for next training; do not retrain on intervened outcomes without causal adjustment for 1 month to avoid label leakage.
* **Policy-induced drift detection:** Compare predicted breach rate for high-risk cohort (p_adj>0.6) vs observed breach rate adjusted for swarms delivered. If observed << predicted beyond expected ARR (e.g., I-04 cohort breach rate drops 0.25 vs expected 0.19), flag as intervention effect, not model improvement – keep threshold, do not tighten.
* **Governance:** Daily swarm board (triage lead + senior specialist) reviews Tier allocations, reserve status at 15:00, and adverse congestion signal. Monthly review includes predictive, causal, and fairness dashboards; any change to reserve policy, tier cutoffs, or equity weights requires documented approval and A/B logging.

All monitoring assumes uncertainty is irreducible: report intervals, archive decisions with counterfactual `p_adj` and ARR interval for retrospective audit, and treat supplied effect ranges as provisional until randomized evidence accumulates.
