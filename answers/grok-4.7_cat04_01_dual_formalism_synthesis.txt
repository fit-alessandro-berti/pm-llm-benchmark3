# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

1. Each activity is one atomic completion event. “B may start only after A is complete” is encoded as precedence from A to B. The log does not contain start events, so real-time interval overlap is represented only by the interleavings that true concurrency produces.

2. Abbreviations used below: RD Register Deviation, CM Contain Material, ARC Analyze Root Cause, PV Plan Verification, DCA Design Corrective Action, SR Supervisor Review, QR Quality Review, RR Regulatory Review, CS Consult Supplier, ET Execute Trial, IT Inspect Trial, AP Adjust Parameters, RM Release Material, AC Archive Case.

3. Every case has exactly one RD, which is the unique source, and exactly one AC, which is the unique sink. RM is the only predecessor of AC.

4. The low-risk / high-risk decision is a free exclusive choice after ARC, because risk is case data and is outside the control-flow model. The low-risk branch is exactly SR. The high-risk branch is QR and RR under true concurrency, so either completion order is allowed and both are required. No trace may contain both SR and a regulatory pair. The model will still accept a low-risk route on a case that was factually high-risk.

5. CS is a free exclusive choice after ARC between one CS and a silent skip. Selection is case data, so the model offers both options on every case. CS occurs on at most one node and therefore at most once. The skip is a routing token: it is ordered after ARC and before RM, and it has no visible event. A log that simply lacks CS therefore fits the skip.

6. A passing inspection is the silent exit of a loop whose body is ET then IT. The body runs at least once. Each failure is the silent decision to take the redo child AP and then another ET–IT body. The last IT is the passing inspection. Every earlier IT is a failed inspection. AP sits only between a completed body and the next body, so AP never follows the exiting IT, never precedes the first IT, and never ends a case by itself.

7. Duplicate visible labels are not permitted. Each activity name is one leaf. Loop repetitions replay that same leaf; they are not cloned nodes. Cloning was considered as a way to recover more process-tree interleavings and rejected: alignments, footprints, and diagnostics would no longer point at a unique task.

8. Process-tree policy: conservative under-approximation with a single copy of every label. Preparation is `(CM → PV) ∥ (ARC → DCA)`. Only after both PV and DCA does the tree open `trial ∥ approval ∥ supplier`, then RM, then AC. The tree language is the intended language restricted by one extra rule: every SR, QR, RR, and CS completion occurs after both PV and DCA. Unsafe orders stay out. In particular ET still waits for PV and DCA, RM still waits for the trial exit, the chosen approval route, and the supplier obligation, and AP still cannot follow the exiting IT. Among single-copy safe block trees, this is the placement that keeps approval and supplier truly concurrent with the trial cycle. Putting approval inside the ARC branch instead would force every review to finish before ET.

9. “May overlap” means true concurrency: simultaneous enabling, with no order edge. In a sequential log that is exactly the set of all interleavings. It is a stronger statement than an exclusive choice among a few total orders. `Operator.PARALLEL` in the tree and incomparability in the POWL partial order both mean that.

10. A POWL operator or partial order that sits as one node of a parent partial order is a subnet. Predecessors complete before any of its internal events; successors start only after it exits; events of incomparable nodes interleave with its internal events. The approval activity may therefore sit between ET and IT, or between iterations. This is the intended reading of overlap with the trial cycle.

11. PM4Py loop children are `(do, redo)`. Here do is `ET → IT` and redo is `AP`. No mandatory activity has a silent bypass. The only deliberate silent leaf is the supplier skip. Loop entry, exit, and redo decisions become additional silent transitions only in a Petri-net translation.

12. CM may still be unfinished when ARC finishes, so ARC’s successors (DCA, approval, CS) may complete before CM or PV. PV may complete before ARC. Those orders are intended: PV depends only on CM, and DCA depends only on ARC.

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

```text
->(
  'Register Deviation',
  +(
    ->( 'Contain Material', 'Plan Verification' ),
    ->( 'Analyze Root Cause', 'Design Corrective Action' )
  ),
  +(
    *( ->( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ),
    X( 'Supervisor Review', +( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ),
    X( 'Consult Supplier', tau )
  ),
  'Release Material',
  'Archive Case'
)
```

Mapping. The outer sequence is registration, preparation, the pre-release parallel block, release, and archive. Preparation is the true parallel of `CM → PV` and `ARC → DCA`, which matches R3–R8. The pre-release block is the true parallel of three children: the trial loop, the risk choice, and the supplier choice. `*(do, redo)` executes `ET → IT` once and then zero or more `(AP, ET → IT)` repetitions, then exits silently after IT. `X(SR, +(QR, RR))` is the risk route. `X(CS, tau)` is the optional supplier. The sequence join into RM is what forces all three pre-release children to finish before release.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def node(operator=None, label=None, children=None):
    tree_node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, label=label)
    for child in children or []:
        child.parent = tree_node
        tree_node.children.append(child)
    return tree_node

rd = node(label="Register Deviation")
cm = node(label="Contain Material")
arc = node(label="Analyze Root Cause")
pv = node(label="Plan Verification")
dca = node(label="Design Corrective Action")
sr = node(label="Supervisor Review")
qr = node(label="Quality Review")
rr = node(label="Regulatory Review")
cs = node(label="Consult Supplier")
et = node(label="Execute Trial")
it = node(label="Inspect Trial")
ap = node(label="Adjust Parameters")
rm = node(label="Release Material")
ac = node(label="Archive Case")
skip_supplier = node(label=None)

contain_then_plan = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[cm, pv])
analyze_then_design = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[arc, dca])
preparation = node(Operator.PARALLEL, children=[contain_then_plan, analyze_then_design])

trial_body = node(Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[et, it])
# children[0] is the do-part; children[1] is the redo-part.
trial = node(Operator.LOOP, children=[trial_body, ap])

high_risk = node(Operator.PARALLEL, children=[qr, rr])
approval = node(Operator.XOR, children=[sr, high_risk])
supplier = node(Operator.XOR, children=[cs, skip_supplier])
pre_release = node(Operator.PARALLEL, children=[trial, approval, supplier])

tree = node(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[rd, preparation, pre_release, rm, ac],
)
```

The finite process tree cannot represent this specification exactly. The dependency graph contains two different synchronization cuts over one copy of the approval and supplier activities: the trial waits for PV and DCA only, while RM waits for the trial exit, the approval, and the supplier obligation, and approval stays ordered only after ARC. That graph is not series-parallel. A block tree has one place where a parallel block opens and one place where it joins.

The tree above keeps the later cut and the trial overlap. It adds a precedence that the narrative does not have: SR, QR, RR, and CS are enabled only when both PV and DCA have completed. Any intended trace in which a review or CS completes while PV or DCA is still outstanding is outside the tree, even though ET has not started and every safety constraint holds. Mixed timing is outside the tree as well: QR finished during design, and RR finished between ET and IT, is intended and is rejected. Once PV and DCA are both done, every interleaving of approval, CS, and the unbounded trial is included, so requirement 7 holds inside that region.

An XOR of this tree with a second tree that finishes approval before ET would recover the early-review traces only by duplicating every visible label, and it would still miss mixed cases such as CS before DCA together with SR during the trial. Duplicate labels are not used. Relative to the intended control-flow language, the tree is a strict under-approximation. It does not add unsafe orders.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

```text
POWL root order (cover relation; concurrency = incomparability):
  RD → CM → PV → TRIAL → RM → AC
  RD → ARC → DCA → TRIAL
  ARC → APPROVAL → RM
  ARC → SUPPLIER → RM

TRIAL    = LOOP( do = (ET → IT), redo = AP )
APPROVAL = XOR( SR, PO(QR, RR) with no edge )
SUPPLIER = XOR( CS, τ )

No edge between {APPROVAL, SUPPLIER} and TRIAL.
No edge among DCA, APPROVAL, and SUPPLIER.
No edge between {CM, PV} and {ARC, DCA, APPROVAL, SUPPLIER}
except the paths that meet at TRIAL or RM.
```

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def activity(label):
    return Transition(label=label)

def bind(operator, children):
    block = OperatorPOWL(operator=operator, children=children)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = block
    return block

rd = activity("Register Deviation")
cm = activity("Contain Material")
arc = activity("Analyze Root Cause")
pv = activity("Plan Verification")
dca = activity("Design Corrective Action")
sr = activity("Supervisor Review")
qr = activity("Quality Review")
rr = activity("Regulatory Review")
cs = activity("Consult Supplier")
et = activity("Execute Trial")
it = activity("Inspect Trial")
ap = activity("Adjust Parameters")
rm = activity("Release Material")
ac = activity("Archive Case")
skip_supplier = SilentTransition()

do_trial = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[et, it])
do_trial.order.add_edge(et, it)
et.parent = do_trial
it.parent = do_trial
# children[0] = do (ET then IT); children[1] = redo (AP).
trial = bind(Operator.LOOP, [do_trial, ap])

# Empty order: QR and RR are truly concurrent and both required.
high_risk = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[qr, rr])
qr.parent = high_risk
rr.parent = high_risk
approval = bind(Operator.XOR, [sr, high_risk])
supplier = bind(Operator.XOR, [cs, skip_supplier])

powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rd, cm, arc, pv, dca, approval, supplier, trial, rm, ac]
)
for child in (rd, cm, arc, pv, dca, approval, supplier, trial, rm, ac):
    child.parent = powl

powl.order.add_edge(rd, cm)
powl.order.add_edge(rd, arc)
powl.order.add_edge(cm, pv)
powl.order.add_edge(arc, dca)
powl.order.add_edge(arc, approval)
powl.order.add_edge(arc, supplier)
powl.order.add_edge(pv, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(dca, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(trial, rm)
powl.order.add_edge(approval, rm)
powl.order.add_edge(supplier, rm)
powl.order.add_edge(rm, ac)
```

Edges above are the cover relation. Precedence is the transitive closure. RD is therefore before every other node, and AC is after every other node, without a direct edge such as RD → RM. CM is before the trial through PV. ARC is before the trial through DCA. Those pairs are ordered, so they are not concurrent.

The root nodes that have no path either way, and why each pair may stay concurrent:

| Incomparable pair | Why concurrency is intended |
| --- | --- |
| CM, ARC | Initial streams, R5. |
| CM, DCA | Design waits for ARC only. Containment may still be open. |
| CM, approval | Reviews wait for ARC only and may overlap containment. |
| CM, supplier | CS waits for ARC only and may overlap containment. |
| ARC, PV | Planning waits for CM only and may overlap analysis. |
| PV, DCA | The two work products are independent, R8. |
| PV, approval | Reviews do not wait for verification planning. |
| PV, supplier | CS may overlap verification planning, R14. |
| DCA, approval | Both need only ARC, R9 and R7. |
| DCA, supplier | CS may overlap corrective-action design, R14. |
| approval, supplier | Reviews and consultation may overlap. |
| approval, trial | The route may overlap the trial and joins only at RM, R19 and R20. |
| supplier, trial | CS may overlap trial work and joins only at RM, R14 and R20. |

Nested pairs follow the same rule. QR and RR have no edge, so either order and true overlap. CS and the skip are XOR alternatives, so they are never concurrent with each other. Inside one iteration ET precedes IT. AP is the redo, so it is between iterations rather than beside ET. Because approval and supplier are incomparable with the trial subnet, SR, QR, RR, CS, and the skip may interleave between ET and IT and between iterations. The skip has no visible image.

# 6. Semantic Comparison

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two initial work streams | After RD, CM and ARC are truly concurrent. | `+(CM → PV, ARC → DCA)` enables them together. | RD → CM and RD → ARC, with CM incomparable to ARC. | Faithful in both. | Containment and investigation can proceed together. A conformance failure on this pair is a real deviation. |
| Split dependencies into planning and design | PV waits only for CM. DCA waits only for ARC. # 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

The canonical model is the POWL model. The narrative is a partial order with two exclusive choices and one unbounded loop. POWL states the two synchronization cuts directly: PV and DCA both precede the trial, and the trial, the approval, and the supplier obligation all precede RM, while approval and supplier stay incomparable to the trial. The process tree remains a safe export for algorithms that accept only block-structured trees. Its language is intended to be a subset. It should not be the model used to judge fitness of real deviation cases, because early reviews and early supplier calls are legitimate.

Validation plan:

1. Convert both objects with `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`. Treat a successful conversion as a wiring check only. Confirm the visible label set is exactly the fourteen activity names, each on one labeled transition. Confirm the supplier skip is silent, and record every extra silent transition introduced for the loop and for partial-order routing.

2. Run `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)` on each converted net. Expected: one input place, one output place, every transition on a path between them, and no residual token when the output place is marked. Soundness supports the absence of deadlocks and leftover tokens. It does not show that the language matches section 2.

3. Sample `pm4py.play_out` on each net with a fixed trace count. Also keep a separate structural argument for the loop, because sampling will not exhaust an unbounded redo. On the sample, check the visible projection: RD first, AC last and only there, CM before PV, ARC before DCA, both PV and DCA before the first ET, each IT immediately explained by a preceding ET in that iteration, AP only between an IT and a later ET, at most one SR or both of QR and RR but not a mixture, at most one CS, and RM only after the last IT and after the approval events that appear. For the POWL sample, confirm that at least one trace places SR, QR, RR, or CS before DCA or before PV (T5’s pattern) and at least one places a review between ET and IT (T2’s pattern). For the tree sample, confirm the opposite early-review absence: no approval or CS event precedes PV or DCA.

4. Build an event log whose eight cases are T1–T8 and evaluate alignment fitness on each converted net. Expected POWL result: fitness 1 on T1–T5 and fitness below 1 on T6–T8. Expected tree result: fitness 1 on T1–T4 and fitness below 1 on T5–T8. T5 is the approximation test. T6 is the cross-branch test. Inspect the alignment move sequence, not only the scalar. For a fitting no-CS trace, every model move must be silent, and there must be no inserted visible activity. That checks that the supplier skip and the loop’s silent control do not smuggle a second visible label.

5. Silent-behavior and duplicate-label checks. Walk `tree` and `powl` and assert that each visible string occurs once. Assert that the only silent leaf constructed by hand is the supplier skip. Replay a variant of T1 that inserts a second CS and assert fitness below 1, so the skip XOR does not enable repetition. Replay T7 and assert that the failing move is at RM after AP, which checks that the redo cannot fall through a silent exit. On T8, read the alignment rather than only the score: an optimal alignment may either insert RR or treat QR as extra and insert SR. Both diagnoses are consistent with the data-free XOR. The test passes if fitness is below 1; the text of the diagnosis is ambiguous by construction.

6. Limitations. Soundness and a clean conversion are not fidelity. Sampled playout under-represents deep loop unfoldings and rare interleavings, so a passing sample does not prove the universal claims about AP. Alignment fitness of a CS-free trace cannot separate “supplier not required” from “supplier forgotten.” Fitness of an SR-only trace cannot separate low risk from a high-risk case that skipped regulatory review. T8’s alignment is not a unique explanation. The comparative claim “tree language ⊂ POWL language” should be probed with T5 and with the sampled early-review check; it is not proved by soundness. If a POWL compiler ever collapsed an operator node to an atomic transition, approval events would no longer interleave inside ET–IT, and T2 would fail on the POWL side. That interleaving check is part of step 4 because the Python construction assumes subnet semantics. This session specifies the plan; it does not record an execution of it.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. Both models are data-blind on purpose, and that is a real semantic hole. A factually high-risk case that skipped RR and took only SR is a perfect trace. Calling the POWL model exact is exact only for the control-flow projection in section 2. It is not an exact compliance control for the manufacturer’s risk rule or for the obligation to consult a supplier.

2. The process-tree gap is concentrated and still operationally large: every review or supplier completion that races ahead of PV or of DCA is rejected, including the ordinary pattern “regulatory review runs while corrective action is still being designed.” I did not estimate how much of a real log sits in that excluded set, so the practical cost of the under-approximation is argued qualitatively. The inclusion tree ⊂ intended ⊂ POWL is argued from the operators and then pinned to eight witnesses, not derived by a language inclusion check.

3. Activity atoms erase duration. Two tasks can overlap in the plant and still produce a completion order that the partial order forbids, or finish in an allowed order while their execution intervals violated a start constraint. The models never see that. A related unverified dependency is mechanical: loop child order `(do, redo)` and non-atomic POWL subnets are assumed from the PM4Py conventions stated in the task, and the constructors were not executed here. If either assumption failed in the installed version, T2’s interleaving and T4’s redo would not mean what this answer says they mean.
