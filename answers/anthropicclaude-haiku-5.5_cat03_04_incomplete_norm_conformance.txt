## 1. Conformance semantics

**Forbidden behavior** is an observed relation that the norm explicitly prohibits (N3, T6, E10). These are closed-world checks: any occurrence is a *violation candidate*. It becomes a confirmed violation only after the underlying records are validated.

**Missing mandatory behavior** is the absence of a mandatory relation for an object or event that falls within the stated scope (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Absence outside scope is not a violation. Scope must be tested explicitly, because "standard Order" and "externally shipped Package" are not defined in the supplied norm.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is an observed relation with no norm entry, or with status *unspecified* (for example N6, T4, T5, E9). Under the open-world assumption, silence is not prohibition. These relations are recorded and described, not flagged as deviations.

**Typical-pattern absence** is the lack of a typical relation (N2, N5, T3). It is a monitoring signal, not a violation, because exceptions are explicitly legitimate.

**Likely data-quality error** is an observed fact that is structurally implausible or contradicted by another source: a reused numeric identifier, a cobirth inside one event, or a foreign key present in one attribute but missing from the relation table.

**Abstraction mismatch** is a relation that conforms at one level of abstraction but appears deviant at another. The clearest case is N1: an eventual business rule is approximated by a directly-follows edge.

**Insufficient evidence** is the classification when the graph cannot distinguish between a real deviation, a data artifact, and an unmodeled legitimate path. The finding remains open.

**Open versus closed world.** Forbidden relations and in-scope mandatory relations are judged closed-world: they are tested against the norm, and absence (for mandatory) or presence (for forbidden) is decisive. Everything else is open-world. A count of zero for an unregulated relation says nothing about compliance. The main risk is confusing the two: treating an unregulated relation as a violation because the norm is silent, or treating a mandatory gap as benign because the norm lacks a scope predicate.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**Can establish:** directly-follows relations per object type, with counts. Forbidden relations (N3) can be detected if the timestamps are reliable. Frequencies show how common a path is relative to its scope.

**Cannot establish:** eventual reachability, causality, or whether an intermediate event was omitted. Ties in timestamps and missing events can create or hide edges. It also cannot tell whether a directly-follows edge is a business violation or merely an ordering artifact.

**Counts and scope.** N1 is 98 of 100, which is high conformance but still two misses. Whether those two are deviations depends on whether they eventually validate. N4 is 198 of 200, and the two gaps are meaningful only if the scope predicate ("externally shipped") is correctly applied. Without the scope, we cannot tell whether the two packages are exceptions or errors.

**Abstraction dependence.** Issue Invoice  Record Payment 92 of 110 conforms to N5 as typical behavior, because the 14 missing successors and 4 write-offs are explicitly allowed or plausible. At the object-level, the same invoice might be paid later through a different payment event, which the directly-follows graph would not show.

**The danger of "eventually expected" rules.** N1 expresses a business expectation: a new order should eventually be validated. Converting it into a directly-follows rule ignores legitimate intermediate events, such as manual review (three observed instances). It can produce false positives (the two orders in Rank 8 may simply include a review) and false negative assurance (an order that is validated much later, after a long stall, would pass a directly-follows check only if the immediate successor matches). An eventual-follows or time-bounded check is required for N1.

### OTG

**Can establish:** which object types interact and how often, including co-birth and co-death. T6 and E10 are checked here, and the OTG edge weights reveal whether forbidden relations exist at all.

**Cannot establish:** the event that produced an edge or whether the relation is temporally valid. A cobirth edge (Order, Payment) with weight 1 shows that both objects appear in a shared birth event, but not whether it is a legitimate prepayment or a merged record.

**Counts and scope.** The OTG records 1450 Order–Item edges and 200 Package–Delivery interaction edges, plus 7 codeath edges. The ET–OT view records 1448 Add Item–Order events and 198 Dispatch–Delivery events. These are not contradictions, because OTG counts object-level relations while ET–OT counts event-level relations. But the mismatch means the 2 Add Item events without an Order relation and the 2 dispatches without a Delivery relation can only be detected in ET–OT, and OTG may hide them through other events.

**Abstraction dependence.** The codeath edges (7) are conforming in the sense that they are unregulated, but at object level they look like Packages and Deliveries ending together. At event level, they are a single confirmation event. The same relation is therefore benign at event level and ambiguous at object-type level.

### ET–OT

**Can establish:** which event types touch which object types, enabling mandatory event-level checks (E1–E8) and the forbidden sensor–financial link (E10).

**Cannot establish:** the object lifecycle, ordering, or whether an event's relation is correct (only that it exists). An event linked to the wrong invoice still satisfies E8 in form.

**Counts and scope.** E2 and E6 are mandatory for every event, so two misses are actionable. E8 requires a Payment and an Invoice for every Record Payment, except typed unapplied cash. Because no event is typed, the single payment without an invoice is a strict E8 failure until the norm recognizes the exception. The E10 event is forbidden and therefore a violation candidate, but the sensor-invoice link was produced by a join on a reused identifier, so the observation is more likely a data error.

**Abstraction dependence.** Manual Compliance Review linked to Customer is unregulated at ET–OT level, because E9 covers only RiskCase. Whether the Customer link is appropriate depends on a policy the norm does not cover.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 0. Normative metadata. Kept OUTSIDE norm_ocdfg / norm_otg / norm_etot,
#    because bare graph structures encode edges only, not status, scope,
#    severity, or rationale. Maintain this table in version control alongside
#    the norm document; each entry names the graph edge it governs.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
NORM_META = {
    "N1": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "standard Order; eventual, not direct"},
    "N2": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"),
           "status": "typical"},
    "N3": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"),
           "status": "forbidden"},
    "N4": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "externally shipped Package"},
    "N5": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"),
           "status": "typical", "exceptions": ["credit note", "write-off", "open terms"]},
    "N6": {"family": "ocdfg", "edge": ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"),
           "status": "unspecified"},
    "T1": {"family": "otg", "edge": ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "Add Item interaction"},
    "T2": {"family": "otg", "edge": ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "Dispatch Package interaction"},
    "T3": {"family": "otg", "edge": ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"),
           "status": "typical"},
    "T6": {"family": "otg", "edge": ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"),
           "status": "forbidden"},
    "E2": {"family": "etot", "edge": ("Add Item", "Order"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "every Add Item event"},
    "E6": {"family": "etot", "edge": ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "every Dispatch Package event"},
    "E8": {"family": "etot", "edge": ("Record Payment", "Invoice"),
           "status": "mandatory", "scope": "except typed unapplied cash"},
    "E10": {"family": "etot", "edge": ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"),
            "status": "forbidden"},
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Discovery on the observed log (unmodified outputs retained).
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def run_conformance(ocel, norm_ocdfg, norm_otg, norm_etot):
    obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
    obs_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
    obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. PM4Py conformance diagnostics. These are stored verbatim.
    #    Signatures are assumed; verify against the installed PM4Py version.
    # ------------------------------------------------------------------
    raw = {
        "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(obs_ocdfg, norm_ocdfg),
        "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(obs_otg, norm_otg),
        "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(obs_etot, norm_etot),
    }

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Semantic layer. Reads raw output and metadata; never mutates raw.
    # ------------------------------------------------------------------
    observed_sets = {"ocdfg": obs_ocdfg, "otg": obs_otg, "etot": obs_etot}
    semantic = []
    for norm_id, meta in NORM_META.items():
        obs = observed_sets[meta["family"]]
        present, count = lookup_edge(obs, meta["edge"])  # helper: edge presence and weight
        semantic.append(classify(norm_id, meta, present, count))

    # Open-world: observed edges with no norm entry are labelled, not flagged.
    known = {m["edge"] for m in NORM_META.values()}
    for fam, obs in observed_sets.items():
        for edge in iter_edges(obs):  # helper
            if edge not in known:
                semantic.append({"norm_id": None, "edge": edge,
                                 "label": "unregulated", "family": fam})

    return {"raw_diagnostics": raw, "semantic_findings": semantic}


def classify(norm_id, meta, present, count):
    status = meta["status"]
    base = {"norm_id": norm_id, "edge": meta["edge"], "count": count}
    if status == "forbidden":
        label = "forbidden_candidate" if present else "conforming"
    elif status == "mandatory":
        # Closed-world within scope. In practice, scope must be computed
        # from object attributes; here the gap is shown only as a candidate.
        label = "missing_mandatory_candidate" if not present else "conforming"
    elif status == "typical":
        label = "typical_absence" if not present else "conforming"
    else:  # unspecified
        label = "unregulated"
    return {**base, "label": label}
```

**Notes on use.** `lookup_edge` and `iter_edges` are placeholder helpers that must be implemented against the chosen PM4Py output structure. The `mandatory` branch must be filtered by scope (for example, Packages with an external shipment attribute) before a gap is reported; otherwise, the code will over-report. Exceptions for N5 and E8 should be read from metadata, not hard-coded. The semantic layer can then be joined with data-quality annotations (for example, E10 flagged as a suspected join artifact) in a separate table.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

**G1. Eventual versus directly-follows (N1).** Risk: a stalled order that eventually validates after a long delay is judged compliant, and an order with an unrecorded intermediate review is judged deviant. Clarification: state N1 as "every Order with status *created* is eventually followed by *validated*, with an allowed intermediate set (review, risk check), within a stated time bound." Classification: **mandatory** (the eventual relation), with the direct-successor form marked **typical**.

**G2. Scope predicates undefined.** "Standard Order" (N1) and "externally shipped Package" (N4) are not defined. Risk: the two N4 gaps and the two N1 gaps cannot be classified as violations or out-of-scope. Clarification: define each scope by an attribute (order type, shipment flag). Classification: **explicitly unspecified** for non-standard types; **mandatory** within the defined scope.

**G3. Unapplied cash exception not typed (E8, T3).** Risk: a legitimate exception cannot be recognized, so a valid event is reported as a missing mandatory relation. Clarification: require an event attribute `payment_type = unapplied` as the only exception. Classification: **mandatory** for typed events to link Invoice; **typical** for unapplied cash, with the attribute mandatory.

**G4. Codeath semantics absent (Package–Delivery, 7 edges).** Risk: co-death at confirmation may be mistaken for a dispatch without delivery, or the reverse. Clarification: state that co-death in the same confirmation event is allowed; co-death in separate events is forbidden if the Package was not dispatched. Classification: **unspecified** for the shared confirmation case; **forbidden** for co-death without a prior Dispatch Package.

**G5. Cobirth scope (T6).** The norm forbids Order–Payment cobirth but does not define cobirth for other pairs or for prepayment. Risk: merged records or legitimate prepayment are indistinguishable. Clarification: specify that cobirth is evaluated per event and that prepayment must be recorded as a separate event. Classification: **forbidden** for Order–Payment cobirth in one event; **unspecified** for other pairs.

**G6. Sensor–financial segregation (E10).** The norm forbids Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice but not Sensor–Payment, Sensor–Order, and similar pairs. Risk: a sensor–payment join passes conformance. Clarification: forbid sensor event types from linking any financial object type. Classification: **forbidden**.

**G7. No tolerance or threshold policy.** The norm does not say how many missing mandatory relations trigger action. Risk: either every single gap escalates, or rare gaps are ignored. Clarification: zero tolerance for forbidden relations; investigation (not automatic violation) for mandatory gaps, with a review threshold set by the business. Classification: **explicitly unspecified** for thresholds, with the threshold itself maintained as policy.

**G8. Reopen and Customer relations.** N6 and T4 are unspecified, so reopen and customer behavior is unregulated. Risk: rework patterns are invisible to governance. Clarification: keep **unspecified** but add a monitoring rule for reopen frequency.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**
- Rank 1 (N3): place the two packages on hold; confirm whether shipment has occurred. Do not reverse dispatch actions without review.
- Rank 2 (E10): remove the sensor–invoice link from all financial reporting and exports. Do not escalate as a business incident until the join is confirmed.
- Rank 3 (T6): exclude the event from cobirth analyses pending inspection.

**Data repair**
- Rank 5: backfill Delivery links from carrier references for the two dispatches.
- Rank 6: backfill the order key for the two add-item events.
- Rank 2: correct the join key and re-run the sensor–financial join with a unique identifier.
- Rank 7: tag the single payment as unapplied cash or correct its invoice link after finance confirmation.

**Process investigation**
- Rank 1: determine whether the two cancelled orders were shipped because of a timing error, a consolidation rule, or an operational bypass.
- Rank 4: verify whether the two packages are in scope and whether packing evidence exists. Check for overlap with Rank 5.
- Rank 8: check whether the two orders eventually validated and whether a review step explains the gap.

**Norm revision**
- Apply G1–G7. Prioritize G1 (eventual N1), G3 (typed unapplied cash), and G6 (sensor–financial scope).
- Do not add new mandatory rules for codeath, Customer, or reopen relations; these are unregulated and should stay that way until a business need is shown.

**Monitoring**
- Track forbidden-candidate counts weekly (must be zero).
- Track mandatory gaps by scope, not raw count.
- Track the N5 open-invoice population and aging.
- Track reopen frequency as a rework indicator, not a violation.

Prioritization: Ranks 1 and 2 receive immediate attention. Rank 2 is probably spurious, so its response is containment and repair, not business escalation. Rare benign behavior (write-offs, manual reviews, codeath) is recorded but not escalated merely because it is uncommon.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions:** Rank 1 (two cancelled orders with dispatch) is a forbidden-behavior candidate that requires verification. Mandatory gaps in E2, E6/T2, and N4 are real at the graph level and merit data inspection. Manual reviews, write-offs, reopen events, codeath edges, and Validate  Issue or Close relations are not violations.

**Dependent on norm incompleteness:** Whether the two N4 gaps and two N1 gaps are violations depends on undefined scopes (G2) and eventual versus direct semantics (G1). Whether the single E8 gap is a violation depends on the untyped unapplied-cash exception (G3). The codeath and customer relations are unregulated and cannot be judged.

**Dependent on suspected data quality:** The E10 sensor–invoice link is probably a join artifact. The T6 cobirth may be a merged event. The two missing Delivery links and two missing Order links may be integration gaps rather than process failures.

**Not decidable from graph summaries:** Whether shipped goods for cancelled orders caused customer or financial harm; whether the two N1 orders eventually validated; whether the single unapplied payment is legitimate; whether the 14 open invoices are overdue; and whether the discrepancies between OTG and ET–OT counts reflect real differences or only abstraction. These require event-level traces, timestamps, and source-system records.
