## 1. Interaction-graph description

In this manufacturing environment, operational dynamics cannot be captured by viewing jobs in isolation. Workpieces, production machinery, staging tools, quality lots, and logistics units interact dynamically. We formalize this system as a typed interaction graph $G = (V_O, V_E, E_{EO}, E_{OO})$, where $V_O$ denotes object instances, $V_E$ denotes execution events, $E_{EO}$ denotes event-to-object participations, and $E_{OO}$ denotes structural object-to-object relations.

### Object and Edge Types
- **Node Types ($V_O$):**
  - **Job ($J \in \{J1, \dots, J6\}$):** Production units representing customer work orders.
  - **Component ($C \in \{C1, \dots, C6\}$):** Physical parts mapped 1:1 to jobs.
  - **ComponentLot ($L \in \{L7, L8\}$):** Quality lots grouping components ($1:N$).
  - **Machine ($M \in \{\text{CNC-2}, \text{OVEN-1}, \text{TEST-1}\}$):** Primary stationary capital assets.
  - **Tool ($T \in \{\text{FIX-A}, \text{RACK-7}, \text{CAL-3}\}$):** Movable or calibrating secondary assets required concurrently with machines.
  - **Batch ($B \in \{B1, B2\}$):** Ephemeral groupings for simultaneous chamber operations ($1:N$ over Jobs).
  - **Shipment ($S \in \{S1, S2, S3\}$):** Outbound logistics consolidations ($1:N$ over Jobs).
- **Edge Types and Qualifiers:**
  - Structural: `requires` ($J \to C$, 1:1), `contains` ($L \to C$, 1:N), `batches` ($B \to J$, 1:N), `consolidates` ($S \to J$, 1:N).
  - Dynamic / Allocation: `executes_on` ($E \to M$), `allocates_tool` ($E \to T$), `processes` ($E \to J$), `governs` ($E \to L$).

### Higher-Order Motifs
1. **Multi-Job Batch Synchronization on Shared Machine and Tool:** $(\{J1, J2, J3\} \xrightarrow{\text{batches}} B1) \cup (B1 \xrightarrow{\text{allocates}} \{\text{OVEN-1}, \text{RACK-7}\})$. Multiple jobs synchronize into an $N:1$ barrier requiring simultaneous availability of a chamber and carrier.
2. **Asynchronous Shared Tool Contention across Machine Cycles:** $(J3 \xrightarrow{\text{holds}} \text{FIX-A}) \wedge (J4 \xrightarrow{\text{requires}} \text{FIX-A}) \wedge (\text{CNC-2} \xrightarrow{\text{executes}} \{J3, J4\})$. Secondary tool retention off-machine starves the primary asset, forcing an idle gap while work is queued.
3. **Quality Lot Coupling with Shadow Policy Divergence:** $L7 \xrightarrow{\text{contains}} \{C2, C5\} \wedge (J2 \xrightarrow{\text{override}} B1) \wedge (J5 \xrightarrow{\text{blocked}} \text{Quarantine})$. Administrative holds halt sibling jobs unless bypassed by unmodeled overrides, injecting variance downstream.
4. **Logistics Convergence Barrier:** $(\{J1, J4\} \xrightarrow{\text{consolidates}} S1) \wedge (J1 \in B1) \wedge (J4 \in B2)$. Jobs completing on divergent operational paths are locked into joint departure.

### Direct vs. Inferred Edges
- **Direct Observations:** Declared structural relations ($J \to C$, $L \to C$, $B \to J$, $S \to J$); logged event participations (`Batch Cure` linking $B1, \text{OVEN-1}, \text{RACK-7}, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3$; `Machine Process` linking $J4, C4, \text{CNC-2}, \text{FIX-A}$; lot quarantine/release; final test).
- **Inferred Edges:** Off-machine tool holding ($J3 \to \text{FIX-A}$ during $\text{CNC-2}$ idle time 10:05–10:47); unlogged waiting states; starvation dependencies ($J4$ blocked by $\text{FIX-A}$; $J6$ blocked by $\text{CAL-3}$); and shadow override execution of $J2$ recorded in an external system.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

We quantify five operational waiting periods using the recorded event timestamps. Each calculation explicitly specifies its counterfactual baseline; these reflect hypothesis tests rather than causal proofs.

### 1. Batch B1 Synchronization Waiting for J1 and J2
- **Timestamps:** $J1$ ready at $08:10$; $J2$ ready at $08:25$; $J3$ ready at $11:55$; $B1$ `Batch Cure` starts at $12:05$.
- **Observed Dwell:** Total wait for $J1 = 235\text{ min}$ ($3\text{ h } 55\text{ min}$); total wait for $J2 = 220\text{ min}$ ($3\text{ h } 40\text{ min}$). Chamber loading/prep window ($J3$ arrival to cure) $= 10\text{ min}$.
- **Decomposition:** Pure synchronization delay waiting for late-arriving $J3$:
  $$\Delta t_{\text{sync}}(J1) = 11:55 - 08:10 = 225\text{ min } (3.75\text{ h})$$
  $$\Delta t_{\text{sync}}(J2) = 11:55 - 08:25 = 210\text{ min } (3.50\text{ h})$$
- **Counterfactual Assumption:** If operating policy allowed variable-size batches (dispatching a 2-job batch upon $J2$'s readiness at $08:25$ with a 10-minute load, starting at $08:35$) or if $J3$ had arrived synchronously at $08:25$, $J1$ would have waited $25\text{ min}$ and $J2$ would have waited $10\text{ min}$, eliminating $210\text{ min}$ of queue dwell.

### 2. Secondary Tool Contention Delay on J4 (FIX-A)
- **Timestamps:** $J3$ holds $\text{FIX-A}$ from $09:00$ to $11:40$; $J4$ reaches $\text{CNC-2}$ at $09:50$; $\text{CNC-2}$ idle from $10:05$ to $10:47$ ($42\text{ min}$); $J4$ `Machine Process` starts at $11:45$.
- **Observed Dwell:** Total queue wait for $J4 = 11:45 - 09:50 = 115\text{ min}$ ($1\text{ h } 55\text{ min}$). Setup duration $= 5\text{ min}$.
- **Decomposition:** Tool contention window $= 11:40 - 09:50 = 110\text{ min}$, overlapping $42\text{ min}$ of unforced $\text{CNC-2}$ machine idling.
- **Counterfactual Assumption:** If $\text{FIX-A}$ were non-exclusive (duplicate fixture available) or if $J3$ had released $\text{FIX-A}$ upon completing its cut prior to $10:05$, $\text{CNC-2}$ could have loaded $J4$ at $10:05$ (starting at $10:10$ post-setup), eliminating $95\text{ min}$ of delay and recovering $42\text{ min}$ of machine capacity.

### 3. Finished-Goods Dock Holding Imposed on J1 by Shipment S1 Consolidation
- **Timestamps:** $J1$ packed at $13:30$; $J4$ packed at $19:10$; Shipment $S1$ dispatches at $19:25$.
- **Observed Dwell:** Total post-pack holding for $J1 = 19:25 - 13:30 = 355\text{ min}$ ($5\text{ h } 55\text{ min}$). Final loading overhead $= 15\text{ min}$ (benchmarked against $S2$ at $25\text{ min}$ and $S3$ at $35\text{ min}$).
- **Decomposition:** Pure partner-synchronization wait for $J4 = 19:10 - 13:30 = 340\text{ min}$ ($5\text{ h } 40\text{ min}$).
- **Counterfactual Assumption:** If outbound logistics permitted decoupled single-item dispatch upon packing with typical handling ($30\text{ min}$ post-pack, departing at $14:00$), $J1$'s delivery lead time would decrease by $325\text{ min}$ ($5.42\text{ h}$).

### 4. Testing Line Stoppage and Delay Imposed on J6 by CAL-3 Recalibration
- **Timestamps:** $J5$ ready for test at $16:00$; $J6$ ready for test at $16:05$; $J5$ tests on $\text{TEST-1}$ from $16:10$ to $16:35$; $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibration from $16:35$ to $17:20$ ($45\text{ min}$); $\text{TEST-1}$ idle from $16:35$ to $17:25$; $J6$ `Final Test` starts at $17:25$.
- **Observed Dwell:** Total wait for $J6 = 17:25 - 16:05 = 80\text{ min}$.
- **Decomposition:** Wait behind active job $J5 = 30\text{ min}$; wait attributable to tool recalibration $= 45\text{ min}$; setup $= 5\text{ min}$.
- **Counterfactual Assumption:** If $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibration were scheduled off-shift or a second calibrated unit ($\text{CAL-3B}$) were available, $J6$ could have initiated testing at $16:40$ post-$J5$, saving $45\text{ min}$ of job queue delay and avoiding $50\text{ min}$ of idle machine capacity.

### 5. Multi-Factor Delay on J4 for Batch B2 (Tool Turnaround vs. Lot Coupling)
- **Timestamps:** $J4$ ready for batch at $13:10$; $\text{OVEN-1}$ idle from $12:55$; $\text{RACK-7}$ cleaning/quarantine completes at $14:35$; $J5$ ready for batch at $14:40$ (following $L7$ release at $13:50$); $B2$ starts at $14:50$.
- **Observed Dwell:** Total wait for $J4 = 14:50 - 13:10 = 100\text{ min}$. $\text{OVEN-1}$ idle gap $= 115\text{ min}$.
- **Decomposition:** Unavailability of $\text{RACK-7}$ was binding from $13:10$ to $14:35$ ($85\text{ min}$). Waiting for late-arriving $J5$ added $5\text{ min}$ ($14:35$ to $14:40$), followed by $10\text{ min}$ chamber loading.
- **Counterfactual Assumption:** If a spare rack were available and solo curing permitted, $J4$ starts at $13:10$, saving $100\text{ min}$. If batching with $J5$ was mandatory, had $L7$ not been quarantined ($10:20$–$13:50$), $J5$ would have arrived earlier, leaving $\text{RACK-7}$ turnaround as the sole gating factor ($85\text{ min}$).

### Ranking Justification
Primary machine capacity ranks lowest because machines are chronically starved or blocked rather than saturated. Batch synchronization ranks highest because it directly inflicts over $9$ cumulative job-hours of pre-cure starvation while idling thermal assets. Secondary tool contention and shipment consolidation rank second and third, imposing major machine starvation and finished-goods holding. Administrative lot coupling injects unpredictable variance, exacerbated by logging blind spots that hide these multi-object dynamics.

## 4. What flattening misses

Flattening an object-centric event log onto a single object type projects a multidimensional execution graph onto a single dimension, producing convergence/divergence anomalies and obscuring cross-object dependencies.

### Viewpoint Analysis

#### 1. Job Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Step-to-step lead times, activity sequences, and rework loops ($2$ of $6$ jobs undergo rework).
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Shared resource contention, multi-job batch synchronization barriers, and non-job events (lot quarantines, tool recalibrations) are omitted.
- **Misleading Conclusion:** Job DFG shows `Pack -> Dispatch` with a median duration of $1.1\text{ h}$ and reports job variance $< 1.8$ standard deviations from the mean. Management concludes the flow is in statistical control, entirely missing that $J1$ waited $5\text{ h } 55\text{ min}$ on the shipping dock.

#### 2. Machine Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Asset utilization rates ($\text{CNC-2}: 54\%$, $\text{OVEN-1}: 44\%$, $\text{TEST-1}: 58\%$) and machine execution sequences.
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Multi-job batch runs appear as single unweighted events or duplicated artificial runs; queue lengths and waiting job identities are invisible; tool dependencies are excluded.
- **Misleading Conclusion:** Low utilizations and short inter-event gaps suggest healthy, balanced operations with ample headroom, failing to reveal that machines are starved by tooling and batch rules.

#### 3. Batch Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Thermal cure cycle runtimes, oven loading times, and batch execution consistency.
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Pre-batch arrival dispersion of constituent jobs ($J1$ waiting $235\text{ min}$ vs. $J3$ waiting $10\text{ min}$); post-cure job paths; non-batched jobs ($J6$).
- **Misleading Conclusion:** `Batch Cure` appears highly standardized and efficient ($\approx 50\text{ min}$), obscuring the $9+$ cumulative job-hours wasted during batch assembly.

#### 4. Tool Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Tool utilization cycles, maintenance events ($\text{RACK-7}$ cleaning, $\text{CAL-3}$ calibration), and checkout durations.
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Customer job identities and delivery urgencies; reasons for extended tool retention (e.g., $J3$ holding $\text{FIX-A}$ while $\text{CNC-2}$ was idle).
- **Misleading Conclusion:** Telemetry shows regular, compliant maintenance, concluding tool health is well-managed while missing that tool unavailability paralyzed $\text{CNC-2}$ and $\text{TEST-1}$.

#### 5. ComponentLot Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Quality hold lifecycles (`Quarantine Lot`, `Release Lot`) and vendor batch containment boundaries.
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Non-quarantined lots ($L8$); physical progress made via shadow overrides ($J2$ consuming $C2$ during quarantine); all machine and logistics execution.
- **Misleading Conclusion:** Quality logs show clean containment of $L7$ from $10:20$ to $13:50$ without process leaks, completely blind to $J2$'s unauthorized override.

#### 6. Shipment Viewpoint
- **Visible:** Logistics loading and dispatch performance (`Create Shipment -> Load -> Dispatch`, median $1.1\text{ h}$).
- **Hidden / Duplicated:** Finished-goods staging dwell prior to shipment creation; compresses multi-job waiting into a single timestamp.
- **Misleading Conclusion:** Dock operations appear rapid and efficient ($1.1\text{ h}$ turnaround), concealing that early-finished jobs spent hours idling on the floor.

### Coexistence of Low Machine Utilization and Long Job Waiting
In classical queuing theory, extensive queues imply machine saturation. Here, low utilization ($44\text{–}58\%$) coexists with severe job delays because:
1. **Secondary Asset Starvation:** Machines cannot operate without secondary tooling. $\text{CNC-2}$ sat idle for $42\text{ min}$ while $J4$ waited because $\text{FIX-A}$ was missing; $\text{TEST-1}$ sat idle for $50\text{ min}$ while $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibrated.
2. **Artificial Batch Accumulation:** Machines remain powered down while waiting for batch accumulation policies to be satisfied ($\text{OVEN-1}$ idle for $115\text{ min}$).
3. **Decoupled Downstream Staging:** Nearly $6\text{ hours}$ of $J1$'s delay occurred after machine processing finished, during finished-goods consignment consolidation.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

We trace three multi-object causal narratives spanning at least four object types, rigorously separating operational facts from causal deductions.

### Narrative A: Cascading Lot Hold, Tool Cleaning, and Thermal Batching
*Objects Spanned:* ComponentLot ($L7$), Component ($C5$), Job ($J5$), Tool ($\text{RACK-7}$), Machine ($\text{OVEN-1}$), Batch ($B2$), Job ($J4$).

- **Temporal Precedence:** $L7$ quarantined at $10:20$; $J2$ receives unlogged override into $B1$, while $J5$ halts; $B1$ unloads at $12:55$, freeing $\text{OVEN-1}$ and sending $\text{RACK-7}$ to cleaning; $J4$ reaches oven staging at $13:10$; $L7$ released at $13:50$; $\text{RACK-7}$ cleaning finishes at $14:35$; $J5$ reaches oven staging at $14:40$; $B2$ starts at $14:50$.
- **Resource Feasibility:** $\text{OVEN-1}$ was operational and idle from $12:55$ to $14:50$; $J4$ was ready from $13:10$. However, $\text{RACK-7}$ was unavailable until $14:35$, and batching required $J5$.
- **Policy Constraints:** Quarantined components cannot be processed without override; $\text{RACK-7}$ must undergo decontamination between cycles; $\text{OVEN-1}$ cannot run single-job batches.
- **Causal Speculation:** Even if $L7$ had released early, $B2$ could not have started before $14:35$ due to $\text{RACK-7}$ decontamination. $\text{RACK-7}$ was the binding constraint from $13:10$ to $14:35$, at which point $J5$'s late arrival added $5\text{ min}$ before loading began.

### Narrative B: Fixture-CNC Starvation Cascading into Logistics Consignment
*Objects Spanned:* Job ($J3$), Tool ($\text{FIX-A}$), Machine ($\text{CNC-2}$), Job ($J4$), Batch ($B2$), Job ($J1$), Shipment ($S1$).

- **Temporal Precedence:** $J3$ acquires $\text{FIX-A}$ at $09:00$; $J4$ reaches $\text{CNC-2}$ at $09:50$; $\text{CNC-2}$ idles from $10:05$ to $10:47$; $J3$ releases $\text{FIX-A}$ at $11:40$; $J4$ machining starts at $11:45$; $J4$ cures in $B2$ ($14:50$), completes packing at $19:10$; $J1$ completes packing at $13:30$; $S1$ dispatches at $19:25$.
- **Resource Feasibility:** $\text{CNC-2}$ was powered and idle at $10:05$; staging docks had capacity to ship $J1$ after $13:30$.
- **Policy Constraints:** Single exclusive fixture $\text{FIX-A}$; strict consignment bundling (no partial shipments for $S1$).
- **Causal Speculation:** $J3$'s fixture retention delayed $J4$'s cut by $95\text{ min}$, pushing $J4$ into the delayed $B2$ cycle and postponing packing to $19:10$. This morning fixture contention directly cascaded across multiple operations to hold finished job $J1$ on the dock for nearly $6\text{ hours}$.

### Narrative C: Shadow Override Disparity and Serial Calibration Starvation
*Objects Spanned:* ComponentLot ($L7$), Job ($J2$), Batch ($B1$), Tool ($\text{CAL-3}$), Machine ($\text{TEST-1}$), Job ($J5$), Job ($J6$), Shipment ($S3$).

- **Temporal Precedence:** $L7$ quarantined at $10:20$; supervisor override allows $J2$ into $B1$ ($12:05$), while $J5$ holds until $13:50$; $J5$ cures in $B2$ ($14:50$), arriving at $\text{TEST-1}$ at $16:00$; $J6$ arrives at $16:05$; $J5$ tests from $16:10$ to $16:35$; $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibrates from $16:35$ to $17:20$; $\text{TEST-1}$ idles; $J6$ tests from $17:25$ to $17:50$; $S3$ dispatches at $18:25$.
- **Resource Feasibility:** $\text{TEST-1}$ was functional between $16:35$ and $17:20$ but lacked a calibrated gauge.
- **Policy Constraints:** Quality protocol requires $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibration after intensive runs; shipment $S3$ requires both $J5$ and $J6$.
- **Causal Speculation:** If $J5$ had received an override alongside $J2$, it would have cured in $B1$ and completed testing before $14:00$. $\text{CAL-3}$ would have recalibrated well before $J6$'s arrival at $16:05$, eliminating $J6$'s $80\text{-minute}$ delay and accelerating $S3$'s departure.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

### 1. Dynamic / Time-Out Batching for Thermal Operations
- **Target Mechanism:** Batch synchronization barriers on $\text{OVEN-1}$ ($B1, B2$).
- **Expected Benefit:** Dispatches partial batches if waiting exceeds $45\text{ min}$, recovering up to $3.5\text{ h}$ of queue dwell for early jobs ($J1, J2, J4$).
- **Jobs / Objects Helped:** $J1, J2, J4$; reduces WIP inventory.
- **Jobs / Objects Potentially Harmed:** Later-arriving jobs face an occupied oven; thermal efficiency drops.
- **New Cost or Risk:** Increased utility costs from smaller thermal runs; thermal cycling wear on $\text{OVEN-1}$.
- **Perverse Incentive Metric:** *Oven Thermal Efficiency / Batch Fill Rate* (encourages holding jobs to maximize batch density).
- **Object-Centric Evaluation:** Track `Job_Queue_Dwell_Time` relative to `Batch_Size` and total power consumption using OCEL relations.

### 2. Secondary Tooling Redundancy and Decoupled Staging
- **Target Mechanism:** Tool contention on $\text{FIX-A}$ and turnaround downtime on $\text{RACK-7}$.
- **Expected Benefit:** Procure duplicate $\text{FIX-B}$ and $\text{RACK-8}$, enabling offline part clamping and continuous rack rotation, saving $110\text{ min}$ on $\text{CNC-2}$ and $85\text{ min}$ on $\text{OVEN-1}$.
- **Jobs / Objects Helped:** $J4, \text{CNC-2}, \text{OVEN-1}, B2$.
- **Jobs / Objects Potentially Harmed:** Tooling maintenance crew (expanded asset registry).
- **New Cost or Risk:** Tool acquisition capital; risk of calibration drift across duplicate fixtures.
- **Perverse Incentive Metric:** *Tool Utilization Rate* (discourages purchasing buffer tools because individual asset utilization drops).
- **Object-Centric Evaluation:** Measure concurrent tool holding states and calculate `Tool_Induced_Machine_Idle_Intervals` using E2O `allocates_tool` relations.

### 3. Off-Shift Preventative Calibration and Hot-Swap Gauge Redundancy
- **Target Mechanism:** Mid-shift testing stoppages on $\text{TEST-1}$ caused by $\text{CAL-3}$ recalibration.
- **Expected Benefit:** Shift calibration routines to shift changes and introduce a pre-calibrated hot-swap unit ($\text{CAL-3B}$), eliminating $50\text{ min}$ of $\text{TEST-1}$ idling and expediting $J6$ and $S3$.
- **Jobs / Objects Helped:** $J6, \text{TEST-1}, S3$.
- **Jobs / Objects Potentially Harmed:** Calibration lab staff (realigned shifts).
- **New Cost or Risk:** Gauge lease/certification expense; potential drift on shelved hot-swap tools.
- **Perverse Incentive Metric:** *Calibration Compliance per Machine Operating Hour* (prompts unnecessary mid-shift shutdowns to meet compliance quotas).
- **Object-Centric Evaluation:** Correlate `Job_Test_Wait_Duration` against mid-shift `Recalibrate` events linked to $\text{TEST-1}$.

### 4. SLA-Triggered Dynamic Shipment Release
- **Target Mechanism:** Finished-goods dock holding induced by shipment consolidation ($S1$).
- **Expected Benefit:** Automatically authorize split-shipment dispatch if partner job arrival delta exceeds $90\text{ min}$, releasing $J1$ at $14:00$ instead of $19:25$ (saving $5.4\text{ h}$).
- **Jobs / Objects Helped:** $J1$, warehouse dock capacity, customer responsiveness.
- **Jobs / Objects Potentially Harmed:** Late jobs ($J4$) absorb single-item freight overhead; dock staging teams.
- **New Cost or Risk:** Higher freight expenses from less-than-truckload (LTL) shipments; customer receiving split shipments.
- **Perverse Incentive Metric:** *Freight Cost per Ton-Mile / Truck Load Fill %* (rewards holding cargo until trucks are fully packed).
- **Object-Centric Evaluation:** Calculate `Job_Pack_Timestamp` to `Shipment_Dispatch_Timestamp` latency across all $(J, S)$ pairs versus freight costs.

### 5. Unified Object-Centric Telemetry and Override Integration
- **Target Mechanism:** Data integration blind spots, unlogged supervisor overrides, and omitted lot relations.
- **Expected Benefit:** Unify QMS, MES, and Tool Tracking into an automated OCEL schema logging `Tool_Requested`, `Tool_Released`, `Wait_For_Resource`, and digital `Supervisor_Override` events, eliminating shadow processes.
- **Jobs / Objects Helped:** Entire enterprise pipeline; quality and planning teams.
- **Jobs / Objects Potentially Harmed:** Personnel accustomed to informal, unrecorded shop-floor overrides.
- **New Cost or Risk:** Software licensing, barcode/RFID scanning hardware, and user training.
- **Perverse Incentive Metric:** *Operator Scan Compliance Rate* (leads operators to log fake scans to satisfy compliance quotas).
- **Object-Centric Evaluation:** Audit variance between physical asset runtimes and digital event coverage; verify $100\%$ linkage of lot holds and overrides to affected jobs.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The following Python script illustrates how an object-centric process mining pipeline analyzes these cross-instance dependencies in PM4Py (version 2.7+ / 3.x), comparing multi-object interaction frequencies and quantifying cross-object synchronization delays without relying on machine idle gaps.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and Inspect Object-Centric Event Log (OCEL)
# Assumes variable `ocel` is already instantiated in the environment.
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("=== 1. OCEL Basic Summary and Object Types ===")
print(f"Object Types Present: {ocel.object_types}")

# Discover interaction summary across object instances
interactions_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
print("\nTop Object-to-Object Interactions Summary:")
print(interactions_summary.head(10))

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Structural Interaction Graph Discovery
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Discover Object Type Graph (OTG) showing how object classes couple
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)

# Discover Event Type to Object Type (ETOT) bindings
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Discover Object Graph showing instance-level inter-object networks
objects_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_object")

# Optionally convert to NetworkX for structural centrality and motif analysis
nx_graph = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
print(f"Interaction Network: {nx_graph.number_of_nodes()} nodes, {nx_graph.number_of_edges()} edges.")

# Discover Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph (OC-DFG)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Comparing Object-Centric Frequency Metrics
# Compare Event-Participation Frequency vs Object-Instance Co-occurrence Frequency
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== 3. Object-Centric Frequency Metrics Comparison ===")
events_df = ocel.events.copy()
rel_df = ocel.relations.copy()

# Metric A: Event Execution Count per Object Type (Participation Frequency)
event_part_freq = rel_df.groupby("ocel:type")["ocel:eid"].nunique()
print("\nMetric A - Unique Event Participation by Object Type:")
print(event_part_freq)

# Metric B: Instance-to-Instance Co-Occurrence Degree (Structural Coupling)
cooccur_df = rel_df.merge(rel_df, on="ocel:eid", suffixes=("_src", "_tgt"))
cross_coupling = (
    cooccur_df[cooccur_df["ocel:type_src"] == "Job"]
    .groupby(["ocel:type_tgt", "ocel:oid_tgt"])["ocel:oid_src"]
    .nunique()
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"ocel:oid_src": "distinct_jobs_coupled"})
)
print("\nMetric B - Distinct Jobs Coupled to Supporting Objects:")
print(cross_coupling[cross_coupling["ocel:type_tgt"].isin(["Tool", "Batch", "Shipment", "ComponentLot"])])

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Flattening and Performance Comparison (Job vs Machine)
# Demonstrates why traditional flattenings hide cross-instance wait times
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== 4. Flattened Viewpoint Comparison ===")
job_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Job")
job_perf_dfg, job_start_act, job_end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(job_log)

machine_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Machine")
machine_perf_dfg, machine_start_act, machine_end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(machine_log)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Non-Machine-Gap Waiting Time Extraction (Cross-Object Synchronization)
# Directly calculates synchronization delay using readiness vs event start
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== 5. Cross-Object Delay Extraction (No Machine-Gap Inference) ===")
batch_events = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Batch"].merge(
    events_df, left_on="ocel:eid", right_on="ocel:eid"
)
job_events = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Job"].merge(
    events_df, left_on="ocel:eid", right_on="ocel:eid"
)

# Identify stage completion (readiness) vs batch cure start
ready_events = job_events[job_events["ocel:activity"] == "Stage Component"][
    ["ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"]
].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "job_id", "ocel:timestamp": "ready_time"})

batch_start_events = batch_events[batch_events["ocel:activity"] == "Batch Cure"][
    ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"]
].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "batch_id", "ocel:timestamp": "cure_start_time"})

# Join through relations to link Job to its Batch
job_batch_map = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Job"].merge(
    rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Batch"], on="ocel:eid"
)[["ocel:oid_x", "ocel:oid_y"]].drop_duplicates().rename(
    columns={"ocel:oid_x": "job_id", "ocel:oid_y": "batch_id"}
)

sync_analysis = (
    ready_events.merge(job_batch_map, on="job_id")
    .merge(batch_start_events, on="batch_id")
)
sync_analysis["sync_wait_minutes"] = (
    (sync_analysis["cure_start_time"] - sync_analysis["ready_time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
)
print("\nComputed Synchronization Waiting Time per Job in Batching:")
print(sync_analysis[["job_id", "batch_id", "ready_time", "cure_start_time", "sync_wait_minutes"]])

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Extract OCEL Feature Table for Machine Learning / Root-Cause Mining
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
ocel_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel)
print("\nExtracted Object-Centric Feature Vector Matrix for Diagnostic Analytics.")
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Observations That Would Weaken Top-Ranked Hypotheses

The top-ranked hypotheses attribute delays primarily to batch synchronization, secondary tool contention, and shipment consolidation. The following six empirical findings would weaken or refute these explanations:

1. **Mandatory In-Chamber Conditioning / Pre-Soak:** If metallurgical logs show components $C1$ and $C2$ required a mandatory, unlogged $3.5\text{-hour}$ ambient degas or pre-soak hold inside the chamber prior to heating, $J1$ and $J2$'s dwell between $08:10$ and $12:05$ reflects essential material science rather than avoidable waiting for $J3$.
2. **Unlogged Primary Machine Breakdown:** If maintenance logs reveal that $\text{CNC-2}$ suffered an electrical drive fault or spindle realignment from $10:05$ to $10:47$, the idle gap was caused by machine breakdown rather than $\text{FIX-A}$ exclusivity.
3. **Dedicated Tool Crib Availability:** If tool crib audits demonstrate that $\text{FIX-A}$ was returned to the crib at $10:05$ and sat available, $J4$'s delay until $11:45$ was driven by operator absence or missing NC programs, invalidating the fixture-contention hypothesis.
4. **Mandatory Carrier Cleaning Turnaround Physics:** If $\text{RACK-7}$ cleaning was completed in 10 minutes (by $13:05$) and the oven operator was reassigned elsewhere until $14:35$, human staffing—not carrier decontamination—was the true operational bottleneck.
5. **Customer Contractual Delivery Windows:** If the customer for Shipment $S1$ specifically scheduled carrier pickup for $19:25$, $J1$'s post-packing wait on the dock was planned schedule slack rather than an avoidable bottleneck.
6. **Regulatory-Mandated Calibration Timing:** If metrology standards mandate that $\text{CAL-3}$ be recalibrated after every aerospace-grade test run regardless of schedule, the recalibration during $\text{TEST-1}$'s shift was an irreducible regulatory compliance constraint.

### Additional Fields and Event Types Required to Discriminate

To definitively separate competing hypotheses, the manufacturer must integrate the following six event types and metadata fields into its logging schema:

1. **Explicit Tool Lifecycle Events (`Tool_Mounted`, `Tool_Unmounted`, `Decontamination_Complete`):** Instrument fixtures and carriers with RFID tracking to log exact physical release timestamps, eliminating inferred tool holds.
2. **Buffer and Queue Telemetry (`Buffer_Enter`, `Buffer_Exit`, `Starved_For_Resource`):** Log when jobs enter and exit physical buffers, capturing categorical delay codes (`Waiting_For_Batch`, `Waiting_For_Tool`, `Machine_Breakdown`).
3. **Formal Override Governance Telemetry (`Override_Requested`, `Override_Granted`):** Capture digital authorization records in the core MES including `Approving_Manager_ID` and `Override_Justification_Code` to eliminate unmonitored shadow bypasses.
4. **Machine Sensor State Telemetry (`Spindle_Active`, `Thermal_Ramp`, `Starved_Idle`, `Blocked_Idle`):** Connect PLC sensors directly to event logs to mathematically separate machine unreliability from external starvation.
5. **Logistics Consignment Booking Attributes (`Carrier_Target_Pickup_Window`, `Split_Shipment_Allowed`):** Include contractual logistics metadata within Shipment objects to determine whether dock dwell represents customer-dictated timing or internal process latency.
6. **Granular Quality Hold Lineage (`Sample_Drawn`, `Quarantine_Scope_Job_List`, `Hold_Disposition_Type`):** Record exact lot-to-job containment mappings upon quarantine creation, providing real-time propagation alerts when jobs are halted by supplier-lot defects.
