**Redesigned flow.**
1. Intake enriches every claim with segment, value, and defect-type attributes; the auto-screen rules are updated to be segment-aware (deployable in two weeks).
2. **Green lane** (low-risk accessories, evidence complete; Premium accessories keep the existing R2 exemption): Auto screen  Fast approval  Dispatch  Close, unchanged. Instead of adding pre-control, QC inspectors run a retrospective sample audit on closed green-lane cases.
3. **Amber lane** (claim value > EUR 400 or high-risk defect type): at intake, a *review bundle* is launched **in parallel** — evidence-completeness check, fraud/risk review, and part-price prefetch run concurrently rather than serially — then technical decision, then either replacement with a mandatory pre-dispatch parts verification, or quote  approval  repair  quality check. R1 and R2 are encoded as *enabling conditions*: the Dispatch activity cannot fire unless the required review and evidence-completeness events exist, making today's move-on-log violations unreachable rather than merely audited.
4. **Reopen handling:** a complaint/reopen event automatically blocks closure and spawns a manual-review task (R3 by construction); reopened high-value claims route to the amber lane.

**Targets.** The 35% of cases that are high-value devices, where fast-path complaint rates (18–22%) and EUR 410 error costs dominate; R1/R2/R3 deviations; the serial 96–120h reviewed path (edges of 14h + 34h + 22h + 30h that can be partially parallelized).

**Mechanism of improvement.** Control moves from after-the-fact conformance checking into routing logic, so compliance stops depending on individual discretion (also raising the discovered model's precision by eliminating ad-hoc variants). Accessories keep their 8–13h speed because the design deliberately does *not* add control where error cost is EUR 95 and complaint deltas are 2–4pp. High-value cases get slower than today's 20–28h fast path but faster than today's 96–120h reviewed path, because the review content is parallelized rather than queued end-to-end.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** It accepts higher per-case control cost on high-value claims and a premium high-value cycle time above today's fast-path 20h. The updated rules will increase manual-review inflow by 15–35% against a resource that degrades above 82% utilization — this redesign is only viable with added capacity or a demand offset, which it does not itself provide. It does not fix evidence-collection friction, part-price waiting, or escalation scalability (left to B and C), and it accepts the residual 6–8% accessory complaint rate as economically rational.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first flow with verify-before-ship

**Redesigned flow.**
1. A new explicit milestone activity, **Evidence completeness confirmed**, is logged for every claim; no dispatch decision may consume a case lacking it (Premium low-risk accessories exempt per R2).
2. **Evidence portal with a single consolidated request**: all required items listed once, with examples, status, and deadline nudges — replacing the ad-hoc loop that currently repeats in 26% of entering cases. An **assisted channel** (agent completes the portal with the customer by phone/chat) is offered to customers flagged or self-identifying as low digital literacy.
3. **Pre-dispatch verification gate** for replacements, staffed by the currently underused quality inspectors: mandatory for high-value devices, sampled or skipped for accessories. Adds 6–18h by design tier.
4. **Repair-quote acceleration:** pre-approved parts price bands/catalog to eliminate the part-price-confirmation wait that drives the 22h Manual review  Repair quote edge and the 30h customer-approval edge; one-click approval in the portal to cut the 70 abandonments after quote.
5. **Proactive status notifications** at every wait state, exploiting the finding that Standard customers tolerate 72h when visibility is good.

**Targets.** The unclear-defect/missing-evidence cohort (22% of cases, 140h median, highest abandonment); the Dispatch  Complaint long tail (260 edges, 96h mean, p90 180h, wrong-part driven); repair-quote waiting; Standard-segment experience.

**Mechanism of improvement.** This redesign removes *failure demand at its source* — repeated evidence loops and wrong-part dispatches — rather than re-routing who gets reviewed. It converts invisible waiting into visible waiting (notifications) and converts inspector slack into error prevention, so it adds almost no load to the constrained resources (manual review, fraud review). Each avoided wrong high-value replacement is worth roughly EUR 410 plus a avoided 96h complaint tail; the portal targets a 30–45% reduction in repeated evidence requests.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** It leaves triage logic untouched: without pairing, high-value claims can still reach fast approval in violation of R1. The portal carries the stated risk of increased abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers — mitigated, not eliminated, by the assisted channel. The gate adds 6–18h elapsed time, which is unacceptable for Premium accessories (24h credit threshold), so they must remain exempt. It requires portal capex and systems integration, the longest build of the three redesigns.

## 4. Redesign C: Premium cell, delegated exceptions, and smoothed specialist capacity

**Redesigned flow.**
1. **Dedicated premium cell:** a small cross-functional team (reviewer, technician, QC-trained agent) owns Premium claims end-to-end with a named case manager as single point of contact. The cell pulls fraud/risk expertise through reserved daily slots rather than the general queue, and performs the R1/R2-required review inside the cell — targeting premium high-value end-to-end well below today's 96h reviewed path, while premium accessories stay on the 8h fast lane.
2. **Standard pool** continues as today but with proactive status communication and an explicit 72h resolution promise (aligned to the observed tolerance).
3. **Escalation redesign:** replace the single daily 45-minute board with a delegated decision matrix — agents resolve goodwill, reshipment, and credit decisions up to defined EUR thresholds (e.g., the EUR 95 accessory error class); the board meets twice weekly for true exceptions only.
4. **Capacity smoothing:** fraud/risk schedules aligned to the known Monday and post-campaign surges (shift patterns tied to the marketing calendar); QC inspectors cross-trained as overflow for manual review to hold utilization below the 82% cliff, which also creates the headroom a future rule update (Redesign A) would consume.
5. **Reopen ownership:** reopened claims return to the original cell/owner, who performs the R3-mandated manual review, instead of re-entering the general queue (today: 310 edges at 40h mean).

**Targets.** Premium high-value cases (14% of cases; 96h reviewed, 22% fast-path complaints, high compliance risk); the reopen/escalation tail (430 complaint outflows at 40–54h); the fraud/risk bottleneck.

**Mechanism of improvement.** This redesign changes *who does the work and what is promised*, not the decision logic: it removes handoffs and queueing for the highest-stakes cases, converts batched escalation into continuous flow, smooths predictable demand peaks, and uses cross-trained flex capacity to decouple the process from the 82% utilization cliff. Cycle-time gains come from queue removal, not from skipping controls — the cell performs the same reviews with fewer transfers.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** A fixed-cost cell serving 14% of volume carries real underutilization risk, and staffing it from the existing 7 reviewers would starve the standard pool unless backfilled. Delegated escalation risks inconsistent decisions and R3 breaches without guardrails (thresholds, sampling audits). Standard customers get predictability and visibility, not speed — their cycle time may even rise when specialists are pulled into premium slots. It does nothing about evidence friction or wrong-part selection by itself, and it is the highest organizational-change complexity of the three.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (gated lanes)**
- *Failure 1 — capacity cliff:* the +15–35% manual-review inflow pushes utilization past 82%, where queues grow sharply; the IntakeManual edge (already 7.5h mean, 18h p90) deteriorates and premium high-value ends up slower than before.
- *Failure 2 — attribute garbage:* mis-tagged segment/defect-type attributes misroute cases; false green-lane admissions recreate R1/R2 violations under a veneer of automation.
- *Failure 3 — gaming/drift:* pressure to hit lane SLAs pushes borderline cases into the green lane.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from manual eligibility review to **fraud/risk review** (2 specialists, Monday/campaign peaks), since the amber lane funnels all high-value cases through it; secondarily to the pre-dispatch gate.

**Redesign B (evidence-first + verify-before-ship)**
- *Failure 1 — abandonment substitution:* the portal improves cycle time for adopters while raising abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers (a stated risk); net customer outcome worsens for a vulnerable cohort even as averages improve.
- *Failure 2 — checklist quality:* a wrong consolidated checklist replaces several small evidence loops with one big one; the 34h evidencemanual edge persists.
- *Failure 3 — gate theater or gate queue:* if the gate's catch rate is near zero it is pure cost; if inspector capacity is not protected after repair campaigns (when QC slack disappears), the 6–18h gate becomes a queue.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from the evidence ping-pong to the **QC/pre-dispatch gate**; also, cleaner evidence pushes more cases earlier into **fraud/risk review** (the evidencefraud edge already carries 220 cases).

**Redesign C (premium cell + delegation)**
- *Failure 1 — cell economics:* premium high-value is only ~14% of volume; the cell is either underutilized or, if staffed from the existing 7 reviewers, starves the standard pool into the >82% regime.
- *Failure 2 — inconsistent justice:* delegated decisions diverge across agents; reopened claims closed without manual review recreate R3 violations; visible premium/standard divergence creates fairness perception risk.
- *Failure 3 — idle reserved slots:* reserved fraud/risk slots sit idle while the standard queue waits, or the cell queues behind the same two specialists it was meant to bypass.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from the daily escalation board to **delegated approvers/case managers**, and the shared **fraud/risk specialists** remain the structural constraint unless scheduling is actually changed.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 — Instrumentation (weeks 1–4).** Before redesigning, close the observability gaps: log an explicit *Evidence completeness confirmed* event; capture complaint-after-close as a first-class event; tag cases with segment, defect type, and rule version; resolve why ~27% of traces lack a terminal event. Build a per-segment baseline dashboard: median/p90 cycle time, complaint/reopen rate by path, R1–R3 violation counts, fitting-trace share, abandonment, reviewer utilization, and **cost per closed claim including rework** (not per-activity cost).

**Pilot 1 (weeks 3–8) — Pre-dispatch gate on high-value replacements only**, staffed by QC slack, with a holdout region/SKU. Chosen first because it is reversible, requires no system build, loads no constrained resource, and attacks the costliest tail (EUR 410 errors; 96h complaint edge).
- *Metrics:* dispatchcomplaint rate and tail; wrong-part catches; added elapsed hours; premium credit exposure; QC utilization.
- *Go:* complaint tail down clearly (on the order of 30% relative) with median added delay  ~12h and no credit uptick. *Stop/redesign:* gate p90 exceeds ~18h (it has become a queue) or catch rate is near zero (theater).

**Pilot 2 (weeks 6–14) — Segment-aware auto-screen rules** (two-week deploy) with a 10–20% holdout, gated on a capacity buffer (cross-trained QC overflow or one additional reviewer) so utilization stays  ~80–85%.
- *Metrics:* manual inflow (+15–35% expected); utilization vs. the 82% cliff; misroute rate; share of >EUR 400 claims reaching fast approval without review; fitting-trace share; accessory cycle time unchanged.
- *Go:* R1-relevant fast approvals essentially eliminated, utilization under the cliff, accessory speed intact. *Stop:* manual-queue p90 roughly doubles or misclassification is material. Pilot 1's catch data calibrates these rules — the reason for this ordering.

**Pilot 3 (weeks 10–18) — Evidence portal + consolidated request** for the unclear-defect cohort, with an assisted-channel control arm.
- *Metrics:* repeat-request rate (baseline 26%); evidence-stage abandonment (baseline 320); evidence-cycle edge times (baseline 34h mean); abandonment within the low-digital-literacy subgroup specifically.
- *Go:* repeat requests down  ~25% with overall abandonment within +2–3pp. *Otherwise:* default to the assisted channel. This pilot structurally offsets the manual-review inflow created by Pilot 2 — deliberately sequenced after rules are tuned but before full rollout.

**Pilot 4 (weeks 16–26) — Premium cell + delegated escalation**, one region, time-boxed, since it presumes stable lanes and carries the highest org-change risk.
- *Metrics:* premium p90 cycle time and credits; escalation-board volume; R3 conformance under delegation; standard-queue externalities; cell utilization.

**Sequencing logic.** Each pilot de-risks the next: the gate generates wrong-part data to calibrate the rules; the portal offsets the manual inflow the rules create; the cell is built last, on stabilized lanes. Reversibility and constraint-load decrease in that order; organizational risk increases, so it comes last.

**Learning whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream.** (a) Evaluate cohorts end-to-end — 30-day reopen, complaint-after-close, abandonment, service credits, cost per closed claim — never the treated edge alone; a faster evidence step that raises fraud-review inflow or abandonment is relocation, not improvement. (b) Keep holdouts throughout and compare treatment vs. holdout, not before vs. after, to control for campaign-driven mix shifts. (c) Watch adjacent edges explicitly: fraud-review inflow after Pilot 2, QC queue after Pilot 1, standard-pool utilization after Pilot 4. (d) Use conformance fitness/fitting-trace share as a guardrail so that "faster" never again means "skipped the mandated work." (e) Report everything segment-stratified, because the central lesson of the current log is that averages across accessories and high-value devices conceal opposite truths.
