## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let \(H(e)\) be the normalized human actor for event \(e\). It is unknown where a service account cannot be reliably attributed.

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

For each case \(c\):

\[
H(\text{Draft\_Change\_Request}, c) \cap H(\text{Approve\_Change}, c) = \varnothing
\]

A violation occurs when the same normalized human drafts and approves the same case. Different literal user IDs do not satisfy RR1 when they are aliases for the same human.

If one event is attributable only to an unresolved service account, the result is **unresolved**, not automatically conformant.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

For each case \(c\):

\[
H(\text{Implement\_Change}, c) \cap H(\text{Verify\_Implementation}, c) = \varnothing
\]

Again, the comparison is between humans, not account names. A service-account verification is not proof that the verifier differs from the implementer unless reliable session evidence identifies the actual person.

### RR3 — HIGH-risk approval authority

For a HIGH-risk `Approve_Change` at time \(t\), the actor must be either:

1. a standing QM at \(t\); or
2. a delegate of a QM under a delegation that is valid at \(t\).

A delegation is valid only if all conditions hold:

\[
\text{ValidDelegation} =
\text{WithinInterval}
\land \text{ScopeMatch}
\land \text{PermittedActivity}
\land \text{PrincipalAbsenceSatisfied}
\land \text{NoProhibitedSubdelegation}
\land \text{ReliableHumanAttribution}
\]

The evaluation is event-time specific. A later role-master status must not be projected backward.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution and retrospective QM review

For a HIGH-risk emergency change:

1. `Approve_Change` must precede `Implement_Change`.
2. Approval must be by an IC, a valid IC delegate, a QM, or a valid QM delegate, depending on the emergency control design.
3. The emergency flag and safety rationale must be credible; `emergency=true` alone is necessary but not sufficient.
4. A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days.

For this review, I use the following auditable counting convention:

- The implementation date is day zero.
- Count the first two site business dates strictly after that date.
- A business day is Monday through Friday, excluding site holidays.
- The review deadline is 23:59:59 UTC on the second such business day.

Both emergency implementations occurred on Monday 2026-08-11:

- First business day: Tuesday 2026-08-12
- Second business day: Wednesday 2026-08-13
- Deadline: Wednesday 2026-08-13 23:59:59 UTC

Therefore:

- CHG-505 review on Thursday 2026-08-14 is late.
- CHG-510 review on Tuesday 2026-08-12 is timely, but timeliness does not cure an authority defect.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

A service account has no human role and cannot independently satisfy an authorization or SoD requirement.

- Strong attribution normally requires individual MFA/token evidence, session binding, or equivalent evidence contemporaneous with the action.
- A workstation location, room badge entry, or device assignment alone is weaker evidence. It can support an investigation but does not reliably establish who executed the transaction.
- Known aliases are normalized before SoD tests.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

The following are risk indicators, not proof of collusion:

- repeated Clara–Ben high-risk production pairings;
- reciprocal approval patterns;
- shared workstations;
- office-origin activity during sick leave;
- concentrated handovers in social-network analysis.

They support targeted review, not a conclusion about intent or misconduct.

### Principal action while on leave

A principal QM acting while on leave remains a QM unless a policy expressly suspends that authority. Leave is evidence that the principal may be formally absent for delegation purposes, but it does not automatically invalidate the principal’s direct action.

For CHG-504, Asha’s annual leave does not remove her standing QM authority. Her VPN session and statement support direct action, while the inability to exclude credential sharing leaves an identity-quality caveat.

A single urgent VPN session also does not automatically terminate D-QM-01. However, the organization should define whether “absent” means scheduled absence, unavailable to act, physically absent, or unavailable through the QMS.

### Later database entry and earlier delegation

A database entry created after an action cannot, by itself, prove that a delegation existed earlier.

For D-QM-01:

- The QMS database record created on 2026-08-12 cannot alone establish authority for CHG-501 on 2026-08-11.
- The signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09 can support earlier validity if its signature, integrity, version, and chain of custody are verified.
- Therefore, the PDF is the relevant contemporaneous evidence; the later migration record is corroborative but not retroactive proof.

def delegation_result(event, delegation):
    reasons = []
    unknown = False

    if not (delegation["start"] <= event[TS] <= delegation["end"]):
        reasons.append("outside-effective-interval")

    if event[ACT] not in delegation["allowed_activities"]:
        reasons.append("activity-not-delegated")

    if event["scope"] != delegation["scope"]:
        reasons.append("scope-mismatch")

    if pd.notna(delegation["incident"]):
        incident = case_incident.get(event[CASE])
        if incident is None:
            reasons.append("incident-link-not-established")
            unknown = True
        elif incident != delegation["incident"]:
            reasons.append("incident-mismatch")

    if delegation["requires_principal_absence"]:
        absence = principal_absence_status(delegation["principal"], event[TS])
        if absence != "ESTABLISHED_ANNUAL_LEAVE":
            reasons.append("principal-absence-not-established")
            unknown = True

    if not delegation["chain_allowed"]:
        reasons.append("prohibited-subdelegation")

    if not reasons:
        return "VALID"

    if unknown:
        return "NOT_ESTABLISHED: " + "; ".join(reasons)

    return "INVALID: " + "; ".join(reasons)

## 6. Control Improvements

### Delegation creation and effective-time recording

1. Require delegations to be created in the controlled QMS before their effective start, not entered retrospectively during migration.
2. Store both:
   - business-effective interval; and
   - system-recorded timestamp, immutable version, approval signature, and revocation status.
3. Require delegation objects to specify:
   - delegated person;
   - principal;
   - role;
   - allowed activity types;
   - risk level;
   - scope taxonomy;
   - incident identifier where applicable;
   - subdelegation rule;
   - absence condition;
   - approval workflow route.
4. Reject delegations with ambiguous scope phrases such as “QM approval” where retrospective review, release, and emergency review may be different controlled acts.
5. Configure the system to block prohibited subdelegation instead of relying on email interpretation.

### Principal availability and absence controls

1. Define “absent” precisely: scheduled leave, unavailable for QMS action, off-call, physically absent, or otherwise formally unavailable.
2. Record availability and on-call status in an auditable system linked to the delegation record.
3. Require a principal-availability attestation or automated on-call lookup when a conditional delegation is used.
4. If a principal acts while on leave, require an urgent-override reason and preserve identity evidence, rather than treating the event as inherently invalid.

### Alias and identity management

1. Maintain a controlled identity-resolution table linking aliases to a stable human identifier.
2. Perform SoD checks using immutable person IDs rather than mutable usernames.
3. Synchronize HR, IAM, QMS, badge, and MFA identity data through governed interfaces.
4. Flag same-person alias combinations automatically before approval submission.
5. Review stale or duplicate accounts periodically and disable inactive aliases.

### Individual authentication and service-account controls

1. Eliminate shared service-account use for approval, verification, and other attributable GMP/GxP actions.
2. Where technical service identities are unavoidable, require:
   - named-person SSO;
   - individual MFA/FIDO;
   - transaction-level identity binding;
   - session ID preservation;
   - immutable audit trail;
   - reason-for-use field.
3. Disable approval or verification submission when the action cannot be bound to an individual human.
4. Require workstation re-authentication after inactivity, shift handover, terminal unlock, and role-sensitive actions.
5. Preserve endpoint, application, VPN, badge, and MFA logs long enough to support investigations.

### Emergency controls and retrospective review

1. Bind emergency changes to a unique incident identifier such as CI-44.
2. Require a structured safety rationale, incident severity, risk acceptance, and approving authority before implementation where feasible.
3. Automatically calculate the two-business-day retrospective-review deadline using the site calendar.
4. Escalate overdue QM reviews to the QM, Site Director, and compliance function.
5. Prevent retrospective review submission by a user whose event-time authority does not match scope and action type.
6. Build a dedicated emergency-QM-review delegation type rather than assuming ordinary approval delegation covers it.

### Pair rotation and network monitoring

1. Define a documented pair-rotation or independent-review policy for repeated high-risk production changes.
2. Use workload balancing to reduce sustained drafter–approver concentration where practical.
3. Monitor:
   - repeated drafter/approver pairs;
   - unusually fast approvals;
   - self-approval attempts;
   - service-account use;
   - repeated emergency use;
   - approvals during leave or sick leave;
   - delegation use outside expected scope.
4. Treat network anomalies as triage indicators, not automated misconduct findings.
5. Combine network signals with delegation validity, audit-trail evidence, decision-quality sampling, and staffing context.

### Evidential retention

1. Retain signed delegation source documents, cryptographic validation data, and revision history.
2. Retain QMS event audit trails with raw resource, canonical human identity, session ID, IP/device identifier, and MFA event correlation.
3. Retain badge, workstation, VPN, and FIDO logs under a synchronized UTC time standard.
4. Maintain an investigation package template that preserves both inculpatory and exculpatory evidence.
5. Record uncertainty explicitly rather than overwriting raw service-account evidence with an inferred human identity.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is mixed rather than uniform.

- There is **clear genuine authority or SoD failure** in CHG-502, CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-508, and CHG-510. The most serious issues are the direct self-approvals, invalid emergency subdelegation, out-of-scope/late retrospective QM review, and an unqualified HIGH-risk approval.
- There is **material identity and audit-trail weakness** in CHG-503 and CHG-506. These cases cannot reliably establish who performed the controlling action. They should not be treated as proof against Diego or Ben without additional forensic evidence.
- There is **valid or plausibly valid substitution** in CHG-501 and direct QM authority in CHG-504, subject to evidential caveats.
- CHG-509 remains **indeterminate** because D-QM-02 matches time and scope but its principal-absence condition is not evidenced.
- The repeated Clara–Ben pattern is a meaningful organizational-pattern risk, especially because the reported 60-day pattern extends beyond the supplied D-QM-01 interval. It is not proof of collusion.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **Emergency authority and review failure:** CHG-505 and CHG-510, including CI-44 linkage, emergency rationale, D-IC-04 use, and the lack of valid QM retrospective review.
2. **Identity compromise/shared-account forensics:** CHG-503 and CHG-506, using QMS, endpoint, badge, VPN, and MFA evidence before attributing actions to individuals.
3. **Historical HIGH-risk approval authority audit:** the full 60-day Clara–Ben production-change population, including all contemporaneous delegations, Asha availability, routing rules, and approval-quality evidence.
