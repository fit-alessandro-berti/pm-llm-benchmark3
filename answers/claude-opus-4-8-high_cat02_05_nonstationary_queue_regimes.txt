# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Estimands and units.** I treat one queue observed daily over 07:00–19:00 (a 12-hour, 720-minute opening window; 720 min is the natural "day length" for any rate that is anchored to opening hours).

- `arrivals` (A): count of cases that entered during opening hours [cases/day]. Arrival intensity λ = A / 720 [cases/min] or A / 12 [cases/hour].
- `completed` (C): count of cases that finished during opening hours [cases/day]. Completion/throughput intensity μ_obs = C / 720 [cases/min]. **This is realized throughput, not service-rate capacity.**
- `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`: order statistics of **active handling time** per case, excluding waiting [min]. These describe the service-time *distribution shape*, not flow time and not the mean.
- `mean_wip` (L): mean number of cases in system from 15-min snapshots [cases]. This is a time-average WIP over opening hours (48 snapshots/day if strictly 07:00–18:45).
- `staff_fte`: mean concurrently available FTE capacity [servers]. Nominal service capacity ≈ staff_fte × (60 / mean_service_min) cases/hour, but only *mean* service, which we do not have, would make that exact.
- `complex_pct`: share of arrivals on a multi-step complex variant [%].
- `outage_min`: minutes of outage [min].
- `peak_08_10_pct`: share of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59 [%] — an intraday concentration/burstiness proxy.

**Opening-hours convention.** All intensities are per-open-minute or per-open-hour. Anything divided by 1440 (calendar day) would be wrong here because no service occurs while closed, yet backlog persists across the closed gap.

**Limitations of the aggregates.**
- Daily counts collapse strong intraday structure (the hourly vectors show arrivals are far from uniform).
- Medians/p90 hide the mean and the tail beyond p90; a right-skewed service law can have a mean well above the median.
- `mean_wip` mixes queueing delay and active service and cannot be decomposed from these fields alone.
- `staff_fte` is a mean; concurrency during peaks may differ sharply from the daily mean.

**At least five threats to naive inference.**
1. **Flow conservation is violated day-to-day.** C ≠ A on most days (e.g., 2026-04-15 A=105/C=58; 2026-04-16 A=112/C=129). Backlog carries over and "completed need not have arrived today," so daily λ vs μ comparisons are not a closed system.
2. **Unknown opening backlog (B₀).** Little's Law and any inventory balance need B₀; it is "not supplied for every day," so absolute WIP interpretation is bounded, not point-identified.
3. **Non-stationarity across the four weeks.** Service medians drift 24→36 min, p90 44→88, complex_pct 18→50, staff_fte 6→7 with an intervention: pooling assumes a single regime that does not exist.
4. **Simpson's-paradox risk from weekday/weekend pooling.** Weekends have low volume, low staff, but *higher* median service; pooling can reverse apparent staff–throughput relationships.
5. **Outage contamination.** 2026-04-15 has 180 outage min; its low completion and inflated WIP are an artifact, not a "slow-service regime," and the recovery days (04-16, 04-17) show C>A catch-up that must not be read as extra capacity.
6. **Median-for-mean substitution.** Using median_service as mean flow time understates delay and ignores waiting entirely.
7. **Serial dependence.** "Daily points are not independent"; backlog links consecutive days, so variance-based CIs assuming i.i.d. days are optimistic.

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

**Primary axis: a change-point/phase segmentation crossed with a weekday/weekend split, with outage and its recovery carved out explicitly.** The daily series shows three demand/mix phases plus a shock:

- **Phase A (baseline):** 04-06 → 04-14. Median ~24–26, p90 44–50, complex ~18–24%, staff 6.0 wk, throughput ≈ arrivals.
- **Shock (outage):** 04-15 only. 180 outage min, C collapses, WIP spikes to 47.
- **Recovery:** 04-16 → 04-17 (arguably to 04-19). Elevated staff (7.0/6.5), C>A catch-up, WIP relaxing.
- **Phase B (post-mix-change / high-complex):** 04-20 → 04-28. Median 34–36, p90 78–88, complex 40–50%, `peak_08_10_pct` 40–50, staff still ~6.0 wk but WIP 37–50 and C<A (persistent backlog build).
- **Intervention recovery:** 04-29 → 05-01 (intervention started 04-29, exact time unlogged), staff 7.0/6.5, C>A, WIP falling 43→32; **treat 04-29 as partially attributed (time-unknown), i.e., a blended day.**
- Weekends (Sat/Sun) sit in a separate low-volume, low-staff sub-regime throughout, and inherit the current phase's service level.

| Boundary (dates) | Sub-window | Regime label | Key evidence | Arrival est. (λ) | Completion est. (μ_obs) | Service behavior | WIP / capacity | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04-06→04-14 | Weekdays | **A: Stable baseline** | med 24–26, p90 44–50, complex 18–24, C≈A | ~100/day → 8.3/h → 0.139/min | ~100/day, C≈A | Low, stable | L≈18–23, staff 6.0 adequate | High |
| 04-11,12 (+04-18,19) | Weekend | **A-wk: Low-volume weekend** | A 36–48, staff 2.5 | ~40/day → 3.3/h | ~40/day | Median slightly higher (28–30) | L 12–16, staff 2.5 | Med-High |
| 04-15 | Single day | **Outage shock** | outage 180, C=58≪A=105, WIP=47 | 105/day (arrivals ~normal) | **57 unmet within day** (C=58) | med 31/p90 68 inflated by congestion | Backlog surge; capacity nullified 11–14 | High (as anomaly) |
| 04-16→04-17 | Weekdays | **Outage recovery** | C>A (129>112, 112>99), staff 7.0/6.5 | ~99–112/day | Elevated (129,112) = catch-up | med relaxing 33→29 | WIP draining 52→35 | High |
| 04-20→04-28 | Weekdays | **B: High-complex / bursty** | complex 40–50, med 34–36, p90 78–88, peak 40–50, C<A | ~110–121/day → 9.6/h | ~94–106/day (**deficit**) | High & heavy-tailed | L 37–50, staff 6.0 **overloaded** | High |
| 04-25,26 (+05-02,03) | Weekend | **B-wk: High-complex weekend** | A 43–53, staff 2.5–3.0, med 34–36 | ~45/day | ~40–47/day | Elevated service | L 19–23 | Med |
| 04-29→05-01 | Weekdays | **Intervention recovery** | staff 7.0/6.5, C>A (121>116…), WIP↓43→32 | ~109–120/day | Elevated (catch-up) | med easing 33→32 | Draining backlog | Med (04-29 blended) |

**Outage/recovery treatment (explicit):** the outage day is excluded from any steady-state fit; its "missing" ~47 cases become backlog. Recovery days are *not* a high-throughput regime — their C>A is backlog drainage, so their μ_obs overstates sustainable capacity. The same logic applies to the intervention-recovery window.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative: staffing/backlog-state segmentation (capacity-relative), ignoring calendar phase labels.** Define ρ-like pressure = arrivals × mean-ish service demand ÷ (staff_fte × 720). Segment by **(i) utilization band** and **(ii) backlog trajectory sign** (ΔWIP or C−A):

- **Underloaded / draining:** days with C≥A and falling WIP (04-16,17, 04-29,30, 05-01, most weekends).
- **Balanced:** C≈A, stable WIP (Phase A weekdays).
- **Overloaded / building:** C<A and rising/high WIP (04-20→04-28, and 04-15 outage as extreme).

**Operational story:** this frames the system as a **single server pool driven across a stability boundary** — the story is "we crossed ρ→1 when service times lengthened without adding staff, then interventions pulled ρ back." It cleanly separates *capacity-pressure* regimes regardless of whether the driver is demand, mix, or an outage.

**Strengths vs. primary.** It is directly decision-relevant (staffing), needs no assumption about *why* service slowed, and naturally flags the outage and both recoveries as the same phenomenon (backlog dynamics). It also mitigates Simpson's paradox by conditioning on utilization rather than calendar.

**Weaknesses.** It **confounds distinct causes** — a mix shift (complex_pct) and a one-off outage land in the same "overloaded" bucket though they demand different remedies (hire/route vs. resilience/DR). It also depends on an assumed mean service time we do not have, so the ρ estimate is soft. The primary segmentation is better for *diagnosis and attribution*; the alternative is better for *reactive staffing*.

---

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Using robust summaries (ranges/medians of daily values), pooling misleads in at least three ways.

**(a) Weekday/weekend composition biases every "daily average."** Pooled arrivals median across all 27 days sits far below weekday level because weekends (A≈36–53) drag it down. Weekday arrivals cluster ~96–121; weekend ~36–53. A pooled "mean arrivals" (~90/day) describes *no actual operating day*. Likewise pooled median service (~32) blends a 24-min baseline with a 36-min high-complex phase.

**(b) Complex-case mix creates a Simpson-style reversal.** Pooled, higher staff_fte appears associated with *higher* service times and *lower* completion-to-arrival ratios — the naive read "more staff → slower/worse." Segmented, within each phase more staff improves drain (recovery days at 7.0 FTE clear backlog). The confounder is complex_pct/backlog, which rose alongside staff. Pooling inverts the sign of the staffing effect.

**(c) The outage/intervention distort throughput and service tails.** Pooled p90 (~44 to 88 range) averaged to ~70 misrepresents both the calm baseline (p90≈45) and the stressed phase (p90≈85). Pooled completion rate looks healthy because recovery-day C>A (129, 126, 121) offsets outage/overload deficits — masking a real, sustained backlog build in 04-20→04-28.

Illustrative segmented ranges (daily values):

| Metric | Baseline wk (04-06→14) | High-complex wk (04-20→28) | Recovery wk | Weekend (all) | Pooled (all days) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrivals/day | 96–107 | 106–121 | 109–120 | 36–53 | ~36–121 |
| median_service | 24–26 | 34–36 | 32–33 | 28–36 | 24–36 |
| p90_service | 44–50 | 78–88 | 72–76 | 53–85 | 44–88 |
| mean_wip | 18–23 | 37–50 | 32–43 | 12–23 | 12–52 |
| C−A sign | ≈0 | **negative** | **positive** | ≈0/± | ≈0 (cancels) |

Because C−A cancels across phases, a pooled "system is balanced" conclusion is exactly the artifact the scenario warns about.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Intraday burstiness (hourly vectors).** For a Poisson process the index of dispersion (Fano factor = variance/mean of hourly counts) ≈ 1.

- 2026-04-07 (ordinary): arrivals [3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4], mean≈8.4, var≈24 → **Fano ≈ 2.9**; CV≈0.58. Moderately over-dispersed, single morning peak (08–10 concentration matches peak_08_10_pct≈31).
- 2026-04-21 (bursty): [2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4], mean≈9.6, var≈~90 → **Fano ≈ 9–10**; CV≈1.0. Strongly over-dispersed with multiple spikes (10:00 and 13:00 secondary bursts) — clearly non-Poisson; batch/scheduled arrivals likely.
- 2026-04-15 (outage): completions [5,7,10,8,0,0,0,4,6,7,6,5] show three consecutive **zero-completion hours** (~11:00–14:00) then a rebound — a structural (deterministic) hole, not stochastic under-dispersion; do not compute a service rate here.
- 2026-04-25 (weekend): smooth, low counts, Fano ≈ 1–1.5 — closest to Poisson-like.

So burstiness is itself regime-dependent: baseline mild over-dispersion, high-complex phase severe over-dispersion.

**Service-time spread.** p90/median rises from ~1.85 (44/24) in baseline to ~2.4 (86/36) in the high-complex phase — heavier right tail, consistent with the complex-variant mix. This is evidence the *service distribution*, not just its location, shifts.

**Daily-sequence serial dependence.** WIP and service medians move in runs (persistent build 04-20→04-28, persistent drain 04-16,17 and 04-29→05-01). A lag-1 autocorrelation of daily WIP (weekdays) is clearly positive — backlog carry-over induces memory. Therefore daily rows are **not iid**; naive CIs and χ² independence tests are invalid.

**Change-point evidence.** Two plausible change points: (1) around 04-15–04-16 (outage shock), and (2) around 04-19→04-20 (step in median_service 26→34, p90 50→78, complex ~24→41, peak ~30→42). The 04-20 shift is the substantive mix/regime change; the 04-15 shift is a transient shock. The intervention (04-29) is a third, capacity-side change point.

**Why four representative days cannot fix a stochastic model.** Four single-day realizations give point estimates of hourly shape but no sampling distribution: we cannot estimate day-to-day variance of the burst pattern, cannot separate arrival-process over-dispersion from measurement binning, and cannot validate a renewal/Poisson assumption. They are illustrative, not inferential.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law L = λ·W holds **in the long run for a stationary system with matched boundaries**. Here, per-window: use λ = arrivals/720 or throughput C/720; W is *implied* flow time = L/λ [min], valid only if boundaries are stable, WIP is comparable, carry-over is negligible, and the window is ~stationary. Throughput proxy: use **completions** for W in an overloaded window (arrivals overstate what the system actually flows), **arrivals** in a balanced window.

| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity | Implied W = L/rate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline weekdays 04-06→14 | Stable (C≈A) | arrivals ≈ completions (~100/day = 0.139/min) | L≈20 comparable | Low | Approx. stationary | L/λ ≈ 20/0.139 ≈ **144 min** | **applicable (approx.)** |
| Weekend 04-11,12 | Stable but different capacity | arrivals ≈ compl (~40/day = 0.056/min) | L≈13 | Low | Stationary within weekends | 13/0.056 ≈ **232 min** (few points) | **approximate only** |
| Outage day 04-15 | **Broken** (3 zero-service hrs) | neither valid | L=47 congestion spike | **High** (unmet ~47) | Non-stationary shock | — | **not defensible** |
| Recovery 04-16→17 | Draining, C>A | completions (catch-up) not sustainable rate | L falling, not steady | **High** | Non-stationary (transient) | — (transient) | **not defensible** |
| High-complex 04-20→28 | Overloaded, C<A, WIP building | **completions** (~100/day=0.139/min) as realized throughput | L≈43 high | **High** (backlog grows) | Weakly non-stationary (trend) | If forced: 43/0.139 ≈ **310 min**, label lower bound only | **approximate only (bound)** |
| Intervention recovery 04-29→05-01 | Draining | completions = catch-up | L falling | High | Non-stationary | — | **not defensible** |

Key caution: for the high-complex window, WIP is *rising*, so L/λ is a snapshot ratio, not a stationary W; report it as an **order-of-magnitude lower bound on delay** and never with the median service time (144–310 min implied W ≫ 34 min median service confirms most time is waiting).

# 7. Operational implications

- **Staff to the regime, not the pooled average.** The pooled mean (~90 arrivals, ~32 median service, 6 FTE) hides that the high-complex phase needs materially more effective capacity: complex_pct doubled and p90 nearly doubled while weekday FTE stayed at 6.0, so C<A for nine straight weekdays. Recovery only appeared when FTE went to 7.0/6.5. **Recommendation:** trigger surge staffing on a *leading* indicator (rising complex_pct and rising WIP), not on realized backlog.
- **Backlog recovery is a distinct mode.** Recovery days can clear ~10–20% more than arrivals; plan explicit catch-up capacity after any outage/overload and do **not** book that catch-up rate as steady-state capacity in planning models.
- **Peak smoothing.** peak_08_10 climbed to ~50% and Fano hit ~10 (04-21). Batch/appointment leveling or a morning fast-lane would cut the 08–10 congestion that drives WIP spikes; the bursty secondary spikes (10:00, 13:00) suggest scheduled/batched inflows worth rescheduling.
- **Data collection.** Log the intervention *timestamp*, record 07:00 opening backlog daily, and capture **mean** (not just median/p90) service and per-case flow time so Little's Law and utilization can be computed properly. Log outage start/stop to isolate shocks.
- **Policy that looks good pooled but is risky segmented:** "Since pooled completions ≈ arrivals, hold staffing flat at 6.0 FTE and even trim weekend FTE." Pooled C−A≈0 (recovery offsets deficits) makes the system look balanced, but segmented it hides a sustained weekday backlog build (04-20→28) — flat staffing would let WIP and flow time grow without bound in the high-complex regime. Similarly, "reduce staff on high-service-time days" (they look inefficient pooled) is exactly backwards: those are the overloaded days needing *more* capacity.
