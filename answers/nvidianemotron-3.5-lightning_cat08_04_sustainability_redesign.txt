- **Mechanism:** Introduce a shared emergency courier lane (e.g., scheduled motorbike or microvan pool) for a proportion (e.g., 30%) of Critical orders when explicit `Criticaldowntime` justification is present (policy SP2). The lane operates on a standby basis, reducing emptykilometer runs.  
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Avg `transport_kgco2e` per Critical order reduces from 38.0 toward ~25–28 kgCO2e; service level protected because dedicated van remains available for the most timecritical cases; resilience improved by adding an alternative emergency lane.  
- **Required processmining evidence to monitor:** Share of Critical orders using `dedicated_van` vs shared lane; presence of explicit justification events (SP2 conformance); ontime delivery for Critical segment; `kgCO2e` per order.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks (at least six)

1. **Batchingobsolescence rebound:** Capping batch wait times (S2) may force more frequent smaller batches, increasing changeover energy and transport fragmentation; if forecasts don’t keep pace, items may still become obsolete, offsetting energy gains.
2. **Transportshift modecompetition:** Replacing air with ground for Standardstock Servicecontract (S1, S4) could prompt some customers to expedite via external couriers or competitors, potentially increasing total supplychain emissions beyond the distributor’s boundary.
3. **Packaging material increase:** Reinforced packaging (baseline for Q1) doubles `package_material_kg`; if sustainability metrics focus only on transport emissions, the material footprint may worsen, creating a tradeoff that requires recycling or lightweight alternatives.
4. **Reworkimprovement cycletime displacement:** Adding inspection gates (S7) or quality checks may lengthen median cycle time; if promised lead times are not adjusted, latedelivery risk could rise, triggering customerside expediting and higher overall emissions.
5. **Obsolescence from dynamic batching:** Smaller, more frequent batches (S2) reduce waiting but may increase the number of partiallycompleted batches; if demand forecasts are inaccurate, the 6% obsolescence rate for batchproduced items could shift across segments.
6. **Customerbehavioral rebound:** Lengthening effective lead times through consolidation waits (S5) or groundonly defaults (S4) may cause Regular customers to reduce order frequency, split orders across suppliers, or stockpile critical parts, ultimately increasing total supplychain energy and emissions.
7. **Dedicatedvan replacement gap:** Shared emergency courier lanes (S8) may have longer standby or response times; if criticaldowntime incidents exceed the lane’s capacity, the system may fall back to higheremission alternatives or fail to meet service levels, negating emissions benefits.
8. **Consolidationinduced air spikes:** Limiting individual ground shipments (S5) could cause a subset of genuinely urgent stock orders to default to `parcel_air`, creating a rebound where the consolidated lane’s emissions savings are offset by a spike in air shipments for the remaining urgent fraction.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

A coherent redesign package should bundle the eight interventions above, each gated by segmentspecific conditions and processmining thresholds, rather than applying isolated green levers. The core logic is:

- **Groundfirst shipping (S1 + S4)** for all Standardstock Servicecontract and Regular orders where `promised_lead_time_hours` permits, eliminating the 22% SP1 violation and the 5% aironly shipments that emit 46× more kgCO2e. Service is preserved because the majority of these orders already have sufficient slack; conformance monitoring ensures the 85% probability rule is met.

- **Dynamic batching with caps (S2)** for Madetoorder, exploiting the CNC_A energyperitem curve (1.3 kWh at batch 1220) while preventing the median Batch wait from ballooning to 38h, which correlates with 1.7× higher latedelivery risk for heattreat queues. Caps are set at 24h (Regular) and 12h (Servicecontract), with automatic release to CNC if the cap is hit.

- **CNC_A preference shift (S3)** for MTO orders when queue length + duedate slack permits, directly cutting the 18% rework rate on CNC_B and the associated hidden material/energy waste. This is conditional: if CNC_A utilization > 85% or promised lead time is at risk, the order stays on CNC_B.

- **Packagingroute compatibility (S6)** and **inspection quality gate (S7)** work together to suppress rework loops: S6 prevents incompatibilitydriven `rework_packaging` events; S7 adds a quality gate after inspection for highrisk machine/part combos (CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell). Together they reduce `material_scrap_kg` and return risk without elongating cycle times excessively.

- **Consolidated truck departures twice daily (S5)** for nonurgent stock, providing a reliable 3555% transport emissions reduction while capping consolidation wait at 12h—absorbed by the typical 31h (Regular) and 18h (Servicecontract) median lead times. Urgent stock orders are exempt and default to individual ground or air as per S1/S4 rules.

- **Shared emergency courier lanes (S8)** for a portion (30%) of Criticaldowntime orders, replacing some dedicatedvan trips (current 38.0 kgCO2e) with a shared lane that operates on a standby basis. The lane is only engaged when explicit `Criticaldowntime` justification is logged (addressing the 31% missingjustification finding for SP2), and a dedicated van remains available as fallback.

**Why this balances the four pillars:**
- **Environmental impact:** Transport emissions cut substantially (ground/consolidated shifts), production energy reduced via better batching and machine routing, material scrap lowered by rework elimination.
- **Service:** All interventions include segmentspecific slack checks (promised lead time, queue status) and fallback mechanisms; no intervention universally degrades cycle time or ontime delivery.
- **Quality:** Rework and return risks are actively reduced through batching caps, CNC_A preference, packaging compatibility, and inspection gates—directly targeting the hidden waste identified in the log.
- **Resilience:** Alternative routes (consolidated lanes, shared couriers, ground defaults) are built into each intervention; policies SP1/SP2/Q1 are enforced with conformance monitoring, ensuring that sustainability improvements do not erode the ability to recover from carrier disruptions.

The package is greater than the sum of its parts because each intervention’s guardrails (segment, slack, policy condition) prevent the rebound and displacement risks outlined in Section 5.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**1. Postimplementation eventlog conformance monitoring**  
- Deploy automated checks on the enriched event log to compute realtime conformance rates for the three core policies:
  - **SP1:** `% of Standardstock Servicecontract orders where ground/consolidated was used and promised lead time was met with 85% probability`. Target: 95% compliance (down from current 78%).
  - **SP2:** `% of dedicatedvan cases with an explicit `Criticaldowntime` justification event`. Target: 100% (from current 69%).
  - **Q1:** `% of Madetoorder orders >8kg using reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk`. Target: 90% (currently variable, driven by the quality gate).
- Use the DFG and pathshare metrics to verify that the intended routemode and machinegroup shifts (S1–S8) are occurring as designed.

**2. Segmentlevel service KPI dashboard**  
- Track the following KPIs monthly, segmented by the five customer/product groups:
  - Median cycle time, p90 cycle time.
  - % late deliveries (relative to promised lead time).
  - Average `energy_kwh` per order, `transport_kgco2e` per order, `material_scrap_kg` per order.
  - Rework rate, reworkloop frequency (Inspection  Reproduction  CNC).
  - Package material kg per order, reinforcedpackaging usage %.
- Set tolerance bands (e.g., ±5% of baseline) to flag when an intervention unintentionally degrades a metric.

**3. Exception governance process**  
- Create a formal “sustainability exception log” for any SP1/SP2/Q1 override. Each entry must capture:
  - Order ID, segment, reason for override (e.g., “urgent customer request”, “carrier disruption”, “forecast error”).
  - The alternative route/mode chosen.
  - Decision maker and timestamp.
  - Posttrip outcome (ontime delivery, damage report, rework needed).
- Review the exception log quarterly to identify patterns; if a particular reason accounts for >15% of overrides, reevaluate the associated intervention’s guardrails (e.g., tighten the slack threshold for S1 or increase the dedicatedvan allocation for S8).

**4. Beforeandafter impact assessment**  
- Extract the same indicator set (Section 2) from the log for a 3month preimplementation window and a 3month postimplementation window.
- Perform segmentwise differenceindifferences analysis to isolate the effect of the redesign from seasonal or demandvolume fluctuations.
- Specifically monitor the “rebound” indicators identified in Section 5 (e.g., airshipment share for urgent stock, obsolescence rate, exceptionlog frequency) to ensure they are not creeping up.

**5. Continuous improvement loop**  
- Every six months, reconvene the processmining and sustainability steering group to:
  - Update the batching energyperitem curve if machine utilization changes.
  - Refine the greenshipping recommender’s probability thresholds based on new routereliability data.
  - Adjust waittime caps (S2) if service-level KPIs drift.
  - Consider adding new interventions (e.g., renewableenergy kWh sourcing) once the core tradeoffs are stabilized.

This validation and governance framework ensures the redesign remains grounded in the eventlog evidence, respects the multicriteria balance, and provides a transparent mechanism for catching and correcting unwanted sideeffects.
