# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

**Notation and status.** Let $c$ index cases and $i\in\{A,B,C\}$ index branches. $a_c$ is `arrival`, $r_c$ is `fork_release`, $s_{c,i}$ and $e_{c,i}$ are branch start/end, $f^s_c$ and $f^e_c$ are final start/end. Quantities computed directly from timestamps are labelled **OBS**; interpretations requiring the stated fork-join rule are labelled **ASSUMP**.

For every case-branch:

- **OBS** Upstream elapsed: $U_c=r_c-a_c$.
- **ASSUMP** Branch queue waiting: $Q_{c,i}=s_{c,i}-r_c$. This requires that branch work is not permitted before $r_c$, so $s_{c,i}-r_c$ is waiting after permission, not work performed earlier.
- **OBS** Branch service (start-to-end elapsed): $S_{c,i}=e_{c,i}-s_{c,i}$. This is elapsed time between start and completion; it is pure active work only if there is no preemption or unlogged interruption.
- Branch elapsed after fork: $E_{c,i}=Q_{c,i}+S_{c,i}=e_{c,i}-r_c$.
- **ASSUMP** Join-ready time: $J_c=\max_i e_{c,i}=r_c+\max_i E_{c,i}$.
- Branch synchronization waiting: $W_{c,i}=J_c-e_{c,i}=\max_j E_{c,j}-E_{c,i}\ge 0$.
- Fork-join stage elapsed: $G_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i E_{c,i}$.
- Critical branch: $i_c^*=\arg\max_i E_{c,i}$, i.e. a branch attaining $G_c$. Ties are reported jointly in the code in fixed $A,B,C$ order; no observed case has a tied maximum.
- **ASSUMP** Downstream queue: $D_c=f^s_c-J_c$. Calling this “queueing” requires that $F$ is permitted and desired to start at $J_c$; otherwise it is merely downstream waiting.
- Final service: $F_c=f^e_c-f^s_c$.
- Total flow time: $T_c=f^e_c-a_c$.

The no-double-count identity is

$$T_c=U_c+G_c+D_c+F_c,$$

and, equivalently, for *any one* branch $i$,

$$T_c=U_c+E_{c,i}+W_{c,i}+D_c+F_c.$$

**Why summing synchronization waits double-counts.** Synchronization waits are overlapping wall-clock intervals $[e_{c,i},J_c]$, not sequential work. For every branch, $E_{c,i}+W_{c,i}=G_c$ by construction, so summing over all three branches gives $\sum_i(E_{c,i}+W_{c,i})=3G_c$, triple-counting the fork-join stage. Example F07: $W_A+W_B+W_C=25+0+52=77$ min, already exceeding the entire fork-join stage $G=75$ min. Only the maximum branch path traces elapsed time; the other branches’ $W$ values describe slack, not additional elapsed duration.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

Means are over 12 cases; all minutes.

| branch | service mean (min–max) | queue mean (min–max) | elapsed $Q+S$ mean (max) | critical freq. | mean sync = mean slack | influence reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A Lab Assay | 50.0 (50–50) | 0.0 (0–0) | 50.0 (50) | 2/12, 16.7% | 11.0 | Rank 2 on mean influence; constant floor |
| B Machine Stress | 37.5 (30–45) | 19.2 (5–30) | 56.7 (75) | 8/12, 66.7% | 4.3 | Rank 1 overall |
| C Documentation | 20.2 (18–30) | 10.4 (5–35) | 30.6 (57) | 2/12, 16.7% | 30.4 | Rank 3 on mean; tail risk |

Context means: upstream 19.6 (15 once, otherwise 20), fork-join stage 61.0 (50–75), downstream waiting 14.7 (8–20), final service 20.0 constant, total flow 115.3 (93–130).

**Reasoned ranking: B first, A second, C third for mean flow-time control — with a tail caveat.**

1. **B dominates by every elapsed-time criterion.** It is critical in 8 of 12 cases; its mean elapsed time (56.7) exceeds A’s constant 50 and C’s 30.6; its conditional elapsed time when critical averages 65; it produces all five cases with fork-join stage $\ge65$ and both 75-min tail cases (F07, F12). Its leave-one-out slack is small on average (mean sync 4.3), meaning little buffer before it determines the join.
2. **A is the largest-service branch but not the most influential.** Equating service with influence would wrongly prioritize A. A service is invariably 50 and A queue invariably 0, so A imposes a hard 50-min floor on $G$ but exceeds that floor never. A is critical only in F01 and F11 (mean winning margin 7.5 min, versus B’s 15.0 min when critical), and Section 4 shows a 20% A-service cut saves only 15 min in total across 12 cases. A is best understood as a *latent* bottleneck: once B is improved, A at 50 becomes the binding constraint in most cases.
3. **C is last on average but not ignorable in the tail.** A and C tie on critical frequency (2 each) by different mechanisms: A is a constant floor, whereas C is usually early (mean sync 30.4, the largest) and only becomes critical through queue spikes — 35 min in F04 and 25 min in F08, giving elapsed times 57 and 55. So ranking by mean criticality gives B $>$ A $>$ C, while ranking by variance/tail risk gives B $>$ C $>$ A, because A has zero variance. That distinction matters after B is fixed: C spikes would then dominate the remaining tail.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumptions (labelled).** Each counterfactual is a *direct* (single-case, join-ready) calculation: other branches’ queues/services, arrival order, downstream waiting (except CF3) and final service are held fixed, and final start is assumed to shift one-for-one with $J_c$. These are upper-bound-style direct effects; Section 4.4 discusses where final-resource coupling can mask them. Savings are reductions in total flow time under those assumptions.

| case | CF1: A service 20% | CF2: B queue 50% | CF3: downstream  0 (upper bound) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| F01 | 5 | 0 | 8 |
| F02 | 0 | 10 | 15 |
| F03 | 0 | 10 | 15 |
| F04 | 0 | 0 | 18 |
| F05 | 0 | 10 | 10 |

| F10 | 0 | 5 | 20 |
| F11 | 10 | 0 | 20 |
| F12 | 0 | 15 | 10 |
| **Total / mean** | **15 / 1.25** | **92.5 / 7.71** | **176 / 14.67** |
| Cases with benefit | 2/12 | 8/12 | 12/12 |
| Max saving | 10 | 15 | 20 |

**CF1 — Reduce A service by 20% (50  40).** Direct benefit only where A is critical and remains above the second branch after the cut: F01 saves 5 (not 10, because B at 45 becomes the new maximum) and F11 saves 10 (A and B tie at 40). The other 10 cases save exactly 0 because B or C remains critical. Secondary effects are adverse-looking but harmless to elapsed time: A’s synchronization wait grows by 10 min in non-benefiting cases (finished work waits longer), the fork-join tail is unchanged (still 75 via B), and any quality/regulatory risk from rushing a laboratory assay is incurred for almost no flow-time gain. CF1 is the clearest demonstration that the largest mean service time is not the lever.

**CF2 — Reduce B queue delay by 50%.** Benefit in exactly the 8 B-critical cases; zero in F01, F04, F08, F11 where B is not critical. Mean saving 7.71 min, maximum 15 min, and the fork-join tail falls from 75 to 60. Diminishing returns are visible: in F02, F05, F06 and F10 the new maximum is A’s 50-min floor (with ties in F02/F06), so further B-queue cuts would yield little until A or C is addressed; in F04/F08, C (57/55) becomes the tail after B improvement. Secondary risk: if B queue falls because B is prioritized over shared resources, A/C queues could rise — unidentifiable from these timestamps because branch resource IDs are absent. If B queue falls because extra B capacity is added, B utilization per unit capacity falls.

**CF3 — Add final-station capacity / reduce downstream waiting.** The table uses the optimistic upper bound “downstream waiting eliminated,” i.e. saving $=D_c$ in every case (mean 14.67). A 50% downstream reduction would give exactly half: total 88, mean 7.33, benefit in all 12 cases. Unlike branch counterfactuals, CF3 is additive with CF1/CF2 in the single-case arithmetic because it acts after the join. But its realistic size is the most uncertain: within the supplied cases, the assigned final resource is already free at $J_c$ in several cases, and final start equals the previous same-resource final end in only F05, F08, F09 and F11 (partially in F04/F10). The in-sample overlap of downstream waiting with the assigned resource being busy totals only 73 of 176 min, leaving 103 min occurring while that resource appears idle in the sample. Therefore adding a server cannot be assumed to remove the idle-period portion; that portion may be a release policy, an unlogged mandatory check, batching, or unlisted cases. Realized CF3 benefit is bounded between (approximately) 0 and $D_c$ per case until the cause is measured.

**Masking and interaction.** Direct branch savings assume final start moves with $J_c$. In F05, F08, F09 and F11, final start is pinned to the previous same-resource final end within the sample, so improving that case in isolation could be fully absorbed by final-resource waiting (realized saving 0) unless the preceding case also shifts or ordering/resource assignment changes. This potentially masks part of CF2 (F05, F09) and CF1 (F11). CF1+CF2 combined direct saving is 107.5 min in this sample because their benefiting case sets are disjoint, but max-based counterfactuals are not generally additive — when two improvements target the same critical path, the second is partially redundant. CF3 interacts positively in one sense: removing downstream congestion makes branch improvements more likely to pass through to final start.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Redesign 1 — B-first dispatch at fork release (attack B queue, protect the A floor).** Reserve/expedite the B machine slot at `fork_release` (target B queue $\le$10 min), without initially cutting A service. *Expected effect:* halving B queue gives direct mean saving 7.7 min and cuts the fork-join tail from 75 to 60; eliminating B queue entirely would give at most mean 10.0 min because A’s 50-min floor then binds. *Throughput:* improves only if B queue reflects real contention; if it reflects priority policy, this is re-sequencing, not capacity, and throughput may be unchanged while other queues shift. *Flow-time tail:* strong reduction in the B-driven tail; residual tail becomes C spikes (57/55). *Utilization:* unknown for B (no resource ID); monitor whether B resource idles awaiting dispatch (utilization gain) or a new B resource idles (utilization loss). *New bottleneck:* A at 50, then C variance. *Synchronization:* B’s sync wait rises in cases where A becomes critical — harmless to elapsed time but a signal that B investment has hit diminishing returns. *Pilot first:* log B queue reason/resource for a few weeks; trial expedited B starts on a subset of cases; measure $J_c$ and total flow (not B_end alone), plus displacement in A/C queues and stress-test quality. Stop/escalate to A only if B queue is already near zero and A is critical in most cases.

**Redesign 2 — Final release discipline before final capacity (audit, then add capacity only if busy-pinned).** Release each case to F1/F2 at $J_c$ in join-ready order with dynamic resource assignment; record, at $J_c$, whether both final resources are busy, idle, or in an unlogged mandatory step. Add a third final server *only* if busy-pinned waiting persists after release discipline. *Expected effect:* up to mean 14.7 min if downstream waiting is truly removable queue, but only about 6.1 min (73/12) corresponds to in-sample busy overlap, so the honest planning range is wide until measurement resolves it. *Throughput:* final service is unchanged at 20 min, so throughput gains come only from earlier resource release cascading to subsequent cases, not from faster final work. *Tail:* downstream max is 20 (F10, F11); even partial removal compresses the total-flow tail (max 130  115 under elimination). *Utilization:* F1 and F2 each serve 6 cases in the sample; dynamic assignment should balance back-to-back clusters, whereas a third server would cut per-server utilization and may sit idle if waiting is policy-driven. *New bottleneck:* branches (especially B, then A) become the dominant share of flow time. *Synchronization:* none upstream; earlier final completion may change which case occupies a final resource next, so pilot must measure neighbour-case effects. *Pilot first:* two to four weeks of busy/idle-at-join logging and reason codes; verify no unlogged mandatory release check is being mislabelled as queue; trial dynamic release before capital spend. Risk: skipping a mandatory check to “remove queue” would be a compliance failure, not a redesign.

# 6. Reproducible Python

The code below embeds the supplied table, computes every decomposition component with pandas (including max-based `join_ready`, synchronization waits and deterministic tie handling), runs the three counterfactuals, and builds a PM4Py interval-event log. Fork-join quantities (`fork_release`-based branch queues, `join_ready = max(branch ends)`, synchronization waiting, critical branch, and $final\_start-join\_ready$) are custom pandas calculations: standard directly-follows performance (e.g. `discover_performance_dfg`) aggregates waiting/service between *directly following* activities, but in a parallel fork-join the successor of a finished branch is not observed as an event, branch interleavings vary, and synchronization waiting is a max-based latent quantity rather than a logged activity transition — so PM4Py alone does not recover it. PM4Py is used where its semantics fit: interval-event formatting, conversion to time intervals, and performance DFG as a cross-check on service/waiting between observed activities.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

pd.set_option("display.width", 250)
pd.set_option("display.max_columns", 50)

# 1. Supplied table, in memory (all times 2026-05-19, HH:MM).
raw = [
    ("F01", "07:55", "08:10", "08:10", "09:00", "08:15", "08:55", "08:20", "08:38", "09:08", "09:28", "F1"),
    ("F02", "08:05", "08:25", "08:25", "09:15", "08:45", "09:25", "08:30", "08:50", "09:40", "10:00", "F2"),
    ("F03", "08:20", "08:40", "08:40", "09:30", "09:00", "09:45", "08:45", "09:03", "10:00", "10:20", "F1"),
    ("F04", "08:35", "08:55", "08:55", "09:45", "09:05", "09:35", "09:30", "09:52", "10:10", "10:30", "F2"),
    ("F05", "08:50", "09:10", "09:10", "10:00", "09:35", "10:10", "09:15", "09:35", "10:20", "10:40", "F1"),
    ("F06", "09:05", "09:25", "09:25", "10:15", "09:55", "10:30", "09:40", "10:00", "10:45", "11:05", "F2"),
    ("F07", "09:20", "09:40", "09:40", "10:30", "10:10", "10:55", "09:45", "10:03", "11:10", "11:30", "F1"),
    ("F08", "09:35", "09:55", "09:55", "10:45", "10:00", "10:30", "10:20", "10:50", "11:05", "11:25", "F2"),
    ("F09", "09:50", "10:10", "10:10", "11:00", "10:35", "11:15", "10:15", "10:35", "11:30", "11:50", "F1"),
    ("F10", "10:05", "10:25", "10:25", "11:15", "10:45", "11:20", "10:30", "10:50", "11:40", "12:00", "F2"),
    ("F11", "10:20", "10:40", "10:40", "11:30", "10:50", "11:20", "10:45", "11:03", "11:50", "12:10", "F1"),
    ("F12", "10:35", "10:55", "10:55", "11:45", "11:25", "12:10", "11:00", "11:18", "12:20", "12:40", "F2"),
]
cols = ["case", "arrival", "fork_release", "A_start", "A_end", "B_start", "B_end",
        "C_start", "C_end", "final_start", "final_end", "final_resource"]
df = pd.DataFrame(raw, columns=cols)

# 2. Parse timestamps and calculate every decomposition component.
time_cols = [c for c in cols if c not in ("case", "final_resource")]
for c in time_cols:
    df[c] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[c], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")

def mins(series):
    return series.dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

df["upstream_elapsed"] = mins(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])
branches = ["A", "B", "C"]  # fixed order => deterministic tie handling
for b in branches:
    df[f"{b}_queue"] = mins(df[f"{b}_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    df[f"{b}_service"] = mins(df[f"{b}_end"] - df[f"{b}_start"])
    df[f"{b}_elapsed"] = df[f"{b}_queue"] + df[f"{b}_service"]

# Custom fork-join logic (not recovered by directly-follows performance):
# join_ready is the MAXIMUM branch completion; sync wait is slack to that max.
df["join_ready"] = df[[f"{b}_end" for b in branches]].max(axis=1)
df["fork_join_stage"] = mins(df["join_ready"] - df["fork_release"])
for b in branches:
    df[f"{b}_sync"] = mins(df["join_ready"] - df[f"{b}_end"])
# Deterministic ties: all branches attaining the max, in A,B,C order (none occur here).
df["critical_branch"] = df.apply(
    lambda r: ",".join(b for b in branches if r[f"{b}_elapsed"] == r["fork_join_stage"]), axis=1)
df["downstream_queue"] = mins(df["final_start"] - df["join_ready"])  # queue only under ASSUMP
df["final_service"] = mins(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
df["total_flow_time"] = mins(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])

assert (df[[f"{b}_queue" for b in branches] + ["downstream_queue"]].values >= 0).all()
assert ((df["upstream_elapsed"] + df["fork_join_stage"] + df["downstream_queue"]
         + df["final_service"] - df["total_flow_time"]).abs().max() < 1e-9)

show = df.copy()
show["join_ready"] = show["join_ready"].dt.strftime("%H:%M")
comp_cols = ["case", "upstream_elapsed", "A_queue", "A_service", "A_sync", "B_queue", "B_service",
             "B_sync", "C_queue", "C_service", "C_sync", "join_ready", "critical_branch",
             "fork_join_stage", "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"]
print("=== Case-level decomposition (minutes) ===")
print(show[comp_cols].to_string(index=False))

branch_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "service_mean": [df[f"{b}_service"].mean() for b in branches],
    "queue_mean": [df[f"{b}_queue"].mean() for b in branches],
    "elapsed_mean": [df[f"{b}_elapsed"].mean() for b in branches],
    "elapsed_max": [df[f"{b}_elapsed"].max() for b in branches],
    "critical_n": [df["critical_branch"].str.contains(b).sum() for b in branches],
    "sync_mean": [df[f"{b}_sync"].mean() for b in branches],
}, index=branches)
print("\n=== Branch summary ===")
print(branch_summary.round(2).to_string())

# 4. Counterfactuals (direct effects; final start shifts 1-for-1 with join_ready).
# CF1: A service -20% (queue unchanged). CF2: B queue -50% (service unchanged).
# CF3: downstream queue eliminated = UPPER BOUND for adding final capacity; also show 50%.
df["join_elapsed_CF1"] = df[["A_queue", "B_elapsed", "C_elapsed"]].assign(
    A_queue=df["A_queue"] + 0.8 * df["A_service"]).max(axis=1)
# (column above is A elapsed under CF1; rename for clarity in the frame below)
df["A_elapsed_CF1"] = df["A_queue"] + 0.8 * df["A_service"]
df["join_elapsed_CF1"] = df[["A_elapsed_CF1", "B_elapsed", "C_elapsed"]].max(axis=1)
df["CF1_saving"] = df["fork_join_stage"] - df["join_elapsed_CF1"]

df["B_elapsed_CF2"] = 0.5 * df["B_queue"] + df["B_service"]
df["join_elapsed_CF2"] = df[["A_elapsed", "B_elapsed_CF2", "C_elapsed"]].max(axis=1)
df["CF2_saving"] = df["fork_join_stage"] - df["join_elapsed_CF2"]

df["CF3_saving_elim_downstream"] = df["downstream_queue"]
df["CF3_saving_half_downstream"] = 0.5 * df["downstream_queue"]

cf_cols = ["case", "CF1_saving", "CF2_saving", "CF3_saving_elim_downstream", "CF3_saving_half_downstream"]
print("\n=== Counterfactual savings per case (minutes) ===")
print(df[cf_cols].to_string(index=False))
print("\n=== Counterfactual aggregates ===")
print(df[cf_cols[1:]].agg(["sum", "mean", "max"]).round(2).to_string())
print("Cases with benefit (>0):", {c: int((df[c] > 0).sum()) for c in cf_cols[1:]})

# 5. PM4Py interval-event DataFrame: one row per branch/final activity.
activity_names = {"A": "A_Laboratory_Assay", "B": "B_Machine_Stress_Test",
                  "C": "C_Documentation_Review"}
rows = []
for _, r in df.iterrows():
    for b in branches:
        rows.append({"case:concept:name": r["case"], "concept:name": activity_names[b],
                     "time:timestamp": r[f"{b}_end"], "start_timestamp": r[f"{b}_start"],
                     "resource": "not_logged"})  # branch resource not supplied
    rows.append({"case:concept:name": r["case"], "concept:name": "F_Release_Decision",
                 "time:timestamp": r["final_end"], "start_timestamp": r["final_start"],
                 "resource": r["final_resource"]})
interval_df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print("\n=== Interval-event DataFrame (first 8 rows) ===")
print(interval_df.head(8).to_string(index=False))

try:
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(interval_df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                                 activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                                 resource_key="resource", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
except TypeError:
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(interval_df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                                 activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                                 start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

# PM4Py cross-checks: interval conversion preserves start/complete semantics;
```

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality is not identified.** Criticality is an accounting statement about the observed maximum completion time. It does not prove that intervening on that branch *causes* the counterfactual saving, because queues, priorities and resource sharing may respond to the intervention. All counterfactuals are mechanical max-based calculations under stated ceteris-paribus assumptions.
- **Branch resource capacity is unobserved.** No resource IDs, staffing levels, equipment counts, or queue disciplines are supplied for A, B or C. B queue could reflect a single busy machine, shared technicians, batching, priority rules, or deliberate sequencing; each implies a different redesign and a different displacement effect on other branches. A’s zero queue likewise could mean ample capacity or top priority.
- **Service time may not be pure work.** Start-to-end elapsed can include preemption, breaks, machine faults, unlogged sub-steps or handoffs. A’s constant 50 min and final service’s constant 20 min are suspiciously regular and may be standard times, rounded values, or scheduled slots rather than measurements of active effort. Minute-level rounding also means small differences (e.g. 5 min) should not be over-interpreted.
- **Final waiting may not be a queue.** $final\_start-join\_ready$ is labelled downstream queue only under the assumption that $F$ can start at $J_c$. In several supplied cases the assigned final resource appears idle in-sample during that interval, consistent instead with a release policy, batching, an unlogged mandatory check/cooling step, priority for unlisted cases, or predetermined resource assignment. These timestamps cannot distinguish those mechanisms, so the benefit of “adding final capacity” is not identified — only bounded.
- **Cross-case dependence and masking.** Cases share F1/F2 (and possibly branch resources), so cases are not independent. A single-case counterfactual that shifts $J_c$ earlier may be absorbed if the assigned final resource is busy with the previous case (observed pinned starts in F05, F08, F09, F11), or may cascade beneficially if earlier final completions free capacity. The direct savings in Section 4 are therefore upper-bound-style estimates, not guaranteed realized flow-time reductions.
- **Small, single-day sample.** $n=12$ cases from one morning (2026-05-19), with arrivals exactly 15 min apart, cannot support statistical generalization, seasonal claims, or reliable tail estimation. Rankings are descriptive for these cases; the A–C critical-frequency tie (2 each) and the absence of ties are sample facts, not population parameters.
- **Throughput vs flow time.** Nothing in the timestamps identifies system throughput: arrival rate, upstream capacity, branch server counts and any work outside the supplied cases are unknown. Reducing a case’s flow time need not raise throughput if an upstream or final server remains the rate-limiting constraint, and reducing waiting by re-prioritization need not create capacity.
- **Costs, quality and safety are absent.** Cutting laboratory assay or machine stress-test service could affect validity, safety or compliance; documentation review may be a regulatory gate. No cost, staffing, rework or defect data are supplied, so recommendations are flow-time hypotheses to be piloted with quality monitoring, not proven improvements.
- **Model scope.** The analysis assumes the stated fork-join rule exactly (all branches permitted at `fork_release`, $F$ after all completions, no rework loops, no branch cancellation). Any unlogged rework, partial release, or conditional skipping would invalidate the max-based decomposition for affected cases.
