## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Prediction time `t0` is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. At that moment the only observed prefix is `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`. Anything that refers to `Documents Requested/Received/Validated`, `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Fraud Investigation Opened`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal` or `Claim Closed` is, by construction, future information. The table applies that test first, then checks lineage (is the stored value really the value as of `t0`?), then process feasibility, then interpretation.

Severity scale: critical = feature encodes the outcome or events strictly after `t0`, explanation is void; high = feature is partially or probably contaminated by post-`t0` information, cannot be trusted until recomputed; medium = admissible at `t0` but interpretation or generalisation is unsafe; low = admissible, minor verification needed.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The suite has two layers: a normative process model (a Petri net/BPMN model of the eight steps, with the document loop, the parallel fraud branch and the appeal loop) plus a set of declarative constraints, and a bitemporal feature store in which every feature value carries `event_time`, `valid_from` (when the value became knowable) and `record_time` (when it was written). Every check below is automated and runs on the historical training set, on each retraining, and on a sampled basis in production.

1. Prefix-time availability
   - Truncation test: rebuild the event log truncated at `t0` for every case, recompute every feature from the truncated log and bitemporal joins, and compare with the stored value. Any feature with a non-zero mismatch rate on the truncated recomputation is complete-trace-dependent or mutable and fails the gate. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role` fail with ~100% mismatch; `final_handler_team` fails with ~35%; `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` fail with case-dependent rates.
   - As-of assertion: for every joined attribute, `valid_from <= t0` must hold; joins that use `extraction_date` or `CURRENT_DATE` are rejected by static SQL linting.
   - Leakage canaries: single-feature models and mutual information with the label; a feature whose univariate AUC approaches 1.0 is quarantined for lineage review. Compare model AUC on the "as stored" versus "prefix-only recomputed" dataset; a large drop confirms leakage was inflating performance.
   - Limitation: recomputation is only as good as the availability of historical snapshots. Where no history exists (risk score, employee experience) the feature cannot be validated and must be dropped rather than assumed.

2. Activity-order feasibility
   - Footprint check: derive the footprint (directly-follows, causality, parallel, choice relations) from the normative model and compare with the footprint of the event log. Relations present in the log but forbidden by the model (e.g. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, `Appeal -> *` without a preceding Partial/Deny decision, anything twice after `Claim Registered`) are flagged as either data-quality defects or genuine deviations.
   - Alignment-based conformance: align each trace to the model. Synchronous moves are legitimate; log moves (events the model cannot explain in that position) and model moves (skipped mandatory steps) are reported per trace. Explanation patterns are only allowed to cite activity pairs that appear as consecutive synchronous moves in the alignment. The alignment cost function must treat the appeal loop and the document loop as zero-cost (they are in the model), otherwise legitimate repetition looks deviant.
   - Declarative constraints (Declare-style), which are easier to maintain for the loop cases: `Init(Claim Registered)`, `End(Claim Closed)`, `Precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Documents Requested)`, `Precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`, `Precedence(Damage Assessment Completed, Settlement Decision Recorded)`, `Precedence(Settlement Decision Recorded[Partial|Deny], Appeal)`, `Response(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded)`, `NotSuccession(Claim Closed, any activity)`, `Response(Documents Requested, Documents Received)`.
   - Role of the DFG and its limit: a frequency-weighted DFG mined from the log is descriptive. A frequent edge is evidence of what the systems recorded, not of what the process allows. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` may be frequent because of batch closures with tied timestamps; that does not make it a rule, and the explainer must not treat DFG edges as normative. Normativity comes from the modelled process and domain sign-off; the DFG is used only to discover candidate deviations and data errors.

3. Same-timestamp ambiguity
   - Detect all pairs of events in a case with identical timestamps. Report the share of cases affected and whether a reliable sequence index exists for them.
   - Where a sequence index exists, use it to order; where it is missing, order tied events by the normative model (the only order the model permits, if unique) and mark the trace `order_uncertain = true`. If the model permits more than one order (e.g. parallel fraud and assessment), the pair is unordered and no directly-follows feature may be computed across it.
   - Boundary rule at `t0`: any event with `timestamp == t0` and no sequence index proving it precedes `Coverage Check Completed` is treated as after `t0` and excluded (this governs the fraud-trigger question).
   - Explanation gate: sequence explainers may not attribute weight to a pattern that spans a tied, unordered pair. Case Z's top driver fails this gate.
   - Limitation: normative tie-breaking can hide genuine deviations; therefore flagged traces are also sampled for manual review, and the source system should be fixed to emit a monotonically increasing sequence number.

4. Complete-trace information
   - Lineage tagging: every feature has a mandatory `horizon` attribute in the feature registry: `prefix`, `external_snapshot`, `mutable_asof`, `complete_trace`. `complete_trace` features are blocked from `t0` models at pipeline compile time.
   - Behavioural test: the truncation test in check 1 detects mis-tagged features. A second test perturbs only post-`t0` events (delete, reorder, relabel) and asserts feature values are invariant.
   - SQL/code lint: aggregates over the case without an upper bound on `event_time <= t0`, joins on `extraction_date`, and columns with names like `final_`, `total_`, `last_`, `current_`, `seen_anywhere` trigger review.

5. Enabled, impossible, or future-possible at `t0`
   - Replay the prefix on the normative model to obtain the marking at `t0`. From that marking classify every activity: enabled (can happen next: `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Documents Requested`, `Fraud Investigation Opened` if a trigger is modelled), future-possible (reachable but not yet enabled: `Documents Received/Validated`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, `Claim Closed`), impossible (`Claim Registered` again; any activity after `Claim Closed`; `Appeal` without prior Partial/Deny).
   - Explanation validator: for every activity referenced by a feature or pattern, look up its class. Only activities that are in the prefix may appear as observed facts. Enabled and future-possible activities may appear only as predicted quantities ("expected number of document cycles"), and impossible activities may never appear. This distinguishes `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` (future-possible, legitimate, inadmissible at `t0`) from `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` (impossible, a data defect).
   - Limitation: the classification depends on the model being complete; undocumented workarounds appear as impossible and must be triaged with process owners rather than silently accepted.

6. Repeated Settlement Decision events after appeal
   - Constraint check: a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate if and only if it is preceded by an `Appeal`, which is preceded by a first decision with outcome Partial or Deny. Report: decisions repeated without appeal (duplicate recording or re-keying), appeals after Approve/Withdrawn (impossible), appeals with no subsequent decision (open cases or missing events).
   - Label consistency: define whether the 10-day target refers to the first or the final decision and whether appeal time is in scope. If the target is the first decision, the appeal loop should not influence the label at all, and any residual association between appeal and breach is via confounding by case difficulty, not via the appeal itself.
   - Coarse-label disambiguation: enrich the label with the decision outcome and an occurrence index (`Settlement Decision Recorded#1`, `#2`) before mining or aligning; otherwise alignments see a loop that the coarse model may not permit.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Four different standards apply and must not be conflated:
- Predictive validity: the value was knowable at `t0`, is computed identically in training and serving, and its association with the label holds out of time. This licenses statements of the form "cases with this value breached more often".
- Causal interpretation: requires an identification strategy (experiment, natural experiment, adjustment for a defensible confounder set). SHAP importance never provides this. It licenses statements like "reducing X would reduce breach".
- Operational actionability: there must be a lever a decision-maker can pull between `t0` and the deadline (capacity, routing, early document request, prioritisation). A feature can be predictive and even causal without being actionable (complexity), or actionable at the system level but not per case (queue length).
- Fairness review: any feature that is, or proxies for, a protected or sensitive characteristic needs disparate-impact analysis and a documented justification, even if it passes the other three standards. Passing fairness review does not require deletion; failing it may.

Classes:

a) Defensible predictive signal
   - `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `claimant_channel` (after lineage confirmation), `initial_reserve` (only if confirmed as the first reserve set at or before `t0`), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after the SQL fix), a fraud-trigger flag if recorded at or before `t0`, and registration-derived features (weekday/hour of registration, registration-to-coverage-check time).
   - May claim: historically, claims with this profile at `t0` breached at a higher/lower rate; the value was known at `t0`; the association is stable under time-split validation.
   - May not claim: that changing the attribute would change the outcome (complexity and reserve describe the claim, they are not levers), or that the feature "explains" the delay mechanism without further analysis.

b) Process-state or congestion signal
   - `queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (double role: it is both an attribute of the case and a reading of process slack), `team_assigned_at_t0` (after repair), team workload or backlog at `t0`, capacity available in the coming 10 days.
   - May claim: the state of the operation when this claim entered the assessment stage was associated with breach risk; this is the part of the prediction the operation can act on at the system level (add capacity, re-route, prioritise).
   - May not claim: that this claim will breach because of the queue (individual counterfactual), or that congestion is causal without controlling for the holiday/policy confounders that also raise queue length. Per-case actionability (jumping the queue) redistributes delay rather than removing it and must be evaluated as a policy, not asserted from the explanation.

c) Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review
   - `claimant_region`, and to a lesser extent `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`, and `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (all correlate with socioeconomic or customer-segment characteristics).
   - May claim: predictive association, provided mediators have been tested. Preferably the explanation cites the mediator ("area with sparse repair-partner coverage") rather than the bundle ("region R4").
   - May not claim: "region is a delay driver". Required review: effect after adding operational mediators, disparate-impact ratios and false-positive/false-negative parity by region and by proxied protected groups, downstream-use analysis (if a high breach score triggers deprioritisation or extra scrutiny, a proxy can create a feedback loop), and a governance decision recorded in the model card. Robustness: check that the region effect is stable over time and not an artefact of one regional office's backlog.

d) Drift/calendar proxy
   - `calendar_week` (and any month/quarter variables in the residual).
   - May claim: within the training window, certain weeks had elevated breach rates.
   - May not claim: that this transports to future weeks with the same number, or that the week is a mechanism. It must be replaced by the structural variables it proxies (holiday period, business days available before the deadline, policy regime, staffing) and monitored for drift; retain it, if at all, only as a diagnostic.

e) Unusable until lineage is repaired
   - `final_handler_team` (-> `team_assigned_at_t0`), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL fix), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (-> event-based count with `upload_ts <= t0`), `risk_score_current` (-> versioned score as of `t0`), `initial_reserve` (until the snapshot is proven), `last_resource_role` (-> `resource_role_at_t0`), employee experience (-> as-of-event join).
   - May claim: nothing until recomputed and re-validated. After repair, they move to class a) or b).
   - Permanently inadmissible at `t0` (not repairable, only relocatable to later prediction points): `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, and all five sequence patterns as observed features.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Priority 1 (block release until done)
1. Remove the permanently inadmissible features and patterns from the `t0` model: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all five sequence patterns as observed features. Retrain and report the honest performance drop; this is the true baseline.
2. Prefix-only recomputation of the entire training set: rebuild every feature from the event log truncated at `t0` with bitemporal joins (`valid_from <= t0`), never from the warehouse's closure-time tables. Recompute `prior_claim_count_before_t0` with the corrected predicate, `same_day_document_count_snapshot` from upload events, employee experience as of event date, `team_assigned_at_t0` from the coverage-check event.
3. Feature-store design: a registry entry per feature with `horizon` (prefix / external_snapshot / mutable_asof / complete_trace), source event(s), computation window, owner and test reference. Compile-time rule: `complete_trace` features cannot be bound to a model whose prediction point is `t0`; `mutable_asof` features require a history table. Store every mutable business attribute (reserve, risk score, team, experience) with `valid_from`/`valid_to` so that as-of reconstruction is possible going forward.

Priority 2 (before explanations are shown to users)
4. Historical feature timestamps: for features without history today (`risk_score_current`, `final_handler_team`, `initial_reserve`), start versioning now and, until enough history exists, exclude them. Do not back-fill by assuming the current value held at `t0`.
5. Process-conformance tests as CI gates: footprint comparison, alignment of each trace with the normative model including the appeal and document loops, the declarative constraint set from section 2, same-timestamp detection with sequence-index coverage metrics, and the enabled/future-possible/impossible classifier applied to every activity referenced by the explainer. Any explanation citing an impossible pattern or a non-prefix activity as an observed fact is rejected automatically. Fix the source system to emit a sequence index.
6. Calibration after feature removal: the cleaned model will be less confident and its scores will shift. Re-fit calibration on a time-based holdout, re-derive the alert threshold from the operational cost of false alerts versus missed breaches, and publish reliability diagrams by complexity, channel and region. Do not reuse the old threshold.
7. Explanation stability: measure SHAP rank stability across bootstrap resamples and across adjacent training windows; report attribution intervals rather than point values; check that local explanations for near-identical prefixes are near-identical; verify that removing a feature does not silently shift its attribution onto a correlated proxy (e.g. region absorbing the deleted variant signal). Compare tree-based and model-agnostic attributions for agreement on the top drivers.

Priority 3 (ongoing governance)
8. Proxy and fairness analysis: for `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, quantify how much of the effect survives after adding operational mediators; compute disparate-impact ratios and error-rate parity across regions and proxied protected groups; document how the score is used downstream and whether a high score can disadvantage the claimant; record the retain/replace/remove decision and its justification in the model card; rephrase explanations to cite mediators, not group labels.
9. Replace calendar proxies with structural variables (holiday period, business days before deadline, capacity, policy regime) and validate with strictly time-ordered splits that cross the policy change; report performance per regime separately.
10. Monitoring for schema and process drift: alert on new activity labels, changes in the directly-follows footprint (e.g. sudden appearance of forbidden edges signalling a timestamp or ordering regression), rising share of tied timestamps, changes in the `t0` feature distributions (queue length, complexity mix), calibration drift, and explanation drift (top-k driver set changing week to week). Re-run the prefix-only recomputation test on a rolling sample in production so that any new complete-trace or as-of-extraction join is caught immediately.
11. Communication standard: every published explanation states the prediction point, lists only prefix-observable facts as facts, labels expected future events as expectations, distinguishes predictive association from causal or actionable statements, and carries a data-quality flag when the underlying trace is order-uncertain.
