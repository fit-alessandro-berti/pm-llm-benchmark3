## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts (Established by Direct Evidence)
1. **Formal Normative Baseline:** Compliance Standard CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01) is the sole formally controlled policy. It mandates `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` before activation, `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`, and separation of duties (SoD) between requester and manual approver.
2. **Model M0 Semantics:** POWL model M0 enforces sequential execution of `Register_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ concurrent (`Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`) $\rightarrow$ XOR((`Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`), `Reject_Supplier`) $\rightarrow$ `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`. M0 lacks resource constraints, data guards, and information-request loops.
3. **Overlay Windows & Scope:**
   - **Pilot PD-3:** Steering-committee minutes/email (2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31) permitted `Auto_Approve` and post-activation `Quality_Review` (within 2 business days) for LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, provided `Sanctions_Check` and master-data validation preceded activation.
   - **Emergency Directive FD-7:** Formal emergency directive (2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59) permitted `Activate_Supplier` before `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` for critical flood-relief suppliers, provided `Sanctions_Check` preceded activation, spend was capped at EUR 20,000, daily monitoring occurred, and `Quality_Review` completed within 2 business days.
4. **Systemic Data & Logging Shifts:** On 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` moved to a CRM service; ~29% of notifications are missing from the onboarding log. Event duplication (SUP-711) and unmapped UI label changes (`QA_Clearance` in SUP-713) occurred in the log pipeline.
5. **High-Risk Operational Breaches:** In SUP-703 (EUR 180k spend) and 13 other cases, `Auto_Approve` was used for clearly ineligible suppliers. In SUP-708, a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before `Sanctions_Check`. In SUP-712, the requester and manual approver resolved to the same human via aliases.

### Disputed Propositions (Dependent on Authority, Scope, or Logging Assumptions)
1. **Normative Force of PD-3:** Whether steering-committee minutes and an ambiguous Compliance observer email ("monitored exception") legally suspended CS-9 between June 1 and July 31, 2026 (affects 289 eligible pilot cases including SUP-702).
2. **De Facto Continuation of Expired Overlays:** Whether factual persistence of the flood emergency on July 13 justifies FD-7 procedures after its midnight expiration (SUP-705), and whether imminent expectation of permanent policy amendment justifies PD-3 usage in August (SUP-714 + 5 others).
3. **Process Scope Boundaries:** Whether M0 governs pre-screen rejected leads (SUP-709) and supplier reactivations with unexpired quality certificates (SUP-710).
4. **Evidentiary Sufficiency of Mutable Logs:** Whether a CRM record linked via a mutable text field entered two days post-facto proves timely notification (SUP-706).
5. **Scope of Conflict-of-Interest (CoI) Rules:** Whether CS-9’s silent stance on spousal relationships between quality reviewers and manual approvers renders SUP-715 compliant with onboarding rules, or noncompliant via corporate CoI policy integration.

### Burden of Proof Framework
- **To Declare Noncompliance (Prosecution Burden):** The reviewer must establish by a *preponderance of reliable evidence* that an observed trace or context violated an active, unambiguous requirement of CS-9 or M0 *within its intended scope*, and that the deviation is not merely a logging artifact, label rename, or duplicate event.
- **To Accept an Exception or Override (Defense Burden):** Once a deviation from CS-9/M0 is shown, the burden shifts to Operations to prove by *clear, contemporaneous, and immutable documentary evidence* that: (a) a duly authorized exception (PD-3, FD-7) was in force at the exact timestamp of execution, (b) all eligibility predicates and compensating controls (risk tier, spend cap, retrospective review SLAs) were strictly satisfied, and (c) where log events are missing, immutable secondary system records (e.g., stable `notification_id`) establish factual performance.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### I. The Primacy of Controlled Governance Over Informal Drift
Compliance Standard CS-9 is the only formally approved governance instrument. Operations seeks to invalidate CS-9 using steering-committee minutes (PD-3), expired emergency orders (FD-7), and unilateral workflow habits. In regulated procurement, a controlled standard cannot be amended by meeting minutes—especially when the Compliance observer explicitly disclaimed approving a weakening of CS-9. Even if PD-3 were generously treated as a temporary waiver, Operations demonstrated an inability to respect boundaries:
1. **Uncontrolled Leakage of Pilot Exceptions:** Out of 312 `Auto_Approve` cases, 14 were blatantly ineligible and 9 lacked eligibility data. In **SUP-703**, an operator routed a HIGH-risk foreign supplier through `Auto_Approve`, bypassed pre-activation quality review, and executed a **EUR 180,000 purchase**. A pilot without technical guardrails that permits a EUR 180k high-risk bypass is a systemic control failure.
2. **Unilateral Extension of Expired Authority:** Operations treated hard expiration timestamps as mere suggestions. **FD-7** expired on 2026-07-12 at 23:59; **SUP-705** was activated without quality or procurement approval on July 13. Similarly, **PD-3** expired on 2026-07-31; **SUP-714** (and 5 other cases) used `Auto_Approve` in August based on a subjective belief that permanent approval was "imminent." Anticipation of a policy change is not policy. Allowing operational convenience to extend expired waivers destroys the concept of time-bounded risk acceptance.

### II. Severe Control-Flow Breaches and Unmitigated Business Risk
Even where no pilot or emergency is claimed, Operations violated foundational CS-9 tenets:
- **Pre-Sanctions Activation (SUP-708):** Every single document—CS-9, M0, PD-3, and FD-7—unanimously mandates `Sanctions_Check` *before* `Activate_Supplier`. In SUP-708, a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes *before* sanctions screening. The defense that "no transactions occurred" in that 40-minute window is pure luck, not a control. Once activated in master data, the ERP cannot prevent automated PO transmission or payment runs.
- **Unverified Reactivation Shortcuts (SUP-710):** Reactivating a closed supplier without `Register_Supplier` or `Quality_Review` violates CS-9 ("Every supplier onboarding requires..."). Relying on a six-month-old quality certificate without a formal reactivation standard creates an unmonitored backdoor into the active vendor master.

### III. Hidden Noncompliance: Why M0 Conformance Is Necessary but Insufficient
Prosecution readily concedes that M0 has defects: it fails to capture resource rules, data guards, and benign clerical loops (`Request_More_Info` in **SUP-707**), and the data pipeline suffered from duplicate extraction (**SUP-711**) and unaligned UI labels (**SUP-713**). However, M0’s greatest weakness is not that it flags benign behavior, but that **it masks severe governance violations**:
- **Identity Laundering (SUP-712):** The trace conforms perfectly to M0, yet the requester and manual approver were the exact same human acting through two system aliases. This is a textbook violation of CS-9’s core segregation-of-duties rule, indicative of either reckless access management or intentional fraud.
- **Undisclosed Conflict of Interest (SUP-715):** While M0 and CS-9 do not explicitly mention spouses, corporate Conflict-of-Interest policy requires disclosure when married employees in the same reporting line sequentially perform `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. Unrecorded spousal sign-offs nullify independent dual control.
- **Unverifiable Audit Trails (SUP-706):** When CRM mirroring failed, Operations relied on a mutable text field edited two days after the fact to claim notification occurred. In conformance auditing, mutable post-hoc text entries do not satisfy the burden of proof.

**Conclusion for Prosecution:** Operations suffers from systemic compliance decay. Model M0 must be enforced alongside strict CS-9 data and resource checks, and unauthorized/expired bypasses (SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714) must be ruled noncompliant.

## 3. Defense Brief

### I. M0 Is an Brittle, Underspecified Artifact Evaluating Corrupted Data
An audit model is only valid to the extent it accurately reflects governing policy and ingests faithful telemetry. Here, M0 fails on both counts. Judging operational compliance by raw M0 conformance conflates model obsolescence and IT pipeline failures with operational misconduct:
1. **Telemetry & Pipeline Artifacts (SUP-702, SUP-706, SUP-711, SUP-713):** 
   - After the July 1 CRM migration (N4), the onboarding log stopped capturing 29% of `Notify_Requester` events. In **SUP-702**, CRM proves notification occurred at +2 hours via an immutable `notification_id`. In **SUP-706**, a CRM message exists at +3 hours. Penalizing Operations because an IT integration dropped 29% of events violates basic audit fairness.
   - **SUP-711** fails M0 solely because a database migration emitted `Register_Supplier` twice with the exact same source transaction ID.
   - **SUP-713** fails M0 because the UI renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance`—an equivalence formally ratified one week later. Substantive quality assurance was performed in full.
2. **Omission of Desirable Due Diligence (SUP-707):** In 47 cases, including SUP-707, operators executed `Request_More_Info` loops to obtain missing safety/quality certificates *before* approval. Compliance explicitly agrees this behavior is desirable (N5). Yet M0’s rigid partial order rejects all 47 cases as nonconformant because the model author forgot to include an information-gathering loop.

### II. Valid Organizational Governance Overlays (PD-3 and FD-7)
M0 reflects a monolithic "one-size-fits-all" flow that ignores legitimate governance agility:
- **Authorized Pilot Execution (SUP-702 & 288 others):** PD-3 was approved by the Steering Committee, with Compliance present and stating in writing that the "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception" (N2). Operations relied in good faith on executive authorization. In SUP-702, every condition of PD-3 (LOW-risk DE supplier, approved corporate group, pre-activation sanctions check, next-day quality review) was meticulously satisfied. Retroactively declaring 289 properly executed pilot cases "noncompliant" because Compliance later regretted its email phrasing would paralyze business innovation.
- **Emergency Resilience (SUP-704, SUP-705):** During catastrophic flooding, FD-7 authorized provisional activation to prevent plant shutdown. **SUP-704** followed every single guardrail of FD-7 (pump supplier, pre-activation sanctions check, EUR 18k spend under the EUR 20k cap, daily monitoring, next-day quality/manual approval). In **SUP-705**, the exact same controls were applied on July 13 (EUR 12k spend) while flood recovery was factually ongoing. While a formal written extension of FD-7 was overlooked amidst crisis recovery, substance over form dictates that protecting critical infrastructure under strict compensating controls is a justified emergency exception, not rogue noncompliance. Similarly, **SUP-714** met every substantive safety check of PD-3 during an administrative gap between pilot expiry and permanent adoption.

### III. Flawed Scope Boundaries and Extraneous Rule Inflation
- **Intake Pre-Screening (SUP-709):** When a prospective lead fails `Sanctions_Check` during initial screening and is rejected (`Reject_Supplier`), running a full `Quality_Review` on a sanctioned entity is a waste of resources. CS-9 explicitly permits an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads (N1). M0’s structure—forcing `checks` (both Sanctions and Quality) to complete before reaching `decision` (`Reject_Supplier`)—is a modeling error that contradicts CS-9.
- **Reactivations (SUP-710):** Re-registering an existing supplier and repeating a `Quality_Review` when a valid certification is already on file is redundant. SUP-710 underwent fresh `Sanctions_Check` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. It is a distinct lifecycle variant outside M0’s greenfield onboarding scope.
- **Conceding Genuine Faults Without Condemning the System:** Defense does not defend the indefensible. **SUP-703** (ineligible high-risk auto-approval), **SUP-708** (activation 40 minutes before sanctions check), and **SUP-712** (same-user alias self-approval) are genuine, serious operational errors requiring remediation. Regarding **SUP-715**, there was an administrative failure to log a CoI disclosure, though CS-9 itself was not violated. However, 3 genuine errors and 9 missing-attribute cases out of hundreds of transactions do not indict the entire operation—they prove the need for automated system guards rather than a return to an obsolete M0 model.

### 2. System-Level Judgment
- **Operational Compliance:** **Materially Deficient in Governance Guardrails and Access Control.** While ~90% of apparent M0 deviations are explained by authorized pilots (289 PD-3 cases), valid due diligence loops (47 `Request_More_Info` cases), and CRM logging drops (29% notification missingness), Operations exhibited unacceptable control breakdowns where it mattered most. Specifically: (1) allowing 14 clearly ineligible cases (like SUP-703, EUR 180k) and 9 unverified cases through `Auto_Approve`; (2) normalizing exceptions past their legal expiration dates (SUP-705, SUP-714 + 5 August cases); (3) permitting pre-sanctions activation (SUP-708); and (4) failing to enforce identity/SoD and CoI boundaries (SUP-712, SUP-715).
- **Model Validity (M0):** **Obsolete and Inadequately Scoped.** M0 generates massive false-positive noise (penalizing `Request_More_Info`, rejected pre-screens, label renames, and authorized overlays) while producing dangerous false-negatives (passing SUP-712 and SUP-715 because it is blind to identity, data attributes, and time). M0 cannot be enforced in its current state.

### 3. Where Each Side’s Argument Is Rejected
- **Rejecting the Prosecution:** We reject the Prosecution's formalist claim that all 289 eligible PD-3 cases (including SUP-702) are noncompliant because CS-9 wasn't formally re-issued. When a Steering Committee approves a time-boxed pilot and Compliance writes that it "may proceed as a monitored exception," Operations has reasonable reliance authority; internal friction within the Compliance department cannot be retroactively weaponized against compliant operators. We also reject treating pre-screen rejections (SUP-709), valid due-diligence loops (SUP-707), and UI renames (SUP-713) as operational breaches.
- **Rejecting the Defense:** We reject the Defense's "substance over form" plea for expired overlays (SUP-705, SUP-714). The defining legal characteristic of an exception window is its expiration timestamp; allowing Operations to unilaterally extend FD-7 or PD-3 because "the flood continued" or "approval was imminent" turns temporary waivers into permanent, unapproved policy revocation. We further reject the Defense's attempt to minimize SUP-708 ("no transactions occurred in 40 minutes") and SUP-715 ("CS-9 doesn't mention spouses"). Process compliance evaluates control integrity at the moment of execution, not whether a breach happened to result in financial loss.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

To resolve the tension between rigid control-flow modeling and multi-dimensional governance, conformance checking must move from a single POWL artifact to a **Four-Layer Normative Architecture**:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 4: ORGANIZATIONAL & IAM CONTROLS (Pre-Execution Prevention)                 |
|  - ERP Role-Based Access Control, Canonical Person_ID Resolution, CoI Registry    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 3: DATA, RESOURCE & OVERLAY GUARDS (Pandas / SQL / Policy Engine)           |
|  - Policy Overlay Table (PD-3, FD-7 time windows, spend caps, risk tiers)         |
|  - SoD & CoI Verification (Requester != Approver != Spouse)                       |
|  - SLA & Retrospective Timers (Post-activation QA <= 2 business days)             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 2: DECLARATIVE SAFETY INVARIANTS (DECLARE / Log Skeleton Constraints)       |
|  - Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier) [UNIVERSAL INVARIANT]           |
|  - Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review) [Covers Pilot & Emergency routes]  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 1: REVISED POWL CONTROL-FLOW MODEL (M1)                                     |
|  - Structured routing for Intake Pre-screen, Standard, Pilot, Emergency,          |
|    Reactivation, and repeatable Request_More_Info loops.                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Layer Allocation and Responsibilities
1. **Layer 1 — Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL `M1`):** Encodes valid structural pathways:
   - *Intake & Pre-Screen:* `Register_Supplier` (or `Reactivate_Supplier`) followed by `Sanctions_Check` (with optional `Request_More_Info` loop). Allows immediate `Reject_Supplier` if sanctions/pre-screen fails (solving SUP-709).
   - *Route Branching:* Exclusive choice (`XOR`) between:
     1. **Standard Route:** `Quality_Review` (with optional `Request_More_Info` loop) $\rightarrow$ `XOR`(`Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`, `Reject_Supplier`, `Withdraw_Case`).
     2. **Low-Risk Auto Route (PD-3):** `Master_Data_Validation` $\rightarrow$ `Auto_Approve` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ `Quality_Review`.
     3. **Emergency Route (FD-7):** `Activate_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ concurrent(`Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`).
     4. **Reactivation Fast-Track:** `Validate_Existing_QA_Cert` $\rightarrow$ `Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier` (solving SUP-710).
   - *Terminal Sequence:* All paths converge to `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`.
2. **Layer 2 — Declarative Safety Invariants (DECLARE / Log Skeleton):** Enforces cross-branch golden rules regardless of POWL complexity:
   - `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)`: Strictly forbids `Activate_Supplier` unless `Sanctions_Check` has already occurred (catches SUP-708 instantly).
   - `Precedence(Register_Supplier | Reactivate_Supplier, Sanctions_Check)`.
   - `Existence(Quality_Review | QA_Clearance | Validate_Existing_QA_Cert)` before `Close_Case` for all activated suppliers.
3. **Layer 3 — Data-Aware, Temporal, and Resource Constraints (Pandas / Conformance Engine):** Because POWL and DECLARE are control-flow formalisms, a tabular rule engine evaluates context guards:
   - *Overlay Authority & Window Check:* Verifies that cases taking the Auto Route or Emergency Route have `case_start_timestamp` strictly within an active `policy_id` window (`effective_from <= ts <= effective_to`) and carry a valid `authority_ref`.
   - *Eligibility & Spend Guards:* For Auto Route, checks `risk_tier == 'LOW'`, `country == 'DE'`, `in_approved_corp_group == True`. For Emergency Route, checks `cumulative_spend_before_approval <= 20000` and `daily_monitoring_active == True`.
   - *Temporal SLA Check:* For Auto and Emergency routes, verifies `business_days(Activate_Supplier.ts, Quality_Review.ts) <= 2`.
   - *Identity & CoI Resolution:* Resolves all user logins (`actor_login`) to canonical `person_id` via an IAM alias table. Asserts `Requester.person_id != Manual_Approver.person_id` (catches SUP-712) and checks the HR relationship graph between `Quality_Reviewer.person_id` and `Manual_Approver.person_id` unless `coi_disclosure_id IS NOT NULL` and `coi_mitigation_approved == True` (catches SUP-715).
4. **Layer 4 — Explicit Scope & Pipeline Pre-Processing Rules:**
   - *ETL Deduplication & Label Harmonization:* Drop duplicate events sharing identical `(case_id, activity, source_tx_id)` (cures SUP-711). Map `QA_Clearance` $\rightarrow$ `Quality_Review` via a versioned synonym table (cures SUP-713).
   - *Scope Rules:*
     - **Rejected Leads:** In-scope for M1 via the early pre-screen rejection branch (`Register_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ `Sanctions_Check` $\rightarrow$ `Reject_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`).
     - **Reactivations:** In-scope via `Reactivate_Supplier` start transition; requires `Validate_Existing_QA_Cert` (checking `cert_age_months <= 12` in Layer 3) instead of a fresh `Quality_Review`.
     - **Withdrawals:** If a requester cancels before decision, transition `Withdraw_Case` routes directly to `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Architectural Note on POWL Limitations and Approximations
1. **Standard Concurrency vs. Early Rejection:** In M0, `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` were inside a single concurrent block (`StrictPartialOrder`), which forced *both* to complete before `Reject_Supplier` could occur (breaking SUP-709). Furthermore, in the Pilot and Emergency routes, `Quality_Review` happens *after* `Activate_Supplier`, whereas `Sanctions_Check` must *always* happen *before* `Activate_Supplier`. In a single POWL model where transitions in different branches must have distinct object instances, we represent:
   - **Standard Route:** Concurrent execution of `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` (`std_checks = StrictPartialOrder([sanctions_std, qr_loop])`), preserving M0's exact concurrency semantics for standard cases, followed by `Request_More_Info` loop availability and decision (`Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`, `Reject_Supplier`, or `Withdraw_Case`).
   - **Pre-Screen Rejection Route:** `Sanctions_Check` followed directly by `Reject_Supplier` (resolving SUP-709 without forcing `Quality_Review`).
   - **Pilot, Emergency, and Reactivation Routes:** Dedicated branches inside the main `Operator.XOR` where `Sanctions_Check` explicitly precedes `Activate_Supplier`, and post-activation `Quality_Review` (and retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval` for Emergency) follows `Activate_Supplier`.
2. **Repeatable `Request_More_Info` Loop:** Constructed using `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, children=[core_activity, req_info])`. In PM4Py POWL semantics, `LOOP(A, B)` executes `A`, then zero or more repetitions of `(B -> A)` (or using a `SilentTransition` as the redo/body child so `Request_More_Info` can execute one or more times before or during review).

### Executable Python Code: Revised POWL (`M1`) and Multi-Layer Conformance Engine

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_revised_onboarding_powl() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """
    Constructs Revised Normative POWL Model M1.
    Covers:
      1. Standard Route (concurrent Sanctions_Check & Quality_Review + Request_More_Info loop)
      2. Pre-Screen Rejection / Withdrawal Route (Sanctions_Check -> Reject/Withdraw without QA)
      3. Low-Risk Pilot Route (Sanctions_Check & Master_Data_Validation -> Auto_Approve -> Activate -> QA)
      4. Emergency Route (Sanctions_Check -> Activate -> concurrent Retrospective QA & Manual Approval)
      5. Reactivation Route (Reactivate -> Sanctions_Check & Validate_Existing_QA_Cert -> Manual Approval -> Activate)
    """
    # --- Entry Transitions (Greenfield Registration vs. Reactivation) ---
    register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
    reactivate = Transition(label="Reactivate_Supplier")
    entry_xor = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[register, reactivate])

    # --- Reusable Repeatable Request_More_Info Loop Helper ---
    # LOOP(tau, Request_More_Info) allows 0, 1, or N executions of Request_More_Info
    def make_rmi_loop() -> OperatorPOWL:
        tau = SilentTransition()
        rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
        return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[tau, rmi])

    # =========================================================================
    # BRANCH 1: STANDARD ROUTE (Concurrent Sanctions & Quality + RMI Loop)
    # =========================================================================
    sanctions_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    quality_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    rmi_loop_std = make_rmi_loop()

    # Within the quality track, allow optional Request_More_Info cycles before/during Quality_Review
    quality_with_rmi = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_loop_std, quality_std])
    quality_with_rmi.order.add_edge(rmi_loop_std, quality_std)

    # Concurrent Sanctions_Check and (RMI_Loop -> Quality_Review)
    std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_std, quality_with_rmi])

    manual_app_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    std_approved_po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_app_std, activate_std])
    std_approved_po.order.add_edge(manual_app_std, activate_std)

    reject_std = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    withdraw_std = Transition(label="Withdraw_Case")
    std_decision = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[std_approved_po, reject_std, withdraw_std]
    )

    branch_standard = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_decision])
    branch_standard.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_decision)

    # =========================================================================
    # BRANCH 2: ABBREVIATED PRE-SCREEN REJECTION (Solves SUP-709)
    # =========================================================================
    sanctions_pre = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    reject_pre = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    branch_prescreen_reject = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_pre, reject_pre])
    branch_prescreen_reject.order.add_edge(sanctions_pre, reject_pre)

    # =========================================================================
    # BRANCH 3: LOW-RISK AUTO-APPROVAL ROUTE (Pilot PD-3 / Authorized Low-Risk)
    # =========================================================================
    sanctions_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    md_val_auto = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
    pre_auto_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_auto, md_val_auto])

    auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
    activate_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    quality_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

    branch_auto = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[pre_auto_checks, auto_approve, activate_auto, quality_auto]
    )
    branch_auto.order.add_edge(pre_auto_checks, auto_approve)
    branch_auto.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_auto)
    branch_auto.order.add_edge(activate_auto, quality_auto)

    # =========================================================================
    # BRANCH 4: EMERGENCY PROVISIONAL ROUTE (Directive FD-7)
    # =========================================================================
    sanctions_emg = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    activate_emg = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    quality_emg = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    manual_app_emg = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

    # Retrospective checks can occur in any order after provisional activation
    retro_checks_emg = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality_emg, manual_app_emg])

    branch_emergency = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions_emg, activate_emg, retro_checks_emg]
    )
    branch_emergency.order.add_edge(sanctions_emg, activate_emg)
    branch_emergency.order.add_edge(activate_emg, retro_checks_emg)

    # =========================================================================
    # BRANCH 5: REACTIVATION ROUTE (Valid Existing Quality Cert - SUP-710)
    # =========================================================================
    sanctions_react = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    val_cert_react = Transition(label="Validate_Existing_QA_Cert")
    react_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_react, val_cert_react])

    manual_app_react = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate_react = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

    branch_reactivation = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[react_checks, manual_app_react, activate_react]
    )
    branch_reactivation.order.add_edge(react_checks, manual_app_react)
    branch_reactivation.order.add_edge(manual_app_react, activate_react)

    # =========================================================================
    # ROUTING XOR & TERMINAL NOTIFICATION / CLOSURE
    # =========================================================================
    core_routing = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            branch_standard,
            branch_prescreen_reject,
            branch_auto,
            branch_emergency,
            branch_reactivation,
        ],
    )

    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

    M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[entry_xor, core_routing, notify, close])
    M1.order.add_edge(entry_xor, core_routing)
    M1.order.add_edge(core_routing, notify)
    M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

    return M1


# Instantiate and verify M1 construction
M1_model = build_revised_onboarding_powl()
print(f"Successfully constructed M1 POWL model with {len(M1_model.nodes)} top-level nodes.")
```

### Event and Data-Collection Schema Proposal

To eliminate indeterminate audits (SUP-706), duplicate events (SUP-711), label drift (SUP-713), alias self-approval (SUP-712), and undisclosed CoI (SUP-715), the onboarding event log and case attribute tables must enforce the following schema:

#### 1. Immutable Event Log Schema (`onboarding_event_log`)
- `event_uuid` (UUID, PK): Globally unique identifier generated by the message broker.
- `source_tx_id` (String, Unique Index per `case_id` + `canonical_activity`): Source system transaction ID; prevents migration duplication (fixes **SUP-711**).
- `case_id` (String, FK): Supplier onboarding case identifier (`SUP-701`, etc.).
- `raw_ui_label` (String): Exact label seen by the user (e.g., `QA_Clearance`).
- `canonical_activity` (String, Not Null): Mapped POWL transition label via versioned ontology table (e.g., `Quality_Review`, fixing **SUP-713**).
- `effective_timestamp_utc` (Timestamp, Immutable): Exact moment the business action occurred in the source system (synchronized via NTP to prevent clock-skew disputes in **SUP-708**).
- `recorded_timestamp_utc` (Timestamp, Immutable): Exact moment the event was persisted to the audit log (detects post-hoc backdating as in **SUP-706**).
- `actor_system_alias` (String): System login ID used (`jdoe_proc`, `john.doe`).
- `actor_person_id` (String, FK to IAM Master, Not Null): Canonical, immutable human or service-principal identifier resolved at event ingestion (fixes **SUP-712**).
- `delegation_evidence_id` (String, Nullable FK): Reference to formal power-of-attorney/delegation record if acting on behalf of another role.
- `notification_id` (UUID, Nullable, Immutable FK): Cryptographically verifiable CRM message UUID written synchronously via transactional outbox pattern upon notification dispatch (fixes **SUP-702** and **SUP-706**).

#### 2. Immutable Case Governance & Eligibility Snapshot (`case_governance_snapshot`)
Captured as an immutable JSON/relational snapshot at the moment of route selection (`Auto_Approve`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, or `Activate_Supplier`):
- `case_id` (String, PK)
- `lifecycle_type` (Enum: `NEW_ONBOARDING`, `REACTIVATION`, `PRE_SCREEN_LEAD`)
- `governed_by_policy_id` (String, FK to `policy_overlay_registry.policy_id`)
- `policy_version` (String)
- `authority_waiver_id` (String, Nullable): Required if any exception route is invoked.
- `eligibility_snapshot_json` (JSONB, Immutable): Frozen attributes at decision time (`{"risk_tier": "LOW", "country": "DE", "parent_group_id": "GRP-104", "qa_cert_expiry": "2027-01-15", "initial_po_amount_eur": 18000.00}`). Prevents operators from selecting a pilot route when attributes are missing or ineligible (prevents **SUP-703**, **SUP-705**, **SUP-714**).
- `sod_verification_hash` (String): System-generated proof that `requester_person_id != manual_approver_person_id`.
- `coi_check_status` (Enum: `NO_RELATIONSHIP`, `DISCLOSED_AND_MITIGATED`, `BLOCKED_UNMITIGATED`): Populated by querying HR reporting-line and familial/spousal registry across all distinct `actor_person_id` values participating in `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` (fixes **SUP-715**).
