## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Observations that would weaken the topranked explanation (batch synchronization as primary bottleneck)

1. **J1 and J2 start independently** when removed from B1, with no increase in their total flow time (i.e., batch sync is not a gating requirement).  
2. **OVEN1 utilization during B1 period exceeds 80%**, contradicting the reported 44% and suggesting capacity, not synchronization, drives waits.  
3. **RACK7 cleaning/quarantine duration is systematically <30 min** across all batches, eliminating the observed 1h35m gap as a major factor.  
4. **J2’s supervisor override at 10:20 allows immediate B1 entry**, yet J2 still waits until 12:05; if this override is repeated and J2 proceeds early, the synchronization hypothesis is falsified.  
5. **Historical data shows batch Cure events consistently trigger 15 min after the latestready job**, implying the 3h40m–3h55m waits are abnormal and likely policy or tooldriven.  
6. **J3’s 10min wait in B1 is the only job that starts near its readiness time**, while J1 and J2 wait hours; if J3’s componentlot status is altered to match J1/J2, the batch start pattern changes, indicating the wait is not inherent to the batch mechanism.

### Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses

1. `batch_initiation_trigger` – categorical field indicating whether a batch starts via “timebased”, “componentlot availability”, or “jobreadiness” logic; discriminates synchronization source.  
2. `tool_release_intent` – explicit reason for tool release (e.g., “job_complete”, “timeout”, “supervisor_order”); clarifies whether FIXA/CAL3 holds are policy or operationdriven.  
3. `shipment_dispatch_reason` – codified value (e.g., “partner_wait”, “threshold_met”, “schedule”) for each dispatch event; tests if consolidation delay is partnerdriven or policydriven.  
4. `component_lot_status_change_timestamp` with granularity (quarantine_start, quarantine_end, release_start, release_end) – replaces the current binary lotevent linking and enables precise calculation of lotinduced job waits.  
5. `machine_event_type` beyond setup/process/release (e.g., “blocked_waiting_tool”, “blocked_waiting_lot”, “idle”) – captures why a machine is idle while jobs wait, directly addressing the utilizationvswait paradox.  
6. `job_priority` or `due_date` field – reveals whether delays are prioritydriven or evenly distributed, helping separate capacity bottlenecks from scheduling artifacts.  
7. `supervisor_override_event` with timestamp, reason, and job ID – distinguishes manual interventions from automatic batch/lot behavior; crucial for evaluating the “J2 override” evidence.  
8. `calibration_status_timestamp` on CAL3 (e.g., “valid_until”: 20240515 16:30) – provides a clear boundary for CAL3 availability, replacing the implicit “recalibrated after each use” assumption and enabling exact overlap calculations with job test readiness.

These observations and fields would allow a rigorous test of whether batch synchronization, tool contention, lot coupling, shipment policy, or machine capacity is the dominant driver of the reported unpredictable waiting and cascading delays.
