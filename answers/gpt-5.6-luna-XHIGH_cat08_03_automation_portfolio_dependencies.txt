## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

The process should be treated as two partially concurrent preparation branches that converge at committee packaging:

```
Receive -> Register -> Classify
                         |\
                         | \-- KYC -> [entity mismatch -> KYC] --\
                         |                                  |
                         \---- Financial review -> [missing docs -> review]
                                      -> Normalize -> Credit analysis --\
                                                                       |
                 [Collateral required] -> Valuation request -> External wait --\
                                                                                v
                                      Committee packaging -> Decision -> Contract -> Signing -> Activation
```

The information system records KYC and Financial document review sequentially in places, but they are often concurrent in reality. Automation should therefore use event timestamps and readiness states rather than assume that one branch always waits for the other. A case is ready for Committee packaging only when the required KYC state, normalized statements, credit notes, and collateral result are all available.

- A1 is upstream of A2, KYC, and document review. It should improve record quality and duplicate detection, but releasing applications faster without capacity or classification safeguards simply moves waiting downstream. A2 must be a recommender with confirmation until its routing accuracy is established by product, exposure class, and variant.
- A5 is a prerequisite for a reliable A6. It improves the input to Financial document review and reduces the `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review` loop. A6 then provides a better input to Credit analysis and Committee packaging. A8 should not be made authoritative until these artifacts have completeness and reconciliation checks.
- A3 sits in front of or within KYC and must end in either a documented clearance path or a human escalation. A4 operates after a KYC issue is identified and may loop back through `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check`; it must not silently convert a mismatch into a clearance. A3 and A4 therefore depend on the same entity data, evidence retrieval, policy versioning, and escalation capacity.
- A4 has a particularly important process dependency: the three compliance specialists are a scarce pool, and two senior specialists receive much of the handover traffic. Automation may reduce preparation time while increasing the number of cases requiring senior judgment. The rollout needs queue limits, service-level alerts, and a staffing or rota plan before broadening coverage.
- A7 depends on identifying the Equipment finance and other collateral-required variants early enough to route the request. It does not eliminate the external valuation dependency. Where policy and available evidence permit, routing can begin as soon as collateral need is known, or valuation can run in parallel with permitted credit work. Otherwise, the 58-hour external wait remains a synchronization point. Partner capacity and turnaround must be monitored as an external dependency, not hidden as an internal automation saving.
- A8 depends on the join of all preceding artifacts: KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation where required, and credit-analysis notes. It also depends on role-based assignment so that the analyst who completed Credit analysis is not the same person completing Committee packaging for High-exposure SME cases.
- A9 depends on a valid Committee decision and clean KYC status. Its legal rule library must be version-controlled, owned by Legal, and tested against the decision record. It should block generation or release when terms do not match the decision, rather than merely flagging a discrepancy after signing.
- A10 depends on A9's contract readiness and a hard KYC clearance gate. E-signature adoption cannot be used to justify provisional signing. A11 is downstream of every control state and should be the final automation considered; it should consume explicit control attestations rather than infer that an earlier step was completed.
- A12 is cross-cutting. It supplies the exception ownership, evidence, and remediation state needed by A3, A4, A9, A10, and eventually A11. It is particularly useful where policies are ambiguous, because it exposes the ambiguity for policy resolution instead of burying it in an automated rule.

There are two classes of control prerequisites. Legal sequence controls are hard gates: KYC clearance must precede Customer signing, and contract terms must match the Committee decision. Segregation controls are role constraints: in High-exposure SME cases, Credit analysis and Committee packaging must be performed by different people. Automation must enforce both in the workflow and record the model suggestion, input data, human override, actor, timestamp, rule version, and final outcome.

The first six months also impose an architectural dependency. Only two modules should receive deep integration with core banking. A sensible allocation is to use those integrations for the KYC/entity case state needed by A4 and the controlled contract/decision interface needed by A9. A5 can use the existing customer portal and A6 can initially use a document/analyst workbench with controlled import and export. This avoids spending the integration budget on a high-volume intake bot before the critical-path controls and artifacts are stable.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A5, A9, A10

This portfolio optimizes visible throughput, high-feasibility delivery, customer completion, and political momentum. A1 gives a near-universal labor saving; A5 reduces missing-document loops; A9 shortens contract preparation while checking terms; and A10 addresses the 44-hour signing wait. It sacrifices some upstream bottleneck depth: classification, KYC mismatch, financial normalization, credit analysis, and external valuation may still dominate end-to-end time. It is only a safe quick win if A9 and A10 enforce KYC, decision-to-contract, identity, and consent gates. Otherwise it could make the process faster at creating control exceptions.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7

This portfolio optimizes end-to-end cycle time and readiness synchronization. It targets the 31-hour entity-mismatch wait and rework, repeated missing-document loops, the normalization prerequisite before credit, and the 58-hour external valuation wait. It sacrifices direct savings at the highest-frequency activity and leaves contract generation, signing, and activation largely manual. It also creates a predictable resource risk: clean documents may increase demand on the nine credit analysts, while difficult KYC cases still require the three compliance specialists. The portfolio therefore needs capacity management as part of the implementation, not as an afterthought.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

This portfolio optimizes control integrity, traceability, and structured exception resolution. It addresses KYC triage and entity mismatches, decision-to-contract correctness, and the remediation evidence needed to handle exceptions. It sacrifices the biggest customer-facing cycle-time opportunities: missing-document response, the external valuation wait, and the 44-hour signing wait remain largely unchanged. It may increase measured waiting initially because conservative models escalate uncertain cases and the workbench makes previously hidden control work visible. That is an acceptable trade if control violations fall and the new work is explicitly staffed.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### Quick-win portfolio

- **Cycle time and waiting:** A1 removes some Register application service time, A5 reduces customer-response delay and the missing-document loop, A9 reduces Contract preparation time, and A10 can reduce the 44-hour Customer signing wait. The end-to-end gain will be less than the sum of those local gains if the work arrives in bursts at Classify product and exposure, KYC, Financial document review, Legal ops, or Loan activation. The 58-hour external valuation wait and 28-hour Credit analysis wait remain potential dominant paths.
- **Rework:** A5 should reduce `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review`; A9 should reduce `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation`. A1 can add rework if duplicate detection suppresses a genuine application or if faster intake exposes classification errors. A10 can create consent or identity exceptions unless it has a hard gate.
- **Handovers:** Back-office registration work falls, but exceptions move to classification, compliance, document review, legal ops, and customer support. RMs must provide better instructions and may receive more portal-related exception cases. Legal ops may see more simultaneous contract exceptions even as average preparation work falls.
- **Resource demand:** The 12-person back office may have spare capacity after 15:00, so A1 is unlikely to need a large redeployment benefit. Demand can increase in compliance and credit through faster clean inflow, and signing/activation support may become more bursty. Month-end legal capacity remains a constraint.
- **Control violations:** A9 can reduce term mismatches and A10 can enforce the KYC-before-signing gate, but neither is safe merely because it is automated. The portfolio does not inherently solve entity mismatches or High-exposure four-eyes segregation. The control metrics must be release gates, not optional reporting.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

- **Cycle time and waiting:** A4 attacks the 31-hour mismatch wait; A5 reduces the response delay and repeated loop; A6 reduces the 20-hour normalization wait and 75-minute service activity; and A7 can reduce variability around the 58-hour external wait through earlier triage or parallel routing. This directly targets the joins that make a case unready for Committee packaging. A7 cannot guarantee a shorter external turnaround, so its benefit is conditional on valuer capacity.
- **Rework:** Expected reductions are concentrated in `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check`, `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review`, and poor inputs to Normalize financial statements. A7 may reduce reassignment and late valuation requests, but it cannot remove valuation-quality disputes.
- **Handovers:** KYC exceptions become structured evidence packages for specialists; document exceptions move earlier to customers and RMs; statement exceptions move to analysts for validation; valuation cases move to a routing/partner-management queue. The handover network may become more visible and more concentrated around the two senior compliance specialists.
- **Resource demand:** Initial demand rises for compliance review of assistant suggestions and analyst validation of extracted statements. As clean files arrive sooner, Credit analysis can become the new queue, exactly as the resource observations warn. External valuers receive more timely or better-triaged requests, but their capacity remains outside the bank's direct control. Staffing and WIP limits should be adjusted before scaling.
- **Control violations:** The portfolio improves evidence quality and KYC exception handling but does not itself enforce contract-to-decision matching, signing gates, or four-eyes separation. Those controls must remain in the existing workflow and must be tested before any downstream acceleration.

### Compliance-first portfolio

- **Cycle time and waiting:** A3's conservative escalation and A4's specialist review may initially add KYC queue time. A9 and A12 can make contract remediation more systematic, but the 44-hour signing wait, missing-document loops, and external valuation wait remain. The correct success measure is a safer and more predictable process, not an assumed immediate reduction in median cycle time.
- **Rework:** The KYC mismatch loop should become shorter or better resolved, and `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` should decline as mismatches are detected before release. A12 should classify recurring issues so that policy or data-quality fixes address causes rather than repeatedly processing symptoms. Financial-document and valuation rework is largely unaffected.
- **Handovers:** More cases are intentionally handed to humans when confidence is low. The aim is to make those handovers explicit, owned, and auditable rather than eliminate them. Senior compliance and legal ops will see more structured cases; unresolved policy questions will be routed to policy owners.
- **Resource demand:** The three compliance specialists may be the limiting resource, and senior-specialist dependency must be measured. Legal ops may initially handle more controlled remediation. A12 creates visible exception work, while some routine KYC and contract review work falls. No FTE reduction should be assumed until exception volumes and quality are stable.
- **Control violations:** This is the strongest portfolio for reducing provisional signing, decision-to-contract mismatches, missing remediation evidence, and four-eyes violations, provided the workflow enforces the rules. A3 must optimize for safe escalation rather than raw auto-clearance. A8 and any future packaging automation must preserve independent actors for Credit analysis and Committee packaging in High-exposure SME cases.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Untraceable automated decisions:** Every automated action should create an immutable case event containing the input snapshot or references, model and rule version, confidence, recommendation, human override and reason, final decision, actor, and timestamp. The event log must retain the distinction between a model suggestion and a human-approved outcome.
- **False negatives and false positives:** For A3, a false-negative KYC hit is more serious than a false positive, so low-confidence or unusual cases must escalate and high-exposure cases should have conservative treatment. A2 must not silently choose a wrong control path. A6 should abstain on poor-quality PDFs or out-of-distribution statements and require analyst validation. A4 should never auto-resolve a mismatch without approved evidence.
- **Model and rule drift:** Measure precision, recall, abstention, override, and error rates separately for Micro, SME, and High-exposure SME applicants and for each product. Monitor changes in document formats, fraud patterns, legal policy, and external-valuer behavior. Revalidate models and rules on a schedule and whenever override or exception rates move materially; retain the prior version for reproducibility.
- **Data-quality propagation:** Validate mandatory fields, duplicate candidates, entity identifiers, document completeness, statement balancing, and reconciliation between the decision record and contract. A downstream bot should receive a positive readiness state, not infer readiness from the mere presence of an earlier event.
- **Exception overload and silent failure:** Create explicit queues with reason codes, ownership, age and service-level monitoring, retry limits, and a manual fallback. Reserve capacity for the three compliance specialists and nine credit analysts. Failed extraction, unresolved mismatch, stale valuation, and policy ambiguity should stop or divert the case rather than loop invisibly.
- **Changed workload and handover concentration:** Track queue age, WIP, utilization, overtime, and handover counts by resource pool. Use back-office capacity after 15:00 for exception preparation or quality checks, but do not assume that registration savings transfer automatically to compliance or credit. Limit concurrent escalations and rebalance work away from the two senior compliance specialists where policy allows.
- **KYC-before-signing breach:** Enforce a system-level hard stop so Customer signing cannot be initiated until a current KYC clearance is present. Do not support provisional signing through a bypass flag. Reconcile signing events against KYC events and alert on any attempted or completed out-of-order sequence.
- **Four-eyes failure:** Use role-based assignment and conflict checks so the person completing Credit analysis cannot complete Committee packaging for High-exposure SME cases. The system should block completion, record the attempted conflict, and require an independent reviewer. This applies to assisted and manually completed work alike.
- **Contract mismatch and stale legal rules:** Legal owns a versioned rule library and approves changes. A9 must compare key terms with the Committee decision, block release on mismatch, and route exceptions to Legal ops. Run parallel shadow comparisons before changing production templates.
- **Identity, consent, and customer friction:** A10, if later deployed, must verify customer identity, consent, signing authority, and the correct contract version. Reminders should be configurable and stop after completion or withdrawal. Keep a human channel and an accessible alternative for customers who cannot use the portal.
- **External valuation dependency:** A7 should record request, assignment, response, lateness, quality dispute, and fallback status. Establish partner SLAs and an approved escalation or alternative-valuer path; do not represent routing as elimination of external risk.
- **Irreversible downstream action:** Do not deploy A11 until KYC, decision, contract, signing, and control attestations are machine-readable and reconciled. Activation should be idempotent, reversible where operationally possible, and blocked on any missing or contradictory control state.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Phase 0: baseline and control design (weeks 0-8)

Instrument the event log so that concurrent KYC and Financial document review are represented as overlapping work, not forced into a false sequence. Establish baselines by applicant segment, product, collateral requirement, and High-exposure status for end-to-end cycle time, each waiting component, loop counts, first-pass completeness, handovers, and resource queue age.

Select A5, A4, A6, and A9 as the controlled first-year portfolio. Define the manual fallback, confidence thresholds, exception reason codes, ownership, and audit record before enabling automation. Freeze the legal definitions of KYC clearance, contract readiness, and four-eyes independence. Explicitly exclude autonomous clearance for uncertain KYC/entity cases and autonomous activation.

### Phase 1: improve upstream completeness and prove the control path (months 2-4)

Deploy A5 first in a limited Micro and standard SME scope, using product-specific checklists and nudges that allow customers and RMs to explain or substitute documents. Measure completion quality, customer drop-off, request-loop frequency, and the resulting load on document review and normalization.

In parallel, build A4 as a read-only or recommendation assistant for selected entity types. It should assemble evidence and route cases to a compliance specialist. Use shadow evaluation against specialist outcomes; do not let it change KYC status automatically. The KYC case-state integration is the first deep core-banking/workflow integration.

Gate criteria for expansion include no material deterioration in document correctness, no unowned exception queue, stable or improving KYC control conformance, complete audit records, and demonstrable capacity for specialist escalations.

### Phase 2: normalize analyst inputs and establish contract safeguards (months 4-6)

Pilot A6 in an analyst workbench using existing document interfaces. Require analyst acceptance or correction before normalized statements enter Credit analysis. Segment performance by PDF quality, product, and applicant type; route low-confidence cases to manual review. Watch whether the faster clean inflow increases the Credit analysis queue or causes four-eyes pressure at month end.

Build A9 in shadow mode against existing contracts and decisions. Legal approves the versioned rule library and templates. When mismatch detection is reliable, enable controlled generation for low-complexity cases while retaining legal exception review. The decision-to-contract/core contract integration is the second deep integration in the first six months. A9 must carry KYC status and role/control attestations forward, not merely populate fields.

Do not add a third deep core integration in this period. A5 uses the existing portal capability and A6 remains a controlled analyst workbench until the integration limit is lifted.

### Phase 3: controlled scale and workload balancing (months 7-9)

Expand A5 and A6 by product and document type only after their gates pass. Expand A4 to additional entity-mismatch patterns with specialist-approved evidence rules. Move A9 from shadow or limited production to broader use only for rule versions that Legal has approved.

Use weekly portfolio reviews to rebalance work. If compliance queue age or senior-specialist handovers rise, reduce A4 coverage or add a rota. If analyst utilization and Credit analysis waiting rise after A6, throttle release, increase validation capacity, or schedule work rather than allowing automation to create a new hidden bottleneck. Protect High-exposure SME cases and Credit-line renewals with stricter review until their error and remediation behavior is known.

### Phase 4: outcome review and next-portfolio decision (months 10-12)

Compare treatment and control groups or shadow baselines by variant. Review median and tail cycle time, waiting at entity mismatch, missing-document loops, normalization readiness, contract-remediation loops, queue age, handover concentration, customer completion, and month-end behavior. The control review must include KYC-before-signing conformance, four-eyes violations, contract-decision mismatches, model overrides, and audit completeness.

A module passes the year-end gate only if it improves its intended process measure without increasing severe control exceptions, leaves a bounded and staffed exception queue, and preserves reproducible evidence. After the review, assess A7 for the next release, especially if Equipment finance cases still show the 58-hour external synchronization delay. A10 may follow once the KYC and contract gates are demonstrably enforced. A1 can then be piloted with capacity controls; A11 remains last.

## 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the bottleneck-aware, control-gated portfolio of A5, A6, A4, and A9.

This portfolio does not select the highest-frequency activity first. Register application occurs in 98% of cases and A1 has high direct labor-saving potential, but its 8-minute service time and 1.5-hour wait are not the dominant end-to-end constraints. The back office is already below 65% utilization after 15:00. Making registration faster would mainly deliver cleaner, earlier work to Classify product and exposure, KYC, Financial document review, and the already central Credit analysts. It could therefore move the queue without materially improving completion time.

A5 improves the input to the process and directly attacks the repeated missing-document loop. A6 improves the artifact required before Credit analysis while making analyst workload explicit and governable. A4 targets only 1,750 cases, but those cases wait 31 hours, rework at 38%, and are on the critical path in 82% of occurrences; its value is synchronization and risk reduction, not volume. A9 supplies the required control-strengthening module and addresses both contract delay and the 8.5% contract-remediation problem, including the higher-risk Credit-line renewal variant.

The portfolio leaves the 58-hour external valuation wait and 44-hour signing wait as important next targets. That is intentional: A7 should be pursued when partner data and routing capacity are ready, and A10 should follow only after the KYC-before-signing gate and contract controls are proven. A1 can be a later, bounded quick win after downstream capacity and classification quality are measured. A11 should not be used to disguise unresolved upstream control risk.

The governing trade-off is to optimize ready-to-decide, compliant cases rather than maximize automated task count. The recommendation should be considered successful only when cycle time and rework improve while KYC ordering, contract consistency, four-eyes independence, auditability, and exception ownership remain intact.
