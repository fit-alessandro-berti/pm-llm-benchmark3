## **1. What is Observed**
The data reveal a staggered rollout of an automated triage system across five branches, with key distinctions between **availability** (`automation=1`), **actual use** (`automated_fast_path_pct`), and **operational context**:

- **Availability** is binary per branch-month, but adoption timing varies (Alpha/Beta: April; Gamma: July; Delta/Epsilon: none by August).
- **Actual use** (`automated_fast_path_pct`) ranges from 4% (pre-automation) to 48% (post), indicating heterogeneous implementation fidelity. For example, Alpha’s `automated_fast_path_pct` jumps from 4% to 46% in July, while Gamma’s rises more modestly to 32%.
- **Branch selection** was non-random: Alpha/Beta were targeted due to poor historical cycle times, while Gamma was pilot-tested. Delta/Epsilon remained untreated, providing partial controls but with potential confounding (e.g., Epsilon’s high `high_complexity_pct`).
- **Contemporaneous changes** include:
  - **Routing shifts**: Alpha/Beta saw ~10% more arrivals post-April; Gamma ~5% post-July.
  - **Staffing**: Alpha lost 2 FTEs in May; Beta gained 1 in April; Gamma lost 1 in June.
  - **Case-mix**: A May product change increased `high_complexity_pct` across branches (e.g., Gamma’s rose from 22% to 27%).
  - **Fraud desk**: Centralized fraud handling began in March, with automated triage routing borderline cases to it (unmeasured spillover).
- **Outcome measurement limitations**:
  - `csat_1to5` has low response rates (20–45%) and no branch-month counts, risking selection bias.
  - `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` aggregate heterogeneous paths (e.g., fast vs. escalated), obscuring substitution effects.
  - `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` may reflect routing changes (e.g., fraud desk) or learning curves.

## **3. Identification Strategy**

### **Primary: Staggered-Adoption Event Study with Branch-Fixed Effects**
| strategy                          | treatment timing representation | comparison group               | adjustment variables                          | estimand targeted          | identifying assumptions                          | diagnostic checks                          | main failure mode                     |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Staggered Event Study**         | Relative-time indicators (`t=0` for Alpha/Beta, `t=3` for Gamma) | Delta/Epsilon (never-treated) | Branch FE, month FE, `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | ITT (Alpha/Beta/Gamma) | Parallel trends pre-adoption; no spillover from fraud desk | Pre-trend tests; placebo branches (Delta/Epsilon) | Nonparallel trends; unmeasured confounders |

### **Alternatives**:
1. **Synthetic Control for Gamma**
   - *Comparison*: Weighted average of Delta/Epsilon (never-treated).
   - *Adjustments*: `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`.
   - *Target*: ITT for Gamma.
   - *Assumption*: Delta/Epsilon are good proxies for Gamma’s counterfactual.
   - *Failure Mode*: Poor fit if Delta/Epsilon differ systematically.

2. **Difference-in-Differences with Matched Controls**
   - *Comparison*: Match Delta/Epsilon to Alpha/Beta on pre-period `median_cycle_h` and `high_complexity_pct`.
   - *Adjustments*: Matching weights + branch FE.
   - *Target*: ITT for matched adopters.
   - *Failure Mode*: Small sample size (only 5 branches).

**Why Primary?**
- **Staggered design** leverages temporal variation, reducing selection bias.
- **Branch FE** captures unobserved heterogeneity (e.g., branch culture).
- **Delta/Epsilon** provide partial controls but require caution due to fraud desk spillovers.
- **Diagnostics** (pre-trends, placebo tests) can validate assumptions.

## **8. Rollout Recommendation**
**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

### **Implementation Plan**:
1. **Branch Subset**: **Expand to Gamma first** (already pilot-tested), then **Delta** (low complexity, high stability).
   - *Rationale*: Gamma provides learning evidence; Delta’s low `high_complexity_pct` may mitigate tail risks.

2. **Treatment Assignment**:
   - **Phase-in**: Deploy automation to Gamma in September, Delta in October.
   - **Intensity Target**: Require `automated_fast_path_pct  40%` within 3 months; escalate if below.

3. **Minimum Measurements**:
   - **Pre-period**: 3 months of `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`, `escalation_pct`, `rework_pct`, `csat_1to5`.
   - **Post-period**: Same + **case-level routing logs** (to track fraud desk spillover).

4. **Process-Mining Outcomes**:
   - **Queue states**: Track specialist desk wait times (fraud desk and branch-level).
   - **Path decomposition**: Separate fast/extended/escalated paths to isolate substitution effects.

5. **Spillover Monitoring**:
   - Compare Delta/Epsilon’s `escalation_pct` and `p90_cycle_h` trends post-Delta adoption.
   - Flag if Delta’s `p90` worsens by >5h relative to pre-period.

6. **Guardrails for Customer Harm**:
   - **Threshold**: Pause expansion if `p90_cycle_h` increases by >10% or `csat_1to5` drops by >0.2 points.
   - **Alerts**: Trigger if `rework_pct` exceeds 15% or `escalation_pct` >12%.

7. **Decision Rule for Scale-Up**:
   - **Scale to Epsilon** only if:
     - Gamma/Delta show **median cycle time improvement  2h** and **`p90` change < +5h**.
     - **CSAT remains stable** ( < 0.1).
     - **Rework/escalation rates are unchanged** ( < 1%).

- **Observed**: Automation’s effect on `median_cycle_h` varies by branch; Gamma shows small improvements, Beta shows stagnation.
