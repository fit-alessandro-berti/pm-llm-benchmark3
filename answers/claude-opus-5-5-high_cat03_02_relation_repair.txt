## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants (a repair violating these is rejected outright)**

- H1. Referential integrity. Every added edge connects existing events and objects. No new events or objects are invented.
- H2. O2O type signature and direction. `contains` goes Order→Item, `packs` goes Package→Item, `carries` goes Delivery→Package, `bills` goes Invoice→Order, and `settles` goes Payment→Invoice. Reverse-direction duplicates are never added.
- H3. `Record Payment` relates to exactly one Payment (rule 7 says "exactly").
- H4. An Item belongs to at most one Order. IT-B1 has `order_hint=ORD-B` and the observed edge `ORD-B contains IT-B1`, so no edge may place it in ORD-A.

**Strong defaults (can be overridden only by explicit contrary evidence)**

- S1. Cardinality defaults from rules 1–4 and 7. `Pack Item` has one Item and one Package. `Dispatch Package` has one Package and one Delivery. `Record Payment` has one Invoice.
- S2. Physical containment. An Item sits in one Package, and a Package travels in one Delivery.
- S3. Lifecycle order. An item is added before it is packed, a package is packed before it is dispatched, a delivery is dispatched before it is confirmed, and an invoice is issued before it is settled. Prepayment is possible but unusual.
- S4. Identifier conventions that the log itself validates. `scanner_ref=PK7-1` maps to PK-7 and `PK8-1` maps to PK-8. `external_ref=N-420` maps to `DEL-X.carrier_ref`. A convention confirmed by observed rows is much stronger than a guessed prefix.

**Weak heuristics (evidence, never proof)**

- W1. Amount arithmetic: 130 + 100 = 230, and payment amounts that mirror order values.
- W2. Timestamp proximity: ev07 and ev08 are one minute apart, and ev09 and ev10 are identical.
- W3. Shared customer. Everything is C17, so this cannot tell candidates apart.
- W4. Shared route. Everything is NORTH, so this also cannot tell candidates apart.
- W5. Gross weight. No item weights are given, so this is uninformative.

**Evidence against a repair**

- X1. Explicit uncertainty markers in the source (`N-421?`, `N-42*`).
- X2. The candidate's slot is already filled. For example, PK-7 already has a packing event and a dispatch event.
- X3. A cardinality or lifecycle violation would be created.
- X4. The only support for the edge is another inferred edge (derivation, not corroboration).
- X5. Observed records that deliberately omit an object may reflect the source system's intent. For example, ev12 lists only ORD-A.

**How the evidence is combined.** I reason roughly additively in log-odds terms. The prior comes from cardinality: an empty slot with exactly one type-compatible candidate raises it. An identifier match validated in the log (S4) adds strongly, and uncertainty markers (X1) subtract. Customer and route add nothing, because all objects share them. Time and amounts add modestly. Lifecycle order acts as a filter: a violation is near-veto, and consistency is only weakly positive.

A derived O2O edge, such as `PK-8 packs IT-B1` following from `ev08→PK-8`, inherits the E2O confidence and never exceeds it; the pair is evaluated jointly. A bundle hanging together coherently is not independent evidence, because I built it to be coherent.

Status thresholds:

- accept: ≥0.90, or retaining an observed record.
- provisional: 0.65–0.90.
- unresolved: 0.25–0.65.
- reject: <0.25, or violates a hard invariant.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative, minimum change.** Adds P01–P04 only; everything else stays as observed.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** ev08→PK-8 and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` are added as provisional. PK-8 becomes a consolidated package holding IT-A2 (ORD-A) and IT-B1 (ORD-B). That is legitimate for the same customer and route, but it is flagged.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** ev10→DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` are added. Two parallel dispatches at 10:00 is a plausible practice, not an error.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** No delivery edge is added. ev11 stays an order-level confirmation (the rule 5 lost-identifier case), and `N-42*` is recorded as ambiguous.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Unresolved. The 230 total is kept as an unexplained attribute.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Unresolved. PAY-2 is treated as potentially unapplied cash.

Lifecycles under A: the physical flow (Item→Package→Delivery) is complete for all three items. ORD-B ends after Add Item, both deliveries end at Dispatch, INV-44 is partially settled (100 of 230), and PAY-2 is isolated. These incomplete lifecycles are honest: they say "evidence missing", not "process violated".

**Bundle B: consolidated customer-level interpretation.** A plus P09–P15.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Same as A.
- **ev10:** Same as A (DEL-Y).
- **ev11:** Joint portal acknowledgment of DEL-X and DEL-Y by customer C17, extended to ORD-B. The wildcard `N-42*` is read as a truncated common prefix. Confirming ORD-A as delivered requires both deliveries, because ORD-A is split across PK-7/DEL-X and PK-8/DEL-Y. That is the best argument against assigning ev11 to DEL-X alone.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** INV-44 bills both orders (ev12→ORD-B, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`).
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** PAY-2 settles INV-44 as a second instalment, so 100 + 130 = 230.

Lifecycle coherence under B: every object completes a plausible, time-ordered lifecycle. Order: Create → Add → Confirm → Invoice. Delivery: Dispatch → Confirm. Invoice: Issue → Pay → Pay. That tidiness is exactly why it is suspect: it is the "complete every lifecycle" solution, and its links reinforce each other circularly (Section 5). A partial B is less coherent. For example, P12 without P11 gives ORD-B the trace Create → Add → Issue Invoice, which suggests invoicing without delivery confirmation.

**Bundle C: bulk dispatch under a single delivery.** P01–P02 plus P05–P06, and ev11→DEL-X uniquely.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** As in A.
- **ev10:** Read as a second row of a bulk dispatch under DEL-X, with `N-421?` as a mis-keyed N-420. DEL-X carries PK-7 and PK-8.
- **ev11:** DEL-X is the only delivery with events, so `N-42*` resolves to it uniquely.
- **DEL-Y:** A planned or cancelled booking with no events (an orphan object, which is allowed).
- **INV-44 and PAY-2:** Handled as in A (unresolved).

Lifecycle: DEL-X gets Dispatch, Dispatch, Confirm, with the two dispatches tied in time. C is physically coherent but contradicts the only explicit identifier. I rate it around 0.15 overall; it shows how the ev10 decision propagates to ev11.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit (Bundle A, with B contrasts)

**Order.** ORD-A: Create → Add → Add → Confirm → Invoice, which is plausible. ORD-B: Create → Add, truncated but not an orphan. ORD-A is split across PK-7 and PK-8, a legitimate split shipment. Order–Invoice is 1:1 in A and 2:1 in B. `contains` and `bills ORD-A` each have both E2O and O2O support.

**Item.** Every item follows Add → Pack, in exactly one order and one package. `PK-8 packs IT-B1` has no independent support: it exists only because of the ev08 repair.

**Package.** PK-7: Pack → Dispatch. PK-8: Pack → Pack → Dispatch, plausible for a multi-item package. PK-8 now links to two orders, making Package–Order many-to-many. That is suspicious but legitimate for consolidation, and it is the most consequential structural change downstream.

**Delivery.** DEL-X and DEL-Y each have only Dispatch and never close. In B, both end with Confirm, but through one shared event. In C, DEL-Y is an orphan object. Before repair, DEL-Y is an orphan object and ev10 is under-related, breaking rule 4.

**Invoice.** INV-44: Issue → Record Payment, partially paid. In B, Invoice–Payment is 1:2 (plausible instalments) and Invoice–Order is 1:2.

**Payment.** PAY-1's invoice link has both E2O and O2O support. PAY-2 has no invoice: potentially unapplied cash, but not an orphan event, since ev14 relates to PAY-2 as H3 requires.

**Circular reasoning to avoid:**

1. Inferring ev08→PK-8, deriving `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, then citing that O2O edge as a second confirmation of ev08→PK-8.
2. Chaining ev10→DEL-Y, `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` into "DEL-Y delivered ORD-B", using that to justify ev11→DEL-Y and ev11→ORD-B, and then using ev11→ORD-B to justify billing ORD-B.
3. Using 230 = 130 + 100 for both `INV-44 bills ORD-B` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, which counts one coincidence twice. "INV-44 covers both orders, so the second payment belongs to it" is circular.
4. Treating observed E2O and O2O pairs as independent sources. They may come from one extraction job, so agreement shows internal consistency, not truth.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.**

- *Unrepaired:* Package has Pack→Dispatch twice. Delivery has a lone Dispatch for DEL-X, and DEL-Y is absent. Order has Add Item as an end activity (ORD-B). Invoice has Issue→Record Payment once.
- *Bundle A:* A new Package self-loop Pack Item→Pack Item (PK-8). Delivery gets two lone Dispatch starts/ends. The Order graph is unchanged.
- *Bundle B:* Adds Delivery Dispatch→Confirm Delivery (twice). Order Add Item→Confirm Delivery rises from 1 to 2, and the Add Item end activity disappears. Invoice gains a Record Payment→Record Payment self-loop.
- *Bundle C:* Adds a Delivery self-loop Dispatch→Dispatch whose direction depends on how the 10:00 tie is ordered.

**OTG.**

- Unrepaired interactions: Order–Item, Item–Package, Package–Delivery, Invoice–Order, Payment–Invoice. There is no Order–Delivery link, because ev11 has only an Order.
- Bundle A adds no new type-level edge; only instance counts change. This is a useful sanity check that A is conservative.
- Bundle B adds Order–Delivery and Delivery–Delivery interactions (both from ev11).
- Co-death relations shift. Unrepaired, INV-44 and PAY-1 both end at ev13, giving Invoice–Payment co-death. In B, INV-44 and PAY-2 end together at ev14 instead, and both deliveries co-die at ev11.

**ET–OT graph.**

- A creates no new activity–type edges. Pack Item–Package goes from 2 to 3 relations, and Dispatch Package–Delivery from 1 to 2.
- B adds a new edge Confirm Delivery–Delivery. It also raises Confirm Delivery–Order from 1 to 2, Issue Invoice–Order from 1 to 2, and Record Payment–Invoice from 1 to 2.

**Flattenings.**

| Type | Unrepaired | Bundle A | Bundle B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order | ORD-A ⟨Create, Add, Add, Confirm, Invoice⟩; ORD-B ⟨Create, Add⟩ | unchanged | ORD-B ⟨Create, Add, Confirm, Invoice⟩ |
| Package | PK-7 ⟨Pack, Dispatch⟩; PK-8 ⟨Pack, Dispatch⟩ | PK-8 ⟨Pack, Pack, Dispatch⟩ | as A |
| Delivery | DEL-X ⟨Dispatch⟩; DEL-Y dropped | DEL-X ⟨Dispatch⟩, DEL-Y ⟨Dispatch⟩ | both ⟨Dispatch, Confirm⟩, ev11 duplicated |
| Invoice | ⟨Issue, Pay⟩ | unchanged | ⟨Issue, Pay, Pay⟩ |
| Payment | PAY-1 ⟨Pay⟩, PAY-2 ⟨Pay⟩ | unchanged | unchanged |

The Payment flattening does not reflect the PAY-2 decision at all. That decision is visible only from the Invoice perspective. Under C, the Delivery flattening gives DEL-X ⟨Dispatch, Dispatch, Confirm⟩ and drops DEL-Y.

**Activity frequencies (events / unique objects / total objects).**

- Pack Item: 3 events in every bundle. The Package total rises from 2 to 3 in A, while unique Packages stay at 2.
- Dispatch Package: 2 events. The Delivery total goes from 1 to 2 (A/B, 2 unique) or to 2 with 1 unique (C).
- Confirm Delivery: 1 event. Its Delivery total is 0 (A), 2 (B), or 1 (C).
- Record Payment: 2 events. In B its Invoice total is 2 with 1 unique, so convergence doubles it in the Invoice flattening.
- Issue Invoice: 1 event, but counted once per Order in B, so the Order-flattened invoice count doubles.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, ORD-A's connected component is {ORD-A, IT-A1, IT-A2, PK-7, PK-8, DEL-X, INV-44, PAY-1}, while ORD-B's is only {ORD-B, IT-B1}, so the two land in very different clusters. The single edge ev08→PK-8 in Bundle A merges them into one component, and DEL-Y joins through ev10. In B, PAY-2 joins too, and the whole log becomes a single execution. Order-rooted extraction instead makes ORD-A and ORD-B look similar in B. They are still not isomorphic (ORD-A has two items and two packages), so exact-equivalence clustering separates them while similarity-based clustering would group them.

                                        "repair_conf", "repair_bundle", "provenance"])

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing a cross-order merge through PK-8.** Accepting ev08→PK-8 unflagged merges ORD-B into ORD-A's execution, so "orders are routinely consolidated" could be reported from a single scanner string.
2. **Bulk-dispatch fabrication.** Reading the shared 10:00 timestamp as bulk dispatch, or adding ev09→PK-8, creates Dispatch→Dispatch self-loops and double dispatches whose order depends only on tie-breaking.
3. **Joint confirmation from a wildcard.** Linking ev11 to both deliveries and ORD-B manufactures an Order–Delivery OTG interaction, and it duplicates one portal click across two delivery cases, inflating confirmation counts. It all rests on `N-42*`, which is evidence the identifier was lost.
4. **Arithmetic-driven billing and settlement.** Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` hides a possibly unbilled order and possibly unapplied cash, and it makes the invoice look fully settled.
5. **Amount-matching across the wrong types.** PAY-1 = 100 = ORD-B and PAY-2 = 130 = ORD-A. Linking payments to orders, or re-pointing PAY-1, overwrites observed data with a coincidence.
6. **Transitive shortcut edges.** Relating Pack or Dispatch events to Orders duplicates fulfilment activities into Order flattening, creating convergence artifacts.
7. **Lifecycle completion as a goal.** Giving DEL-Y a confirmation or ORD-B an invoice to avoid truncated traces turns missing evidence into observed behaviour, so conformance checking can no longer find the real gaps.
8. **Self-confirmation loops.** Counting derived O2O edges, or `ocel_o2o_enrichment` output, as corroboration inflates confidence in edges that came from one identifier guess.
