## 1. Equivalence design space

### 1.1 Activity-sequence equivalence
**Definition**: Two executions are equivalent if their sequences of activity labels (ignoring object identifiers and O2O qualifiers) are identical when normalized for order-insensitive same-label bursts.

**Operational definition**:
- Extract the sequence of activity names (e.g., `['Create Order', 'Add Item', 'Pack Package', ...]`).
- Collapse consecutive identical activities into a single occurrence (e.g., two `Add Item` events become one).
- Compare the resulting normalized sequences.

**Invariants preserved**:
- The *pattern* of activities (ignoring object identities and multiplicities).
- The *relative order* of distinct activity types.

**Information discarded**:
- Object identifiers and their types.
- O2O qualifiers (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch`).
- External object flags (`EXT-`).
- Multiplicities (e.g., number of items, packages, payments).
- Concurrency or timing information.

**Properties**:
- Reflexive: Yes (sequence trivially matches itself).
- Symmetric: Yes (equality is symmetric).
- Transitive: Yes (sequence equality is transitive).
- Concurrency: Handled by collapsing same-label bursts; concurrent same-label events are indistinguishable from sequential ones.

**Example**:
- X1 and X2 are equivalent under this relation (both have the sequence `['Create Order', 'Add Item', 'Pack Package', 'Dispatch', 'Issue Invoice', 'Record Payment']` after normalization).
- X7 is equivalent to X1/X2 because the replacement semantics are not visible at the activity-label level.

---

### 1.2 Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming
**Definition**: Two executions are equivalent if there exists a bijection between their object identifiers (of the same type) that preserves:
- The *types* of objects.
- The *incidence structure* (which objects participate in which events).
- The *multiplicities* of objects per type per event.
- The *O2O qualifiers* (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch`).
- The *order of events* (total order preserved).

**Formal definition**:
Let \( E_1 \) and \( E_2 \) be two executions. \( E_1 \sim E_2 \) if there exists a family of bijections \( \phi_T \) (indexed by object type \( T \)) such that:
1. For every event \( e_1 \) in \( E_1 \) with activity \( a \), there is an event \( e_2 \) in \( E_2 \) with activity \( a \), and the mapping \( \phi \) preserves the incidence of objects of type \( T \) in \( e_1 \) and \( e_2 \).
2. The O2O qualifiers are preserved under \( \phi \).
3. The event order is preserved (i.e., if \( e_1 \) precedes \( e_2 \) in \( E_1 \), then \( \phi(e_1) \) precedes \( \phi(e_2) \) in \( E_2 \)).

**Invariants preserved**:
- The *topology* of object-event incidences.
- The *types* of objects and their roles in events.
- The *qualitative relationships* (e.g., "item packed in package").
- The *temporal order* of events.

**Information discarded**:
- The *specific identities* of objects (e.g., `I11` vs. `I22`).
- The *exact counts* of objects (e.g., number of items) unless they affect structure (e.g., two items packed separately vs. together).
- External object flags (`EXT-`) unless they are part of the structural relationship (e.g., shared packages).

**Properties**:
- Reflexive: Yes (identity mapping).
- Symmetric: Yes (inverse bijection).
- Transitive: Yes (composition of bijections).
- Concurrency: Handled by preserving the total order of events.

**Example**:
- X1 and X2 are equivalent under this relation (rename `O1O2`, `I11I22`, `I12I21`, etc.).
- X1 and X5 are *not* equivalent because X5 involves an external object (`EXT-OA`, `EXT-IA`) that is structurally integrated into the execution, altering the incidence relationships.

---

### 1.3 Business-semantic equivalence
**Definition**: Two executions are equivalent if they exhibit the same *business-relevant motifs*, defined by:
- The *multiplicities* of objects per type (e.g., single vs. split packages).
- The *sharing* of objects across orders (external objects).
- The *lifecycle motifs* (e.g., replacements, returns, partial payments).
- The *financial consolidation* (e.g., shared invoices).

**Operational definition**:
1. Extract the following features for each execution:
   - Object multiplicities: Counts of items, packages, deliveries, invoices, payments per order.
   - External object flags: Presence of `EXT-` objects.
   - O2O qualifiers: Presence of `removed_before_dispatch`, `partial_settles`, etc.
   - Financial consolidation: Whether invoices or payments are shared across orders.
   - Logistics topology: Single vs. split packages/deliveries.
2. Compare the feature vectors. Two executions are equivalent if their feature vectors match.

**Invariants preserved**:
- The *business semantics* of the execution (e.g., "split delivery" vs. "consolidated delivery").
- The *degree of sharing* (e.g., cross-order packages or invoices).
- The *presence of lifecycle anomalies* (e.g., replacements, returns).

**Information discarded**:
- The *exact object identifiers*.
- The *order of events* unless it affects semantics (e.g., replacement order is preserved as a qualifier).
- The *temporal spacing* of events.

**Properties**:
- Reflexive: Yes.
- Symmetric: Yes.
- Transitive: Yes (feature vector equality is transitive).
- Concurrency: Handled by treating same-label bursts as a single feature (e.g., two `Add Item` events  "multi-item order").

**Example**:
- X1, X2, and X5 are *not* equivalent under this relation because X5 involves cross-order package sharing, which is a distinct business motif.
- X3 and X4 are distinct because X3 has consolidated delivery while X4 has split deliveries.
- X7 is distinct from X1 because it involves replacement semantics (qualified by `removed_before_dispatch`).

---

### 1.4 Optional: Qualified behavioral equivalence
**Definition**: Two executions are equivalent if their *behavioral profiles* (in terms of object lifecycles and constraints) are identical, including:
- The *ordering constraints* between events (e.g., "item must be added before packing").
- The *qualifiers* attached to events (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch`).
- The *multiplicities* of objects per event.

**Formal definition**:
Two executions are equivalent if their *behavioral signatures* match, where the signature includes:
1. For each object type, the sequence of events it participates in (with qualifiers).
2. The partial order of events (e.g., "Pack Package must follow Add Item").
3. The multiplicities of objects per event.

**Invariants preserved**:
- The *lifecycle constraints* (e.g., "item cannot be dispatched before packing").
- The *qualitative relationships* (e.g., "item was replaced before dispatch").

**Information discarded**:
- The *exact object identifiers*.
- The *absolute timing* of events.

**Properties**:
- Reflexive, symmetric, transitive.
- Concurrency: Handled by preserving partial orders.

**Example**:
- X1 and X7 are *not* equivalent because X7 has a qualifier (`removed_before_dispatch`) that alters the behavioral profile.
- X4 and X8 are distinct because X8 involves a return and repack, which introduces additional lifecycle constraints.

## 2. Canonical signatures for business-semantic equivalence

We choose **business-semantic equivalence** as the preferred relation for clustering, as it captures meaningful business distinctions while being robust to identifier renaming and activity-label variations.

**Canonical signature scheme**:
```python
def canonical_signature(execution):
    # Extract features relevant to business semantics
    features = {
        "order_id": "O*",  # Placeholder; identifiers are normalized
        "num_items": len([e for e in execution if e.activity == "Add Item"]),
        "num_packages": len([e for e in execution if e.activity == "Pack Package"]),
        "num_deliveries": len([e for e in execution if e.activity == "Dispatch"]),
        "num_invoices": len([e for e in execution if e.activity == "Issue Invoice"]),
        "num_payments": len([e for e in execution if e.activity == "Record Payment"]),
        "has_split_packages": any("Package" in obj for e in execution for obj in e.objects if e.activity == "Pack Package" and len(e.objects["Package"]) > 1),
        "has_split_deliveries": any("Delivery" in obj for e in execution for obj in e.objects if e.activity == "Dispatch" and len(e.objects["Delivery"]) > 1),
        "has_external_objects": any("EXT-" in obj for e in execution for obj_type in e.objects for obj in e.objects[obj_type]),
        "has_partial_payment": any("partial_settles" in e.qualifiers for e in execution if e.activity == "Record Payment"),
        "has_replacement": any("removed_before_dispatch" in e.qualifiers for e in execution),
        "has_return": any(e.activity == "Return Item" for e in execution),
        "has_consolidated_delivery": any("Delivery" in obj for e in execution for obj in e.objects if e.activity == "Dispatch" and len(e.objects["Delivery"]) == 1 and len(e.objects.get("Package", [])) > 1),
        "financial_consolidation": "shared_invoice" if any("EXT-OB" in obj for e in execution for obj_type in e.objects for obj in e.objects[obj_type] if e.activity == "Issue Invoice") else "order_level"
    }
    return tuple(sorted(features.items()))  # Sort for reproducibility
```

**Notes**:
- Object identifiers are normalized (e.g., `O1`, `O2`  `O*`).
- External objects (`EXT-`) are flagged but not distinguished by identity.
- Qualifiers (e.g., `partial_settles`, `removed_before_dispatch`) are explicitly captured.
- Multiplicities (e.g., number of items) are preserved as counts.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### Cluster: Single-package, order-level (X1, X2)
**Signature**:
- `num_packages=1`, `num_deliveries=1`, no split packages/deliveries, no external objects, order-level financials.

**Business behavior**:
- Standard order fulfillment: items packed into one package, dispatched together, invoiced and paid at the order level.
- No sharing or anomalies.

**Operational risks/opportunities**:
- Risk: Bottlenecks in packaging if items are bulky or require special handling.
- Opportunity: Simplified logistics and invoicing.

**Poorly represented by**:
- Activity-sequence equivalence (would group X1, X2, X5, X6, X7).
- Typed-structural equivalence (would group X1 and X2 but not X5/X6 due to external objects).

**Stability**:
- Stable under data repair (e.g., correcting mislabeled events) as long as the core motif is preserved.

---

### Cluster: Split packages, consolidated delivery (X3)
**Signature**:
- `num_packages>1`, `num_deliveries=1`, split packages, no split deliveries.

**Business behavior**:
- Items packed into separate packages but dispatched together (e.g., for weight distribution or carrier requirements).
- Still order-level financials.

**Operational risks/opportunities**:
- Risk: Higher packaging costs; potential for package loss if dispatched together.
- Opportunity: Flexibility in packaging (e.g., fragile items in separate packages).

**Poorly represented by**:
- Activity-sequence equivalence (would group with X4, which has split deliveries).
- Typed-structural equivalence (would distinguish X3 and X4 based on delivery multiplicity).

**Stability**:
- Stable if the consolidation of deliveries is intentional.

- `num_packages>1`, `num_deliveries>1`, split packages, split deliveries, partial payment.

**Stability**:
- Stable if the split deliveries are intentional.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

### What `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` preserves
- The *exact object identifiers* and their *incidence relationships* in events.
- The *total order* of events.
- The *activity labels* and *object types*.

### Why raw identifiers make clusters too fine
- Identifiers like `I11` vs. `I22` are arbitrary and do not encode business semantics. Clustering based on raw identifiers would:
  - Split X1 and X2 into separate clusters (undesirable).
  - Fail to group executions with shared motifs (e.g., X1 and X5).
- External objects (`EXT-`) are treated as distinct, even when structurally equivalent (e.g., `EXT-OA` in X5 is structurally similar to a local order object).

### Canonicalization/post-processing steps
To adapt `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` for business-semantic clustering:
1. **Normalize identifiers**:
   - Replace local identifiers (e.g., `O1`, `I11`) with placeholders (e.g., `O*`, `I*`).
   - Preserve `EXT-` flags but treat `EXT-OA` as a generic external object.
2. **Collapse same-label bursts**:
   - Treat consecutive identical activities as a single event (e.g., two `Add Item` events  one).
3. **Aggregate object multiplicities**:
   - Replace counts of objects (e.g., two items) with a flag (e.g., `multi_item=True`).
4. **Preserve qualifiers**:
   - Keep O2O qualifiers (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch`) as event attributes.
5. **Post-process clusters**:
   - Merge clusters that differ only in normalized identifiers or same-label bursts.

**Python outline**:

### When exact equivalence is preferable
- **Data quality audits**: Exact equivalence can identify duplicate or near-duplicate executions (e.g., for deduplication).
- **Low-level process mining**: When the goal is to analyze event-level details (e.g., timing, specific object interactions).

### Impact of `max_objs` or large executions
- `max_objs` limits the number of objects considered, which can:
  - Speed up clustering but miss structural differences (e.g., external objects).
  - Fail to distinguish executions with many objects but similar motifs.
- Large executions may:
  - Exceed memory limits in exact equivalence checks.
  - Require approximate methods (e.g., feature-based clustering) for scalability.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

### Recommendations by use case

| Use case               | Recommended equivalence | Why                                                                 |
|------------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Compliance monitoring  | Typed-structural        | Ensures all required events and qualifiers are present (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch` for replacements). |
| Operational segmentation | Business-semantic     | Groups executions by logistics and financial motifs (e.g., split deliveries vs. consolidated). |
| Root-cause analysis    | Qualified behavioral    | Captures lifecycle constraints (e.g., "item must be packed before dispatch"). |
| Descriptive reporting  | Business-semantic       | Provides meaningful business insights (e.g., "cross-order invoice sharing"). |

### Why no single equivalence is best
- **Compliance monitoring** requires strict adherence to process rules (e.g., no replacements without qualifiers), so typed-structural equivalence is ideal.
- **Operational segmentation** needs to group executions by business impact (e.g., split deliveries), so business-semantic equivalence is ideal.
- **Root-cause analysis** requires understanding constraints (e.g., "why was this item returned?"), so qualified behavioral equivalence is ideal.
- **Descriptive reporting** benefits from high-level motifs (e.g., "how many orders have cross-order invoices?"), so business-semantic equivalence is ideal.

### Limitations
- **Business-semantic equivalence** may over-simplify in cases where exact timing or object identities matter (e.g., for auditing).
- **Typed-structural equivalence** may group executions with different business impacts if they share the same structure (e.g., X5 and X1 both have one package, but X5 involves sharing).
- **Qualified behavioral equivalence** is sensitive to the presence of qualifiers; missing qualifiers can lead to incorrect groupings.

### Final recommendation
For most business purposes, **business-semantic equivalence** strikes the best balance between meaningful grouping and robustness to identifier variations. It is:
- **Reproducible**: Canonical signatures are easy to compute.
- **Interpretable**: Clusters correspond to real-world business motifs.
- **Robust**: Insensitive to arbitrary identifier renaming.

However, for compliance-critical applications, **typed-structural equivalence** should be used to ensure all qualifiers and relationships are preserved.
