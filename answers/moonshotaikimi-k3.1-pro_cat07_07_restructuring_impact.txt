# 2. Scenario forecasts

These are conditional planning forecasts, not statistical confidence intervals. A numerical cycle-time forecast would require arrival patterns, staffing calendars, active service-time distributions, multitasking, and the workload added by triage and difficult Quality Check.

- **Handover pattern:** Total handovers may remain near or above the current level, but they are more purposeful. Standard case notes support direct routing and clean returns; cross-team absence cover falls without an increase in central-local ping-pong. Working-together ties shift to a stable central pool rather than continually changing individuals.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** The technical pool is staffed for demand and variability, legal/risk capacity is separately protected, and difficult Quality Check is not allowed to consume unbudgeted technical capacity. Management’s 4.0-hour and 2.2-hour central medians are achievable, while p90 waits and legal/risk waits do not deteriorate.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency and censored open cases.** Routine cases may close faster while difficult cases remain open and are excluded from a completed-case average. Report p90/p95, open-case age, SLA misses, and time-to-completion methods that retain censored cases.

2. **Case-mix improvement rather than process improvement.** More simple cases, fewer East cases, or different priority mix can improve the average without improving any comparable case. Standardize results to a common case mix and inspect within-stratum changes.

3. **High-complexity cases funding routine-case gains.** Mandatory central routing can lengthen exactly the cases with the greatest need for contextual advice. Their p90 waiting, number of specialist interactions, and outcomes must be separate guardrails.

4. **Local knowledge loss and invisible work.** Informal advice may disappear, or local staff may continue providing it without logging it. The log could then show either higher referrals or apparently stable performance while staff effort and case-note quality deteriorate.

5. **Fairness hidden by aggregation.** East may improve while North/South decline, or high-priority cases may be protected by overtaking low-priority customers. A standardized queue can still produce unequal waiting, rework, outcomes, or access to accommodation.

6. **Delayed learning and deskilling.** A short pilot can benefit from central specialist enthusiasm while local diagnostic capability slowly erodes. The cost appears later as higher referral dependency, poorer case notes, and reduced ability to operate during central disruption.

7. **Accountability diffusion.** Average completion time may remain stable while customers are contacted by more resources, approvals have no identifiable owner, and exceptions require repeated escalation. This creates operational and legal risk even without an immediate time penalty.

8. **Exception handling and resilience.** Rare legal, regional, absence-cover, and system-failure cases contribute little to an average but can dominate tail delay and severe outcomes. They require dedicated variant analysis and case review.

9. **Quality shifted downstream.** Faster specialist or approval activity can increase later Quality Check failures, Rework, or adverse outcomes. A shorter time to an intermediate decision is not necessarily a shorter or better end-to-end process.

  - Add capacity or separate duties if p90 queue waits breach their agreed limit for two reporting periods.  
  - Adjust or roll back a stratum if its adjusted p90 cycle time, SLA misses, or rework breaches a pre-set non-inferiority margin—for illustration, more than 10% p90 worsening or more than one percentage point of additional rework—across adequately followed cohorts.  
  - Stop shared approval immediately for a serious quality or compliance incident attributable to unclear ownership.  
  - Restore local capacity if East does not benefit while North/South or a customer segment deteriorates, or if local backup can no longer handle a simulated absence.

Rollback should mean restoring local technical/approval capacity, separating queues, tightening routing, or adding staff—not merely changing dashboard thresholds.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, and modify the design before the pilot.** The current business case does not justify immediate full rollout: its own estimates show more listed handovers and longer waits on several major routes, while Legal/Risk specialists have little spare capacity and the shared technical pool is assigned additional, unquantified work.

The pilot should preserve a named local case owner, standardized case notes, some local consultation and learning, separate capacity for scarce specialties, and a credible local fallback. Shared approval and additional Quality Check/triage duties should be introduced in measurable stages.

Scale up only if the combined model demonstrates:

1. Stable queues during peaks, not merely acceptable median waits during ramp-up.
2. Improvement for East without material North/South harm.
3. Non-inferior p90/p95 performance, SLA, rework, and outcomes for high-complexity and customer-segment strata.
4. Clear ownership, acceptable reassignment and exception patterns, and sustainable staffing.
5. Adequate absence recovery and a credible plan to prevent local deskilling.

Management should **not** infer from early averages that:

- higher utilization means higher productivity;
- fewer visible edges mean fewer actual coordination steps;
- fewer variants mean better exception handling;
- standardized decisions are automatically fair or correct;
- completed-case improvements represent open difficult cases;
- or an early pooled-capacity benefit proves long-term resilience and learning.

If averages and utilization improve while tails, quality, fairness, or ownership deteriorate, the correct response is adjustment or rollback—not full rollout.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The following is PM4Py-style Python/pseudocode; exact keyword arguments can vary by installed version. It deliberately derives an **origin team** so that filtering for East does not remove the later shared-service events from East’s cases.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

C = "case:concept:name"
A = "concept:name"
T = "time:timestamp"
R = "org:resource"

CASE_ATTRS = [
    "case_complexity", "required_specialty", "priority",
    "customer_segment", "location", "local_expertise_flag"
]

def prepare(raw, period):
    d = raw.copy()
    d[T] = pd.to_datetime(d[T], utc=True)
    d["period"] = period
    d = d.sort_values([C, T])

    # In production, derive this from the first local Intake event, with an
    # explicit fallback. Do not use a later central-team assignment as origin.
    first = d.groupby(C, sort=False).first()
    d["origin_team"] = d[C].map(first["team"])

    # These mappings assume the attributes are case-consistent. If they vary,
    # define an explicit case-level aggregation instead.
    for col in CASE_ATTRS:
        d["case_" + col] = d[C].map(first[col])

    d = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        d, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T
    )
    return d.sort_values([C, T])

def as_log(d):
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(d.sort_values([C, T]))

def case_meta(d):
    s = d.sort_values([C, T])
    g = s.groupby(C)

    cols = ["origin_team"] + ["case_" + x for x in CASE_ATTRS]
    m = g[cols].first()
    m["start"] = g[T].min()
    m["end"] = g[T].max()
    m["closed"] = g[A].apply(lambda x: x.eq("Close").any())
    m["cycle_d"] = (m["end"] - m["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    m.loc[~m["closed"], "cycle_d"] = np.nan
    m["reassignments"] = g["reassignment_count"].max()
    m["rework_flag_case"] = g["rework_flag"].max()  # normalize encoding first
    m["outcome"] = g["outcome"].last()

    # Apply one externally documented SLA policy to both periods:
    # m["sla_miss"] = apply_sla_policy(
    #     m, snapshot_date=..., include_open_overdue_cases=True
    # )
    return m

def select_cases(d, **criteria):
    """
    Example:
      select_cases(after, origin_team=["East"],
                   case_case_complexity=["high"])
    Select case IDs first, then retain ALL events for those cases.
    """
    m = case_meta(d)
    keep = pd.Series(True, index=m.index)

    for col, value in criteria.items():
        values = value if isinstance(value, (list, tuple, set)) else [value]
        keep &= m[col].isin(values)

    ids = m.index[keep]
    sd = d[d[C].isin(ids)].copy()
    return sd, as_log(sd), m.loc[ids]

def mine_structure(d):
    log = as_log(d)
    return {
        # Normalize edge weights by started cases and compare edge composition.
        "handover": pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log),
        "working_together": pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log),
        "subcontracting": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log),
        "resource_similarity":
            pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log),
        "roles": pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log),

        "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(log),
        # Usually expressed in seconds; check version/aggregation settings.
        "performance_dfg": pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log),

        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(log),
        # Repeated activity occurrences; not identical to rework_flag.
        "rework_cases_per_activity":
            pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
    }

def variant_counts(d):
    sequences = (
        d.sort_values([C, T]).groupby(C)[A].agg(tuple)
    )
    return (
        sequences.value_counts()
        .rename_axis("variant")
        .reset_index(name="cases")
    )

def adjacent_handovers(d):
    """
    Complements PM4Py's network with explicit latency quantiles.
    This is inter-event latency after an adjacent resource change, not
    necessarily pure queue time.
    """
    z = d.sort_values([C, T]).copy()
    for source, target in [
        (R, "to_resource"), (A, "to_activity"),
        (T, "to_time"), ("team", "to_team")
    ]:
        z[target] = z.groupby(C)[source].shift(-1)

    z = z[z["to_resource"].notna() & z[R].ne(z["to_resource"])].copy()
    z["latency_h"] = (
        z["to_time"] - z[T]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    # Add origin_team, case attributes, handover_reason, and a fixed-horizon
    # post-handover rework outcome before grouping. Do not use an unlimited
    # future horizon for one period and a short horizon for another.
    return z

before = prepare(before_raw, "before")
after = prepare(after_raw, "after")

structural = {
    "before": mine_structure(before),
    "after": mine_structure(after)
}

# Compare variant frequency and new post-change variants.
vb, va = variant_counts(before), variant_counts(after)
new_variants = set(va["variant"]) - set(vb["variant"])

focus_variants = list(va["variant"].head(10))
after_focus_log = pm4py.filter_variants(
    as_log(after), focus_variants, retain=True

# Example of PM4Py event-attribute filtering. Use level="case" only when the
# attribute semantics justify retaining every case with one of these values.
specialty_after_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    as_log(after),
    "required_specialty",
    ["legal", "risk", "security"],
    level="case",
    retain=True

# Example of a complete-case cohort that retains central events.
east_high_after, east_high_log, east_high_meta = select_cases(

east_high_performance = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(east_high_log)
```

For interpretation:

- Normalize handover and collaboration networks by case volume, and compare node centrality, local versus central ties, and resources per case.
- Compare resource similarity and discovered roles for resources present in both periods. A broad central “mega-role” or narrower local roles are hypotheses to investigate, not direct proof of clarity or deskilling.
- Use subcontracting and reassignment patterns to investigate repeated delegation, especially during absence and exception handling.
- Bootstrap confidence intervals by **case**, not by event, and use contemporaneous controls or case-mix-standardized before/after comparisons.
- Instrument the pilot with queue-entry, acceptance, start, completion, actual worker, named owner, decision, and SLA-deadline events. Otherwise, a single virtual shared-service resource can make the mined organization look simpler while concealing its internal queues and handovers.
