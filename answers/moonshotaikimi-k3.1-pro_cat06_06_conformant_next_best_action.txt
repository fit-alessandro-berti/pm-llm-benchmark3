## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

- **Deadline benefit is joint-probability dependent.** The relevant quantity is approximately the probability of *valid approval and execution by minute 90 under the intervention*, multiplied by the €20k–€35k value. That joint probability is not supplied. The **0.55–0.75** ultimate-approval estimate cannot simply be multiplied by one minus the **0.48–0.64** normal-processing breach estimate: those estimates concern different conditions, may be dependent, and are observational rather than intervention-specific.
- **A and B are not made attractive by asymmetric loss arithmetic.** Their gross operational upside is capped by the deadline value, while invalid execution exposes the case to €100k–€500k plus potentially more consequential non-monetary harm. More fundamentally, missing mandatory controls makes them infeasible paths, not expensive but permissible ones.
- **C/D preserve decision option value.** They can lead to approval, rejection, further evidence, or hold without prematurely committing to execution or rejection. The alternate screen is valuable because it can reduce both false-clear regulatory exposure and false-rejection/customer harm. It is not valuable because it “clears” the mandatory review.
- **E and F have different customer harms.** E preserves the possibility of a better eventual decision but almost certainly sacrifices the current deadline and may create a prolonged hold. F acts quickly but can irreversibly impose false-rejection harm. E is ranked above F only because F’s threshold is currently unestablished; once that threshold and required review posture are verified, F should move above E, and may become the preferred disposition if new evidence strongly supports rejection.
- **No strict portfolio dominance is available among ranks 1–3.** Immediate S2 offers the best P-884 timing, but P-901’s loss per breach is not provided. Conversely, S4 avoids that spillover but may sacrifice all current-deadline value. The ranking therefore uses explicit conditions rather than pretending to know a precise euro optimum.

## 4. Conformance constraints

- **A:** Violates rules **6 and 9**. The operative score is 72 and the fuzzy match is unresolved, so independent Second Officer Review is mandatory before approval or execution. There is also no valid approval path for immediate execution. Rule **3** Auto Clear is unavailable, and rule **10** supplies no current override.
- **B:** Violates rules **6 and 7** because R17 cannot supply the independent second review. If used to support execution, it also violates rule **9**. Calling the activity a “second review” does not make it independent.
- **C:** Can satisfy rules **5–9** if the required post-evidence Analyst Review/routing is completed, S2 independently reviews the evidence, S2 is distinct from R17, and execution follows only a valid approval. Rejection, evidence request, or hold/SAR must remain non-execution outcomes; rule **11** applies to any SAR.
- **D:** Alternate screening is supplemental evidence, not Auto Clear, Analyst Review, or Second Officer Review. While either rule **6** trigger remains, S2 or S4 must still review before approval/execution. A validated change in both the score and match status could justify reranking the control requirements, but the 12-minute-old score should not simply be assumed to have improved.
- **E:** Directly follows rule **5**: Evidence Requested  Evidence Received  Evidence Validated  Analyst Review, with repetition allowed. It does not waive rule **6** or authorize eventual execution without the required approval path.
- **F:** Rule **6** specifically names approval and execution, not rejection, but that does not create blanket permission for an unsupported immediate rejection. F requires the applicable documented rejection threshold, case-specific facts showing that it is met, completion of the required post-evidence review/sign-offs, and an authorized, auditable rationale. The prompt has not established that an unresolved 0.46-confidence match alone satisfies that threshold.
- **G:** Rule **10** requires both explicit Compliance Director authorization and a valid incident identifier. Evidence needed includes case-specific authorization and scope, a genuinely created and justified incident record, and an audit trail establishing that the proposed action falls within the exception and is otherwise lawful. A customer deadline or potential operational loss is not, by itself, proof of a genuine incident. Until both conditions exist, G cannot authorize execution.

Neither a favorable approval forecast nor a lower predicted SLA breach can supply the control missing from A or B.
