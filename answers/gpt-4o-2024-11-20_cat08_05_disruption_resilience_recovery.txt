# 3. Resilience Versus Concealed Noncompliance

**Resilience:** True resilience involves adaptations that preserve mission-critical operations while maintaining minimum critical compliance thresholds. For instance, **B (manual authorization check)** showed resilience, as it preserved control for high-risk segments using limited resources. 

**Concealed noncompliance:** Risky shortcuts accelerate throughput but eliminate necessary controls, creating hidden risks. **C (skip system authorization check)** and **G (dispatch without scan)** are examples where compliance gaps undermined traceability and regulatory obligations.

**Ambiguous cases:** Some adaptations, like **A (Hospital-critical fast lane)** and **J (Backlog prioritization)**, have both resilient and risky elements. Their utility depends on establishing clear, fair, and enforceable limits upfront.

# 4. Recovery Process Model

**Trigger conditions**:  
- Loss of warehouse system functionality or data synchronization affecting more than 30% of orders.

**Proposed Process**:
1. **Triage Rules**:  
   - **Hospital-critical or Controlled-substance orders**: Require at least manual authorization checks and handwritten proof-of-delivery.
   - **Cold-chain products**: Must include temperature checks at both release and delivery.
   - **Pharmacy-standard orders**: Secondary priority; resume after high-criticality triage.
   - **Wholesale-bulk orders**: Lowest priority; defer until partial system functionality.

2. **Minimum Controls by Product/Customer Risk**:  
   - **Critical items (e.g., hospitals, controlled substances)**: 100% manual compliance check (authorization, traceability).  
   - **Lower-priority items**: Minimum manual form completion for inventory and dispatch.  

3. **Manual Evidence Capture**:  
   - Standard manual logs for inventory, proof of delivery, cold-chain temperature, and dual-control authorization maintained on paper or local spreadsheets.

4. **Backlog Prioritization**:  
   - Post-disruption, explicitly rank backlog using weighted criteria (e.g., hospital affiliation, regulated product type, days delayed).

5. **Reconciliation and Remediation**:  
   - Systematic inventory/order validation at T3.  
   - Dedicated compliance audit remediation tracked until completion during stabilization. Assign additional staff for this phase.

# 7. Management Trade-Off Statement

Management should prioritize resilience mechanisms that maintain critical supply chains (e.g., hospitals, regulated goods) without sacrificing essential compliance controls. Shortcuts like skipping authorization checks or dispatching without traceability should not be accepted under any circumstances due to regulatory and reputational risks. Evidence (e.g., reduced backlog age, traceability exceptions below threshold) could validate tolerating short-term increases in throughput risks only with explicit manual safeguards and audit-ready protocols.
