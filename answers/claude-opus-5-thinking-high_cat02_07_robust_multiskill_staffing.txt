# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Status of every number below

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| **[O]** observation | given directly in the prompt |
| **[A]** assumption | my choice, needed to close an under-specified gap |
| **[D]** derivation | arithmetic on [O] and [A] |
| **[E]** estimate | model output / judgement band, with uncertainty |
| **[H]** hypothesis | plausible mechanism, not yet evidenced |
| **[R]** recommendation | action |

## 1.2 Service-time model

**[A1] Ranges → distributions.** Each range is read as a Uniform distribution, and each activity is a two-component mixture: `X = U(ord_lo, ord_hi)` with prob `1−p`, `U(tail_lo, tail_hi)` with prob `p`. Needed because the prompt gives ranges, not distributions. Uniform is the maximum-entropy choice given only endpoints. *Sensitivity:* a triangular or lognormal fit (endpoints as p10/p90) would raise the mean ~2–4% and fatten p95 by ~10–15%; the policy ranking below is not sensitive to this, but the absolute SLA numbers are.

**[A2] Multipliers are deterministic and multiplicative on the base draw**, applied to the whole activity, including any restarted remainder. `-` in the matrix means ineligible (not "slow").

**[D1] Mean and dispersion before multipliers:**

| activity | mean (min) | sd | CV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Intake | 6.94 | 1.74 | 0.25 |
| Mechanical | 26.30 | 8.59 | 0.33 |
| Electrical | 32.82 | 10.29 | 0.31 |
| Software | 38.50 | 13.39 | 0.35 |
| Validation | 15.74 | 5.37 | 0.34 |

Most of the variance comes from the mixture, not the within-component spread — i.e. **tail risk is driven by the complex-case indicator, which is exactly what U4 doubles.**

**[A3] Independence.** Complex-tail draws are independent across activities and cases. This is the optimistic assumption; if "complex" is a case property (correlated across branches), ME/ES flow-time tails are materially worse. Flagged as a validation target in §9.

## 1.3 Demand model

**[A4]** Block arrival counts are Poisson with mean = `expected_arrivals`; arrival instants uniform inside the block; type and priority drawn independently from the block's percentages. The prompt gives expected counts only, so any distributional choice is an assumption; Poisson is the standard, and it matters because a deterministic-arrivals model would systematically understate queueing.

**[A5]** Day starts empty at 08:00 and the horizon ends at 20:00 + 30 min overtime. Unfinished cases are **deferred**, not "infinitely delayed" — I report deferral rate separately and treat their flow times as right-censored. Without this, mean-delay numbers are meaningless in an overloaded system.

**[D2] Branch demand** (branches, not cases): Mechanical = M+ME, Electrical = E+ME+ES, Software = S+ES.

| block | cases | Mech br. | Elec br. | Soft br. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 08–10 | 7 | 3.50 | 3.85 | 1.75 |
| 10–12 | 9 | 4.50 | 5.40 | 2.25 |
| 12–14 | 7 | 2.94 | 4.20 | 2.10 |
| 14–16 | 10 | 4.00 | 6.20 | 3.00 |
| 16–18 | 6 | 2.10 | 3.30 | 2.40 |
| 18–20 | 3 | 1.05 | 1.35 | 1.35 |
| **day** | **42** | **18.09** | **24.30** | **12.85** |

## 1.4 Overhead model

**[A6] Handover (7 min) and cross-family (5 min) delays block the receiving resource**, not just the case. Rationale: they are read-in / briefing time. This is the pessimistic reading; the optimistic reading (pure case-side delay) frees ~450–600 resource-minutes/day system-wide and would move every utilization figure down ~6–8 points. Both are defensible; I state which one every number uses and expose the switch as a flag in the code.

**[A7] Setup (5 min)** applies when a resource's *previous diagnostic activity* was in the other skill family (MECH vs ELEC/SOFT). Intake and Validation do not set a family and do not reset it.

**[A8] Breaks are not interruptible and tasks do not span them**: a task starts only if it is expected to finish before the break, otherwise it waits. Overtime cap 30 min is a hard start-gate: a task starts only if its *expected* finish ≤ shift end + 30. Actual overruns beyond that are permitted (you cannot abandon a case mid-activity) and counted as overtime breach.

## 1.5 ME/ES parallelism — the decision, not a constant

For an ME case leaving Intake (held by resource `r0`):

| option | case-time on the critical path | resource-minutes consumed |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel Ben(M) ‖ Chen(E) | 7 (handover) + max(26.3, 32.8) + 8 coord = **48.1** | 7+7+26.3+32.8 = 73.1 |
| Sequential on Deepa (M×1.25 then E×1.00) | 7 + 32.9 + 32.8 = **72.7** | 72.7 |
| Sequential across two resources | 7 + 26.3 + 7 + 5 + 32.8 = **78.1** | 78.1 |

For ES: parallel Chen(E) ‖ Emil(S) = 7 + max(32.8, 38.5) + 8 = **53.5**; sequential on Emil (E×1.3 then S×1.0) = 7 + 42.6 + 38.5 = **88.1**.

**[D3] Two conclusions.** (i) Parallel is strictly better on *case latency* by 25–35 min, and on ES it is also better on *resource-minutes* because sequential forces someone to work off-speciality. (ii) Parallel requires **two servers simultaneously free**; under heavy load the synchronisation wait dominates the 25–35 min saving, and holding two servers starves the queue. Therefore parallelism must be **state-dependent**, not a policy constant:

> **Parallelize iff** (case is P1) **or** (two eligible tier-1/tier-2 resources are idle *now*) **or** (predicted sequential completion breaches the case's SLA clock by >0 while predicted parallel does not). Otherwise run sequentially on the cheapest cross-skilled resource, which also avoids the 7+5 min cross-family handover.

## 1.6 Objectives

Let `s` index scenarios {base, U1…U5}, `k` seeds.

**O1 — SLA/tail risk (primary).**
```
J1(π,s) = 10·(0.90 − A_P1)+ + 3·(0.90 − A_P2)+ + 1·(0.90 − A_P3)+
        + 0.004·p95_flow_P2  + 2·deferral_rate
```
where `A_p` = attainment, `(x)+ = max(0,x)`. Weights encode "a P1 breach is ~3× a P2 breach"; the p95 term prices tail pain that attainment alone hides; the deferral term prevents a policy from scoring well by silently pushing work to tomorrow.

**O2 — Robustness + fairness (selection criterion).**
```
J2(π) = CVaR_0.25 over (s,k) of J1(π,s,k)
      + 4·Gini(utilization)  + 8·(max_r util_r − 0.90)+
```
A **minimax-style tail objective over scenarios**, not a scenario-probability-weighted mean, because no scenario probabilities are supplied. The fairness terms operationalise the prompt's "utilization above 90% is unfair and fragile."

**O3 — Throughput/efficiency (diagnostic only).** Cases closed same-day per 100 productive resource-minutes; used to expose how much capacity a policy burns on handover/setup/coordination.

**Recommendation is made on O2.** O1 alone would reward pooling; O3 alone would reward strict specialisation with sequential branches.

## 1.7 Decision variables

`E[activity][resource]` eligibility (tiered, possibly time-varying) · `θ_overflow` slack threshold that opens tier-2/tier-3 · `R(t)` reserved P1 capacity (which skill, which hours, release rule) · `π_parallel` parallelization gate · `preempt gate` (slack trigger, victim rule, min-remaining floor) · `dwell` minimum family batch before a setup-incurring switch · overtime allocation · queue discipline weights.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

## 2.1 The headline: the base scenario is capacity-infeasible

**[D4] Nominal work (multiplier 1.0), whole day:**

| stream | branches/cases | mean min | work (std-min) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Intake | 42 | 6.94 | 291.5 |
| Mechanical | 18.09 | 26.30 | 475.8 |
| Electrical | 24.30 | 32.82 | 797.5 |
| Software | 12.85 | 38.50 | 494.7 |
| Validation | 42 | 15.74 | 661.1 |
| **total** | | | **2 720.6** |

**[D5] Net available resource-minutes:** Ada 450, Ben 450, Chen 450, Deepa 450, Emil 450, Farah 240 → **2 490**.

> **ρ_base ≥ 2720.6 / 2490 = 1.09 at multiplier 1.0 — before any handover, setup or coordination minute is spent, and before anyone is forced to work off-speciality.** With realistic assignment (some work necessarily lands at 1.10–1.40) and [A6] handovers, effective offered load is **1.15–1.30**.

This is the single most important finding, and it reframes the whole exercise: **no routing policy makes this day close.** Policy choice determines *who waits and which SLA class absorbs the shortfall*, not whether there is a shortfall. Any answer reporting "mean delay 40 min under pooling" has mis-modelled the arithmetic.

## 2.2 Where the constraint actually binds — skill-level supply

| skill | demand (std-min) | tier-1 supply (mult ≤1.0) | tier-2 (≤1.25) | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Electrical | 797.5 | Chen 450, Deepa 450 → 900 | — (Emil 1.30, Ada 1.35, Farah 1.40 are tier-3) | **binding**: consumes 89% of Chen+Deepa's entire shifts |
| Software | 494.7 | Emil 450 | Chen 360 std-equiv | **binding**: Emil alone is short; and Emil starts at 12:00 |
| Mechanical | 475.8 | Ada 450, Ben 450 | Deepa 360 | slack |
| Intake+Validation | 952.6 | everyone | — | absorbs whatever is left |

**[D6] The structural squeeze.** Electrical + Software = 1 292 std-min must be served by the E/S-capable set {Chen, Deepa(E only), Emil} = 1 350 raw minutes. That leaves essentially nothing of those three for Intake/Validation. The remaining 1 428 std-min (Mech + Intake + Validation) falls on {Ada, Ben, Farah} = 1 140 min. **Shortfall ≈ 290 min ≈ the 231-min global gap plus multiplier waste.**

**[D7] Two structural holes independent of load:**
1. **Morning software hole (08:00–12:00).** Software demand 4.0 branches ≈ 154 std-min, but Emil (1.00) does not arrive until 12:00. Only Chen (1.25 → 193 actual min) or Farah (1.40) can serve it — and Chen is simultaneously the only tier-1 electrical resource before 10:00. **Every morning S/ES case competes head-on with every morning E/ME case for Chen.**
2. **Evening single-point-of-failure (18:00–20:00).** Only Emil is on shift. 1.35 elec + 1.35 soft + 1.05 mech branches + intake + validation ≈ 192 std-min in a 120-min block with one person, and Emil cannot do Mechanical at all. **Every 18:00–20:00 ME case is unstartable on its mechanical branch and is structurally deferred to the next day.** This is a design defect no dispatch rule can fix.

## 2.3 Block-level load vs block-level capacity (base)

| block | work (std-min) | on-shift net min | ρ_block | note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 08–10 | 444.6 | 480 (Ada,Ben,Chen,Farah) | 0.93 | tight; Chen contended for E and S |
| 10–12 | 586.3 | 570 (+Deepa, −Farah 0, breaks) | 1.03 | Farah leaves at 12:00 |
| 12–14 | 454.8 | 570 (−Farah, +Emil) | 0.80 | only genuine slack window |
| 14–16 | 650.9 | 570 | **1.14** | **peak overload** |
| 16–18 | 391.8 | 210 (Deepa to 18:00, Emil) | **1.87** | **worst ratio of the day** |

Work is measured at arrival block; the 16:00–20:00 ratios are inflated because work arriving at 14:00 can be served at 16:00. The correct reading is **cumulative**: the system runs a growing backlog from ~10:30 onward, and the backlog does not clear before 20:30. **Bottleneck sequence in the base case: Chen (electrical/software contention, 08:00–12:00) → the E/S pool as a whole (14:00–16:00) → Validation and the Emil-only tail (17:00–20:00).**

## 2.4 Bottleneck migration by scenario

| scenario | offered load (std-min) | net capacity | ρ | dominant bottleneck | migration signature |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| **Base** | 2 721 | 2 490 | **1.09** | Chen (a.m.), E/S pool (p.m.), Validation (evening) | steady drift to Validation |
| **U1** morning surge | 3 442 | 2 490 | **1.38** | Intake first (16→27 cases before noon), then Electrical | bottleneck moves *upstream* to Intake, then whipsaws to Validation ~13:00 |
| **U2** E/S incident | ~2 780 (mix-shift reading) | 2 490 | **1.12** | Electrical 12:00–16:00: 12.3 branches ≈ 404 std-min vs Chen+Deepa 420 net | bottleneck *concentrates* on one skill; Mechanical goes idle |
| **U3** Chen absent | 2 721 | 2 040 | **1.33** | Electrical, catastrophically: tier-1 = Deepa alone (450 min vs 797 demand) | overflow forced onto Ada 1.35 / Emil 1.30 → effective capacity falls further than headcount |
| **U4** long tail | 2 962 | 2 490 | **1.19** | tail variance, not the mean: Soft mean 38.5→44.5, Elec 32.8→37.1 | p95 explodes ~2× while means rise ~10%; ME/ES sync waits worst hit |
| **U5** combined | 3 442 | 2 040 − outage | **≈1.69** | everything; Intake outage 09:30–10:00 detonates a surge queue | unrecoverable within the day |

**U1 detail.** Morning arrivals 7→11.9 and 9→15.3 (27.2 cases before noon vs 16). P1 share 18%/23% → **≈5.7 P1 cases in the morning vs 2.05 in base.** Morning capacity 08:00–10:00 is 480 min against 756 std-min of arriving work (ρ=1.57). Intake alone is 27.2 × 6.94 = 189 std-min, which is *fine*; **the P1 SLA is what breaks, not throughput** — with a 120-min clock and a queue that deep, P1 attainment cannot reach 0.90 under any policy without explicit reservation.

**U2 ambiguity — flagged, both read.** "E and ES demand doubles, proportions renormalized" admits two readings. *(A, primary)* mix reweighted, arrival counts unchanged: 12–14 becomes M15.7/E40.0/S12.9/ME14.3/ES17.1, 14–16 becomes M14.1/E42.3/S12.7/ME14.1/ES16.9; electrical branches 4.2→5.0 and 6.2→7.3; ρ rises only to ~1.12 but Mechanical capacity strands. *(B, sensitivity)* E and ES *counts* double so totals rise 7→9.8 and 10→14.2; ρ_afternoon → ~1.45 and U2 becomes as severe as U3. The word "renormalized" points to (A); I report (A) and treat (B) as a stress check. **This matters: under (A) the story is *stranded mechanical capacity*; under (B) it is *raw overload*, and the correct fix differs.**

**U4 detail.** The mean effect is modest (+8.9% total work) but the mixture probability on Electrical goes 0.18→0.36, i.e. **more than a third of electrical branches are 40–65 min**. For an ES case run in parallel, flow time is `max(E, S)`; doubling both tail probabilities raises `E[max]` far more than either mean, so **U4 punishes parallel branches specifically** and is the one scenario where sequential execution loses less than expected.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three share: SLA clock starts at case arrival; ties broken by earliest arrival; the coordination, handover, setup and preemption penalties of §1.4 are charged explicitly.

## Policy A — Strict specialisation with fixed queues

- **Eligibility (fixed all day, no overflow ever):** Intake {Ben, Deepa, Farah}; Mechanical {Ada, Ben}; Electrical {Chen, Deepa}; Software {Chen, Emil}; Validation {Ada, Emil, Farah}. Each resource holds at most two fixed duties, so no one ever works above multiplier 1.25 and almost no one incurs a family setup.
- **Queue discipline:** per-activity FIFO within priority class; classes served strictly P1 → P2 → P3.
- **Reservation:** none.
- **Preemption:** P1 may preempt P3 only, with the §3.4 victim rule.
- **Parallelization:** **always** for ME/ES — the pools are family-disjoint, so a single resource cannot hold both branches. Always pays 8 min coordination + a cross-family 5 min on the second branch handover.
- **Handover minimisation:** none available by construction. This is A's defining cost.
- **Overflow trigger:** none. A case waits for its own pool however long that takes.

*Why it is in the set:* it is the most auditable and the most skill-preserving policy, it minimises setup, and it is what most real teams actually run.

## Policy B — Full pooling with a transparent dispatch rule

- **Eligibility:** any resource with a defined multiplier for the activity, including 1.30–1.40.
- **Case selection (which task next):** score
  `V = w_p / max(5, slack)` with `w_P1=10, w_P2=3, w_P3=1`, `slack = target_p − (now − arrival) − E[remaining work]`. Highest `V` first. This is a least-slack rule with priority weights — one line, auditable on a whiteboard.
- **Resource selection (who serves it):** minimise **expected completion time** = handover overhead + setup + `mean_activity × multiplier_r`, with a −6 min bonus for the resource that held the previous stage of the same case (a direct, priced incentive against handover churn).
- **Reservation:** none.
- **Preemption:** P1 over P2 and P3, same victim rule.
- **Parallelization:** parallelize iff two eligible resources are idle at branch release; else sequential on the cheapest cross-skilled resource.
- **Overflow trigger:** none needed — everyone is always eligible. Tier-3 resources (Farah at 1.40, Ada on Electrical at 1.35) get work whenever they are the earliest finisher, which under queueing is often.

*Why it is in the set:* it is the throughput-maximising benchmark and the resource-pooling ideal. Its failure modes — fast-server saturation, fairness collapse, setup thrash — are exactly what the prompt's fairness constraint is designed to catch.

## Policy C — Hybrid: tiered overflow + reserved P1 capacity (proposed primary)

**C.1 Tiered eligibility.** For each activity: **tier 1** = multiplier ≤ 1.00, **tier 2** = ≤ 1.25, **tier 3** = > 1.25.
- Tier 1 always eligible.
- **Tier 2 opens** for a task when `slack < 0.5 × target_p` **or** `projected tier-1 wait > 20 min`.
- **Tier 3 opens** when `slack < 30 min`, or the task is P1, or `projected tier-1+2 wait > 45 min`.

This is the operational core: *speed matters more than availability when the clock is loose; availability matters more than speed when the clock is tight.*

**C.2 Reserved P1 capacity.** Between 10:00 and 18:00, keep **at most one idle tier-1 Electrical/Software-capable resource** (preference order Chen → Deepa → Emil) unassigned to P2/P3 work. Release the reservation immediately if any of: a P1 is already in service on that skill; any P2 has slack < 45 min; the reserved resource has been idle > 25 min; fewer than three E/S-capable resources are on shift (which is exactly the U3 condition — the reservation must *not* fire when it would idle a scarce server).

**C.3 Parallelization gate.** Parallelize iff P1, **or** two eligible tier-1/2 resources idle now, **or** `E[sequential finish] > deadline ≥ E[parallel finish]`. Else sequential, preferring the cross-skilled holder (Deepa for ME, Emil or Chen for ES) so the 7 + 5 min cross-family handover is avoided entirely.

**C.4 Handover minimisation.** (i) +6 min preference bonus for retaining a case across stages; (ii) **family dwell**: a resource does not switch skill family until it has completed 2 tasks or 45 min in the current family, unless the incoming task is P1 or has slack < 30 min. Directly targets the 5-min setup.

**C.5 Overflow triggers, stated as operator rules.**
- *Electrical queue depth ≥ 3 or oldest electrical wait > 25 min* → open Ada (1.35) and Emil (1.30) to Electrical.
- *Software waiting with Emil off-shift and Chen busy > 20 min* → open Farah (1.40), morning only.
- *Validation queue ≥ 4* → open Ada (1.15) and Farah (1.20) to Validation and suspend the family-dwell rule.
- *Any resource projected > 88% utilisation for the next hour* → that resource is skipped for non-P1 work while an alternative exists (the fairness governor).

**C.6 Fallback mode "C-triage"** (a mode of C, not a separate policy). Triggered by §5 thresholds. Changes: reservation is *widened* to two P1-capable servers; P3 admission to diagnostic work is **suspended** (P3 still gets Intake, so the clock and the record start, then parks); ME/ES forced sequential to stop server fragmentation; preemption gate loosened to P1-over-P2.

## 3.4 Shared preemption rule (explicit)

P1 task `t` may preempt only if **all** hold:
1. no eligible resource is free, and none becomes free within 10 min;
2. `slack(t) < 40 min`;
3. the victim case has not been preempted before (prompt limit);
4. victim priority > P1 (Policy C additionally requires priority = P3 unless in C-triage);
5. **victim remaining ≥ 12 min** — never interrupt work about to finish, since waiting 8 min beats restarting.

**Victim choice:** among qualifying tasks, take the one **minimising the restart penalty** `0.10 × remaining`, i.e. the *smallest* remaining above the 12-min floor, tie-broken by largest victim slack. Rationale: preemption's benefit to the P1 is identical whoever is bumped (the server is freed instantly), so the objective is purely to minimise destroyed work and secondary SLA damage.

# 4. Scenario comparison

**Scale and uncertainty statement — read before the table.**
- `mean_delay` and `p90` are **flow time** (arrival → Validation complete) **in minutes, over cases completed within the horizon**; deferred cases are excluded from those two columns and reported in `main_failure_mode`. Excluding them biases both columns *downward*, most severely for the worst policy-scenario cells — so the table understates the spread.
- SLA columns are fraction of that priority class completed within target (P1 120 min, P2 480 min).
- `utilization_fairness` = `1 − Gini(utilisation across the 6 resources)`; 1.00 = perfectly equal. `max_resource_utilization` counts service + handover + setup over net available minutes ([A6] pessimistic convention).
- `handover_or_setup_burden` = mean overhead minutes per case (handover + cross-family + setup + parallel coordination).
- `resilience` is qualitative (low/med/high) = degradation of J1 from base to the worst U-scenario.
- **These are model-based point estimates from the §8 scaffold's assumptions plus judgement, not measurements.** Treat SLA figures as ±0.10 absolute, delay figures as ±25%, and rely on the **ordering** far more than the levels. Every cell inherits ρ > 1: in an overloaded system these numbers depend heavily on [A5] (horizon) and [A6] (handover blocks the resource).

**Reading the table.**
1. **B beats C on base mean delay; C beats B on every tail and fairness measure and on P1 attainment in all six scenarios.** This is exactly the tension the prompt asks to expose, and it is why base-case mean delay must not be the deciding metric.
2. **A is dominated**, and instructively so: its failure is *stranded capacity*, not slow work. Under U2, Ada and Ben sit idle on Mechanical while the Electrical queue grows — A's max utilisation is 1.00 and its fairness is poor *simultaneously*, which is the signature of rigid pooling.
3. **No policy reaches 0.90 P1 attainment outside the base case**, and none reaches 0.90 P2 attainment anywhere. That is the ρ > 1 finding surfacing in the metrics, and it is the honest answer.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## 5.1 Primary: **Policy C (hybrid, tiered overflow with reserved P1 capacity), with C-triage as fallback**

Under **O2**, C has the lowest CVaR₀.₂₅ of J1 across scenarios *and* is the only policy that respects the ≤90% utilisation fairness constraint in the base case. It does not dominate B on O1's mean-delay component in the base case, and I am not claiming it does — it dominates on the tail, on P1 protection, and on scenario-worst-case, which is what "robust" was defined to mean in §1.6.

**Why C works mechanically, not just nominally:**
- **The tiering converts speed into a resource spent on urgency.** Sending an electrical branch to Ada (1.35) costs 11.5 extra minutes of system capacity. That is worth paying for a P1 with 30 min of slack and wasteful for a P3 with 20 hours. A and B both get this wrong in opposite directions: A never pays it, B pays it whenever the arithmetic of expected finish says so, including for P3s.
- **The reservation is self-cancelling.** C.2's release clauses mean the reservation evaporates precisely when it would be harmful (U3's thin roster, U1's P2 slack crunch). A reservation without release rules would make U3 worse than B; with them, C degrades gracefully toward B.
- **The dwell rule and retention bonus cut overhead ~25%** versus A (18 vs 24 min/case), which is worth roughly 250 resource-minutes/day — about **the entire capacity gap identified in §2.1**. Handover discipline is not a rounding term here; it is the largest controllable lever in the system.

## 5.2 Mode-switch triggers (auditable, computable from the live log)

Switch **C → C-triage** when any two hold for 20 consecutive minutes:
- system WIP > 12 cases; **or**
- P1 cases in system ≥ 3; **or**
- projected tier-1 electrical wait > 40 min; **or**
- E/S-capable headcount on shift < 3 (structural: fires automatically under U3/U5); **or**
- an infrastructure flag is set (Intake outage in U5).

Switch **back to C** when WIP < 8 and projected tier-1 wait < 20 min for 30 consecutive minutes. Hysteresis is deliberate — mode flapping causes setup thrash.

Escalate to **management action** (call in overtime, defer intake, borrow staff) when day-ahead forecast load / capacity > 1.20 — under **U1, U3, U4 and U5 this trigger fires on the forecast, before the day starts.** This is the most valuable output of §2: four of five scenarios are staffing problems, and treating them as routing problems is a category error.

## 5.3 When another policy is preferable

- **If arrivals were ~25% lower (ρ < 0.85)**, B is preferable outright: with slack in the system, the fast-server pinning that ruins B's fairness never materialises, and pooling's queueing advantage is free.
- **If the objective is throughput per resource-minute (O3) and SLAs are advisory**, a *sequential-preferring* variant of A wins: it minimises coordination and setup and never sends work to a 1.40 server.
- **If tail correlation turns out to be real** ([A3] false, complex cases complex on all branches), sequential execution and C-triage's forced-sequential rule become better than C's default parallelization, because `max(E,S)` sync waits blow up. This is a genuine open question, not a settled one.
- **Under U2 reading (B)** — where E/ES *counts* double — the binding constraint is raw electrical capacity, and no routing policy helps; the right answer is to pre-position Deepa's shift earlier and cross-train, not to re-tune thresholds.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

**Gaming of estimates.** C's tier-opening depends on `E[remaining work]` and hence on slack. Any resource or intake clerk who learns that "complex-tail" classification opens faster servers will classify generously; any who learns that low estimates mean fewer interruptions will under-report. **Mitigation:** the estimate used for dispatch must be model-generated from case attributes, never operator-entered; log both the model estimate and the actual and monitor the residual by resource (a per-resource bias drift is the tell).

**Starvation of P3.** This is C's designed sacrifice and C-triage's explicit one. With ρ > 1, P3 is the only class with enough slack to absorb the shortfall — but P3's target is 24 h, and **carryover into a day that is itself at ρ ≈ 1.09 means P3 breaches accumulate across days, not within one.** Mitigation: a hard **age-escalation rule** — any P3 older than 10 h is promoted to P2 weight, and older than 16 h is promoted to P1 weight regardless of mode. Without this, the recommended policy quietly manufactures a growing P3 breach population that never appears in single-day metrics.

**Skill erosion.** B and C route by speed, so the fast resource on each skill gets almost all of that skill's volume, and the 1.25–1.40 multipliers *worsen over time* through disuse. Chen doing 90% of electrical work makes Chen faster and Ada slower — the policy is self-reinforcing and it manufactures the exact single-point-of-failure that U3 exposes. **Mitigation:** a deliberate training quota (e.g. Ada takes ≥2 electrical branches/week, Deepa ≥2 mechanical) charged to the fairness budget, run in the 12:00–14:00 slack window identified in §2.3 — the only block with ρ < 1.

**Excessive preemption.** The 10% restart penalty looks cheap, but each preemption also triggers a possible 5-min setup, a 7-min handover on resumption if the resource changed, and a P3 that must re-enter a queue that is by then longer. **True cost per preemption ≈ 0.1×remaining + 12 min ≈ 15–20 min, not 2–3 min.** The 12-min-remaining floor and the 40-min slack gate exist for this reason. Monitor preemptions/day; if > 3, the reservation is mis-sized and should be widened instead.

**Coordination overhead and the Validation bottleneck.** Parallel ME/ES adds 8 min *and* synchronises two branch completions into a single Validation arrival. Under C, parallelization is state-dependent, which desynchronises arrivals somewhat. But §2.3 shows Validation demand (661 std-min) is the second-largest stream and lands disproportionately in 16:00–20:00 when only Deepa and Emil are on shift. **[H] Bottleneck migration hypothesis: any policy that successfully speeds up diagnostics moves the constraint to Validation between 16:00 and 20:00.** This is testable and is the first thing I would look for in the pilot.

**Fairness.** B violates the stated ≤90% constraint in every scenario. C's C.6 governor holds max utilisation at 0.93–0.98. Note that under overload, *some* resource must be near 100% or work is being left undone — so the fairness constraint and the throughput objective are in direct conflict, and C resolves it by capping the fast servers and accepting more deferral. **That is a value judgement embedded in the policy, and it should be made by management, not by the dispatch rule.**

**Human interpretability.** A is trivially auditable. B's least-slack score is one formula. C has five thresholds, a reservation with four release clauses, a dwell rule and a mode switch — **this is the real risk with C.** Mitigation: present C to operators as a two-column card — "who is eligible right now" and "why this case is next" — computed and displayed by the system, with every dispatch decision logged with its reason code (§7). If an operator cannot explain a dispatch after the fact, the policy is not deployable regardless of its simulated metrics.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## 7.1 Event attributes to capture (beyond the five mandatory columns)

| attribute | why |
|---|---|
| `queue_entry_ts` | true waiting time per stage; without it, waiting can only be inferred and (with parallel branches) inferred wrongly |
| `assignment_ts`, `service_start_ts`, `service_end_ts` | separates dispatch latency from handover/setup latency from actual service |
| `eligible_resource_set` (list) + `eligible_tier` | the counterfactual: reveals whether a delay was *capacity* (set empty) or *policy* (set non-empty but gated) |
| `dispatch_reason_code` | e.g. `TIER1`, `TIER2_SLACK`, `TIER3_P1`, `RESERVED_HOLD`, `DWELL_BLOCK`, `OVERFLOW_QDEPTH` — the audit trail for §6 interpretability |
| `skill_used`, `multiplier_applied` | detects skill erosion and off-speciality routing volume |
| `planned_service` vs `actual_service` | estimate-bias monitoring (gaming detection) |
| `pause_ts` / `resume_ts`, `preempted_by_case`, `restart_penalty_min` | preemption cost accounting |
| `handover_from_resource`, `handover_min`, `cross_family_flag`, `setup_min` | the overhead budget of §5.1 |
| `branch_mode` ∈ {parallel, sequential}, `coordination_min`, `sync_wait_min` | the §1.5 trade-off, measured |
| `policy_version`, `mode` (C / C-triage), `mode_switch_ts` | without this, before/after comparisons are uninterpretable |
| `scenario_flag` / `shock_flag` (surge, absence, outage) | separates policy effect from environment effect |
| `sla_clock_start`, `sla_target`, `sla_deadline`, `priority`, `priority_escalations` | attainment and age-escalation audit |
| `complex_tail_realized` (post hoc) | tests [A3] tail-correlation |

## 7.2 PM4Py analyses and what each would reveal

- `format_dataframe` + `insert_case_service_waiting_time` → case-level `@@service_time`, `@@waiting_time`, `@@sojourn_time`. **Critical correctness note:** pm4py computes case service as the **sum** of activity durations. For parallel ME/ES branches these intervals **overlap**, so summed service is inflated and the derived waiting time is **understated** (it can even go negative). Correct handling: compute service as the **union of activity intervals** and waiting as `sojourn − union_service`. I do this explicitly in §8 and label the pm4py column as *sum-service*, not *true service*.
- `get_service_time(aggregation_measure='mean'/'median')` per activity → detects U4-style service-time drift; run it **split by resource** to separate "the work got harder" from "the wrong person did it" (multiplier drift). A rising Electrical mean with a stable per-resource mean is pure routing drift.
- `discover_handover_of_work_network` → quantifies §5.1's largest lever. Compare edge weights across policy versions: C should show heavier self-loops (retention bonus) and lighter Ada↔Chen-type cross-family edges than A. Also surfaces the U3 dependency structure — a network with a high-betweenness node is a single point of failure.
- `discover_dfg` / performance DFG on `(activity, queue)` pairs → **bottleneck migration**: watch the edge `diagnostic → Validation` waiting time hourly to test the §6 hypothesis that speeding up diagnostics moves the constraint to Validation after 16:00.
- **Concept-drift detection** by windowing (hourly) and comparing DFG performance matrices and variant distributions across `policy_version` — the standard test for "did the change do anything, or did the day just get easier".
- `discover_batches` / resource-activity matrix → verifies the family-dwell rule is actually batching and quantifies setup savings.
- **Conformance checking** against a normative model of the dispatch rule (each dispatch must carry a legal `dispatch_reason_code` given the logged `eligible_resource_set` and slack) → catches operators overriding the policy, which is the most common reason a good policy underperforms in the field.

"""
Incident-process policy comparison: transparent DES + capacity approximation + PM4Py post-analysis.

EXPLICIT MECHANISMS (all as named constants / flags, none hidden):
  handover, cross-family, setup, parallel coordination, preemption penalty,
  overtime gate, break non-interruption, tiered eligibility, P1 reservation.

KNOWN OMISSIONS (deliberate, see section 9 of the write-up):
  - no multi-day carryover (day starts empty; unfinished work counted as 'deferred')
  - no correlation between complex-tail draws across branches of one case ([A3])
  - no learning/fatigue effects on multipliers; no travel time; no rework loops
  - no stochastic absence; U3/U5 absences are deterministic
  - handover/setup are modelled as blocking the RECEIVING resource (flag HANDOVER_BLOCKS)

from collections import defaultdict
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

# ============================================================
# 1. TABLES (all input data encoded in memory)

SERVICE = {  # (ord_lo, ord_hi, tail_lo, tail_hi, p_tail)
    'Intake':     (5, 8, 10, 14, 0.08),

    'Chen':  {'Intake':1.10,'Electrical':1.00,'Software':1.25,'Validation':1.10},

    'Emil':  {'Intake':1.10,'Electrical':1.30,'Software':1.00,'Validation':1.00},
    'Farah': {'Intake':1.00,'Mechanical':1.40,'Electrical':1.40,'Software':1.40,'Validation':1.20},

# minutes from 08:00; (shift_start, shift_end, (break_start, break_end) or None)

    'Ada':  (0, 480, (255, 285)), 'Ben':   (0, 480, (240, 270)),
    'Chen': (0, 480, (270, 300)), 'Deepa': (120, 600, (360, 390)),
    'Emil': (240, 720, (480, 510)), 'Farah': (0, 240, None),
}
DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    # blk_start, n, P1,P2,P3, M,E,S,ME,ES
    (  0, 7, 10,55,35, 30,25,15,20,10),
    (120, 9, 15,55,30, 25,25,15,25,10),
    (240, 7, 12,58,30, 22,28,18,20,12),
    (360,10, 18,52,30, 20,30,18,20,12),
    (480, 6, 10,50,40, 20,25,25,15,15),
    (600, 3,  5,45,50, 25,20,30,10,15),
], columns=['t0','n','P1','P2','P3','M','E','S','ME','ES'])

BRANCHES = {'M':['Mechanical'], 'E':['Electrical'], 'S':['Software'],
            'ME':['Mechanical','Electrical'], 'ES':['Electrical','Software']}
FAMILY = {'Mechanical':'MECH', 'Electrical':'ES', 'Software':'ES'}
TARGET = {1:120.0, 2:480.0, 3:1440.0}

# ---- explicit penalty / rule constants -------------------------------------
HANDOVER_MIN      = 7.0    # resource change on any stage boundary
CROSS_FAMILY_MIN  = 5.0    # mech <-> elec/soft move, waived if same resource
SETUP_MIN         = 5.0    # resource switches diagnostic family between cases
PARALLEL_COORD    = 8.0    # coordination before Validation for parallel ME/ES
PREEMPT_PENALTY   = 0.10   # of remaining active service
PREEMPT_MIN_REM   = 12.0   # never interrupt work with < this remaining
PREEMPT_SLACK     = 40.0   # only preempt for a P1 this close to breaching
OVERTIME_MIN      = 30.0
HORIZON           = 720.0
HANDOVER_BLOCKS   = True   # [A6]: overhead occupies the receiving resource
FAIR_UTIL_CAP     = 0.88   # C's fairness governor
PRIO_W            = {1:10.0, 2:3.0, 3:1.0}

MEAN_SVC = {a: (1-p)*(lo+hi)/2 + p*(tl+th)/2 for a,(lo,hi,tl,th,p) in SERVICE.items()}

# ============================================================
# 2. SCENARIOS
# ============================================================
def build_scenario(name):
    """Return (demand_df, skill, service, outage_windows). Deep-copied per scenario."""
    dem = DEMAND.copy(); skill = {r: dict(v) for r,v in SKILL.items()}
    svc = {a: list(v) for a,v in SERVICE.items()}; outage = []
    if name in ('U1','U5'):
        m = dem['t0'] < 240
        dem.loc[m,'n'] = dem.loc[m,'n']*1.7
        # +8 pp to P1, taken proportionally from P2/P3
        for i in dem.index[m]:
            p1 = dem.at[i,'P1']+8; rest = 100-p1; old = dem.at[i,'P2']+dem.at[i,'P3']
            dem.at[i,'P1']=p1; dem.at[i,'P2']=dem.at[i,'P2']/old*rest
            dem.at[i,'P3']=dem.at[i,'P3']/old*rest
    if name == 'U2':
        # reading (A): E and ES weights double, mix renormalised, counts unchanged
        for i in dem.index[(dem['t0']>=240)&(dem['t0']<480)]:
            w = {'M':dem.at[i,'M'],'E':dem.at[i,'E']*2,'S':dem.at[i,'S'],
                 'ME':dem.at[i,'ME'],'ES':dem.at[i,'ES']*2}
            tot = sum(w.values())
            for k,v in w.items(): dem.at[i,k] = 100*v/tot
    if name in ('U3','U5'):
        skill.pop('Chen')
    if name == 'U4':
        for a in ('Mechanical','Electrical','Software'):
            svc[a][4] = min(1.0, svc[a][4]*2)
    if name == 'U5':
        outage = [('Intake', 90.0, 120.0)]   # 09:30-10:00
    return dem, skill, svc, outage

SCENARIOS = ['base','U1','U2','U3','U4','U5']

# ============================================================
# 3. DETERMINISTIC OFFERED-LOAD APPROXIMATION (audit layer)
# ============================================================
def offered_load(name):
    dem, skill, svc, _ = build_scenario(name)
    mean = {a: (1-p)*(lo+hi)/2 + p*(tl+th)/2 for a,(lo,hi,tl,th,p) in
            ((k,tuple(v)) for k,v in svc.items())}
    work = defaultdict(float)
    for _,r in dem.iterrows():
        n = r['n']
        work['Intake']     += n*mean['Intake']
        work['Validation'] += n*mean['Validation']
        work['Mechanical'] += n*(r['M']+r['ME'])/100*mean['Mechanical']
        work['Electrical'] += n*(r['E']+r['ME']+r['ES'])/100*mean['Electrical']
        work['Software']   += n*(r['S']+r['ES'])/100*mean['Software']
    cap = 0.0
    for res in skill:
        s,e,br = SHIFT[res]; cap += (e-s) - (30 if br else 0)
    tot = sum(work.values())
    return dict(scenario=name, work_std_min=round(tot,1), capacity_min=cap,
                rho=round(tot/cap,3), **{k:round(v,1) for k,v in work.items()})

# ============================================================
# 4. SIMULATION PRIMITIVES
# ============================================================
def draw_service(act, svc, rng):
    lo,hi,tl,th,p = svc[act]
    return rng.uniform(tl,th) if rng.random() < p else rng.uniform(lo,hi)

def on_shift(res, t):
    s,e,br = SHIFT[res]
    if not (s <= t < e): return False
    return not (br and br[0] <= t < br[1])

def next_free_window_end(res, t):
    """Latest time a task started at t may run without hitting a break/shift end."""
    s,e,br = SHIFT[res]
    if br and t < br[0]: return br[0]
    return e + OVERTIME_MIN

class Task:
    __slots__=('case','act','ready','rem_std','prev_res','prev_act','branch_id','started')
    def __init__(self, case, act, ready, rem_std, prev_res, prev_act, branch_id=None):
        self.case=case; self.act=act; self.ready=ready; self.rem_std=rem_std
        self.prev_res=prev_res; self.prev_act=prev_act; self.branch_id=branch_id
        self.started=False

class Case:
    __slots__=('cid','arr','prio','ctype','stage','branch_mode','branches_left',
               'branch_end','preempted','done_t','last_res','val_ready')
    def __init__(self, cid, arr, prio, ctype):
        self.cid=cid; self.arr=arr; self.prio=prio; self.ctype=ctype
        self.stage='intake'; self.branch_mode=None; self.branches_left=0
        self.branch_end=0.0; self.preempted=False; self.done_t=None
        self.last_res=None; self.val_ready=None

def generate_cases(dem, svc, rng):
    cases=[]; cid=0
    for _,r in dem.iterrows():
        n = rng.poisson(r['n'])
        for _ in range(n):
            arr = r['t0'] + rng.uniform(0,120)
            pr  = rng.choice([1,2,3], p=np.array([r['P1'],r['P2'],r['P3']],float)/100)
            tw  = np.array([r['M'],r['E'],r['S'],r['ME'],r['ES']],float); tw/=tw.sum()
            ct  = rng.choice(['M','E','S','ME','ES'], p=tw)
            cases.append(Case(cid, arr, int(pr), ct)); cid+=1
    return sorted(cases, key=lambda c:c.arr)

# 5. POLICIES
# ============================================================
STRICT_ELIG = {'Intake':{'Ben','Deepa','Farah'}, 'Mechanical':{'Ada','Ben'},
               'Electrical':{'Chen','Deepa'}, 'Software':{'Chen','Emil'},
               'Validation':{'Ada','Emil','Farah'}}

POLICIES = {
    'A_strict': dict(kind='strict', reserve=False, dwell=0, retain_bonus=0.0,
                     parallel='always', preempt_targets=(3,), tiered=False, fair_cap=None),
    'B_pool':   dict(kind='pool',   reserve=False, dwell=0, retain_bonus=6.0,
                     parallel='if_two_idle', preempt_targets=(2,3), tiered=False, fair_cap=None),
    'C_hybrid': dict(kind='pool',   reserve=True,  dwell=2, retain_bonus=6.0,
                     parallel='slack', preempt_targets=(3,), tiered=True, fair_cap=FAIR_UTIL_CAP),
}

def eligible_resources(act, cfg, skill, slack, prio, tier1_wait):
    if cfg['kind']=='strict':
        return [r for r in skill if r in STRICT_ELIG[act] and act in skill[r]]
    cands=[r for r in skill if act in skill[r]]
    if not cfg['tiered']: return cands
    t1=[r for r in cands if skill[r][act]<=1.00]
    t2=[r for r in cands if 1.00<skill[r][act]<=1.25]
    t3=[r for r in cands if skill[r][act]>1.25]
    out=list(t1)
    if slack < 0.5*TARGET[prio] or tier1_wait > 20 or not t1: out += t2
    if prio==1 or slack < 30 or tier1_wait > 45 or not out:   out += t3
    return out

# ============================================================
# 6. DISCRETE-EVENT SIMULATION
# ============================================================
def simulate(policy, scen, seed, verbose=False):
    cfg = POLICIES[policy]
    dem, skill, svc, outage = build_scenario(scen)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    cases = generate_cases(dem, svc, rng)
    by_id = {c.cid:c for c in cases}

    busy_until = {r:-1.0 for r in skill}
    last_family = {r:None for r in skill}
    fam_run     = {r:0 for r in skill}          # dwell counter
    busy_min    = defaultdict(float)            # utilisation numerator
    pending=[]; log=[]; overhead_min=0.0; n_pre=0; n_par=0; n_seq=0
    in_service={}                               # res -> (task, start, end, mult)

    def blocked(act, t):
        return any(a==act and s<=t<e for a,s,e in outage)

    def overhead_for(task, res):
        oh = 0.0
        if task.prev_res is not None and task.prev_res != res: oh += HANDOVER_MIN
        if task.prev_act and FAMILY.get(task.prev_act) and FAMILY.get(task.act) \
           and FAMILY[task.prev_act]!=FAMILY[task.act] and task.prev_res!=res:
            oh += CROSS_FAMILY_MIN
        f = FAMILY.get(task.act)
        if f and last_family[res] and last_family[res]!=f:
            if cfg['dwell'] and fam_run[res] < cfg['dwell'] and \
               by_id[task.case].prio != 1: return None   # dwell blocks the switch
            oh += SETUP_MIN
        return oh

    def slack_of(c, act, now):
        return TARGET[c.prio] - (now - c.arr) - MEAN_SVC[act] - MEAN_SVC['Validation']

    for c in cases:
        pending.append(Task(c.cid,'Intake',c.arr,draw_service('Intake',svc,rng),None,None))

    events=[(t,0) for t in sorted({c.arr for c in cases})]
    heapq.heapify(events)
    for r in skill:
        s,e,br = SHIFT[r]
        for t in ([s,e] + ([br[0],br[1]] if br else [])): heapq.heappush(events,(t,0))
    for a,s,e in outage: heapq.heappush(events,(e,0))
    t = 0.0; guard = 0

    while events and guard < 200000:
        guard += 1
        t = heapq.heappop(events)[0]
        if t > HORIZON + OVERTIME_MIN: break
        progress = True
        while progress:
            progress = False
            ready = [x for x in pending if x.ready <= t and not blocked(x.act,t)]
            ready.sort(key=lambda x: -PRIO_W[by_id[x.case].prio] /
                                      max(5.0, slack_of(by_id[x.case], x.act, t)))
            free = [r for r in skill if busy_until[r] <= t and on_shift(r,t)]
            for task in ready:
                c = by_id[task.case]; sl = slack_of(c, task.act, t)
                t1w = 0.0
                cands = eligible_resources(task.act,cfg,skill,sl,c.prio,t1w)
                avail = [r for r in cands if r in free]
                # C's fairness governor: skip near-cap resources for non-P1 work
                if cfg['fair_cap'] and c.prio!=1 and len(avail)>1:
                    net = {r:max(1.0,min(t,SHIFT[r][1])-SHIFT[r][0]) for r in avail}
                    ok=[r for r in avail if busy_min[r]/net[r] < cfg['fair_cap']]
                    if ok: avail=ok
                # C's P1 reservation: hold one idle tier-1 E/S server, with releases
                if cfg['reserve'] and c.prio!=1 and 120<=t<600:
                    es_on=[r for r in skill if on_shift(r,t) and
                           ({'Electrical','Software'} & set(skill[r]))]
                    if len(es_on)>=3 and sl>45:
                        for pref in ('Chen','Deepa','Emil'):
                            if pref in avail and len(avail)>1: avail.remove(pref); break
                best=None
                for r in avail:
                    oh = overhead_for(task,r)
                    if oh is None: continue
                    dur = task.rem_std*skill[r][task.act]
                    if t+oh+dur > next_free_window_end(r,t): continue
                    score = oh+dur - (cfg['retain_bonus'] if r==task.prev_res else 0.0)
                    if best is None or score<best[0]: best=(score,r,oh,dur)
                if best is None and c.prio==1 and sl<PREEMPT_SLACK:
                    vic=None
                    for r,(vt,st,en,mu) in list(in_service.items()):
                        if task.act not in skill.get(r,{}): continue
                        vc=by_id[vt.case]
                        if vc.prio not in cfg['preempt_targets'] or vc.preempted: continue
                        rem=(en-t)
                        if rem<PREEMPT_MIN_REM: continue
                        if vic is None or rem<vic[0]: vic=(rem,r,vt,mu)
                    if vic:
                        rem,r,vt,mu = vic
                        vt.rem_std = (rem/mu)*(1+PREEMPT_PENALTY)
                        vt.ready=t; vt.started=False
                        by_id[vt.case].preempted=True
                        pending.append(vt); del in_service[r]
                        busy_until[r]=t; n_pre+=1
                        oh=overhead_for(task,r) or 0.0
                        dur=task.rem_std*skill[r][task.act]
                        best=(oh+dur,r,oh,dur)
                if best is None: continue
                _,r,oh,dur = best
                st = t+ (oh if HANDOVER_BLOCKS else 0.0); en = st+dur
                busy_until[r]=en; busy_min[r]+= (dur+oh if HANDOVER_BLOCKS else dur)
                overhead_min+=oh
                f=FAMILY.get(task.act)
                if f:
                    fam_run[r] = fam_run[r]+1 if last_family[r]==f else 1
                    last_family[r]=f
                in_service[r]=(task,st,en,skill[r][task.act])
                pending.remove(task); free.remove(r)
                log.append(dict(case=task.case, act=task.act, start=st, end=en,
                                res=r, prio=c.prio, ctype=c.ctype, overhead=oh))
                heapq.heappush(events,(en,0)); progress=True
        # completions
        for r,(tk,st,en,mu) in list(in_service.items()):
            if en<=t:
                del in_service[r]; c=by_id[tk.case]; c.last_res=r
                if tk.act=='Intake':
                    br=BRANCHES[c.ctype]
                    if len(br)==1:
                        c.branches_left=1; c.branch_mode='single'
                        pending.append(Task(c.cid,br[0],en,draw_service(br[0],svc,rng),r,'Intake'))
                    else:
                        idle=[q for q in skill if busy_until[q]<=en and on_shift(q,en)]
                        el=[q for q in idle if any(b in skill[q] for b in br)]
                        par = (cfg['parallel']=='always' or
                               (cfg['parallel']=='if_two_idle' and len(el)>=2) or
                               (cfg['parallel']=='slack' and (c.prio==1 or len(el)>=2)))
                        if par:
                            c.branch_mode='parallel'; c.branches_left=len(br); n_par+=1
                            for i,b in enumerate(br):
                                pending.append(Task(c.cid,b,en,draw_service(b,svc,rng),r,'Intake',i))
                        else:
                            c.branch_mode='sequential'; c.branches_left=len(br); n_seq+=1
                            pending.append(Task(c.cid,br[0],en,draw_service(br[0],svc,rng),r,'Intake',0))
                elif tk.act=='Validation':
                    c.done_t=en
                else:
                    c.branch_end=max(c.branch_end,en); c.branches_left-=1
                    br=BRANCHES[c.ctype]
                    if c.branch_mode=='sequential' and c.branches_left>0:
                        nb=br[1]
                        pending.append(Task(c.cid,nb,en,draw_service(nb,svc,rng),r,br[0],1))
                    elif c.branches_left==0:
                        coord = PARALLEL_COORD if c.branch_mode=='parallel' else 0.0
                        rd=c.branch_end+coord
                        pending.append(Task(c.cid,'Validation',rd,
                                            draw_service('Validation',svc,rng),r,tk.act))
                        heapq.heappush(events,(rd,0))
        if pending and not in_service:
            nxt=[x.ready for x in pending if x.ready>t]
            if nxt: heapq.heappush(events,(min(nxt),0))

    # ---- metrics ----
    ldf=pd.DataFrame(log)
    done=[c for c in cases if c.done_t is not None]
    flows=np.array([c.done_t-c.arr for c in done]) if done else np.array([np.nan])
    def sla(p):
        grp=[c for c in cases if c.prio==p]
        if not grp: return np.nan
        return sum(1 for c in grp if c.done_t and (c.done_t-c.arr)<=TARGET[p])/len(grp)
    util={}
    for r in skill:
        s,e,br=SHIFT[r]; net=(e-s)-(30 if br else 0)
        util[r]=busy_min[r]/net
    u=np.array(list(util.values()))
    gini=(np.abs(np.subtract.outer(u,u)).mean())/(2*u.mean()) if u.mean()>0 else 0.0
    return dict(policy=policy, scenario=scen, seed=seed, n_cases=len(cases),
                completed=len(done), deferral=1-len(done)/max(1,len(cases)),
                mean_delay=float(np.nanmean(flows)), p90_delay=float(np.nanpercentile(flows,90)),
                P1_SLA=sla(1), P2_SLA=sla(2), P3_SLA=sla(3),
                max_util=float(u.max()), util_fairness=float(1-gini),
                overhead_per_case=overhead_min/max(1,len(cases)),
                preemptions=n_pre, parallel=n_par, sequential=n_seq), ldf, cases

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and service ranges alone

1. **Nothing about variability of arrivals.** Expected counts per block fix the mean, not the process. Poisson [A4] is a choice; if arrivals are batched (a shift handover dumps five tickets at 08:05), queueing is materially worse than modelled at identical ρ. **The delay numbers are more sensitive to this than to any policy parameter.**
2. **Nothing about the shape inside the ranges.** All my p90/p95 figures rest on [A1]. Uniform-with-mixture is defensible but arbitrary; the SLA attainment numbers are the most fragile outputs in this document.
3. **Nothing about dependence.** [A3] independence across branches is optimistic and untested. If "complex" is a case attribute, ME/ES tails worsen substantially and the parallelization recommendation weakens.
4. **Nothing about whether ρ > 1 is real or an artefact.** The §2 finding depends on "expected arrivals" being a daily mean rather than a bad day, and on handover blocking the receiving resource [A6]. Under the optimistic handover reading and 10% fewer arrivals, ρ falls to ~0.98 and the entire diagnosis changes from "staffing crisis" to "routing problem." **This single fact should be settled before any policy is deployed** — it is worth more than all the policy comparison above.
5. **Nothing about the multipliers' stability.** They are given as constants; in reality they vary by case complexity and drift with practice.
6. **Nothing about human behaviour**: override rates, informal help, batching by preference — often larger than the modelled effects.
7. **Nothing about cross-day dynamics.** Every scenario is a fresh empty day. With ρ > 1, the *only* interesting question is the multi-day backlog trajectory, and this model is structurally blind to it.

## 9.2 Pilot design (P1-safe, no unsafe full rollout)

**Stage 0 — measurement only, 3 weeks, zero policy change.** Instrument the log with the §7 attributes under the current policy. Goals: estimate the true arrival process and service distributions (settling limits 1, 2, 4); measure actual handover and setup minutes to resolve [A6] empirically; fit the tail-correlation structure (limit 3). **If ρ > 1 is confirmed, escalate as a staffing decision immediately and treat the policy pilot as secondary.**

**Stage 1 — shadow mode, 2 weeks.** Run Policies B and C as *recommenders* alongside the live policy. Log the recommended dispatch and the actual one. Compare on counterfactual-safe metrics: how often does the live policy leave an eligible tier-1 server idle while a P1 waits? Zero patient risk, and it directly measures the "stranded capacity" mechanism that dooms Policy A.

**Stage 2 — guarded live pilot, 4–6 weeks, with a P1 safety carve-out.**
- **Randomisation unit: the day**, not the case. Case-level randomisation is invalid here because policies compete for shared servers — a pooled case and a specialised case contend for Chen simultaneously, so treatment contaminates control. Randomise C vs incumbent by day, blocked by weekday and by forecast load, ≥20 days per arm.
- **P1 safety guardrail (non-negotiable):** P1 cases are *always* dispatched under the more aggressive of the two policies' recommendations, in both arms. This deliberately biases the comparison against detecting a P1 benefit for C — accepted, because no patient of the SLA should be the price of an experiment. C's P1 advantage is instead evidenced from Stage 1 shadow data.
- **Stopping rules:** halt the C arm immediately if P1 attainment in any rolling 5-day window falls below the incumbent's trailing baseline by >5 pp, if any resource exceeds 0.95 utilisation for two consecutive days, or if preemptions exceed 5/day.
- **Primary endpoint:** P2 p90 flow time (adequately powered at ~40 days; P1 counts, ~4/day, are far too few to power a P1 endpoint — this is why the shadow stage matters).
- **Secondary:** overhead minutes/case, utilisation Gini, deferral rate, P3 age distribution at 24 h.

**Stage 3 — backtest the shock scenarios without causing them.** Replay Stage 0's real arrival stream through the simulator with U1–U5 transformations applied, comparing policies on identical realised demand (common random numbers). This is the only ethical way to test U3 and U5: **you do not remove Chen for a day to see what happens.** Validate the simulator first by checking that it reproduces Stage 0's observed flow-time distribution within ~15% before trusting any of its scenario extrapolations.

**Stage 4 — staged rollout** of C with C-triage armed, one shift-window at a time (12:00–16:00 first, since §2.3 identifies it as the peak-load window and therefore the highest-signal test), with weekly conformance checking against the dispatch rule to catch operator override drift.
