**1. Model scope and state variables**

The digital twin models the municipal building-permit process from portal submission to permit issuance or rejection, including all listed activities and rework loops. Entities are individual cases (by case_type: Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance), architects (top-20 architect_id create correlated submissions), districts (D1–D5 with travel effects), and resources (clerks, planners, fire reviewers, environmental reviewers, inspectors, legal officers, system). State variables include per-activity queues with FIFO or district-batching discipline, resource calendars with FTE availability and interruptions, case attributes (requires_fire, requires_environment, requires_public_notice, application_value, district, architect_id), pending missing-document requests, public-notice objection flags (inferred), and cross-case bundles (same architect + district for inspection). Cross-case dependencies are represented by architect submission bursts, geographic inspector batching, and shared neighborhood objection effects on public-notice duration. The model boundary excludes post-issuance enforcement and applicant internal processes beyond response-time distributions.

**2. Parameter-estimation plan**

Use PM4Py to load and format the event log, discover the directly-follows graph (DFG) and performance DFG, compute variants with `pm4py.get_variants`, and derive case-arrival statistics via `pm4py.stats.get_case_arrival_average`. Fit non-stationary arrival rates by weekday and month from timestamps. Estimate service-time and waiting-time distributions separately by activity and case_type using start/completion timestamps where available; otherwise treat the observed duration as an upper bound and fit log-normal or Weibull distributions with heavy-tail parameters. Branching probabilities (X operators) and rework rates are obtained from variant shares and activity frequencies. Resource calendars are calibrated against the supplied HR-derived schedules, adjusting for observed Monday spikes and council-meeting gaps. Cross-case dependencies (architect bursts, district batching) are quantified by grouping cases on architect_id and district within weekly windows. Missing objection events are handled by fitting an extended public-notice duration mixture model conditioned on case_type and district.

**3. Assumptions**

- **arrivals**: Non-homogeneous Poisson process with weekday (Monday 2.2× Wednesday) and seasonal (1.7× March–May, Sep–Nov) multipliers; architect bursts modeled as clustered arrivals.
- **service times**: Log-normal or Weibull per activity; start timestamps missing for many human tasks, so service/waiting split inferred from resource calendars and queue statistics.
- **routing**: Deterministic rules for fire/environmental/public-notice based on attributes plus stochastic missing-document probability; decision drafting waits for all applicable parallel reviews.
- **resources/calendars**: Fixed FTE schedules with public-holiday and council-meeting interruptions; inspectors support district batching; system tasks have infinite capacity.
- **rework**: After Planning, Inspection, Legal, or appeal; probability and loop count estimated from variant groups; reasons treated as unstructured.
- **applicant/third-party response**: Heavy-tailed (median 5 d, long tail) for missing documents and readiness; modeled as separate delay distributions.
- **cross-case dependencies**: Architect-level submission correlation and geographic inspector batching; neighborhood objections affect multiple cases via shared public-notice extension factor.
- **non-stationary demand**: Explicit time-varying arrival intensity; no assumption of steady state.
- **missing data**: Objection events unobserved (use public-notice duration proxy); some emergency cases removed; resource calendars reconstructed from HR data.

**5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment**

Warm-up: run 3–6 months of simulated time until queue lengths and backlog stabilize (monitored by moving-average convergence). Run length: 24 months (including 18-month calibration window + 6-month projection). Replications: minimum 30 independent runs per scenario with distinct random seeds (Mersenne Twister). Report median and 90th-percentile duration with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals; use common random numbers for paired comparisons. Heavy-tailed durations handled by fitting Weibull or log-normal with shape <2 and importance sampling for tail events. Sensitivity analysis: vary arrival multipliers (±20 %), service-time scale (±15 %), objection probability, and batching threshold; produce tornado plots on median and p90 duration.

**6. Validation strategy**

Face validation: workshops with process owners to confirm process tree, calendars, and dependency rules. Event-log replay validation: replay historical cases with discovered DFG and compare activity frequencies and variant shares. Distributional validation: Kolmogorov–Smirnov or Anderson–Darling tests on simulated vs. empirical service/waiting times per activity. Resource-utilization validation: compare simulated FTE occupancy against observed completion volumes and calendar constraints. Bottleneck validation: match simulated vs. observed waiting-time hotspots (Planning, Legal, Inspection). Out-of-sample validation: hold out last 3 months of log; compare simulated vs. actual backlog, median duration, and district-level SLA compliance.

**7. Decision criteria**

Primary: median end-to-end duration reduction 15 % and p90 reduction 20 % versus baseline for the dominant case types, with SLA compliance (permit within 30/60/90 days by case_type) 90 %. Secondary: district and architect fairness (no group experiences >10 % increase in median duration), resource utilization 85 % sustained, rework rate reduction, backlog growth <5 % under stress. Combined policy (D10) selected only if it dominates single interventions on at least four of the above metrics and remains robust (SLA breach rate increase <3 % under +30 % demand). No claim of optimality; selection is comparative under calibrated uncertainty.
