## 1. Normalization Assumptions

Evaluating temporal conformance under uncertainty requires formal interval arithmetic rather than collapsing timestamps to point estimates. For an event $e$, let its true occurrence time in UTC be denoted $t^*(e)$. Because recorded values are subject to discretization, local time-zone translations, network/entry latencies, and clock synchronization errors, $t^*(e)$ is bounded by a feasible interval $I(e) = [t_{\min}(e), t_{\max}(e)] \subset \mathbb{R}$, or a union of disjoint intervals $\bigcup_k I_k(e)$.

### 1.1 Interval Arithmetic & True-Time Interpretation
For two events $e_1$ (source) and $e_2$ (target) with feasible intervals $I(e_1) = [t_{1,\min}, t_{1,\max}]$ and $I(e_2) = [t_{2,\min}, t_{2,\max}]$, the true elapsed duration $D(e_1, e_2) = t^*(e_2) - t^*(e_1)$ is bounded by the Minkowski difference:
$$\text{Feasible Duration } D(e_1, e_2) = [t_{2,\min} - t_{1,\max}, \; t_{2,\max} - t_{1,\min}]$$

The source clock error is defined by the prompt as:
$$\text{clock\_offset} = t_{\text{recorded}} - t_{\text{true}} \implies t_{\text{true}} = t_{\text{recorded}} - \text{clock\_offset}$$
If the clock offset is bounded by $[a, b]$, where $a$ and $b$ are given in seconds:
- A positive offset ($+b$) indicates a fast clock (the recorded time is ahead of true time); hence true time is earlier: $t_{\text{true}} \ge t_{\text{recorded}} - b$.
- A negative offset ($a < 0$) indicates a slow clock (the recorded time lags true time); hence true time is later: $t_{\text{true}} \le t_{\text{recorded}} - a$.
Therefore, applying an offset bound $[a, b]$ to an uncorrected interval $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ yields:
$$I_{\text{true}} = [r_{\min} - b, \; r_{\max} - a]$$
In accordance with the prompt's instruction, timestamp-resolution uncertainty is applied to determine $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ *before* applying the clock-offset correction.

### 1.2 System-Specific Rounding, Resolution, and Clock Error Semantics
1. **HUB (Central High-Trust System)**:
   - *Time zone*: UTC (+00:00).
   - *Resolution*: Displayed to the second with measurement uncertainty of $\pm 0.5$ s. For displayed $T$, the recorded interval is $[T - 0.5\text{ s}, T + 0.5\text{ s}]$.
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-1\text{ s}, +1\text{ s}]$.
   - *Normalized True UTC Interval*: $[(T - 0.5) - 1, \; (T + 0.5) - (-1)] = [T - 1.5\text{ s}, \; T + 1.5\text{ s}]$. Total width = 3.0 s.
2. **OPS-BER (Berlin Operations)**:
   - *Time zone*: Europe/Berlin. On 2026-06-01, Central European Summer Time (CEST = UTC+2) applies.
   - *Resolution*: "Floor to minute". A displayed timestamp $hh:mm$ represents an unrounded continuous clock time in $[hh:mm:00.000, \; hh:mm:59.999]$ (modeled in continuous time as $[hh:mm:00, \; hh:mm:60]$).
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-90\text{ s}, +120\text{ s}]$ (derived from daily NTP diagnostics).
   - *Normalized True UTC Interval*: Converting nominal time to UTC (subtracting 2 hours) and subtracting the offset bound:
     $$t_{\min} = hh:mm:00 - 2\text{h} - 120\text{ s} = hh:mm:00 - 2\text{h} - 2\text{ min}$$
     $$t_{\max} = hh:mm:60 - 2\text{h} - (-90\text{ s}) = hh:mm:00 - 2\text{h} + 1\text{ min } 30\text{ s}$$
     Total width = $60 + 120 + 90 = 270\text{ s}$ (4.5 minutes).
   - *Exception (Case T-409)*: Missing NTP diagnostics leave only a weak engineering estimate of $[-12\text{ min}, +12\text{ min}] = [-720\text{ s}, +720\text{ s}]$. Displayed 18:04 CEST (16:04 UTC) yields $[16:04:00 - 720\text{ s}, \; 16:05:00 + 720\text{ s}] = [15:52:00, \; 16:17:00]$ UTC (width = 25 minutes).
3. **SENSOR (Automated Field Device)**:
   - *Time zone*: UTC (+00:00).
   - *Resolution*: "Round to nearest 10 seconds". A displayed timestamp $hh:mm:ss$ rounds values within $[hh:mm:ss - 5\text{ s}, \; hh:mm:ss + 5\text{ s}]$.
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-5\text{ s}, +5\text{ s}]$.
   - *Normalized True UTC Interval*: $[(T - 5) - 5, \; (T + 5) - (-5)] = [T - 10\text{ s}, \; T + 10\text{ s}]$. Total width = 20.0 s.
   - *Lifecycle semantics*: Device events are explicitly completion events.
4. **VENDOR-NY (External Vendor System)**:
   - *Time zone*: America/New_York. On 2026-06-01, Eastern Daylight Time (EDT = UTC-4) applies.
   - *Resolution*: "Floor to minute" $\implies [hh:mm:00, \; hh:mm:60]$.
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-180\text{ s}, +180\text{ s}]$ ($\pm 3\text{ min}$, no event-level synchronization).
   - *Normalized True UTC Interval*: Adding 4 hours for UTC conversion:
     $$[hh:mm:00 + 4\text{h} - 180\text{ s}, \; hh:mm:60 + 4\text{h} + 180\text{ s}] = [hh:mm:00 + 4\text{h} - 3\text{ min}, \; hh:mm:00 + 4\text{h} + 4\text{ min}]$$
     Total width = $60 + 180 + 180 = 420\text{ s}$ (7 minutes).
5. **LEGACY-BER (Legacy Server, Fall DST Transition)**:
   - *Time zone*: Europe/Berlin. On 2025-10-26, the seasonal transition from CEST (UTC+2) back to CET (UTC+1) occurs at 03:00 local time, turning clocks back to 02:00. Local times between 02:00:00 and 02:59:59 occur twice.
   - *Resolution*: "Floor to minute" $\implies [hh:mm:00, \; hh:mm:60]$.
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-60\text{ s}, +60\text{ s}]$. Local true clock interval is $[hh:mm:00 - 60\text{ s}, \; hh:mm:60 + 60\text{ s}]$ (width = 180 s).
   - *Fold Disambiguation*:
     - Fold 0 (first pass, CEST, UTC+2): $\text{UTC} = \text{Local} - 2\text{ hours} \implies [hh:mm - 2\text{h} - 1\text{m}, \; hh:mm - 2\text{h} + 2\text{m}]$.
     - Fold 1 (second pass, CET, UTC+1): $\text{UTC} = \text{Local} - 1\text{ hour} \implies [hh:mm - 1\text{h} - 1\text{m}, \; hh:mm - 1\text{h} + 2\text{m}]$.
6. **MOBILE-LON (User Mobile Entry)**:
   - *Time zone*: Europe/London. On 2026-06-01, British Summer Time (BST = UTC+1) applies.
   - *Resolution*: "Round to nearest minute" $\implies [hh:mm:00 - 30\text{ s}, \; hh:mm:00 + 30\text{ s}]$.
   - *Clock-offset bound*: $[-300\text{ s}, +300\text{ s}]$ ($\pm 5\text{ min}$).
   - *Device True Time*: Converting to UTC (subtract 1 hour): $[hh:mm:00 - 1\text{h} - 330\text{ s}, \; hh:mm:00 - 1\text{h} + 330\text{ s}]$.
   - *Treatment of Mobile Entry Delay*: The user may have entered the time up to 5 minutes ([0, 300] s) late. We formally distinguish two operational interpretations:
     - **Model A (Occurrence Time Shifted Earlier - Primary Assumption)**: The user commenced investigation at $t_{\text{occur}}$, but did not open the app to record it until up to 5 minutes later ($t_{\text{entry}} = t_{\text{occur}} + \delta_{\text{entry}}$). If the recorded time reflects $t_{\text{entry}}$, the true occurrence time took place earlier: $t_{\text{occur}} = t_{\text{entry}} - \delta_{\text{entry}}$. Subtracting up to 300 s expands the lower bound:
       $$t_{\min} = 12:13:30 - 300\text{ s (clock)} - 300\text{ s (entry)} = 12:03:30\text{ UTC}$$
       $$t_{\max} = 12:14:30 - (-300\text{ s (clock)}) - 0\text{ s (entry)} = 12:19:30\text{ UTC}$$
       Total feasible occurrence window = 16 minutes (960 s).
     - **Model B (Record Latency Only / Remembered Time - Secondary Assumption)**: The user manually keyed in "13:14" as their remembered occurrence time, and the 5-minute delay merely affected transmission/database ingestion. Under Model B, entry delay does not shift the occurrence estimate, leaving the interval at $[12:08:30, \; 12:19:30]$ UTC (width = 11 minutes). Both models are evaluated in our sensitivity analysis.

### 1.3 Disambiguation of DST Folds via Ingestion Order (Case T-405)
Case T-405 records three events on 2025-10-26 with missing DST folds:
- `Alert_Received`: displayed 02:50
- `Investigation_Start`: displayed 02:05 (ingested after Alert)
- `Containment_Complete`: displayed 02:42 (ingested after Investigation Start)

Without temporal constraints, each timestamp generates a disjoint union of Fold 0 and Fold 1 intervals:
- Alert: $[00:49, 00:52] \cup [01:49, 01:52]$ UTC
- Inv: $[00:04, 00:07] \cup [01:04, 01:07]$ UTC
- Cont: $[00:41, 00:44] \cup [01:41, 01:44]$ UTC

We apply physical causality and the prompt's ingestion order ($t(\text{Alert}) < t(\text{Inv}) < t(\text{Cont})$):
1. If `Alert_Received` occurred in Fold 1 ($[01:49, 01:52]$ UTC), then `Investigation_Start` must be $> 01:49$ UTC. But `Investigation_Start` can only be in Fold 0 ($[00:04, 00:07]$) or Fold 1 ($[01:04, 01:07]$), both of which are strictly earlier than 01:49 UTC. This is a physical impossibility. Therefore, **`Alert_Received` MUST belong to Fold 0 (CEST)**, placing it in $[00:49:00, \; 00:52:00]$ UTC.
2. For `Investigation_Start` to follow `Alert_Received` ($> 00:52:00$ UTC), it cannot belong to Fold 0 ($[00:04, 00:07]$ UTC). Therefore, **`Investigation_Start` MUST belong to Fold 1 (CET)**, placing it in $[01:04:00, \; 01:07:00]$ UTC.
3. For `Containment_Complete` to follow `Investigation_Start` ($> 01:07:00$ UTC), it cannot belong to Fold 0 ($[00:41, 00:44]$ UTC). Therefore, **`Containment_Complete` MUST belong to Fold 1 (CET)**, placing it in $[01:41:00, \; 01:44:00]$ UTC.

Causal ingestion sequencing uniquely prunes the non-physical branches, collapsing the disjoint unions into single surviving intervals while preserving all clock and rounding uncertainties.

### 1.4 Missing Events, Inferred Starts, and Semantic Mismatches
1. **Inferred Investigation Start (Case T-404)**:
   `Investigation_Start` was not logged. A signed work record specifies an investigation duration $d_{\text{inv}} \in [18\text{ min}, 25\text{ min}] = [1080\text{ s}, 1500\text{ s}]$. `Investigation_Complete` is logged on HUB at 10:35:00 UTC ($[10:34:58.5, 10:35:01.5]$ UTC). By interval subtraction:
   $$t(\text{Investigation\_Start}) = t(\text{Investigation\_Complete}) - d_{\text{inv}}$$
   $$t_{\min} = 10:34:58.5 - 1500\text{ s} = 10:09:58.5\text{ UTC}$$
   $$t_{\max} = 10:35:01.5 - 1080\text{ s} = 10:17:01.5\text{ UTC}$$
   Feasible interval: $[10:09:58.5, \; 10:17:01.5]$ UTC (width = 423 s = 7 min 3 s).
2. **Missing Receipt Event vs. Alert Sent (Case T-407)**:
   `Alert_Received` is unrecorded; only `Alert_Sent` is logged at 14:00:00 HUB ($[13:59:58.5, 14:00:01.5]$ UTC). Delivery latency is bounded by $[0, 20\text{ min}] = [0, 1200\text{ s}]$, with no receipt acknowledgment.
   - *Strict Semantic Perspective*: S1 governs `Alert_Received`. Because an alert transmission is definitionally distinct from alert receipt, S1 is formally **INDETERMINATE** in the absence of receipt telemetry.
   - *Engineering Proxy Perspective*: Under an operational surrogate model, $t(\text{Alert\_Received}) = t(\text{Alert\_Sent}) + \text{latency} \in [13:59:58.5, 14:20:01.5]$ UTC. However, `Investigation_Start` is logged at 14:17:00 HUB ($[14:16:58.5, 14:17:01.5]$ UTC). Under the natural causal axiom that an investigation cannot begin before an alert is received, the receipt interval is causally truncated at $\max(t(\text{Investigation\_Start})) = 14:17:01.5$ UTC, yielding $[13:59:58.5, \; 14:17:01.5]$ UTC. We report findings under both perspectives.
3. **Missing NTP Diagnostics (Case T-409)**:
   OPS-BER lost NTP synchronization during 18:00 CEST. Under a strict governance standard requiring verified synchronization diagnostics, Case T-409 is **INDETERMINATE**. Under the provided weak engineering estimate of $\pm 12$ minutes, it yields feasible durations spanning $[-481.5\text{ s}, 1021.5\text{ s}]$ (evaluated as a **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**).

### 1.5 Classification Criteria
- **CERTAIN_PASS**: The entire feasible duration interval lies within the acceptance region ($D(e_1, e_2) \subseteq \text{Acceptance Region}$).
- **CERTAIN_VIOLATION**: The feasible duration interval is completely disjoint from the acceptance region ($D(e_1, e_2) \cap \text{Acceptance Region} = \emptyset$).
- **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**: The feasible duration interval partially overlaps the acceptance boundary ($D(e_1, e_2) \cap \text{Acceptance Region} \neq \emptyset$ and $D(e_1, e_2) \setminus \text{Acceptance Region} \neq \emptyset$).
- **INDETERMINATE**: Essential activity events are missing without surrogate bounds, or synchronization telemetry is unverified/unbounded.

### 4.3 Detailed Narrative of Material Classification Changes
1. **Case T-401**: Under base SLA S1, T-401 is a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION because its upper feasible limit of 991.5 s exceeds 900 s by 91.5 s. Adding a 2-minute tolerance increases the S1 limit to 1020 s, causing S1 to transition to **CERTAIN_PASS**. On the temporal profile, expanding $\zeta$ from 2 ($[240, 960]$) to 3 ($[60, 1140]$) fully covers $[718.5, 991.5]$ s, shifting the classification from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS**.
2. **Case T-402**: Under base SLA S1, T-402 is a **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** (minimum feasible duration is 958.5 s > 900 s). Granting a 2-minute tolerance (1020 s) softens this to **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**, as 958.5 s is now within the threshold, although the upper bound (1231.5 s) still exceeds it. Even at a 5-minute tolerance (1200 s), T-402 remains a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION because 1231.5 s > 1200 s. For S2, T-402 transitions from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS** at +2m ($2761.5 \le 2820$). On the profile, (Alert, Inv) transitions from CERTAIN_VIOLATION at $\zeta=1$ to POSSIBLE_VIOLATION at $\zeta=2, 3$; while (Alert, Cont) transitions directly from CERTAIN_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS** at $\zeta=2$.
3. **Case T-403**: Displays extreme sensitivity to vendor clock uncertainty ($\pm 180$ s). S1 transitions from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS** only at the +5m tolerance ($1141.5 \le 1200$). On the temporal profile, (Alert, Inv) remains a **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** across all $\zeta \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ because its upper bound of 1141.5 s exceeds the $\zeta=3$ upper limit (1140 s) by 1.5 seconds.
4. **Case T-404**: S2 is a baseline POSSIBLE_VIOLATION by a razor-thin margin of 3.0 seconds (maximum duration 2703.0 s vs. 2700.0 s). A 2-minute tolerance easily absorbs this, converting S2 to **CERTAIN_PASS**. S1 transitions from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS** at +5m. On the profile, (Inv, Cont) achieves **CERTAIN_PASS** at $\zeta=2$, and (Alert, Inv) achieves **CERTAIN_PASS** at $\zeta=3$.
5. **Case T-405**: DST disambiguation leaves a clean interval of $[720, 1080]$ s for S1. This transitions from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to **CERTAIN_PASS** at the +5m tolerance (1200 s), and on the profile transitions to **CERTAIN_PASS** at $\zeta=3$.
6. **Case T-406**: Materially demonstrates the impact of behavioral assumptions. Under Model A (occurrence shifted earlier), S2 is a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION (max 2911.5 s > 2700 s) and requires a +5m tolerance to reach **CERTAIN_PASS**; whereas under Model B (record-latency only), S2 is an invariant **CERTAIN_PASS** at base tolerance.
7. **Case T-408**: Exhibits total resilience to SLA tolerances for S2 and S3. S2 exceeds 2700 s by nearly 10 minutes (feasible minimum is 3297 s), remaining a **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** even at +5m (3000 s). S3 exceeds 36 hours by over 2.4 hours (feasible minimum is 38.41 hours = 138297 s), remaining an indisputable **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** under all SLA tolerances. However, under the statistical temporal profile, broadening the Gaussian acceptance region to $\zeta=3$ absorbs these delays, flipping (Inv, Cont), (Alert, Cont), and (Cont, Close) to **CERTAIN_PASS**. This highlights the profound distinction between hard legal deadlines and statistical process expectations.

## 7. Governance Recommendation

To eliminate temporal ambiguity and transition from probabilistic interval conformance to legally defensible process conformance, organizations must implement systemic time-governance controls across enterprise architectures.

### 7.1 Universal UTC Ingestion and Storage
- **Canonical Clock Mandate**: All enterprise logging daemons, message buses, databases, and microservices must generate and store timestamps strictly in UTC, formatted to ISO 8601 / RFC 3339 extended format:
  `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.sssZ`
- **Ban Local Time at Rest**: Local time representation must be relegated entirely to presentation/UI layers. Under no circumstances should database records or event broker messages persist timestamps in local wall-clock time without an explicit UTC offset.

### 7.2 Raw Timestamp and Time-Zone Offset Retention
- **Dual Timestamp Schema**: To support forensic replay and non-repudiation, event schemas must capture both:
  1. `timestamp_utc`: Normalized UTC ISO 8601 timestamp with sub-millisecond precision.
  2. `source_raw_timestamp`: Unaltered string as emitted by the originating system.
  3. `source_tz_offset`: Recorded time-zone offset (e.g., `+02:00`).

### 7.3 Source Offset Telemetry & Synchronized Clock Governance
- **Continuous Clock Auditing**: Every distributed node emitting process events must run an active NTP daemon (`chrony` or `systemd-timesyncd`) configured against stratum-1 enterprise time servers.
- **Embedded Telemetry Envelope**: Every emitted event must include clock synchronization metadata within its tracing context:
  ```json
  {
    "event_id": "evt-98234-a8",
    "timestamp_utc": "2026-06-01T08:14:22.145Z",
    "clock_telemetry": {
      "ntp_synced": true,
      "stratum": 2,
      "offset_ms": 4.12,
      "root_dispersion_ms": 1.85,
      "uncertainty_bound_ms": 12.0
    }
  }
  ```
- **Out-of-Sync Circuit Breakers**: Nodes whose clock uncertainty bound exceeds $\pm 1.0$ s must be flagged as degraded in APM dashboards, and their emitted logs marked with `clock_trust_level: DEGRADED`.

### 7.4 Resolution & Discretization Metadata
- System registries must catalog the native timestamp resolution and rounding mode of every event source:
  - `resolution`: `MILLISECOND`, `SECOND`, `TEN_SECOND`, or `MINUTE`.
  - `rounding_mode`: `EXACT`, `FLOOR`, `CEILING`, or `NEAREST`.
  Process mining ingestion pipelines must automatically incorporate these parameters into dynamic interval bounds during conformance checking.

### 7.5 Strict Activity Lifecycle State Machines
- **Mandatory Lifecycle Pairing**: Enforce transactional logging of start and complete events. Single completion events must not serve as proxies for multi-state activities.
- **Forbidden Duration Back-Calculation**: Prohibit the practice of logging only completion events and inferring starts through manual text annotations (as observed in T-404). If work is performed, systems must enforce an explicit `Investigation_Start` state transition.

### 7.6 DST Fold Disambiguation Standards
- For legacy systems that continue to log in local time, the logging engine must be upgraded to Python standard library `datetime` with `fold` attributes (PEP 495) or standard library ZoneInfo.
- During fall DST transitions, local timestamps between 02:00:00 and 02:59:59 must explicitly record `fold=0` (daylight summer time, first pass) or `fold=1` (standard winter time, second pass), or append the unambiguous numerical offset (`+02:00` vs. `+01:00`).

### 7.7 Causal Identifiers and Distributed Tracing
- All incident-response pipelines must propagate W3C Trace Context headers (`traceparent`, `tracestate`) across system boundaries.
- Events must maintain parent-child relationship identifiers (`parent_event_id`). Ingestion engines must reject or flag any event sequence where an alleged child event precedes its parent, automatically enforcing causal monotonicity $t(\text{parent}) \le t(\text{child})$ without relying on clock alignment.

### 7.8 Enforcement Policy for Interval Overlap in SLA Compliance
Organizations must adopt a clear, tiered contractual policy defining how interval overlap is handled in SLA enforcement:

1. **Tier 1: Conclusive Non-Conformance (`CERTAIN_VIOLATION`)**:
   - *Condition*: The minimum feasible duration strictly exceeds the contractual threshold ($\min(D) > \text{SLA}$).
   - *Policy*: Automatic contractual breach. Financial penalties, vendor SLA credits, or regulatory incident reports are triggered immediately.
2. **Tier 2: Ambiguous Conformance (`POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`)**:
   - *Condition*: The feasible interval straddles the SLA threshold ($\min(D) \le \text{SLA} < \max(D)$).
   - *Legal & Enforcement Policy*:
     - **In Dubio Pro Reo (Vendor/Operator Protection)**: In commercial contracts, penalties cannot be assessed on ambiguous evidence alone. The burden of proof rests on the auditor.
     - **Mandatory Evidence Hardening**: Triggers a mandatory 5-business-day discovery window where the audited entity must produce secondary telemetry (NTP logs, firewall flow records, sub-second audit traces) to narrow the uncertainty window.
     - **Regulatory Safety Standard**: In high-assurance domains (e.g., critical infrastructure, healthcare, NIS2 compliance), a `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` is treated as a provisional breach requiring root-cause justification and preventive action.
3. **Tier 3: Conclusive Conformance (`CERTAIN_PASS`)**:
   - *Condition*: The maximum feasible duration satisfies the SLA threshold ($\max(D) \le \text{SLA}$).
   - *Policy*: Fully certified compliance. No further evidentiary review required.
