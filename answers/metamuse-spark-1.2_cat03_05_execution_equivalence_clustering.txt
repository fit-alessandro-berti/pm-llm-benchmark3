## 1. Equivalence design space

We view each execution as a small OCEL fragment centred on a lead `Order` object `O*`. An execution is a tuple `E = (Ev, Obj, E2O, O2O, <)` where `Ev` is the ordered event set, `Obj` typed objects with a flag `ext(v)  {local, EXT}`, `E2O  Ev × Obj × Qual`, `O2O  Obj × Obj × Qual`, and `<` is the observed total order by timestamp (standing in for a partial order when concurrency exists).

No clustering on raw identifiers is object-centric: `O1O2` and `I11I21` carry no business meaning. `EXT-OA`/`EXT-IA` do.

### R1: Activity-sequence equivalence — `_act`

**Definition.** `E _act E'` iff the sequence of activity labels ordered by `<` is identical: `[a(e1),...,a(en)] = [a(e1'),...,a(en')]`. No object information is consulted. Two `Add Item` events are equal regardless of which `Item` they refer to; `Pack Package` with 1 vs. 2 items is equal.

**Preserves:** multiset and order of activity names, case length modulo labels, repetitions counted as repetitions.

**Discards:** all object types, identities, E2O incidence, cardinalities, O2O topology, qualifiers, external flags, attributes.

**Properties:** Equivalence relation in the strict sense: reflexive, symmetric, transitive. A classic trace equivalence on the flattened log on `Order`.

**Concurrency:** If two events share a timestamp, `_act` must impose a totalisation to remain transitive. Common repair is to sort tied events lexicographically by activity name or to define equivalence on multisets per time-tick. This is lossy: concurrent `Pack Package` x2 vs. sequential `Pack` then `Dispatch` then `Pack` become indistinguishable if collapsed to a bag. Better defined on the partial order `` : `E _act^po E'` iff transitive closures of label-posets are isomorphic. In X1–X8 timestamps are total, so both formulations coincide.

*Usefulness/Limit:* Cheap baseline. Fails as object-centric: it groups X1 with X5/X6/X7 despite completely different resource sharing and substitution semantics.

### R2: Typed structural equivalence under renaming — `_struct`

**Definition.** `E _struct E'` iff there exist bijections `: EvEv'` and `: ObjObj'` such that:

1. `a(e)=a((e))` and `e< f  (e) <' (f)`
2. `type(o)=type((o))` and `ext(o)=ext((o))` (externality preserved)
3. `(e,o,q)E2O  ((e),(o),q)E2O'` including qualifier `q` if present
4. `(o1,o2,q)O2O  ((o1),(o2),q)O2O'` including qualifier `q`

In words: E2O and O2O graphs are labelled isomorphic, events ordered identically, after renaming local ids but **not** collapsing distinct typed nodes or qualifiers. Cardinalities are preserved structurally: `Pack Package{Item:I11,I12}` has degree 2 to `Item`; `Pack{Item:I31}` has degree 1 – therefore not isomorphic.

A controlled variant `_struct^{-q}` drops condition on `q`, treating O2O/E2O as unlabeled. `_struct^{-ext}` drops `ext`. We define canonical `_struct` as qualifier- and ext-sensitive; variants are useful for sensitivity analysis.

**Preserves:** activity labels + order, object types and multiplicities per event, E2O incidence structure, O2O structure including qualified relations like `removed_before_dispatch` or `replaces`, and whether an object is external to the lead Order component.

**Discards:** concrete identifier strings of local objects, order of listing inside an event (e.g., `Item:I11,I12` vs. `I12,I11` is the same set), attribute values like amounts or timestamps beyond order.

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, transitive iff `,` are required to be type- and qualifier-preserving bijections. If qualifiers are ignored only for comparison but kept in data, transitivity still holds. If ad-hoc heuristic matching is used instead of isomorphism, transitivity can break.

**Concurrency:** Replace `<` by its poset `` (causal order). Two executions with concurrent `Add Item` events have isomorphic posets with two incomparable `Add Item` nodes, regardless of listing order. `_struct` then requires poset isomorphism, not list equality, making X1 vs X2 equivalent despite opposite identifier order in the input listing.

*This is the object-centric analogue of exact structural equivalence.*

### R3: Business-semantic equivalence — `_biz` (preferred)

**Definition.** `E _biz E'` iff they share the same **lifecycle motif** after canonicalisation of R2, abstracting away identifier counts that do not change the business category but retaining motifs that change risk/control:

Motifs evaluated on the canonical signature (see §2):

- `logistics_pattern`: {`single-package` | `split-packages-consolidated-delivery` | `split-packages-split-deliveries` | `shared-package`}
- `financial_pattern`: {`isolated-invoice` | `shared-invoice` | `split-payments`}
- `item_lifecycle`: {`normal` | `substituted_before_dispatch` | `returned_repacked`}
- `collaboration`: ` ext object  (ext  E2O(ev)  O2O closure)`  `collaborative` else `isolated`

Two executions are `_biz` iff the tuple `(logistics_pattern, financial_pattern, item_lifecycle, collaboration)` is equal. Within a pattern, cardinalities beyond the threshold that defines the pattern are abstracted: e.g., 2 vs. 3 packages both map to `split-packages`, 2 vs. 3 payments both map to `split-payments` if they settle the same invoice. Qualifiers that imply a different motif (`replaces`, `removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces` on packages) are not abstracted.

**Preserves:** Business-relevant multiplicities (one vs many), external sharing, qualified lifecycle state changes, presence of rework loops (`Return  Inspect  Repack  Redispatch`).

**Discards:** Exact identifiers, exact order of interchangeable `Add Item` events, exact number of items if >1 not causing split, linear extensions inside a motif (e.g., interleaving of `Pack`/`Dispatch` irrelevant if motif is `split-packages-split-deliveries`).

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, transitive by construction (equivalence induced by motif function `m(E)`). Coarser than `_struct`: `E _struct E'  E _biz E'` but not conversely. Borderline pairs are made explicit: e.g., X3 vs X4 are distinct under `_struct` but can be merged under a `split-fulfillment` motif if financial splitting is deprioritised – decision must be documented.

**Concurrency:** Motifs are defined on the partial order. `Pack` and `Dispatch` interleaving does not change `split-packages` motif; concurrent `Add Item` events map to same motif as sequential.

### R4 (auxiliary): Weak behavioral equivalence — `_behav`

`E _behav E'` iff the languages of object-type projections are equivalent modulo stuttering, ignoring exact cardinalities but preserving causal dependencies: single vs. consolidated vs. split delivery distinguished, single vs. multiple payments collapsed if `sum(amount)=invoice.total`. Useful to test robustness to `treat payment multiplicity as amount attribute` perturbation (§7). We use R4 only for sensitivity discussion.

## 2. Canonical signatures

Preferred relation is `_biz`, which is built on a canonical `_struct` signature then abstracted to motifs. The canonical `_struct` signature is identifier-invariant but lossless except for identifier strings.

Properties: sorting and multiset hashing eliminate renaming; external flag and qualifiers are hashed; repeated activities create distinct event nodes with order edges.

```python
def canonical_struct_signature(exec):
    # exec: {events: [{id, activity, ts, e2o: [(obj_id, obj_type, qual, ext_flag)]}],
    #        objects: {obj_id: (obj_type, ext_flag)},
    #        o2o: [(src_id, tgt_id, qual)] }
    
    # 1. Normalize events: sort by (ts, activity) for total order;
    #    for partial order, compute transitive reduction dag and sort topologically
    ev_sorted = sorted(exec.events, key=lambda e: (e.ts, e.activity, e.id))

    # 2. Canonicalize object ids per type: replace local ids by position in sorted order
    #    EXT ids keep prefix EXT but are normalized per type+appearance order
    obj_by_type = {}
    for o_id, (otyp, is_ext) in exec.objects.items():
        obj_by_type.setdefault((otyp, is_ext), []).append(o_id)
    # deterministic renaming
    remap = {}
    for (otyp, is_ext), ids in sorted(obj_by_type.items()):
        for i, old in enumerate(sorted(ids)):
            remap[old] = f"{otyp}#{'EXT' if is_ext else 'LOC'}_{i}"

    # 3. E2O signature: for each event, bag of (obj_type, ext_flag, qual), sorted
    e2o_sigs = []
    for e in ev_sorted:
        bag = sorted([(exec.objects[oid][0], exec.objects[oid][1], q) 
                      for oid,q in e.e2o])
        # keep multiplicities: bag length matters
        e2o_sigs.append( (e.activity, tuple(bag)) )

    # 4. O2O signature: set of (src_type+ext, tgt_type+ext, qual), sorted
    o2o_sigs = sorted([
        (exec.objects[s][0], exec.objects[s][1], 
         exec.objects[t][0], exec.objects[t][1], q)
        for s,t,q in exec.o2o
    ])

    # 5. Order signature: for total order keep sequence; for partial order keep DAG edges as activity pairs
    order_sig = tuple(e.activity for e in ev_sorted)  # or tuple of edges for poset
    # inclusion of interleaving: edge list between event indices
    dag_sig = tuple((e2o_sigs[i][0], e2o_sigs[j][0]) for i,j in order_edges(ev_sorted))

    return (tuple(e2o_sigs), tuple(o2o_sigs), order_sig, dag_sig)

def motif_abstract(struct_sig):
    # business abstraction on top of struct signature
    e2o_sigs, o2o_sigs, order_sig, dag_sig = struct_sig
    # count packages, deliveries, payments, external involvement, qualifiers
    n_pack_events = order_sig.count("Pack Package")
    n_dispatch_events = order_sig.count("Dispatch")
    n_payment_events = order_sig.count("Record Payment")
    has_ext = any("EXT" in str(x) for x in e2o_sigs+o2o_sigs)
    has_removed = any(q=="removed_before_dispatch" for *_,q in o2o_sigs) or \
                  any(q=="removed_before_dispatch" for sig in e2o_sigs for *_,q in [sig])
    has_return = "Return Item" in order_sig
    has_replaces_pkg = any(q=="replaces" for *_,q in o2o_sigs)
    # simplified derivation
    if has_ext:
        logistics = "shared-package" if has_ext and n_pack_events==1 else "shared"
    else:
        if n_pack_events==1 and n_dispatch_events==1: logistics="single-package"
        elif n_pack_events>1 and n_dispatch_events==1: logistics="split-packages-consolidated"
        elif n_pack_events>1 and n_dispatch_events>1: logistics="split-packages-split"
        else: logistics="other"
    financial = "shared-invoice" if has_ext and "Issue Invoice" in str(e2o_sigs) else \
                "split-payments" if n_payment_events>1 else "isolated-invoice"
    lifecycle = "returned_repacked" if has_return else \
                "substituted_before_dispatch" if has_removed else "normal"
    collaboration = "collaborative" if has_ext else "isolated"
    return (logistics, financial, lifecycle, collaboration)

def canonical_biz_signature(exec):
    return motif_abstract(canonical_struct_signature(exec))
```

Invariance: `canonical_struct_signature` is unchanged by permutation of local ids or by listing `I11,I12` vs `I12,I11` because bags are sorted. It changes if a qualifier appears, an external flag flips, multiplicity changes, or order changes. `canonical_biz_signature` is idempotent under R3.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

**C1 – Clean Isolated Monolithic (X1, X2)** 
*Signature:* `single-package (1 Pack 2 Items, 1 Delivery 1 Package) + isolated-invoice single-payment + normal lifecycle + isolated`
*Behavior:* textbook Order-to-Cash: pick-pack-ship together, invoice locally, single settlement.
*Risks/Opportunities:* Lowest handling cost and variance; baseline for SLA and automation. Opportunity: straight-through processing, benchmark for deviation.
*Flattening failure:* Flattening on `Order` loses ItemPackage containment or on `Package` duplicates Order events, but for C1 flattening distortion minimal because cardinalities are 1:1. Still, payment-amount analysis flattened on Order hides nothing; on Invoice it would duplicate Order events.
*Stability:* Very stable. Repair choices (ignore qualifier, ignore EXT, collapse repeats) keep X1/X2 together. Only perturbation collapsing Item multiplicities would still keep C1.

**C2 – Split Preparation, Consolidated Shipment (X3)**
*Signature:* `split-packages-consolidated (2 Packs 1 Item each 1 Delivery 2 Packages)`
*Behavior:* Items prepared separately (e.g., different warehouse zones) but shipped together – pick-split, pack-split, transport-consolidated.
*Risks/Opportunities:* Extra handling, risk of incomplete consolidation, opportunity for parallel picking. Delivery cost similar to C1 but warehouse cost higher.
*Flattening failure:* Flattening on Order shows `[Pack, Pack, Dispatch]` correctly; flattening on Package creates two cases each with `Add Item` duplication and misleading `Dispatch` concurrency. Traditional case-id=Delivery would merge two packages incorrectly.
*Stability:* Stable as motif. If Pack/Dispatch order treated as partial order, still distinct from C1. If `max_objs` truncates to 1 package, collapses to C1 falsely.

**C3 – Fragmented Fulfillment & Settlement (X4)**
*Signature:* `split-packages-split (2 Packs 2 Deliveries) + split-payments (2 Record Payments partial+final)`
*Behavior:* Each item packed and shipped independently, invoice settled in installments – fragmented logistics and financial handling.
*Risks/Opportunities:* Highest cost-to-serve, double shipping, payment risk and reconciliation overhead, but enables partial delivery for out-of-stock items. Opportunity to detect cash-flow or credit-risk patterns.
*Flattening failure:* Flattening on Order hides which payment settles which delivery; flattening on Invoice duplicates logistics events 2× and on Payment splits into two cases with incomplete logistics.
*Stability:* Relatively stable but sensitive to payment abstraction: if payment multiplicity treated as amount attribute, it would merge with a single-payment split-logistics motif; if qualifiers ignored, unchanged.

**C4 – Collaborative / Shared Execution (X5 logistics, X6 financial)**
*Signature:* `collaborative=True`; C4a: `shared-package (Pack covers EXT-IA + EXT-OA)` ; C4b: `shared-invoice (Invoice bills EXT-OB)`
*Behavior:* Resources spanning Orders: X5 shares physical package/delivery (co-packing, pooling), X6 shares financial document (group invoice, joint account).
*Risks/Opportunities:* Cross-order dependencies, contagion risk (delay in one order blocks shared delivery), allocation disputes, compliance about revenue recognition. Opportunity for bundling discounts, consolidated freight.
*Flattening failure:* Catastrophically misrepresented by standard flattening: centering on Order either drops EXT objects (if filtered) or injects events from another Order (`EXT-OA`’s items) without causal context, creating phantom correlations. `Order`-flattened trace appears identical to C1, hiding shared risk.
*Stability:* Unstable if external objects ignored – both collapse to C1 (see §7). Stable if EXT flag preserved, even under qualifier-ignore.

**C5 – Substitution Before Dispatch (X7)**
*Signature:* `substituted_before_dispatch (O2O qual removed_before_dispatch, I72 replaces I71, Dispatch E2O contains only I72)`
*Behavior:* Item I71 packed then removed; dispatch carries only replacement I72. Activity labels hide substitution.
*Risks/Opportunities:* Return-to-stock, waste, potential compliance issue if invoice still bills removed item. Opportunity to detect picking errors or product substitution policy.
*Flattening failure:* Flattening on Item or Package collapses `replaces` semantics to simple co-occurrence; Order-flattened sequence identical to C1, so substitution invisible without qualifier.
*Stability:* Fragile: ignoring O2O qualifiers merges C5 into C1 (§7), which is precisely why qualifiers must be first-class for compliance.

**C6 – Return & Rework Loop (X8)**
*Signature:* `returned_repacked (Return Item  Inspect  Pack P82 replaces P81  Redispatch) + split-packages-split logically`
*Behavior:* Post-delivery exception: item dispatched, returned, inspected, repacked and redispatched – rework loop extends lifecycle.
*Risks/Opportunities:* Highest exception handling cost, customer satisfaction risk, reverse logistics. Identifies quality or damage root causes.
*Flattening failure:* Flattening on Order interleaves return loop as linear activities but loses which package is replaced; flattening on Package splits into P81 vs P82 cases, disconnecting the replacement relation.
*Stability:* Stable unless repeated same-label events collapsed (`Pack`×2, `Dispatch`×2) – would then collapse toward C3 or C1 incorrectly. Stable under EXT/qualifier ignore (still distinct due to activity `Return`).

## 5. Borderline cases

**X1 vs X2:** *Defensibly same or distinct.* Under `_act` and `_struct` (with bag sorting) and `_biz`, they are identical: listing order `I22 then I21` is serialization artefact, not business difference. Under a naive string-equality implementation that hashes `Item:I21,I22` order-dependently or that hashes raw object ids `I11I21`, they appear distinct – an artefact of non-canonical keys. Disagreement signals need for multiset + renaming canonicalisation. Concurrency view reinforces sameness: two `Add Item` events are concurrent picks; list order is arbitrary linearisation.

**X1 vs X5:** *Defensibly same under label-only, different under object-centric.* `_act` groups them (identical 7 labels). `_struct`/`_biz` separate them because `Pack` E2O degree is 3 vs 2 and `ext=True`. A business analyst ignoring collaboration might merge them as “single-package” for capacity planning (transport sees one package either way), while a risk/compliance analyst must separate (shared delivery means delay propagation). This is the canonical example of flattening illusion.

**X1 vs X6:** *Same dilemma, but financial.* `_act` and even `_struct^{-ext}` (if EXT ignored) group them; `_struct` and `_biz` separate. At R2, local multiplicities around O6 are identical to X1 (1 Pack 2 Items,1 Dispatch), so a logistics-typed isomorphism that drops financial EXT would merge, while a full O2O/E2O isomorphism splits. `_biz` treats X6 as collaborative financial risk, distinct from ideal. A reporting use that aggregates revenue per Order without allocation will double-count or misallocate X6 if merged.

**X1 vs X7:** *Label vs qualifier.* `_act` (labels only) and `_struct^{-q}` (ignore qualifiers) merge X1/X7 – both appear as single-package dispatch of 2 items. Qualifier-sensitive `_struct` separates: Dispatch E2O in X7 contains only `I72`, and O2O has `removed_before_dispatch`. Under `_biz`, this is a different lifecycle motif (substitution). Two defensible business positions: “counts as successful single shipment” (merge) vs “counts as substitution exception” (split). Compliance monitoring must split; high-level throughput reporting may justifiably merge if substitution is considered normal variant.

**X3 vs X4:** *Gradation of fragmentation.* Under `_act` they are different lengths (8 vs 10 events). Under `_struct` distinct (1 vs 2 Dispatch,1 vs 2 Payments). Under `_biz`, they can be either merged as `split-fulfillment` (abstracting delivery/payment fragmentation) or split as we do (C2 vs C3). Merging is defensible if research question is “was picking split?”; splitting is defensible if question is “was fulfillment fragmented end-to-end including settlement?”. Interleaving `PackDispatchPackDispatch` in X4 vs `PackPackDispatch` in X3 is partially ordered; if order treated as partial order, X4 still distinct due to causal dependency Packagingits own Delivery, not all packagessingle delivery.

**X4 vs X8:** *Both have two Packs and two Dispatches – but motif differs.* `_act` separates (X4 has two `Record Payment`, X8 has `Return, Inspect`). A coarse `_biz` that only counts Pack/Dispatch multiplicities would merge them as “split-packages-split”. A refined `_biz` that distinguishes `replaces, return` qualifiers and activity `Return Item` separates: X4 is planned split, X8 is rework loop (temporal gap with return). Ignoring `Return` or collapsing repeated labels can conflate them, overstating planned splits and understating quality issues. Conversely, abstracting X8’s replacement semantics as just another split could be defensible for freight-cost analysis (two shipments either way).

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

`pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, lead_type="Order")` (OCEL 2.0) builds **connected components** centred on the lead object type, then hashes each component to a key for exact clustering.

*What the key preserves:* In current implementation, for each component it collects (a) ordered list of events with activity names, (b) for each event the multiset of related object types and the **raw object identifiers**, and (c) object-type counts and E2O incidence per event. Effectively it implements a strict `_struct` with **identifiers as literals**, ordered by identifier string, without external-flag special handling and without qualifier abstraction. O2O qualifiers, if present in OCEL 2.0 `o2o` table, are often not included in the key unless explicitly configured. E2O qualifiers are typically included if stored. Order is total order by timestamp.

*Why raw ids make clusters too fine:* Local ids `I11` vs `I21` or `P11` vs `P21` make isomorphicExecutions hash to different keys, as do `O1` vs `O2`. Thus X1 and X2 – business-identical – fall in different clusters. Large logs explode to nearly singleton clusters, defeating segmentation. External ids `EXT-IA` are also treated as distinct values, but the distinction local vs external is lost: two executions both sharing with *some* external order but different external id will be considered distinct, even though business motif is same (“shared”).

*Canonicalisation / post-processing:* Hash on abstract signatures, not raw ids.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

# 1. Build lead-type components and exact clusters
ocel = pm4py.read_ocel2("orders.jsonocel")
clusters_exact = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, lead_type="Order")
# clusters_exact: dict[key -> list[component_ids]] where key includes raw ids

# 2. Custom canonicalization: re-key by biz motif
def biz_key_for_component(comp):
    # comp is sub-OCEL with events/objects/o2o
    struct_sig = canonical_struct_signature(comp)  # from §2
    return canonical_biz_signature_from_struct(struct_sig)

agg = defaultdict(list)
for key, comp_ids in clusters_exact.items():
    for cid in comp_ids:
        comp = extract_component(ocel, cid)  # helper to slice OCEL
        agg[biz_key_for_component(comp)].append(cid)

# Alternative: bypass raw key, directly cluster by canonical signature
from pm4py.ocel.utils import ocel_connected_components
components = ocel_connected_components(ocel, lead_type="Order")
by_canonical = defaultdict(list)
for c in components:
    k = canonical_biz_signature(c)  # identifier-invariant
    by_canonical[k].append(c)

# 3. Inspect and optionally merge collaborative subclusters
# merge C4a and C4b if analysis wants single collaborative cluster
collaborative_key = lambda k: k[3]=="collaborative"
```

We keep `lead_type="Order"` fixed; connected-component extraction must use `ObjectType:Order` as centre, otherwise shared objects `EXT-IA/V61` would fuse multiple orders into one huge component (see `max_objs` below).

*When exact equivalence is preferable:* For **conformance & audit** (exact replay, data-quality debugging, pinpointing duplicate events), for **deduplication** and **legal traceability** where identifier equality matters, and as a reproducible baseline before abstraction.

*When coarser business clustering is preferable:* For **operational segmentation**, capacity planning, variant analysis, and reporting where local ids are noise and motif (single vs split, shared vs isolated) is signal – i.e., all sections 4 use-cases except fine-grained compliance.

*`max_objs` / large components:* `cluster_equivalent_ocel` may limit component size via `max_objs` or `max_events` to avoid memory blow-up. Executions with many related objects (e.g., bulk order with 50 items, or a shared package linking dozens of orders via `EXT` objects) produce giant connected components that span multiple lead objects if external bridging is not cut. Truncation or lead-type filtering can then split or distort a true business case: X5 would either be truncated to look like X1 (external item dropped) or merged into a multi-order super-component containing `EXT-OA`’s full lifecycle, which `cluster_equivalent_ocel` would either reject as too large or mis-cluster. Practically, set `lead_type="Order"` strictly, filter O2O to 1-hop from lead Order before clustering, or preprocess to mark `is_external` to exclude cross-order closure, otherwise shared collaborations become outliers.

## 7. Robustness analysis

| Perturbation | Effect on `_struct` / `_biz` | Cluster movement |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignore all O2O qualifiers** (`removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`) | `_struct^{-q}` merges executions differing only by qualifier. E2O-only structure kept. | **C5 (X7)  C1**. X7’s `Pack` degree 2 and Dispatch E2O with Item still distinguishes it from X1 if E2O item-in-dispatch preserved; but if qualifier alone distinguished, X7 now appears as normal single-package. Systematically hides substitution. X8’s `P82 replaces P81` qualifier lost, but `Return/Inspect` activities keep X8 separate, so C6 unchanged. |
| **Ignore external objects** (drop `EXT` flag or filter `EXT-*` objects) | Collaboration dimension erased. Any `ext` difference disappears. | **C4a (X5)  C1**, **C4b (X6)  C1**. Both collapse to isolated motif; shared logistics/financial risk disappears. Other clusters unchanged. This is the most dangerous simplification for cross-order analysis. |
| **Collapse repeated same-label events** (deduplicate consecutive same activity or treat multiset as set) | Multiplicity information lost: `Pack×2  Pack×1`, `Payment×2  Payment×1`, `Dispatch×2  Dispatch×1`. | **X3  C1**, **X4  C1 or C6**: split patterns erased, fragmented and rework clusters conflate with monolithic. X8’s two Packs/Dispatches also collapse, merging C6 toward C3/C1. Activity-sequence clustering alone would already do this. |
| **Treat Pack/Dispatch order as partial order** (instead of total order) | Linear extensions become equivalent if causal dependencies preserved. | Within-motif stability improves: X4’s interleaving `PackDispatchPackDispatch` vs. hypothetical `PackPackDispatchDispatch` would become equivalent (both represent split-split). X3 (`PackPackDispatch`) remains distinct from X4 because DAG edges differ (2 Packs1 Dispatch vs 1-1 mapping). No collapse of C1/C2/C3; reduces sensitivity to timestamp noise. If order ignored entirely, X3 vs X4 could incorrectly merge – so keep DAG, not set. |
| **Treat payment multiplicity as amount attribute** (structure  attribute `totalAmount`, `nPayments` ignored) | `split-payments` motif abstracted away; financial pattern reduces to `invoice-settled`. | **C3 (X4)  C2-like**: X4 becomes `split-packages-split + isolated-invoice` (like a split-logistics version of X3 but with split delivery). Distinction between X3 and X4 narrows to delivery consolidation only. For finance team this merges importantly different cash-flow behaviours; for logistics team this is acceptable abstraction. |

Overall, `_biz` is robust to order linearisation noise if partial-order aware, but fragile to dropping EXT or qualifiers or collapsing repetitions – each corresponds to a deliberate business abstraction and must be chosen per analysis goal, not by default.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

No single granularity optimizes completeness, interpretability and actionability simultaneously. The hierarchy `_act  _biz  _struct` (coarse  fine) trades off.

**Compliance monitoring  `_struct` (qualifier- and EXT-sensitive, partial-order).** Must distinguish X7 substitution and X6 shared invoice for control violations, and must not hide cross-order dependencies (X5). Exact structural isomorphism gives reproducible evidence, auditableReplay, and detects qualifier misuse (`removed_before_dispatch` without proper authorization). Business abstraction would hide violations.

**Operational segmentation  `_biz` (motif level, ~5–6 clusters).** For capacity, cost-to-serve and SLA, grouping by logistics/financial motif (C1–C6) provides manageable, actionable segments: monolithic vs split vs collaborative vs rework. Exact structural splitting (e.g., X1 vs X2 separate) or payment-count splitting creates too many micro-variants for staffing or routing decisions.

**Root-cause analysis  `_biz` refined with `_struct` drill-down.** Start with motif clusters (C5 substitution, C6 return) to isolate exception families, then drill into exact structural signatures to find causal configurations (e.g., which package was substituted, which delivery failed). Pure `_act` lumps root causes; pure `_struct` fragments the exception set too finely to see pattern.

**Descriptive reporting  `_biz` with quantitative attributes.** Reports need stable, business-readable variants plus KPIs (avg handling time, ship cost). Motif clusters provide consistent denominators; exact clusters produce unreadable sparse reporting, while activity-only clusters misrepresent object-centric reality (shared packages appear isolated  freight cost under-allocated).

*Why no single notion wins:* Fineness increases precision (good for audit) but decreases support and interpretability (bad for segmentation/reporting). Coarseness increases coverage and business meaning but erases compliance-relevant distinctions (qualifiers, externality). The identifier-renaming canonicalisation is universally required; beyond that, the choice of what to abstract (qualifiers, EXT, repetitions, payment count, order linearisation) encodes a business value judgement. Best practice is to compute the canonical `_struct` signature once (lossless, rename-invariant), then derive multiple `_biz` abstractions by projection, and explicitly document which abstraction was used per analysis purpose rather than relying on raw `pm4py` exact keys or activity-only flattening.
