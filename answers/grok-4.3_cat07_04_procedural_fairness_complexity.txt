# 1. Fairness dimensions
Procedural fairness can be evaluated across multiple dimensions. Outcome parity compares raw approval, appeal, and rework rates across groups but must be interpreted only after case-mix adjustment. Adjusted outcome disparity measures differences in approval probability, cycle time, supervisor review probability, and appeal probability after controlling for risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month. Procedural burden captures the frequency and sequence of extra activities such as Request Missing Documents, Language Support Contact, and Accommodation Review. Timeliness assesses waiting times at specific routing points and overall cycle-time distributions. Consistency of routing examines whether groups follow comparable variants after stratification on observable complexity. Quality/accessibility of support evaluates whether language and accommodation steps introduce additional handovers or delays that affect applicant experience. Appeal/rework burden tracks the incidence and duration of post-decision activity. Resource-allocation fairness considers whether certain groups are disproportionately routed to overloaded specialists or experience more fragmented assignments.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy
Stratify or match on risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month to isolate residual group differences. Compare variant distributions and path frequencies within strata using pm4py.get_variants and pm4py.filter_variants. Perform resource-assignment comparison via pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network and pm4py.discover_working_together_network, restricted to matched cases. Decompose waiting times with pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time followed by pm4py.get_service_time at each transition. Conduct sensitivity analysis by re-running adjusted models after removing or coarsening risk_score_band and documentation_completeness. Perform subgroup checks by region and channel to detect effect heterogeneity. Use pm4py.discover_log_skeleton or pm4py.discover_declare to test whether procedural constraints differ across strata after matching.

# 3. Path and resource disparities
Variant V1 (direct path) occurs far less often for Groups B, C, and D, while V2, V4, and V5 (which insert Request Missing Documents, Language Support Contact, or Accommodation Review) are markedly more common for those groups. These insertions align with documented higher shares of incomplete documentation, language preference, and accommodation requests, yet the adjusted cycle-time penalties remain after controlling for those factors. Waiting-time gaps are largest at Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment (3.8 days) and Accommodation Review  Eligibility Assessment (4.7 days), consistent with limited bilingual and specialist capacity. Group B cases show more handovers involving bilingual staff, and Group C cases more handovers involving accommodation specialists. Resource concentration on only seven bilingual caseworkers and five accommodation specialists explains part of the observed delays. The elevated use of V3 for Group D is largely justified by its higher risk_score_band share, but the persistence of longer waits and higher appeal rates after adjustment raises concern.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms
Legitimate mechanisms include higher rates of incomplete documentation and language requests that correctly trigger additional review steps, and higher risk_score_band that correctly routes Group D cases to Fraud Review. Concerning mechanisms include capacity bottlenecks that disproportionately affect applicants routed through language or accommodation steps, creating extra waiting time and handovers not fully explained by measured complexity. Another mechanism is regional backlog interacting with in-person channel share, lengthening waits for Groups B and C. A fourth mechanism is possible historical encoding in risk_score_band that inflates measured risk for Group D, leading to more intensive routing even when underlying eligibility is comparable.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes
Risk_score_band may embed prior disparities, so controlling for it risks masking rather than isolating unfairness. Documentation_completeness can be affected by language barriers, making it a partially endogenous covariate. Daily-granularity workload data cannot distinguish whether delays stem from specialist scarcity or assignment practices. Appeal_flag records occurrence but not quality of explanation or dignity of treatment. Household_size_band and region do not capture unmeasured applicant needs such as digital literacy or transportation barriers. Prior_case_count may reflect repeated exposure to burdensome procedures rather than independent complexity.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects
Adding capacity for language support or accommodation review can reduce waiting times at those steps but requires sustained funding and may temporarily increase handovers during onboarding. Introducing service-level targets for support steps can improve timeliness yet risks incentivizing rushed reviews that compromise decision quality. Routing redesign to reduce handovers (for example, allowing bilingual staff to complete eligibility assessment) may shorten cycle time but could concentrate workload on already scarce specialists. Decision-explanation improvements can lower appeal rates without altering approval probabilities but add staff time per case. Monitoring adjusted disparities on an ongoing basis supports accountability yet may lead to over-correction if unmeasured confounders remain. Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness for measurement bias can refine future controls but requires external validation data that may not exist.
