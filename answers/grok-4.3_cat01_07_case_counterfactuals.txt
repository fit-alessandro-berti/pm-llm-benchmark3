## 1. Feasibility model
```
Register  Triage  {Identity Check, Document Check (after Originals if required)}
{Identity Check, Document Check}  Assessment
Assessment  Compliance Review (high-risk/high-value only)
Assessment  Decision (non-high-risk) | Compliance Review  Decision (high-risk/high-value)
Decision  Notify
Assessment and Compliance Review must be performed by different resources (four-eyes)
Request More Info  new Document Check  new Assessment (and new/reaffirmed Compliance if high-risk)
```

```
- Identity and Document checks may run concurrently when originals not required.
- High-risk/high-value cases require final Compliance Review after latest Assessment.
- No resource may hold two overlapping service intervals.
- Preliminary compliance work does not replace final Compliance Review.
- External response times remain stochastic; interventions cannot shorten supplier delays.
- Queue forecasts are uncertain; earlier readiness does not guarantee earlier service start.
```

K-104 and K-411 alter timing/parallelism only. K-219 alters routing (resource substitution). K-337 alters allowed decision policy by forbidding the second rework loop.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** Sanctions ambiguity surfaced only at Assessment; C1 performed full review from scratch after late Escalation Advice.  
**Minimal intervention:** Send preliminary packet to S1 at Triage and reserve provisional C1 slot.  
**Temporal propagation:** Identity and Document finish earlier relative to Assessment; preliminary compliance work overlaps with Document Check, trimming C1 service time by 1.5-3 h; final Compliance slot moves from Wed 13:10 to Tue 20:00-23:00 range.  
**Process-feasibility check:** Four-eyes and final-Compliance-after-Assessment rules preserved; provisional slot does not replace final review.  
**Best/base/worst case:** Best 18 h (packet complete), base 12 h, worst 8 h (packet incomplete, extra 2 h C1 work).  
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 slot reservation rejected by capacity manager or preliminary packet missing key sanctions data.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** Routing to D1 created overnight wait despite earlier Originals arrival.  
**Minimal intervention:** Route to D2 at Originals Receipt.  
**Temporal propagation:** D2 begins Tue 08:00 instead of Tue 09:30; slower service time shifts Assessment start from Tue 20:00 to Tue 18:30-19:30 unless clarification requested.  
**Process-feasibility check:** Originals precede Document Check; no high-risk compliance required.  
**Best/base/worst case:** Best 10 h (no clarification), base 6 h, worst +4 h (clarification + rework).  
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Clarification probability >0.35 or D2 queue unexpectedly longer than modeled.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** Two external loops driven by inconsistent supplier data.  
**Minimal intervention:** Force decision after Assessment 2.  
**Temporal propagation:** Eliminates Thu-Mon external delay and third Document/Assessment pair; Decision moves from Wed 08:30 to Tue 11:00-14:00.  
**Process-feasibility check:** Decision policy changed; four-eyes and Compliance still satisfied.  
**Best/base/worst case:** Best 60 h (approve with conditions), base 42 h, worst 36 h (reject, possible appeal).  
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Unresolved discrepancy triggers mandatory external escalation regardless of policy.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** Legacy lock serialized Identity then Document.  
**Minimal intervention:** Parallel task creation with real-time timestamps.  
**Temporal propagation:** Both checks finish Tue 19:30 instead of Wed 09:30; Assessment queue arrival advances but service start depends on A2 backlog.  
**Process-feasibility check:** Concurrency allowed when originals not required; timestamps prevent false overlap.  
**Best/base/worst case:** Best 9 h, base 6 h, worst 4 h (Assessment queue congestion).  
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Timestamp workaround creates audit finding or Assessment queue grows faster than expected.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|--------------|------------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|------------------|
| K-104 | + provisional C1 reservation | Compliance (target) | Compliance (others) | Other high-risk cases | negative | Reservation blocks C1 even if packet incomplete |
| K-219 | + D2 load,  D1 load | Document (target) | Document (D2 backlog) | Standard D2 cases | mixed | Rework probability couples D2 speed to extra loops |
| K-337 |  two Document/Assessment pairs | Assessment, Document | none | none directly | positive | Policy change removes external stochastic delay |
| K-411 | + concurrent Identity/Document | none | Assessment | Later standard cases | negative | Earlier arrivals shift bottleneck downstream |

When scaled to 100 similar cases per week, K-104 reservations consume 30-40 % of C1 capacity, displacing 25-35 other high-risk cases by 6-14 h each. K-219 substitution increases D2 utilization from 55 % to 78 %, raising clarification probability from 0.15 to 0.28 and generating 12-18 additional rework loops. K-337 removes 180-240 external delay hours but raises appeal exposure on 8-12 % of decisions. K-411 advances 100 cases into Assessment, adding 35-50 h of A-queue backlog and delaying non-target cases by 3-7 h. Weekend external responses remain unaffected by any intervention. Timestamp workarounds risk audit flags if concurrency logging is incomplete.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 (K-337) | intervention 2 (K-219) |
|---------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| eligibility | High-risk/high-value cases with at least one supplier inconsistency after Assessment 2 | Standard-document cases requiring originals |
| assignment rule | Random 50 % force-decision vs continue normal loop | Random 50 % route to D2 vs D1 |
| sample/support logic | 60 cases per arm over 6 weeks | 80 cases per arm over 4 weeks |
| primary outcome | Cycle time from Register to Notify | Cycle time from Originals Receipt to Decision |
| process-path outcome | Occurrence of second Request More Info | Clarification request rate and rework count |
| quality outcome | Appeal rate within 30 days; compliance audit score | Rework-induced cycle time; final accuracy score |
| queue-spillover outcome | None expected | D2 queue length and wait time for non-pilot cases |
| logging validation | Decision rationale captured; four-eyes sign-off timestamped | Resource assignment and start timestamps audited daily |
| stopping rule | Appeal rate >12 % or compliance score <95 % | Clarification rate >30 % or D2 backlog >8 h average |

## 7. Final synthesis
K-337 ranks most robust because the policy change removes stochastic external loops without relying on resource substitution or capacity reservations. K-104 is most dangerous despite speed potential: provisional C1 reservations create immediate negative spillovers to other high-risk cases and are difficult to reverse once capacity is allocated. Local counterfactuals are least transportable for K-104 and K-411; both interventions shift congestion downstream or to non-target cases, an effect invisible in single-case analysis. Most valuable additional data are (a) C1 utilization and reservation-rejection logs, (b) D2 clarification rates stratified by document complexity, (c) timestamp-audit flags on concurrent tasks, and (d) external-response delay distributions conditional on day-of-week.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. C1 can honor provisional reservations without rejecting other high-risk cases (interference).  
2. D2 clarification probability remains constant under increased load (behavioral).  
3. Four-eyes rule is strictly enforced after policy change (structural).  
4. External supplier response times are unaffected by internal interventions (temporal).  
5. Assessment resources have spare capacity to absorb earlier arrivals (resource).  
6. Decision quality remains comparable when evidence is incomplete (quality).  
7. Timestamp workarounds do not create false concurrency detectable by audit (behavioral).  
8. Queue forecasts used for propagation remain directionally stable over one week (interference).
