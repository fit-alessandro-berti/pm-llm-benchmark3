At both sites, Assessment needs two completed checks. When both checks are ready and staff can execute them independently, overlapping their intervals can shorten the elapsed time until the join. Earlier completion also changes when cases reach Assessment. Staffing, arrival variability and queue discipline determine how much of that gain survives downstream. The graph represents potential effects through shared queues, so one case's release can affect another case's wait.

B's statutory receipt gate and legal review create paths with different dependencies and resource demands. B's lock changes whether release produces real overlap, and its specialized pools change how quickly each branch obtains a reviewer. Readiness, originals delay, specialization, lock state, role utilization, complexity and legal status can all modify the effect. The amount of verification remaining by receipt is especially important for original-required cases. Logging quality changes how well recorded overlap measures work as performed.

At A, rollout coincided with changes in staffing, API latency, intake and queue management. The before/after comparison cannot separate those paths, and the recorded overlap increase needs validation. Reweighting A's before/after figures by complexity would leave those problems and the differences in treatment versions unresolved. A similar diagram identifies a common join but omits guards, task ownership, queue capacity and the legal feedback loop. Transport therefore requires evidence on the mechanisms and subgroups that B can actually expose to the intervention.

For a first-pass comparison, let identity start at registration and take \(I\) hours, let eligible documents become ready \(R\) hours after registration, and let document work take \(D\) hours. With idle staff and serial document release after identity, time to the join is \(\max(I,R)+D\). With lawful concurrent release it is \(\max(I,R+D)\). The gain is \(\min(D,\max(0,I-R))\). Using B's reported branch medians as representative durations yields 3.4 h for a ready case and zero with an 18 h receipt delay. These are examples for individual paths. B's actual baseline includes some recorded overlap, and all scenarios concern additional gains beyond baseline operations.

The digital scenario's 2–4 h gain brackets the 3.4 h example. A's typical identity duration was also about 3 h, while its reported wait before Assessment fell by 6 h during the bundled rollout. The optimistic 4–10 h gains require additional handoff or queue savings, or more overlap in longer cases. That assumption is informed by the size of the waiting-time movement at A and requires verification at B. The optimistic eligibility illustration adds 15–25 percentage points of original-required cases with a new overlap opportunity to the roughly 30% ready digital share. The 18 h receipt median and variability leave this opportunity unresolved. The adverse queue additions are sensitivity inputs to test against B's role-specific logs.

To combine shifts, assume the baseline cycle-time distribution is locally linear around the 44 h median and 110 h p90, and that the proportions receiving each shift are roughly constant across the neighboring ranks crossed by those shifts. Under this approximation the quantile moves by the local share-weighted hour changes. For example, the bottleneck p90 change is \(0.50(4\text{ to }8)-0.10(2\text{ to }4)\), or +1.6 to +3.8 h. At illustrative midpoints, if 30% of all claims gain 3 h and 10% incur 6 h of added wait, the overall mean improves by 0.3 h while p90 worsens. The local shares, including the legal concentration near p90, are assumptions. Estimating them requires joint case-level data. The reported marginal medians and percentages cannot be combined to estimate a population quantile directly.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

- At Register, record original requirements, legal-review eligibility, channel, complexity and verified document readiness. Begin the pilot with ready digital claims with neither a statutory original requirement nor a mandatory legal review. Verify the relevant prerequisites before exposing each case to the release rule.
- Release Identity Check after Register. Start the Originals Receipt branch in parallel where originals are required. Document Check may be created as a blocked placeholder. It becomes assignable and executable only after the required originals have arrived and been validated, or after accepted inputs are complete for a case without that requirement. With supported ownership, identity and document work can run concurrently from that point. Fall back to the compliant scheduling path when task ownership cannot be obtained.
- The identity and document pools own their own checks. Registration staff verify receipt and record its timestamp. The assessor starts only after both checks are complete and valid against the current inputs. Legal staff own the required clearance and supplement decisions. The designated decision owner records exactly one final Approve or Reject after the applicable gates, followed by Notify.
- Cap ready and in-progress WIP separately by role using audited service demand, staffing and queue ages from the run-in. Include repeat visits in per-role load and collect productive hours and touch time to estimate capacity. Dispatch by ready-time order within existing urgency and statutory priority bands, with aging escalation. Keep blocked and receipt-pending cases visible outside executable WIP. Meter releases against the verification and assessment caps and monitor legal backlog. Record throttling and preserve statutory deadlines.
- Where required, route Assessment to Legal Review. A Request Supplement initiates collection. Validate any supplemental originals before new document work that depends on them. If the supplement invalidates the document finding or assessment, repeat the required Document Check and Assessment before returning to Legal Review. Use current valid verification results for each assessment, and require legal disposition before final decision. Track each loop until resolution.
- Log case and attempt IDs, gating facts and input versions, role and task owner, readiness and queue entry, assignment, actual start/pause/resume/completion, lock and fallback events, assessment readiness, supplement and final decisions. Retain event time, recording time and source separately. Audit a sample of lifecycles against source and ownership records.

```text
R = Register
O = XOR(Originals Receipt, tau), selected by the statutory originals requirement
I = Identity Check
D = Document Check
A = Assessment
V = partial order over {I, O, D, A} with I < A and O < D < A
M = XOR(Supplement Originals Receipt, Supplement Receipt), selected by the supplement requirements
U = Request Supplement < M < XOR(sequence(Document Check, Assessment), tau)
L = LOOP(Legal Review, U)
Full = R < V < XOR(L, tau) < XOR(Approve, Reject) < Notify
```

Each XOR selects one branch. Legal status selects the legal branch, and input impact selects the supplement recheck branch. The loop performs Legal Review once and allows repeated supplement cycles, each followed by another review. The executable workflow also guards document readiness and current valid verification results.

Start with a run-in that validates timestamps against source events and exercises dual ownership, receipt gates and writes. During randomization, compare assigned arms on verified release, actual overlap and time to both completed checks. If assigned cases remain blocked or create no additional verified overlap, inspect readiness, lock and throttle events. Those distinguish an infeasible or poorly delivered release policy from a failure of the critical-path mechanism.

If overlap rises but time to the join barely moves, check whether the shorter branch was already off the critical path or whether measurement captured an open task rather than active work. If the join gets earlier but cycle time does not, trace where the gain is absorbed, including assessor queues, legal revisits and repeat checks. Monitor contemporaneous untreated work for congestion. Use randomized assignment for the outcome estimate. Treat comparisons by realized overlap as diagnostic, since readiness and workload select which cases actually overlap.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def sequence(*nodes):
    """Build a total order as a transitively closed strict partial order."""
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for position, earlier in enumerate(nodes):
        for later in nodes[position + 1:]:
            model.order.add_edge(earlier, later)
    return model


site_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')"
)
site_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register', +('Identity Check', 'Document Check'), 'Assessment', "
    "X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')"
)

identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
originals_gate = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Transition(label="Originals Receipt"), SilentTransition()],
)
document = Transition(label="Document Check")
assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")
verification = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[identity, originals_gate, document, assessment]
)
verification.order.add_edge(identity, assessment)
verification.order.add_edge(originals_gate, document)
verification.order.add_edge(document, assessment)
verification.order.add_edge(originals_gate, assessment)  # Transitive edge.

supplement_receipt = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        Transition(label="Supplement Originals Receipt"),
        Transition(label="Supplement Receipt"),
    ],
)
repeat_checks_if_needed = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        sequence(
            Transition(label="Document Check"),
            Transition(label="Assessment"),
        ),
        SilentTransition(),
    ],
)
collect_and_apply_supplement = sequence(
    Transition(label="Request Supplement"),
    supplement_receipt,
    repeat_checks_if_needed,
)
# A two-child loop executes Legal Review first, then zero or more
# repetitions of the supplement sequence followed by another Legal Review.
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[Transition(label="Legal Review"), collect_and_apply_supplement],
)
legal_if_required = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[legal_loop, SilentTransition()],
)
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Transition(label="Approve"), Transition(label="Reject")],
)
site_b = sequence(
    Transition(label="Register"),
    verification,
    legal_if_required,
    decision,
    Transition(label="Notify"),
)

# Case-data guards select the lawful receipt, supplement, and legal branches.
# This control-flow model also abstracts from input version validity, pool
# capacity, queue discipline, ownership locks, service times, and delays.
# Net soundness checks concern the modeled control flow. Case-level guard
# compliance and operational performance require separate validation.
models = {
    "Site A before": site_a_before,
    "Site A after": site_a_after,
    "Site B candidate": site_b,
}
for name, model in models.items():
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    workflow_net = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
    sound, _ = pm4py.check_soundness(net, initial_marking, final_marking)
    print(f"{name}: workflow net={workflow_net}, sound={sound}")
    if not workflow_net or not sound:
        raise RuntimeError(f"{name} failed a structural check")
```

## 9. Recommendation

**conditional go**

Pilot earlier release of independent, ready verification work. At Site B, Identity Check can start after registration, and original receipts can progress during identity verification. Document Check can run concurrently once its own lawful inputs are ready and the system supports separate task ownership. Creating executable document tasks at registration for every claim would breach the receipt prerequisite for cases awaiting mandatory originals. Keep the Assessment join, legal clearance and supplement cycle in the executable workflow. Site A's nine-hour median improvement is a change observed during a bundled rollout and provides no numerical target for B.

Start with verified ready digital claims that require neither originals nor mandatory Legal Review. This stratum offers the clearest test of whether independent execution shortens the join. Its size depends on the joint distribution of readiness and statutory flags. The expected first benefit is fewer hours to complete verification in some eligible cases. The site-wide median effect may be modest. Faster releases can also lengthen the Assessment queue or divert capacity from untreated cases, and input-version mistakes can increase repeat work.

Expand one stratum at a time when randomized evidence shows a shorter eligible cycle of operational value, audited overlap and earlier joins, and adequate precision against the prespecified tail and quality limits. Require verified ordering compliance and stable backlog for untreated claims. Add receipt-gated cases where timing evidence shows material verification opportunity after receipt. Stage legal-required cases with separate monitoring of legal arrivals, queue age and supplement loops.

Modify release or WIP limits if the join improves while downstream queues deteriorate. Repair the ownership mechanism and retest if the assigned policy rarely produces real overlap. Pause on any statutory breach or unsafe write. Abandon the affected treatment version if lawful dual ownership cannot be sustained, or if verified overlap yields no worthwhile cycle gain or causes persistent tail or quality harm after feasible changes.

## 10. Assumption register

1. Ready digital claims exist in sufficient numbers with neither required originals nor mandatory legal review — the first-stage effect needs an eligible population with usable support — count all screened registrations by the joint readiness and statutory flags — extend accrual or revise the eligible cohort and its estimand if the intersection is too small.
2. Identity and document work can use the same validated inputs independently once each is ready — a true dependency would prevent safe concurrent execution — review required fields, trace historical input dependencies and audit controlled execution — add the missing dependency and exclude those cases from concurrent release.
3. Statutory and readiness flags are accurate before work release — these flags govern document and legal eligibility — audit flags against governing rules and source records and test exception cases — suspend the affected route until its guards are corrected.
4. Legacy IT can support safe independent task ownership — concurrent release needs concurrent authorized execution and consistent writes — exercise normal and conflicting updates, lock rollback and log capture during run-in — use compliant serial assignment until the capability is reliable.
5. Lifecycle timestamps distinguish occurrence from back-entry and capture actual work — mechanism testing depends on valid overlap and queue measurements — reconcile sampled traces with audit events and record start, pause, finish and entry times separately — treat raw overlap as unreliable and repair capture before drawing mechanism conclusions.
6. Site B has some usable capacity at the intended pilot saturation — the earlier release can otherwise add queueing throughout shared pools — measure arrival load, staffing, touch work including repeats, role WIP and cross-arm queue age — lower saturation or stage release by role and reassess.
7. Typical receipt delays and supplemented legal paths vary with preassignment case attributes — expansion needs supported strata and explicit treatment versions — measure receipt and loop distributions by eligibility flags, complexity and load — collect local trial evidence for each new route or limit expansion to tested cohorts.
8. Registered-cohort follow-up captures long cycles and 90-day appeals — incomplete outcomes can mask tail and quality harms — audit linked notifications and appeals, cohort maturity and censoring by arm — extend follow-up or retain uncertainty bounds and defer expansion claims.
