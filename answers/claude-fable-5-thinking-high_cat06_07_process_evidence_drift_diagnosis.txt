## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March (healthy baseline).** All monitors are stable: breach rate 0.18–0.19 with mean predicted risk matching it, AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE ≤0.025, unknown activities ≤0.3%, variant mix ~45/35/20, parts edge 28–35 h. This is the reference vintage for everything that follows.

**April (pipeline incident, not drift).** The workflow migration renamed the anchor activity. Because predictions fire "immediately after Initial Diagnosis Completed," a renamed anchor means prefixes were cut at the wrong event or never built. The signature is diagnostic: unknown-activity share exploded to 17%, the queue feature went missing in 14% of cases, 18% of variants became unmapped, and the model *over*-predicted (0.28 vs. an unchanged true rate of 0.19). Real drift does not usually announce itself with a synchronized spike in three data-quality indicators while the matured outcome stays flat. April is a measurement failure; the process itself was healthy.

**May (partial recovery with residue).** The 6 May mapping patch restored most signals, but ECE (0.050) and unknown share (2%) did not fully return to baseline — consistent with the audit finding that the cached pre-diagnosis event-count feature was not fully backfilled and duplicates before 20 April were never cleaned. May is mostly usable but not pristine.

**June (real process drift in routing; not concept drift).** The remote-routing policy pushed V2 to 52%. The observed breach rate *fell* to 0.15 simply because V2 is the low-risk path (matured V2 breach 0.09). Crucially, per-variant matured rates barely moved and the diagnosis→Remote Fix edge time was unchanged. This is a mix/covariate shift with intact conditional relationships. The mild AUROC/ECE softening (0.79/0.060) is what you expect when the score distribution compresses toward low-risk cases; it does not warrant retraining, only a calibration check.

**July (onset of supplier-driven concept drift).** The parts edge jumped to 65 h and V3's matured breach rate rose to 0.46. Aggregate metrics moved only slightly because V3 was still ~22% of volume. This is the earliest month where P(breach | same V3 path, same features) demonstrably changed.

**August–September (overlapping real deteriorations).** Three things stack: (1) the supplier problem became severe (130–148 h edge; V3 matured breach 0.64/0.61) — concept drift for a model blind to supplier lead time; (2) V3's share grew to 35–42% — covariate/process mix drift amplifying the miscalibrated segment; (3) from September, Partner Z (24% of volume) added a population shift with out-of-model features and extra V3 routing. Mean predicted risk stayed ~0.19–0.21 while true risk climbed toward 0.28–0.31+: the model now systematically *under*-predicts — the opposite failure direction from April, which is itself evidence the causes differ. Maturity (93%/82%) means the true Aug–Sep rates are, if anything, somewhat higher than observed.

**October (censoring dominates; judgment must be suspended).** Only 55% of labels matured, median delay 52 d, and the backlog selectively censors long-path (V3, breach-prone, Partner Z) cases. The observed breach rate of 0.16 is not a recovery — it is survivorship. Oct's AUROC 0.64 and ECE 0.130 are computed on a biased, mostly short-path subsample. October tells us almost nothing about current model quality; treating it as comparable to mature months would be the single worst analytical error available here.

Hence April (measurement), June (benign routing shift), August–September (genuine, overlapping concept + process-performance + population drift), and October (censoring artifact) are four different phenomena requiring four different responses.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

Each test lists expected results under competing hypotheses (H).

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each start-month cohort, plot cumulative matured breach rate vs. days-since-cutoff; re-evaluate AUROC/Brier/ECE at fixed vintages (30/60/90 d after month end).
   - H-censoring (Oct is artifact): Oct's breach rate climbs steeply as labels arrive, converging toward or above Aug–Sep levels; Oct metrics recomputed at 90 d look like Sep's, not like the cutoff snapshot.
   - H-real-improvement: Oct's curve plateaus near 0.16 even at full maturity — then something (e.g., supplier recovery) genuinely improved and the low rate is real.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning.** Rebuild April(–May) features from raw events with `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` mapped to the canonical label, duplicates removed for the entire migration window, and the cached event-count feature recomputed; re-score with the frozen model.
   - H-pipeline-failure: April AUROC recovers to ~0.78–0.82 and ECE to ~0.03; mean predicted risk falls back to ~0.19.
   - H-real-April-drift: metrics stay degraded even on clean recomputed features, implying something real changed in April (currently contradicted by the flat matured April outcome).

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant × source × month.** Reliability diagrams and AUROC per (V1/V2/V3) × (Partner Z / other) × month.
   - H-segment-local concept drift: V3 shows large under-prediction (observed ≫ predicted) from Jul onward while V1/V2 remain calibrated; the aggregate decay is explained by the V3 stratum plus its growing share.
   - H-global concept drift: all strata decalibrate together — would demand full retraining rather than segment repair.
   - H-mix-only (June): every stratum stays calibrated; aggregate shifts are pure composition effects (Simpson-style).

4. **Process-variant and DFG / performance-DFG comparison.** Discover monthly DFGs and performance DFGs; compare structure (new/removed edges) and edge durations, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis→`Remote Fix`.
   - H-performance-drift-only: structure identical across months, only the parts edge slows — matches the data (supplier issue).
   - H-structural process drift: new activities/edges or reordered flows appear (e.g., post-migration artifacts) — would implicate the migration or undocumented process change instead.

5. **Conditional-outcome test on identical paths.** Within exact-variant matches (e.g., V3 cases with the same prefix features), compare breach rates across periods (Jan–Jun vs. Jul–Sep), e.g., via a logistic model with a period indicator, or CUSUM on residuals of the frozen model restricted to V3.
   - H-concept-drift: the period term is large and significant for V3 only — the same inputs now produce worse outcomes.
   - H-covariate-only: the period term vanishes once features are conditioned on; deterioration was composition, not relationship change.

6. **Population reweighting / transport check for Partner Z.** Reweight Sep non-Partner-Z cases to Partner Z's observable covariate profile (variant, region proxies, device age where available); compare the reweighted breach rate to Partner Z's matured rate; alternatively train a domain classifier (Partner Z vs. other) and inspect separability and top features.
   - H-shift-explained-by-observables: reweighted non-Z rate ≈ Partner Z rate → the gap is variant/geography mix; recalibration plus adding the missing features suffices.
   - H-residual-population-effect: a gap persists after reweighting → Partner Z carries unmodeled risk factors; a segment-specific model or new features are needed. (Both branches must use matured labels — Partner Z's slower maturation otherwise fabricates a gap.)

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audit.** For every model feature, monthly missingness/imputation-default rates by source and by pipeline version; trace the cached event-count feature's lineage across the April backfill; verify duplicate-removal coverage by source and date.
   - H-pipeline: missingness spikes align exactly with 1 Apr–6 May and with the 1–20 Apr duplicate window, and are absent afterwards.
   - H-drift: missingness is flat; degraded months have clean features, so the decay is real.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0 — Immediate containment (this week).**
- Publish all Aug–Oct metrics with maturity annotations; suppress October headline metrics entirely or label them "biased subsample, 55% mature."
- Apply an interim risk uplift for V3 cases (e.g., a variant-specific intercept adjustment estimated on matured Jul–Aug V3 labels) so operations stops under-prioritizing parts-dependent cases. This is a documented, reversible overlay, not a model change.
- Route Partner Z cases with out-of-support feature values (extreme rural distance, device age) to a rules-assisted review queue.

**Stage 1 — Short-term recalibration (2–4 weeks).**
- Recalibrate (Platt/isotonic) *stratified by variant*, using only mature, pipeline-clean data: Jan–Mar, May–Jun (after the residual feature recomputation), and Jul–Aug once ≥95–98% mature. Do not recalibrate on April (pipeline) or October (immature).
- Recalibration is justified now because discrimination within V1/V2 appears intact and the dominant defect is calibration in V3; it is insufficient alone for V3 if test 5 confirms conditional-relationship change, but it buys time safely.

**Stage 2 — Feature and pipeline repair (parallel).**
- Recompute April–May features from raw normalized events; extend duplicate cleaning to 1–20 April; then re-admit April to history if test 2 passes.
- Add features: supplier lead-time indicators (rolling median of `Order Part -> Part Received`, open parts backlog at prediction time), variant/route indicator available at diagnosis, source/partner, rural travel distance, device age.

**Stage 3 — Full retraining (after Aug–Sep labels are ≥95% mature and Stage 2 lands).**
- Training-set safety: **Safe:** Jan–Mar; May–Jul (post-recomputation, fully mature). **Conditionally safe:** April only after recomputation passes; Aug–Sep only after maturity ≥95% and with the new supplier features (otherwise you teach the model a supplier regime it cannot observe). **Unsafe:** October until maturity recovers (~ Dec 2026+), and any Partner Z labels before their maturation lag is measured and corrected.
- Consider time-decay weighting so the supplier-crisis regime informs but does not dominate.

**Segment/variant-specific model?** A fully separate V3 model is justified only if, after adding supplier features, V3 residuals remain structurally different — otherwise prefer one model with variant interactions and supplier-lead-time features, which shares statistical strength and avoids per-segment maintenance. For Partner Z, start with the transport check (test 6): if observables explain the gap, a shared model with the new features plus per-source calibration suffices; build a Partner Z-specific model only if a residual gap persists on matured labels and Partner Z volume (~25%) sustains it. Segment-specific *calibration* layers are cheap and justified immediately; segment-specific *models* need evidence.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Monitors and their dependency logic:

1. **Data-quality monitor (first gate).** Unknown-activity share, unmapped-variant share, feature missingness by source, duplicate rate, schema/label-vocabulary contract checks (alert on any new activity name). *Fires first and suppresses drift interpretation:* while data-quality is red (as in April), predictive-metric alarms are annotated "possible pipeline artifact" and cannot trigger retraining.
2. **Label-maturity monitor.** Maturity % and median label delay per cohort and per source (Partner Z tracked separately); expected-vs-actual maturation curves. *Gates the predictive monitor:* performance metrics are only "trustworthy" when cohort maturity ≥ a threshold (e.g., 90%); below it, only vintage-adjusted metrics are shown. A label-delay alarm (like October's) escalates to the outcome-API team, and explicitly blocks automated retraining triggers.
3. **Predictive-performance monitor.** AUROC/Brier on fixed-vintage matured cohorts, stratified by variant and source, with CUSUM-style change tracking on frozen-model residuals.
4. **Calibration monitor.** ECE and reliability curves per stratum; mean-predicted vs. matured-observed gap with sign (April over-prediction vs. August under-prediction are different alerts).
5. **Process-variant monitor.** Monthly variant distribution vs. a versioned baseline; baselines are re-versioned at documented interventions (1 Jun policy, 1 Sep onboarding) so expected mix changes do not alarm.
6. **Performance-DFG monitor.** Edge-duration percentiles for critical edges (`Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis→`Remote Fix`, dispatch→repair); alarms here are *leading indicators* of concept drift and pre-authorize segment recalibration review.
7. **Population monitor.** Source/partner mix, covariate distributions (incl. new features), domain-classifier separability score, out-of-support case rate.
8. **Intervention/deployment monitor.** Registry of policy changes, migrations, onboardings, patches, model/calibration versions; every alarm is auto-annotated with interventions in a ±30-day window.

**Dependency rules (examples):** retraining proposals require (data-quality green) AND (maturity ≥90% on the evidence cohort) AND (stratified performance degradation confirmed on matured labels); a maturity alarm alone routes to data-engineering, never to ML retraining; a performance-DFG alarm plus stratified calibration drift in the matching variant routes to "segment recalibration review"; a population alarm routes to "feature-coverage and transport check."

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CANONICAL_DIAGNOSIS = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
LABEL_MAP = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": CANONICAL_DIAGNOSIS}

def load_and_clean(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    # 1) Normalize the renamed diagnosis activity BEFORE any feature extraction,
    #    and keep UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY visible as a data-quality signal (never silently drop).
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(LABEL_MAP)
    df["dq_unknown"] = df["activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")
    # 2) Duplicate handling: identical (case, activity, timestamp) rows from the
    #    dual-source migration window are collapsed; the removal rate is reported.
    before = len(df)
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]).drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first")
    dup_rate = 1 - len(df) / before
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case_id",
                                 activity_key="activity",
                                 timestamp_key="timestamp")
    return log, dup_rate

def monthly_summaries(log, months=pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M")):
    case_start = log.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    rows = []
    for m in months:
        t0, t1 = str(m.start_time), str(m.end_time)
        # Event-calendar cohort: all events occurring in the month -> workload /
        # performance view (edges executed this month, whatever the case age).
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, t0, t1, mode="events")
        # Case-start cohort: cases that BEGAN this month -> the prediction cohort;
        # recent months are right-censored, so outcome-linked stats need maturity flags.
        ids = case_start[(case_start >= m.start_time) & (case_start <= m.end_time)].index
        cs = log[log["case:concept:name"].isin(ids)]

        dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(cs)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev)  # durations from this month's events
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cs)
        n_cases = cs["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        rows.append({
            "month": str(m),
            "cases_started": n_cases,
            "events_in_month": len(ev),
            "unknown_share": cs["dq_unknown"].mean() if n_cases else None,
            "top_variants": sorted(((v, len(c)) for v, c in variants.items()),
                                   key=lambda x: -x[1])[:5],
            "parts_edge_perf_s": perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received")),
            "remote_edge_perf_s": perf_dfg.get((CANONICAL_DIAGNOSIS, "Remote Fix")),
            "diagnosis_edge_present": any(CANONICAL_DIAGNOSIS in e for e in dfg),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

log, dup_rate = load_and_clean()
# Case-level temporal features (throughput/arrival dynamics) for the audit trail;
# no model fitting here — outputs are auditable evidence tables only.
temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
summary = monthly_summaries(log)
print(f"duplicate removal rate: {dup_rate:.2%}")
print(summary.to_string(index=False))
```

(Deliberately no invented "pm4py drift detector": drift assessment is done by comparing these monthly summaries and stratified metrics over time, not by a nonexistent library call.)

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature October labels.** The censored cohort teaches the model that recent cases (especially long-path/Partner Z) rarely breach — because the breaching ones haven't matured. The retrained model would suppress risk exactly where risk is highest, and the error would look like an improvement at the next cutoff. *Mitigation:* hard maturity gates on training data (≥95%), fixed-vintage evaluation, and inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting or exclusion of unmatured cohorts.

2. **Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.** If April is treated as concept drift and folded into recalibration, the model learns to correct for corrupted prefixes that no longer exist post-patch, degrading May-onward calibration; it also normalizes silent schema breakage. *Mitigation:* quarantine April until features are recomputed from normalized raw events; require the data-quality monitor to be green before any month enters a calibration set; add schema contract tests so renames fail loudly at ingestion.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift** by attributing everything to the migration or to label delay. The model keeps predicting ~0.19 while V3 truly breaches at ~0.6; dispatchers under-escalate parts-dependent cases, breaching SLAs and eroding trust precisely for the customers most affected by the supplier failure. *Mitigation:* the matured per-variant breach table and the performance-DFG edge are the arbiters — both are label-maturity-robust for Jul–Aug; act on the V3 uplift immediately and add supplier lead-time features rather than waiting for aggregate metrics to "confirm."

4. **Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair model behavior (or to partner "quality") without stratified evidence.** The raw 0.45 vs. 0.27 gap conflates variant mix, the supplier crisis, slower label maturation, and missing features. Acting on it naively could mean penalizing Partner Z commercially, or bolting on a hasty "fairness correction" that miscalibrates everyone. *Mitigation:* compare only matured labels; run the reweighting/transport check to see how much of the gap observables explain; add rural distance and device age to the feature store before judging; report Partner Z metrics with their own maturity curve.

5. **Treating June's routing shift as model failure and retraining on the new mix mid-transition.** June's metric softening was composition, not broken relationships; a panic retrain on June–July data would bake in a transient mix and add churn risk while the real (supplier) problem was just beginning. *Mitigation:* stratified calibration checks before any retraining decision; version variant-mix baselines at documented policy dates; require evidence of *conditional* relationship change (test 5) before retraining, not just aggregate metric movement.
