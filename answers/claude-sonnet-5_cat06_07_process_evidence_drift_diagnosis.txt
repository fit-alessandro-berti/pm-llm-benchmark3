## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Jan–Mar (baseline).** AUROC .81–.82, ECE .02–.025, matured per-variant breach rates stable (V1 ~.16, V2 ~.10, V3 ~.31). Reference period for everything below.

**April — pipeline incident, not real deterioration.** The schema rename broke prefix construction: 17% unknown-activity, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped variant. Predicted risk rose to 0.28 while observed stayed 0.19 — a textbook feature-corruption signature (over-prediction because prefixes were truncated/misbuilt), not a world change. Matured per-variant rates for April (.18/.13/.34) are only mildly above March, confirming the *world* barely moved while the *measurement* broke.

**May — partial recovery, residual debt.** The May 6 patch fixed relabeling (unknown-activity 2%, unmapped variant ~1%) but a cached "events-before-diagnosis" feature wasn't recomputed, leaving missing-queue-feature at 3% (still 3× baseline) and ECE at 0.05. This is leftover pipeline debt, not new drift — the matured V3/V2/V1 rates are back near baseline.

**June — real but mechanical: mix shift, not concept shift.** The remote-routing policy pushed V2 from ~34% to 52%. Aggregate observed breach fell to 0.15, but per-variant matured rates barely moved. This is process/covariate drift in composition; treating it as concept drift and forcing a full retrain would be disproportionate — reweighting/recalibration suffices.

**July — onset of a real, localized concept shift.** Supplier delays begin (Jul 10); Order PartPart Received jumps 42h65h. V3 matured breach ticks to 0.46 while V1/V2 stay flat — the first genuine sign that P(Y|X=V3) is changing, driven by an external process-performance shock the model can't see.

**August — the shock peaks.** Edge time hits 130h; V3 matured breach hits 0.64. AUROC .68, ECE .12 — the model, calibrated on pre-crisis V3 behavior, badly underestimates current V3 risk. This is compound: real concept drift (outcome given variant changed) plus a process/performance cause plus a modest mix contribution (V3 share drifting up even before Partner Z).

**September — three drivers overlap.** Partner Z onboards (24% share, 58% of its cases on V3), pushing overall V3 share to 42% and introducing unmodeled covariates (rural distance, device age). The supplier crisis is still active. Maturity drops to 82% (delay 42d), so part of the picture is provisional. AUROC/ECE are the worst in the series (.66/.14), but this reflects supplier-driven concept drift + Partner-Z population shift + variant-mix drift + partial censoring simultaneously — not one cause.

**October — dominated by measurement, not necessarily by improvement.** The outcome-API backlog (maturity 55%, delay 52d) disproportionately excludes long-resolution (V3/parts) cases from the observed-label pool. The headline breach rate (0.16) is therefore likely a survivorship artifact of short, easy, already-closed cases — not evidence that things improved. The matured-by-variant table is explicitly marked insufficient. AUROC/ECE computed here are unreliable as performance verdicts.

**Why not one story:** April is almost entirely a schema/pipeline event with little real-world signal; June is a genuine but "benign" mix shift that doesn't imply the model's learned relationships are wrong; August–September is a genuine multi-mechanism deterioration (concept + process + population); October is a different pipeline failure mode (censoring) that can make things look artificially better. Collapsing these into a single "model has degraded" verdict would justify the wrong remediation in at least three of the four periods.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves / fixed-vintage backtesting.** Compare breach rate at matched maturity horizons (e.g., 60-day-matured) across all cohorts. *Real deterioration hypothesis:* Aug/Sep stay elevated vs Jan–Jun even at matched horizons. *Pure label-delay hypothesis (Oct):* once Oct reaches the same horizon, its rate converges toward Sep-like levels (~0.28–0.31) rather than staying at 0.16 — if it does, October's 0.16 is falsified as a real signal.

2. **Recompute after normalization + dedup.** Rerun April fully (not just relabel) with normalized activity and consistent dedup across old/new sources. *Pipeline-failure hypothesis:* AUROC/ECE return near baseline (~.80/.02–.03). *Real-drift-in-April hypothesis:* metrics stay poor after full recompute — would falsify the pipeline explanation.

3. **Stratified calibration/discrimination (variant × source × month).** *Mix-shift-only hypothesis (June):* per-cell (V1/V2/V3) metrics stay near baseline even though aggregate metrics move. *Genuine concept drift hypothesis (Jul–Sep):* the V3 cell itself degrades over time, independent of mix.

4. **DFG / performance-DFG comparison.** Discover monthly DFG and performance-DFG; track Order PartPart Received, DiagnosisRemote Fix, DispatchRepair Completed. *Process-driven hypothesis:* parts-path edges lengthen exactly when V3 risk rises; Remote Fix edge stays flat (localizing the cause). *No-process-change hypothesis:* edges flat while outcomes worsen — not what the data show, so this is rejected.

5. **Same-path conditional-outcome test.** Within V3, bucket cases by observed Order PartPart Received duration and check P(breach | bucket) over time. *Omitted-covariate/process-only hypothesis:* conditional-on-delay risk is stable — the model would be fine with a delay feature (favors "covariate drift", not true concept drift). *True concept drift hypothesis:* even within the same delay bucket, breach probability rises over time (e.g., compounding scheduling backlog) — genuine P(Y|X) change beyond delay.

6. **Partner Z reweighting / transport check.** Reweight pre-Sep population to match Partner Z's known covariate distribution (variant mix, etc.) and compare to Partner Z's matured breach rate. *Pure population-shift hypothesis:* reweighted historical rate  Partner Z's mature rate — relationship unchanged, only mix changed. *Partner-Z-specific concept shift hypothesis:* a residual gap remains after reweighting — indicating genuinely different risk given the same measured X (candidate: unmeasured rural/device-age effect).

7. **Feature missingness / lineage audit.** Trace each feature's build path across pipeline versions (pre/post Apr 1, pre/post May 6, pre/post Sep partner onboarding). *Resolved-debt hypothesis:* missing-feature/unknown-activity shares return fully to baseline post-patch except where explicitly flagged (matches May's residual 3%). *New-unrelated-issue hypothesis:* a fresh spike with no matching deployment date would falsify "it's just legacy debt."

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 1 — Immediate containment.**
- Quarantine April rows from any metric/training use pending recompute (Test 2).
- Tag Sep/Oct dashboards "provisional" (maturity <90%); suppress auto-triggered retraining from these cohorts.
- No emergency full retrain — risk of baking in artifacts from both the schema incident and the API backlog.

**Stage 2 — Short-term recalibration (weeks).**
- Once April/May features are verified recomputed, recalibrate (isotonic/Platt) per-variant and per-source using clean Jan–Jun + corrected Apr/May data — this addresses June's mix-driven calibration drift without touching the model's learned relationships.
- Do **not** calibrate to April's raw predicted-vs-observed gap (that gap is a pipeline artifact, not a model miscalibration to be "corrected away").

**Stage 3 — Feature repair (weeks–months).**
- Add supplier lead-time/parts-delay feature, rural distance, device age, partner-source indicator.
- Backfill the cached "events-before-diagnosis" feature for all affected historical cases before reuse.

**Stage 4 — Full retraining (only after maturity + pipeline validated).**
- Use Jan–Mar + recomputed Apr/May + Jun–Aug (mature, clean) + Sep once >90% matured; exclude Oct until maturity improves or apply explicit censoring-correction (discrete-time hazard/survival labeling) if urgency demands earlier inclusion.
- Retrain with the new parts-delay and Partner Z features so the Jul–Sep signal becomes a modeled relationship rather than an unexplained residual.

**Per-month training safety:** Jan–Mar safe; Apr unsafe until recompute validated; May conditionally safe pending missing-feature backfill; Jun safe (real, matured, clean — should be *included*, not discarded); Jul–Aug safe on maturity/cleanliness grounds but represent a real regime change that should be weighted/featured, not filtered out; Sep usable only with caution/partial-maturity correction; Oct unsafe for training currently.

**Segment-specific model:** Justified for **V3** — persistently different and now non-stationary risk (0.300.64) with a distinct causal driver (parts lead time) absent from V1/V2. A separate **Partner Z** model is not yet justified; first run the reweighting test (Test 6) — if the gap disappears after reweighting/adding covariates, a shared model with better features suffices; only a persistent residual gap would justify partner-specific modeling.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitors**
- *Predictive:* rolling AUROC/Brier computed **only on matured labels** (fixed-vintage), reported separately from an "all-available-labels" snapshot metric.
- *Calibration:* ECE/reliability diagrams stratified by variant and source/partner, monthly, vs Jan–Mar control limits.
- *Label-maturity:* % matured and delay percentiles by cohort-start month and variant; produces a "data immaturity" flag distinct from a "model decay" flag.
- *Data-quality:* unknown-activity share, missing-queue-feature %, duplicate-event rate, schema-version canaries per source system.
- *Process-variant:* variant distribution per month/source with control charts, annotated against a business-change log (routing policy, partner onboarding dates).
- *Performance-DFG:* median/percentile duration on key edges (Order PartPart Received, DiagnosisRemote Fix, DispatchRepair Completed) with change-point detection.
- *Population:* source/partner share and available covariate distributions (rural distance, device age) vs training-reference population.
- *Intervention/deployment:* logs model version, feature-pipeline version, recalibration/retrain timestamps, paired with before/after metric deltas.

**Alert dependencies** — label-maturity and data-quality monitors act as *gates*:
- If maturity < threshold (e.g. 90%) or data-quality flags are elevated for a cohort, predictive/calibration alerts for that cohort are auto-annotated "low confidence," routed to human review, and **blocked from auto-triggering retraining**.
- Only when maturity and data-quality are green (or a bias-corrected mature-equivalent estimate exists) can a predictive/calibration alert escalate to "retrain candidate."
- A variant/population-mix alert *without* a corresponding per-segment calibration/discrimination alert routes to "recalibration/reweighting candidate," not full retrain.
- A performance-DFG alert *combined with* a same-path conditional-outcome alert (Test 5) escalates to "concept-drift/feature-gap candidate," triggering the feature-repair workstream rather than a blind retrain.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RENAME_MAP = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}

def load_and_normalize(path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"])
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(RENAME_MAP)      # undo Apr-1 rename
    df["raw_activity"] = df["activity"]
    df.loc[df["activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY"), "activity"] = pd.NA

    # Duplicate-handling: same case + same normalized activity within 1s
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
    dup_mask = (
        df.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity"], keep="first")
        & (df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].diff().dt.total_seconds().abs() <= 1)
    )
    df["is_duplicate_flag"] = dup_mask          # keep for audit, do not silently drop
    df_clean = df.loc[~dup_mask].copy()

    df_clean = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    return df_clean

def monthly_process_summary(df, month_start, month_end) -> dict:
    """
    Two cohort definitions, different questions:
    - event-calendar cohort: events occurring in [start,end) regardless of case
      start -> answers 'what happened this month' (DFG/performance-DFG).
    - case-start cohort: cases whose FIRST event falls in [start,end)
      -> matches how the audited monthly label table is built (outcome analysis).
    """
    event_cal = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, month_start, month_end, mode="events")
    case_start = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, month_start, month_end, mode="traces_contained")

    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_cal)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_cal)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(case_start)
    variant_counts = {v: len(c) for v, c in variants.items()}
    temporal_feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(case_start)

    mask = df["timestamp"].between(month_start, month_end)
    unknown_share = df.loc[mask, "raw_activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()
    dup_share = df.loc[mask, "is_duplicate_flag"].mean()

    return {
        "month": month_start,
        "n_cases_event_calendar": event_cal["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "n_cases_case_start": case_start["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "variant_counts": variant_counts,
        "dfg_edges": dfg,
        "perf_dfg_edges": perf_dfg,           # e.g. Order Part->Part Received durations
        "unknown_activity_share": unknown_share,
        "duplicate_share": dup_share,
        "temporal_features_sample": temporal_feats.head(),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    events = load_and_normalize("field_service_events.csv")
    months = pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-11-01", freq="MS")
    summaries = [
        monthly_process_summary(events, str(a.date()), str(b.date()))
        for a, b in zip(months[:-1], months[1:])
    ]
    pd.DataFrame(summaries).to_csv("monthly_process_audit.csv", index=False)
    # Auditable summary only -- no model is fit here.
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature labels (Sep/Oct).** Treating October's 0.16 as a real improvement and retraining toward lower predicted risk would systematically under-predict breach risk once true (higher) labels mature, especially for V3/Partner Z. *Mitigation:* enforce a maturity gate (fixed-vintage only) or apply explicit censoring correction before any cohort enters training.

2. **Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April).** Fitting a calibration correction to April's predicted-vs-observed gap (0.28 vs 0.19) would permanently bake in a temporary schema artifact, degrading calibration in all later, correctly-functioning months. *Mitigation:* exclude uncorrected April/May-residual data from calibration fitting; require pipeline-health sign-off (Test 2) before inclusion.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift (Jul–Sep V3).** Attributing the AUROC/ECE decline solely to Partner Z or to label immaturity, and skipping the parts-delay feature, leaves the model chronically blind to a real, possibly recurring mechanism, harming SLA management for parts-dependent cases indefinitely. *Mitigation:* run the same-path conditional test (Test 5) and add supplier lead-time as a monitored, modeled feature regardless of what else is happening.

4. **Attributing Partner Z's shift to model unfairness without stratified evidence.** Concluding the model is "broken" or biased for Partner Z purely from the raw 0.45-vs-0.27 gap — without correcting for slower label maturation or reweighting on known covariates — could trigger an unjustified blanket override for Partner Z cases, or conversely a defensive dismissal of a real coverage gap (rural distance, device age unmodeled). *Mitigation:* run the reweighting/transport check (Test 6) and mature-cohort comparison first; base any partner-specific policy or feature change on the residual gap that survives those controls, not on the raw snapshot gap.

5. **Over-reacting to June's mix shift as concept drift.** Forcing a full retrain in response to June's aggregate breach-rate drop (when per-variant rates were stable) wastes remediation effort, risks introducing instability, and can mask the true (small) ECE bump's real cause. *Mitigation:* confirm via stratified metrics (Test 3) that per-variant performance is intact before escalating beyond reweighting/recalibration.
