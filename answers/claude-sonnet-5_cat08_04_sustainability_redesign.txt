# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope (gate-to-gate, order-level operational footprint):** The boundary covers everything visible in the event log from `Receive order` to `Close`/`Cancel due to delay`: production energy (CNC_A, CNC_B, heat treatment, inspection_cell), packaging material (standard vs. reinforced), outbound transport (parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck), rework loops (rework production, rework packaging), and scrap material generated during production/rework.

**Inside the boundary:**
- Energy consumption per production/inspection event (`energy_kwh`)
- Transport emissions per shipment leg (`transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode`)
- Packaging material mass (`package_material_kg`)
- Material scrap from production and rework (`material_scrap_kg`)
- Batching-induced waiting and its energy-efficiency trade-off
- Obsolescence of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (treated as a downstream material-waste proxy)

**Outside the boundary (excluded, but flagged as material uncertainty):**
- Upstream embodied impact of raw materials and purchased components
- Machine/vehicle manufacturing and end-of-life impacts (capital goods)
- Grid carbon intensity and time-of-day variation for `energy_kwh` (log gives kWh, not kgCO2e-equivalent for energy)
- Fate of scrapped/obsolete parts (recycled vs. landfilled) — we only observe non-shipment, not disposal outcome
- Return-leg transport for cancelled or refused orders
- Customer-side impacts (e.g., customer's own downtime cost of a late Critical-downtime part)

**Key uncertainties:** (1) Emission factors converting kWh and transport mode into comparable CO2e are not given, so energy and transport are analyzed on separate scales, not summed into one number. (2) The discovered process model has low precision around emergency/partial-shipment combinations, so path-level averages for those variants are directionally indicative, not exact. (3) Obsolescence's environmental cost (scrap vs. resale vs. discount-sale) is unknown, so it is treated as a risk indicator rather than a quantified impact.

# 3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender (target: all segments at shipment decision point)**
Mechanism: recommend ground/consolidated_truck whenever `promised_lead_time_hours` slack and historical route reliability support 85% on-time probability (operationalizes SP1). Environmental gain from shifting orders off parcel_air/dedicated_van.
Service/quality/resilience: neutral-to-positive if slack thresholds are respected; risk only if reliability estimates are stale during disruption (mitigated by R1 alternate-route rule).
Evidence to monitor: SP1 conformance %, late-delivery rate pre/post by segment, route_mode distribution shift.

**S2. Dynamic batching with waiting-time caps (target: Plan production  Batch wait  CNC_A/heat treatment)**
Mechanism: allow batch accumulation for energy efficiency (2.81.3 kWh/item) but cap wait time by segment (tighter cap for Service-contract/Critical, looser for Regular Made-to-order) to avoid the 38h tail and heat-treatment-driven late risk (1.7x).
Service/quality/resilience: reduces energy per item without pushing p90 cycle time into the danger zone; caps limit obsolescence exposure.
Evidence to monitor: batch_size distribution, median/p90 Batch wait, late-delivery % for Made-to-order, obsolescence rate (14-day unshipped).

**S3. Shift Made-to-order from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows (target: MTO segment, Plan production  CNC routing)**
Mechanism: CNC_A has half the rework rate of CNC_B (9% vs 18%); less rework means less duplicated energy, scrap, and packaging.
Service/quality/resilience: improves quality (fewer rework loops) and indirectly cycle time; risk only if CNC_A queue length increases wait for due-date-tight orders (must check queue before shifting).
Evidence to monitor: rework rate by machine_group post-change, CNC_A queue/wait time, late-delivery rate for MTO Regular vs. Service-contract.

**S4. Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract (target: Standard-stock/Service-contract segment)**
Mechanism: directly targets the documented anti-pattern (air used even when ground+priority picking meets promise). Cuts transport emissions ~4-6x per shifted order with negligible energy trade-off.
Service/quality/resilience: minimal service risk since these orders already have low late-delivery (5%) and short cycle times (18h median); priority picking preserves speed.
Evidence to monitor: SP1 violation rate for this segment (currently 22%), late-delivery %, air-share of this segment before/after.

**S5. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders (target: Stockstandard packground/consolidated_truck path, Standard-stock Regular)**
Mechanism: shifts more Standard-stock Regular volume from individual parcel_ground to consolidated_truck, cutting transport kgCO2e 35-55% per shifted order.
Service/quality/resilience: adds 12-24h consolidation wait — acceptable for Regular stock orders with 31h median/62h p90 cycle time and long promised lead times, but should exclude near-deadline orders.
Evidence to monitor: consolidated_truck share, p90 cycle time drift, late-delivery % for Standard-stock Regular.

**S6. Packaging-route compatibility checks pre-shipment (target: Package standard/reinforced  Rework packaging loop)**
Mechanism: eliminates a chunk of the ~620 rework-packaging events by catching route/geometry mismatches before shipment, avoiding duplicate packaging material and re-handling energy/transport.
Service/quality/resilience: reduces cycle time and packaging material waste with no service downside; improves quality (fewer damage-driven returns).
Evidence to monitor: rework-packaging rate, package_material_kg/order, Q1 conformance for MTO >8kg items.

**S7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations (target: Inspection  Rework production loop, CNC_B and heavy MTO parts)**
Mechanism: adds targeted, risk-based inspection rigor (not blanket slowdown) at the queue points shown to cause rushed inspections and missed defects, cutting the InspectionRework production edge (470/12000) and downstream scrap.
Service/quality/resilience: may add small time at inspection but avoids much larger rework cycle-time/energy/material cost (rework path shows 160h cycle, 32kWh, 1.8kg scrap).
Evidence to monitor: rework_flag rate by machine_group/part, inspection queue time, net cycle-time change (inspection add vs. rework avoided).

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes (target: Critical-downtime, dedicated_van path)**
Mechanism: shared/multi-stop emergency couriers reduce the empty-return inefficiency of dedicated vans (transport avg 52 kgCO2e/order) while still meeting tight windows.
Service/quality/resilience: must preserve the 3% late-delivery performance of the dedicated-van path; only apply where multi-stop routing doesn't breach promised_lead_time_hours.
Evidence to monitor: SP2 justification completeness, late-delivery % for Critical-downtime, van empty-return rate, transport kgCO2e/order for this segment.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into true-downtime vs. precautionary-urgent (target: Critical-downtime Service-contract, Availability check branch)**
Mechanism: precautionary-urgent orders (no immediate line-down risk) can be routed to consolidated/ground or shared courier (S8) instead of dedicated van, since not all 11% share genuinely needs the highest-emission path.
Service/quality/resilience: protects true emergencies at current service level while opening a lower-impact lane for the rest; resilience preserved by keeping dedicated van as the fallback.
Evidence to monitor: new sub-segment late-delivery/cancel rates, SP2 justification rate, transport kgCO2e split between the two new sub-segments.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments via revised availability-check rules (target: Pick from stock  Partial shipment/Backorder wait, Standard-stock)**
Mechanism: better availability logic (e.g., don't split an order across warehouses/time unless it clearly improves due-date performance) reduces the 1010 partial-shipment cases, each of which nearly doubles transport events and scrap risk (7.2 kgCO2e, 0.08kg scrap, 24% late).
Service/quality/resilience: reduces late-delivery risk for this variant (currently the worst-performing stock path) while cutting duplicate transport.
Evidence to monitor: partial-shipment rate, backorder-wait duration, late-delivery % on this path, cancel-due-to-delay volume from Backorder wait.

**S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides (governance intervention, cross-cutting)**
Mechanism: not an emissions lever itself, but makes every override auditable, enabling S1–S9 to be tuned safely and preventing silent policy drift.
Service/quality/resilience: enables faster detection of resilience-degrading side effects (e.g., R1 violations) before they cause service failures.
Evidence to monitor: override volume/reason codes, correlation of overrides with late-delivery/rework spikes.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  obsolescence rebound:** Larger CNC/heat-treatment batches cut kWh/item but raise the 6% obsolescence rate; if demand forecasts are wrong, "saved" production energy is offset by scrapped/unshipped items — a net material-waste increase disguised as an energy win.
2. **Batch wait  late-delivery displacement:** Tighter batching for energy savings pushes some Made-to-order orders past due dates, triggering emergency production (Availability check  Emergency production), which has *higher* energy (7.0 kWh) and much higher transport (52 kgCO2e) than the batch path it was meant to protect — displacing the impact rather than removing it.
3. **Consolidated truck/ground shift  hidden express fallback:** If S1/S5 push more orders to ground/consolidated truck and any slack estimate is wrong, customers or planners may compensate with late expedite requests (air/dedicated van), reversing the transport gain and adding a second shipment leg's emissions on top.
4. **Packaging changes  quality rebound:** Standardizing or lightening packaging to cut package_material_kg could increase damage rates for MTO heavy parts, feeding back into rework production/rework packaging loops (Q1 policy exists precisely because reinforced packaging cuts damage 40%) — a packaging-material win could become a rework/energy loss.
5. **Rework-reduction (S3/S7)  capacity displacement:** Shifting MTO volume from CNC_B to CNC_A reduces rework but may lengthen CNC_A queues, increasing Batch wait times, which under evidence increases late-delivery risk and may trigger more Emergency production — displacing the problem from "quality" to "schedule/emissions."
6. **Dedicated-van reduction  resilience erosion:** Replacing dedicated vans with shared courier lanes (S8) or splitting Critical-downtime (S9) reduces transport emissions per order, but if shared lanes are less reliable during disruption, R1 (alternate route availability for Service-contract) could be violated, raising cancellation risk for genuinely critical parts — an environmental gain that creates a resilience liability.
7. **Availability-check tightening  stockout/backorder shift:** Reducing partial shipments (S11) by restricting split-picking could increase full-order backorder wait or cancellations instead, shifting the negative outcome from "extra transport leg" to "lost sale" or "expedite later," which can reintroduce emissions via emergency fulfillment.
8. **Customer behavior adaptation:** If Service-contract customers notice slower (ground) delivery under S4, they may start marking more orders as urgent or requesting expedite override, eroding the very emissions gain the change was designed to capture (a behavioral/administrative rebound, monitorable via `expedite_flag` trend).

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a **segmented, evidence-gated redesign** rather than a single low-emission lever, because the data shows impacts are concentrated in specific segments/paths with different risk tolerances:

**Core package:**
- **Standard-stock (55% of volume, already low emissions per order):** S4 (airground+priority pick for Service-contract) + S5 (twice-daily consolidated truck for Regular, excluding near-deadline orders) + S11 (tighten availability-check to cut partial shipments). This targets the largest volume segment's clear SP1 violations (22%) and the worst-performing variant (partial shipment path: 24% late, extra transport) with low service risk given large existing slack (18-31h median cycle vs. much longer promised windows implied by low late rates).
- **Made-to-order (34% of volume, highest energy/scrap intensity):** S3 (CNC_A over CNC_B when queue/due-date slack allows) + S7 (risk-based inspection gate on high-rework combinations) + S2 (dynamic batching with segment-differentiated wait caps — generous for Regular, tight for Service-contract) + S6 (packaging-route compatibility check to cut rework packaging). This attacks the dominant environmental driver in the data — rework (11% Regular / 10% SC, and the CNC_B/rework path shows 32 kWh and 1.8kg scrap) — before pursuing batching efficiency, since rework reduction is a "no-regret" move that improves energy, scrap, cycle time, and quality simultaneously.
- **Critical-downtime (11%, small volume but highest transport intensity):** S9 (split true-downtime vs. precautionary-urgent) + S8 (shared courier lanes for the precautionary subset only), keeping dedicated vans (with mandatory SP2 justification) exclusively for verified true-downtime cases. This avoids the constraint violation of blanket dedicated-van elimination while still shrinking the 38-52 kgCO2e/order footprint for a meaningful share of this segment.
- **Cross-cutting governance:** S1 (green shipping recommender, encoding SP1/SP2 thresholds) and S12 (exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides) operationalize and monitor all of the above, ensuring R1 (alternate route for Service-contract) is preserved and that overrides are visible rather than silent.

**Why this is balanced:** It does not chase the single lowest-emission action (e.g., forcing all Critical-downtime onto ground/stock exception, which the data shows carries real late-delivery/cancellation risk) nor treats all air/van transport as equally unjustified (Critical-downtime's 52 kgCO2e dedicated-van path is kept where SP2-justified). It prioritizes rework and partial-shipment reduction first because these are "win-win" (lower emissions *and* better service/quality), uses waiting-time caps rather than unconstrained batching to avoid the obsolescence and late-delivery rebounds documented in the evidence, and adds governance (S12, S1) so that policy conformance (SP1/SP2/Q1) and resilience (R1) are continuously enforced rather than assumed.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Phased rollout with log-based validation:**
1. **Baseline freeze:** Snapshot current segment-level indicators (Section 2) for at least one full cycle before changes, per segment and per path variant, to serve as control.
2. **A/B or staged deployment:** Roll out S4/S5/S11 first (Standard-stock, lowest risk), then S3/S6/S7/S2 (Made-to-order), then S8/S9 (Critical-downtime), each with a defined observation window (e.g., 4-8 weeks) before the next phase, so effects can be attributed to specific changes.

**Conformance monitoring:**
- **SP1:** Track % of orders where route_mode = air/dedicated_van despite 85% ground/consolidated feasibility; target material reduction from current 22% (Std-stock SC) violation rate toward single digits, tracked weekly.
- **SP2:** Track % dedicated-van cases missing justification events; require this to approach 0% post-S9/S8, with automated blocking of unjustified van dispatch.
- **Q1:** Track reinforced-packaging usage for MTO >8kg vs. inspection-certified low-risk exceptions; monitor correlation with rework/return loops to confirm S6/S7 are closing the gap, not just relabeling risk.

**Segment-level service monitoring:**
- Late-delivery %, median and p90 cycle time, and cancel-due-to-delay rate, tracked per segment (not blended) after each phase, with explicit alert thresholds (e.g., no more than +1-2pp late-delivery increase tolerated per change) tied to rollback triggers.
- Partial-shipment and backorder-wait rates specifically for S11, to confirm reduction without a corresponding rise in stockout/cancellation.
- Obsolescence rate (%items unshipped >14 days) for batch-produced MTO items, to catch S2 rebound risk early.

**Exception governance (S12):**
- All SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides logged with reason codes (e.g., "carrier disruption," "forecast uncertainty," "customer escalation") and reviewed periodically (e.g., monthly) by process owners.
- Spikes in override volume correlated with late-delivery or rework upticks should trigger a review of caps/thresholds (batch wait caps, slack thresholds) rather than ad hoc reversal.
- Resilience check (R1): confirm, via route-mode diversity per Service-contract case, that an alternate route remains genuinely available (not just nominally) during simulated or actual carrier disruption periods — this is the key safeguard against S1/S4/S5/S8 eroding resilience while chasing emissions reductions.

**Success criteria:** Environmental indicators (transport kgCO2e/order, energy kWh/item, scrap kg/order, package material kg/order) trend down at the segment level *without* late-delivery, rework, or cancellation rates exceeding pre-defined tolerance bands, and without SP1/SP2/Q1 violation rates or override volumes increasing.
