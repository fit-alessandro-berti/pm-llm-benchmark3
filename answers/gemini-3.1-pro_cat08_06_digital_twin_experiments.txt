# 1. Model scope and state variables

**Digital Twin Boundary**: The simulation encompasses the end-to-end building-permit process from portal submission to the final decision (issue or reject permit), including municipal processing, cross-case interactions (e.g., inspections), and applicant/third-party interactions.

**Level of Detail**: The simulation models discrete activities. It distinguishes between service time (active work) and waiting time (queuing or applicant delays). It explicitly models resource constraints, shift calendars, and case attribute-driven routing.

**Entities**:
*   **Cases (Applications)**: The primary moving entities through the process.
*   **Applicants/Architects**: mode of generation dictating submission clustering.
*   **Inspection Bundles**: Grouped cases awaiting site visits in specific districts.

**State Variables**:
*   *Case Attributes*: `case_id`, `case_type` (Simple, New residential, Commercial, Complex), `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_fire` (boolean), `requires_environment` (boolean), `requires_public_notice` (boolean), `has_objections` (boolean).
*   *Process State*: Current activity, missing documents count, rework loop counts, timestamps (arrival, start, end).
*   *System State*: Queue lengths per activity, current time, day of the week, seasonality phase (peak vs. non-peak).

**Resources & Calendars**:
*   *Portal/System*: Infinite capacity, 24/7.
*   *Clerks*: 5 FTE (Monday-Friday 08:00-16:00), 1 dedicated to portal support on Mondays.
*   *Planners*: 8 FTE (Monday-Friday 09:00-17:00), 2 restricted to Simple renovation.
*   *Fire Reviewers*: 2 FTE (Tuesday-Thursday only).
*   *Environmental Reviewers*: 3 FTE (Monday-Friday), 60% capacity (40% non-permit duties).
*   *Inspectors*: 4 FTE (Tuesday-Friday field visits).
*   *Legal Officers*: 2 FTE (restricted availability during council meeting weeks).

**Cross-Case Dependencies**: 
*   **Arrival Bursts**: Top architects submitting multiple related applications simultaneously.
*   **Inspection Batching**: Bundling cases by `district` to optimize travel times for Inspectors.
*   **Legal Queue Stalls**: Blockages affecting multiple cases during council weeks.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

**Arrivals**: Estimate a non-stationary Poisson process. Calculate base arrival rates and apply multipliers for season (1.7x for Mar-May/Sep-Nov) and day-of-week (2.2x on Mondays). Model top-20 architect bursts using batch-arrival distributions.
**Service Times**: Since start timestamps are largely missing, service times must be estimated using the empirical summaries (median and p90). Fit theoretical right-skewed distributions (e.g., Lognormal or Gamma) matching these percentiles.
**Waiting Times**: Waiting times are emergent properties of the simulation queues, but applicant/third-party delays (e.g., "Receive missing documents" and "Public-notice period" with objections) will be explicitly sampled from heavy-tailed distributions derived from time-between-events in the log.
**Branching Probabilities**: Calculate empirical routing probabilities from the event log using PM4Py DFG transition frequencies conditionally split by `case_type`, `application_value`, and flags.
**Rework**: Estimate the probability of backward transitions in the log (e.g., post-planning or post-inspection rework) and model these as decaying probabilities to prevent infinite loops.
**Resource Calendars**: Use the given HR definitions as the baseline schedule. Align inferred active hours from the log's timestamps to validate schedule boundaries.
**Dependencies**: Calculate empirical inspection batch sizes per district and travel time modifiers. Use public-notice durations to infer objection frequencies.

**PM4Py Usage**: Use PM4Py to import the log, format the dataframe, extract the Direct Follows Graph (DFG) for baseline transition matrices, and analyze variants. DFG edge weights will be conditionalized based on case attributes using custom Pandas slicing combined with PM4Py discovery.

# 3. Assumptions

*   **Arrivals**: Non-stationary seasonal and weekly patterns will persist. Architect burst behaviors remain structurally identical to the historical 18 months.
*   **Service Times**: True service times follow parametric distributions (Lognormal/Gamma) matching the stated median/p90 summaries. Start timestamps can be robustly ignored since service distributions are provided.
*   **Routing**: Routing strictly adheres to the stated logic (e.g., fire review triggers). Where deterministic thresholds are missing (e.g., area threshold), probabilistic routing conditioned on historical proxy indicators is assumed.
*   **Resources/Calendars**: Resources are perfectly strict about their calendars. No unplanned overtime occurs. Environmental reviewers' 40% non-permit work is modeled as a uniform reduction in hourly capacity or blocked calendar slots.
*   **Rework**: Rework times are drawn from the same distribution as initial service times, but routing probabilities into rework decay after the first loop.
*   **Applicant/Third-party Response**: Modeled as independent exogenous delays (Pareto or Log-normal distributions) not constrained by municipal working hours.
*   **Cross-case Dependencies**: Inspection batching is the only shared-resource dependency explicitly modeled. Cases do not strictly block each other except through resource queue consumption.
*   **Non-stationary Demand**: The simulation does not model long-term multi-year trend growth, only annual seasonality.
*   **Missing Data**: Cases lacking objection logs will be imputed: if public notice exceeds 30 days, it is classified as "with objections." Rare emergency cases removed from the log are statistically negligible regarding capacity constraints.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up Approach & Run Length**: The model requires a warm-up period to populate the system, as the longest variants take up to 260 days. We will use Welch's graphical method to determine steady-state queue stabilization, discarding the first 4 months of simulation time. Run length will be 22 months (4 months warm-up + 18 months valid data) to capture the full seasonality cycle.

**Replications and Random Seeds**: We will execute 50 independent replications per scenario. We will use Common Random Numbers (CRN) across experiments (e.g., matching the arrival stream and case attribute streams) to isolate the variance caused by interventions rather than stochastic noise. 

**Uncertainty Treatment**: 
*   Heavy-tailed durations (e.g., applicant responses, public objections) will be modeled with Lognormal or Pareto distributions to capture extreme outliers rather than truncating empirical bounds.
*   Results will be reported with 95% Confidence Intervals for medians and 90% Credible Intervals for p90 metrics across the 50 replications.
*   Sensitivity analysis will be run on the "objection proxy" threshold to ensure assumptions about missing data do not drastically swing decision metrics.

# 6. Validation strategy

*   **Face Validation**: Review simulation routing logic, process trees, and calendar configurations with municipal domain experts to ensure logical accuracy.
*   **Event-log Replay Validation**: Generate a synthetic event log from the Baseline simulation. Compare DFG edge frequencies and start/end activity transition probabilities between historical and synthetic logs.
*   **Distributional Validation**: Compare the Cumulative Distribution Functions (CDFs) of end-to-end cycle times by `case_type` between the historical data and baseline simulation output using Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S) tests.
*   **Resource-utilization Validation**: Verify that the simulated busy time of resources matches historical HR estimates (e.g., Planners should show high utilization; Environmental reviewers ~60%).
*   **Bottleneck Validation**: Ensure the simulation reproduces the "Monday Clerk queue spike" and "Council meeting Legal stall" dynamically as observed in reality.
*   **Out-of-sample Validation**: Calibrate the simulation on the first 12 months of the event log data. Run the simulation to predict performance for the remaining 6 months and compare predictions against the actual hold-out data.

# 7. Decision criteria

Interventions will be evaluated based on the following metrics:
1.  **Median and p90 Duration**: Measured end-to-end overall, and stratified by `case_type`. Primary goal: reduce p90 duration for Complex cases by >15%.
2.  **SLA Compliance**: Percentage of cases meeting statutory limits (e.g., Simple renovation < 30 days, Complex < 180 days).
3.  **Fairness**: Variance in cycle times across districts (particularly D3/D5) and between burst (top architects) vs. individual applicants. Goal: K-S test showing no significant geographic bias.
4.  **Resource Utilization**: Ensure no human resource exceeds 85% utilization (to prevent queue explosion) while maximizing inspector field efficiency.
5.  **Rework and Backlog**: Total number of rework loops per case and total active cases in the system (WIP) at the end of peak months.
6.  **Robustness**: Performance degradation under S1, S2, and S3. Interventions that cause catastrophic failure (infinite queues) under stress are heavily penalized.
