## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Immediate expansion to Delta and Epsilon is risky without further data. The evidence suggests that automation availability alone does not improve cycle times when accompanied by increased volume and case complexity. Alpha and Beta, despite automation, saw median cycle times rise. Gamma, which had a pilot rollout and lower initial complexity increase, showed improvement. This indicates that "automation" is not a silver bullet; it requires sufficient capacity and appropriate case-mix.

The next branch to target should be a **high-volume, low-complexity** branch similar to Gamma, avoiding the high-complexity profile of Alpha/Beta. Treatment assignment should be a **phased rollout**: enable automation for 50% of cases initially to monitor specialist queue backlogs.

Minimum measurements include **pre-period (3 months)** and **post-period (3 months)** cycle times, specifically tracking the **90th percentile** to detect tail degradation. We must collect **queue-state data** (specialist wait times) to monitor if automation is displacing work to a bottleneck. Spillover monitoring requires tracking the **fraud desk wait times** to ensure borderline cases are not piling up there.

Guardrails for customer harm include a **CSAT floor of 3.8**; if CSAT drops below this, the rollout pauses for a root-cause analysis. A decision rule for scale-up is: **Expand only if the 90th percentile cycle time is stable or decreases in the first 2 months of adoption**, and **escalation_pct does not exceed 15%**. This ensures we do not trade speed for quality or burden the specialist team.

print("Branch Pre/Post Contrasts (Median Cycle):", contrasts)

# 4. Construct relative-time indicators for staggered adoption
df['treated'] = df['automation']
df['event_time'] = df['month'] - df.groupby('branch')['automation'].transform('max') # Month of last availability
# Correct logic: Event time relative to first month of automation
df['first_treatment_month'] = df.groupby('branch')['automation'].transform('idxmax')
df['months_since_treatment'] = (df['month'] - df['first_treatment_month']).dt.days / 30

# 5. Estimation: Descriptive Panel Model (Fixed Effects)
# Note: Interpreting aggregated medians as continuous outcomes
model = sm.WLS(df['median_cycle_h'], sm.add_constant(df[['high_complexity_pct', 'arrivals', 'automation']]), weights=df['arrivals']).fit()
print("Fixed Effects Coefficient for Automation:", model.params['automation'])

# 6. Treatment Intensity Model
# Intensity = automated_fast_path_pct
df['intensity'] = df['automated_fast_path_pct']
model_intensity = sm.WLS(df['median_cycle_h'], sm.add_constant(df[['intensity', 'high_complexity_pct']])).fit()

# 7. Warnings about small N and aggregation

## 10. Final claim ladder

*   **Observed:** Automation availability (`automation=1`) and usage (`automated_fast_path_pct`) are positively correlated with increased `high_complexity_pct` and `arrivals` in Alpha and Beta.
*   **Suggestive:** There is no evidence that automation reduces median cycle time for the branches that adopted it (Alpha, Beta), as their cycle times increased post-adoption compared to controls (Delta, Epsilon).
*   **Conditionally causal:** Under strict assumptions that case-mix and staffing changes are exogenous, automation may have a neutral effect on tail performance (P90) for branches with low initial complexity (e.g., Gamma).
*   **Not supported:** The claim that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" is not supported by the provided data, as the effect is confounded by selection bias, volume increases, and case-mix deterioration.
