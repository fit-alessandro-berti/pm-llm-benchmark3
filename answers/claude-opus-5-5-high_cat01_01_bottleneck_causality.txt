## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 70/100.**

The claim that Orion causes longer cycle times rests on the crude contrast: Orion 21.96 h vs Nova 15.24 h (+6.72 h). That gap is what case mix predicts. Orion receives 42.9% High-complexity cases (Nova: 12.5%) and 64.3% compliance-required cases (Nova: 25%). Within every complexity stratum the sign reverses: Low −2.14 h, Medium −2.21 h, High −3.43 h. Standardizing to the pooled complexity mix gives −2.51 h (Orion 17.14 h, Nova 19.65 h).

**Strongest evidence for.** Besides the reversal, the process structure matches at equal complexity. Variant shares are similar, High-complexity rework is 50% in both groups, and non-queue, non-review time follows the same pattern: about 0.8 h base, +1.4 h with compliance, +1.5 h with Request Info. Queue wait makes up 75–96% of the within-stratum gap; review service differs by at most 0.4 h.

**Strongest evidence against.** Stratification does not identify a causal effect. Complexity is only a three-level proxy for risk. All of Orion's Medium and High cases ran at night, while 4 of Nova's 6 did not, so Medium/Day and High/Day have no overlap. Key cells hold one or two cases, only completed cases are observed, and there are just two appeals. An Orion benefit through shorter queues is suggested but not identified, and a harmful effect hidden by completed-case selection cannot be ruled out.

**Why the reassignment would probably backfire.** It would move about seven mostly Medium/High, compliance-bearing night cases to a day-centred group with fewer certified reviewers, raising Nova's volume by about 44%. If the stratum contrasts carried over, that would add about 19 case-hours (+0.6 h per case overall) before congestion, which grows non-linearly with utilization. Orion's mean could still fall just because its mix gets easier, which would look like a success when it is not.

## 2. Causal graph

```text
unobserved_case_risk      -> complexity, compliance_required, routing_rule, review_service, rework, quality_outcome, case_completed
complexity                -> routing_rule, compliance_required, review_service, rework, queue_wait
shift                     -> routing_rule, arrival_load, queue_wait, unobserved_reviewer_skill
arrival_load              -> routing_rule, queue_wait
compliance_required       -> compliance_performed, routing_rule
compliance_performed      -> queue_wait, cycle_time
routing_rule              -> resource_group
resource_group            -> unobserved_reviewer_skill, queue_wait, review_service, rework
unobserved_reviewer_skill -> review_service, rework, quality_outcome
rework                    -> review_service, cycle_time, quality_outcome
queue_wait                -> cycle_time
review_service            -> cycle_time
cycle_time                -> case_completed, quality_outcome
case_completed            -> (selection node: the sample is conditioned on case_completed = 1)
```

**Supported by the supplied facts:** complexity→routing_rule and complexity→compliance_required (routing and policy); shift→routing_rule (Orion covers nights); routing_rule→resource_group; compliance_required→compliance_performed (assumed); queue_wait, review_service and rework→cycle_time (these are components of cycle time); and conditioning on case_completed.

**Assumptions:** every arrow out of resource_group, both unobserved nodes, arrival_load→routing_rule, and cycle_time→quality_outcome.

**What a static graph hides:** the routing engine probably reacts to current backlogs. Queue wait at time t then shapes load and routing at t+1, which is time-varying confounding. Night backlog spilling into the day shift, and fatigue building over a shift, also make the shift effect depend on state.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Case-mix confounding:** the crude gap reflects what Orion receives; Orion's own effect is about zero | Routing sends High/compliance cases, which have longer service, an extra compliance step and more Request Info, to Orion | Reversal in all three strata; standardized −2.51 h; identical "other"-time structure; equal High rework | The within-group advantage is consistently negative in 3/3 strata and 4/4 shift cells, which a pure null would not predict so systematically; complexity is coarse | Exact matching on complexity × compliance × shift, plus propensity scores on richer pre-routing features (claim value, line of business, classifier score); overlap diagnostics and trimming to common support | **Favors H1:** the matched contrast is about 0 h, with an interval covering 0, and the crude +6.7 h disappears. **Weakens H1:** the matched contrast stays at −2 h or below after richer adjustment. |
| 2 | **Beneficial Orion effect masked by case mix** (capacity or prioritization) | More certified reviewers give more servers per queued case, so queue_wait is shorter | 75–96% of the stratum gap is wait; service nearly equal; Orion waits less despite 13/14 high-load arrivals; the gap grows with complexity (−1.6, −1.9, −3.3 h) | Night/high-load collinearity; n=2 Nova High; Nova's day queue may be shared with other work | Queue-state reconstruction from timestamps (WIP ahead, certified reviewers on shift at routing); front-door-like analysis estimating group→wait given queue state and then wait→cycle; within-reviewer comparisons for staff who work in both groups | **Favors H2:** Orion's wait advantage persists at equal WIP per available reviewer, or shrinks exactly in proportion to staffing (capacity that could be transferred by staffing). **Weakens H2:** no wait difference once WIP per reviewer is equalized and shift is fixed. |
| 3 | **Shift/load explanation:** night operations are faster, and Orion's "advantage" is a night advantage | Fewer interruptions and less competing work at night; day cases wait overnight for elapsed-time reasons | Within Nova, night is faster in every stratum (−0.9, −1.6, −2.0 h); 92.9% of Orion cases are at night | Within night cells Orion is still faster (−1.70, −1.03, −2.43 h), though with only 1–2 Nova cases; "high load" at night should slow cases, not speed them up | Within-shift contrasts on a larger log; negative-control outcome: Register→Classify duration, which routing cannot affect; regression discontinuity on classification time around shift change | **Favors H3:** Orion−Nova is about 0 within night; Register→Classify is also faster at night; there is a jump at the shift boundary regardless of group. **Weakens H3:** Orion stays faster at equal shift and clock time. |
| 4 | **Selection into completed cases** hides slow cases (possibly a harmful Orion effect) | Right truncation: long-running, abandoned or transferred difficult cases are missing, and Orion carries more of them | Only completed cases are present; Orion's cases are longer and so more exposed to truncation | No direct evidence about open cases; the wait advantage is consistent across strata | Extract open, abandoned and transferred cases with an extraction date; Kaplan–Meier and Cox models by group within strata, treating open cases as censored; age distribution of WIP | **Favors H4:** Orion has disproportionately many aged open cases, and survival curves within strata converge or flip. **Weakens H4:** open-case shares and ages are similar by group, and censored analysis reproduces about −2.5 h. |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Freeze and instrument (2–4 weeks) | Do **not** execute the 50% reassignment. Extract the full event log with start/complete timestamps and reviewer IDs, including open, abandoned and transferred cases. Log the routing score and reason, certification, and queue length at routing. | Measurement and selection (H4, H6); positivity mapping | All cases (observational) | No operational change; data-protection review | WIP aging, censored cycle-time curves, queue-state reconstruction, a DFG with real performance times, conformance of compliance against policy, negative-control segment durations | Tests truncation and logging artifacts; builds the pre-routing risk covariates and baseline needed for the pilot | If more than 10% of Orion cases are open beyond the observed 36 h maximum, or negative controls differ by group, re-baseline before Phase 1. |
| 1. Small randomized routing pilot | Among cases the engine rates eligible for both groups (a certified reviewer is available in both; Low and Medium, both shifts), randomize 10–15% to the non-default group, **in both directions** | Total effect of group at equal case risk (H1, H2, H3, H7) | Case, block-randomized by complexity × shift × load; use a switchback by time block if queues interfere | Exclude High and cases for which Nova lacks certification; cap Nova's extra WIP at +10%; keep compliance rules unchanged; escalation path back to the default group | Per-arm cycle time, wait, service, "other" time, variant and DFG shares, Request Info rate, conformance, appeals, and queue length in **both** groups (spillover) | Unbiased intention-to-treat contrast per block, a direct test of transportability, and a check on whether Orion's advantage holds at equal shift | Stop a block if, after 20 or more cases per arm, the randomized arm's median cycle time exceeds control by more than 2 h, SLA breaches rise by more than 5 percentage points, Nova's queue exceeds its threshold, or any compliance deviation occurs. |
| 2. Queue and compliance capacity, not the label | Add certified reviewer capacity, or a compliance fast lane, to Nova's day shift; introduce complexity-aware priority in Nova's queue | queue_wait and compliance waiting (H2, H5) | Time blocks (stepped-wedge by week, or day-shift switchback) | Same reviewers and routing rule; quality audit sample; utilization cap | Reconstructed queue waits, WIP, utilization, wait around the compliance step, cycle time of Nova Medium/High day cases | Tests whether Orion's advantage is capacity that can be transferred by staffing, independent of the group label | Revert if wait falls by less than 1 h after 4 blocks at acceptable cost, or if appeals or audit errors rise above baseline. |
| 3. Conditional rollout | Adopt a routing change only for the strata where Phase 1 shows non-inferiority (e.g., move Low cases to Nova when Orion is congested); expand capacity where Phase 2 worked | The policy estimand itself | Stepped-wedge by team or week | Guardrail metrics; retain the ability to override the routing rule | Continuous conformance and performance monitoring; drift detection on case mix and waits | Confirms that the effects hold at scale under realistic congestion | Roll back any stratum whose mean cycle time rises more than 1 h above the pre-rollout benchmark for 2 consecutive weeks. |

**Final recommendation.** Reject the proposed 50% reassignment. Its rationale, Orion's higher overall mean, is a case-mix artifact: at equal complexity, Orion is faster in every stratum, and the standardized contrast is about −2.5 h. The comparison is still not identified, because Orion's complex work is almost entirely night work and the sample contains only completed cases. Moving Orion's difficult, compliance-bearing night cases into Nova would probably lengthen them and congest Nova's queue, while Orion's mean could fall merely because its mix gets easier. First, instrument the log with real timestamps, reviewer IDs, queue state and open cases, and test for truncation and logging artifacts. Then run a capped, block-randomized, two-way routing pilot among cases both groups can legitimately handle, excluding High cases. In parallel, test whether adding queue or compliance capacity on Nova's day shift reproduces Orion's shorter waits. Change routing only for the strata where randomized evidence shows non-inferiority in cycle time and no loss of quality.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case risk within the coarse complexity labels.** Routing may send Orion systematically harder, or easier, cases within each label. *Most valuable addition:* a pre-routing risk score recorded at Classify, together with the routing engine's decision reason (claim value, line of business, classifier confidence).
2. **Group vs shift collinearity.** Orion's complex work is observed only at night, so an Orion effect cannot be separated from a night-operations effect. *Most valuable design change:* quasi-random or randomized two-way routing within both shifts (Phase 1), so that Orion-day and Nova-night cells gain support.
3. **Queue vs service mechanism.** Wait mixes queueing with compliance waiting, and the review service values do not reflect repeated reviews. *Most valuable addition:* activity-level start and complete timestamps with reviewer IDs, allowing reconstruction of the queue state (WIP and available certified reviewers) at routing and at review start.
4. **Selection into the completed-case sample.** Long-running, abandoned or transferred cases may differ by group and reverse the within-stratum picture. *Most valuable addition:* a full extraction that includes open, abandoned and transferred cases with a censoring indicator and extraction date, enabling survival analysis.
5. **Speed–quality trade-off.** Two appeals and a possibly incomplete 30-day window cannot detect a quality cost of faster (Orion) or reassigned (Nova) handling. *Most valuable addition:* a complete, longer outcome window with an appeal date plus a finer quality measure (reopen flag, audit error rate) linked to the reviewing resource.
