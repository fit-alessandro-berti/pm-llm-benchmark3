# Audit of Prefix-Time Explanations for Claim Settlement Delay

**Target**: a model that scores, at the moment of a coverage check, the probability that a claim will exceed a 10-day settlement target.
**Audit principle**: the only defensible unit of analysis is the information *actually available at the scoring instant*. Every explanation below is graded against that, and against the normative process the team published — which does **not** always match the process in the warehouse.

---

## 1. Leakage & Temporal-Consistency Findings

The critical structural point: **most of the top-ranked features are not prefix features at all.** They are computed over the *complete* trace and then joined back onto the prediction row. Their SHAP values are therefore *descriptions of already-finished cases*, not evidence available at the time of prediction.

### Global feature table

| # | Feature | Finding type | Severity | Why | Remediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `total_case_duration_hours` | Target-derived leakage / complete-trace | **Critical** | Equals the outcome by construction. If the label is "10-day breach", this field contains the answer. It ranks #1 — a diagnostic of pipeline order, not of model skill. | Drop entirely. Never compute at scoring time; never join. Re-run with this removed and report the true ranking. |

### Sequence-pattern table

| Pattern | Finding type | Severity | Why | Remediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` | **Process impossible** + same-timestamp ambiguity + leakage | **Critical** | In the normative process, closure is terminal — nothing can follow it. In the warehouse, closure and damage assessment share a timestamp and the ingestion sequence index is missing, so the observed order is an artifact of ingest order, not of the real process. Any DFG/prefix model trained on these traces learns a fiction. | Do not learn from warehouse traces without reconstruction. Determine the true order (event `as_of` vs. ingestion order), re-sort with a deterministic tiebreak, and **hard-exclude impossible edges** (post-closure events) from all training and inference data. |
| `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` | Post-t0 leakage; also process-valid but wrongly used | **Critical** | This edge *is* legitimate in the normative process — an appeal can trigger a second settlement decision. The defect is not the edge; it is that it is **impossible at t0**: an appeal has not occurred yet. Using it as an "early" pattern is pure hindsight. Also, `Settlement Decision Recorded` fires twice after an appeal, so a naive occurrence/one-hot encoding silently conflates "first decision" with "re-decision" and can double-count. | The *edge* may be kept as a label-side or downstream construct. As a **t0 feature** it must be dropped. If any downstream use is intended, expose separate features: `n_settlement_decisions`, `first_decision_type`, `has_reconsideration`. |
| `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` | Temporal misuse; not leakage per se | Medium | The pair is process-valid, but at t0 the request may not yet have been satisfied — the *pair* may not exist yet. Its negative SHAP presumably reflects that cases which complete the pair fast are fast, but that is only knowable later. | Replace with prefix-count features: `doc_requests_so_far`, `doc_received_so_far`, `outstanding_request_at_t0`, `median_request_to_receipt_hours_in_prefix`. |
| `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` | Future-possible; post-t0 | **High** | Legitimate *process* (fraud may open after a trigger) but strictly post-t0. Using it at scoring time is leakage. It may also be a *response* to delay. | Drop as a t0 feature. If the intent is to predict fraud, model it separately as a downstream event. |
| `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` | Future-possible; post-t0 | **High** | Process-valid but only exists later. | Drop as a t0 feature. |

## 2. Process-Model Consistency Checks

Build a **versioned reference model of the normative process** and validate every event trace against it, then re-run scoring on validated traces only.

**2.1 Reference model.** Encode the normative process as a declarative state machine with explicit event vocabulary, guard conditions, and terminal states. E.g. a state machine with states `REGISTERED  COVERAGE_CHECKED  {FAST_TRACK: DAMAGE_ASSESSED, SLOW_TRACK: EVIDENCE_CYCLING}  DECIDED  (if PARTIAL|DENY and appealed) RECONSIDERED  DECIDED  CLOSED`. Guards: `Damage Assessment Completed` only after coverage check; `Documents Requested` only after coverage check; `Settlement Decision Recorded` only after damage assessment (or fraud investigation); `Appeal` only after a Partial/Deny decision; `Claim Closed` only after a final decision or withdrawal. Fraud investigation may run in parallel and may be opened after a trigger.

**2.2 Prefix-time availability.** Every feature must carry an `as_of` timestamp, and the extractor must assert `as_of <= t0`. Implement this as a *gate*: the scoring job refuses to run if any feature's `as_of` exceeds `t0`. Also verify no feature's *value* was computed from an event set that includes post-t0 events — timestamp-level checks alone are insufficient (see `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`).

**2.3 Activity-order feasibility.** Run a conformance pass that rejects or flags traces containing forbidden transitions. Critically: the warehouse's actual trace grammar **differs from the normative model** (e.g. `Claim Closed` appears *before* `Damage Assessment Completed`, which the normative order forbids). So the warehouse order cannot be used as the reference without human adjudication. Resolution: for every trace, determine the *true* event times (ingest `as_of` vs. business `event_time`), and for same-timestamp pairs use the documented business tiebreak (or a deterministic heuristic). Record an adjudication flag and exclude unresolvable traces from training.

**2.4 Same-timestamp ambiguity.** Detect all event pairs sharing a timestamp. For each, require a documented ordering rule; otherwise assign a `AMBIGUOUS_ORDER` flag. Measure prevalence — if ambiguous pairs are common and concentrated on the modeled edges, the trace-based features are unrecoverable and sequence features should be abandoned in favour of prefix aggregates.

**2.5 Complete-trace vs prefix features.** Assert in code that all scored features derive from the prefix event set `E(t0) = {e : e.event_time <= t0}`. Add a static check on the feature SQL/registry forbidding any query that references the full event table without a `event_time <= t0` filter — this is what let `total_case_duration_hours` and `full_trace_variant_id` in.

**2.6 Explanation-referent validity.** Each explanation must name either (a) an event that has occurred at or before t0, (b) a process state enabled at t0, or (c) a t0-time contextual attribute. It must not reference events that are merely *future-possible*. Distinguish three categories explicitly: **enabled** (the activity is the currently active stage at t0 — e.g. evidence cycling), **impossible** (the activity cannot occur at all under the model — e.g. an event after `Claim Closed`), **future-possible** (legal but not yet observed). Only (a) and (b) are admissible for prefix explanations; (c) is admissible only as a *risk statement about a future branch*, phrased probabilistically and never as an observed fact.

**2.7 Repeated settlement decisions.** Verify that each `Appeal` event is followed by exactly one additional `Settlement Decision Recorded`, and that appeals occur only after Partial/Deny. This is a *semantic* rule, invisible to a frequency-based DFG (the edge `Decision  Decision` looks identical to an appeal loop). Model the distinction with a typed event (decision *type*, decision *sequence index*) rather than raw activity names.

**2.8 DFG / prefix-annotated DFG — role and limits.** A DFG is a frequency-weighted projection of the traces; it can detect impossible-looking edges and repeated patterns, and a **prefix-annotated DFG** (edges annotated with the earliest time they can occur) supports prefix-time features. But a DFG is *descriptive*, not *normative*: a frequent edge may be an artifact of ingestion ordering rather than a real process rule (as with `Claim Closed  Damage Assessment Completed`). Therefore the DFG must never be the sole source of truth — it is used to *cross-check* the declarative model, and the declarative model wins.

**2.9 Footprint / alignment checks.** Footprint-style checks can show that a claim's observed path is incompatible with a candidate sequence (e.g. a claim whose damage assessment precedes coverage check). These are useful for detecting *which* traces to trust and for quantifying how much of the warehouse data is non-conformant.

**2.10 Negative controls.** A useful diagnostic: score claims whose traces are *non-conformant* and confirm the model gives them no elevated risk (or that performance on them collapses). If performance on impossible traces is high, the model has learned artifacts.

## 3. Alternative Feature Interpretations

**Defensible predictive signals (available at t0, non-outcome-derived)**
- `initial_damage_complexity` — frozen at registration; reflects genuine case mix. Non-causal label: the operational lever is estimation and triage, not the field itself.
- `queue_length_at_t0` — a *process-state / congestion* signal. Predictive and operationally actionable at the **queue-management** level (staffing, routing), but not manipulable at the claim level, and not a causal claim about any individual claim.
- `initial_reserve` — valid if truly the initial, frozen value. A severity proxy.
- `claimant_channel` — available at registration; proxy for language, region, broker network. Requires fairness review.

**Process-state / prefix signals (must be reconstructed from true prefix timestamps)**
- `num_doc_requests_so_far`, `doc_received_so_far`, `outstanding_request_at_t0`, `days_in_coverage_to_t0`, `fraud_investigation_open_at_t0` (only if opened  t0), `current_stage_at_t0`.
- These are the *correct* replacements for the leaked document and trace features. They describe where the claim is in the process and how much slack remains.

**Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review**
- `claimant_region` — correlated with repair-shop availability and language; risk of disparate impact via routing.
- `claimant_channel` — correlated with language and region.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after fixing the SQL) — correlated with repeat-claimant tenure, policy age, product mix.
- `initial_reserve` — correlated with claim size and customer segment.

**Drift / calendar proxy**
- `calendar_week` — encodes holiday and policy regimes. Keep only with explicit controls; it is a monitoring aid, not a causal lever, and is the most likely source of silent drift.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**
- `total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `employee_experience`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (as currently computed), `final_handler_team` (unless replaced by a true t0 handler field).

**Explicitly not admitted at t0, but legitimate downstream constructs** — `Appeal  Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Coverage Check  Fraud Investigation Opened`, `Damage Assessment  Settlement Decision`. These belong to *later* explanation surfaces (at the decision point, at closure), where they are the right objects. The fix is not to ban them but to **route them to a decision-time model with its own t0**.

## 5. Recommendations for Explanation Validation

**P0 — Stop the bleeding (this week)**
1. **Freeze and version the feature registry.** Every feature gets: owner, definition, source table, `as_of` semantics, mutability flag, and an explicit `available_at` field. The scoring job **fails closed** if any feature lacks `available_at <= t0`.
2. **Automated leakage gate in CI.** Static + dynamic checks: no SQL over the event table without `event_time <= t0`; no reference to derived outcome columns (`duration`, `closed_at`, `decision_*`, `appeal_*`); no join to snapshot tables whose refresh time exceeds t0; SHAP value non-zero for any feature whose `available_at > t0` triggers a build failure.
3. **Triage SHAP by lineage, not magnitude.** Produce a leakage matrix (feature × `available_at` vs `t0`) and report the top contributions *after* filtering. The current top-3 should be expected to collapse; treat any residual high contribution as a new leakage hunt.
4. **Fix the known SQL defect** (`prior_claim_count_before_t0`  `created_at < t0`) and re-derive importance.
5. **Re-derive t0** via stable event id and true event time, and stop relying on fuzzy-matched string labels.

**P1 — Rebuild the feature set (2–4 weeks)**
6. **Prefix-only feature family:** stage-at-t0, elapsed time per stage, doc request/receipt counts so far, outstanding-request indicator, fraud-investigation-open-at-t0, coverage-to-t0 duration, channel, complexity, region, initial reserve.
7. **Event-time fidelity:** backfill true `event_time`; record ingestion sequence index; where ties remain, apply a documented business tiebreak and flag `AMBIGUOUS_ORDER`.
8. **Conformance layer:** declarative state machine; per-trace validation flags for impossible order, post-closure events, appeal-before-decision, decision without damage assessment; quantify non-conformance rate and exclude or repair flagged traces.
9. **Snapshot redesign:** intraday queue snapshots (5–15 min) with explicit grain and a true join key; timestamped document counts computed from event times rather than nightly snapshots.
10. **Mutable-field versioning:** bitemporal store for `risk_score`, `reserve`, `handler`, `employee_experience`; as-of queries at t0.

**P2 — Model and explanation integrity**
11. **Rebuild and recalibrate** after leakage removal; the base rate and all local explanations change materially, so publish new calibration curves and decision thresholds, and version the model.
12. **Explainability alignment:** assert that every feature in the explanation is admissible at t0; introduce a "consequence-time" model for downstream explanation surfaces (decision point, closure) so that `Appeal  Settlement Decision` and `Coverage Check  Fraud Investigation` are explained *at the time they occur*, not back-projected.
13. **Explanation stability testing:** perturbation and subsampling tests on prefix features; report rank stability of SHAP across resamples and across the 5-minute snapshot tolerance. High instability signals reliance on fragile artifacts.

**P3 — Fairness, robustness, monitoring**
14. **Proxy analysis:** for `region`, `channel`, `prior_claim_count`, `reserve`, test (a) univariate disparity in selection and error rates, (b) partial dependence on protected-adjacent attributes, (c) leave-one-group-out performance, (d) performance with the proxy removed. Use a "provably insufficient proxy" style test: if a proxy alone reproduces most of the model's accuracy, treat it as suspect.
15. **Policy/regime controls:** include holiday and policy-regime flags alongside `calendar_week`; verify the model's reliance on week does not shift abruptly across training windows.
16. **Monitoring:** per-feature drift (PSI) and *availability-lag* monitoring (share of rows where `available_at > t0` must be 0), trace-conformance rate, and unexplained-score alarms. Any non-zero availability-lag rate is an incident.
17. **Audit trail:** for every published explanation, log feature version, `available_at`, SHAP, and the model version, so any prior explanation can be replayed and invalidated deterministically.
```

**Bottom line:** the model's apparent skill is largely an artifact of features computed over completed traces. The reported ranking (`total_case_duration_hours` #1, `appeal_seen_anywhere` #2, `full_trace_variant_id` #7) is a pipeline-order problem, not a modelling problem. The credible early-warning signal is a small set of prefix and environment features — queue depth, stage, complexity, case size, region — and even those support *association* only. The most urgent engineering work is making feature availability a first-class, enforced property of the feature store, and treating the warehouse's event ordering as untrustworthy until it is reconstructed and validated against a declarative process model.

## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

**Framing.** The model team reports *mean absolute importance across a scoring run*, but most of the reported features cannot exist at `t0`. A SHAP value attached to a feature that encodes the completed case is not an "early explanation" — it is a restatement of the label in feature form. The audit below therefore separates (a) *unavailable at `t0`* (temporal leakage), (b) *available but process-inconsistent* (impossible or mis-ordered event derivations), (c) *available and usable but semantically over-claimed* (predictive  causal  actionable), and (d) *repairable lineage defects*.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The validation suite should be executable, versioned, and run both offline (on historical claims) and online (as a scoring-time gate). Four layers:

### 2.1 Normative model specification (declarative reference model)

Encode the normative process once, as data, not as code scattered across notebooks: states `{REGISTERED, COVERAGE_CHECKED, EVIDENCE_CYCLING, DAMAGE_ASSESSED, FRAUD_INVESTIGATION(optional, parallel), DECIDED, RECONSIDERED, CLOSED}`, activity vocabulary, guard predicates, and multiplicity rules. Examples the model must encode:

* `Coverage Check Completed` requires `Claim Registered`.
* `Documents Requested` may repeat; `Documents Received` requires an open request; `Documents Validated` requires the matching receipt.
* `Damage Assessment Completed` requires either a completed fast track or a closed evidence cycle.
* `Settlement Decision Recorded` requires `Damage Assessment Completed`; allowed values Approve/Partial/Deny/Withdrawn.
* `Appeal` requires a prior `Partial` or `Deny` decision; a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` requires an `Appeal` in between (multiplicity  2 for `Settlement Decision Recorded` in the simplified model, and only after appeal).
* `Claim Closed` requires a final decision (or withdrawal); nothing may follow it.
* `Fraud Investigation Opened` may occur after a trigger and may overlap evidence collection or damage assessment.

**Role and limitations.** A declarative model gives an *entailment* check ("can this event happen here?") and is the only artifact that can distinguish *impossible* from *future-possible*. Its cost is that it encodes one simplification of reality; the model must be versioned and exceptions (e.g. manual overrides, emergency fast lanes) recorded as explicit deviations rather than as silent tolerance, otherwise "impossible" quietly becomes "usually not".

### 2.2 Prefix-time availability checks (temporal)

For every feature in the store, require an `as_of_timestamp` (the logical time the value describes) and an `available_at_timestamp` (when it could have been computed). Tests:

1. `as_of_timestamp <= t0` for all admitted features.
2. `available_at_timestamp <= t0` — a value can be *true* at `t0` but not *computable* then (e.g. nightly snapshot). Both must hold.
3. **Definition-level check:** the feature's SQL/logical definition must reference only tables/events bounded by `t0`. This is the only check that catches `total_case_duration_hours` and `full_trace_variant_id`, which pass a naive "does the field exist at `t0`" test.
4. **Extraction-bound check:** `extraction_date`-style predicates are forbidden in any feature used for online scoring (`prior_claim_count_before_t0`); replace with event-time predicates and test with synthetic rows dated after `t0`.
5. **Snapshot-version check:** for snapshot features, verify the snapshot instance used is the latest one with `snapshot_time <= t0` (catches `same_day_document_count_snapshot` at 23:00 refresh).
6. **Mutable-feature check:** features flagged mutable must resolve to the version with `version_time <= t0`; if no such version exists, the feature is dropped, not imputed (catches `risk_score_current`).

### 2.3 Activity-order feasibility checks

Run the observed trace of each claim through the normative model and classify each consecutive transition as:

* **enabled** — permissible by the model given the prefix;
* **impossible** — violates a guard (e.g. `Damage Assessment Completed` after `Claim Closed`);
* **future-possible** — permissible but not yet observed at `t0` (e.g. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` in general, `Coverage Check -> Fraud Investigation Opened`);
* **unresolved** — same timestamp with missing sequence index.

Enforce three separate obligations: (a) *no impossible transition may be used as a feature*; (b) *no future-possible transition may be used in a `t0` explanation*, even if legally permitted; (c) *legality of a transition says nothing about its predictive value* — `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is legal but still useless at `t0`.

**Alignment/footprint checks** (does the observed sequence fit the reference model, and where does it deviate?) and **DFG checks** (which transitions actually occur, with what frequency) complement the normative model: they *discover* behaviour, the normative model *adjudicates* it. **Limitation:** a frequent DFG edge is not a rule. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` would appear in a DFG built on unindexed, same-timestamp data and would look frequent; frequency cannot license an impossible transition, and a DFG must never be used to *generate* features without a normative feasibility filter. Prefix-annotated DFG features (edges restricted to events with `event_time <= t0`) are usable; whole-trace DFG features are not.

### 2.4 Same-timestamp ambiguity handling

* Detect all event pairs with identical timestamps (Case Z: `Claim Closed`, `Damage Assessment Completed`).
* If a reliable sequence index exists, use it as the ordering key and record `order_source = sequence_index`.
* If absent, mark the pair **unordered**: do not create a directed edge, do not use either order as a feature, exclude the claim from pattern-based training, and log a data-quality event for the source system.
* Enforce a maximum accepted ambiguity rate per dataset (e.g. <1% of transitions) as a release gate; otherwise the ordering repair must precede any sequence feature work.
* Tie-break by *business* logic only as a last resort and label it as such (`order_source = business_rule`), never as observed fact.

### 2.5 Complete-trace vs prefix-only test

For each feature, run the *same definition* twice: once on the full trace and once on the truncated prefix `events  t0`, and require that the prefix version is used for scoring. Where the two differ (they will for `num_document_requests_total`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `last_resource_role`, `total_case_duration_hours`), the feature is by construction out-of-time and must be replaced by the prefix counterpart. This "truncation differential" is a cheap, high-yield audit and is the primary defence against future re-introduction of leakage via feature requests.

### 2.6 Explanation-referent validity (does the story point to something enabled?)

For every local explanation item, assert that the referenced object is one of: (i) a feature with a valid `as_of  t0`; (ii) an activity **observed** in the prefix; (iii) an activity **enabled** at `t0` by the normative model; (iv) an exogenous context (calendar, queue). Reject: references to activities not yet occurred (`Appeal`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Claim Closed`, `Fraud Investigation Opened`), to activities that are *impossible* at `t0`, and to fields whose semantics depend on the completed case. Additionally assert **narrative/attribution consistency**: an explanation must not use a pattern with negative contribution to imply a *cause*, and must not describe a future branch as if observed.

### 2.7 Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` semantics

* The coarse label may occur twice; the validator must accept `DEC -> APP -> DEC` and must **not** flag it as a duplicate/ordering violation.
* It must distinguish `decision_sequence_no = 1` (first decision) from `= 2` (reconsideration after appeal) and must reject a third.
* `Appeal` must be preceded by `Partial` or `Deny`; `Appeal` after `Approve` is impossible.
* Any feature keyed on "a decision occurred" must be defined at a decision-time prediction point, not at `t0`; and any model that treats the *first* decision as the outcome must use `decision_sequence_no = 1` only, otherwise the label itself becomes ambiguous (a claim that breaches after a re-decision and one that breaches on the first decision are not the same event).
* `Claim Closed` must be terminal in the validator, which is exactly why `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is caught as impossible.

### 2.8 Release gates

A claim is eligible for training/inference only if: prefix availability passes; no impossible transition in the prefix; ambiguous-order share below threshold; all features prefix-safe. Otherwise the claim is quarantined with a reason code, and the quarantine rate is itself a monitored metric (a rising rate means the source system's ordering guarantee degraded).

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Defensible predictive signal (available at `t0`, semantically stable, non-outcome-derived)
* `initial_damage_complexity` (registration-frozen). May claim: at `t0` the case is expected to require more work. May **not** claim: that complexity causes breach; that re-scoring complexity would fix it; that it is a trait of the claimant.
* `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (prefix timestamp). May claim: claims that spent longer in early stages have less observed progress. May not claim: that this is "slack" in the settlement clock, or that speeding registration accelerates settlement.
* `initial_reserve` (if registration-time). May claim: larger expected loss  longer handling. May not claim: that setting reserves differently changes outcomes; and reserve is not a policy variable for a case handler.

### Process-state or congestion signal
* `queue_length_at_t0` (timestamped snapshot). May claim: at `t0` the processing environment was congested, and congestion is associated with delay — this is the strongest legitimate *process* signal here and the most operationally actionable at the **capacity** level (staffing, routing). May not claim: that the queue causes this specific claim to breach; that adding staff guarantees fewer breaches; or that the queue reading is exact to the minute.
* Prefix-derived WIP features (`docs_received_before_t0`, `open_request_age_at_t0`, `roles_seen_before_t0`, `stage_at_t0`, `doc_request_to_receipt_hours_so_far`). May claim: current work-in-progress state predicts remaining duration. May not claim: that current state determines the outcome.

### Proxy requiring fairness / robustness review (retain with controls, monitor)
* `claimant_region` — entangled with repair-network coverage, language, channel, socioeconomic composition. May claim: region is a useful *operational* grouping variable for capacity planning. May **not** claim: region causes delay; must not be used to deprioritise or route claimants by region; requires group calibration, equal-opportunity testing, and a residual check against repair-network availability (if the signal vanishes once network coverage is controlled, it is a coverage-deficit proxy and needs a fairness decision, not a feature).
* `claimant_channel` — correlated with region/language/document burden. Same standards.
* `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after SQL repair) — history proxy; may be a stable risk signal but must not be used to set expectations or treatment intensity toward repeat claimants.

### Drift / calendar proxy
* `calendar_week`, `month`. May claim: the pipeline's *training data* has regime structure (holiday, policy change) that shifts base rates; useful for monitoring and for capacity planning. May **not** claim: a specific week "causes" delay; and a week effect is not a lever. Use only with regime indicators and drift alarms; retrain/refresh base rates after policy changes.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired (not admissible now)
* `total_case_duration_hours` (label), `full_trace_variant_id`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team` (until a `t0` handler snapshot exists), `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (until the SQL predicate is fixed), `employee_experience` (joined as of extraction date — a post-hoc join for a pre-`t0` activity, and a plausible proxy for handler tenure/region; must be re-joined with an `event_date` predicate and then reviewed as a proxy).

### Retained but re-framed (legitimate, non-causal)
* `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` — legitimate *process* rule, legitimate *post-decision* feature in a decision-time model; inadmissible as an early explanation. The distinction is temporal admissibility, not validity of the rule.
* `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` — legitimate transition; usable only after damage assessment (a different prediction point).

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

**P0 — Stop the bleeding (immediate).**
1. **Quarantine and remove** `total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `last_resource_role`, and all whole-trace pattern features from scoring and from the SHAP report. Re-run the global ranking; the reported ordering is void.
2. **Publish a lineage inventory** for every feature with: definition, source tables, `as_of` semantics, `available_at` semantics, mutability flag, historical-availability status, and admissibility verdict. Features without a defensible `as_of`/`available_at` are not shippable.
3. **Fix the known SQL defect** (`claim_created_at < t0`, exclude current claim) and **re-join `employee_experience` by event date**; until fixed, both are flagged as out-of-time.

**P1 — Feature-store redesign.**
4. **Dual-timestamp feature store**: every feature row carries `as_of_timestamp` (time the value describes) and `available_at_timestamp` (time it could have been produced), plus `version` and `is_mutable`. Serving-time assertion `available_at <= t0` is enforced in the online path, not by convention.
5. **Point-in-time-correct joins**: replace extraction-date predicates with event-time predicates; replace nightly snapshot features with event-timestamp-derived counts; implement `risk_score_as_of_t0` style versioning for mutable scores and reserves.
6. **Prefix-first feature library**: build the prefix WIP/state features (`stage_at_t0`, `docs_received_before_t0`, `open_request_age_at_t0`, `request_to_receipt_hours_so_far`, `roles_seen_before_t0`, `elapsed_in_stage_at_t0`) as first-class, because they are the legitimate successors of the removed leaked features and preserve real predictive structure.
7. **Handler/team snapshot at assignment** to give `final_handler_team` a legitimate form (`handler_team_at_t0`) instead of a closure-time guess that agrees only 65% of the time.

**P2 — Prefix-only recomputation and conformance testing.**
8. **Truncation-differential harness**: for every feature, compute the full-trace and prefix versions on the same claims; any feature whose scoring version differs from the prefix version is rejected automatically. Run this on every feature-request PR.
9. **Normative-model conformance suite** (Section 2) executed in CI: prefix-availability, transition feasibility, same-timestamp ambiguity, complete-trace detection, explanation-referent validity, repeated-decision semantics. Publish a conformance dashboard (impossible-transition rate, ambiguous-order rate, quarantine rate) and treat regressions as build failures.
10. **Ordering repair upstream**: work with the source system to guarantee a monotonic sequence index for same-timestamp events; until then exclude unordered transitions from DFG/prefix-annotated-DFG features and record a data-quality incident.
11. **Explicit sequence scope discipline**: maintain two separate model families and two separate explanation surfaces — a coverage-check-time model (prefix only) and a decision-time model (may use `DEC -> APP -> DEC`), each with its own feature timestamps and its own narrative templates, so that a legal-but-future transition is never migrated into an early explanation.

**P3 — Calibration and explanation stability.**
12. **Recalibrate after feature removal** (Platt/isotonic on a held-out, prefix-safe period) and re-derive base rates; report the full SHAP/stability profile anew. The pre-repair probabilities (0.82 / 0.71 / 0.64) must be marked withdrawn in any dashboard or saved report.
13. **Explanation stability tests**: bootstrap over cases and time blocks; require rank stability of the top admissible features, and flag local explanations whose direction flips across resamples or across adjacent prediction points.
14. **Pointwise sanity checks**: assert for every case that the sum of named contributions plus the residual reconstructs the reported log-odds, that no contribution comes from an inadmissible feature, and that the narrative mentions only features with non-trivial attribution (no invented drivers).
15. **Counterfactual and stability-of-t0 checks**: perturb `t0` by ±15 minutes and confirm the admissible features and score move smoothly; a large jump indicates timestamp-boundary leakage.

**P4 — Proxy, fairness and drift governance.**
16. **Proxy review** for `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `initial_reserve`: (a) regress admissible features on group membership to see what the proxy encodes; (b) measure within-group vs between-group error and calibration; (c) test whether the signal survives controlling for repair-network availability, language and channel; (d) forbid using these features for triage, pricing, deposits or customer communication without a documented fairness decision.
17. **Drift/regime monitoring**: track feature distributions, base rate and error by `calendar_week`, month and policy-regime version; add explicit holiday and policy-version indicators so a regime change is not silently absorbed into a "week effect"; refresh base rates after policy changes.
18. **Consequence and label hygiene**: formalise the outcome definition on `decision_sequence_no = 1` with a documented rule for breaches decided on reconsideration, and keep the label window independent of any feature's computation window.
19. **Recurring explanation audit**: every release ships a leakage-diff table (features added/removed, admissibility verdicts), a conformance report, a calibration report, and a fairness report; unexplained residual buckets must be reported and bounded, and large unexplained residuals are treated as a leakage symptom until proven otherwise.
