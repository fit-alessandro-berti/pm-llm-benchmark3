def suppress_lead_copies(ocel, lead_type='Order', rule='earliest_created'):
    df = ocel.get_df()
    # creation timestamp per object of lead type
    creation = {}
    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        for oid, ot in zip(row['object_ids'], row['object_type']):
            if ot == lead_type and row['activity'] == 'Create Order':
                creation[oid] = row['timestamp']
    new_ot = dict(ocel.object_types)
    kept = []
    for (eid, oid) in ocel.object_types[lead_type]:
        # find other lead objects related to the same event
        rel = [o for (e2, o) in ocel.object_types[lead_type] if e2 == eid]

        if oid == owner:

    new_ot[lead_type] = kept
    return OCEL(events=df, activities=..., object_types=new_ot, ...)

## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is the *assignment of executions to units of accountability*, realised mechanically as the `case_id` produced by `pm4py.ocel_flatting(ocel, object_type=X)`. I treat each of the six flattenings as a *claim* about what the business considers a unit of work — not as ground truth. In this snapshot the question "what is a case?" is not neutral: `Order` says "a customer commitment", `Package` says "a physical shipment", `Invoice` says "a receivable", `Delivery` says "a truck movement", `Payment` says "a cash event", `Item` says "a unit". The six are not nested partitions of the same fact; they are different projections of a process whose aggregation logic (packages that mix orders, invoices that mix orders) lives *between* the levels.

**Convergence.** Convergence = distinct business actors being *brought together* into one execution unit, either by copy (one event appearing in several cases) or by shared physical/financial containers (P3, P4, D2, V2). It inflates frequencies and creates the illusion of co-occurrence that may be logistical (real) or merely semantic (e.g. `V2 bills O2` and `V2 bills O3` share an invoice but not an invoice *event* per order).

**Divergence.** Divergence = one actor's story being *split* across cases, or evidence being dropped because an event has no relation to the chosen type. Divergence is the more insidious failure: it produces confident-looking but truncated conclusions (e.g. `Payment` flattening yields four single-event cases).

**Copied shared events.** I treat them as *both*: duplication for counting purposes (I never report a flattened frequency as a physical frequency without the multiplicity correction), and evidence for structure (the shared `event_id` and the O2O graph are the *only* record that I21 and I31 travelled together in P3/D2, that O2 and O3 share receivable V2, and that M2/M3 are two instalments of one invoice). The multiplicity table (Section 3) is the bridge between the two readings.

**Permitted transformations.** (T0) direct E2O, as given; (T1) copy-suppression with an explicit incidence table recording every suppressed assignment; (T2) *typed, depth-limited* relation propagation with a written semantic contract (never a blind transitive closure); (T3) importing O2O qualifiers (`partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`) as case/event attributes; (T4) annotating flattened logs with `copy_multiplicity` and `shared_event_id`. No events are invented, and no activity is renamed without declaring it.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**D1 — [Cross-order package P3] Order-level directly-follows fabricates packagedelivery pairings.** In O1's trace the pack events are e11 (P1, 10:00) and e12 (P2, 10:10), followed by e15 (D1, 11:00) and e16 (D2, 11:05). The DF edge *Pack Package  Dispatch* therefore reads e12e15, i.e. "P2 dispatched via D1" — **false**: P2 went out on D2 at 11:05. P1's packing is followed by another packing (e11e12), so P1's dispatch has no adjacent predecessor at all. The same corruption appears in O3: e14 (P4, 10:20)  e16 (D2, 11:05) claims P4 rode D2, while P4 actually left on D3 (e17, 11:15). Only O2 (e13e16) and O4 (e14e17) pair correctly. *Harmful*: any pack-to-dispatch latency computed from the order DFG is computed on wrong pairs (O1 would show 50 min for a package that waited 55 min on D2). Curiously, the **item** flattening preserves the pairing correctly (I21: e13 P3  e16 P3/D2), so the two views disagree about causality.

**D2 — [Cross-order package P4] The shortest order trace hides the longest physical journey.** O4's order trace has 8 events (the fewest), yet its item I41 sits in P4, dispatched on D3 at 11:15 and confirmed only at 2026-02-04 10:00 — 22 h 45 m, versus D1's 5 h 00 m and D2's 4 h 55 m. P4 also carries I32 (O3), so the delay contaminates two orders. *Harmful* if "short trace = simple/fast case" is assumed; *informative* as a demonstration that trace length is a property of the case notion, not of the order.

**D3 — [Cross-order packages P3/P4] Metric incoherence across views.** Order log: 6 "Pack Package" occurrences for 4 physical packings (e13, e14 each copied)  "1.5 packs per order". Item log: 6 packs for 6 items  "1 package per item". Package log: 4 packs for 4 packages. The truth (4 packages, 6 items, 4 package-order incidences) is recoverable from none of the three. *Harmful* for inventory-consolidation KPIs; *context-dependent* for process structure.

**D4 — [Consolidated delivery D2] Double-counted dispatch and delivery.** e16 and e24 are copied into O1, O2 and O3, so the order log reports **6 Dispatch and 6 Confirm Delivery occurrences against 3 physical deliveries (D1–D3)** — a 2× inflation of the fulfilment step — and generates DispatchDispatch self-loops (e15e16 in O1, e16e17 in O3) that look like repeated shipments. D2's 4 h 55 m transit is counted three times. *Harmful* for any "how many shipments?" statement; *informative* as the signature of consolidation (the multiplicity of an event equals the number of orders the delivery touches).

**D5 — [Consolidated invoicing V2] One receivable becomes two order-level payment plans.** e19, e22 and e25 are copied into O2 and O3. Both order traces read *Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Record Payment  …*, implying two invoices each settled in two instalments; reality is one invoice V2 with one two-part settlement. Consequences: order log counts 4 invoice occurrences vs 3 invoices; the Record PaymentRecord Payment self-loop is observed in **two** cases although it is one business event pair (e22e25) — the instalment phenomenon appears twice as prevalent as it is; and *which* order was short-paid first is unrecoverable. *Harmful*.

**D6 — [Partial payments M2/M3] The one distinguishing fact is invisible to the case notion.** `M2 partially_settles V2` and `M3 completes_settlement V2` differ only as O2O qualifiers; both events are labelled "Record Payment". - *Where does the process aggregate, and what is the shape of the object network?*  `discover_otg` (the six types and the five relation kinds: contains, packs, carries, bills, settles/partially/completes) plus `ocel_objects_interactions_summary` for the pair counts (Order–Item 6, Package–Item 6, Delivery–Package 4, Invoice–Order 4, Payment–Invoice 4).

- *Where is the time spent?*  object-interaction timing on D3/P4/I32/I41.

Where a flattened and an object-centric view disagree (e.g. conformance 0% vs 100%, or pack-to-dispatch pairing), I do not average them: I record both, explain them by the identity, and treat the object-centric view as authoritative for *structure* and the flattened view as authoritative for *per-case narrative*, with the shared-event incidence table as the arbiter of *frequency*.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Decision: B — primary lead object (`Order`) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (Package/Delivery, Invoice/Payment, Item).**

**Decision criteria.** (i) event and activity coverage; (ii) minimum viable trace length; (iii) case count for statistical support; (iv) alignment with the unit of accountability; (v) resistance to copy-induced inflation; (vi) ability to attribute latency and responsibility.

**Strongest supporting evidence for `Order` as primary.** It is the only flattening that retains **all 31 events and all 8 activities** with usable trace lengths (O1 12, O2 9, O3 13, O4 8), and it is the only one that anchors both ends of the commercial story (e01–e04 Create Order; e28–e31 Close Order) and the money steps (e18–e20, e21/e22/e25/e26). `Item` loses 15/31 events including all invoices and payments; `Delivery` and `Payment` retain 6 and 4. `Order` is also the unit on which the business closes the case (e28–e31 all target orders), so it is the accountability anchor.

**Strongest counterargument.** `Order` is precisely the object where consolidation bites hardest: 9 of 31 events are copied, aggregate frequencies inflate 1.35× (Dispatch and Confirm 2×, Pack 1.5×), the directly-follows edges mis-pair packages with deliveries (e12e15, e14e16), and the two order traces O2/O3 become near-clones for reasons that are copy artefacts rather than process standardisation. A lead object that systematically corrupts *physical* causality cannot carry an "end-to-end" analysis alone. Note also the deeper structural fact: the aggregation logic of this process lives in Package, Delivery and Invoice, not in Order — P3 mixes O2/O3, P4 mixes O3/O4, D2 consolidates three orders, V2 bills two orders. Any single-object view must cross those junctions.

**Why not C (reject a single lead)?** Because the object-centric alternative still needs a *narrative unit*: the OC-DFG and OTG describe structure but not per-case lifecycles, and the order case is the only one with a defensible "story" spanning creation to closure. Rejecting a lead entirely would leave no place to put cycle time, conformance, or ownership.

**Why the secondaries are mandatory and named.** *Fulfilment secondary:* Package (lane timing; P4 outlier) with Delivery (transit latency; D2 fan-in). *Settlement secondary:* Invoice (receivable latency; V2 instalments) with Payment as attributes (M2 partial / M3 completes). *Unit secondary:* Item (dwell; co-packing of I21&I31, I32&I41). Each secondary is declared with its own question set and its own multiplicity account, and none is allowed to answer questions outside its jurisdiction (e.g. the Payment view must never be asked for cycle time).

**Residual risk.** (1) With only 4 orders, 6 items, 3 invoices, 4 payments and 3 deliveries, *no* flattening supports statistical inference; every conformance number is a small-N artefact (0% vs 100% from D11). (2) O2/O3 similarity may persist as an artefact even after copy-suppression, if the underlying consolidation is real — that is a finding, not a bug, but it must be reported as such. (3) Cross-type comparisons (e.g. order cycle time vs delivery latency) require manual bridging that a single-object log cannot automate.

**Conditions under which I would change the decision.** (i) If the research question becomes *"where and why did fulfilment stall?"*, the lead becomes `Delivery` (with `Package` as its unit child) and `Order` is demoted to an attribution dimension — the current snapshot would then be analysed as D3/P4/I32/I41. (ii) If the business re-cuts packages and deliveries so that each is order-exclusive (a plausible operational change), the junctions that motivate the secondaries disappear and `Order` can legitimately stand alone (decision A). (iii) If instalment payments grow to dominate (more M objects per V), `Invoice` becomes co-lead with `Order`. (iv) If the OCEL is extended to many orders per case-family, I would re-derive the choice empirically from the coverage/multiplicity table rather than from these four cases.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
def suppress_lead_copies(ocel, lead_type="Order", rule="earliest_created"):
    """Keep one Order per shared event; record every suppressed copy."""
    df = ocel.get_df().copy()
    rels = ocel.object_types[lead_type]
    ev_rel = {}
    for eid, oid in rels:
        ev_rel.setdefault(eid, []).append(oid)
    creation = {}
    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        for oid, ot in zip(row["object_ids"], row["object_type"]):
            if ot == lead_type:
                creation.setdefault(oid, row["timestamp"])
    def owner(cands):
        if rule == "earliest_created":
            return sorted(cands, key=lambda o: (creation.get(o, creation and max(creation.values())), o))[0]
        if rule == "lexicographic":
            return sorted(cands)[0]
        return sorted(cands)[0]
    kept, incidence = [], []
    for eid, cands in ev_rel.items():
        own = owner(cands)
        for c in cands:
            incidence.append({"event_id": eid, "order": c, "kept": c == own,
                              "n_copies": len(cands)})
        kept.append((eid, own))
    new_object_types = dict(ocel.object_types)
    new_object_types[lead_type] = kept
    sup = OCEL(events=df, activities=ocel.activities,
               object_types=new_object_types, ocel_id=getattr(ocel, "ocel_id", None),
               qualifiers=getattr(ocel, "qualifiers", None))
    return sup, pd.DataFrame(incidence)

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**S1 — Copy-suppression sensitivity.** Rebuild the order log with three ownership rules (earliest-created order, lexicographic, most-direct-relations) and compare DFGs, variant sets, and per-case cycle times. The claim to test is whether the O2/O3 near-clone and the Record PaymentRecord Payment self-loop survive suppression: if the loop collapses (e22e25 both owned by O2), it was partly a copy artefact; if it persists in V2's invoice log, the instalment structure is real. The multiplicity table must explain every difference; unexplained differences invalidate the transform.

**S2 — Direct E2O vs propagated-relation variant.** Compare T0 (as given) with T2, where a *typed, one-hop* propagation is applied along the declared backbone OrderItemPackageDelivery and OrderInvoicePayment. Critical validation: propagation must **not** license the false inference "Confirm Delivery of D1 (e23) confirms D2" — i.e. a propagation from D1 must not reach D2 via O1's second item. Concretely, check that after propagation no event gains a Delivery object that it did not physically touch, and that the resulting per-order edges still pair P2 with D2 and P4 with D3. Compare resulting DFGs and check that the *packdelivery* pairing defect of D1 is repaired rather than merely relocated.

**S3 — Changing the lead object.** Run the whole pipeline with each of the six leads and with a compound "physical chain" lead (PackageDelivery), and re-evaluate the decision rule: if the headline question is "why did I32/I41 arrive on 2026-02-04?", the Item/Delivery/Package views must be primary and Order demoted to attribution. Record which findings are stable across leads (e.g. V2's two-instalment structure appears in Order and Invoice views) and which are lead-dependent (e.g. conformance 0% vs 100%).

**S4 — Frequency basis in the OC-DFG.** For every (activity, object-type) pair compute three numbers: raw event count (Dispatch = 3), count of *distinct objects* (Dispatch×Delivery = 3 distinct D objects), and *total object incidences* (Dispatch×Order = 6). Report each against the question it supports: throughput for the first, "how many distinct carriers" for the second, "how many order-shipment incidences" for the third. Any headline metric must name its basis.

**S5 — Timestamp-tie and batch-event sensitivity.** e23 and e24 share the timestamp 2026-02-03 16:00; recompute DFGs with alternative tie-breaks and report the stability of the ConfirmConfirm edge and of the resulting conformance. Separately, recompute cycle times with the batch Close events (e28–e31) removed to expose the discrimination hidden by D9 (all 26 h vs D3's 22 h 45 m transit).

**S6 — Consolidation ablation.** Remove the shared events (e16, e24, and separately e13/e14/e19/e22/e25) and recompute per-order conformance, variants, and directly-follows pairing; the residual should reveal which conclusions depend on consolidation (all order-level physical pairings) and which do not (V2's two-instalment pattern survives in the invoice view; the item/package/delivery uniformities are unaffected).

**S7 — Semantic-provenance validation.** Verify that the qualifier information (`partially_settles` vs `completes_settlement` for M2/M3, and the `packs`/`carries`/`bills` relations) is recoverable from the OCEL's object attributes/qualifiers; if it is not, the analysis must import it explicitly as T3, and a variant log with the enriched attributes must be re-run to confirm that D6's "two instalments" and D12's "mixed vs exclusive packing" become observable rather than assumed.
