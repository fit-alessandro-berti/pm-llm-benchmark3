# 1. Assumptions and notation

**Event-order convention**
- A1: When timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes, we treat the reported time as the midpoint of a 5-minute interval. Thus, a reported time `t` represents an actual time in `[t-2.5, t+2.5]` minutes.
- A2: For simultaneous rounded events (e.g., S1 end and S2 start at the same reported time), we define the order as:
  - S1 end  S2 start (S2 cannot start before S1 ends).
  - If timestamps are identical after rounding, we assume S1 ends first (since S2 queue entry depends on S1 completion).
- A3: For cases with `MISSING` S1 start, we assume the start occurred no earlier than the arrival time and no later than the S1 end time, with nonnegative service time.

**Capacity and availability**
- A4: S1 has two resources (I1, I2), each handling one case at a time. S2 has two resources (C1, C2), each handling one case at a time.
- A5: C2 was unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10 (rounded times). Actual unavailability is in `[10:52.5, 11:12.5]`.

**Censoring and missingness**
- A6: Cases marked `OPEN_IN_S1` or `OPEN_IN_S2` are right-censored at 14:00. Their service times are lower bounds (elapsed time).
- A7: Cases with `MISSING` S1 start (P05, P07, P11) require imputation. We derive bounds from:
  - Resource availability (no overlapping service for the same resource).
  - Queue entry (S1 start  arrival time).
  - Completion (S1 start  S1 end).
- A8: Cases with `WAITING_FOR_S2` at 14:00 (P16, P18, P19, P20) have censored S2 start times. Their S2 waiting time is a lower bound ( time from S1 end to 14:00).

**Little’s Law**
- A9: Little’s Law (L = W) is applied only to intervals where:
  - Arrivals and completions are fully observed (no censoring).
  - Queue lengths are stable (no open cases entering/leaving the interval).

---

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

**Definitions**
- **Waiting time (W)**: Time from queue entry to service start.
  - S1: `max(0, s1_start - arrival_s1)`
  - S2: `max(0, s2_start - s1_end)`
- **Service time (S)**: Time from service start to completion.
  - S1: `s1_end - s1_start` (if not missing)
  - S2: `s2_end - s2_start` (if not missing)
- **Sojourn time (T)**: Total time in station = waiting + service.
- **WIP (L)**: Number of cases in the system at a given time.
- **Throughput ()**: Cases completed per unit time.
- **Busy time**: Time a resource is actively serving cases.
- **Utilization ()**: Busy time / available time (2 resources × interval length).

**Imputation methods**
- **Missing S1 start (P05, P07, P11)**:
  - Lower bound: `arrival_s1` (service starts immediately).
  - Upper bound: `s1_end - min_service_time` (service time  5 mins, the smallest possible).
  - Central estimate: Midpoint of bounds.
- **Open cases (P15, P17, P21)**:
  - S1 service time: `[elapsed, )` (elapsed = current time - s1_start if known, else arrival to 14:00).
  - S2 waiting/service: `[elapsed, )` (if in S2 queue).
- **WAITING_FOR_S2 (P16, P18, P19, P20)**:
  - S2 waiting time: `[s1_end - 14:00, )` (if s1_end  14:00).

**Queue length convention**
- At time `t`, a case is in the queue if:
  - S1: `arrival_s1  t < s1_start` (or `arrival_s1  t` if `s1_start` is missing/OPEN).
  - S2: `s1_end  t < s2_start` (or `s1_end  t` if `s2_start` is missing/OPEN).
- Simultaneous events (e.g., S1 end and S2 start at 08:30) are ordered as S1 end first (A2).

**Uncertainty handling**
- Report ranges for metrics where bounds are derived from imputation/censoring.
- Use central estimates for comparisons but highlight sensitivity.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence**:
- **08:00-10:00**: Both stations have high utilization (S1: 95-100%, S2: 88-93%). S2 waiting times are higher (up to 55 mins for P08, delayed by C2 outage). S1 is the primary bottleneck early on, as cases queue for I1/I2.
- **10:00-12:00**: S1 utilization drops (75-85%) due to fewer arrivals, but S2 utilization remains moderate (50-55%). The C2 outage (10:55-11:10) causes delays (P08 waits 55 mins for S2). S2 becomes the bottleneck during this period.
- **12:00-14:00**: S1 utilization is low (50-60%), but S2 is overwhelmed (0-5% utilization due to censored cases). All cases arriving after 12:00 are stuck in S2 queue (P16-P20) or S1 queue (P21-P22). S2 is the clear bottleneck.

**Hypotheses**:
- The C2 outage exacerbated S2 bottlenecks but did not create them. S2 was already constrained by high demand and limited capacity.
- S1's bottleneck shifts to S2 as the morning progresses, likely due to:
  - S2's fixed capacity (2 resources) vs. S1's ability to handle cases faster (average S1 service=27.5 mins vs. S2=40 mins).
  - Accumulation of cases in S2 queue during the C2 outage.

**Conclusion**: S1 is the bottleneck early (08:00-10:00), S2 becomes the bottleneck mid-morning (10:00-12:00), and remains the bottleneck late morning (12:00-14:00), with the C2 outage acting as a catalyst for S2 delays.

---

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

**Scenarios**:
1. **Optimistic (O)**: Assume minimal service times and no delays.
   - Missing S1 starts: Lower bound (arrival time).
   - Open cases: Use elapsed time as final time.
   - C2 outage: Assume no impact (cases routed to C1 immediately).
2. **Central (C)**: Midpoint imputations and observed delays.
   - Missing S1 starts: Central estimate (midpoint of bounds).
   - Open cases: Report ranges.
   - C2 outage: Account for 15-min outage.
3. **Pessimistic (P)**: Assume maximal delays and worst-case imputations.
   - Missing S1 starts: Upper bound (S1 end - 5 mins).
   - Open cases: Treat as infinite service time.
   - C2 outage: Assume all cases during outage are delayed by full outage duration.

**Stable conclusions**:
- S1 is the bottleneck early (08:00-10:00) in all scenarios.
- S2 is the bottleneck mid/late morning (10:00-14:00) in all scenarios.
- The C2 outage worsens S2 delays but does not change the bottleneck ranking.

**Sensitive conclusions**:
- **S1 utilization**: Ranges from 75-100% in central scenario. Pessimistic scenario shows higher utilization due to longer service times.
- **S2 waiting times**: Pessimistic scenario shows S2 waiting times up to 90 mins (vs. 55 mins central), but the bottleneck conclusion remains.
- **Peak WIP**: Central scenario shows peak WIP of 6 in S2 (12:00-14:00), but pessimistic could be higher if cases are delayed indefinitely.

**Recommendation**: Focus on S2 capacity expansion or dynamic routing to mitigate bottlenecks.
