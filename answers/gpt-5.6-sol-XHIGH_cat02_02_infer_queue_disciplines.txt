# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

### Evidence conventions and assumptions

- **Assumption A1 — arrival visibility:** I treat `arrival` as the recorded scan time. Physical arrival may have occurred up to two minutes earlier, but whether dispatch knew about the job before the scan is unknown. Near-boundary jobs are therefore marked uncertain rather than definitely eligible.
- **Assumption A2 — non-preemption:** Except for the explicit QB segments, FIFO, priority, SPT, and appointment choices are evaluated non-preemptively at completion or resource-release times.
- **Assumption A3 — appointments:** No tolerance is supplied. I distinguish an exact-slot rule from an early/late appointment window; I do not infer a specific window.
- **Assumption A4 — skills:** Compound requirements are conjunctive. For example, `firmware+electrical` requires both skills, consistent with the note that only D-F is fully qualified for D05.
- **Assumption A5 — tie-breaking:** Where needed, FIFO is used only as an illustrative tie-break within equal priority.
- **Assumption A6 — remaining estimates:** For QB counterfactuals, estimated remaining time is arrival `est_min` minus logged active service. This may differ from an unrecorded updated estimate.

**Observation:** For every supplied job, logged active service minutes equal `est_min`, including B01’s three segments totaling 45 minutes. Thus no estimate error is visible retrospectively in this sample. That does not prove that dispatch trusted the estimates, that estimates were not revised, or that switching costs were zero.

QB is not strict preemptive priority. A more coherent reconstruction is:

1. Priority-1 urgency may cross a preemption threshold, as B02 did.
2. Once urgent work finishes, suspended B01 tends to regain service rather than compete normally with priority-2 jobs.
3. Otherwise the queue often behaves FIFO: B04 then B06.
4. The 09:48 dispatcher reassignment is a genuine exception unless the dispatch decision was committed before B05 became visible.

The immediate transitions show no positive, minute-visible setup gaps. Thus large time-based resumption costs are not observed, although non-time costs of repeated interruption or prolonged suspension remain plausible.

- a frozen or gated dispatch list;
- aging that raises C04 over the newly arrived C06;
- an unrecorded readiness hold on C06.

QD exhibits unmistakable skill feasibility, but skill routing alone is not a complete sequencing rule. Its most plausible operating model is:

1. Route or reserve jobs to qualified resources, with scarce compound skills protected.
2. Honor D06’s appointment on D-X.
3. Use roughly FIFO order inside the resulting resource-specific streams.
4. Avoid starting D05 before D-F’s meeting because it cannot be completed non-preemptively.
5. Keep assignments sticky, which would explain why D10 waits for D-X rather than moving to idle D-F.

The sticky-assignment explanation is a hypothesis, not an observed fact.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

**Supporting cases**

- **A03:** The clearest appointment case. At 08:20 it is neither oldest, highest priority, nor shortest, yet starts exactly at its appointment.
- **A06:** It starts at the first completion after its 09:15 appointment. This supports a non-preemptive, late-tolerant appointment mechanism, although FIFO makes the same prediction at 09:20.
- **A02, A05, A07:** Once appointment overrides are set aside, these jobs follow recorded arrival order, supporting FIFO fallback.

**Challenges and explanations**

- **A05/A06:** Starting A05 at 09:05 guarantees, based on its visible 15-minute estimate, that an exact 09:15 slot cannot be honored. Non-preemption explains why A06 is not started at 09:15 once A05 is underway, but it does not explain the 09:05 commitment. An undocumented tolerance or “serve at next resource release” rule is required.
- **A04 and A08:** Both priority-1 jobs wait behind lower-priority work, ruling out ordinary priority as QA’s general rule.
- **A02’s 08:32 start:** There is a two-minute idle gap after A03. No known exception explains it, but it does not alter the selected job.
- **A1 calibration:** The 08:52–08:58 gap is a known resource constraint and should not be treated as a dispatch violation.

## QB

**Supporting cases**

- **B02:** Priority 1 and explicitly associated with an “urgent arrival”; it preempts B01 immediately after a two-minute recorded response interval.
- **B05 at 10:00:** It is selected over older B01 and shorter B04, consistent with a priority-1 override.
- **B06 at 10:36:** It wins over B07 under both FIFO and priority, although this episode alone cannot distinguish those rules.
- **B01 resumptions:** B01 resumes at 09:31 and 10:14, supporting a resume-first component below the top-priority threshold.

**Challenges and explanations**

- **B03 over B05 at 09:48:** This directly contradicts strict preemptive priority because B05 had been scanned two minutes earlier. It can be reconciled only if the reassignment to B03 was committed before 09:46, B05 was temporarily ineligible, or a manual override occurred. The recorded “dispatcher reassignment” supports an override but does not give its logic.
- **B01 over B03 at 09:31 and over B06 at 10:14:** These selections contradict strict priority and SPT. A resume-first rule or cost of leaving work suspended can explain them, but that rule is undocumented.
- **B04 over B06 at 10:28:** This is FIFO-like and contradicts priority.
- **B07:** Its 10:30 appointment is not protected: B04 starts at 10:28 and crosses the appointment, then B06 starts before B07. This is strong evidence against appointment scheduling in QB.
- **Resumption costs:** No positive minute-level setup gaps are visible, so a large time cost is not supported. Quality, cognitive, or future restart costs could still motivate finishing B01.

## QC

**Supporting cases**

- **C02, C03, C04:** They form an exact 8–14–22 minute SEPT progression from the recorded initial backlog.
- **C06:** At 11:34 it is the shortest candidate by a wide margin and is selected.
- **C05:** Its selection at 10:54 supports a high-priority or SLA escalation layered over SEPT.

**Challenges and explanations**

- **C04 over C06 at 10:32:** A rolling SEPT rule would choose C06. A frozen batch, aging, or unrecorded readiness restriction is required.
- **C05 over C06 at 10:54:** This violates pure SEPT but is explainable by C05’s priority 1 and passed deadline. Those two explanations cannot be separated here.
- **C01 over C07 at 11:40:** This contradicts SPT, priority, and earliest-deadline scheduling simultaneously. It is consistent with anti-starvation, an initial-batch completion requirement, or an unrecorded hold on C07.
- **C07’s deadline:** Selecting C07 first at 11:40 would have allowed both C07 and C01 to meet their recorded deadlines using visible estimates. Thus the observed choice is not a reasonable EDF consequence.
- **C05 scan uncertainty:** A delayed scan could make C05 physically present at 10:10, but by 10:18 it is unquestionably recorded and waiting. Scan delay therefore cannot explain its continued deferral through C03 and C04.

## QD

**Supporting cases**

- **D02 and D05:** Firmware requirements direct them to D-F. D05’s compound requirement makes D-F uniquely qualified.
- **D07:** Its mechanical-plus-electrical requirement makes D-X uniquely qualified.
- **D01, D04, D08, D09:** Mechanical work is consistently handled by D-M despite D-X also becoming mechanically capable at 11:20, consistent with preserving the flexible resource.
- **D06:** D-X idles before starting it exactly at its 11:30 appointment.
- **D10:** It starts on D-X while D-F is still in its meeting, so the observed resource is valid under the known availability state.

**Challenges and explanations**

- **D10 at 11:36:** D-F can perform electrical work and could finish D10 by 11:48, before its 12:00 meeting. Skill compatibility alone therefore does not explain the idle time. Sticky routing, setup, meeting preparation, or an unrecorded hold is needed.
- **D-X from 11:20 to 11:30:** D08’s visible eight-minute estimate would fit before D06’s appointment with two minutes of slack. Idling is explainable by appointment protection, distrust of estimates, setup time, or a prior assignment of D08 to D-M, but none is logged.
- **D07 over D10 at 11:48:** Both are compatible with D-X, while priority and SPT favor D10. Local FIFO or a sticky assignment explains D07.
- No observed assignment violates the published skills. The contradiction is therefore to **skill routing as a sufficient dispatch rule**, not to skill eligibility itself.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

The ranking below reflects plausibility as an explanation for the pivotal anomalies, not merely whether the phenomenon exists.

|---:|---|---|---|

| 2 | Appointment windows and capacity reservation | High: A03 starts exactly on time, A06 starts at the first release after its slot, and D-X idles before D06 | Changes “exact appointment” into a hybrid of slot protection, tolerance, and FIFO fallback; explains much of QA and QD |

A particularly important distinction is between a **temporary eligibility exception** and a **policy exception**. For example, if D10 was not ready at 11:36, D-F’s idle time is not a dispatch violation. If D10 was ready but had been irrevocably assigned to D-X, it instead supports sticky skill routing. The current event data cannot distinguish these.

# 6. Additional event data needed

A normal case-centric event log records only what happened to the chosen job. Dispatch inference also requires the unchosen alternatives. The preferred design is therefore a **decision-centric or object-centric event log** relating each dispatch event to its resource, selected job, and all considered jobs.

| priority | data to collect | required fields and purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Dispatch-decision snapshot | `decision_id`, `dispatch_decision_time`, `service_start_time`, resource, chosen job, complete eligibility set, excluded jobs with reason codes, ranking scores, offers and rejections. This directly resolves B03/B05, C04/C06, and D10/D-F |
| 2 | Queue-entry and readiness history | Physical arrival, scan time, dispatch-visible queue-entry time, release/ready time, hold intervals, and hold reasons. This separates delayed scans from true ineligibility |
| 3 | Policy state and version | Policy name/version, effective interval, resource or queue scope, tie-break rule, threshold values, SLA mode, and whether a manual override occurred. Needed to detect policy switching rather than fit one rule to the whole queue |
| 4 | Resource-state timeline | Effective skills and certifications, availability, calibration, meetings, prior work, setup state, travel, breaks, reservations, and reason-coded idle intervals. Especially important for QD and QA |
| 5 | Routing and assignment events | Initial assigned resource or skill pool, assignment timestamp, alternative qualified resources, whether reassignment is allowed, sticky-routing flag, and transfer costs. This tests whether D10 and D08 were genuinely available to D-F/D-X |
| 6 | Appointment semantics | Target time, earliest acceptable start, latest acceptable start, grace period, protected buffer, expected slot duration, no-show status, and whether the appointment reserves a specific resource. Needed for A06, B07, and D06 |
| 7 | Estimate history | `est_min` at arrival, current estimated total and remaining time at every decision, confidence interval, estimator source, update timestamps, and whether dispatch can override or distrust the estimate | Tests rolling SEPT and shortest-remaining explanations, especially for B01 and QC |
| 8 | Priority and SLA history | Original/current priority, priority-change events, reason, deadline semantics—start-by versus completion-by—computed SLA risk, escalation threshold, and breach status | Separates priority from deadline escalation for C05 and clarifies why C07 was not protected |
| 9 | Preemption and resumption detail | Stop decision time, actual stop time, reason, initiator, interrupting job, whether the active job was at a safe checkpoint, setup/restart duration, estimated remaining work, and non-time interruption cost | Determines whether QB is truly preemptive priority or a manual threshold hybrid |
| 10 | Batch, aging, and queue-position state | Batch ID, batch-freeze time, original and current rank, accumulated waiting-time score, anti-starvation threshold, and resequencing events | Directly tests the gated-SEPT interpretation of QC |
| 11 | Higher-resolution execution data | Second-level start/complete times, handoff times, setup events, and reason-coded idle time | Reduces false conclusions from same-minute ordering and identifies short switching costs hidden by one-minute timestamps |

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable:** **QD**, at the mechanism level. Exclusive and compound skills, resource availability, and D05/D07 assignments provide strong evidence of skill-based routing. Its within-resource tie-break and reassignment rules remain uncertain.
- **Least identifiable:** **QB**. It clearly permits urgent preemption, but strict preemptive priority is contradicted by B03 over B05, repeated B01 resumptions, and FIFO-like later choices. Missing decision times and interruption costs prevent a unique hybrid reconstruction.
- **Cross-queue conclusion:** QA is best described as appointment override with FIFO fallback; QB as priority-1 preemption plus resume/FIFO/manual exceptions; QC as SEPT with gating, escalation, and aging; and QD as skill routing with appointment reservation and sticky local sequencing.
- **Most dangerous claimed policy to infer:** **strict preemptive priority**. The salient B02 interruption could invite that conclusion, but the remaining QB decisions materially contradict it. Implementing or auditing against strict priority based on this log alone would mischaracterize both lower-priority sequencing and the treatment of interrupted work.
