## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A credible audit should validate both the **data-time semantics** of each feature and the **process semantics** of the events from which the feature or explanation is derived. The following validation suite should run before training, scoring, and explanation publication.

### A. Prefix-time availability checks

For each claim, establish a canonical prediction timestamp:

- `t0 = timestamp of first Coverage Check Completed`
- Use a reliable sequence index to identify the first occurrence if timestamps are equal.
- If neither reliable sequence index nor a defensible event-ordering key exists, mark the claim as ordering-ambiguous and exclude it from strict prefix-based training/evaluation or handle it in a separately documented sensitivity analysis.

For every feature value, persist at least:

- `event_time`: when the underlying operational event occurred;
- `computed_at`: when the feature was calculated;
- `available_at`: when the value became available to the model;
- `source_snapshot_time`, where relevant;
- feature version and source table/version.

Then enforce:

\[
available\_at \leq t0
\]

and, for event-derived facts:

\[
event\_time \leq t0
\]

A feature must be rejected if it was merely stored before extraction but was not operationally available at t0. This catches closure-populated fields, nightly refreshes, mutable risk scores, and “current” employee attributes.

Examples:

- `queue_length_at_t0` is admissible only if its selected queue snapshot is at or before t0, not merely within a symmetric ±5-minute window.
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot` fails if its refresh time is 23:00 after t0.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` must be recomputed per claim with the cutoff `< t0`, not with a dataset-wide extraction date.
- `final_handler_team` fails because its populated-at-closure lineage does not demonstrate availability at t0.

### B. Complete-trace and future-event dependency checks

Build a feature-lineage dependency graph from every model feature to raw events, tables, and aggregation windows. Flag features that depend on:

- any event after t0;
- terminal events such as Claim Closed;
- final outcome values;
- full-trace counts or variants;
- “last,” “final,” “ever,” “anywhere,” or “total” aggregations without an explicit as-of cutoff;
- warehouse fields populated after operational completion.

This check should reject or require reconstruction of:

- `total_case_duration_hours`;
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`;
- `num_document_requests_total`;
- `full_trace_variant_id`;
- `final_handler_team`;
- `final_decision`;
- `last_resource_role`;
- the reported sequence patterns;
- nightly-refreshed document counts.

A complete-trace feature is not made safe by its apparent correlation with a t0 field. For example, final handler team matching the t0 team in many cases does not establish that the particular feature value was available at t0.

### C. Activity-order feasibility checks

Construct a normative process model with at least these constraints:

1. Claim Registered precedes first Coverage Check Completed.
2. After coverage completion, a claim may proceed directly to Damage Assessment Completed for low-complexity cases, or enter one or more document cycles:
   - Documents Requested  Documents Received  Documents Validated;
   - another Documents Requested may follow validation.
3. Damage Assessment Completed precedes Settlement Decision Recorded.
4. Fraud Investigation Opened may occur after its trigger and may run in parallel with evidence collection or damage assessment.
5. Appeal is allowed only after a Partial or Deny settlement decision.
6. A post-appeal Settlement Decision Recorded is legitimate and represents reconsideration.
7. Claim Closed follows final decision or withdrawal.

Apply this model to observed traces and explanation patterns.

- `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` should fail a hard precedence check. It is not merely unusual; under the normative model it is invalid.
- `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` can pass if the preceding decision instance is Partial or Deny and the later decision is explicitly a reconsideration decision. It is semantically legitimate retrospectively but remains unavailable at t0.
- `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is process-feasible as a future continuation. It must not be described as already true at t0 unless the investigation event itself is recorded no later than t0.
- `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` and `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` are feasible continuation patterns, but they are not admissible t0 evidence unless the model is instead being scored later, after those events are observed.

### D. Same-timestamp ambiguity checks

Identical timestamps require special handling because timestamp order is not causal or process order.

For events sharing a timestamp:

1. Prefer a reliable source sequence index.
2. If unavailable, use only a documented, source-authoritative ordering key.
3. If neither exists, mark their order as unresolved rather than ordering by ingestion order.
4. Do not create ordered sequence features from unresolved ties.
5. For conformance purposes, classify cases as:
   - conformant under all plausible orders;
   - nonconformant under all plausible orders;
   - indeterminate because at least one plausible order conforms and another does not.

Case Z belongs in the indeterminate or likely nonconformant category for the reported `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern. The missing sequence index means the explanation cannot claim that this order occurred. Even if the correct order were Damage Assessment  …  Closed, both events remain future relative to t0.

### E. Enabled, impossible, and future-possible activity checks at t0

The explanation system should label referenced activities relative to the observed prefix.

Immediately after `Coverage Check Completed`:

- **Already observed:** Claim Registered; Coverage Check Completed.
- **Enabled or future-possible:** Damage Assessment Completed may be the next substantive path; Documents Requested may initiate evidence collection; Fraud Investigation may later be opened if a trigger arises.
- **Not yet enabled / impossible at t0:** Appeal, because no Partial or Deny decision has yet been recorded.
- **Future-only:** Settlement decision, claim closure, final decision, final handler, final trace variant, total duration, total document requests.

This distinction matters. “Fraud investigation may later be opened” is a valid process forecast statement, but it is not evidence that an investigation is already influencing the current prediction. “Appeal occurred” is not merely future-possible; it is impossible at this stage absent a preceding partial/deny decision.

### F. Repeated settlement-decision checks

Because `Settlement Decision Recorded` can recur after appeal, coarse labels must be disambiguated by instance:

- `decision_instance = 1, 2, ...`
- `decision_type = initial / reconsideration`
- `decision_outcome = Approve / Partial / Deny / Withdrawn`
- `appeal_reference = prior decision instance`, when applicable.

Validation rules should require:

- Appeal follows decision instance 1 with Partial or Deny;
- a second decision, if present, follows the appeal;
- Claim Closed follows the final decision or withdrawal.

A sequence miner that ignores event instances may incorrectly treat repeated decisions as noise or construct misleading patterns. Conversely, a frequent directly-follows graph edge is descriptive, not normative: it can reflect logging artifacts, event abstraction, or population frequency. DFGs should be used to discover observed behavior and anomalies, while precedence/declarative constraints and alignment against the normative model test whether a behavior is permitted.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Defensible predictive signal

Potentially defensible signals, after verifying their exact as-of lineage, include:

- `initial_damage_complexity`;
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`;
- an immutable, registration-time `initial_reserve`;
- a genuinely as-of-t0 `claimant_channel`;
- a repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`;
- an event-time, as-of-t0 document count if documents can genuinely precede the coverage-completion prediction point.

What may be claimed:

- These features are associated with differing breach probabilities in historical data.
- Complexity, reserve, and prior history may summarize early case characteristics associated with longer workflows.
- Pre-coverage elapsed time measures time already consumed before the decision point.

What may not be claimed:

- That high complexity, reserve, prior claims, channel, or elapsed time independently causes a breach.
- That changing a feature would necessarily change the outcome.
- That the claimant or broker is responsible for delay.

Operational actionability is narrower than predictiveness. A feature can be valid and predictive yet not directly actionable. For example, initial complexity may justify earlier triage or capacity allocation, but not a claim that complexity itself can be “fixed.”

### Process-state or congestion signal

The strongest operationally meaningful candidate is:

- repaired `queue_length_at_t0`, using the relevant queue’s latest snapshot at or before t0.

Potentially useful additional process-state features could include:

- current assigned team as of t0;
- current workbasket/queue;
- elapsed registration-to-coverage time;
- whether a validated fraud trigger exists at t0;
- actual document state observed at t0, if any;
- staffing/capacity state as of t0.

What may be claimed:

- Queue length is an observed workload/congestion indicator associated with risk.
- It may be useful for workload management, prioritization, staffing, or proactive claimant communication.
- It is more operationally interpretable than a final team or final trace.

What may not be claimed:

- That queue length alone causes a specific claim to breach.
- That reducing the queue count for one claim would necessarily remove its risk, because queue composition, skill matching, service policies, and unmeasured workload may confound the association.

### Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review

These include:

- `claimant_region`;
- `claimant_channel`;
- possibly `prior_claim_count_before_t0`;
- handler/team features, even if historically repaired, because they may proxy staffing, geography, escalation, or claimant mix.

These should not be deleted automatically. They can capture legitimate service-delivery variation and may improve calibration. But their use requires a stronger governance standard:

- subgroup discrimination and calibration metrics;
- false-positive and false-negative rate comparisons;
- analysis of intersections, such as region × channel;
- performance on new regions, networks, and policy periods;
- examination of whether direct operational variables can replace broad proxies;
- review of whether use in downstream interventions could create unequal service levels.

What may be claimed:

- The model historically observes different risk levels across these groups or contexts.

What may not be claimed:

- Region, channel, or prior claims is the causal reason a particular claim will be delayed.
- A proxy is an acceptable action target merely because it is predictive.
- A group-level association justifies lower service or adverse treatment.

### Drift/calendar proxy

`calendar_week` is available at t0 but is especially vulnerable to temporal confounding because specific weeks coincide with holiday periods and a policy change.

What may be claimed:

- Historical claims submitted or reaching t0 during certain operational periods had different observed risk.
- Calendar may help short-term prediction if validated prospectively and across years.

What may not be claimed:

- Week 52 intrinsically causes breaches.
- The apparent relationship will persist after holiday staffing or policy conditions change.

Preferred treatment is to capture known mechanisms separately where possible:

- holiday calendars;
- staffing plans;
- policy-version indicator;
- system outage periods;
- queue capacity metrics.

Calendar effects should be tested using rolling-origin validation, post-policy holdouts, and monitoring for coefficient/SHAP drift.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired

These features are not acceptable for a t0 model until reconstructed with historical as-of semantics:

- `total_case_duration_hours`;
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`;
- `num_document_requests_total`;
- `full_trace_variant_id`;
- `final_handler_team`;
- `final_decision`;
- `last_resource_role`;
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot`;
- `risk_score_current`;
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, under its current SQL;
- employee experience joined as of extraction date;
- all reported full-trace sequence patterns.

Some can be repaired:

- `final_handler_team`  historically versioned `assigned_team_as_of_t0`;
- `risk_score_current`  latest score whose availability time is at/before t0;
- same-day document count  event-time count through t0;
- total document requests  count through t0;
- full trace variant  prefix-only variant;
- prior claims  strict history before t0.

Others, such as total duration or final decision, are fundamentally post-outcome for this task and should not be repurposed as t0 predictors.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately block critical leakage features from t0 scoring and retrain/recompute.**  
   Remove duration, final decision, appeal, complete-trace counts/variants, final handler, last role, post-t0 document snapshots, and all future sequence-pattern features. Do not merely suppress them in the explanation layer while leaving them in the model.

2. **Build an as-of feature-store contract.**  
   Every feature should have a formal specification containing:
   - business definition;
   - entity key;
   - event-time cutoff;
   - availability-time cutoff;
   - source system;
   - refresh behavior;
   - mutable versus immutable status;
   - versioning policy;
   - allowed prediction horizons;
   - ownership and data-quality tests.

   The feature store should support point-in-time joins by `available_at <= t0`, rather than extraction-date joins.

3. **Preserve historical timestamps and slowly changing dimensions.**  
   Add or recover:
   - assignment/team history;
   - employee role and experience history;
   - risk-score version history with score computation and publication times;
   - queue snapshot timestamps and queue identifiers;
   - document upload and availability timestamps;
   - policy/version effective dates.

   “Current,” “final,” “last,” “total,” and “anywhere” fields should be prohibited in early-prediction feature definitions unless explicitly bounded by t0.

4. **Implement prefix-only recomputation as a mandatory release gate.**  
   For a sample of historical claims, reconstruct inputs using only events and source records available at each claim’s t0. Compare them with production training data. Any nonzero future-event dependency should fail the release gate unless the task definition explicitly permits it.

5. **Create process-conformance tests for event data and explanations.**  
   Test:
   - first Coverage Check Completed selection;
   - document-cycle ordering;
   - damage assessment before decision;
   - appeal only after Partial or Deny;
   - reconsideration decisions only after appeal;
   - closure only after final decision/withdrawal;
   - parallel fraud investigation rules;
   - repeated decision-instance semantics;
   - timestamp-tie handling.

   Use alignment or declarative conformance checking to identify deviations, but retain the distinction between a normative rule and an observed frequent path. A DFG can identify common transitions or suspicious edges; it cannot by itself validate process legality.

6. **Add explanation-time admissibility checks.**  
   Before returning SHAP-like contributions or sequence explanations, validate each referenced feature and event against t0. The explanation service should reject:
   - future events;
   - complete-trace features;
   - unknown-time mutable features;
   - impossible activity order;
   - unresolved same-timestamp order;
   - claims about an activity that is not enabled under the observed prefix.

   Publish an explanation eligibility status when a score was generated using only audited features.

7. **Recalibrate and re-evaluate after feature removal.**  
   Leakage removal will likely reduce apparent offline performance. Reassess with strictly time-ordered, rolling-origin evaluation:
   - discrimination;
   - calibration curves;
   - Brier score;
   - precision/recall at operational thresholds;
   - lead time before breach;
   - calibration by complexity, queue band, region, channel, and policy period.

   Refit calibration after retraining rather than retaining probabilities from the leaked model.

8. **Test explanation stability and reproducibility.**  
   For identical historical prefixes, rerun feature generation and explanation generation from versioned data. Explanations should be stable except for explicitly versioned model changes. Measure:
   - rank stability of top drivers;
   - sign stability;
   - sensitivity to queue-snapshot lag;
   - sensitivity to timestamp-tie resolution;
   - changes after backfills or source corrections.

9. **Conduct proxy, fairness, and actionability review separately from predictive validation.**  
   For region, channel, prior-history features, and repaired team features:
   - report subgroup calibration and error rates;
   - examine intersectional groups;
   - test robustness across regions, channels, networks, and time periods;
   - compare models with direct operational variables versus broad proxies;
   - establish intervention policy safeguards.

   A feature may remain predictive yet be unsuitable for individualized adverse action or unsupported as a causal explanation.

10. **Monitor data, schema, and process drift continuously.**  
    Monitor:
    - feature missingness and freshness;
    - timestamp lag distributions;
    - queue snapshot timing;
    - prevalence of same-timestamp ties;
    - sequence/conformance deviations;
    - decision/appeal rate changes;
    - policy and holiday regime changes;
    - shifts in feature attribution distributions;
    - model calibration and subgroup calibration over time.

    Treat new event labels, changed closure behavior, altered refresh schedules, or increased missing sequence indices as schema/process changes requiring feature and explanation revalidation before continued deployment.
