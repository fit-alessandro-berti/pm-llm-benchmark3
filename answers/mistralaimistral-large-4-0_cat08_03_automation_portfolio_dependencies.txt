## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies.** A2 (classification) depends on clean registration data from A1; misclassification propagates wrong control paths into KYC and financial review. A6 (normalization) depends on completed financial document review and benefits directly from A5 reducing missing-document loops. A8 (committee pack) is the most fragile: it requires reconciled artifacts from KYC status, normalized financial statements (A6), collateral valuation (A7), and credit-analysis notes. Deploying A8 before A6 and A4 are stable would amplify garbage-in/garbage-out. A9 (contract generation) depends on clean KYC clearance and committee decision data; the DFG shows Contract preparation  Remediate control issue (650) and back (510), so A9 must embed control-rule checks to prevent the 8.5% remediation loop (14% for Credit-line renewal).

**Legal/compliance controls.** KYC clearance must precede Customer signing; provisional signing occurs in 6.5% of cases with 1.1% requiring remediation. A10 (e-signature) and A11 (loan activation) are downstream of this control and cannot be safely automated until KYC status is reliable. The four-eyes principle requires that Credit analysis and Committee packaging are not completed by the same person in High-exposure SME cases; violations occur in 5.8% of cases during month-end rush. A12 (control remediation workbench) directly addresses this by enforcing separation and tracking overrides. A3 (KYC pre-classifier) must preserve an auditable trail of model suggestion, human override, and final decision.

**Human escalation capacity.** Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE with high dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. A3 increases the volume of pre-classified hits but must route exceptions to these specialists without overwhelming them; false negatives are severe. A4 (entity-mismatch assistant) handles a low-frequency but high-wait activity (31h median) that currently consumes specialist time; it must include escalation for the 38% exception rate. Credit analysts (9 FTE) are central in the working-together network; automation that increases clean inflow (e.g., A1, A2) without A6 (normalization) will overload them.

**External valuation dependency.** Wait for external valuation has a 58h median wait and is a synchronization point outside direct control. A7 (triage and routing) can parallelize requests and reduce the 19% late rate but cannot eliminate the external dependency. Committee packaging waits for this valuation in 1,980 cases (DFG: Wait for external valuation  Committee packaging), so A7 must be paired with upstream stability before A8 can be effective.

**Core-banking integration constraints.** IT can integrate only two modules deeply with core banking in the first six months. A6 (financial normalization) requires deep integration with credit systems and document management. A4 (entity-mismatch assistant) requires deep integration with KYC/compliance systems and entity master data. A5 (portal nudging) is a lighter integration. A12 (control workbench) requires medium workflow integration. Therefore, the first six months should carry A6 and A4 as the two deep integrations, with A5 as a light-integration quick win; A12 follows in months 6–12.

**Concurrency caveat.** Financial document review and KYC check are often concurrent in practice but recorded sequentially. Automation must not create artificial serialization. A4 sits on the KYC path but also blocks Contract preparation directly (DFG: Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation: 380), meaning unresolved mismatches create a hidden parallel blockage. A6 sits on the financial path. These parallel paths converge at Committee packaging, so improvements must be balanced across both paths to avoid shifting the bottleneck.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Portfolio A — Quick-win portfolio
**Modules:** A1 (Auto-registration), A2 (Classification recommender), A5 (Missing-document checklist), A10 (E-signature adoption)

**Optimizes:** Direct labor savings on high-frequency activities, implementation speed, political buy-in from management seeking visible wins. A1 and A2 target the 11,880-case registration and 11,820-case classification steps; A5 reduces the 52% repeated document-request loop; A10 addresses the 44h signing wait.

**Sacrifices:** Does not address the deepest process constraints. The 31h entity-mismatch wait (A4), the 75-min normalization service time and 20h wait (A6), and the 58h external valuation wait (A7) remain. Accelerating inflow via A1/A2 without A6 risks overloading the 9 FTE credit analysts. Control strengthening is weak: A2 prevents some misrouting, but provisional signing (6.5%), four-eyes violations (5.8%), and contract-remediation loops (8.5%) are largely unaddressed. This portfolio optimizes labor metrics while leaving cycle-time and control-risk drivers untouched.

### Portfolio B — Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio
**Modules:** A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant), A5 (Missing-document checklist), A6 (Financial normalization), A7 (Collateral valuation triage)

**Optimizes:** End-to-end cycle time, waiting time, and rework reduction. A4 attacks the 31h median wait and 38% rework on a low-frequency but critical-path activity (82% of cases when present). A6 relieves the credit-analyst bottleneck and enables credit analysis (DFG: Normalize  Credit analysis: 5,000). A7 addresses the 58h external synchronization point. A5 cuts the customer-response delay dominating the missing-document loop.

**Sacrifices:** Lower direct labor savings on high-frequency activities; no explicit control-strengthening module (A4 indirectly reduces provisional-signing risk by unblocking KYC, but does not remediate existing violations). Compliance may view this as insufficient because it does not directly reduce the 6.5% provisional signing or 5.8% four-eyes violations. Some modules (A4, A6, A7) have medium feasibility and require careful exception handling.

### Portfolio C — Compliance-first portfolio
**Modules:** A2 (Classification recommender), A3 (KYC hit pre-classifier), A9 (Contract generation with control checks), A12 (Control remediation workbench)

**Optimizes:** Control violations, auditability, and regulatory safety. A12 directly targets provisional signing, contract-remediation loops, and four-eyes violations. A3 ensures KYC hits are properly triaged with human escalation, reducing false-negative risk. A9 embeds control-rule checks to ensure contract terms match committee decisions. A2 prevents wrong control paths from misclassification.

**Sacrifices:** Less labor reduction and slower visible cycle-time improvement. The credit-analyst bottleneck (A6) and the 31h entity-mismatch wait (A4) are not addressed, so waiting times at Financial document review (18h) and Credit analysis (28h) persist. The 58h external valuation wait remains. Customer-facing improvements are limited; RM friction may increase if A9 introduces rigid rule checks. This portfolio satisfies compliance but leaves major process-level inefficiencies in place.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced by automation | Affected candidates | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| **False positives/false negatives** — misclassification or missed KYC hits | A2, A3, A4 | Human-in-the-loop for all high-risk decisions; A3 must route all hits to compliance specialists with override logging; A2 suggestions require analyst confirmation for High-exposure SME; A4 escalates 38% exception cases to specialists |
| **Model drift** — performance degrades as applicant mix changes | A2, A3, A4, A6 | Continuous monitoring of suggestion accuracy, override rates, and exception rates; quarterly model review; champion/challenger deployment |
| **Auditability gap** — lack of trail for model suggestion, human override, final decision | All | Mandatory audit log capturing model input, suggestion, human action, override reason, and final decision; immutable storage for regulatory inspection |
| **Exception handling overload** — automation shifts work to exceptions, overwhelming small specialist pools | A3, A4 | Exception queues with SLAs; capacity planning for 3 FTE compliance specialists; A4 reduces specialist load by handling routine mismatches; A3 pre-classifies to reduce specialist volume |
| **Four-eyes compliance violation** — same person completes Credit analysis and Committee packaging in High-exposure SME | A6, A8 (if deployed later) | Workflow enforcement preventing same-user completion; A12 workbench tracks and alerts on violations; month-end rush controls |
| **Downstream bottleneck amplification** — faster upstream inflow overloads credit analysts or legal ops | A1, A2 | Do not deploy A1/A2 without A6; capacity buffers for credit analysts (9 FTE); legal ops month-end staffing plan |
| **Legal/regulatory risk** — contract terms mismatch committee decision; outdated rule library | A9 | A9 must embed control-rule checks validated by legal; rule library version control; A12 tracks contract-remediation loops (8.5% overall, 14% Credit-line renewal) |
| **Customer friction** — rigid instructions or e-signature consent issues | A5, A10 | Customer feedback loop; RM consultation before A5 rollout; A/B testing of nudge messages; clear consent flows for A10 |
| **Control bypass** — downstream bots activate loans or sign contracts before controls complete | A10, A11 | Hard system gates: A11 cannot execute unless KYC clearance, signed contract, and control checklist are complete; A10 blocked until KYC status = cleared; A12 monitors provisional signing (6.5%) |
| **Concurrency artifacts** — sequential recording of concurrent KYC and Financial doc review creates false dependencies | A4, A6 | Process-mining-aware workflow design that allows parallel execution; do not serialize KYC and Financial doc review in automation logic |
| **External dependency** — valuer delays and quality disputes | A7 | Triage and routing to multiple valuers; parallel requests; SLA monitoring; escalation path for 19% late cases |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Constraint compliance:** Four modules total; at least one strengthens controls (A12); at most two deep core-banking integrations in first six months (A6 and A4); A5 is light integration.

### Phase 1 — Months 1–3: Foundation and quick win
**Module:** A5 (Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging)

**Rationale:** High feasibility, light integration, immediate rework reduction. Addresses the 52% repeated document-request rate and the Financial doc review  Request missing docs loop (DFG: 3,300 / 2,800). Leverages back-office capacity after 15:00 (utilization <65%). Reduces RM-triggered incomplete-instruction loops.

**Monitoring metrics:** Missing-document repeat rate; customer-response delay; Financial document review waiting time (18h baseline); customer friction score; RM feedback.

**Gate criteria:** Repeat rate reduced by 30%; no increase in customer complaints; Financial document review wait reduced by 20%; RM adoption 70%.

### Phase 2 — Months 3–6: Deep integrations (the two allowed)
**Modules:** A6 (Financial-statement extraction and normalization) — deep integration #1; A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant) — deep integration #2

**Rationale:** A6 relieves the 9 FTE credit analysts and enables credit analysis (DFG: Normalize  Credit analysis: 5,000). A4 attacks the 31h median wait and 38% rework on entity mismatch, which is on the critical path in 82% of cases when present and blocks KYC and signing. These are the two deepest integrations with core banking and KYC/compliance systems.

**Monitoring metrics:** Normalization accuracy and rework (16% baseline); analyst utilization and queue depth; entity-mismatch resolution time (31h baseline); KYC waiting time (4h baseline); exception escalation time to 3 FTE specialists.

**Gate criteria:** Normalization accuracy 95% on standard PDFs; analyst queue depth stable or reduced; entity-mismatch median wait reduced by 50%; no increase in KYC rework (11% baseline); exception escalation within SLA.

### Phase 3 — Months 6–9: Control strengthening
**Module:** A12 (Control remediation workbench)

**Rationale:** Satisfies compliance requirement. Addresses provisional signing (6.5%), contract-remediation loops (8.5% overall, 14% Credit-line renewal), and four-eyes violations (5.8% in High-exposure SME during month-end). Medium integration with workflow and control systems.

**Monitoring metrics:** Provisional signing rate; contract-remediation loop rate (DFG: Contract prep  Remediate control issue: 650; back: 510); four-eyes violation rate; remediation cycle time; audit trail completeness.

**Gate criteria:** Provisional signing reduced by 80%; contract-remediation loops reduced by 40%; four-eyes violations <1%; 100% audit trail coverage for overrides.

### Phase 4 — Months 9–12: Optimization and evaluation
**Activities:** Tune models based on drift monitoring; evaluate whether to add A2 (classification) or A7 (collateral triage) in year two; assess resource rebalancing (compliance specialists, credit analysts, legal ops); prepare business case for A9 (contract generation) contingent on A12 control maturity.

**Monitoring metrics:** End-to-end cycle time; waiting time at each activity; rework rates; resource utilization across all pools; control violation rates; customer satisfaction.

**Gate criteria:** End-to-end cycle time reduced by 25%; no resource pool >90% sustained utilization; control violations reduced by 50%; model drift within acceptable bounds.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommended portfolio: A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), A5 (Missing-document checklist), A6 (Financial-statement normalization), A12 (Control remediation workbench).**

This hybrid portfolio is the defensible choice because it directly addresses the process-mining evidence that **frequency alone is misleading**. A4 is the clearest example: only 1,750 cases (low frequency) but a 31-hour median wait, 38% rework rate, and critical-path presence in 82% of cases when it occurs. It blocks KYC clearance and, via the DFG edge Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation (380), directly delays contract preparation. Automating the highest-frequency activity first — Register application (11,880 cases, A1) — would be a politically attractive quick win, but it would simply accelerate cases into already-congested KYC (4h wait, 11% rework, 3 FTE specialists) and Financial document review (18h wait, 24% rework) queues. The process-mining data shows that back office is the most visible queue but utilization falls below 65% after 15:00, meaning the constraint is not registration labor but downstream synchronization and exception handling.

The recommended portfolio makes a deliberate trade-off: it sacrifices the immediate labor savings of A1 (auto-registration) and A2 (classification) in year one to avoid **downstream bottleneck amplification**. Credit analysts (9 FTE) are central in the working-together network; increasing clean inflow without A6 (normalization) would overload them. A6 is therefore sequenced before any high-frequency upstream automation. A5 is included as a high-feasibility, light-integration module that reduces the 52% repeated missing-document loop and customer-response delay, providing an early visible win while the deep integrations (A6 and A4) are built.

A12 is non-negotiable: compliance insists that at least one module strengthen controls. A12 directly reduces provisional signing (6.5%), contract-remediation loops (8.5% overall, 14% for Credit-line renewal), and four-eyes violations (5.8% in High-exposure SME during month-end). It also creates the audit trail required for all other automation modules.

This portfolio respects the IT constraint: A6 and A4 are the two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months; A5 is light integration; A12 is medium integration in the second half. It accounts for exception handling by preserving human escalation for the 38% entity-mismatch exceptions and the 3 FTE compliance specialist capacity. It does not automate downstream bots (A11 loan activation) until controls are reliable, and it does not assume automation removes compliance responsibility — A12 explicitly reinforces control ownership.

In summary: **automate the constraints, not the frequencies.** The highest-frequency activity should not be automated first because doing so would create a faster conveyor belt into a traffic jam, increasing waiting times, rework, and control risk without improving end-to-end performance.
