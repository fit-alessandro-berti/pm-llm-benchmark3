# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail Latency (90th Percentile):** Average cycle time may improve due to high volume of simple cases, while high-complexity cases (requiring central specialists) suffer extreme delays. This masks dissatisfaction among high-value customers.
2.  **High-Complexity Case Degradation:** If `case_complexity` is correlated with `required_specialty`, central queueing may disproportionately affect the most critical cases, hidden by the average of simple cases.
3.  **Local Knowledge Loss:** Rework caused by lack of regional context (`location`, `customer_segment`) may appear as standard process variance rather than a structural flaw until accumulated costs are analyzed.
4.  **Fairness Across Segments:** Specific `customer_segment` groups (e.g., those requiring language accommodation) may face longer waits if central specialists lack regional nuance, invisible in aggregate metrics.
5.  **Learning and Skill Atrophy:** Local staff (`team` North/South) stop handling complex cases. This is invisible in cycle time but visible in `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` showing divergence between local and specialist skills over time.
6.  **Accountability Diffusion:** Shared queues mean multiple `org:resource` IDs touch a case during `Supervisor Approval`. Average time may be stable, but the ability to trace errors to specific decisions is lost, increasing compliance risk.

# 5. Pilot design

*   **Pilot Scope:** Centralize **Technical Shared Service** only (merge N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech). Keep Legal/Risk and Approval local for now.
*   **Comparison Group:** **Team North** remains fully local (Control). **Team East** and **Team South** adopt the shared technical pool (Treatment).
    *   *Rationale:* East is overloaded and likely to show benefit; North is efficient and likely to show risk. This provides contrast.
*   **Pre/Post Observation Window:** 3 months baseline (pre-change) vs. 3 months pilot (post-change).
*   **Segmentation:**
    *   **Team:** Compare North vs. East/South.
    *   **Complexity:** Split analysis by `case_complexity` (Low vs. High).
    *   **Specialty:** Track `required_specialty` routing accuracy.
    *   **Customer Segment:** Monitor `customer_segment` SLA adherence.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   90th Percentile Cycle Time (must not increase >5%).
    *   Rework Rate on High-Complexity cases (must not increase >2%).
    *   Local Staff Sentiment (qualitative feedback on skill development).
*   **Staff Feedback Collection:** Weekly surveys on "ability to resolve cases" and "access to expertise."
*   **Rollback/Adjustment Triggers:**
    *   If `pm4py.get_service_time` for `Specialist Review` exceeds 6 hours median.
    *   If North team SLA miss rate increases by >3%.
    *   If `reassignment_count` within central queue exceeds 2 per case on average.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed with Pilot Only (Conditional).**

Do not authorize full rollout. The restructuring offers clear benefits for **Team East** (load balancing) but poses significant risks for **Team North/South** (loss of local efficiency and expertise). The aggregate data masks these divergent outcomes.

**Conditions for Proceeding:**
1.  **Monitor Variance, Not Just Averages:** Management must not infer success from improved median cycle time alone. The 90th percentile and `case_complexity` segmented performance are the true success criteria.
2.  **Protect Local Learning:** Implement a rotation policy where local caseworkers spend time in the Shared Service to prevent skill atrophy. Monitor this via `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`.
3.  **Fairness Guardrails:** Explicitly track `customer_segment` outcomes. If regional segments show degraded service due to loss of local context, the routing rules must be adjusted to allow local override.
4.  **Accountability Preservation:** Ensure `org:resource` logging captures the final decision-maker in shared queues, not just the queue owner, to maintain auditability.

**What Management Should Not Infer:**
*   Do not infer that reduced handover counts equal faster throughput (queueing may replace handovers).
*   Do not infer that standardized decisions equal better outcomes (local nuance may be required for quality).
*   Do not infer that high specialist utilization is positive if it creates a single point of failure.
