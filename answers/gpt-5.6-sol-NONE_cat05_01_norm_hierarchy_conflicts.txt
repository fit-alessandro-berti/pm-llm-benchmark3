## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts taken as given

- GPIP-4 is a board-approved, enterprise-wide policy effective throughout the incident.
- Aachen SOP revision 6 is a controlled local procedure applicable to maintenance and production-support purchases at Aachen.
- ECD-2 is a signed incident directive with a defined subject scope and an effective window of 2026-07-14 09:00 through 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST.
- The CFO email is an authorized interpretation of Finance controls, but is neither a controlled policy nor an identified Chief Risk Officer exception.
- No Chief Risk Officer exception or ratification was issued.
- The ERP outage affects evidential weight but creates no substantive exception.
- GPIP-4 does not resolve whether a COO/Incident Commander directive qualifies as an A7 exception.
- Incident Command members are not established to be independent budget owners merely by membership.

### Interpretive assumptions

1. **Authority is issue-specific, not a single total ordering.** GPIP-4 presumptively controls integrity, segregation, Finance/budget thresholds, Compliance clearance, and formal exceptions. The SOP controls stricter Aachen workflow and evidence. ECD-2 controls the local timing and supplier-onboarding issues it expressly addresses, within its time and operational scope. The CFO controls interpretation of Finance processes, but not Compliance or segregation.

2. **Specificity does not itself overcome reserved authority.** ECD-2 is more specific in incident, time, and subject matter, but its express precedence over “ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements” does not silently waive GPIP-4 controls that it does not mention.

3. **Later-in-time status is relevant but not decisive.** ECD-2 and the CFO email are later and incident-specific, yet neither automatically overrides the board policy’s reserved A7 mechanism.

4. **ECD-2 validly displaces SOP B1 and B3** for covered purchases: commitment may precede PO creation, and unapproved suppliers may be used. It also supplies a special two-person authorization process. I do not treat that authorization as automatically satisfying GPIP-4’s independent budget-owner or Finance Controller requirements.

5. **CFO email interpretation.** Within ECD-2’s valid scope and window, joint Incident Command authorization is treated as sufficient for the local Plant approval and the separate Finance approval up to EUR 150,000. Above EUR 150,000, Legal is additionally required. This is defensible as a Finance-framework interpretation, but it does **not** displace GPIP-4 A2’s independent-budget-owner requirement unless the authorizers are shown to have that role, and it cannot waive A1 or A3.

6. **Emergency necessity is not self-proving.**
   - SOP emergency status requires imminent safety harm or expected production stoppage within six hours.
   - ECD-2 coverage requires a “critical sterilization-line lot” connected to containment. On the present record, parts for the affected line needed the same day or within hours qualify; unrelated site continuity does not.
   - Backup-line inventory needed only after three days is materially ambiguous and, under the narrower audit reading adopted here, outside ECD-2.

7. **Temporal compliance is tested at the supplier commitment.** Later approvals may mitigate consequences or prove an earlier act, but do not cure missing pre-commitment controls.

8. **Retrospective recording is distinguished from retrospective authorization.** A late record can prove a timely authorization if reliable contemporaneous evidence supports the earlier act. A genuinely late approval remains a substantive timing breach.

9. **Burden of proof.** The process owner must show that an asserted exception applied, including authority, scope, time, actors, and required record. For high-risk controls—segregation, new-supplier Compliance, and pre-commitment approvals—material unresolved facts ordinarily produce either NONCOMPLIANT where a breach is affirmatively shown, or INDETERMINATE where the act may have complied but proof is insufficient.

10. **Severity reflects control purpose and exposure**, not merely count of deviations: integrity and pre-commitment Compliance failures are generally more serious than late workflow records.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### P-501 — possible justified emergency treatment

**Alternative classification:** JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, or a narrower NONCOMPLIANT limited to documentation.

This requires reading SOP B4 as authorizing a Shift Lead to execute the emergency commitment whenever a six-hour stoppage exists, with “absent” meaning unavailable to act quickly rather than physically unreachable, and reading B2 approvals as capable of following an emergency commitment. The four-hour engineering forecast supports necessity.

The alternative is weak because B4 expressly conditions substitution on an absent Plant Manager and active delegation, while B5 says Finance is not expressly waived. The evidence affirmatively says the Manager was reachable and no formal delegation existed.

### P-502 — possible compliant ECD overlay

**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT or JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

This requires two premises:

1. ECD-2’s authorization to use an unapproved supplier and its precedence over onboarding requirements includes GPIP A3 Compliance timing, not only the local supplier register; and
2. the CFO’s “all operational approvals” language validly treats joint IC authorization as A2 Finance and budget approval.

The alternative is credible because ECD-2 was signed by senior incident authorities, prior practice recognized incident directives as temporary overlays, and same-day sterile filters lie at the center of its purpose. It remains weaker because A7 reserves exceptions to the CRO, the CRO did not ratify, C5 omits Compliance, and past overlay practice did not involve new-supplier clearance.

### P-503 — possible covered but still defective purchase

**Alternative classification:** INDETERMINATE rather than NONCOMPLIANT.

Operations’ reading could treat backup-line components as “critical” because maintaining redundant sterilization capacity is part of contamination containment, even if current stock lasts three days. If that scope premise is accepted, ECD excuses PO timing, and the CFO email might satisfy ordinary Plant/Finance steps.

That change does not readily produce COMPLIANT: the purchase exceeded EUR 100,000 without Compliance clearance, A2 roles are unproved, and PROC-3’s requester/committer/authorizer combination creates serious A1 and independence concerns. A compliant result would additionally require treating ECD as a valid A7 exception and treating PROC-3 as not the final approver.

### P-505 — possible compliant in-window commitment

**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT.

The supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 may be the best evidence that a binding supplier commitment existed before 18:00. The ERP timestamp can be discounted if clock records prove it was nine minutes fast. If at least one IC member was an independent budget owner and another was an authorized Finance Controller—or if D1 validly supplies both approvals—the purchase was compliant.

This is credible because a supplier acknowledgment is contemporaneous external evidence and is normally stronger than a disputed ERP timestamp. It remains unproven because the acknowledgment’s contractual effect and the authorizers’ roles are not supplied.

**Opposite alternative:** NONCOMPLIANT if the email merely acknowledged receipt or negotiation and binding acceptance occurred after 18:00, or if neither authorizer held the A2 roles.

### P-506 — possible SOP emergency justification

**Alternative classification:** JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION with residual approval defects.

The verbal ECD extension is weak, but the purchase might independently qualify under SOP B4 if the chemicals were needed to prevent a production stoppage within six hours. That would excuse PO timing without relying on an invalid extension.

The alternative requires contemporaneous operational forecasts not currently present. It would not cure the missing pre-commitment Plant approval or establish the independent budget owner, although Finance did approve before commitment.

### P-507 — possible compliant purchase

**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT.

This requires authenticating the signed PDF as the required C3 record created at 12:40, within four hours of the 08:55 verbal authorization, and establishing that the two IC authorizers collectively included an independent budget owner and Finance Controller under the CFO’s framework. The 14:30 ERP timestamp would then be only a late system entry.

This is credible because the scenario identifies a document-system outage and a queued signed PDF. It does not solve A2 unless role/delegation evidence is produced.

### P-508 — strongest case for broad CFO interpretation

**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT or JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

Because the purchase was plainly critical, above EUR 150,000, and had pre-commitment Compliance, Legal, and two independent IC authorizers, one can read D1–D2 as a complete emergency approval matrix replacing separate Plant and Finance events. If an IC authorizer held budget authority and the CFO’s interpretation counts as Finance Controller approval, all material controls could be satisfied.

A broader theory that the email itself waived A2 is weaker. The email was uncontrolled, D3 limits CFO authority, D4 preserves essential integrity controls, and no CRO exception exists.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

### Distinguishing defect types

1. **Substantive policy breach**
   - A required actor did not approve before commitment.
   - An unapproved supplier was used outside a valid exception.
   - Compliance clearance occurred after a new-supplier or over-EUR-100,000 commitment.
   - A requester or committer served as final business approver.
   - Examples: P-502’s late Compliance; P-503’s absent Compliance; P-504’s out-of-scope supplier and sequencing; P-506’s post-expiry reliance.

2. **Logging defect**
   - The required action occurred on time, but the system entry was delayed.
   - Likely possibility: P-507, if the signed PDF was completed at 12:40 and merely entered into ERP at 14:30.

3. **Late evidence of a timely act**
   - A record created later can establish earlier verbal authorization only where the norm permits verbal action followed by recording and the record is timely under that rule.
   - P-502’s 12:55 record validly documents the 10:10 verbal authorization.
   - P-507’s 12:40 PDF may do so; its authenticity and completion time require validation.

4. **Inability to prove compliance**
   - The record does not establish required roles, exact commitment time, or exception scope.
   - P-505 is the principal example: in-window contract formation and A2 role compliance remain unresolved.
   - Missing role evidence affects P-502, P-503, P-507, and P-508.

5. **Non-curative post-hoc approval**
   - P-501’s afternoon Plant/Finance approvals, P-502’s 18:30 Compliance, P-505’s 19:00 Finance approval, and P-506’s 09:10 Plant approval mitigate accountability and financial exposure but do not automatically cure timing failures.

### Immediate case-level remediation

- **P-501:** document the emergency forecast, confirm who made the final business decision, record the lack of delegation, and conduct a retrospective Finance/Plant risk review. Correct the Shift Lead delegation protocol rather than relabeling the approval as timely.
- **P-502:** perform enhanced new-supplier due diligence, sanctions/beneficial-owner review, quality qualification, and conflict checks. Record the late Compliance and Finance approvals as exceptions, not cures.
- **P-503:** suspend additional orders under the same rationale; obtain Compliance and Finance review; determine whether PROC-3 was the final business approver; review for self-approval or conflict; document why three-day backup inventory was considered incident-critical.
- **P-504:** stop using the supplier pending qualification, inspect the transaction for food-safety necessity, and classify it outside ECD reporting. Consider purchase ratification or termination under ordinary controls.
- **P-505:** preserve the full email headers, supplier call recording, contract terms, mail-server time, ERP clock logs, and IC role records. Determine the exact contract-formation event before assigning a final breach outcome.
- **P-506:** reject the alleged oral extension as a control basis; identify whether a six-hour SOP emergency independently existed; obtain missing budget-owner review and document the post-expiry process failure.
- **P-507:** forensically validate PDF creation, signatures, metadata, queue logs, and outage records. Obtain evidence of the authorizers’ Finance and budget roles. Treat the ERP delay separately from any substantive A2 failure.
- **P-508:** identify the authorizers’ delegated roles and whether the CFO framework treated one as Finance Controller. If not, record an A2 breach despite successful Legal and Compliance reviews.

### Monitoring and systemic actions

- Maintain a single incident-purchase register containing commitment time, governing exception, scope rationale, amount, supplier status, authorizer identities and roles, and evidence source.
- Reconcile supplier acknowledgments, call recordings, PO records, PDFs, and ERP events daily during incidents.
- Flag automatically:
  - purchases at or above EUR 50,000 lacking pre-commitment A2 role evidence;
  - purchases above EUR 100,000 or from new suppliers lacking pre-commitment Compliance;
  - requester/committer/approver identity overlap;
  - records made more than four or 24 hours later;
  - commitments within 30 minutes of exception expiry;
  - purchases described as “continuity” without a defined incident nexus.
- Require post-incident independent review of every emergency purchase, but explicitly state that review is not retroactive approval.
- Track repeat emergency use by buyer, supplier, plant, and category to detect normalization or deliberate order timing around thresholds.
- Preserve source-specific clocks and outage records so evidence corrections are auditable rather than manually overwritten.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

> **Emergency hierarchy and exception clause**
>
> Board-approved policy controls segregation of duties, independent budget-owner approval, Finance Controller approval, Compliance clearance, monetary thresholds, and exception authority. A local SOP may add stricter controls. An incident directive may override only the local workflow, timing, tendering, and supplier-register requirements that it identifies expressly, and only for purchases demonstrably within its stated operational scope and effective period.
>
> Any waiver or substitution of a board-policy control must be written, identify each affected clause, state the incident scope, thresholds, authorized substitute roles, evidence requirements, start and expiry times, and be signed by the Chief Risk Officer or a formally designated written alternate. A COO, CFO, Incident Commander, or local manager may not waive segregation of duties or Compliance clearance unless the written delegation expressly grants that authority.
>
> For purchases of at least EUR 50,000, emergency authorization must still include an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller, identified by role. The requester and supplier committer may not serve as the final business approver. For a new supplier or a purchase above EUR 100,000, Compliance clearance is required before commitment unless the written exception expressly provides an alternative Compliance review. Purchases above any directive-specific threshold must also receive the stated Legal review.
>
> Verbal emergency authorization is effective only where the directive permits it, is given before commitment by all required actors, and is recorded in a durable signed record within four hours. A later database entry may evidence a timely act when supported by reliable contemporaneous material, but a later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
>
> Exceptions expire automatically at the stated time. Extensions must satisfy the same written authority and signature requirements before expiry; oral extensions and retrospective ratifications do not extend authority.
>
> Each emergency purchase must state the facts establishing necessity and incident scope. Repeated use, purchases for stock beyond the defined recovery horizon, and purchases initiated near expiry require independent post-incident review. Emergency status must not become a standing procurement channel, and no supplier or category may remain on emergency treatment after the immediate containment period without conversion to ordinary controls.
