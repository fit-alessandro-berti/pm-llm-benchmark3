## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk**: If `risk_score` omits critical risk factors (e.g., fraud indicators), flagged cases may be inherently slower, and suppressing the flag (P1) could worsen outcomes.
2. **Queue interference**: If unflagged cases are delayed more by flagged cases than vice versa, removing priority triage (P1) could paradoxically increase total cycle time.
3. **Decision quality**: If manual review improves decision consistency (e.g., fewer false rejects), removing mandatory review (P2) could harm compliance.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If `manual_start` includes "pending" time (not true queue wait), the observed 6.5h delay may overestimate the causal effect of the flag.
5. **Positivity**: If no unflagged cases have `risk_score > 0.8`, within-risk-score comparisons (E2) are invalid.
6. **Reviewer behavior**: If reviewers ignore the flag under P2 (no mandatory review), the policy may have no effect.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed**:
- Flagged cases experience **faster triage** (0.25h vs 3.0h) but **longer total cycle times** (23h vs 5h), driven by manual review (6.5h queue wait) and escalation (6h service).
- High complexity and load correlate with delays, but flagged cases are slower even within complexity strata.

**Plausible mechanisms**:
1. **Priority triage** accelerates initial handling but creates downstream bottlenecks (shared queues).
2. **Mandatory review** ensures high-risk cases are scrutinized but adds fixed delays.
3. **Escalation** is resource-constrained, with flagged cases competing for senior reviewers.

**Unidentified**:
- **Unobserved risk**: `risk_score` may not capture all risk factors, confounding flag effects.
- **Interference**: Flagged cases may delay unflagged cases, but the magnitude is unknown.
- **Reviewer discretion**: How often reviewers override flags or escalate cases is unmeasured.

**Most useful intervention**:
**Pilot P2 (no mandatory review)** for medium-high-risk flagged cases. This tests whether:
- Reviewer discretion reduces delays without harming decision quality.
- Downstream queues shorten when review is optional.
- The flag’s priority triage benefit persists without mandatory review.

The pilot’s guardrails (e.g., review rate monitoring) and randomization will clarify whether the flag’s current tradeoffs are necessary or avoidable.
