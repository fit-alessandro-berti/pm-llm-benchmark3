## 1. Normalization Assumptions

All timestamps are normalized to closed UTC uncertainty intervals $[t_{\min}, t_{\max}]$ (or finite unions of disjoint intervals when DST ambiguity applies) before computing durations or conformance verdicts. Midpoints are never substituted into interval reasoning.

1. **Resolution-First Interval Construction & Clock-Offset Direction**
   - Let $T_{\text{disp}}$ be the displayed local timestamp converted to UTC under its applicable time-zone offset (or fold).
   - First, apply display/rounding uncertainty to obtain the feasible **source-clock time** interval $[R_{\min}, R_{\max}]$:
     - **Exact to second (HUB):** $[T_{\text{disp}} - 0.5\text{ s},\; T_{\text{disp}} + 0.5\text{ s}]$.
     - **Floor to minute (OPS-BER, VENDOR-NY, LEGACY-BER):** $[T_{\text{disp}},\; T_{\text{disp}} + 59.999\text{ s}]$, per the stated `[10:14:00, 10:14:59.999]` semantics.
     - **Round to nearest 10 seconds (SENSOR):** $[T_{\text{disp}} - 5\text{ s},\; T_{\text{disp}} + 5\text{ s}]$.
     - **Round to nearest minute (MOBILE-LON):** $[T_{\text{disp}} - 30\text{ s},\; T_{\text{disp}} + 30\text{ s}]$.
   - Second, apply clock correction using $\text{clock\_offset} = t_{\text{recorded}} - t_{\text{true}} \in [a, b]$, which implies $t_{\text{true}} = t_{\text{recorded}} - \text{clock\_offset}$:
     $$t_{\text{true}} \in [R_{\min} - b,\; R_{\max} - a]$$
   - Note the asymmetry for **OPS-BER** (`[-90 s, +120 s]`): subtracting $+120\text{ s}$ shifts the lower bound earlier by $2\text{ min}$, while subtracting $-90\text{ s}$ shifts the upper bound later by $1.5\text{ min}$, yielding $[T_{\text{utc}} - 120\text{ s},\; T_{\text{utc}} + 149.999\text{ s}]$.

2. **Time-Zone Conversion & DST Folds**

   - **T-405 Causal/Ingestion Pruning:** Assuming the ingestion sequence reflects true chronological occurrence ($t_{\text{Alert}} \le t_{\text{InvStart}} \le t_{\text{ContComp}}$), we test all $2^3 = 8$ fold tuples $(f_{\text{Alert}}, f_{\text{Inv}}, f_{\text{Cont}})$. Only $(0, 1, 1)$ is chronologically feasible (`00:50Z` $\to$ `01:05Z` $\to$ `01:42Z`). Pruning eliminates impossible folds but leaves each surviving event with its full `[-60 s, +119.999 s]` rounding-and-offset interval.

3. **Duration Interval Arithmetic & Clock-Offset Independence**

### 4.3 Discussion of Every Classification Transition

- **T-401 (`S1`, `P1`):** A `+2 min` SLA tolerance (`1020 s`) absorbs OPS-BER's asymmetric offset and minute floor (`max 991.5 s`), flipping `S1` from `PV` to `CP`. On `P1`, the interval exceeds $\zeta=2$ (`960 s`) by `31.5 s` and only clears at $\zeta=3$ (`1140 s`).
- **T-402 (`S1`, `S2`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3`):** Most sensitive operational case. `S1` is a `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` at `0 min` (`min 958.5 s > 900 s`), softens to `PV` at `+2 min` and `+5 min` (`958.5 <= 1020, 1200 < 1231.5`), and never becomes `CP`. `S2` flips from `PV` to `CP` at `+2 min` (`2761.5 <= 2820`). On the temporal profile, `P1` drops from `CV` at $\zeta=1$ to `PV` at $\zeta=2$ solely because `HUB`'s $\pm 1.5\text{ s}$ brings the lower bound to `958.5 s` (`1.5 s` inside `960 s`); `P2` and `P3` both clear at $\zeta=2$.
- **T-403 (`S1`, `P3`; plus `P1` knife-edge):** `S1` needs the full `+5 min` tolerance (`1200 s`) to clear `VENDOR-NY`'s `1141.5 s` upper bound. `P3` clears at $\zeta=2$. Notably, `P1` **never clears even at $\zeta=3$** because `1141.5 s` exceeds `1140 s` by `1.5 s`.
- **T-404 (`S1`, `S2`, `P1`, `P2`):** `S1` remains `PV` at `+2 min` because `1023 s` exceeds `1020 s` by `3 s` (propagated from `HUB` uncertainty on both endpoints), clearing only at `+5 min`. `S2` is a `PV` at `0 min` solely because `HUB`'s $\pm 3\text{ s}$ duration uncertainty pushes the exact `2700 s` boundary to `2703 s`; `+2 min` tolerance immediately makes `S2` `CP`. Similarly, `P2` clears at $\zeta=2$, and `P1` clears at $\zeta=3$.
- **T-405 (`S1`, `P1`):** Under independent `LEGACY-BER` clock offsets, `S1` (`[720, 1080] s`) requires `+5 min` tolerance to become `CP`, and `P1` clears at $\zeta=3$. (If `LEGACY-BER`'s clock offset were proven constant across the `15 min` gap, `[840, 960] s` would clear `S1` at `+2 min` and `P1` at $\zeta=2$.)
- **T-406 (`S1`, `S2`, `P2`):** `S1` clears only at `+5 min` (`1171.5 <= 1200`). Under primary late-capture semantics, `S2` (`max 2911.5 s`) also needs `+5 min` to clear, and `P2` clears at $\zeta=2$. Under the back-dated alternative semantics, `S2` and `P2` are `CP` everywhere—proving that entry-delay semantics are decisive.
- **T-407 (`S1` conditional) & T-409 (`S1`, `P2` conditional):** Under strict audit semantics, missing receipt acknowledgment (`T-407`) and missing NTP telemetry (`T-409`) keep these rules `INDETERMINATE` regardless of tolerance or $\zeta$. Conditionally, both `S1` proxy/weak intervals (`max 1023 s` and `1021.5 s`) sit just above `+2 min` (`1020 s`) and would only flip to `CP` at `+5 min`, while `T-409 P2` under the weak bound would flip from `PV` at $\zeta=1$ to `CP` at $\zeta=2$.
- **T-408 (`P2`, `P3`, `P4` vs. invariant `S2`, `S3`):** `S2` (`55 m`) and `S3` (`38.42 h`) remain `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` even at `+5 min` tolerance. Yet statistically, `P2` transitions twice (`CV` $\to$ `PV` $\to$ `CP` across $\zeta=1,2,3$) because `3300 s` lands directly on the $+2\sigma$ edge; `P3` flips `CV` $\to$ `CP` at $\zeta=2$; and `P4` flips `CV` $\to$ `CP` at $\zeta=3$ (`138303 <= 151200`), illustrating how a hard SLA breach can sit inside a wide statistical envelope.

Interval = Tuple[datetime, datetime]


def parse_naive_dt(ts_str: str) -> datetime:
    fmt = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S" if len(ts_str) == 19 else "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    return datetime.strptime(ts_str, fmt)


def normalize_row_intervals(row: pd.Series, apply_entry_delay_to_occ: bool = True) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    meta = SOURCE_META[row["system"]]
    tz = ZoneInfo(meta["tz"])
    naive = parse_naive_dt(row["recorded"])

    # Determine clock offset bounds (using weak bound if NTP telemetry is missing)
    ntp_missing = bool(row.get("ntp_missing", False)) if pd.notna(row.get("ntp_missing")) else False
    weak_bounds = row.get("weak_offset_bounds")
    if isinstance(weak_bounds, tuple):
        off_lo, off_hi = weak_bounds
    else:
        off_lo, off_hi = meta["off_lo"], meta["off_hi"]

    res_lo, res_hi = meta["res_lo"], meta["res_hi"]
    entry_delay = float(row["entry_delay_max"]) if pd.notna(row.get("entry_delay_max")) else 0.0
    occ_shift_lo = -entry_delay if apply_entry_delay_to_occ else 0.0

    # Detect DST ambiguity by comparing fold=0 and fold=1 UTC instants
    utc_candidates: List[Tuple[int, datetime]] = []
    seen_utc = set()
    folds = (0, 1) if (bool(row.get("dst_missing", False)) if pd.notna(row.get("dst_missing")) else False) else (0,)
    for fold in folds:
        dt_local = naive.replace(tzinfo=tz, fold=fold)
        dt_utc = dt_local.astimezone(timezone.utc)
        if dt_utc not in seen_utc:
            seen_utc.add(dt_utc)
            utc_candidates.append((fold, dt_utc))

    # Resolution first, then subtract clock_offset in [off_lo, off_hi]
    intervals: List[Interval] = []
    for _, base_utc in utc_candidates:
        lo = base_utc + timedelta(seconds=res_lo - off_hi + occ_shift_lo)
        hi = base_utc + timedelta(seconds=res_hi - off_lo)
        intervals.append((lo, hi))

    return {
        "intervals": intervals,
        "auditable": not ntp_missing,
        "is_inferred": False,
    }

def duration_union(ivs_a: List[Interval], ivs_b: List[Interval]) -> List[Tuple[float, float]]:
    out = []
    for (a_lo, a_hi) in ivs_a:
        for (b_lo, b_hi) in ivs_b:
            out.append(((b_lo - a_hi).total_seconds(), (b_hi - a_lo).total_seconds()))
    return out


def classify_sla_union(durs: List[Tuple[float, float]], limit: float, auditable: bool) -> str:
    if not auditable or not durs:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    all_pass = all(hi <= limit for _, hi in durs)
    all_viol = all(lo > limit for lo, _ in durs)
    if all_pass:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if all_viol:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def classify_profile_union(durs: List[Tuple[float, float]], mean: float, std: float, zeta: float, auditable: bool) -> str:
    if not auditable or not durs:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    band_lo, band_hi = mean - zeta * std, mean + zeta * std
    all_inside = all(lo >= band_lo and hi <= band_hi for lo, hi in durs)
    all_outside = all(hi < band_lo or lo > band_hi for lo, hi in durs)
    if all_inside:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if all_outside:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


case_intervals = build_case_interval_map(events_df)

interval_profile_results: Dict[Tuple[str, str, str, int], Tuple[str, List[Tuple[float, float]]]] = {}
interval_sla_results: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []

for case_id in sorted(case_intervals.keys()):
    ev_map = case_intervals[case_id]
    # Hard SLAs
    for rule_id, (a, b, base_limit) in SLA_RULES.items():
        if a in ev_map and b in ev_map:
            durs = duration_union(ev_map[a]["intervals"], ev_map[b]["intervals"])
            auditable = ev_map[a]["auditable"] and ev_map[b]["auditable"]
        elif case_id == "T-407" and a == "Alert_Received" and b in ev_map:
            # Missing Alert_Received (only Alert_Sent exists without receipt ACK)
            durs, auditable = [], False
        else:
            continue
        row_out = {"case": case_id, "rule": rule_id, "durs": durs}
        for tol_min in (0, 2, 5):
            row_out[f"tol_{tol_min}m"] = classify_sla_union(durs, base_limit + tol_min * 60.0, auditable)
        interval_sla_results.append(row_out)

    # Temporal Profile
    for (a, b), (mean, std) in TEMPORAL_PROFILE.items():
        if a in ev_map and b in ev_map:
            durs = duration_union(ev_map[a]["intervals"], ev_map[b]["intervals"])
            auditable = ev_map[a]["auditable"] and ev_map[b]["auditable"]
        elif case_id == "T-407" and a == "Alert_Received" and b in ev_map:
            durs, auditable = [], False
        else:
            continue
        for zeta in (1, 2, 3):
            verdict = classify_profile_union(durs, mean, std, zeta, auditable)
            interval_profile_results[(case_id, a, b, zeta)] = (verdict, durs)

midpoint_rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
for case_id in sorted(case_intervals.keys()):
    for act, info in case_intervals[case_id].items():
        # Preserve only single-interval (or pruned) events; skip missing Alert_Received in T-407
        lo, hi = info["intervals"][0]
        midpoint_utc = lo + (hi - lo) / 2
        midpoint_rows.append({
            "case_id": case_id,
            "activity": act,

            "approx_note": "LOSSY_MIDPOINT_APPROXIMATION",
        })

midpoint_df = pd.DataFrame(midpoint_rows).sort_values(
    ["case_id", "timestamp_midpoint_lossy"]
).reset_index(drop=True)

formatted_midpoint_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    midpoint_df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp_midpoint_lossy",

def parse_pm4py_temporal_diagnostics(
    raw_diag: Any, ordered_case_ids: List[str]
) -> Dict[str, List[Tuple[str, str, float, float]]]:
    """
    Version-isolated parser for pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile output.
    Assumption: in PM4Py 2.7.x, conformance_temporal_profile returns a list of
    lists of (act_a, act_b, duration_sec, z_score) tuples aligned with sorted
    case IDs when given a formatted pandas DataFrame. If a future PM4Py version
    returns a dict keyed by case_id or a DataFrame, adapt only this function.
    """
    if isinstance(raw_diag, dict):
        return {str(k): list(v) for k, v in raw_diag.items()}
    if isinstance(raw_diag, pd.DataFrame):
        parsed: Dict[str, List[Tuple[str, str, float, float]]] = {c: [] for c in ordered_case_ids}
        for _, r in raw_diag.iterrows():
            c = str(r.get("case:concept:name", r.get("case_id")))
            parsed.setdefault(c, []).append((r["source"], r["target"], float(r["duration"]), float(r["z"])))
        return parsed
    # Standard PM4Py 2.7.x list-of-lists aligned to case order
    return {cid: list(devs) for cid, devs in zip(ordered_case_ids, raw_diag)}


ordered_cases = sorted(midpoint_df["case_id"].unique().tolist())

pm4py_midpoint_deviations: Dict[int, Dict[str, set]] = {}
for zeta in (1, 2, 3):
    raw_res = pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(
        formatted_midpoint_log,
        TEMPORAL_PROFILE,
        zeta=float(zeta),
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    parsed = parse_pm4py_temporal_diagnostics(raw_res, ordered_cases)
    pm4py_midpoint_deviations[zeta] = {
        cid: {(d[0], d[1]) for d in devs} for cid, devs in parsed.items()
    }

print(pd.DataFrame(interval_sla_results)[["case", "rule", "tol_0m", "tol_2m", "tol_5m"]].to_string(index=False))

print("\n=== INTERVAL vs. LOSSY MIDPOINT PM4Py TEMPORAL-PROFILE COMPARISON ===")
comparison_rows = []
for (case_id, a, b, zeta), (iv_verdict, durs) in sorted(interval_profile_results.items()):
    has_both_in_mid = (
        a in case_intervals[case_id] and b in case_intervals[case_id]
    )
    if not has_both_in_mid:
        pm4py_verdict = "NOT_EVALUATED_MISSING_ACTIVITY"
    elif (a, b) in pm4py_midpoint_deviations[zeta].get(case_id, set()):
        pm4py_verdict = "MIDPOINT_DEVIATION"
    else:
        pm4py_verdict = "MIDPOINT_PASS"

    disagrees = (
        (iv_verdict in {"POSSIBLE_VIOLATION", "INDETERMINATE"})
        or (iv_verdict == "CERTAIN_PASS" and pm4py_verdict != "MIDPOINT_PASS")
        or (iv_verdict == "CERTAIN_VIOLATION" and pm4py_verdict != "MIDPOINT_DEVIATION")
    )
    if disagrees:
        comparison_rows.append({
            "case": case_id,
            "pair": f"{a}->{b}",
            "zeta": zeta,
            "interval_verdict": iv_verdict,
            "pm4py_midpoint_lossy": pm4py_verdict,
            "feasible_dur_s": [(round(lo, 1), round(hi, 1)) for lo, hi in durs] if durs else "UNAUDITED/MISSING",
        })

print(pd.DataFrame(comparison_rows).to_string(index=False))

## 7. Governance Recommendation

To prevent recurring evidential failures like `T-405` (missing DST fold), `T-406` (ambiguous mobile entry semantics), `T-407` (missing receipt ACK), and `T-409` (missing NTP telemetry), incident-response platforms should implement the following controls:

1. **Canonical UTC Storage with Raw Timestamp Retention**
   - Store every event timestamp canonically in **UTC with sub-second precision** (ISO 8601 / RFC 3339 with millisecond or microsecond granularity, e.g., `2026-06-01T08:14:23.412Z`).
   - Immutably retain the **raw source timestamp string**, **IANA time-zone identifier** (e.g., `Europe/Berlin`, never ambiguous abbreviations like `CET`/`BST`), and **explicit UTC offset / DST fold** (`+02:00` vs. `+01:00`, `fold  {0,1}`) in provenance columns so conversion errors can be audited and replayed.

2. **Mandatory Clock-Offset Telemetry & Drift Provenance**
   - Instrument every participating system (`HUB`, `OPS-BER`, `SENSOR`, `LEGACY-BER`, mobile backends) to log continuous NTP/PTP synchronization health (`root_dispersion`, `estimated_offset_ms`, `stratum`, `sync_status`) at $\le 5\text{ min}$ intervals and attach the bounding offset interval `[off_min, off_max]` to exported logs.
   - Treat missing sync telemetry (as in `T-409`) as an automatic data-quality fault that flags affected events `UNVERIFIED_CLOCK` rather than silently inheriting nominal offsets.
   - For external vendors (`VENDOR-NY`), enforce contractual SLA clauses requiring UTC logging, NTP sync $\le \pm 5\text{ s}$, and server-side ingestion timestamps at the receiving boundary.

3. **Explicit Resolution & Rounding Metadata**
   - Record per-source timestamp resolution and rounding policy (`exact`, `floor_minute`, `round_10s`, `round_minute`) in a versioned source-metadata registry joined at ETL time. Never strip seconds on export or display in audit tables.

4. **Lifecycle Semantics (`start` / `complete` / `record` Separation)**
   - Separate **occurrence time** (`event_occurred_at`), **source capture time** (`event_recorded_at`), and **central ingestion time** (`ingested_at`). For human-entered mobile/UI events (`MOBILE-LON`), capture both the user-asserted occurrence time and the server-verified submission time, preventing the `T-406` ambiguity.
   - Mandate explicit lifecycle transitions (`Alert_Sent`, `Alert_Received` with delivery ACK, `Investigation_Start`, `Investigation_Complete`, `Containment_Complete`, `Close_Incident`) rather than inferring starts from completions (`T-404`) or substituting `Sent` for `Received` (`T-407`).

5. **Causal & Provenance Identifiers**
   - Propagate immutable **case IDs**, **event UUIDs**, **parent/triggering event IDs** (`caused_by_event_id`), and monotonic **Lamport/ingestion sequence counters** across systems. Causal links let analysts verify whether an alert triggered an investigation (`T-407`) and disambiguate clock/DST anomalies (`T-405`) without relying on unlogged assumptions.

6. **Enforcement & Escalation Policy for Interval Overlap**
   - **Financial penalties, legal SLA breaches, and disciplinary enforcement:** Require **`CERTAIN_VIOLATION`** (entire feasible interval strictly outside the SLA under verified telemetry bounds). **Interval overlap (`POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`) or `INDETERMINATE` must NOT trigger punitive SLA enforcement**, as penalizing a responder on unverified clock or rounding error fails evidentiary due process.
   - **Operational triage & audit remediation:** Treat **`POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`** and **`INDETERMINATE`** as **mandatory investigation and telemetry-remediation triggers** (not free passes). Specifically:
     - Escalate recurring `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` cases to collect stronger evidence (Section 6) and tighten source synchronization.
     - Assign **data-governance non-compliance** (separate from response-time SLA breach) whenever a system's missing telemetry or missing lifecycle events (`T-407`, `T-409`) render SLA conformance `INDETERMINATE`, eliminating any perverse incentive to leave clocks unsynchronized or events unlogged.
   - **Statistical Temporal-Profile Deviations:** Use interval-aware temporal conformance as an early-warning diagnostic (`CERTAIN_VIOLATION` $\to$ process review; `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` $\to$ monitor/refine sensors), never as a legal substitute for hard SLA rules.
