## 1. Materiality Framework

The supplied alignments should be interpreted in three layers:

1. **Control-flow semantics:** what the selected alignment says was synchronous, extra in the log, missing from the log, or silently routed.
2. **Business-policy conformance:** whether amount, fraud risk, beneficiary-bank changes, timing, and notification evidence satisfy P1–P6.
3. **Diagnostic reliability:** whether a deviation represents actual behavior, missing or weak evidence, a label mismatch, harmless telemetry, or a model-scope problem.

A missing model event is evidence that the activity is absent from the aligned event log; it is **not by itself proof that the business activity never occurred**.

| Dimension                    | Materiality question                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Control objective            | Did the deviation affect authorization, fraud screening, payment sequencing, claimant communication, or merely event-log cleanliness?                |
| Amount and exposure          | What amount was paid or potentially exposed? Do HIGH risk or a recent bank change increase fraud or regulatory consequences?                         |
| Reversibility                | Can the action be corrected before funds leave, or has the bank already accepted an irreversible payment?                                            |
| Timing                       | Did a required control occur before payment? Was notification within 24 hours? Could timestamps reflect late entry or clock differences?             |
| Customer and regulatory harm | Could the deviation cause an unauthorized payment, delayed communication, duplicate messages, wrongful denial, or unreliable audit evidence?         |
| Recurrence                   | Is this an isolated case or a systemic pattern, such as 74 retry duplicates or four policy-invalid auto approvals?                                   |
| Evidence reliability         | Are timestamps and identifiers immutable and independently corroborated, or based on mutable links, verbal assertions, or unstable case identifiers? |
| Model scope                  | Is the model valid for the case population, especially denied and withdrawn claims, or is it forcing payable-claim behavior onto non-payable cases?  |

### Severity scale

|         Severity | Interpretation                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **5 — Critical** | Actual or strongly evidenced payment outside a mandatory approval gate, with major financial, fraud, or regulatory exposure. Immediate containment and incident handling are warranted. |
|     **4 — High** | Confirmed policy-invalid approval or other material control bypass, usually involving payment, but with lower exposure or somewhat greater reversibility than a level-5 case.           |
| **3 — Moderate** | Potentially material evidence, correlation, or communication weakness. The underlying activity may have occurred, but compliance is not reliably demonstrable.                          |
|      **2 — Low** | Predominantly model-scope, label-mapping, or documentation mismatch, with no present evidence of an unauthorized payment.                                                               |
|  **1 — Minimal** | Harmless telemetry, retry duplication, or a fitting silent-routing transition with no meaningful control impact.                                                                        |

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Severity describes the consequences of the supported finding; confidence describes how securely that finding is established. CL-602 is high severity because a €480,000 HIGH-risk payment lacks mandatory fraud-screen evidence, but confidence is higher for the evidentiary failure than for the proposition that no screening of any kind occurred. Conversely, CL-603 is low severity but high confidence because the common message ID and connector retry strongly identify a duplicate technical record.

Portfolio priority additionally considers recurrence and remediation leverage. A low-severity pattern occurring 74 times deserves engineering attention, but it should not displace containment of a single critical pre-approval payment.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

For a fixed model, event abstraction, alignment, and cost function, the raw cost is essentially:

[
C_{\text{alignment}}=\sum_{\text{log moves}} c_L+\sum_{\text{visible model moves}} c_M
]

Here every visible discrepancy costs one. The calculation contains no direct term for euros, fraud risk, bank changes, customer harm, irreversibility, evidence strength, or recurrence. Consequently, business severity is not monotonic in raw cost.

| Limitation                                    | Supplied examples                                                                                                                                                                               | Consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Uniform cost-function design**              | CL-601 charges one unit each for opening a workbench, autosaving, and closing it. CL-602 charges one unit for a missing fraud screen on a €480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change.   | Three harmless telemetry records cost more than one potentially critical missing control. The costs are technically consistent but materially incomparable.                                                                      |
| **Model scope**                               | CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 because denial and withdrawal are absent from a payable-claim model.                                                                                              | Large cost can primarily measure model undercoverage rather than operational misconduct. The inserted approval and payment moves do not prove those activities should have occurred.                                             |
| **Unencoded data conditions**                 | CL-609 and CL-610 have cost 0 despite violating P1, and CL-610 also violates P2.                                                                                                                | Perfect control-flow fitness can coexist with an invalid approval decision. A control-flow model cannot enforce attributes that it does not represent.                                                                           |
| **Hidden routing transitions**                | CL-605 contains `T(tau,c=0)`.                                                                                                                                                                   | A zero-cost silent move is ordinary model routing, not evidence of an unlogged business control. Hidden moves should not be assigned material meaning merely because they appear in the alignment.                               |
| **Irreversibility and timing**                | CL-604 costs 3, but the bank accepted payment before approval.                                                                                                                                  | The alignment represents the ordering problem using an early log move and a later missing model move. Unit edit cost does not capture that funds may already be irretrievable or that P3 treats this ordering failure as severe. |
| **Missing-but-external evidence**             | CL-606 has cost 1 for missing `Assess Loss`, although a signed external report exists. CL-608 has cost 1 for missing core notification, although CRM evidence exists at +3 hours.               | A model move can mean absent core-system evidence rather than absent real-world activity. Business conclusions depend on authenticity, timing, correlation, and evidence retention.                                              |
| **Activity granularity and vocabulary**       | CL-611 costs 2 because `Assess Damage` is extra and `Assess Loss` is missing.                                                                                                                   | One semantic label mismatch can produce two technical edits without representing two business failures.                                                                                                                          |
| **Duplicated transport or telemetry records** | CL-603 costs 1 for a retry duplicate; CL-601 costs 3 for UI events.                                                                                                                             | Log moves can reflect recording granularity, retries, and telemetry rather than additional business behavior.                                                                                                                    |
| **Evidence confidence**                       | CL-602’s missing screen is supported by the lack of retained evidence but does not logically prove no off-system check occurred. CL-608’s notification evidence is plausible but weakly linked. | Alignment cost contains no epistemic confidence measure. The same cost can accompany high-confidence benign noise or medium-confidence severe risk.                                                                              |
| **Alternative optimal alignments**            | Another alignment engine or cost configuration may place skips differently while retaining the same total cost.                                                                                 | Risk interpretation should rest on the supplied authoritative move semantics, source evidence, and control objective—not on assuming one alignment rendering is uniquely correct.                                                |

Raw cost remains useful for **technical diagnostics**: locating trace/model disagreement, comparing fitness within a stable and correctly scoped population, identifying repeated label pairs, and measuring the effect of data-cleaning or model repairs. It becomes misleading when used directly as a severity score or when heterogeneous deviations are aggregated without policy and evidence context.

The supplied cases demonstrate the inversion clearly:

* CL-601: cost **3**, severity **1**.
* CL-607 and CL-612: cost **4**, severity **2**.
* CL-602: cost **1**, severity **5**.
* CL-609 and CL-610: cost **0**, severity **4**.
* CL-604: cost **3**, severity **5** because payment timing and irreversibility dominate edit count.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The code below reproduces alignments from in-memory traces, while preserving the supplied alignments and supplied raw costs as the authoritative diagnostic dataset. The final priority ordering is an explicit narrative judgment rather than a computed composite score.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- Model ---
PROCESS_TREE_STRING = (
    "->('Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',"
    "X(->('Fraud Screen','Manual Approve'),'Auto Approve'),"
    "'Pay Claim','Notify Claimant')"
)
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(PROCESS_TREE_STRING)
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# --- Observed traces represented by the supplied alignments ---
# Model moves are absent from these traces; log moves are present.
traces = {
    "CL-601": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Open Workbench", "Assess Loss",
        "Autosave", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
        "Close Workbench",
    ],
    "CL-602": [

        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-603": [

        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-604": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Pay Claim",
        "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-605": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve",
        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-606": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Fraud Screen",
        "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-607": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-608": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Fraud Screen",
        "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim",
    ],
    "CL-609": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve",
        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-610": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve",
        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-611": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Damage", "Fraud Screen",
        "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",

    "CL-612": [

    pd.DataFrame(rows),
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# Calling PM4Py one case at a time makes the case/result mapping explicit.
reproduced = []
for case in traces:
    one_case = log_df.loc[
        log_df["case:concept:name"] == case
    ].copy()

    result = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
        one_case,
        net,
        im,
        fm,
    )[0]

    reproduced.append(
        {
            "case": case,
            "pm4py_cost": result.get("cost"),
            "pm4py_fitness": result.get("fitness"),
            "pm4py_alignment": result.get("alignment"),
        }
    )

pm4py_df = pd.DataFrame(reproduced)

print(
    "NOTE: The supplied alignments are authoritative. Another PM4Py version "
    "or parameterization may choose an alternative optimal alignment, display "
    "skip or silent moves differently, or use a different numerical cost scale."
)

        "S(Notify Claimant) → L(Close Workbench,c=1)",

        "S(Register Claim) → S(Validate Coverage) → S(Assess Loss) → "

raw_cost = {
    "CL-601": 3,
    "CL-602": 1,
    "CL-603": 1,
    "CL-604": 3,
    "CL-605": 0,
    "CL-606": 1,
    "CL-607": 4,
    "CL-608": 1,
    "CL-609": 0,
    "CL-610": 0,
    "CL-611": 2,
    "CL-612": 4,

# Parse the supplied notation into one row per move.
move_rows = []

for case, alignment in supplied.items():
    for position, token in enumerate(alignment.split("→"), start=1):
        token = token.strip()
        move_type = token[0]
        inside = token[2:-1]  # Remove "X(" and the final ")".

        if ",c=" in inside:
            activity, cost_text = inside.rsplit(",c=", 1)
            move_cost = int(cost_text)
        else:
            activity = inside
            move_cost = 0

        move_rows.append(
            (
                case,
                position,
                move_type,
                activity.strip(),
                move_cost,
            )
        )

moves = pd.DataFrame(
    move_rows,
    columns=[
        "case",
        "position",
        "move_type",
        "activity",
        "move_cost",
    ],
)

# Verify that parsing recovers every supplied raw cost.
computed_costs = moves.groupby("case")["move_cost"].sum().to_dict()
assert computed_costs == raw_cost, (computed_costs, raw_cost)

# Severity and priority are explicit narrative judgments.
# They are deliberately not calculated from raw alignment cost.
context = pd.DataFrame(

        (

    "sync_moves",

    "silent_moves",

    if column not in move_counts:

    .merge(
```
