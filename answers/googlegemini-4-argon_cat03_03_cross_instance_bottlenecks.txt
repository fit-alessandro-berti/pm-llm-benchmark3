## 1. Interaction-graph description

To expose the cross-instance couplings that cause cascading delays, the manufacturing system must be modeled as a typed, directed multigraph $G = (V, E, \mathcal{T}_V, \mathcal{T}_E)$. Traditional single-case views collapse this topology, whereas an object-centric graph preserves the structural bridges through which delays propagate.

### Node Types ($\mathcal{T}_V$) and Edge Qualifiers ($\mathcal{T}_E$)
*   **Primary Flow Objects:** `Job` (J1–J6) and `Component` (C1–C6). These represent the focal customer orders and their physical material inputs.
*   **Aggregation/Synchronization Objects:** `ComponentLot` (L7, L8), `Batch` (B1, B2), and `Shipment` (S1–S3). These nodes enforce *many-to-one* and *one-to-many* coupling barriers across jobs.
*   **Reusable Resource Objects:** `Machine` (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1) and `Tool` (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3). These nodes create sequential queueing and hidden blocking dependencies across otherwise independent jobs and batches.

Edges are categorized by their ontological grounding in the event log:
1.  **Direct Static O2O (Observed):** Explicit structural relations declared in the master data or O2O tables (`requires`, `contains`, `batches`, `consolidates`).
2.  **Direct Dynamic E2O-Derived (Observed):** Co-participation of objects in a shared event (`co_executes_at`, `mounts_on`, `tested_with`). For example, `Batch Cure` at 12:05 binds B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3, and C1–C3 into a single hyperedge.
3.  **Inferred Temporal/Contention Edges (Inferred):** Derived by joining resource-state intervals (acquire-to-release or turnaround cycles) with job readiness timestamps (`blocks_tool`, `delays_batch_start`, `withholds_release`, `starves_consolidation`).

### Higher-Order Motifs (Involving $>2$ Object Types)
*   **Motif 1: Tool-Gated Machine Starvation (`Job` $\times$ `Component` $\times$ `Machine` $\times$ `Tool`).** A job (J4) and its component (C4) arrive at an idle primary resource (CNC-2 at 10:05), but execution is blocked because a secondary auxiliary resource (FIX-A) is still held by an upstream job (J3 until 11:40). The machine appears underutilized while the job experiences severe waiting.
*   **Motif 2: Asymmetric Synchronization-to-Consolidation Bowtie (`Job` $\times$ `Batch` $\times$ `Machine` $\times$ `Tool` $\times$ `Shipment`).** Jobs J1, J2, and J3 converge into B1 on OVEN-1 (with RACK-7) and diverge afterward. Subsequently, J1 and J4—which passed through separate batches (B1 and B2)—re-converge into S1. A delay to J3 (holding FIX-A) delays B1 (affecting J1), while simultaneously delaying J4 (waiting for FIX-A), which in turn delays B2 and ultimately penalizes J1 a *second* time at S1 consolidation.
*   **Motif 3: Unlogged Lot-Quarantine Fork (`ComponentLot` $\times$ `Component` $\times$ `Job` $\times$ `Batch`).** Lot L7 contains C2 and C5, coupling J2 and J5. When L7 is quarantined (10:20–13:50), J5 is blocked from advancing toward B2, whereas J2 bypasses the barrier via an unintegrated supervisor override to enter B1 at 12:05. This creates an asymmetric compliance and timing split across two batches.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Because multiple constraints overlap in time, delay intervals cannot be treated as mutually exclusive causal proof. Below are six explicit delay decompositions, paired with their required counterfactual assumptions.

### Calculation 1: Batch B1 Synchronization Waiting for J1 and J2
*   **Data:** J1 ready at 08:10; J2 ready at 08:25; J3 ready at 11:55; B1 `Batch Cure` starts at 12:05.
*   **Computation:**
    *   $\text{Total Wait}_{J1} = 12:05 - 08:10 = 3\text{ h } 55\text{ min } (235\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Total Wait}_{J2} = 12:05 - 08:25 = 3\text{ h } 40\text{ min } (220\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Total Wait}_{J3} = 12:05 - 11:55 = 10\text{ min}$ (baseline loading/dispatch lag).
    *    10 min baseline loading lag: $\text{Sync Wait}_{J1} = 235 - 10 = 225\text{ min } (3\text{ h } 45\text{ min})$; $\text{Sync Wait}_{J2} = 220 - 10 = 210\text{ min } (3\text{ h } 30\text{ min})$.
    *   **Combined J1+J2 Sync Delay:** $225 + 210 = 435\text{ job-minutes } (7\text{ h } 15\text{ min})$.
*   **Counterfactual Assumption:** Assumes OVEN-1 and RACK-7 were idle and available at 08:10 (for single-job curing) or 08:25 (for a two-job batch J1+J2), and that J2’s readiness at 08:25 was legally executable despite L7’s subsequent quarantine at 10:20.

### Calculation 2: FIX-A Contention Delay on J4 at CNC-2
*   **Data:** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50. CNC-2 is busy until 10:05, then idle 10:05–10:47 (and apparently 11:40–11:45). J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40. J4 starts `Machine Process` at 11:45.
*   **Computation:**
    *   $\text{Total Queue Wait}_{J4} = 11:45 - 09:50 = 1\text{ h } 55\text{ min } (115\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Machine Busy Overlap} = 10:05 - 09:50 = 15\text{ min}$.
    *   $\text{Post-Release Setup/Transfer Lag} = 11:45 - 11:40 = 5\text{ min}$.
    *   $\text{Plausible FIX-A Blocking Window} = 11:40 - 10:05 = 1\text{ h } 35\text{ min } (95\text{ min})$, or $11:40 - 09:50 = 1\text{ h } 50\text{ min } (110\text{ min})$ if FIX-A were duplicated and CNC-2 not busy.
    *   Strictly observed idle-machine blocking (where CNC-2 is proven idle while J4 waits for FIX-A): $10:47 - 10:05 = 42\text{ min}$.
*   **Counterfactual Assumption:** Assumes J4’s `Machine Process` duration would fit within CNC-2’s 42-minute idle window (10:05–10:47) or that preempting/delaying whatever used CNC-2 between 10:47 and 11:40 was possible if FIX-A had been available at 10:05.

### Calculation 3: Shipment S1 Consolidation Delay Imposed on J1
*   **Data:** J1 packed at 13:30. J4 packed at 19:10. S1 dispatches at 19:25. Other shipments (S2, S3) dispatch 25 min and 35 min after their final job is packed.
*   **Computation:**
    *   $\text{Total Post-Pack Wait}_{J1} = 19:25 - 13:30 = 5\text{ h } 55\text{ min } (355\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Partner Wait (until J4 packed)} = 19:10 - 13:30 = 5\text{ h } 40\text{ min } (340\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Post-Consolidation Dispatch Lag}_{S1} = 19:25 - 19:10 = 15\text{ min}$ (faster than S2’s 25 min and S3’s 35 min).
*   **Counterfactual Assumption:** Assumes J1 could be dispatched independently upon packing at 13:30 (plus a standard 15–35 min loading/dispatch window, yielding a dispatch around 13:45–14:05) without violating customer delivery contracts or carrier departure schedules.

### Calculation 4: CAL-3 Recalibration and Contention Delay on J6 at TEST-1
*   **Data:** J5 ready at 16:00, tests 16:10–16:35 (25 min duration, 10 min setup/load lag). J6 ready at 16:05. CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20 (45 min). J6 tests 17:25–17:50. TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25.
*   **Computation:**
    *   $\text{Total Wait}_{J6} = 17:25 - 16:05 = 1\text{ h } 20\text{ min } (80\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Direct Test Contention (J5 occupying TEST-1 & CAL-3)} = 16:35 - 16:05 = 30\text{ min}$.
    *   $\text{CAL-3 Recalibration Blocking (while TEST-1 sits idle)} = 17:20 - 16:35 = 45\text{ min}$.
    *   $\text{Post-Recalibration Transfer/Setup Lag} = 17:25 - 17:20 = 5\text{ min}$.
*   **Counterfactual Assumption:** To attribute the 45-minute window (16:35–17:20) solely to CAL-3 recalibration, we assume TEST-1 was fully operational, an operator was present, and a secondary calibrated unit of CAL-3 (or a longer calibration interval) would have allowed J6 to begin testing at 16:35 or 16:40.

### Calculation 5: Overlapping Delay on J4 in Batch B2 (RACK-7 Cleaning vs. J5 Lot-Quarantine/Sync)
*   **Data:** J4 ready for B2 at 13:10. OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50. RACK-7 in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. L7 (holding C5 for J5) released at 13:50. J5 ready for B2 at 14:40. B2 starts at 14:50.
*   **Computation:**
    *   $\text{Total Wait}_{J4\text{ in }B2} = 14:50 - 13:10 = 1\text{ h } 40\text{ min } (100\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{RACK-7 Turnaround Blocking on J4} = 14:35 - 13:10 = 1\text{ h } 25\text{ min } (85\text{ min})$.
    *   $\text{Incremental Sync Wait for J5 (after RACK-7 becomes free)} = 14:40 - 14:35 = 5\text{ min}$.
    *   $\text{Loading Lag} = 14:50 - 14:40 = 10\text{ min}$.
*   **Counterfactual Assumption:** If B2 batching is strict (J4 *must* wait for J5), eliminating RACK-7’s cleaning delay saves **0 minutes** for B2, because J5 isn't ready until 14:40 (due to L7’s quarantine until 13:50 + 50 min upstream processing). Conversely, if J4 could run unbatched, RACK-7 cleaning directly costs J4 85 minutes (13:10 to 14:35). This demonstrates *overdetermination*: RACK-7 turnaround and J5 synchronization mask one another between 13:10 and 14:35.

## 4. What flattening misses

Flattening an Object-Centric Event Log (OCEL) projects a multi-dimensional hypergraph onto a single case notion $c \in \mathcal{T}_V$. This forces three structural distortions: **deficiency** (dropping events not directly linked to $c$), **convergence** (duplicating shared events across multiple instances of $c$), and **divergence** (collapsing distinct causal loops of sub-objects into false sequential self-loops).

| Viewpoint | What Flattening Makes Visible | What It Hides or Duplicates | Misleading DFG / Performance Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Job** | • End-to-end lifecycle per job (`Release` $\to$ `Dispatch`).<br>• Individual rework loops (2 of 6 jobs).<br>• Aggregate stage-to-stage elapsed times. | • **Hides:** L7 `Quarantine`/`Release` (deficiency); tool states (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`); why a job waits.<br>• **Duplicates:** `Batch Cure` (replicated 3× for B1, 2× for B2) and `Dispatch` (replicated 2× per shipment). | **False Homogeneity & Calm Medians:** Medians look normal (`Stage -> Machine` = 1.1h; `Pack -> Dispatch` = 1.1h) and all jobs are within $1.8\sigma$ of the mean because *every* job hits at least one different delay (J1/J2 in B1, J3 holds FIX-A, J4 at CNC-2/B2, J5 in L7, J6 at CAL-3), shifting the mean upward and compressing Z-scores. |
| **Machine** | • Direct active utilization (44%–58%).<br>• Internal machine cycles (`Setup -> Process -> Release`).<br>• Maximum inter-event idle gaps (39–73 min). | • **Hides:** Jobs waiting in front of an idle machine due to missing tools or batch partners.<br>• **Hides:** Cross-machine cascades (e.g., CNC-2 delay pushing B2 later on OVEN-1). | **Illusion of Excess Capacity:** Managers see 44% utilization on OVEN-1 and 54% on CNC-2 and conclude that adding shop-floor resources is unnecessary, missing that machines are starved by single-unit tools (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`). |
| **Batch** | • Batch execution sequence (`Load -> Cure -> Unload`).<br>• Turnaround time between B1 and B2 on OVEN-1. | • **Hides:** Pre-batch job waiting (J1 waiting 3h 55m before B1 is even instantiated/started).<br>• **Hides:** Post-batch divergence into different shipments (B1 splits into S1 and S2). | **Understated Batching Cost:** If a Batch object is only logged when its last member is ready (or at `Load Batch`), batch cycle time looks crisp and efficient, hiding the 435 job-minutes of synchronization wait suffered by J1 and J2. |
| **Tool** | • High tool duty cycles (hold time + cleaning/recalibration time).<br>• Direct sequential handoffs (J3 $\to$ J4 on `FIX-A`; B1 $\to$ B2 on `RACK-7`). | • **Hides:** Downstream shipment consolidation waits (S1) and quality holds (L7).<br>• **Duplicates:** Multi-tool events if any exist. | **Uncontextualized Hold Times:** Shows `FIX-A` held for 2h 40m by J3, but cannot show whether that duration is normal processing or includes J3 waiting for something else, nor how it cascades into S1 via J4. |
| **ComponentLot** | • Quality hold duration (`Quarantine Lot` at 10:20 $\to$ `Release Lot` at 13:50 = 3.5h). | • **Hides:** Machine/tool contention and shipment waits.<br>• **Distorts:** Cannot explain why C2 (J2) entered B1 at 12:05 *during* quarantine while C5 (J5) waited until 13:50. | **Assumed Uniform Compliance:** Suggests both C2 and C5 were held for 3.5 hours, hiding the informal supervisor override on J2 and misattributing J2's timeline. |
| **Shipment** | • Final logistics handling (`Create Shipment -> Load -> Dispatch` = 0.8h + 0.3h). | • **Hides:** The 5h 40m that J1 sat packed (13:30–19:10) waiting for J4 to finish manufacturing. | **Fake Logistics Efficiency:** A total shipment DFG duration of 1.1h convinces logistics managers that dispatch is rapid, hiding that S1 consolidation is the single longest single-job wait in the factory (355 min for J1). |

### Why Low Machine Utilization Coexists with Long Job Waiting
In classical single-resource queueing ($M/M/1$), long waiting times require high server utilization ($\rho \to 1$). In an object-centric manufacturing network, execution requires the **simultaneous conjunction of multiple distinct object types** (a hyperedge precondition):
$$\text{Execute}(e) \iff \text{Ready}(\text{Job}) \land \text{Free}(\text{Machine}) \land \text{Ready}(\text{Tool}) \land \text{Released}(\text{ComponentLot}) \land \big(\forall j \in \text{Batch}, \text{Ready}(j)\big)$$
Whenever an auxiliary resource (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`) is occupied or in turnaround (cleaning/recalibration), or when a synchronization barrier (`B1`, `B2`, `L7`) holds back one required input, the primary machine (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, `TEST-1`) is **blocked from starting** despite having both a waiting job and available capacity. Thus, coupling constraints simultaneously **inflate job waiting time** and **depress machine utilization**.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative A: The Tool-to-Batch-to-Shipment Bowtie Cascade
*   **Objects Spanned (6 Types):** `Job` (J1, J3, J4), `Component` (C1, C3, C4), `Tool` (FIX-A, RACK-7), `Machine` (CNC-2, OVEN-1), `Batch` (B1, B2), `Shipment` (S1).
*   **Temporal Precedence:**
    1. J1 (with C1) becomes ready for B1 at 08:10.
    2. J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00.
    3. J4 (with C4) reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 goes idle at 10:05.
    4. J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40; J4 starts `Machine Process` on CNC-2 with FIX-A at 11:45.
    5. J3 becomes ready for B1 at 11:55; B1 (`J1, J2, J3`) starts `Batch Cure` on OVEN-1 with RACK-7 at 12:05.
    6. OVEN-1 finishes B1 at 12:55; RACK-7 enters cleaning/quarantine until 14:35.
    7. J4 becomes ready for B2 at 13:10; J1 is packed at 13:30.
    8. B2 (`J4, J5`) starts on OVEN-1 with RACK-7 at 14:50.
    9. J4 is packed at 19:10; S1 (`J1, J4`) dispatches at 19:25.
*   **Resource Feasibility:** CNC-2 cannot process J4 without FIX-A. OVEN-1 cannot cure B1 or B2 without RACK-7. RACK-7 cannot be reused for B2 until its post-B1 cleaning finishes at 14:35.
*   **Policy Constraints:** B1 requires J1, J2, and J3 to cure together. S1 requires J1 and J4 to ship together.
*   **Causal Speculation:** J3’s extended hold on FIX-A (09:00–11:40) is a plausible root amplifier that hits J1 **twice**: first by delaying B1 until 12:05 (forcing J1 to wait 235 min pre-cure), and second by delaying J4 at CNC-2 (until 11:45), which pushes J4 into the afternoon B2 window and forces J1 to wait an *additional* 355 min post-pack (13:30–19:25) for S1 consolidation. However, we cannot prove FIX-A is the sole cause of J4's late packing without knowing J4's downstream rework or testing times between 14:50 and 19:10.

### Narrative B: The Lot-Quarantine Fork and Asymmetric Compliance Split
*   **Objects Spanned (6 Types):** `ComponentLot` (L7), `Component` (C2, C5), `Job` (J2, J4, J5), `Batch` (B1, B2), `Tool` (RACK-7), `Machine` (OVEN-1).
*   **Temporal Precedence:**
    1. J2 (requiring C2 $\in$ L7) is recorded ready for B1 at 08:25.
    2. L7 (`C2, C5, J2, J5`) undergoes `Quarantine Lot` at 10:20.
    3. B1 (including J2/C2) executes `Batch Cure` on OVEN-1 at 12:05 (after a supervisor override in an external system).
    4. J4 becomes ready for B2 at 13:10.
    5. L7 undergoes `Release Lot` at 13:50, freeing C5/J5.
    6. RACK-7 finishes cleaning at 14:35; J5 becomes ready for B2 at 14:40; B2 (`J4, J5`) starts on OVEN-1 at 14:50.
*   **Resource Feasibility:** Physical components C2 and C5 are available, and OVEN-1 is idle from 12:55 to 14:50, though RACK-7 is unavailable until 14:35.
*   **Policy Constraints:** Quality policy blocks L7 components from processing between 10:20 and 13:50 unless overridden. Batch policy pairs J4 and J5 into B2.
*   **Causal Speculation:** If the supervisor had *not* overridden L7 for J2, B1 would either have been held until after 13:50 (delaying J1 and J3 by another ~2 hours and pushing B2 even later via RACK-7) or run without J2. Conversely, because J5 was *not* overridden, L7's quarantine delayed J5's arrival at B2 until 14:40—which happened to mask RACK-7’s cleaning delay (ending at 14:35) by a margin of just 5 minutes.

### Narrative C: Late-Stage Calibration Contention and Consolidation Tail
*   **Objects Spanned (5 Types):** `ComponentLot` (L7), `Component` (C5, C6), `Job` (J5, J6), `Machine` (TEST-1), `Tool` (CAL-3), `Shipment` (S3).
*   **Temporal Precedence:**
    1. Following L7 release (13:50) and B2 curing (14:50), J5 reaches TEST-1 readiness at 16:00.
    2. J6 (with C6) reaches TEST-1 readiness at 16:05 (5 minutes behind J5).
    3. J5 executes `Final Test` on TEST-1 using CAL-3 from 16:10 to 16:35.
    4. CAL-3 undergoes recalibration from 16:35 to 17:20 while TEST-1 sits idle.
    5. J6 executes `Final Test` on TEST-1 using CAL-3 from 17:25 to 17:50.
    6. J5 and J6 are packed and consolidated into S3, which dispatches 35 minutes after the last job (J6) is packed.
*   **Resource Feasibility:** TEST-1 is physically capable of testing J6 at 16:35, but cannot operate without a calibrated CAL-3 unit.
*   **Policy Constraints:** Every `Final Test` requires CAL-3, and calibration policy triggers a 45-minute recalibration immediately after J5's test rather than deferring it until after J6.
*   **Causal Speculation:** Because J5 and J6 are paired into S3, delaying J6 by 45 minutes via inline CAL-3 recalibration directly delays the dispatch of **both** J5 and J6 in S3 by ~45 minutes. Had CAL-3 been recalibrated *before* 16:00 (while J5 was still in B2) or replaced with a hot-swap spare, J6 would have finished testing at ~17:00, advancing S3 dispatch by nearly an hour.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The following executable-looking Python script demonstrates how to analyze `ocel` in `pm4py`, comparing object-centric frequency metrics and computing multi-object contention intervals without relying solely on machine-event gaps.

```python
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Structural & Topology Discovery (O2O, OTG, ETOT, Interaction Summary)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Summarize pairwise object interactions (e.g., Job-Tool, Job-Batch, Lot-Job)
interactions_df = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
print("Pairwise Object Interactions Summary:")
print(interactions_df.groupby(["ocel:type_1", "ocel:type_2"]).size())

# Discover Object-Level Graph, Object-Type Graph (OTG), and Event-to-Object-Type (ETOT)
obj_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Optional: Convert OCEL to NetworkX for higher-order motif detection (e.g., J1-B1-J3-FIXA-J4-S1)
nx_graph = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
bowties = [
    n for n, d in nx_graph.degree() 
    if d >= 4 and str(n).startswith(("B", "S", "L", "FIX", "RACK", "CAL"))
]
print(f"High-degree coupling hubs in NetworkX graph: {bowties}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. OC-DFG Discovery: Comparing Two Object-Centric Frequency Metrics
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Metric A: 'events' (Unique Event Frequency) - counts distinct hyperedges (e.g., 1 Batch Cure for B1)
ocdfg_events = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel, business_hours=False)

# Metric B: 'total_objects' (Total Object-Pair Frequency) - counts all object instances traversing edge
# This exposes convergence amplification (e.g., Batch Cure counts 3 Jobs + 3 Components + 1 Batch + 1 Oven + 1 Rack)
print("\nComparing OC-DFG Frequency Metrics (Unique Events vs. Total Object Traversals):")
for act, ev_cnt in ocdfg_events["activities_indep"]["events"].items():
    tot_obj_cnt = ocdfg_events["activities_indep"]["total_objects"].get(act, 0)
    uniq_obj_cnt = ocdfg_events["activities_indep"]["unique_objects"].get(act, 0)
    print(f"Activity: {act:20s} | Event Freq: {ev_cnt:2d} | Unique Obj: {uniq_obj_cnt:2d} | Total Obj Freq: {tot_obj_cnt:2d}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Multi-Viewpoint Flattening & Performance DFG Comparison
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
object_types_to_compare = ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]
flattened_perf_summaries = {}

for ot in object_types_to_compare:
    try:
        flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
        perf_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_log)
        flattened_perf_summaries[ot] = perf_dfg
    except Exception as e:
        print(f"Could not flatten on {ot}: {e}")

# Show how Shipment flattening hides J1's 5h 55m post-pack consolidation wait
print("\nShipment-Flattened Performance DFG (hides Job-level Pack -> Dispatch wait):")
for edge, metrics in flattened_perf_summaries.get("Shipment", {}).items():
    print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: median={metrics.get('median', 0)/3600:.2f}h, mean={metrics.get('mean', 0)/3600:.2f}h")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Multi-Object Contention Decomposition (Beyond Machine-Event Gaps)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Instead of inferring waiting solely from Machine gaps, join Job readiness, Machine state,
# Tool holds/maintenance, ComponentLot quarantines, and Batch synchronization barriers.
relations = ocel.relations.copy()
relations["ocel:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(relations["ocel:timestamp"])

def get_object_intervals(rel_df, obj_type, start_acts, end_acts):
    """Extract active/blocked state intervals for a specific object type."""
    sub = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == obj_type].sort_values("ocel:timestamp")
    starts = sub[sub["ocel:activity"].isin(start_acts)][["ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:activity"]]
    ends = sub[sub["ocel:activity"].isin(end_acts)][["ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:activity"]]
    return pd.merge_asof(
        starts.rename(columns={"ocel:timestamp": "start_ts", "ocel:activity": "start_act"}),
        ends.rename(columns={"ocel:timestamp": "end_ts", "ocel:activity": "end_act"}),
        by="ocel:oid", left_on="start_ts", right_on="end_ts", direction="forward"
    )

# Extract Lot Quarantine intervals and Tool Busy/Maintenance intervals
lot_quarantine_iv = get_object_intervals(relations, "ComponentLot", ["Quarantine Lot"], ["Release Lot"])
tool_busy_iv = get_object_intervals(
    relations, "Tool",
    ["Acquire Tool", "Setup", "Load Batch", "Start Recalibration", "Clean Tool"],
    ["Release Tool", "Release", "Unload Batch", "End Recalibration", "Release Clean"]
)

# Extract Job transition intervals (e.g., Stage Component -> Machine Process, Pack -> Dispatch)
job_events = relations[relations["ocel:type"] == "Job"].sort_values(["ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"])
job_events["next_act"] = job_events.groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:activity"].shift(-1)
job_events["next_ts"] = job_events.groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].shift(-1)
job_events["wait_mins"] = (job_events["next_ts"] - job_events["ocel:timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# Attribute Job waiting to overlapping Tool/Lot/Shipment barriers (rather than Machine idle gaps)
print("\nJob Transition Waits Overlapped with Non-Machine Coupling Barriers:")
for _, row in job_events.dropna(subset=["next_ts"]).iterrows():
    if row["wait_mins"] > 60:
        # Check overlap with Tool hold/recal or Lot Quarantine
        t_overlaps = tool_busy_iv[
            (tool_busy_iv["start_ts"] < row["next_ts"]) & (tool_busy_iv["end_ts"] > row["ocel:timestamp"])
        ]["ocel:oid"].tolist()
        l_overlaps = lot_quarantine_iv[
            (lot_quarantine_iv["start_ts"] < row["next_ts"]) & (lot_quarantine_iv["end_ts"] > row["ocel:timestamp"])
        ]["ocel:oid"].tolist()
        print(
            f"  Job {row['ocel:oid']} ({row['ocel:activity']} -> {row['next_act']}): "
            f"{row['wait_mins']:.0f} min | Active Tools: {t_overlaps} | Quarantined Lots: {l_overlaps}"
        )

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Feature Extraction for Predictive Delay Attribution
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
ocel_features_df, feature_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, obj_type="Job")
print(f"\nExtracted {len(feature_names)} object-centric features for Job-level delay modeling.")
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Six Observations That Would Weaken the Top-Ranked Explanations
1.  **Unconstrained Tool Pool (Weakens Rank 1 – Tool Contention):** Discovering in inventory records that FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3 are *tool classes* with multiple identical physical units available on the rack during the exact windows J4 (09:50–11:45), B2 (13:10–14:35), and J6 (16:35–17:25) were waiting, proving tools did not physically block execution.
2.  **Long Job-4 Runtime Exceeding the Idle Slot (Weakens Rank 1 – FIX-A Blocking at CNC-2):** Observing that J4’s `Machine Process` requires $\ge 60\text{ minutes}$ of continuous CNC-2 time, and CNC-2 had a hard scheduled preventive maintenance or high-priority reservation starting at 10:47. In that case, J4 could not have fit into the 42-minute idle window (10:05–10:47) even if FIX-A had been free at 09:50.
3.  **Premature / Speculative Job Release Timestamps (Weakens Rank 2 – Batch Synchronization):** Finding that `Ready for batch` at 08:10 (J1) and 08:25 (J2) represents an automated ERP print timestamp, whereas physical staging of C1 and C2 did not finish until 11:45, or OVEN-1 was occupied by an unlogged batch B0 until 11:50.
4.  **Strict Carrier Cutoff / Fixed Milk-Run Schedule (Weakens Rank 3 – Shipment Consolidation):** Discovering that S1’s freight carrier has a fixed contractual daily pickup window at 19:30. Under that constraint, even if J1 had been decoupled from J4 at 13:30, J1 would still have sat on the loading dock until 19:25.
5.  **Cross-Machine Operator Starvation (Reframes Ranks 1–2 as Labor Contention):** Observing that CNC-2, OVEN-1, and TEST-1 share a single cross-trained setup/calibration technician who was busy elsewhere from 10:05–10:47, 13:10–14:35, and 16:35–17:20, meaning human labor—not tools or batch rules—caused the idle gaps.
6.  **Sub-Lot Physical Segregation (Weakens Rank 4 – ComponentLot Coupling):** Finding that L7 was physically split into inspected sub-lot L7a (containing C2, cleared prior to 08:25) and uninspected sub-lot L7b (containing C5), meaning the 10:20 quarantine applied solely to L7b and J2’s supervisor override was merely correcting a clerical lot-tagging error.

### Six Additional Data Fields or Event Types to Discriminate Among Hypotheses
1.  **Explicit Resource-Binding Lifecycle Events (`Acquire Tool`, `Release Tool`, `Start Cleaning`, `End Cleaning`, `Start Recalibration`, `End Recalibration`):** Necessary to distinguish active processing time from passive holding time and turnaround maintenance on FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3.
2.  **Granular Queue-Reason / Blocking-State Codes (`Wait_Reason_Code`, `Blocking_Object_ID`):** Whenever a job is ready (`Stage Component` complete) but `Machine Process`, `Batch Cure`, or `Final Test` does not start within 5 minutes, log an explicit blocking tuple—e.g., `(J4, Blocked_By, FIX-A)` vs. `(J4, Blocked_By, CNC-2_Busy)`.
3.  **Transitive E2O / O2O Lineage Propagation (`Inherited_Lot_ID`, `Override_Auth_ID`, `Override_Timestamp`):** Enrich the OCEL extraction pipeline so events on `ComponentLot` (L7, L8) and external supervisor override logs are explicitly linked to affected `Component` and `Job` objects with timestamped authorization codes.
4.  **Operator / Technician Object Type (`Operator`: OP-1, OP-2, etc.):** Introduce `Operator` as a first-class object type in the OCEL to separate physical tool/machine contention from hidden human-resource contention during setups, inspections, rework, and recalibrations.
5.  **Batch Formation Policy Metadata (`Batch_Min_Size`, `Batch_Max_Wait_Policy`, `Oven_Ready_Timestamp`):** Record on `Batch` objects whether a batch start is triggered by a fixed schedule, a volume threshold, or dynamic dispatch, alongside the exact timestamp OVEN-1 and RACK-7 become jointly available.
6.  **Shipment SLA & Carrier Cutoff Attributes (`Earliest_Carrier_Slot`, `Partial_Ship_Allowed_Flag`, `Job_Pack_Timestamp` on Shipment E2O):** Record on `Shipment` objects whether consolidation is a hard customer mandate or an optional freight-saving heuristic, and bind each `Pack` event directly to both `Job` and `Shipment` so post-pack waiting is natively visible in shipment analytics.
