## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**Framework choice.** I do **not** use natural direct/indirect effects as the primary framework. Natural effects require cross-world independence (the mediator distribution under the counterfactual exposure must be well defined for a unit assigned the other exposure), which fails here because (a) shared queues create interference so a unit's mediator depends on *other* units' flags, and (b) queue state is an exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounder (being flagged speeds triage, which changes the queue state the case meets later). Instead I use **separable/interventional effects**: the flag bundle is decomposed into separable components (priority lane vs. mandatory review vs. escalation routing), and indirect effects are defined via *stochastic or fixed interventions on mediator stages* applied to a well-defined population. Controlled direct effects (CDEs) are reported only as fixed-stage analogues, not as "what would happen if the flag were hidden."

Where it arises (180 words): **Interference** is pervasive: triage, review, and escalation queues are shared, so one flagged case's priority lane lengthens unflagged cases' triage waits, and mandatory review of flagged cases consumes shared review capacity, shifting unflagged review waits (unit-level causal effects are not defined without a cluster/queue-level estimand). **Exposure-induced confounding** of mediatoroutcome paths: the flag accelerates triage, which changes *when* a case enters the review queue; queue state at that entry time (arrival_load, backlog) both mediates upstream effects and confounds waitingcycle-time and waitingescalation relations. **Treatment–confounder feedback**: being flagged and prioritized alters time-of-day and congestion for later stages, which feed back into resource assignment (T1/T2 get flagged cases) and reviewer workload; resource assignment is thus a confounder affected by prior treatment stages, so adjusting for it naively (or not) both bias different contrasts. Unobserved underlying risk confounds flagoutcome through case mix throughout.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change my conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the lane, not hidden risk, cause fast triage? | Within-risk-score (or local-threshold) comparison: cases just above vs just below the flag threshold on risk_score | risk_score (narrow bands), complexity, arrival_load, time-of-day | Threshold is sharp; risk_score continuous near cutoff; no precise manipulation of the score | McCrary-style density test of risk_score at threshold; covariate balance of complexity/load near cutoff | Discontinuity in complexity or arrival_load at threshold, or smooth triage-wait change across it  lane effect is confounded or absent |
| Are downstream waits congestion, not case attributes? | Queue-state-aware analysis: model manual/escalation wait as function of backlog at triage_done, stratified by flag | Backlog at stage entry, arrival time, resource, complexity | Queue state measured well; no unmeasured within-queue priority | Compare wait vs queue length slopes for flagged vs unflagged; flag×backlog interaction | Flagged cases wait long even in empty queues  wait is process design (routing), not congestion |
| What is the total effect of the whole bundle? | Policy-change (interrupted time series) or randomized encouragement to the flag bundle | Pre/post time trends, seasonality, load | No concurrent changes; SUTVA at cohort level | Placebo policy dates; pre-trend test on cycle time and triage wait | Pre-existing trends in cycle time parallel to the policy change  attribution invalid |
| How much runs through review occurrence vs waiting? | Mediation-oriented sequential intervention design: randomize (i) review mandate and (ii) review scheduling priority, separately | Complexity, risk_score, arrival_load, queue state at triage_done | Consistency of stochastic interventions; no interference beyond measured backlog | Compare E3-type (occurrence) and E4-type (waiting) estimates; check monotonicity of review yield by flag | Effects collapse when only occurrence is manipulated  waiting pathway dominated; or vice versa |
| Is the flag proxying unmeasured risk? | Negative-control exposure (pre-policy pseudo-flag from old risk score) and negative-control outcome (admin residual-to-close time) | Same adjustment sets | Negative controls unaffected by flag handling | Pseudo-flag should show no triage-lane effect; flag should not affect admin residual time | Pseudo-flag predicts triage speed or flag predicts residual admin time  unmeasured risk or labeling effects present |
| How sensitive to hidden risk? | Sensitivity analysis: bias parameters for U_underlying_risk linking flag and outcome/wait (Rosenbaum-style E-values on mediation contrast) | — | Correct outcome model under measured confounding | Report the unmeasured-confounder strength needed to nullify the review-occurrence indirect effect | A modest hidden risk correlate (e.g., complexity-like variable) suffices to null the indirect effect  treat mediation as provisional |
| Do flagged cases harm unflagged ones? | Interference-aware design: cohort-level randomization of daily flagged-lane capacity; estimate spillover on unflagged waits | Cohort load, day-of-week, arrival volume | Only cohort-level interference; excludability of cohort assignment | Dose–response of unflagged review wait vs cohort flagged share | Monotone rise in unflagged waits with flagged share  spillover is real; unit-level estimates invalid |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0: current bundle | Fast triage for flagged (observed) | Large review queue load from mandatory review; high escalation demand | Flagged 16.5h vs unflagged 8.2h observed | Escalation concentrates correct rejections — apparently valuable | Unflagged cases displaced in shared queues | Baseline observation only | Keep as comparator |
| P1: hide flag display | Triage lane removed or inconsistently applied | Downstream unchanged if humans re-derive same decisions | Likely  pre-policy triage waits; total effect ambiguous | Removes anchoring; but humans may still infer risk | Minimal immediate spillover | Weak — display removal  behavior removal | Only as a diagnostic arm, not a fix |
| P2: lane without mandatory review | Fast triage retained for flagged | Review queue relieved; but risky cases may skip review | Mixed: flagged non-escalated paths 9.4h observed; risk of missed review for genuinely risky cases | Highest quality risk — review is where escalation/rejection signal originates | Reduces spillover on unflagged | Moderate — inferred from non-escalated flagged subgroup | Promising, pilot with audit |
| P3: protected review capacity for flagged | Unchanged | Flagged review waits fall (6.5h potentially 1–2h) | Likely largest cycle-time gain for flagged High complexity | Unchanged decision path | Capacity pulled from unflagged  their waits rise | Moderate — mechanism directly observed | Strong pilot candidate with guardrails |
| P4: change escalation criteria/capacity | Unchanged | Escalation queue wait (6–10h) falls if capacity added, or fewer cases escalate | Gains limited to the 6–10h escalation wait portion | Could under-escalate truly risky cases — 3/6 flagged escalations rejected, so escalation appears substantively necessary | Frees or burdens senior team | Weak — criteria quality unobservable in data | Pilot only with adjudicated outcome audit |

**Selected pilot: P3 (protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases).**
- *Eligibility:* all arriving cases; flag assigned by the existing threshold rule; pilot runs 6 weeks.
- *Assignment:* cluster-level randomized crossover by day — intervention days route flagged cases to a protected reviewer slot; control days use current shared queue. Randomization stratified by expected daily load and complexity mix.
- *Queue-capacity guardrail:* if unflagged review backlog at 12:00 exceeds the 90th percentile of pre-pilot backlog, the protection is suspended for the rest of the day (pre-specified) to bound spillover.
- *Primary outcome:* triage_donemanual_start wait for flagged cases. *Secondary:* flagged cycle time, unflagged review wait (spillover), escalation rate and wait, decision reversals in a blinded audit of 10% of flagged cases, and rework/return rate.
- *Minimum process data:* all stage start/complete timestamps, queue length at each stage entry, resource IDs, flagged share per day, and review findings text.
- *Stopping conditions:* unflagged review wait rises >30% over control days for 3 consecutive intervention days; audit reversal rate exceeds pre-registered threshold; or flagged review wait fails to improve by day 15 (protection ineffective).

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** if a risk dimension omitted from `risk_score` (correlated with complexity) both drives the flag and independently causes long review/escalation paths, the "flag causes downstream delay" interpretation collapses into case-mix confounding, and P2/P3 would not shorten cycles for the truly risky.
2. **Queue interference:** if unflagged cases' short waits are largely *because* flagged cases absorb shared review capacity, the observed 8.2h vs 16.5h gap understates the bundle's system-wide cost, and P3's protected capacity would shift rather than reduce total waiting.
3. **Decision quality:** if flagged-case escalations are mostly unnecessary (anchoring on the label) rather than needed — e.g., an adjudicated audit shows flagged escalation rejections mirror unflagged base rates — P4 criteria relaxation, not capacity, becomes the preferred policy.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** if local clocks at different resources are skewed, or `manual_start` actually logs queue *entry* rather than service start (a common EL semantics error), the 6.5h "pre-review wait" could partly be service time or clock artifact, reversing the queueing-vs-routing diagnosis.
5. **Positivity/overlap:** if unflagged cases essentially never receive review at high risk scores (only 4/10 here, none high-risk-unflagged-reviewed except R09/R20), no unflagged comparison supports high-risk cases, and mediation contrasts over that region are extrapolation, not evidence.
6. **Reviewer behavior change:** if reviewers, knowing a case waited long, expedite or shortcut subsequent stages (compensating behavior), the waitingcycle-time pathway is partially offset and a pilot adding review capacity could show little total-cycle benefit, reversing the P3 recommendation.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** flagged cases reach triage in ~15 minutes versus ~2.5 hours, receive manual review universally (unflagged: 40%), are escalated three times as often, and wait ~6.5 hours between triage completion and review start — the single largest stage gap. Their longer cycles are concentrated in flagged high-complexity cases (24h vs 9.5h for flagged medium).

**Plausible mechanism:** the flag operates as a *policy bundle* — priority lane (fast triage), mandatory review (adds a scarce shared-queue stage), and escalation propensity (adds a slower, scarcer senior stage). The cycle-time gap is mostly mediated by queue waiting at review and escalation, not by slow triage. The flag is a risk-score threshold, so underlying risk — imperfectly measured — plausibly confounds the bundle–outcome relation.

**Unidentified:** how much delay is caused by the flag's routing rules versus needed by genuinely riskier cases; whether review waits reflect congestion or deliberate scheduling; whether escalation improves decisions; and how flagged volume displaces unflagged cases through shared queues. Natural direct/indirect effects are not identified from this data.

**Most useful next evidence:** the P3 pilot — cluster-randomized protected review capacity with queue-state logging, a spillover guardrail, and a blinded outcome audit — because it manipulates one separable bundle component, measures both the waiting pathway and its displacement cost, and yields decision-quality evidence simultaneously.
