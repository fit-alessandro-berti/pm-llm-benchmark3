## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I use interventional policy contrasts plus controlled direct effects (CDEs) and interventional indirect effects (IIEs) as sensitivity targets, not natural direct/indirect effects, because the flag is a policy bundle, shared queues create interference, and the risk score is an imperfect proxy for underlying risk. “Set flag to zero” is not the same intervention as “change what the organization does after seeing the flag.”

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs P1: current flag policy vs suppress flag display while keeping risk score and human information unchanged | cases arriving in the eligible population | total cycle time, close minus arrival | total interventional policy effect | conditional: case-level only under no interference; cluster-level better | shared-queue interference, unobserved risk, noncompliance |
| E2 | P0 vs P1 | cases arriving in the eligible population | arrival-to-triage-done | total stage effect | conditional: no interference, correct timestamps | arrival load, complexity, resource assignment |
| E3 | P0 vs P1; mediator `manual_review` set to its P1 distribution | cases arriving in the eligible population | total cycle time | interventional indirect analogue via manual-review occurrence | no, not identified here; requires sequential exchangeability and no interference | exposure-induced confounding, unobserved risk, interference |
| E4 | P0 vs P1; mediator `manual_queue_wait` set to its P1 distribution among reviewed cases | cases arriving in the eligible population | total cycle time | interventional indirect analogue via manual-review waiting | no, not identified here; requires queue-state ignorability and no interference | queue state, shared queues, unobserved risk |
| E5 | P0 vs P1; mediator `escalation` set to its P1 distribution | cases arriving in the eligible population | total cycle time | interventional indirect analogue via escalation | no, not identified here; requires no unmeasured escalation-outcome confounding | unobserved risk, senior-queue interference |

| E8 | P0 vs P3: retain routing and add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases | cases arriving in the eligible population | total cycle time | total policy effect | conditional: capacity policy fully specified; cluster-level better | capacity spillover to unflagged cases, interference |

Interference arises because manual and escalation queues are shared: flag-driven routing changes one case’s queue position and can change waiting times for other cases, so case-level SUTVA is false. Exposure-induced confounding arises because risk score and complexity, which predict flag, also predict manual-review occurrence, service times, escalation, and outcomes; after flag, manual review is a mediator but also carries unobserved underlying risk. Treatment-confounder feedback arises if triage staff use observed risk or complexity to choose review or escalation, making post-treatment variables confounded with unobserved risk. Arrival load is only a coarse snapshot, so residual queue state can confound waiting times. Therefore, simple regression of cycle time on flag or mediators can conflate direct flag effects, case mix, and queue spillovers.

Flagged cases start triage much sooner, mean 14.4 versus 148.8 minutes, and complete triage faster, 36.0 versus 57.0 minutes, consistent with a priority lane. Their total cycle is longer, 990.6 versus 478.2 minutes, mainly because nearly all are reviewed, have much longer manual waits, 390.0 versus 112.5 minutes among reviewed cases, and escalate more often. High complexity and high arrival load show similar downstream delays. These contrasts are not automatically causal: flagged cases are enriched for high risk and complexity, review and escalation decisions are made by humans after seeing risk, and shared queues mean flagged priority can increase unflagged waits. The data show association, not the causal share of the flag.

## 4. Direct and mediated pathway analysis

| pathway | candidate interpretation | observable evidence | required assumptions | competing explanation | intervention targeting this pathway | expected downstream side effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flag -> priority_lane -> triage_wait / triage_service | automatic priority lane accelerates triage start and service | flagged arrival-to-triage-start 14.4 vs 148.8; triage service 36.0 vs 57.0 | priority lane is as recorded; no unmeasured case mix; no interference | staff notice risk score or complexity and triage faster anyway | P1 suppress flag display; P2 retains priority lane | unflagged triage waits may rise if triage capacity is fixed |
| flag -> manual_review occurrence | mandatory review for flagged cases increases review incidence | 10/10 flagged reviewed vs 4/10 unflagged | policy compliance; review decision not solely driven by hidden risk | triage staff choose review based on risk score, complexity, or judgment | P2 remove automatic mandatory review | fewer reviews may miss high-risk cases |
| flag -> manual_queue_wait | flagged cases enter shared manual queue in larger volume and/or without queue priority, increasing waits | flagged manual wait 390.0 vs unflagged reviewed 112.5; flagged high-load 445.0 | shared queue; queue state measured; no hidden priority rule | reviewed unflagged cases are less complex; exogenous congestion | P3 protected manual-review capacity or queue priority | unflagged manual waits may increase if capacity is carved out |
| flag -> escalation | review of flagged cases leads to more escalation | 6/10 flagged escalated vs 2/10; escalated flagged total 1285.0 vs 549.0 | escalation follows review; senior queue shared | underlying risk drives escalation independent of flag | P4 change escalation criteria or senior capacity | senior overload or missed escalations |
| complexity/risk confounding | complexity and underlying risk drive both flag and longer stages | high complexity total 1164.7 vs low 261.0; flagged enriched in high complexity | complexity is a valid intake proxy; stable assignment | flag may change how complexity is handled after intake | stratify or randomize within complexity and risk-score bands | reduced power in narrow strata |

| reviewer/resource assignment | flagged cases are assigned to resources with different service times or workloads | flagged cases use T1/T2 and M1-M3; unflagged cases use T3/T4 more often | resource skill and workload not fully observed | assignment is driven by complexity or risk, not flag itself | randomize or standardize resource routing | workload imbalance across resources |
| decision-quality tradeoffs | more review and escalation improve detection but add delay | reject rate is 2/10 in both groups; quality is not measured | outcome is a proxy for quality and can be audited | risk score is imperfect and hidden risk matters | P2 or P4 with quality audit | false approvals or compliance risk |

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag threshold itself change triage and downstream timing? | within-risk-score or local-threshold design comparing cases just above and below the flag threshold | complexity, arrival_load, time of day if available | continuity around threshold, no manipulation, no unobserved jump, known flag rule | covariate balance around threshold, smooth outcome trend, placebo threshold | no jump in triage wait or manual wait would weaken a causal flag effect |
| Is the flag effect on waiting mainly due to queue congestion? | queue-state-aware analysis modeling waits as functions of flag, estimated queue length, resource busy state, and arrival load | queue length, arrival_load, complexity, resource | correct queue model, no unmeasured congestion, valid queue-state proxy | residualized flag effect by queue state; queue length predicts waits | flag effect disappearing at low queue would support queue mediation |
| What is the effect of the full policy bundle? | policy-change or randomized encouragement design, e.g. show/hide flag or encourage/remove mandatory review, clustered by review pool | baseline covariates for precision only | random assignment, compliance, limited interference | ITT effect, compliance rate, spillover on unflagged waits | null ITT or large spillover would change policy recommendation |
| How much of the total effect is mediated by manual occurrence, waiting, or escalation? | mediation-oriented analysis using CDEs and IIEs with sensitivity for unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding | pre-treatment risk_score, complexity, arrival_load; post-treatment mediators | no unmeasured treatment-mediator or mediator-outcome confounding, no interference, positivity | E-value, negative-control mediator, bootstrap confidence intervals | indirect effects small or highly sensitive would weaken mechanism claim |
| Are observed differences spurious or driven by unmodeled confounding? | negative-control outcome or exposure, e.g. pre-introduction cycle time for similar cases or a fake flag from an irrelevant score | same as primary analysis | no true effect on negative-control outcome or exposure | negative-control estimates near null with intervals including zero | significant negative-control effect would indicate confounding or data artifact |
| How robust are conclusions to hidden risk not captured by risk_score? | sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk using latent-variable bounds, E-values, or proxy-balanced comparisons | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load | explicit sensitivity model for U_underlying_risk | E-value, bounds, proxy balance between flagged and unflagged | conclusion requiring strong hidden-risk confounding would reduce confidence |
| Does flag policy affect unflagged cases through shared queues? | interference-aware cluster-randomized design measuring unflagged waits as spillover outcomes | cluster, time, queue state | local interference within cluster, random clusters | unflagged wait changes, cluster-level balance | large negative spillover would make case-level benefit misleading |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current system | flagged fast, unflagged slower | manual and escalation queues congested; flagged waits long | flagged long, unflagged moderate | likely higher compliance if review is protective | unflagged waits may increase | weak observational evidence | safety baseline, not preferred if performance is a problem |
| P1 suppress flag display, keep risk score and human information | less systematic priority; high-risk triage may slow | less automatic review; manual queue may fall | flagged cycle may increase, unflagged may decrease | risk if humans underreact to visible risk score | unflagged triage may improve | moderate for isolating display effect | diagnostic only, not default |
| P2 retain priority triage, remove automatic mandatory manual review | flagged triage remains fast | fewer reviews, lower manual wait, less escalation | flagged cycle likely decreases | missed-risk risk if review is needed | unflagged manual waits may improve | strong for occurrence mediation | preferred limited pilot |
| P3 retain routing, add protected manual-review capacity for flagged | unchanged | flagged manual wait decreases; unflagged may increase | flagged cycle decreases, unflagged may increase | low if review remains required | negative spillover to unflagged | strong for waiting mediation | use if review is mandatory for quality |
| P4 retain manual review, change escalation criteria or capacity | unchanged | manual wait unchanged; escalation wait/service changes | cycle may decrease if escalation is reduced or senior capacity increases | depends on criteria; may miss or over-escalate | senior-team spillover | strong for escalation mediation | consider after P2 or P3 |

Pilot P2 for flagged medium-complexity cases with risk_score 0.60–0.85 and no prior adverse outcome. After triage, randomize 1:1 to current automatic mandatory review, control, or reviewer-discretion review, treatment, with a hard rule that any reviewer concern triggers review. Randomize by review-resource day cluster to reduce interference. Guardrail: if flagged manual-queue wait exceeds 240 minutes or unflagged manual wait exceeds 120 minutes for two consecutive days, automatically restore mandatory review for the treatment arm. Primary outcome: median total cycle time from arrival to close. Secondary outcomes: manual-review occurrence, manual wait, escalation rate, senior wait, approve/reject rate, and audited decision quality. Minimum data: case ID, timestamps for triage/manual/escalation/close, resources, flag, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, queue length, reviewer ID, override reason, outcome. Stop if audited quality drops by >5 percentage points, if safety-critical misses occur, if unflagged SLA breaches exceed baseline by >20%, or if treatment compliance is <70%. This pilot identifies whether occurrence mediation explains the flagged cycle-time penalty while preserving a safety valve.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk: if cases with similar observed risk score and complexity but different flags still show large cycle-time differences, or if an unobserved risk proxy explains most of the flagged delay, then case mix and hidden risk, not flag routing, may be the main driver.
2. Queue interference: if a cluster-randomized test shows that flagged priority or mandatory review substantially increases unflagged manual or escalation waits, then the apparent flagged benefit is a negative externality and capacity expansion, not routing alone, is required.
3. Decision quality: if removing automatic mandatory review increases false approvals, safety-critical misses, or compliance violations, then P2 is not acceptable and the recommendation shifts to P3 or P4.
4. Timestamp semantics: if stage start timestamps are not actual work starts, or local clock skew makes waits and services unreliable, then the stage decomposition reverses and the observed triage acceleration or downstream delay may be an artifact.
5. Positivity or lack of overlap: if there are no unflagged cases with similar risk, complexity, and load, or no flagged cases without review, then the relevant mediation or policy contrasts cannot be estimated and the mechanism interpretation becomes speculative.
6. Reviewer behavior change: if reviewers respond to the flag by spending more time in manual review, escalating more, or changing decision thresholds, then the flag has a direct behavioral effect on service and escalation, not only a routing effect.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases start and complete triage faster, are reviewed almost universally, wait longer in manual review, escalate more often, and have longer total cycle times. Unflagged cases wait longer in triage, are less often reviewed, and close sooner on average. High complexity and high arrival load are associated with longer downstream times. Plausible mechanism: the flag is a policy bundle that buys triage speed but triggers mandatory manual review and escalation, placing cases into shared queues where waiting and service times dominate total cycle. Case mix and human judgment also contribute. Unidentified: the causal share of the flag versus underlying risk or complexity, the degree to which manual-review occurrence versus queue waiting drives delay, spillover effects on unflagged cases, and decision-quality consequences. The data do not identify natural direct or indirect effects because of interference and unobserved risk. Most useful next evidence: a limited randomized P2 pilot that retains priority triage but removes automatic mandatory review for a bounded flagged cohort, with queue guardrails and quality audits. It would test whether occurrence mediation explains the cycle-time penalty and whether quality remains acceptable.
