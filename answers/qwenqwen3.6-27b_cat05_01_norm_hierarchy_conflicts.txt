## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

1. **P-501  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** *Premise change:* B4’s “actively delegated Shift Lead” is interpreted as implied delegation during a documented ERP outage and imminent line stoppage, or ECD-2’s 09:00 start is treated as operationally retroactive to 08:30 (outage start). *Credibility:* Weak. B4 explicitly requires active delegation; ECD-2 has a hard start time. Implied delegation contradicts A5’s prohibition on silently weakening controls.
2. **P-502  COMPLIANT:** *Premise change:* ECD-2’s silence on Compliance clearance (C5) is read as an implicit waiver under C4’s “ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements,” and D1’s “operational approvals” is expanded to include Compliance for new suppliers during ECD-2. *Credibility:* Weak. GPIP-4 A3 explicitly mandates pre-commitment Compliance for new suppliers. D3/D4 explicitly preserve integrity controls. Implicit waivers are disfavored in compliance frameworks.
3. **P-503  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** *Premise change:* “Critical sterilization-line lots” (C6) is interpreted per Operations’ view to include backup-line components, and emergency necessity doctrine functionally satisfies GPIP-4 A2/A3 when IC authorizes. *Credibility:* Medium-Weak. The 3-day stock buffer negates imminent necessity. A1 segregation violation is structural, not timing-related, and cannot be waived by necessity without CRO exception.
4. **P-505  NONCOMPLIANT:** *Premise change:* ERP timestamp (18:06) is treated as conclusive despite the clock note, placing commitment post-ECD-2 expiry. This triggers standard GPIP-4 A2 (Finance pre-commit), which was missed. *Credibility:* Medium. ON-44 explicitly warns against treating ERP timestamps as conclusive during recovery. However, if the organization strictly enforces ERP-ledger timing for audit purposes, the classification flips. Supplier ack and clock note would need to be formally rejected.
5. **P-506  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** *Premise change:* Organizational custom (noted in incomplete facts) accepts verbal IC extensions as valid temporary overlays, overriding the written record requirement. *Credibility:* Weak. A6 and controlled document management standards require written records for exceptions. Verbal claims without corroboration fail the burden of proof.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Issue Typology:**
- *Policy Breach:* P-501 (unauthorized committer), P-502 (post-commit Compliance), P-503 (segregation/thresholds), P-504 (scope/supplier), P-506 (expired directive/Plant timing).
- *Logging Defect / Late Evidence:* P-505 (ERP post-expiry timestamp), P-507 (ERP auth lag). Both resolved by contemporaneous records.
- *Inability to Prove Compliance:* P-506 (verbal extension claim lacks record).

**Case-Level Remediation:**
- **P-501:** Retrain Shift Leads on B4 delegation protocol. Require written delegation template for emergency coverage. Monitor shift-lead commitments for 90 days.
- **P-502:** Conduct retroactive Compliance review for the new supplier. Document exception request acknowledging A3 timing gap. Implement automated Compliance hold for new suppliers in ERP.
- **P-503:** Initiate disciplinary review for segregation violation. Redesign backup stock procurement to separate request/commit/authorization roles. Audit all backup-line purchases.
- **P-504:** Terminate unapproved supplier engagement; reallocate cost. Clarify emergency scope boundaries in comms. Flag non-production emergency claims for Finance review.
- **P-505 / P-507:** Accept with evidential adjustment notes. Update ERP recovery procedures to auto-tag outage-window entries for manual timestamp verification.
- **P-506:** Enforce hard system expiry for ECD-2. Require written IC extension for any post-window emergency. Monitor post-directive purchases for 30 days.

**Monitoring Actions:**
- Implement real-time segregation checks in ERP (block commit if requester = committer = approver).
- Add mandatory Compliance clearance gate for new suppliers, bypassable only with CRO or explicit ECD-2 clause.
- Deploy directive expiry hard-stops in procurement workflow.
- Require contemporaneous evidence upload (recording/PDF) for all emergency commitments during outages.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Emergency Procurement Exception Framework (EPEF) v2.0**

1. **Hierarchy & Authority:** GPIP-4 remains the baseline. Incident directives may override timing and supplier-onboarding rules only within explicitly defined scopes and time windows. Formal exceptions to segregation of duties (A1), Finance thresholds (A2), or Compliance clearance (A3) require written CRO approval. COO/Incident Commander directives do not satisfy A7.
2. **Scope Definition:** “Critical sterilization-line lots” means components required to prevent production stoppage or safety harm within 12 hours. Backup-line components require separate IC justification and CRO notification.
3. **Threshold Retention:** Emergency status waives competitive tendering and PO timing. It does not waive pre-commitment Finance approval (€50k), Compliance clearance (>€100k or new supplier), or segregation of duties. IC joint authorization may substitute Finance approval only up to €150k if documented within 4 hours; amounts >€150k require Legal clearance plus Finance Controller acknowledgment.
4. **Retrospective Records:** All emergency commitments must be recorded in ERP within 24 hours. Contemporaneous evidence (recordings, signed PDFs, supplier acks) supersedes ERP timestamps during system outages. Post-commitment approvals mitigate risk but do not cure pre-commitment gaps.
5. **Expiry & Normalization Safeguard:** Directives expire at stated time. Verbal extensions are invalid. Any emergency procurement exceeding 72 hours or €500k cumulative requires automatic CRO review and transition to standard workflow. Post-incident audit must verify scope adherence and trigger process normalization within 5 business days.
