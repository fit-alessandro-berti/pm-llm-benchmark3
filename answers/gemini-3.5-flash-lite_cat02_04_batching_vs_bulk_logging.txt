# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

## Cluster G1 (`RBT-7` - Genuine Simultaneous Batch)
* **Waiting Time:** Underestimated if measured from queue arrival (`08:54`) to start (`09:00`) for cases L02–L04, because L02–L04 arrive earlier/later but wait for the batch fixture slot.
* **Service Time:** Correctly estimated as 3 minutes (`09:00` to `09:03`), reflecting physical processing duration.
* **Resource Utilization:** Understated if calculated per item without accounting for multi-item capacity (fixture holds 4 items simultaneously).
* **Bottleneck Detection:** May mask true capacity; RBT-7 processes 4 units per cycle, effectively multiplying throughput.

## Cluster G2 (`T-B` - Start Batching / Common Setup)
* **Waiting Time:** Inflated for early arrivals (`S01` arrived at `09:42`, started at `10:00`) due to batch setup queuing.
* **Service Time:** Artificially inflated for earlier cases if measured from common start (`10:00`) to individual completion (`S01` completes at `10:08` = 8 min service; `S04` completes at `10:27` = 27 min service, reflecting serial processing disguised under a group start).
* **Resource Utilization:** Distorted; appears continuously busy during long completion spreads.
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Misidentifies work center as an exceptionally slow serial processor rather than a batched setup with sequential execution.

## Cluster G3 (`T-C` - Concurrent Work / Multitasking)
* **Waiting Time:** Varies; queue arrivals span `10:40` to `11:05` while starts range from `11:02` to `11:24`.
* **Service Time:** Severely distorted; all cases share completion `11:30`, making early cases (`E01` duration 28 min) look much longer than late cases (`E01` vs `E04` duration 6 min), despite overlapping actual review work.
* **Resource Utilization:** Overestimated for individual cases; looks like sequential single-tasking when work is actually concurrent/overlapping.
* **Bottleneck Detection:** False positives for downstream review delays due to bulk form submission timing.

## Cluster G4 (`T-D` - Sequential Batching)
* **Waiting Time:** Low, as items are picked up immediately upon prior completion (zero idle gap between P01 completion `12:09` and P02 start `12:09`).
* **Service Time:** Consistent 4-minute per item service time (`12:05–12:09`, `12:09–12:13`, etc.).
* **Resource Utilization:** 100% active utilization across the 20-minute span (`12:05` to `12:25`).
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Appears as a steady, perfectly paced pipeline; misdiagnosed as parallel concurrency if chained start/completes are misinterpreted.

## Cluster G5 (`analyst_pool` - Shared-Account Artifact)
* **Waiting Time:** Appears normal or slightly queued based on queue arrival vs start.
* **Service Time:** Distorted by overlapping intervals under a single pooled resource ID (`analyst_pool`), masking individual task durations.
* **Resource Utilization:** Mathematically impossible utilization metrics (>100% concurrent active time for a single resource if aggregated naively).
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Falsely flags `analyst_pool` as an extreme bottleneck due to overlapped work being attributed to a single logical worker.

## Cluster G6 (`T-F` - Bulk End Submission & Queue Accumulation)
* **Waiting Time:** **Extremely Inflated.** Queue arrivals span `14:05` to `16:02`, but work does not start until `17:00`. Wait times appear to be 1 to 3 hours.
* **Service Time:** **Near-Zero (0 minutes).** Start (`17:00`) and complete (`17:00`) are identical. Service time collapses to zero.
* **Resource Utilization:** Appears completely idle until a sudden 1-minute spike at `17:00` where all 7 tickets are closed instantaneously.
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Complete failure of standard process discovery; portrays the resource as infinitely fast (0 min service) yet catastrophically backlogged (huge wait times), reflecting administrative batching rather than true operational capacity limits.

## Cluster G7 (`T-G` - Timestamp Coarsening)
* **Waiting Time:** Quantization error causes false waiting times (e.g., M05 arriving at `14:11` shows start at `14:00`, creating a negative wait time anomaly if uncorrected).
* **Service Time:** Artificially forced into uniform 15-minute blocks (`14:00` to `14:15`) for all items regardless of actual work duration.
* **Resource Utilization:** Overestimated block utilization.
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Completely obscures variance in task durations; makes processing times appear uniformly quantized.

## Cluster G8 (`T-H` - Queue Accumulation After Capacity Loss)
* **Waiting Time:** High initial wait due to safety check blocking work until `15:35` (queue arrivals at `15:00` wait until `15:38` for Q01).
* **Service Time:** Consistent 7-minute service windows (`15:38–15:45`, `15:45–15:52`, etc.) executed serially.
* **Resource Utilization:** Burst utilization following downtime recovery.
* **Bottleneck Detection:** Correctly attributable to upstream downtime (safety check), but would be misdiagnosed as internal resource slowness without safety-check correlation.

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### Assumptions and Sensitivity Analysis
1. **Resource ID Reliability (`analyst_pool` vs. Individual Badges):** 
   - *Trusted Resource IDs:* If `analyst_pool` is treated as a single physical agent, utilization metrics break down (overlapping tasks compute to >100% capacity) and multitasking appears as serial bottlenecking.
   - *Shared-Account Correction:* Treating `analyst_pool` as pooled up to 3 concurrent analysts resolves the overlapping interval paradox, distributing cases across parallel worker threads.
2. **Timestamp Precision & Censoring:**
   - *Exact Timestamps:* Treated as precise operational moments in G1, G4, and G8.
   - *Interval-Censored Timestamps:* In G7 (`legacy_MES`), 15-minute precision masks sub-minute task variation. If uncorrected, performance models assume uniform 15-minute work chunks, miscalculating queueing delay and true resource speed.

### Ambiguity in Temporal Patterns (Multiple Physical Explanations)
- **Identical Start Timestamps (G1 vs. G2 vs. G6):**
  - *G1 (`RBT-7`):* Identical starts and completions point to a genuine multi-item fixture batch.
  - *G2 (`T-B`):* Identical starts with staggered completions point to common setup / start batching followed by sequential execution.
  - *G6 (`T-F`):* Identical starts and completions at `17:00` with staggered historical arrivals point to administrative bulk end-of-shift data entry.
- **Chained Start-Complete Transitions (G4):**
  - *G4 (`T-D`):* Consecutive items where `complete[i] == start[i+1]` can indicate either a genuine conveyor/pipeline flow (sequential batching) or a worker rapidly logging items one after another in a continuous stream.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

To eliminate ambiguity in future process mining analyses, the data collection architecture requires the following enhancements:

1. **Execution Timestamps:** Capture distinct micro-events (`item_loaded`, `processing_started`, `processing_completed`, `item_unloaded`) rather than relying solely on coarse aggregate lifecycle markers.
2. **Recording Timestamps:** Maintain strict separation between `source_timestamp` (edge device clock) and `database_inserted_at` (server ingestion time) to audit network lag and bulk-entry anomalies.
3. **Source Clocks & NTP Synchronization:** Enforce Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronization across all PLCs, tablets, scanners, and legacy systems to prevent clock skew between subsystems (e.g., safety-check clocks vs. repair queues in G8).
4. **Resource & Badge Identity:** Eliminate shared credentials (e.g., `analyst_pool`, shared VDIs). Mandate individual RFID badge taps or unique user authentication for every task initiation and completion.
5. **Group & Batch Identifiers:** Introduce explicit `batch_id` or `fixture_id` foreign keys in event logs whenever multiple items are processed together on multi-capacity equipment (e.g., `RBT-7`).
6. **Workstation Capacity Metadata:** Store static or dynamic capacity attributes (e.g., max fixture capacity = 4) in the event stream or process model metadata to correctly interpret multi-item concurrency.
7. **Pause / Resume Events:** Log explicit `suspended` and `resumed` states during unexpected interruptions (such as safety checks or equipment jams) rather than letting unrecorded downtime inflate queue waiting times.
8. **Timestamp Precision Metadata:** Standardize precision logging (e.g., ISO-8601 with fractional seconds) and flag coarse-grained systems (like legacy MES 15-minute rounding) at the ETL layer.
