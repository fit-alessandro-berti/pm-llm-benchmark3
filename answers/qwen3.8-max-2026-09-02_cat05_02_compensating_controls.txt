## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 should not be reduced to “Prepare_Payment and Release_Payment were performed by different user IDs.” The underlying control objective is to prevent one human actor from unilaterally causing an erroneous, unauthorized, fraudulent, or unrecoverable payment. The following decomposition separates the main threat scenarios and shows what AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 can and cannot do.

| # | Control objective / threat scenario | AV-2 — Automated validation | MG-4 — Independent manager approval | RA-7 — Retrospective audit and recall | ED-1 — Emergency single-operator permission |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unilateral initiation and authorization**: one person prepares and releases an erroneous or fraudulent payment. | Can prevent or flag rule-based anomalies, but does not replace independent human authorization. A warning that can be overridden is weak prevention. | Preventive if independent and performed before release or before irrevocable settlement. Strongest when the manager can stop the payment. | Detective. Becomes partly corrective only if the payment can still be stopped, recalled, or recovered. | Does not remove the four-eyes objective. It permits a temporary exception only if all ED-1 conditions are satisfied. |
| 2 | **Vendor or bank-account redirection / sanctions exposure**: payment is sent to a changed, fraudulent, sanctioned, or otherwise invalid account. | Directly relevant: bank-account ownership, recent bank change, sanctions, duplicate invoice, and limit checks. Still limited if overridden or unavailable. | Preventive if manager independently verifies bank details and supporting documents before release/settlement. | Detective/corrective only if bank change or fraud is discovered before the recall window closes. | ED-1 explicitly excludes recent bank changes because this threat is heightened in emergency single-operator mode. |
| 3 | **Duplicate, mismatched, or commercially invalid invoice**: payment is made twice, without PO match, or for an invalid amount. | Preventive/detective for duplicate and PO/amount anomalies, but only for checks actually performed. | Preventive if manager reviews invoice, PO, receipt/service evidence before release/settlement. | Can detect after payment and support recovery if timely. | Requires AV-2 full pass without override; otherwise ED-1 is not valid. |
| 4 | **Goods/services not received / lack of commercial substance**: payment is technically valid-looking but unsupported by actual receipt or business purpose. | Explicitly does **not** establish receipt or commercial reasonableness. | Strongest compensating control if the manager reviews receipt/service evidence and does not rubber-stamp. | Can detect fabricated or missing evidence later, but recovery may be uncertain. | ED-1 does not itself validate receipt; it relies on MG-4 and RA-7. |
| 5 | **Collusion or coordinated circumvention**: two users, or a user and supervisor, defeat nominal separation. | Limited. Automated checks may not detect collusion, shared credentials, or coordinated false documentation. | Helpful only if the manager is truly independent, not conflicted, and performs substantive review. | Can detect repeated pairing, shared workstations, unusual override patterns, and late approvals. | Not inherently collusion-resistant. Emergency exceptions can increase collusion risk if monitoring is weak. |
| 6 | **Irrecoverability after bank settlement**: payment becomes irrevocable before defect detection. | Preventive only before release. Once settlement occurs, AV-2 cannot recover funds. | Preventive if performed before settlement; after settlement it is mostly detective ratification. | Corrective only within the practical recall window, usually around 24 hours; later audits are mainly detective. | ED-1 requires timely audit, but audit alone cannot compensate if settlement already occurred and recall is no longer realistic. |

Key point: a nominal four-eyes violation is serious, but the risk meaning depends on whether an independent human could still prevent loss before irrevocable settlement, whether the controls covered the relevant threat, and whether detection occurred while recovery was still possible.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating-control package should be assessed across the following dimensions:

1. **Timing**
   - Strongest: independent prevention before `Release_Payment`.
   - Still potentially preventive: independent approval after release but before irrevocable `Bank_Settlement`, if the approver can actually stop settlement.
   - Weak: approval or audit only after settlement.

2. **Independence**
   - The reviewer must not be the preparer/releaser, must not control a shared credential used by the operator, and must not have an unresolved conflict of interest.
   - A direct supervisor who controls performance ratings or override credentials is weaker, especially where fraud or collusion is plausible.

3. **Coverage**
   - AV-2 covers duplicates, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank changes, limits, and basic PO match.
   - AV-2 does not cover receipt confirmation, commercial reasonableness, collusion, or compromised-account control.
   - MG-4 can cover some AV-2 gaps, but only if the manager reviews the right evidence.

4. **Overrideability**
   - A clean AV-2 pass is meaningful.
   - An AV-2 warning that is overridden by the same operator, especially using another person’s credential, materially weakens the control.
   - An unavailable AV-2 service removes a key preventive layer.

5. **Recoverability**
   - Controls before settlement can prevent loss.
   - Controls within the bank recall window can sometimes correct.
   - Controls after the recall window are mainly detective and do not restore the preventive objective.

6. **Evidence quality**
   - Manager approval should be supported by evidence: invoice, bank details, receipt/service confirmation, emergency justification, and override rationale.
   - Unsigned checklists, missing token logs, missing workstation evidence, and absent receipt documentation reduce assurance.

7. **Collusion resistance**
   - Formal separation of preparer and releaser is insufficient if the same pair repeatedly operates together, shares a workstation, shares credentials, or has a conflicted supervisor.
   - Repeated pairing is not proof of collusion, but it reduces the independence assurance normally provided by four-eyes.

### Sufficiency categories

| Category | Meaning | Typical characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent / OBJECTIVE_MET** | The compensating package credibly achieves the four-eyes control objective despite a nominal exception. | Independent human challenge before release or before irrevocable settlement; AV-2 available and passed without override; emergency conditions satisfied; timely audit; no unresolved conflict or collusion indicator; recoverability preserved or not needed because prevention occurred. |
| **Partially equivalent / OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET** | The package materially reduces risk and may be tolerable, but one or more elements are missing, late, or unresolved. | Independent review before settlement but after release; minor timing defect; low case risk; evidence mostly present but incomplete; technical nonconformance rather than outright control failure. |
| **Merely mitigating** | Controls reduce expected loss but do not restore the control objective. | Post-settlement ratification, late audit, AV-2 pass without receipt validation, formal four-eyes with unresolved collusion indicators. |

### Why “same expected loss” is not enough

Expected loss alone is an inadequate standard because:

- A payment may be legitimate by luck, not by control. AP-208 had a legitimate outcome but catastrophic control gaps.
- Controls must address **tail risk**, not only average loss. A low-probability high-impact fraud can be missed by outcome-based reasoning.
- Control sufficiency includes **deterrence, detection latency, recoverability, accountability, and evidence quality**.
- Collusion and credential misuse can produce low observed loss for a long time before a large loss occurs.
- Governance requires predictable exceptions. If emergency exceptions are tolerated without conditions, the control environment degrades even when no immediate loss occurs.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Primary timing | Preventive role | Detective role | Corrective role | Main limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FEP-1 separation of preparer and releaser** | Before release. | Primary prevention against unilateral payment initiation and authorization. | Limited; it may reveal anomalies only if the second person actively challenges. | None by itself. | Can be defeated by collusion, shared credentials, shared workstations, or rubber-stamp release. |
| **AV-2 automated validation** | Before release. | Blocks or warns on duplicates, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank changes, limits, and PO mismatches. | Detects rule-based anomalies and creates audit evidence. | Can prevent completion by blocking release; cannot recover funds after settlement. | Does not validate receipt or commercial reasonableness; can be overridden; may be unavailable. |
| **MG-4 independent manager approval** | Ideally before release; alternatively before settlement. | Prevents loss if manager can stop payment before release or before irrevocable settlement. | Detects issues in already prepared/released payment if review is substantive. | Can stop pre-settlement; can initiate recall if still within bank window. | Depends on independence, competence, time available, and evidence reviewed. Conflicted or late approval is weak. |
| **RA-7 retrospective audit and recall** | After release/payment. | Generally not preventive, except when audit occurs before settlement or before recall deadline and can stop/recover payment. | Main detective control for emergency exceptions and hidden defects. | Partly corrective only while payment can still be stopped, recalled, or recovered. | Late audit after recall window is mostly detective and does not compensate for missing prevention. |
| **ED-1 emergency single-operator permission** | Exception governance before and around payment. | Does not itself prevent; it permits single-operator action only when the surrounding compensating package is complete. | Requires audit and manager evidence, creating detective assurance. | Relies on RA-7 and bank recall capacity. | Becomes invalid if token, amount, vendor age, bank-change, AV-2, manager, or audit conditions fail. |

### Semantics for manager approval after release but before settlement

I treat a manager action performed **after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement`** as a **hybrid preventive/detective control**, not as purely preventive or purely detective.

- It is **detective relative to the release decision** because the payment has already been released by the operator.
- It is **preventive relative to loss** if the manager still has the authority, information, and time to stop the payment before irrevocable bank settlement.
- If the manager cannot stop settlement, or acts only after settlement, the control should be treated as **detective ratification**, not prevention.

This matters for AP-201 and AP-207: both had manager ratification after release but before settlement. The control can reduce loss risk, but it is weaker than manager approval before release because the operator has already performed both preparation and release.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### Evidence collection recommendations

1. **Emergency exception packet**
   - Emergency token creation timestamp, activation timestamp, expiry, authorizer, and business justification.
   - Outage or incident ticket referencing the emergency.
   - Evidence that token was active before `Prepare_Payment`.

2. **Manager approval evidence**
   - Manager ID, timestamp, and relationship to preparer/releaser.
   - Checklist showing review of invoice, bank details, receipt/service evidence, and emergency justification.
   - Explicit indicator of whether approval occurred before release, after release but before settlement, or after settlement.

3. **AV-2 evidence**
   - Full AV-2 result log: pass, warning, block, or unavailable.
   - Warning reason, override identity, override credential owner, and override justification.
   - Service outage log when AV-2 is unavailable.

4. **Bank and vendor evidence**
   - Vendor age, bank-account change history, sanctions check, and bank-ownership confirmation.
   - Independent confirmation for any bank change within seven days.

5. **Receipt and commercial-substance evidence**
   - Goods receipt, service report, contract, PO, and invoice match.
   - Explicit acknowledgment that AV-2 pass does not prove receipt.

6. **Workstation and session evidence**
   - Workstation ID, IP address, session ID, and concurrent-session indicators for prepare/release/manager actions.
   - Shared-workstation flag where preparer and releaser use the same physical or logical endpoint.

7. **Audit and recall evidence**
   - Audit start and completion timestamps.
   - Recall request timestamp, bank response, and recovery outcome.
   - Whether audit occurred before the practical recall window closed.

### Monitoring rules

| Metric / rule | Suggested alert logic | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-user prepare/release rate** | Any completed payment where preparer = releaser and ED-1 conditions are not fully evidenced. | Alert and exception review. Not proof of fraud, but a nominal FEP-1 violation. |
| **AV-2 override rate** | Any override of bank-change, sanctions, duplicate, or operator-limit warning; especially if override credential belongs to another person. | High-risk alert. Credential sharing or misuse should be investigated. |
| **Late manager approval** | Manager approval after release but before settlement: flag. Manager approval after settlement: stronger flag. | Hybrid or detective control only. Post-settlement approval should not be counted as prevention. |
| **Audit before recall window** | Emergency payments should have audit started/completed within one business day; ideally within 24 hours where recall is possible. | Missed timely audit reduces corrective capacity and weakens ED-1. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Pair appears together above an organizational threshold, e.g. more than 10 payments in 30 days or more than 20% of one user’s payments. Illustrative thresholds only. | Risk signal for reduced independence or normalized deviance. Not proof of collusion. |
| **Shared workstation / same endpoint** | Preparer and releaser use same workstation, same session, or overlapping credentials. | Alert. Requires investigation; may indicate weak separation or operational convenience. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Vendor bank account changed within seven days before payment. | Require independent verification and prohibit single-operator emergency release unless ED-1 conditions are fully met. |
| **Control outages** | AV-2 unavailable or degraded during payment release. | High-risk event. Payments should be blocked or require enhanced dual control and manual bank verification. |
| **Emergency token timing** | Token created or activated after `Prepare_Payment`. | ED-1 precondition failure unless logging delay is evidenced. Requires root-cause analysis. |
| **Post-payment fabricated documentation** | Audit finds false receipt, false service report, or missing commercial substance. | Escalate as potential fraud/control failure, regardless of nominal four-eyes compliance. |

### Alert versus proof

An alert is a **risk signal**. It justifies investigation, evidence preservation, and possibly temporary access restrictions. It is not, by itself, proof of misconduct. Proof requires corroborated evidence: credential logs, document forensics, vendor relationships, bank confirmations, interviews, and loss/recovery records.

```python
        audit_resource=("org:resource", "first"),
    )
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case"})
)

settlement = (
    df.loc[df["concept:name"] == "Bank_Settlement"]
    .groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(settlement_time=("time:timestamp", "min"))
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case"})
)

override = (
    df.loc[df["concept:name"] == "Override_Warning"]
    .groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(
        override_time=("time:timestamp", "min"),
        override_resource=("org:resource", "first"),
    )
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case"})
)

av_mask = df["concept:name"].str.contains(
    "Automated_Validate|Validation_Service",
    case=False,
    regex=True,
)
av = (
    df.loc[av_mask]
    .groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(
        av_event=("concept:name", "first"),
        av_time=("time:timestamp", "min"),
    )
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case"})
)

for frame in [manager, audit, settlement, override, av]:
    summary = summary.merge(frame, on="case", how="left")

summary["completed_release"] = summary["release_time"].notna()
summary["same_user_prepare_release"] = summary["same_user_prepare_release"].fillna(False)

summary["pair"] = summary.apply(
    lambda r: f"{r['prepare_resource']}->{r['release_resource']}"
    if pd.notna(r.get("prepare_resource")) and pd.notna(r.get("release_resource"))
    else None,
    axis=1,
)

pair_counts = completed_renamed.copy()
pair_counts["pair"] = (
    pair_counts["prepare_resource"].astype(str)
    + "->"
    + pair_counts["release_resource"].astype(str)
)
pair_counts = (
    pair_counts.groupby("pair")["case"]
    .nunique()
    .reset_index(name="pair_case_count")
)

summary = summary.merge(pair_counts, on="pair", how="left")
summary["repeated_pair"] = summary["pair_case_count"].fillna(0) > 1

summary["audit_hours_after_release"] = (
    summary["audit_time"] - summary["release_time"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

summary["audit_within_24h"] = summary["audit_hours_after_release"].between(0, 24)
summary["audit_within_24h"] = summary["audit_within_24h"].fillna(False)

# Conservative approximation: one business day is treated as 24 hours.
summary["audit_within_1_business_day_approx"] = summary["audit_within_24h"]

def derive_manager_timing(row):
    if pd.isna(row.get("manager_time")):
        return "none"
    if pd.notna(row.get("release_time")) and row["manager_time"] <= row["release_time"]:
        return "before_release"
    if pd.notna(row.get("settlement_time")):
        if row["manager_time"] <= row["settlement_time"]:
            return "after_release_before_settlement"
        return "after_settlement"
    return row.get("manager_timing_fact", "unknown")

summary["manager_timing_derived"] = summary.apply(derive_manager_timing, axis=1)
summary["manager_before_settlement_flag"] = summary["manager_timing_derived"].isin(
    ["before_release", "after_release_before_settlement"]
)

summary["av_full_pass_no_override"] = (
    summary["av_available"] & summary["av_full_pass"] & (~summary["av_override"])
)
summary["av_coverage_flag"] = summary["av_full_pass_no_override"]

summary["emergency_eligibility_ED1_all_conditions"] = (
    summary["completed_release"]
    & summary["emergency_token_before_prepare"]
    & (summary["amount_eur"] < 100000)
    & (summary["vendor_active_days"] >= 180)
    & (~summary["bank_change_7d"])
    & summary["av_full_pass_no_override"]
    & summary["manager_before_settlement_flag"]
    & summary["audit_within_1_business_day_approx"]
)

print("PM4Py nominal violating completed-payment cases:",
      pm4py_violations if PM4PY_FILTER_OK else "unavailable")
print("Pandas deterministic same-user completed-release cases:", fallback_violations)

if PM4PY_FILTER_OK and set(pm4py_violations) != set(fallback_violations):
    print(
        "WARNING: PM4Py four-eyes result differs from pandas cross-check; "
        "inspect PM4Py version semantics."
    )

print("Attempted/blocked releases without completed Release_Payment:", blocked_cases)
print("Blocked attempted releases with same prepare/attempt resource:", sorted(blocked_same_user))

out_cols = [
    "case",
    "amount_eur",
    "completed_release",
    "same_user_prepare_release",
    "av_coverage_flag",
    "manager_timing_derived",
    "audit_within_24h",
    "emergency_eligibility_ED1_all_conditions",
    "bank_change_7d",
    "repeated_pair",
    "shared_workstation",
]

print("\nPreliminary case evidence flags:")
print(summary[out_cols].to_string(index=False))
```

### Notes on PM4Py behavior

I cannot guarantee identical PM4Py behavior across versions:

- `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` may differ in whether it returns an event log, a dataframe, or a filtered set of cases.
- It may handle cases without a completed `Release_Payment` differently.
- It may or may not treat `SYSTEM` as a resource in a way that affects four-eyes calculations, although in this log `SYSTEM` does not perform `Prepare_Payment` or `Release_Payment`.
- The function may have version-specific expectations about column names after `pm4py.format_dataframe`.

For that reason, the code includes a deterministic pandas cross-check and separately identifies blocked attempts such as AP-205. The generated flags are preliminary evidence inputs, not final compliance verdicts.
