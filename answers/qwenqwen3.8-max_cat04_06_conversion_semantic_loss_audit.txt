def fire_transition(net, marking, trans):
    try:
       new_marking = copy.deepcopy(marking)
    except Exception:
       new_marking = dict(marking)
    in_arcs, out_arcs = get_in_out_arcs(net, trans)
    for arc in in_arcs:
       place = getattr(arc, "source", None)
       if place is None: continue
       w = arc_weight(arc)
       current = new_marking.get(place, 0)
       if current < w: return None
       if current - w <= 0:
           try: del new_marking[place]
           except Exception: new_marking[place] = 0
       else:

       w = arc_weight(arc)

# 1. Source-Semantics Baseline

Abbreviations used in witnesses:

- `R` = Register Change  
- `A` = Assess Risk  
- `P` = Prepare Evidence  
- `L` = Legal Review  
- `` = silent skip of Legal Review  
- `C` = Configure Trial  
- `I` = Inspect Trial  
- `D` = Correct Defect  
- `Rel` = Release Change  
- `Arch` = Archive Change  

The witnesses are visible linearizations of the partial-order semantics. Because the source is concurrent, a positive witness is one valid linearization, not the only one.

| Invariant ID | Invariant | Positive witness | Negative witness | Observable after hiding tau? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | Register Change occurs first. | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `A,R,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | Yes |
| I2 | Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence are unordered after Register. | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` and `R,P,A,C,I,Rel,Arch` | A converter that accepts only one of those orders as if the other were forbidden. | Yes |
| I3 | Verification cannot start until both Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence are complete. | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,A,C,I,Rel,Arch` | Yes |
| I4 | Legal Review is optional and may occur only after Assess Risk. | With Legal: `R,A,L,P,C,I,Rel,Arch`; without Legal: `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,L,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | Yes, as presence or absence of `L`; the skip itself is hidden. |
| I5 | Optional Legal Review and verification are unordered and may overlap at model level. | `R,A,P,C,L,I,Rel,Arch` | Rejecting `C,L,I` or forcing `L` always before `C` or always after `I`. | Yes |
| I6 | Every verification iteration executes Configure Trial before Inspect Trial. | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,A,P,I,C,Rel,Arch` | Yes |
| I7 | A repeated verification iteration is separated by Correct Defect. | `R,A,P,C,I,D,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,A,P,C,I,C,I,Rel,Arch` | Yes |
| I8 | At least one verification iteration occurs. | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,A,P,Rel,Arch` | Yes |
| I9 | Release Change waits for both the optional-legal branch and the verification loop. | `R,A,L,P,C,I,Rel,Arch` | `R,A,P,C,I,Rel,L,Arch` | Partially: visible when `L` occurs; hidden when the branch is skipped by ``. |

# 4. Preservation and Loss Audit

The following audit is conservative. Successful object construction is not treated as proof of semantic fidelity. Items marked `expected preserved` should be upgraded to `validated preserved` only after the round-trip validation code in Section 5 runs cleanly for the relevant invariant.

| Source invariant | POWLPetri | PetriBPMN | PetriTree | BPMN round trip | Evidence or uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Register Change occurs first. | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Positive and negative witness traces in Section 5 check that no visible activity precedes Register Change. |
| Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence are unordered after Register. | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Petri net can represent unordered branches. BPMN layout or gateway insertion may suggest a false order. Bounded trace exploration includes both `R,A,P` and `R,P,A`. |
| Verification cannot start before both Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence. | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Negative witness `verification_before_prepare` should be rejected. |

| Configure Trial precedes Inspect Trial in every iteration. | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Negative witness `inspect_before_configure` should be rejected. |

Additional audit discussion:

- **True concurrency versus arbitrary interleaving.**  
  The POWL source expresses partial-order concurrency, not merely one chosen interleaving. The accepting Petri net is the best low-level representation for preserving this because tokens can represent simultaneously enabled branches. However, the validation code in Section 5 observes behavior through linearized visible traces. That validates the visible language up to a bound, but it does not prove causal concurrency preservation.

- **Lack of an edge between Optional Legal Review and verification.**  
  This is one of the most fragile semantic properties. The source intentionally does not order `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`. Legal Review may occur before verification, during verification, or after verification, provided it occurs after Assess Risk and before Release. A converter that imposes a block-structured parallel split/join may accidentally force Legal Review to complete before verification starts, or vice versa. The positive witness `Register, Assess, Prepare, Configure, Legal, Inspect, Release, Archive` is designed to detect that loss.

- **Silent optional behavior.**  
  The XOR containing `Legal Review` and `SilentTransition` means the legal branch is optional in the visible projection. After hiding tau, the only observable difference is whether `Legal Review` appears. A BPMN diagram may show an unlabeled flow, an unnamed task, or a gateway path. Stakeholders can easily misread this as a modeling error unless the silent path is explicitly annotated.

- **Binary-loop semantics.**  
  The POWL `LOOP` operator has two children: the body and the redo part. The intended semantics is: execute the body once, then optionally repeat by executing the redo child and then the body again. Therefore the loop has at least one iteration, and `Correct Defect` separates iterations. A process-tree rediscovery step may misclassify this as a generic loop, a sequence, or a fall-through construct if the surrounding partial order is not block-structured.

- **Join before Release Change.**  
  Release Change is a synchronization point for both the optional legal branch and the verification loop. In Petri net terms, this should be represented by a place/transition join requiring tokens from both branches. In BPMN, this may appear as a parallel or inclusive join. If the silent skip path is drawn poorly, the join may look like an optional or incomplete synchronization.

- **Activity-label uniqueness.**  
  The source uses unique visible labels. This is important for trace-based validation. If BPMN conversion duplicates labels, introduces unnamed visible tasks, or renames activities, positive/negative witness checks may no longer correspond to the source invariant table.

- **Initial and final markings.**  
  A BPMN model does not expose the accepting Petri net markings in a stakeholder-friendly way. Therefore, round-trip validation must check that each generated net has non-null initial and final markings, is sound or at least has a reachable exact final marking for completed traces, and does not enable visible behavior after the final marking.

- **Gateway or routing-node ambiguity in BPMN visualization.**  
  BPMN conversion from a Petri net may introduce gateways that are structural artifacts of the conversion. These gateways may not correspond to decisions in the source POWL. A parallel gateway may be read as a strict split into independent branches; an exclusive gateway may be read as a business choice; an unnamed routing node may be mistaken for a missing activity. Visual acceptability is not semantic acceptability.

def place_key(place):
    name = getattr(place, "name", None)

def marking_to_tuple(marking):
    """
    Normalize a marking for equality checks inside one net.
    Zero-count places are removed.
    """
    if marking is None:
        return ()

    try:
        items = marking.items()
    except AttributeError:
        items = []

    return tuple(
        sorted(
            (place_key(place), int(count))
            for place, count in items
            if count is not None and count > 0
        )
    )


def copy_marking(marking):
    try:
        return Marking(marking)
    except Exception:
        return copy.deepcopy(marking)


def get_in_out_arcs(net, trans):
    """
    PM4Py Petri net transitions usually expose in_arcs/out_arcs.
    The fallback scans net.arcs defensively.
    """
    if hasattr(trans, "in_arcs") and hasattr(trans, "out_arcs"):
        return list(trans.in_arcs or []), list(trans.out_arcs or [])

    in_arcs = []
    out_arcs = []

    for arc in getattr(net, "arcs", []):
        if getattr(arc, "target", None) == trans:
            in_arcs.append(arc)
        elif getattr(arc, "source", None) == trans:
            out_arcs.append(arc)

    return in_arcs, out_arcs

        return int(weight) if weight is not None else 1
    except Exception:
        return 1

    transition is not fireable under the local marking copy.

    in_arcs, out_arcs = get_in_out_arcs(net, trans)

        place = getattr(arc, "source", None)

            continue

        weight = arc_weight(arc)
        current = new_marking.get(place, 0)

        if current < weight:
            return None

        remaining = current - weight
        if remaining <= 0:
            try:
                del new_marking[place]
            except Exception:
                new_marking[place] = 0
        else:
            new_marking[place] = remaining

    for arc in out_arcs:
        place = getattr(arc, "target", None)
        if place is None:
            continue

        weight = arc_weight(arc)
        new_marking[place] = new_marking.get(place, 0) + weight

    return new_marking


def norm_label(trans):
    """
    Hide silent transitions. PM4Py silent transitions usually have label None.
    We also hide empty labels and common tau-like labels.
    """
    label = getattr(trans, "label", None)

    if label is None:
        return None

    label = str(label).strip()

    if label == "" or label.lower() in {"tau", "silent", "none"}:
        return None

    return label


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Bounded visible-trace exploration
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def bounded_completed_visible_traces(
    net,
    im,
    fm,
    visible_bound=VISIBLE_BOUND,
    max_total_steps=MAX_TOTAL_STEPS,
    max_tau_total=MAX_TAU_TOTAL,
    max_consecutive_tau=MAX_CONSECUTIVE_TAU,
    label_max=None,
    default_label_max=DEFAULT_LABEL_MAX,
):
    """
    Explore completed visible traces up to a bound.

    A trace is completed only when the current marking is exactly equal
    to the normalized final marking. Silent transitions are hidden from
    the trace but are explored under tau bounds.
    """
    if label_max is None:
        label_max = LABEL_MAX

    completed = set()

    if net is None or im is None or fm is None:
        return completed

    final_tuple = marking_to_tuple(fm)

    queue = deque()
    queue.append((im, (), 0, 0, 0))  # marking, visible trace, total steps, tau total, consecutive tau

    seen = set()

    while queue:
        marking, trace, total_steps, tau_total, consecutive_tau = queue.popleft()
        marking_tuple = marking_to_tuple(marking)

        if marking_tuple == final_tuple and len(trace) > 0:
            completed.add(trace)

        if len(trace) >= visible_bound:
            continue

        if total_steps >= max_total_steps:
            continue

        try:
            enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
        except Exception:
            enabled = set()

        for trans in enabled:
            label = norm_label(trans)

            if label is None:
                if tau_total + 1 > max_tau_total:
                    continue
                if consecutive_tau + 1 > max_consecutive_tau:
                    continue

                new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, trans)
                if new_marking is None:
                    continue

                new_tuple = marking_to_tuple(new_marking)
                new_tau_total = tau_total + 1
                new_consecutive_tau = consecutive_tau + 1

                state_key = (new_tuple, trace, new_tau_total, new_consecutive_tau)
                if state_key in seen:
                    continue

                seen.add(state_key)
                queue.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        trace,
                        total_steps + 1,
                        new_tau_total,
                        new_consecutive_tau,
                    )
                )

            else:
                if len(trace) + 1 > visible_bound:
                    continue

                counts = Counter(trace)
                if counts[label] + 1 > label_max.get(label, default_label_max):
                    continue

                new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, trans)
                if new_marking is None:
                    continue

                new_trace = trace + (label,)
                new_tuple = marking_to_tuple(new_marking)

                state_key = (new_tuple, new_trace, tau_total, 0)
                if state_key in seen:
                    continue

                seen.add(state_key)
                queue.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        new_trace,
                        total_steps + 1,
                        tau_total,
                        0,
                    )
                )

    return completed


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Trace acceptance with hidden tau
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def accepts_visible_trace(
    net,
    im,
    fm,
    visible_trace,
    max_total_steps=MAX_TOTAL_STEPS,
    max_tau_total=MAX_TAU_TOTAL,
    max_consecutive_tau=MAX_CONSECUTIVE_TAU,
):
    """
    Return True if the exact visible trace can be executed, allowing any
    number of bounded silent transitions before, between, and after visible
    activities. Completion requires exact final-marking equality.
    """
    if net is None or im is None or fm is None:
        return False

    target = tuple(str(x).strip() for x in visible_trace)
    final_tuple = marking_to_tuple(fm)

    queue = deque()
    queue.append((im, 0, 0, 0, 0))  # marking, target index, total steps, tau total, consecutive tau

    seen = set()

    while queue:
        marking, idx, total_steps, tau_total, consecutive_tau = queue.popleft()

        if idx == len(target) and marking_to_tuple(marking) == final_tuple:
            return True

        if total_steps >= max_total_steps:
            continue

        try:
            enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
        except Exception:
            enabled = set()

        for trans in enabled:
            label = norm_label(trans)

            if label is None:
                if tau_total + 1 > max_tau_total:
                    continue
                if consecutive_tau + 1 > max_consecutive_tau:
                    continue

                new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, trans)
                if new_marking is None:
                    continue

                new_tuple = marking_to_tuple(new_marking)
                new_tau_total = tau_total + 1
                new_consecutive_tau = consecutive_tau + 1

                state_key = (new_tuple, idx, new_tau_total, new_consecutive_tau)
                if state_key in seen:
                    continue

                seen.add(state_key)
                queue.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        idx,
                        total_steps + 1,
                        new_tau_total,
                        new_consecutive_tau,
                    )
                )

            else:
                if idx < len(target) and label == target[idx]:
                    new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, trans)
                    if new_marking is None:
                        continue

                    new_tuple = marking_to_tuple(new_marking)
                    new_idx = idx + 1

                    state_key = (new_tuple, new_idx, tau_total, 0)
                    if state_key in seen:
                        continue

                    seen.add(state_key)
                    queue.append(
                        (
                            new_marking,
                            new_idx,
                            total_steps + 1,
                            tau_total,
                            0,
                        )
                    )

    return False

def remove_none_keys(obj):
    """
    Remove None keys from nested footprint structures so that silent
    transitions do not dominate visible footprint comparison.
    """
    if isinstance(obj, dict):
        return {
            key: remove_none_keys(value)
            for key, value in obj.items()
            if key is not None
        }

    if isinstance(obj, list):
        return [remove_none_keys(item) for item in obj]

    if isinstance(obj, tuple):
        return [remove_none_keys(item) for item in obj]

    if isinstance(obj, set):
        return sorted(
            [remove_none_keys(item) for item in obj if item is not None],
            key=str,
        )

    return obj


def footprint_signature(net, im, fm):
    """
    Compute a comparable JSON signature from pm4py.discover_footprints.
    The exact return shape can vary by PM4Py version, so this helper is
    defensive.
    """
    try:
        result = pm4py.discover_footprints(net, im, fm)

        if isinstance(result, tuple) and len(result) > 0:
            footprints = result[0]
        else:
            footprints = result

        cleaned = remove_none_keys(footprints)
        return json.dumps(cleaned, sort_keys=True, default=str)
    except Exception as exc:
        return f"footprint-error:{exc}"


def reachability_info(net, im, fm):
    """
    Attempt to construct a reachability graph. This is used as a diagnostic.
    We do not use raw state counts as semantic equivalence proof because
    converted nets can have different place names and structural encodings.
    """
    try:
        rg = pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(net, im, fm)
        info = {
            "constructed": True,
            "type": type(rg).__name__,
        }

        for attr in ("states", "nodes", "markings"):
            if hasattr(rg, attr):
                value = getattr(rg, attr)
                if callable(value):
                    try:
                        value = value()
                    except Exception:
                        value = None
                try:
                    info["state_count"] = len(value)
                    break
                except Exception:
                    pass

        return info
    except Exception as exc:
        return {"constructed": False, "error": str(exc)}


def soundness_result(net, im, fm):
    try:
        result = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)

        if result is None:
            return "unknown"
        if isinstance(result, tuple):
            return bool(result[0])
        return bool(result)
    except Exception as exc:
        return f"soundness-error:{exc}"


def workflow_net_result(net):
    try:
        result = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
        return bool(result) if result is not None else "unknown"
    except Exception as exc:
        return f"workflow-net-error:{exc}"

# 6. Visual-Semantics Review

A BPMN rendering can be behaviorally acceptable while still being semantically misleading to humans. The main risks are:

- **Parallel gateways can be misread as strict branch independence.**  
  The source has dependencies that are not simple parallel branches. Assess Risk enables both the optional legal branch and verification, but verification also depends on Prepare Evidence. A BPMN parallel split/join drawing may suggest that the branches are fully independent until Release, hiding the fact that verification has an additional precondition.

- **Exclusive gateways can be misread as business decisions.**  
  The XOR for Legal Review versus silent skip is a modeling representation of optionality. In BPMN, an exclusive gateway may look like a deliberate business choice between two visible paths. If the silent path is unnamed, stakeholders may think the diagram is incomplete or that Legal Review is mandatory but accidentally disconnected.

- **Loop visualization can hide the at-least-one semantics.**  
  BPMN loop markers or repeated subprocess shapes can be interpreted as “zero or more” by readers unfamiliar with the exact loop semantics. The source requires at least one verification iteration. The diagram should explicitly state: “At least one Configure Trial / Inspect Trial iteration is required.”

- **Silent routing nodes can be mistaken for missing activities.**  
  The silent skip transition may become an unlabeled BPMN flow, an unnamed task, or a routing artifact. Without annotation, reviewers may ask what activity is missing. The correct interpretation is that the legal branch can complete silently.

- **Graph layout can imply ordering where none exists.**  
  Left-to-right or top-to-bottom placement is not semantic ordering. Legal Review may be drawn above or below verification, making it look like it must occur before or after verification. The source explicitly does not order optional legal review and verification, except that both must finish before Release.

Recommended annotations and documentation:

1. Add a note near the legal XOR:  
   “The unlabeled path is a silent skip. Legal Review is optional.”

2. Add a note near the Release join:  
   “Release Change waits for both the optional legal branch and the verification loop.”

3. Add a note near verification:  
   “At least one iteration is required. Configure Trial precedes Inspect Trial. Correct Defect separates iterations.”

4. Add a note about unordered behavior:  
   “Legal Review and verification may overlap. The diagram layout does not define their relative order.”

5. Use color or line style for silent paths, with a legend.

6. Ship the invariant table from Section 1 with the BPMN diagram.

7. Include positive and negative witness traces in the documentation so reviewers can see allowed and forbidden executions.

# 7. Rejection Criteria

A conversion result should be rejected or quarantined if any of the following holds:

1. **Unsound accepting net.**  
   The round-tripped Petri net fails `pm4py.check_soundness(...)`, deadlocks before Archive Change, or leaves residual tokens after completion.

2. **Missing or null markings.**  
   A converter returns a net without a usable initial or final marking. Even if visualization succeeds, the artifact is not an accepting net and should not be treated as semantically valid.

3. **Release before verification or before completed legal branch.**  
   Any negative witness that allows `Release Change` before verification completion, or before a chosen visible `Legal Review`, is a semantic rejection.

4. **Loss of required interleaving.**  
   If a valid positive witness such as `Register, Assess, Prepare, Configure, Legal, Inspect, Release, Archive` is rejected, the converter has likely collapsed the unordered relationship between Legal Review and verification.

5. **Collapse of optional behavior.**  
   If the no-Legal trace is rejected, the optional legal branch has become mandatory. Conversely, if Legal Review can occur before Assess Risk, optionality has been preserved but precedence has been lost; that is also a rejection.

6. **Failed exact-final-marking test.**  
   A completed visible trace must end in the exact final marking. If a trace reaches a marking that is merely “similar” to the final marking but not equal, the artifact should be quarantined.

7. **Admission of a negative witness.**  
   Any accepted negative witness, such as `Inspect Trial` before `Configure Trial`, repetition without `Correct Defect`, or `Archive Change` before `Release Change`, indicates semantic loss.

8. **Process-tree conversion succeeds but changes behavior.**  
   If `pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(...)` returns a tree, but footprints or bounded trace sets differ from the source Petri net, the tree/BPMN derivative should be rejected or treated as an approximation only.

# 8. Canonical Representation Recommendation

**Authoritative canonical representation for storage:** the source POWL model.

**Stakeholder visualization representation:** generated BPMN.

Recommended governance:

- **Authoritative representation.**  
  The POWL model should be authoritative because it directly expresses the partial-order dependencies, the absence of order between Legal Review and verification, the silent optional branch, and the binary loop structure. BPMN and Petri net artifacts should be treated as derived views.

- **Derivative artifacts.**  
  The following artifacts are derivative:
  - accepting Petri net generated from POWL;
  - BPMN generated from the Petri net;
  - optional process tree, if conversion succeeds;
  - BPMN generated from a process tree;
  - round-trip Petri nets generated from BPMN for validation.

- **Validation evidence required for each derivative.**  
  Every derivative should be accompanied by:
  - converter name/version and input hash;
  - workflow-net check result;
  - soundness check result;
  - footprint comparison against the source-derived Petri net;
  - bounded visible-trace comparison;
  - positive/negative witness results;
  - exact-final-marking acceptance results;
  - known bounds and residual uncertainty statement.

- **Process-tree intermediate.**  
  For this particular source, a process-tree intermediate should not be allowed as an authoritative conversion path. The overlap between optional legal review and verification is not naturally block-structured. If a process-tree conversion succeeds, it should be treated as an optional visualization only after passing the same footprint and witness validations as the BPMN path. If it fails, that failure should be recorded as semantically meaningful, not suppressed.

- **Avoiding round-trip drift.**  
  Future edits should be made only to the authoritative POWL model. The pipeline should regenerate all derivatives from that source. BPMN diagrams should not be manually edited and then re-imported as the source of truth. A CI job should:
  1. load the POWL source;
  2. regenerate the Petri net and BPMN;
  3. rerun the validation suite;
  4. store hashes of source and derivatives;
  5. reject the change if any invariant test fails.

# 9. Residual Uncertainty

Even if every test in Sections 3 and 5 passes, the following claims remain uncertain:

1. **Unbounded loop equivalence is not proven.**  
   The bounded trace exploration checks only a finite number of verification iterations. It cannot prove equivalence for arbitrarily many repetitions of the loop.

2. **Causal concurrency is not fully proven by trace checks.**  
   Visible-trace comparison validates linearized behavior, not the full partial-order/causal structure. Two models can have the same visible traces but differ in true concurrency, conflict, or synchronization structure.

3. **Silent branch commitment cannot be directly observed.**  
   Because the skip transition is hidden, validation can observe that completion without Legal Review is possible, but it cannot directly observe whether the silent branch was truly taken as a branch-completion event rather than being structurally bypassed.

4. **BPMN stakeholder interpretation is not guaranteed.**  
   A BPMN diagram can pass technical validation and still be misread because of layout, gateway placement, unnamed routing nodes, or loop markers. Validation does not prove human interpretability.

5. **PM4Py converter semantics may change across versions.**  
   The audit depends on the observed behavior of PM4Py conversion functions. A future PM4Py version could change Petri-net encoding, BPMN gateway insertion, silent-transition handling, or process-tree conversion behavior. Validation evidence should therefore always be tied to a specific environment and dependency version.
