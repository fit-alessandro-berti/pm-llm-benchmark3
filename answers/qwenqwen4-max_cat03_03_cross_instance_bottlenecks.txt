Derived: `Job–waits_forObject` (never observed; inferred from readiness vs. barrier start).

**Compact edge list** (`[o]` observed relation/event, `[i]` inferred, `[p]` declared policy, `[d]` pipeline-dependent):

```
J1..J6 –requires C1..C6                                   [o]
L8 –contains {C1,C3}; L7 –contains {C2,C5}               [o]
```

**Higher-order motifs (>2 types).**
- **M1 ToolJobBatchMachineShipment:** FIX-A held by J3 (09:00–11:40)  J3 batch-ready 11:55  B1 starts 12:05 on OVEN-1/RACK-7  J1 packed 13:30  S1. A tool hold on one job becomes a barrier for three.
- **M2 LotComponentJobBatch (divergent policy):** L7  {C2,C5}  {J2,J5}  {B1,B2}. One lot spans two batches and two shipments; J2 bypassed it by override, J5 did not.
- **M3 BatchToolBatchMachine:** B1–RACK-7–B2 serializes OVEN-1 through a *non-machine* object; OVEN-1 idles 12:55–14:50 while J4 is ready at 13:10.
- **M4 JobShipmentJob (cross-batch fan-in):** S1 joins J1 (B1) and J4 (B2), exporting B2's timing into B1's downstream.
- **M5 ToolJobMachine (maintenance inside idle):** CAL-3 recalibration 16:35–17:20 sits *inside* TEST-1's idle window with J6 waiting.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are **waiting attributable under a stated counterfactual**, not causal estimates.

**C1–C2. B1 synchronization.** J1: 08:1012:05 = **235 min**; J2: 08:2512:05 = **220 min**; J3: 11:5512:05 = 10 min. Observed rule  "start 10 min after last member ready". *Counterfactual:* same rule, J3 ready at 08:25  excess = (23510)+(22010) = **435 min job-minutes**. *Assumes* readiness timestamps are actual, and OVEN-1/RACK-7 were free before 12:05 (unverified).

**C3. Batch-split counterfactual.** B1a={J1,J2} starting 08:35  waits 25 and 10 min  **420 min saved**; J3 cured separately at 12:05. *Assumes* recipe/quality permits a 2-job cure and a second rack cycle is feasible. Note: this saving may be **absorbed downstream** (C7).

**C4. FIX-A and J4.** Arrival 09:50  `Machine Process` 11:45 = **115 min** pre-process dwell; FIX-A released 11:40, i.e. 5 min changeover. Documented CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 = **42 min** lies entirely inside J3's hold  lower bound 42 min, upper bound 115 min. *Counterfactual:* a second FIX-A. **Critical-path caveat:** J4 ready 13:10, but B2 starts at max(13:10, J5 14:40, rack 14:35)+10 = 14:50, so J4 has ~90 min slack; removing the fixture delay (ready 11:15) changes B2 **not at all**. The 115 min is real waiting with 0 min proven end-to-end impact.

**C5. RACK-7 / B2 conjunctive barrier.** Cure ends 12:55; rack unavailable to 14:35 = **100 min**; J4 waits 13:1014:50 = **100 min**. Rack-only fix  start still 14:50 (**0 min**); lot-only fix (J5 ready 13:10)  14:45 (**5 min**); both  13:20 (**90 min**). Superadditive: constraints are conjunctive, so single-resource fixes are near-worthless.

**C6. CAL-3 and J6.** Ready 16:05  test 17:25 = **80 min**. Recalibration 16:35–17:20 = **45 min**; *counterfactual:* no inter-job recalibration (or spare CAL-3), same 5-min changeover  start 16:40, wait 35 min  **45 min attributable to calibration policy**, 35 min to legitimate queueing behind J5.

**C7. S1 consolidation and J1.** Packed 13:30  dispatch 19:25 = **355 min** post-pack dwell; partner-attributable 13:3019:10 = **340 min**; own overhead 15 min (cf. S2 25, S3 35). *Counterfactual:* solo/split shipment with 25-min overhead  dispatch 13:55, **330 min saved**. J1's documented dwell = 235 + 355 = **590 min**, yet its flattened duration passes a 1.8-SD screen (n=6, variance inflated by outliers — an underpowered test).

**C8. L7 coupling.** Quarantine 10:2013:50 = **210 min** exposure for C2/C5. J2 was cured at 12:05, i.e. **105 min before release** (override); J5 waited, becoming batch-ready 14:40 (50 min after release). Upper bound of lot-induced delay on J5 = 210 min; the causal share depends on J5's would-be readiness — unknown.

**C9. Rack-limited oven ceiling.** Rack cycle  50 min cure + 100 min cleaning = 150 min vs. 50 min oven occupancy  strict serialization caps OVEN-1 at **33%**. Observed 44% exceeds this, so cleaning sometimes overlaps cure or denominators differ — a testable discrepancy, not a settled fact.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job.** Visible: stage order, rework loop (4 vs 2), median stage times. Hidden/duplicated: lot quarantine/release (dropped by source-only extraction), tool identity, batch partners, post-pack dwell if Dispatch is shipment-owned. Misleading: "PackDispatch 1.1 h" while J1 actually dwells 355 min; "no job >1.8 SD" with n=6.
- **Machine.** Visible: utilization, setup/cure/test sequence. Hidden: *why* idle; demand present but blocked. Duplicated: one cure event counted per machine while gating three jobs. Misleading: "OVEN-1 44%, longest gap 73 min" contradicts idle 12:55–14:50 (115 min) and implies spare capacity that is actually rack-limited (C9).
- **Batch.** Visible: membership, cure start, fill rate. Hidden: per-member readiness spread (235/220/10 min) and the tool/lot constraints upstream. Misleading: "B1 cured on time, 3/3 filled" reads as efficiency while generating 435 excess job-minutes.
- **Tool.** Visible: acquire/release, cleaning, recalibration. Hidden/duplicated: holds inferred from intervals; a single RACK-7 cleaning appears as one tool event but blocks a batch, a machine, two jobs and (via J4) shipment S1. Misleading: "CAL-3 recalibration is scheduled maintenance, 45 min" — it is 45 min of J6 waiting.
- **ComponentLot.** Visible: quarantine/release, containment. Hidden: job consequences, and the override that made J2 and J5 diverge. Duplicated: 4 Lot–Job interactions vs 0 in the Job view. Misleading: "lot released in 3.5 h, within SLA" while it sets B2's start.
- **Shipment.** Visible: CreateLoadDispatch (0.8 h + 0.3 h). Hidden: the entire packed-job dwell (340 min for J1). Misleading: "shipments are fast, no bottleneck" — the fastest-looking view carries the largest single documented delay.

**Why low utilization coexists with long waits:** jobs wait *off* machine, at conjunctive barriers (batch fill, lot release, tool exclusivity, shipment partners). Tool maintenance is booked inside machine idle, so idle  no demand. Batching makes arrivals lumpy, so utilization averages hide multi-hour starvation. Utilization also has the wrong denominator: it measures busy time, not blocked demand — J4 was ready at 13:10 while OVEN-1 sat idle.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**N1 — FIX-A  J3  B1/RACK-7/OVEN-1  J1  S1 (Tool, Job, Batch, Machine, Component, Shipment).**
*Temporal precedence:* FIX-A hold 09:00–11:40  J3 ready 11:55  B1 cure 12:05  J1 packed 13:30  S1 dispatch 19:25.
*Resource feasibility:* one RACK-7 and one OVEN-1 serve B1 and B2; one FIX-A serves J3 and J4 (CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47).
*Policy:* batch starts after last member is ready (+10 min); S1 dispatches after last pack (+15 min).
*Causal speculation:* that shortening J3's 160-min fixture hold would advance B1 and cut J1/J2 waiting; the hold may be irreducible cutting time, and any J1 gain may be re-absorbed by S1's 340-min partner wait.

**N2 — L7  C5  J5  B2/RACK-7  J4  S1 (Lot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Machine, Shipment).**
*Precedence:* quarantine 10:20  release 13:50  J5 ready 14:40; rack free 14:35; J4 ready 13:10  B2 14:50  J4 packed 19:10  S1 19:25.
*Feasibility:* rack unavailable 12:55–14:35; oven free from 12:55.
*Policy:* lot disposition gates progress — except J2's supervisor override (12:05 cure, 105 min pre-release).
*Speculation:* that L7 disposition is the true pacemaker of S1; arithmetic (C5) shows J5 is binding, but J5's readiness depends on unrecorded post-release work.

**N3 — CAL-3  J5/J6  TEST-1  S3 (Tool, Job, Machine, Shipment).**
*Precedence:* both ready 16:00/16:05  J5 test 16:10–16:35  recal 16:35–17:20  J6 17:25–17:50  S3 dispatch 35 min after last pack.
*Feasibility:* TEST-1 idle 50 min while J6 waits; CAL-3 is the exclusive object.
*Policy:* every Final Test requires CAL-3; recalibration appears triggered after each test.
*Speculation:* that recalibration is count-based and could be time/usage-based, saving 45 min; alternatively it is a metrology requirement tied to J5's result (e.g. out-of-tolerance check), in which case it is non-negotiable.

```python
import pandas as pd, pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

# ---------- structural views (no waiting inferred here) ----------
inter = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)      # object-type pair frequencies

# ---------- flattening (kept only to expose what it hides) ----------
flat_job = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
pdfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_job)
feat = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")            # lot/tool features may be absent

# ---------- event index built from relations, NOT from machine gaps ----------
ev = ocel.events.set_index("ocel_id")
rel = ocel.relations  # ocel_id, ocel_type, ocel_qualifier
by_event = defaultdict(set)
for r in rel.itertuples():
    by_event[r.ocel_id].add((r.ocel_type, getattr(r, "ocel_object_id", None)))

def ts(eid): return ev.loc[eid, "ocel_timestamp"]

def wait_pairs(barrier_type, ready_act, start_act):
    """Job readiness -> barrier start, joined on the SHARED barrier object."""
    ready, starts = {}, {}
    for eid, objs in by_event.items():
        act = ev.loc[eid, "ocel_activity"]
        ids = {t: o for t, o in objs}
        if act == ready_act and "Job" in ids: ready[ids["Job"]] = ts(eid)
        if act == start_act and barrier_type in ids:
            for t, o in objs:
                if t == "Job": starts.setdefault(o, (ts(eid), ids[barrier_type]))
    rows = []
    for j, (st, barrier) in starts.items():
        if j in ready:
            rows.append(dict(job=j, barrier=barrier,
                             wait_min=(st - ready[j]).total_seconds()/60))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

batch_wait = wait_pairs("Batch", "Job Ready For Batch", "Batch Cure")
ship_wait  = wait_pairs("Shipment", "Pack", "Dispatch")

# ---------- tool-hold overlap: exclusivity, not machine idleness ----------
holds = []
for eid, objs in by_event.items():
    d = {t: o for t, o in objs}
    if "Tool" in d and "Job" in d:
        holds.append((d["Tool"], d["Job"], ev.loc[eid, "ocel_activity"], ts(eid)))
holds = pd.DataFrame(holds, columns=["tool","job","act","t"]).sort_values("t")

# ---------- compare TWO object-centric frequency metrics ----------
raw = inter.groupby(["ocel_source_type","ocel_target_type"]).size()      # metric A: event-relation count
leverage = {}                                                          # metric B: distinct jobs gated per object
for eid, objs in by_event.items():
    jobs = {o for t, o in objs if t == "Job"}
    for t, o in objs:
        if t in ("Batch","Tool","ComponentLot","Shipment"):
            leverage.setdefault((t, o), set()).update(jobs)
lev = pd.Series({k: len(v) for k, v in leverage.items()})
print("A: raw interaction counts\n", raw)
print("B: jobs gated per synchronizing object\n", lev.sort_values(ascending=False))
# Batch-Tool has only 2 interactions but RACK-7 gates 5 jobs -> low frequency != low impact

print(batch_wait, ship_wait)   # explicit, cross-object waiting; machine gaps never used
assert not batch_wait.empty, "readiness events missing -> logging intervention I6 first"
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Would weaken the top-ranked (batch-synchronization) explanation:**
1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 records show unavailability between 08:10 and 12:05  the 235 min is resource contention, not fill policy.
2. A cure recipe or quality rule mandates three jobs / full rack  the "avoidable" 435 min is infeasible.
3. J3's FIX-A hold 09:00–11:40 is verified cutting time with no faster parameter set  lateness is processing, not synchronization.
4. "Ready for batch" timestamps are planned/estimated, not actual  C1–C3 inflated.
5. Replay shows an earlier B1 delays B2/S3 by a comparable amount (rack cycle constraint, C9)  net system gain 0.
6. J1's dwell is conserved: advancing B1 only lengthens its S1 wait (340 min)  shipment policy dominates.
7. Historical split-batch days show no end-to-end lead-time improvement.
8. Reconciliation shows the 73/39-min "longest gap" figures use a different window, dissolving the artifact claim (rank 5 falls).

**Fields/event types that would discriminate:**
1. Explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Wait for Batch` / `Wait for Partner` events carrying a **blocking object ID** and reason code.
2. Tool `acquire`/`release` with *actual processing* sub-intervals (separates holding from working, tests falsifier 3).
3. RACK-7 cleaning start/end, mandatory quarantine duration, and whether overlap with cure is permitted (tests C9).
4. Lot quarantine reason, disposition decision, override authorization (who/when/why) linked to J2 and to any rework.
5. Batch composition policy: minimum fill, maximum wait, recipe compatibility, and per-member readiness semantics (actual vs planned).
6. Shipment consolidation policy: cut-off times, split permissions, customer constraints; plus pack timestamps for J2, J3, J5, J6.
7. CAL-3 recalibration trigger (count-, time-, or result-based) and whether a spare exists.
8. Rework events linked to Job **and** Component/Lot (tests the J2-overriderework conjecture).
9. Event-to-object linkage provenance per pipeline (which relations exist under which extraction rule).
10. Shift/operator calendars and second-unit availability for FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3 (feasibility of I2/I3/I7).
