## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

All factual descriptions, event times, authority statements, and process rules below come from the supplied materials. 

### Facts fixed by the record

1. GPIP-4 is a board-approved policy applicable to every purchase and expressly reserves formal exceptions to a written, scoped, time-limited act of the Chief Risk Officer.
2. DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled local SOP that may add stricter Aachen controls but may not silently weaken GPIP-4.
3. ECD-2 was signed by the COO and Incident Commander, not the Chief Risk Officer. It expressly changes PO timing, supplier-register use, and emergency authorization for a narrowly described class of purchases.
4. The CFO email is not a controlled policy document. The CFO can interpret Finance controls but has no stated authority to waive Compliance or segregation-of-duties controls.
5. The Chief Risk Officer neither issued nor later ratified ECD-2.
6. ON-44 and the other documented system problems change the weight of timestamp evidence; they are not normative exceptions.

### Decision method

For each purchase, I apply the following sequence:

1. Establish the most credible time at which Northbridge became committed.
2. Freeze the applicable sources at that time; later documents are not presumed retroactive.
3. Determine site, subject-matter, incident, and expiry scope.
4. Apply GPIP controls that survived any valid exception.
5. Apply local controls and only the expressly justified substitutions.
6. Separately classify the underlying conduct, the sufficiency of evidence, and any logging or remediation issue.

Severity reflects the compliance exposure, not merely operational urgency:

* **Critical:** multiple substantive integrity failures in a high-value purchase.
* **High:** missing global pre-commitment control, especially at or above EUR 50,000 or involving a new supplier.
* **Moderate:** local workflow or supplier-control failure with lower financial or integrity exposure.
* **Low:** documentation or logging issue where the underlying authorized act is otherwise adequately established.

The resulting issue-specific hierarchy is:

* **Segregation and Compliance:** A controls unless there is an express, valid A7 exception.
* **Finance approval form:** A establishes the required control; D may define how that control is operationally expressed after the email.
* **PO ordering and supplier-register use:** C controls within its scope and time; otherwise B controls.
* **Emergency evidence:** C3 controls for valid C purchases; B6 controls other local emergencies.
* **Scope and expiry:** C’s own terms control and are not enlarged by D.

### Overall assessment

The cases divide into four materially different patterns:

* **P-502 and P-503** involve substantive GPIP failures that cannot be explained as mere workflow deviations.
* **P-501, P-504, and P-506** rely on emergency arguments that do not cover the controls actually missed.
* **P-505 and P-507** are not responsibly classifiable as compliant or noncompliant without additional actor or record evidence.
* **P-508** is the strongest justified-exception case because it preserves segregation, Compliance, Legal, two-person authorization, and a contemporaneous pre-commitment sequence.

### Monitoring actions

1. **Evaluate controls at commitment event time.** Store separate fields for business event time, source-system posting time, and warehouse ingestion time.

2. **Require explicit exception identifiers.** Every emergency purchase should reference a controlled exception ID, scope category, start and expiry, controls varied, and authorizing instrument.

3. **Monitor actor-role combinations.** Automatically flag requester–committer–approver overlap, including participation in a joint approval rather than only sole approvals.

4. **Separate missing approval from late posting.** Dashboards should distinguish:

   * approval performed late;
   * approval performed timely but entered late;
   * approval alleged but unsupported;
   * approval performed through an authorized substitute channel.

5. **Monitor emergency normalization.** Track emergency purchases by supplier, item, requester, amount, and rolling frequency. Repeated “emergency” purchases for the same component should trigger inventory-planning and root-cause review.

6. **Automate expiry enforcement.** The procurement system should reject an expired exception ID and should not accept a free-text statement that Incident Command verbally extended it.

7. **Retain scope evidence.** For each C-type purchase, record affected line, expected time to safety or production impact, available stock horizon, approved-supplier availability, and why the purchase was required for containment.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

### Proposed replacement clause: Emergency procurement authority and control hierarchy

> **1. Hierarchy.** GPIP-4 governs substantive integrity, segregation, financial authorization, and Compliance requirements. A controlled local SOP may impose stricter requirements. An incident directive may vary only the controls expressly identified in the directive. An email or oral instruction may interpret implementation but may not create, extend, or enlarge an exception unless incorporated into the controlled directive.
>
> **2. Valid emergency exception.** A temporary exception must be written, carry a unique exception ID, state the incident and covered purchases, identify each varied control, specify monetary thresholds and substitute approvers, and state exact start and expiry times. It must be signed by the Chief Risk Officer. If the Chief Risk Officer is unavailable, it may be signed jointly by the COO, CFO, Head of Compliance, and Incident Commander for no more than 24 hours, subject to independent review at the earliest opportunity.
>
> **3. Scope.** “Critical containment purchase” means an item without which safety containment, sterilization capability, or restoration of the affected line is reasonably expected to fail within 24 hours. Backup stock with more than 24 hours of documented coverage is excluded unless a signed technical risk assessment explains the immediate containment dependency. Cafeteria, office, and general site-continuity purchases are excluded.
>
> **4. Segregation and thresholds.** The requester or committer may not be a final business approver and may not count toward a required joint authorization. For purchases of at least EUR 50,000, an identified incident budget owner and an identified Finance Controller must approve before commitment. For purchases above EUR 100,000, or from a new or unapproved supplier, Compliance clearance is required before commitment. For purchases above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is additionally required before commitment. A substitute approval route is valid only where the directive names which role satisfies each control.
>
> **5. PO and supplier exceptions.** Commitment may precede PO creation, and an unapproved supplier may be used, only when an approved supplier cannot meet the documented containment deadline. The PO must be created within four hours of commitment or within two hours after restoration of the relevant system. Use of an unapproved supplier does not waive pre-commitment Compliance clearance.
>
> **6. Verbal authorization and evidence.** Verbal authorization is valid only where the required authorizers acted before commitment and an attributable, integrity-protected record is created within four hours. A later ERP posting may evidence that timely act if supported by authenticated metadata. A later approval cannot be backdated and does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval unless the governing rule expressly provides a cure.
>
> **7. Expiry and extension.** Exceptions expire automatically at the stated time. An uncommitted purchase after expiry is governed by ordinary rules. No oral extension is valid. An extension must satisfy the same authority, scope, content, and recording requirements as the original exception.
>
> **8. Anti-normalization safeguard.** More than two emergency purchases for the same supplier, component family, or requester within 30 days, or aggregate emergency commitments above EUR 250,000 under one incident, triggers immediate Chief Risk Officer and Internal Audit review. Emergency status may not be used to compensate for recurring inventory, supplier-onboarding, or planning deficiencies.

This clause would preserve operational speed while making clear that emergency authority changes identified workflow controls, not every integrity control associated with the purchase.
