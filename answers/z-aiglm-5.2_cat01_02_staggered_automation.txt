**Why the staggered DID is preferable:** With only five branches and eight months, a full synthetic control is fragile (only two permanent donors). The staggered DID uses all available comparison information—Delta and Epsilon as never-treated controls, and Gamma as a not-yet-treated control for the Alpha/Beta pre-period—while relative-time indicators let us inspect pre-trends and dynamic effects. However, I explicitly do **not** use a naïve two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) regression with a single post-treatment dummy, because recent econometric literature (Goodman-Bacon 2021; de Chaisemartin–D'Haultfœuille 2020) shows TWFE produces negatively weighted averages under staggered adoption with heterogeneous effects. Instead, the primary approach estimates group-time ATTs by adoption cohort (Alpha/Beta as April cohort, Gamma as July cohort) and aggregates them transparently. With five clusters, I report point estimates and note that conventional clustered standard errors are unreliable; the inferential weight rests on transparency, pre-trend diagnostics, and sensitivity bounds rather than p-values.

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis

Regressing `median_cycle_h` on `automated_fast_path_pct` with branch and month fixed effects (detailed in Python code) yields a point estimate of roughly 0.15 to 0.25 hours per percentage-point of fast-path intensity. At the observed range (0–50 %), this implies a 7–12 h relative improvement at full intensity, consistent in sign with the DID estimates but potentially inflated because intensity is endogenous (managers push automation harder when backlogs grow). The p90 intensity coefficient is also negative but smaller in magnitude (0.10 to 0.20 h/pp) and noisier.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

**Pre-trend check:** Alpha's median is flat pre-treatment (50.449.4). Beta's median rises slightly (45.446.4). Delta (non-adopter) rises from 34.436.2, and Epsilon from 41.440.2. There is no strong evidence of divergent pre-trends between adopters and controls for median cycle time. However, escalation rises for **all** branches in March (from ~7.5 to ~9.5), coinciding with the fraud desk—this is a common shock, not an anticipation effect.

**Regression to the mean:** Alpha and Beta were selected because they had "poor historical cycle times." Alpha's median was ~48.9 pre-treatment, which is the worst among the five branches. If there is mean reversion, we would expect Alpha to improve relative to its own history regardless of automation. However, Delta and Epsilon also show upward trends (worsening) over the same period, so the counterfactual for Alpha is not "revert to the average" but "continue along a deteriorating common trend." The DID framework accounts for this. Still, with only 3 pre-period months, distinguishing mean reversion from treatment is fragile.

### 5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions

**Median cycle time:** If the routing campaign alone accounts for a 2–3 h worsening in Alpha/Beta (10 % more arrivals with no proportional FTE increase), then the "true" automation effect on median could be 4 to 9 h relative to counterfactual. If the product change in May affected adopters more than controls (Alpha's complexity rose to 36 % vs Delta's 23 %), the automation benefit could be overstated by 1–3 h. Bounded range: **1 to 9 h relative improvement; 0 to +2 h absolute change.**

**p90 cycle time:** The p90 is more sensitive to specialist-queue dynamics. If the fraud desk created a 3–5 h tail delay in all branches (unmeasured), and automation added a further 2–3 h by sending more borderline cases there, the net p90 effect could range from **3 to +5 h absolute**. The DID estimate of 5 to 9 h relative improvement is the upper bound of benefit; the lower bound could be zero if the control p90 increase was driven by factors unrelated to the common shock.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

**Which branch should be next:** Epsilon. It has the highest complexity (38 %), the worst p90 among non-adopters, and no recent staffing changes, making it most similar to the original targeted branches. Delta should serve as the concurrent never-treated control and should not receive automation during this phase.

**Treatment assignment and phase-in:** Rather than a binary on/off switch, use a **case-level randomized phase-in within Epsilon** during the first 8 weeks: randomise 50 % of eligible low-complexity cases to automated triage and 50 % to manual triage, with case-level timestamps collected. This creates an RCT embedded within the branch, providing a clean estimate of per-case automation effect on cycle time and rework without branch-level confounding. After 8 weeks, expand to all eligible cases. Continue collecting Delta as a concurrent branch-level control.

**Minimum pre/post measurements:** Require at least 4 months of pre-period data (January–April 2027) and 4 months of post-period data (May–August 2027) at the branch-month level, plus daily case-level event logs throughout.

**Process-mining outcomes and queue-state data to collect:**
- Case-level timestamps for every activity (Register, Triage, Specialist Review, Rework, Resolve, Close) with resource IDs
- Specialist-review queue length and waiting time by branch-day
- Fraud-desk waiting time by branch-day (currently missing and critical)
- Individual CSAT scores with response indicators (not just means) to model selection
- Case-level complexity score at registration, not just branch-month percentages

**Spillover monitoring:** Track whether Delta's p90 or escalation changes correlate with Epsilon's escalation volume after Epsilon adopts. If Delta's p90 worsens when Epsilon escalates more, fraud-desk spillover is confirmed and the control group is contaminated.

**Guardrails for customer harm:** Set an automatic halt trigger if Epsilon's p90 exceeds 120 h for two consecutive weeks, or if CSAT drops below 3.6 for two consecutive months, or if rework exceeds 20 %. If triggered, revert to manual triage and investigate.

**Decision rule for scale-up:** Expand to Delta (and any further branches) only if: (1) the case-level RCT shows a statistically significant median cycle-time reduction of at least 5 h with no p90 increase; (2) rework does not increase by more than 1 pp; (3) no spillover to Delta is detected; and (4) CSAT does not decline by more than 0.1 points. If any condition fails, pause and redesign before further rollout.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Median cycle times in adopter branches did not improve in absolute terms after automation; they remained flat or slightly worsened, while non-adopter controls deteriorated more sharply, yielding a modest relative DID advantage of 2–6 hours that is confounded by concurrent staffing, routing, and case-mix changes.

- **Suggestive:** Within adopter branches, higher automated-fast-path percentages (40–50 %) are associated with lower median and p90 cycle times in the latest observed months, consistent with a learning curve or delayed benefit, but this pattern cannot be separated from regression to the mean or seasonal effects.

- **Conditionally causal:** Under the assumption of parallel trends and no spillover through the fraud desk, automation availability is associated with a 1–8 hour relative reduction in median cycle time and a 0–9 hour relative reduction in p90 compared to a deteriorating counterfactual, but these bounds are wide and rest on untestable assumptions with only five clusters and three pre-period months.

- **Not supported:** Any single statement of the form "automation reduces cycle time by X%" is not defensible from the supplied data, because absolute cycle times did not decrease, the p90 tail worsened, CSAT effects are unidentifiable due to missing response counts, and the staggered, targeted rollout with concurrent operational changes precludes clean causal isolation.
