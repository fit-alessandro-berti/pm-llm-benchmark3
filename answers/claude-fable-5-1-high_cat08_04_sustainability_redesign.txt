# Sustainability redesign of the spare-parts order process

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope used: "order-to-confirmed-delivery, including loops"** – the operational footprint of one spare-part order from `Receive order` to `Close`, measured with the attributes already in the log, plus the loops that the DFG shows but that a naive per-order view hides (rework production, rework packaging, partial/second shipments, returns, cancellations).

Inside the boundary:
- Production energy (`energy_kwh` on CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell), including energy spent in `Rework production -> CNC production` loops (390 loops) and in production for orders later cancelled.
- Outbound transport (`transport_kgco2e` for parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck), including the second shipment of partial-shipment cases and the empty return leg of dedicated vans (currently not attributed to any order – see uncertainty).
- Materials: `material_scrap_kg` (scrap from first-pass and rework production) and `package_material_kg` (standard vs reinforced, plus packaging redone in `Rework packaging`).
- Quality-driven waste: damage-related returns (reverse transport + replacement production) and obsolescence of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (6% when forecasts are wrong) – treated as embodied energy and material that produced no service.
- Service, quality and resilience outcomes as constraints, not as externalities: late delivery, cancellation due to delay, rework/return rate, availability of alternative routes (R1).

Outside the boundary (stated, not ignored):
- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier transport for bar stock/castings (only visible indirectly through scrap).
- Customer-side impacts of machine downtime (idle plant, restart energy, scrapped WIP). These can be large and are the reason Critical-downtime transport is *not* treated as simply "unjustified emissions". They are unmeasured in the log, so the redesign uses the customer's segment (true downtime vs precautionary) as a proxy rather than pretending to quantify avoided downtime.
- Facility overheads (HVAC, lighting), IT, commuting, capital equipment, end-of-life of parts.
- Any displacement to third parties (outsourced emergency production, customer self-collection) – these must be flagged whenever an intervention would move impact across the boundary.

Assumptions and remaining uncertainty:
- The log is flattened by order; `energy_kwh` per order is a batch allocation across line items, so "energy per item" (batching evidence) and "energy per order" (segment table) are not directly comparable. Directions are robust; absolute per-item numbers are not.
- `transport_kgco2e` relies on emission factors (parcel air ≈ 4–6x ground) rather than measured fuel; consolidated-truck savings (35–55%) depend on fill rate, which is not logged.
- Path shares and DFG counts do not fully reconcile (e.g., the van path is 8% ≈ 960 orders while only 620 `Dedicated van` events exist; `Cancel due to delay -> END` is 530 while the inbound arcs sum to 470). This matches the stated low precision of the model during disruptions; all figures below are treated as directional.
- Returns and reverse logistics are only partly visible (packaging rework, "customer returns" mentioned qualitatively). No attribute records van fill/backhaul or truck fill rate; these must be added.
- Rough hotspot arithmetic from the segment table (12,000 orders): Made-to-order (34% of orders) accounts for roughly 85–90% of production energy and scrap; Critical-downtime (11% of orders) accounts for roughly 40% of transport kgCO2e; the single path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` (5% of orders) carries roughly a fifth of all energy and a quarter of all scrap. These ratios drive prioritisation.

## 3. Intervention options

**A. S4 + S1 – Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract, embedded in a slack-based shipping recommender**
- Target: `Stock -> standard pack -> air` (5%, 18.5 kgCO2e) and the 22% of SS SC cases violating SP1.
- Mechanism: air is chosen out of habit for Service-contract customers even when `promised_lead_time_hours` minus elapsed time allows ground (median SS SC cycle time is 18 h, well within most contracts). The recommender computes slack at `Package standard` and picks the lowest-emission `route_mode` whose historical reliability meets the 85% threshold; priority picking removes the internal delay that made air "necessary". Ground is roughly a fifth of air emissions per order.
- Service/quality/resilience: service neutral by construction (only fires when slack is sufficient); air remains available as the R1 alternative; quality unaffected.
- Evidence to monitor: SP1 conformance rate, late-delivery rate for SS SC (baseline 5%), share of orders where the recommendation was overridden (S12), realised route reliability vs assumed.

**B. S5 – Twice-daily consolidated-truck departures for non-urgent stock orders**
- Target: `Stock -> standard pack -> ground` (42%, 3.6 kgCO2e, 8% late) for Regular customers with ≥24 h slack; extend current 850 consolidated shipments.
- Mechanism: 35–55% fewer kgCO2e than individual ground parcels, at the cost of 12–24 h consolidation wait. Applies only to cases with slack, so the wait is absorbed by the promise, not by the customer.
- Service/quality/resilience: cycle time rises for eligible orders; late-delivery risk controlled by the slack rule; resilience slightly improves route diversity (a third mode), but truck fill must be monitored to avoid low-fill departures being worse than parcels.
- Evidence: fill rate per departure (new attribute), late-delivery rate on the consolidated path (baseline 830/850 confirmed), share of Regular stock orders on consolidated truck, p90 cycle time SS Regular (baseline 62 h).

**C. S7 – Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**
- Target: `Inspection -> Rework production` (470 cases) and the path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` (5%, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 160 h, 32% late); also Q1-related return loops.
- Mechanism: inspection_cell queues cause rushed inspections that miss defects; defects then surface as packaging rework, customer returns, or a full CNC rework loop. A gate (minimum inspection time and checklist for CNC_B parts and heat-treated parts, first-article check on new batches) catches defects earlier, cutting the doubling of energy and scrap seen on the rework path and eliminating reverse transport for returns.
- Service/quality/resilience: quality up; short-term cycle time up slightly at inspection unless capacity is added; fewer loops shorten p90 cycle time; no resilience impact.
- Evidence: first-pass yield by `machine_group`, return rate, inspection dwell time vs later rework (correlation should weaken), rework energy share.

**D. S3 – Route Made-to-order to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow; keep CNC_B for emergencies and overflow**
- Target: MTO cases planned on CNC_B (18% rework vs 9%).
- Mechanism: halving rework probability avoids the second CNC pass, second inspection, extra scrap and an extra ~40 h. CNC_A's better energy curve at moderate batch size compounds the gain.
- Service/quality/resilience: quality up; risk that CNC_A queue lengthens and `Batch wait` grows, so routing must be slack-conditional; CNC_B stays warm as capacity reserve (resilience protected). CNC_B is faster, so Critical `Emergency production` (760 cases) may still use it under the inspection gate.
- Evidence: rework rate by machine, CNC_A queue length and batch wait, late delivery for MTO Regular (18%) and MTO SC (13%), CNC_B utilisation.

**E. S2 – Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with segment-specific waiting-time caps**
- Target: `Plan production -> Batch wait` (3,020 cases) and heat-treatment batching (1,820 cases).
- Mechanism: today batching is optimised for energy per item (batch 12–20 → 1.3 kWh/item) at the cost of a 38 h median wait, 21% lateness and 6% obsolescence. Dynamic batching releases a batch when either a target size (e.g., 6–12 items, where most of the energy curve is already captured) or a wait cap (e.g., ~12–16 h for Service-contract, ~24 h for Regular, none for Critical) is reached. Batches are built only from confirmed orders, not forecast fill items, which addresses obsolescence. Heat-treatment batching gets the same cap because waiting cases carry a 1.7x late-delivery risk.
- Service/quality/resilience: cycle time and lateness improve versus current large batches; energy per item rises slightly versus the extreme batch sizes but falls versus the "CNC immediately" path (24 kWh); fewer obsolete items. Resilience improves (less WIP locked in half-built batches).
- Evidence: batch-size distribution, batch wait median/p90 per segment, energy per item per batch band, 14-day unshipped rate, late delivery for MTO paths.

**F. S9 + S8 – Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent; replace part of dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes and use van backhauls**
- Target: `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` (8%, 52 kgCO2e, 14 h) and the 31% of van cases without SP2 justification.
- Mechanism: a justified true-downtime order keeps the dedicated van (customer downtime impact outside the boundary is likely larger than 52 kgCO2e). Precautionary urgent orders (the ones now lacking SP2 justification) go to a shared emergency lane (pooled express courier or milk-run van) or, when the part is in stock and the customer accepts ~24 h, to the existing `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path (23 h, 4.8 kgCO2e). Empty return legs are used for returns/cores/supplier pickups, roughly halving the per-order attribution of van emissions.
- Service/quality/resilience: true-downtime service protected (3% late must not rise); precautionary orders accept a defined, contracted longer promise; resilience is protected only if the shared lane is a second carrier, not a replacement for the van contract (R1).
- Evidence: SP2 justification presence (target 100%), late delivery split by the two new subcategories, kgCO2e per Critical order, van empty-return rate, share of Critical orders served from stock.

**G. S6 – Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment (plus Q1 enforcement by weight/inspection certificate)**
- Target: `Rework packaging` (620 events, both directions) and Q1 violations.
- Mechanism: packaging rework consumes double material and delays shipping; it occurs when packaging type conflicts with `route_mode` or part geometry. A rule at `Package` selects reinforced for MTO parts >8 kg (Q1) unless inspection certifies low damage risk, standard for light stock items, and checks compatibility with the chosen route. This removes both over-packaging (material) and under-packaging (damage returns: reinforced cuts damage returns 40% for heavy MTO parts, and a return costs more transport and possibly replacement production than the extra packaging).
- Service/quality/resilience: fewer delays from repacking; fewer damage returns; no resilience effect.
- Evidence: rework-packaging rate, `package_material_kg` per shipped kg by segment, damage-return rate, Q1 conformance.

**H. S11 – Availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments**
- Target: `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010) and the path `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder` (7%, 79 h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e, 60 cancellations after backorder).
- Mechanism: partial shipments double packaging and transport and still produce the worst stock-segment lateness. Rule: ship complete when the backorder line is due within the promise window; ship partial only if the customer is Critical or explicitly requests the available lines; otherwise consolidate the backorder onto the next consolidated truck. Improved availability data (reserving stock at `Availability check`) reduces the loop entirely.
- Service/quality/resilience: fewer cancellations and second contacts; for Critical customers partial shipment remains allowed because the first line may stop the downtime.
- Evidence: partial-shipment rate, backorder-cancel rate, kgCO2e per order on the partial path, customer-contact events.

**I. S10 – Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers**
- Target: MTO Regular (22%, 94 h median, 18% late, 8.0 kgCO2e, 18.5 kWh).
- Mechanism: offer a longer, reliable promise with ground/consolidated transport and a fuller CNC batch, versus a fast promise at a surcharge. Turns the batching trade-off into an explicit customer choice, reducing both air use (`Package reinforced -> Ship air`, 800 cases) and small emergency batches.
- Service/quality/resilience: lateness falls because promises match capability; risk that customers order earlier or inflate urgency; resilience neutral.
- Evidence: distribution of chosen promise options, on-time rate per option, route mix per option, share of Regular customers who "upgrade" to urgent.

**J. S12 – Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
- Target: all override decisions; needed by A, F and G.
- Mechanism: no direct impact, but makes premium transport and packaging exceptions auditable, and provides the labelled data to recalibrate the recommender's reliability estimates.
- Service/quality/resilience: protects service by making override easy but visible; prevents "green" rules from being silently bypassed or silently enforced against a customer in real need.
- Evidence: override rate by reason code, correlation of overrides with actual lateness avoided, repeated override patterns by planner/customer.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching → expedite rebound.** Larger CNC/heat-treatment batches cut kWh/item but raise `Batch wait` to 38 h and lateness to 21%; late MTO orders are then rescued by `Ship air` (23 kgCO2e) or repeated as `Emergency production`, so energy saved in the machine shop is re-spent several times over in transport. Cap the wait, and measure kgCO2e per on-time order, not kWh per item alone.
2. **Batching → obsolescence.** Filling batches with forecast items leaves 6% unshipped after 14 days when forecasts are wrong; the embodied energy and material is pure waste and the items may end up scrapped. Build batches from confirmed orders only, and log the 14-day unshipped rate as a scrap indicator.
3. **Transport → cancellation and re-ordering.** Pushing slack-poor orders to ground or consolidated truck increases lateness, `Cancel due to delay` (≈530 cases today) and then a second, more urgent order – double handling and premium transport. The 85% reliability rule must be based on realised, segment-level route reliability, not nominal transit times.
4. **Consolidation → low fill.** Fixed twice-daily truck departures with low fill can emit more per order than parcels and add 12–24 h for nothing. Departures need a minimum-fill or "release anyway if any order's slack is expiring" rule.
5. **Packaging → damage returns (and the reverse).** Cutting `package_material_kg` by using standard packaging on heavy MTO parts raises damage returns (reinforced reduces them 40%), each return costing reverse transport, rework packaging and possibly replacement production and scrap. Conversely, reinforced packaging for light stock items doubles material and causes `Rework packaging` when incompatible with parcel routes. Q1 by weight plus inspection certification is the balancing rule.
6. **Rework → inspection queue.** A stricter inspection gate without capacity lengthens inspection_cell queues, which is exactly what causes rushed inspections; cases then miss due dates and are expedited. Gate capacity must be planned, and the early effect may be a *rise* in detected rework (scrap up) before defects at source are eliminated.
7. **Machine shift → capacity displacement.** Moving MTO to CNC_A reduces rework but lengthens the CNC_A queue and batch wait; if CNC_B is idled it is unavailable for peaks and `Emergency production`, weakening resilience. Slack-conditional routing keeps CNC_B in reserve.
8. **Van substitution → single-carrier dependency.** Replacing dedicated vans with one shared emergency lane can leave Service-contract customers with a single route during carrier disruption (R1 violation), and detours on shared lanes can turn a true-downtime delivery late, pushing the customer to demand vans for everything.
9. **Customer behaviour – urgency inflation.** Splitting Critical-downtime into true and precautionary categories, or offering carbon-aware promise dates, invites customers (and account managers) to label orders as true downtime to keep the van/air service. Requires contract definitions and monitoring of the true-downtime share per customer.
10. **Customer behaviour – safety-stock rebound.** Longer green promise dates may lead Regular customers to order earlier or in larger quantities as safety stock, moving obsolescence and inventory impact to the customer site (outside the boundary) rather than eliminating it.
11. **Governance rebound.** An exception log can degenerate into boilerplate justifications (SP2 already lacks justification in 31% of van cases); the log must be reviewed against realised outcomes (did the override actually avoid lateness?).
12. **Boundary displacement.** Outsourcing emergency production or asking customers to collect parts lowers logged kgCO2e/kWh without lowering real impact. Any intervention that removes events from the log must be checked for displacement rather than reduction.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

The package is organised around the three hotspots the log exposes – Made-to-order production energy and scrap (≈85–90% of kWh and scrap, dominated by rework and oversized batching), Critical-downtime transport (≈40% of kgCO2e on 11% of orders), and habitual premium transport on Standard-stock Service-contract – and it follows one rule: **remove waste that also hurts service first, then trade time for emissions only where slack demonstrably exists, and never remove a route or machine alternative that R1 or true downtime relies on.**

**Wave 1 – no-regret, service-neutral (weeks):**
- **S6 packaging–route compatibility with Q1 enforcement** and **S11 availability-check rules**: both cut material, second shipments and repacking while *reducing* lateness (partial path 24% late, 79 h). Partial shipment retained for Critical orders.
- **S4 + S1 slack-based shipping recommender** for Standard-stock (SP1 embedded, ≥85% reliability), with **S12 exception log** so that a planner can still choose air with a reason code. Air remains the R1 fallback.
- **S9 Critical split** with contractual definitions of true downtime vs precautionary urgent; **SP2 justification becomes a mandatory event**, not a form.

**Wave 2 – quality-first production redesign (1–2 quarters):**
- **S7 inspection gate** (with inspection capacity sized so the queue does not become the new bottleneck) before **S3 slack-conditional routing to CNC_A**. Quality first because the rework path is the single largest energy/scrap sink and also the worst service path (160 h, 32% late) – fixing it improves every criterion at once. CNC_B is kept as reserve and for emergency production.
- **S2 dynamic batching** with wait caps by segment (tightest for Service-contract, none for Critical) and confirmed-order-only batch composition. This deliberately gives up the last part of the energy curve (batch 12–20) in exchange for cutting the 38 h wait, the 1.7x heat-treatment lateness risk and the 6% obsolescence; net lifecycle impact is still favourable because late orders no longer migrate to air or emergency runs.

**Wave 3 – transport network changes that need contracts and partners (2–4 quarters):**
- **S8 shared emergency lanes for precautionary urgent orders and backhaul use of dedicated vans**, keeping the dedicated-van contract for justified true downtime so that service (3% late) and R1 are intact. Where the `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path (23 h, 4.8 kgCO2e) proves reliable, extend forward stocking of high-frequency critical parts so fewer Critical orders need emergency production at all.
- **S5 consolidated truck departures** for Regular stock orders with ≥24 h slack and a minimum-fill/slack-expiry release rule.
- **S10 carbon-aware promise dates** for Regular MTO, offered only once Wave 2 has made the longer promise reliable; otherwise customers are being sold a "green" option that is also late.

Why this is balanced: environmental gains come mostly from eliminating loops (rework, repacking, second shipments, obsolete batches) and from *choice architecture* on transport constrained by slack, not from blanket slower service. Service is protected by segment-specific caps and the 85% reliability rule; quality improves because inspection and Q1 are strengthened rather than traded away; resilience is protected because no mode or machine is removed – CNC_B, air and dedicated vans are demoted to justified alternatives, which is exactly the R1 requirement. The lowest-emission options (ground for everything Critical, maximum batches, no reinforced packaging) are explicitly rejected because the log shows they violate service (9% vs 3% late on the Critical stock/ground exception, 21% late on large batches) or quality (damage returns).

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Design of the evaluation**
- Baseline: the current 12,000-order log, stratified by the five segments and the eight path variants. After each wave, recompute the same indicator set on a comparable period and case mix (case-mix shifts, e.g., more Critical orders, would otherwise masquerade as emission increases).
- Phased/A-B rollout where possible: e.g., the shipping recommender on half of the Standard-stock Service-contract customers first; inspection gate on CNC_B parts first; consolidated departures on one region first. Compare treated vs untreated segments on kgCO2e per on-time-in-full order, late-delivery rate, rework rate and cancellation rate.
- Rediscover the process model after each wave and check that new loops have not appeared (e.g., `Consolidated truck -> Rework packaging`, `Shared lane -> Dedicated van` re-dispatch) and that precision improves once disruption cases carry an explicit disruption attribute.

**Conformance monitoring**
- **SP1**: for every shipped order compute slack at `Package` (`promised_lead_time_hours` minus elapsed time) and the historical on-time probability of each `route_mode` for that lane; flag air/van/dedicated choices where ground or truck had ≥85% probability. Target: SS SC violation rate from 22% to below ~5%, with every remaining violation carrying an S12 reason code. Also check the inverse error: ground chosen with <85% probability (a service violation the sustainability rule must not create).
- **SP2**: every `Dedicated van` event must be preceded by a justification event tied to the true-downtime subcategory; target 100% presence (from 69%). Review justification reason codes quarterly against the customer's actual downtime status; flag customers whose true-downtime share rises abnormally.
- **Q1**: every MTO part >8 kg shows `Package reinforced` or an inspection low-damage-risk certificate; track Q1 violations against subsequent `Rework packaging` and return events – the correlation should disappear.
- **R1**: for each Service-contract order, record the set of feasible route modes at `Package`; alert if any customer or lane has only one feasible mode, or if one carrier exceeds a concentration threshold. Run a simulated carrier-disruption drill twice a year and measure recovery time in the log.

**Segment-level service and quality monitoring (guardrails with stop rules)**
- Late-delivery, median/p90 cycle time, cancellation and partial-shipment rates per segment, reviewed weekly. Stop rule: if a segment's late-delivery rate rises more than ~2 percentage points above baseline for two consecutive weeks (e.g., Critical true-downtime above ~5%, SS SC above ~7%), the corresponding rule (batch cap, consolidation, shared lane) is relaxed automatically for that segment until root cause is found.
- Rework and return: first-pass yield by `machine_group`, inspection dwell time, damage-return rate, rework-packaging rate; batch-size and wait distributions; 14-day unshipped rate for batch-produced items.
- Environmental: kWh and kgCO2e per order and per on-time-in-full order, scrap and package material per shipped kg, van empty-return and truck fill rates (new attributes to be added to the log), all per segment and per path.

**Exception governance**
- S12 exception log with mandatory reason codes (customer downtime confirmed, carrier disruption, inspection certificate, geometry, customer request). Monthly review by operations, quality and sustainability leads: override rate per planner and customer, whether overrides actually avoided lateness (join override to outcome), and recurring override patterns that indicate the rule threshold is wrong rather than the planner.
- Threshold recalibration: reliability estimates for SP1 and wait caps for S2 are re-estimated from the log each quarter, so the rules follow realised performance rather than fixed assumptions.
- Displacement check: any indicator improvement accompanied by fewer logged events (fewer emergency productions, fewer shipments) triggers a check for outsourcing or customer self-collection before it is booked as a reduction.
- Publish a segment-level scorecard combining environmental, service, quality and resilience indicators so that no single metric (kWh per item, kgCO2e per order) can be optimised in isolation.
