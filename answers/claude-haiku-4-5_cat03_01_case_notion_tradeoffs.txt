# Comparative Flattening Analysis: Order-Fulfilment OCEL

## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion:** A case is a bounded, causally coherent sequence of activities leading to a defined terminal state (e.g., an Order  Closed, or a Delivery  Confirmed). In a multi-object OCEL, a case is not a pre-given entity but a *choice of projection*. Flattening imposes a case notion by selecting a lead object and copying all related events into its trace.

**Convergence:** Two or more independent objects (Orders, Deliveries, Invoices) become entangled in a single shared event—e.g., D2 carries both P2 and P3 (serving three orders). Flattening on Delivery will collapse this convergence; flattening on Order will dualize it across three separate traces.

**Divergence:** One object spawns or participates in multiple downstream objects. For example, O1 splits into P1 and P2, each carried by separate Deliveries (D1 and D2). Flattening on Order captures this split; flattening on Package hides the fact that both packages belong to the same original order.

**Copied shared events and duplication:** When an event e touches multiple lead objects, flattening copies e into each corresponding case. This is *both* evidence (the event genuinely occurred in relation to all objects) and distortion (frequency statistics treat one physical event as n logical events). I treat copied shared events as **information-preserving but frequency-inflating** unless explicitly adjusted.

**Analytical transformations:** I permit:
- Propagation of O2O relations through the object graph (e.g., if e relates O1 to V1 and V1 bills O1, we may infer e's relevance to O1–O2 relationships).
- Post-hoc filtering of shared events to distinguish "directly related" from "indirectly related" objects.
- Explicit clustering of equivalent flattened traces (e.g., all Order traces that follow the same pattern).

I do *not* silently aggregate events or invent missing relations.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### Distortion 1: Cross-order package P3 (Order flattening)
- **Event:** e13 (Pack Package: O2, O3, I21, I31, P3)
- **Flattening affected:** Order (O2 and O3)
- **Distortion:** e13 appears in both O2 and O3 case traces. In O2's trace, Pack(P3) is followed immediately by Dispatch(D2) at e16 (which also includes O3). In O3's trace, Pack(P3) is preceded by Pack(P4) at e14, then Dispatch(D2) at e16. The DFG edge pack  dispatch is duplicated; frequency of Pack activity inflates from 1 true event to 2 recorded instances. A time-series analyst may interpret O2 and O3 as two independent packing operations when they are one.
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. Inflates packing KPI and obscures the fact that O2 and O3 items were consolidated in transit, not processed independently. Leads to false dual-path discovery.

### Distortion 2: Cross-order package P4 (Order flattening)
- **Event:** e14 (Pack Package: O3, O4, I32, I41, P4)
- **Flattening affected:** Order (O3 and O4)
- **Distortion:** e14 appears in both O3 and O4 traces. O3's trace also includes e13 (P3) and e14 (P4), appearing as divergence (O3 items split across two packages). O4's trace shows a single Pack step (e14). The two orders appear to follow different packing strategies; in reality, the packing is based on items, not order identity.
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. Creates false DFG variants; suggests O3 requires multiple pack steps while O4 requires one, when the pattern is driven by item assignment to packages, not order logic.

### Distortion 3: Consolidated delivery D2 (Order flattening)
- **Event:** e16 (Dispatch: O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2)
- **Flattening affected:** Order (O1, O2, O3)
- **Distortion:** e16 is copied into all three order traces. In O1's trace, e16 follows e12 (Pack P2), skipping O1's involvement with O2 and O3. In O2 and O3's traces, e16 appears after e13 (Pack P3), also skipping the fact that e16 carries P2 (not O2/O3's items). The DFG node "Dispatch" has inflated frequency (3 instances, 1 event). A time-to-delivery analyst would measure e12  e16 for O1, but also count e13  e16 for O2 and e13  e16 for O3, triple-counting the Dispatch step. Performance metrics become nonsensical.
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. SLA analysis on fulfillment cycle time is severely distorted; makes D2 appear in parallel to D1 and D3 when it is causally entangled with both.

### Distortion 4: Consolidated invoice V2 (Order flattening)
- **Event:** e19 (Issue Invoice: O2, O3, V2)
- **Flattening affected:** Order (O2 and O3)
- **Distortion:** e19 is copied into both O2 and O3 traces. In both, it is preceded by pack and dispatch steps, but O2's dispatch is e16 (shared with O3) and O3's dispatch is also e16 (and e17). The timeline suggests O2 and O3 were invoiced separately, when in fact a single consolidated invoice V2 was issued. A process miner would see two "Issue Invoice" events and might infer that the system creates one invoice per order; in reality, the system consolidated billing.
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. Obscures billing consolidation logic; falsely suggests per-order invoicing is standard practice. Cash-flow analysis becomes misleading if V2 is treated as two independent receivables.

### Distortion 5: Partial payment consolidation (Invoice flattening)
- **Event:** e22 (Record Payment: O2, O3, V2, M2) and e25 (Record Payment: O2, O3, V2, M3)
- **Flattening affected:** Invoice (V2)
- **Distortion:** Both e22 and e25 relate to V2 and appear in a single Invoice case. M2 (at e22, 12:40) is a partial_settles relation; M3 (at e25, 14:00 next day) is completes_settlement. In the flattened V2 trace, the sequence is: Create Order (O2)  Create Order (O3)  Add Items  Pack  Dispatch  Issue Invoice (e19)  Record Payment (e22, partial)  Record Payment (e25, complete). The timeline suggests a ~25-hour gap between two payment steps for a single invoice, when in reality M2 and M3 are separate payment events that happen to settle the same invoice. A cash-reconciliation analyst may think the process is "issue invoice, wait, receive payment," missing the fact that two distinct payments were required to fully settle V2. If frequency filtering is applied, both e22 and e25 appear as "Record Payment," inflating the step count to 2 for V2's lifecycle when the logical settlement required 2 steps.
- **Harm assessment:** Informative but context-dependent. Harmful for understanding per-transaction payment patterns; useful for understanding that V2 required multiple payments. Requires explicit notation of settlement semantics (partial vs. complete).

### Distortion 6: Near-singleton lifecycle (Item flattening)
- **Event:** I41 appears in e10 (Add Item), e14 (Pack), e17 (Dispatch), e27 (Confirm Delivery)
- **Flattening affected:** Item (I41)
- **Distortion:** I41's case trace contains only 4 events. Its lifecycle is: Add Item (O4)  Pack  Dispatch  Confirm. There is no intermediate packing variability, no rework, no rejection. Compared to I11's trace (Add  Pack  Dispatch  Confirm, also 4 events), I41 and I11 appear to follow identical patterns. In reality, I11 is packed alone (P1) and delivered alone (D1), while I41 is packed with I32 (P4) and delivered with P4 (D3). The near-singleton lifecycle obscures packing strategy; flattening hides consolidation at the item level.
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. Machine-learning models trained on item traces will fail to distinguish between items that are packed individually vs. consolidated, because the trace is too short and the distinguishing context (package identity, co-packed items) is absent.

### Distortion 7: Package–Delivery cardinality mismatch (Package flattening)
- **Event:** e16 (Dispatch: P2, P3, D2) and e17 (Dispatch: P4, D3)
- **Flattening affected:** Package (P2, P3, P4)
- **Distortion:** P2 and P3 both appear in e16; their case traces both include this single event. P2's case is: Pack (e12)  Dispatch (e16)  Confirm (e24). P3's case is: Pack (e13)  Dispatch (e16)  Confirm (e24). Both traces are isomorphic, suggesting P2 and P3 follow the same lifecycle. However, P2 carries one item (I12 from O1), while P3 carries two items (I21, I31 from O2, O3). The consolidation of P2 and P3 in a single delivery (D2) is not explicit in the flattened Package trace; it appears as if each package is independently dispatched when they are actually grouped.
- **Harm assessment:** Informative. Acceptable for logistics process discovery if the goal is per-package handling, but misleading for understanding delivery economics (e.g., cost per shipment vs. cost per package).

### Distortion 8: Divergent order lifecycle due to packing split (Order flattening)
- **Event:** e11 (Pack P1: O1, I11) and e12 (Pack P2: O1, I12)
- **Flattening affected:** Order (O1)
- **Distortion:** O1's case trace includes two Pack events (e11 and e12) at different timestamps (10:00 and 10:10). The DFG for O1 shows: Add Item (e05)  Add Item (e06)  Pack (e11)  Pack (e12). This appears as a two-phase packing operation, suggesting items I11 and I12 require separate handling. In reality, both are from the same order and are packed at nearly the same time into different packages for logistics consolidation (P1 goes via D1, P2 goes via D2). The flattened trace does not reveal why items were split; the DFG edge "Pack  Pack" is unique to O1 and may be misinterpreted as a process requirement (e.g., "items must be packed in two batches").
- **Harm assessment:** Harmful. Process discovery tools may learn spurious patterns; "Pack  Pack" becomes a variant when it is actually a consequence of item-to-package assignment logic hidden in the O2O graph.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Recommended flattened logs

**Order flattening:** Safe for single-order lifecycle analysis and KPI measurement *if* shared events are explicitly down-weighted or flagged. Create, but **annotate** with "shared_event_flag" to distinguish e13/e14 (copied) from e05/e06 (order-specific).

**Package flattening:** Safe for logistics and warehouse operations. DFG edges Pack  Dispatch  Confirm are reliable per-package; time-in-warehouse analysis is sound. Avoid per-delivery cost allocation.

**Invoice & Payment flattening:** Safe for accounting and AR workflows. Use Invoice flattening for cash-reconciliation, Payment flattening for payment-method and settlement-timing analysis.

**Item flattening:** Use with caution. Suitable only for inventory-tracking and unit-level traceability; unsuitable for understanding packing strategy or delivery economics.

**Delivery flattening:** High risk; avoid for SLA and performance analysis. Use only if explicitly interested in "what orders are shipped together" (i.e., treat convergence as a feature, not a bug).

### Question–Analysis mapping

| Question | Recommended approach | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| "How long does an order take from creation to closure?" | OC-DFG on Order (lead) + OTG visualization | Avoids double-counting shared events; OTG shows when orders converge in packages/deliveries. |
| "Which orders are consolidated in each delivery?" | Object Interaction Summary (OIS) on Delivery + ETOs (Event–Type–Object graph) | Direct O2O and O2E relations are clearer than flattened traces. |
| "What is the throughput of the packing operation?" | Package flattening + frequency analysis of Pack activity | Pack is directly related to Package in E2O; no shared-event inflation. |
| "How many items require rework or are lost?" | Item flattening with per-item status tracking | Item granularity is appropriate; but omit delivery/invoice context. |
| "What is the payment delay distribution?" | Payment flattening + Invoice flattening (cross-reference) | M1, M2, M3, M4 are independent; compare with V1, V2, V3 issuance dates. |
| "Which packing strategy minimizes shipping cost?" | OC-DFG on Package (lead) with O2O propagation to Order | Requires visibility into package composition (items and orders). |
| "Are there delivery consolidation patterns?" | OIS on Delivery + filtering by object count | Identify multi-order deliveries (D2, D3) systematically. |

### Reconciliation of contradictory findings

**Scenario:** Order flattening shows O2 and O3 each have a "Pack  Dispatch" path at e13  e16, suggesting 2 Pack events. Package flattening shows 1 Pack event (e13) followed by 1 Dispatch event (e16).

**Resolution:** Inspect E2O relations. e13 explicitly relates to P3, not O2 or O3 separately. When flattening on Order, the relation is propagated through O2 contains I21, P3 packs I21, creating an implicit O2  e13 relation. This is valid only if propagation is explicitly enabled. If propagation is disabled (direct E2O only), e13 does not appear in O2's trace at all—which is harmful because e13 genuinely involves O2's items. **Solution:** Enable propagation, but label propagated relations as "inferred" and separately report "direct" (E2O only) vs. "transitive" (E2O + O2O) causality.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position: C – Reject a single lead object for this process.**

### Decision criteria

1. **Convergence severity:** Three distinct O2O patterns create irreconcilable conflicts:
   - Cross-order packages (P3 serves O2+O3; P4 serves O3+O4).
   - Consolidated deliveries (D2 and D3 each carry multiple packages from multiple orders).
   - Consolidated invoices (V2 bills O2+O3).

2. **Frequency distortion tolerance:** Events e13, e14, e16, e19, e24 are copied into multiple case traces. For Order flattening, this represents 11 duplicate event instances across 4 cases—a 36% inflation (11 copies / 31 total events). No business question can be reliably answered using Order traces without explicitly filtering or adjusting for this distortion.

3. **Semantic consistency:** No single object type is semantically "complete." Order captures intent but not logistics; Delivery captures logistics but not billing; Invoice captures billing but not fulfillment. A single flattening sacrifices one dimension to preserve another.

4. **Trace length and variant count:** Flattening on Order yields 4 traces of lengths ~13–17 events each; flattening on Delivery yields 3 traces of lengths ~10–18 events each (D2 is an outlier with 18). This variance is not driven by process complexity but by the degree of convergence. Traces are not directly comparable.

### Strongest supporting evidence

- **Event-object many-to-many relationships:** e16 touches 6 objects (O1, O2, O3, P2, P3, D2); e24 touches 7 (O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2). No lead object isolates e16 or e24 cleanly.
- **Divergence and convergence coexist:** O1 diverges (I11, I12  P1, P2  D1, D2); D2 then converges (O1's P2 + O2's P3 + O3's P3). Neither Order nor Delivery is a natural "start" or "end."
- **Partial-payment semantics:** V2 is paid in two instalments (M2 @ e22, M3 @ e25). A single Order or Invoice flattening flattens this temporal structure into a sequence, losing the fact that the two payments are atomic and negotiated separately.

### Strongest counterargument

An analyst focused exclusively on order fulfillment (ignoring invoicing/payment) might argue that **Order is defensible as the primary lead object** because:
- All items originate in orders.
- Packing, dispatch, and delivery are downstream of orders.
- Order closure is a clear terminal state.

However, this argument ignores that orders do not fully determine fulfillment logistics; D2 and D3 consolidate items across orders, introducing dependencies not visible in Order traces. Moreover, invoicing and payment are post-fulfillment processes that determine cash realization; skipping them loses the complete order-to-cash cycle.

### Residual risk

If a single lead object were forced:

- **Order lead:** Risk of SLA/delivery distortion due to multi-order consolidation (D2, D3) appearing as simultaneous parallel events in separate order traces. Analytics on "time to delivery" would be inflated for orders involved in consolidation.
- **Delivery lead:** Risk of obscuring order-level intent. Would require back-propagation to orders to understand why consolidation happened. Payment analysis would be nearly impossible.
- **Package lead:** Risk of missing order context and business logic. Suitable for pure logistics optimization but fails for end-to-end process understanding.

### Conditions for reverting to a single lead object

If the business requirements **explicitly scoped** to one domain, a single lead object becomes acceptable:

1. **"Optimize warehouse packing and delivery logistics"**  Package + Delivery (multi-lead, not single).
2. **"Improve order fulfillment KPIs (from order creation to delivery confirmation)"**  Order, with explicit suppression of consolidated events (flag, don't delete).
3. **"Analyze cash-to-order realization and AR performance"**  Invoice + Payment, with order context as secondary attributes.

None of these are truly single-lead; all require secondary objects for context.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

### Sensitivity check 1: Down-weighting or removing copied shared events

**Procedure:** Create two versions of the Order-flattened log:
- **Version A (baseline):** All events included as-is (e13, e14, e16, e19, e24 copied).
- **Version B (filtered):** Remove e13, e14, e16, e19, e24 from all traces except the trace of the order that "owns" (i.e., created) the affected items.

**Metrics to compare:**
- DFG edge frequency for "Pack  Dispatch" and "Dispatch  Confirm."
- Average trace length per order.
- Number of distinct variants.

**Expected finding:** Version B will show O1  (P1  D1, P2  D2) as true divergence, not duplication. Version A will show O1, O2, O3 each with a Pack  Dispatch path, inflating frequency. If Version B yields fewer variants, the filtering removed genuine distortion.

### Sensitivity check 2: Direct E2O vs. propagated relations

**Procedure:**
- **Variant 1 (direct):** Flatten on Order using only direct E2O relations; e13 relates only to P3, not O2/O3, so e13 does *not* appear in O2's trace.
- **Variant 2 (propagated):** Flatten on Order using E2O + O2O propagation; e13 relates to P3, P3 packs I21, I21 is in O2, so e13 *is* inferred to relate to O2.

**Metrics to compare:**
- Trace coverage (events per trace).
- DFG completeness (can we trace from order creation to closure?).
- Causal correctness (do edges represent real dependencies?).

**Expected finding:** Variant 1 (direct) will have incomplete traces for O2 and O3 because packing and dispatch events are missing. Variant 2 (propagated) will include them but will inflate frequency. This validates the choice: propagation is necessary for completeness but must be labeled as "inferred."

### Sensitivity check 3: Changing the lead object and comparing DFG isomorphism

**Procedure:**
- Flatten on Order, Package, Delivery separately.
- For each, compute the DFG and count distinct paths from first activity to last.
- Check if the set of distinct paths differs across lead objects.

**Metrics:**
- Variant count (number of unique activity sequences).
- Path count (DFG edges).
- Reachability (can all orders reach a terminal state in each flattening?).

**Expected finding:** Order flattening will show higher variant count due to split packages (O1 has Pack  Pack). Package flattening will show lower variant count because Pack  Dispatch  Confirm is consistent. Delivery flattening will show anomalous paths due to multi-order consolidation (D2  Confirm spanning three orders). Isomorphism fails; lead object choice significantly affects DFG topology.

### Sensitivity check 4: Frequency metrics—events vs. unique objects vs. total objects in E2O

**Procedure:** For the shared event e16 (Dispatch), compute:
- **Event count:** 1 (one Dispatch event occurred).
- **Unique object count:** 6 (O1, O2, O3, P2, P3, D2).
- **Trace count in Order flattening:** 3 (e16 appears in traces for O1, O2, O3).
- **Total object instances in E2O:** Sum of all O1, O2, O3 instances in the event.

**Comparison:**
- If we count by event, Dispatch frequency is 1.
- If we count by unique lead object (Order), Dispatch frequency is 3.
- If we count by total objects in E2O, Dispatch frequency is affected by the order in which objects appear in the E2O relation.

**Expected finding:** Frequency metrics diverge significantly depending on the counting basis. Event-based frequency is conservative; object-based frequency inflates. A process miner who reports "3 Dispatch events" when only 1 occurred is misleading. This validates the need for explicit notation of "copied" vs. "unique" event instances.

## Summary

This OCEL exhibits **high structural complexity** due to convergence (multi-order packages and deliveries, consolidated invoicing and split payments) and divergence (orders split across packages, items split across deliveries). No single lead object can simultaneously preserve order semantics, logistics consolidation patterns, and payment settlement logic without distortion.

**Recommended approach:** Reject a single lead object. Instead, use:
- **OC-DFG** to discover the true activity flow across object types.
- **OTG** to visualize object-type dependencies.
- **OIS** to identify convergence and divergence patterns.
- **Domain-specific flattened logs** (Order for KPI, Package for warehouse, Invoice for AR) with explicit annotation of shared/copied events.

This portfolio view accommodates both traditional PM and object-centric perspectives, reducing risk of misinterpretation.
