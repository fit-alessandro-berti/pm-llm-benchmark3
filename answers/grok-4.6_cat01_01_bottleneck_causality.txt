## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded**. Confidence: **62/100**.

Orion’s crude mean cycle time is 21.96 h versus Nova’s 15.24 h, but that contrast is not a treatment effect of the resource-group label. After `Classify`, a routing engine sends difficult work to Orion; Orion also covers almost all night work. The completed sample matches that policy: Orion is 42.9% High complexity, 92.9% night, and 64.3% compliance-required, against 12.5%, 25.0%, and 25.0% at Nova. Inside every observed complexity stratum the sign reverses (Low 7.50 vs 9.64 h; Medium 17.17 vs 19.38 h; High 31.57 vs 35.00 h). A complexity-standardized contrast on the pooled mix (12/30 Low, 10/30 Medium, 8/30 High) is about −2.5 h (Orion shorter). The same direction appears in both `wait_before_review_h` and `review_service_h`.

That is the strongest evidence that the crude gap is compositional. It is not identification. Overlap is thin: Orion has one Day case and no Medium/High Day cases; High–Nova has two cases; Low–Orion has two. Shift, arrival load, and group are nearly collinear, so a harmful night-queue or high-load mechanism bundled with the Orion label is not separately identified. A beneficial certified-reviewer effect is equally compatible with the within-stratum pattern. Only completed cases are observed, so differential completion could distort both crude and stratified means. The 30-day appeal flag is too coarse and too rare (one appeal per group) to read as quality.

A total-effect claim that “Orion causes longer times” is not supported. Any leftover group contrast is at most **partly causal through mechanisms** (night coverage, shared queues, skill mix). Compliance requirement is a case-policy attribute driven by complexity, not a mediator of group. Blindly moving 50% of future Orion cases to Nova can backfire: it would push the hard night mix onto a day-oriented team, raise Nova queues, idle Orion night capacity, and lengthen moved cases if Orion’s within-stratum speed is real. It would help only if Orion’s queue is the true bottleneck and Nova has spare capacity for the specific cases transferred.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| 3 — policy, not 50% blind shift | Only if Phases 1–2 agree, rewrite the router: move *Low-Day* volume first; keep High night with certified Orion capacity unless the lottery says otherwise | Separate easy day work from hard night work instead of moving a random half of Orion’s current mix | Routing-rule cohort (policy version), rolled out by week | Preserve night High coverage; pre-specify mix envelopes so Nova High share cannot jump in one step | Difference-in-differences on cycle and wait by complexity; DFG stability; abandonment/open-case rates | Tests whether a *policy* change, not a label change, improves the total-effect estimand management actually cares about | Rollback the new rule if High cycle or open WIP rises, or if Nova Medium/High wait exceeds the Phase 0 baseline |

**Final recommendation.** Do not reassign 50% of future Orion cases to Nova on the basis of the crude 21.96 vs 15.24 h comparison. That gap is the routing policy made visible: Orion is the night, High-complexity, compliance-heavy lane. Inside every complexity bin Orion is shorter on both wait and service, which is the opposite of a harmful group treatment, though it is not a proven benefit. A blunt transfer would move the cases Orion is staffed to absorb onto Nova’s day-oriented roster and could lengthen High-case queues while leaving night capacity idle.

Treat resource group as a bundle and intervene on the mechanism with the most hours attached — High-case waiting around classification and compliance — before changing labels. Use a small, overlap-restricted lottery on Low (then Medium Night) to test any residual group effect. Scale a routing change only after wait falls or the lottery contradicts the within-stratum pattern. Keep High night work with certified reviewers until those tests exist.

# full Cartesian so empty cells are visible
idx = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
    [["Low", "Medium", "High"], ["Day", "Night"]], names=["complexity", "shift"]
)
combo = combo.reindex(idx, fill_value=0)
print(combo.to_string())
for (cx, sh), rec in combo.iterrows():
    for g in ["Orion", "Nova"]:
        warn_poor_support(f"complexity={cx}, shift={sh}, group={g}", int(rec[g]))
    if rec["Orion"] == 0 or rec["Nova"] == 0:
        print(
            f"WARNING: no overlap for complexity={cx}, shift={sh} "
            f"(Orion={int(rec['Orion'])}, Nova={int(rec['Nova'])})"
        )
print()

# 5. Trace variants and DFGs (no fabricated timestamps)
print("=== Trace variants by group ===")
for g, sub in df.groupby("group"):
    print(f"\n-- {g} (n={len(sub)}) --")
    vc = sub["trace"].value_counts()
    for tr, c in vc.items():
        print(f"  {c:2d}  {tr}")
        warn_poor_support(f"{g} variant {tr}", int(c))

print("\n=== Directly-follows relations by group (string parse + PM4Py DFG) ===")
# parse_event_log_string expects comma-separated activity names per trace
for g, sub in df.groupby("group"):
    trace_strings = [t.replace(">", ",") for t in sub["trace"].tolist()]
    log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)
    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    print(f"\n-- {g} DFG --")
    print("  start:", dict(start_act))
    print("  end:  ", dict(end_act))
    for (a, b), c in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: (-kv[1], kv[0])):
        print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {c}")

print("\n=== Variant mix difference (share Orion minus share Nova) ===")
orion_n, nova_n = (df["group"] == "Orion").sum(), (df["group"] == "Nova").sum()
orion_v = df.loc[df["group"] == "Orion", "trace"].value_counts(normalize=True)
nova_v = df.loc[df["group"] == "Nova", "trace"].value_counts(normalize=True)
all_v = sorted(set(orion_v.index) | set(nova_v.index))
for tr in all_v:
    o, n = float(orion_v.get(tr, 0.0)), float(nova_v.get(tr, 0.0))
    print(f"  {o - n:+.3f}  Orion {o:.3f}  Nova {n:.3f}  {tr}")

print("\n=== Done. These outputs are descriptive diagnostics, not causal estimates. ===")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Within-bin case risk still differs after recorded complexity.** The single most valuable addition is the router’s actual difficulty score (or the features it used) plus reviewer identity, so matching can move beyond the three-level `complexity` label.

2. **Group, night coverage, and High load are almost collinear, so a shift/load mechanism cannot be separated from the Orion name.** The single most valuable design change is a stratified after-Classify lottery in currently empty cells (especially Medium/High Day and Low Night), with a pre-specified night WIP cap.

3. **`wait_before_review_h` mixes classify-queue and compliance waiting, so mediation through compliance is not identified.** The single most valuable extra fields are activity-level timestamps for Classify, Compliance Check, first Review start, and Request Info, without inventing clocks.

4. **The sample is conditioned on completion; differential still-open or abandoned work could reverse both crude and stratified contrasts.** The single most valuable addition is the full case population with open/abandoned status and last-activity time for right-censored survival estimates.

5. **Quality side-effects of moving hard work off certified night reviewers are not measurable from two appeals.** The single most valuable addition is a process-proximate quality record (reviewer override, leakage, complaint reason, or a 30-day outcome not contaminated by post-process handling) linked to the reviewing person and variant.
