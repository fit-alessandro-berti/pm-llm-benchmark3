## 1. Conformance semantics

- **Forbidden behavior** is an observed instance of a relation explicitly marked forbidden, within its stated scope. It is a conformance finding even if later attributed to bad data; the business event and the recorded linkage must be distinguished.
- **Missing mandatory behavior** is the absence of a mandatory relation for an in-scope instance. It is conclusive only when the denominator, scope, observation window, and abstraction are reliable.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is present in the observation but neither mandatory, typical, nor forbidden. Under the open-world norm, this is not a violation.
- **Typical-pattern absence** is a departure from a desirable common path. It warrants explanation or monitoring, but legitimate exceptions prevent automatic noncompliance.
- **Likely data-quality error** means the recorded graph is nonconforming or anomalous, but evidence suggests erroneous joining, object identity, typing, timestamps, or missing links rather than actual business conduct.
- **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the graph relation is a stricter or different proxy for the business rule—for example, directly-follows versus eventually-follows.
- **Insufficient evidence** applies when aggregate counts cannot establish instance-level scope, temporal order, completeness, or whether observations concern the same objects.

The norm is **closed-world** for forbidden relations and mandatory columns within scope: observed forbidden edges and demonstrated mandatory omissions matter. It is otherwise **open-world**: an edge omitted from the norm is not prohibited. Thus `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, rare review relations, codeath, and reopening cannot be rejected merely because they are unmodeled.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG establishes observed directly-follows relations along object lifecycles and their aggregate prevalence. It supports the N3 finding because two Order lifecycles contain a dispatch directly after cancellation. However, raw traces are still needed to validate timestamps and object correlation.

It cannot equate absence of a direct edge with absence of eventual behavior. N1 says Orders should **eventually** enter validation, while the summary uses `Create -> Validate` as a direct-follow proxy. An intermediate review would remove that direct edge even though validation later occurs. Conversely, an aggregate graph path from creation to validation does not prove that each of the two affected Orders followed it. Treating an eventually-expected rule as directly-follows is therefore a major false-positive risk.

N4 is a stronger candidate gap because the denominator is explicitly 200 externally shipped Packages, but missing events, repacking, or intervening events remain possible. N2 and N5 are typical, so their counts do not establish violations. The 14 invoices without successors also depend on maturity and observation horizon.

### OTG

An OTG establishes that object types participate in interaction, cobirth, or codeath relations and provides aggregate weights. It cannot normally establish that every scoped event had the required objects, nor whether two similarly sized weights refer to the same event instances.

Thus T1 and T2 appear globally present, but this does not cure the ET–OT omissions: 1450 Order–Item interactions can coexist with two Add Item events missing Orders, and 200 Package–Delivery interactions can coexist with two Dispatch events lacking Delivery links. The interactions may originate from other events or use different weight semantics.

T6 is different because the observed cobirth relation itself is forbidden, subject to validating how birth and “same business event” were inferred. The seven codeath edges are unregulated, not violations.

### ET–OT

ET–OT establishes event-type-to-object-type associations and, with reliable event totals, can identify missing mandatory links. It supports the E2, E6, and E8 gaps and the E10 forbidden recorded association.

It cannot establish temporal sequencing, eventual outcomes, valid object identity, or whether an attribute should count as an object relation. A carrier-reference attribute does not satisfy E6 unless the norm or data model explicitly permits it as a Delivery substitute.

A relation may therefore conform at one abstraction and remain suspicious at another: the Package–Delivery OTG edge exists with weight 200, while only 198 Dispatch events have Delivery objects. Similarly, Invoice–Payment interaction is merely typical under T3, but each non-unapplied `Record Payment` event is subject to mandatory E8.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory:** N1 has 98 direct matches and two unresolved eventual-validation cases; they become violations only if no eventual validation occurred for in-scope standard Orders. N4 has two candidate gaps. T1/T2 are present in aggregate but do not prove per-event compliance. E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 appear satisfied; E2 lacks two Order links, E6 lacks two Delivery links, and E8 lacks one Invoice link unless the event is validly retyped as unapplied cash.
- **Forbidden:** N3 has two observed cases; T6 has one cobirth; E10 has one recorded link. All are conformance findings in the snapshot, although E10 is probably a data defect and T6 may depend on birth-inference semantics.
- **Typical:** N2’s 83 direct closures show the common path without requiring it universally. Under N5, 92 payments, four write-offs, and 14 unresolved invoices are not automatically violations. T3’s 92 Invoice–Payment interactions are normal; the 93rd payment is allowed by T3 only as unapplied cash, but still fails E8 until explicitly typed.
- **Unregulated or unspecified:** `Validate -> Issue Invoice`, `Create -> Manual Compliance Review`, Manual Review–Customer, Order–Customer, Order–RiskCase, Manual Review–RiskCase, `Reopen -> Close`, and Package–Delivery codeath are nonviolating under this norm. They could change category only if another applicable policy regulates them.

**Immediate containment**

1. Prioritize the two N3 cases: block further fulfilment, determine whether packages are still interceptable, and preserve source timestamps and associations.
2. Quarantine the T6 cobirth case from automated reconciliation until Order and Payment origins are verified.
3. Isolate the E10 sensor-derived Invoice link from financial reporting; its likely data origin does not make downstream contamination harmless.

**Data repair**

- Correct the reused-identifier join and assess whether similar Sensor–financial links exist.
- Resolve the two missing Delivery objects from authoritative carrier data; do not fabricate objects merely to satisfy E6.
- Recover the two missing Add Item–Order links.
- Classify the unmatched payment as explicitly unapplied cash or attach the correct Invoice.
- Reconcile OTG weights of 1450 and 200 with ET–OT event-level counts.

**Process investigation**

- Reconstruct the two cancelled Order lifecycles and the cobirth source event.
- Inspect the two N4 package traces and the two N1 Order traces, including intermediate and late events.
- Age the 14 invoices with no successor before treating them as operational exceptions.

**Norm revision**

Adopt the clarifications in Section 6, especially eventual-versus-direct semantics, per-event cardinalities, exception typing, and cobirth derivation.

**Monitoring**

Implement status-aware controls: zero tolerance for N3, T6, and E10; per-instance completeness checks for E2/E6/E8; eventual-validation monitoring for N1; and trend-only reporting for typical relations. Do not escalate reviews, codeath, reopening, or write-offs merely because they are rare.
