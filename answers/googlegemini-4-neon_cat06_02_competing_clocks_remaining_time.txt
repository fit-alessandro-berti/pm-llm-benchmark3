## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Key calendar anchors from $t = 0$ (**Fri 18 Sep, 15:30**): Fri chem/micro close = **+2.5 h** (18:00); Fri QA close = **+4.5 h** (20:00); Sat QA = **+17.5 to +21.5 h** (09:00–13:00); Mon open = **+64.5 h** (Mon 21 Sep, 08:00); Mon chem/micro close = **+74.5 h** (18:00); Mon QA close = **+76.5 h** (20:00); Tue open = **+88.5 h** (Tue 22 Sep, 08:00); Wed open = **+112.5 h**; Thu open = **+136.5 h**; Fri 25 Sep open = **+160.5 h**; Mon 28 Sep open = **+232.5 h**.

### Shared parallel-branch mechanics & QA queue baseline

- **Why Chemistry cannot finish Friday (without overtime):** Only $2.5\text{ h}$ remain until Friday 18:00 ($5.0$ analyst-hours across 2 analysts). Current work remaining is $0.4\text{–}0.9\text{ h}$ (Analyst B) and $1.2\text{–}2.0\text{ h}$ (Analyst A). Even under risk-priority (only 1 urgent sample ahead of B-417), Analyst B takes the urgent sample at 15:54–16:24 and Analyst A starts B-417 at 16:42–17:30—leaving at most $1.3\text{ h}$ before 18:00, which is strictly less than the $1.5\text{ h}$ minimum service time. B-417 therefore **must pause over the weekend** and resume Monday 08:00.
- **QA queue evolution before Monday join:** At Friday 15:30, 5 batches wait in QA. Over Friday 15:30–20:00 ($4.5\text{ h}$) and Saturday 09:00–13:00 ($4.0\text{ h}$), **1 reviewer** works $8.5\text{ h}$ at an average service time of $\sim 2.3\text{ h}$ ($\mu \approx 0.43\text{ /h}$), while arrivals average $\lambda = 0.35\text{ /h}$ ($\rho \approx 0.80\text{–}0.88$). Expected completions ($\sim 3.7$) barely outpace expected arrivals ($\sim 3.0$), leaving an expected **Monday 08:00 backlog of $\sim 4.3$ batches** (wide plausible range **1–8 batches** due to overdispersion and heavy-tailed review times). From Monday 08:00, **2 reviewers** double capacity ($\mu_{\text{eff}} \approx 0.87\text{ /h}$, $\rho \approx 0.40$), draining the backlog at $\sim 0.52\text{ batches/h}$ until reaching a bursty steady state (mean wait $\sim 0.5\text{–}1.5\text{ h}$, tail $3\text{–}6\text{ h}$) by Monday mid-afternoon.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Deviation Investigation opening and type (doc-only vs. 3–7 calendar-day laboratory investigation)** — **Path uncertainty** *(with secondary service-time and calendar uncertainty)*.
   - Drives the widest time spread in the entire process: separates Monday/Tuesday release (~71–96 h) from mid-week doc-only rework (~116–144 h) and the multi-day/second-weekend laboratory investigation tail (~160–290 h), while governing Batch Rejection risk.
2. **Unknown upstream temperature-excursion flag (sensor gap)** — **Observation uncertainty** and **Dependence uncertainty**.
   - Acts as a latent common cause: confirmation ($P = 0.20\text{–}0.40$) simultaneously elevates chemistry rerun risk, microbiology contamination risk, and—even if both lab branches pass—directly raises QA Deviation Investigation probability from $4\text{–}10\%$ to $30\text{–}55\%$, creating strong positive correlation across parallel and downstream anomalies.
3. **Microbiology contamination indication coupled with weekend on-call authorization** — **Path uncertainty** and **Calendar uncertainty**.
   - A contamination indication ($5\text{–}12\%$ baseline, higher under excursion) adds $20\text{–}30\text{ h}$ of confirmation incubation plus weekday readout, immediately making microbiology the dominant clock and triggering elevated investigation/rejection risk. Weekend on-call authorization ($25\text{–}45\%$) has low leverage on a clean path (blocked by Sunday QA closure) but high leverage here—saving ~12–18 wall-clock hours on confirmation start.
4. **QA backlog evolution and congestion at the future parallel join** — **Queue uncertainty** *(amplified by service-time uncertainty from heavy-tailed review durations and overdispersed arrivals)*.
   - With 5 batches currently waiting and 1 reviewer near saturation ($\rho \approx 0.80\text{–}0.88$) across Friday afternoon and Saturday morning, the Monday 08:00 queue could plausibly span 1–8 batches. Heavy congestion can push Monday afternoon completions over the Monday 20:00 QA closure into Tuesday morning (+12 h jump).
5. **Chemistry queue discipline (FIFO 55% vs. risk-priority 45%) and weekday closing cliffs** — **Queue uncertainty** and **Calendar uncertainty** *(plus path uncertainty if a rerun occurs)*.
   - Determines whether clean chemistry finishes Monday morning (~09:30–10:30, neck-and-neck with routine microbiology) or Monday afternoon (~13:30–15:30, dominating the smooth join), and strongly determines whether a priority rerun completes Monday afternoon or hits the Monday 18:00 lab close for a 14-hour overnight pause to Tuesday morning.
