# 1. Model scope and state variables

Build a calendar-aware, discrete-event simulation from submission through permit, rejection, or a modeled appeal and final closure. For D1 and D7, also report applicant time before submission; otherwise those policies could appear to save time by moving it outside the measured boundary. Simulate individual applications, shared work queues, staff shifts, statutory timers, applicant responses, and groups of related cases. The simplified process tree is a control-flow starting point, not a complete executable specification: its visible rework labels do not match every repeat in the excerpt, it omits appeals, and it has no cross-case or calendar semantics.

Each case carries submission time, case type, district and location, architect and related-project identifiers, value, fire/environment/notice flags, any verified prior-violation and environmental-sensitivity attributes, current enabled tasks, completion times of prerequisite tasks, queue-entry times, assigned staff, applicant readiness, document/review/inspection revisions, objection state, deadlines, and disposition. Missing risk attributes remain unknown rather than being silently set to “no.” Track outstanding Planning, Fire, Environmental, notice, and Inspection results separately; Decision drafting is enabled only when every *applicable* result is complete. Keep minimum public-notice time as a statutory timer, with extensions as a separate latent process.

Queues include completeness, each specialist review, scheduling, district visit routes, drafting, legal work, and aging-case priority. Resources have skills, capacity, shifts, leave, holidays, council interruptions, and non-permit commitments. Applicant and consultant responses are delay processes, not municipal staff service. Inspectors have travel, visit duration, no-show/rebooking, and geographic batching constraints. The state also includes backlog and queue ages by type and district, daily resource occupancy, and future booked visits.

Cross-case mechanisms share architect-side preparation/response capacity, generate clustered submissions, optionally link objections in the same neighborhood or project, and group geographically compatible inspections. A common architect or district alone is evidence for a possible link, not proof of one. Use explicit linkage when available and sensitivity scenarios for unobserved links. Reproduce the observed 18-month case mix and seasonal/weekday patterns before evaluating changes.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

Ingest the full log into pandas, parse timestamps and attributes, and use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, and `time:timestamp`. Convert to an event log for variants, directly-follows and performance graphs, arrivals, and conformance diagnostics. Parse the supplied tree as a provisional process model; compare alignments/token replay with actual traces, then revise its rework and appeal representation before using it for routing. PM4Py organizes and checks the evidence; a separate discrete-event engine handles calendars, queues, demand, and interactions. The two illustrative cases are a schema example, not an estimation sample.

Fit submissions by hour/day/month and case type using an inhomogeneous count model with weekday and seasonal terms, overdispersion, and architect-level effects or clustered batches. Preserve the stated March–May and September–November 1.7× and Monday-versus-Wednesday 2.2× contrasts as calibration targets, not universal multipliers to stack indiscriminately. Estimate mix and covariate distributions jointly, including district, value, architect, and review flags. With only 18 months, test alternative seasonal amplitudes and trends rather than assuming a precisely known annual cycle.

Estimate task service distributions by role, case type, district, complexity, and rework number from cases with both start and completion timestamps, supplemented by roster/booking records or a short time study. Use partial pooling for sparse groups. Completion-to-completion gaps include queueing, off-hours, parallel work, and external delays; PM4Py performance DFG edges therefore are *not* service times. Reconstruct each task's earliest enablement from its applicable predecessors, then estimate `start − enablement` as waiting and `completion − start` as elapsed service where starts exist. Convert elapsed service to effort only with calendar and interruption evidence. For missing starts, treat service and waiting as latent components constrained by totals, known calendars, sampled start-time data, and the supplied median/p90 summaries. Quantify the resulting identification uncertainty. Audit whether the notice event denotes the start or end of the statutory period. The excerpt also shows clerk, fire, and inspection completions on weekends outside their stated calendars; check posting delays, overtime, timezone, and roster errors before treating these as worked hours.

Estimate conditional branch and repeat probabilities with models for type, value, district, flags, architect, and prior loop history; include missing-document, inspection, legal/planning rework, rejection, and appeal paths. Routing flags may be incomplete because area, sensitive-district data, objections, and prior violations are incomplete: link registries or use an “unknown” stratum and scenario bounds. Estimate applicant document-return and consultant-report times separately, with right censoring for open cases. Model notice as at least 14 days, plus an objection-related extension whose incidence and duration are uncertain because objection events are absent. Check HR calendars against work records, and estimate non-permit utilization rather than interpreting all idle time as available capacity. Match travel times to geography and visit schedules; estimate batching's travel saving and added first-case wait. Calibrate all parameters jointly against case-type/variant p50 and p90 durations, daily queue/backlog patterns, route frequencies, and resource occupancy, avoiding a fit that gets total duration right for the wrong reason.

# 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals

Submissions are stochastic and clustered within architects, with conditional case mix and district/value profiles. Appointment slots in D7 change the submission-time distribution and may create pre-submission delay; they do not erase demand.

### Service times

Human effort is positive, skewed, task- and case-dependent, and correlated within a difficult case. Portal/system events use small processing times; zero-duration “Receive missing documents” marks an external response, not zero applicant delay. Observed p90 values constrain, but do not determine, tail shapes.

### Routing

Observed flags apply when reliable; missing area, sensitive district, or violations require linked data or probabilistic routing. D2 eligibility requires value below EUR 50,000 **and verified no prior violations**, plus legal authority to waive inspection. D8 changes priority/intensity only within statutory review rules. Parallel reviews can overlap, but drafting joins all applicable results.

### Resources/calendars

Use the stated FTE and weekday schedules, Monday portal-support clerk, specialist availability, 40% environmental non-permit allocation, field-visit days, travel, holidays, leave, and council weeks. FTE is capacity over time, not necessarily a count of interchangeable people. Where daily hours are unspecified, 09:00–17:00 is only a provisional value to verify and vary. Test uncertain effective capacity and urgent legal exceptions.

### Rework

Rework probability and effort depend on the problem found and prior attempts; loops have explicit stopping/escalation rules. Appeal is represented even though absent from the tree. Unstructured reasons are imperfect labels, so use manual coding samples and sensitivity ranges.

### Applicant/third-party response

Document returns, consultant reports, site readiness, and objections have separate calendars and heavy-tailed delays. D1 may increase preparation effort before submission. D9 may change when objections become known, but cannot remove the legal notice minimum.

### Cross-case dependencies

Architect batches, linked neighborhood objections, and inspector routes can correlate cases. Link rates and effects are estimated when identifiers/geography permit, otherwise varied; do not assume that every same-architect case is causally linked.

### Non-stationary demand

The seasonal and weekday patterns recur in the baseline, while stress runs change peak intensity, burstiness, or mix. Future trends and policy-induced demand are uncertain.

### Missing data

Start times, objection events, risk attributes, and rare emergency cases are incomplete. Preserve unknown values, use external administrative records or audited samples where possible, handle open cases as censored, and report results under plausible missingness mechanisms. The flattened log may miss dependencies; no scenario treats an unobserved objection as an observed event.

# 4. Experiment matrix

All levels below are **illustrative test levels**, to be refined with operations staff and legal review. Run each policy under identical baseline demand draws and paired random streams; re-sample policy-dependent outcomes through the same underlying random quantiles. Evaluate main effects and interactions, not just individual policy rankings.

| Experiment | Factors and levels to simulate | Expected trade-off to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Current routing, staffing, shifts, seasonality, and calendar interruptions | Reference duration, backlog, quality, and cost |
| D1 Document checker | Relative missing-document-loop reduction 15/30/45%; applicant preparation +0.5/1/2 days | Less clerk rework and downstream delay versus applicant effort and access burden |
| D2 Low-risk inspection waiver | Skip visits only for verified eligible Simple renovations below EUR 50,000; uptake/eligibility 50/75/100% of verified eligible cases | Freed inspector time versus missed-defect risk and unequal eligibility |

| D5 Parallel legal pre-check | Commercial/Complex cases: pre-check at planning start or after first review; 0.5/1/2 h added early effort; retain final legal gate if required | Earlier issue detection versus duplicated legal work and pre-check staleness |

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

If a reliable opening backlog snapshot exists, initialize the twin from it. Otherwise simulate a 12-month seasonal warm-up before a 24-month measurement window, then check that backlog age and utilization no longer depend materially on the initial empty state. Preserve calendar dates and seasonality; do not delete the first few weeks of each peak as an arbitrary warm-up. Track all cases submitted in the measurement window through closure or a common administrative follow-up cutoff. For open cases, report age and survival-adjusted duration estimates as well as completed-case results; never call incomplete cases fast by excluding them.

Start with 100 independent replications per parameter set, using named streams for arrivals, case attributes, service, applicant responses, objections, and routing. Use the same exogenous stream/seed across compared policies and paired differences; add replications until important p90 and SLA differences have usable precision (for example, a 95% interval half-width under 5 days or 5 percentage points, where feasible). Report 95% replication-based confidence intervals for paired policy differences, plus bootstrap or posterior intervals for calibrated parameters. Draw full parameter sets from a fitted bootstrap/posterior in an outer loop, with simulation randomness inside, so uncertainty from missing starts and objections is visible.

Use conditional empirical or flexible lognormal/gamma mixtures with a validated heavy-tail component for external responses, objections, and complex rework; retain extreme observations and censoring. Check p90/p95, mean, backlog, and extreme-case counts separately. Do not force every activity into an exponential distribution or winsorize the long tail merely to stabilize a mean. Include structural sensitivity for hidden objections, linkage rates, calendar availability, D2 eligibility/safety, travel savings, and demand growth.

# 6. Validation strategy

- **Face validation:** Walk clerks, planners, inspectors, legal staff, and applicants through representative Simple, Commercial, and Complex traces, including parallel reviews, public notice, rework, appeals, council weeks, and district routes. Confirm statutory rules and D2/D8 eligibility with policy owners.
- **Event-log replay:** Compare generated case paths and conditional activity counts with PM4Py variants and conformance diagnostics. Investigate deviations, including the tree's rework labels and missing appeal path; do not force low-fitness traces into impossible routes.
- **Distributional validation:** On held-out baseline data compare by type, district, and submission month the p50/p90/p95 total time, stage gaps, response times, notice lengths, rework counts, rejection rates, and weekly arrival counts. Use predictive intervals and coverage, not only average error.
- **Resource-utilization validation:** Match roster-adjusted busy time, weekday queues, inspector field/travel split, fire Tuesday–Thursday restriction, environmental non-permit share, and legal council-week downtime. Check that simulated completions do not occur while a required pool is closed.
- **Bottleneck validation:** Check that simulated queue ages and delay contributions reproduce known Monday completeness spikes, environmental/legal queues, and D3/D5 inspection travel. Perturb capacity slightly; a bottleneck should respond in the expected direction.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** Fit on earlier months and predict later months, preserving the seasonal sequence; repeat with rolling cutoffs where feasible. Compare backlog and duration forecasts during peaks and after holidays. Validate new intervention effects with a limited, instrumented pilot before high-stakes rollout, because historical replay cannot validate a policy never observed.

# 7. Decision criteria

Agree targets with the municipality before selecting a policy. An illustrative scorecard uses submission-to-closure median, p90 and p95, applicant pre-submission plus submitted-case time, and the share meeting type-specific provisional SLAs: Simple 30 days, New residential 90, Commercial 120, Complex variance 240. Report absolute changes and 95% intervals, with a primary target such as at least a 10% reduction in overall p90 and a 5-percentage-point improvement in peak-month SLA compliance. These thresholds are decision rules to negotiate, not claims about current legal deadlines.

Apply safeguards: no case type or district p90 deterioration exceeding 5% or 5 days without an explicitly accepted reason; no widening of district or top-architect-versus-other SLA gaps by more than 3 percentage points; and no transfer of more than 2 days of median effort/wait to applicants without accounting for it. Report access for small architects, especially under D7, and the number of unresolved cases past each SLA. Track missing-document and inspection rework, appeals, reversals, objections, and rejection quality, not just speed. D2 requires an independently supported safety/legal non-inferiority margin for serious missed violations; if prior-violation data or safety evidence is unavailable, it is not ready for automatic adoption.

Keep sustainable pool utilization roughly in the 65–85% range over working time, with explicit allowance for bursty specialist work; watch any prolonged >90% utilization, growing month-end backlog, and aging >30/60/90 days. Compare staffing cost, overtime, travel, technology cost, and applicant cost per permit. Prefer interventions that meet safeguards and yield a positive improvement under central assumptions and most plausible parameter/stress draws; report probability of meeting each target and worst-case tail/backlog, not an exact optimum. If policies trade safety, fairness, and speed, present the Pareto set and pilot the uncertain candidates.

# 8. Python skeleton

The excerpt supplies completion timestamps only. The code deliberately leaves `service_minutes` and `waiting_minutes` missing until reliable starts and task-enable times are available; `observed_gap_minutes` is only a descriptive inter-event gap. Production calibration uses the full log and external roster/risk/linkage tables.

def load_excerpt() -> pd.DataFrame:
    # For the real log, replace this construction with pd.read_csv(path).
    rows = [dict(**{"case:concept:name": cid, "concept:name": act,
                    "time:timestamp": stamp, "org:resource": resource},
                 **CASE_ATTRS[cid]) for cid, act, stamp, resource in EVENTS]
    df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )


TREE = """->(
  'Submit application',
  *(->('Completeness check',X(tau,->('Request missing documents',
      'Receive missing documents'))),'Completeness rework'),
  +('Planning review',X(tau,'Fire-safety review'),
    X(tau,'Environmental review')),
  X(tau,'Public-notice period'),
  *(->('Inspection scheduling','Site inspection'),'Inspection rework'),
  'Decision drafting',X(tau,'Legal check'),
  X('Issue permit','Reject application')
)"""


def inspect_log(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict[str, Any]:
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    arrival_average = pm4py.get_case_arrival_average(log)
    try:
        tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
    except Exception as exc:
        # Tree text is illustrative; validate syntax/version and revise loops.
        tree = None
        print(f"Illustrative tree needs review: {exc}")

    timed = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).copy()
    timed["observed_gap_minutes"] = (
        timed.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
        .diff().dt.total_seconds() / 60
    )
    # In a richer log, starts are observed and enablement is reconstructed from
    # applicable predecessors, including joins and rework; neither is in the excerpt.
    for col in ("start:timestamp", "enabled_at"):
        if col in timed:
            timed[col] = pd.to_datetime(timed[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
        else:
            timed[col] = pd.to_datetime([pd.NaT] * len(timed), utc=True)
    timed["service_minutes"] = (
        timed["time:timestamp"] - timed["start:timestamp"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    timed["waiting_minutes"] = (
        timed["start:timestamp"] - timed["enabled_at"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    # Performance DFG edges and observed gaps are inter-completion times,
    # never substitutes for the two columns above.
    return dict(variants=variants, dfg=dfg, starts=start_activities,
                ends=end_activities, performance_dfg=performance_dfg,
                case_arrival_average=arrival_average, tree=tree, timed=timed)
