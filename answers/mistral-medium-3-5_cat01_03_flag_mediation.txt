**Framework**: We use **interventional indirect effects (IIE)** for mediators (e.g., manual review) because natural effects are invalid under interference. **Controlled direct effects (CDE)** are used for triage timing under P1 (suppressing the flag). Separable effects are attempted for queue waits but are invalid due to shared resources.

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| P0 | Fast triage for flagged | Congestion in manual review | Long cycle time for flagged | Low (current) | High (shared queues) | Strong | Baseline |
| P1 | Slower triage for flagged | Reduced manual review congestion | Shorter cycle time for flagged | Moderate (missed risk) | Low | Moderate (cross-world) | Pilot if decision quality robust |
| P2 | Fast triage | Reduced manual review congestion | Shorter cycle time | High (missed escalations) | Low | Weak (mediator-outcome confounding) | Not recommended |
| P3 | Fast triage | Protected manual review capacity | Shorter cycle time for flagged | Low | Moderate (unflagged may wait) | Moderate | Strong candidate |
| P4 | Fast triage | Reduced escalation congestion | Shorter cycle time for escalated | Moderate (false approves) | Low | Moderate | Pilot with outcome monitoring |

**Pilot Selection**: **P3 (protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases)**
- **Eligibility**: Flagged cases with `complexity=High` or `risk_score > 0.8`.
- **Assignment**: Randomize 50% of eligible cases to protected queue (dedicated reviewers) vs. shared queue.
- **Guardrail**: Stop if protected queue utilization > 90% or unflagged cases’ manual wait > 4h.
- **Outcomes**:
  - Primary: Cycle time (flagged cases).
  - Secondary: Decision outcome (Approve/Reject), manual review service time.
- **Data**: Timestamps for all stages, `risk_score`, `complexity`, reviewer ID.
- **Stopping**: If cycle time reduction < 10% or decision quality (e.g., false approves) degrades by > 5%.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk**: If `underlying_risk` is strongly correlated with `flag` and independently increases cycle time (e.g., flagged cases are inherently slower), the observed delay is not causal.
2. **Queue interference**: If unflagged cases’ cycle times increase when flagged cases are prioritized (due to shared queues), P3 may harm overall performance.
3. **Decision quality**: If suppressing the flag (P1) leads to more false approves (e.g., high-risk cases skipped), the policy may be harmful despite shorter cycle times.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If `triage_done` or `manual_start` timestamps are recorded inconsistently (e.g., manual_start is when the reviewer *begins* vs. when the case *enters* the queue), waiting times are misestimated.
5. **Positivity violation**: If no unflagged cases with `complexity=High` exist, comparisons are invalid (lack of overlap).
6. **Reviewer behavior change**: If reviewers slow down when they know a case is flagged (e.g., due to perceived importance), the manual service time effect is not purely structural.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Observed**: Flagged cases have faster triage (18m vs. 2h45m to start; 12m vs. 42m service) but longer manual review (6h48m vs. 2h when it occurs) and escalation (5h vs. NA). Total cycle time is longer for flagged cases (18h vs. 6h30m). High `complexity` and `arrival_load` correlate with longer delays.

**Plausible mechanism**: The flag triggers **priority triage** (direct acceleration) but also **mandatory manual review and escalation** (mediated delay). Shared queues mean flagged cases congest downstream stages, affecting all cases. `Complexity` and unobserved `underlying_risk` may confound the relationship.

**Unidentified**: The **causal effect of the flag** on cycle time cannot be separated from:
- Confounding by `underlying_risk` (imperfectly captured by `risk_score`).
- Interference via shared queues (flagged cases slow others).
- Mediator-outcome confounding (manual review affects escalation).

**Next evidence**: **Pilot P3 (protected manual-review capacity)** is most actionable. It isolates the queue interference pathway while preserving triage acceleration. If cycle time drops for flagged cases without harming unflagged cases or decision quality, the mechanism is validated. If not, hidden risk or reviewer behavior may dominate. Randomization and outcome monitoring are critical to distinguish these.
