## 1. Target definition

**Primary target:** At Application Started, estimate the probability that an application is activated by the originating lender within 180 days of the prediction time and that the activated account defaults within 90 days of Account Activated. Re-estimate after event 2 and event 3 for cases still in the predecision risk set. The unit is an application at a specified prediction landmark; the event-1 analysis covers every started application. Each later landmark has its own explicitly reported denominator. The estimand is P(activation within 180 days and default_90d = 1 | events and attributes available at the landmark, calendar period, observed lending policy). Report this probability alongside the probabilities of activation without 90-day default, rejection, withdrawal, transfer, and no activation by day 180. These are process outcomes, not interchangeable “non-default” labels. A default label is ascertained only after the 90-day window and the feed delay.

This is a realized-exposure, policy-dependent risk: it answers how often an early application becomes a defaulting account *through this lender's observed process*. It does not estimate how a rejected applicant would perform if lent to. Transfer is a competing exit for the originating lender; a network-wide target would require reliable cross-platform linkage.

A credible alternative is 90-day default conditional on eventual activation. Its population is activated accounts, and its estimand conditions on a future selection event; it can support monitoring of accounts likely to reach activation, but it cannot be advertised as default risk for every application. A time-to-default analysis after activation is another useful account-level alternative with right censoring. “Default if every applicant were activated” is a separate, policy-specific counterfactual estimand requiring assumptions about selection, treatment consistency, and overlap that this export cannot establish.

## 2. Cohort construction

Start from the 48,000 application identifiers in the full event and status source, not the 21,600-row labeled export. Preserve a first Application Started timestamp, original channel and region, event-time state changes, activation time, terminal exit, source-system history, and label and label-availability times. Use 15 February 2026 as the outcome/warehouse as-of cutoff, but recognize that the supplied event history stops on 31 December 2025. Do not infer an absence of January–February 2026 transitions from that event file; obtaining status updates or a later event extract is necessary before treating such cases as resolved. Restrict features to the prediction landmark even when cohort outcomes are assembled later.

| Snapshot group | Cases | Outcome treatment at 15 February 2026 |
|---|---:|---|
| Activated, mature non-default | 18,900 | Observed default_90d = 0, conditional on an observed outcome feed |
| Activated, mature default | 2,700 | Observed default_90d = 1, conditional on an observed outcome feed |
| Activated, outcome/feed unresolved | 3,600 | Pending 90-day horizon, delayed feed, or possibly missing feed; separate these by dates rather than assigning a label |
| Rejected | 9,100 | Competing application exit; default under activation is unobserved/undefined |
| Withdrawn | 5,700 | Competing application exit; default under activation is unobserved/undefined |
| Transferred | 2,900 | Competing exit for the originating lender; downstream outcome selectively unobserved |
| Still open | 5,100 | Process follow-up is right censored if the relevant horizon has not elapsed; a completed 180-day activation window establishes only “no activation by 180 days” |
| **Total** | **48,000** | |

For an event-1 analysis, include all valid starts. For event-2/3 models, include only cases that actually reached that predecision event while still at risk, and report that change in denominator. For the 180-day endpoint, an activation must occur after the landmark and by landmark + 180 days; a prior rejection, withdrawal, or transfer is a competing exit. An activated case needs 90 days after activation and a label available by the analysis cutoff to enter an observed default category. The latest *conservatively fully ascertainable* event-1 starts at this snapshot are approximately mid-April 2025: 180 days to activate, 90 days for the outcome, and up to 35 days of feed lag. Individual cases can resolve sooner. Later starts are retained with their actual censoring or pending state, not selected solely because they matured quickly.

Reconcile duplicate or reused case IDs, event order, and contradictory terminal/activation paths before modeling. Only about 60% of transferred cases have a cross-platform link; preserve the original transfer event and audit linkage coverage by cohort and channel. Do not silently stitch linked IDs while leaving unlinked transfers behind.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

- **Ordinary right censoring:** An open case may be observed only until the event-history cutoff, or a post-activation account may have less than 90 days of follow-up. Record its last known state and observation time. If 180 days have elapsed without activation and event capture is complete through that date, “no activation by 180 days” is observed for this endpoint even if the process remains open.
- **Delayed labels:** A completed 90-day horizon is not enough when the external feed can arrive 7–35 days later. Use label_available_at to determine what was knowable as of each backtest cutoff. Treat labels inside the expected lag as pending. A feed absent after the expected deadline is an overdue/missing feed, not an automatic non-default.
- **Selection through activation:** Default_90d is defined and observed only on the activated branch. Model activation as a transition in the primary process target; do not use inverse censoring weights to pretend that rejected people had an observed default outcome. The 2,700/21,600 = 12.5% rate describes labeled activated accounts, not all applications or all activated accounts.
- **Transfers:** For the originating-lender estimand they are a competing exit. For a network-wide estimand they may instead be continuing cases with incomplete linkage and follow-up; the 40% unlinked portion can cause informative missingness.
- **Permanently missing feeds:** Among activated accounts, particularly partner-originated accounts, labels may never arrive. Separate these from ordinary horizon censoring using activation date, expected feed deadline, source, and later refreshes. The supplied aggregate of 3,600 cannot be partitioned reliably without those dates and refreshes.

Primary analysis: estimate process transition or discrete-time cause-specific probabilities from application state to activation and competing exits, then the conditional 90-day outcome probability after activation. Combine transitions to produce the 180-day cumulative probability of activation followed by default. Handle incomplete observation with risk-set censoring or inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting where follow-up times and covariates support it. For missing activated-account feeds, use an explicit observation model conditional on prelabel source, channel, period, and other measured predictors; report weight distributions and overlap. Do not fit the outcome model on the 21,600 labeled accounts and present its score as an all-application probability.

Sensitivity analyses should vary the assumed default rate in overdue feed-missing activated accounts, including worst/best plausible bounds, and compare transfer-as-competing with a linked, network-wide analysis while bounding outcomes for unlinked transfers. A separate activated-only model can show how conditioning on activation changes conclusions. Timestamp order, observed feed-lag distributions, linkage coverage, and measured covariate overlap are testable. Conditional independent censoring and missing-at-random are only partly probeable through observed history and subsequent refreshes. Outcomes under forced activation, outcomes of unlinked transfers, and outcomes behind permanently missing feeds are fundamentally untestable from this snapshot alone.

## 4. Prefix extraction

At each prediction timestamp, first cut the event stream at that timestamp and then compute features. For fixed event counts, generate k = 1, 2, and 3 chronological prefixes; PM4Py's get_prefixes_from_log(log, length=k) truncates long traces but also returns shorter traces, so require exactly k observed events for the kth-event landmark. Event 1 is the full-population baseline. Do not pad a short case with later events or classify it as a negative. If an application closes before event k, it leaves that landmark's risk set, with its exit reported separately. Specify a deterministic ordering or quarantine ambiguous same-time events when no source sequence ID exists.

For elapsed-time landmarks, use, for example, 1 and 7 days after Application Started. Include only events with event time no later than the landmark *and* records available by that time. Cases with no new events remain eligible if still active; elapsed silence is itself an observable feature. Cases that exited before a time landmark are recorded as exits, not retrospectively scored as active. Repeated document-request/review loops remain separate events; count loops and time spent in them instead of collapsing them into one review. Apply a versioned, event-time-specific map of pre/post-migration activity names, retain raw values for audit, and remove migration duplicates only with a reliable event ID or documented duplicate rule.

## 5. Process-aware features

| Source | Valid examples at the landmark |
|---|---|
| Static case attributes as first known | Channel, region, requested amount, application start date; verify that each was recorded before the landmark |
| Prefix-derived control flow | Last canonical activity, observed activity sequence, counts of KYC checks and document requests, review returns, number of unique stages, current predecision state |
| Prefix-derived timing | Time since start, time since last event, observed inter-event gaps, elapsed time in document loops, pending time since latest request; never use a task's eventual completion time before it happens |
| Prefix-derived resource/queue | Resource currently handling the case, handoffs so far, queue length or active workload at that time if reconstructible from contemporaneous events |
| Cohort/calendar | Application month, day-of-week, channel, source system, policy regime at the landmark, migration regime; allow regime-specific calibration and interactions |
| Historical reference data | Resource experience computed only from prior completed work, prior-period process frequencies or DFG statistics, historical queue baselines and region-level rates fitted within each training fold |

Risk_score can be used only at the value last available by the landmark, with missingness and update count as possible features if those updates were actually observable. Historical aggregates must be recomputed inside each temporal training fold and applied forward; whole-log process maps or resource statistics would leak future operational changes.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| final_limit | Final decision and acceptance proxy | None at early predecision landmarks | Drop | Reveals downstream underwriting and activation selection |
| decision_reason | Recorded at/after Accept or Reject | Only if prediction is deliberately made after that decision, with a different target | Drop here | Directly exposes a future competing outcome |
| collection_status | Updated after activation and may reveal default | No predecision value is valid | Drop | Direct target leakage |
| Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded, and other future events | Future path and target disclosure | Event time and record-availability time must be no later than landmark | Truncate | Prefix construction must precede feature construction |
| documents_total nightly snapshot | May count uploads later on the same day | Rebuild cumulative count from individually timestamped uploads received by landmark | Repair or drop | Calendar-day equality is not sufficient |
| Updated risk_score | Later updates may incorporate review decisions or future documents | Latest version recorded by landmark; retain version time | Repair | Only contemporaneous score is predictive input |
| Resource experience at extraction | Encodes work performed after the prediction | Reconstruct from work completed strictly before landmark, using fold-safe history | Repair or drop | Extraction-time value uses the future |
| default_90d and label_available_at | Outcome and its observability can reveal default or branch selection | Use only in outcome ascertainment as of evaluation cutoff, never in X | Drop from features | Missingness/availability can itself leak state |
| source_system and activity names | Migration-period proxy and inconsistent semantics | Use contemporaneous value and a versioned canonical map | Keep with audit | Needed for shift assessment; raw names may fragment equivalent paths |
| requested_amount, channel, region | A supposedly static value might be filled or corrected later | Use first value actually recorded by landmark | Keep if verified | Case-level repetition in a snapshot does not prove early availability |

The audit should also inspect record creation times: an event timestamp earlier than the landmark is insufficient if that row was backfilled later and would not have existed at deployment.

## 7. Validation design

Split by Application Started date, group all prefixes of one case into one split, and rebuild preprocessing and historical reference features from preceding data only. A conservative fully matured event-1 backtest can train on January–September 2024 starts, tune on October–December 2024 starts, and test on January–15 April 2025 starts, subject to complete event capture and the 180 + 90 + 35 day worst-case ascertainment check. Apply that check per landmark because event-2/3 predictions can occur later than the start. Rolling-origin repeats can assess temporal variability. Use each fold's own as-of cutoff when simulating historical deployment; never let later labels or feature aggregates into an earlier fold.

The July 2025 policy change and October 2025 migration require separate cohort diagnostics and eventual out-of-time tests. With this event window and February 2026 extraction, a fully matured 180-day all-application default endpoint is unavailable for those regimes. A post-change test restricted to quickly activated, quickly labeled cases is selected and should be marked exploratory; migration cohorts can currently support process and feature-drift checks but not a credible fully matured default assessment. Extend both event capture and outcome extraction before claiming post-migration default performance (roughly late August 2026 or later for full follow-up of 15 October 2025 starts).

Evaluate the joint outcome probabilities with multiclass log loss, Brier scores, calibration curves and observed-versus-predicted cumulative incidence by horizon, using censoring/observation weights when their assumptions are credible. Report default-component precision–recall or discrimination only alongside calibration and activation rates; use case-level confidence intervals and effective weighted sample sizes. Show performance by policy regime, source system, channel, region, requested-amount band, and transfer/partner-origin group where outcomes exist. Monitor changes in prefix activities, activation and exit rates, feed completeness, calibration slope/intercept, and covariate overlap. A random case split mixes policy regimes, migration semantics, and label-maturity patterns; it also makes future-derived historical aggregates look available in the past.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The primary score is predictive association for realized defaulting exposure under the observed origination process. A low score may reflect a high probability of rejection or transfer, not a low default propensity under credit. Conditioning on activation creates selection effects; neither an activated-only score nor an all-application realized-exposure score identifies a rejected applicant's counterfactual default risk. Feed nonresponse, incomplete transfer linkage, changing policy, and the limited post-change follow-up further bound what can be claimed.

Deployment can change the data-generating process by altering which applications receive review or credit, changing document requests and queue delays, and changing activation thresholds or limits. It may also alter partner routing, collection intensity, or the completeness and speed of the outcome feed. Such feedback changes both target prevalence and label observation; monitor transitions and recalibrate or redesign the study before using the score to change approval policy. It is suitable for early monitoring and triage under a stated policy, not for asserting that rejected or withdrawn applicants would have repaid.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

The skeleton keeps the outcome table separate from the feature table. It assumes the supplied snapshot contains all application IDs and that timestamped records can be compared with the stated as-of cutoff. A production build needs a source sequence/event ID, record-availability timestamps for backfills, and refreshed January–February process status.

~~~python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
EVENT_END = pd.Timestamp("2025-12-31 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
WINDOW, OUTCOME, LAG = (pd.Timedelta(days=d) for d in (180, 90, 35))
K = 3
EXITS = {"Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"}
# Supply a reviewed, versioned migration map; empty here because names are unspecified.
RENAMES = {}

raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv", dtype={CASE: "string"})
for col in (TS, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"):
    raw[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
raw["risk_score"] = pd.to_numeric(raw["risk_score"], errors="coerce")
raw["default_90d"] = pd.to_numeric(raw["default_90d"], errors="coerce")
observed_rows = raw.loc[
    raw["data_extract_time"].le(CUTOFF)
    & raw[TS].between(pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01", tz="UTC"), EVENT_END)
].copy()
observed_rows[ACT] = observed_rows[ACT].replace(RENAMES)
observed_rows = observed_rows.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
assert observed_rows[CASE].notna().all()
# Exact timestamp ties and migration duplicates need event IDs before deduplication.
tie_count = observed_rows.duplicated([CASE, TS], keep=False).sum()
print("same-case timestamp ties to audit:", tie_count)

starts = observed_rows.groupby(CASE, sort=False)[TS].min()
case = pd.DataFrame(index=starts.index)
case["start_at"] = starts
first_activity = observed_rows.groupby(CASE, sort=False)[ACT].first()
print("cases without first Application Started:",
      int(first_activity.ne("Application Started").sum()))
activated = observed_rows.loc[observed_rows[ACT].eq("Account Activated")]
case["activation_at"] = activated.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
terminal = observed_rows.loc[observed_rows[ACT].isin(EXITS)]
terminal = terminal.drop_duplicates(CASE, keep="first").set_index(CASE)
case["terminal_at"] = terminal[TS]
case["terminal_type"] = terminal[ACT]
bad_order = case["terminal_at"].notna() & case["activation_at"].notna()
print("cases with both activation and terminal exit to reconcile:",
      int(bad_order.sum()))

known = observed_rows.loc[
    observed_rows["default_90d"].isin([0, 1])
    & observed_rows["label_available_at"].le(CUTOFF)
].copy()
assert known.groupby(CASE)["default_90d"].nunique().le(1).all()
known = known.sort_values("label_available_at").drop_duplicates(CASE, keep="last")
known = known.set_index(CASE)
case["label"] = known["default_90d"]
case["label_at"] = known["label_available_at"]
case["label_ok"] = (
    case["activation_at"].notna()
    & case["label"].isin([0, 1])
    & case["label_at"].ge(case["activation_at"])
    & (case["activation_at"] + OUTCOME).le(CUTOFF)
)
case["feed_overdue"] = (
    case["activation_at"].notna()
    & ~case["label_ok"]
    & (case["activation_at"] + OUTCOME + LAG).le(CUTOFF)
)
case["snapshot_group"] = np.select(
    [
        case["activation_at"].notna() & case["label_ok"] & case["label"].eq(0),
        case["activation_at"].notna() & case["label_ok"] & case["label"].eq(1),
        case["activation_at"].notna(),
        case["terminal_type"].eq("Rejected"),
        case["terminal_type"].eq("Withdrawn"),
        case["terminal_type"].eq("Transferred"),
    ],
    [
        "activated_nondefault", "activated_default", "activated_unresolved",
        "rejected", "withdrawn", "transferred",
    ],
    default="open",
)
print("snapshot cohort counts:")
print(case["snapshot_group"].value_counts(dropna=False))
print("feed-overdue activated:", int(case["feed_overdue"].sum()))
print("total unique applications:", len(case))

# Explicit allowlist: no outcome, post-decision, collection, or nightly fields.
safe_cols = [
    CASE, ACT, TS, "org:resource", "case:channel", "case:region",
    "case:requested_amount", "risk_score", "source_system",
]
safe = observed_rows[safe_cols].copy()
safe = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    safe, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
)
safe = safe.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
shortened = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(safe, length=K, case_id_key=CASE)
shortened = shortened.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
sizes = shortened.groupby(CASE).size()
prefix = shortened.loc[shortened[CASE].isin(sizes.index[sizes.eq(K)])].copy()
# For the all-case baseline, rerun with K=1; never pad short traces.
group = prefix.groupby(CASE, sort=False)

def last_observed(series):
    values = series.dropna()
    return values.iloc[-1] if len(values) else np.nan

X = pd.DataFrame(index=group.size().index)
X["prefix_at"] = group[TS].max()
X["activities"] = group[ACT].agg(tuple)
X["last_activity"] = group[ACT].last()
X["document_requests"] = group[ACT].apply(
    lambda s: int(s.eq("Documents Requested").sum())
)
X["review_repeats"] = group[ACT].apply(
    lambda s: max(0, int(s.eq("Credit Review").sum()) - 1)
)
X["elapsed_hours"] = (group[TS].max() - group[TS].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
X["risk_score_asof"] = group["risk_score"].apply(last_observed)
for col in ("case:channel", "case:region", "case:requested_amount",
            "org:resource", "source_system"):
    X[col] = group[col].apply(last_observed)
X["policy_after_2025_07_01"] = X["prefix_at"].ge(
    pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01", tz="UTC")
).astype(int)

# Retain only preactivation, preexit landmarks; outcomes remain in Y.
a = case["activation_at"].reindex(X.index)
e = case["terminal_at"].reindex(X.index)
active_at_landmark = (a.isna() | a.gt(X["prefix_at"])) & (
    e.isna() | e.gt(X["prefix_at"])
)
X = X.loc[active_at_landmark].copy()
t = X["prefix_at"]
a, e = a.reindex(X.index), e.reindex(X.index)
term_kind = case["terminal_type"].reindex(X.index)
a_in = a.ge(t) & a.le(t + WINDOW) & (e.isna() | a.lt(e))
e_in = e.ge(t) & e.le(t + WINDOW) & (a.isna() | e.lt(a))
label_ok = case["label_ok"].reindex(X.index).fillna(False)
label = case["label"].reindex(X.index)
Y = pd.DataFrame(index=X.index)
Y["state"] = np.where(
    (t + WINDOW).le(EVENT_END), "no_activation_by_180d", "right_censored"
)
Y.loc[e_in, "state"] = term_kind.loc[e_in].str.lower()
Y.loc[a_in & ~label_ok, "state"] = np.where(
    (a.loc[a_in & ~label_ok] + OUTCOME + LAG).le(CUTOFF),
    "feed_missing", "label_pending"
)
Y.loc[a_in & label_ok & label.eq(0), "state"] = "activated_nondefault"
Y.loc[a_in & label_ok & label.eq(1), "state"] = "activated_default"
Y["split"] = np.select(
    [
        case["start_at"].reindex(X.index).lt(pd.Timestamp("2024-10-01", tz="UTC")),
        case["start_at"].reindex(X.index).lt(pd.Timestamp("2025-01-01", tz="UTC")),
        case["start_at"].reindex(X.index).le(pd.Timestamp("2025-04-15", tz="UTC")),
    ], ["train", "validation", "test"], default="shadow"
)
Y["full_window_observable"] = (
    (t + WINDOW).le(EVENT_END)
    & (t + WINDOW + OUTCOME + LAG).le(CUTOFF)
)
for forbidden in ("final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status",
                  "documents_total", "default_90d", "label_available_at"):
    assert forbidden not in X.columns
assert X["prefix_at"].le(EVENT_END).all()
print("landmark outcome and split counts:")
print(pd.crosstab(Y["split"], Y["state"]))
~~~
