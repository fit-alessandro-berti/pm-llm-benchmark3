# 1. Model scope and state variables

## 1.1 Boundary and purpose

The twin is a **calibrated, resource-constrained, calendar-aware discrete-event simulation (DES)** of the permit process, used for *counterfactual comparison* of D1–D10 — not for trace replay.

| Inside the boundary | Explicitly outside (exogenous / interface) |
|---|---|
| Portal submission  completeness (incl. missing-doc loops)  planning/fire/environmental/public-notice (partly parallel)  inspection scheduling & site inspection (incl. travel, no-shows, bundling)  decision drafting (AND-join)  legal check  issue/reject  appeal re-entry | Construction/execution phase; enforcement of violations after issuance (only a *risk proxy* for D2); applicant-side design work; consultant report production (modelled as a delay distribution); council politics (modelled as a calendar); HR hiring pipelines |
| Municipal resource pools, skills, calendars, holidays, council weeks, batching, priorities, aging | Statutory minima (14-day notice) = hard constraints, never "optimised away" |
| Cross-case coupling: architect bursts, neighbourhood objection shocks, geographic inspection bundles, shared councils/pools | Long-run land-use/demand feedback (only scenario elasticities, ±5%) |

**Two clocks are always reported:** *gross applicant-perceived lead time* (submissiondecision, and for D1 also pre-submission effort) and *net municipal-controllable lead time* (gross minus statutory notice minimum, applicant response, third-party report time). Without both, D1/D9 look artificially good by pushing work outside the measured window.

## 1.2 Level of detail

Activity-level DES with (a) individual cases carrying attributes, (b) finite resource units with weekly availability grids, (c) three-clock time accounting, (d) latent-state sub-models for objections and missing attributes, (e) an object layer (architect, district/neighbourhood, inspection bundle) so cases are **not independent**. No keystroke-level or agent-based applicant cognition modelling; applicant behaviour is a delay distribution plus policy-response parameters.

## 1.3 Entities and state variables

**Case (primary entity)**
- Identity/attributes: `case_id, case_type, district, architect_id, application_value, area (partly latent), requires_fire/env/notice (observed | imputed + latent flag), prior_violations (latent, needed for D2), env_sensitivity (latent)`.

- Time accounting (never merged): `t_queue`, `t_service`, `t_external_blocked` (applicant/consultant), `t_calendar_blocked` (council week, fire pool Tue–Thu, inspector field days), `t_statutory` (notice minimum).
- Control state: `sla_class, sla_deadline, age_days, priority_score, bundle_id, objection_state  {none, latent_objected, resolved}, no_show_count, travel_charge`.

**Object-layer entities (cross-case dependency carriers)**

- `Neighbourhood/district`: latent objection shock (shared random effect active over a time window), environmental sensitivity, travel-time row.

| Scheduler | *not in HR extract*  assumed ~1 FTE from clerks (flagged gap) | inspection scheduling | FIFO, batch formation trigger |
| System/portal | unbounded | submit, receive docs, issue permit, notice timer | none |

**Global state:** simulated local time (`Europe/...`), holiday set, council-week flag, seasonal/DOW arrival intensity, per-pool queue lengths and busy units, WIP and backlog by (stage, type, district), aging index, bundle registry, objection-shock field, inspector locations, appointment book.

**Emission:** the engine writes a simulated event log with the *same schema* as the real log plus `lifecycle:transition  {start, complete}`, `org:resource`, `bundle_id`, `objection_state`, `clock_class`. This makes PM4Py validation symmetric between real and simulated logs (Section 6).

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

## 2.1 PM4Py's role (log/model layer only)

PM4Py is used for **ingestion, discovery, statistics, conformance and validation** — never as the simulation engine. `pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.process_tree` can generate structurally valid traces but has no resource contention, calendars, batching or cross-case coupling, so it is used only as a structural sanity check.

## 2.2 Arrivals (non-stationary, clustered)

1. First `Submit application` per case  arrival series. Fit **NHPP** `(t) = 0 · s(month) · d(dow) · trend(t) · holiday_offset`, estimated by Poisson GLM with month dummies + annual harmonics + DOW dummies; anchor ratios **1.7× (Mar–May, Sep–Nov vs Jan–Feb)** and **Monday = 2.2× Wednesday**. Unspecified months (Jun–Aug, Dec) are *not* identified by the summary  treat as uncertain factors, scale so total  31,200/18 months (~1,733/month, ~80/business day).
2. Test overdispersion (variance/mean of daily counts, Pearson ²). If >1.5  negative-binomial or **Cox process** (log- with OU/weekly component).
3. **Architect clusters**: top-20 architects = 31% of cases. Fit a Neyman–Scott/Hawkes-lite cluster process: parent burst intensity, offspring count per week (empirical distribution), offspring attribute correlation (district, type, value) via cluster-level draws or a Gaussian copula. Remaining 69%: thinned NHPP for independent applicants.
4. Intra-day profile from submission hour histogram (portal is 24/7; human queues are not).
5. Arrival-only validation: daily-count distribution, dispersion index, lag-7 autocorrelation, Monday share, same-architect weekly clustering statistic.

## 2.3 Service times — and the identifiability trap

**Never fit "duration between events" as service.** Steps:

1. **Split the log**: subset A has `start_timestamp`/lifecycle pairs  direct MLE per (activity × case_type) of lognormal/gamma/Weibull, mixtures where bimodal; subset B completion-only  *unsplit sojourn*.
2. For B, three complementary estimators: (i) **quiet-period identification** (events completing when the pool's queue is provably empty, e.g., first task after a weekend); (ii) **busy-period/resource-sequence constraints** (consecutive completions by the same resource give lower bounds on effort); (iii) **simulation-based calibration** (indirect inference / ABC-SMC): treat effort scale and wait/service split as parameters, simulate, and match observed sojourn distributions, per-activity waiting medians and monthly throughput.
3. **Effort vs elapsed duration.** Human "service times" in the summary are almost certainly *elapsed session spans*, not work content. A capacity feasibility check makes this unavoidable (21.7 working days/month, 8 h/FTE/day, lognormal implied mean = median·exp(²/2) with  from median/p90):

| Activity | Implied mean | Monthly demand (est.) | Effective capacity | Load/capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Completeness check | ~41 min | ~2,000 events  ~1,370 h | ~870 h (5 FTE) | **~1.6×** |
| Planning review | ~6.3 h | 1,733  ~10,900 h | ~1,390 h (8 FTE) | **~8×** |
| Fire-safety review | ~4.1 h | ~780  ~3,200 h | ~208 h (2 FTE, Tue–Thu) | **~15×** |
| Legal check | ~7.1 h | ~520  ~3,700 h | ~260 h (2 FTE, council weeks) | **~14×** |

A model fed these numbers is permanently saturated: every capacity intervention would look transformative and every routing intervention irrelevant. **Therefore an explicit effort-fraction parameter `_a  (0,1]` (elapsed  work content) is introduced per activity, with the hard feasibility constraint `_a _a·E[effort_a]  _max·capacity_pool` (_max  0.9) used to reject implausible parameter draws.** Competing hypotheses, each with a test: H1 elapsed-vs-effort (time study / start-timestamp subset); H2 hidden external capacity (contracted fire/env reviewers — check `org:resource` values outside HR pools); H3 upward-biased service inference from completion-only logs; H4 volume/case-definition mismatch (multi-lot or duplicate cases). This reconciliation is a **pre-condition for any intervention comparison** and is the highest-value missing measurement.
4. Heavy tails: lognormal body + GPD/Pareto tail above a fitted threshold (Hill estimator) for applicant response, objection-extended notice, Complex variance. Right-censor open cases at log end  Kaplan–Meier / censored likelihood, else the tail is systematically shortened.
5. Rare activities: hierarchical partial pooling by case type; empirical bootstrap when n < 30; never a point fit on 3 observations.

## 2.4 Waiting times are outputs, not inputs

Observed waiting medians (1.5 d clerks, 6 d planning, 8 d env, 8 d inspection, 7 d legal) are **validation targets**. Only genuinely exogenous waits are parameters: applicant response (median 5 d, heavy tail), consultant reports, statutory notice minimum (14 d, deterministic), council-week blocking (calendar).

## 2.5 Routing / branching

- Per XOR gate, build features with `log_to_features` + case attributes; fit interpretable classifiers (depth  3 decision tree / logistic) for `P(fire)`, `P(env)`, `P(notice)`, `P(legal)`, `P(issue|reject)`. Report calibration (Brier, reliability curve), not just accuracy.
- **Latent triggers stay probabilistic**: area threshold not in log  `P(fire | value, type, district)` with an imputed area distribution; district environmental sensitivity incomplete  latent Bernoulli with prevalence `p_sens`  [0.1, 0.4] as a scenario parameter. Never hard-code missing attributes as "no".
- Public notice: deterministic for Complex variance; estimated probability for New residential; validate against the 18% variant share.
- Check discovered tree vs the supplied normative tree; reconcile discrepancies before simulation (Section 8 docstring lists them).

## 2.6 Rework

Count repeats per stage (`pm4py.statistics.rework.log.get` + pandas duplication of (case, activity)). Fit **stage-specific loop-count distributions** (geometric baseline, but test count-dependence: hazard of a 2nd loop  1st), conditioned on case type, district, architect quality, and inspector identity (inspector strictness random effect). Cap loops (e.g., 4) with an escape branch to rejection/escalation. Reasons are uncoded text  use keyword/topic clustering **only to design scenarios**, and represent the reason mix as a Dirichlet-distributed uncertain categorical. Appeal: `P(appeal | reject, type)` + external appeal delay; appeals re-enter at planning/legal, not at submission.

## 2.7 Calendars

Encode HR data as a weekly availability grid per unit + holiday list + council-week schedule. **Cross-validate against event timestamps**: hour-of-day/DOW histograms per pool. Off-calendar events (e.g., fire completions on Monday) quantify either reconstruction error or overtime  model as a small overtime probability or corrected grid, with the discrepancy rate as an uncertainty knob. Environmental 40% reserve  effective 1.8 FTE, varied ±10 pp. Council weeks: schedule uncertain  randomised placement with the observed frequency.

## 2.8 Dependencies

- **Architect bursts**: offspring distribution + attribute copula (above).
- **Objections (latent!)**: fit a **two-component mixture** to public-notice durations: component 1  14 d + small noise (statutory), component 2 = lognormal/GPD tail (median ~45–60 d). EM with left-truncation at 14 d gives mixture weight `(type, district)`. Then induce **correlation** via a neighbourhood-week random effect (Gaussian copula, correlation ``). `` and `` are *inferred proxies*, never observed objection counts; `` is often weakly identified  scenario range [0, 0.6]. Validate only against notice-duration clustering, and label the objection sub-model "unvalidated mechanism".
- **Travel/batching**: from consecutive site inspections by the same inspector-day, decompose gap = travel + service; estimate a district travel matrix (D3/D5 larger), no-show probability from repeated inspections/rework variants, and the *current implicit* bundle-size distribution.

## 2.9 Calibration and parameter uncertainty

Stage 1: direct MLE where identified. Stage 2: simulation-based calibration of the weakly identified vector
` = (_a, wait/service split, effort scales, latent routing prevalences, , , tail shape , travel matrix, no-show, calendar deviations, D2 eligibility prevalence)`.
Discrepancy = weighted distance between simulated and observed summary vectors (per-variant median/p90, per-activity waiting medians, monthly throughput, variant shares, rework rates, backlog drift). Output an **ensemble/posterior of ** (ABC-SMC or Sobol-filtered behavioural set), not a single point. Months 1–15 = calibration, months 16–18 = out-of-sample.

# 3. Assumptions

**Arrivals**
- NHPP with seasonal + DOW modulation is adequate for independent applicants; architect submissions are a cluster process. Weekend portal arrivals allowed but small.
- Total demand is conserved across interventions (no market feedback) except an explicit ±5% elasticity scenario for D1 (pre-submission effort) and D7 (appointment friction).
- *Risk*: appointment slots may shift, not remove, Monday peaks  tested via compliance levels.

**Service times**
- Reported "service times" are elapsed spans; **effort = _a × elapsed**, _a calibrated under capacity feasibility. Lognormal body + EVT tail; effort independent of queue length; per-resource speed heterogeneity (lognormal multiplier, CV 0.2).
- No multitasking within a unit; long reviews are **preempt-resume** at shift end; site inspection is **non-preemptable** and must fit in the field window including travel.
- *Risk*: _a dominates all capacity conclusions  Morris/Sobol sensitivity + time-study measurement request.

**Routing**
- Conditional independence of gate decisions given observed attributes and latent flags; latent triggers are probabilistic, never deterministically "no".
- Missing-doc loop cap 4; appeal re-enters at planning/legal; rejection possible after rework.
- Statutory notice minimum and legally mandated reviews are hard constraints (D2 requires a legal basis — flagged, not assumed granted).

**Resources/calendars**
- HR calendars are authoritative-but-noisy; small overtime probability; env reserve = 40% (±10 pp); legal fully blocked in council weeks except urgent; scheduler capacity assumed from clerk pool (data gap).
- Temporary inspector (D4) has 2-week ramp-up at 60% productivity and no legal/planning skills.

**Rework**
- Stage-specific loop-count distributions, capped; inspector-strictness random effect; rework consumes the same pool as the original activity; rework reasons uncertain (Dirichlet mix), used for scenarios only.
- Interventions do not change rework *propensity* except where explicitly hypothesised (D1  completeness loops, D2/D5/D8 possibly  downstream rework) — those effects are elicited ranges, not log-derived facts.

**Applicant/third-party response**
- Exogenous, heavy-tailed (lognormal + GPD), right-censored-corrected; independent of municipal load; reminder policy optional (D1 side effect). Consultant reports a separate delay feeding environmental review; applicant no-shows Bernoulli per inspection.

**Cross-case dependencies**
- Architect bursts share attributes and completeness quality; neighbourhood objections are a common shock over a district-week window (`` uncertain); bundling only within district and within a hold window; travel matrix asymmetric-lite (route-order approximation); council weeks and pools are shared blockers.
- Bundles reduce travel by an elicited 15–35% per extra co-located case but add holding delay to early-ready cases.

**Non-stationary demand**
- Seasonal factors, DOW factors, mild trend; unspecified months uncertain; council-week and holiday calendars shift year to year; peak-month definition for D4 = Mar–May + Sep–Nov (testable alternative: data-driven trigger on backlog).

**Missing data**
- Missing start timestamps: MAR *within* resource groups is **not** assumed; split treated as uncertain.
- Objections: latent, proxy-only; never imputed as observed events.
- Emergency decisions removed from log: **MNAR / tail truncation**  an adversarial scenario adds a small fast-track class and inflates tails; baseline results reported as "conditional on non-emergency cases".
- Flattened log underrepresents dependencies  object-centric reconstruction; where correlation is unidentifiable, use scenario ranges and report robustness, not point predictions.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up.** The system is *non-stationary by design*, so classical steady-state warm-up is inappropriate. Use a **terminating / rolling-horizon** design: initialise WIP from the log's open-case snapshot (stage, age, remaining loops) rather than empty-and-idle; simulate 36 months, discard the first 12 as transient, measure months 13–36 ( 2 full seasonal cycles). Additionally run a *frozen-average-arrival* counterfactual and apply Welch's moving-average on WIP/queue length to confirm the initialisation transient is < 5% of the measurement window. Report all metrics per season as well as pooled.

**Run length & completed-case counts.** Tail metrics need volume: with ~1,733 cases/month, 24 measurement months  41,600 completions  p95 estimated on ~2,000 exceedances, adequate; p99 reported with explicit instability warnings. Complex variance (10%) yields ~4,100 cases  its p95 rests on ~200 cases: report bootstrap CIs, never bare point estimates.

**Replications.** Pilot 10 replications  variance estimate  `n  (t_{1/2}·s/)²` with  = 1 day (median) and 3 days (p90). Screening: 10–15 reps; finalists:  30; sequential top-up until CI half-width targets are met. Screening uses fewer reps but the *same*  draws to keep comparisons paired.

**Random numbers.** `numpy.random.SeedSequence(master).spawn(n_streams)` with **separate streams** for arrivals/clusters, per-pool service, routing, applicant delays, objections, travel/no-show, calendar jitter. **Common random numbers across configurations**, implemented as *case-locked randomness*: per-case uniform vectors derived from `hash(case_id, stream_id)` so that removing an activity (D2) does not desynchronise downstream draws — the classic CRN pitfall when routing changes.

**Two uncertainty layers (do not conflate).**
- *Aleatory*: within-run stochasticity  replication CIs.
- *Epistemic*: parameter uncertainty  **nested design**: outer 60 Sobol/ABC draws of  (including _a, wait/service split, , , , eligibility prevalence, calendar deviations), inner 5–10 CRN replications. Report **credible intervals for  vs baseline that include  uncertainty**, plus a variance decomposition (ANOVA/Sobol) showing which uncertainty dominates. Typically _a and the objection tail dominate — that is a *finding*, and it directs measurement effort.

**Interval construction.** Paired bootstrap (BCa) on per-replication differences for medians; distribution-free order-statistic or bootstrap intervals for p90/p95; never normal-approximation CIs for tail quantiles. Multiplicity: Holm/BH across the ~25 configurations. Report `P(intervention beats baseline)` and `P(best)` from the bootstrap/ensemble rather than a single ranking.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Morris elementary-effects screening over ~25 parameters  Saltelli/Sobol indices on the top 6–8 for p90 and SLA; one-at-a-time tornado charts for stakeholders; **structural sensitivity** across model forms (preempt-resume vs restart, mixture vs single-lognormal notice, correlated vs independent arrivals, alternative batching rules, _a=1 ablation).

**Heavy tails.** Report median, IQR, p90/p95, and **CVaR95** plus `P(T > SLA)`; avoid mean/variance-based summaries when the fitted GPD shape   0.5 (infinite variance) — use quantile/rank-based comparisons. Monitor sequential quantile stability; fit tails with censored likelihood; run cap-sensitivity (e.g., 365 d vs 730 d maxima) because caps silently determine tail conclusions. For very rare SLA breaches use splitting/importance sampling rather than brute-force replications.

# 6. Validation strategy

Validation is **staged with gates**; a configuration that fails a gate is not used for intervention ranking.

| Stage | Method | Target / tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| **Face validation** | Workshops with clerks, planners, inspectors, legal; walkthrough of 20 animated case histories incl. a bundled inspection, a council-blocked legal check, an objection case; structured "is this plausible?" scoring | No qualitative mechanism judged wrong; documented sign-off on calendars, batching and priority rules |
| **Structural validation** | Discover tree/DFG from log; compare with normative tree; token-based replay fitness + precision on full log; alignments on stratified sample (rare variants oversampled) | Simulated log's fitness/precision within ±0.05 of real log's; variant shares within ±3 pp for the six major groups; no simulated variant that is structurally impossible |
| **Event-log replay (trace-driven)** | Force observed arrivals, routing decisions and rework counts; only queues/calendars stochastic | Reproduces observed per-case throughput times with median absolute error  15%; isolates *routing* error from *queueing* error |
| **Distributional validation** | Per-variant throughput KS / Anderson–Darling / Wasserstein distance; QQ plots incl. tail; PIT histograms and predictive-interval coverage | Major-variant median within ±10–15%, p90 within ±20%; 90% predictive intervals cover 85–95% of held-out cases; tail index within fitted CI |
| **Waiting/service split** | Validate *only* on the lifecycle-complete subset (held out from calibration) | Predicted wait vs service medians within ±25% per activity; explicit statement that split is unvalidated elsewhere |
| **Resource utilisation** | Simulated busy-fraction and completions-per-resource-day vs HR capacity and log completion counts; hour-of-day/DOW histograms; off-calendar work rate; feasibility ratios of §2.3 | Utilisation within ±5 pp; no pool >95% sustained; DOW/hour histograms match (² / Wasserstein); feasibility constraints satisfied |
| **Bottleneck validation** | Rank activities by simulated queue time vs observed waiting medians; check qualitative signatures: Monday completeness spike, fire Tue–Thu starvation, env 8 d wait, inspection 8 d wait with D3/D5 excess, legal council-week stall | Same top-3 bottlenecks and same ordering of district effects; Monday/other-day wait ratio reproduced within ±20% |
| **Dependency validation** | Same-architect weekly clustering statistic; district-week clustering of long notice durations; empirical bundle-size distribution | Clustering statistics within observed bootstrap CI (objection correlation validated *only as a proxy*, explicitly labelled) |
| **Out-of-sample** | Temporal split: calibrate months 1–15, test 16–18 (unseen peak season); leave-one-district-out and leave-one-case-type-out; hindcast a known historical staffing/holiday change | Same tolerances as above on the test window; degradation > 2× triggers recalibration, not "explanation" |
| **Continuous validation (live twin)** | Monthly re-ingest, drift detection via conformance/temporal-profile deviation and arrival-model residuals; automatic recalibration trigger | Drift alarm when fitness drops > 0.05 or waiting medians shift > 25% |

**Explicit non-validatable claims.** D2 (no inspection) and D9 (objection early warning) act on mechanisms *absent from the log*: they cannot be validated by replay. They require expert elicitation with documented ranges, bounding/worst-case analysis, and a **staged pilot with monitoring** before full rollout. No claim of optimality is made anywhere: outputs are *conditional rankings under a stated  ensemble and scenario set*.

# 7. Decision criteria

SLA anchors are derived from the log's variant durations and must be ratified by the municipality (calendar days, gross clock; the net clock is reported alongside to prevent cost-shifting to applicants).

| Metric | Definition | Baseline anchor | Threshold / decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median lead time | submissiondecision, per case type | Simple clean 12 d; Simple+docs 22 d; New res. 48 d; Commercial 68 d; Complex 140 d | No type's median worsens > 5% (90% CI);  1 priority type improves  10% |
| Tail lead time | p90, p95, CVaR95 per type | 24 / 45 / 95 / 130 / 260 d (p90) | **Primary criterion**: p90  5% with 90% credible interval excluding 0 for  1 priority type; CVaR95 not worse |
| SLA compliance | share finishing within: Simple 30 d, New res. 90 d, Commercial 120 d, Complex 240 d | computed at B0 | + 3 pp overall and no type > 1 pp |
| Gross vs net gap | gross  net lead time (applicant/statutory burden) | computed at B0 | D1/D9 must not increase gross time while improving net (veto rule) |
| Fairness — districts | max/min ratio of p90 across D1–D5; per-district  | D3/D5 already worse | Ratio  1.15× baseline ratio; **no district worsens > 5%** (D3 must not pay for D3-batching gains elsewhere) |
| Fairness — architects | median lead time small/independent applicants ÷ top-20 architects; Theil index | computed at B0 | Ratio  1.2 and not increased; D7 must not advantage large architects |
| Resource utilisation | busy fraction per pool, mean and peak-week | to be established at B0 (feasibility-corrected) | Target 70–85% mean; peak  92%; fire/legal  90% given calendar concentration |
| Rework rate | loops per case by stage; mis-triage returns (D8); post-planning legal rework (D5) | inspection rework variants 9% | No stage +> 1 pp; D2/D8 require a monitored risk proxy with an explicit stop-rule |
| Backlog | end-of-horizon WIP, peak WIP, aging backlog (age > SLA/2) | computed at B0 | No positive WIP drift over 24 months; peak WIP  baseline +10%; aging backlog   15% for D6 |
| Cost-effectiveness | € per case, € per day of p90 saved, FTE-months (D4) | — | Dominated options removed; D4 justified only if peak-season benefit survives S1/S4 |
| Risk proxy (D2) | expected post-issuance violations/rework under risk multipliers 1–2 | not in log  elicited | Requires legal basis + pilot; veto if worst-case expected harm exceeds agreed bound |
| Robustness | `P(all thresholds met)` over  ensemble and over S1–S7 | — |  0.80 across ;  0.60 in every stress scenario; **minimax regret** on p90  5 d |

**Selection procedure.** (1) Hard filters: statutory legality, no district/architect fairness regression, no gross-clock gaming. (2) Build the Pareto front on (p90, SLA, cost). (3) Robustness filter over  ensemble and stress scenarios; report minimax regret. (4) Prefer *reversible, low-risk* options (D7, D6, D3 with small k) for immediate rollout; gate D2/D9 behind pilots; D4 as a seasonal, trigger-based option. (5) Ship with a monitoring plan and monthly recalibration — the twin's recommendation is a **staged decision**, not a one-shot optimum.

# LAYER A1 -- process model: the supplied string is NOT machine-readable PTML

def build_normative_tree() -> ProcessTree:
    """
    Limitations of the supplied simplified string (documented, not silently 'parsed'):

    * It is a human shorthand. PM4Py's PTML importer (pm4py.read_ptml) expects XML;
      `pm4py.parse_process_tree` is not available in all releases, so we construct the
      tree explicitly with pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj (guarded attempt below).
    * In PM4Py, Operator.PARALLEL is '+' (INTERLEAVING is '<>'). So
      +(Planning, X(tau,Fire), X(tau,Env)) is a PARALLEL block: the tree asserts full
      concurrency, while the log shows only partial overlap -> timing must be
      calibrated, and 'Decision drafting' needs an explicit AND-join in the engine.
    * *(->(Completeness check, X(tau,->(Request,Receive))), 'Completeness rework')
      is a loop with do-block / redo-block. The log shows repeated 'Completeness check'
      but NO 'Completeness rework' event -> the redo label is effectively unobservable.
      We keep it as a tau (silent) redo and count loops from repeated checks.
    * Missing from the tree: appeal re-entry loop, rework after Planning / Legal,
      rejection reachable after rework, data-driven gate conditions (requires_fire,
      area threshold, district sensitivity), resource/calendar semantics, and the
      cross-case objection coupling. These live in the engine + routing model, not
      in the tree; the tree is used for conformance, not as the executable process.
    """
    def seq(*ch): return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=list(ch))
    def xor(*ch): return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(ch))
    def par(*ch): return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=list(ch))
    def loop(*ch): return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=list(ch))
    def act(a): return ProcessTree(label=a)
    tau = ProcessTree()                                   # silent transition

    completeness = loop(seq(act("Completeness check"),
                            xor(tau, seq(act("Request missing documents"),
                                         act("Receive missing documents")))),
                        tau)                              # redo made silent (see docstring)
    reviews = par(act("Planning review"),
                  xor(tau, act("Fire-safety review")),
                  xor(tau, act("Environmental review")))
    inspection = loop(seq(act("Inspection scheduling"), act("Site inspection")),
                      tau)                                # 'Inspection rework' unobservable
    return seq(act("Submit application"), completeness, reviews,
               xor(tau, act("Public-notice period")), inspection,
               act("Decision drafting"), xor(tau, act("Legal check")),
               xor(act("Issue permit"), act("Reject application")))


def try_parse_tree_string(tree_str: str) -> ProcessTree:
    """Guarded: use a native parser only if the installed PM4Py exposes one."""
    parser = getattr(pm4py, "parse_process_tree", None)
    if callable(parser):
        try:
            return parser(tree_str)
        except Exception as exc:                          # shorthand != PTML
            print(f"[tree] native parse failed ({type(exc).__name__}); using explicit builder")
    return build_normative_tree()

def compute_variants(df: pd.DataFrame, log: Any) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """PM4Py first, deterministic pandas fallback (always available)."""
    try:
        variants = pm4py.stats.get_variants(log)            # dict: variant -> cases
        rows = [{"variant": k if isinstance(k, str) else " -> ".join(k),
                 "count": len(v)} for k, v in variants.items()]
        out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    except Exception:
        ordered = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "event_order"])
        tuples_ = ordered.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].apply(tuple)
        out = (tuples_.value_counts().rename_axis("variant").reset_index(name="count"))
        out["variant"] = out["variant"].apply(lambda t: " -> ".join(t))
    out["share"] = out["count"] / out["count"].sum()
    return out.sort_values("count", ascending=False).reset_index(drop=True)


def discover_dfgs(log: Any) -> Tuple[Dict, Dict, Dict, pd.DataFrame]:
    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)[:3]
    perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    perf_dfg = perf[0]
    rows = []
    for (a, b), val in perf_dfg.items():
        agg = val if isinstance(val, dict) else {"mean": val}   # API differs across versions
        rows.append({"from": a, "to": b,
                     "mean_s": agg.get("mean"), "median_s": agg.get("median"),
                     "max_s": agg.get("max"), "count": agg.get("count", dfg.get((a, b)))})
    return dfg, start_act, end_act, pd.DataFrame(rows)

    try:
        from pm4py.statistics.traces.generic.log import case_arrival
        return float(case_arrival.get_case_arrival_avg(log))

        first = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().sort_values()

def conformance_sample(df: pd.DataFrame, log: Any, tree: ProcessTree, n: int = 200) -> Dict:
    """Token-based replay on a stratified sample (alignments are too costly for 31.2k)."""
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    sample_ids = (df.groupby("case:concept:name")["case_type"].first()
                    .groupby("case_type", group_keys=False)
                    .apply(lambda s: s.sample(min(len(s), max(1, n // 4)),
                                              random_state=MASTER_SEED)).index)
    sample_df = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(sample_ids)]
    sample_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(sample_df)
    return {"fitness_tbr": pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(sample_log, net, im, fm),
            "precision_tbr": pm4py.precision_token_based_replay(sample_log, net, im, fm)}

# LAYER A3 -- timing columns: waiting vs service vs external, with honest quality flags

def add_timing_columns(df: pd.DataFrame, start_key: str = "start_timestamp") -> pd.DataFrame:
    d = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "event_order"]).copy()
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=False)
    d["prev_activity"] = g["concept:name"].shift(1)
    d["prev_ts"] = g["time:timestamp"].shift(1)
    d["gap_since_prev_s"] = (d["time:timestamp"] - d["prev_ts"]).dt.total_seconds()

    if start_key in d.columns and d[start_key].notna().any():
        start = pd.to_datetime(d[start_key], utc=True, errors="coerce")
        end = pd.to_datetime(d["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
        d["service_time_s"] = (end - start).dt.total_seconds()
        d["waiting_time_s"] = d["gap_since_prev_s"] - d["service_time_s"]
        d["timing_quality"] = np.where(d["service_time_s"].notna(),
                                       "observed_start_complete", "completion_only")
    else:
        # Completion-only log: the gap is wait + service + external + calendar-blocked.
        # We DO NOT split it. The split is a calibrated uncertain parameter (phi_a).
        d["service_time_s"] = np.nan
        d["waiting_time_s"] = np.nan
        d["timing_quality"] = "completion_only_unsplit"

    d["clock_class"] = np.select(
        [d["prev_activity"].isin(EXTERNAL_GAP_AFTER),
         d["concept:name"].isin(STATUTORY_ACTIVITIES),
         d["concept:name"].isin(SYSTEM_ACTIVITIES)],
        ["applicant_external", "statutory_plus_latent_objection", "system"],
        default="municipal_sojourn_unsplit")

    d["is_repeat_event"] = (d.duplicated(["case:concept:name", "concept:name"], keep="first")
                            & ~d["concept:name"].isin(SYSTEM_ACTIVITIES))
    d["case_throughput_s"] = g["time:timestamp"].transform("max") - g["time:timestamp"].transform("min")
    return d


def arrival_features(df: pd.DataFrame) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Descriptive targets for the NHPP + architect-cluster fit (needs the full log)."""
    subs = df[df["concept:name"] == "Submit application"].copy()
    if subs.empty:
        return {}
    daily = subs.set_index("local_ts").resample("D").size()
    dow = daily.groupby(daily.index.dayofweek).mean()
    return {
        "daily_mean": float(daily.mean()),
        "dispersion_index": float(daily.var() / max(daily.mean(), 1e-9)),
        "dow_factor_vs_wed": (dow / dow.get(2, np.nan)).to_dict(),
        "month_factor": (daily.groupby(daily.index.month).mean() / daily.mean()).to_dict(),
        "architect_week_counts": subs.groupby(
            ["architect_id", pd.Grouper(key="local_ts", freq="W")]).size().describe().to_dict(),
        "n_cases": subs["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "warning": "illustrative excerpt: NOT estimable; use the 18-month export",
    }


def capacity_feasibility_check(monthly_cases: float,
                               service_summary: pd.DataFrame,
                               pool_capacity_h_per_month: pd.Series) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Guards against the elapsed-vs-effort trap: lognormal implied mean from median/p90,
    then load/capacity per pool. Ratios >> 1 mean the 'service times' cannot be effort.
    Returns phi_max = the largest admissible effort fraction per activity.
    """
    z90 = 1.2815515655446004
    rows = []
    for _, r in service_summary.iterrows():
        med, p90 = float(r["median_h"]), float(r["p90_h"])
        sigma = max((math.log(p90) - math.log(med)) / z90, 1e-6)
        mean_h = med * math.exp(0.5 * sigma ** 2)
        demand_h = monthly_cases * float(r["share_of_cases"]) * mean_h
        cap = float(pool_capacity_h_per_month[r["pool"]])
        rows.append({**r.to_dict(), "sigma_lognormal": sigma, "implied_mean_h": mean_h,
                     "demand_h": demand_h, "capacity_h": cap,
                     "load_ratio": demand_h / cap,
                     "phi_max": min(1.0, 0.9 * cap / max(demand_h, 1e-9))})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def paired_bootstrap_ci(delta: np.ndarray, alpha: float = 0.10, B: int = 10_000) -> Tuple[float, float]:
    """BCa-lite percentile bootstrap on PAIRED per-replication differences (CRN)."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(MASTER_SEED)
    draws = rng.choice(delta, size=(B, len(delta)), replace=True).mean(axis=1)
    return float(np.quantile(draws, alpha / 2)), float(np.quantile(draws, 1 - alpha / 2))


def metrics_from_log(sim_df: pd.DataFrame, cfg: TwinConfig) -> Dict[str, float]:
    d = sim_df[sim_df["local_ts"] >= sim_df["local_ts"].min() + pd.DateOffset(months=cfg.warmup_months)]
    per_case = d.groupby(["case:concept:name", "case_type", "district"])["time:timestamp"].agg(["min", "max"])
    tt = (per_case["max"] - per_case["min"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    out = {"median_d": tt.median(), "p90_d": tt.quantile(0.90), "p95_d": tt.quantile(0.95)}
    for ct, grp in tt.groupby(level="case_type"):
        out[f"p90_d::{ct}"] = grp.quantile(0.90)
    return out


def run_experiment(configs: List[TwinConfig], thetas: List[ParameterSet],
                   n_reps: int = 30, dry_run: bool = True) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Nested design: theta (epistemic) x replication (aleatory), CRN across configs."""
    rows = []
    for th in thetas:
        for rep in range(n_reps):
            streams = make_streams(MASTER_SEED, rep)          # identical across configs
            for cfg in configs:
                if dry_run:
                    rows.append({"theta": th.theta_id, "rep": rep, "config": cfg.name,
                                 "status": "planned"})
                    continue
                traces = PermitEngine(th, cfg, streams).run()
                rows.append({"theta": th.theta_id, "rep": rep, "config": cfg.name,
                             **metrics_from_log(emit_simulated_log(traces), cfg)})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

**Reading of the skeleton.** Layer A is fully PM4Py-idiomatic and runnable on the excerpt; Layers B–D are intentionally incomplete but fix the *interfaces that matter*: effort-vs-elapsed service (`phi_effort_fraction`), calendar-aware acquisition, AND-join before decision drafting, latent objection mixture with neighbourhood correlation, district bundling with a hold timeout, case-locked CRN, and a nested  × replication experiment design. The `capacity_feasibility_check` output is a **gate**: until the load/capacity ratios are reconciled, no D1–D10 ranking should be presented as evidence, and no claim of optimality is made.
