## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3), and Event (activity instances, which carry timestamps and link to several objects).

**Edge types and qualifiers.**
- `requires` (JobComponent) and `contains` (LotComponent) are direct O2O relations. A Job–Lot relation is indirect, through JobC2L7C5J5.
- `batches` (BatchJob) and `consolidates` (ShipmentJob) are direct synchronization relations.
- Event–object edges (E2O) are the main evidence: Job–Machine (use/release), Job–Tool (acquire/release), Batch–Tool and Batch–Machine (Batch Cure), Final Test (Job, TEST-1, CAL-3), and Quarantine/Release (L7, C2, C5, J2, J5).

**Direct versus inferred edges.**
- Direct: the O2O table; the B1 Batch Cure event (J1, J2, J3, C1–C3, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7); the FIX-A acquire/release times for J3 and the J4 Machine Process event; the L7 quarantine and release events; the Final Test events; the pack and dispatch times for J1 and J4 and the S1 dispatch.
- Inferred: all tool holds other than FIX-A, which come from acquire/release pairing; "ready" times, which are trusted only if the readiness event is correct; waiting intervals, which are differences between events and not recorded waits; the J2 override, which sits in a separate system; J2's and J5's relation to the lot events, which exists in the OCEL but not in the Job flattening; the S2 and S3 consolidation holds, which can only be bounded.

**Higher-order motifs (more than two object types).**
- **M1, Batch–Tool–Machine:** B1 and B2 share RACK-7 and OVEN-1. J1 (B1) and J4 (B2) are coupled through one rack.
- **M2, Job–Tool–Job:** J3  FIX-A  J4, a sequential exclusive hold of a fixture across jobs in different batches.
- **M3, Job–ComponentLot–Job:** J2  C2  L7  C5  J5, a shared lot with a single quarantine and release.
- **M4, Job–Batch–Job–Shipment:** J1 (B1) and J4 (B2) are consolidated in S1, so batch membership and shipment membership together create a cross-batch dependency.
- **M5, Job–Machine–Tool–Job:** J5  TEST-1 + CAL-3  J6, a serial dependency through a shared test resource and a calibration tool.

**Dormant link.** L8 contains C1 (J1) and C3 (J3), so J1 and J3 share a lot. No quarantine events are recorded for L8, so this is a latent coupling, not an observed delay.

**Edge list (compact).**
```
J1 req C1 | J2 req C2 | J3 req C3 | J4 req C4 | J5 req C5 | J6 req C6
L7 cont C2,C5 | L8 cont C1,C3
B1 bat J1,J2,J3 | B2 bat J4,J5
S1 cons J1,J4 | S2 cons J2,J3 | S3 cons J5,J6
{B1,B2} --uses--> RACK-7 ; {B1,B2} --uses--> OVEN-1
J3 --holds[09:00,11:40]--> FIX-A ; J4 --holds(inferred)--> FIX-A
J5,J6 --test--> TEST-1 ; J5,J6 --test--> CAL-3
L7 --quarantine[10:20]/release[13:50]--> {J2,J5}
```

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

The calculations below are arithmetic on the supplied timestamps. Each is an accounting of attributable time under a stated counterfactual, not a causal estimate.

**C1. B1 synchronization, J1.** Actual wait is 08:1012:05 = 235 min. The barrier is the last ready member, J3 at 11:55. Pre-barrier wait is 11:5508:10 = 225 min (3 h 45 min). Residual is 10 min.
*Counterfactual:* OVEN-1 and RACK-7 were free at 11:55 and the batch could have started then. Under that assumption, 225 of 235 min (96%) is synchronization-induced.

**C2. B1 synchronization, J2.** Actual wait is 08:2512:05 = 220 min. Pre-barrier wait is 11:5508:25 = 210 min (3 h 30 min). Residual is 10 min.
*Counterfactual:* as C1. Under that assumption, 210 of 220 min (95%) is synchronization-induced.

**C3. B1 aggregate.** Total member wait is 235+220+10 = 465 min. Pre-barrier wait is 225+210 = 435 min, which is 93.5% of batch-member wait. Note that a batch barrier is a policy cost, not a waste per se: it trades early-job wait for oven efficiency. The calculation shows where the cost lands, not that it is avoidable.

**C4. B2 synchronization and RACK-7, J4.** Actual wait is 13:1014:50 = 100 min. The barrier is J5 at 14:40, so pre-barrier wait is 90 min and residual is 10 min. RACK-7 is recorded as quarantine until 14:35, which is before the barrier, so under a J5-barrier counterfactual the rack is not binding for J4's start. The 15 min from 14:35 to 14:50 is unexplained. A separate reading treats RACK-7 as binding: a solo start for J4 would be feasible at 14:35, saving 15 min against the actual start. Either way, the rack does not explain most of J4's wait. OVEN-1 was idle for 115 min (12:55–14:50), of which 100 min (12:55–14:35) overlaps the rack quarantine. Idle capacity here reflects unavailability of a complementary object, not spare capacity.

**C5. FIX-A and J4.** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts Machine Process at 11:45, so wait is 115 min. FIX-A is released by J3 at 11:40, so the hold explains 11:4009:50 = 110 min. The residual 5 min is handoff.
*Counterfactual:* FIX-A free at 09:50 and no other gate, so J4 starts at 09:50. Under that assumption, 110 min is tool-attributable. A diagnostic caveat: CNC-2 was idle from 10:05 to 10:47 (42 min) during J4's wait. That window is not explained by FIX-A alone, so the defensible range is 42–110 min, with 110 min under the single-gate assumption.

**C6. S1 consolidation, J1.** J1 is packed at 13:30 and S1 dispatches at 19:25, so J1's packed-to-dispatch hold is 355 min (5 h 55 min). J4's pack at 19:10 accounts for 340 min (5 h 40 min) of this, and the remaining 15 min is dispatch lag.
*Counterfactual:* S1 would have dispatched J1 alone at its pack time, or under a 2-hour hold cap. Under either, J1's shipment hold falls to 0 or 120 min, avoiding 355 or 235 min respectively. J4 is the dominant controlling object, and J4's own lateness is largely upstream (C4, C5).

**C7. CAL-3 and J6.** J6 is ready at 16:05 and tests at 17:25, so wait is 80 min. Decomposition: 30 min (16:05–16:35) while TEST-1 and CAL-3 are occupied by J5's test; 45 min of recalibration (16:35–17:20); 5 min residual.
*Counterfactual:* no recalibration is required in the window, and TEST-1 is idle from 16:35 to 17:25. Then J6 starts at 16:35, saving 50 min. CAL-3-attributable delay is 50 min; the 30 min is a shared TEST-1/CAL-3 occupancy with J5.

**C8. L7 quarantine, J2 and J5.** The quarantine runs 10:20–13:50 (210 min). J2's batch start at 12:05 lies inside it, so J2 proceeded in the cure for 105 min (12:05–13:50) under a supervisor override recorded elsewhere. J5 is ready at 14:40, 50 min after release. That lag is unexplained.
*Counterfactual:* if J5 had been ready at 13:50, B2 could have started at max(13:10, 13:50, 14:35) = 14:35, saving 15 min for J4. The lot coupling therefore has a small direct effect on J4's timing, much smaller than C4 or C5.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job viewpoint.** Makes visible the per-job sequence and median stage durations. Hides cross-job holds (FIX-A, L7, RACK-7, TEST-1/CAL-3) and the S1 hold, because they are attached to other objects. Duplicates shared events: a single B1 Batch Cure event appears in J1, J2 and J3, inflating counts and overlapping durations. *Misleading conclusion:* "no individual job exceeds 1.8 SD," which is an outlier rule that masks a single 5 h 55 min pack-to-dispatch hold. The PackDispatch median of 1.1 h is insensitive to J1.

**Machine viewpoint.** Makes visible utilization and gaps. Hides which job is waiting and why. A gap is not a wait: CNC-2's 42-minute idle window coexists with J4 waiting. *Misleading conclusion:* "OVEN-1 is 44% utilized, so it is not a bottleneck," when the oven is idle because the rack and batch barrier are gating it.

**Batch viewpoint.** Makes visible synchronization and the batch start time. Hides tool constraints and the readiness distribution. A single cure event relates to three jobs, so the batch view double-counts. *Misleading conclusion:* cure duration looks like processing time, whereas members spent 3 h 30 min to 3 h 55 min before it.

**Tool viewpoint.** Makes visible acquire/release holds. Hides waiting for tools, because no Wait-for-Tool activity exists, and recalibration is only visible if logged. *Misleading conclusion:* tool holds look like productive use rather than blocking.

**ComponentLot viewpoint.** Makes visible quarantine and release. Hides its impact on Job timings, because Job flattening omits lot events and the Job–Lot link has inconsistent direction. *Misleading conclusion:* "no lot issues in the job DFG," when J2 was processed during quarantine.

**Shipment viewpoint.** Makes visible createloaddispatch (0.8 h and 0.3 h). Hides the pack-to-dispatch hold of already-packed jobs. *Misleading conclusion:* "no severe shipment bottleneck," when J1 waited 5 h 55 min after packing.

**Why low utilization coexists with long waits.** Utilization measures the fraction of time a machine is processing anything. Waits are caused by the availability of a complementary object: a second batch member, a fixture, a calibration tool, a quarantine release, or a shipment partner. The machine is idle exactly when the blocked job is waiting. OVEN-1 (44%), CNC-2 (54%) and TEST-1 (58%) all show this pattern in the supplied windows.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J3  FIX-A  J4  CNC-2  S1  J1 (Job, Tool, Machine, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts at 11:45; J4 packs at 19:10; S1 dispatches at 19:25 with J1 packed at 13:30.
- *Resource feasibility:* FIX-A is a single item; CNC-2 was idle 10:05–10:47, so the machine was not the gate during that window.
- *Policy constraints:* S1 consolidates J1 and J4; S1 is dispatched only after its last member.
- *Causal speculation:* FIX-A contention delayed J4 by up to 110 min, which pushed J4's pack late and held J1 for 340 min. Each link is ordered in time, but the fixture hold has not been shown to be the cause of J4's pack delay beyond C5.

**Narrative B: J1, J2, J3  B1  RACK-7  OVEN-1  B2  J4, J5 (Job, Batch, Tool, Machine).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J1 ready 08:10, J2 08:25, J3 11:55; B1 starts 12:05; OVEN-1 idle from 12:55; RACK-7 quarantine until 14:35; J4 ready 13:10; J5 ready 14:40; B2 starts 14:50.
- *Resource feasibility:* RACK-7 was used by B1 at 12:05 yet recorded as quarantined until 14:35. Either quarantine began after B1 unloaded (about 12:55), or the status is stale. This must be resolved before the rack can be treated as binding.
- *Policy constraints:* batches start only when all members are ready.
- *Causal speculation:* B1's barrier set the early waits (C1–C3); B2's barrier, not the rack, sets J4's wait (C4). Oven idle time is a consequence of rack unavailability, not of absent demand.

**Narrative C: J2  L7  J5  B2  TEST-1/CAL-3  J6  S3 (Job, ComponentLot, Batch, Machine, Tool, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* L7 quarantined 10:20; J2 enters B1 at 12:05 under override; L7 released 13:50; J5 ready 14:40; B2 starts 14:50; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatches 35 min after its last pack.
- *Resource feasibility:* CAL-3 is required for every Final Test; TEST-1 is idle during recalibration.
- *Policy constraints:* quarantine rules (overridden for J2), calibration policy, and consolidation policy.
- *Causal speculation:* the lot quarantine's direct timing effect on J5 is unexplained (50 min between release and ready). The clearest measured delay in this narrative is J6's 80-minute wait at CAL-3 (C7). S3's hold for J5 cannot be measured: the lower bound is the 75 min between J5's test end (16:35) and J6's test end (17:50), assuming J5 is packed only after its test.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Batch-formation rule with maximum hold (reduces synchronization).**
- *Target:* barrier formation in B1 and B2.
- *Benefit:* could recover up to 225 min for J1 and 210 min for J2 (C1, C2), and 90 min for J4 (C4), if oven and rack are available.
- *Helped:* J1, J2, J4. *Harmed:* batch fill and oven throughput; J3 may wait longer for partner-free starts; RACK-7 cycling increases.
- *Cost/risk:* more cure cycles, energy, and possibly weaker cure uniformity for partial batches.
- *Perverse metric:* OVEN-1 utilization or batch fill rate, which would reward holding larger batches.
- *Evaluation:* stepped-wedge rollout across batch cohorts; per-object ready-to-start waits (not machine gaps) compared before/after; stratify by batch size.

**I2. Fixture reservation with explicit Wait-for-Tool logging (addresses tools).**
- *Target:* FIX-A contention (J3J4).
- *Benefit:* up to 110 min for J4 (C5) if a second fixture or reservation is available.
- *Helped:* J4 and queued jobs on CNC-2. *Harmed:* J3 may be preempted, delaying B1 further (its pre-barrier time would rise only if it is in the batch barrier, so this is a real risk).
- *Cost/risk:* capital for a second fixture, or scheduling overhead.
- *Perverse metric:* CNC-2 utilization, which reinforces keeping the fixture busy and may favour bulk allocation over due-date order.
- *Evaluation:* interrupted-time-series on tool holds per object; count of Wait-for-Tool events; per-job time from machine arrival to start.

**I3. Calibration scheduling in idle windows (addresses tools).**
- *Target:* CAL-3 recalibration between J5 and J6.
- *Benefit:* up to 50 min for J6 (C7); also frees TEST-1 time.
- *Helped:* J6 and later tests. *Harmed:* J5 if recalibration is pulled earlier, or if the tool is off-line for planned work.
- *Cost/risk:* calibration accuracy if intervals are lengthened; capacity for a second standard.
- *Perverse metric:* test on-time rate, which rewards deferring calibration and risks out-of-tolerance results.
- *Evaluation:* compare test-ready-to-start waits per object; guard metric of calibration compliance.

**I4. Shipment hold cap and partial dispatch (addresses shipment policy).**
- *Target:* consolidation hold in S1, S2, S3.
- *Benefit:* up to 235 min for J1 (C6), with a 2-hour cap; similar gains for S2 and S3 members.
- *Helped:* J1, J2, J3, J5 (early-packed jobs). *Harmed:* J4 is unaffected, but shipment count rises, and downstream consolidation savings are lost.
- *Cost/risk:* transport cost per shipment and more handling events.
- *Perverse metric:* on-time dispatch per shipment, which rewards early splitting of consolidated orders.
- *Evaluation:* per-job pack-to-dispatch time; shipments per consolidated order; cost per unit; A/B by lane.

**I5. Lot-release integration and enforced override (addresses lot coupling; logging and policy).**
- *Target:* L7 quarantine and J2's override.
- *Benefit:* compliance and an accurate linkage of L7 events to J2 and J5; removes the 105-min quarantine exposure of J2.
- *Helped:* compliance and downstream visibility. *Harmed:* J2 would be held out of B1 until 13:50, which could further delay B1's barrier (possibly the batch would start without J2 or wait), so J1 and J3 could be harmed.
- *Cost/risk:* operational friction; possible stock-out for lots needed in the same batch.
- *Perverse metric:* override count, which can be reduced by logging overrides elsewhere, hiding the issue.
- *Evaluation:* count of unlinked lot events before and after; batch composition checks.

**I6. Logging improvements (logging rather than operations).**
- *Target:* missing Wait-for-Tool, batch-wait-start, and tool/rack state events; the Job–Lot link; readiness definitions.
- *Benefit:* makes the waiting visible in every flattening and enables the discriminating tests in Section 8.
- *Helped:* analysts, all objects. *Harmed:* none operationally, but changing readiness definitions will shift measured waits.
- *Cost/risk:* logging overhead; possible Hawthorne effects.
- *Perverse metric:* logging completeness, which can be gamed by logging trivial events.
- *Evaluation:* before/after reconciliation of events across systems; re-derivation of C1–C8 from the new logs.

**I7. RACK-7 cleaning scheduled into known idle windows (addresses shared batch tool).**
- *Target:* RACK-7 quarantine.
- *Benefit:* removes up to 100 min of OVEN-1 unavailability (C4), though only binding if the batch can start earlier.
- *Helped:* B2 members. *Harmed:* B1 members if cleaning overlaps B1.
- *Cost/risk:* cleaning labour; a second rack.
- *Perverse metric:* rack availability, which rewards skipping cleaning.
- *Evaluation:* rack state timeline; oven-idle attributable to rack versus barrier.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# ---- 1. Object-type / interaction structure -------------------------------
summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
print(summary)                 # compare with the Object-type pair table

ograph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)        # object-type graph
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)      # event-type -> object-type graph

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
print(ocdfg.keys())            # inspect; use the object-type edge counts

ev = ocel.events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]]
rel = ocel.relations.merge(ev, on="ocel:eid")

# Metric A: event-object pair frequency (object-centric, duplicates kept)
pair_freq = rel.groupby(["ocel:activity", "ocel:type"])["ocel:eid"].nunique()
# Metric B: unique event frequency (each event counted once)
event_freq = ev.groupby("ocel:activity")["ocel:eid"].nunique()
duplication = (rel.groupby("ocel:activity")["ocel:eid"].count()
               / event_freq).rename("pairs_per_event")
print(duplication)             # > 1 indicates shared events (e.g. Batch Cure)

# ---- 3. Flattenings and their performance views ----------------------------
flat_job = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
flat_batch = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Batch")
flat_ship = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Shipment")
dfg_job, start_job, end_job = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_job)

# Medians computed directly (flattened logs carry shared events repeatedly)
def median_edge_hours(flat):
    d = flat.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).copy()
    d["next_act"] = d.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].shift(-1)
    d["next_ts"] = d.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift(-1)
    d["dur_h"] = (d["next_ts"] - d["time:timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return d.groupby(["concept:name", "next_act"])["dur_h"].median()

print(median_edge_hours(flat_job))

# ---- 4. Object-level waiting (NOT inferred from machine-event gaps) --------
# Pairs of (object type, readiness activity, start activity) to be confirmed
# against the log's activity vocabulary.
pairs = [
    ("Job", "Ready for Batch", "Batch Cure"),
    ("Job", "Ready for Final Test", "Final Test"),
    ("Job", "Ready for Machine", "Machine Process"),
    ("Job", "Ready for Pack", "Pack"),
]
waits = []
for otype, ready_act, start_act in pairs:
    r = rel[(rel["ocel:type"] == otype) & (rel["ocel:activity"] == ready_act)]
    s = rel[(rel["ocel:type"] == otype) & (rel["ocel:activity"] == start_act)]
    r_ts = r.groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].min()
    s_ts = s.groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].min()
    w = (s_ts - r_ts).dropna().dt.total_seconds() / 60
    waits.append(w.rename(f"{start_act}_wait_min"))
wait_table = pd.concat(waits, axis=1)
print(wait_table)

# ---- 5. Object feature extraction for comparison ---------------------------
feat_job = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
feat_tool = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Tool")
print(feat_job.head(), feat_tool.head())
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):**
1. Readiness timestamps for J1 and J2 turn out to be set at cure-loading or at batch formation, not at actual readiness, which would remove most of the 225 and 210 min.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was occupied or unavailable at 11:55 so that a barrier-free start would not have occurred anyway.
3. Batches formed with partial membership show no throughput or wait change in a comparison period.
4. J4's and J5's pre-barrier waits are dominated by non-batch gates (e.g., CNC-2 or inspection), so B2's barrier explains little.
5. Cure durations or quality outcomes change materially with batch size, making a barrier-free policy infeasible.
6. The B1 members' post-cure waits (for pack or shipment) are longer than their pre-barrier waits, so the cascade is dominated by downstream holds.

**Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**
1. Wait-for-Tool events (start and end) linked to the job and the tool, to separate FIX-A waiting from machine processing.
2. Rack state-change events (quarantine start, cleaning end, load, unload), to resolve the RACK-7 conflict.
3. Explicit batch-formation events (batch open, member join, batch close) with the policy rule used.
4. Calibration-trigger events with a reason code (per-test, interval, or drift), to test recurrence of CAL-3 recalibration.
5. Lot-release events linked to the job at the point of use, plus the supervisor override record from the separate system.
6. Shipment-hold events (pack-ready, consolidation-wait start) and shipment-cutoff policy parameters.
7. Machine queue or staging events for CNC-2, to explain the 42-minute idle window.
