|:---|:---|
| **Model drift in A6/A9** | Periodic retraining on fresh loan books; quarterly accuracy benchmarks; human review of low-confidence extractions (<85% confidence triggers manual normalization). |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 1: Months 1–3 (Foundational & Critical-Path Unblockers)**
- *Modules:* A5 (Missing-doc checklist), A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant)
- *Integration:* Shallow/API-based. No core banking changes.
- *Metrics:* Doc-loop rate, mismatch resolution time, KYC clearance rate.
- *Gate criteria:* >30% reduction in mismatch median wait (<10h); <10% override rate for A4; A5 adoption >70% by RMs.

**Phase 2: Months 4–6 (Data Quality & Control Enforcement)**
- *Modules:* A6 (Financial normalization), A9 (Contract generation w/ controls)
- *Integration:* A9 uses 1st deep core integration (live credit limits, committee decision sync). A6 uses middleware/API.
- *Metrics:* Normalization accuracy, contract rework rate, four-eyes compliance rate, analyst queue depth.
- *Gate criteria:* A9 rule coverage >90%; normalization confidence >85%; zero four-eyes violations in high-exposure cases; analyst utilization <85%.

**Phase 3: Months 7–9 (Synchronization & External Coordination)**
- *Modules:* A7 (Valuation triage), A10 (E-sign & reminders)
- *Integration:* Shallow. A10 uses e-sign provider API.
- *Metrics:* External valuation wait time, signing cycle time, reminder loop frequency.
- *Gate criteria:* Valuation SLA adherence >80%; signing wait drops from 44h to <20h; e-sign adoption >80% across products.

**Phase 4: Months 10–12 (Packaging & Governance)**
- *Modules:* A8 (Committee pack auto-assembly), A12 (Control remediation workbench)
- *Integration:* Shallow. A12 uses policy engine.
- *Metrics:* Packaging wait time, control violation rate, policy ambiguity tickets.
- *Gate criteria:* Packaging wait <10h; control violations <1%; A12 ticket resolution <5 days.

*Integration constraint compliance:* Only A9 (Phase 2) and A1 (optional, deferred) use deep core integration. All others operate via APIs/middleware, respecting the two-module limit in the first six months.

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommended Portfolio:** Bottleneck & Synchronization (A4, A6, A7, A9)

**Defensible Trade-Off Argument:**
Senior management’s instinct to automate the highest-frequency activity (A1, Register application) is understandable but process-misaligned. Process-mining evidence shows that `Register application` has a median waiting time of only 1.5h, low rework (4%), and high automation feasibility, meaning it is already a well-oiled, low-impact node. Automating it first yields visible labor savings but shifts the bottleneck downstream, exposing misrouting, KYC delays, and document loops without reducing end-to-end cycle time. It also risks increasing control violations if misclassified cases bypass four-eyes checks.

The recommended portfolio targets the activities that dominate end-to-end performance: entity mismatch resolution (31h wait, 38% rework, critical path in 82% of cases), financial normalization (20h wait, 75m service time), external valuation (58h wait), and contract preparation (8.5% rework, 14% renewal loops). By unblocking these synchronization points and enforcing control rules via A9, the portfolio directly reduces waiting time, rework, and handovers across the critical path. It respects compliance requirements (A9 strengthens controls and preserves four-eyes segregation), accounts for resource constraints (manages analyst inflow via phased rollout), and adheres to IT integration limits (only A9 uses deep core integration in months 4–6).

While the quick-win portfolio offers political appeal, it sacrifices process-level impact and control integrity. The bottleneck/synchronization portfolio, augmented with A9 for compliance, delivers measurable cycle-time reduction, lower rework, and stronger governance. It is the only approach that aligns automation with process reality rather than frequency alone, ensuring sustainable throughput, reduced risk exposure, and preserved human expertise for judgment-heavy and exception-driven work.
