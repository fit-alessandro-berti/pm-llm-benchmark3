# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automated intake (Nora).** A1 and A2 are core by both volume and process position: Nora starts the mass of low-complexity paths and never leaves intake. There are no peripheral human activities. Authority is procedural, not discretionary. Treating Nora’s A2 as equivalent to Amira/Ben’s A2 would hide the later human re-validation on non-trivial cases.

**Front-office case handlers (Amira, Ben).** Core work is register / validate / standard response / close. That is the high-frequency, low-to-medium-complexity backbone (sequence 1 and the human A2 in sequence 2). Diagnose, configure, implement, negotiate, and reopen are peripheral: they exist, but counts are an order of magnitude smaller and concentrated on Amira more than Ben. Amira’s higher medium/high/critical load and slightly richer tail (more A7/A8/A13) are a seniority gradient **inside** the role, not a second formal role. Occasional A7 by Amira (18) is not commercial ownership.

**Temporary surge capacity (Kai, Lina).** The same four front-office activities are core **only** on simple cases. Everything else is effectively forbidden or unused. Weeks 8–10 show that their A2/A4/A12 are not interchangeable with Amira/Ben: incomplete entitlement notes produced 17 reopens (sequence 6). Substitution here is load-shedding, not demonstrated competence.

**Technical fulfillment (Chen, Dina).** Diagnose, configure, and implement are core; they own sequence 2. Close, standard response, and KB updates are peripheral wrap-around. Chen’s 24 A6 events and 42 critical cases are still not enough to make him a security specialist; they are a **complexity-conditioned extension** of technical work. Dina’s nearly identical activity mix at median 0.48 means the role is shared, but risk exposure is not.

**Security specialist (Erik).** A6 is the only activity that is both frequent *for him* and rare *in the log*. Peripheral implement/diagnose/quality/KB events are aftermath or adjacency, not a second job. Organizational authority is exception control, not case ownership. Weeks 7–8 confirm the distinction: when Mateo filtered “low-risk” security referrals, Erik’s A6 count fell, complaints fell, and two critical cases waited more than two days. Volume went down while residual risk went up.

**Commercial specialist (Fatima).** A7 and A8 are core. A9 is peripheral collaboration with Hana, not supervisory authority (16 approvals vs Hana’s 138). Weeks 3–4 are decisive: removing Fatima moved A7/A8 onto Giorgio and raised median pricing-exception duration from 38 to 74 minutes. Frequency of substitution ≠ equivalence of skill.

**Senior hybrid / commercial shadow (Giorgio).** He has **two cores**: front-office handling and commercial exception work. A9 (20) is occasional escalation support, not the supervisor role. The weeks-3–4 episode is temporary substitution with degraded performance, so A7/A8 are licensed shadow activities, not proof he should be re-badged Commercial Specialist.

**Supervisor (Hana).** A9 is the authority-bearing core. A13 and high/critical involvement are the escalation core. Small counts on A2/A3/A8 are oversight, not production work. Week 5 shows the authority boundary: Mateo can emit A9 events, but 13 of 21 were rechecked. Event performance ≠ decision rights.

**Quality / KB (Ivan, Julia).** A11 and A14 are core gatekeeping and organizational learning. Implement/configure appearances are likely in-line rework or inspection context, not a technical role. Complexity is medium because they see finished work across the mix, not because they own hard cases.

**Escalation broker (Mateo).** No single activity is “the job.” The core is **routing and coordination**: A13, A9 (preliminary), mixed specialist handoffs, and close-out of messy cases (sequences 3 and 5). Individual A3/A5/A6/A7/A8 counts are peripheral instruments of brokerage. High critical-case volume plus week-5 rechecks and week-7/8 security triage show influence without final authority.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow (and senior front-office bridge).** Formal title and home team are Front Office; certifications include entitlement **and** pricing. Activity similarity is high with Amira (0.84) and only moderate with Fatima (0.71), which is exactly a shadow pattern: he can enter commercial work, especially on complex front-office cases, without being the commercial owner. Benefits: resilience when Fatima is absent; fewer hard handoffs on cases that start as ordinary requests and become pricing exceptions; a career path that keeps commercial judgment close to the customer. Risks: slower and possibly less consistent pricing (38 → 74 minutes); informal authority that audits cannot see; training debt if the organization assumes “Giorgio can cover commercial” permanently. Temporary substitution is evidence of **partial fungibility**, not of interchangeable competence.

**R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker.** Mateo is the only human who is structurally present on every activity and on the highest critical-case count after the specialist/supervisor pair. Similarity to Hana (0.54) and Erik (0.39) is deliberately modest: he shares **contact points**, not role identity. He bridges security (sequence 3), reopen recovery (sequence 5), and, under load, preliminary A9 and security intake filtering. Benefits: a single coordination spine; fewer false escalations when he screened Enterprise Security referrals; faster assembly of technical/commercial/security specialists on reopened work. Risks: hidden single point of failure; authority leakage (stand-in approvals that later need Hana); risk of over-filtering (two critical cases waited >2 days). Mateo should be staffed and measured as a broker, not as a backup supervisor or a junior security analyst.

**R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist.** Erik’s activity vector is sparse; a frequency cluster would call him peripheral. Case-complexity evidence says the opposite: median 0.86 and 55 critical cases. Sequence 3 is short, expensive, and non-substitutable. Benefits of keeping the role narrow: concentrated security judgment, clear accountability, less dilution of A6. Risks of misreading the role: under-staffing because “he is not busy”; using Mateo as a volume filter without a critical-path SLA; assuming Chen’s 24 A6 events mean technical analysts can absorb security. Chen’s security-adjacent work is complementary, not a shadow security role.

**Additional bridging roles worth naming rather than forcing into clean clusters.**

- **Automation boundary (Nora ↔ human front office).** Nora absorbs commodity A1/A2. Humans re-validate when complexity rises. The bridge fails when temps treat Nora’s precheck as sufficient (reopen fragment 6).
- **Temporary-workforce bridge (Kai/Lina ↔ Amira/Ben).** Same activity names, different risk. Useful for seasonal A4/A12 throughput; dangerous if used on entitlement-sensitive or medium-complexity work.
- **Quality gate (Ivan/Julia).** They are not a “closing” variant of front office. They sit after technical fulfillment and own KB capture. Collapsing them into a generic back-office cluster would hide the control point that should catch temp-agent and substitution defects.

Shadow and bridging roles are not improper. They are how this process absorbs absence, seasonality, and exceptions. They become a problem only when mined activity overlap is mistaken for formal mandate.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_discovery
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.sna import algorithm as sna
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.resource_profiles import algorithm as resource_profiles

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format (PM4Py dataframe convention)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Keep analytical attributes outside the mandatory XES keys
extra_case_cols = ["case_complexity", "customer_segment", "product_line"]
extra_event_cols = ["temporary_substitution", "escalation_level"]

log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Standard role discovery (activity–resource clustering)
#    Treat this as a hypothesis generator, not as the org chart.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
# roles: list of Role objects with .activities and .originator_importance

print("=== discover_organizational_roles (frequency-based) ===")
for i, role in enumerate(roles, 1):
    members = sorted(role.originator_importance, key=role.originator_importance.get, reverse=True)
    print(f"Cluster {i}: activities={role.activities}")
    print(f"           members={members}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-profile similarity (explains Chen~Dina, Kai~Lina, Fatima~Giorgio)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
# sim is typically a dict/matrix of resource-resource similarities

def top_pairs(sim_matrix, k=12):
    pairs = []
    resources = list(sim_matrix.keys()) if isinstance(sim_matrix, dict) else []
    if resources:
        for i, r1 in enumerate(resources):
            row = sim_matrix[r1]
            for r2, val in row.items():
                if r1 < r2:
                    pairs.append((r1, r2, float(val)))
        return sorted(pairs, key=lambda x: -x[2])[:k]
    return sim_matrix

print("=== activity-based resource similarity (top pairs) ===")
print(top_pairs(sim))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Social networks: handover, working-together, subcontracting
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
subcontract = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)

# Inspect broker-like nodes (high weighted degree, diverse counterparts)
def degree_table(net):
    rows = []
    connections = getattr(net, "connections", net)
    if isinstance(connections, dict):
        # connections typically {(src, tgt): weight}
        from collections import defaultdict
        strength = defaultdict(float)
        neighbors = defaultdict(set)
        for (src, tgt), w in connections.items():
            if src == tgt:
                continue
            strength[src] += float(w)
            strength[tgt] += float(w)
            neighbors[src].add(tgt)
            neighbors[tgt].add(src)
        for r in strength:
            rows.append((r, strength[r], len(neighbors[r])))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["resource", "weighted_degree", "n_counterparts"]) \
             .sort_values("weighted_degree", ascending=False)

print("=== handover degree (brokerage signal for Mateo / Hana / Nora) ===")
print(degree_table(handover))
print("=== working-together degree ===")
print(degree_table(together))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join mined clusters with complexity and substitution — the anti-naïve step
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Per-resource activity profile
profile = (
    df.groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"])
      .size()
      .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# Case-level complexity attributed to resources who touched the case
case_res = (
    df.groupby(["case:concept:name", "org:resource"], as_index=False)
      .agg(
          case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
          n_events=("concept:name", "size"),
          n_subst=("temporary_substitution", lambda s: (s.astype(str).str.lower() == "true").sum()),
          max_escalation=("escalation_level", "max"),
      )
)

resource_risk = (
    case_res.groupby("org:resource")
      .agg(
          n_cases=("case:concept:name", "nunique"),
          median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
          p90_complexity=("case_complexity", lambda s: s.quantile(0.9)),
          n_high=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s >= 0.7).sum()),
          subst_events=("n_subst", "sum"),
          mean_max_escalation=("max_escalation", "mean"),
      )
)

# Map each resource to its frequency cluster (originator_importance > 0)
cluster_rows = []
for i, role in enumerate(roles, 1):
    for res, imp in role.originator_importance.items():
        cluster_rows.append({
            "org:resource": res,
            "freq_cluster_id": i,
            "freq_cluster_activities": tuple(sorted(role.activities)),
            "originator_importance": imp,
        })
cluster_map = pd.DataFrame(cluster_rows)

enriched = (
    cluster_map.merge(resource_risk, on="org:resource", how="left")
               .merge(profile.reset_index(), on="org:resource", how="left")
)

# Flag likely misclassification patterns
enriched["low_volume_high_risk"] = (
    (enriched["n_cases"] < enriched["n_cases"].median())
    & (enriched["median_complexity"] > enriched["median_complexity"].median())
)
enriched["high_subst_share"] = (
    enriched["subst_events"] > enriched["subst_events"].quantile(0.75)
)
enriched["broad_activity_span"] = (profile.gt(0).sum(axis=1) >= 10).reindex(enriched["org:resource"]).values

print("=== frequency clusters joined to risk / substitution ===")
print(enriched.sort_values(["freq_cluster_id", "median_complexity"], ascending=[True, False]))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Guardrails against over-interpreting frequency clusters as authority
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# (a) Re-run role discovery on slices that frequency clustering ignores
high = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df[df["case_complexity"] >= 0.7],
    "case_complexity",
    df.loc[df["case_complexity"] >= 0.7, "case_complexity"].unique(),
    level="case",
    retain=True,
) if "case_complexity" in df.columns else df
# Safer explicit slice:
df_high = df[pd.to_numeric(df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce") >= 0.7]
df_low = df[pd.to_numeric(df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce") < 0.4]
df_human = df[df["org:resource"] != "R14_Nora"]
df_nosub = df[df["temporary_substitution"].astype(str).str.lower() != "true"]

roles_high = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(df_high, "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp")
)) if len(df_high) else []
roles_low = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(df_low, "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp")
)) if len(df_low) else []
roles_nosub = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(df_nosub, "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp")
)) if len(df_nosub) else []

# (b) Compare variants and service times on substitution windows
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)

# Optional: annotate waiting/service time before interpreting "who is slower"
df_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df, case_id_column="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_column="time:timestamp",
)

# (c) Decision rule used in this analysis (document in the process-owner pack)
# - Frequency clusters = routine specialization candidates only
# - Confirm with: median/p90 case_complexity, escalation_level, certifications
# - temporary_substitution events cannot promote a resource into a standing role
# - Similar activity profiles (cosine) ≠ similar authority or risk exposure
# - Low-count resources with high critical-case share (Erik) are kept as roles
# - Broad, mid-count profiles with high handover degree (Mateo) are brokers
# - Automation resources are an interface role, not members of the human cluster
# - Shadow roles (Giorgio) are reported separately when a resource is central
#   in two otherwise distinct activity sets AND substitution degrades SLA

def interpret_roles(freq_roles, enriched_df):
    notes = []
    for _, row in enriched_df.iterrows():
        if row.get("low_volume_high_risk"):
            notes.append(
                f"{row['org:resource']}: keep as specialist/authority candidate; "
                "do not drop because frequency cluster is small."
            )
        if row.get("high_subst_share"):
            notes.append(
                f"{row['org:resource']}: substitution-heavy; compare quality/SLA "
                "against the incumbent before inferring competence."
            )
        if row.get("broad_activity_span"):
            notes.append(
                f"{row['org:resource']}: possible broker/hybrid; inspect handover "
                "and working-together, not activity cosine alone."
            )
    return notes

print("=== interpretation guards ===")
for note in interpret_roles(roles, enriched):
    print("-", note)
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Use the mined structure as a **staffing and control map**, not as a re-org already decided by the algorithm.

**Staffing.** Keep four production pools that frequency clustering actually gets right once complexity is added: automated intake, experienced front office, technical fulfillment, and quality/KB. Staff Kai/Lina as a fifth, capped pool for low-complexity sequence-1 work only, with a hard stop on entitlement-sensitive and medium-complexity cases. Do not size Erik, Fatima, or Hana by event count; size them by critical-case arrival and exception SLA. Size Mateo by coordination load (reopens, security referrals, multi-skill cases), and plan a deputy broker — the log currently has no resilient backup who is not also a specialist or the supervisor.

**Training.** Train temps on A2 documentation quality before giving them unsupervised A4/A12; the 17 reopens are a training defect, not a capacity win. Separate Chen and Dina’s development paths: Dina needs complexity progression, Chen needs an explicit boundary with Erik on A6. If Giorgio’s commercial shadow is desired, train and certify the exception band he may decide, and measure his A7 duration against Fatima’s 38-minute baseline. Do not use week-5 Mateo approvals as on-the-job supervisor training without a defined preliminary-approval policy.

**Risk control.** Leave A6, A7, and A9 as controlled gates. Mateo’s Enterprise Security filter reduced false escalations and also aged two critical cases — add a time-box after which filtered work must reach Erik. Require Hana (or a named deputy with real authority) to confirm stand-in A9. Route reopen fragment 6 back to experienced agents, not to another temp. Keep Nora’s A2 visible as a precheck so human validation is not skipped.

**Resilience.** The useful redundancies are already in the log: Giorgio for Fatima (degraded), Mateo for Hana (partial, rechecked), Chen for some security adjacency (not equivalent), temps for simple throughput (quality-limited). Formalize those as playbooks with expected SLA degradation rather than pretending the substitute is the incumbent. Cross-train a second commercial reviewer and a second security reviewer; today both crafts are single-threaded.

**Caution 1 — do not restaff from activity-frequency clusters alone.** Merging Kai/Lina with Amira/Ben, or Giorgio with either Front Office or Commercial, would look efficient on a role-discovery printout and would raise reopen, pricing, and escalation risk. Similar cosine scores (Chen–Dina 0.94, Amira–Ben 0.93, Kai–Lina 0.98) describe task mix, not interchangeable people.

**Caution 2 — do not promote substitutes or quiet specialists by misreading the log.** Temporary substitution is evidence of coverage, not of permanent competence (Giorgio slower on A7; Mateo’s A9 rechecked). Conversely, do not cut Erik or shrink Hana because they are “low volume.” Their scarcity is the point. Changing headcount or reporting lines from mined roles without outcome metrics (exception duration, reopen rate, critical waiting time, approval reversal rate) will confuse shadow resilience with spare capacity and confuse brokerage with authority.
