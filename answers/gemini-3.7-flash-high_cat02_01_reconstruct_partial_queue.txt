# 1. Assumptions and notation

### 1.1 Process Context and Notation
The system under analysis is a two-station serial precision-component repair process operating over a 6-hour observation window on 2026-05-12, from $T_0 = \text{08:00}$ ($t = 0\text{ min}$) to $T_{\text{end}} = \text{14:00}$ ($t = 360\text{ min}$).
The two mandatory stations are:
1. **Station 1 ($\text{S1}$)**: Incoming Inspection, staffed by two dedicated single-task resources: $I_1$ and $I_2$.
2. **Station 2 ($\text{S2}$)**: Calibration, staffed by two dedicated single-task resources: $C_1$ and $C_2$.

For each case $i \in \{\text{P01}, \dots, \text{P22}\}$, we define the operational timeline variables:
- $a_1(i)$: External arrival time at Station 1.
- $s_{1,s}(i), s_{1,e}(i)$: Start and completion timestamps of activity $\text{S1}$ on resource $R_1(i) \in \{I_1, I_2\}$.
- $a_2(i)$: Arrival timestamp at Station 2 queue.
- $s_{2,s}(i), s_{2,e}(i)$: Start and completion timestamps of activity $\text{S2}$ on resource $R_2(i) \in \{C_1, C_2\}$.

---

### 1.2 Explicit Assumptions

- **Assumption A1 (Mandatory Consecutive Flow & Bufferless Transfer):** Every case must visit $\text{S1}$ and then $\text{S2}$ in strict order with zero intentional bypass ($a_2(i) \equiv s_{1,e}(i)$). A case enters the $\text{S2}$ queue immediately upon completing $\text{S1}$.
- **Assumption A2 (Timestamp Rounding & Discretization Window):** All logged operational timestamps ($a_1, s_{1,s}, s_{1,e}, s_{2,s}, s_{2,e}$) were rounded to the nearest 5 minutes ($\Delta \tau = 5\text{ min}$). A recorded timestamp $t$ denotes an underlying continuous event time $\tau \in [t - 2.5\text{ min}, t + 2.5\text{ min}]$. For any duration $D = t_2 - t_1$, the maximum bounding uncertainty is $\pm 5.0\text{ min}$, with a standard error of $\sigma_D = \sqrt{\frac{2.5^2}{3} + \frac{2.5^2}{3}} \approx 2.04\text{ min}$ assuming independent uniform quantization noise.
- **Assumption A3 (Resource Single-Tasking & Non-Preemption):** Each resource $r \in \{I_1, I_2, C_1, C_2\}$ executes at most one case at any continuous time instant $\tau$ ($B_r(\tau) \in \{0, 1\}$). Once an activity starts on resource $r$, it is processed continuously until completion without preemption.
- **Assumption A4 (Simultaneous Event Ordering Convention):** When discrete timestamps coincide due to 5-minute rounding, physical state transitions are evaluated using the standard flow-conserving order:
  $$\text{Service Completions / Departures} \longrightarrow \text{External Arrivals / Queue Entries} \longrightarrow \text{Service Starts / Dispatches}$$
  Under this convention, a resource completing a task at time $t$ is immediately eligible to start a waiting case at time $t$ without phantom concurrency.
- **Assumption A5 (Resource Maintenance Outage):** Resource $C_2$ is unavailable due to scheduled maintenance during the closed interval $[10:55, 11:10]$ ($t \in [175, 190]$, duration $15\text{ min}$). During this window, $C_2$'s active processing capacity is zero, reducing $\text{S2}$ capacity to 1 active server ($C_1$). Total nominal gross capacity over 360 min is $720\text{ resource-min}$ for $\text{S1}$ and $720\text{ resource-min}$ for $\text{S2}$; total net available capacity for $\text{S2}$ is $720 - 15 = 705\text{ resource-min}$.
- **Assumption A6 (Missing Start Bounding Principles):** For activities marked `MISSING` ($s_{k,s}$ unknown, $s_{k,e}$ known), the unobserved start timestamp is bounded by two fundamental physical constraints:
  1. *Resource Availability Lower Bound (Work-Conserving ASAP Bound)*: The resource cannot start case $i$ before completing its prior assigned task $j$, nor before case $i$ arrived at the station queue:
     $$s_{k,s}^{\min}(i) = \max\left(a_k(i), \text{end of previous task on } R_k(i)\right)$$
  2. *Non-Negative Duration Upper Bound (ALAP Bound)*: The activity cannot start after its recorded completion, nor after the next task started on that resource:
     $$s_{k,s}^{\max}(i) = \min\left(s_{k,e}(i), \text{start of subsequent task on } R_k(i)\right)$$
- **Assumption A7 (Right-Censoring at Window Boundary):** The observation window terminates at $T_{\text{end}} = \text{14:00}$ ($t = 360\text{ min}$).
  - Cases in active service at 14:00 (`OPEN_IN_S1`: P21; `OPEN_IN_S2`: P15, P17) have fully observed queue wait times and right-censored active service times: true service time $S_k(i) \ge 360 - s_{k,s}(i)$.
  - Cases waiting in queue at 14:00 (`WAITING_FOR_S1`: P22; `WAITING_FOR_S2`: P16, P18, P19, P20) have right-censored queue waiting times: true wait time $W_{q,k}(i) \ge 360 - a_k(i)$, and unobserved future service times.
- **Assumption A8 (Queue Dispatch Discipline):** The nominal queue discipline is First-In-First-Out (FIFO) per station. Minor deviations in dispatch order reflect operator selection among ready cases or multi-server race conditions.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

### 2.1 Case-Level Metrics and Formulas

For any case $i$:
1. **Station 1 Queue Waiting Time ($W_{q,1}$)**: Time spent in queue before inspection commences:
   $$W_{q,1}(i) = s_{1,s}(i) - a_1(i)$$
2. **Station 1 Active Service Time ($S_1$)**: Physical processing duration on inspector $R_1(i)$:
   $$S_1(i) = s_{1,e}(i) - s_{1,s}(i)$$
3. **Station 1 Total Sojourn Time ($W_1$)**: Total time residing in Station 1:
   $$W_1(i) = s_{1,e}(i) - a_1(i) \equiv W_{q,1}(i) + S_1(i)$$
4. **Station 2 Queue Waiting Time ($W_{q,2}$)**: Time spent in queue before calibration commences:
   $$W_{q,2}(i) = s_{2,s}(i) - a_2(i) = s_{2,s}(i) - s_{1,e}(i)$$
5. **Station 2 Active Service Time ($S_2$)**: Physical processing duration on calibrator $R_2(i)$:
   $$S_2(i) = s_{2,e}(i) - s_{2,s}(i)$$
6. **Station 2 Total Sojourn Time ($W_2$)**: Total time residing in Station 2:
   $$W_2(i) = s_{2,e}(i) - a_2(i) = s_{2,e}(i) - s_{1,e}(i) \equiv W_{q,2}(i) + S_2(i)$$
7. **Total Process Sojourn / Cycle Time ($W_{\text{total}}$)** (for completed cases):
   $$W_{\text{total}}(i) = s_{2,e}(i) - a_1(i) = W_1(i) + W_2(i)$$

*Crucial Invariance Property*: Because $a_1(i)$, $s_{1,e}(i)$, and $s_{2,e}(i)$ are fully recorded for all completed cases, the station sojourn times $W_1(i), W_2(i)$ and total case sojourn time $W_{\text{total}}(i)$ are **exact and invariant** to missing start timestamps. Imputation only shifts the partition between waiting time and active service time within that station.

---

### 2.2 Time-Varying State Trajectories and System WIP

At any continuous time $t \in [0, 360]$:
- **Station Queue Length ($Q_k(t)$)**: Number of cases waiting in queue:
  $$Q_1(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{a_1(i) \le t < s_{1,s}(i)\}}, \quad Q_2(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{s_{1,e}(i) \le t < s_{2,s}(i)\}}$$
- **Active Service Count / Busy Servers ($B_k(t)$)**: Number of cases currently being processed:
  $$B_1(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{s_{1,s}(i) \le t < s_{1,e}(i)\}}, \quad B_2(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{s_{2,s}(i) \le t < s_{2,e}(i)\}}$$
- **Station Work-in-Process ($\text{WIP}_k(t)$ or $L_k(t)$)**:
  $$L_1(t) = Q_1(t) + B_1(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{a_1(i) \le t < s_{1,e}(i)\}}$$
  $$L_2(t) = Q_2(t) + B_2(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{s_{1,e}(i) \le t < s_{2,e}(i)\}}$$
- **Total System WIP ($L_{\text{sys}}(t)$)**:
  $$L_{\text{sys}}(t) = L_1(t) + L_2(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{22} \mathbf{1}_{\{a_1(i) \le t < s_{2,e}(i)\}}$$

*WIP Invariance Property*: Notice that $L_1(t)$, $L_2(t)$, and $L_{\text{sys}}(t)$ depend strictly on arrival and completion events ($a_1, s_{1,e}, s_{2,e}$). Consequently, **WIP time series, mean WIP, and peak WIP are mathematically invariant to missing start timestamps**.

---

### 2.3 Throughput, Utilization, and Little's Law

1. **Arrival and Departure Rates**: For any sub-interval $[t_a, t_b)$ of duration $\Delta t = (t_b - t_a)/60$ hours:
   $$\lambda_k = \frac{A_k([t_a, t_b))}{\Delta t}, \quad \mu_k = \frac{D_k([t_a, t_b))}{\Delta t}$$
   where $A_k$ is the arrival count and $D_k$ is the completion count.
2. **Resource Busy Time and Utilization**:
   - Busy time: $T_{\text{busy}}(r) = \int_{0}^{360} B_r(t) \, dt$
   - Gross utilization: $\rho_{\text{gross}}(r) = \frac{T_{\text{busy}}(r)}{360\text{ min}}$
   - Net utilization (adjusting for maintenance): $\rho_{\text{net}}(r) = \frac{T_{\text{busy}}(r)}{360\text{ min} - T_{\text{maint}}(r)}$
3. **Little's Law Validity Conditions ($L = \lambda W$)**:
   Little's Law holds asymptotically in systems satisfying:
   - Conservation of flow ($\lambda_{\text{in}} = \lambda_{\text{out}} = \text{throughput}$);
   - Statistical stationarity (no secular accumulation or depletion of queue backlog, i.e., $L(t_a) \approx L(t_b)$).
   - *Full Window Invalidation*: Over the entire 6-hour window ($[08:00, 14:00]$), initial WIP is $L(0) = 0$ while final WIP is $L(360) = 8$. Total arrivals ($22$) far exceed completions ($14$). Applying Little's Law globally is theoretically invalid.
   - *Defensible Quasi-Stationary Sub-Interval*: The sub-interval $[09:00, 13:00]$ for Station 1 exhibits stable flow ($15\text{ arrivals}$, $14\text{ departures}$, $\lambda \approx 3.6\text{ cases/hr}$), providing a defensible domain for Little's Law consistency testing.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

### 5.1 Evidence vs. Hypotheses
In rigorous process-mining analysis, physical evidence derived directly from event logs must be separated from operational hypotheses:
- **Direct Evidence**:
  1. *Processing Pace*: Observed completed service times at $\text{S1}$ average $\approx 28.1\text{ min}$ (sample range: $20–30\text{ min}$), whereas observed completed service times at $\text{S2}$ average $\approx 40.0\text{ min}$ (sample range: $25–50\text{ min}$).
  2. *Theoretical Steady-State Capacities*:
     $$\mu_{\text{S1}}^{\max} = 2 \times \frac{60\text{ min/hr}}{28.06\text{ min}} \approx 4.28\text{ cases/hr}$$
     $$\mu_{\text{S2}}^{\max} = 2 \times \frac{60\text{ min/hr}}{40.00\text{ min}} = 3.00\text{ cases/hr}$$
  3. *Inflow Surge*: External arrivals between 08:00 and 10:00 arrived at an average rate of $\lambda_1 = 6.50\text{ cases/hr}$ (13 cases in 2 hours), with inter-arrival gaps as short as 5 minutes.
  4. *Maintenance Event*: Resource $C_2$ was completely offline from 10:55 to 11:10, removing $0.375\text{ cases}$ of processing capacity during peak flow.
  5. *Ending State*: At 14:00, $\text{S1}$ has only 1 case in service (P21) and 1 waiting (P22), while $\text{S2}$ is overwhelmed with 2 cases in service (P15, P17) and 4 cases waiting in queue (P16, P18, P19, P20).
- **Plausible Hypotheses**:
  1. *Dispatch Delay vs Rounding Noise*: Minor gaps (e.g., 5-minute pauses between consecutive tasks on $I_1$ and $I_2$) represent either physical setup/cleaning time or timestamp rounding artifacts rather than voluntary resource idleness.
  2. *Upstream Batching*: External arrivals were likely injected in a morning batch rush from an upstream supplier or receiving dock.

### 5.2 Dynamic Bottleneck Shift Across the Morning

The system experiences a classic **time-varying, multi-phase bottleneck migration**:

```
08:00                                10:00                                12:00                                14:00
  |------------------------------------|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
                PHASE 1                              PHASE 2                              PHASE 3
      [Intake Surge: λ = 6.5/h]             [Peak Congestion + C2 Outage]          [Inflow Halts: λ = 0.5/h]
       S1 = Transient Bottleneck             S1 & S2 at ~95-98% Utilization        S2 = Severe Structural Choke
       S1 WIP builds: 0 -> 8 cases           S1 outputs 3.5/h -> S2 takes 2.5/h    S1 clears; S2 WIP surges to 7
```

1. **Phase 1 (08:00–10:00): Transient Intake Bottleneck at Station 1**
   - External arrivals arrive at $\lambda_1 = 6.50\text{ cases/hr}$, dramatically exceeding the maximum processing capacity of Station 1 ($4.28\text{ cases/hr}$).
   - Both inspectors ($I_1, I_2$) operate continuously. S1 queue length escalates rapidly from $0$ to $6$ cases, and $\text{WIP}_{\text{S1}}$ hits the peak of $8$ cases at 09:25.
   - Station 2 during this initial phase is starvation-buffered: it receives cases only as S1 completes them ($3.0\text{ cases/hr}$). Station 2 WIP remains low ($\text{mean } 1.83$), and calibration queues are minimal ($5–15\text{ min}$).
   - *Interpretation*: S1 acts as a temporary dam/gatekeeper, absorbing the front-loaded arrival shock.

2. **Phase 2 (10:00–12:00): System-Wide Saturation & Maintenance-Induced Gridlock**
   - External arrivals moderate to $\lambda_1 = 4.00\text{ cases/hr}$, matching S1 capacity. S1 maintains maximum sustained throughput ($3.50\text{ cases/hr}$, utilization $97.9\%$).
   - S1 pumps $7\text{ completed cases}$ directly into the S2 buffer.
   - However, Station 2's nominal capacity is only $3.00\text{ cases/hr}$. This structural mismatch is exacerbated when $C_2$ goes offline for maintenance between 10:55 and 11:10.
   - As a direct consequence, S2 departure rate falls to $2.50\text{ cases/hr}$ (only 5 completions).

   - S2 queue length rises steadily from $1$ to $3$ cases, and S2 net utilization reaches $94.4\%$.

3. **Phase 3 (12:00–14:00): Starvation of S1 & Massive Queue Accumulation at S2**
   - External arrivals virtually cease after 12:10 (only P22 arrives; $\lambda_1 = 0.50\text{ cases/hr}$).

   - Station 2, operating at its absolute ceiling ($3.00\text{ cases/hr}$, net utilization $95.8\%$, completed service durations averaging $45.0\text{ min}$), cannot absorb the incoming volume.

### 5.3 Little's Law Diagnostic Check

To evaluate theoretical consistency, we examine the **Midday Quasi-Stationary Interval** ($\text{09:00–13:00}$, $\Delta t = 4.0\text{ hours}$):

```

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Arrival count A_1 = 15 cases               Departure count D_1 = 14 cases

Little's Law Implied Sojourn (via λ_arr):  W_implied = 6.95 / 3.75 * 60 = 111.2 min
Empirical vs Theoretical Agreement:        Relative discrepancy = 4.1% - 11.5%
```

*Discussion*: The agreement between empirical mean sojourn time ($106.8\text{ min}$) and Little's Law prediction ($111.2–119.1\text{ min}$) is remarkably tight given 5-minute discrete rounding and finite sample size. The slight elevation in predicted $W$ reflects the mild inventory accumulation ($L$ grew from $5$ to $6$ over this window).

    return f"2026-05-12 {h:02d}:{mins:02d}:00"
