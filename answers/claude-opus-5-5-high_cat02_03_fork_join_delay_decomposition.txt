# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

## Notation

For case \(c \in \{F01,\dots,F12\}\) and branch \(i \in \{A,B,C\}\):

| Symbol | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| \(a_c\) | `arrival` | logged |
| \(r_c\) | `fork_release` | logged |
| \(s_{c,i}, e_{c,i}\) | branch start / end | logged |
| \(J_c\) | `join_ready` | **derived** |
| \(S^F_c, E^F_c\) | `final_start` / `final_end` | logged |

All durations are in minutes.

## Component formulas

| Component | Formula | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream elapsed | \(U_c = r_c - a_c\) | case-level, additive |
| Branch queue wait | \(Q_{c,i} = s_{c,i} - r_c\) | branch-level |
| Branch active service | \(P_{c,i} = e_{c,i} - s_{c,i}\) | branch-level |
| Branch elapsed (queue + service) | \(D_{c,i} = Q_{c,i} + P_{c,i} = e_{c,i} - r_c\) | branch-level |
| Join ready (AND-join rule) | \(J_c = \max_i e_{c,i}\) | derived |
| Branch synchronization wait | \(W_{c,i} = J_c - e_{c,i} \ge 0\) | branch-level, **overlapping** |
| Fork-join stage elapsed | \(FJ_c = J_c - r_c = \max_i D_{c,i}\) | case-level, additive |
| Critical branch | \(i^*_c = \arg\max_i D_{c,i}\) (tie rule below) | derived |
| Criticality margin | \(M_c = D_{c,i^*} - \max_{i \ne i^*} D_{c,i}\) | derived |
| Downstream wait before F | \(DQ_c = S^F_c - J_c\) | case-level, additive |
| Final service | \(P^F_c = E^F_c - S^F_c\) | case-level, additive |
| Total flow time | \(T_c = E^F_c - a_c\) | case-level |

**Tie rule (deterministic).** A branch is critical if \(D_{c,i} = \max_j D_{c,j}\), with a tolerance of 0 minutes. Tied branches are reported jointly in the fixed order A, B, C (for example `A=B`), and each receives \(1/k\) critical credit. No baseline case has an exact tie. Near-ties (margin 5 minutes or less) are flagged separately.

## Additive, non-double-counting identity

The case timeline is a chain of consecutive, non-overlapping intervals:

\[
T_c = U_c + FJ_c + DQ_c + P^F_c
\]

Inside the fork-join window, only the critical branch belongs on the additive path. Because \(W_{c,i^*} = 0\),

\[
FJ_c = Q_{c,i^*} + P_{c,i^*}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
T_c = U_c + Q_{c,i^*} + P_{c,i^*} + DQ_c + P^F_c .
\]

## Why summing synchronization waits double counts

For **every** branch, queue, service and synchronization wait partition the same wall-clock window \([r_c, J_c]\):

\[
Q_{c,i} + P_{c,i} + W_{c,i} = FJ_c \qquad \forall i .
\]

Summing over the three branches gives

\[
\sum_i (Q_{c,i} + P_{c,i} + W_{c,i}) = 3\,FJ_c,
\qquad
\sum_i W_{c,i} = 3\,FJ_c - \sum_i D_{c,i}.
\]

So adding \(\sum_i W_{c,i}\), or \(\sum_i Q_{c,i}\), to a flow-time decomposition counts the same minutes of elapsed time up to three times. F07 is a concrete example:
- \(FJ = 75\) and \(W_A + W_B + W_C = 25 + 0 + 52 = 77\), which is already more than the whole stage.
- Across all cases, \(\sum_c \sum_i W_{c,i} = 549\) minutes, compared with a total fork-join elapsed time of only 732 minutes and a total flow time of 1,383 minutes.

Synchronization waits are **slack diagnostics**: \(W_{c,i}\) is how much branch \(i\) could have been delayed without moving \(J_c\). They are not additive delay components.

## Optional split of downstream wait (diagnostic, not additive to the above)

\(DQ_c = DQ^{busy}_c + DQ^{idle}_c\), where:
- \(DQ^{busy}_c = \max(J_c, \text{free}_{k(c)}) - J_c\) is the wait while the **assigned** final resource \(k(c)\) was still serving an earlier case.
- \(DQ^{idle}_c = S^F_c - \max(J_c, \text{free}_{k(c)})\) is the wait while the assigned resource was apparently idle. It could be handover, a release policy, or unlogged work.

A stricter lower bound on true capacity queueing is \(DQ^{both}_c\): the minutes of \(DQ_c\) during which both F1 and F2 were serving other cases.

## Scrutiny of the given definitions (assumptions)

- **A1 – fork release is real.** `A_start = fork_release` in all 12 cases. If `fork_release` was actually derived from the first branch start, then \(Q_A = 0\) holds by construction and true A queueing is unobservable. I treat `fork_release` as an independently recorded release, because otherwise no branch queue can be measured.
- **A2 – pure AND-join.** F becomes possible exactly at \(J_c = \max_i e_{c,i}\), with no further precondition such as a dossier check or approval. This is needed to call \(S^F - J\) a downstream wait.
- **A3 – intervals mean what their names say.** \(s - r\) is waiting, with no setup, transport or batching. \(e - s\) is active service, with no pauses or preemption. Complete-event data with start and end times cannot show interruptions.
- **A4 – upstream is opaque.** \(U_c\) (15 minutes for F01, 20 for all others) mixes preparation service and waiting and cannot be split.
- **A5 – branches are independent.** Changing one branch does not change another branch's timestamps, i.e. no shared staff between A and B. This is needed for the counterfactuals.

## Worked calculations

**F02** (arrival 08:05, fork 08:25)
- Upstream: 08:25 − 08:05 = **20**.
- A: queue 08:25 − 08:25 = 0; service 09:15 − 08:25 = 50; \(D_A = 50\).
- B: queue 08:45 − 08:25 = **20**; service 09:25 − 08:45 = 40; \(D_B = 60\).
- C: queue 08:30 − 08:25 = 5; service 08:50 − 08:30 = 20; \(D_C = 25\).
- Join: \(J = \max(09:15, 09:25, 08:50) = 09:25\). **Critical branch B**, with a margin of 10 minutes over A. \(FJ = 60\).
- Sync waits: \(W_A = 10\), \(W_B = 0\), \(W_C = 35\). Their sum of 45 is branch slack, not added to flow time.
- Downstream: 09:40 − 09:25 = **15**. F2 had no earlier job, so all 15 minutes are idle-resource lag, not capacity queueing.
- Final service: 10:00 − 09:40 = 20.
- Total: 10:00 − 08:05 = **115** = 20 + 60 + 15 + 20. On the critical path this is 20 + (20 + 40) + 15 + 20.
- Note: B has less service than A (40 vs 50) yet is critical, because its 20-minute queue pushes its elapsed time to 60.

**F04** (arrival 08:35, fork 08:55)
- Upstream: **20**.
- A: 0 + 50 = 50.
- B: queue 09:05 − 08:55 = 10; service 30; \(D_B = 40\).
- C: queue 09:30 − 08:55 = **35**; service 09:52 − 09:30 = 22; \(D_C = 57\).
- Join: \(J = \max(09:45, 09:35, 09:52) = 09:52\). **Critical branch C**, margin 7 over A. \(FJ = 57\).
- Sync waits: \(W_A = 7\), \(W_B = 17\), \(W_C = 0\).
- Downstream: 10:10 − 09:52 = **18**. F2 was busy with F02 until 10:00 (8 minutes of assigned-busy wait). F04 then started 10 minutes after F2 became free (idle lag). F1 was idle from 09:28 to 10:00, so the both-busy wait is 0.
- Final service: 20.
- Total: 10:30 − 08:35 = **115** = 20 + 57 + 18 + 20.
- Note: C is critical because of queueing (35 minutes), not service (22 minutes, less than half of A's). F05, released 15 minutes later, started its documentation review at 09:15, i.e. before F04. So the C "queue" is not FIFO and may be a readiness wait, such as missing documents.

**F07** (arrival 09:20, fork 09:40)
- Upstream: **20**.
- A: 0 + 50 = 50.
- B: queue 10:10 − 09:40 = **30**; service 10:55 − 10:10 = 45; \(D_B = 75\).
- C: queue 5; service 18; \(D_C = 23\).
- Join: \(J = \max(10:30, 10:55, 10:03) = 10:55\). **Critical branch B**, margin 25. \(FJ = 75\), the maximum in the log.
- Sync waits: \(W_A = 25\), \(W_B = 0\), \(W_C = 52\). Their sum (77) exceeds \(FJ\), which illustrates the double-counting trap.
- Downstream: 11:10 − 10:55 = **15**. F1 had been free since 10:40, so all 15 minutes are idle lag. F08 (ready 10:50) went to F2 and waited for F2 until 11:05 even though F1 was idle, which is consistent with static round-robin assignment.
- Final service: 20.
- Total: 11:30 − 09:20 = **130** = 20 + 75 + 15 + 20, the longest case.
- Note: B's queue alone (30 minutes) is larger than F07's downstream wait. F08, released 15 minutes after F07, started its stress test at 10:00, 10 minutes before F07.

**F11** (arrival 10:20, fork 10:40)
- Upstream: **20**.
- A: 0 + 50 = 50.
- B: queue 10:50 − 10:40 = 10; service 30; \(D_B = 40\).
- C: queue 5; service 18; \(D_C = 23\).
- Join: \(J = \max(11:30, 11:20, 11:03) = 11:30\). **Critical branch A**, margin 10 over B. \(FJ = 50\), the structural floor.
- Sync waits: \(W_A = 0\), \(W_B = 10\), \(W_C = 27\).
- Downstream: 11:50 − 11:30 = **20**. The assigned resource F1 was busy with F09 from 11:30 to 11:50, so all 20 minutes are assigned-busy. F2 was idle from 11:25 to 11:40 and then served F10, so only 10 minutes (11:40–11:50) were true both-busy congestion.
- Final service: 20.
- Total: 12:10 − 10:20 = **110** = 20 + 50 + 20 + 20.
- Note: this case shows masking. Speeding up A would move \(J\) earlier, but F1 would still not be free before 11:50 (see Section 4).

## Interpretation

- **Service time is misleading.** A has by far the largest mean service (50 minutes), but it is critical in only 2 of 12 cases. A never queues, so its elapsed time is exactly 50, which is a constant floor. B has less service (37.5 minutes), but adding its queue (mean 19.2 minutes, rising through the morning) gives it the largest elapsed time, the largest variance and every tail case.
- **Contribution to `join_ready`.** A useful way to write the stage is \(FJ_c = 50 + \text{excess}_c\). The mean \(FJ\) of 61.0 minutes is a 50-minute floor set by A in every case plus an 11-minute excess. Of that excess, B causes 10.0 minutes per case and C causes 1.0.
- **Tails.** Every case with \(FJ \ge 65\) or \(T \ge 120\) (F03, F06, F07, F09, F12) is B-critical. The two 75-minute stages both carry a 30-minute B queue.
- **Queue plus service matters.** B's critical time is 38% queue. C becomes critical only through queue spikes (35 and 25 minutes, both non-FIFO), never through service alone.
- **Robustness to timestamp resolution.** The smallest margin is 5 minutes (F01, F08 and F10). With one-minute accuracy, the error on a difference of two completions is at most about 2 minutes, so no critical label flips because of rounding. These three cases are still near-ties in a business sense: a 5-minute change in the runner-up would swap the critical branch.

## Influence ranking (with honest ties)

1. **B – Machine Stress Test: clearly most influential.** It is critical in 8 of 12 cases, causes 120 of the 132 minutes of excess over the floor, drives all tail cases, and has the largest counterfactual pay-off (Section 4).
2. **A – Laboratory Assay: second, but a near-tie with C on current metrics.** Measured by present criticality, A and C tie (2 vs 2 cases; margins of 15 vs 12 minutes). I rank A above C on structural grounds:
   - A sets the 50-minute floor in every case.
   - A is the runner-up in 10 of 12 cases.
   - A becomes the binding branch as soon as B improves: under counterfactual 2 it is critical or tied in 6 cases.

   The influence A has *today* is small, though.
3. **C – Documentation Review: third.** It is critical twice, only through anomalous queueing. In the other cases it has large slack (mean 36.5 minutes). It is a tail risk rather than a systematic driver.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

## How the downstream station is modelled (assumption A6)

Branch changes alter \(J_c\). Whether that reaches \(T_c\) depends on how the final station behaves, so I report two models:

- **Fixed downstream wait (pass-through bound).** \(DQ_c\) is held constant, so the flow saving equals the join saving.
- **Final-station replay (preferred).** For each case, \(S^F_c = \max(J_c, \text{free}_{k(c)}) + \text{lag}_c\).
  - Observed F1/F2 alternation (round-robin by case order) is kept, as are FIFO order by \(J\), the observed case-specific idle lag \(\text{lag}_c = DQ^{idle}_c\), and 20-minute service.
  - This model reproduces all 12 observed final starts exactly, and it captures both masking (the assigned resource is still busy) and knock-on relief (earlier cases free a resource sooner).
  - Holding \(\text{lag}_c\) invariant under intervention is itself an assumption.

## CF1 – reduce A service by 20% (50 → 40 minutes)

| case | new D_A | new FJ | new critical | join saving | flow saving (replay) | reason |
|---|---:|---:|:--:|---:|---:|---|
| F01 | 40 | 45 | B | 5 | 5 | A was critical; B (45) now binds |
| F11 | 40 | 40 | A=B (tie) | 10 | **0** | masked: F1 busy with F09 until 11:50 |
| Other 10 | 40 | unchanged | B or C | 0 | 0 | A not critical |
| **Total** | | | | **15** | **5** | |

- **Direct benefit.** 120 minutes of A work removed (10 per case) yields at most 15 minutes of join saving, and only 5 minutes of flow saving under replay. That is 0.42 minutes per case, or 0.4% of mean flow time.
- **Zero-benefit cases.** 10 cases gain nothing because B or C remains critical. F11 gains nothing because final-station congestion absorbs its 10-minute join gain. Maximum flow time stays at 130.
- **Secondary effects.** A spare assay capacity of 20% could be redeployed if A staff can help B or C, but A and B skills may not overlap. Shortening a fixed-duration assay may threaten analytical validity: exactly 50 minutes in all 12 cases looks like a protocol duration. This is the clearest example of why the largest service time is not the most influential branch.

- **Direct benefit.**

  - Mean flow time falls from 115.25 to 105.08, and maximum flow time from 130 to 115. This is the strongest single branch intervention.

  - Masking also occurs (F06 loses 7 of 15 minutes).

  - Halving B queue requires either more B capacity or a different dispatching rule. That changes B utilization, but not the B work content.

Only 73 of the 176 downstream minutes occurred while the assigned resource was busy, and only **35** while both F1 and F2 were busy. **103** minutes (59%) elapsed while the assigned resource was idle. Most "downstream queueing" is therefore not capacity queueing, so adding capacity and changing the release policy lead to very different results.

- **CF3b – third final resource, pooled.**
  - **Direct benefit:** this removes exactly the 73 assigned-busy minutes (6.1 per case).
  - **Zero benefit:** 6 cases gain nothing, and maximum flow time stays at 130 because F07's wait is pure idle lag.
  - **Secondary effects:** final-station utilization falls from about 57% (240 busy minutes over 2 × 212 minutes of the 09:08–12:40 window) to about 38%. That is expensive for a modest gain.
- **CF3c – event-triggered release (remove idle lag).**
  - **Direct benefit:** all 12 cases gain, 171 minutes in total (14.25 per case). Mean flow time falls to 101.0 and maximum to 115.
  - **Zero benefit:** none. The remaining 5 minutes (F11) are genuine congestion.
  - **Secondary effects:** pooling on top of this (CF3d) adds nothing (still 171). The upside is conditional on the lag being avoidable. If the lag is unlogged preparation work (reading the dossier), removing it will partly move into final service time.
- **CF3a – pooled dispatch only.**
  - **Direct benefit:** the net saving is small (20 minutes) and redistributes waits: +15, +15 and +10 for three cases, −10 and −10 for two.
  - **Secondary effects:** maximum flow time *worsens* to 140, under my assumption that each case's lag holds the server it was dispatched to. Pooling alone cannot fix waits that are not capacity-driven, so this result is indeterminate and should not be taken as evidence that pooling is harmful.
- **Masking.** Downstream behaviour masked 10 of CF1's 15 join-saving minutes and 7 minutes of F06's gain in CF2. A join-triggered release removes that masking.
- **Interaction (CF2 + CF3c).**
  - The combined saving is 268.5 minutes (22.4 per case). Mean flow time falls to 92.9 and maximum to 100.
  - This is sub-additive (122 + 171 = 293), because part of CF2's knock-on relief acted on the same downstream minutes.

Half-minute values come from applying 50% to odd queue lengths. They are kept for consistency; a real intervention would produce whole minutes.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

## Redesign 1 – slack-aware scheduling and targeted capacity at the Machine Stress Test (B)

- **Idea.** The goal is not zero B queue. A B test only matters if it finishes after the A floor. For each case the B queue budget is \(50 - P_{c,B}\): 5 minutes for 45-minute tests, 20 minutes for 30-minute tests. The queue beyond that budget sums to exactly 120 minutes over 8 cases, the whole B-driven excess. Measures:
  - Book the B slot at *arrival*, using the 20-minute upstream lead time.
  - Give priority to long tests, which have the least slack.
  - Add capacity only at the late-morning peak, when the B queue grows from about 5 to 30 minutes.
  - In the observed data, the 30-minute tests had the shortest queues (F08 even overtook F07). That is the reverse of what synchronization requires, although this is a hypothesis from a small sample.
- **Throughput.** The arrival rate is 4 per hour. The B offered load is about 37.5 / 15 ≈ 2.5 busy test stations on average, and at least 4 concurrent tests were observed (10:50–10:55). Whether B limits throughput depends on the unknown number of stations. Reordering the queue does not change B capacity. Added capacity helps only if B is near saturation, which the rising queue suggests but does not prove.
- **Flow-time tail.** The CF2 gain is a maximum of 130 → 115. Perfect budget adherence would make \(FJ = \max(50, D_C)\), giving a mean \(FJ\) of 51.0 instead of 61.0, with only F04 (57) and F08 (55) above 50.
- **Resource utilization.** B work content is unchanged. A priority rule costs nothing in capacity. Extra peak capacity lowers B utilization and needs a cost case.
- **New bottlenecks.** A becomes the binding branch in most cases. Its fixed 50 minutes then sets the pace, and further gains require an assay protocol change. C's queue spikes (F04, F08) become the tail driver, and the final station receives more tightly clustered joins.
- **Synchronization effects.** A's slack shrinks towards zero and many A=B ties appear. Any disruption in either technical branch then goes straight to the join. The system becomes faster but more fragile.
- **Pilot and measure first.**
  - Log the B queue-entry time, the test station ID and station availability or downtime, plus the test type.
  - Pilot the priority rule on alternate days.
  - Measure the share of B-critical cases, the distribution of \(J - r\), p90 of \(T\), and the change in A-critical frequency.
  - Verify that A truly never queues.

## Redesign 2 – event-triggered, pooled release at the final station

- **Idea.**
  - Emit a system `join_ready` event when the last branch completes, and have final resources pull from one FIFO queue ordered by `join_ready`. This replaces the apparent static F1/F2 alternation (strictly odd cases to F1, even to F2) and whatever causes the idle lag.
  - Only 35 of 176 waiting minutes occurred with both resources busy. So capacity should be added only if both-busy waiting persists after the policy change.
- **Throughput.** Capacity is 2 servers × 3 per hour = 6 per hour against 4 per hour demand (ρ ≈ 0.67). The redesign changes neither the requirement nor the capacity, but it raises effective capacity by removing idle-but-assigned time.
- **Flow-time tail.** CF3c reduces the maximum from 130 to 115 and the mean by 14.25 minutes. It also removes masking, so branch improvements pass through almost one-for-one. The combination with Redesign 1 reaches a maximum of 100 (CF2 + CF3c).
- **Resource utilization.** Service minutes stay the same, and idle gaps inside cases become idle gaps between cases. If the lag is actually unlogged preparation, measured service will grow and the benefit will shrink.
- **New bottlenecks.** Genuine congestion appears when joins cluster (5 minutes remain for F11). Combined with Redesign 1, which makes joins earlier and more regular, both-busy waits could increase, so monitor it.
- **Synchronization effects.** The final station becomes tightly coupled to `join_ready`, so the F start mirrors branch variability directly. The downstream buffer that currently hides branch improvements (and branch deteriorations) disappears.
- **Pilot and measure first.**
  - Instrument `join_ready` (dossier complete) and "picked up by F" events, and add reason codes for idle gaps: other duties, batching, review preparation, handover transport.
  - Measure the lag distribution by resource and time of day.
  - Pilot pooled pull on selected days, and measure \(DQ^{idle}\), \(DQ^{both}\), final service and decision quality (rework or reversals).

    df[c] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[c])

def mins(delta):
    return delta.dt.total_seconds() / 60.0


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Decomposition (custom pandas: fork-join logic is max-based, not DFG-based)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
d = pd.DataFrame({"case": df["case"]})
d["upstream_elapsed"] = mins(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])
for b in BR:
    d[f"{b}_queue"] = mins(df[f"{b}_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    d[f"{b}_service"] = mins(df[f"{b}_end"] - df[f"{b}_start"])
    d[f"{b}_elapsed"] = d[f"{b}_queue"] + d[f"{b}_service"]    # end - fork_release

join_ready = df[[f"{b}_end" for b in BR]].max(axis=1)           # not logged: derived
d["join_ready"] = join_ready.dt.strftime("%H:%M")
d["fork_join_elapsed"] = mins(join_ready - df["fork_release"])
for b in BR:
    d[f"{b}_sync"] = mins(join_ready - df[f"{b}_end"])          # branch-local, overlapping


def critical(row_elapsed, tol=0.0):
    """Deterministic critical-branch label: all branches within tol of the max,
    joined with '=' in fixed A,B,C order; credit is split equally among ties."""
    m = max(row_elapsed.values())
    tied = [b for b in BR if m - row_elapsed[b] <= tol]
    return "=".join(tied), {b: (1.0 / len(tied) if b in tied else 0.0) for b in BR}


labels, credits, margins = [], [], []
for _, r in d.iterrows():
    el = {b: r[f"{b}_elapsed"] for b in BR}
    lab, cr = critical(el)
    labels.append(lab)
    credits.append(cr)
    s = sorted(el.values(), reverse=True)
    margins.append(s[0] - s[1])                                # criticality margin
d["critical_branch"] = labels
d["crit_margin"] = margins
d["downstream_queue"] = mins(df["final_start"] - join_ready)
d["final_service"] = mins(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
d["total_flow_time"] = mins(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])

# identity check: no double counting along the case timeline
chk = d["upstream_elapsed"] + d["fork_join_elapsed"] + d["downstream_queue"] + d["final_service"]
assert (chk == d["total_flow_time"]).all()
for b in BR:  # every branch partitions the same fork-join window
    assert (d[f"{b}_queue"] + d[f"{b}_service"] + d[f"{b}_sync"] == d["fork_join_elapsed"]).all()

print("\n=== Case-level decomposition (minutes) ===")
print(d[["case", "upstream_elapsed", "A_queue", "A_service", "B_queue", "B_service",

         "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"]].to_string(index=False))
print("\n=== Synchronization waits per branch (overlapping; do NOT sum across branches) ===")
print(d[["case", "fork_join_elapsed", "A_sync", "B_sync", "C_sync"]].to_string(index=False))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Aggregate branch comparison
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
cred = pd.DataFrame(credits)
rows = []
for b in BR:
    rows.append({
        "branch": b,
        "svc_mean": d[f"{b}_service"].mean(), "svc_min": d[f"{b}_service"].min(),
        "svc_max": d[f"{b}_service"].max(),
        "q_mean": d[f"{b}_queue"].mean(), "q_max": d[f"{b}_queue"].max(),
        "elapsed_mean": d[f"{b}_elapsed"].mean(),
        "elapsed_p90": d[f"{b}_elapsed"].quantile(0.9), "elapsed_max": d[f"{b}_elapsed"].max(),
        "crit_count": cred[b].sum(),
        "sync_mean": d[f"{b}_sync"].mean(),
        # join-time removed if branch were made infinitely fast = sum of margins where critical
        "max_join_gain_total": sum(m for m, c in zip(margins, cred[b]) if c == 1.0),
    })
agg = pd.DataFrame(rows).round(2)
print("\n=== Branch comparison ===")
print(agg.to_string(index=False))

comp = d[["upstream_elapsed", "fork_join_elapsed", "downstream_queue", "final_service",
          "total_flow_time"]].agg(["mean", "sum"]).round(2)
print("\n=== Stage components ===")
print(comp.to_string())


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Final-station replay model and counterfactuals
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Replay model (assumption): start = max(ready, server_free) + lag_c, where lag_c
# is the observed part of downstream wait during which the ASSIGNED server was idle
# (handover/pickup/policy delay). With observed round-robin assignment this
# reproduces every observed final_start exactly.
res_free = {"F1": pd.Timestamp.min, "F2": pd.Timestamp.min}
busy_wait, lag = [], []
for _, r in df.sort_values("final_start").iterrows():
    jr = join_ready[r.name]
    free = res_free[r["final_resource"]]
    ready_eff = max(jr, free)
    busy_wait.append((r.name, (ready_eff - jr).total_seconds() / 60))
    lag.append((r.name, (r["final_start"] - ready_eff).total_seconds() / 60))
    res_free[r["final_resource"]] = r["final_end"]
d["dq_assigned_busy"] = pd.Series(dict(busy_wait))
d["dq_idle_lag"] = pd.Series(dict(lag))


def pooled_busy_overlap():
    """Minutes of each case's downstream wait during which BOTH final resources
    were serving other cases (lower bound of true capacity queueing)."""
    out = []
    for i, r in df.iterrows():
        t0, t1 = join_ready[i], r["final_start"]
        grid = pd.date_range(t0, t1, freq="1min", inclusive="left")
        n = 0
        for t in grid:
            busy = {o["final_resource"] for j, o in df.iterrows()
                    if j != i and o["final_start"] <= t < o["final_end"]}
            n += len(busy) == 2
        out.append(n)
    return out


d["dq_both_busy"] = pooled_busy_overlap()
print("\n=== Downstream wait split ===")
print(d[["case", "downstream_queue", "dq_assigned_busy", "dq_idle_lag", "dq_both_busy"]]
      .to_string(index=False))
print("sums:", d[["downstream_queue", "dq_assigned_busy", "dq_idle_lag", "dq_both_busy"]].sum().to_dict())


def simulate_final(join_min, servers=("F1", "F2"), dispatch="round_robin", lag_scale=1.0):
    """join_min: Series of join_ready in minutes since midnight (index = df index).
    Returns final_end in minutes. FIFO by join_ready; ties broken by case id.
    Assumption: the case-specific lag is incurred after dispatch and holds the server."""
    order = sorted(df.index, key=lambda i: (join_min[i], df.at[i, "case"]))
    free = {s: -1e9 for s in servers}
    end = {}
    for i in order:
        if dispatch == "round_robin":
            s = servers[df.index.get_loc(i) % len(servers)]    # observed F1/F2 alternation
        else:  # pooled: earliest-free server, ties by server name
            s = min(servers, key=lambda x: (free[x], x))
        start = max(join_min[i], free[s]) + lag_scale * d.at[i, "dq_idle_lag"]
        free[s] = start + FINAL_SERVICE
        end[i] = free[s]
    return pd.Series(end).sort_index()


fork_m = (df["fork_release"] - df["fork_release"].dt.normalize()).dt.total_seconds() / 60
arr_m = (df["arrival"] - df["arrival"].dt.normalize()).dt.total_seconds() / 60
base_end = simulate_final(fork_m + d["fork_join_elapsed"])
assert ((base_end - arr_m) == d["total_flow_time"]).all()     # replay is exact


def scenario(name, a_el, b_el, c_el, **fin):
    fj = pd.concat([a_el, b_el, c_el], axis=1).max(axis=1)
    join_new = fork_m + fj
    naive = d["fork_join_elapsed"] - fj                       # downstream wait held fixed
    end = simulate_final(join_new, **fin)
    replay = d["total_flow_time"] - (end - arr_m)
    crit = [critical({"A": a, "B": b, "C": c})[0] for a, b, c in zip(a_el, b_el, c_el)]
    out = pd.DataFrame({"case": d["case"], "new_crit": crit, "join_saving": naive,
                        "flow_saving_replay": replay})
    return name, out


A0, B0, C0 = d["A_elapsed"], d["B_elapsed"], d["C_elapsed"]
scens = [
    scenario("CF1 A service -20%", d["A_queue"] + 0.8 * d["A_service"], B0, C0),
    scenario("CF2 B queue -50%", A0, 0.5 * d["B_queue"] + d["B_service"], C0),
    scenario("CF3a pooled dispatch (2 servers)", A0, B0, C0, dispatch="pooled"),
    scenario("CF3b 3 pooled servers", A0, B0, C0, servers=("F1", "F2", "F3"), dispatch="pooled"),
    scenario("CF3c remove idle-lag (RR, 2 servers)", A0, B0, C0, lag_scale=0.0),
    scenario("CF3d pooled + no lag", A0, B0, C0, dispatch="pooled", lag_scale=0.0),
    scenario("CF2+CF3c B queue -50% & no lag", A0, 0.5 * d["B_queue"] + d["B_service"], C0,
             lag_scale=0.0),
]
summary = []
for name, out in scens:
    print(f"\n=== {name}: per-case savings (minutes) ===")
    print(out.to_string(index=False))
    fl = d["total_flow_time"] - out["flow_saving_replay"]
    summary.append({"scenario": name,
                    "join_saving_total": out["join_saving"].sum(),
                    "flow_saving_total": out["flow_saving_replay"].sum(),
                    "flow_saving_mean": out["flow_saving_replay"].mean(),
                    "cases_zero_benefit": int((out["flow_saving_replay"] <= 0).sum()),
                    "new_mean_flow": fl.mean(), "new_max_flow": fl.max()})
print("\n=== Counterfactual summary (baseline mean flow %.2f, max %.0f) ==="
      % (d["total_flow_time"].mean(), d["total_flow_time"].max()))
print(pd.DataFrame(summary).round(2).to_string(index=False))

perf_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log, case_id_key="case:concept:name",
                                                  activity_key="concept:name",
                                                  timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
print("\n=== PM4Py performance DFG (mean of next.start - prev.complete, minutes) ===")

Running this script reproduces every number in Sections 2–4.

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality.**
  - There are 12 cases from one morning, observational only, with no randomization and no covariates such as product type or urgency.
  - "Critical branch" is an accounting statement about which completion was last. It is not a causal claim that the branch's resource or process caused the delay.
  - The counterfactuals are deterministic replays that assume invariance: other branches' timestamps, the final-station lags, the round-robin assignment and arrivals are unchanged by the intervention. Real interventions would change behaviour, for example queues re-forming elsewhere or staff reprioritizing.
  - The savings are therefore scenario estimates with a model-dependent range (for example 92.5–122 minutes for CF2), not predictions.
- **Branch resource capacity.**
  - No resource IDs are logged for A, B or C. Concurrency gives only lower bounds: A at least 4 concurrent assays, B at least 4 concurrent tests (10:50–10:55), C at least 2.
  - Without capacity, it is impossible to say whether B's queue is caused by capacity (it rises through the morning), scheduling (F08 overtook F07; short tests waited less), or availability such as downtime or calibration.
  - The 35- and 25-minute C queues are non-FIFO and may be waits for missing information rather than queueing.
  - Whether A has spare capacity, or shares staff with B, is unknown. That limits any claim about redeploying A capacity.
- **Work preemption and interval semantics.**
  - Complete-event start and end times cannot reveal pauses, multitasking or setup inside the service interval. "Service" is elapsed time between start and end, not effort.
  - The constant values (A = 50, F = 20, upstream = 20) suggest scheduled or fixed-duration steps rather than measured work.
  - The start event may record when the worker began or when the job was logged. With one-minute resolution, margins of 5 minutes or more are robust to rounding, but not to systematic logging lags.
- **Fork and join definitions.**
  - `fork_release` coincides with `A_start` in every case, so A's queue may be zero by construction.
  - `join_ready` is derived under a pure AND-join. If F also requires an unlogged event, for example document sign-off, part of what I call downstream wait belongs to the join itself.
- **Is final-station waiting a queue or a policy?**
  - Only 35 of 176 minutes occurred with both F1 and F2 busy, and 103 minutes elapsed while the assigned resource appeared idle.
  - Assignment strictly alternates F1/F2, which is consistent with static routing.
  - The data cannot distinguish handover or transport delay, batching or scheduled review slots, preparation work before the logged start, other duties of F1/F2 not in this log, or a deliberate release policy.
  - Treating \(S^F - J\) as queueing would wrongly point to "add capacity". The evidence instead favours instrumenting and redesigning the release, but that also remains a hypothesis until the idle gaps are explained.
- **Scope.** One day and a steady 15-minute arrival stream give no information about demand variability, time-of-day effects beyond the observed trend, or tails beyond 12 observations. Percentiles such as p90 over 12 values are unstable. There is no right-censoring in the data, but cases that were still open at the end of the window are not visible at all.
