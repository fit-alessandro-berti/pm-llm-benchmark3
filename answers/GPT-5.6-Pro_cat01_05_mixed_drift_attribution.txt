## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-fitness collapse is mostly a representation/provenance discontinuity. Baseline raw and mapped fitness averaged 0.925; in March raw fell to 0.55 while mapped was 0.90. Thus mapping and duplicate removal recovered 0.35 points, about 93% of the apparent loss from baseline. This supports a label/schema mismatch as the main cause of raw nonconformance, but it does not prove process execution was unchanged or that the remaining 0.025 gap is substantive.

Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` asserts semantic equivalence; conformance recovery only shows that the old model can replay canonicalized traces. It cannot establish that screening criteria, timing, actor, or granularity remained equivalent. Likewise, deleting duplicated `Open Case` events repairs a technical artifact but could conceal a real operational opening step if the rule is too broad.

Duplicated technical events inflate trace length, create spurious start/DFG relations and variants, and depress raw fitness. Missing resources mainly damage organizational analyses—handover networks, team workloads, role discovery, and resource-conditioned waiting—and because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, complete-case analysis would be biased. Label-only normalized DFGs are less directly affected if case IDs, activities, and timestamps remain valid.

Later drift cannot be dismissed as logging. From April to May, mapped fitness fell 0.91→0.88, rework rose 13%→24%, `Assess→Request Info` doubled to 24 per 100 cases, and median/p90 cycle time rose 32→36.5/71→84 hours. These normalized behavioral and performance changes then intensified despite a stable label inventory, indicating real process and population mechanisms beyond migration.

**2. Normalized DFG-fragment distance.**

**Assumption D1 — fragment comparison:** only the seven supplied normalized relations are compared. Omitted edges are ignored, not treated as zero or unchanged in the full process.

For rate vector (r_m), measured in occurrences per 100 cases, define:

[
D_{L1}(m)=\sum_{e\in E_f}|r_{m,e}-r_{Jan,e}|
]

and

[
D_{rel}(m)=D_{L1}(m)\Big/\sum_{e\in E_f}r_{Jan,e}.
]

The January fragment has total rate mass 403. Because these are repeatable relation rates rather than probabilities, this is not total-variation or Jensen–Shannon distance.

**5. Global versus mechanism-specific change.** The evidence rejects a useful one-global-change-point account. March changes logging artifacts while normalized behavior and performance remain nearly stable. May changes supplier exposure, rework, mapped fitness, and performance. June–July adds an organizational capacity shock and then a routing policy. October introduces a recurring seasonal mix/load regime. December is a joint reversal. A single detector run on a composite score might identify March or May depending on feature weighting, but it would collapse substantively different mechanisms and misdirect remediation.

The **primary monitoring population** should be all cases grouped by `Register` date, analyzed on a canonical-label copy while preserving a raw shadow log and retaining cases with missing resources. This population reflects the total effect on incoming work, avoids completion-cohort survivorship, and does not condition on rework, priority realization, team assignment, or outcome. Mix-standardized estimates should be maintained as a parallel diagnostic view, not substituted for the primary population.

Seasonality should be handled by comparing renewal-season windows with renewal-season references rather than forcing October–November into the January–February distribution. Where multiple years are available, use same-calendar-week references and model weekday, holiday, volume, and high-complexity effects. With only the supplied year, “recurring” remains historically informed and should be prospectively verified.

Multiple testing should be hierarchical. Schema failures and explicitly forbidden ordering violations are gating controls and need no multiplicity discount. Global DFG, rework, queue, and outcome monitors form the primary family and should generally require persistence or sequential error control. Edge-, team-, and interaction-level searches are diagnostic and should use false-discovery-rate control, such as a 5% Benjamini–Hochberg target, minimum support, and confirmation in the next window. Different statistics retain different practical and statistical thresholds; there should be no universal z-score or percentage rule.

def load_and_validate() -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    monthly = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV))
    rates = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(DFG_RATE_CSV))

    monthly["month"] = pd.PeriodIndex(monthly["month"], freq="M")
    rates["month"] = pd.PeriodIndex(rates["month"], freq="M")

    if monthly["month"].duplicated().any() or rates["month"].duplicated().any():
        raise ValueError("Month keys must be unique.")
    if not monthly["month"].is_monotonic_increasing:
        raise ValueError("Monthly data must be chronological.")

    expected = pd.period_range(
        monthly["month"].min(), monthly["month"].max(), freq="M"
    )
    if list(monthly["month"]) != list(expected):
        raise ValueError("The monthly summary must contain every month.")

    if monthly.isna().any().any() or rates.isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Unexpected missing values in supplied tables.")
    if (monthly["cases"] <= 0).any():
        raise ValueError("Case counts must be positive.")

    percentage_columns = [
        "high_complexity_pct",
        "supplier_z_pct",
        "fast_variant_pct",
        "rework_variant_pct",
        "missing_resource_pct",
        "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    ]
    if not monthly[percentage_columns].apply(
        lambda s: s.between(0, 100)
    ).all().all():
        raise ValueError("Percentage columns must be in [0, 100].")

    fitness_columns = ["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]
    if not monthly[fitness_columns].apply(
        lambda s: s.between(0, 1)
    ).all().all():
        raise ValueError("Fitness values must be in [0, 1].")
    if (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] <
            monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).any():
        raise ValueError("Mapped fitness is unexpectedly below raw fitness.")

    edge_columns = [c for c in rates.columns if c != "month"]
    if (rates[edge_columns] < 0).any().any():
        raise ValueError("DFG occurrence rates cannot be negative.")
    if not set(rates["month"]).issubset(set(monthly["month"])):
        raise ValueError("Every DFG month must exist in the monthly table.")

    return monthly, rates


def baseline_relative_changes(
    monthly: pd.DataFrame,
    baseline_months: Sequence[str] = ("2026-01", "2026-02"),
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    periods = [pd.Period(m, freq="M") for m in baseline_months]
    baseline = monthly.loc[
        monthly["month"].isin(periods)
    ].mean(numeric_only=True)

    numeric = monthly.set_index("month").select_dtypes(include="number")
    changes = numeric.subtract(baseline, axis="columns")
    changes.attrs["baseline"] = baseline
    return changes


def fragment_l1_distances(rates: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Transparent fragment-only distance.

    The supplied values are occurrences per 100 cases, not a probability
    distribution. Therefore this is a normalized L1 rate distance, not
    total variation or Jensen-Shannon divergence.
    """
    indexed = rates.set_index("month")
    reference = indexed.iloc[0]
    baseline_mass = float(reference.sum())

    result = pd.DataFrame(index=indexed.index)
    result["l1_rate_points"] = (
        indexed.sub(reference).abs().sum(axis=1)
    )
    result["mean_abs_edge_change"] = (
        indexed.sub(reference).abs().mean(axis=1)
    )
    result["relative_l1"] = (
        result["l1_rate_points"] / baseline_mass
    )
    result["max_edge_change"] = (
        indexed.sub(reference).abs().max(axis=1)
    )

    step_l1 = [np.nan]
    step_relative = [np.nan]
    for i in range(1, len(indexed)):
        previous = indexed.iloc[i - 1]
        current = indexed.iloc[i]
        distance = float((current - previous).abs().sum())
        step_l1.append(distance)
        step_relative.append(distance / float(previous.sum()))

    result["step_l1_rate_points"] = step_l1
    result["step_relative_l1"] = step_relative
    return result


def raw_to_mapped_recovery(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    baseline_fit = monthly.loc[
        monthly["month"].isin(
            [pd.Period("2026-01", "M"), pd.Period("2026-02", "M")]
        ),
        "mapped_model_fitness",
    ].mean()

    result = monthly.loc[
        monthly["month"] >= pd.Period("2026-03", "M"),
        ["month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"],
    ].copy()

    result["fitness_recovered"] = (
        result["mapped_model_fitness"] -
        result["raw_model_fitness"]
    )
    apparent_raw_loss = baseline_fit - result["raw_model_fitness"]
    result["fraction_of_apparent_loss_recovered"] = np.where(
        apparent_raw_loss > 0,
        result["fitness_recovered"] / apparent_raw_loss,
        np.nan,
    )
    return result.set_index("month")


def practical_change_point_flags(
    monthly: pd.DataFrame,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Simple screening rule: flag a month when its absolute month-to-month
    change exceeds a predeclared practically material threshold.

    These are candidate change points, not formal hypothesis tests.
    """
    thresholds = {
        "cases": 80.0,
        "high_complexity_pct": 8.0,
        "supplier_z_pct": 10.0,
        "fast_variant_pct": 8.0,
        "rework_variant_pct": 5.0,
        "median_cycle_h": 4.0,
        "p90_cycle_h": 10.0,
        "raw_model_fitness": 0.15,
        "mapped_model_fitness": 0.025,
        "distinct_activity_labels": 1.0,
        "missing_resource_pct": 2.0,
        "duplicate_open_case_pct": 2.0,
    }

    records = []
    for metric, threshold in thresholds.items():
        deltas = monthly[metric].diff()
        for row_index, delta in deltas.items():
            if pd.notna(delta) and abs(float(delta)) >= threshold:
                records.append(
                    {
                        "month": monthly.loc[row_index, "month"],
                        "metric": metric,
                        "month_to_month_delta": float(delta),
                        "threshold": threshold,
                    }
                )

    if not records:
        return pd.DataFrame(
            columns=[
                "month", "metric",
                "month_to_month_delta", "threshold"
            ]
        )

    return (
        pd.DataFrame(records)
        .sort_values(["month", "metric"])
        .reset_index(drop=True)
    )

def format_event_dataframe(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Copy, validate, timestamp-normalize, and format an event dataframe."""
    _require_pm4py()

    required = {case_id_key, activity_key, timestamp_key}
    missing = required.difference(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(
            f"Missing mandatory event-log columns: {sorted(missing)}"
        )

    work = df.copy(deep=True)
    work[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(
        work[timestamp_key], utc=True, errors="raise"
    )

    if work[
        [case_id_key, activity_key, timestamp_key]
    ].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError(
            "Case ID, activity, and timestamp must be non-null."
        )

    if "_source_row_id" not in work.columns:
        work["_source_row_id"] = np.arange(
            len(work), dtype=np.int64
        )

    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id=case_id_key,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )

    return (
        formatted.sort_values(
            [case_id_key, timestamp_key, "_source_row_id"],
            kind="mergesort",
        )
        .reset_index(drop=True)
    )


def slice_time_window(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    start: str | pd.Timestamp,
    end_inclusive: str | pd.Timestamp,
    mode: str = "events",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Slice with PM4Py's events or trace-based time-range semantics."""
    _require_pm4py()

    valid_modes = {
        "events",
        "traces_contained",
        "traces_intersecting",
    }
    if mode not in valid_modes:
        raise ValueError(
            f"mode must be one of {sorted(valid_modes)}"
        )

    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        log,
        pd.Timestamp(start).isoformat(),
        pd.Timestamp(end_inclusive).isoformat(),
        mode=mode,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
    )


def registration_cohort(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    start: str | pd.Timestamp,
    end_exclusive: str | pd.Timestamp,
    register_label: str = "Register",
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Identify Register events in [start, end) using filter_time_range,
    then recover each selected case's complete trace from the source log.
    """
    _require_pm4py()

    start_ts = pd.Timestamp(start)
    end_ts = pd.Timestamp(end_exclusive)
    if end_ts <= start_ts:
        raise ValueError("end_exclusive must be later than start.")

    event_slice = slice_time_window(
        log,
        start=start_ts,
        end_inclusive=end_ts - pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1),
        mode="events",
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
    )

    selected_case_ids = event_slice.loc[
        event_slice[activity_key].eq(register_label),
        case_id_key,
    ].dropna().unique()

    return log.loc[
        log[case_id_key].isin(selected_case_ids)
    ].copy()


def discover_window_artifacts(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    include_log_skeleton: bool = False,
    include_declare: bool = False,
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Discover the requested process artifacts for one complete window."""
    _require_pm4py()

    if log.empty:
        return {
            "cases": 0,
            "events": 0,
            "dfg": {},
            "start_activities": {},
            "end_activities": {},
            "variants": {},
            "temporal_profile": {},
        }

    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        log,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
    )

    artifacts: Dict[str, Any] = {
        "cases": int(log[case_id_key].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(log)),
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": starts,
        "end_activities": ends,
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(
            log,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
        ),
        "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
            log,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
        ),
    }

    if include_log_skeleton:
        artifacts["log_skeleton"] = (
            pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
                log,
                activity_key=activity_key,
                timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
                case_id_key=case_id_key,
            )
        )

    if include_declare:
        artifacts["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(
            log,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
        )

    return artifacts


def discover_monthly_artifacts(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    first_month: str,
    last_month: str,
    register_label: str = "Register",
    include_log_skeleton: bool = False,
    include_declare: bool = False,
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]]:
    """
    Produce monthly DFGs, variants, temporal profiles, and optional
    declarative models using registration-month cohorts.
    """
    _require_pm4py()

    output: Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]] = {}

    for period in pd.period_range(
        first_month, last_month, freq="M"
    ):
        start = period.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        end_exclusive = (
            period + 1
        ).start_time.tz_localize("UTC")

        window = registration_cohort(
            log,
            start=start,
            end_exclusive=end_exclusive,
            register_label=register_label,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        )

        output[str(period)] = discover_window_artifacts(
            window,
            include_log_skeleton=include_log_skeleton,
            include_declare=include_declare,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        )

    return output


def make_raw_and_normalized_logs(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: Mapping[str, str],
    technical_labels: Iterable[str] = ("Open Case",),
    remove_exact_technical_duplicates: bool = True,
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, Dict[str, Any]]:
    """
    Construct independent raw and normalized logs without modifying df.

    Only exact technical duplicates with the same case, canonical activity,
    and timestamp are removed. Broader deduplication requires a documented
    source-specific event-ID or retry rule.
    """
    raw_source = df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_source = df.copy(deep=True)

    normalized_source[f"{activity_key}:raw"] = (
        normalized_source[activity_key]
    )
    normalized_source[activity_key] = (
        normalized_source[activity_key]
        .replace(dict(label_mapping))
    )
    normalized_source[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(
        normalized_source[timestamp_key],
        utc=True,
        errors="raise",
    )

    removed_mask = pd.Series(
        False, index=normalized_source.index
    )

    if remove_exact_technical_duplicates:
        removed_mask = (
            normalized_source[activity_key].isin(
                set(technical_labels)
            )
            & normalized_source.duplicated(
                subset=[
                    case_id_key,
                    activity_key,
                    timestamp_key,
                ],
                keep="first",
            )
        )
        normalized_source = normalized_source.loc[
            ~removed_mask
        ].copy()

    raw_log = format_event_dataframe(
        raw_source,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
        activity_key=activity_key,

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is the March discontinuity to **logging/semantic migration, 95% confidence**. Raw fitness fell from a January–February mean of 0.925 to 0.55, while mapped fitness remained 0.90; labels rose from 9 to 11, duplicated `Open Case` appeared in 4% of cases, and resource missingness jumped to 4.8%. January-to-March normalized DFG-fragment distance was only 0.7%, and cycle-time statistics were essentially unchanged. This combination is difficult to explain as a real operational collapse.

The May change is most plausibly a combination of **supplier-driven population and behavioral drift, 80–90% confidence**. Supplier Z’s share rose 18%→43%, rework rose 13%→24%, and both normalized rework-loop edges doubled. Because Z already served harder cases, part is case-mix drift; because incomplete bundles generate `Request Info`, part is causal rework mediation. The aggregates cannot separate those channels.

The June–August deterioration is attributed mainly to **capacity-driven queueing after assessor departures, 75–85% confidence**, amplified by supplier rework. `Assess` service time increased only about 0.6 hours, whereas reconstructed waiting and cycle tails rose much more. The July priority rule is a **plausible but unproven redistribution mechanism, 50–65% confidence**: it may protect high-complexity cases while worsening standard-case waits, but aggregate medians cannot show this fairness trade-off.

The October–November peak is strongly consistent with **seasonal population/load drift, 80–90% confidence**: volume, high-complexity share, Senior Assessment use, and p90 time all rose together. Yet seasonality interacts with unresolved capacity constraints and priority routing, so it is not a clean standalone effect. December’s improvement is assigned jointly to lower seasonal pressure, reduced Supplier Z exposure, overtime, and the quality campaign; intervention-specific attribution is only **45–60% confidence** because all changed in the same monthly window.

These confidence levels are structured judgments, not estimated posterior probabilities. I would revise the account if supplier-stratified DFGs showed equal rework increases for non-Z cases, event-level waiting did not rise after June 15, priority-stratified waits showed no redistribution after July 1, or historical renewal seasons lacked the October–November pattern. Evidence that `Eligibility Screen` changed decision semantics—not merely its label—would also lower confidence in the “mostly logging” March diagnosis.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness falls by at least 0.15 while mapped fitness remains within 0.03 of its reference and label or duplicate rates change sharply, THEN classify the alert provisionally as logging-semantic drift and perform a lineage audit before escalating a business-process failure, BECAUSE vocabulary and technical-event mismatches can mimic severe control-flow nonconformance.

2. IF the adjusted `Assess→Request Info→Collect Documents` loop rate rises by at least 5 percentage points and the increase is concentrated in Supplier Z after complexity and region adjustment, THEN initiate supplier document-quality remediation and separately manage the affected backlog, BECAUSE incomplete bundles create repeat work and additional assessment demand.

3. IF `Assess` service time remains within 15% of reference while pre-`Assess` p50 or p90 waiting exceeds its capacity-adjusted threshold, THEN prioritize queue-capacity actions rather than worker-speed interventions, BECAUSE the deterioration is arising mainly before service rather than during execution.

4. IF high-complexity waiting falls or remains stable after July 1 while standard-case p90, overtaking frequency, or age-at-service rises, THEN conduct a fairness review and introduce priority aging or maximum-overtake guardrails, BECAUSE non-preemptive prioritization can improve one class by transferring delay to another.

5. IF October–November aggregate DFG, rework, and performance drift largely disappears after seasonal and case-mix standardization, THEN classify it as recurring seasonal drift and use a renewal-season reference regime, BECAUSE changing volume and complexity can move aggregate process measures without a new within-stratum behavior.

6. IF normalized DFG or variant drift persists within supplier, complexity, and team strata among new post-recovery cases after inherited backlog is excluded, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation and consider revising the monitored process model, BECAUSE within-stratum persistence is not explained by population composition or the March label migration.

7. IF December performance improves while intervention exposure, volume, complexity, and Supplier Z share all change in the same window, THEN withhold single-cause credit and run a daily exposure-based interrupted-time-series analysis, BECAUSE simultaneous intervention and population changes prevent clean attribution from monthly co-movement alone.
