## 1. Decision rule
### 1.1 Eligibility and timing gates (hard rules)
- **Stage gate**: eligible only if current stage is strictly **before** `Diagnosis Complete` (i.e., `Incident Opened`, `Triage`, `Diagnosis Started`). If `Diagnosis Complete` has occurred, do **not** use a swarm (expected \(\Delta p \approx 0\)).
- **Time gate**: require **≥ 60 minutes** to SLA deadline to schedule a 45-minute swarm plus immediate follow-through (otherwise default to non-swarm escalation).
- **Repeat-swarm gate**: at most **one swarm per “diagnosis episode.”** A second swarm requires (i) a *materially new event* after the prior swarm and (ii) explicit duty-manager approval.

### 1.2 Alert persistence and “material change” (to stop refresh spam)
Maintain a **case watchlist** with a stored “evidence snapshot” from the last alert. Suppress repeated alerts unless **any** of the following occurs:
- **New diagnostic evidence**: new customer log / dump / repro, new error signature, scope change, or new dependency implicated.
- **Process change**: stage transition (e.g., `Triage` → `Diagnosis Started`), ownership/resource change, or queue position jumps materially.
- **Risk shift**: calibrated risk midpoint changes by **≥ 0.10**, or crosses an operational band (e.g., 0.50, 0.70).
- **Data-quality change**: missing/imputed key feature becomes observed (or vice versa).
- **Deadline threshold crossed**: time-to-deadline drops below **4h** or **2h** while still eligible.

### 1.3 Robustifying breach probabilities for known calibration issues
Use a **segment-aware calibrated interval** \(p_{\text{cal}}\) rather than raw `p_now`:
- **SME**: \(p_{\text{cal}} \in [p_{\text{now}}+0.04,\; p_{\text{now}}+0.09]\) (clip to \([0,1]\)).
- **Strategic Enterprise**: \(p_{\text{cal}} \in [\max(0,p_{\text{now}}-0.12),\; \max(0,p_{\text{now}}-0.05)]\).
- **Public Sector**: use \(p_{\text{cal}} \approx p_{\text{now}}\) but treat uncertainty as moderate (operate on an interval, not a point).
- **Non-Native-Language Support**: treat both calibration and effects as **high-uncertainty**; use a wider interval (e.g., \(p_{\text{now}} \pm 0.10\), clipped) until better evidence is collected.

Important: `p_now` is **observational**; it drives *urgency* and *triage*, but intervention choice is driven primarily by **expected incremental benefit** and **externalities**, not by `p_now` alone.

### 1.4 Prioritization score (decision-theoretic, capacity-aware)
For each eligible case \(i\), compute a **robust net-impact score** using conservative effect and a spillover penalty:
\[
S_i \;=\; \underbrace{\Delta p^{(\text{rob})}_i \cdot V_i}_{\text{gross avoid-breach value}} \;-\; \underbrace{(450 + FA_i)}_{\text{direct intervention costs}} \;-\; \underbrace{\text{SpillPenalty}}_{\text{queue externality}}
\]
Where:
- \(\Delta p^{(\text{rob})}_i\) is a conservative swarm effect:
  - default: **midpoint** of the supplied range for ranking, but **must remain positive** under a pessimistic check (lower bound or “one-case-wrong” adverse scenario for the portfolio);
  - if **data-quality is degraded** (missing/imputed key feature) or **segment evidence is sparse**, use **lower-bound** effect for gating and downgrade the tier unless urgency is extreme.
- **SpillPenalty** approximates harm to untreated diagnosis-stage cases:
  - base model: each used slot increases breach probability of each of **six** untreated diagnosis-stage cases by **0.01–0.03**;
  - operationally: estimate \(\bar V_{\text{diag}}\) (rolling average “value of avoiding breach” among diagnosis-stage cases) and set
    \(\text{SpillPenalty} \approx 6 \cdot \delta_{\text{spill}} \cdot \bar V_{\text{diag}}\) with \(\delta_{\text{spill}}\) chosen from the low/mid/high end depending on congestion.

### 1.5 Capacity allocation rule (including reserve)
- Maintain **one slot in reserve until 15:00** (policy preference) unless there are ≥2 Tier-A cases that are both (i) urgent (≤3h to deadline) and (ii) strongly positive under pessimistic checks.
- Use remaining slots on the highest \(S_i\) cases **subject to**:
  - **stop-loss**: if predicted spillover implies ≥0.5 additional expected breaches in the untreated diagnosis queue, cap further swarms until congestion drops;
  - **fairness guardrail**: do not let any segment go unserved over a day when it has Tier-A risk/effect cases (details in §5).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
### 4.1 Cool-down, suppression, escalation
- **Alert cool-down**: once a case alerts, suppress further alerts for **2 hours** unless “material change” triggers re-entry (§1.2). This prevents 30-minute refresh spam from dominating attention.
- **Swarm cool-down**: after a swarm, suppress additional swarm recommendations for **4 hours** (or until a materially new event), and require manager approval for any repeat swarm.
- **Escalation ladder** (before consuming a swarm slot):
  - Tier A, urgent (≤3h): try swarm unless spillover stop-loss triggers.
  - Tier B or data-quality degraded: do a **10-minute verification** step (data integrity + human sanity check) before committing the slot.
  - Tier C repeated/unchanged: suppress alerts; keep watchlist; escalate only as deadline bands are crossed.

### 4.2 Explicit comparison: I-05 vs I-07
- **I-05 (third alert, no new event)**:
  - Treat as **unchanged evidence** → **suppress** repeated alerts.
  - Keep a single persistent watchlist entry with the last decision (“deferred due to capacity/spillover”) so it is not forgotten.
  - Re-enter only on material change or when time-to-deadline crosses a band (e.g., <3h) with still-high calibrated risk.
- **I-07 (third alert, new customer log uploaded)**:
  - New log is a **material change** → valid **re-entry** even if `p_now` is slightly lower.
  - Because time-to-deadline is 1.5h, escalate immediately with **non-swarm fast-track** actions; consider a swarm only if a slot is idle and congestion is low (to avoid harming many others for a small-value case).

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
### 5.1 Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases
Risk: maximizing \(\Delta p \cdot V\) can systematically starve SMEs or lower-value incidents even when their calibrated risk is high.
- **Safeguard**: impose a **daily minimum service constraint**: reserve at least **1 of 4** swarm slots (or equivalent senior time) for cases that meet a high-risk/high-uplift condition **regardless of value** (e.g., \(p_{\text{cal,mid}}>0.70\) and \(\Delta p_{\text{low}}>0.15\)), unless doing so violates the spillover stop-loss.
- **Safeguard**: publish a **“deferred due to capacity”** list reviewed every 2 hours so low-value cases aren’t silently ignored.

### 5.2 Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support
Risk: sparse calibration/effect estimates can lead to either over-intervention (wasted slots) or under-intervention (systematic neglect).
- **Safeguard**: route these cases through a **human-in-the-loop verification** (translator/support specialist + senior) before the swarm decision; treat them as **high-variance** and avoid using them as the “one-case-wrong” sacrificial pick.
- **Safeguard**: allocate a small **exploration budget** (randomization within Tier A/B) to learn effects in this segment without making it bear all deferral or all spillover.

### 5.3 Differential calibration across segments
Risk: raw `p_now` induces unequal alerting and unequal triage.
- **Safeguard**: operate on \(p_{\text{cal}}\) intervals and track segment-wise reliability; do not use a single raw-probability threshold.
- **Safeguard**: when uncertain, prioritize **actions with low externalities** (fast-track triage, documentation requests) before swarms.

### 5.4 Selective intervention changing future training labels
Risk: swarms reduce breaches; future models trained naively on outcomes will encode policy effects, causing biased predictions and unstable feedback loops.
- **Safeguard**: log a **policy decision record** per case prefix: eligibility, score components, congestion estimate, and whether swarm was applied. Train predictive models with intervention indicators and use **off-policy / causal evaluation** rather than treating outcomes as i.i.d.
- **Safeguard**: maintain a “no-swarm” counterfactual estimator (e.g., uplift model or doubly robust estimator) to separate risk from treatment effect.

### 5.5 Spillover harms falling systematically on untreated groups
Risk: if swarms are disproportionately used for Strategic Enterprise, the added delay may disproportionately harm SMEs/Public Sector cases waiting in the same diagnosis queue.
- **Safeguard**: include spillover penalties explicitly in the score and enforce a **congestion cap** (max swarms per 90 minutes when queue indicators exceed thresholds).
- **Safeguard**: monitor spillover outcomes by segment; if untreated SMEs show systematic delay/breach increases on high-swarm days, tighten the cap or increase the spillover penalty for high-value segments.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
### 6.1 Operational monitoring (capacity + workflow)
- Swarm utilization: slots used, start delay, adherence to “before Diagnosis Complete,” and time spent per swarm.
- Queue health: diagnosis WIP, wait times, interruption counts for the two specialists, and time-to-stage transitions.
- SLA outcomes: breach rate, and “time remaining at swarm” vs. outcomes.

### 6.2 Predictive monitoring (risk model)
- Segment-level calibration: reliability curves / Brier decomposition per **SME / Strategic Enterprise / Public Sector / Non-Native-Language**.
- Drift checks around workflow changes (esp. Strategic Enterprise redesign): monitor systematic overprediction and update the segment offsets.
- Alert quality: precision/recall *at the decision tier level* (Tier A/B/C), not only at probability cutoffs.

### 6.3 Causal-effect monitoring (what swarms actually do)
- **Randomized rationing when oversubscribed**: if > available slots in Tier A, allocate remaining slots by a **lottery within a narrow score band** to create causal identification while staying operationally fair.
- Estimate uplift with **doubly robust** or matched comparisons using logged features, stage, and congestion; report effects by segment and by stage (Triage vs Diagnosis Started).
- Track heterogeneous effects: which alert reasons (e.g., missing info vs complex dependency) produce real benefit.

### 6.4 Spillover monitoring (externalities)
- Treat “number of swarms per hour” as an exposure; measure impact on untreated diagnosis-stage cases’ waiting time and breach outcomes.
- Build an empirical mapping from congestion indicators → \(\delta_{\text{spill}}\) to replace the 0.01–0.03 heuristic with calibrated estimates.

### 6.5 Fairness monitoring
- Intervention rates, deferral rates, and breach outcomes by segment/context (including Non-Native-Language Support).
- Spillover distribution: which groups are most represented among the “six untreated diagnosis-stage cases” and how their outcomes change on high-swarm days.
- Audit the “deferred due to capacity” list for persistent exclusion patterns.

### 6.6 Recalibration and policy-change triggers
- **Weekly**: refresh segment calibration offsets; widen intervals where sample size is small; shrink where stable.
- **Monthly**: recompute the spillover penalty function from observed data.
- **Change the allocation rule** when any of the following holds for 2+ consecutive weeks:
  - observed spillover-induced breaches exceed the base model by >50%;
  - net economic impact (including spillover) turns negative in retrospective evaluation;
  - a segment shows persistent under-service relative to its calibrated risk burden.
