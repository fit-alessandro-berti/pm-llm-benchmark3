## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-fitness fall from the Jan–Feb range of 0.92–0.93 to 0.55 is primarily a measurement discontinuity. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` back to `Validate Eligibility` and removing verified duplicate `Open Case` events recovers fitness to 0.90, a 0.35-point gain. April's gain is 0.40. That large recovery supports the migration explanation for most of the raw conformance collapse. It does not establish that every trace still follows the intended workflow: mapped fitness remains below the early baseline, the mapping may hide genuine changes within the eligibility step, and fitness is only one summary of alignment behavior.

Duplicated technical events create spurious directly-follows edges and variants and can introduce artificial alignment moves. They should be removed only when identified as duplicates, retaining the raw log and an audit trail. Missing resource identifiers have a different effect: they weaken team, handoff, and capacity analyses and may bias comparisons because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities. They need an explicit “unknown” stratum and sensitivity bounds, not silent complete-case deletion.

After May, the normalized `Assess→RequestInfo` rate climbs from 12 to 24, then 29 per 100 cases; mapped fitness also slips to 0.86–0.88. Median and tail cycle times rise, while recorded `Assess` service time changes modestly and reconstructed queue waiting rises more. Operational-table variant and duration measures were not calculated from the same normalized log as fitness, so cross-source reconciliation remains necessary. Still, the supplier disruption, repeated document collection, and growing waits jointly resist a pure logging explanation. A changed label alone would not explain those independent duration and supplier patterns.

**Normalized DFG distance.** For the seven supplied relation rates, use the unweighted L1 distance: sum of absolute differences in occurrences per 100 cases. Against January, the distances are March 3, May 40, July 59, September 28, October 64, December 36 edge-occurrences per 100 cases. May's 40 includes +12 each for `Assess→RequestInfo` and `RequestInfo→CollectDocs`; October's 64 includes +18 for `Assess→SeniorAssessment`. Thus the later shift survives eligibility-label normalization, although the October senior edge may reflect changed recording or case mix. These rates are *not* a probability distribution: loops can create repeated occurrences, the decision relation combines two source activities, and omitted edges must not be assumed zero. The distance describes only this fragment and cannot be interpreted as a fraction of cases changing variants.

**Cycle-time decomposition.** The observed median rises 13.85 h from Jan–Feb to August and 17.85 h to November; the p90 rises 42 h and 53 h respectively. An exact additive decomposition of medians is impossible. Case mix plausibly raises work and wait as high-complexity share moves from 20.5% to 29% in August and 45% in November; Supplier Z's selected population also changes. Rework adds repeat collection/assessment visits: its variant share rises from 11.5% to 27–29%, and the two rework edges rise together. Recorded service per `Assess` grows only about 0.6 h by July–August, so direct change in that step's service is small relative to the cycle increase, though extra visits add service. Queueing is the leading proximate explanation for the remaining delay because reconstructed pre-assessment wait rose much more; staffing loss, supplier-generated workload, priority, and seasonal volume can all produce it. No percentage shares are identifiable without case-level counterfactual standardization, visit counts, service across every step, and censoring-aware waits.

**Candidate change points, not formal detections:** logging around March 1 (eligibility label, duplicates, missing resources) and October 1 (senior-assessment recording versus routing; weaker); behavioral around May 5 (rework), July 1 (queue priority), and October 1 (senior path, mixed with season); performance around May, June 15–July (capacity and queue), October–November (renewal load), and December 5 (intervention and seasonal easing). Multiple mechanism-specific points fit the evidence. A single global point would conflate a March schema break with later operational changes. Monthly sampling cannot distinguish a June 15 effect from a July 1 effect or date December's within-month recovery.

The primary monitoring population is **all cases by registration (intake) cohort**, retaining open cases and unknown supplier/complexity/resource categories. It preserves the denominator when routing, completion speed, or staffing changes. Report outcome and cycle statistics with follow-up/censoring rules; use event-time windows separately for operational queue alerts. Publish standardized as well as observed rates, with fixed pre-change weights and later seasonal weights shown explicitly.

# Part B — reusable event-level PM4Py functions; not run without a raw event log.
# Required columns: case_id, activity, timestamp; optional duplicate_open_case flag.
def format_event_log(events):
    import pm4py
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
    absent = required - set(events.columns)
    if absent:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing event columns: {sorted(absent)}")
    copy = events.copy(deep=True)
    copy["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(copy["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="raise")
    if copy[list(required)].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Required event fields contain missing values")
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(copy, case_id="case_id",
                                  activity_key="activity",
                                  timestamp_key="timestamp")


def normalized_copy(events, duplicate_flag_col="duplicate_open_case"):
    """Map label; remove only Open Case events explicitly verified as duplicates."""
    if duplicate_flag_col not in events.columns:
        raise ValueError("A verified duplicate flag is required for removal")
    mapped = events.copy(deep=True)
    duplicate = (mapped["activity"].eq("Open Case")
                 & mapped[duplicate_flag_col].eq(True).fillna(False))
    mapped = mapped.loc[~duplicate].copy()
    mapped["activity"] = mapped["activity"].replace(
        {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"})
    return mapped


def month_artifacts(formatted_log, month, with_skeleton=False):
    import pm4py
    period = pd.Period(month, freq="M")
    start = period.to_timestamp().tz_localize("UTC")
    next_start = (period + 1).to_timestamp().tz_localize("UTC")
    # PM4Py time slicing; enforce a half-open calendar month afterward.
    window = pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_log, start, next_start,
                                     mode="events")
    window = window.loc[window["time:timestamp"] < next_start].copy()
    if window.empty:
        return {"month": str(period), "event_count": 0}
    result = {
        "month": str(period),
        "event_count": len(window),
        "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(window),
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(window),
        "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window),
    }
    if with_skeleton:
        result["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(window)
    return result


def monthly_artifacts(formatted_log, months, with_skeleton=False):
    """Compute one PM4Py artifact bundle for each supplied calendar month."""
    return {str(pd.Period(m, freq="M")): month_artifacts(formatted_log, m, with_skeleton)
            for m in months}


def compare_raw_and_normalized(events, month, duplicate_flag_col="duplicate_open_case"):
    """Return both views; raw events remain untouched for provenance checks."""
    raw = format_event_log(events)
    mapped = format_event_log(normalized_copy(events, duplicate_flag_col))
    return {"raw": month_artifacts(raw, month),
            "normalized": month_artifacts(mapped, month)}

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion concerns March. The migration changed the observed vocabulary and event stream: most eligibility checks acquired a new label, some `Open Case` events were duplicated, and resource IDs became less complete. Raw fitness drops by 0.375 points from the Jan–Feb mean, while mapped fitness drops only 0.025. I assign high confidence (about 95/100) that semantic and provenance drift explains most of this specific collapse. The residual mapped difference cannot be assigned confidently.

The May–July rework rise is most consistent with Supplier Z's incomplete bundles (moderately high confidence, about 80/100). The normalized request-information edge doubles from 12 to 24 occurrences per 100 cases by May and reaches 29 by July, in step with rework variants. Yet Supplier Z serves a selected, somewhat more complex population. Its changing share can create population drift alongside a real within-supplier defect effect. The aggregate increase needs stratified traces and bundle-quality evidence.

Queueing is the leading proximate source of cycle-time deterioration (about 80/100): pre-assessment waits reportedly rise much more than the roughly 0.6 h increase in recorded assessment service. Four assessor departures plausibly reduce capacity (about 75/100), but supplier-driven repeat work raises arrivals to the same queue. The July priority rule may shift delay from high-complexity to standard cases (about 55/100); aggregate medians cannot verify the direction in either class. Supplier, staffing, and priority effects interact and cannot be split into additive portions of the 13–18 h median rise.

October–November's volume and complexity surge is consistent with the known recurring renewal season (about 80/100), although only one year's monthly data are supplied. The senior-assessment edge jump may be real routing, a larger high-complexity population, better recording, or all three (low-to-moderate confidence in a true within-stratum behavior change). September and December improvements are likewise mixed: supplier recovery, changing intake, backlog clearance, overtime, and the document campaign overlap. I would revise this account if matched Supplier Z cases show no post-May defect/rework increase, if verified queue timestamps fail to show added waiting after capacity loss, if priority-class waits do not diverge after July, or if audit records show that October senior assignments did not increase within comparable cases. Harmonized intake-cohort traces and prior renewal seasons would sharpen the estimates.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops but mapped fitness and verified case paths remain near baseline while new labels or duplicate technical events spike, THEN audit and repair the schema mapping and preserve both log views, BECAUSE semantic drift can create false conformance alarms.
2. IF mapped `Assess→RequestInfo→CollectDocs` incidence rises within Supplier Z cases relative to matched non-Z cases after May, THEN investigate bundle completeness and the supplier interface, BECAUSE an exposure-specific rework increase supports supplier-driven behavior.
3. IF assessment queue wait rises after capacity loss while within-stratum per-visit service remains roughly stable, THEN rebalance assessor capacity and track backlog by intake cohort, BECAUSE queue congestion can dominate cycle-time deterioration.
4. IF high-complexity wait falls relative to standard-case wait after July while standard-case p90 rises, THEN review the priority rule and standard-case service level, BECAUSE scheduling can redistribute delay without improving aggregate flow.
5. IF October volume and high-complexity share rise within historical renewal bounds but standardized rework and waits stay stable, THEN classify the aggregate change as seasonal mix and retain the seasonal reference, BECAUSE recurrence alone does not establish a new workflow rule.
6. IF senior-assessment edges rise within fixed complexity strata and verified reviewer assignments rise too, THEN update the process model and staffing forecast, BECAUSE concordant trace and assignment evidence supports genuine routing change over label drift.
7. IF December cycle and rework improve while new-case defect rates and backlog also fall after adjusting for seasonal intake, THEN continue and evaluate overtime and document-quality measures with matched cohorts, BECAUSE simultaneous mechanism-level improvement is stronger evidence than an aggregate December decline alone.
