## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

Raw fitness fell from 0.925 to 0.55 in March, but mapped fitness stayed at 0.90. Every month after that, the mapping recovers 0.32–0.40 fitness points, which is 85–96% of the raw drop. This means the old model cannot parse the new vocabulary. In March, 78% of eligibility events carried `Eligibility Screen` and about 4% of cases gained a duplicated `Open Case`. It does not mean the process changed in March, and it does not prove the process stayed the same.

A mapping restores conformance syntactically by asserting that `Eligibility Screen` equals `Validate Eligibility`. If the new screen is partly automated, uses different criteria, or is done by another role, the mapped log still conforms while the business content of the check has changed. That equivalence has to be validated against durations, performers and outcomes, not against fitness. The residual 0.025 mapped-fitness drop in March, with unchanged performance, is small but unexplained.

Duplicated technical events distort case-start timestamps, variant counts, start edges and alignment costs. They barely affect decision rates. Missing resources are concentrated in migrated activities, so they are not missing at random. This biases handover networks, workload-per-resource and staffing analyses, but leaves control-flow analyses intact.

The post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift. They appear after normalization (request-info rises from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases). They also appear in label-independent operational tables: rework goes from 12% to 29% and the median from 31 h to 45 h. The vocabulary had been constant since March. And the patterns partly reversed after Supplier Z's September recovery, which a static label change cannot produce.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG distance

Measures:
1. **L1 distance over the seven supplied edge rates (occurrences per 100 cases) against January.** This captures changes in both how often edges occur and how flow is shaped.
2. **The same L1 with `RequestInfo → CollectDocs` dropped** ("loop counted once"). That edge is almost collinear with `Assess → RequestInfo`, so plain L1 double-counts the rework loop.
3. **Total variation (TV) distance on edge *shares*** (each month's rates normalized to sum to 1). This captures shape only.

Assumptions:
- Omitted edges (`Decision → Close`, repeated `Request Info`, `Senior Assessment → Request Info`, `Open Case` edges, and so on) are assumed unchanged. Every distance is therefore a **lower bound** on true DFG change.
- An edge absent in one month would count as 0.
- January is the only reference, because there is no February DFG.

| 2026-05 | 40 | 28 | 0.062 | 13 | 24 | 3 | 37 (18.5) |
| 2026-07 | 59 | 42 | 0.089 | 20 | 34 | 5 | 19 (9.5) |
| 2026-09 | 28 | 20 | 0.044 | 9 | 16 | 3 | 31 (15.5) |

How to read it:
- **March:** a distance of 3 is noise. After normalization, control flow did not change at migration.
- **May–July:** distance is dominated by the rework loop (24 of 40, then 34 of 59).
- **October:** a *different* signature, with the senior part (18) as large as the main-path part. So October is not a recurrence of the supplier regime.
- **September → October:** the fastest change per month (36 in one month).
- **Main-path decline** (`Assess/Senior → Decision` 83 → 72 per 100 in July). This could be diversion into loops, decisions lagging arrivals (backlog growth, if the windows are event-time windows) or right-censoring. The fragment cannot separate these.
- **Shape vs volume:** TV never exceeds about 0.10. Most of the drift is extra loop occurrences rather than wholesale replacement of paths.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Medians and p90s are not additive, so the components below are **bounded ranges** that roughly bracket the observed change. They are not an identity.

One structural fact constrains the median. Rework never exceeds 29%, so the median case is always a *non-rework* case. Rework therefore moves the median in only two ways:
- A mixture effect. If most rework cases lie above the median, the median shifts from about the 56th to about the 70th percentile of the non-rework distribution. With the baseline dispersion (p90/median ≈ 2.2, lognormal σ ≈ 0.45–0.6), that is roughly +4–7 h as an upper-leaning estimate.
- Congestion: extra assessments lengthen queues for everyone. This effect is counted under queueing.

The load index is 1.47–1.52 in summer and 1.58–1.68 in autumn (excluding senior work), against capacity reduced by four assessors. Waiting grows nonlinearly with utilization |

Two constraints on interpretation:
- **Queueing is largely *mediated* by rework and mix.** Rework adds assessments, which raise utilization, which lengthens queues. The counterfactual "effect of Supplier Z" therefore includes part of the queueing share. The components describe *where* time accrues, not *which root cause* owns it.
- **May shows queueing before staffing.** The median rose 4.5 h with the load index at 1.31, before the staffing cut, so queueing had already started. The cut then acted on an already stressed system (interaction).

### 3.5 One global change point or several?

The data clearly favor **multiple mechanism-specific change points**:
- **Logging** changed in March with no performance or normalized control-flow response (DFG distance 3, median +0.2%).
- **Rework and supplier mix** changed in May.
- **Performance** kept deteriorating through August, after rework peaked. Its lag and persistence differ from those of the rework loop.
- **Senior routing and case mix** changed in October with a new DFG signature.

A single global change point would be forced to either March or May. At March, the fitness collapse would be read as behavior. At May, the migration would be ignored and one regime would span a partial reversal and a seasonal regime with different edge signatures. With two baseline months and aggregates only, the *exact* month of each point is uncertain (±1 month, especially for gradual performance drift), but their *ordering* is robust.

## 6. Segmentation plan

Expected cell sizes guide the plan. With 600–800 cases per month, Z share of 20–45% and high-complexity share of 20–45% (positively correlated), the Z × high-complexity cell holds roughly 40–150 cases per month. That is often too small for monthly variant or p90 analysis, so pooling rules are needed.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 global | whole normalized log; weekly and monthly windows | overall health, volume, schema | none (always reported) | trend and seasonality at system level | overaggregation (Simpson's paradox across supplier/complexity) |
| L1 provenance | label provenance (old/new), duplicated-technical flag, resource-missing flag | separate logging drift from process drift | ≥ 30 events per activity × provenance × week, else pool 4 weeks | raw vs normalized; complete vs incomplete resource cases | resource missingness is outcome-related, so comparing complete vs incomplete cases conditions on a consequence of workload |
| L2 mechanism core | supplier (Z / non-Z) × intake complexity (high / standard), cohort by registration month | main attribution cells for rework, case mix, cycle time | ≥ 150 cases per cell for variant shares; ≥ 30 occurrences per DFG edge for rate estimation; ≥ 50 completed cases for median, ≥ 200 for p90; below support, pool to 2–3 months, then collapse to the parent level | within-cell pre/post comparisons around 5 May, 10 Sep, 1 Oct | must use *intake* complexity; using assessed complexity or senior-assessment presence conditions on a post-treatment variable |
| L3 queue policy | L2 × priority class × arrival week (post 1 July) | waiting redistribution and staffing effects | ≥ 40 queue entries per class-week, else 2-week windows | waiting by class before and after 1 July and 15 June | priority class is partly defined by complexity; conditioning on "reached Assess" excludes abandoned or still-waiting cases (censoring) |
| L4 organization | team / resource (resource-complete subset), tenure band | staffing redistribution, behavioral adaptation of assessors | ≥ 30 assessments per resource-month; teams with < 3 resources pooled | request-info rate per assessor, service time, handovers | team assignment is endogenous (the priority rule and redistribution route cases to teams); MNAR missing IDs |
| L5 context | region × supplier, customer type | transportability of findings and supplier-specific effects | quarterly only; ≥ 100 cases per cell | region-specific supplier effects | many cells, so multiple-testing risk; region and supplier nearly collinear |

**Primary monitoring population:** registration-week cohorts of **non-Supplier-Z, standard-complexity-at-intake cases**, on normalized labels, including open cases (survival-style handling of censoring). Reasons:
- It is the largest stable cell (roughly 40–60% of cases), so weekly monitoring has adequate support.
- By construction it is least exposed to the supplier disruption and to the seasonal complexity surge. Drift here therefore signals system-wide mechanisms: capacity loss, the priority rule (standard cases carry its waiting penalty), assessor behavioral adaptation, or logging changes.
- It serves as the cleanest reference for telling seasonal recurrence apart from genuinely new drift.

Supplier Z and high-complexity segments are monitored as secondary populations with their own references.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 label and schema | set of activity labels and attribute schema; share of each raw label per activity family; unknown-label count | frozen label dictionary plus the last 8 stable weeks | any new or vanished label or attribute is a deterministic alert; label-share change > 5 pp week-over-week | update the mapping table under version control; check semantic equivalence (duration, performer, outcome) before mapping | planned releases, test cases in production | data engineering |
| M2 event ordering and technical events | duplicated `Open Case` rate; out-of-order events (e.g., `Assess` before `Collect Documents`); events after `Close`; identical-timestamp ties | post-migration stable level (Mar–Apr: 4–5% duplicates) | CUSUM on the weekly rate with a shift size of 1.5 pp; any ordering-violation rate > 2× reference | inspect ETL batches, timestamp granularity, time zones | backfills, batch loads, daylight-saving shifts | data engineering |
| M3 resource completeness | missing-resource % by activity × team | post-migration level per activity (not a global rate) | p-chart per activity with limits from the reference; alert if 2 consecutive weeks above the upper limit | test whether missingness relates to workload, temporary staff or activity; restrict resource analyses accordingly | onboarding of new staff, system outages | data engineering + operations |
| M4 DFG distance | L1 on per-100 edge rates (loop counted once) and TV on edge shares, on the normalized DFG, per L2 segment | same season of the prior year and a trailing 8-week stable window | distance above the 95th percentile of a bootstrap distribution of week-to-week distances within the reference, for 2 consecutive weeks | decompose by edge group (main path / rework / senior); inspect top contributing edges | small cells (Z × high complexity) inflate distances; holiday weeks | process mining team |
| M5 variant distribution | top-20 variant shares plus "other"; variant entropy | as M4 | Jensen–Shannon divergence above its bootstrap reference quantile; new variant with > 2% share | align new variants to the normative model; check whether they are label artifacts | label changes (should be caught by M1 first) | process mining team |
| M6 rework | `Request Info` per 100 cases and share of cases with ≥ 2 collection rounds, per supplier × complexity | segment-specific pre-disruption level (Jan–Apr) and the prior-year same season | EWMA (λ ≈ 0.3) with segment-specific limits; supplier-specific alert if the Z rate exceeds the non-Z rate by > 2× the reference ratio | request-info reason codes; bundle completeness by supplier; per-assessor rates | backlog releases after supplier recovery; seasonal complexity | supplier management + operations |
| M7 temporal profile | pm4py temporal-profile deviation share (zeta = 2) for pairs such as `Register → Eligibility`, `Collect Documents → Assess`, `Request Info → Collect Documents`, `Assess → Decision` | temporal profile discovered on a reference window per segment | deviation share > reference share + 3 binomial SD; identify which pair deviates | separate customer-wait pairs (supplier / customer) from internal-wait pairs (queue) | holidays, weekend effects, timestamp granularity | process mining team |
| M8 queue waiting | median and p90 waiting before `Assess` by priority class; WIP; arrivals vs completions per week | pre-July for the class contrast; same season prior year | alert if standard-class p90 rises > 20% while high-complexity waiting falls (redistribution); alert if completions < arrivals for 3 consecutive weeks (backlog growth) | queue-position analysis; capacity vs load; simulate alternative priority rules | reconstructed queue entry imprecise; batch assignment | operations / workforce planning |
| M9 service time | median Assess and Senior service time by team and complexity (from start/complete events) | pre-disruption per team and complexity | ratio to reference > 1.15 for 2 consecutive weeks, risk-adjusted for complexity mix | check new or redistributed staff, tool changes, multitasking | mix shifts (hence risk adjustment); missing start timestamps | operations |
| M10 case mix | weekly volume; high-complexity-at-intake %; Z %; region shares | seasonal expectation (prior years' same weeks, or a seasonal model) | observation outside the 95% seasonal prediction interval; a large Z share shift with an unchanged customer base triggers a definition check | verify flag definitions and case-counting rules; supplier communications | genuine demand shocks; definition changes | planning / analytics |
| M11 conformance | alignment fitness on normalized labels; deviation type counts (log moves per activity); raw fitness as a provenance indicator only | post-migration normalized reference (Mar–Apr ≈ 0.90–0.91) | normalized fitness drop > 0.02 sustained 2 months, or a new dominant deviation type; raw-mapped gap change > 0.05 triggers M1 review | decide whether the model or the process changed; update the normative model through governance | model not updated for `Senior Assessment` or new legitimate paths | compliance / process owner |
| M12 outcomes | approve/reject rate and senior reject rate by segment; reopen rate | segment- and season-specific reference | risk-adjusted funnel plot per segment (binomial limits); alert on 2 consecutive months beyond the limit | check senior routing and document completeness; audit samples | seasonal population with different eligibility | compliance / business owner |

**Seasonality.** Monitors whose drivers are known to be seasonal (M6, M8–M10, M12) compare against **seasonal references**: the same calendar weeks in prior years, or a seasonal model (e.g., STL or regression with week-of-year and case-mix terms). They alert on the *residual*, not the raw level. A change is classed as **recurrence** if it:
- falls inside the prior-season prediction band;
- has the same edge signature (DFG edge-group profile); and
- decays on schedule after the season.

It is classed as **new drift** if the residual is large or the signature differs. For example, the October senior surge beyond the mix effect would count as new drift. Logging monitors (M1–M3) are not seasonal and use fixed references.

**Multiple testing.** 12 monitors × about 6 segments × weekly evaluation gives hundreds of tests per quarter. Controls:
- A **hierarchy**: M1–M3 gate the rest, because a logging alert suppresses downstream process alerts until it is resolved.
- **Persistence rules**: 2 consecutive windows.
- **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control** (q = 0.10) across segments within each monitor per week.
- **Sequential methods** (CUSUM or EWMA with tuned in-control run lengths) rather than repeated fixed-alpha tests.

Primary-population alerts page owners. Secondary-segment alerts go to a weekly review queue.

def validate(monthly, dfg):
    issues = []
    expected = list(pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-12", freq="M").astype(str))
    if list(monthly["month"]) != expected:
        issues.append("monthly table: months missing, duplicated or unordered")
    if monthly.isna().any().any() or dfg.isna().any().any():
        issues.append("missing values present")
    for c in PCT_COLS:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 100).all():
            issues.append(f"{c} outside [0, 100]")
    for c in FIT_COLS:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 1).all():
            issues.append(f"{c} outside [0, 1]")
    if (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] < monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).any():
        issues.append("mapping lowered fitness in some month - inspect the mapping")
    if (monthly["p90_cycle_h"] < monthly["median_cycle_h"]).any():
        issues.append("p90 below median")
    if not set(dfg["month"]).issubset(set(monthly["month"])):
        issues.append("DFG months not in monthly table")
    if (dfg["reg_elig"] != 100).any():
        issues.append("Register->Eligibility is not 100 per 100 cases after mapping")
    if (dfg["ri_cd"] > dfg["assess_ri"]).any():
        issues.append("RequestInfo->CollectDocs exceeds Assess->RequestInfo")
    # Cross-checks: operational rework share vs normalized DFG loop rate, and raw snippets.
    m = dfg.merge(monthly[["month", "rework_variant_pct", "cases"]], on="month")
    worst = (m["assess_ri"] - m["rework_variant_pct"]).abs().max()
    if worst > 3:
        issues.append(f"Assess->RequestInfo rate and rework_variant_pct differ by up to {worst} points")
    raw_snippets = {  # (count, cases, normalized rate reported or None)
        "2026-01 Assess->Request Info": (75, 620, 12),
        "2026-05 Assess->Request Info": (163, 680, 24),
        "2026-07 Assess->Request Info": (207, 715, 29),
        "2026-10 Assess->Senior Assessment": (167, 760, 22),
        "2026-03 Open Case->Register vs duplicate_open_case_pct": (26, 640, 4),
        "2026-03 Eligibility (both labels)->Collect Documents": (490 + 135, 640, 98),
    }
    print("Raw-snippet consistency (per 100 cases):")
    for k, (cnt, n, reported) in raw_snippets.items():
        print(f"  {k:<58s} raw={100 * cnt / n:6.1f}  reported={reported}")
    print(f"  2026-03 share of eligibility events with migrated label: {500 / 640:.1%}")
    return issues


def baseline_changes(monthly):
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE)]
    out = pd.DataFrame({"month": monthly["month"]})
    for c in REL_COLS:
        out[c + "_rel%"] = (100 * (monthly[c] / base[c].mean() - 1)).round(1)
    for c in PCT_COLS:
        out[c + "_pp"] = (monthly[c] - base[c].mean()).round(2)
    for c in FIT_COLS:
        out[c + "_abs"] = (monthly[c] - base[c].mean()).round(3)
    # Crude assessor-workload index: cases x Assess executions (1 + rework share) x service hours.
    # Service is known only for Jan-Feb (4.8 h) and Jul-Aug (5.4 h); linear interpolation Mar-Jun,
    # held at 5.4 h afterwards (an assumption, not an observation). Senior Assessment excluded.
    service = pd.Series([4.8, 4.8, 4.9, 5.0, 5.1, 5.3, 5.4, 5.4, 5.4, 5.4, 5.4, 5.4])
    load = monthly["cases"] * (1 + monthly["rework_variant_pct"] / 100) * service
    out["assess_load_index"] = (load / load[:2].mean()).round(2)
    return out


def dfg_distances(dfg):
    edges = [c for c in dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    X = dfg.set_index("month")[edges].astype(float)
    ref = X.loc["2026-01"]
    shares = X.div(X.sum(axis=1), axis=0)
    res = pd.DataFrame(index=X.index)
    res["L1_per100_vs_Jan"] = (X - ref).abs().sum(axis=1)
    # Rework loop counted once (Assess->RequestInfo and RequestInfo->CollectDocs are nearly collinear).
    res["L1_loop_once_vs_Jan"] = (X.drop(columns="ri_cd") - ref.drop("ri_cd")).abs().sum(axis=1)
    res["TV_shares_vs_Jan"] = 0.5 * (shares - shares.loc["2026-01"]).abs().sum(axis=1)
    for g, cols in EDGE_GROUPS.items():
        res[f"L1_{g}"] = (X[cols] - ref[cols]).abs().sum(axis=1)
    months = pd.PeriodIndex(X.index, freq="M")
    gaps = np.r_[np.nan, (months[1:] - months[:-1]).map(lambda d: d.n)]
    res["L1_vs_previous"] = (X - X.shift(1)).abs().sum(axis=1)
    res["L1_vs_previous_per_month"] = res["L1_vs_previous"] / gaps
    return res.round(3)


def fitness_recovery(monthly):
    base_raw = monthly.loc[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE), "raw_model_fitness"].mean()
    out = monthly[["month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]].copy()
    out["mapping_gain"] = (out["mapped_model_fitness"] - out["raw_model_fitness"]).round(3)
    drop = base_raw - out["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["share_of_raw_drop_recovered"] = np.where(drop > 0.05, (out["mapping_gain"] / drop).round(2), np.nan)
    out["residual_gap_to_baseline"] = (base_raw - out["mapped_model_fitness"]).round(3)
    return out


def change_points(monthly):
    """Explainable rule. A month t is flagged as a STEP if |x_t - x_(t-1)| >= tol and the mean of
    (t, t+1) differs from the mean of (t-2, t-1) by >= tol (i.e. the step persists). A step that is
    not persistent is TRANSIENT. GRADUAL is flagged when the 3-month cumulative change >= 1.5 tol
    without any single step >= tol inside that window."""
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(BASELINE)]
    rows = []
    for c, (kind, t) in TOLERANCE.items():
        tol = t * base[c].mean() if kind == "rel" else t
        x = monthly[c].to_numpy(float)
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            step = x[i] - x[i - 1]
            after = x[i:i + 2].mean()
            before = x[i - 2:i].mean()
            label = None
            if abs(step) >= tol:
                label = "step" if abs(after - before) >= tol else "transient"
            elif i >= 3 and abs(x[i] - x[i - 3]) >= 1.5 * tol and \
                    np.all(np.abs(np.diff(x[i - 3:i + 1])) < tol):
                label = "gradual"
            if label:
                rows.append({"family": METRIC_FAMILY[c], "metric": c, "month": monthly["month"][i],
                             "type": label, "change": round(step, 3), "tolerance": round(tol, 3)})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["family", "month", "metric"]).reset_index(drop=True)


CAVEATS = """
CAVEATS
- The normalized DFG is a 7-edge fragment. Omitted edges (e.g. Decision->Close, Assess->Assess,
  SeniorAssessment->RequestInfo, Open Case edges, repeated RequestInfo) are assumed unchanged; any
  shift in them is invisible and distances are lower bounds on true DFG change.
- Assess->RequestInfo and RequestInfo->CollectDocs are nearly collinear, so plain L1 double-counts

- DFG rates exist for 7 of 12 months; intervals between them are 1 or 2 months.

- Monthly assignment rule (case start, case end, or active cases) is unknown; 'active case' counting

  depress decision and completion rates in the most recent months.
- Only fitness was computed on normalized labels; other metrics come from operational tables.
- With 2 baseline months, variance is unknown; tolerances are judgement-based, not statistical.
- December improvements coincide with seasonal decline and two interventions; not separable here.
"""

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
NORM_ACT = "activity_normalized"
LABEL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
TECHNICAL = {"Open Case"}


def format_log(df_raw, case_col, act_col, ts_col, start_ts_col=None, ts_format=None):
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted copy; the caller's dataframe is never modified."""
    import pm4py
    kwargs = {"case_id": case_col, "activity_key": act_col, "timestamp_key": ts_col}
    if start_ts_col is not None:
        kwargs["start_timestamp_key"] = start_ts_col
    if ts_format is not None:
        kwargs["timest_format"] = ts_format
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df_raw.copy(), **kwargs)
    df["raw_label"] = df[ACT]
    return df


def add_normalized_view(df, label_map=LABEL_MAP, technical=TECHNICAL, drop_all_technical=False):
    """Raw labels stay in concept:name; normalized labels go to a separate column.
    Returns (raw_view, normalized_view, provenance) - three separate objects."""
    raw = df.copy()
    raw[NORM_ACT] = raw[ACT].replace(label_map)
    raw["is_technical"] = raw[ACT].isin(technical)
    raw["technical_rank"] = raw[raw["is_technical"]].groupby([CASE, ACT]).cumcount()
    raw["is_duplicate_technical"] = raw["is_technical"] & (raw["technical_rank"] >= 1)
    drop = raw["is_technical"] if drop_all_technical else raw["is_duplicate_technical"]
    norm = raw.loc[~drop].copy()
    prov = raw.groupby(CASE).agg(
        n_events=(ACT, "size"),
        has_migrated_label=(ACT, lambda s: s.isin(label_map.keys()).any()),
        n_duplicate_technical=("is_duplicate_technical", "sum"),
        missing_resource_share=("org:resource", lambda s: s.isna().mean())
        if "org:resource" in raw.columns else (ACT, lambda s: np.nan),
    )
    return raw, norm, prov


def case_attributes(df, attrs=("supplier", "complexity_at_intake", "priority_class", "region", "team")):
    """Case-level, pre-treatment attributes taken from the first event of each case."""
    present = [a for a in attrs if a in df.columns]
    first = df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE).first()
    out = first[present].copy()
    out["case_start"] = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    out["case_end"] = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].max()
    out["start_month"] = out["case_start"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    return out


def slice_window(df, start, end, mode="traces_intersecting"):
    """mode='events' for logging/schema monitors; 'traces_intersecting' or 'traces_contained'
    for case-level views ('traces_contained' drops long, unfinished cases - censoring bias)."""
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, mode=mode, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def month_windows(df):
    months = pd.period_range(df[TS].min().to_period("M"), df[TS].max().to_period("M"), freq="M")
    for p in months:
        yield (str(p), p.start_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"),
               p.end_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"))


def dfg_per_100(df, activity_key):
    import pm4py
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(df, activity_key=activity_key, timestamp_key=TS,
                                           case_id_key=CASE)
    n = df[CASE].nunique()
    return {edge: 100.0 * c / n for edge, c in dfg.items()}, n


def monthly_dfgs(df, activity_key=NORM_ACT, mode="traces_intersecting"):
    out = {}
    for label, start, end in month_windows(df):
        sub = slice_window(df, start, end, mode=mode)
        if len(sub):
            out[label] = dfg_per_100(sub, activity_key)
    return out


def dfg_distance(a, b, drop_edges=()):
    """L1 on per-100 rates over the UNION of edges; an edge absent in one month counts as 0."""
    keys = (set(a) | set(b)) - set(drop_edges)
    return sum(abs(a.get(k, 0.0) - b.get(k, 0.0)) for k in keys)


def variant_distribution(df, activity_key=NORM_ACT):
    import pm4py
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df, activity_key=activity_key, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    counts = {" > ".join(map(str, v)): (c if isinstance(c, (int, np.integer)) else len(c))
              for v, c in variants.items()}
    total = sum(counts.values())
    return pd.Series(counts, dtype=float).div(total).sort_values(ascending=False)

def temporal_profile_shift(ref_profile, cur_profile, pairs=None):
    """Mean/stdev (seconds) per activity pair, reference vs current, reported in hours."""
    pairs = pairs or sorted(set(ref_profile) & set(cur_profile))
    rows = [{"pair": f"{a}->{b}", "ref_mean_h": ref_profile[(a, b)][0] / 3600,
             "cur_mean_h": cur_profile[(a, b)][0] / 3600,
             "ref_sd_h": ref_profile[(a, b)][1] / 3600} for a, b in pairs]
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    if len(out):
        out["z_shift"] = (out["cur_mean_h"] - out["ref_mean_h"]) / out["ref_sd_h"].replace(0, np.nan)
    return out

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions (high confidence):**
- **March: logging and semantics.** The March fitness collapse is primarily a semantic/provenance change. The mapping recovers 85–96% of the raw drop, normalized DFG distance is about 3, and performance did not move. The remaining uncertainty is whether `Eligibility Screen` is semantically identical to `Validate Eligibility` (the 0.025 residual).
- **May: supplier-driven rework.** The May control-flow drift is real and largely supplier-driven (confidence about 75%). It appears in the normalized DFG and in label-independent tables, coincides with the Z surge, and partially reverses after Z's recovery.
- **The deterioration is multi-regime:** logging changed in March, rework in May, queueing from May to August, senior routing and mix in October.

**Plausible conclusions (moderate confidence):**
- **Queueing is the largest single source of median deterioration** in summer (30–60%). It is driven by rework-inflated load (index about 1.5) meeting capacity reduced on 15 June (confidence about 60%). Its persistence in September, when Z had recovered, fits unreplaced capacity and backlog.
- **The autumn deterioration is mostly seasonal population drift** acting on reduced capacity (about 60%). The October senior surge per high-complexity case is only partly mix: roughly half looks like a recording or routing change (about 45%).
- **The rise in the tail from July to August reflects redistribution by the priority rule** onto standard cases (about 35%). It is equally consistent with backlog alone.

**Where attribution cannot be clean:**
- **Supplier → rework → load → queue is a mediation chain.** Supplier effects are partly realized as queueing, so "queueing share" and "supplier share" overlap.
- **Staffing and the priority rule are two weeks apart** and interact with rework load.
- **The December improvement cannot be split** between the interventions and seasonal decline.
- **Resource missingness may itself be a consequence of staffing pressure.** This biases any resource-based test of the staffing hypothesis.

**What would make me revise:**
- If within-segment (non-Z, standard) rework rose in May, I would downgrade the supplier story toward behavioral adaptation.
- If the Z share jump turns out to be an active-case counting artifact, the population drift in May is smaller than it appears.
- If standard-class waiting did not rise after 1 July, I would drop the priority redistribution hypothesis.
- If the old and new eligibility labels differ in duration or outcome, the migration included real process change.
- If prior-year renewal seasons show a similar senior-routing surge, October is recurrence rather than new drift.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF a new or vanished activity label, or a label-share change above 5 pp, appears while the normalized DFG distance stays within its reference band, THEN freeze downstream process alerts for the affected activities and open a mapping review that checks duration, performer and outcome equivalence before extending the label map, BECAUSE a vocabulary change without normalized control-flow change is provenance drift, as in March, and a mapping must be validated semantically rather than accepted because it restores fitness.`
2. `IF request-info per 100 cases rises by at least 5 points within a single supplier segment while it stays within limits for other suppliers of the same intake complexity, THEN trigger a bundle-completeness audit and escalate to supplier management, BECAUSE a supplier-specific rework rise with stable within-complexity rates elsewhere indicates supplier-driven document incompleteness rather than assessor behavior or case mix.`
3. `IF request-info or rework rises within the primary population (non-Z, standard complexity at intake) for two consecutive windows, THEN review per-assessor request-info rates, tenure and reason codes, BECAUSE drift in the segment least exposed to supplier and seasonal mechanisms points to persistent behavioral adaptation or staffing-related practice change.`
4. `IF waiting before Assess rises by more than 20% while median Assess service time changes by less than 10% and weekly completions fall below arrivals for three consecutive weeks, THEN raise a capacity alert to workforce planning with a backlog projection, BECAUSE rising waiting with stable service and a negative flow balance is the signature of capacity-driven queueing, which grows nonlinearly as utilization approaches saturation.`
5. `IF standard-class p90 waiting rises while high-complexity-class waiting falls after a queue-policy change, THEN report class-specific waiting and simulate alternative priority rules before judging the policy on aggregate cycle time, BECAUSE prioritizing longer-service cases redistributes, and can increase, total waiting in ways the aggregate median hides.`
6. `IF volume, high-complexity share or senior routing rises but stays inside the prior-season prediction band with the same DFG edge-group signature, THEN classify it as seasonal recurrence and apply seasonally adjusted limits; if the residual exceeds the band or the edge signature differs, THEN treat it as new drift and segment by complexity, BECAUSE recurrence and new drift look alike in raw levels and differ only relative to a seasonal reference and in their process signature.`
7. `IF resource missingness or duplicated technical events rise in specific activities or teams, or correlate with workload, THEN restrict resource and handover analyses to the resource-complete subset, report the missingness rate beside every resource metric, and open a data-quality ticket, BECAUSE resource IDs missing not at random bias staffing and workload conclusions toward the teams that log completely, and the missingness may itself be caused by the staffing mechanism being evaluated.`
