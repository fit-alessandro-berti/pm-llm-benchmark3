# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining evidence shows a process that is simultaneously efficient for some segments and systematically unsafe for others. The central contradiction is that the **most frequent path is fast and cheap at intake but expensive downstream**, while the slower reviewed/repair paths cost more up front but avoid many complaints and compliance breaches.

Key problems, grounded in specific artifacts:

- **Frequency DFG dominance of the fast path**: `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` (2,550 cases) and `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (2,300 cases) show that the default system steers most claims into a rapid replace-first flow. This explains why the process looks “cheap and fast” in aggregate.
- **Performance DFG long-tail quality failure**: `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` has mean 96h / median 70h / p90 180h. The fast replacement is not actually “done” when it closes; a meaningful tail of cases reopens with long delays.
- **Conformance and rule violations**: Alignment diagnostics explicitly flag `Fast approval before Evidence completeness` and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. These violate **R1** (claims >EUR 400 must have Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval) and **R2** (no dispatch before evidence completeness except Premium low-risk accessories). Only **68% of traces are fitting** and log fitness is 0.91, so one-third of real behavior is non-conforming.
- **Rework statistics expose hidden cost**: Complaint/reopen occurs in **14.1% of Fast approval cases vs 3.3% of Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device cases**. Fast approval is **31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after** complaints, reshipments, and service credits. The apparent cost advantage is largely erased downstream.
- **Segment outcomes show the contradiction is not uniform**: For **Premium high-value devices**, fast path complaint/reopen is **22% vs 5%** on the reviewed/repair path, and compliance risk is high. But for **Premium low-risk accessories**, fast path median cycle is 8h vs 46h manual, and service-credit exposure is high if cycle exceeds 24h; here the slow path is worse on CX even though its complaint rate is slightly lower (3% vs 6%). The slow path is therefore not universally better.
- **Evidence loops drive the worst cycle times**: `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` (890 cases) and the finding that **26% of cases entering missing evidence repeat it** align with the `Unclear defect / missing evidence` segment’s median cycle of 140h and highest abandonment.

In short: the frequent fast path is attractive for low-risk accessories and for aggregate cost snapshots, but it generates compliance exceptions on high-value claims and a long-tail complaint/reopen pattern that destroys the cost advantage. The slower paths are better only where value/risk is high or evidence is clear.

**Stage 2 – Pilot Redesign B on Unclear defect / missing evidence cohort**
- *Why next*: Largest cycle-time problem (median 140h) and 22% share; portal can be tested with assisted upload to limit abandonment.
- *Metrics*: Repeated evidence-request rate (baseline 26%); median cycle time; abandonment rate; fitting-trace percentage.
- *Stop/go*: Scale if repeated requests fall 30%+ and abandonment does not rise >5 percentage points; stop if low-digital abandonment spikes.
- *Learning check*: Track whether `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` volume truly falls or merely shifts to portal support.

**Stage 3 – Pilot Redesign C on Standard high-value with added repair capacity**
- *Why last*: Requires capacity investment and cultural shift from replacement to repair; should follow once A proves compliance control.
- *Metrics*: Rework repair rate on high-value (baseline 12.5%); complaint/reopen (baseline 18% fast); repair-quote wait; post-rework cost per case.
- *Stop/go*: Go if rework and complaints fall sharply and technician utilization stays <85%; hold if repair-quote queue grows beyond p90 60h.
- *Learning check*: Monitor `Claim abandoned` and `Escalation board` load to ensure complaints are not displaced into abandonment.

**Cross-cutting logic**: Each experiment uses a segmented control group and includes 90-day downstream complaint, service-credit, and rework cost tracking. This prevents declaring victory on upstream cycle time while missing displaced bottlenecks or hidden cost, and respects that exact ROI/Pareto dominance cannot be asserted from the provided artifacts alone.
