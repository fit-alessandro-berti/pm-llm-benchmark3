- **RMs:** They trigger many missing-document loops, so A5 needs their co-design.

**External valuation dependency**
- Valuation is required for 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Working-capital cases. It currently starts after Credit analysis (Credit analysisCollateral request 2,400).
- Early triggering at classification for likely-collateral cases (a rule change, not a module) parallelizes the 58 h wait. A7 adds SLAs, ETA tracking, valuer routing and late-risk flags.
- The weekly committee cadence quantizes lateness: a late valuation (19%) can cost a whole week. Align valuation deadlines to committee cut-offs.
- Quality disputes need a second-opinion rule and tracked override reasons.

**Core-banking integration constraints**
- Only two modules can be integrated deeply in the first six months. I classify A4 (customer/entity master, KYC status) and A10 (non-bypassable signing gate) as the deep ones.
- A5, A6 and A7 are overlays (portal, document pipeline, partner API).
- A1, A9, A11 and A12 are deep and deferred. A11 must not be given a slot while upstream control reliability is unproven.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**P1: Quick-win (A1, A2, A5, A9)**
- *Optimizes:* high feasibility, visible front-end automation, high direct labor saving (registration ~1,584 h, classification, contract drafting), and a control element through A9's rule checks.
- *Sacrifices:* it leaves the KYC/entity blockage (A4), analyst normalization (A6), signing wait and provisional signing untouched. A1 saves hours in a slack pool. A9 depends on upstream readiness. It uses both deep slots (A1, A9) on low-constraint items. I exclude A11 deliberately because of the unreliable-upstream constraint.

**P2: Bottleneck/synchronization (A4, A5, A6, A7)**
- *Optimizes:* the long waits and loops: Resolve entity mismatch (31 h), missing-document loops, Normalize (20 h) and external valuation (58 h). Only one deep slot is needed.
- *Sacrifices:* it has no dedicated control module. A4 adds auditability but does not address provisional signing, four-eyes or contract mismatch, so compliance's first-wave condition is only weakly met. It leaves signing wait (44 h) and contract remediation untouched.

**P3: Compliance-first (A4, A10, A12, A9)**
- *Optimizes:* control findings: provisional signing (6.5%), contract-decision mismatch (8.5%/14%), remediation loops (650), audit trail and four-eyes evidence.
- *Sacrifices:* throughput. Document loops, Normalize and analyst load stay unchanged. A12 and A9 are deep, so only A4 and A10 can go live in the first half and A9/A12 land late in the year. The gate may add visible delay for the 6.5% provisional cases before A4 shortens KYC blocking.

**P4 (recommended): Critical-path plus control-gate (A4, A5, A6, A10)**
- *Optimizes:* the largest waits and loops on the critical path (KYC/entity, documents, normalization, signing). It includes two explicit control strengtheners (A4 audit-traceable resolution, A10 KYC-gated signing), and respects both the deep-integration limit and RM friction concerns.
- *Sacrifices:* high-frequency direct hours (A1, A9) and the valuation wait (A7 only as a rule change). It will shift the bottleneck to Credit analysis and Contract preparation, which must be managed.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend P4: A4 + A5 + A6 + A10 (KYC-gated), sequenced as in Section 6.**

**Why not start with the highest-frequency activity**
- Registration is high-volume but low-leverage:
  - It is an 8-minute task with a 1.5 h wait and 4% exceptions, about 3% of weighted waiting.
  - It sits in a back-office pool with slack after 15:00.
  - It is not on the binding constraint.
- Automating it mostly releases non-constraint capacity. It also increases arrival rates into KYC (4 h), Financial document review (18 h) and Credit analysis (28 h), moving queues downstream without reducing end-to-end time.
- A1 also consumes a scarce deep-integration slot and budget line, and it adds nothing to the control findings.
- Frequency measures how often work occurs, not where cases wait, loop or create risk. The cases that dominate cycle-time tails and SLA breaches are the 14.6% with entity mismatches (31 h wait, 38% rework, blocking signing), the 36% of document-review cases that loop through missing-document requests, and the 44 h signing wait.

**Why this portfolio**
- **A4** targets a low-frequency, high-impact blocker and gives compliance an auditable resolution trail. It also produces the reliable KYC-cleared status other controls depend on.
- **A5 + A6** attack the loop and normalization chain together. A5 reduces repeat loops at source, and A6 gives analysts capacity before clean inflow rises, which avoids overloading the central 9-person pool.
- **A10** addresses the largest average wait and closes the provisional-signing control gap (6.5%). It depends on A4 for flag reliability, which is why they are sequenced together. Together, A4 and A10 satisfy compliance's control requirement.
- It fits the constraints: four modules, two deep integrations (A4, A10) with overlays for A5/A6, and RM friction addressed through co-design.

**Trade-offs accepted**
- Direct labor savings are lower than P1.
- External valuation and the weekly committee cadence remain. I mitigate the first by an early-trigger rule now and A7 later.
- Contract remediation (8.5%/14%) and four-eyes (5.8%) are handled in year 1 by workflow rules and monitoring, with A9/A12 following in year 2.
- Bottlenecks will shift to Credit analysis and Contract preparation. That shift is expected, and the re-mining gates exist for it.

**Management message:** the first-year KPI should be end-to-end days and control violations avoided, not hours saved. A visible early win is available through A5 and the e-sign experience, which are customer-visible. The high-frequency activity can be automated later, cheaply, once downstream capacity and controls can absorb the extra flow.
