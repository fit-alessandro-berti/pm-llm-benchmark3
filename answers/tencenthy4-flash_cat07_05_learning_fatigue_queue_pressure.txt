# 2. Integrated explanation

The aggregate pattern is not evidence for one mechanism. It is the combined result of changing case mix, worker adaptation, queue dynamics, batching, shift design, and automation.

Early in the shift, service time is high because workers are handling backlog and carryover cases with higher complexity and higher priority. Seniors appear to absorb complex work early, especially after night or weekend backlog. This makes the first events look slow and error-prone even before any true warm-up effect is considered. Some learning or warm-up is still plausible because all experience groups show lower service times after the first 20 events, and early error rates fall through positions 16–30. But the early “learning curve” is partly a case-mix curve.

The middle of the shift shows stabilization: service time falls from 18.8 to about 12.9–13.6 minutes, and error rate falls to 2.5–2.7%. This can reflect genuine familiarization with the queue, activity, and shift context, but also the declining complexity and high-priority share. Under low WIP, later error rates for Seniors and Mid-level staff are close to their early rates, suggesting that once case mix and queue pressure are controlled, quality does not necessarily deteriorate with time-on-task.

The late shift is different. Service time continues to fall, but errors and rework rise sharply. That pattern is inconsistent with a simple fatigue story, because fatigue alone would often be expected to slow work or create a broad quality decline. The stronger explanation is a speed-quality tradeoff under queue pressure. By positions 51–75 and 76+, queue lengths rise to 62 and 88, WIP is high, and workers process cases faster: 11.4 and 10.8 minutes. At the same time, error rate rises to 4.6% and 7.9%, and seven-day rework rises to 8.2% and 13.7%. The WIP table reinforces this: high WIP cases are faster but much more error-prone, especially high-complexity cases.

Fatigue and circadian effects may still amplify the late pattern, but they are not sufficient alone. Day and Late shifts have high late error rates, while Night has long service times and urgent exceptions. Early shift ends with relatively lower errors despite substantial work, suggesting clock time, demand peak timing, inherited backlog, and exception mix all matter. Circadian effects must therefore be separated from time-on-task: 04:00–06:00 Night work is not equivalent to 15:00–17:00 Day work, even if both are “late in shift.”

Batching and workarounds add another layer. Short Document Review service times in Junior_H and Junior_I late in the Day shift may reflect efficient sequential processing, but they may also reduce per-case attention and delay feedback. Delayed Quality Check creates correction bursts in the last 45 minutes, making late errors more visible. Automation also shifts the measurement boundary: Bot_Risk is effectively instantaneous, but human corrections under high queue length reveal that some “fast” classifications create downstream work.

So the core explanation is: apparent learning lowers service time early and mid-shift, while declining complexity amplifies that decrease. Later, service time remains low or falls further because workers speed up, batch, and adapt to queue pressure; errors rise only where that speed is combined with high WIP, high complexity, delayed quality control, automation corrections, or vulnerable worker-resource combinations. The mechanism is conditional, not universal.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

- **Raw comparisons are not fair comparisons.** A worker with high late-shift errors may have received high-complexity cases, high WIP, Bot_Risk corrections, delayed Quality Checks, or urgent exceptions. Worker identity is intertwined with assignment policy.

- **Selection bias is central.** Seniors taking complex cases early can make Juniors look faster later and Seniors look slower or more error-prone on raw measures. The correct comparison is within complexity, priority, activity, WIP, and shift conditions.

- **Measurement error is likely.** `error_flag` depends on when errors are detected. Quality Check batching at the end of shift can make late periods appear worse. `rework_flag` within seven days captures only some downstream failures. Bot service time also hides correction effort.

- **Queue pressure is often management-created.** WIP and queue length reflect staffing, arrival patterns, prioritization, and handovers. Blaming individual workers for high-WIP error rates ignores the production system that created the pressure.

- **Automation quality must not be blamed on humans automatically.** More human corrections of Bot_Risk under high queue could mean better vigilance, harder cases, or genuine bot weakness. Corrections should be modeled as part of the human-automation system.

- **Do not use punitive raw dashboards.** Late-shift error rates should trigger process review, staffing review, and support, not punishment. If accountability is needed, use adjusted measures, multiple indicators, and human review.

- **Worker-load fairness matters.** A “high performer” may simply receive easier cases; a “low performer” may consistently absorb backlog, complex cases, and high-WIP periods. Fair evaluation requires exposure-adjusted workload metrics.

- **Interventions need monitoring.** Even well-intended changes can transfer burden to other shifts, hidden rework, or next-day queues. Use controlled rollout where possible and compare before/after with WIP, complexity, and shift controls.

| Waiting time | Time waiting before event/case processing; queue age at start. | Activity, priority, shift, WIP band, case | Detect whether “fast service” is achieved by letting cases wait or by skipping upstream checks. |
| WIP / queue pressure | `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`, percent of events above WIP 70, time above threshold. | Shift, clock hour, activity, resource | Primary leading risk indicator. Pair with error/rework to identify speed-quality tradeoff. |
| Error rate | Share of events with `error_flag = 1`; also adjusted error residual. | Resource group, activity, WIP band, complexity band, sequence bin, bot involvement | Do not interpret raw rates alone. Show alongside WIP and complexity. |
| Rework / detection delay | Seven-day rework rate, rework by activity, time from original event to correction. Use `get_rework_cases_per_activity` and rework paths. | Activity family, shift, resource, bot correction path | Captures errors not visible in source-event `error_flag`; detects delayed Quality Check effects. |
| Complexity mix | Mean `case_complexity_score`, high-complexity share, priority mix, high-priority share. | Sequence bin, shift, resource, first hour vs later | Essential for explaining early slowness and senior/junior differences. Alert when complexity mix shifts abruptly. |
| Batching indicators | Number of batches, share of events in batches, maximum consecutive run length, same-queue-batch flag. Use `discover_batches` and dotted chart. | Activity, resource, shift, clock hour | Detect workarounds such as late-Day Document Review batching or Quality Check bursts. |
| Handover / queue ownership changes | Number of resources per case, cross-shift handovers, ownership transfers, events after queue reassignment. Use case-level trace views and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`. | Shift boundary, priority, complexity, case duration | High handover counts may indicate fragmented ownership and delayed accountability. |
| Worker-load fairness | Events/hour, total handle time, high-complexity share, high-WIP exposure, high-priority share, late-shift share by worker/group. | Resource, experience group, shift, week | Prevents unfair comparisons and reveals whether some workers absorb disproportionate pressure. |
| Automation interaction | Bot_Risk volume, human correction rate, correction time, rework after bot classification. | WIP band, complexity, shift, activity path | Shows whether automation reduces total work or shifts it into correction/rework. |
| Quality Check timing | Share of Quality Check events in final 45 minutes, QA backlog by clock hour, deferred-check count. | Shift, activity, resource, case priority | Detects end-of-shift correction bursts without removing quality checks. |
| Flow / bottleneck performance | Transition times and performance DFG; slow paths and slow cases. Use `discover_performance_dfg`, `filter_paths_performance`, `filter_case_performance`. | Activity pair, shift, WIP band, rework loop | Shows where speed-up occurs and whether it creates downstream rework loops. |

Dashboard filters should include shift, clock hour, day/weekend, resource group, activity family, priority, complexity band, WIP band, batch flag, and bot involvement. Each performance metric should be shown both raw and adjusted, because the central analytical lesson is that apparent late-speed and late-error patterns are conditional on case mix, queue pressure, batching, automation, and shift design.
