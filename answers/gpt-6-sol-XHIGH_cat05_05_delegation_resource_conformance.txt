## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1:** For each case, compare the humans who drafted and approved. Equality is a violation even if two different aliases or accounts were used. Unknown attribution yields an unresolved result, not a pass.
- **RR2:** Apply the same human comparison to implementation and verification. Different account strings alone do not prove separation; an unresolved service-account user cannot prove it either.
- **RR3:** For each HIGH-risk ordinary approval, check the actor's identity and QM role at that timestamp, or a delegation already effective then. Check the action type, change scope, principal-absence condition, and subdelegation restriction independently. A principal QM acting personally retains authority while on leave; verify identity and any internal availability procedure, but do not treat leave as an automatic revocation. A credible, authenticated PDF signed before use can establish an earlier delegation despite later migration of its database record. The later entry alone cannot backdate or prove one; authenticate the PDF and its timestamp.
- **RR4:** Treat a credible HIGH-risk emergency approval by the IC or valid IC delegate as a conditional substitution for ordinary prior QM approval, then require an actual QM or delegate authorized **for retrospective review of that change** to review within two business days. Exclude the approval day, weekends, and site holidays; the first following business day is day one and the second ends at 23:59:59 UTC. Thus an 08-11 Tuesday approval is due by end of Thursday 08-13; CHG-505's Friday review is late, while CHG-510's Wednesday review is timely. The stated calendar has no holiday. An emergency flag by itself does not establish a credible safety rationale or the CI-44 link.
- **RR5:** Keep the raw login, normalized human, attribution basis, and uncertainty separately. For CHG-503, `svc_qms` is unresolved; for CHG-509, Eva is a strong but reviewable attribution. Recheck SoD if either attribution changes.
- **RR6:** Count repeated pairs and network edges as leads, controlling for workload and team assignment. They establish neither misconduct nor a policy breach absent a relevant rule and supporting evidence.

Technical rule results and the underlying control objective differ. For example, approving and verifying the same change is not expressly prohibited by RR1 or RR2, but may weaken independent review; a distinct verifier's login does not establish competence or actual independent inspection.

def emergency_review(case_events):

    authorized = authorized or not review_delegations.empty
    return ("PASS" if timely and authorized else
            f"FAIL: timely={timely}, authorized_reviewer={authorized}, due={due_day.date()}")

## 6. Control Improvements

- Create delegations in a controlled register before use, with signed origin, immutable creation/effective timestamps, permitted activities, case scope, conditions, and explicit subdelegation status. Preserve migration provenance and validate pre-migration signatures.
- Record principal availability at approval time, including leave exceptions and weekend/on-call status. Require a contemporaneous activation check for absence-conditioned delegations.
- Maintain a versioned human-to-alias map and use normalized human IDs in SoD rules; alert when one human uses multiple IDs in a case.
- Replace shared-account approvals and verifications with individual authentication or cryptographically bound individual session attribution. Capture MFA, workstation/session, badge, and action-level audit links.
- Enforce emergency case-to-incident linkage and safety rationale before IC substitution; route the retrospective review to an in-scope QM and calculate its business-day deadline automatically. Escalate missing or late reviews.
- Introduce risk-based reviewer independence and periodic pair rotation where feasible; review workload-adjusted concentration rather than treating repeated pairs as misconduct.
- Monitor handover and working-together networks alongside role, shift, cell, and case mix. Investigate changes in concentration, reciprocal approvals, and same-person approval/verification with record samples.
- Retain original delegation documents, event-time role snapshots, identity/session logs, verification artifacts, incident files, and audit trails long enough to reconstruct each decision.
