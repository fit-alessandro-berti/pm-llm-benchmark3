# **Decision Memo: Fiber-Installation Process Redesign (Redesign R)**

---

## **1. Executive Memo**

**Recommendation:** **Deploy a hybrid approach (Option E)**—scale Redesign R *only* for **simple low-risk houses** (34% of pilot cases) while **rolling back survey skipping, post-billing activation, and coordinator-only ownership for apartments/old buildings**. Simultaneously, **pause full national rollout** and **prioritize measurements M1, M3, M4, and M7** to clarify causal drivers of tail risk, rework, and compliance gaps. If segment-specific improvements stabilize and downstream costs align with expectations, proceed with a phased expansion.

### **Key Trade-offs**
Redesign R delivered **clear wins for simple cases** (6-day median improvement, +3pp first-time-right) but **exposed fragilities in complex segments** (apartments, old buildings) where **tail risk, rework, and compliance violations worsened**. The conflict stems from:
- **Simplification trade-offs:** Fewer handovers improved accountability but **centralized coordination underutilized field-survey expertise**, leading to skipped checks and manual overrides.
- **Predictive rule failures:** The survey-skipping rule **missed permit-sensitive cases**, creating long-tail delays and complaints.
- **Activation ownership ambiguity:** Moving activation checks post-billing **reduced upfront testing** but **shifted risk to downstream billing disputes**.

**National scaling without fixes would risk:**
- **Eroding customer trust** (NPS decline, complaint surge).
- **Increasing operational cost** (second visits, manual activations, escalations).
- **Regulatory/compliance exposure** (e.g., billing before activation).

**Rolling back entirely would lose:**
- **Proven efficiency gains** for simple cases (60% of pilot volume).
- **Lessons on coordination** (coordinator ownership reduced handovers but revealed resilience gaps).

**Hybrid deployment balances these risks** by:
1. **Preserving wins** for simple cases (where Redesign R works).
2. **Mitigating tail risk** by reverting problematic changes in complex segments.
3. **Buying time for measurement** to validate causal hypotheses before broader rollout.

**Immediate actions:**
- **Deploy Redesign R for simple houses only** (with coordinator ownership but *no* survey skipping or post-billing activation).
- **Pause full national rollout** of Redesign R.
- **Launch experiments M1, M3, M4, M7** to isolate root causes of tail risk and rework.
- **Monitor segment-specific KPIs** (e.g., p90 duration, complaint rates, manual activation frequency) for 12 weeks.

**Threshold for broader rollout:**
- If **segment-specific p90 duration improves or stabilizes** and **downstream costs (M4) do not exceed baseline**, expand to **medium-risk apartments** with stricter controls (e.g., mandatory field surveys for high-risk buildings).
- If **complaint rates or manual activations persist above 10% in any segment**, revert to baseline for that segment.

## **3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts**

### **A. High Fitness but Low Precision**
- **Fitness (0.94):** The pilot model matches observed cases well because **most cases now follow the simplified coordinator path** (e.g., merged validation/scheduling).
- **Precision (0.51):** The model **allows invalid sequences** (e.g., billing before activation, skipped surveys for high-risk buildings) that were rare in the baseline. The **low precision reflects design looseness**—the process now permits more exceptions than the baseline’s stricter rules.

### **B. Improved Median but Worse Tail**
- **Median improvement:** Simplified paths (e.g., merged validation, skipped surveys for low-risk cases) **accelerate the "happy path."**
- **Tail worsening:** **Long-tail cases now lack safeguards** (e.g., no field surveys for apartments, no permit checks for old buildings). The **predictive rule’s false negatives** (skipping surveys where needed) create **new bottlenecks** (e.g., failed visits requiring second attempts).

### **C. Fewer Handovers but Lower Resilience**
- **Handovers reduced:** Coordinator ownership **eliminated redundant checks** (e.g., no handoffs between validation and scheduling).
- **Lower resilience:** **No backup teams**—if a coordinator is absent, cases stall because **context isn’t shared** with other roles (e.g., field surveyors). The baseline’s **distributed handoffs** acted as a **resilience buffer**.

### **D. Lower Visible Cost but Higher Rework/Downstream Cost**
- **Visible cost (EUR 184  161):** Fewer handovers and simplified steps **reduced upfront labor**.
- **Hidden costs (EUR 198  207):** **Second visits, manual activations, and complaints** now dominate costs. The **incomplete downstream data** suggests these costs are **underestimated**—full measurement (M4) is critical.

### **E. Improvement for Simple Houses but Deterioration for Apartments**
- **Simple houses:** Benefit from **merged validation/scheduling** and **predictive survey skipping**.
- **Apartments/old buildings:** **Rule failures** (e.g., skipped surveys where permits were needed) and **coordinator overload** (93% utilization) **create delays and errors**.
- **Segment data confirms** the redesign’s **heterogeneous impact**—it **works for low-risk cases** but **fails for high-risk ones**.

## **6. Final Recommendation**

### **Immediate Action**
1. **Deploy Redesign R for simple low-risk houses only** (34% of pilot volume):
   - Retain **coordinator ownership** (to reduce handovers).
   - **Revert survey skipping** and **post-billing activation** for this segment.
   - **Monitor p90 duration, first-time-right rate, and complaint rate** weekly.
2. **Pause full national rollout** of Redesign R.
3. **Launch experiments M1, M3, M4, and M7** to isolate causal drivers of tail risk and rework.

### **What to Pause**
- **Scaling Redesign R nationally** until segment-specific metrics stabilize.
- **Further simplification** (e.g., additional survey skipping) without validation.

### **What to Scale**
- **Hybrid deployment** to simple houses (with controls) if:
  - **Segment-specific p90 duration improves or stabilizes** ( baseline +5 days).
  - **Downstream costs (M4) do not exceed EUR 200/case** (adjusted for segment).
  - **Complaint rates remain below 8%** for simple houses.

### **What to Measure Next**
Prioritize **M1 (risk-stratified survey skipping)** and **M3 (reason codes)** to:
- Validate the **predictive rule’s accuracy** for different building types.
- Identify **specific failure modes** (e.g., permit checks, activation disputes).

### **Threshold for Broader Rollout**
- **Expand to medium-risk apartments** only if:
  - **M1 confirms the predictive rule can be refined** (e.g., with stricter thresholds for high-risk buildings).
  - **M4 shows downstream costs are controlled** ( EUR 200/case).
  - **Complaint rates for simple houses remain stable** (<8%).
- **Avoid scaling to old buildings/permit-sensitive cases** until **M6 validates rule improvements** or **mandatory field surveys are reinstated**.

---
**Bottom Line:** *Redesign R has merit for simple cases but exposes fragilities in complex segments. A hybrid, data-driven approach—combining targeted deployment with rigorous measurement—is the safest path to learning and scaling.*
