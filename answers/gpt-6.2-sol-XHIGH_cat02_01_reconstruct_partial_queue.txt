# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1. Window and case coverage.** Use the supplied cases as the workload under study and assume no unlisted carry-in or competing work on the four resources. The cutoff $H$ is exactly 14:00. Count flows using half-open intervals $[a,b)$ and reported timestamps. P01 is counted as arriving at 08:00 in this convention. Under rounding it could already be waiting at 08:00, so actual within-window external arrivals could be 21 or 22.
- **A2. Event order and rounding.** The point reconstruction uses the reported times as clock anchors. At each clock time, apply completions and transfers, external arrivals, and then service starts, reporting the state after the entire batch. A service can start at the instant its resource is released or the case enters the queue. For sensitivity, allow each reported timestamp to vary by up to 2.5 minutes on a common clock while preserving case precedence and resource exclusivity. An S1 completion and its S2 queue entry are the same event. Rounded ties do not establish the order of other cases.
- **A3. Resources.** Treat service as a continuous, nonpreemptive interval on the recorded single-task resource. I1, I2 and C1 have 360 available minutes each, and C2 has 345, taking the maintenance boundaries as fixed. Leave gaps in the schedule as idle time when the resource is available. Allow out-of-order starts and idle time with queued work. The busy-time bounds are conditional on the recorded assignments and this service model. Logging delays beyond the rounding bands, unrecorded breaks or off-log work would require revisiting the bounds.
- **A4. Missing starts.** Use the resource order supported by the surrounding visits. The earliest start is the latest of queue entry, the prior service's completion and the start of the relevant availability block. The latest start is bounded by the visit's own completion, the next service's start and the end of the availability block. For all four visits, own completion sets the latest nominal bound. Use early, midpoint and late starts as sensitivity scenarios. The midpoint is a descriptive estimate with no implied probability distribution. The late endpoint permits zero service as a mathematical limit because no minimum service time is given.
- **A5. Censoring.** A visit with an OPEN completion contributes a measured wait and elapsed service through $H$. A queue-only visit contributes elapsed wait through $H$ and zero busy minutes. Its future service duration is unknown. P21 and P22 have no S2 entry by the cutoff. Reconstruction ends at the cutoff.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For a station visit, let $e$ be queue entry, $s$ service start, and $d$ service completion. At S1, $e$ is the external arrival. At S2, $e=d_{\mathrm{S1}}$. Waiting time is $s-e$, active service time is $d-s$, and station sojourn is $d-e$. A completed case's total sojourn is $d_{\mathrm{S2}}-e_{\mathrm{S1}}$, the sum of its two station sojourns. For an OPEN visit, elapsed service is $H-s$ and elapsed sojourn is $H-e$. For a still-waiting visit, elapsed wait and elapsed sojourn are both $H-e$. Each unfinished duration is at least its realized elapsed duration, with no finite final upper bound supplied.

In an interior time slice, queue length $Q_j(t)$ counts visits in $[e,s)$, in-service count $B_j(t)$ counts visits in $[s,d)$, and station WIP $L_j(t)=Q_j(t)+B_j(t)$ counts visits in $[e,d)$. For time integrals, clip all intervals to the observation window, using $H$ as the integration endpoint for pending exits. The snapshot at $H$ retains pending visits. Total WIP is $L_1+L_2$, equivalently the count from external arrival to S2 completion or censoring. Sweep the interval endpoints, sum net changes at each tie, and multiply the resulting count by the length of each step. Divide the summed case-minutes by interval length for a time-weighted mean. The largest step gives the peak. Arrival rate and throughput are counts of station entries and service completions, respectively, divided by clock hours.

Busy time is the length of a resource's occupied service intervals within its available time, including elapsed OPEN service. Utilization divides by available resource-minutes. Station utilization uses the sum of occupied resource-minutes divided by the sum of available resource-minutes. At the midpoint, queue area plus busy time is 1,515 + 570 = 2,085 case-minutes at S1 and 797.5 + 542.5 = 1,340 at S2. These equal the corresponding WIP areas. System WIP area is 3,425 case-minutes.

A duration from two reported endpoints has a conservative rounding band of ±5 minutes, clipped at zero. With the fixed cutoff as one endpoint, the band is ±2.5 minutes. For a missing start, propagate its feasible early and late bounds into the wait and service formulas. These marginal bounds can share endpoints and need not be jointly attainable. The outer bounds for time-weighted occupancy use guaranteed intervals $[x_{\mathrm{hi}}, y_{\mathrm{lo}})$ and possible intervals $[x_{\mathrm{lo}}, y_{\mathrm{hi}})$, with empty intervals contributing zero. For resource busy time, take the union within each resource's availability so possible overlaps are counted once. The midpoint and early/late scenarios give coherent schedules on the rounded clock. The rounding envelopes bound all feasible schedules under A1–A5 and can be wider than their attainable range.

For Little's Law, the queue version is $\overline Q=\lambda\overline W_q$, and the station version is $\overline L=\lambda\overline T$. A finite-window check of the queue identity is valid for **S2 from 08:20 to 08:50**. The queue is empty at both endpoints even under rounding, and both queue visits, P01 and P02, enter and start within the interval. Their rounded waits sum to 10 case-minutes, giving $\overline Q=10/30=1/3$. The entry rate of 4 per hour multiplied by a mean wait of $5/60$ hours gives the same value. This checks accounting for the two queue visits. The full window has changing arrival intensity, a maintenance outage, and unfinished visits, so a steady-state check with completed-case durations would be inappropriate.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Morning buildup.** S1 is the main location of congestion before noon. In the first two hours, 13 external arrivals and 6 inspection completions leave seven cases at S1 just before 10:00 on the reported clock. From 10:00 to noon, S1's mean queue is about six, against two at S2. The rounding-inclusive subperiod mean-queue bounds preserve this ordering before noon. S2 initially receives work at the rate inspection releases it, yet S2 backlog also builds as transfers outpace calibration completions.

**Maintenance and S2 capacity.** The outage removes one of two nominal S2 servers for 15 minutes. C2's observed gap is 10:50–11:15, comprising five available minutes, the outage and five available minutes after it. P08 enters the S2 queue at 10:20 and waits until 11:15. At the reported times, its 45-minute service could not fit into the five-minute pre-outage gap. Conditional on keeping the C2 assignment, P06's release time and an immediate dispatch policy whenever the full visit can fit, the earliest C2 start shifts from 10:50 in a no-outage comparison to 11:10 with the outage. That 20-minute shift is a scheduling comparison, and the observed start is five minutes later. The data cannot establish what dispatch, assignment or timing would actually have occurred without maintenance. P11's missing start is after P08 on C2, so P08's 12:00 release gives the tighter start bound for P11.

**Downstream pressure later in the window.** After noon, only one external arrival joins S1, while seven S1 completions feed S2. At 14:00 S2 still has both servers occupied and four queued cases. S1 has one active case and one queued case, with I2's last recorded work ending at 13:45. S2's longer observed service times and sustained deficit of completions relative to transfers are consistent with calibration becoming the constraint on clearing the remaining workload. The larger S1 queue and longer S1 waits earlier are consistent with startup demand overwhelming inspection. Together the observations support a time-varying diagnosis, with S1 carrying most of the early queue and S2 limiting late clearance. They leave the intrinsic capacities under steady demand unidentified.

**Dispatch evidence.** P09 starts S2 ahead of P08 despite arriving at S2 later, and P17 starts while earlier-ready P16 remains queued. Neither reversal can be removed by rounding. P22 stays queued while I2 has apparent availability late in the window. Resource eligibility, prioritization, breaks and logging gaps could affect how work is assigned. The supplied events cannot distinguish those explanations.

Earlier imputed starts shorten queues and increase busy time. Later ones move the same elapsed station time into waiting. Across the three scenarios, the station and system WIP paths, throughputs, and sojourns are identical because entries and completions are fixed. The full-window S1 mean queue remains larger even with the rounding envelopes (about 3.81–4.60 at S1 and 1.89–2.54 at S2). For noon to 14:00 the conservative mean-queue bounds overlap (about 3.15–3.58 at S1 and 3.38–4.08 at S2), so the late-window comparison rests most firmly on the terminal backlog, occupancy and changes in flows. Utilization alone cannot rank the stations. Later starts at S1 can coexist with earlier starts at S2, yielding about 74% at S1 and 81% at S2, while the opposite choice yields about 84% and 73%.

Open service contributes 30 nominal resource-minutes at S1 and 35 at S2. Completed-only accounting would miss those minutes as well as the unfinished queue exposures. In particular, P22 and P16 have already waited about 110 and 85 minutes, respectively. The elapsed observations cannot determine their final waits or the final service durations of P15, P17 and P21. The rounded-clock order of P07's C1 release and P09's C1 start can be realized without double-booking. Small true gaps may be hidden and some independent timestamp perturbations would create overlaps, which A2 and A3 rule out.

# 7. Reproducible Python

The Pandas calculations use station entry and censoring explicitly. The PM4Py demonstration uses the 11 completed cases with observed starts and finishes at both stages. Its case sojourn runs from the first activity start to the last activity completion. Its case waiting time therefore covers the gap between the two activities. The arrival and finish features are case-level spacings within that projection, in seconds, and exclude cases removed from the projection. Station counts and the case totals from external arrival use the full reconstruction above.

```python
from io import StringIO

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

data = """case,arrival_s1,s1_start,s1_end,s1_resource,s2_start,s2_end,s2_resource,state_at_14_00
P01,08:00,08:05,08:25,I1,08:30,09:00,C1,COMPLETE
P02,08:05,08:10,08:35,I2,08:40,09:20,C2,COMPLETE
P03,08:10,08:30,08:55,I1,09:05,09:45,C1,COMPLETE
P04,08:20,08:40,09:10,I2,09:25,10:05,C2,COMPLETE
P05,08:25,09:00,09:25,I1,MISSING,10:15,C1,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P06,08:35,09:15,09:45,I2,10:10,10:50,C2,COMPLETE
P07,08:45,MISSING,10:00,I1,10:20,11:00,C1,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P08,09:00,09:50,10:20,I2,11:15,12:00,C2,COMPLETE
P09,09:05,10:05,10:35,I1,11:00,11:40,C1,COMPLETE
P10,09:15,10:25,10:55,I2,11:45,12:10,C1,COMPLETE
P11,09:25,10:40,11:05,I1,MISSING,12:25,C2,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P12,09:35,11:00,11:30,I2,12:15,13:00,C1,COMPLETE
P13,09:50,11:10,11:40,I1,12:30,13:20,C2,COMPLETE
P14,10:00,11:35,12:00,I2,13:05,13:50,C1,COMPLETE
P15,10:10,MISSING,12:15,I1,13:30,OPEN,C2,OPEN_IN_S2
P16,10:25,12:05,12:35,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P17,10:45,12:20,12:50,I1,13:55,OPEN,C1,OPEN_IN_S2
P18,11:00,12:40,13:10,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P19,11:15,12:55,13:25,I1,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P20,11:30,13:15,13:45,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P21,11:50,13:30,OPEN,I1,,,,OPEN_IN_S1
P22,12:10,,,,,,,WAITING_FOR_S1
"""
raw = pd.read_csv(StringIO(data), keep_default_na=False)
base = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 08:00")
H, r = 360, 2.5
outage = {"C2": [(175, 190)]}  # 10:55 to 11:10, fixed clock times here

def minutes(values):
    statuses = values.isin(["MISSING", "OPEN", ""])
    times = pd.to_datetime(
        "2026-05-12 " + values.mask(statuses), format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    )
    return (times - base).dt.total_seconds() / 60

frames = []
for station, entry_key in [("S1", "arrival_s1"), ("S2", "s1_end")]:
    prefix = station.lower()
    start_key, end_key = prefix + "_start", prefix + "_end"
    entry = minutes(raw[entry_key])
    visits = pd.DataFrame({
        "case": raw["case"], "station": station,
        "resource": raw[prefix + "_resource"],
        "entry": entry, "start": minutes(raw[start_key]), "end": minutes(raw[end_key]),
        "start_status": raw[start_key].replace({"": "BLANK"}).where(
            raw[start_key].isin(["MISSING", ""]), "OBSERVED"
        ),
        "end_status": raw[end_key].replace({"": "BLANK"}).where(
            raw[end_key].isin(["OPEN", ""]), "OBSERVED"
        ),
        "state": raw["state_at_14_00"],
    })
    frames.append(visits.loc[entry.notna()])
visits = pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)
missing = visits["start_status"].eq("MISSING")
waiting = visits["start_status"].eq("BLANK")
for col in ["entry", "start", "end"]:
    visits[col + "_lo"] = visits[col] - r
    visits[col + "_hi"] = visits[col] + r
visits["end_at_h"] = visits["end"].fillna(H)
for edge in ["lo", "hi"]:
    visits["end_" + edge] = visits["end_" + edge].fillna(H)

# Given the single-task assumption, completion-order on each resource
# fixes the immediate predecessor for these missing-start visits.
visits["early"] = visits["start"]
visits["late"] = visits["start"]
assigned = visits.loc[visits["resource"].ne("")]
for resource, group in assigned.groupby("resource"):
    group = group.sort_values("end_at_h")
    for i, (idx, row) in enumerate(group.iterrows()):
        if row["start_status"] != "MISSING":
            continue
        prior_end = group.iloc[i - 1]["end"] if i else float("-inf")
        following_start = group.iloc[i + 1]["start"] if i + 1 < len(group) else H
        # For the missing C2 start, completion is securely after maintenance.
        available_from = max((up for _, up in outage.get(resource, [])
                              if up <= row["end_lo"]), default=float("-inf"))
        early = max(row["entry"], prior_end, available_from)
        late = min(row["end"], following_start)
        assert early <= late
        visits.loc[idx, ["early", "late"]] = [early, late]
        # Earliest start allows both the entry and preceding end to shift earlier.
        floor = max(row["entry_lo"], prior_end - r, available_from)
        visits.loc[idx, ["start_lo", "start_hi"]] = [
            floor, min(row["end_hi"], following_start + r, H)
        ]

visits.loc[waiting, ["early", "late", "start_lo", "start_hi"]] = H
visits["start_lo"] = visits[["start_lo", "entry_lo"]].max(axis=1)
visits["case_done"] = visits["state"].isin(["COMPLETE", "COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START"])
scenarios = {}
for name, weight in [("early", 0), ("midpoint", 0.5), ("late", 1)]:
    scenario = visits.assign(
        queue_exit=visits["early"] + weight * (visits["late"] - visits["early"])
    )
    scenario["wait"] = scenario["queue_exit"] - scenario["entry"]
    scenario["service"] = (scenario["end_at_h"] - scenario["queue_exit"]).mask(waiting)
    scenario["sojourn"] = scenario["end_at_h"] - scenario["entry"]
    scenarios[name] = scenario
center = scenarios["midpoint"]

# These are marginal observed-duration bounds, with tighter maximum waits
# for unknown starts obtained by allowing zero service at completion.
center["wait_lo"] = (center["start_lo"] - center["entry_hi"]).clip(lower=0)
center["wait_hi"] = (center["start_hi"] - center["entry_lo"]).clip(lower=0)
center["service_lo"] = (center["end_lo"] - center["start_hi"]).clip(lower=0).mask(waiting)
center["service_hi"] = (center["end_hi"] - center["start_lo"]).clip(lower=0).mask(waiting)
center["sojourn_lo"] = (center["end_lo"] - center["entry_hi"]).clip(lower=0)
center["sojourn_hi"] = (center["end_hi"] - center["entry_lo"]).clip(lower=0)
s1 = center.loc[center["station"].eq("S1")].set_index("case")
s2 = center.loc[center["station"].eq("S2")].set_index("case")
system = s1[["entry", "entry_lo", "entry_hi"]].join(
    s2[["end_at_h", "end_lo", "end_hi"]].fillna(H)
).fillna(H)
system["sojourn"] = system["end_at_h"] - system["entry"]

def interval_metrics(left, right, a=0, b=H, union=False):
    spans = pd.DataFrame({"left": left.clip(lower=a), "right": right.clip(upper=b)})
    spans = spans.loc[spans["left"] < spans["right"]]
    events = pd.concat([
        spans.set_index("left").assign(delta=1)["delta"],
        spans.set_index("right").assign(delta=-1)["delta"],
        pd.Series([0, 0], index=[a, b]),
    ])
    counts = events.groupby(level=0).sum().sort_index().cumsum()
    if union:
        counts = counts.gt(0).astype(int)
    durations = -counts.index.to_series().diff(-1).fillna(0)
    return (counts * durations).sum(), int(counts.max())

def active_at(left, right, t):
    return ((left <= t) & (t < right)).sum()

# For point-in-time occupancy, waiting and open visits continue beyond H.
# The censoring endpoints H are used only for the area calculations.
def snapshot(group, t, exit_key, open_mask):
    return active_at(group["entry"], group[exit_key].mask(open_mask, float("inf")), t)
```
