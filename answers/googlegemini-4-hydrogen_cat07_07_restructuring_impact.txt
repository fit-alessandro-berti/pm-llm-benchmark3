# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Evaluating the restructuring on **mean or overall median cycle time** creates dangerous blind spots because ~60% of cases are low/medium complexity that bypass or lightly touch central specialist queues. Improvements in automated intake (88  83/100 local handovers) or East's routine backlog can easily keep average cycle time flat or slightly negative while severe operational damage accumulates underneath:

1. **Tail latency explosion (p90 / p95 / p99):** Queueing systems near 90%+ utilization exhibit extreme right-skew. Even if 75% of cases finish in 5–7 days, the top 10–15% in Legal/Risk (already at 93%/91%) or Technical Shared Service can stretch from 18 days to 30+ days without shifting the median.
2. **High-complexity case degradation:** Mandatory routing sends *all* `case_complexity == 'high'` and `{legal, risk, security}` cases through multiple centralized queues (Tech Pool + Specialty Pool + Shared Approval + difficult Quality Check). Because these represent a minority of total volume, their multi-week delays are mathematically drowned out by high-volume simple cases in aggregate averages.
3. **Silent loss of local knowledge (`local_expertise_flag`):** When `N_Tech` and `S_Tech` leave local teams, subtle regional conventions and informal early "sanity checks" disappear. Average cycle time may look fine initially while `Local Customer Contact` rework loops and customer complaints quietly climb in specific locations.
4. **Fairness divergence across teams and customer segments:** Averages across all 2,100 monthly cases mask cross-subsidization and segment penalties. East's improvement (8.6d  ~7.3d) can offset North's degradation (6.8d  ~7.8d) to yield an unchanged company average. Worse, minority customer segments needing language or regional accommodation suffer extra handovers and higher SLA misses that vanish in global averages.
5. **Long-term local deskilling and learning decay:** Routing every difficult case away from local caseworkers boosts short-term throughput ("specialization efficiency"), keeping early averages low. Over 3–6 months, however, local staff stop learning technical judgment, driving up defensive escalations (`Local  Tech Pool`) and progressively starving the central pool of future talent.
6. **Diffusion of accountability and queue gaming:** Under shared approval and shared technical queues, nobody owns end-to-end case momentum. Staff may cherry-pick easy tickets from the shared queue to keep personal throughput metrics high—making *average* queue wait look healthy while difficult, ambiguous, or multi-specialty cases sit untouched ("aging WIP" hidden from completed-case averages).
7. **Brittle exception handling:** Standardizing routing rules works for canonical variants (`filter_variants`), but rare edge cases (e.g., VIP priority + missing regional docs + concurrent legal/technical conflict) fall between queues. Because exceptions are rare by definition, their multi-handover `reassignment_count` spikes and 20%+ rework rates barely move the overall mean, yet they drive regulatory risk, escalations, and brand damage.

# 5. Pilot design

A "big-bang" rollout risks triggering simultaneous queue collapse in Legal/Risk and North/South expertise loss. Because **pooled resources and cross-team queues interact**, a pure single-team pilot can distort shared-queue dynamics unless structured carefully.

### Pilot Scope & Comparison Group
- **Scope:** A **10-week controlled phased pilot** focused on **one functional slice + targeted regional pairing** rather than stripping all local specialists immediately:
  - **Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4, Shadow-Pool & East Relief):** Keep `N_Tech` and `S_Tech` anchored to North/South for local complex & regional-accommodation cases, but allocate **30% of `N_Tech` and `S_Tech` capacity** into a virtual shared overflow queue paired with `E_Tech` (100%) to absorb **East's** excess load and **all `high`-complexity overflow**. Keep supervisor structure intact initially; test the **Shared Approval Queue** only on *medium-value* cases (1 supervisor rotating daily while 2 remain local).
  - **Phase 2 (Weeks 5–10, Expanded Shared Service without Legal/Risk Overload):** Route *all* `high`-complexity technical reviews from **East and South** into the Technical Shared Service (with mandatory structured handoff templates capturing `local_expertise_flag`), while keeping **North** as an intact embedded-specialist **comparison group** (receiving only standardized intake templates and Legal/Risk triage support).
  - **Crucial constraint on Legal/Risk:** Do *not* expand Legal/Risk routing criteria (+33% volume) during the pilot without first testing whether Tech triage actually reduces net Legal/Risk arrivals, given their 93%/91% baseline utilization.

### Pre/Post Observation Window
- **Pre-pilot baseline:** 12 weeks of historical event logs (ensuring coverage of absence periods and month-end spikes), segmented identically to pilot metrics.
- **Pilot window:** 10 weeks active observation (Weeks 1–2 stabilization excluded from statistical inference, Weeks 3–10 primary evaluation) + **4-week post-pilot tail window** to capture late-finishing complex cases and avoid right-censoring bias.

### Segmentation Strategy
Every metric must be stratified across four event-log dimensions (`filter_event_attribute_values`):
1. **`team` / `location`:** East (expected beneficiary) vs. South (test group) vs. North (control/comparison).
2. **`case_complexity`:** Low / Medium / High (to prevent simple-case volume from masking high-complexity tail latency).
3. **`required_specialty`:** None / Technical-only / `{legal, risk, security}` (to isolate saturated Legal/Risk queues).
4. **`customer_segment` & `local_expertise_flag`:** Standard vs. regional/language-accommodation segments (to audit fairness).

### Guardrail Metrics (Hard Thresholds)
- **Tail cycle time:** p90 cycle time for `high`-complexity cases must not increase by >10% in South/Pilot vs. North/Control or pre-baseline.
- **Legal/Risk saturation:** Median wait in Legal/Risk must not exceed **12 hours** (baseline 9.6h), and WIP inflow/outflow ratio must stay  1.05.
- **Rework & context loss:** Rework rate on `local_expertise_flag == True` or accommodation segments must not increase by >1.5 percentage points; clarification loops (`A  B  A`) must remain 10% of central handovers.
- **North/South protection:** South's CSAT must not drop below 3.9/5 (baseline 4.1) and SLA miss rate must stay 8.0% (baseline 7.2%).

### Staff Feedback Collection
- **Weekly 3-minute pulse check** for caseworkers, pooled techs, supervisors, and Legal/Risk: (1) ticket note completeness, (2) clarity on who owns stalled cases, (3) perceived ability to ask quick informal questions.
- **Bi-weekly structured retrospectives** sampling 10 high-rework or high-`reassignment_count` cases per team using `discover_performance_dfg` trace replays to diagnose *why* handoffs looped.

### Rollback and Adjustment Triggers
- **Immediate pause / rollback trigger:**
  - Legal/Risk median wait >15h for 5 consecutive days, OR Technical Shared Service WIP grows >25% above baseline for 2 consecutive weeks.
  - p90 cycle time degrades >15% in South across all complexities or >20% for high-complexity cases.
  - Rework in regional/accommodation `customer_segment` jumps >3 percentage points.
- **Targeted adjustment trigger (modify rather than abort):**
  - If clarification loops spike (`handover_reason = "missing_info"`), enforce mandatory structured intake fields + reinstate a daily 15-minute "local tech office hours" slot for informal advice before formal routing.
  - If Shared Approval wait exceeds 4h, restore the 3rd supervisor role or raise the value threshold so fewer cases enter the shared approval queue.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first, with targeted modifications—do not proceed to full rollout.**

Management's proposal solves a real problem—East's `E_Tech` overload (82% utilization, 22.8d p90, 9.8% SLA misses) and poor ad-hoc cross-team cover (8.9h wait, 18% rework)—but its current design carries three structural flaws that make full rollout high-risk:
1. **Capacity arithmetic does not add up for Legal/Risk and Approvals:** Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) have zero spare capacity to absorb a projected +33% handover increase (9  12/100) without triggering severe tail delays. Likewise, cutting 1 supervisor while expanding approval routing (42  49/100) manufactures a new bottleneck.
2. **Formalizing informal advice inflates nominal handovers:** Management's own model shifts total handovers from 343 to 359 per 100 cases while stripping local teams of embedded expertise that prevents rework in North and South.
3. **Unmodified central pooling sacrifices North/South and vulnerable segments to relieve East:** Pooled overflow can fix East *without* dismantling all local technical ties.

### Required Modifications Prior to Piloting
1. **Retain a "virtual-hybrid" specialist model:** Pool overflow capacity (starting with 30% of `N_Tech`/`S_Tech` capacity + `E_Tech`) and standardize intake/note templates to fix cross-team cover, while keeping scheduled "local office hours" so caseworkers retain informal coaching and regional expertise.
2. **Do not increase Legal/Risk referral volume without adding capacity or verified triage deflection:** Pilot automated/tech pre-screening first; only expand `{legal, risk, security}` triggers if Legal/Risk utilization drops below 85%.
3. **Keep 3 supervisors (or narrow shared approval scope):** Centralize approval *rules* and checklists before cutting approval headcount.
4. **Gate full rollout on passing the 10-week pilot guardrails:** Proceed to broader pooling *only if* East's p90 and rework improve without North/South p90 degradation, Legal/Risk waits remain <12h, clarification loops stay <10%, and accommodation `customer_segment` equity holds.

### What Management Must Not Infer from Early Averages
During the first 4–6 weeks, management must explicitly resist three misleading inferences from aggregate averages:
- **Do not infer success from stable or slightly lower mean cycle time:** Fast low-complexity cases and East's early relief can hold the global mean steady even while high-complexity p90/p95 tails and Legal/Risk queues are silently compounding.
- **Do not infer queue health from completed-case averages in Weeks 1–4:** Completed cases in early weeks are biased toward fast, simple tickets (*right-censoring*). Difficult cases stuck in shared queues have not closed yet—meaning WIP aging and inflow/outflow ratios, not completed-case averages, tell the true story.
- **Do not infer higher productivity from higher specialist utilization:** Moving `Tech` utilization from ~73% to ~88%+ looks efficient on a utilization dashboard, but in a variable arrival system with difficult `Quality Check` added, >85–90% utilization directly causes exponential wait-time instability and burnout.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The following Python script uses `pm4py` (2.x functional API) to compare **Pre-Restructuring** vs. **Post-Restructuring / Pilot** event logs across social networks, control-flow/performance DFGs, service vs. waiting times, organizational roles, multi-dimensional segmentation, rework, and SLA misses.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ==============================================================================
# 1. Load & Format Event Logs (Pre vs. Post/Pilot)
# ==============================================================================
def prepare_log(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    # Extract temporal features (e.g., hour, day-of-week, week) for peak/absence analysis
    df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    return df

pre_log = prepare_log("event_log_pre.csv")
post_log = prepare_log("event_log_post.csv")


# ==============================================================================
# 2. Organizational & Social Network Analysis (SNA)
# ==============================================================================
def analyze_sna_and_roles(log: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> dict:
    # Handover-of-work (beta=0 captures direct handoffs; beta=0.5 captures indirect paths)
    handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, beta=0)
    # Working-together network (team cohesion / fragmentation)
    working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
    # Subcontracting network (A -> B -> A ping-pong / clarification loops)
    subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)
    # Resource similarity & organizational role discovery
    resource_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
    org_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)

    print(f"\n=== {label}: Organizational Summary ===")
    print(f"Discovered role clusters: {len(org_roles)}")
    for idx, role in enumerate(org_roles):
        print(f"  Role {idx}: activities={role.activities}, resources={list(role.originators.keys())}")

    # Top subcontracting (ping-pong) pairs
    top_subcontract = sorted(
        subcontracting_net.connections.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True
    )[:5]
    print(f"Top A->B->A subcontracting loops: {top_subcontract}")

    return {
        "handover": handover_net,
        "working_together": working_together_net,
        "subcontracting": subcontracting_net,
        "resource_similarity": resource_sim,
        "roles": org_roles,
    }

pre_sna = analyze_sna_and_roles(pre_log, "PRE")
post_sna = analyze_sna_and_roles(post_log, "POST")


# ==============================================================================
# 3. Control-Flow Variants & Performance DFG (Waiting vs. Service Times)
# ==============================================================================
def analyze_flow_and_times(log: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    # Frequency DFG and Performance DFG (edge sojourn/waiting times)
    dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

    # Variants count & concentration (top 5 variants share)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    total_cases = sum(variants.values())
    top_5_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:5]
    top_5_share = sum(cnt for _, cnt in top_5_variants) / max(total_cases, 1)

    # Filter log to top variants vs. long-tail exception variants
    top_variant_keys = [v for v, _ in top_5_variants]
    mainstream_log = pm4py.filter_variants(log, top_variant_keys)

    # Service & waiting times
    median_service = pm4py.get_service_time(log, aggregation_measure="median")
    enriched_log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)

    print(f"\n=== {label}: Flow & Timing Summary ===")
    print(f"Total variants: {len(variants)} | Top-5 variant share: {top_5_share:.1%}")
    print(f"Median active service time (case-level): {median_service / 3600:.2f}h")
    print(f"Mainstream variant cases: {mainstream_log['case:concept:name'].nunique()}")

    # Inspect top 5 slowest DFG transitions (queue bottlenecks)
    slow_edges = sorted(perf_dfg.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:5]
    for edge, sec in slow_edges:
        print(f"  Slowest edge {edge}: mean {sec / 3600:.2f}h")

    return enriched_log

pre_enriched = analyze_flow_and_times(pre_log, "PRE")
post_enriched = analyze_flow_and_times(post_log, "POST")


# ==============================================================================
# 4. Segmented KPIs: Team, Complexity, Specialty, Customer Segment, Rework, SLA
# ==============================================================================
SLA_THRESHOLD_DAYS = 14.0  # Example SLA threshold

def compute_segment_kpis(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    segment_col: str,
    segment_values: list,
    label: str,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for val in segment_values:
        sub_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key=segment_col,
            values=[val],
            level="case",
            retain=True,
        )
        if sub_log.empty:
            continue

        # Case-level summary
        case_grp = sub_log.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
            start=("time:timestamp", "min"),
            end=("time:timestamp", "max"),
            rework=("rework_flag", "max"),
            reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
            local_exp=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
            waiting_sec=("@@case_waiting_time", "max")
            if "@@case_waiting_time" in sub_log.columns
            else ("time:timestamp", lambda s: np.nan),
        )
        case_grp["cycle_days"] = (case_grp["end"] - case_grp["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
        case_grp["sla_miss"] = case_grp["cycle_days"] > SLA_THRESHOLD_DAYS

        # Activity-level rework counts via PM4Py
        rework_per_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(sub_log)
        qc_rework = rework_per_act.get("Quality Check", 0)

        rows.append({
            "period": label,
            "segment_col": segment_col,
            "segment_val": val,
            "cases": len(case_grp),
            "median_days": case_grp["cycle_days"].median(),
            "p90_days": case_grp["cycle_days"].quantile(0.90),
            "p95_days": case_grp["cycle_days"].quantile(0.95),
            "mean_wait_hours": case_grp["waiting_sec"].mean() / 3600.0,
            "rework_rate": case_grp["rework"].mean(),
            "qc_rework_cases": qc_rework,
            "sla_miss_rate": case_grp["sla_miss"].mean(),
            "avg_reassignments": case_grp["reassignments"].mean(),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

segments_to_check = {
    "team": ["North", "South", "East"],
    "case_complexity": ["low", "medium", "high"],
    "required_specialty": ["none", "technical", "legal", "risk", "security"],
    "customer_segment": ["standard", "regional_accommodation", "high_value"],
}

comparison_tables = []
for col, vals in segments_to_check.items():
    pre_seg = compute_segment_kpis(pre_enriched, col, vals, "PRE")
    post_seg = compute_segment_kpis(post_enriched, col, vals, "POST")
    merged = pre_seg.merge(
        post_seg,
        on=["segment_col", "segment_val"],
        suffixes=("_pre", "_post"),
    )
    merged["delta_p50_days"] = merged["median_days_post"] - merged["median_days_pre"]
    merged["delta_p90_days"] = merged["p90_days_post"] - merged["p90_days_pre"]
    merged["delta_rework_pp"] = (merged["rework_rate_post"] - merged["rework_rate_pre"]) * 100
    merged["delta_sla_pp"] = (merged["sla_miss_rate_post"] - merged["sla_miss_rate_pre"]) * 100
    comparison_tables.append(merged)

final_report = pd.concat(comparison_tables, ignore_index=True)
print("\n=== Pre vs. Post Segmented Comparison ===")
print(
    final_report[
        [
            "segment_col",
            "segment_val",
            "delta_p50_days",
            "delta_p90_days",
            "delta_rework_pp",
            "delta_sla_pp",
        ]
    ].to_string(index=False)
)
```

### Key Diagnostic Extensions in Practice
- **Inter-arrival vs. completion WIP check:** Group `post_log` by week and `concept:name` (`Specialist Review`, `Supervisor Approval`) to verify whether queue arrivals exceed completions before cases finish.
- **Fairness & local-expertise interaction:** Filter jointly on `local_expertise_flag == True` and `customer_segment == "regional_accommodation"` via stacked `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` calls to verify that regional customers do not absorb disproportionate `Rework` and `Local Customer Contact` loops after embedded specialists leave.
