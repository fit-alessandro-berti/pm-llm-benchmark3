# Diagnostic Lab Process Redesign: Robust Strategy for Variable Demand and Disruption

## 1. Stress Scenarios

### Scenario A: Severe Demand Mix Shift (Winter Infection Wave)
- **Volume**: 40% above normal baseline (25,760 cases over equivalent period)
- **Mix**: Urgent cases rise to 35%, Complex-panel to 25%, Routine compressed to 30%, Repeat-after-failure 10%
- **Primary stressor**: High-priority cases dominate, contractual penalty exposure spikes
- **Secondary stressor**: Medical validation and specialist review queues explode due to Complex-panel concentration
- **Realistic trigger**: Respiratory infection surge with elderly comorbidities requiring multi-assay panels

### Scenario B: Assay Machine Degradation with High Routine Volume
- **Volume**: Near-normal total (18,000) but Routine cases at 70% due to backlogs from prior peak
- **Disruption**: M2 experiences 40% throughput reduction for 5 days (maintenance parts delay) OR M1 fails entirely for 48 hours
- **Mix**: Routine-heavy with residual Urgent (15%) and Complex-panel (10%)
- **Primary stressor**: Assay run queue balloons; batch wait times cascade; M1 overloaded if M2 degraded
- **Secondary stressor**: Increased repeat rates if M2 forced to handle overload while degraded (already higher failure rate)

### Scenario C: Specialist and Validator Absence During Moderate Peak
- **Volume**: 25% above normal (23,000) with peak-like mix
- **Disruption**: Specialist reviewer unavailable for 2 consecutive Fridays (scheduled absence plus illness); one medical validator out for 10 days
- **Primary stressor**: Hold-for-specialist-review queue reaches >48h median; Complex-panel cases cascade into medical validation backlog
- **Secondary stressor**: Technical validators overloaded as cases accumulate in validation queues; rework loops (Repeat-after-failure) cannot clear without medical validation

### Scenario D: Courier Compression with Registration Surge
- **Volume**: Peak-level (9,900 over 3 weeks) but arrivals compress into 09:00-11:00 window (60% of daily intake)
- **Mix**: Routine 50%, Urgent 22%, Complex-panel 18%, Repeat-after-failure 10%
- **Disruption**: Courier schedule change concentrates arrivals; registration queue spikes to >4h; downstream resources idle then flooded
- **Primary stressor**: Registration and pre-analytical queues oscillate between starvation and overwhelm
- **Secondary stressor**: Batch formation logic breaks down; urgent bypass triggers uncontrolled mixing at assay machines

## 2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis

### Scenario A: Demand Mix Shift

**Sequence of bottlenecks:**

1. **Medical validation** (initial bottleneck)
   - Peak data: 93% utilization, 22h median queue. Complex-panel cases at 25% (3.1× medical validation probability) drives demand beyond capacity.
   - DFG evidence: Technical validation  Medical validation edge = 3,420 cases peak vs 3,900 normal; absolute volume similar but Complex-panel concentration means more validation-minutes per case.
   - Hold-for-specialist-review queue (36h peak median) feeds medical validation, compounding delay.

2. **Specialist review** (cascading bottleneck)
   - Peak: 87% utilization, 36h median. Complex-panel cases 2.4× more likely to enter this queue.
   - DFG: Technical validation  Hold for specialist review = 610 peak vs 290 normal (2.1× increase).
   - Edge delay: Hold  Medical validation = 48h peak median (up from 18h normal).

3. **If I1 (strict Urgent-first) applied alone:**
   - Urgent cases accelerate through preparation and assay, **but** Routine and Complex-panel cases stall at preparation benches and assay machines.
   - Batch wait inflates for Routine cases: already 14h peak median, could exceed 30h.
   - Work-in-process rises across entire system; pre-analytical check queue grows (9.5h peak median) as downstream backpressure slows release.

4. **If I5 (evening medical validation shift) applied:**
   - Medical validation queue clears  Technical validation queue becomes next bottleneck.
   - Technical validators at 101% peak utilization; 16h median queue. Cases exit technical validation faster than medical validation consumes them initially, but once medical validation accelerates, technical validators become the constraint.
   - **Migration pattern**: Medical validation  Technical validation  Assay run (if technical validators release assay capacity by clearing backlog; assay machines at 98% peak utilization cannot absorb more throughput).

5. **If I10 (outsource Complex-panel) applied:**
   - Specialist review queue relieved  12h transport delay added to Complex-panel cycle time (p90 already 210h peak).
   - Medical validation load shifts partially but partner lab results require re-validation on return, adding traceability steps.
   - **Risk**: Pre-analytical check and preparation benches gain capacity (fewer Complex-panel samples) but registration complexity increases with dual lab routing.

### Scenario B: Assay Machine Degradation

1. **If M2 degrades with Routine-heavy mix:**
   - Assay run queue immediate bottleneck: normal median 4h  likely >25h with 40% M2 throughput loss.
   - Batch wait explodes: Routine cases dominate; batch wait already 14h peak median with both machines running.
   - Preparation benches upstream stall: test preparation queue grows (peak 11h median); pre-analytical check backs up.
   - Technical validation starved downstream: validators idle waiting for assay output, then flooded when machines recover.

2. **If M1 fails entirely for 48h:**
   - All volume forced to M2. M2 has higher failure rate (variant note: repeats clustered after M2 usage).
   - Repeat assay volume spikes: normal 1,140 repeats from 15,890 assay runs (7.2%); with M2-only operation, could exceed 15% repeat rate.
   - Repeat-after-failure cases (already 9% mix peak) compound: each repeat consumes assay capacity twice.
   - **Migration**: Assay  Repeat assay loop becomes reinforcing bottleneck; medical validation receives more repeat cases requiring scrutiny.

3. **If I3 (dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex, M2 to Routine) applied during M2 degradation:**
   - M2 Routine backlog immense; M1 underutilized if Urgent/Complex volume insufficient to fill capacity.
   - **Or worse**: If Urgent and Complex-panel volume is high enough, M1 saturates; M2 still degraded; no capacity for Routine at all.
   - **Migration**: Assay queue  Batch wait  Preparation benches  Pre-analytical  Registration (full system stall).

### Scenario C: Specialist/Validator Absence

1. **Initial bottleneck**: Specialist review and medical validation simultaneously constrained.
   - One specialist unavailable every second Friday already (normal note); two Fridays in a row = continuous specialist deficit.
   - Medical validator absence removes ~25% of medical validation capacity during moderate peak.
   - DFG peak: Medical validation  Hold for specialist review (560 cases) + Technical validation  Medical validation (3,420 cases) + Repeat assay  Medical validation (120 cases) = 4,100 total medical validation inputs over 3 weeks.
   - With reduced capacity, medical validation queue exceeds 40h median (peak baseline 22h).

2. **Cascading bottleneck**: Technical validation stalls as medical validation cannot accept output.
   - Technical validation  Medical validation edge delay grows from 24h peak to >40h.
   - Technical validators at 101% peak utilization cannot absorb queue growth; cases accumulate pre-technical-validation.
   - **If I4 (cross-train technical validators to accession correction) applied:** 2 technical validators diverted  technical validation capacity drops; queue worsens.

3. **Rework exacerbation**: 
   - Repeat-after-failure cases (9% peak) require medical validation for some paths (DFG: Repeat assay  Medical validation = 120 cases peak).
   - With medical validation unavailable, repeat cases accumulate in assayrepeat loop without resolution.
   - Recollection loops also stall: pre-analytical  Request recollection  Receive sample edge = 60h peak median; if pre-analytical check queue grows (backpressure from downstream), recollection loop cycle time exceeds 80h.

### Scenario D: Courier Compression

1. **Initial bottleneck**: Registration clerks at intake spike.
   - Peak utilization 88% courier intake normal, but compression concentrates 60% of volume into 2-hour window.
   - Registration queue from 2.8h peak median  likely >6h within spike window.
   - Error rate rises: "Can work overtime but error rate rises after 9h shifts" — compressed intake forces overtime concentration.

2. **Accession correction cascade**:
   - Registration errors  Manual accession correction queue grows (already 14h peak median; only 2 specialists).
   - DFG: Pre-analytical check  Manual accession correction = 420 peak cases; if registration errors increase, this edge grows.
   - Accession correction  Test preparation = 310 peak cases (74% success); remainder  Request recollection (110 cases), adding to recollection loop.

3. **Preparation batch disruption**:
   - Spiky arrival means batches form unevenly: either long waits for batch fill or small inefficient batches.
   - Batch wait edge: Test preparation  Batch wait = 1,180 peak cases (up from 760 normal). With compression, batch wait oscillates.
   - Urgent bypass (58% peak vs 74% normal) fragments machine schedules further.

4. **If I7 (smooth courier slots) applied:** Registration bottleneck flattens but downstream resources now receive steady flow; no single queue explodes but all queues remain elevated (peak-level sustained rather than spike-recovery pattern).

## 3. Robust Redesign Package

### Core Principle: Absorptive Capacity + Selective Acceleration + Failure-Path Containment

The redesign must handle mix shifts, machine degradation, staff absence, and arrival compression without brittle priority rules that collapse under edge cases.

### R1: Dynamic Batch-Gating with Urgent Escape Lane (Modifies I2 with controls)

**Design**: 
- Normal batch target = 12 (current); peak-period automatic reduction to 8 when assay queue exceeds 8h median.
- Urgent cases always bypass batch wait (maintain current behavior).
- Routine cases use batch but with **maximum batch formation time** of 3h — if batch not filled within 3h, release partial batch.
- Complex-panel cases can batch but are individually flagged for release if batch formation exceeds 2h (they have long downstream paths; early delay compounds).

**Why robust**:
- Under Scenario A (mix shift): Routine cases don't starve because batch formation time-cap prevents indefinite hold; Urgent cases unaffected.
- Under Scenario B (assay degradation): Batch size automatically reduces when assay queue grows, preventing batch wait from compounding assay queue delay. Smaller batches = less setup efficiency but faster flow; efficiency sacrifice is bounded (8 vs 12 = 33% setup penalty).
- Under Scenario D (compression): Maximum formation time prevents sparse arrival windows from creating batch starvation; partial batches maintain flow.

**Assumptions and failure modes**:
- Assumes batch size reduction actually reduces queue time (true if setup time is principal constraint; fails if machine runtime dominates).
- Fails if preparation bench staff resist partial batches (operational discipline required).
- Requires real-time queue depth visibility.

**Next bottleneck risk**: Reducing batch size improves assay throughput but increases preparation bench touches per case; preparation utilization rises. Normal preparation benches at 69%  may reach 85%+ under combination of smaller batches and peak volume.

### R2: Strategic Cross-Training with Activation Triggers (Modified I4)

**Design**:
- Cross-train 2 technical validators in accession correction **but do not routinely divert them**.
- Activate diversion only when accession correction queue exceeds 8h median AND technical validation queue is below 10h median.
- Train 1 additional technical validator in basic medical validation triage (can clear straightforward Routine releases, escalating only ambiguous cases). Activates when medical validation queue exceeds 30h.

**Why robust**:
- Under Scenario C (specialist/validator absence): Medical validation triage capacity partially compensates; Routine final validation can proceed without medical validator bottleneck.
- Under Scenario A (mix shift): Accession correction surge (from registration errors during high urgency) can be absorbed without permanently weakening technical validation.
- Avoids the trap of I4: doesn't create technical validation shortage except during deliberate, monitored activation.

**Assumptions and failure modes**:
- Triage-trained validator must not exceed scope; incorrect Routine releases create clinical risk.
- Activation thresholds must be enforced; "temporary" diversion easily becomes permanent under sustained pressure.
- Fails if both medical validators and technical validators are simultaneously constrained (no slack to divert).

**Next bottleneck risk**: Medical validation triage accelerates Routine releases  downstream Release result step receives higher volume; currently rapid (2h median peak) but could queue if release clerks not scaled.

### R3: Pre-Analytical Fast-Fail Triage (Modified I6)

**Design**:
- Implement early defect detection within 2h of sample receipt specifically for known high-failure patterns: hemolysis, insufficient volume, wrong tube type.
- Reject failing samples immediately with electronic notification to ordering physician, bypassing full registration-to-pre-analytical delay.
- Track rejection reasons to provide feedback to collection sites.

**Why robust across scenarios**:
- Recollection loops are universally toxic: Request recollection  Receive sample = 60h peak median. Every avoided recollection saves 60h+ cycle time for that case AND preserves intake capacity for new cases.
- Scenario D (compression): Pre-analytical check queue already 9.5h peak; early rejection reduces queue entries (DFG: 780 peak cases go to request recollection from pre-analytical check).
- Scenario B (assay degradation): Fewer bad samples entering assay reduces repeat rate (some repeats caused by poor sample quality rather than machine failure).
- Peak data: 11.8% rework vs 7.5% normal; even 30% reduction in recollection loops saves significant capacity.

**Assumptions and failure modes**:
- Requires investment in pre-analytical automation or dedicated triage staff (not cost-free).
- Fails if triage specificity is low; rejecting acceptable samples creates unnecessary recollections and physician frustration.
- False negatives (accepting bad samples) still flow downstream and fail at assay stage.

**Next bottleneck risk**: Pre-analytical check becomes slightly **more** utilized (triage adds inspection time per sample) before producing **fewer** downstream failures. Net effect positive if triage adds <20% time per sample and catches >40% of eventual failures.

### R4: Two-Regime Operating Model (Normal vs. Surge Protocols)

**Design**: Formalize switch between normal operating mode and surge operating mode based on leading indicators (see Section 7).

**Normal mode**: 
- Batch size 12, full validation paths, no cross-training diversion, M1/M2 balanced by assay type rather than case priority, courier schedule as-is.

**Surge mode** (activated when 2 activation criteria met for >4h):
- Batch size reduces to 8 with max formation time 3h (R1 active).
- Accession correction diversion activates if thresholds permit (R2).
- Machine dedication (I3-lite): M1 preferentially receives Urgent and Complex-panel, **but** M2 receives Routine unless M2 failure rate exceeds 2× baseline, then revert to balanced assignment.
- Evening medical validation shift (I5) activates for 4h during surge, processing oldest cases first (not Urgent-first; priority by age to prevent Routine starvation).

**Why robust**:
- Avoids permanent optimization for peak conditions that degrades normal-period efficiency.
- Machine dedication is conditional on M2 health; prevents Scenario B trap.
- Age-based priority in surge prevents indefinite Routine delay while still meeting Urgent contractual targets (18h).

**Failure modes**:
- Frequent oscillation between modes creates operational confusion.
- Surge mode activation lag (4h confirmation period) means initial surge damage already done.
- Requires clear deactivation criteria to avoid mode-lock.

## 4. Scenario-Specific Policies

### S-A: Activate for Severe Demand Mix Shift (Scenario A)

**Trigger conditions**:
- Urgent case proportion >30% for >8h AND Complex-panel >22%
- OR medical validation queue >25h median

**Policy**: 
- Activate full R4 surge mode.
- **Additional**: I10 (outsource Complex-panel overflow) activates when Complex-panel queue at medical validation exceeds 35h. Route only Complex-panel cases that have NOT yet entered assay run (pre-assay diversion minimizes rework). Transport delay acceptable because alternative is 210h+ p90 peak.
- **Additional**: Temporary priority inversion protection — every 5th batch slot reserved for oldest Routine case regardless of priority, preventing indefinite starvation.

**Deactivation**: Urgent proportion falls below 25% AND medical validation queue <15h for 12h continuous.

### S-B: Activate for Assay Machine Degradation (Scenario B)

**Trigger conditions**:
- M1 or M2 throughput drops >30% vs baseline for >2h
- OR assay run queue exceeds 20h median

**Policy**:
- Immediate batch size reduction to 8 (R1, already in surge mode if applicable).
- **Critical**: IF M2 is degraded machine, suspend I3 machine dedication entirely. Route Routine cases to M1 and M2 proportionally by available capacity (not by case type). Urgent cases still get priority queuing but not dedicated machine.
- **Additional**: Repeat assay cases from M2 flagged for M1 re-run preferentially to break M2 failure loop.
- **Additional**: If M1 fails entirely, declare emergency protocol: Urgent cases only on M2; Routine cases held (not entering assay) until M1 recovery or outsourced if >24h outage expected. Complex-panel diverted per I10 if pre-assay stage.

**Deactivation**: Machine throughput restored to >90% baseline AND assay queue <12h for 8h continuous.

### S-C: Activate for Specialist/Validator Absence (Scenario C)

**Trigger conditions**:
- Medical validation queue >30h median
- AND/OR specialist review queue >40h median AND Complex-panel proportion >18%

**Policy**:
- Medical validation triage by cross-trained technical validator activates (R2, medical path).
- **Additional**: Routine cases that pass technical validation with no flags auto-release if medical validation queue >35h (requires pre-approved criteria: normal ranges, no critical values, no delta checks triggered). This is risk-elevating but prevents Routine p90 exceeding 72h target entirely.
- **Additional**: Hold-for-specialist-review cases pre-screened by most senior technical validator; cases with clear normal results released without specialist review.
- Evening validation shift (I5) activated if not already in surge mode.

**Deactivation**: Medical validation queue <18h AND specialist review queue <25h for 12h continuous.

### S-D: Activate for Courier Compression (Scenario D)

**Trigger conditions**:
- Registration queue exceeds 4h for >3h within a single day
- OR >50% of daily intake occurs within any 3h window for 2 consecutive days

**Policy**:
- I7 (courier slot smoothing): Temporary schedule adjustment — offer collection sites incentives for pre-10:00 or post-18:00 drop-off during compression periods.
- I8 (automated registration): Activate fully for orders with complete digital metadata; bypass manual registration entirely. Peak data: even 40% of 9,900 cases auto-registered saves ~3,960 manual registrations.
- **Additional**: Pre-analytical triage (R3) expanded to include registration-time checks: accession errors caught at registration rather than downstream.
- Batch formation time maximum reduced to 2h (from 3h surge baseline) to handle spiky arrival without batch starvation.

**Deactivation**: Registration queue <2h AND arrival distribution normalized (no 3h window exceeds 35% of daily total) for 3 consecutive days.

## 6. Residual Vulnerabilities

### V1: Combined Scenario Stacking
The redesign handles individual scenarios but if Scenarios A + B + C coincide (winter infection wave + M2 failure + validator absence), no feasible intervention set maintains acceptable performance. All buffers exhausted simultaneously. The evening validation shift, cross-training, and outsourcing together still leave assay capacity as hard constraint. **This is the fundamental residual vulnerability**: the system has finite assay machines and no rapid capacity expansion option.

### V2: Surge Mode Calibration Drift
Two-regime model (R4) creates risk of **mode hysteresis**: staff become accustomed to surge protocols and resist returning to normal mode, or conversely, delay surge activation due to false hope of quick recovery. The 4h activation confirmation window means 4h of unmitigated degradation before response. If the system oscillates frequently (3+ mode changes per week), staff confusion and protocol non-compliance increase.

### V3: Cross-Training Competency Decay
Cross-trained validators doing accession correction or medical triage infrequently will lose proficiency. In Scenario C (validator absence), they are activated at precisely the moment their skills are rustiest. Missed errors in medical triage create clinical risk that may not surface until days later (released results later found questionable).

### V4: Batch Size Reduction Efficiency Loss
R1 dynamic batch-gating reduces batch size from 12 to 8 during surge. Over a 3-week peak period with 8,140+ assay runs (peak DFG), the additional setups cost approximately 340 extra setup cycles compared to batch-12. Each setup consumes preparation bench time and machine setup time. In prolonged surge (>4 weeks), cumulative efficiency loss stresses preparation benches, potentially making preparation the new bottleneck.

### V5: Outsourcing Traceability Gap (Self-Inflicted)
I10 relieves Complex-panel bottleneck but introduces **result fragmentation**: patient record contains some results from internal lab, some from partner lab. Medical validators must reconcile results from two sources with different reference ranges and quality assurance. The "traceability complexity" warning in the scenario description manifests as additional medical validation time per outsourced case, partially offsetting the queue-time savings.

### V6: Pre-Analytical Triage False Rejections (Self-Inflicted)
R3 fast-fail triage with <100% specificity rejects some acceptable samples. Each false rejection triggers an unnecessary recollection (60h cycle peak), physician frustration, and patient harm from delayed diagnosis. If false rejection rate exceeds 5% of rejections, the net rework savings become negative (new unnecessary recollections outweigh prevented legitimate ones).

### V7: Age-Priority During Urgent Surge
R4 surge mode uses age-based priority (oldest cases first) rather than strict Urgent-first (I1). This is intentional to prevent Routine starvation, but creates **contractual penalty risk**: if Urgent cases age beyond 18h because a 70h-old Routine case is being processed, contractual penalties apply. The system must maintain Urgent cases within policy window (e.g., oldest Urgent case never exceeds 15h before absolute priority override), which is an additional control loop requiring monitoring.

## 7. Minimum Monitoring Dashboard

### Leading Indicators (Daily, with Thresholds)

**1. Incoming Case Mix (rolling 4h)**
- **Metric**: % Urgent, % Complex-panel of last 4h registrations
- **Green**: Urgent <22%, Complex <18%
- **Amber**: Urgent 22-30% OR Complex 18-22%
- **Red**: Urgent >30% OR Complex >22% (Scenario A trigger zone)
- **Action**: Amber = prepare surge mode briefing; Red = evaluate surge activation

**2. Assay Machine Throughput Ratio (rolling 2h)**
- **Metric**: Actual throughput / baseline throughput for M1 and M2 separately
- **Green**: Both >90%
- **Amber**: Either machine 70-90%
- **Red**: Either machine <70% for >2h (Scenario B trigger)
- **Action**: Amber = investigate cause, alert maintenance; Red = activate Scenario B policy

**3. Queue Depth at 3 Chokepoints (real-time)**
- **Metrics**: Medical validation queue (hours), Assay run queue (hours), Pre-analytical check queue (hours)
- **Green**: Medical <15h, Assay <10h, Pre-analytical <5h
- **Amber**: Medical 15-25h OR Assay 10-18h OR Pre-analytical 5-9h
- **Red**: Medical >25h OR Assay >18h OR Pre-analytical >9h
- **Action**: Any Red = surge mode evaluation; Medical Red + Complex mix high = Scenario C/A evaluation

**4. Recollection Request Rate (rolling 8h)**
- **Metric**: Cases sent to Request recollection / total pre-analytical completions
- **Green**: <4%
- **Amber**: 4-7%
- **Red**: >7% (normal peak baseline ~4.8% from DFG: 780/8530)
- **Action**: Amber = audit pre-analytical triage performance; Red = investigate collection site quality or triage false-positive rate

**5. Batch Formation Time (rolling 8h, for Routine cases)**
- **Metric**: Median and p90 time Routine cases spend in Batch wait state
- **Green**: Median <4h, p90 <10h
- **Amber**: Median 4-8h, p90 10-16h
- **Red**: Median >8h, p90 >16h (current peak: median 14h, note: this is already Red; target improvement)
- **Action**: Red = reduce batch size to 8, enforce max formation time, evaluate arrival distribution

**6. Registration Queue and Arrival Concentration (daily)**
- **Metric**: Registration median queue hours; % of daily registrations in busiest 3h window
- **Green**: Queue <2h, max 3h concentration <40%
- **Amber**: Queue 2-4h OR concentration 40-50%
- **Red**: Queue >4h OR concentration >50% (Scenario D trigger)
- **Action**: Red = activate Scenario D policy; automated registration expansion

**7. Staff Availability Flag (start-of-day)**
- **Metric**: Medical validators available / expected; specialist reviewer available today (binary)
- **Green**: Full staffing
- **Amber**: 1 medical validator absent OR specialist reviewer absent (standard Friday pattern)
- **Red**: >1 medical validator absent OR specialist reviewer absent on non-standard day OR technical validator absence >1
- **Action**: Red = pre-activate Scenario C components; alert cross-trained staff

**8. Urgent Case Age Distribution (rolling 4h)**
- **Metric**: p90 and maximum age of in-process Urgent cases (registration to current state)
- **Green**: p90 <10h, max <14h
- **Amber**: p90 10-14h, max 14-17h
- **Red**: p90 >14h OR max >17h (18h contractual limit approaching)
- **Action**: Red = absolute Urgent priority override regardless of age-priority policy; investigate blockage

### Lagging Outcome Metrics (Weekly Review, Validate Leading Indicators)

- Overall median and p90 cycle time by case type (compare to normal/peak baselines)
- Contractual penalty events (Urgent >18h) — count and root cause
- Overall rework percentage (target: <10% peak vs 11.8% baseline peak)
- Outsourced case volume and partner lab TAT compliance
- Cross-trained staff activation hours and error audit results
- False-positive pre-analytical rejection count (from physician complaints and recollected-then-normal results)
- Surge mode activation frequency and duration (target: <30% of operating hours in surge mode over quarter)

---

**Summary Architecture**: The redesign creates a **conditional, multi-regime system** that absorbs disruption through dynamic batching, cross-training buffers, fast-fail triage, and formal surge protocols, while avoiding the traps of static priority rules (I1), blanket batch enlargement (I2), unconditional machine dedication (I3), and naive capacity addition. The residual fragility is the hard assay-machine constraint under combined scenarios and the operational complexity of mode transitions — both require explicit monitoring and governance rather than further process changes.
