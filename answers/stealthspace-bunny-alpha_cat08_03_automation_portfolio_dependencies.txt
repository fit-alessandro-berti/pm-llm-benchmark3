## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Interpretation rule:** DFG frequencies are observed transitions, not necessarily unique-case totals or proof of strict finish–start precedence. Case identifiers, timestamps, and variant data are needed to distinguish concurrency, loops, and different case cohorts.

- **Intake and classification: A1  A2  downstream routing.**  
  The 11,880 Receive  Register transitions and 11,680 Register  Classify transitions show a strong mainline. Register remains in position 2 for 98% of cases, so A1 must preserve that common pattern while supporting exceptions. However, A1 mainly changes where the queue appears next: **Classify product and exposure** has two-hour waiting and an 8% rework rate. A2 should therefore precede or accompany any scaled intake automation. Accurate classification is also a prerequisite for A5’s document checklist, A3/A4’s KYC handling, and A7’s collateral applicability.

- **Concurrency-aware KYC/document dependency: A2  A3 and A5.**  
  The DFG contains 7,250 Classify  KYC transitions and 3,940 Classify  Financial document review transitions. In practice, KYC and document review often overlap even though the system records them sequentially. Their waiting and resource demand must not simply be added as if every case completed both serially. KYC resolution can block signing while document normalization or credit work proceeds where otherwise permitted. The hard legal dependency is **KYC clearance before signing**, not an assumed universal requirement that all financial work wait for KYC.

- **KYC exception chain: A3 complements A4; A12 supplies the control boundary.**  
  KYC  Resolve entity mismatch occurs 1,750 times, with 1,160 returns to KYC. The direct Resolve  Contract edge occurs 380 times, but that trace is not permission to bypass KYC. The mismatch variant’s 82% critical-path incidence makes A4 a strong first target despite its lower frequency. A3 can pre-classify other KYC hits, but it should not create a second competing specialist queue. Both need:
  - complete and current entity information;
  - a shared case-level KYC state;
  - human escalation for all uncertain or consequential hits;
  - protected capacity for the three specialists and their two-person senior dependency;
  - a non-circumventable signing and activation gate through A12.

- **Document quality chain: A5 and A6 are complementary.**  
  Financial document review  Request missing documents occurs 3,300 times, followed by 2,800 returns to review. A5 attacks missing or repeatedly requested source material. A6 then extracts and normalizes the completed statements. Deploying A6 without A5 risks automating low-quality inputs more efficiently; deploying A5 without A6 merely moves completed files into a slow normalization queue. Their shared prerequisite is a **product/exposure-aware completeness definition**, co-designed with RMs so portal instructions address the observed source of incomplete customer instructions.

- **Credit and collateral chain: A6  Credit analysis  A7 where required  A8.**  
  The observed Normalize statements  Credit analysis count is 5,000. Credit analysis  Committee packaging accounts for 2,700 transitions, while 1,980 transitions from external valuation waiting reach packaging. Committee packaging nevertheless requires all relevant artifacts, not merely one predecessor. A6 can improve credit-input quality, but faster normalized files can increase arrival rates at the nine-person analyst pool. A7 can improve readiness and routing for applicable collateral cases, but cannot directly control the external 58-hour wait. Collateral triage must not sacrifice valuation independence, quality review, or dispute handling.

- **Decision, contract, signing, and activation chain: A12  A8/A9/A10/A11.**  
  The DFG shows Committee decision  Contract preparation on 3,650 transitions, Contract preparation  Customer signing on 7,300, and signing  Loan activation on 7,100. These must be treated as case-linked, control-gated states, not a reason to assume every shortfall is a bot failure. A8 requires reconciled artifacts; A9 requires clean decision data; A10 requires legally valid identity and consent; A11 must remain blocked until all controls are complete. The 650 Contract  Remediate control issue and 510 remediation  Contract transitions show a recurring work loop, not isolated incidents.

- **Human escalation and shifted demand are architectural dependencies.**
  - **Back office, 12 FTE:** useful capacity for A5’s request handling, particularly outside the post-15:00 underutilization period. Automation must not merely move timestamps into a late-day queue.
  - **Compliance, 3 FTE:** A3/A4/A12 can increase specialist touch time through better prioritization and clearer escalations. They do not justify assuming an immediate specialist headcount reduction.
  - **Credit, 9 FTE:** A6 reduces some normalization work but may increase clean credit-analysis inflow. Capacity gates must measure total analyst and quality-review demand.
  - **Legal Ops, 4 FTE:** upstream KYC delays can leave them underutilized, while improved flow can create month-end bursts. A9 would amplify those bursts unless scheduling and control completion are addressed.
  - **External valuers:** remain outside direct management control. Internal automation should improve routing, readiness, and status visibility, not merely add another external queue.

- **Core-banking integration is a capacity constraint, not merely an IT checklist.**  
  The recommended portfolio reserves the first-six-month deep integration budget for **A4 and A6**. A12 can use a case/workflow control overlay, and A5 can use the portal/CRM and case-status events. Later A8–A11 should not be given independent core write authority merely because their interfaces are labeled “lightweight.” Case IDs, timestamps, source versions, and reconciliation must be consistent across all modules.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Automation risk | Affected candidates | Compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| **False positives and false negatives** | A2, A3, A4, especially A3’s KYC decisions | Use conservative thresholds, human disposition of all hits/uncertain cases, representative validation before launch, and ongoing adjudication of sampled recommendations. A “no hit” or entity match is never full KYC clearance. Segment performance by case type and risk rather than relying only on an overall accuracy rate. |
| **Poor source extraction or misclassification** | A1, A2, A6 | Validate against source documents, preserve page/field provenance, prohibit silent overwriting, and require analyst or operations review of low-confidence changes. Treat duplicate detection separately from merge authorization. Reconcile extracted figures to the original statement and approved accounting taxonomy. |
| **Model drift and changing operating conditions** | A2, A3, A6, A7, A8 | Maintain model/version registry, approved training-data lineage, change control, feature/input monitoring, periodic revalidation, champion/challenger testing, and rollback. Monitor shifts in document format, product mix, exposure mix, KYC typology, and missing-data patterns—not just model confidence. |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out generation** | A8, A9, and potentially A11 | Assemble only from approved, versioned artifacts. Missing or conflicting data must stop generation. Contracts must use approved templates and effective-dated rules; legal changes require an accountable legal owner. Any post-decision change to pricing, limits, collateral, or covenants should trigger reapproval. |
| **Provisional signing, identity, or consent failure** | A10, A11 | Enforce a hard state-machine requirement for verified KYC clearance before signing. Use appropriate identity proofing, consent capture, and tamper-evident records. An automation exception must stop activation and enter legal/compliance remediation; it must not backdate consent or treat a provisional signature as automatically valid. |
| **Four-eyes violations** | A8, A9, A12; also affected by overall flow changes | Check independence between Credit analysis and Committee packaging for every High-exposure case, especially during month-end. Block the same person from completing both roles. A separate reviewer must not merely rubber-stamp work generated by that person. No blanket month-end override should be available. |
| **Insufficient exception-handling capacity** | A3, A4, A6, A9, A12 | Capacity-plan the **new** human workflow, including QA, ambiguous cases, overrides, and remediation. Train and rotate specialists, protect senior-review availability, stagger month-end work, and throttle automation when queue age or handoffs exceed agreed limits. Do not remove FTE capacity merely because machine touch time falls. |
| **Incomplete auditability** | All candidates, especially model-assisted ones | Record case ID, source artifact/version, model or rule version, suggestion, confidence/reason, human override and reason, final decision, actor, and timestamp in an immutable, access-controlled trail. A12 must provide reconstruction and evidence retrieval, not merely a status screen. Retention and access should support independent review. |
| **Stale, duplicated, or conflicting integrations** | A1, A4, A6, A7, A8, A9, A11, A12 | Use common case identifiers, event timestamps, version checks, idempotent processing, source reconciliation, and exception queues. Test replay and manual fallback. Do not count a microservice as “shallow” if it actually creates an unreconciled second core state. |
| **Customer friction and poor adoption** | A5, A10 | Show customers why a document is needed, accept alternative formats/channels, provide accessible support, and offer RM assistance. Avoid rigid repeated requests or automatic rejection. Monitor repeat contacts, abandonment, complaints, completion time, and RM override effort. |
| **Policy ambiguity and outdated rules** | A9, A11, A12 | Establish a rule inventory with Compliance, Legal, Credit, and Operations ownership; effective dates; exception expiry; and escalation routes. Unknown cases go to a human owner. A12 can identify ambiguity but cannot invent or quietly relax policy. |
| **External partner variability** | A7 | Use approved routing criteria, valuer quality and independence checks, status/service monitoring, and documented dispute escalation. Do not reward speed at the expense of valuation adequacy. Track external elapsed time separately from internal handling time. |

These controls do not transfer accountability away from the bank. Compliance remains responsible for KYC/entity decisions, Credit for analytical judgments, Legal for contractual rules, and authorized Operations and committee personnel for final approvals.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Phased plan

| Phase and timing | Modules and integration | Main work and gate criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — Discover and baseline, months 0–2** | A12 in read-only/control-design mode; architecture for A4 and A6. | Establish case-level event data, including concurrent KYC/document timestamps, variant IDs, source versions, and handovers. Define rule ownership, KYC/activation states, High-exposure four-eyes logic, and escalation SLAs. Baseline the reported control findings by product, exposure, and month-end status. Capacity-plan the three specialists, nine analysts, 12 Back office staff, and four Legal Ops staff. |
| **Phase 1 — Controlled first wave, months 3–6** | **A12 live first as a shallow workbench; A4 and A6 as the only two deep integrations; A5 as a narrow portal pilot.** | A4 assists—not automatically clears—entity cases. A6 sends ambiguous/low-quality statements to analysts. A5 uses soft, personalized nudges and RM support. A12 records control evidence and routes exceptions. Gate on audit completeness, KYC sign-state correctness, normalization quality, specialist/analyst queue capacity, and customer friction. |
| **Phase 2 — Controlled scale, months 7–9** | Retain the same four modules and the same two deep integrations. No fifth automation module. | Expand only after the first-wave gates pass. Use existing events to monitor collateral waiting and other deferred candidates without adding production automation. Scale by Micro/SME, High-exposure, Equipment finance, and Credit-line renewal cohorts. Do not simply expand the highest-volume segment if it overloads scarce human review. |
| **Phase 3 — Validate and decide the next wave, months 10–12** | Four-module portfolio only. | Reassess end-to-end cycle time, net human effort, exception capacity, control performance, and customer outcomes. A1 remains a strong later quick win if the measured critical path has moved or management explicitly trades other benefits for Back office capacity. Do not add it silently as a fifth module. A9/A10/A11 remain conditional on upstream evidence and future integration capacity. |

### Monitoring metrics

1. **End-to-end flow and waiting**
   - Median, mean, and upper-tail cycle time by product and exposure.
   - Active service time, internal waiting, customer waiting, and external waiting reported separately.
   - Entity mismatch waiting, document-request waiting, normalization waiting, credit waiting, external valuation waiting, and signing waiting.
   - Concurrent KYC/document work and resource overlap, rather than assuming DFG precedence is finish–start precedence.

2. **Rework and dependency integrity**
   - Classification, document, normalization, entity, valuation, contract, and signing exception rates.
   - Unique-case rework rates separately from loop counts.
   - Post-deployment versions of the observed loops: KYC  mismatch  KYC, document review  request  review, and contract  remediation  contract.
   - Percentage of committee packages with the correct KYC, normalized financial, credit, and conditional collateral artifacts.

3. **Control performance**
   - Reported High-exposure four-eyes violation rate, especially month-end.
   - Signing without verified KYC clearance; distinguish attempted/provisional cases from valid signings.
   - Contract-remediation rates overall and for Credit-line renewals.
   - Activation attempts blocked for incomplete controls and subsequent safe remediation.
   - **100% traceability** of model/rule suggestions, human overrides, and final decisions. Actual violations and detection/remediation speed must be separate measures.

4. **Automation quality**
   - Confirmed false-positive and false-negative performance on adjudicated samples, segmented by Micro/SME/High-exposure and relevant product.
   - Extraction/classification confidence, analyst overrides, low-quality PDF handling, and model/input drift.
   - Reviewer agreement and ambiguous-case rates; no model target may be declared acceptable without representative validation.

5. **Resource and customer outcomes**
   - Arrival rates, touch time, queue time, handovers, overtime, and escalation backlog for Compliance, Credit, Back office, Legal, and RM teams.
   - Net human effort, including QA and exception work—not just machine execution time.
   - Portal abandonment, repeat customer contacts, signing adoption, complaints, and RM assistance demand.
   - Financial value should be assessed net of exception handling and control work, without turning the observed mean waits into guaranteed savings.

### Scale and stop gates

- No scaling without tested manual fallback, accountable rule owners, reconciled case data, and sufficient exception capacity.
- No model or workflow may authorize signing or activation without verified KYC and control completion.
- Any four-eyes breach, missing decision/contract linkage, or new unremediated control bypass stops expansion of the affected automation.
- Quality or customer-friction deterioration keeps the module in assistance-only mode; it does not justify adding another module to compensate.
- Improvements must be assessed at case level. A shorter KYC wait is not a success if it merely moves delay into Credit analysis, Committee packaging, Legal Ops, or customer remediation.
- Numeric service-level and quality thresholds should be set after the baseline/shadow period. The existing control findings are baselines and mandatory remediation targets, not proof that deployment will immediately eliminate them.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend **P4: A4 + A5 + A6 + A12**, subject to the first-six-month controls and capacity gates.

This portfolio spends the limited integration capacity on the two deep capabilities that address critical upstream work:

- **A4** targets the lower-frequency but critical-path entity exception: 31-hour waiting, 38% exceptions, repeated KYC handling, and 82% critical-path incidence.
- **A6** targets normalization’s 20-hour wait and 16% rework while improving inputs to the central nine-person Credit network.
- **A5** addresses the repeated document loop—3,300 requests and 2,800 returns—and reduces the risk that A6 merely processes incomplete source material.
- **A12** provides the control foundation needed before faster flows expose weaknesses in KYC, four-eyes separation, contract consistency, and activation.

This is deliberately **not** the maximum direct-labor-savings portfolio. It sacrifices A7’s external-valuation routing, the visible A1 registration gain, and downstream contract/signing automation. Those are real sacrifices: external waiting remains 58 hours, and customer signing remains 44 hours. A7 remains a strong targeted alternative if segment-level evidence shows that collateral delay outweighs the document-quality opportunity. But external routing has less controllable savings than A4 or A6 and still requires human collateral-quality review.

The highest-frequency activity should **not** set the initial priority:

- **Receive application** is already 72% digital and has only 0.2-hour waiting, so additional front-end automation has limited marginal scope.
- **A1 registration** is a genuinely attractive, very feasible quick win: 11,880 cases, eight-minute service, and 1.5-hour waiting. It is therefore a credible quick-win alternative and a plausible later release.
- However, automating registration primarily advances the queue into Classification, with its two-hour wait and 8% rework, and then into scarce Compliance, Credit, collateral, and control-capacity pools. Back office also has post-15:00 slack, making local labor relief less urgent than the evidence suggests.

The defensible trade-off is therefore to automate **the work that releases and improves constrained end-to-end flows**, while explicitly funding human escalation and controls. A1 can follow once the bank has measured whether front-office capacity has become the next binding constraint. Automating A11, A10, or A9 ahead of that evidence would risk making a fast downstream transaction out of an unreliable loan file.
