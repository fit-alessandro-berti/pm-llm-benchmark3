# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Assumptions:**
- The process flow is strictly sequential: `Intake -> Diagnostic Work -> Validation`.
- Diagnostic work allocation is based on the case type and resource availability.
- Priority cases (P1) may preempt lower-priority work but cannot be preempted more than once.
- Overtime is limited to 30 minutes per resource.
- The base scenario is the reference for calculations and adjustments.

**Decision Variables:**
- Resource allocation for each activity type (Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, Validation).
- Policy for prioritization and preemption of tasks.
- Handling of parallel branches for ME and ES cases.
- Handling of handover and setup times between activities.

**Objectives:**
1. **Minimize Mean Delay:** This objective focuses on reducing the average time taken to complete a case, aiming for the fastest possible processing while meeting SLA requirements.
2. **Minimize Tail Delay Risk:** This objective emphasizes minimizing the risk of exceeding SLA deadlines, particularly for P1 and P2 cases, which have stricter completion times.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

**Base Scenario Analysis:**
- **Offered Load by Skill and Time Block:**
  - The demand varies by time block and case type, with higher demands in the morning and higher priority blocks.
  - For example, in the 08:00-12:00 block, Mechanical cases make up 30% of the demand, Electrical 35%, Software 25%, ME 20%, and ES 10%.

- **Resource-Specific Speed and Availability:**
  - Ada, Ben, and Deepa have varying speeds and availability, affecting their ability to handle high-demand cases.
  - For instance, Ben operates at 90% of average speed but has a shorter break, potentially increasing his availability.

- **Parallel vs. Sequential Branch Trade-offs:**
  - Parallel ME or ES branches add coordination time (8 minutes) before validation, potentially lengthening the critical path.
  - Sequential execution avoids coordination delays but may extend the overall processing time.

- **Handover/Setup Penalties:**
  - Switching resources or between activity types incurs delays, such as 7 minutes for Intake changes and 5 minutes for cross-skill team switches.

**Scenario Adjustments:**
- **U1 Morning Surge:** Increases morning demand by 70% and raises P1 share by 8 percentage points.
- **U2 Electrical/Software Incident:** Doubles demand from 12:00-16:00 for Electrical and ES cases.
- **U3 Absence:** Chen is absent for the entire day, increasing the load on Ada, Ben, Deepa, Emil, and Farah.
- **U4 Long-tail Day:** Doubles the probabilities of complex-tail activities, increasing variability in processing times.
- **U5 Combined Stress:** U1 plus U3 with a 30-minute IT outage at 09:30-10:00, affecting Intake capacity.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Policy 1: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues**
- **Eligibility:** Assign cases to resources based on case type and skill match.
- **Priority Score:** Prioritize P1 cases first, then P2, and finally P3.
- **Reservation:** Reserve P1 capacity for P1 cases only.
- **Preemption:** Allow preemption of lower-priority tasks only once per case.
- **Parallelization:** Avoid parallelizing ME and ES branches to minimize coordination delays.

**Policy 2: Full Pooling with Transparent Dispatch Rule**
- **Eligibility:** Any qualified resource can handle any case type.
- **Priority Score:** Use a weighted priority score based on case importance and urgency.
- **Reservation:** No reserved P1 capacity; prioritize based on score.
- **Preemption:** Allow preemption of lower-priority tasks without restrictions.
- **Parallelization:** Allow parallel execution of ME and ES branches, with coordination delays accounted for.

**Policy 3: Hybrid Policy with Reserved P1 Capacity and Controlled Overflow**
- **Eligibility:** Reserve P1 capacity for P1 cases, with overflow handled by qualified resources.
- **Priority Score:** Use a priority score that considers both case urgency and skill match.
- **Reservation:** Reserve P1 capacity for P1 cases only.
- **Preemption:** Allow preemption of lower-priority tasks with a limit of one per case.
- **Parallelization:** Controlled overflow for ME and ES branches, with coordination delays minimized.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary Policy Recommendation: Policy 2 (Full Pooling with Transparent Dispatch Rule)**
- **Rationale:** This policy offers a balanced approach by allowing any qualified resource to handle any case type, ensuring flexibility and resilience to disruptions. It minimizes coordination delays and maximizes resource utilization while maintaining fair distribution of workloads.
- **Trigger Thresholds:** If utilization exceeds 90% for any resource, dynamically adjust the dispatch rule to prioritize less loaded resources. If Chen's absence persists beyond 2 consecutive days, switch to a more specialized policy to mitigate the impact on Mechanical cases.

**Fallback Policy: Policy 1 (Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues)**
- **Rationale:** In cases where resource availability is highly variable or when specific skills are critical (e.g., Software cases), this policy ensures that specialized resources are utilized efficiently, reducing the risk of delays in critical tasks.
- **Trigger Thresholds:** Activate this policy when specific skill shortages are detected or when the system experiences unexpected spikes in demand that cannot be handled by the full pooling approach.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of Estimates:** Resources may manipulate their availability or processing times to appear more efficient, leading to potential inaccuracies in the simulation.
- **Starvation of P3 Work:** If P1 and P2 cases dominate the queue, P3 cases may be delayed. Implementing strict SLA monitoring and alerts can mitigate this risk.
- **Skill Erosion:** Frequent changes in task assignments may lead to skill erosion, particularly for less frequently used skills. Regular training and skill reinforcement programs can help mitigate this risk.
- **Excessive Preemption:** Frequent preemption of tasks can lead to increased processing times and reduced efficiency. Limiting preemption to once per case can help manage this risk.
- **Coordination Overhead:** Parallel execution of ME and ES branches incurs coordination delays, which can be minimized by optimizing the dispatch rule to avoid unnecessary parallelization.
- **New Bottlenecks at Validation:** High demand on the Validation stage can become a new bottleneck. Ensuring adequate resource allocation and minimizing handover delays can help alleviate this risk.
