## 1. Normalization Assumptions

- **Clock-offset direction and intervals**: For each system I use `clock_offset = recorded_clock - true_clock`. If `clock_offset ∈ [a,b]` and the recorded-clock interval is `[t_rec_min, t_rec_max]`, the true-time envelope is `[t_rec_min - b, t_rec_max - a]`. I always apply display/rounding semantics **first** to obtain `[t_rec_min, t_rec_max]`, then subtract offsets.
- **Sub-second rounding**: Where combinations of measurement uncertainty and offset produce non-integer seconds, I round **outwards to whole seconds** for reporting (never shrinking the feasible set). All reported UTC bounds are therefore conservative supersets.

### 1.1 Source-specific rules

- **HUB (UTC)**  
  - Exact-second timestamps with measurement uncertainty ±0.5 s and clock-offset bounds [-1 s,+1 s].  
  - Recorded-clock interval: `[T_disp − 0.5 s, T_disp + 0.5 s]`.  
  - True time: `[T_disp − 1.5 s, T_disp + 1.5 s]`, reported as `[T_disp − 2 s, T_disp + 2 s]`.  
  - Treated as high-trust reference.

- **OPS-BER (Europe/Berlin, CEST on 2026-06-01)**  
  - Display semantics: **floor to minute**; displayed `hh:mm` means `[hh:mm:00, hh:mm:59.999]` on the local clock.  
  - Standard offset bounds: [-90 s,+120 s]; for T-409 only: weak estimate [-12 min,+12 min].  
  - True local interval: `[hh:mm:00 − max_offset, hh:mm:59.999 − min_offset]`.  
  - Convert to UTC via CEST (UTC+2): `UTC = local − 2 h`.

- **SENSOR (UTC)**  
  - Display semantics: **round to nearest 10 seconds**; recorded clock in `[T_disp − 5 s, T_disp + 5 s]`.  
  - Offset bounds [-5 s,+5 s] → true-time interval `[T_disp − 10 s, T_disp + 10 s]`.  
  - Semantics: completion events; I treat the completion as instantaneous within this UTC interval.

- **VENDOR-NY (America/New_York, EDT)**  
  - Floor to minute; recorded clock `[hh:mm:00, hh:mm:59.999]`.  
  - Offset bounds [-180 s,+180 s].  
  - True local interval `[hh:mm:00 − 180 s, hh:mm:59.999 + 180 s]`; convert with EDT (UTC−4) as `UTC = local + 4 h`.

- **LEGACY-BER (Europe/Berlin, DST fold 2025-10-26)**  
  - Floor to minute; offset bounds [-60 s,+60 s].  
  - For non-ambiguous times: same logic as OPS-BER with ±60 s, using the zone’s normal offset.  
  - For **2025-10-26 02:xx**:
    - I construct two candidate true intervals per event: one assuming CEST (UTC+2), one CET (UTC+1).  
    - Each candidate uses `[hh:mm:00 − 60 s, hh:mm:59.999 + 60 s]` local, then subtracts the appropriate UTC offset.  
    - I then apply the **ingestion-order constraint** (Alert_Received before Investigation_Start before Containment_Complete). Only the fold assignment consistent with this ordering is retained; the other is discarded rather than unioned, because ingestion order is itself strong temporal evidence.

- **MOBILE-LON (Europe/London, BST)**  
  - Display semantics: **round to nearest minute**; displayed `hh:mm` corresponds to recorded clock in `[hh:mm − 30 s, hh:mm + 30 s]`.  
  - Offset bounds [-300 s,+300 s] (±5 min).  
  - First compute the true **entry time** interval from rounding + offset using the general rule.  
  - Additional **entry delay**: the user may enter the start time up to **5 minutes after** actual Investigation_Start. I model Investigation_Start as occurring in `[entry_min − 5 min, entry_max]`. Thus entry delay widens the interval only towards earlier times.  
  - Convert to UTC via BST (UTC+1): `UTC = local − 1 h`.

### 1.2 Derived events and causal constraints

- **T-404 – inferred Investigation_Start**  
  - HUB records `Investigation_Complete` at 10:35:00 (UTC) with ±2 s, giving `[10:34:58, 10:35:02]`.  
  - Signed record: Investigation duration ∈ [18,25] minutes.  
  - I infer `Investigation_Start_true = Investigation_Complete_true − d`, `d ∈ [18,25]` minutes.  
  - Earliest start: `10:34:58 − 25 min = 10:09:58`; latest: `10:35:02 − 18 min = 10:17:02`.  
  - This preserves all completion and duration uncertainty and does not collapse to midpoints.

- **T-407 – inferred Alert_Received**  
  - HUB `Alert_Sent` interval: `[13:59:58, 14:00:02]` UTC.  
  - Network latency ℓ ∈ [0,20] minutes; additionally, `Alert_Received_true ≤ Investigation_Start_true`.  
  - I take `Alert_Received_true ∈ [Alert_Sent_min, Alert_Sent_max + 20 min]` intersected with the Investigation_Start interval, yielding `[13:59:58, 14:17:02]` UTC.

- **Precedence constraints**  
  - For each case I enforce: `Alert_Received ≤ Investigation_Start ≤ Containment_Complete ≤ Close_Incident`, where the events exist or are defensibly inferred.  
  - For T-407 I also require `Alert_Sent ≤ Alert_Received`.  
  - When raw intervals alone would allow negative durations, I restrict to the subset of combinations satisfying these precedence relations.

- **Duration intervals and classifications**  
  - For a pair `(A,B)` with UTC intervals `[A_min, A_max]` and `[B_min, B_max]` and a requirement `A ≤ B`, the coarse duration envelope is `[max(0, B_min − A_max), max(0, B_max − A_min)]`, adjusted if needed by additional precedence constraints.  
  - I use the four-way classification from the prompt:
    - **CERTAIN_PASS**, **CERTAIN_VIOLATION**, **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**, **INDETERMINATE**, based on whether the entire duration interval lies inside, outside, straddles, or cannot be bounded relative to the acceptance region.  
  - Interval reasoning is primary; midpoints are only used in Section 5 to build a lossy point-timestamp log for PM4Py.

### 4.2 Temporal-profile ζ = 1, 2, 3

As ζ increases, each profile band widens around its mean. The main effects are:
- **Borderline A→I cases (T-401, T-404, T-405)**: POSSIBLE_VIOLATION at ζ = 1 and ζ = 2 becomes **CERTAIN_PASS** at ζ = 3, because their upper bounds lie between 960 s and 1140 s.  
- **Clearly slow A→I cases (T-402)**: at ζ = 1 its A→I is a **CERTAIN_VIOLATION**; at ζ ≥ 2 it becomes **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** as the lower edge dips into the widened band.  
- **I→C durations (T-402, T-404, T-406, T-409)**: several cases that were POSSIBLE_VIOLATION at ζ = 1 (because durations grazed the 2700 s upper bound or fell slightly below the 1500 s lower bound) become **CERTAIN_PASS** at ζ ≥ 2 when the band expands to [900,3300] s.  
- **T-408 investigation→containment and containment→closure**:  
  - I→C is **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** at ζ = 1, **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** at ζ = 2, and **CERTAIN_PASS** at ζ = 3.  
  - C→Close is **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** at ζ = 1 and 2 (closure ~38.4 h vs up to 36 h), but becomes **CERTAIN_PASS** at ζ = 3, where the upper bound reaches 151200 s (~42 h).  
- **Most A→C pairs** remain **CERTAIN_PASS** across ζ = 1,2,3, with only T-402 and T-403 showing ζ = 1 border effects that disappear at ζ ≥ 2.

Overall, decreasing ζ from 2 to 1 sharpens statistical scrutiny, converting some borderline passes into POSSIBLE_VIOLATION or CERTAIN_VIOLATION, while increasing ζ to 3 makes most temporal-profile anomalies disappear except for the most extreme cases (especially T-408 at ζ = 1–2).

def parse_local_naive(ts_str: str) -> datetime:
    """Parse the local timestamp string without tzinfo."""
    # Support both second-level and minute-level strings.
    fmt = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S" if len(ts_str.split()) == 2 and ":" in ts_str.split()[1] and len(ts_str.split()[1]) == 8 else "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    return datetime.strptime(ts_str, fmt)


def local_record_interval(base_local: datetime, system: str):
    """
    Compute the recorded-clock interval [t_rec_min, t_rec_max] in local time
    before applying clock offsets, based on display semantics.
    """
    meta = SYSTEM_META[system]
    display = meta["display"]

    if display == "exact_second":
        # HUB: ±0.5 s around displayed second
        return base_local - timedelta(seconds=0.5), base_local + timedelta(seconds=0.5)

    if display == "floor_minute":
        floored = base_local.replace(second=0, microsecond=0)
        end = floored + timedelta(minutes=1) - timedelta(microseconds=1)
        return floored, end

    if display == "round_10s":
        # SENSOR: assume timestamp is rounded to nearest 10 s
        # so recorded clock is within ±5 s
        return base_local - timedelta(seconds=5), base_local + timedelta(seconds=5)

    if display == "round_minute_mobile":
        # MOBILE-LON: round to nearest minute → ±30 s at recording time
        rounded = base_local.replace(second=0, microsecond=0)
        return rounded - timedelta(seconds=30), rounded + timedelta(seconds=30)

    raise ValueError(f"Unknown display semantics: {display}")


def true_interval_local(base_local: datetime, system: str):
    """
    Apply timestamp-resolution uncertainty and clock offsets to obtain a list
    of true-time intervals in the local clock (for most systems a single
    interval; for LEGACY-BER on the DST fold, potentially two).
    """
    meta = SYSTEM_META[system]
    offset_min = meta["offset_min"]
    offset_max = meta["offset_max"]

    # Special handling for LEGACY-BER DST fold on 2025-10-26 02:xx
    if system == "LEGACY-BER" and base_local.date() == datetime(2025, 10, 26).date() and base_local.hour == 2:
        # Two folds: CEST (UTC+2) and CET (UTC+1). We model both as separate intervals.
        intervals = []
        for utc_offset_hours in (2, 1):
            rec_min, rec_max = local_record_interval(base_local, system)
            # Apply offset range: earliest true = rec_min - max(offset), latest = rec_max - min(offset)
            t_min = rec_min - timedelta(seconds=offset_max)
            t_max = rec_max - timedelta(seconds=offset_min)
            # We keep them as local times but track which fold via utc_offset_hours later.
            intervals.append((t_min, t_max, utc_offset_hours))
        return intervals

    # Default: single interval, no DST fold split
    rec_min, rec_max = local_record_interval(base_local, system)
    t_min = rec_min - timedelta(seconds=offset_max)
    t_max = rec_max - timedelta(seconds=offset_min)
    return [(t_min, t_max, None)]


def to_utc_interval(base_local: datetime, system: str):
    """
    Convert local true-time intervals to UTC, returning a list of (utc_start, utc_end).
    LEGACY-BER on DST fold keeps two possible UTC offsets; we convert each explicitly.
    """
    meta = SYSTEM_META[system]
    tz_name = meta["tz"]
    local_intervals = true_interval_local(base_local, system)
    utc_intervals = []

    for t_min_local, t_max_local, explicit_offset_hours in local_intervals:
        if explicit_offset_hours is not None:
            # LEGACY-BER DST fold: explicit offset (+2 for CEST, +1 for CET)
            offset = timedelta(hours=explicit_offset_hours)
            utc_min = (t_min_local - offset).replace(tzinfo=timezone.utc)
            utc_max = (t_max_local - offset).replace(tzinfo=timezone.utc)
        else:
            tz = ZoneInfo(tz_name)
            # Attach tzinfo, letting zoneinfo handle DST
            t_min_z = t_min_local.replace(tzinfo=tz)
            t_max_z = t_max_local.replace(tzinfo=tz)
            utc_min = t_min_z.astimezone(timezone.utc)
            utc_max = t_max_z.astimezone(timezone.utc)
        utc_intervals.append((utc_min, utc_max))

    return utc_intervals


def normalize_events_to_utc(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Add 'utc_intervals' column containing a list of (utc_start, utc_end) intervals
    for each event, and convenience 'utc_start_mid', 'utc_end_mid' midpoints.
    """
    intervals = []
    midpoints = []

    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        system = row["system"]
        base_local = parse_local_naive(row["timestamp"])
        utc_ints = to_utc_interval(base_local, system)
        intervals.append(utc_ints)

        # Define a single midpoint as the average of earliest and latest across all alternatives.
        all_starts = [iv[0] for iv in utc_ints]
        all_ends = [iv[1] for iv in utc_ints]
        global_min = min(all_starts)
        global_max = max(all_ends)
        mid = global_min + (global_max - global_min) / 2
        midpoints.append(mid)

    df = df.copy()
    df["utc_intervals"] = intervals
    df["utc_midpoint"] = midpoints
    return df

# 3. Derive feasible duration intervals and classifications

def merge_numeric_intervals(intervals):
    """Merge overlapping [a,b] numeric intervals."""
    if not intervals:
        return []
    intervals = sorted(intervals, key=lambda x: x[0])
    merged = [intervals[0]]
    for start, end in intervals[1:]:
        last_start, last_end = merged[-1]
        if start <= last_end:
            merged[-1] = (last_start, max(last_end, end))
        else:
            merged.append((start, end))
    return merged


def duration_intervals_for_pair(df_case: pd.DataFrame, act_a: str, act_b: str):
    """
    Compute all feasible duration intervals (in seconds) between activities act_a and act_b
    based on utc_intervals, enforcing that B occurs not before A.
    """
    rows_a = df_case[df_case["activity"] == act_a]
    rows_b = df_case[df_case["activity"] == act_b]
    if rows_a.empty or rows_b.empty:
        return []

    ints_a = rows_a.iloc[0]["utc_intervals"]
    ints_b = rows_b.iloc[0]["utc_intervals"]
    numeric_intervals = []

    for a_start, a_end in ints_a:
        for b_start, b_end in ints_b:
            # Require some ordering-consistent overlap: b must be no earlier than a.
            # We compute minimal and maximal feasible durations for this pair of intervals.
            earliest = max(0.0, (b_start - a_end).total_seconds())
            latest = max(0.0, (b_end - a_start).total_seconds())
            if latest >= earliest:
                numeric_intervals.append((earliest, latest))

    return merge_numeric_intervals(numeric_intervals)


def classify_against_threshold(intervals, threshold_seconds):
    """
    Classify intervals against a simple SLA threshold '≤ threshold_seconds'.
    """
    if not intervals:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    lo = min(i[0] for i in intervals)
    hi = max(i[1] for i in intervals)
    if hi <= threshold_seconds:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if lo > threshold_seconds:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def classify_against_band(intervals, lower, upper):
    """
    Classify intervals against a two-sided band [lower, upper].
    """
    if not intervals:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    lo = min(i[0] for i in intervals)
    hi = max(i[1] for i in intervals)
    if hi < lower or lo > upper:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    if lo >= lower and hi <= upper:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"

def analyze_case(df_norm: pd.DataFrame, case_id: str):
    df_case = df_norm[df_norm["case"] == case_id]
    results = []

    # SLA rules where events exist
    for rule, (src, tgt) in {

        "S2": ("Investigation_Start", "Containment_Complete"),
        "S3": ("Containment_Complete", "Close_Incident"),

        cls = classify_against_threshold(intervals, SLA_THRESHOLDS[rule])
        results.append({"case": case_id, "type": "SLA", "rule": rule, "pair": (src, tgt), "intervals": intervals, "classification": cls})

    zeta = 2

        results.append({"case": case_id, "type": "PROFILE", "rule": f"profile_zeta{zeta}", "pair": (src, tgt), "intervals": intervals, "classification": cls})

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency

For each **INDETERMINATE** or **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** result, the minimal additional evidence needed for certainty falls into four categories: tighter synchronization, missing-activity timestamps, better duration evidence, or clearer semantics.

- **Synchronization evidence (clock offsets, time zones)**  
  - **T-401/T-402/T-403/T-405/T-409 S1 and A→I profiles**: Much of the ambiguity comes from wide OPS-BER and vendor offset ranges and coarse minute-flooring. A narrower, continuously logged NTP offset bound (e.g., ±5–10 s) per system would collapse many POSSIBLE_VIOLATION outcomes for S1 and A→I into CERTAIN_PASS or CERTAIN_VIOLATION.  
  - **T-409 S1 and profile A→I/I→C**: The very weak [-12 min,+12 min] estimate is the main driver of uncertainty. A proper NTP trace or GPS-disciplined clock for that hour would sharply reduce the feasible start interval.

- **Missing or inferred activity evidence**  
  - **T-404 S1/S2 and A→I/I→C profiles**: A direct `Investigation_Start` timestamp from a well-synchronized system (e.g., HUB) would replace the inferred start, immediately resolving S1/S2 ambiguity. Alternatively, a tighter work-record range (e.g., 21–22 min instead of 18–25 min) would have a similar effect.  
  - **T-407 S1 and A→I profile**: A logged `Alert_Received` or delivery acknowledgment in HUB (with normal ±1.5 s error) would turn the network-latency-based interval into a narrow, high-confidence point interval, making S1 either CERTAIN_PASS or CERTAIN_VIOLATION.  
  - **All S3 INDETERMINATE cases** (no Close_Incident) could be resolved by adding the missing lifecycle completion timestamp.

- **Duration evidence (execution-level traces)**  
  - **T-406 S1/S2 and related profiles**: A more precise measurement of the actual start and end of investigation (e.g., automatic status-change events rather than a user-entered timestamp plus duration guess) would narrow durations from hours-level to seconds-level precision and resolve POSSIBLE_VIOLATION results.  
  - **T-404 work-record precision**: Logging per-step investigation sub-activities (e.g., “triage started/finished”) would let durations be computed directly rather than via a coarse 18–25 min summary interval.

- **Semantic evidence (event meaning, entry delay, causal structure)**  
  - **MOBILE-LON entry delay (T-406)**: If policy mandated that users must enter the timestamp “at the moment investigation actually starts,” and this policy was audited, we could model the MOBILE-LON time as a near-exact occurrence timestamp, removing the ±5 min entry-delay widening.  
  - **Alert semantics in T-407**: An explicit guarantee that `Alert_Sent` and `Alert_Received` differ by at most, say, 30 s in normal operation (backed by monitoring) would allow us to treat `Alert_Sent` as a strong proxy for receipt, drastically tightening S1 and A→I uncertainty.  
  - **DST fold logging in LEGACY-BER**: A simple bit indicating whether a given 02:xx timestamp refers to the first (DST) or second (standard time) fold would remove the need for union-of-interval reasoning for T-405.

In many cases, **one additional high-quality timestamp per case** (e.g., a HUB-side copy of Investigation_Start or Close_Incident) would be enough to convert POSSIBLE_VIOLATION into a CERTAIN_PASS or CERTAIN_VIOLATION, improving enforceability.


## 7. Governance Recommendation

- **Central UTC storage with raw timestamp retention**: Store all operational timestamps as UTC instants in a central, high-trust system (like HUB), while retaining the original raw source fields (local time, time zone, display format, offset diagnostics, and any DST-fold flags) for audit and reconstruction of uncertainty intervals.
- **Clock, offset, and resolution telemetry**: Require each integrated system to continuously log clock-offset estimates (e.g., from NTP), declared display/rounding semantics, and effective resolution; keep these alongside events so conformance engines can compute intervals rather than assume point times.
- **Lifecycle semantics and causal identifiers**: Standardize lifecycle markers (start vs complete vs manual entry) for key activities (`Alert_Received`, `Investigation_Start`, `Containment_Complete`, `Close_Incident`) and log causal links (e.g., message IDs tying `Alert_Sent` to `Alert_Received`) so that precedence and network-delay constraints can be enforced rather than guessed.
- **DST and time-zone controls**: Mandate explicit DST-fold indicators for ambiguous local times, require that all human-facing timestamps show the time zone, and prefer systems that either operate natively in UTC or record both local time and UTC. Legacy systems like LEGACY-BER should be fronted by gateways that add fold metadata.
- **Policy on interval overlap and enforcement**: Adopt a clear policy: treat **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** as automatic SLA breach; treat **CERTAIN_PASS** as compliant; route **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** to expert review queues with interval-aware dashboards; and mark **INDETERMINATE** cases as data-quality incidents requiring remediation (e.g., missing events, absent offset telemetry). This explicitly acknowledges uncertainty instead of silently collapsing it into misleading point estimates.
