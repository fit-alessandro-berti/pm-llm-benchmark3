# 1. Objectives and constraints

**Optimization Objectives (Soft/Preference)**
- **Throughput & SLA Compliance:** Minimize waiting/service time while keeping SLA breach probability low.
- **Workload Fairness:** Distribute new assignments evenly, respecting capacity limits and overtime risk.
- **Skill Development:** Route normal/low-priority cases to align with documented training priorities.
- **Continuity & Handover Minimization:** Preserve case ownership when complexity and handover costs are high.
- **Resilience:** Avoid over-reliance on single specialists; maintain cross-coverage to absorb absences.

**Hard Constraints (Safety/Compliance)**
1. **Batch Limit:** 4 new assignments per resource in this decision cycle.
2. **Priority-Skill Floor:** High-priority cases require skill 2, or skill 1 with explicit supervision.
3. **Security Rule:** Security Review must be skill 3, or skill 1–2 under supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. Skill 0 is prohibited.
4. **Approval Rule:** Final Approval requires skill 2–3. Skill 1 may only prepare, not finalize.
5. **Quality Batching:** Quality Check may be batched only if priority is normal/low and SLA remaining >24h.
6. **Overtime Protection:** Do not assign to high-overtime-risk resources unless SLA is urgent and no safer alternative exists.
7. **Continuity Exception:** High-complexity + high-handover-cost cases retain current owner unless skill is 0/1 and supervision is infeasible.
8. **Training Quota:** 20% of normal/low-priority assignments must support documented training priorities, provided SLA risk remains acceptable.

**Trade-off Philosophy**
No single metric dominates. The policy explicitly accepts higher handover costs when SLA risk or overtime exposure is critical, and accepts slightly lower immediate throughput to enable training and protect fairness. Decisions are logged with explicit trade-off justifications for auditability.

# 2. Assignment policy

**Algorithm: Constraint-First Multi-Criteria Scoring (CF-MCS)**

```
INPUT: Queue Q, Resource Pool R, Skill Matrix S, Constraints C
OUTPUT: Assignment Map A

1. ELIGIBILITY FILTERING (Hard Constraints)
   For each case q in Q:
     Candidates = {r in R | r.skill[q.required_skill] >= min_skill(q.priority, q.type)}
     Apply type-specific rules:
       - If Security: keep only skill=3 OR (skill=1,2 AND supervisor in {R7,R8} available)
       - If Approval: keep only skill=2,3
       - If Quality & batch_eligible: flag for batch grouping
     Remove r if r.overtime_risk=="high" AND q.priority != "urgent"
     Remove r if r.current_assignments_in_batch >= 4

2. SCORING (Soft Objectives)
   For each candidate r in Candidates:
     score = 0
     a) SLA_URGENCY: 0.30 * (1 - min(q.sla_remaining / q.estimated_service_time, 1))
        # Higher score = tighter SLA, favors faster/available resources
     b) SKILL_MATCH: 0.25 * (r.skill[q.required_skill] / 3.0)
        # Rewards competence without forcing specialists on routine work
     c) CONTINUITY_HANDOVER: 0.20 * continuity_bonus(q, r)
        # +1.0 if r == q.current_owner AND q.handover_cost == "high"
        # -0.5 if handover_cost == "high" AND r != current_owner
     d) WORKLOAD_FAIRNESS: 0.15 * (1 - r.open_cases / max_open_cases)
        # Favors less loaded resources
     e) TRAINING_DEV: 0.10 * training_match(q, r)
        # +1.0 if q.priority in {normal, low} AND r.training_priority == q.required_skill
        # +0.5 if q.notes contains "training" AND supervision available
     f) RESILIENCE: 0.00 * (dynamic penalty applied in step 3)
     total_score = sum(a..e)

3. RESILIENCE & BOTTLENECK ADJUSTMENT
   If specialist_count(skill=X) <= 1 AND queue_depth(skill=X) > threshold:
     Apply -0.15 penalty to candidates with skill=X to encourage cross-training or delegation.
   If r.overtime_risk == "medium" AND r.hours_remaining < 4.0:
     Apply -0.10 penalty unless q.priority == "urgent".

4. RANKING & ASSIGNMENT
   Sort candidates by total_score DESC.
   Select top candidate r*.
   Assign q -> r*.
   Increment r*.batch_count.
   If supervision required, attach supervisor pair (r*, supervisor).
   Log assignment_reason, trade_off_accepted, training_flag.

5. TIE-BREAKING & EXCEPTION HANDLING
   Tie-break order:
     1. Lowest current open cases
     2. Highest skill level
     3. Match to training priority
   Exceptions:
     - If no candidate passes hard filters, escalate to manager override with audit flag.
     - If SLA < 2h, override continuity penalty and assign to fastest available competent resource.
     - If batch limit reached, defer case to next cycle or route to secondary team with handover cost logged.
```

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

| Dimension | First-Available Routing | Specialist-Only Routing | Proposed Dynamic Policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expected Throughput** | High initially (fast assignment), but degrades as rework and context-switching increase. | High for specialized steps, but bottlenecks form at Security/Approval, causing queue starvation elsewhere. | Moderate-to-high; stabilizes by balancing load and preventing bottleneck starvation. |
| **SLA Risk** | Elevated for complex/urgent cases due to skill mismatch and longer service times. | Low for specialist tasks, high for non-specialist steps that get delayed in specialist queues. | Controlled; SLA urgency scoring prioritizes tight deadlines while respecting capacity. |
| **Error/Rework Risk** | High; historical evidence shows skill mismatch increases rework by ~3.3h median + higher appeal rates. | Low immediate errors, but rigid routing masks capability gaps and creates single-point failures. | Moderate; supervised trainees and continuity rules reduce rework, while skill floors prevent critical errors. |
| **Fairness of Workload** | Poor; starves some resources while overloading others, ignoring overtime risk. | Poor; specialists become overloaded, others sit idle, exacerbating burnout. | Explicit fairness scoring + 4-assignment cap + overtime protection ensures balanced distribution. |
| **Skill Development** | None; routing ignores training priorities. | None; trainees are excluded from specialist queues entirely. | Structured; 20% normal/low cases align with training priorities with supervision. |
| **Continuity of Ownership** | Low; frequent reassignments increase handover costs and rework loops. | Moderate; specialists may own multiple cases but handovers occur when specialists are unavailable. | High for high-complexity/high-handover cases; continuity explicitly weighted unless SLA/skill constraints override. |
| **Resilience to Absence/Bottlenecks** | Fragile; first-available collapses when capacity drops, causing cascading delays. | Brittle; specialist absence halts entire activity streams. | Robust; cross-coverage scoring, resilience penalties, and supervised delegation maintain flow during absences. |

*Trade-off Summary:* The proposed policy sacrifices peak theoretical throughput and pure specialization efficiency to gain stability, fairness, and developmental capacity. It accepts controlled handover costs when SLA or overtime risk demands it, and accepts slightly longer cycle times for trainees to build long-term resilience.

# 5. Explanation to workers

**Why Your Assignments Look This Way**

We design case assignments to balance three priorities: protecting your well-being, keeping customers within their deadlines, and growing your skills. Here’s how those priorities shape today’s queue:

- **Continuity matters when cases are complex.** Cases like C007 and C016 stayed with their current owners because switching hands on high-complexity work typically adds hours of waiting and increases rework. We only break continuity when deadlines are tight or capacity is at risk.
- **We protect against burnout.** Resources flagged with high overtime risk received no new assignments today unless a deadline was critical and no one else could safely take it. This keeps the team sustainable over time.
- **Training is built into normal work.** Cases C009 and C012 were routed to build specific skills (diagnosis and quality handling) with explicit supervision. This ensures you gain competence without risking SLAs or quality.
- **Specialists aren’t silos.** While Security and Approval require higher skill levels, we avoid overloading those roles. When a specialist queue grows, the system gently shifts eligible work to competent peers to keep the whole process moving.
- **Transparency over opacity.** Every assignment logs why it was made, what trade-off was accepted (e.g., “handover cost accepted to protect SLA”), and what to monitor. If you disagree with an assignment, the reason is visible, and manager override is available with audit tracking.

This isn’t a rigid algorithm dictating work; it’s a shared framework that respects your expertise, protects your time, and keeps service reliable.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

| Indicator | Definition & Calculation | Audit Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Handover Frequency** | Count of `org:resource` changes per case / total activities. Filter by `handover_cost_band`. | Detects excessive context-switching; validates continuity policy. |
| **Reassignment Count** | Max `reassignment_count` per case; distribution across cases. | Flags cases stuck in loops; correlates with rework/appeal rates. |
| **Skill Mismatch** | % of assignments where `resource.skill < required_skill_floor` (without supervision flag). | Ensures compliance with priority/skill constraints; tracks error risk. |
| **Workload Distribution** | Gini coefficient or std dev of `current open cases` per resource over time windows. | Monitors fairness; detects starvation or overloading. |
| **Overtime Risk Exposure** | % of assignments given to resources with `overtime_risk="high"` outside urgent SLA windows. | Validates constraint 4 compliance; tracks burnout precursors. |
| **SLA Misses** | Count of cases where `time:timestamp` of completion > `sla_deadline`. Group by priority/activity. | Measures throughput vs compliance trade-offs. |
| **Rework/Appeal After Assignment** | % of cases entering rework/appeal variant within 7 days of assignment. Correlate with `reassignment_count` and skill mismatch. | Validates historical evidence; measures policy quality impact. |
| **Training Assignment Share** | % of normal/low-priority assignments where `assignment_reason` contains "training" or matches `training_priority`. | Ensures 20% development quota is met. |
| **Specialist Bottleneck Indicators** | Queue depth & average waiting time for activities requiring skill=3. Track via `discover_performance_dfg` & `get_service_time`. | Detects resilience risks; triggers cross-training or delegation. |
| **Deviations from Assignment Policy** | Count of assignments violating hard constraints (C1–C8) or scoring thresholds. Logged via `assignment_reason` audit trail. | Ensures policy adherence; flags manual overrides for review. |

import pm4py

# 4. Service & waiting times for SLA risk scoring

                                             activity_key='concept:name',

                                       activity_key='concept:name')
# Use service_times to estimate SLA breach probability in scoring step

perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log, case_id='case:concept:name',

# Identify high-waiting-time edges (e.g., Security->Approval) to trigger resilience penalties

high_priority = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, attribute_key='case_priority',
