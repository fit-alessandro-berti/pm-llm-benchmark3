**Facts:**
- GPIP-4 is Board-approved and effective during the incident.
- ECD-2 was signed by COO and Incident Commander, not the Chief Risk Officer (CRO).

- CFO Email D is uncontrolled but originates from the control owner for Finance.

**Assumptions:**
- **Authority Hierarchy:** I assume GPIP-4 Clause A7 is binding regarding *integrity* exceptions (Segregation of Duties, Compliance Clearance). An Incident Directive (ECD-2) signed by the COO cannot override a Board Policy requirement for CRO sign-off unless the Board has delegated that specific power (not evidenced).

- **Evidence Weight:** I assume contemporaneous external evidence (supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs) supersedes ERP timestamps during the documented outage period (Evidence Note ON-44).

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Time & Scope Check:** Determine if the act falls within the ECD-2 window and scope. If not, GPIP-4 and Local SOP apply strictly.
2.  **Conflict Resolution:** If ECD-2 applies, check if it conflicts with GPIP-4. If the conflict involves Integrity (SoD, Compliance), GPIP-4 A7 controls (requires CRO). If the conflict involves Operations (PO timing, Supplier Register), ECD-2 controls (per SOP B7).
3.  **Evidence Validation:** Corroborate timestamps against the Outage Note. If ERP is the only record during outage, confidence decreases.
4.  **Classification:** Assign status based on the strictest applicable valid norm. "Justified Exception" is used only if the deviation was necessary for safety/continuity and authorized by a competent authority (even if procedural gaps exist).

2.  **Case P-503 (Justified Exception vs Noncompliant):**
    *   *Interpretation:* In a severe emergency, SoD (A1) is secondary to line restoration. PROC-3 acted as IC Member, not just Procurement.

3.  **Case P-505 (Noncompliant vs Compliant):**
    *   *Interpretation:* ERP Timestamp (18:06) is the legal record of commitment. ECD-2 expired at 18:00.
    *   *Premise Change:* Evidence Note ON-44 is advisory, not normative. The system of record (ERP) dictates compliance status regardless of latency.
    *   *Credibility:* Weak. Audit standards generally allow corroborating evidence (Supplier Ack) when system integrity is compromised (Outage Note). Ignoring the 17:59 email would be evidentially unsound.
4.  **Case P-506 (Compliant vs Noncompliant):**
    *   *Interpretation:* The "spirit" of ECD-2 continued as long as contamination recovery was active. Verbal extension by IC is sufficient under emergency doctrine.
    *   *Premise Change:* GPIP-4 A7 "written exception" requirement is suspended during active contamination events.
    *   *Credibility:* Weak. GPIP-4 A6 explicitly states later approval does not cure missing pre-commitment approval. Allowing verbal extensions creates unmanageable risk post-incident.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed Clause: GPIP-4 Amendment A7.1 (Emergency Exception Protocol)**

> "During a declared Level 1 Operational Incident, the Incident Commander (IC) may issue a Directive (ECD) that temporarily supersedes GPIP-4 Clauses A2 (Finance Thresholds), B1 (PO Timing), and B3 (Supplier Register).
>
> **Limitations:**
> 1.  **Integrity:** GPIP-4 Clause A1 (Segregation of Duties) and A3 (Compliance Clearance for New Suppliers) **cannot** be waived by the IC. Exceptions to A1 or A3 require written ratification by the Chief Risk Officer within 24 hours.
> 2.  **Scope:** ECD authority is limited to purchases directly mitigating the declared incident scope.
> 3.  **Expiry:** ECD authority expires automatically at the declared end time. Extensions require written re-issuance.
> 4.  **Evidence:** Verbal authorizations under ECD must be recorded in a controlled system (not just email/PDF) within 2 hours to be valid."

**Safeguard Against Normalization:**
> "Any ECD lasting longer than 72 hours triggers an automatic review by Internal Audit and the CRO to prevent emergency procedures from becoming permanent operational norms."
