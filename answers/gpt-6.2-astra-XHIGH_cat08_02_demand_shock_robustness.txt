# 1. Stress scenarios

The peak brought 3,300 orders per week versus 2,300 normally, a 43% increase. The change in workload was much less uniform. Using the reported mix, weekly Routine orders stayed near 1,400 (1,403 to 1,386), while Urgent rose from 414 to 957, Complex-panel from 299 to 660, and Repeat-after-failure from 184 to 297. Urgent and Complex-panel orders together generated about 2.3 times their normal weekly demand for medical validation.

| Scenario | Stress to apply |
|---|---|
| S1 — Winter mix shift | Replay the observed peak volume and mix, including multi-assay panels. Routine demand continues even as its share falls from 61% to 42%. |
| S2 — Machine degradation | At peak mix, M2 begins failing more often on a night shift, then goes offline for a shift. Fresh Urgent orders and repeats from the previous shift compete for compatible M1 time. |
| S3 — Clinical cover gap | A panel-heavy Thursday feeds into the known alternate-Friday specialist absence. Also test an evening with reduced medical-validator cover, which affects Urgent orders even when they need no specialist opinion. |
| S4 — Courier compression | Keep daily demand fixed. Delay part of the 10:00 route until the 17:00 arrival. Urgent orders arrive with little time left, alongside Routine tubes and panels that must still be accessioned and checked. |

Also test S2 and S3 together during S1. Restoring a machine just before a gap in clinical cover is a particularly demanding recovery test.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

- **S1 — From intake to sign-off.** Pre-analytical check reaches 96% utilization with a 9.5h median queue. Clearing that queue feeds preparation, then machines at 98% with an 18h median queue. Urgent bypass of Batch wait falls from 74% to 58%, and Batch wait → Assay run alone has a 22h peak median. Giving Urgent orders the next run may save a deadline, but repeated interruptions cost setups and leave panels unfinished. Faster assay output reaches technical validators already at 101% reported utilization. Of the recorded outgoing technical-validation edges, the share going to medical validation or specialist hold rises from 26% to 50%. Technical validation → Medical validation grows from 8h to 24h. An earlier assay finish helps little if the sign-off shift has ended.
- **S2 — From lost machine time to repeat work, then a recovery queue.** Repeat assay accounts for 1,140 of 17,030 recorded outgoing assay edges normally (6.7%) and 1,260 of 8,500 at peak (14.8%). Repeat assay → Assay run stretches from 10h to 26h. A fast first pass on degraded M2 can consume a second slot and displace a fresh order on M1. As preparation outpaces the surviving machine, prepared tubes also approach their stability limits. On restart, releasing all the accumulated runs together transfers the queue to technical validation, then to medical validation for Urgent orders and completed panels.
- **S3 — From clinical holds back into work in progress.** Complex panels are 2.4 times as likely to enter specialist hold. The peak specialist queue is 36h at 87% utilization, and Hold for specialist review → Medical validation takes 48h versus 18h normally. Calendar gaps matter even when average utilization looks tolerable. An evening medical shift can sign eligible cases but cannot clear a hold requiring the absent specialist. When the specialist returns, a group of cleared panels arrives at medical validation, already at a 22h peak median queue. Expediting every component assay in the interim ties up machines for results that still cannot be released.
- **S4 — From a late handoff to a second intake wave.** Register order → Receive sample rises from 5h to 8h and Receive sample → Pre-analytical check from 2h to 9h. Recorded check exits to correction or recollection rise from about 7.0% to 12.3%. Correction has only two specialists and a 14h peak median queue. Request recollection → Receive sample takes 60h at peak, and returned tubes and physician calls consume intake capacity again. Faster registration by itself delivers the same burst to checking. Clearing checking in one push can miss an assay window and deliver the remaining work to an evening shift with limited validation cover.

These DFG percentages describe transitions. A returning tube or repeated assay can contribute more than once. Order-level flattening also hides parallel panel components. Use linked tube, run, shift and order records to locate the component holding up each release and to check the M2/night association by assay and case mix. Use actual end-to-end traces for tail estimates. Adding edge medians would misstate an individual order's elapsed time.

# 3. Robust redesign package

1. **Schedule through release.** At each dispatch, estimate each order's slack as time to its deadline minus the estimated remaining time through all required checks and sign-offs. Estimate remaining time from comparable traces, current queues and staffed calendars. Reserve short Urgent windows at preparation and assay, release unused slots to compatible work, and protect recurring service for aging Routine, panel and repeat work. Within compatible runs, favor a panel's last missing assay when it can catch an available reviewer. The cost of one Urgent override is then visible in the displaced order's slack. This replaces strict I1 and permanent machine dedication under I3. It depends on reliable due times, assay compatibility and visibility of validator cover.
2. **Prevent avoidable loops at receipt.** Use I8 for complete, identity-matched digital orders, with a manual path for exceptions. Use I6 for a brief defect screen at receipt, aiming to resolve or notify the sender about a suspect sample within 2h. Correct recoverable metadata once and request recollection promptly for a confirmed unacceptable tube. Link the replacement to its order and expected route. This saves time before a potentially 60h return loop. It depends on agreed acceptance criteria and reachable senders. A surge of referrals can swamp correction, and false rejects can increase courier and clerical work.
3. **Match batch starts to downstream capacity.** Plan from compatible machine slots, tube stability and staffed technical and medical minutes in the next shift. Keep 12 as the default Routine batch size and impose a latest start based on the oldest order's slack. Stage preparation to that plan. Count outstanding panel components and expected repeats as work. A queue moved into unprepared orders still counts towards the original deadline. Release signed results promptly. I9 would add waiting after scarce validation work was complete, with little evidence that report assembly is the limiting resource.

Judge the package by replaying each scenario against current dispatch using the same arrivals, run eligibility, repeat loops and staff calendars. Set the objective first to minimize individual Urgent breaches of 18h and their penalties, with a provisional Routine gate of p90 at or below 72h. Track Complex-panel and Repeat-after-failure p90 and oldest open age, with no worsening against current dispatch under the same shock. Require the excess backlog to drain after capacity returns. Include open orders and cancellations so slow cases cannot disappear from the comparison. Even the normal Urgent p90 is 19h. Treat a missed gate as a quantified service shortfall requiring escalation, especially in the combined shock.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

The thresholds below are initial operating settings for replay and calibration. Reassess hourly and at each shift handover. Load means remaining work due in the planning window divided by qualified, staffed capacity in that window, using machine or staff minutes as appropriate.

| When to activate | Action and switch-off |
|---|---|
| **S1, deadline pressure.** An Urgent order has zero or negative predicted slack. | Use the next compatible protected slot and confirm a validator can receive it. Escalate a predicted miss immediately. End overrides once exposed orders have a feasible release path. Allow I2 batches up to 24 only when a full compatible Routine batch is already available, machine and validation slots fit, and no protected start or latest start would slip. Revert to 12 or a smaller timed run as soon as any condition fails. |
| **S2, M2 degradation or outage.** Activate on a quality-control failure, unavailability, or a repeat-rate alert above M2's assay-specific control band with a confirmed performance problem. | Hold affected M2 work for quality review. Route eligible time-critical and stability-limited work to M1, reschedule other starts and stagger recovery runs to technical capacity. Resume affected M2 routing after qualified staff clear the instrument and monitored runs meet local quality criteria. End recovery pacing when technical load is below 90% for two reviews. |
| **S3, clinical cover gap.** The roster shows an uncovered review window or forecast medical work exceeds the staffed shift. | Use I5 for work an evening medical validator is qualified to sign. For specialist-dependent panels use I10 selectively when the partner confirms capacity and predicted release including 12h transport, handoff and local result reconciliation beats the local staffed release time. Confirm tube stability, custody and responsibility for sign-off before transfer. Stop new diversions when the local path is faster again or partner acceptance lapses. Stand down I5 when normal cover can clear due work for two reviews. |
| **S4, compressed arrivals.** Route ETAs predict more receipts in the next two hours than intake and checking can clear. | Use I7 to stagger movable pickups within stability and deadline limits. Preserve transport for time-critical tubes and align receiving staff with the revised ETA. If the burst has already arrived, triage at receipt. Restore normal slots once two successive forecasts fit capacity and the oldest untriaged tube is within 2h. |
| **S1/S4, correction backlog.** Oldest correctable order waits over 8h. | Prepare I4 skills before winter, allowing four weeks for training. Loan one trained technical validator at a time only if projected technical load after the loan stays below 90%. Recall at that threshold or when the correction queue falls below 4h. At the reported 101% technical load, keep validators on sign-off and prioritize corrections that unblock time-critical orders. |

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

A simultaneous M2 outage and specialist absence can leave a panel with neither a compatible run nor timely review. A partner facing the same wave may lose its 12h-plus route advantage. The redesign itself can also create delays. An Urgent slot held too long can idle M1 while a Routine batch ages, and triage that overcalls defects creates another round of collections. Audit unused reservations, rejected specimens and the age of deferred work. If no feasible release path exists, give the responsible clinical team the affected orders and revised release estimates while alternatives are still actionable.

# 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Review these five **leading indicators** daily during peaks, refreshing intrashift for dispatch decisions.

| Indicator | Warning or action band |
|---|---|
| Arrivals due versus two-hour intake/check capacity, plus age of oldest untriaged tube | Amber when expected demand exceeds capacity. Act when a tube reaches 2h without triage. Include delayed and recollected tubes in the forecast. |
| Due work versus staffed minutes by station and shift | Amber at 90%, red at 100% or an uncovered required review window. Show correction, machine, technical, medical and specialist capacity separately so a migrating queue is visible. |
| Deadline slack and oldest open order by class | Amber when one more missed compatible run or review window would cause a breach, red at zero slack. Flag a panel or repeat missing its protected service opportunity even without a contractual deadline. |
| Incoming rework burden | Flag check-to-correction/recollection edges approaching the observed peak share of 12%, and assay-to-repeat edges approaching 15%. Drill into machine, assay, route and shift. A quality-control failure warrants immediate review. |
| Panels blocked on one component or sign-off | Flag a panel likely to miss the last staffed review before an absence. For transferred panels, flag any missing custody acknowledgment at the agreed handoff time. |

**Lagging outcomes** are the Urgent share released after 18h and penalties, Routine share after 72h, median and p90 release time by class, cancellations, overall rework, the share experiencing a queue over 12h, and time to clear the post-shock backlog. Compare like scenarios and arrival cohorts. Peak reference values include Urgent p90 of 33h, Routine 120h, Complex-panel 210h, Repeat-after-failure 240h, 11.8% rework and 54% experiencing a queue over 12h. Keep unfinished orders visible alongside released-order outcomes.
