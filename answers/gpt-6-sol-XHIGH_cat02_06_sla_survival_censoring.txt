# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

Time zero is each case's Submit, measured in elapsed hours; the common decision horizon is 72 hours. A recorded Resolve is an internal success. Withdraw, Reject, and Transfer end internal observation or work without a recorded Resolve. An open row gives follow-up only through its age at the extract cutoff. The target population is submitted cases, including open and non-resolved cases.

**Primary estimand: actual internal timely-resolution probability**, (F_R(72)=P(Tleq72, J=R)), where (T) is time to the first internal terminal outcome and (J) its cause. Its complement, (1-F_R(72)), is the probability of *no internal Resolve by 72 hours*. This is the most direct submitted-case SLA success measure if every submission requires a Resolve. It includes cases that withdraw, are rejected, or transfer as non-successes; whether those should instead be exempted is a governance rule that must be specified, not inferred from their status. Aalen–Johansen estimates (F_R) with the other internal terminal outcomes as competing events and open cases right censored at their observed ages, subject to the censoring assumption.

**Active-tail estimand:** (P(T>72)), the probability of still having no *internal terminal disposition* at 72 hours. This measures queue exposure and staffing needs. It differs from no resolution: a case withdrawn at 27 hours has no Resolve by 72 but is no longer active. Transfer counts as an internal terminal disposition here.

**Late-resolution estimand:** (F_R(	au)-F_R(72)) for a stated (	au>72). It measures the chance of a Resolve specifically in the late interval, while retaining withdrawals, rejections, and transfers as competing outcomes. The probability of *eventual* late resolution is not identified at this freeze without a follow-up horizon and assumptions about censored cases. Four recorded Resolves are late: S03, S13, S20, and S28. That count is not a population estimate of eventual late resolution.

The following are observed case contributions, not fitted probabilities:

| Case | No internal Resolve by 72? | Internal terminal-free at 72? | Late internal Resolve? |
|---|---|---|---|
| S03, Resolve at 76 | Yes; breach already determined at 72 | Yes | Yes, event at 76 |
| S09, open at age 92 | Yes; breach already determined at 72 | Yes through 92 | Unknown after right censoring at 92 |
| S06, Withdraw at 27 | Yes, under a Resolve-required KPI | No; competing outcome at 27 | No later internal Resolve |
| S08, Transfer at 68 | Yes for *internal* Resolve; external outcome unknown | No; internal transfer at 68 | No observed later internal Resolve; external late outcome unknown |

In the 32 submitted cases, nine Resolved by 72. Eighteen are *known* not to have Resolved internally by 72, including four late Resolves and four open cases already older than 72. Five open cases are younger than 72, so their 72-hour outcomes are unresolved. Thus the finite-table proportion with no internal Resolve by 72 lies between 18/32 and 23/32 without an independent-censoring assumption; these are identification bounds for this extract, not confidence intervals or a population estimate. Similarly, nine cases are known to have had no internal terminal outcome by 72, and five younger open cases remain uncertain, giving 9/32 to 14/32 for the extract's terminal-free proportion. An estimator can interpolate these unknowns only under explicit assumptions.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Use event time equal to duration_or_age_h, with cause codes 1 = Resolve, 2 = Withdraw, 3 = Reject, 4 = Transfer, and 0 = right censoring. All four terminal types remain in the submitted-case denominator. For an internal cause-specific analysis, a competing event leaves the risk set at its observed time; it is not an ordinary independent censoring event for the actual-world cumulative incidence.

| Observed status | Internal Resolve indicator and cause code | Observation/censoring rule | Sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| resolved | Indicator 1, cause 1 at recorded duration, including after 72 | Fully observed terminal event; a Resolve at 76 is a known 72-hour miss | For a separate all-terminal endpoint, count as any terminal event |
| withdrawn | Indicator 0, cause 2 at recorded duration | Competing internal terminal event, not right censoring for actual-world Resolve incidence | Analyze a disposition-based SLA separately; censor only for an explicitly hypothetical world in which withdrawal were removed |
| rejected | Indicator 0, cause 3 at recorded duration | Competing internal terminal event | Same hypothetical-censoring alternative, with its strong exchangeability assumption |
| transferred | Indicator 0, cause 4 at recorded duration in the primary *internal* analysis | Competing internal terminal event; external follow-up stops | **Scenario T1:** absorbing internal non-success/competing event. **T2:** right censor at Transfer when targeting all-organization resolution, requiring independent external follow-up loss conditional on measured history. **T3:** count Transfer as immediate success, an optimistic proxy for external resolution. A pessimistic all-organization bound treats every Transfer as permanent non-success. |
| open | Indicator 0, cause 0 at age at cutoff | Administrative right censoring for event-time analysis. If age is at least 72, its 72-hour non-resolution and terminal-free state are already known; if younger, those 72-hour outcomes are unknown. | Use stratified or inverse-probability censoring adjustments if cutoff age depends on cohort, start date, or observed history; stress-test outcomes for younger open cases |

T1 and the pessimistic external assumption can produce the same *internal* timely-Resolve indicator, but they answer different questions. T2 is not innocuous: Transfer plausibly depends on difficulty, capacity, and escalation, and the external outcome is unobserved. T3 is deliberately optimistic, not evidence that the external organization resolved the case. S21 and S08 transferred before 72 and can change the all-organization timely-success count under T3; S32 transferred at 96 and had already missed the 72-hour internal target.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Among completed **resolved** cases alone, 4/6 in A and 5/7 in B met 72 hours. Those fractions condition on reaching Resolve and discard three types of competing terminal outcome and nine open cases. They answer neither (F_R(72)) nor the probability that a submitted case breaches. Late cases such as S13 and S28 must survive unresolved long enough to appear among late Resolves; cases that leave via Reject, Withdraw, or Transfer cannot appear in that subset. This is outcome selection, not random missingness.

Open cases are heterogeneous at the horizon. S09 (age 92), S11 (81), S25 (79), and S26 (104) have *already* missed a 72-hour Resolve, despite having no observed terminal outcome. S10 (47), S15 (35), S24 (67), S29 (51), and S31 (23) remain at risk of either timely success or failure. Dropping all open cases loses confirmed breaches and hides uncertainty about the younger five. Calling S09 censored at 72 also throws away observed information: it is censored only at 92 for its later event time.

Withdrawal at 27 in S06 and rejection at 9 in S05 preclude subsequent *internal* Resolve. Treating them as independent censoring asks a counterfactual net-risk question and generally overstates actual timely Resolve incidence. S08 and S21 transferred before 72, so treating transfers as independent censoring also assumes their unobserved external trajectories resemble retained cases after conditioning. Their escalation and transfer paths make that assumption doubtful. Long last-event gaps in open S11 (55 hours), S25 (48), and S26 (72) may signal stalled work and informative future risk; they do not undo those cases' already observed 72-hour misses.

The cohorts also differ in observable composition: A has 15 cases, including three P1 cases; B has 17, including five P1 cases. AutoCheck appears in 2/15 A prefixes and 13/17 B prefixes, so policy uptake changed sharply but was incomplete. B began later, making cutoff ages potentially different; exact start dates are missing. Any crude cohort difference can reflect priority mix, unequal follow-up, selection into automation or escalation, contemporaneous workload, and the policy. The observed timely-Resolve counts are 4/15 and 5/17, but these are incomplete horizon outcomes because two A and three B open cases are younger than 72. The extract cannot establish a policy effect.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Primary:** use a four-cause Aalen–Johansen analysis from Submit, with Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, and Transfer as distinct absorbing *internal* outcomes and open cases censored at age. Report (F_R(72)), (1-F_R(72)), each competing cumulative incidence, and terminal-free survival (S_{	ext{terminal}}(72)), by cohort and priority where risk sets are nonempty. Show the number at risk and censored before 72, and the direct finite-table bounds above. This matches the actual internal workflow and avoids treating alternative exits as independent missing data. With 32 cases and sparse cohort-by-priority cells, emphasize event counts and uncertainty rather than precise rankings or fitted effects.

**Secondary:** estimate cause-specific hazards for Resolve and each exit to diagnose *when* cases leave each state, and compute restricted mean terminal-free time through 72, (int_0^{72} S_{	ext{terminal}}(t),dt), as expected hours of active internal queue exposure. A corresponding (int_0^{72}[1-F_R(t)],dt) is restricted mean hours without internal Resolve, but it keeps competing-outcome cases in the non-resolved state and has a different operational meaning. A Kaplan–Meier curve for time to *any* internal terminal disposition is legitimate; a KM curve for Resolve that censors Withdraw/Reject/Transfer estimates a hypothetical net Resolve risk only under independent censoring, not the primary actual-world probability.

Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards could compare covariate associations with (F_R), but its risk-set construction and hazard ratio are harder to interpret operationally here, and the sample is too small for stable multivariable fitting. Direct cumulative incidence by strata is preferable. At a prespecified 24- or 48-hour landmark, a dynamic model could use only the stage and inactivity observed by that landmark to predict subsequent timely Resolve or remaining terminal-free time. The supplied prefixes have order but no event times, so such landmark features cannot actually be reconstructed from this extract. With timestamped events and a larger sample, a multi-state model could estimate stage-specific transition intensities and time spent in Review, Escalate, and Rework. For these rows it remains a design, not an estimable timed model.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

The prefixes support **order-based** features now: presence and ordering of AutoCheck and Screen; current recorded activity; number of Review visits; Rework count; an Escalate flag; whether Escalate followed Review or AutoCheck; and whole-path variants. Examples include S03's Review → Escalate → Review loop, S07's Review → Rework → Review loop, and B paths with AutoCheck before Screen (S16) or after Screen (S17). Repeated Review or Rework may indicate higher service complexity, but these are hypotheses rather than observed hazard effects. Priority and cohort are known at Submit if recorded then; policy rollout and case-mix variables are baseline context. The final prefix, ever-escalated flag, total Rework count, and last_event_gap_h are **post-Submit** measurements. If inserted as baseline predictors, they reveal future events or require the case to survive long enough to acquire the feature. Escalate is especially exposed to immortal-time bias. It may also be a response to emerging risk and a mediator of the policy, so adjusting for it in a cohort-policy comparison can distort the total association.

At a valid landmark, derive state and elapsed inactivity from events timestamped **no later than that landmark**, then start a new risk set among cases still eligible. Alternatively, represent Escalate, Rework, and Review as time-varying states or covariates in counting-process intervals; use their actual occurrence times and update risk only afterward. The supplied last-event gap is measured at the terminal event or cutoff, so it cannot be used as a historical 24- or 48-hour gap. No stage durations or escalation times can be inferred from prefix order alone.

Proposed state transitions, allowing the observed loops: Submit → Screen or AutoCheck or Escalate; AutoCheck ↔ Screen as observed ordering permits; Screen or AutoCheck → Review or Escalate or a terminal outcome; Review → Rework → Review, Review → Escalate → Review, and Review/Escalate → Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, or Transfer where observed. Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, and internal Transfer are absorbing for the internal model; an external-outcome model would add unobserved post-Transfer states. “Open” is an observation state at cutoff, not a process transition. Exact permitted edges should be validated against timestamped event logs before fitting transition hazards.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

1. **Primary internal workflow:** keep Withdraw, Reject, and Transfer as competing outcomes; censor open cases at their ages. Report timely-Resolve incidence and active-tail survival. This measures actual internal behavior, under conditionally non-informative administrative censoring for open cases younger than 72.
2. **External-resolution interpretation:** censor at Transfer (T2), then compare with immediate-success Transfer (T3) and permanent non-success Transfer (T1/pessimistic). The censor-at-Transfer analysis is a hypothetical missing-outcome model; the immediate-success analysis credits S21 and S08 before 72, while S32 remains a known 72-hour miss. A claimed cohort advantage could reverse if external success differs systematically by cohort or priority.
3. **Alternative disposition policy:** first count Withdraw/Reject as non-success competitors, then report a distinct *any internal terminal disposition by 72* KPI. As a further hypothetical stress test, censor Withdraw/Reject in a net-Resolve KM analysis, explicitly labeling the independence assumption. That estimate may be higher than actual Resolve incidence and can change which cohort seems better if exit rates differ.
4. **Informative cutoff and policy mix:** for five open cases younger than 72, compare an optimistic scenario in which all Resolve by 72 with a pessimistic one in which none do; the observed internal no-Resolve bounds are 18/32–23/32. Stratify by cohort and priority; if start dates become available, model the censoring distribution or use inverse-probability weights with diagnostics. Compare raw cohort curves with priority-standardized curves, and separately describe AutoCheck uptake. Vary assumptions about unmeasured complexity, time-varying workload, and incomplete policy compliance rather than interpreting B versus A as randomized treatment.

The apparent size or direction of a cohort-policy benefit, a priority ranking, and an all-organization SLA success rate could reverse under these choices. The fact that S03 and the four open cases already older than 72 missed the *internal* 72-hour Resolve target cannot reverse. None of these sensitivity analyses converts the observed cohort association into a causal effect.

# 7. Operational interpretation

At Submit, provide a baseline estimate of timely Resolve and of the chance of remaining active at 72, with the estimand named on the dashboard. At a timestamped 24- or 48-hour landmark, update the estimate from the observed stage and elapsed inactivity. Alert on a calibrated probability of missing the remaining window, and pair alerts with concrete actions: review a stalled case, confirm ownership after Escalate, clear a Rework loop, or arrange capacity for a growing active queue. An already aged-over-72 open case should be flagged as a realized SLA miss and a backlog item, not merely a prospective high-risk case.

Governance should publish both submitted-case timely-Resolve incidence and timely terminal-disposition incidence, plus counts of Withdraw, Reject, Transfer, open-below-72, and open-above-72. Keep transferred cases visible until external outcome reconciliation or label them unresolved externally. Set any legitimate exemption rules for withdrawal or rejection before measuring performance. Monitor predicted versus observed 72-hour outcomes in mature cases, with censor-aware calibration and uncertainty for immature cases. Check calibration, false-alert rates, and alert/action coverage separately by priority and cohort; sparse P1 cells require broad intervals and restraint. Reassess after policy changes and workload shifts. A risk score supports triage and workload planning; it is not an estimate of the causal benefit of escalation, automation, or an individual staff action.

# 8. Python or precise pseudocode

The following self-contained Python uses pandas and a direct Aalen–Johansen calculation, so no survival library is required. If PM4Py is installed it builds an untimed event log and calls get_variants; otherwise the order-only pandas variant count is the control-flow fallback. It creates no event timestamps.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd

DATA = """S01 A P2 18 resolved Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Resolve no 0 0
S02 A P3 41 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S03 A P2 76 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Review>Resolve yes 0 0
S04 A P1 29 resolved Submit>Screen>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S05 A P3 9 rejected Submit>Screen>Reject no 0 0
S06 A P2 27 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S07 A P2 54 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Resolve no 1 0
S08 A P1 68 transferred Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0
S09 A P3 92 open Submit>Screen>Review no 0 31
S10 A P2 47 open Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate yes 0 19
S11 A P1 81 open Submit>Screen>Escalate yes 0 55
S12 A P3 15 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Withdraw no 0 0
S13 A P2 110 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Escalate>Resolve yes 1 0
S14 A P3 63 rejected Submit>Screen>Review>Reject no 0 0
S15 A P2 35 open Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review no 0 2
S16 B P2 16 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve no 0 0
S17 B P3 32 resolved Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S18 B P1 12 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S19 B P2 58 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S20 B P3 74 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S21 B P1 26 transferred Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0
S22 B P2 44 withdrawn Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S23 B P3 11 rejected Submit>Screen>Reject no 0 0
S24 B P2 67 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review no 0 4
S25 B P1 79 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate yes 0 48
S26 B P3 104 open Submit>Screen>Review no 0 72
S27 B P2 39 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Rework>Resolve no 1 0
S28 B P2 88 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S29 B P1 51 open Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Review yes 0 6
S30 B P3 70 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S31 B P2 23 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen no 0 3
S32 B P1 96 transferred Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0"""
cols = ("case cohort priority duration_or_age_h observed_status "
        "prefix escalated rework_count last_event_gap_h").split()
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(DATA), sep=r"\s+", names=cols)
df["events"] = df["prefix"].str.split(">")
df["variant"] = df["events"].map(tuple)
df["repeat_review"] = df["events"].map(lambda x: x.count("Review") > 1)
df["autocheck_seen"] = df["events"].map(lambda x: "AutoCheck" in x)
# These are whole-path descriptions, not baseline prediction features.

try:
    import pm4py
    from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
    log = EventLog()
    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": row["case"]})
        for activity in row["events"]:
            trace.append(Event({"concept:name": activity}))
        log.append(trace)
    print("PM4Py distinct control-flow variants:", len(pm4py.get_variants(log)))
except ImportError:
    print("PM4Py unavailable; untimed variant fallback:",
          df["variant"].nunique())

def aj72(group, scenario="competing", all_terminal=False):
    """Return (Resolve CIF or any-terminal CIF, event-free survival) at 72."""
    d = group.copy()
    d["t"] = d["duration_or_age_h"]
    if all_terminal:
        d["cause"] = d["observed_status"].ne("open").astype(int)
    else:
        codes = {"open": 0, "resolved": 1, "withdrawn": 2,
                 "rejected": 3, "transferred": 4}
        d["cause"] = d["observed_status"].map(codes)
        if scenario == "censor":
            d.loc[d["observed_status"].eq("transferred"), "cause"] = 0
        elif scenario == "instant_resolve":
            d.loc[d["observed_status"].eq("transferred"), "cause"] = 1
        elif scenario != "competing":
            raise ValueError(scenario)
    survival, cif = 1.0, 0.0
    event_times = sorted(d.loc[d["t"].le(72) & d["cause"].gt(0), "t"].unique())
    for t in event_times:
        risk = d["t"].ge(t).sum()  # censoring at a tied time follows events
        at_t = d.loc[d["t"].eq(t)]
        all_events = at_t["cause"].gt(0).sum()
        target_events = at_t["cause"].eq(1).sum()
        cif += survival * target_events / risk
        survival *= 1 - all_events / risk
    return cif, survival

timely = (df["observed_status"].eq("resolved")
          & df["duration_or_age_h"].le(72)).sum()
immature_open = (df["observed_status"].eq("open")
                 & df["duration_or_age_h"].lt(72)).sum()
known_no_resolve = len(df) - timely - immature_open
print(f"Submitted={len(df)}; known timely={timely}; "
      f"known no internal Resolve by 72={known_no_resolve}; "
      f"open before 72={immature_open}")
print(f"Extract-level no-Resolve fraction bounds: "
      f"{known_no_resolve}/{len(df)} to "
      f"{known_no_resolve + immature_open}/{len(df)}")

print("Transfer treatment sensitivity at 72:")
for scenario in ("competing", "censor", "instant_resolve"):
    f_resolve, _ = aj72(df, scenario)
    print(f"  {scenario}: Resolve CIF={f_resolve:.3f}, "
          f"no-Resolve probability={1-f_resolve:.3f}")
_, active72 = aj72(df, all_terminal=True)
print(f"Internal terminal-free probability at 72={active72:.3f}")

print("Cohort x priority, primary internal competing-risks analysis:")
for (cohort, priority), group in df.groupby(["cohort", "priority"]):
    f_resolve, _ = aj72(group)
    observed_timely = (group["observed_status"].eq("resolved")
                       & group["duration_or_age_h"].le(72)).sum()
    print(f"  {cohort}/{priority}: n={len(group)}, "
          f"observed timely={observed_timely}, "
          f"Resolve CIF(72)={f_resolve:.3f}")
print("AutoCheck presence by cohort:",
      df.groupby("cohort")["autocheck_seen"].sum().to_dict())
print("Repeated Review cases:",
      df.loc[df["repeat_review"], "case"].tolist())
```

# 9. Limitations and additional data

First obtain exact timestamps for Submit, every activity, terminal events, and extract cutoff. These are necessary to validate elapsed times, distinguish pre- and post-landmark features, estimate stage dwell times, and identify when escalation or inactivity arose. Next obtain external outcomes and their timestamps after Transfer, including whether an external Resolve counts for the SLA. Record case start dates and explicit sampling rules to quantify administrative censoring and any calendar or cohort maturity imbalance. Capture time-varying queue length, staffing, ownership, and workload so stage hazards and censoring assumptions can be assessed.

For policy inference, record assignment mechanism, eligibility, the dates of policy availability, actual rollout compliance, automation decisions, and reasons for escalation, withdrawal, rejection, and transfer. These distinguish intended policy exposure from actual uptake and help address confounding and post-treatment selection. The current 32 cases are sparse, with few competing events per stratum; any estimated curves will be noisy, and the long-term tail is weakly observed. Exact external outcomes and event times could materially change the all-organization SLA conclusion, while the already observed internal misses at 72 remain established.
