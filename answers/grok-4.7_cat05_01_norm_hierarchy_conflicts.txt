## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts relied on.** GPIP-4 is a board policy in force from 2026-01-01 for every Northbridge purchase. The Aachen SOP is a controlled local procedure in force from 2025-03-01 for maintenance and production-support purchases at Aachen. ECD-2 is an incident directive signed by the COO and the Incident Commander, in force only from 2026-07-14 09:00 through 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST, and only for “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.” The CFO email of 2026-07-15 07:20 is an ordinary email outside the controlled policy repository. The Chief Risk Officer issued no A7 writing and did not later ratify ECD-2. ON-44 makes ERP timestamps evidential only: during the 2026-07-14 recovery they can sit up to 12 minutes later than the act, and on P-505 the ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast. A later approval can mitigate harm. GPIP-4 says it does not, by itself, cure a missing pre-commitment approval.

**Assumptions, stated so they can be challenged.**

- Authority is issue-specific. Later time, global reach, and strong wording are tie-breakers only inside an issue the issuer actually controls.
- Harmonized primary reading: ECD-2 and SOP clause B7 can displace ordinary local timing and supplier-register steps inside ECD-2’s window and subject scope. They leave GPIP segregation, the pre-commitment Compliance rule, and the “no automatic cure” rule in place. The CFO can interpret Finance controls and, by the email’s “operational approvals” sentence, the local Plant and Finance sign-offs. The email cannot waive segregation or Compliance. Above EUR 150,000 the email’s own “as well” restores the need for the baseline finance control and adds Legal clearance.
- Aachen implementation mapping, adopted for the primary labels and flagged as the main lever: Plant_Approve is the observable budget-owner control, and Finance_Approve is the observable Finance Controller control. From 2026-07-15 07:20 until 2026-07-18 18:00, a joint Incident Command authorization evidences those two observable controls when the purchase is inside ECD-2 scope, the amount is at most EUR 150,000, the two authorizers are distinct from the requester and the committer, and any required Compliance clearance already exists before commitment. Incident Command is not retitled as a budget owner. The charter never gives that title. The email is used only as a time-bounded way of evidencing controls the CFO and the local procedure already operate.
- “Unapproved supplier” is not treated as “new supplier.” The new-supplier limb of the Compliance rule is applied only where the file says the supplier is new.
- P-502’s undated clock times are treated as 2026-07-14. That assumption changes which finance theory applies. It does not change the label, because Compliance is late on any date inside the incident.
- ECD-2 subject scope, primary test: a sterilization-line part required for containment within 24 hours. Operations’ broader “backup line is critical” view and a self-applied label (“critical contingency,” “site continuity”) do not expand the test. Stock expected to last three days fails it. The SOP’s own emergency definition remains separate: imminent safety harm, or a production stoppage expected within six hours. That definition governs SOP permissions. It does not define ECD-2.
- The cafeteria repair is treated as Aachen “maintenance,” so the SOP applies. GPIP applies either way.
- Role conflict is taken as present when the case states it, and is not invented from silence.
- The binding act is the supplier-facing commitment. An ERP stamp yields to a call recording, a supplier acknowledgment, or a signed PDF when those sources exist and bear on the same act. A delayed ingest is a logging fact.
- The four-hour recording period runs from the verbal authorization. A signed record created inside that period satisfies it even if ERP shows the document later.
- Built-in permissions of the same instrument that governs the act are scored as compliance. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is reserved for a facial departure that stands only because a cross-instrument overlay is valid. INDETERMINATE is used when a predicate that selects between two different labels is genuinely unresolved. If the default breach is already visible and the exception is unproven, the label is NONCOMPLIANT.
- Burden: the party relying on an exception must show its predicates at the time of commitment. Absence of a required approval, where the file specifies the approvals that did occur, is treated as absence, not as a neutral gap. Silence on a conflict is not proof of a conflict.

**Method.** Reconstruct commitment time first. Decide which instruments are in force at that minute. Test subject scope and the SOP emergency definition. Apply GPIP and the SOP. Allow an overlay only inside the issuer’s authority, scope, cap, and clock. Separate a logging delay from a late act. Apply the no-cure rule to any approval that the governing text required before commitment. Then classify. Recommendations in Sections 6 and 7 do not rewrite these labels.

**Severity.** Critical means self-approval combined with a missing pre-commitment integrity clearance at high value and weak necessity. High means a failed pre-commitment integrity rule on a new supplier, a large value, or finance above the email cap, with some independent controls still intact. Moderate means an unjustified breach with partial compliance and contained value or a real operational reason. Low means residual formal risk where the operative controls are accepted. None means no material deviation. Conditional means severity depends on an unresolved predicate. Confidence is qualitative because several labels rest on a stated lever rather than on a single score.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**Global lever.** If the CFO email can evidence the Finance Controller prong only, and cannot evidence Plant_Approve or the budget-owner limb, the primary mapping is false. P-507 then becomes NONCOMPLIANT, because no budget-owner approval precedes commitment. P-505’s in-window branch becomes NONCOMPLIANT for the same reason. P-502, P-503, P-506, and P-508 stay NONCOMPLIANT. That lever is credible because the email sits outside the policy repository, the CFO’s documented authority is finance interpretation, and the charter does not make Incident Command a budget owner. It is not adopted as the primary view because it gives the email’s phrase “all operational approvals” no work to do on the plant control the local procedure actually uses, and because Aachen’s observable pair for the two A2 limbs is Plant plus Finance.

**P-501, COMPLIANT to NONCOMPLIANT.** The alternative reads R9 as an exclusive authority rule: only the Plant Manager, or an actively delegated Shift Lead when the manager is absent, may bind the site in an emergency. Here the manager was reachable and did not delegate, and SHIFT-1 committed. The later Plant_Approve would then be mitigation under the spirit of R4, not performance of the emergency authority. This reading is credible because the delegation sentence otherwise has little work to do. It is weaker than the primary view because the sentence is written as a permission, the SOP records Plant_Approve as a separate event that did occur, and EUR 38,000 never enters the GPIP pre-commitment rule.

**P-502, NONCOMPLIANT to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** The alternative reads “supplier-onboarding requirements” in ECD-2 as including the new-supplier Compliance clearance, so a same-day clearance at 18:30 would be an allowed deferred onboarding step. That reading is textually available because a new supplier must be onboarded, and the filters were a same-day affected-line need with a timely dual authorization. It is weak. GPIP says non-tender controls survive an emergency unless the CRO writes an exception. ECD-2 never mentions Compliance, the EUR 100,000 rule, or the CRO. The organization has not previously treated an incident directive as a substitute for new-supplier Compliance. The no-cure rule would still have to be set aside. Even a later email date would not supply an A7 writing.

**P-504, NONCOMPLIANT to COMPLIANT.** The alternative holds that “maintenance and production-support” means production-related maintenance only, so a cafeteria freezer is outside the SOP. GPIP then has no EUR 50,000 duty, no above-EUR 100,000 duty, and no proven new-supplier duty. A solo commitment of EUR 24,000 with the PO on the next business day would not break a GPIP rule that actually applies. This is the strongest flip in the set, because the scope sentence is genuinely broad and the purpose facts were careful to deny a production effect. It stays alternative because a site SOP whose scope text says “maintenance … at the Aachen site” still covers repair of site equipment, and the emergency label was used without an emergency definition.

**P-505, INDETERMINATE to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, or to NONCOMPLIANT.** Favorable flip: the 17:59:31 email is the supplier’s acceptance, the fast-clock possibility aligns ERP 18:06 with the 17:57 call, the 17:50 authorization was recorded inside four hours, and the authorizers are not the caller. R14 and R11 then do the same work they do in P-507, at a lower amount and on an approved supplier. Adverse flip: the email is only a pre-contract acknowledgment, the clock was accurate, and commitment is 18:06. The window and the email are then closed, and Finance at 19:00 cannot cure R2. Both flips are reasonable. The adverse one is slightly weaker because it treats the ERP stamp as conclusive despite a stated clock defect and a cluster of external events before 18:00. The favorable one is slightly weaker because the acknowledgment’s legal character and the four-hour record are simply not in the file. That is why the primary label stays unresolved.

**P-507, JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION to NONCOMPLIANT.** Two independent premises each flip the label. First, the global lever above: no Plant or Finance event, and the email cannot evidence the budget-owner limb. Second, the seals are not shown to be sterilization-line parts, so a critic applying the exception-proponent’s burden holds R13 unproven. R11 and R14 then drop away. What remains is an unapproved supplier, commitment before the PO, no finance event, and only a timely Compliance clearance plus an undocumented asset story. The second premise is credible because this file, unlike P-501, P-502, P-505, and P-508, never names the line. It is not adopted because an eight-hour “critical replacement” during this incident fits the 24-hour test more readily than the cafeteria or the three-day spare pumps, and because the buyers behaved as if R3 applied and met it first.

**P-508 does not flip on a small-cap argument.** Treating EUR 155,000 as close enough to EUR 150,000, or treating Legal clearance as a superior substitute for finance, would move it to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. That alternative is weak. The email states a cap, and above the cap it adds Legal “as well,” which is language of addition. Finance never happened at all. Necessity is at its strongest in this case and still does not move a numeric finance gate the CFO himself restated.

P-503 does not flip by adopting Operations’ broad scope alone. Backup-line coverage would repair R13 and might repair the sequence deviation. It would leave PROC-3’s self-approval and the total absence of Compliance on a EUR 140,000 purchase. A flip to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION needs those two integrity rules to be waived as well. That combined alternative is weak for the same reason P-502’s Compliance waiver is weak, with an added segregation problem the other files were careful to avoid.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Policy breach.** A required control was missing, or was completed only after commitment, and no valid exception covers it. P-502 is a Compliance-timing breach on a new supplier, plus a pre-commitment finance and budget-owner breach on the 14 July assumption. P-503 is a segregation breach, a total Compliance miss, and a missing finance and plant pair, aggravated by scope. P-504 is an approved-supplier and PO-sequence breach outside every emergency permission. P-506 is a budget-owner timing breach and an unjustified PO sequence, plus an invalid extension claim. P-508 is a finance and plant breach above the email cap. Later signatures in P-502 and P-506 mitigate harm and do not close these findings.

**Logging defect.** The act happened on time and the system showed it late. P-507’s authorization exists on a signed PDF at 12:40; ERP at 14:30 is ingest delay during a document outage. P-501’s possible 12-minute ERP lag, and P-505’s possible fast clock, are logging or clock defects to be corrected in the file. They are not themselves breaches.

**Late evidence of a timely act.** P-502’s telephone recording and supplier acknowledgment rightly fix commitment at 10:15. That corroboration makes the later Compliance clearance look later, not earlier. P-507’s PDF is timely evidence of a timely authorization. P-505’s call and email may be timely evidence of a timely commitment, and they may instead be evidence of negotiations before a later commitment. Their existence defeats blind reliance on 18:06. It does not yet identify the legal act.

**Inability to prove compliance.** P-505 cannot yet be closed, because the acknowledgment’s content, the clock offset, the authorization record, and the caller’s identity are missing, and those facts select the label. P-504’s form time and the novelty of its supplier are unproven; the SOP breaches do not depend on them. P-506’s missing extension document is different: the exception was affirmatively unproven, while the default timing breach is visible, so the case is a breach rather than an open file. P-501’s missing approver names and missing supplier tape reduce confidence and do not open a breach on the primary reading.

**Case-level actions.**

- P-501: keep as a conforming exemplar. Add the approver names and an outage reconstruction note. No sanction.
- P-502: record an integrity finding for pre-commitment clearance on a new supplier. Do not backdate the 18:30 clearance. Complete due diligence as mitigation, hold further releases to that supplier until the file is accepted, and have Legal review voidability of the 10:15 commitment.
- P-503: payment hold if still open. Separate investigation of PROC-3’s combined request, authorization, and commitment. Remove the “critical contingency” label as a scope basis. Any continued need for the pumps goes through ordinary approvals. A later CRO note may address consequences only.
- P-504: reclass the emergency record as invalid. Regularize or cancel under ordinary facilities buying. Do not pay it as incident spend. Corrective action for the solo out-of-scope commitment.
- P-505: preserve the recording, the raw acknowledgment, and an NTP or clock audit before anyone closes the file as compliant or noncompliant. If the reconstructed commitment is after 18:00, open an R2 finding and treat 19:00 finance as mitigation. If it is before 18:00 and the four-hour record and identities are clean, reclass to the same family as P-507.
- P-506: reject the verbal-extension statement in the record. Keep the finance sign-off as the one pre-commitment control that worked. Record the late plant sign-off as mitigation. Coach the buyer that an overnight oral extension does not move the clock.
- P-507: file the 12:40 PDF as the official authorization and annotate 14:30 as ingest time. Ask Finance to record, prospectively for the archive, that the email was the finance authority relied on. Do not invent a pre-commitment finance timestamp. Revisit the label if the seals are shown to be outside sterilization containment or if an authorizer was the committer.
- P-508: escalate to the CFO and the CRO. Payment hold until a finance decision and a supplier-qualification decision exist. Keep the timely Compliance and Legal records; they are real and they are not substitutes. The operational necessity should drive speed of the consequence decision, not deletion of the finding.

**Monitoring.** Review every commitment in the directive window and for 48 hours after expiry, which is the pattern P-506 exposes. Match requester and committer identities to every authorizer. Hard-stop amounts at EUR 25,000, 50,000, 100,000, and 150,000. Put the directive’s end time in the system so an oral extension cannot be selected as a reason code. Sample out-of-scope use, including facilities and any buy whose own stock cover exceeds 24 hours. Alert when a verbal authorization has no record at four hours. After any ERP or clock incident, require an external corroboration field before a commitment timestamp is treated as proved. Report emergency-path counts by person and cost center to the executive committee so a contamination event does not become a standing bypass.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

Replace the open seam among A7, B7, ECD-2, and the CFO email with one controlled clause:

**Emergency overlay rule.** A Chief Risk Officer writing under A7 is the only instrument that may suspend segregation, the pre-commitment independent budget-owner approval, the pre-commitment Finance Controller approval, or the pre-commitment Compliance clearance. The CRO may appoint a named deputy in advance for periods of unavailability. Unavailability does not move that pen to the COO, the Incident Commander, or the CFO.

A directive signed by the COO and the Incident Commander may, inside a stated clock and a stated asset list, allow commitment before the PO and allow a supplier that is not yet on the register. It may resequence ordinary local workflow evidence. It may not suspend segregation, Compliance, or the EUR 50,000 pre-commitment rule. “Critical,” “contingency,” and “site continuity” do not add assets. Parts with stock cover beyond the stated horizon, and purchases with no effect on the named containment assets, sit outside the directive.

The CFO may issue a controlled finance interpretation, effective only when stored in the policy repository, stating that joint authorization by two Incident Command members evidences the Finance Controller approval and the local Plant approval up to a numeric cap, for in-scope commitments inside the directive clock. Above that cap, the Finance Controller approval remains mandatory before commitment, and any added Legal clearance is additional. The two authorizers must be distinct from each other and from both the requester and the person who commits. A requester or committer cannot complete the pair by signing it.

A new supplier, and any purchase above EUR 100,000, still needs Compliance clearance before commitment, including when the register rule has been lifted. Verbal authorization counts at the moment it is given if a signed record exists within four hours. Call recordings, supplier acknowledgments, and signed PDFs outrank an ERP stamp affected by an outage, a queue, or clock drift. A later approval is logged as mitigation and is not backdated into a cure. The directive ends at its stated minute. An oral extension is void. Continuation requires a new writing by the role that owns the control being extended.

**Normalization safeguard.** Every emergency-path commitment is reviewed within two business days against the asset list, the cap, the identity match, and the four-hour record. Use outside the list is an integrity incident even if the goods were useful. A second emergency commitment for the same function inside 30 days requires CRO countersignature. After expiry, a cooling-off period blocks re-labeling of ordinary demand as emergency. Emergency-path value and repeat users are reported to the board with the incident close-out. The directive cannot be renewed by Incident Command alone.
