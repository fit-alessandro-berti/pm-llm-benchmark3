# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network

A handover network records directed succession between resources in the same case. Mira’s out-degree centrality of 0.82, in-degree of 0.51, and betweenness of 0.77 indicate that many exception-related paths enter and leave through her.

**What it implies**

- She is a major routing point between `Bot_Rules`, adjusters, and specialists.
- The edges `Bot_Rules  Mira`, `Mira  Omar/Priya`, and specialist returns to Mira are consistent with exception triage and specialist scheduling.
- Her broad outgoing neighborhood suggests cross-team reach rather than ownership of only North or South.
- The absence-period queue growth supports the operational importance of these connections.

**What it does not imply**

- It does not establish that she performs substantial claims work or has formal authority.
- High centrality may reflect many brief assignment, clarification, and reopen events.
- It does not distinguish helpful coordination from avoidable centralized routing.
- Directly-follows edges are not necessarily explicit transfers: event granularity, batching, and missing events can create apparent handovers.

## Working-together network

This network measures whether resources participate in the same cases, generally without direction or close temporal requirements. It emphasizes sustained case overlap more than brokerage.

**What it implies**

- Mira is not embedded in a stable case-working cluster comparable with Alice–Carla–Elise or Bruno–Diego–Farid.
- Her low weighted degree is consistent with episodic interventions across many cases rather than continuing case ownership.
- The low pairwise rates can coexist with 905 distinct cases because her contacts are dispersed among several resources and concentrated in the 22% exception subset.

**What it does not imply**

- It does not show that Mira is unimportant or isolated.
- It can understate short routing interventions and asymmetric coordination.
- Percentages need a clearly documented denominator. A rate measured over all cases will make exception-only relationships look weak.
- It cannot by itself distinguish a queue cleaner from a highly consequential broker.

Thus, the apparent contradiction is largely a difference in network semantics: handover and subcontracting reward **position in sequences**, whereas working-together rewards **repeated co-membership and team embeddedness**. Mira can be a temporal bridge without being a durable teammate.

## Subcontracting network

An \(A \rightarrow B \rightarrow A\) pattern within two later events identifies work that leaves a resource and soon returns. It can represent delegation, consultation, review, rework, or orchestration—not necessarily contractual subcontracting.

**What it implies**

- Mira’s subcontracting betweenness of 0.69 and the recurring loops with Omar and Priya fit a coordinator who frames a problem, obtains specialist input, and resumes routing or reopens the case.
- The patterns are stronger evidence of orchestration than simple co-occurrence.
- Similar loops involving team leads and adjusters show that such patterns can also arise from review and approval.

**What it does not imply**

- It does not prove managerial authority over specialists.
- `Reopen Claim` or `Assign Specialist` after a review may be administrative logging rather than substantive return of ownership.
- The chosen \(n=2\) horizon favors short loops and may miss longer consultations or overstate fragments created by sparse logging.

## Performance/time statistics

Mira’s median event service time is only 4.2 minutes, but her case-cycle contribution has high variance. Exception cases accumulated during her absence, while ordinary low-value cases did not deteriorate.

**What it implies**

- Her own task execution is unlikely to be the sole source of long delays.
- Queue availability, routing decisions, and downstream specialist capacity may matter more than event duration.
- The absence intervention is consistent with a capacity-sensitive exception function and possible key-person dependency.
- Omar, Priya, `Vendor_Verify`, and `Vendor_Pay` have substantial service or waiting-time components, so delays should not all be attributed to Mira.

**What it does not imply**

- The absence period is not a randomized causal experiment. Case mix, staffing, weekdays, and specialist/vendor availability may have changed.
- A short recorded service time does not include pre-event queue waiting or post-routing delay.
- High cycle-time variance may be intrinsic to the difficult cases selected for Mira.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity

Activity-based similarity and role discovery group resources by similar activity profiles. Mira’s mix—missing-information requests, reopenings, complaints, and specialist assignment—should make her dissimilar to adjusters, specialists, vendors, bots, and conventional team leads.

**What it may imply**

- A distinct exception-coordination role may emerge.
- Partial similarity with Alice and Bruno on reopening or routing could indicate overlapping escalation responsibilities.
- Similarity with junior adjusters on missing-information work could reveal ambiguous role boundaries.

**What it does not imply**

- Clusters do not prove formal organizational roles or authority.
- Rare activities, generic labels, and volume differences can dominate similarity.
- `Assign Specialist` was listed in Mira’s profile but not among the numbered activities; inconsistent activity naming could distort discovery.
- Bots and vendors should generally be analyzed separately or explicitly tagged, because their activity profiles and timestamps have different meanings from human work.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

**Mechanism:** Bot-generated exceptions are routed to a designated desk. Mira clarifies missing information, selects a specialist or adjuster, and reactivates cases after specialist advice.

**Supporting evidence**

- `Bot_Rules  Mira` is the largest human exception-routing edge.
- She touches 64% of reopenings and 71% of complaints.
- Her activities align with exception administration.
- She routes work to all four adjusters and both specialists.
- Exception queues grew during her absence.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- The process owner denies team-lead status.
- Only 22% of claims involve her, so this is not general process leadership.
- Some routing could be routine logging or correction of automated decisions.
- The data do not document formal decision rights.

**Predictions**

- Exception-filtered logs will show much greater Mira centrality than ordinary-case logs.
- Her events will occur near exception detection, specialist assignment, reopen, and complaint states.
- Coverage by a trained backup should preserve exception cycle times during future absences.

## Hypothesis B: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism:** Exception knowledge and routing authority are concentrated in Mira. Cases wait for her availability even though her recorded handling events are short.

**Supporting evidence**

- Very high handover and subcontracting betweenness.
- Exception queues and tail cycle times worsened during her six-day absence.
- Adjusters describe her as knowing where difficult cases should go.
- High exception share and cross-team routing imply specialized tacit knowledge.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- Her event service time is short, and no direct pre-Mira queue times are supplied.
- Downstream specialists and vendors are slower and may be the binding capacity constraint.
- Accumulation during absence may show missing coverage rather than excessive centralization in normal operations.
- Ordinary cases were unaffected.

**Predictions**

- Waiting time immediately before Mira events will rise during high-load or absence periods.
- Arrival-to-service ratios will approach or exceed capacity for exception work.
- A small number of variants will disproportionately depend on her.
- Adding a trained backup or documented routing rules will reduce tail times without materially increasing specialist demand—unless Mira is also filtering unnecessary referrals.

## Hypothesis C: Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker

**Mechanism:** Mira links automation, North and South teams, medical and legal specialists, and complaint/reopen processes. She bridges organizational domains without belonging strongly to any one delivery team.

**Supporting evidence**

- Broad incoming and outgoing connections plus high betweenness.
- Low working-together embeddedness despite many directed handovers.
- Interactions span bots, both adjuster teams, and specialist functions.
- Specialist results frequently return through her.
- Adjusters’ comments describe routing knowledge rather than hierarchical control.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- Some important routes bypass her: team leads assign routine work and send approvals to `Vendor_Pay`.
- Her low team-lead co-occurrence may reflect logging conventions rather than genuine boundary separation.
- Similar network structure could arise from a centralized system account or clerical role.

**Predictions**

- Community detection will place North and South in distinct clusters, with Mira bridging them or lying outside both.
- Removing Mira from the graph will disproportionately reduce cross-community connectivity for exception paths.
- Her involvement will correlate more with cross-functional transitions than with claim volume alone.

## Hypothesis D: Quality gatekeeper and automation monitor

**Mechanism:** Mira reviews ambiguous outputs from `Bot_Rules`, catches routing or fraud-screen problems, and sends corrected information back to the automated process.

**Supporting evidence**

- `Bot_Rules  Mira` occurs 920 times.
- The `Bot_Rules  Mira  Bot_Rules` loop appears 62 times.
- Nearly half of her cases carry the exception flag.
- Her work includes clarification and reopening.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- The loop frequency is small relative to bot volume and may represent normal status updates.
- No decision-quality, override, false-positive, or error labels are given.
- Her other activity with complaints and specialists is broader than automation monitoring.

**Predictions**

- Mira-touched bot decisions will have high override or rule-correction rates.
- Particular rule versions, case types, channels, or regions will generate disproportionate referrals.
- Better rule calibration should reduce bot–Mira loops without increasing later reopenings or complaints.

## Hypothesis E: Informal discretionary decision-maker

**Mechanism:** Although not formally a team lead, Mira effectively determines which specialists receive difficult cases and when cases are reopened, creating de facto influence.

**Supporting evidence**

- Specialist selection and reopening are concentrated around her.
- Compliance specifically identifies combined detection and routing discretion.
- Her network position gives her influence over access to scarce expertise.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- Routing may follow documented rules, with Mira merely recording the outcome.
- Specialists return work to her because of workflow design, not authority.
- There is no evidence of favoritism, unauthorized decisions, or harmful outcomes.

**Predictions**

- Referral choices will vary after controlling for case type, region, amount, priority, and exception reason if discretion is substantial.
- Audit logs may reveal undocumented overrides.
- Different coordinators handling matched cases may choose different specialists or escalation paths.

# 3. Risk assessment

| Risk | Assessment | Calibrated interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bottleneck risk** | Medium–high for exceptions; low for ordinary claims | The absence evidence identifies vulnerability in the exception stream. The bottleneck may be access to Mira, poor queue ownership, or scarce downstream specialists rather than her 4.2-minute tasks. |
| **Coordination risk** | High if the function is removed without replacement | Mira appears to prevent misrouting and specialist overload. Decentralizing routing without rules, ownership, and training could increase complaints, duplicate referrals, or formal escalations. |
| **Key-person risk** | High | Cross-team routing knowledge appears concentrated in one person, and no backup is visible. Dependence may concern tacit knowledge and permissions as much as capacity. |
| **Compliance/control risk** | Medium, requiring testing | Combining problem recognition, specialist selection, and reopening may weaken segregation of duties. The log does not demonstrate wrongdoing. The concern is insufficiently transparent or reviewable discretion. |
| **Workload fairness risk** | Medium | Mira may receive a disproportionate share of difficult and emotionally demanding complaints. Conversely, her referrals may unevenly burden Omar, Priya, or certain adjusters. Raw counts are inadequate because case complexity differs. |
| **Data-quality risk** | Medium–high | Short administrative events, mixed human/bot/vendor semantics, lifecycle handling, and inconsistent activity naming may inflate or distort network edges. Vendor timestamps may represent asynchronous waiting rather than active service. Shared accounts or missing completion events would further bias conclusions. |

Additional control concerns include whether Mira can both initiate and finalize a reopen, whether specialist assignment criteria are documented, and whether team leads review high-value or sensitive referrals. These are governance questions, not evidence of misconduct.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

1. **Prepare and validate the log**
   - Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with explicit case, activity, timestamp, and resource keys.
   - Check timestamp order, duplicate events, lifecycle transitions, time zones, missing resources, shared accounts, and activity-name consistency.
   - Separate human, bot, and vendor resource classes; compare start–complete durations where lifecycle data permit.

2. **Stratified handover analysis**
   - Run `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` separately for exception/non-exception cases, case types, priorities, amount bands, regions, and channels.
   - Compare Mira’s centrality and edge weights across strata and time windows.
   - Examine activity-to-resource transitions around `Bot_Rules  Mira`, not only resource succession.
   - Remove bot and vendor nodes in a sensitivity analysis, while retaining the full graph as the operational view.
   - Visualize with `pm4py.view_sna` or `pm4py.save_vis_sna`.

3. **Working-together analysis**
   - Use `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` on all cases and on exception-only cases.
   - Recalculate co-case rates with alternative denominators: all claims, Mira-touched claims, and cases eligible for each specialist.
   - Compare stable team communities with exception communities. This tests whether Mira remains peripheral after correcting for opportunity to co-occur.

4. **Subcontracting sensitivity**
   - Apply `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with several horizons, such as \(n=1,2,3,5\).
   - Inspect whether Mira–specialist–Mira loops are true consultation cycles, repeated administrative logging, or rework.
   - Compare successful and reopened/complaint variants and measure time inside each loop.

5. **Role and activity-profile analysis**
   - Use `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`.
   - Run once for humans only and once including bots/vendors as separately labeled resource types.
   - Test whether Mira forms a distinct role, clusters with team leads, or overlaps with junior adjusters.
   - Inspect activity vectors rather than treating cluster labels as formal roles.

6. **Filtering and variant analysis**
   - Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` for `exception_flag`, activities such as `Reopen Claim`, `Escalate Complaint`, and `Assign Specialist`, and resource-specific traces.
   - Use `pm4py.filter_variants` to compare dominant exception paths with and without Mira.
   - Contrast the six-day absence with matched control periods, controlling for weekday, arrivals, case mix, and specialist/vendor availability.
   - Analyze whether cases bypassing Mira have different reopen, complaint, or cycle-time outcomes after stratification.

7. **Service and waiting-time analysis**
   - Use `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`.
   - Decompose time into waiting before Mira, Mira service, waiting after assignment, specialist service, and vendor waiting.
   - Report medians and tail percentiles because the operational signal is concentrated at the 90th percentile.
   - Distinguish active vendor processing from elapsed external turnaround.

8. **Counterfactual and capacity-oriented tests**
   - Model daily exception arrivals, completions, queue size, and Mira availability.
   - Use regression, matching, or interrupted-time-series methods around the absence, with appropriate caution about causality.
   - Simulate backup coverage and rule-based routing. Include downstream capacity constraints so that faster referrals are not incorrectly assumed to improve total flow.

9. **Compliance and fairness analysis**
   - Model specialist choice and reopen decisions using case attributes.
   - Look for unexplained variation by region, channel, amount, and case type, while avoiding causal or discriminatory conclusions from association alone.
   - Test approval and segregation-of-duties rules for sensitive cases and document manual overrides of `Bot_Rules`.

# 5. Recommendation

## Change immediately

- Establish trained backup coverage for the exception queue, with shared access, a rota, and explicit ownership during absence.
- Document the routing matrix, specialist eligibility criteria, reopening authority, and escalation thresholds currently used by Mira.
- Add auditable reason codes for manual overrides, specialist selection, reopenings, and complaint escalation.
- Introduce proportionate dual review for high-value, legally sensitive, or unusual discretionary decisions—not for every routine routing event.
- Create queue alerts for exception age and tail delay, including downstream specialist and vendor queues.

These steps preserve the coordination function rather than simply removing its current holder.

## Monitor

- Exception arrival rate, queue age, pre- and post-Mira waiting time, 90th-percentile cycle time, reopenings, complaints, and specialist utilization.
- Routing concentration and outcome differences across Mira, backups, team leads, and automated routes.
- `Bot_Rules` override rates and whether specific rule versions or case segments create avoidable referrals.
- Workload adjusted for case complexity, including complaint burden and referrals to Omar and Priya.
- Network measures over time, interpreted alongside volumes and case mix rather than as targets.

## Do not change until more evidence is available

- Do not remove Mira, decentralize all routing, or fully automate specialist selection solely because centrality is high.
- Do not promote her to formal team lead solely from network position; high brokerage is not equivalent to supervisory authority.
- Do not infer misconduct or impose broad segregation controls without testing whether her decisions are discretionary and consequential.
- Do not optimize away her short routing events before determining whether they prevent inappropriate specialist referrals, complaints, or rework.

A controlled pilot is preferable: train one backup, codify part of the routing logic, and compare matched exception cohorts for speed, quality, and specialist load.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to perform a specialized exception-coordination function, but the log does not establish whether this is a formal role, an informal practice, or partly a logging artifact. Her high handover and subcontracting centrality means difficult cases often pass through her before and after specialist work. Her low working-together score is not contradictory: she makes brief interventions across many cases rather than remaining embedded in a stable North or South team.

The six-day absence suggests genuine key-person exposure: ordinary claims were unaffected, while exception queues and tail cycle times worsened. However, this does not prove that Mira herself is the bottleneck; specialist capacity, vendor delays, and missing backup ownership may contribute. Compliance concerns about combined detection, routing, and reopening authority are credible control questions, but there is no direct evidence of wrongdoing.

Management should retain the coordination function, immediately establish backup coverage, document routing rules, and add auditable reason codes and selective review. It should monitor exception waiting times, referrals, overrides, specialist load, and outcomes before deciding whether to decentralize, automate, or formally redesign Mira’s role.
