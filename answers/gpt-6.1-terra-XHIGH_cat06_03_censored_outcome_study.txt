## 1. Target definition

The primary target is the probability of `default_90d = 1` among applications that will eventually activate under the historical operating policy, conditional on information available immediately after their third observed event. The outcome window starts at Account Activated and ends 90 elapsed days later; an event at the horizon is included. The unit of analysis is one application, with one prediction at that prefix. The study population consists of applications started in the observation window that reach a reliably ordered third event strictly before activation and are eventually observed to activate. The estimand is the conditional probability of default within 90 days of activation given the prefix information and eventual activation under the relevant historical policy. It is not a 90-day risk measured from application start. Time to activation varies and can exceed 90 days.

At prediction time, eventual activation is unknown. The model may be evaluated or used as a conditional risk estimate for cases that will activate, but it cannot identify those cases on its own or provide an unconditional risk for all applicants. Its meaning depends on the selection policy and on the mixture of applicants eventually activated. Later policy changes can alter both.

A useful alternative is a multi-state prediction for all applications reaching the same prefix: probabilities of activation, rejection, withdrawal and transfer within a specified horizon, plus remaining open. A subsequent state can describe default within 90 days of activation. Transfer is a distinct outcome unless reliable linkage and follow-up support modeling the later path. This answers what may happen to an application under the current process, not the hypothetical default risk if every applicant were granted credit. A policy-specific counterfactual default target under universal or alternative activation would answer a different question again; it requires a well-defined activation intervention, consistency, positivity, exchangeability conditional on sufficiently rich predecision variables, and adequate outcome follow-up. These assumptions are strong and cannot be established from this export.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

Ordinary right censoring occurs when observation ends before the relevant event or horizon, such as an open application at the process-observation boundary or an activated account with less than 90 days of reliable outcome follow-up. A known positive event can contribute time-to-event information before 90 days if its timestamp and feed are reliable; absence of a default record does not establish a negative without complete follow-up. A 90-day horizon that has passed with no received label is instead a delayed or missing outcome feed, not automatically ordinary censoring. A permanently missing feed is an outcome missingness problem; the partner association makes a missing-completely-at-random assumption especially doubtful.

Activation selects who has a defined observed default outcome. It is not ordinary right censoring of rejected applicants: their outcome under activation is counterfactual. Transfer can be a competing process event for an originating-platform multi-state target, or a loss of follow-up for a cross-platform target. The latter requires linkage and observation assumptions; the 40% without linkage cannot be silently treated as event-free.

For the primary analysis, use the activated, prefix-eligible cohort and model the 90-day binary outcome with inverse probability of label-observation weights estimated from pre-prediction variables, activation timing, source/partner and other variables available by the relevant analysis cutoff. Estimate observation probabilities within appropriate time and source strata, use cross-fitting or separate training data, inspect overlap and weight distributions, and report effective sample size and results with prespecified weight truncation. The outcome model itself uses only prefix-available predictors; later operational variables may support the observation model but must not enter predictions. Evaluate with correspondingly weighted metrics. This approach assumes conditional exchangeability of label observation given the variables used, positivity, a correct observation model, and accurate labels and activation ascertainment. Accounts still inside the 90-day horizon should be handled as censored in a survival analysis with actual follow-up times, or held out of the binary analysis until their horizon is complete; a label-availability weight alone does not make their follow-up complete. If adequate follow-up times are unavailable, restrict the main binary analysis to an activation vintage for which the full horizon and a prespecified reporting lag have elapsed, then weight remaining missing feeds, while acknowledging that a lag alone cannot guarantee receipt.

As sensitivity analyses, compare unweighted mature-label results with weighted results, vary the reporting lag and weight truncation, and apply missing-not-at-random scenarios that increase or decrease default odds among missing feeds, especially for partner-originated accounts. For example, report results across a prespecified range of missing-feed default odds ratios relative to observed outcomes, rather than presenting one assumed value as truth. Where event and last-reliable-observation times exist, also fit a time-to-default analysis with right-censoring adjustments and compare 90-day risks. Analyze transfer as a separate competing outcome and repeat cross-platform analyses under plausible linkage failures. For the activated estimand, examine cases whose activation might occur after the event window and state the resulting ascertainment limitation.

Observed label rates, delay distributions, overlap, linkage rates, covariate balance, event ordering and performance by source and vintage are testable or partially testable. Conditional independence of missing labels can be probed using observed predictors and earlier vintages but is not verified for missing outcomes; positivity is only empirically assessable in observed strata. Default rates among permanently missing feeds, the eventual fate of unlinked transfers, and counterfactual defaults under forced activation are not identified from these data without further assumptions or external follow-up.

## 4. Prefix extraction

For the primary fixed-count prefix, sort each case by event timestamp and a trustworthy event sequence or ingestion order, and take exactly the first three events. Predict immediately after the third event has actually become available. If ties cannot be ordered reliably, including ties straddling the prefix boundary or activation at the same timestamp, exclude and count them or apply a validated source sequence. Do not select a prefix because of a later outcome. A case with fewer than three observable events has no third-event prediction; report this coverage rather than padding it with future events.

For an elapsed-time alternative, set a landmark such as 48 hours after application start and include only events both occurring and available by that landmark. Generate it for cases known to be under observation and eligible at the landmark; separately report cases closed, activated, transferred or not yet observed for the full interval. If predicting earlier for short cases is operationally desired, define a separate decision rule and estimand, not a retrospectively chosen prefix length. Prefixes from the same case must remain in the same split if multiple landmarks are studied.

Preserve every genuinely repeated document request and review loop in chronological order. Deduplicate only using a validated event identifier or source-specific duplication rule; equal activity and timestamp alone can represent real separate events. Maintain a versioned mapping of pre- and post-migration names to common activities, inspect unmapped names and compare counts across the migration. Derive any mapping without using outcomes, and retain original activity and source for audit.

## 5. Process-aware features

Static attributes, provided they were actually available and unchanged or versioned as of prediction, include channel, region, requested amount and source system. Prefix-derived features include ordered activity sequence, activity counts, last activity, observed transitions, number of review visits and document-request loops, elapsed time since start, inter-event durations, and time in the current observed state. A risk score can be used only from its latest version available at or before prediction. Calendar, policy-era and migration-era indicators can capture changes in the environment; their use does not by itself solve transport across a policy change.

Resource and queue features require historical reference data. Examples include the responsible resource at the last event, workload or queue size computed from cases and events known at that moment, and employee experience calculated from verified employment or completed work history available then. Construct reference aggregates using only records available before each prediction, with appropriate source and process coverage; never compute them using the full extraction. A process graph or transition-frequency reference must be learned on the training period alone, and a case's prefix can then be compared to that reference. Avoid using a whole-case duration or final process variant as if it were known at the prefix.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Set after final decision; reveals later process information | Only usable for a separately defined prediction made after it was available | Drop for primary model | Third-event model cannot assume a final limit exists |
| `decision_reason` | Decision and its reason are post-decision | Require a separately timed post-decision prediction | Drop for primary model | Decision-related fields can encode selection and future outcome proxies |
| `collection_status` | Post-activation and may directly reveal default | Never use at the primary prediction time | Drop | Direct outcome leakage |
| Future events and whole-case variants/durations | Reveal activation, collections, default or later process path | Use only events occurred and available by prediction time; derive summaries from prefix | Repair | Outcome and process completion must not determine features |
| `documents_total` | Nightly snapshot can contain later same-day uploads | Recompute from timestamped, actually available uploads through prediction time | Repair or drop | Calendar-day totals are not necessarily as-of features |
| `risk_score` | A repeated or overwritten value may be a later update | Use the last historically versioned score available by prediction time | Repair or drop | Extraction-time values do not establish historical availability |
| Resource experience | Extraction-time experience incorporates future tenure and work | Reconstruct from time-stamped history available by prediction | Repair or drop | Current experience is not event-time experience |
| `default_90d` and `label_available_at` | Repeated labels expose outcomes; availability creates delayed and selective observation | Use only as target and outcome-observation metadata at analysis cutoff; never as predictors | Exclude from features; retain for audit | Knowing whether a label arrived is itself future information |
| Activation, rejection, transfer and terminal status | Later selection or outcome can leak into prediction | Use only for cohort/target construction or at a later defined landmark | Exclude from features | Eventual activation conditions the estimand but is unknown at prediction |
| Static attributes and resource identity | Values may be overwritten, backfilled or unavailable at the event | Verify creation and version timestamps; reconstruct as of prediction | Keep if verified, otherwise repair/drop | A column's apparent constancy does not prove historical availability |
| Activity names, timestamps and duplicates | Migration or duplicated events can create spurious changes and prefixes | Apply versioned mapping and validated duplicate resolution using available records | Repair and audit | Preserve actual loops while preventing artificial event counts |
| Case identifiers and extraction timestamps | Encode ordering, source or future availability | Retain only for grouping and audit | Drop from model | Neither represents a legitimate prospective predictor by default |

## 7. Validation design

Split by application start date, keeping all prefixes from each application together. Use pre-1 July 2025 starts for initial training, July–August 2025 starts for validation, and September–December 2025 starts for a later test, with explicit reports for pre- and post-15 October migration. Use rolling-origin backtests with training features and labels frozen at each simulated model-build date, and later starts for evaluation. Refit transformations, process references and observation weights using the appropriate training information only. A policy indicator or separate post-change recalibration can be examined, but a model trained before 1 July is not assumed valid under the new policy.

At the 15 February 2026 extract, test cases started after the migration may activate too late to have complete 90-day outcomes and the usual additional 7–35 day feed delay. Even earlier-start cases can activate late. Report each split's activation-date distribution, horizon completion and label receipt, and do not claim an unbiased full post-migration test from the currently matured subset. Reserve that test until adequate event coverage, follow-up and feed receipt are available; 35 days is a common delay, not a guaranteed maximum. If early results are shown, label them provisional and report the selection and missingness adjustments and remaining uncertainty. Historical model-build cutoffs require archived event and feature versions; current extraction values alone cannot prove an honest historical backtest.

For mature binary outcomes, report weighted and unweighted sample counts, default prevalence, AUROC and precision-recall performance, Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope and calibration plots, with confidence intervals clustered at application level. Use weighted calibration and scoring when evaluating the weighted estimand; assess discrimination as appropriate with observation weights. For survival sensitivity analyses, use censoring-adjusted 90-day Brier score and time-dependent discrimination and calibration if assumptions and follow-up support them. Assess operational thresholds with sensitivity, specificity, precision and workload, rather than choosing thresholds on the test set. Report uncertainty and missingness by channel, region, source/partner, policy era and migration era; suppress or flag unstable small groups and check intersectional groups where sample size permits. Monitor prefix coverage, activity mappings, feature and score distributions, activation rates and label delays for shift. A random split mixes periods, leaks later operating conditions into training, and can hide policy, migration, delay and selection shifts even when cases themselves are separated.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The primary model estimates associations between early observed prefixes and default among those observed to activate under the historical process, subject to follow-up and missingness assumptions. It does not establish that a prefix feature causes default, identify the risk of rejected or withdrawn applicants if they were activated, or provide an unconditional default probability for every application. The current observed non-default fraction among the 21,600 labeled accounts is not an unbiased estimate for all applications or necessarily for all activated accounts. Unlinked transfers, permanently missing feeds, snapshot-only features and incomplete post-window activation coverage limit identification and transportability.

Deployment can change the data-generating process: staff may request documents or intervene earlier in response to scores; thresholds may change who is accepted and activated; applicants may withdraw or alter behavior; accounts may be routed to partners with different follow-up; and collections or reporting practices may change observed outcomes and delays. These changes can invalidate calibration and make labels selectively observed. Use the model only with a stated operational purpose, human and outcome monitoring, and a plan to reassess policy, subgroup performance and feedback effects. Do not use the conditional score as a causal justification for denying credit. A prospective registry, complete time-stamped data and outcome follow-up, and where appropriate a carefully designed policy evaluation would be needed for broader claims.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

The skeleton illustrates a conservative third-event cohort and a fixed extraction cutoff. It assumes the supplied file contains all applications, label availability is reliable, and recorded event attributes are immutable as of their event; these assumptions must be verified against source systems. It does not reconstruct historical ingestion times or unresolved cross-platform identities. Replace the mapping and terminal rules with validated local definitions, resolve duplicates using source event IDs, and reconcile its counts with the supplied snapshot before modeling. The cutoff is inclusive at the event-window end and extraction instant. Historical backtests require archived snapshots or availability timestamps.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
K = 3
EXTRACT = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15 23:59:59.999999999", tz="UTC")
EVENT_END = pd.Timestamp("2025-12-31 23:59:59.999999999", tz="UTC")
START = pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01", tz="UTC")
EVENT_CUTOFF = min(EXTRACT, EVENT_END)
HORIZON = pd.Timedelta(days=90)
LAG = pd.Timedelta(days=35)  # analysis convention, not a guaranteed feed SLA

raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv")
for col in [TS, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
    raw[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
if raw[CASE].isna().any() or raw[TS].isna().any():
    raise ValueError("Resolve missing case IDs and event timestamps first")
if raw["data_extract_time"].isna().any():
    raise ValueError("Unknown extraction time requires investigation")
# This filters snapshots, not event ingestion; ingestion history is not supplied.
raw = raw.loc[raw["data_extract_time"] <= EXTRACT].copy()
raw["row_order"] = range(len(raw))
raw = raw.sort_values([CASE, TS, "row_order"], kind="stable")

# Supply a versioned, validated local mapping; examples are not assumed here.
rename_map = {}
raw[ACT] = raw[ACT].replace(rename_map)
print("Activities requiring mapping review:", sorted(raw[ACT].dropna().unique()))
# Equal timestamps are NOT enough evidence to delete a duplicate.
dup_candidates = raw.duplicated([CASE, TS, ACT, "source_system"], keep=False)
print("Possible duplicate event rows:", int(dup_candidates.sum()))

# Events through the observation cutoff only; do not use later events to set starts.
ev = raw.loc[raw[TS] <= EVENT_CUTOFF].copy()
ev = pm4py.format_dataframe(ev, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
ev = ev.sort_values([CASE, TS, "row_order"], kind="stable")
starts = ev.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("start")
ids = starts.index[(starts >= START) & (starts <= EVENT_END)]
ev = ev.loc[ev[CASE].isin(ids)].copy()
starts = starts.loc[ids]

def first_event_time(activity):
    return ev.loc[ev[ACT].eq(activity)].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()

case = starts.to_frame()
case["activation"] = first_event_time("Account Activated")
terminal_names = ["Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]
terminal_events = ev.loc[ev[ACT].isin(terminal_names), [CASE, ACT, TS]]
first_terminal = (terminal_events.sort_values(TS)
                  .drop_duplicates(CASE).set_index(CASE))
case["terminal"] = first_terminal[ACT]
case["terminal_time"] = first_terminal[TS]
case["censor_time"] = EVENT_CUTOFF
case["horizon_end"] = case["activation"] + HORIZON
case["horizon_complete"] = case["horizon_end"] <= EXTRACT
case["lag_eligible"] = case["horizon_end"] + LAG <= EXTRACT

# Detect conflicting process paths; transfer linkage requires separate reconciliation.
conflict = (case["activation"].notna() & case["terminal_time"].notna())
if conflict.any():
    raise ValueError("Resolve activation and terminal paths, including transfers")
case["state"] = case["terminal"].fillna("Open")
case.loc[case["activation"].notna(), "state"] = "Activated"

# The label is target metadata, not an event feature. Reject inconsistent labels.
labels = raw.loc[raw[CASE].isin(ids) & raw["default_90d"].notna()
                 & raw["label_available_at"].notna()
                 & (raw["label_available_at"] <= EXTRACT),
                 [CASE, "default_90d", "label_available_at"]].copy()
labels["default_90d"] = pd.to_numeric(labels["default_90d"], errors="raise")
if not labels["default_90d"].isin([0, 1]).all():
    raise ValueError("Invalid default label")
if (labels.groupby(CASE)["default_90d"].nunique() > 1).any():
    raise ValueError("Conflicting historical labels need reconciliation")
labels = labels.sort_values("label_available_at").drop_duplicates(CASE)
case = case.join(labels.set_index(CASE))
case["usable_label"] = (case["activation"].notna()
                        & case["horizon_complete"]
                        & case["default_90d"].notna())
case["label_status"] = "not applicable / selection unresolved"
case.loc[case["activation"].notna(), "label_status"] = "horizon incomplete"
case.loc[case["activation"].notna() & case["horizon_complete"],
         "label_status"] = "feed delayed or missing"
case.loc[case["usable_label"], "label_status"] = "labeled"

# Use calendar splits by start; evaluation labels must be available at each
# evaluation cutoff, and training labels at each simulated model-build cutoff.
case["split"] = "test"
case.loc[case["start"] < pd.Timestamp("2025-09-01", tz="UTC"), "split"] = "validation"
case.loc[case["start"] < pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01", tz="UTC"), "split"] = "train"

# Reject any tie in the first K+1 events: no trusted tie-breaker is supplied.
ev["event_num"] = ev.groupby(CASE).cumcount() + 1
first = ev.loc[ev["event_num"] <= K + 1]
tied = first.loc[first.duplicated([CASE, TS], keep=False), CASE].unique()
case["n_events"] = ev.groupby(CASE).size()
third = ev.loc[ev["event_num"].eq(K)].set_index(CASE)[TS]
case["prediction_time"] = third
case["prefix_eligible"] = (case["n_events"].ge(K)
                           & ~case.index.isin(tied)
                           & (case["activation"].isna()
                              | (case["prediction_time"] < case["activation"]))
# Do not predict after a terminal outcome either.
case["prefix_eligible"] &= (case["terminal_time"].isna()
                            | (case["prediction_time"] < case["terminal_time"]))

print("All cases:", len(case))
print(case.groupby(["state", "label_status"], dropna=False).size())
print(case.groupby(["split", "prefix_eligible", "state", "label_status"],
                   dropna=False).size())
print("Lag eligible among activated:", int(case["lag_eligible"].sum()))

# Explicit allowlist: no case outcome, decision, snapshot, or future columns.
allowed = [CASE, ACT, TS, "org:resource", "case:channel", "case:region",
           "case:requested_amount", "risk_score", "source_system", "event_num"]
forbidden = {"default_90d", "label_available_at", "final_limit",
             "decision_reason", "collection_status", "data_extract_time"}
assert not (set(allowed) & forbidden)
eligible_ids = case.index[case["prefix_eligible"]]
pre = ev.loc[ev[CASE].isin(eligible_ids) & ev["event_num"].le(K), allowed].copy()
assert pre.groupby(CASE).size().eq(K).all()
assert (pre[TS] <= pre[CASE].map(case["prediction_time"])).all()

# Feed PM4Py only the already truncated, allowlisted event data.
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(pre)
prefix_log = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, K)
# All supplied traces already have exactly K events; audit conversion semantics.
assert len(prefix_log) == len(eligible_ids)
assert all(len(trace) == K for trace in prefix_log)

# Example features derived exclusively from the prefix. Risk score is retained
# only if source audit confirms this row contains its historical event-time value.
features = (pre.sort_values([CASE, "event_num"]).groupby(CASE).agg(
    sequence=(ACT, lambda s: " > ".join(s.astype(str))),
    elapsed_seconds=(TS, lambda s: (s.max() - s.min()).total_seconds()),
    review_visits=(ACT, lambda s: int(s.eq("Credit Review").sum())),
    document_requests=(ACT, lambda s: int(s.eq("Request Documents").sum())),
    last_resource=("org:resource", "last"),
    channel=("case:channel", "first"), region=("case:region", "first"),
    requested_amount=("case:requested_amount", "first"),
    risk_score=("risk_score", "last")))
assert not (set(features.columns) & forbidden)
# Attach target and metadata separately, never as input columns to the model.
metadata = case.loc[features.index, ["split", "state", "usable_label",
                                    "lag_eligible", "default_90d"]]
print("Feature rows:", len(features), "metadata rows:", len(metadata))
print("Leakage allowlist audit passed; availability audit remains mandatory.")
# Fit the primary binary model only after observation-weight estimation and
# maturity checks; connect survival or competing-risk models separately when
# exact event and last-reliable-follow-up times are available.
```
