## 1. Interaction-graph description

Model this as a typed, temporal, qualified multigraph, or as a bipartite event–object graph where a single event can connect several objects. Nodes include Jobs, Components, ComponentLots, Machines, Tools, Batches, Shipments, and timestamped Events. Object–object edges retain qualifiers such as `requires`, `contains`, `batches`, and `consolidates`; event–object edges record participation, including acquisition, release, quarantine, and processing. Times and lifecycle states belong on events or intervals, not on an unqualified aggregated edge. An event shared by three jobs is one physical event, even though it can appear in three job traces.

Compact edge list, with `->` indicating the stated relation or participation, not causal direction:

```text
J1..J6 --requires--> C1..C6, respectively
L7 --contains--> C2, C5;  L8 --contains--> C1, C3
B1 --batches--> J1, J2, J3;  B2 --batches--> J4, J5
S1 --consolidates--> J1, J4;  S2 --> J2, J3;  S3 --> J5, J6
Batch Cure@12:05 --> B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3
J3 --acquires/releases--> FIX-A;  J4 Machine Process@11:45 --> C4, CNC-2, FIX-A
Quarantine@10:20 and Release@13:50 --> L7, C2, C5, J2, J5 [integrated evidence]
J5/J6 Final Test --> TEST-1, CAL-3;  B2 --> OVEN-1, RACK-7
```

The given O2O links and stated event participants are observations in the supplied evidence. Lot-event links to J2 and J5 depend on the integration: the source-job-only flattening omits them, whereas the alternative pipeline attaches them. Preserve provenance and distinguish direct source links from relationships derived through lot membership. Tool occupancy between recorded acquire and release, a wait-for-tool edge from J4 to FIX-A, and any propagation of delay between jobs are inferred, not explicitly logged.

Higher-order motifs include: (1) J1, J2 and J3 synchronizing through B1, OVEN-1 and RACK-7; (2) J3 and J4 potentially competing for FIX-A while J4 needs CNC-2; (3) J2 and J5 sharing L7 through C2 and C5, with different override decisions; (4) J5 and J6 sharing CAL-3 and TEST-1 before joining S3; and (5) J1 and J4 joining S1 after traversing different production paths. These overlapping motifs connect jobs that otherwise have separate job traces. L8 is another shared-lot relation, but no disruption is supplied for it.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Each number below is an observed interval or a comparison against an explicit hypothetical baseline. The intervals can overlap and must not be summed as a total causal delay.

- **B1, J1:** 12:05 − 08:10 = **3 h 55 min**. If readiness is accurate and an otherwise identical feasible batch could start at 08:10 with no minimum-load, material, tool, or capacity constraint, this is the maximum synchronization-associated wait for J1, not demonstrated avoidable time.
- **B1, J2:** 12:05 − 08:25 = **3 h 40 min**, under the same immediate-start counterfactual and assuming the recorded override permits processing. Without the override, L7 quarantine would be an additional constraint; attributing the entire interval to batch policy would be unsafe.
- **B1, last-ready baseline:** J3 waits 12:05 − 11:55 = **10 min**. If the only batch rule is to wait for all three, then the first-ready jobs wait **3 h 45 min** (J1) and **3 h 30 min** (J2) beyond J3's post-readiness wait. This comparison assumes equal post-readiness dispatch overhead and does not prove that starting before J3 was feasible.
- **B2:** J4 waits 14:50 − 13:10 = **1 h 40 min** and J5 waits 14:50 − 14:40 = **10 min**. If the batch could start as soon as J4 was ready, J4's interval is its potential reduction; however, both RACK-7 quarantine until 14:35 and J5's readiness at 14:40 independently constrain the actual start. If both must clear and the recorded times are correct, 14:50 is only **10 min** after the later constraint. The tool and partner delays cannot simply be added.
- **FIX-A/J4:** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts at 11:45, an interval of **1 h 55 min**. FIX-A is held by J3 until 11:40, **1 h 50 min** after J4 arrives; the start follows release by 5 min. If J4 was otherwise fully ready at 09:50, required FIX-A throughout, and an equivalent fixture and machine slot were immediately available, up to 1 h 50 min is plausibly fixture-associated. CNC-2's 10:05–10:47 idle interval is **42 min** inside that overlap; it does not establish why J4 could not run. The remaining 5 min may reflect setup or other unobserved work.
- **S1/J1:** 19:25 − 13:30 = **5 h 55 min** packed-to-dispatch dwell. If J1 could dispatch alone at packing time, all 5 h 55 min is potentially associated with consolidation and dispatch handling. More conservatively, if a separate shipment could leave in the observed 15 min from J4's packing to S1 dispatch, the hypothetical departure is 13:45 and the excess is **5 h 40 min**. This assumes the 15-minute handling time and a vehicle are available at 13:30; customer and shipping rules may prevent it.
- **CAL-3/J6:** J6 waits 17:25 − 16:05 = **1 h 20 min** for test to start. J5 occupies the test slot until 16:35; CAL-3 recalibration then lasts **45 min**, followed by 5 min to J6's start. If J6 could start at 16:35 with another calibrated tool and the same machine and operator available, its start would be **50 min** earlier. The 45-minute recalibration is a plausible tool-associated component; the final 5 min has no recorded cause.
- **L7:** quarantine lasts 13:50 − 10:20 = **3 h 30 min**. If J5 was ready and otherwise eligible to proceed at 10:20, this could bound a 3 h 30 min restriction; its readiness at that time is not supplied, so actual J5 delay cannot be calculated. J2's B1 participation at 12:05 during quarantine demonstrates an override, not universal blocking.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job:** Shows each job's recorded sequence and elapsed transitions. It can duplicate B1's single cure across three traces and omit L7 events under the stated extraction. A normal median or no job beyond 1.8 standard deviations can wrongly imply no important waiting; six observations and relative outlier scores do not establish acceptable absolute lead time.
- **Machine:** Shows utilization, recorded machine sequences, and event gaps. It hides readiness, queues, tool states, and partner dependencies unless joined; batch work can be counted differently from job work. Concluding that OVEN-1's 44% utilization rules out a batch barrier confuses machine occupation with job waiting.
- **Batch:** Shows B1/B2 membership, batch lifecycle, and potentially member readiness. Flattening can repeat one job or resource event across batches and hides cross-batch rack cleaning, lot restrictions, and shipments unless linked. A short load-to-cure edge could misleadingly imply no pre-batch wait.
- **Tool:** Shows recorded acquire/release, servicing, and use by multiple jobs or batches. It hides unlogged requests and the reasons for holds; a shared event may appear in several tool traces. Inferring that FIX-A is a proven bottleneck from its hold alone ignores J4's unobserved readiness and scheduling.
- **ComponentLot:** Shows L7's common quarantine/release and components. It hides or inconsistently attaches affected jobs and supervisor override; lot events can be replicated to both components or jobs. Treating both J2 and J5 as blocked for the entire quarantine would contradict J2's override and assume J5's readiness.
- **Shipment:** Shows S1 membership and create/load/dispatch, but the supplied DFG omits each partner's packing time and can repeat job events across shipment traces. Its 0.8 h and 0.3 h medians cannot establish short packed-to-dispatch waiting for J1.

Low utilization and long waits coexist when a machine is ready but a fixture, qualified rack, material release, batch partner, operator, or downstream policy is not. Job waits accumulate outside machine processing; averages also conceal short peaks and the chosen measurement window. Machine-event gaps alone do not identify any of these causes.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

1. **J3 → FIX-A → J4/CNC-2 → B2/OVEN-1/RACK-7 → S1/J1.** Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50, starts 11:45, is batch-ready 13:10, and packs 19:10; J1 packs 13:30 and S1 dispatches 19:25. Resource feasibility: an exclusive FIX-A requirement would prevent J4's processing while J3 holds it, and B2 also needs rack and oven. Policy constraint: B2's actual membership and S1's consolidation couple the jobs if enforced. Causal speculation: J3's hold may have delayed J4 and thus J1's shipment, but we lack J4's full readiness, counterfactual batch schedule, and evidence that earlier J4 completion would advance S1.

2. **L7 → C2/J2/B1 → OVEN-1/RACK-7, alongside C5/J5/B2.** Temporal precedence: L7 is quarantined 10:20–13:50; B1 starts 12:05 with J2 under a separately recorded override; J5 does not proceed until release and is batch-ready 14:40. Resource feasibility: both batches use the same oven and rack; B2 also has J4 as a member. Policy constraint: quarantine normally restricts movement, subject to the documented exception. Causal speculation: quarantine may contribute to J5's late readiness and J4's B2 wait, but no pre-release readiness or actual counterfactual path is recorded. Job links to lot events are integration-dependent.

3. **J5/C5/L7 → CAL-3/TEST-1 → J6/S3.** Temporal precedence: L7 releases 13:50; J5 and J6 are test-ready at 16:00 and 16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35, CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20, and J6 tests 17:25–17:50. ## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

Evaluate the portfolio at the level of linked jobs, tools, lots, batches, and shipments, with timestamps and policy eligibility recorded. Use comparable periods or eligible matched jobs and account for changes in mix, shift, and demand; randomize when operationally feasible. Report distributions and downstream outcomes as well as averages.

1. **Reduce batch synchronization:** Permit smaller or time-triggered eligible batches when an early-ready job exceeds a defined age. J1/J2-like jobs may finish sooner; later arrivals such as J3, other batches, or customers may lose oven slots or wait longer. Extra cycles, energy, rack cleaning, and quality variation are risks. A perverse metric is batch fill rate alone, which rewards holding early jobs. Compare eligible cohorts or randomize thresholds; measure member-ready-to-cure waits, oven/rack occupancy, quality, energy per good unit, and shipment delivery for all affected jobs.
2. **Increase tool availability:** Provide a validated backup FIX-A or CAL-3, or schedule preventive calibration and RACK-7 cleaning outside critical windows. J4, J6, and B2 could benefit; maintenance staff, other tool users, or later batches could be displaced. Acquisition, validation, contamination and maintenance costs matter. Minimizing recorded tool downtime alone may encourage unsafe deferral of calibration. Compare matched contention episodes with and without qualified backup; track request-to-acquire time, tool state, test quality, machine idle causes, and downstream job and shipment outcomes.
3. **Change shipment policy:** Allow eligible split shipment or a maximum hold time for S1-like partners. J1 may ship earlier, while J4's separate shipment and other customers may face fewer carrier slots or higher costs. Freight, packaging, emissions, and customer receiving constraints increase. Optimizing dispatch speed alone could encourage excessive partial shipments. Trial eligible shipments under agreed customer rules; compare per-job pack-to-dispatch and delivery times, on-time delivery, cost, and all partner outcomes.
4. **Improve lot disposition:** Accelerate risk-based review or preposition approved substitute components for L7-like quarantines, with controlled, auditable overrides. J5 and its batch partners could benefit; scarce quality staff or substitute stock may delay other lots and jobs. Premature release, scrap, and traceability failures are risks. Measuring only quarantine duration could reward unsafe releases. Compare matched lot incidents with quality adjudication and downstream traceability, measuring time from eligible request to disposition, defect/escape rates, affected batches, and delivery.
5. **Improve logging and integration:** Record tool requests, grants, releases, readiness, reasons for waits, lot-event provenance, overrides, batch eligibility, and per-job shipment milestones. This helps diagnosis across all six jobs rather than directly speeding them; operators and data stewards bear extra work, and inaccurate derived links can harm decisions. A metric rewarding the number of logged events may encourage duplicates. Reconcile event IDs and source links against system records on sampled lots and tools; compare completeness, timestamp accuracy, duplicate rates, and whether reconstructed waits agree with observations before and after instrumentation.
6. **Coordinate resource scheduling:** Schedule jobs using both machine and qualified tool availability, with explicit batch and shipment dependencies. J4/J6-like jobs may see fewer stranded machine slots; other jobs may be deprioritized and schedules can become brittle. Planning overhead and model error are costs. Optimizing machine utilization alone may increase WIP and early-job waiting. Pilot against comparable shifts, measuring job wait distributions, late orders, tool queues, rework, and fairness across all jobs, not just machine utilization.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The following assumes a recent PM4Py version, a valid OCEL with event–object relations, and the standard OC-DFG edge structure. API signatures and return formats can differ by version; inspect results and adapt the named arguments if needed. Object type labels must match the log. The graphs and DFGs identify candidates; actual waits require the readiness, resource-state, policy, and override events described above.

```python
import pm4py

# Basic object-centric views; keep the original event IDs and source provenance.
print("Object interactions:", pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel))
interaction_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    ocel, graph_type="object_interaction"
)
job_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel, object_type="Job")
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
print("Object interaction graph:", interaction_graph)
print("Job object transition graph:", job_otg)
print("Event-to-object-type result:", etot)

# OC-DFG edge measures have different units. A multi-object event pair
# may count once as a distinct event pair and once for each object.
# Never add these measures together or interpret either as a wait count.
edges = ocdfg["edges"]
for object_type in sorted(set(edges.get("event_couples", {})) |
                          set(edges.get("unique_objects", {}))):
    event_pairs = edges.get("event_couples", {}).get(object_type, {})
    objects = edges.get("unique_objects", {}).get(object_type, {})
    for edge in sorted(set(event_pairs) | set(objects), key=str):
        print(object_type, edge,
              "distinct event pairs:", len(event_pairs.get(edge, set())),
              "distinct objects:", len(objects.get(edge, set())))

# Compare views, retaining their distinct units and extraction assumptions.
for object_type in ("Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"):
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    performance_dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
    print("Flattened", object_type, "performance:", performance_dfg)
    print("Starts:", starts, "ends:", ends)

job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
print("Job features:", job_features)
```

In particular, compare distinct event pairs with distinct participating objects: a single B1 cure can occur once in event-pair frequency while participating in several job lifecycles. The supplied interaction-summary counts use particular event conventions, so reciprocal Job–ComponentLot rows are not independent evidence to add together. Validate feature meanings and aggregation before modeling.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Evidence that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation includes:

1. Corrected timestamps show J1 or J2 was not actually ready until close to 12:05.
2. Earlier starts were prohibited by documented minimum load, cure recipe, or safety requirements, so the observed dwell was not avoidable by changing synchronization.
3. OVEN-1, RACK-7, required labor, or another prerequisite was unavailable throughout the purported early-start window.
4. J1 or J2 had an unresolved material or quality hold, including a limit on J2's override, until near 12:05.
5. The cure event's timestamp or B1 membership is wrong, or it records a start different from the physical cure.
6. Broader, representative observations show early-ready batch waits are rare or negligible, or that non-batched jobs have comparable waits after controlling for mix.
7. Earlier feasible batch starts in comparable cases fail to improve completion or delivery because another constraint absorbs the time.
8. Independent records show B2's wait is entirely explained by a necessary rack or material restriction rather than waiting for a partner; this weakens a broad synchronization interpretation even though B1 remains a separate observation.

Collect at least the following discriminating data: timestamped job and component readiness with eligibility criteria; tool request, grant, release, reservation, and availability state; machine occupancy, setup, downtime, shift and operator calendars; batch formation decisions, minimum loads, recipes and alternative eligible start times; rack cleaning/quarantine start, end, reason and qualification; lot hold, release, exception, approval and component substitution records with source provenance; test requests, calibration due rules and backup-tool qualification; and per-job pack, shipment creation, load, dispatch, delivery, customer split-shipment rules and carrier capacity. Capture unique event IDs, clock synchronization, missing-link indicators, and the observation window so duplicated associations and inconsistent pipelines can be audited. These data can distinguish a necessary policy wait from a resource conflict and from a logging artifact without treating sequence alone as proof.
