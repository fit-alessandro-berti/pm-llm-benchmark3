# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

A core duty is one the process regularly depends on that role to deliver. Its frequency within the resource’s work, the cases on which it occurs, and its position in the trace all matter. The counts describe ten weeks of execution. Formal titles, certifications, delegation records and sign-offs answer different questions about authority.

- **Automated intake.** Nora routinely creates the request and can perform a precheck. In fragment 2 a human validates after bot registration. The shared A2 label needs the executor and outcome to distinguish those responsibilities.
- **Front-office resolution.** Taking a request through validation, response and closure is core. Amira’s 75 diagnoses and 18 pricing reviews indicate a broader observed remit than Ben’s 60 and 5. They warrant examining exceptions before assigning either agent specialist coverage.
- **Seasonal resolution.** Closure is a genuine core duty in the simple-case lane. Rare diagnoses and negotiations provide little evidence of independent exception handling. The later actor who reopens a case can differ from the person who validated or closed it.
- **Technical delivery.** Both analysts own substantial diagnosis, configuration and implementation work. A6 is peripheral by volume for both, but Chen has 24 A6 events to Dina’s 6 and greater critical-case exposure. That distinction matters when staffing a difficult shift.
- **Security review.** A6 is a core intervention even though most process paths bypass Erik. His implementation and knowledge entries are consistent with helping resolve or document a security issue. Trace context would establish how often he also owns delivery.
- **Commercial review.** A7 and A8 recur for Fatima, including a path where she reviews and Giorgio negotiates. That is different from replacing her for the review itself. Fatima’s 16 A9 entries and Giorgio’s 20 need decision records before they can support an approval delegation.
- **Supervisory approval.** Hana’s review and sign-off are central to her position. Reopening a case or joining a negotiation can support that decision. A previous A9 recorded by a colleague can still require her review.
- **Incident coordination.** For Mateo the recurring responsibility is getting a case to the appropriate specialist or decision maker, particularly after reopening. “Coordinate” describes his position in the supplied fragments. It has no separate activity code in A1–A14. Workshop cover explains some of his A9 events.
- **Quality and knowledge.** Both checking deliverables and recording reusable knowledge recur across the two quality analysts. An A10 preceding a check could be corrective assistance. Establish who implemented and who checked the same case before treating the two duties as interchangeable.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

This example assumes one row per event and the stated PM4Py column names. It uses case-level complexity as supplied and retains missing or unrecognized substitution labels as unknown. The CSV path stands for the operational event log.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T, R = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
K = "case_complexity"
raw = pd.read_csv("order_events.csv")
raw[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw[T], utc=True, errors="raise")
if raw[[C, A, T, R]].isna().any().any():
    raise ValueError("Resolve missing case, activity, timestamp or executor before mining")
if raw.groupby(C)[K].nunique().gt(1).any():
    raise ValueError("Reconcile conflicting case complexity values within a case")
raw[K] = raw.groupby(C)[K].transform("first")
flags = raw["temporary_substitution"].astype("string").str.strip().str.lower()
raw["cover"] = flags.map({
    "true": "cover", "1": "cover", "yes": "cover",
    "false": "ordinary", "0": "ordinary", "no": "ordinary",
}).fillna("unknown")
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)

# These are activity groupings with resource participation weights.
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
for name, network in [("activity similarity", similarity),
                      ("handover", handover), ("shared cases", together)]:
    print(name, sorted(network.connections.items(),
                       key=lambda edge: edge[1], reverse=True)[:10])

# Join on BOTH activity and executor. Count actual matched events.
rows = [(i, activity, resource, weight)
        for i, role in enumerate(roles)
        for activity in role.activities
        for resource, weight in role.originator_importance.items()]
members = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["mined_role", A, R, "participation_weight"])
tagged = log.merge(members, on=[A, R], how="left")
by_context = (tagged.groupby(["mined_role", R, K, "cover"], dropna=False)
              .agg(events=(C, "size"), cases=(C, "nunique")).reset_index())
print(by_context.to_string(index=False))

# For numeric complexity, take a median over distinct cases in each stratum.
if pd.api.types.is_numeric_dtype(tagged[K]):
    cases = tagged.drop_duplicates(["mined_role", R, "cover", C])
    print(cases.groupby(["mined_role", R, "cover"], dropna=False)[K].median())

# Compare activity groups and participants across runs, since role IDs are local.
ordinary = log.loc[log["cover"].eq("ordinary")]
if not ordinary.empty:
    ordinary_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(ordinary)
    for role in ordinary_roles:
        print(role.activities, role.originator_importance)
```

The participation weight belongs to the mined role/resource pair. Recount events from the log as above. A case can involve several roles or both ordinary and cover events, so distinct-case counts across groups need not add to the total. Check the similarity metric before comparing its scores with the supplied cosine values.

Inspect directed handovers around A6, A7, A9 and A13, including low-weight edges involving Erik. Working-together edges indicate shared cases and can be strong simply because complicated cases involve more people. For network comparisons by product, complexity or customer segment, select case IDs and retain their complete traces. Removing intermediate events before computing handovers can create spurious adjacency. Resolve tied timestamps with source sequence information where available. If substitution is flagged for a whole case, verify the covering executor and dates before labeling an individual action. Compare proposed memberships with rosters, certifications, approval limits and final sign-offs before changing responsibilities.

- **Staffing.** Size routine capacity from simple-case arrivals by shift. Plan difficult technical work using complexity and product mix, including late-shift coverage. Chen and Dina’s 0.94 similarity supports shared technical training. It gives insufficient evidence for replacing one with the other on critical security-adjacent cases.

- **Commercial and supervisory cover.** Give Giorgio an explicit exception range, a named escalation contact and practice on matched cases. Track time, rework and final disposition during cover. Record preliminary and final approval separately and maintain a queue for Hana or an authorized deputy. Counting every A9 as independent signing capacity would overstate available cover.
- **Security and resilience.** Maintain qualified security backup and an urgent referral path that can reach Erik or that backup directly. Review the two delayed critical cases and monitor time from high-risk referral to qualified review alongside complaint rates. Cutting specialist hours solely because A6 volume fell would make capacity depend on how aggressively referrals were filtered.
- **Continuity.** Document Mateo’s routing criteria and assign cover for the coordination duty as well as for the specialist receiving the case. Test absence plans against actual handoffs, decision rights and waiting times. This preserves the benefits of cross-functional work while making its dependencies visible.
