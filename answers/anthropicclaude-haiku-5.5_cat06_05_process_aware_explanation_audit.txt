## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A validation suite should run before any explanation is shown. Each check has a pass criterion and a known limitation.

**Check 1: Prefix-time availability (point-in-time replay).**
- For each feature, record its source, event time, availability timestamp, and as-of semantics.
- Recompute every feature from the event log truncated at `t0`, then compare it with the value used in the model. Any mismatch is a leak.
- Perturbation test: remove all events after `t0` and check that the explanation is unchanged. Any change indicates complete-trace dependence.
- Limitation: this catches leaks only where the replay is complete. Features sourced from external warehouses (such as `final_handler_team`) need lineage metadata, not replay alone.

**Check 2: Activity-order feasibility (enabled, future-possible, impossible).**
- At `t0`, classify each activity:
  - Enabled: Documents Requested; Damage Assessment Completed (fast track); Fraud Investigation Opened (only if a trigger exists).
  - Future-possible: Documents Received; Documents Validated; Settlement Decision Recorded; Claim Closed.
  - Impossible at `t0`: Appeal (requires Partial or Deny); Documents Received (requires a prior request); Claim Closed (requires a final decision or withdrawal).
- Every explanation term that references an activity or pattern must lie in an enabled or already-observed category. Future-possible or impossible references are rejected.
- Limitation: "possible" is defined by the normative model, not by the data. Model incompleteness can misclassify legitimate exceptions.

**Check 3: Alignment against the normative model.**
- Align each trace against a normative Petri net or a process tree. Count deviations by type (move on log, move on model).
- Alignment is the most principled check for sequence explanations. It is expensive and depends on the cost function. Costs should be set so that a timestamp-tie reorder is cheap but an activity that never occurs before its prerequisite is expensive.
- Limitation: alignment reports deviations but does not explain why. It also cannot distinguish a data artifact from a real exception without additional metadata.

**Check 4: Footprint and DFG checks.**
- Build a footprint matrix (causal ``, parallel ``, choice `#`) from the normative model and compare it with the log-derived footprint.
- Flag log-derived `` relations absent from the model, such as `Claim Closed  Damage Assessment Completed`.
- A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule. Frequency can come from a data-ordering artifact, a batch-logging practice, or a shortcut. Use the DFG as a diagnostic, not as evidence of permissibility.
- Limitation: footprints are sensitive to noise and to infrequent but legitimate behavior.

**Check 5: Declarative constraints.**
- Examples:
  - Precedence(Damage Assessment, Settlement Decision).
  - Precedence(Settlement Decision, Claim Closed).
  - Response(Partial or Deny, Appeal) as an optional constraint.
  - Precedence(Partial or Deny, Appeal).
- Declarative constraints are easier to explain to domain experts than alignments.
- Limitation: they can be overly strict for real exceptions and generally do not quantify deviation severity.

**Check 6: Same-timestamp ambiguity.**
- Compute the tie rate by activity pair and by source.
- For any tied pair, require a reliable sequence index. Without one, treat the two events as unordered and do not support an explanation that depends on their order.
- Pass criterion: no explanation term depends on an unordered tied pair.
- Limitation: a sequence index may itself be unreliable. Validate it against a sample of known traces.

**Check 7: Complete-trace dependence.**
- Static: tag every feature with its lineage stage (registration, t0, post-t0, closure).
- Dynamic: run the perturbation test from Check 1.
- Pass criterion: no feature tagged post-t0 or closure appears in a t0 explanation.

**Check 8: Repeated Settlement Decision semantics.**
- The coarse label "Settlement Decision Recorded" hides instance identity. Link each decision to a decision-instance ID and a preceding decision outcome.
- Legitimate pattern: Settlement (Partial or Deny)  Appeal  Settlement (reconsideration). The second decision follows an appeal.
- Anomaly patterns:
  - Settlement  Settlement with no intervening appeal.
  - Appeal with no prior Partial or Deny.
  - An appeal that follows an Approve decision.
- Pass criterion: every repeated settlement is preceded by an appeal that is preceded by a Partial or Deny.

**Check 9: Explanation-to-enabled-set check.**
- For every explanation term at `t0`, verify that the referenced activity is in the enabled or already-observed set.
- This check is the operational version of Check 2 applied to the explanation text rather than the model input.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Four standards should be kept separate:
- **Predictive validity:** does the feature improve calibrated out-of-sample prediction at `t0`, and does this hold across time and subgroups?
- **Causal interpretation:** does intervening on the feature change breach probability? This requires identification (randomization, instruments, or a credible quasi-experiment). Historical SHAP values do not meet this standard.
- **Operational actionability:** can a manager change the feature at `t0` in a way that is feasible, legal, and not harmful? Queue length may be actionable through staffing. Registration complexity is fixed and is not actionable.
- **Fairness review:** does the feature act as a proxy for protected or socioeconomic status, and does it produce disparate error rates or treatment?

A feature can pass one standard and fail another. Region passes predictive validity in some models but fails fairness review and causal interpretation.

**Defensible predictive signals**
- Features: `initial_damage_complexity`; `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`.
- May claim: these features are associated with breach risk in the historical population at `t0`, and they improve calibration in out-of-sample tests.
- May not claim: that they cause breach, or that they identify individual-level mechanisms.

**Process-state or congestion signals**
- Features: `queue_length_at_t0`; the prefix variant (REG-COV); the team assigned at `t0`; the enabled-next-activity set.
- May claim: the claim's state at `t0` (workload and current stage) is associated with breach risk. Congestion is a plausible operational mechanism.
- May not claim: that queue length is the cause of delay, or that reducing queue length will reduce breach rates, without an intervention study.

**Proxy requiring fairness or robustness review**
- Features: `claimant_region`; `claimant_channel`; `initial_reserve` (as a severity proxy); prior claims history.
- May claim: these features carry predictive information correlated with the outcome in the historical data.
- May not claim: that region or channel causes delay, or that they justify differential handling. Any use must be justified through fairness analysis and must be robust to proxy removal.

**Drift and calendar proxies**
- Features: `calendar_week`; any policy-regime or holiday indicator.
- May claim: the historical breach rate varied across calendar periods, and this variation may reflect policy changes or holiday staffing.
- May not claim: that a specific week is intrinsically worse. The effect is confounded with the policy regime and must be monitored over time.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**
- Features: `final_handler_team` (repair to t0 assignment); `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (repair to an as-of-t0 count); `risk_score_current` (repair to a score history with timestamps); `last_resource_role` (repair to `role_at_t0`); `initial_reserve` (verify the as-of-t0 value).
- Until repaired, these features cannot be cited in explanations, even when they carry strong importance.

**Removed outright (outcome or complete-trace)**
- `total_case_duration_hours`; `appeal_seen_anywhere`; `full_trace_variant_id`; `num_document_requests_total`; `final_decision`; all sequence patterns that reference post-t0 events.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Priority order reflects the risk to decisions made from explanations today.

1. **Contain current use (immediate).** Suspend the global explanation table and sequence-pattern explainer for early-warning purposes. Label existing SHAP outputs as full-trace, post-hoc diagnostics. Quarantine the features and patterns marked critical.

2. **Feature-store design with bitemporal records.** Store each feature with event time, availability time (when it became knowable), and an as-of semantic. Require that every feature used at `t0` has availability  `t0`. Enforce point-in-time joins for all warehouse fields, including `final_handler_team` and `risk_score`. Store the source lineage (table, snapshot time, refresh schedule).

3. **Historical feature timestamps and repairs.** Fix the SQL for `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (use `< t0`). Replace the 23:00 document snapshot with event-log counts. Add a history table for risk scores and assignments. Verify `initial_reserve` and `claimant_channel` capture times.

4. **Prefix-only recomputation.** For each claim and each candidate `t0`, truncate the event log and recompute all features from scratch. Compare the recomputed values with the production values. Any difference attributable to post-`t0` events is a leak. Keep the replay code under version control and test it against known cases.

5. **Process-conformance tests.** Run alignments and footprint comparisons on the full log. Set up the declarative checks from Section 2. Track the same-timestamp tie rate. Verify the repeated-settlement semantics using decision-instance IDs. Validate the enabled-set check for every explanation term.

6. **Calibration after feature removal.** Retrain the t0 model without leaked features. Compare reliability curves, Brier score, and expected calibration error on a temporal hold-out and within key subgroups. Recalibrate if probabilities shift. Document the change in predictive performance (the 0.82 and 0.71 values will likely change).

7. **Explanation stability.** Test SHAP or other attributions across bootstrap samples, random seeds, and correlated-feature perturbations. Check sign consistency for features like `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`. Use partial-dependence and interventional (not just observational) attributions where possible. Report stability intervals. Do not cite rankings whose order changes under resampling.

8. **Proxy and fairness analysis.** Evaluate performance and error-rate disparities across region, channel, and other available groups. Run proxy-removal experiments (drop region and channel and measure both predictive loss and fairness change). Document the decision on whether proxies enter the model. Keep fairness review separate from the explanation narrative.

9. **Monitoring for schema and process drift.** Monitor:
   - Schema changes and new or renamed fields.
   - Feature availability lag (distribution of `available_ts  t0`).
   - Same-timestamp tie rate by activity pair.
   - New or unexpected activity transitions (DFG drift, new alignment deviations).
   - Calendar regime changes and policy-change dates.
   - Prediction and feature drift, with alerts when explanation rankings change beyond a threshold.

Across all stages, keep four labels separate in every explanation: predictive (does it help forecast), causal (does intervening change the outcome), actionable (can anyone change it at `t0`), and fair (does it create disparate impact). An explanation is publishable only when its claims are scoped to the labels it has passed.
