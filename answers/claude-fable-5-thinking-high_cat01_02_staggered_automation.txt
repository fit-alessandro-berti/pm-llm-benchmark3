## 1. What is observed

Five branches were observed monthly, January–August 2026. Availability: Alpha and Beta had automated triage available from April, Gamma from July; Delta and Epsilon had not adopted by August. `automation=1` is a branch-month availability flag, not case-level treatment. Actual use: realized treatment intensity, `automated_fast_path_pct`, ramped gradually — Alpha 25%→48%, Beta 27%→50%, Gamma 25%→32% — so early adoption months represent low-dose exposure, and "treated" months are heterogeneous in dose. Selection into rollout: adopters were chosen deliberately; Alpha and Beta were high-volume branches with the worst historical cycle times, and Gamma passed a readiness pilot, so treated and untreated branches differ systematically at baseline. Contemporaneous operational changes: a centralized fraud desk began serving all branches in March (its waiting times unrecorded); a routing campaign raised arrivals to Alpha/Beta by roughly 10% from April and to Gamma by 5% from July; staffing shifted (Alpha −2 FTE in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma −1 in June, Delta +1 in June); a May product change raised the high-complexity share in all branches by 5–8 percentage points; managers could alter local work practices. Measurement limitations: outcomes are branch-month aggregates (medians and p90s of unobserved case distributions); `csat_1to5` comes from surveys with 20–45% response and unknown branch-month response counts; several metrics repeat identical values across consecutive months, suggesting smoothing or infrequent updating; and fraud-desk waiting time is absent from cycle-time attribution.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRIMARY: Staggered-adoption event study with group-time contrasts (Callaway–Sant'Anna-style logic, computed as transparent cohort-by-event-time DiDs given only 5 units; never/not-yet-treated as controls) | Relative time since branch's adoption month; cohorts Apr-2026 (Alpha, Beta) and Jul-2026 (Gamma); never compare newly-treated to already-treated | Delta and Epsilon (never/not-yet treated); Gamma serves as a not-yet-treated control for Alpha/Beta through June | Calendar-month effects (seasonality/product change), branch effects; report contrasts with and without arrivals/FTE and `high_complexity_pct` as descriptive covariates | E1, E3, E4, E6 | Parallel trends between adopters and controls conditional on calendar month; no anticipation; no spillover via fraud desk; adoption timing not driven by transitory shocks | Pre-trend inspection Jan–Mar (and Jan–Jun for Gamma); placebo DiD assigning fake adoption to Delta/Epsilon; leave-one-control-out; permutation of adoption labels across branches | With 5 branches, one idiosyncratic control shock (e.g., Epsilon's complexity surge to 38%) moves the estimate materially; parallel trends untestable beyond 3–6 pre months |
| ALT 1: Comparative interrupted time series per adopter | Branch-specific level and slope break at own adoption date | Each adopter's own pre-period trend, with Delta/Epsilon series subtracted to net out common shocks | Common seasonal profile from controls; arrivals per FTE as load control (descriptive) | E1, E3 per branch (branch-specific effects, no pooling) | Pre-period trend would have continued absent treatment after removing the control-series pattern; no coincident branch-specific shock at adoption date | Fit pre-period only and forecast; check break at placebo dates (e.g., March fraud-desk start); residual autocorrelation | Only 3–6 pre points per branch; May product change and routing campaign coincide closely with Alpha/Beta adoption, making the "break" a bundle |
| ALT 2: Matched/synthetic control per adopter with permutation inference | Binary post-adoption indicator per adopter | Weighted combination of Delta and Epsilon matched on pre-period outcome levels and trends (and complexity mix) | Weights chosen on pre-period outcomes only | E1, E3 | Convex combination of donors reproduces the adopter's counterfactual; donor pool unaffected by treatment (no fraud-desk spillover) | Pre-period fit quality (RMSPE); in-space placebos on Delta/Epsilon; in-time placebos | Donor pool of 2 cannot reproduce Alpha/Beta levels (both donors are structurally faster and smaller); ratio of post/pre RMSPE uninformative with so few units |
| ALT 3 (supporting): Dose–response using `automated_fast_path_pct` within adopters, with calendar-month effects | Continuous intensity, entering with event-time to separate dose from tenure | Within-branch months at different intensities, plus controls pinned at ~4% baseline | Branch and calendar-month effects | E2, E6 | Intensity changes within a branch are unrelated to other within-branch shocks conditional on calendar month — a strong, likely violated assumption | Compare dose-slope estimates across the three adopters for stability; check whether intensity leads or lags outcome improvements | Learning raises intensity exactly when teams also improve practices generally; slope conflates dose with tenure |

The primary strategy is preferable for three reasons. First, adoption is staggered with genuine never/not-yet-treated units, which is exactly the structure event-study designs exploit; Gamma's July adoption additionally lets Gamma serve as a control for Alpha/Beta through June and then contribute its own cleanly timed cohort with six pre-months. Second, the dominant confounders here — the May product change, the demand seasonality that peaks in May–June and recedes in July–August, and the March fraud-desk start — are common calendar shocks, and calendar-time effects estimated from Delta/Epsilon absorb them, which neither a naïve pre/post nor a single-series ITS can do. Third, the design avoids the known pathology of naïve two-way fixed effects under staggered timing and heterogeneous, growing effects: because intensity ramps from ~25% to ~50%, treatment effects are almost certainly dynamic, and a static TWFE coefficient would average early low-dose months with late high-dose months and implicitly use already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma. Explicit cohort-by-event-time contrasts keep every comparison against untreated units and make the dynamic profile visible. The honest limitation is inference: with five branches, clustered standard errors are unreliable, so uncertainty must come from permutation/placebo reasoning and sensitivity to leaving out one control, and all conclusions should be presented as ranges rather than point estimates.

| branch | Δmedian (DiD) | Δp90 (DiD) | Δrework (DiD) | Δescalation (DiD) | Δcsat (DiD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | −5.6 h (−11% of pre) | −6.4 h | −1.4 pp | +1.1 pp | +0.10 |

Arithmetic fact: controls deteriorated sharply over April–June (Delta median +6.0 h, Epsilon +4.4 h vs. Jan–Mar) and partially recovered in July–August; adopters deteriorated less or improved. Interpretation (causal only under parallel trends and no spillover): availability is associated with a 3–6 h (7–11%) relative median reduction and a 6–7 h relative p90 reduction — but note the p90 result is a *relative* improvement against an absolute worsening of ~+10 h in Alpha/Beta; automation did not deliver absolutely faster tails in the early cohort.

**5.3 Treatment intensity rather than the binary flag.** Event-time DiD for the median tracks intensity: Alpha −3.6 h at 25% intensity (April) → −6.6 at 32% → −4.0 at 39% → −5.8 at 46% → −8.1 h at 48% (August); Beta −1.6 → −4.1 → −5.5 → −2.2 → −3.4 h as intensity rose 27%→50%. Dividing each branch's window DiD by its average intensity above the ~4% baseline gives a crude dose slope of roughly −0.07 h (Beta) to −0.18 h (Alpha, Gamma) of median cycle time per percentage point of fast-path share. Extrapolated to a mature ~50% intensity, that is roughly −3 to −8 h. The consistency of Gamma's slope (−0.16 h/pp at only ~28% intensity) with Alpha's is the strongest internal replication in the data. Caveat: intensity growth is confounded with tenure and with the July–August seasonal easing, so the slope is descriptive.

**5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** Adopter pre-trends are close to flat (Alpha 50.4→49.4, Beta 45.4→46.4 over Jan–Mar; Gamma 36.4→45.9 over Jan–Jun, rising like the controls). Controls drifted mixedly (Delta +1.8, Epsilon −1.2 over Jan–Mar) — roughly parallel on average, but three pre-points cannot establish this convincingly. Regression to the mean is a real concern because Alpha/Beta were selected for poor historical cycle times; however, their pre-period levels are stable rather than transiently spiked, and the adopter-control gap *widens over event time* rather than snapping back immediately, which is the opposite of the RTM signature. RTM cannot be fully excluded for the selection into rollout (historical months before January are unobserved), and it plausibly inflates the Alpha estimate more than Gamma's (Gamma was chosen for readiness, not weakness — and shows a similar effect, which is reassuring).

**5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions.** (a) Median cycle: swapping the control from the Delta–Epsilon average to either single control moves the DiDs across branches within roughly −2.6 to −6.4 h; adding the concern that adopters absorbed ~10% extra routed volume and Alpha lost 2 FTE (both biasing *against* adopters, i.e., the true benefit may be larger) versus selection/RTM and manager co-interventions (biasing *toward* benefit), a defensible interval is a 0–13% median reduction with a central tendency around 4–9%. (b) p90: raw change +9.7 h (Alpha/Beta) versus relative DiD −5 to −8 h; given unrecorded fraud-desk waits could worsen true tails for everyone, the honest conclusion is bounded between "modest relative tail protection" and "no tail benefit, possibly absolute harm"; the sign of the absolute tail effect is not established. (c) Escalation: DiD +0.6 to +1.2 pp across all three adopters and both control choices — the most stable finding in the panel; a +0.5 to +1.5 pp increase is a reasonable range. (d) CSAT: DiD +0.06 to +0.11 is within survey noise given unknown response counts; range −0.05 to +0.15, effectively uninformative.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

The observed pattern (consistent relative median improvement, manageable but real escalation increase, unresolved tail behavior, no sign of customer harm) justifies continued expansion, but the same evidence shows why expanding without a designed evaluation would forfeit the only remaining chance to learn: Delta and Epsilon are the last untreated units.

Which branches and assignment: expand to exactly one of Delta or Epsilon first, chosen by explicit randomization (a documented coin flip), with the other scheduled 3–4 months later — a two-step stepped wedge. Randomizing the order removes selection-on-weakness from this cohort and gives one clean not-yet-treated comparator during the first wave. If the triage system permits it, additionally randomize at the case level within the adopting branch for the first 8–12 weeks (eligible cases assigned to automated vs. manual triage), which would deliver genuinely randomized case-level evidence for the first time.

Pre- and post-periods: freeze at least 3 further months of clean pre-period measurement in both branches before the first switch (January–August history already exists, giving 11+ pre-months), and commit to a minimum of 4 post-months per wave before any verdict, because Alpha/Beta show effects maturing over 4–5 months as intensity climbs toward 50%.

Data to collect: case-level event logs with timestamps for every activity, including fraud-desk hand-off and return times; branch-week `automated_fast_path_pct`; specialist and fraud-desk queue lengths and waiting times; FTE and absence records; registered risk class plus a periodic audited complexity sample to detect reclassification; CSAT response counts and case-path linkage.

Spillover monitoring: track fraud-desk arrival volumes and waits by originating branch monthly; if control-branch fraud-desk waits rise materially after the new adoption, the comparison group is contaminated and the analysis must model the desk explicitly.

Guardrails: pre-registered stopping rules — pause automated routing if branch p90 exceeds its pre-period mean by more than 15% for two consecutive months, if rework rises more than 3 percentage points, or if complaint volume or CSAT (with response counts) deteriorates beyond agreed thresholds.

Decision rule for scale-up: after wave one, scale to the second branch on schedule if the median-cycle DiD is at least −3% with p90 and rework guardrails intact; declare full rollout policy only after both waves replicate the Alpha/Beta/Gamma dose–response direction. If guardrails trip, pause and redesign triage thresholds rather than abandoning the program.

# (never/not-yet-treated Delta & Epsilon) evaluated over the SAME calendar

controls = ["Delta", "Epsilon"]
rows = []

    post_c = df[(df.branch.isin(controls)) & (df.mnum >= a)]

for out in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
    fit(f"{out} ~ automated_fast_path_pct + C(branch) + C(month)",
        f"DESCRIPTIVE treatment-intensity model: {out}")

    "this cluster count. Treat all SEs as heuristic; prefer permutation/"

warnings.warn(

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Adopting branches escalated 0.6–1.2 percentage points more cases to specialist/fraud review than non-adopters over matched windows, and their median cycle times moved 3–6 hours below the contemporaneous trajectory of non-adopters, while absolute p90 cycle times worsened by roughly 10 hours in the April cohort and improved by 9 hours in the July cohort.
- `Suggestive:` The bundled rollout (system availability plus accompanying practice changes) reduced median cycle time by roughly 4–9% at realized fast-path usage of 25–50%, with the effect growing as usage matured — supported by replication across two adoption cohorts and a broadly consistent dose–response slope, but resting on short pre-periods and only two comparison branches.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends hold, if the shared fraud desk did not degrade control-branch outcomes, and if manager co-interventions are considered part of the treatment, then automation availability also provided relative tail protection of roughly 5–8 hours at p90 and did not increase rework — conditions that are plausible but unverifiable in this panel.
- `Not supported:` Any single universal statement of the form "automation reduces cycle time by X%," any claim that automation improves customer satisfaction, any claim about absolute tail improvement in the April cohort, and any assumption that effects observed in branches selected for weakness or readiness will transfer at the same magnitude to Delta and Epsilon.
