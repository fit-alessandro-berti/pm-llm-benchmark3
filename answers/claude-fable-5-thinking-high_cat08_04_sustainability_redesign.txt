# Sustainability-Oriented Process Redesign for the Spare-Parts Distributor

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope used: gate-to-customer operational footprint**, covering everything the event log actually instruments:

**Inside the boundary**
- Production energy (`energy_kwh`) on CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, and inspection_cell, including energy consumed by rework loops (Rework production -> CNC production, 390 cases) and emergency production (760 cases).
- Material losses: machining scrap (`material_scrap_kg`), rework-induced scrap, and packaging material (`package_material_kg`), including double packaging caused by Rework packaging (620 occurrences).
- Outbound transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`) across all four route modes, including second shipments from partial-shipment paths and empty return legs of dedicated vans.
- Obsolescence waste from over-batched Made-to-order (MTO) production (the 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days), treated as embodied energy + material written off.

**Outside the boundary (explicitly excluded, with rationale)**
- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier transport: not in the log; I treat scrap kg as a *proxy* for upstream burden (every scrapped kg carries unobserved upstream impact, so scrap reduction is under-credited, never over-credited).
- Facility overheads (warehouse HVAC, lighting), customer-side use phase, and end-of-life of shipped parts.
- Inbound logistics for purchased components.
- Embodied carbon of machines and vehicles (capital goods).

**Assumptions and remaining uncertainty**
- `transport_kgco2e` is assumed to be allocated per order consistently across modes; if consolidated-truck figures are allocated per truck rather than per order, the 35-55% saving claim needs re-verification.
- Energy per order for MTO is assumed to already amortize batch energy over items in the batch; batching evidence (2.8 -> 1.3 kWh/item on CNC_A) is taken as given but its demand-mix sensitivity is unknown.
- The log is flattened by order, so line-item-level attribution (e.g., which line triggered a partial shipment) is uncertain.
- Low model precision around emergency paths and partial shipments (stated in the conformance findings) means effect estimates for disruption-time behavior carry the widest error bars.
- The cancellation path (530 cases, ~3.4% of orders) represents wasted effort whose environmental cost is partially observed (energy already spent) but whose demand-displacement effect (customer reorders elsewhere) is unobservable.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

All indicators are computable from the listed event attributes and should be reported **per segment** (case_type x customer_type) and **per path variant**, not only as global averages, because the case mix (e.g., Critical-downtime at 38 kgCO2e/order vs Standard-stock Regular at 4.2) makes global averages misleading.

**Energy**
- E1: kWh per shipped order and kWh per *good* (non-reworked, non-cancelled, non-obsolete) item, by machine_group and batch_size band. The gap between the two exposes waste (e.g., CNC_B rework path at 32 kWh vs CNC_A batch path at 16 kWh).
- E2: Share of MTO energy consumed in rework loops and emergency production (first-pass energy ratio).

**Transport**
- T1: kgCO2e per order by route_mode and segment; specifically air share of Standard-stock Service-contract shipments (currently inflated: 7.8 kgCO2e/order vs 4.2 for Regular).
- T2: SP1 conformance rate (ground/consolidated used when lead time achievable with >85% probability); baseline violation rate 22% in Standard-stock Service-contract.
- T3: Dedicated-van utilization: orders per van trip and empty-return share; SP2 justification-event presence (baseline: missing in 31%).

**Materials**
- M1: material_scrap_kg per good item by machine_group (CNC_B vs CNC_A) and by first-pass vs rework.
- M2: package_material_kg per order, and Rework-packaging rate (currently 260/6,300 standard + 360/3,830 reinforced entries into rework packaging).
- M3: Obsolescence rate: share of batch-produced items unshipped after 14 days (baseline 6%).

**Rework and quality**
- R1: rework_flag rate by machine/part combination (CNC_B 18% vs CNC_A 9%); inspection -> Rework production loop frequency (470 of 5,180 inspections).
- R2: Damage-related return rate by packaging type and route_mode (Q1 conformance).

**Batching**
- B1: Distribution of batch_size and Batch-wait duration by segment; share of orders exceeding a segment-specific wait cap.
- B2: kWh/item vs batch_size curve drift (to detect whether dynamic batching keeps the energy benefit).

**Service**
- S1: Late-delivery rate vs promised_lead_time_hours by segment (baselines: 4% Critical, 18% MTO Regular); p90 cycle time by segment.
- S2: Cancellation-due-to-delay rate (baseline ~3.4%) and partial-shipment rate (1,010/7,050 picks).

**Resilience**
- Z1: Route-mode diversity per Service-contract lane (R1 policy): number of viable alternative modes actually used per lane per quarter.
- Z2: Expedite/emergency share (expedite_flag, Emergency production 760 cases) as a stress indicator; a rising trend signals eroding buffers.

## 3. Intervention options

**S4 — Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract (replaces unnecessary air)**
- *Target*: Stock -> standard pack -> air variant (5% of orders, 18.5 kgCO2e) and the 22% SP1-violating Service-contract cases; evidence states air is often used "even when promised lead time still allows ground plus priority picking."
- *Mechanism*: Substituting ground (3.6 kgCO2e) for air (18.5) is a ~4-6x per-order transport reduction on every converted order, with zero production-side change.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Neutral if gated on slack: Service-contract median cycle is 18h against ground's 34h median, so the gate must use promised_lead_time_hours and route reliability, not averages. Priority picking compresses internal time to buy transport time. Air stays available (resilience-neutral; supports R1 by keeping two live modes).
- *Monitoring evidence*: SP1 conformance rate (T2), Service-contract late-delivery rate before/after, distribution of remaining-slack-at-ship for converted orders.

**S1 — Green shipping recommender (generalizes S4 across all segments)**
- *Target*: Every Package -> Ship decision point (Ship ground 6,550 / air 1,420 / van 610 / consolidated 830).
- *Mechanism*: Chooses the lowest-emission mode meeting the >85% on-time probability from SP1, using segment, promised lead time, and historical route reliability mined from the log.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Service protected by construction (probabilistic gate). Quality-neutral. Improves resilience if the recommender is trained on disruption periods and always keeps a fallback mode (R1); degrades resilience if it hard-optimizes to a single cheap carrier — the design must include mode-diversity constraints.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Per-recommendation acceptance/override rate, realized on-time probability vs the 85% threshold, kgCO2e per order by segment (T1), and drift checks on route reliability.

**S2 — Dynamic batching with segment-specific waiting caps**
- *Target*: Plan production -> Batch wait (3,020 cases) feeding CNC and heat treatment; the 118h-median, 21%-late CNC_A batch path.
- *Mechanism*: Batch up to the energy sweet spot (CNC_A: 1.3 kWh/item at size 12-20; heat treat cheapest above size 10) but release the batch when the oldest order's due-date slack falls below a cap. Keeps most of the 2.8 -> 1.3 kWh/item gain while cutting the 38h median batch waits that drive lateness and the 1.7x heat-treatment late-delivery risk.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Late delivery should fall for MTO (currently 13-18%); quality-neutral; resilience improves because smaller, cadence-driven batches reduce the obsolescence exposure (6% unshipped) that comes from forecast-driven over-batching. Trade-off: some batches release below optimal size, giving back part of the energy gain.
- *Monitoring evidence*: B1 (wait-cap breaches), B2 (kWh/item vs realized batch size), M3 (obsolescence), MTO late-delivery rate, cancellation rate.

**S3 — Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows**
- *Target*: The CNC_B rework-dominated variant (5% of orders; 160h, 32% late, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
- *Mechanism*: CNC_A's 9% rework rate vs CNC_B's 18% halves the probability of the rework loop, which is the single largest energy-and-scrap multiplier in the log (rework roughly doubles energy and scrap on affected cases). Avoided rework is avoided energy, scrap, and re-inspection.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Quality improves directly. Cycle time improves *in expectation* despite CNC_B being faster first-pass, because the 32%-late figure shows rework loops dominate. Resilience risk: concentrating load on CNC_A creates a queue and a single point of failure — hence the "when queue and due-date slack allow" condition, and CNC_B retained for overflow.
- *Monitoring evidence*: R1 rework rate by machine, CNC_A queue length and utilization, comparative first-pass yield, and late-delivery for orders routed under the new rule.

**S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**
- *Target*: Inspection -> Rework production (470 loops); evidence says inspection_cell queues cause rushed inspections that miss defects, which later surface as packaging rework and customer returns.
- *Mechanism*: Slower, gated inspection for known-risky combinations (e.g., CNC_B + heavy parts) catches defects before packaging and shipping, avoiding the worst-case waste chain: reinforced packaging material + transport emissions + return transport + re-production.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Adds minutes-to-hours at inspection for flagged cases (small cycle-time cost) but reduces downstream returns and the 160h rework variant. Quality clearly improves. Resilience-neutral.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Escaped-defect rate (returns after passed inspection), inspection duration by flag status, rework-loop frequency, Q1 conformance.

**S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders**
- *Target*: Stock -> standard pack -> ground variant (42% of orders, 3.6 kgCO2e) — the largest single flow, so even a 35-55% per-order transport cut on a subset is material in absolute terms.
- *Mechanism*: Moves eligible parcels from individual ground shipments to consolidated trucks (35-55% kgCO2e reduction) at the cost of a 12-24h consolidation wait; a fixed twice-daily cadence caps that wait at ~12h.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Eligibility must be slack-gated: 34h median + up to 12h wait still fits typical Regular promises but not tight ones. Quality-neutral. Resilience improves slightly (an additional routinely-exercised mode satisfies R1's alternative-route requirement).
- *Monitoring evidence*: Consolidation fill rate and realized wait, kgCO2e/order for migrated flow, late-delivery delta for Standard-stock Regular, share of orders bounced back to parcel ground for slack reasons.

**S8 + S9 — Restructure the Critical-downtime channel (shared emergency lanes + demand triage)**
- *Target*: Critical -> emergency production -> dedicated van (8% of orders but 52 kgCO2e each — the highest per-order transport in the log), with vans "often returning empty" and SP2 justification missing in 31% of van cases.
- *Mechanism*: Two combined levers. S9 splits demand: cases mis-labeled as downtime (precautionary urgency) move to the stock/ground exception path (4.8 kgCO2e) or air, immediately shrinking the van population — the 31% missing SP2 justifications suggest a sizable mis-classified share. S8 pools the remaining true-downtime cases into shared emergency courier lanes, cutting empty-return kilometers per order.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: This is the intervention with real service risk: true downtime cases must keep the 15h median / 4% late performance. Mitigation: triage errs toward downtime when ambiguous; dedicated vans remain available for cases failing the shared-lane SLA. Sharing lanes adds coordination dependency (mild resilience cost), offset by keeping the van option live (R1).
- *Monitoring evidence*: SP2 justification-event completeness, Critical-downtime late rate and p90 (30h baseline) split by true-downtime vs precautionary, van trips per order and kgCO2e/order for the segment, reclassification error rate (precautionary cases that turned into real downtime).

**S6 — Packaging-route compatibility check before shipment**
- *Target*: Rework packaging (620 occurrences), caused by packaging incompatible with route_mode or part geometry; also Q1 violations that correlate with return loops.
- *Mechanism*: A pre-ship rule check (part weight/geometry x packaging type x route_mode) eliminates double packaging material and the associated handling delay; enforcing Q1 (reinforced for MTO >8 kg unless inspection certifies low risk) cuts the 40%-reducible damage returns, avoiding return transport and replacement production.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Removes a delay loop, so service improves slightly; quality improves via fewer damage returns. Watch the counter-effect: broader reinforced-packaging use doubles package material — the "unless inspection certifies low damage risk" branch of Q1 should be actively used to right-size packaging.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Rework-packaging rate (M2), damage-return rate by packaging x route (R2), package_material_kg per order trend, Q1 conformance.

**S11 — Availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments**
- *Target*: Partial-shipment path (7% of orders; 79h median, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e — double transport for the same goods, plus 220 backorder waits of which 60 end in cancellation).
- *Mechanism*: Hold-and-consolidate when the missing line is due within the customer's slack, or offer an explicit split/hold choice; each avoided split saves a full second shipment's emissions and packaging.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Holding complete orders can *increase* perceived lateness for the available lines, so the rule must be slack- and segment-aware (never hold Critical-downtime lines). Reduces the cancellation tail (60 cancellations after backorder wait). Quality-neutral; resilience-neutral.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Partial-shipment rate, second-shipment kgCO2e, cancellation rate on the backorder path, customer-choice uptake if offered.

**S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO customers**
- *Target*: MTO Regular (22% of orders, 94h median, 18% late, 18.5 kWh) — the segment where a modestly longer promise unlocks fuller batches and ground/consolidated transport.
- *Mechanism*: Offering a longer, *reliable* promise date lets orders join energy-optimal batches (S2) and low-carbon routes (S1) without violating SP1; it converts hidden lateness (18%) into honest lead times.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Service *as measured against the promise* improves; absolute cycle time lengthens for opt-in customers. Commercial risk if competitors promise faster — hence restrict to Regular (not Service-contract) and make it opt-in.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Uptake rate, late-delivery vs (renegotiated) promise, batch-size distribution for opted-in orders, cancellation rate as an early-warning signal for customer defection.

**S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides** (governance enabler, analyzed here because every gated intervention above depends on it)
- *Target*: All override decisions; baseline gaps are the 22% SP1 violations and 31% missing SP2 justifications.
- *Mechanism*: No direct footprint reduction; it makes violations attributable and auditable, which is what turns S1/S4/S8 gates from aspiration into enforced practice.
- *Service/quality/resilience effect*: Neutral-to-positive; slight administrative overhead. Critically, it *protects* service and resilience by making legitimate overrides fast and legal rather than driving workarounds.
- *Monitoring evidence*: Override rate by policy, reason-code distribution, correlation of overrides with subsequent lateness or returns.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound -> obsolescence and cancellations.** Pushing batch sizes toward the energy optimum (12-20 on CNC_A, >10 in heat treat) raises waiting (4h -> 38h median), the 1.7x heat-treat lateness risk, the 6% obsolescence rate, and ultimately cancellations — and every obsolete or cancelled item wastes 100% of its embodied energy and material. Energy-per-item optimization can therefore *increase* total footprint. Mitigation: S2's wait caps and M3/S2-cancellation monitoring as guardrails.

2. **Transport de-escalation -> expedite backlash.** Converting air to ground/consolidated (S1/S4/S5) shaves slack from every order. When anything upstream slips, more orders will miss the window and be rescued by air or emergency production — the highest-emission responses in the log. A modest rise in expedite_flag share can erase the savings. Mitigation: track Z2 (expedite share) as a first-class KPI alongside T1.

3. **Consolidation wait -> lateness -> partial shipments.** The 12-24h consolidation wait interacts badly with the partial-shipment path: orders held for a truck may split when a line's promise tightens, producing *two* shipments where one individual parcel would have sufficed. Mitigation: S11 and S5 must share one slack calculation.

4. **Packaging displacement: damage vs material.** Enforcing Q1 more strictly (S6) reduces damage returns by 40% but doubles package material on every reinforced order; over-applying reinforced packaging displaces impact from transport (returns) to materials. Conversely, economizing on packaging to cut material rebounds into damage returns — return transport plus replacement production. Mitigation: use Q1's inspection-certified low-risk branch to right-size, and watch M2 and R2 together.

5. **Machine-shift displacement: quality gain -> queue-driven lateness and resilience loss.** Shifting MTO to CNC_A (S3) halves rework but concentrates load; a CNC_A queue lengthens Batch wait, pushes cases toward the late/cancel tail, and a CNC_A outage would then hit most MTO flow at once (R1-style single-point-of-failure, on machines rather than routes). Worse, dispatchers under queue pressure may quietly route back to CNC_B *and* skip the S7 gate. Mitigation: slack-conditioned routing, retained CNC_B overflow, queue monitoring.

6. **Customer-behavior rebound on promise negotiation.** Longer carbon-aware promises (S10) can push impatient Regular customers to order earlier "just in case," inflate order quantities, or split orders across distributors — increasing total shipments and obsolescence in the wider system while our own per-order metrics look greener (impact displaced outside the measurement boundary). Mitigation: monitor order frequency/size per customer and cancellation rates, keep the scheme opt-in.

7. **Triage displacement in the critical channel.** Reclassifying precautionary-urgent cases (S9) to slower paths saves 52-kgCO2e van runs, but each triage false-negative (a true downtime case sent by ground) risks customer machine downtime — a service and possibly *environmental* cost at the customer (idle industrial equipment, emergency re-orders) far exceeding the transport saving. Mitigation: asymmetric triage thresholds and monitoring of reclassification errors.

8. **Governance rebound: gates -> shadow processes.** Stricter SP1/SP2 gates without a fast override path (S12) push staff into workarounds — mislabeling orders as Critical-downtime or as expedites to unlock fast modes — which corrupts exactly the attributes the monitoring depends on. The 31% missing SP2 justifications show this pattern already exists. Mitigation: make compliant overrides cheaper than workarounds, audit label drift.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

I recommend a package of three coordinated layers plus one enabler, sequenced by risk. The logic: **first eliminate pure waste (no service trade-off), then re-time flows within existing slack, then restructure the highest-emission channel under explicit safeguards.**

**Layer 1 — Waste elimination (deploy immediately: S3, S7, S6).** Rework is the largest hidden multiplier in this log: the CNC_B rework variant consumes 32 kWh and 1.8 kg scrap per order — roughly double the normal MTO batch path — and packaging rework plus damage returns add material and transport with zero customer value. Shifting slack-permitting MTO work to CNC_A, gating inspection for risky machine/part combinations, and checking packaging-route compatibility before shipment reduce energy, scrap, packaging material, *and* lateness simultaneously. These are the rare moves with no service downside, so they go first and fund credibility for the rest.

**Layer 2 — Slack-based re-timing (S1/S4, S5, S2, S11).** The log proves substantial slack is being burned unnecessarily: Standard-stock Service-contract orders fly air despite an 18h median cycle and SP1 violated in 22% of cases; consolidated trucks are underused (830 of 8,410 confirmed shipments) despite 35-55% savings. The green shipping recommender with the SP1 85% on-time probability gate, priority-pick-plus-ground for Service-contract stock orders, twice-daily consolidation for non-urgent flow, dynamic batching with segment wait caps, and slack-aware partial-shipment rules all monetize slack rather than service. Crucially, batching caps (S2) and consolidation cadence (S5) are tuned *together* with one shared slack model, because their waits compound.

**Layer 3 — Critical-channel restructure (S9 then S8, piloted).** The Critical-downtime channel is 11% of orders but by far the highest per-order transport (38 avg, 52 on the emergency-van path). I deliberately do **not** recommend simply moving Critical cases to the stock/ground exception (23h, 9% late) even though it is the lowest-emission option — a 9% late rate on downtime cases violates the service intent of this segment and the spirit of the constraints. Instead: split true downtime from precautionary urgency (the 31% missing SP2 justifications indicate real reclassification headroom), pool remaining urgent flow into shared emergency lanes, and *keep dedicated vans as the guaranteed fallback*. Air and vans are not treated as illegitimate: they are the correct mode when slack is genuinely insufficient — the redesign only removes their *unjustified* use.

**Enabler — S12 exception log**, deployed with Layer 2, so every SP1/SP2/Q1 override is fast, reasoned, and auditable. This is what prevents the gates from spawning shadow workarounds and preserves resilience: R1's alternative-route requirement is satisfied because every mode (air, van, ground, consolidated) remains live and routinely exercised somewhere in the network, and S10 (carbon-aware promises for Regular MTO) is added as an opt-in commercial complement once Layer 2 stabilizes batching.

**Why this is balanced.** Environmental impact falls on all three fronts (energy via rework and batching; transport via mode shifts and channel restructuring; materials via scrap, packaging, and obsolescence). Service is protected by construction: every mode downgrade and every batch hold is gated on promised_lead_time_hours and an explicit on-time probability, and the segments with the tightest requirements (Critical-downtime, Service-contract) keep their fast paths. Quality improves rather than being traded away, because rework reduction is itself the biggest energy lever. Resilience is preserved by mode diversity, retained fallbacks (CNC_B, dedicated vans, air), and governed overrides instead of hard prohibitions.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Design: staged rollout with baseline freeze and segment-level difference measurement.**
- Freeze a 3-6 month pre-change baseline of all Section-2 indicators per segment and per path variant (the current tables are the seed).
- Roll out Layer 1 first, then Layer 2 with A/B or phased-by-lane deployment where feasible (e.g., recommender live for half the shipping lanes), then the Layer-3 pilot on a subset of Critical-downtime customers who consent. Compare against contemporaneous control lanes/segments, not just the historical baseline, to avoid seasonality confounds.

**Conformance monitoring (SP1/SP2/Q1).**
- SP1: monthly conformance checking of route-mode choice against the 85% feasibility rule; target the 22% Standard-stock Service-contract violation rate downward; every violation must carry an S12 exception record with a reason code. Alert on violation clusters by lane or dispatcher.
- SP2: automated check that every Dedicated-van and shared-emergency-lane case contains a justification event; drive the 31% missing rate toward zero within two release cycles. Audit a sample of justifications for substance, not just presence, to catch label gaming (rebound risk 8).
- Q1: cross-check packaging events against part weight and inspection certifications; correlate violations with return/rework loops as today, and verify the correlation weakens post-S6.
- Because model precision is low on emergency/partial paths, complement conformance checking with periodic re-discovery of those subprocesses and variant analysis during disruption windows, so new workaround variants are detected rather than silently classified as noise.

**Segment-level service and environmental monitoring.**
- Weekly dashboard per segment: late-delivery vs promise, p90 cycle time, cancellation rate, expedite/emergency share (Z2), kgCO2e/order (T1), kWh/good item (E1), scrap and packaging kg (M1/M2), obsolescence (M3), rework rates (R1), batch-wait cap breaches (B1).
- Hard guardrails with pre-agreed rollback triggers: e.g., if Critical-downtime late rate exceeds its 4% baseline band for two consecutive weeks during the Layer-3 pilot, revert affected lanes to dedicated vans; if expedite share rises above baseline after Layer 2, loosen the consolidation cadence before it cannibalizes savings.
- Rebound watchlist tied to Section 5: obsolescence and cancellations (batching rebound), expedite share (transport rebound), package material vs damage returns (packaging displacement), CNC_A queue length (machine-shift displacement), per-customer order frequency/size (behavioral rebound), triage false-negative count (critical-channel risk).

**Exception governance.**
- The S12 exception log is reviewed in a monthly cross-functional forum (operations, quality, sustainability, key-account management). Outputs: approve recurring exceptions as explicit policy variants, close gaming patterns, and re-tune gate thresholds (e.g., the 85% probability, wait caps) from observed outcomes rather than assumption.
- Quarterly: recompute the batching energy curves (B2) and route-reliability tables feeding S1/S2, because both drift with demand mix and carrier performance; re-run the segment-level case-mix and outcome summary to confirm the case-mix assumptions still hold.
- Annually: revisit the system boundary (Section 1) — if consolidation and triage succeed, the next-largest untracked impacts (inbound logistics, facility energy) may warrant inclusion.
