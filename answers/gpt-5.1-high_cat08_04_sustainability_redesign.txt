### 1. System boundary and assumptions

We define the system boundary from **Receive order** to **Close/Cancel**, covering internal production, packaging, and outbound logistics for spare-part orders. This includes:
- **On-site production and inspection energy**: energy used by `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, and `inspection_cell`, represented by `energy_kwh`.
- **Packaging materials**: standard vs reinforced packaging represented by `package_material_kg`, including additional material from packaging rework.
- **Outbound transport**: emissions represented by `transport_kgco2e` for `parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, and `consolidated_truck`.
- **Rework and re-shipments**: extra energy, transport, and materials associated with `rework_flag`, `Rework production`, `Rework packaging`, and any additional shipments (e.g., partial shipments and backorders).
- **Short-term obsolescence**: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days, leading to effective material waste (L91).

Outside the boundary are:
- Upstream impacts of **raw material extraction and component manufacturing** at suppliers.
- The **use phase** and end-of-life of parts at customer sites, and customer travel.
- Embodied impacts of **buildings, machines, and IT systems**.

We approximate climate impact using the given proxies:
- `energy_kwh` as a direct energy footprint indicator.
- `transport_kgco2e` as outbound logistics emissions.
- `material_scrap_kg` and `package_material_kg` as material use and waste proxies.

Service, quality, and resilience are evaluated using:
- **Service**: median and p90 cycle times, `Late delivery %`, partial shipments, and cancellations.
- **Quality**: `rework_flag`, `Inspection -> Rework production`, `Rework packaging`, and damage-related returns.
- **Resilience**: reliance on emergency production, dedicated_van, availability of alternative route_mode options (R1), and behavior during disruptions.

Uncertainties remain around exact emission factors (energy mix, vehicle load factors), exact mapping from scrap to upstream impacts, and customer behavior changes. Therefore, all estimated effects are **directional** (increase/decrease/no change) rather than precise numeric savings.


### 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

We propose the following indicator set, grounded in the event attributes and observed paths.

- **Energy indicators**
  - **E1**: `Avg energy_kwh per order` by `case_type`, `customer_type`, `machine_group`, and main path (e.g., MTO batched vs immediate CNC + air).
  - **E2**: `Energy_kwh per produced item` vs `batch_size` for `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, and `heat_treat` to quantify batch-efficiency curves (L88–L90).
  - **E3**: Share of orders using high-energy path `MTO -> CNC immediately -> reinforced pack -> air` vs efficient batched ground paths.

- **Transport indicators**
  - **T1**: `Avg transport_kgco2e per order` by `route_mode` (`parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`) and segment.
  - **T2**: Share of orders with `route_mode = parcel_air` or `dedicated_van` split by **slack** (difference between `promised_lead_time_hours` and actual cycle time) and SP1/SP2 conformance.
  - **T3**: Share of orders on `consolidated_truck` vs `parcel_ground` and their contribution to total `transport_kgco2e`.

- **Material and packaging indicators**
  - **M1**: `Avg material_scrap_kg per order` by `case_type`, `machine_group`, and path (e.g., CNC_B vs CNC_A, batched vs immediate).
  - **M2**: `Avg package_material_kg per order` by packaging type (standard vs reinforced) and segment, including rework.
  - **M3**: Rate of **packaging rework** (events `Rework packaging`) and associated additional `package_material_kg` and re-shipments.
  - **M4**: **Q1 violation rate**: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg that do not have reinforced packaging and lack an inspection-based low-risk certification.

- **Rework and return indicators**
  - **R1**: `Rework loop rate` = share of orders with `rework_flag = true` or path `Inspection -> Rework production`.
  - **R2**: Rework share attributable to `CNC_B` vs `CNC_A` and to specific high-risk part families.
  - **R3**: Damage-related return and re-shipment rate by combination of `machine_group`, packaging type, and `route_mode`.

- **Batching indicators**
  - **B1**: Distribution of `batch_size` and corresponding `Batch wait` by `machine_group`, segment, and path.
  - **B2**: `Late delivery %` for cases with **large batches** (e.g., batch_size ≥ 12) vs small batches, especially for `heat_treat` where late-risk is 1.7x higher (L90).
  - **B3**: **Obsolescence rate**: share of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (L91) by product family and segment.

- **Service indicators**
  - **S1**: Median and p90 **cycle time** and `Late delivery %` by segment (Standard-stock Regular/Service-contract, MTO Regular/Service-contract, Critical-downtime).
  - **S2**: **Partial shipment rate** and count of backorders per order, plus their incremental cycle time and emissions (`transport_kgco2e`).
  - **S3**: Rate of **emergency production** events by segment and slack.

- **Quality indicators**
  - **Q1**: Combined **rework and return rate** per path (e.g., MTO via CNC_B vs CNC_A, packaging type, and route_mode).
  - **Q2**: Correlation between **Q1 policy violations** and rework/return loops.
  - **Q3**: Share of customer complaints and damage-related claims by packaging and route combination.

- **Resilience indicators**
  - **Z1**: For Service-contract customers, share of cases where at least **two route_mode options** remain feasible at planning time, in line with R1.
  - **Z2**: Dependence on `dedicated_van` and `parcel_air` during disruptions (e.g., events with many emergency productions and partial shipments).
  - **Z3**: Time and performance degradation during disruption periods (late deliveries, cancellations) vs normal time, segmented by mix of `route_mode` (ground, air, truck, van).


### 3. Intervention options

Below we analyze at least eight of the candidate interventions, focusing on target segments/paths, environmental mechanisms, service/quality/resilience impacts, and required evidence.

#### S1. Green shipping recommender (slack-aware SP1 enforcement)

- **Target path or segment**: All Standard-stock and MTO orders, with special focus on **Standard-stock Service-contract** where SP1 is violated in 22% of cases and air is overused (L100).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: A recommender uses `promised_lead_time_hours`, historical transit times, and customer segment to propose the **lowest-impact `route_mode`** (ground or consolidated_truck) that still keeps on-time probability ≥ 85% (SP1). Air and dedicated_van are reserved for genuinely urgent or low-slack cases.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Slightly longer transport times for some orders that previously defaulted to air, but late-delivery remains within contractual thresholds due to explicit slack modeling. By keeping both low- and high-speed options configured, resilience is preserved and route diversity (R1) can improve.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - SP1 conformance over time and by planner.
  - `route_mode` mix by segment and slack (pre/post recommender).
  - `transport_kgco2e per order` and late-delivery rates.
  - Override events where planners reject the green recommendation and associated reasons (see S12).

#### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with waiting-time caps

- **Target path or segment**: **Made-to-order** paths going through `Batch wait -> CNC_A/heat_treat -> inspection`, especially the efficient path where energy is low for batches ≥ 12 but waiting is long (L81, L88–L90).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Instead of static large batches, use **dynamic batching** with segment-specific maximum `Batch wait`:
  - Critical-downtime and Service-contract MTO: small batches, low waiting caps.
  - Regular MTO: allow larger batches and longer waits where promised lead time safely accommodates it.
  This keeps most energy-per-item savings while avoiding extreme delays and unnecessary obsolescence.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: For Regular MTO, median cycle time may increase somewhat but remains within promised lead times; for Service-contract MTO, caps prevent excessive wait and late deliveries. Resilience may improve by having explicit rules that can be relaxed under disruptions rather than ad-hoc decisions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - `batch_size`, `Batch wait`, and `energy_kwh per item` distributions by segment.
  - `Late delivery %` and cycle time distributions pre/post caps.
  - Obsolescence rate (items not shipped within 14 days) vs batch size.
  - Incidence of emergency production triggered by long batch waits.

#### S3. Shift Made-to-order work from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows

- **Target path or segment**: High-impact path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`, which currently has 32 kWh energy, 1.8 kg scrap, 160h median cycle time, and 32% late deliveries (L83).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: For MTO orders with sufficient due-date slack, preferentially route to `CNC_A` instead of `CNC_B`. CNC_A has **lower rework rate** (9% vs 18% for CNC_B) and better energy efficiency at scale (L88). CNC_B remains available for rush cases and specific geometries but is no longer the default.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Slightly higher utilization and possible modest queue increase at CNC_A, partially offset by fewer rework loops and less scrap, leading to better reliability. Late-delivery risk decreases for cases moved off CNC_B. Resilience is preserved by retaining CNC_B as surge/emergency capacity.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Share of MTO orders on `CNC_A` vs `CNC_B` over time.
  - `rework_flag`, `material_scrap_kg`, and rework loops per machine_group.
  - `energy_kwh per order` and late-delivery statistics by machine_group and segment.
  - Emergency production events linked to machine capacity constraints.

#### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground when slack exists

- **Target path or segment**: **Standard-stock Service-contract** orders currently shipped by air even though ground plus internal prioritization would still meet promised lead time (L100, SP1 violations).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Introduce a rule/routing policy:
  - If the combination of **priority picking** and `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` still provides >85% on-time probability, use that instead of `parcel_air`.
  - Reserve air only for low-slack or disrupted cases.
  This substantially reduces `transport_kgco2e` in a relatively large and premium segment.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Slight increase in median delivery time for affected orders but still within agreed SLAs; late-delivery rates should remain stable with better internal prioritization. Resilience is maintained because air remains available as an escalation route, consistent with R1 and SP1.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Before/after share of `parcel_air` vs ground/truck for this segment.
  - `transport_kgco2e per order`, cycle time, and `Late delivery %`.
  - SP1 violation rate over time.
  - Number and reasons for overrides that still choose air.

#### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders

- **Target path or segment**: **Standard-stock Regular** orders primarily on `Stock -> standard pack -> ground` with low urgency and relatively low transport impact per order (L78).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Route eligible non-urgent stock orders to **consolidated_truck** with fixed departure windows (e.g., twice daily), instead of individual `parcel_ground` shipments. L97 suggests consolidated trucks reduce `transport_kgco2e` by 35–55% vs individual parcels.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Additional 12–24h consolidation wait for some orders; because these are Regular, promised lead times can be configured accordingly so late-delivery risk stays acceptable. Resilience risk increases moderately if consolidated lanes are disrupted, so design must keep parcel_ground as a backup route (R1).
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Share of volume and orders on `consolidated_truck` vs `parcel_ground`.
  - Cycle time and `Late delivery %` for consolidated vs non-consolidated flows.
  - `transport_kgco2e` per order and per unit shipped.
  - Behavior during disruptions (how often shipments are reassigned to air or vans).

#### S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment

- **Target path or segment**: All orders going through `Package standard` or `Package reinforced`, particularly those that later hit `Rework packaging` or see damage-related returns. Heavy Made-to-order parts and air shipments are key.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Add compatibility checks (geometry vs packaging, packaging vs `route_mode` constraints) **before the main packaging step**:
  - Flag incompatible combinations that would cause packaging rework or damage in transit.
  - Suggest correct packaging earlier (e.g., reinforced for heavy MTO if not certified low-risk under Q1).
  This reduces double-packaging, packaging scrap, and re-shipments.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Slightly longer handling time in the packaging decision step but fewer `Rework packaging` loops and customer returns. Overall cycle time and service improve on paths that currently suffer from packaging-related rework.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Frequency and share of `Rework packaging` and related path variants.
  - `package_material_kg` and `material_scrap_kg` before vs after implementation.
  - Damage-related return and re-shipment rates by packaging and `route_mode`.
  - Q1 violation rate and its correlation with returns.

#### S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path or segment**: Paths where `Inspection -> Rework production` loops are common, especially for `CNC_B` and specific part families (L92).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Introduce **targeted quality gates** at inspection:
  - Additional checks or stricter criteria for combinations historically associated with high rework or returns.
  - Potential secondary review for these high-risk cases.
  This catches defects earlier, reducing scrap, rework energy, and the need for re-shipments.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Potential for slightly longer queues in `inspection_cell`, but overall fewer rework loops and lower cycle times on previously problematic variants. Quality and customer satisfaction improve, and resilience benefits from fewer incidents during disruptions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Rework and return rates by machine_group and part family.
  - Inspection queue and processing times after gate introduction.
  - `material_scrap_kg` and `energy_kwh` spent on rework.
  - Late-delivery risk for affected variants.

#### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes

- **Target path or segment**: Critical-downtime Service-contract cases on `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`, with 52 kgCO2e transport per order (L84).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Implement **shared emergency courier lanes**:
  - Pool urgent shipments from multiple customers/regions into multi-stop emergency routes instead of single-order dedicated vans that often run empty (L96).
  - Maintain dedicated vans for true line-stop events where even small delays are unacceptable.
  This reduces per-order `transport_kgco2e` while preserving service for the highest criticality cases.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: For precautionary urgent orders, service impact is minimal (they remain fast, but not necessarily faster than required); for true downtime, dedicated vans are retained. Resilience can improve if shared lanes provide more paths and carrier flexibility in disruptions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Share and count of Critical cases shipped via dedicated_van vs shared emergency lanes.
  - `transport_kgco2e` per Critical order before and after.
  - Downtime-related penalties or proxy metrics where available.
  - SP2 justification event coverage and conformance.

#### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path or segment**: All `Critical-downtime Service-contract` orders (L33), currently co-mingling true line-stops and high-risk but non-stopped situations.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: At order intake or soon after, capture an attribute distinguishing:
  - **True downtime**: confirmed line-stop or severe impact—keeps access to emergency production and highest-speed routes (including dedicated vans).
  - **Precautionary urgent**: high-risk but not yet stopped—steered towards lower-emission but still reliable options (e.g., ground or consolidated truck when slack exists, or shared emergency lanes).
  This ensures carbon-intensive options are reserved for genuinely critical situations.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Service to true downtime cases is protected and potentially improved through clearer prioritization. Precautionary urgent orders may experience slightly longer delivery times, but still within acceptable risk levels. Resilience improves by clarifying priorities during disruptions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Volume and performance metrics (cycle time, late-delivery) for each Critical subtype.
  - `route_mode` choices and `transport_kgco2e` by subtype.
  - Emergency production and cancellation patterns.

#### S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO customers

- **Target path or segment**: `Made-to-order Regular` segment (22% of cases) with high energy (18.5 kWh) and notable transport emissions when air is used (L31, L82).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: At order entry, present options such as:
  - **Green standard date**: allows batching (CNC_A + heat_treat) and ground/consolidated transport, minimizing `energy_kwh` per item and `transport_kgco2e`.
  - **Fast date**: may use immediate CNC and air, with higher environmental impact and possibly higher price.
  This shifts part of demand toward lower-impact variants by aligning expectations at promise-date negotiation.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Customers choosing green dates have longer promised lead times but more reliable deliveries and potentially lower costs; urgent customers retain access to fast options. Resilience improves via smoother load on constrained resources (CNC, heat_treat, emergency paths).
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Uptake rate of green vs fast options and their respective paths.
  - Associated `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, and `transport_kgco2e`.
  - `Late delivery %` vs promised lead times.
  - Changes in emergency production and expedite_flag usage.

#### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path or segment**: Path `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder`, which has 79h median cycle time, 24% late deliveries, higher energy (1.4 kWh), and higher transport emissions due to second shipments (L80).
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Tighten **availability-check rules** so that:
  - Partial shipments are primarily used for **Critical-downtime** and key Service-contract lines where downtime risk justifies extra shipments.
  - For Regular and non-critical items, favor waiting a short time to ship complete or near-complete orders, or offer substitution where acceptable.
  This reduces secondary shipments and associated packaging and transport impacts.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: Some Regular customers will wait longer to receive all items, but with clearer communication and adjusted promised dates, perceived service remains acceptable. Resilience improves because capacity for partial shipments is freed for the most critical cases during disruptions.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Partial shipment frequency and backorder counts per order by segment.
  - `transport_kgco2e` per order including backorders.
  - `Late delivery %`, cancellations, and complaint rates before vs after changes.
  - Shift in emergency production and express transport triggered by stockouts.

#### S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides

- **Target path or segment**: Any case where operators override green defaults or policies:
  - SP1: choosing air or van despite feasible ground/truck.
  - SP2: using dedicated_van without clear Critical justification.
  - Q1: deviating from reinforced/standard packaging rules.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**: Require a **structured exception event** whenever SP1/SP2/Q1 are overridden, recording reason codes (e.g., disruption, customer escalation, new risk evidence). This does not force decisions but makes high-impact choices transparent, enabling targeted improvements over time.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**: No immediate operational impact; increases accountability and learning. Over time, unjustified high-impact exceptions should fall, preserving justified ones for resilience and service.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:
  - Frequency and distribution of exceptions by segment, planner, and reason.
  - Associated `transport_kgco2e`, `energy_kwh`, and quality outcomes.
  - Trends in SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance and correlation with service and resilience metrics.

### 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching vs service and obsolescence**: Aggressive batching at CNC and heat_treat can reduce energy per item but increase `Batch wait`, raising late-delivery risk (L90) and potentially triggering more expedite_flag and air shipments to recover service. Large batches also increase obsolescence (L91), causing scrap of unsold items and offsetting material and energy gains.

2. **Reducing air and van usage vs emergency workarounds**: Strict SP1/SP2 enforcement that aggressively limits air and dedicated_van can push planners to use more emergency production, partial shipments, or rush inspections to meet promised dates. These workarounds increase `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, and total `transport_kgco2e` through extra setups, rework, and additional shipments.

3. **Packaging strength vs materials and transport**: Reinforced packaging cuts damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO parts (L98) but doubles `package_material_kg` and slightly increases shipment weight, which can increase `transport_kgco2e`. Overly conservative application of reinforced packaging to low-risk parts could therefore raise overall material and transport impact despite fewer returns.

4. **Inspection quality gates vs bottlenecks and shortcuts**: Strong inspection gates for high-risk combinations can reduce scrap and returns, but if inspection_cell becomes a bottleneck, local teams might develop shortcuts or bypasses under pressure (e.g., skipping the gate during peaks). This can reintroduce defects downstream and cause new rework loops or returns, shifting problems rather than eliminating them.

5. **Reducing partial shipments vs late deliveries and cancellations**: Reducing partial shipments lowers emissions from second shipments, but if not coupled with better inventory and promise-date management, more orders may breach promised dates, driving up `Cancel due to delay` rates and shifting demand to other suppliers who may use more carbon-intensive logistics overall.

6. **Customer behavior under carbon-aware promise negotiation**: Some Regular MTO customers offered green, later promise dates may still escalate to sales or service teams for faster deliveries, resulting in last-minute express shipments and emergency production. Others might split orders across multiple suppliers to hedge risk, fragmenting logistics and possibly increasing total system-wide emissions.

7. **Consolidated trucks vs resilience**: Increasing reliance on consolidated_truck reduces per-order transport emissions but concentrates risk in fewer, larger departures. Disruptions to truck lanes may then force last-minute switches to parcel_air or dedicated_van to protect service, temporarily increasing `transport_kgco2e` and challenging R1 if alternate routes are not kept active.

8. **Critical segmentation errors**: Misclassifying precautionary urgent orders as true downtime may cause overuse of emergency production and high-impact transport, while misclassifying true downtime as precautionary may delay recovery and increase downtime costs. Both misclassifications can distort the intended balance between sustainability and resilience.


### 6. Balanced recommendation

A balanced redesign should integrate **segment-specific routing**, **smart batching**, and **quality/packaging improvements**, rather than prioritizing the absolute lowest-emission options at the expense of service or resilience.

- For **Standard-stock** and other non-critical stock flows, prioritize:
  - **S1** (green shipping recommender) and **S4** (ground + priority pick for Service-contract with slack) to systematically replace unjustified air with ground or consolidated truck while respecting SP1 and segment SLAs.
  - **S5** (twice-daily consolidated trucks) for Regular stock orders to capture consolidation benefits where promised lead times allow.
- For **Made-to-order** flows, emphasize:
  - **S2** (dynamic batching with caps) and **S3** (shift from CNC_B to CNC_A with slack) to cut energy and scrap while bounding late-delivery risk.
  - **S7** (inspection quality gate) to stabilize quality and reduce rework loops from high-risk machine/part combinations.
  - **S10** (carbon-aware promise negotiation) so Regular MTO customers can voluntarily choose greener options with adjusted promise dates.
- For **Critical-downtime**:
  - Combine **S8** (shared emergency courier lanes) and **S9** (true vs precautionary split) so that true downtime continues to receive high-speed, high-impact options when necessary, while precautionary urgent orders are routed to lower-impact but reliable modes.

Across all segments, implement:
- **S6** (packaging-route compatibility checks) and **S11** (partial-shipment reduction) to eliminate avoidable rework, excess packaging, and second shipments.
- **S12** (sustainability exception log) to ensure SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides are transparent, enabling continuous governance without blocking justified resilience- or service-driven decisions.

This package focuses high-carbon options where they are genuinely needed (true downtime, real low-slack situations) and systematically reduces avoidable emissions in high-volume, lower-criticality flows, while explicitly controlling for cycle time, late-delivery risk, quality, and resilience.


### 7. Validation and governance plan

- **Indicator and log configuration**:
  - Extend the event log to include additional attributes/events: recommender-suggested vs chosen `route_mode`, Critical subtype (true vs precautionary), applied batching rule, packaging decision reason, and exception codes for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides.
  - Recompute the indicators from Section 2 on a rolling basis, segmented by case_type, customer_type, and key variants, to compare pre- and post-redesign performance.

- **Conformance monitoring for SP1/SP2/Q1**:
  - Implement conformance dashboards checking:
    - SP1: proportion of orders where ground or consolidated_truck could have met promised lead time with ≥85% probability but air/van was used.
    - SP2: dedicated_van usage without a Critical justification event.
    - Q1: heavy Made-to-order parts lacking reinforced packaging or inspection-based low-risk certification.
  - Track these violations by segment, planner, and timeframe and correlate them with `transport_kgco2e`, `material_scrap_kg`, rework, and late-delivery outcomes.

- **Segment-level service and resilience monitoring**:
  - For each segment (Standard-stock Regular/Service-contract, MTO Regular/Service-contract, Critical true vs precautionary), monitor:
    - Median and p90 cycle time, `Late delivery %`, `Cancel due to delay`, partial shipment and backorder rates.
    - Emergency production usage and mix of `route_mode` (including shared emergency lanes).
  - During disruption windows (e.g., carrier outages), analyze adherence to R1 by checking whether at least one alternative route remains used in practice and whether exception logs justify deviations.

- **Exception governance via S12**:
  - Use the sustainability exception log to run regular cross-functional reviews (operations, sales, sustainability).
  - Identify patterns of frequent unjustified overrides (e.g., recurring air shipments for non-critical, high-slack cases) and adjust policies, thresholds, or training accordingly.
  - Preserve or formalize overrides that consistently protect service and resilience with limited environmental downside.

- **Phased rollout and A/B testing**:
  - Roll out key interventions in phases or via A/B testing where feasible (e.g., S1/S4/S5 in selected regions, S2/S3/S7 in particular plants, S8/S9/S10 for selected customers).
  - Compare treatment vs control groups on energy, transport, scrap, rework, cycle time, late deliveries, and resilience performance during disruptions.
  - Use these findings to tune batching caps, routing thresholds, and customer-facing options before full-scale deployment.

- **Continuous improvement loop**:
  - Establish quarterly reviews of sustainability, service, quality, and resilience KPIs using the enriched event log.
  - Update recommender models, batching rules, packaging criteria, and exception policies based on evidence, ensuring that environmental gains continue to accrue without drifting into unacceptable service or resilience degradation.
