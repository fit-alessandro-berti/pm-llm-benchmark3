# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## Emergency approvals and emergency escalation loops

1. **Benign explanation:** Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo are specialized emergency channels with established urgent workarounds.
   - Supporting evidence: contemporaneous emergency records, clinical or facilities work orders, delegated authority, independent approval, and comparable outcomes for emergency and non-emergency cases.
   - Refuting evidence: emergency flags added after the approval, no underlying operational need, approvals made before the emergency was known, or repeated use outside recognized emergency categories.

2. **Benign explanation:** The short times reflect a policy-backed rush workflow, template approvals, or legitimate parallel work.
   - Supporting evidence: documented service levels, preapproved emergency limits, evidence that quotes or price checks were completed when practicable, and no override of amount thresholds.
   - Refuting evidence: the same effective human both created and approved without authority, templates contained material errors, or retrospective review merely repeated the original rationale.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- **Actor identity and authority:** SSO, application audit logs, delegation records, or account-directory data confirming that the same human created and approved the PO or changed the bank data and approved payment without authority; shared credentials; assistants entering approvals outside their permissions.
- **Emergency legitimacy:** Override records created after the procurement decision; absent or inconsistent emergency reasons; no clinical/facilities work order; no evidence the urgency existed when approval was made; retrospective justification authored by the initiator; failure to find the original timely review.
- **Vendor-bank controls:** No migration or maintenance ticket; changes outside the approved platform window; new beneficiary accounts without verified callbacks; callbacks to numbers introduced in the change request; unexplained activity across regions; payments released immediately to the changed account.
- **Payment evidence:** Missing PO, contract, goods receipt, or three-way match; duplicate invoices; invoices just below authority thresholds; payments made despite unresolved match exceptions; no evidence that the vendor received the goods or the intended beneficiary received the funds.
- **Source-system integrity:** Manual files without version history, uploader identity, or approval; original timestamps inconsistent with server logs; evidence that records were backdated or manually inserted to appear timely.
- **Collaboration-specific indicators:** Emails or chat messages directing reciprocal approvals, shared devices or credentials, coordinated case splitting, unexplained rotation among participants, or multiple unrelated cases with the same nonstandard sequence. These would raise concern but would still require testing alternative operational explanations.
- **Control impact:** A confirmed unauthorized payment, unrecovered funds, deliberate policy override, or evidence that the same control exception occurred outside V-77x or the documented emergency population.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- **Emergency evidence:** Contemporaneous work orders or incident records, documented delegation, evidence that the urgency existed before approval, independent approval within authority, and a timely retrospective justification by a different named employee.
- **Original audit trails:** Immutable source-system records showing that the twelve spreadsheet records were migrated from timely, independently reviewed justifications; scripts and manifests explaining identical timestamps.
- **Actor mapping:** A validated mapping table proving that `legacy_erp` bot/human collisions and the Approver_Gita shared account caused false attribution, together with reliable individual audit logs.
- **V-77x controls:** A platform-migration ticket, authorized before/after bank records, a callback to a previously verified vendor contact, confirmation that beneficiary ownership remained unchanged, and evidence that no affected invoice or payment was actually released.
- **Role design:** Approved job descriptions and RACI showing why VendorMaster_Lena or VendorMaster_Marc performs both vendor check and bank maintenance while payment approval remains independently controlled.
- **Operational normality:** Workload rosters, staffing and SLA data, standard templates, and case mix showing that the routine pairs and automated feedback loops match expected operating design.
- **Access and outcome testing:** Access logs showing appropriate role use and no anomalous logins or bulk exports, vendor confirmation of legitimate payments, reconciliation showing no loss, and targeted sampling finding no unexplained control exceptions.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Internal audit identified several control exceptions in procurement-to-payment processing, but the available event log does not establish misconduct or collusion. The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim population linked to vendor family V-77x: it combines bank-detail changes and payment approvals, nine reported same-resource four-eyes cases, a 16-case fast-approval subgroup with a 17-minute median, 83% manual uploads, 72% vendor concentration, and a high share of high-value cases. These facts warrant targeted validation because they affect payment integrity, although a legitimate banking-platform migration and a missing maintenance-ticket link provide plausible benign explanations.

The emergency Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan–Manager_Hugo populations are also concerning. Their 8–10 minute approval times and repeated escalation loops are consistent with documented emergency specialization, but 42 PO creation/approval four-eyes exceptions, late retrospective justifications, and 17 same-resource justifications require targeted testing. Twelve retrospective records appear to have been migrated from a spreadsheet, and shared-account and legacy-ERP mapping errors may have inflated identity-based violations.

Accordingly, recommendations are to reconcile V-77x bank changes to platform-migration tickets and payment records; validate emergency authority, underlying demand, and independent retrospective review; and review only exception/high-value cases in the other clusters. Audit should test actor identity, original and system timestamps, delegation records, access logs, vendor callbacks, invoice/contract/receipt evidence, and source-system lineage. Case-level conclusions should remain open until those records are obtained. High co-working frequency alone will not be treated as evidence of collusion.
