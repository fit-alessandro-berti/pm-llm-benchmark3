## 1. Materiality Framework

**The highest priorities are CL-602 and CL-604, followed by CL-610 and CL-609—not the cases with the highest alignment costs.** These four cases involve EUR 592,500 in nominal claim value. That is the value associated with the prioritized control investigations, **not an estimate of fraud, realized loss, or recoverable funds**.

The observation boundary has three layers: the core event log, corroborating business evidence such as adjuster reports and portal messages, and the combined requirements of the process model and P1–P6. A mismatch in one layer does not automatically establish a failure in another.

The supplied alignments remain authoritative diagnostic inputs. A synchronous move matches a recorded activity to the model; it does not independently prove that the activity was effective, authorized, or timely. A visible model move identifies an unmatched model requirement, not proof that nobody performed the activity. Hidden model moves are fitting routing behavior. These distinctions also match PM4Py’s documented alignment semantics. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

**Severity scale**

| Severity                                                          | Interpretation                                                                                                                                              | Application here                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **5 — Critical**                                                  | A severe payment-control failure, or a material assurance failure around a highly consequential payment, requiring immediate containment and investigation. | CL-602’s unsupported fraud control on EUR 480,000; CL-604’s payment before recorded approval. |
| **4 — Major**                                                     | A substantive policy bypass or invalid approval route, without the same combination of exposure and aggravating evidence as severity 5.                     | CL-609 and CL-610.                                                                            |
| **3 — Material uncertainty or bounded control weakness**          | An unresolved evidence, correlation, or customer-communication problem requiring targeted investigation.                                                    | CL-606, CL-608, and CL-612.                                                                   |
| **2 — Limited operational impact; governance/data repair needed** | A likely scope or labeling problem, with no demonstrated substantive payment-control failure.                                                               | CL-607 and CL-611, subject to verification.                                                   |
| **1 — No material business deviation established**                | Fitting behavior or well-supported technical noise. This is the lowest permitted score, not an assertion that a deviation exists.                           | CL-601, CL-603, and CL-605.                                                                   |

Severity considers the **control objective**, nominal amount and plausible exposure, whether funds have already moved, reversibility, the order and freshness of controls, potential customer or regulatory harm, recurrence, evidence reliability, and model applicability. For example, a mutable notification link is not automatically a EUR 250,000 payment loss, and seventy-four retry artifacts are not automatically more serious than one unsupported high-value payment.

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Confidence concerns how strongly the evidence supports a particular conclusion. In CL-602, confidence is high that retained fraud-screen evidence is absent, but lower that screening never occurred. That uncertainty does not make the large, already-paid claim a low-priority investigation. Severity is provisional and may change when evidence is authenticated; it should not be mechanically multiplied by a confidence score.

Two interpretive assumptions matter:

* **Timing:** P1 is evaluated using approval-time facts; P2 must hold before payment. CL-610 clearly triggers P2 at payment, but whether the bank change preceded Auto Approve is not supplied. Its historical P1 verdict is therefore conditional.
* **Notification:** For payable claims, I use payment as the P4 clock origin; for terminal nonpayment outcomes, disposition finalization. The policy owner should formalize these anchors. Except for CL-608’s stated +3 hours, the supplied data does not establish notification elapsed time.

P2 is not an exhaustive list of circumstances in which Auto Approve is prohibited. **CL-609 does not trigger P2, but fails P1.** Under the supplied payable-claim model, the remaining policy-compatible route is Fraud Screen followed by Manual Approve.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

For a supplied alignment \(\gamma\), the configured cost is

$$
C(\gamma)=\sum_{m\in\gamma}c(m)
          =N_{\text{log-only}}+N_{\text{visible model-only}},
$$

because visible deviations cost 1 and synchronous/hidden moves cost 0. It measures discrepancies between an observed activity sequence and a model execution under the chosen representation and costs. PM4Py likewise reports alignment cost relative to the supplied cost function. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

There is no defensible universal transformation from that scalar to business severity.

**Equal cost, radically different consequences.** CL-601 and CL-604 both cost 3. The former contains interface telemetry; the latter contains a payment-ordering failure and an unsupported fraud-screen requirement. CL-602 and CL-603 both cost 1, despite the difference between an unsupported control on EUR 480,000 and a retry record. Therefore, severity cannot be a function of raw cost alone.

**Zero cost does not establish policy compliance.** CL-605 is fitting and has supported automatic eligibility. CL-609 and CL-610 are also fitting at the control-flow level but violate, or fail to evidence satisfaction of, applicable policy conditions. The unguarded XOR admits Auto Approve without checking amount, risk, or bank history. Similarly, synchronizing Notify Claimant does not test the 24-hour deadline.

**The cost function expresses technical preferences, not monetary exposure.** Multiplying all visible costs by 100 changes every score’s numerical scale without changing the business events. Giving Fraud Screen a larger model-move cost may influence which diagnostic alignment is selected, but cannot make an unguarded tree evaluate P1. Weighted costs can improve explanation selection; they are not a validated loss model.

**Scope can dominate edit distance.** CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4 partly because the model insists on finishing a payable path. That is an inappropriate obligation for legitimate denial or withdrawal. Nevertheless, CL-612’s notification requirement must be assessed separately: identifying a scope mismatch does not make every associated discrepancy harmless.

**Representation and activity granularity affect cost.** CL-611’s potential rename produces two visible moves for one underlying work item. CL-601’s three interface events produce three moves despite no extra business activity. Splitting a single control into several event labels can increase discrepancy counts without proportionately increasing business risk.

**Duplicated records are not necessarily duplicated business acts.** CL-603 and the 74-case connector pattern concern attempts sharing a message ID. They must not automatically be reported as 74 extra customer-notification decisions or 74 customer-harm incidents.

**External evidence changes interpretation without changing the supplied core alignment.** CL-606’s report is affirmative evidence of work absent from the core. CL-608’s message may establish timely communication, but weak linkage reduces confidence in attribution. Their identical one-move costs encode neither the strength nor the weakness of that evidence.

**Hidden moves are structurally different from missing visible work.** CL-605’s tau is fitting routing behavior, not an unrecorded employee task. Counting hidden transitions as missing business activities would create false noncompliance. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

**An alignment repair is not a real-world reversal.** CL-604’s `L(Pay Claim)` and `M(Pay Claim)` relocate one observed payment in the model explanation. They do not describe two actual payments, undo bank acceptance, or make approval retrospective. Timing and reversibility therefore matter even when the edit cost is small.

The appropriate management output is consequently a **portfolio of control findings, exposures, evidence states, and actions**, with technical alignment cost retained as a separate diagnostic measure.

def before(trace: list[str], first: str, second: str) -> bool:
    return (
        first in trace and second in trace
        and trace.index(first) < trace.index(second)
    )

def overlay(row: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    """Evaluate supplied facts without interpreting absence as nonperformance."""
    trace = traces[row["case"]]

    if not row["auto_used"]:
        p1 = "not_applicable_no_auto"
    elif row["case"] == "CL-610":
        p1 = "conditional_bank_change_vs_approval_time"
    elif pd.isna(row["auto_eligible_at_payment"]):
        p1 = "unknown_attributes"
    else:
        p1 = (
            "supported" if row["auto_eligible_at_payment"]
            else "violated"
        )

    if not row["payment_observed"]:
        p2 = "not_applicable_no_payment"
    elif pd.isna(row["p2_trigger_at_payment"]):
        p2 = "unknown_requiredness"
    elif not row["p2_trigger_at_payment"]:
        p2 = "not_triggered"
    elif (
        before(trace, "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve")
        and before(trace, "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim")
    ):
        p2 = "sequence_supported_in_core_not_substantive_proof"
    else:
        p2 = "required_sequence_not_fully_evidenced"

    if row["case"] == "CL-608":
        p4 = "external_3h_if_correctly_linked;integrity_unverified"
    elif "Notify Claimant" in trace:
        p4 = "core_event_present;24h_unverified"
    else:
        p4 = "notification_not_evidenced;24h_unverified"

    return pd.Series({
        "p1_status": p1, "p2_status": p2, "p4_status": p4,
    })

def known_sum(s: pd.Series):
    """Do not turn an entirely unknown amount group into EUR 0."""
    return s.sum(min_count=1)


def distinct_text(s: pd.Series) -> str:
    return ", ".join(sorted(set(s.dropna().astype(str))))

def run_pm4py() -> pd.DataFrame:
    import pm4py
    from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import (
        algorithm as pn_align,
    )

    # Parse the exact supplied tree and obtain an accepting Petri net.
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = (
        pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    )

    # Reconstruct the core log only. External evidence stays separate.
    # Artificial timestamps preserve ONLY sequence, not real timing.
    log_df = observed[["case", "activity"]].copy()
    log_df["ordinal"] = log_df.groupby("case").cumcount()
    log_df["timestamp"] = (
        pd.Timestamp("2000-01-01", tz="UTC")
        + pd.to_timedelta(log_df["ordinal"], unit="s")
    )
    log_df["timestamp_is_synthetic"] = True
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        log_df,
        case_id="case",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )

    # Match the supplied unit-cost convention explicitly.
    model_cost = {
        t: (0 if t.label is None else 1)
        for t in net.transitions
    }
    sync_cost = {t: 0 for t in net.transitions}
    P = pn_align.Parameters
    results = []

    # Run each case separately so its trace-cost vector has the right length.
    for case, frame in log_df.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=True):
        parameters = {
            P.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION.value: [1] * len(frame),
            P.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION.value: model_cost,
            P.PARAM_SYNC_COST_FUNCTION.value: sync_cost,
            P.SHOW_PROGRESS_BAR.value: False,
        }
        diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
            frame,
            net,
            initial_marking,
            final_marking,
            multi_processing=False,
            return_diagnostics_dataframe=False,
            **parameters,
        )
        if len(diagnostics) != 1 or diagnostics[0] is None:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"No complete alignment returned for {case}"
            )
        result = diagnostics[0]
        results.append({
            "case": case,
            "pm4py_unit_cost": result["cost"],
            "pm4py_alignment": str(result["alignment"]),
        })

    print("PM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)

    # Routes, alternative optima, or formatting may differ.
    # NEVER overwrite supplied moves/costs or assert rerun equality.
    # Normalized PM4Py fitness is not used as a business-risk measure.
    return pd.DataFrame(results)
