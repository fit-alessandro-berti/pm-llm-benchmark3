## 1. Materiality Framework

Business severity is separate from raw alignment cost. Raw cost is edit distance under a uniform cost function; it does not know whether a move is a harmless retry, a missing high-risk fraud screen, or an irreversible payment-before-approval.

Severity framework used below:

| Severity | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No business impact: fully compliant, or only technical/model noise such as telemetry, duplicate retries, or silent routing transitions. |
| 2 | Low business impact: model-scope mismatch, label mapping, or evidence/documentation issue with no confirmed payment-control failure and low customer/regulatory harm. |
| 3 | Moderate: evidence gap or data-aware policy deviation with moderate amount/exposure, likely reversible but requiring investigation and correction. |
| 4 | High: significant control bypass, auto-approval under recent bank change, or substantial financial/customer/regulatory exposure; may be recoverable but needs urgent remediation. |
| 5 | Critical: mandatory high-risk fraud/approval control not evidenced before payment, or payment before applicable approval, especially with high amount, HIGH risk, recent bank change, or irreversibility. |

Confidence is not severity. Confidence records how strongly the available evidence supports the narrative: “High” where logs/timestamps/data are clear; “Medium” where an external document or mutable link may show the activity occurred; “Low” where evidence is absent, disputed, or only inferential. A missing event is not proof that activity did not occur. Silent/tau moves can be technically fitting and business-neutral. Zero raw cost can still hide a policy-overlay violation when the process tree permits a branch that business policy forbids.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is optimized under a uniform edit-distance cost, not a business-risk cost. The supplied cost function charges visible log/model moves at 1, with hidden/tau moves at 0, but it does not weight amount, risk tier, bank-change trigger, irreversibility, regulatory duty, or evidence quality. This makes raw cost a diagnostic of model/log fit, not a compliance severity score.

Examples:
- **CL-610** has raw cost 0 because the process tree permits Auto Approve and the trace is fully synchronous. Business policy P1/P2 forbids Auto Approve with a beneficiary-bank change one hour earlier. The model is technically fitting but the business control fails. This is a zero-cost policy failure.

The low raw cost reflects that the alignment can “repair” missing moves with M(Fraud Screen) and M(Pay Claim), but it does not reflect the irreversibility of an early payment.
- **CL-602** has raw cost 1 because only M(Fraud Screen) is missing. Business exposure is €480k, HIGH risk, with a recent beneficiary-bank change, and no fraud evidence. The cost is low because the model only needs one missing event; the risk is high because the missing event is a mandatory high-risk control.

Raw cost underrepresents the high claim amount but may overstate actual control failure; missing-but-external evidence requires document-link validation, not automatic severity escalation.

Other cost limitations:
- **Hidden moves:** T(tau) can make a trace fit at zero business meaning, as in CL-605.
- **Activity granularity:** UI telemetry and retries are treated as moves unless explicitly filtered.
- **Duplicated telemetry:** CL-603’s duplicate notification and the 74 retry cases inflate costs without adding business risk.
- **Scope:** the process tree covers payable claims; denial/withdrawal branches are missing, so valid outcomes appear as missing payable steps.
- **Data conditions:** P1/P2 depend on amount, risk, and bank-change data that the process tree does not encode. CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous yet policy-invalid.
- **Irreversibility:** a raw cost of 1 for missing approval evidence is not comparable to an already-executed payment or bank-change diversion.

Therefore, business prioritization must combine alignment semantics, policy overlay, decision-time data, external evidence, amount, risk, timing, reversibility, recurrence, and customer/regulatory harm. Raw cost should be retained only as a separate technical diagnostic.
