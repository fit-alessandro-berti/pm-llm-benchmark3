## 1. Conformance semantics

Interpreting object-centric event logs against an incomplete normative specification requires a formal taxonomy that distinguishes between actual operational violations, expected variations, modeling limitations, and data pipeline defects. Under a naive closed-world assumption (CWA), any observed behavior not explicitly modeled in the normative graph is treated as noncompliant. In enterprise systems, where specifications are intentionally incomplete, CWA leads to severe false-positive inflation. Conversely, a pure open-world assumption (OWA) permits any unmodeled behavior, risking the omission of critical control failures. 

The normative framework here employs a hybrid semantic: it is closed-world strictly for relations explicitly marked **forbidden** and for **mandatory** relations within their defined scope. For all other relations, types, and lifecycle paths, an open-world semantic governs.

To evaluate observed differences rigorously, the following semantic categories are defined:

- **Forbidden behavior**: An observed relationship that matches an explicitly prohibited normative rule (e.g., N3, T6, E10). Under the closed-world sub-logic for negative constraints, any non-zero occurrence constitutes a definitive noncompliance that threatens system integrity, regulatory adherence, or financial controls, provided it is not an artifact of data corruption.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: The observed omission of a relationship that the norm stipulates as required whenever its operational activation conditions are satisfied (e.g., N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Determining whether an omission is a true violation requires establishing that the activation scope held and that the omission is not an artifact of graph abstraction.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: Observed relationships, entity interactions, or event transitions that are entirely absent from the normative model but are not covered by any forbidden rule (e.g., `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`). Under OWA, these are considered permissible business variations or unmodeled operational paths rather than violations.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: A deviation from a normative relationship marked `typical` (e.g., N2, N5, T3). Typical relations define desirable or dominant business flows, but explicitly acknowledge legitimate operational exceptions (e.g., bad-debt write-offs, open credit terms, unapplied cash). Absence of a typical relation warrants operational monitoring or statistical auditing, but never automatic noncompliance sanctioning.
- **Likely data-quality error**: Discrepancies produced by upstream logging defects, extract-transform-load (ETL) pipeline flaws, identifier collisions, or dropped foreign keys rather than real-world process noncompliance (e.g., sensor telemetry joining to financial invoices via integer key reuse; orphan item additions). These represent measurement failure rather than process deviance.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: Discrepancies caused by projecting complex, asynchronous, multi-object business realities onto oversimplified graph representations. A prime example is compressing an "eventually expected" business obligation into a local "directly-follows" edge, where intervening valid events cause false nonconformance reports.
- **Insufficient evidence**: Observations where aggregate graph summaries lack the trace-level execution timestamps, attribute payloads, or case lifecycles necessary to determine whether a deviation is a physical process breach, an allowed edge-case exception, or a data-quality bug.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph)

**What it can establish**:  
The OC-DFG establishes localized, direct sequential transitions between activities projected onto individual object types (e.g., how an `Order`, `Package`, or `Invoice` moves from one state to the next). It is effective at identifying explicit negative sequence violations where one activity must never immediately follow another, such as `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3, observed in 2 orders). It also establishes baseline direct-routing execution counts for standard paths, such as `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (N4, observed in 198 of 200 packages).

**What it cannot establish**:  
The OC-DFG cannot evaluate long-range temporal dependencies ("eventually follows"), concurrent multi-object synchronization, interleaved events across multiple lifecycles, or conditional attribute guards. If an object undergoes an intermediate activity—whether planned, exceptional, or unmodeled—the directly-follows relationship between its preceding and succeeding activities is permanently severed.

**Counts and scopes**:  
In N1 (`Create Order -> Validate Order`), 98 of 100 orders exhibit the direct transition. The count of 2 missing edges cannot determine whether the orders failed to enter validation (a critical business breach) or entered validation after an intermediate step. Crucially, the log shows 3 orders with `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`. If the 2 missing orders are among these 3, their validation path was routed through compliance review, satisfying the business policy while displaying zero directly-follows edges in the OC-DFG.

**The danger of interpreting "eventually expected" as "directly-follows"**:  
Equating an eventual business requirement with a directly-follows graph edge is a major pitfall in process conformance. N1 explicitly states that standard orders should "eventually enter validation," but the model approximates this via directly-follows. In reality, standard enterprise processes involve non-disruptive intermediate events: fraud screening, credit checks, address normalization, or manual compliance reviews. Enforcing directly-follows causes compliant cases that underwent diligence to be flagged as severe noncompliances, creating false alarms while blinding auditors to the true question: did validation eventually occur?

**Multi-level abstraction tensions**:  
In N2 (`Validate Order -> Close Order`), 83 orders show the direct transition, while 72 orders show `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`. In a single-object control flow, this appears as an unmodeled deviation or competing path. In an object-centric process, however, invoicing is a distinct business lifecycle linked to the order. An order may validate, trigger an invoice, wait for shipment, and eventually close. At the single-object directly-follows level, intermediate invoicing breaks the `Validate Order -> Close Order` edge, creating apparent nonconformance despite full operational compliance.

---

### OTG (Object Type Graph)

**What it can establish**:  
The OTG establishes the structural, architectural topology of the domain by defining which object types are permitted, required, or forbidden to interact, co-occur, or share lifecycle milestones. It validates data architecture integrity, relational foreign-key models, and separation-of-duties rules. For example, it detects structural violations such as `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` (T6), which directly breaches system constraints prohibiting the simultaneous generation of orders and financial transactions.

**What it cannot establish**:  
The OTG is entirely atemporal. It cannot establish event order, execution timing, lifecycle phase, activity context, or process state. An edge such as `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` confirms that items and orders interact, but cannot reveal whether items are added before validation, during fulfillment, or after cancellation.

**Counts and scopes**:  
Observed edge weights reflect aggregate relational density across the log. The edge `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` has a weight of 1450, establishing a rich one-to-many relationship. In contrast, `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` has a weight of 1. Because this structural violation is an isolated instance rather than a widespread architectural failure, it points to an isolated defect—such as an unapproved batch script, database migration bypass, or synthetic test injection—rather than an intentional systemic redesign.

**Multi-level abstraction tensions**:  
Consider `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` with an observed weight of 7. The normative OTG contains no specification for `object_codeath`, defining only `object_interaction`. Under CWA, this edge would be flagged as a structural violation. In business reality, a package and its associated delivery consignment naturally reach their lifecycle end-state simultaneously when the carrier logs a proof-of-delivery event. The edge is entirely conforming to physical operations, appearing anomalous only because the normative schema omitted codeath semantics.

---

### ET–OT (Event Type to Object Type Graph)

**What it can establish**:  
The ET–OT schema establishes interface contracts and participation boundaries between event types and object types. It dictates which entities must, may, or must not participate in specific business events. It enforces negative domain constraints, such as E10 (`Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice`, forbidden), and detects omissions in required relational links, such as E2/E3 for `Add Item` and E5/E6 for `Dispatch Package`.

**What it cannot establish**:  
The ET–OT schema cannot establish sequence, lifecycle progression, causal dependence, or attribute-dependent conditional rules. For instance, E8 mandates that `Record Payment` must link to `Invoice`, but provides an exception for "unapplied cash." The ET–OT structure alone cannot parse event payload attributes to verify whether an unlinked payment was legitimately flagged as unapplied cash or represents an unlinked transaction.

**Counts and scopes**:  
Edge weights in the ET–OT graph expose participation discrepancies:
- `Add Item -> Item` has a weight of 1450, but `Add Item -> Order` has a weight of 1448. This discrepancy reveals that 2 item additions occurred without an associated order, representing orphan records.
- `Dispatch Package -> Package` has a weight of 200, but `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` has a weight of 198. This reveals that 2 dispatch events lack an explicit delivery object link.
- `Record Payment -> Payment` has a weight of 93, but `Record Payment -> Invoice` has a weight of 92. This reveals exactly 1 unlinked payment event.

**Multi-level abstraction tensions**:  
The 2 `Dispatch Package` events lacking a `Delivery` link appear noncompliant against E6 and T2 at the graph schema level. However, additional context reveals that both events contain carrier references in their event attributes. Operationally, carrier assignment occurred, fulfilling the business requirement. The deviation is purely an artifact of data modeling: carrier information was captured as a scalar string attribute rather than reified into a first-class `Delivery` object graph node.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

### Mandatory behavior
- **Order creation and validation (N1, E1)**: 98 instances of `Create Order -> Validate Order` strictly conform. The 2 non-directly-following orders belong to this mandatory bucket as **violations** if they never validated, but shift to **abstraction mismatches (compliant)** if they validated downstream after compliance review.
- **Package packing prior to dispatch (N4, E4, E5)**: 198 instances of `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` strictly conform. The 2 missing instances are **process violations** if physical goods were dispatched without packing/verification, but shift to **data-quality omissions** if warehouse Wi-Fi dropped scanner events, or **unregulated** if fulfilled via third-party drop-shipment.
- **Item addition participation (E2, E3, T1)**: 1448 instances linking `Add Item` to both `Order` and `Item` strictly conform. The 2 instances lacking `Order` links represent **mandatory participation failures** (orphan line items).
- **Dispatch package participation (E5, E6, T2)**: 198 instances linking `Dispatch Package` to both `Package` and `Delivery` strictly conform. The 2 instances lacking `Delivery` links are **graph-level mandatory omissions**, but shift to **compliant** under attribute-reified delivery semantics.
- **Payment recording participation (E7, E8)**: 92 instances linking `Record Payment` to both `Payment` and `Invoice` strictly conform. The 1 unlinked instance is a **mandatory participation failure** unless the condition holds that it represents legitimate unapplied cash, which shifts it to a **typical exception**.

### Forbidden behavior
- **Post-cancellation package dispatch (N3)**: 2 orders exhibited `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`. This is **forbidden noncompliance** unless physical audit proves a logging race condition (cancellation event logged after physical dispatch was already in transit).
- **Order and payment co-birth (T6)**: 1 instance of `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`. This is **forbidden noncompliance** unless system logs prove it was generated by an offline database migration or synthetic test script rather than a live business event.
- **Sensor telemetry linking to financial objects (E10)**: 1 instance of `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice`. While formally matching a forbidden rule, this is classified as a **data-quality error (false positive violation)** due to verified integer key collision across disjoint system domains.

### Typical behavior
- **Order closure following validation (N2)**: 83 orders exhibited `Validate Order -> Close Order`, conforming to the primary typical path. The remaining 17 orders represent legitimate in-flight or intermediate processing.
- **Invoice payment settlement (N5, T3)**: 92 instances of `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` conform to the primary typical path.
- **Invoice write-offs (N5)**: 4 instances of `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` represent legitimate bad-debt exceptions explicitly permitted by N5.
- **Open in-flight invoices (N5)**: 14 instances of `Issue Invoice` with no successor represent standard aging receivables within open credit terms, fully permitted by N5.
- **Unapplied cash payments (T3, E8 context)**: 1 instance of `Record Payment` without an invoice link represents a legitimate typical exception, provided it is properly validated and labeled by finance staff.

### Unregulated behavior
- **Intermediate billing from validation (N2 context)**: 72 orders exhibited `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`. Unmodeled in the normative graph, but permitted under OWA and explicitly acknowledged in N2 notes.
- **Pre-validation compliance review (N1 context, T5)**: 3 orders exhibited `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`. Permitted under OWA as unmodeled risk triage.
- **Order reopening (N6)**: 1 order exhibited `Reopen Order -> Close Order`. Explicitly designated as outside the norm's governance.
- **Customer relationship interactions (T4)**: 100 instances of `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)`. Explicitly designated as maintained under separate policy.
- **Risk case interactions (T5, E9)**: 3 instances of `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` and 3 instances of `Manual Compliance Review -> RiskCase`. Explicitly permitted as unmodeled risk review.
- **Customer participation in compliance reviews (E9 context, T4)**: 3 instances of `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`. Permitted under OWA as unmodeled multi-object participation.
- **Package and delivery joint termination**: 7 instances of `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)`. Permitted under OWA as unmodeled lifecycle completion synchronization.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

### Gap 1: Temporal semantics of N1 (`Create Order -> Validate Order`)
- **Operational risk**: The norm models an "eventually expected" business policy as a directly-follows edge. This creates systemic false positives whenever legitimate intermediate events occur (such as risk triage, manual review, or credit holds), while failing to detect orders that stall or are abandoned before validation.
- **Proposed clarification**: Formally replace the directly-follows relation with an explicit temporal logic rule: `Create Order` must be followed by `Validate Order` within an SLA window (e.g., $\le 24$ hours), permitting defined intermediate triage activities (`Manual Compliance Review`, `Address Normalization`).
- **Norm status**: **Mandatory** (with eventual-follows temporal semantics and SLA parameters).

### Gap 2: Scope and temporal mechanics of cancellation in N3 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`)
- **Operational risk**: N3 prohibits dispatching a package after order cancellation, but does not define behavior for multi-package consignments or split shipments. If a cancellation occurs while one package is in flight and another is staged, logistics operators lack clear rules on whether carrier intercept is mandatory or if cancellation applies only to unpicked packages.
- **Proposed clarification**: Specify that upon logging `Cancel Order`, any associated package in state `Created`, `Packed`, or `Staged` is forbidden from transitioning to `Dispatch Package`. Any package already dispatched prior to the cancellation timestamp is exempt from violation but must automatically trigger an RMA return workflow.
- **Norm status**: **Forbidden** (for any dispatch event timestamped equal to or greater than the cancellation timestamp).

### Gap 3: Validation and administrative typing for unapplied cash (E8, T3)
- **Operational risk**: E8 exempts unapplied cash from requiring an invoice link, but the snapshot contains untyped payments lacking invoices. Without strict schema validation rules, unapplied cash becomes an audit loophole that can mask lost invoice references, stolen funds, or unlinked ledger entries.
- **Proposed clarification**: Mandate that an unlinked `Record Payment` event is valid if and only if it contains the explicit metadata attribute `payment_nature = 'unapplied_cash'` and links to a valid `Customer` object. An untyped payment lacking an invoice link must be treated as a mandatory compliance failure.
- **Norm status**: **Mandatory** (with strict attribute-guarded exception criteria).

### Gap 4: Entity reification vs. scalar event attributes for logistics tracking (E6, T2)
- **Operational risk**: Two dispatches captured carrier details in event attributes rather than instantiating `Delivery` object nodes. The norm does not define whether logistics entities must exist as graph vertices or scalar attributes, producing false-positive nonconformance reports against an operationally compliant fulfillment step.
- **Proposed clarification**: Define that `Delivery` entity reification is required for external logistics tracking. Ingestion pipelines must be standardized to automatically generate a `Delivery` object whenever `carrier_reference` or `tracking_number` attributes appear in a `Dispatch Package` event.
- **Norm status**: **Mandatory** (with normalized ingestion ETL requirements).

### Gap 5: Object lifecycle termination and codeath governance (`Package object_codeath Delivery`)
- **Operational risk**: The norm defines no rules for object lifecycle termination. Unregulated codeath relations could conceal instances where carrier delivery failures prematurely terminate package lifecycles, or conversely flag valid, synchronized handovers as unmodeled anomalies.
- **Proposed clarification**: Incorporate `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` into the normative OTG as the standard terminal state for courier consignments, triggered exclusively by `Confirm Delivery` or `Proof of Delivery` events.
- **Norm status**: **Typical** (expected for standard direct deliveries, with exceptions for lost freight or split shipments).

### Gap 6: Order reopening governance and post-closure authorization (N6)
- **Operational risk**: Leaving `Reopen Order` entirely unspecified allows arbitrary modifications to closed, billed, and fulfilled orders without managerial sign-off, creating severe risks of unauthorized refunds, fraudulent re-shipments, or revenue misstatements.
- **Proposed clarification**: Define a formal post-closure lifecycle: `Reopen Order` must require an explicit managerial authorization event (`Authorize Reopen`) linking to a `CustomerDispute` or `Return` object prior to moving to `Close Order`. A direct transition `Reopen Order -> Close Order` without intermediate review must be prohibited.
- **Norm status**: **Mandatory** sequence (`Reopen Order -> Authorize Reopen -> Review -> Close Order`), with direct transitions lacking authorization designated as **Forbidden**.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The conclusions in this assessment are subject to specific structural and observational boundaries:

- **Robust conclusions**:  
  The identification of post-cancellation dispatches (N3) and simultaneous order-payment co-birth (T6) as critical operational noncompliances is definitive. These breach explicit, closed-world negative constraints. Similarly, classifying bad-debt write-offs, open in-flight invoices, customer interactions, risk review participation, and package-delivery codeath as non-violations is fully robust under open-world semantics. Finally, the discrepancies in `Add Item -> Order` and `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` counts are unambiguously confirmed as schema-level omissions.

- **Conclusions dependent on norm incompleteness**:  
  Determining whether `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 orders) and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1 order) conform to business policy depends entirely on resolving the norm's deliberate gaps. If company policy mandates fulfillment prior to billing, the 72 advance-invoiced orders represent a major billing process deviation; under standard open-world assumptions, they remain benign. Similarly, whether the 2 orders lacking direct validation represent noncompliance depends on whether the unmodeled `Manual Compliance Review` is formally recognized as a legitimate pre-validation step.

- **Conclusions dependent on suspected data-quality issues**:  
  Classifying `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` as a benign data-quality defect rather than a catastrophic system compromise relies on the technical plausibility of numeric key collisions across disjoint systems. Similarly, classifying the unlinked `Record Payment` event as an acceptable typical variation depends on confirming that finance staff legitimately accepted unapplied cash rather than losing an invoice foreign key.

- **What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone**:  
  Aggregate graph summaries cannot establish true execution causality, millisecond-level race conditions, or the physical reality of warehouse operations. It is impossible to determine from graph counts alone whether the 2 post-cancellation dispatches were loaded onto trucks before the cancellation arrived or knowingly dispatched in defiance of the hold; whether the 2 unpacked packages physically bypassed verification or simply suffered scanner Wi-Fi drops; or whether unapplied payments represent legitimate cash reserves or audit reconciliation failures. Resolving these questions requires case-level event traces, database transaction isolation logs, and physical operational records.
