# Warranty Process Redesign: Balancing Speed, Quality, Compliance, and Customer Experience

---

## 1. Current-Process Diagnosis

### Main Performance, Quality, and Compliance Problems

The current process exhibits a fundamental pathology: **the fastest, most frequent path (Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement) achieves speed and low unit cost, but externalize quality and compliance costs downstream through complaints, rework, and policy violations.**

#### Key Problems:

**1. Speed-Quality Paradox (Direct Contradiction)**

The most-traveled path (Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement, 2,550 cases) has:
- **Median cycle time to dispatch: 3.2 hours (very fast)**
- **Cost before rework: ~31% cheaper than reviewed path**
- **BUT complaint/reopen rate of 14.1%**, compared to only 3.3% for the slower Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device path
- Post-rework accounting eliminates 73% of the cost advantage (only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits)

This reveals that **speed is purchased by deferring quality gatekeeping until after fulfillment, when correction is expensive.**

**2. Compliance Violations (Normative Rule Violations)**

- **R1 violation (high-value claims without review before fast approval):** Alignment diagnostics identify "Fast approval before Evidence completeness" and "Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices" as frequent move-on-log deviations. Premium high-value devices show 22% complaint/reopen on the fast path vs. 5% on the reviewed path—this 17-percentage-point gap suggests rule R1 breaches.
- **R2 violation (dispatch before evidence completeness):** The fast path skips evidence-completion confirmation except for Premium low-risk accessories. The evidence portal data suggests 26% of cases requesting missing evidence enter a loop at least twice, indicating evidence completeness is not enforced upstream of dispatch.
- **R3 failure (reopened claims not returned to manual review):** 310 cases flow from Customer complaint/reopen directly back to Manual eligibility review, but the DFG shows no formal enforcement; some cases likely close or move to escalation without re-review.

**Token replay fitness of 0.91 and only 68% fitting traces confirm systematic non-conformance; 32% of cases violate normative routing.**

**3. Evidence-Request Loop Trap (22% of cases)**

- The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment has a median cycle time of 140 hours and highest abandonment (320 of 1,110 cases in this path abandon).
- Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review creates a 34-hour mean delay before retry.
- 26% of cases in this loop enter it more than once; repeated evidence requests with no self-service path (evidence portal exists but is not integrated) cause both abandonment and frustration.
- This segment is 22% of the intake, yet drives outsized cycle time and abandonment—it is a hidden cost multiplier.

**4. High-Value Device Mishandling (Quality Failure)**

- Premium and Standard high-value devices show 22% and 18% complaint/reopen rates on the fast path, vs. 5–6% on reviewed paths.
- Temporal profile deviations note "Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen linked to incorrect replacement part selection."
- Auto-screen rules are not segment-aware; they were designed for accessories but are applied to devices where part matching, value, and compliance matter.
- The cost of wrong replacement (EUR 410 for high-value devices) far exceeds any process-cost savings for this segment.

**5. Manual-Review Queue Congestion (Resource Bottleneck)**

- 7 manual reviewers, 36 productive hours/week each, 252 hours/week capacity; at current load (1,400 cases starting manual review + feedback loops), utilization is rising sharply.
- Feedback from Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review (890 cases) and Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review (310 cases) add 1,200 cases to the queue.
- Queue begins to grow sharply above 82% utilization; current demand is ~2,500 manual-review activities per week, approaching saturation.
- As queue grows, mean cycle time for Manual eligibility review  Repair quote rises from 22 to 36+ hours (p90: 60), eroding the competitive advantage of the reviewed path for time-sensitive Premium segments.

### Summary of the Contradiction

| Dimension | Fast Path (Auto  Fast Approval) | Reviewed Path (Manual/Fraud  Quote/Approval) |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit cost (pre-rework)** | ~31% lower | — |
| **Median cycle time** | 3–4 hours to dispatch | 60–120 hours to fulfillment |
| **Complaint/reopen rate** | 14.1% overall; 22% Premium high-value | 3.3% (repair) to 5% (reviewed); 5% Premium high-value |
| **Post-rework net cost** | 9% lower (margin erased) | Higher upfront, but stable |
| **Compliance risk** | High (R1, R2 violations) | Low |
| **Best for** | Standard accessories, low-risk | High-value, Premium, unclear defects |

**The organization cannot optimize both simultaneously with a single path.** The contradiction is not a measurement error—it reflects a fundamental design choice: **the current process defaults to speed-first triage for all segments, accepting compliance and quality costs for segments where they should not be tolerated.**

## 2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Triage with Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate

### Process Flow

```
START  Intake claim
    
Segment classifier [NEW; 0.5 hr, rule-based]
     Premium + Low-risk accessory  Expedited auto-screen [special rules, 0.8 hr]
            Fast approval [1.0 hr]
            Dispatch replacement [3.0 hr]
            Quality/compliance close [5.0 hr]
           [Target: 13 hr median, 6% complaint rate, full R2 compliance]
    
     Standard + Low-risk accessory  Standard auto-screen [0.8 hr]
            Fast approval [1.0 hr]
            Pre-dispatch quality gate [NEW; 6 hr]  checks part match, stock status
            Dispatch replacement [3.0 hr]
            Quality/compliance close [5.0 hr]
           [Target: 22 hr median, 8% to 5% complaint rate]
    
     Any + High-value device (>EUR 400)  Mandatory manual + fraud review [7.5 + 18 hr]
            Repair quote [22 hr]
            Customer approval [30 hr]
            Repair device [20 hr]
            Quality check [9 hr]
            Quality/compliance close [5 hr]
           [Target: 120 hr, 5% complaint rate, R1/R2/R3 compliant]
    
     Unclear/missing-evidence flag [NEW classifier]  Evidence intake portal [NEW; self-service]
             Auto-resolved (30–45% lift)  return to appropriate segment [24 hr portal latency]
             Unresolved  Manual eligibility review [7.5 hr]
             [Target: reduce 140 hr median to 85 hr; reduce abandonment 29%  20%]

Request missing evidence  Evidence portal [self-service; NEW]
     Customer provides evidence [24 hr SLA, automated reminder at 12 hr]
     Portal triggers auto-reclassification  appropriate segment
     [Reduce repeated requests 26%  10%; reduce loop delay from 34 hr to 12 hr]

Customer complaint/reopen [9.2% of cases]  Escalation board + Manual eligibility review [enforced]
     [Ensure R3 compliance; prevent silent close after reopened claim]
```

### Target Segments and Pathways

**Primary targets:**
- **Premium low-risk accessories (18%):** Reduce cycle time to <13 hrs (currently 8 hrs, maintain), eliminate service-credit exposure, keep 6% complaint rate stable.
- **Standard low-risk accessories (25%):** Trade 9 hours (+38%) cycle time for reducing complaint rate from 8% to ~5%, justified by lower cost of wrong dispatch.
- **High-value devices (35% combined):** Enforce mandatory review; accept 120-hour cycle time; reduce complaint rate from 18–22% (fast) to 5–6% (reviewed), compliance-compliant.
- **Unclear defects / missing evidence (22%):** Introduce self-service evidence portal; reduce cycle time from 140 hrs to ~85 hrs and abandonment from 29% to ~20%.

### Mechanism of Improvement

**1. Segment-aware auto-screen rules (deployed in 2 weeks):**
   - Update auto-screen to reject high-value devices and route them to mandatory manual + fraud review before any approval.
   - Create Premium low-risk accessory bypass (allow Fast approval + pre-dispatch gate instead of full manual review).
   - Enforce R1 (no Fast approval for >EUR 400 without review) and R2 (no dispatch without evidence clearance or Premium accessory exemption).

**2. Pre-dispatch quality gate (6–12 hour activity):**
   - Lightweight inspection by quality check team or technicians (existing capacity underused): verify part-part selection matches device, stock is available, customer address is valid.
   - Cost: ~EUR 3–5 per case (0.15 hr × EUR 38/hr), but prevents EUR 95–410 wrong-replacement costs.
   - Applied to Standard low-risk and all Standard/Premium high-value devices *before* dispatch.
   - For Premium low-risk only: expedited gate (6 hr, automated) by exception; fulfills R2 requirement.

**3. Evidence portal integration (new capability):**
   - Self-service portal for customers to upload missing evidence (photos, receipts, serial numbers) with 24-hour SLA and auto-reminders.
   - Automated parser tags evidence quality; 30–45% of cases auto-resolve without manual review (aligns with evidence-request loop analysis).
   - Prevents repeat-evidence syndrome (currently 26% re-enter loop); reduces mean cycle time in evidence path from 140 to ~85 hours.
   - Mitigates abandonment risk if portal includes video guidance for low-digital-literacy customers.

**4. Escalation board enforcement (R3 compliance):**
   - Every reopened claim (310 cases/week) must pass the escalation board and manual re-review before close or denial.
   - Currently: some reopen cases likely exit without re-review; this enforces policy and prevents silent non-conformance.

### What It Deliberately Sacrifices or Leaves Unresolved

**1. Cycle time for Standard low-risk accessories increases ~38% (13  22 hrs):**
   - Standard customers tolerate 72-hour completion if visibility is good; 22-hour median is still well within tolerance.
   - Trade-off is explicit and justified by complaint-rate reduction and compliance.

**2. Premium high-value devices still face 120-hour median (no speed gain):**
   - This segment has high compliance and quality risk; speed is not appropriate.
   - Redesign does not attempt to optimize this segment for cycle time—it optimizes for compliance and quality.

**3. No expansion of manual-review capacity:**
   - Redesign assumes current 7-reviewer team can handle the mandatory inflow for high-value devices (21% + 14% = 35% of cases).
   - If high-value case volume grows, queue congestion will emerge; this redesign does not solve it through hiring.
   - Mitigation: evidence portal auto-resolves 30–45% of missing-evidence cases, reducing manual-review feedback loops.

**4. Evidence portal has implementation risk:**
   - May increase abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers (e.g., elderly users with accessories).
   - Redesign includes video guidance and phone support to mitigate, but cannot guarantee zero increased abandonment.

## 3. Redesign B: Risk-Tiered Evidence Completion and Dynamic Escalation

### Process Flow

```
START  Intake claim
    
Intake claim [0.5 hr]
    
Risk classifier [NEW; 1.5 hr rule-based on claim amount, device type, defect clarity]
    
     LOW-RISK (accessories <EUR 200, clear defect): Auto-screen [0.8 hr]
            Fast approval [1.0 hr]
            Dispatch replacement [3.0 hr]
            Quality/compliance close [5.0 hr]
           [Median ~13 hrs; complaint rate remains ~8–10%; R2 compliant by product type]
    
     MEDIUM-RISK (device EUR 200–400, or clear defect on device >EUR 400):
            Tiered evidence completion [NEW process]
               Auto evidence request [2.0 hr] with portal
               Customer responds via portal [24 hr SLA]
               Auto-scoring of evidence [1.5 hr] (good/borderline/poor)
                  GOOD [70%]  proceed to Fast approval [1.0 hr], then Quality gate [6 hr], then Dispatch [3.0 hr]
                  BORDERLINE [20%]  Manual eligibility review [7.5 hr], then Fraud review [18 hr], then Quote [22 hr]
                  POOR [10%]  Claim denial or re-evidence request [loop back, max 2 cycles]
            Quality check (gate) [6 hr for ~70%, skipped for ~20% manual route]
            Dispatch or Repair quote & Approval [per path]
            Close [5 hr]
           [Target: median ~45 hr (weighted); complaint ~6%; R1 compliance 95%+]
    
     HIGH-RISK (device >EUR 400, unclear defect, premium customer, or suspicious signals):
             Mandatory manual eligibility review [7.5 hr]
             Fraud risk review [18 hr]
             Repair quote or explicit denial [22 hr]
             Customer approval [30 hr for repair]
             Repair/check/close [34 hr]
             [Median ~120 hr; complaint ~5%; R1/R2/R3 compliant; escalation board catch-all]

Request missing evidence (within medium-risk or high-risk path):
     Directed via evidence portal (not generic email request)
     24-hour SLA with auto-reminder
     Max 2 re-evidence cycles; after that, escalate to manual review or deny
     [Reduce repeat loops from 26% to <8%; prevent abandonment from timeout]

Customer complaint/reopen:
     Escalation board (mandatory; 45-min daily slot) [54 hr mean delay, but 1-day SLA]
     Root cause: repair quality (45 cases) vs. part mismatch (260 cases) vs. eligibility dispute (310 cases)
     Re-route based on root cause:
        Part mismatch  return to quality gate and re-dispatch (if accessory) or re-quote (if device)
        Repair quality  Rework repair [14 hr] or refund
        Eligibility dispute  Manual eligibility review [7.5 hr] + escalation board sign-off [45 min]
     [Enforce R3; reduce repeat complaint rate from 9.2% overall to ~4% by root-cause rerouting]
```

### Target Segments and Pathways

**Primary targets:**
- **Low-risk accessories (43% combined, currently 8–13 hr, 6–8% complaints):** Preserve fast path; no regression.
- **Medium-risk devices (30%, currently variable 20–96 hrs):** Introduce tiered evidence completion to avoid either pure-fast (high complaint) or pure-manual (slow); median outcome ~45 hrs with auto-sorting.
- **High-risk devices (14%) + Premium high-value (14%):** Mandate manual review; target 5% complaint rate; high compliance.
- **Reopened cases (9.2%):** Add deterministic root-cause routing and escalation board integration to enforce R3 and reduce repeat complaints.

### Mechanism of Improvement

**1. Risk classifier (1.5 hr, one-time per claim):**
   - Rule-based decision tree: claim amount, device category, defect clarity (from claim description), customer status, historical signals (e.g., address unusual).
   - Routes to low/medium/high-risk paths automatically; reduces manual-reviewer decision variance.
   - Complements auto-screen; the two together (intake 0.5 hr + classifier 1.5 hr = 2 hrs) replace the current unstructured split between Auto and Manual paths.

**2. Tiered evidence completion (medium-risk only):**
   - Instead of all-or-nothing (Fast approval vs. Manual review), introduce graduated evidence assessment.
   - Auto-request missing evidence upfront (not after Fast approval fails); customer provides via portal.
   - Auto-scorer (ML or rule-based) tags evidence as good/borderline/poor in 1.5 hours.
   - **GOOD evidence (70%):** proceed to Fast approval + Quality gate + Dispatch. Median path: 2 + 1.5 + 1 + 6 + 3 + 5 = 18.5 hrs.
   - **BORDERLINE evidence (20%):** escalate to manual + fraud review + quote; longer but proportional to risk. Median path: 2 + 24 (portal wait) + 7.5 + 18 + 22 + 30 + 20 + 9 + 5 = ~137.5 hrs (pessimistic; many skip manual if evidence improves).
   - **POOR evidence (10%):** deny or request re-evidence (max 2 cycles); reduces claim abandonment by explicit boundary.
   - **Outcome:** Medium-risk median cycle time is weighted average: 0.70 × 18.5 + 0.20 × 60 + 0.10 × (25 + denial)  35–45 hrs, vs. current 45–80 hrs for this segment. Complaint rate drops because auto-sorted cases avoid misrouting.

**3. Evidence portal (as in Redesign A, but integrated into tiered process):**
   - Medium-risk and high-risk paths direct customers to portal instead of email/phone.
   - 24-hour SLA; auto-reminder at 12 hrs; max 2 re-requests, then escalate.
   - Reduces repeat-evidence loop from 26% to <8%; prevents 890 cases/week from re-entering manual review.

**4. Root-cause escalation routing:**
   - Escalation board reviews all reopened claims (currently 820 cases).
   - Tag each reopen: **repair quality** (equipment defect), **part mismatch** (wrong replacement selected), or **eligibility dispute** (customer disagrees with decision).
   - Route accordingly:
     - **Part mismatch  quality gate retry + re-dispatch or re-quote** (avoids manual review loop).
     - **Repair quality  rework repair or refund** (technical escalation, not administrative).
     - **Eligibility dispute  manual re-review + board sign-off** (enforces R3).
   - **Outcome:** Reduce repeat complaint rate (cases with >1 complaint) from current 9.2% to ~4% by precise rerouting; escalation board becomes deterministic, not bottleneck.

### What It Deliberately Sacrifices or Leaves Unresolved

**1. Upfront risk classification adds ~1.5 hours to all claims:**
   - Increases median cycle time for fast path from 8–13 hrs to 9.5–14.5 hrs.
   - Trade-off is justified if complaint rate decreases and compliance improves, but it is visible to customers.
   - Risk: Premium low-risk customers may perceive slower service despite no actual service degradation.

**2. Auto-evidence scorer (ML/rules) requires development and calibration (4–6 weeks):**
   - Error rate in auto-scoring (misclassifying borderline as good) would increase complaints, offsetting benefits.
   - If auto-scorer is too conservative, it pushes too many cases to manual review, reducing speed gain.
   - Redesign assumes organization can build or acquire this capability; if not, falls back to manual triage (same as current).

**3. Escalation board capacity is still 1 slot/day (45 min):**
   - Currently 820 reopened cases/week; escalation board can handle ~24–30 cases/week.
   - Root-cause routing reduces the number that need escalation board (e.g., part-mismatch cases can auto-retry), but capacity is still limited.
   - If reopened cases grow, escalation board becomes a bottleneck again; redesign does not address this directly.

**4. Does not expand manual-review capacity for high-risk cases:**
   - Similar limitation as Redesign A; if high-value device volume grows, queue congestion returns.
   - Redesign mitigates by diverting 70% of medium-risk cases away from manual path, but high-risk path is still bound by 7 reviewers.

## 4. Redesign C: Outcome-Based Parallel Processing with Deferred Fulfillment Commitment

### Process Flow

```
START  Intake claim
    
Intake claim [0.5 hr]
     PARALLEL PATH A: Claim Assessment
        Auto-screen [0.8 hr]
        [Outcome: eligible/conditional/deny, with confidence score]
        If deny: immediate notification + appeal option [0.5 hr]; claim ends
    
     PARALLEL PATH B: Evidence & Risk Completion
        Auto-evidence request [1.5 hr] (portal-first)
        Customer provides evidence [24 hr SLA]
        Evidence auto-scoring [1.5 hr; good/fair/poor]
        If poor: additional review or explicit re-request loop [max 2 cycles, 72 hr total]
    
     PARALLEL PATH C: Fraud & Risk Review (for medium/high-risk only)
        Async fraud risk check [18 hr, can run while evidence is being collected]
        [Outcome: clear/flag/deny]
    
     CONVERGENCE: Decision Gate [1 hr, rule-based merge]
             Auto-screen eligible + Good evidence + Fraud clear  FAST TRACK
                    Quote/approval path [skip manual review; 30 hr total for repair, 3 hr for dispatch]
                    [Target: 35–40 hr for fast-track devices, vs. 120 hr if sent to full manual path]
            
             Auto-screen conditional + Fair evidence + Fraud flag  MANUAL REVIEW + FRAUD SPECIALIST CONSULT
                    Manual review [7.5 hr] + Fraud specialist opinion [18 hr in parallel, not serial]
                    [Target: 30 hr total, not 25.5 hr serial]
            
             Auto-screen eligible + Good evidence + Fraud clear, BUT product >EUR 400  R1-COMPLIANT FAST TRACK
                    Qualify as "eligible for fast-track by rule exception" (pre-approved by policy)
                    Quality gate [6 hr] + Dispatch [3 hr]
                    [Bypass manual review without violating R1; compliance mechanism]
            
             Any path  Condition: NO FULFILLMENT COMMITMENT UNTIL EVIDENCE COMPLETE + DECISION GATE PASSED
                     [Prevents R2 violation: no dispatch before evidence confirmed]
                     BUT: provide customer with estimated completion time and status transparency from Day 1
                     [Trade speed of dispatch for confidence in correctness; reduce complaints via visibility]

Quality Gate [6 hr, ALL paths except low-risk accessories]
     Part matching, stock validation, address verification
     Catch-all for part-mismatch complaints (currently 260 Dispatch  Reopen cases)

Repair device path [for Quote  Approval  Repair routes]:
     Lightweight quality check [9 hr, built-in]
     Rework repair [14 hr] only if inspection fails
     [Reduce rework rate for high-value devices from 12.5% to <5% by tighter integration]

Customer complaint/reopen [4% target, down from 9.2%]:
     Auto-tag root cause (same as Redesign B)
     Part mismatch  Quality gate re-run; re-dispatch [12 hr, parallel with customer comms]
     Repair quality  Rework escalation [inline with Repair device team]
     Eligibility/policy dispute  Escalation board + denial appeal [45 min + async review]
     [Outcome: reduce repeat complaints by precise routing and parallelization]
```

### Target Segments and Pathways

**Primary targets:**
- **Standard low-risk accessories (25%):** Fast path preserved; no regression; cycle time 8–13 hrs.
- **Standard high-value devices (21%) and Premium high-value (14%):** Parallel assessment allows R1 compliance without serial delay; reduce cycle time from 96–120 hrs to 40–60 hrs by running manual + fraud in parallel and allowing fast-track if all gates pass. Complaint rate reduced from 18–22% (fast, non-compliant) to ~6% (compliant, faster than pure manual).
- **Medium-risk mixed (30%):** Parallel evidence + fraud completion; conditional merge into manual review only if needed. Median cycle time ~35–50 hrs (vs. current 45–80 hrs).
- **Unclear/missing-evidence segment (22%):** Evidence portal integrated into parallel path; evidence collection starts immediately (not after auto-screen fails); reduces total cycle time by parallelizing instead of serializing assessment and evidence gathering. Median ~85–100 hrs vs. current 140 hrs.

### Mechanism of Improvement

**1. Parallel assessment instead of serial gating:**
   - Current: Intake  Auto-screen  [if conditional] Manual  [if still conditional] Fraud  Quote
   - Redesigned: Intake  {Auto-screen, Evidence collection, Fraud review} run in parallel
   - **Outcome:** Even if manual review is needed, fraud review findings are ready when manual reviewer starts (not 18 hours later in serial). Mean reduction: ~18 hours for conditional cases.
   - Evidence collection starts immediately, so customers begin uploading evidence while auto-screen runs; no waiting for preliminary decision to request evidence.

**2. Decision gate rule engine (compliance mechanism for R1):**
   - Rule: "High-value device (>EUR 400) is eligible for fast-track *if* auto-screen eligible + fraud clear + evidence good."
   - This is not "bypass manual review"—it is "manual review pre-approved by policy engine."
   - Enables R1 compliance (no fast approval without sufficient gatekeeping) while avoiding manual-reviewer bottleneck for cases that do not need expert judgment.
   - Reduces high-value device cycle time from 120 hrs (mandatory manual + fraud + quote + repair) to 40–50 hrs (parallel assessment + quality gate + dispatch/quote) when all gates pass.
   - **Outcome for Premium high-value devices:** Current fast path is 20 hrs but 22% complaint rate (non-compliant). Redesigned fast-track is 40 hrs but 5% complaint rate (compliant, better outcome despite 2× cycle time).

**3. Quality gate as universal prevent-rework (6 hrs, all non-accessory paths):**
   - Before dispatch or repair authorization, lightweight check: part match, stock, address.
   - Catches 260 cases currently flowing Dispatch  Complaint/reopen due to wrong part.
   - Cost: EUR 3–5 per case; saves EUR 95–410 wrong-replacement cost; eliminates 96-hour complaint cycle.
   - Integrated into parallel paths: added to decision gate (no additional serial delay if run in parallel with manual review).

**4. Inline rework integration for repair path:**
   - Currently: Repair device [20 hr]  Quality check [9 hr]  Rework repair [14 hr] (serial).
   - Redesigned: Quality check embedded in repair planning; technician validates repairability and part sourcing *before* starting repair.
   - Rework repair [14 hr] triggered immediately if validation fails, not after repair completion.
   - **Outcome:** Reduce rework rate for high-value devices from 12.5% to <5%; reduce total repair-cycle time by ~14 hours for ~45 cases/week that currently need rework.

**5. Transparency-first customer experience (visibility mitigates cycle-time perception):**
   - Current: Fast dispatch (8 hrs) looks good, but complaint arrives 96 hours later (long surprise).
   - Redesigned: Slower path (40–50 hrs), but customer receives:
     - Day 1: status and expected completion date (from decision gate).
     - Day 2: "Part ordered, quality check in progress" (not silent waiting).
     - Day 3: "Quality gate passed, shipping tomorrow" (confirmation before fulfillment).
   - **Outcome:** Longer cycle time is offset by reduced uncertainty and complaint rate. Customer satisfaction improves despite slower completion.

### What It Deliberately Sacrifices or Leaves Unresolved

**1. Parallel processing adds coordination overhead:**
   - Three parallel paths (assessment, evidence, fraud) must be synchronized at decision gate.
   - If one path is slow (e.g., fraud review on Monday, peak load), entire case waits.
   - Fraud review is currently a bottleneck (2 specialists); parallelization does not eliminate bottleneck, just makes it more visible.
   - **Mitigation:** Redesign includes fraud-review asynchronous processing; if fraud specialist is busy, case proceeds to manual reviewer (who can incorporate fraud findings later), but redesign does not hire more specialists.

**2. Decision gate rule engine requires policy codification and calibration:**
   - Current organization does not have explicit rules for "when is high-value case eligible for fast-track?"
   - Redesign assumes rules can be defined (e.g., "auto-eligible + fraud clear + evidence score >80 + device <3 years old").
   - If rules are too loose, compliance violations increase. If too strict, no benefit.
   - Implementation: 3–4 weeks of policy review + testing; risk of stakeholder disagreement.

**3. No expansion of Quality Check or Fraud Review capacity:**
   - Quality gate runs in parallel for all non-accessory cases (currently ~3,500 cases/week, but ~2,000 skip today).
   - This increases demand for quality inspection from current underutilized state to possible bottleneck.
   - Fraud review is already a Monday/peak-campaign bottleneck (2 specialists); parallel demand does not reduce load, may increase it.
   - **Mitigation:** Partial automation of quality gate (stock verification, address validation via APIs) and fraud pre-screening (rule-based flagging) to reduce manual touches.

**4. Transparency-first approach requires operational discipline:**
   - If case status updates are inconsistent or missing, transparency backfires and increases complaints.
   - Requires integration between multiple systems (claim, quality, repair, shipping) to provide real-time status; not all organizations have this.
   - Redesign assumes claim system can be updated hourly; if not, transparency is illusory.

**5. Customers uncomfortable with "deferred commitment" may increase anxiety:**
   - Current fast dispatch gives psychological satisfaction ("decision made quickly").
   - Redesigned parallel path postpones commitment until decision gate (1–2 days into process).
   - Some customers (especially Premium) may perceive this as slower decision-making, even if final fulfillment is faster.
   - **Mitigation:** Transparent communication ("we are ensuring the right outcome before commitment") required.

### Key Insights from Comparison

1. **No single redesign dominates all metrics:** Each trades off complexity, cost, or cycle time in different ways.
2. **Segment is the key:** Reimagining redesigns perform differently by segment. Redesign A is best for Premium low-risk; C is best for high-value.
3. **Compliance and quality are non-negotiable:** All three improve on current; current's 32% non-fitting traces are a liability.
4. **Evidence portal is common to B and C:** Addressing the 22% unclear-defect segment is a prerequisite for all three.
5. **Parallel processing (C) is the only redesign that materially improves high-value cycle time:** This is the organization's biggest pain point (22% complaint rate on fast, but 120-hour slow path). A and B do not improve it; C compresses it to 40–60 hrs with parallel assessment.

## 6. Failure Modes and Displaced Bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Segment-Aware Triage + Quality Gate

**Failure Mode 1: Segment Classifier Miscategorization**
- **Risk:** If classifier incorrectly routes high-value devices to fast path (e.g., claims with damage described as "cosmetic" but actual cost >EUR 400), R1 violations reappear.
- **Frequency:** 5–10% of edge cases may be ambiguous (e.g., "cracked screen, device is still functional").
- **Consequence:** Compliance gains erased; complaint rate remains 18–22% for misrouted cases.
- **Mitigation:** Classifier design requires domain expertise (engineer + legal review); periodic audit (monthly) of misroutes; secondary check for ambiguous cases.

**Failure Mode 2: Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate Becomes Bottleneck**
- **Risk:** Quality check team (2 inspectors, 76 hours/week capacity) must now inspect all Standard and Premium high-value cases (currently ~1,200/week fast-path cases + ~1,000 medium-risk = 2,200 cases/week if all go through gate).
- **Resource math:** 2,200 cases × 0.15 hours = 330 hours/week needed; capacity is 76 hours/week. Severe bottleneck.
- **Consequence:** Quality gate creates 36+ hour delays; defeats purpose of pre-dispatch check.
- **Mitigation:** Partial automation (stock API + address validation = 60% of checks without human touch). Hire 1 FTE (EUR 38/hr) to expand quality capacity. Accept that low-risk accessories skip gate (acceptable, low error rate).

**Failure Mode 3: Evidence Portal Adoption Failure Among Low-Digital-Literacy Segments**
- **Risk:** Elderly customers or those without smartphones/email may abandon claims rather than use portal (similar to current evidence-request loop).
- **Frequency:** 5–15% of accessories segment; 10–20% of high-value segment (purchasers may be less digitally skilled).
- **Consequence:** Abandonment rate increases from 390/5,000 (7.8%) to 10–12%; revenue loss and customer dissatisfaction.
- **Mitigation:** Offer non-digital alternative (phone/fax evidence submission). Include video guidance in portal. Staff phone line for portal support. Test with pilot group before full rollout.

**Displaced Bottleneck 1: Manual-Review Queue**
- Current bottleneck: manual eligibility reviewers (82%+ utilization).
- Redesign A impact: By routing high-value to mandatory manual path, inflow increases by ~15% (560 additional cases/week). But evidence portal feedback loop shrinks by ~30% (270 cases/week). **Net effect: +290 cases/week, utilization rises from 82% to ~95%; queue grows.**
- **Recovery:** Hiring 1 additional reviewer (EUR 34/hr) is needed; costs ~EUR 1,700/week.

**Displaced Bottleneck 2: Fraud Review**
- Current bottleneck: 2 fraud specialists (peak utilization 90%+ on Mondays).
- Redesign A impact: No change in fraud-review demand. Bottleneck persists; cases requiring fraud review still wait 18 hours.
- **Recovery:** No improvement in A; fraud bottleneck unaddressed.

### Redesign B: Risk-Tiered Evidence + Dynamic Escalation

**Failure Mode 1: Auto-Evidence Scorer Miscalibration**
- **Risk:** ML/rules-based auto-scorer misclassifies evidence quality. If too lenient, 30–40% of borderline-evidence cases are marked "GOOD" and sent to fast approval, but later fail (high complaint rate). If too strict, 40–50% are marked "POOR," reverting to current slow path (no benefit).
- **Frequency:** 15–25% of medium-risk cases (450–750 cases/week) are sensitive to scorer calibration.
- **Consequence:** Either complaint rate increases back to 10–12% or cycle time remains 80+ hours (no improvement).
- **Mitigation:** Phased rollout with human validation for 1–2 weeks; recalibrate weekly. Accept 2–3% mis-scoring during ramp-up.

**Failure Mode 2: Risk Classifier Decision Quality Inconsistency**
- **Risk:** Risk classifier assigns 30% of cases to MEDIUM-RISK (should be ~20–25%, based on design), overloading the tiered evidence path.
- **Frequency:** Classifier rules are imperfect; variance in claim descriptions (e.g., "water damage" categorized differently by different customers).
- **Consequence:** Evidence portal swamped; 24-hour SLA breached for 20–30% of medium-risk cases; some escalate to manual review anyway.
- **Mitigation:** Classifier tuning during pilot (1–2 weeks); acceptance criteria: <15% false-MEDIUM-RISK categorization. Escalate ambiguous cases to manual reviewer for triage.

**Failure Mode 3: Escalation Board Capacity Still Insufficient for Root-Cause Routing**
- **Risk:** Root-cause routing directs all reopened cases to escalation board first. Currently, board handles 24–30 cases/week; if reopened cases grow to 150/week (9.2% of 1,600 new cases/week), board is 5–6× over capacity.
- **Frequency:** Current: 820 reopened cases/week; board capacity: 30/week. Redesign assumes root-cause routing reduces repeats to ~4% (64/week), but even 64/week exceeds board capacity.
- **Consequence:** Escalation board queue grows; cases wait 10–15 days for board review (currently mean 54 hours, p90 130 hours). Repeat complaints (cases with 2+ complaints) remain high.
- **Mitigation:** Add 2nd daily escalation board slot (+45 min, trained facilitator). Or route 50% of simple part-mismatch cases directly to quality gate retry (no board needed). Accept that eligible-dispute cases still need board.

**Displaced Bottleneck 1: Manual-Review Queue**
- Current utilization: 82%+ (growing).
- Redesign B impact: Evidence portal auto-resolves 30–45% of missing-evidence feedback loop (270 to 400 cases/week). But tiered evidence also triggers manual review for ~20% of medium-risk cases (up to 150–200/week additional inflow). **Net effect: 0 to 50 cases/week on manual queue; utilization stays ~80–85%.**
- **Recovery:** Slight improvement; hiring not needed. But queue remains fragile at high utilization.

**Displaced Bottleneck 2: Evidence Portal Infrastructure**
- Redesign B relies heavily on evidence portal (24-hour SLA, auto-reminders, auto-scoring).
- If portal is underprovisioned (slow response, frequent downtime), customer abandonment increases.
- **Risk:** 5–10% of customers do not successfully submit evidence within 24 hrs due to portal latency; cases escalate to manual review or abandon.
- **Recovery:** Capacity planning for portal; 99.9% uptime SLA; adequate server resources for 5,000 cases/month.

---

### Redesign C: Parallel Processing + Deferred Commitment

**Failure Mode 1: Parallel-Process Orchestration Delays (Sync Bottleneck)**
- **Risk:** Three parallel paths (assessment, evidence, fraud) must synchronize at decision gate. If any path is slow, entire case waits.
  - **Assessment:** 0.8 hrs (fast).
  - **Evidence:** 24 hrs SLA (slow).
  - **Fraud review:** 18 hrs (bottleneck when queue is long).
- **Consequence:** Case must wait until all three are complete; median wait time is 24 hrs (evidence SLA), not parallelism benefit. If evidence is poor or fraud flags the case, manual review is still needed (serial, not parallel).
- **Frequency:** 30% of medium-risk cases (300–400/week) have one slow path. 10–15% of high-risk (50–75/week) have multiple slow paths.
- **Consequence:** Redesign C achieves only modest cycle-time reduction (35–40 hrs, not 25 hrs as optimistically estimated).
- **Mitigation:** Accept evidence 24-hr SLA as critical path; run fraud review asynchronously (start immediately, but do not block decision if delayed). Tolerate 5–10% cases where decision gate must wait for fraud findings (fallback: manual review + async fraud).

**Failure Mode 2: Decision Gate Rule Engine Compliance Miscalibration**
- **Risk:** Rules for "eligible for fast-track despite >EUR 400" must be tightly calibrated to avoid new R1 violations.
  - **Too loose:** "high-value + auto-eligible + fraud clear" is 40% of >EUR 400 cases, many with risky product/customer profiles. Fast approval violates policy intent. Complaints rise to 12–15%.
  - **Too strict:** Rules allow only 5% of >EUR 400 cases; most still go to manual review. No cycle-time benefit; complex rule engine wasted.
- **Frequency:** Rules are built during pilot; first week may see 20–40% misalignment.
- **Mitigation:** Involve policy/legal team in rule design. Audit first 500 cases; recalibrate rules. Accept that some >EUR 400 cases still go to manual review (fine; rules are conservative).

**Failure Mode 3: Quality Gate Demand Surge Causes New Bottleneck**
- **Risk:** Quality gate now applies to *all* non-accessory cases (currently ~3,500 cases/week), not just fast-path failures. Current quality-check team (2 inspectors, 76 hours/week) is underutilized (handle ~200–300 cases/week).
- **Capacity math:** 3,500 cases × 0.10 hrs (parallel gate, not inline) = 350 hours/week needed; capacity is 76. **Severe bottleneck; quality gate adds 25–30 hrs to median cycle time.**
- **Consequence:** Redesign C fails; high-value devices, instead of 40–60 hrs, now 65–90 hrs (little improvement over current).
- **Mitigation:** **Automation is critical:** stock checking (API to inventory system, 0.05 hrs), address validation (address-database lookup, 0.02 hrs). These cover ~50% of checks. Remaining 50% (part matching, rework risk) still need manual inspection (0.05 hrs). Total: 0.07 hrs per case. 3,500 × 0.07 = 245 hours/week; with 76 hours staff, add ~4 FTE quality inspectors (cost: ~EUR 6,000/week) or accept that quality gate is partial (skip for <EUR 200 devices). **This is a major cost impact not identified in initial design.**

**Failure Mode 4: Transparency Backfire (Deferred Commitment)**
- **Risk:** Customers are accustomed to fast dispatch (8–13 hrs). Redesign C postpones decision-gate commitment to 24–48 hrs into process, then adds quality gate (6 hrs) before fulfillment commitment.
- **Perception:** "Why is your decision taking 2 days? Your competitor gave me an answer in 2 hours."
- **Frequency:** Complaints about slow decision are registered in 5–10% of cases; some customers escalate or seek alternative vendors.
- **Consequence:** Customer satisfaction decreases despite lower complaint rates after fulfillment; net NPS may decline.
- **Mitigation:** Transparent communication strategy: "We verify your claim thoroughly before commitment to avoid errors. You will receive a decision update within 2 hours, and expected fulfillment date by 24 hours." Automated status emails/texts (hourly updates). Premium segment SLA: decision by 12 hours.

**Displaced Bottleneck 1: Fraud Review Becomes Async Bottleneck**
- Current: Fraud review is 18-hr serial step in manual path.
- Redesign C: Fraud review runs in parallel, but asynchronous. If fraud specialist is busy (Monday, post-campaign), findings arrive at decision gate after 24+ hours (instead of 18 hrs in serial).
- **Risk:** Fraud bottleneck becomes a decision-gate blocker for ~10–15% of high-risk cases.
- **Consequence:** Expected cycle-time gain of 18 hrs for conditional cases is realized as 8–10 hrs (fraud delay absorbs benefit).
- **Mitigation:** Hire 1 fraud specialist (EUR 52/hr) to reduce fraud queue to <8 hrs. Cost: EUR 2,000/week.

**Displaced Bottleneck 2: Claim-System Integration Load**
- Redesign C requires real-time updates from multiple systems (auto-screen, evidence portal, fraud, quality, repair, shipment) to feed decision gate and status transparency.
- **Risk:** Legacy claim system cannot handle event-driven architecture; decision gate must poll systems (added latency) or system integrations are loose (status updates are delayed/missing).
- **Consequence:** Decision gate has stale data; some cases are auto-approved with incomplete information. Or status transparency is illusory (updates lag by hours).
- **Mitigation:** Invest in claim-system modernization or middleware (BPM/API gateway). Estimated: EUR 50K–100K + 6 weeks. **This is a major hidden cost.**

## 7. Recommended Experiment Sequence

### Staged Experiment Plan

#### **Stage 0: Foundation (Weeks 1–4) — Applicable to All Redesigns**

**Objective:** Establish measurement infrastructure and baseline conformance.

**Pilot Design:**
1. **Baseline audit (1 week):**
   - Re-measure conformance on 500 recent cases using Petri net replay (validate fitness 0.91 assertion).
   - Quantify cycle time and complaint rates by segment.
   - Count and analyze the 32% non-fitting cases: which rules are violated most?

2. **Metrics definition (2 weeks):**
   - Define primary metrics: cycle time (median, p90), complaint/reopen rate (%), rework rate (%), compliance fitness (% fitting traces).
   - Define segment-specific SLAs: Premium low-risk <20 hrs, Standard low-risk <50 hrs, high-value <100 hrs, unclear <100 hrs.
   - Define business metrics: cost per case (direct labor + materials), customer satisfaction (CSAT post-close, NPS post-complaint).

3. **Instrumentation (1 week):**
   - Ensure claim system captures: segment/customer-type, defect type, value, timestamps for all process steps, final outcome (close/deny/abandon), post-close complaint flag.
   - Set up automated alerting for compliance deviations (fast approval on >EUR 400 without review, dispatch before evidence confirmed).
   - Log all manual-review, fraud-review, and quality-check activities with reviewer IDs and timestamps.

**Success Criteria:** Baseline metrics established and stable over 2 weeks (coefficient of variation <10% week-to-week); automated alerts working; team trained on new metrics.

#### **Stage 1a: Redesign A Pilot (Weeks 5–14) — Segment-Aware Triage + Quality Gate**

**Pilot Scope:**
- **Population:** 1,000 cases/week (20% of intake) over 6 weeks = 6,000 test cases.
- **Segments:** Premium low-risk (180/week) and Standard low-risk (250/week) into expedited auto-screen. Standard high-value (210/week) into mandatory manual + quality gate.
- **Control:** 4,000 cases/week follow current process (baseline).

**Deployment:**

**Week 5: Segment Classifier Rollout**
- Deploy rule-based classifier: segment premium/standard by customer history + device value. Live on intake.
- Manual audit: first 100 cases; validate classifier accuracy (target: >95% correct segment).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If classifier accuracy <90%, pause; re-tune rules.

**Week 6–7: Expedited Auto-Screen for Premium Low-Risk**
- Pilot auto-screen with updated rules for Premium low-risk accessories (bypass manual review if <EUR 200, clear defect).
- Metrics: cycle time from intake to fast approval (target: 3–4 hrs, current ~2 hrs; acceptable if complaint stays <6%).

**Week 7–8: Standard Low-Risk + Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate**
- Extend auto-screen to Standard low-risk (same rules as Premium, but stricter).
- Introduce pre-dispatch quality gate (part matching, stock, address) for all Standard low-risk cases approved by auto-screen.
  - Gate performed by quality-check team; 0.15 hrs per case; target: 1 reviewer can handle 150 cases/week (10 hrs/week).
- Metrics: cycle time (target: +6 hrs for quality gate; total ~12–15 hrs), complaint rate (target:  8%  5%), parts-mismatch complaints (target: ).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If cycle time >18 hrs (gate taking >8 hrs per case, not 0.15 hrs), bottleneck emerging; pause and automate gate steps.

**Week 9–10: High-Value Device Mandatory Manual Path**
- Route all high-value devices (>EUR 400, all segments) to mandatory manual eligibility review (no bypass to fast approval).
- Metrics: compliance (target: R1 violations drop to 0); cycle time (accept 90+ hrs); complaint rate (target:  18–22%  6%).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If complaint rate <4% (over-optimistic), but cycle time >130 hrs (queue congestion), accept trade-off. If complaint rate >8%, suspect over-routing (some mid-risk devices sent here); audit.

**Week 11–12: Evidence Portal Lightweight Integration**
- For cases in mandatory manual review, send evidence request via portal (not email).
- Track portal adoption: % of customers who submit evidence via portal vs. phone/mail.
- Metrics: repeat-evidence rate (target:  26%  15%), cycle time in evidence loop (target:  34 hrs mean  20 hrs).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If portal adoption <50%, customers do not trust it; investigate barriers (mobile UX, language, support).

**Week 13–14: Stabilization & Audit**
- Run full 6,000-case sample through metrics.
- Compare pilot (A) vs. control (current process) on cycle time, complaint, compliance, cost.
- Expected outcomes:
  - **Premium low-risk:** cycle time 8–10 hrs (stable), complaint 6% (stable), compliance R1/R2 100%.
  - **Standard low-risk:** cycle time 15–18 hrs (+38% vs. current 13 hrs, acceptable), complaint 5% ( from 8%), cost +2% (quality gate), parts-mismatch complaints  30%.
  - **High-value:** cycle time 110–130 hrs (stable), complaint 5% ( from 18%), compliance 100%, cost +5% (mandatory review).

**Go-No-Go Threshold:**
- **GO:** Cycle time increase <50% for all segments, complaint rate decrease >2 percentage points for at least 2 segments, compliance violations  0, cost impact < +3%.
- **NO-GO:** Cycle time >20% above target OR complaint rate increases OR compliance violations persist OR quality gate causes >8 hr delays.

**Decision:** If GO, proceed to Stage 1b (evidence portal full rollout). If NO-GO, pause; investigate bottleneck (likely quality gate); consider automation or resource addition.

#### **Stage 2a: Redesign B Pilot (Weeks 23–36) — Risk-Tiered Evidence + Dynamic Escalation**

**Pilot Scope:**
- **Population:** 1,500 cases/week (30% of intake) over 8 weeks = 12,000 test cases. Specifically: 600/week Standard low-risk (control), 400/week Standard high-value, 500/week unclear-defect.
- **Control:** 3,500 cases/week follow current process.
- **Note:** Stage 1 (Redesign A) is NOT rolled back; it runs in parallel as operational baseline.

**Deployment:**

**Week 23–24: Risk Classifier Rollout**
- Deploy risk classifier (value, product type, defect clarity) to pilot population; assign LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH.
- Manual validation: first 200 cases; measure classifier accuracy (target: >85% correct risk tier).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If accuracy <80%, re-tune rules.

**Week 25–26: Medium-Risk Tiered Evidence Path**
- Route 400/week Standard high-value cases to MEDIUM-RISK tier.
- Implement tiered evidence completion:
  - Auto-request evidence via portal [2 hrs].
  - Customer provides evidence [24 hr SLA].
  - Auto-scorer tags GOOD/BORDERLINE/POOR [1.5 hrs].
    - GOOD (70% target)  Fast approval + Quality gate + Dispatch [median 20 hrs from auto-score].
    - BORDERLINE (20% target)  Manual review + Fraud [median 30 hrs from auto-score].
    - POOR (10% target)  Deny or re-request [max 2 cycles].
- Metrics: 
  - Evidence submission rate (target: >70% within 24 hrs; acceptable: >50%).
  - Auto-score accuracy (target: 90%; GOOD cases that later have complaints; BORDERLINE cases that proceed to fast approval without issues).
  - Cycle time by tier (target: GOOD 20 hrs, BORDERLINE 35 hrs, POOR 25 hrs).
- **Stop-go threshold:** 
  - STOP if: Evidence submission <50% (portal adoption low), auto-score accuracy <80% (GOOD cases have >8% complaints), cycle time GOOD >25 hrs.

**Week 27–28: Auto-Evidence Scorer Tuning**
- Analyze first 1,000 scored cases; identify misclassifications.
- Recalibrate scorer rules (e.g., if 50% of GOOD cases have complaints, lower GOOD threshold).
- Retest on next 500 cases; measure improvement in accuracy.
- **Stop-go threshold:** If recalibration accuracy >85%, proceed. If <80%, consider reverting to manual triage (no auto-scorer, just BORDERLINE redirection).

**Week 29–30: Root-Cause Escalation Routing**
- Escalation board now tags all reopen cases (Standard high-value + pilot): root cause = part mismatch / repair quality / eligibility dispute.
- Route each tag:
  - Part mismatch  Quality gate retry + re-dispatch [parallel, 6 hrs].
  - Repair quality  Rework escalation [inline, 14 hrs].
  - Eligibility dispute  Manual re-review + board sign-off [7.5 + 0.75 hrs].
- Metrics: Repeat complaint rate (target:  from 18% fast-path to 5%), time-to-resolution for repeats (target: <50 hrs, vs. current 96+ hrs).
- **Stop-go threshold:** 
  - STOP if: Repeat complaint rate  <2 percentage points (routing is not effective).
  - GO if: Repeat complaint rate  >4 percentage points & time-to-resolution <60 hrs.

**Week 31–32: Unclear-Defect Path Integration**
- 500/week unclear-defect cases enter tiered evidence path.
- Evidence portal is critical here (evidence from Stage 1); auto-scorer tries to resolve 30% auto (GOOD) without manual review.
- Metrics: Auto-resolve rate (target: >25%), cycle time (target:  140  85 hrs), abandonment (target: stable or ).
- **Stop-go threshold:** 
  - STOP if: Auto-resolve <15%, cycle time <75 hrs (not improved).
  - GO if: Auto-resolve >20%, cycle time 80–95 hrs, abandonment <7%.

**Week 33–36: Stabilization & Full Cohort Audit**
- Run 12,000-case sample through metrics.
- Compare Redesign B (pilot) vs. Redesign A (parallel control) on cycle time, complaint, compliance, cost.
- Expected outcomes:
  - **Standard high-value:** cycle time 45–60 hrs (+20–40% vs. current 28–96 hrs), complaint 6% ( from 18%), cost –8% (fewer manual reviews).
  - **Unclear-defect:** cycle time 85–100 hrs ( from 140 hrs), auto-resolve 25%, abandonment 5% ( from 8%).
  - **Repeat complaint rate:** 5% ( from 9.2% overall, 14.1% fast-path).

**Go-No-Go Threshold:**
- **GO:** Cycle time 65 hrs for Standard high-value, complaint  >5 percentage points, auto-resolve >20%, cost  >5%, compliance violations  0.
- **NO-GO:** Cycle time >80 hrs, complaint  <3 percentage points, auto-score accuracy <80%.

**Decision:** If GO, compare B vs. A on key metrics (cycle time, complaint, cost, complexity). If both meet thresholds, run A+B in parallel to determine best-segment fit. If NO-GO, pause; redesign auto-scorer or evidence portal UX.

#### **Stage 2b: Redesign C Pilot (Weeks 37–52) — Parallel Processing + Deferred Commitment**

**Pilot Scope:**
- **Population:** 800 cases/week (16% of intake) over 10 weeks = 8,000 test cases. Specifically: 200/week Premium high-value, 300/week Standard high-value, 300/week unclear-defect.
- **Control:** Combination of Stage 1 (Redesign A, running operationally) and Stage 2a (Redesign B, running in parallel) as baseline.

**Deployment:**

**Week 37–38: Process Orchestration Setup**
- Build parallel-process orchestration (BPM tool or custom workflow):
  - **Path A (Assessment):** Auto-screen [0.8 hrs, starting immediately].
  - **Path B (Evidence):** Auto-evidence request [1.5 hrs], customer submits [24 hr SLA].
  - **Path C (Fraud):** Async fraud review [18 hrs, starts immediately, non-blocking].
- All three run in parallel; proceed to decision gate once all three complete (or fraud is async-skipped).
- **Stop-go threshold:**
  - STOP if: BPM setup takes >3 weeks (over budget).
  - GO if: Orchestration working; parallel paths confirmed to run concurrently (audit first 50 cases).

**Week 39–40: Decision Gate Rule Engine**
- Define and code rules for fast-track eligibility:
  - Rule A1: Low-risk accessory + auto-eligible + fraud clear  Fast approval + Dispatch [3 hrs].
  - Rule A2: Medium-risk + auto-eligible + good evidence + fraud clear  Fast approval + Quality gate + Dispatch [22 hrs].
  - Rule A3: High-value (>EUR 400) + auto-eligible + good evidence + fraud clear + (device <3 years OR refurbished)  R1-compliant fast-track  Quality gate + Dispatch [35 hrs].
  - Rule B: Conditional or fraud-flag  Manual review + Fraud specialist async [parallel, 30 hrs total].
  - Rule C: Auto-denied or poor evidence  Explicit denial + appeal [2 hrs].
- Manual validation: first 100 cases; check rule accuracy (target: >90% correct route).
- **Stop-go threshold:** If rule accuracy <85%, recalibrate or add domain expert review for edge cases.

**Week 41–42: Quality Gate Automation**
- Integrate quality gate with stock/inventory API (0.05 hrs per case, automated).
- Integrate address validation via address-database API (0.02 hrs, automated).
- Remaining 50% of quality checks (part matching, rework risk) remain manual (0.05 hrs).
- Total quality-gate time: 0.07 hrs per case.
- **Stop-go threshold:** 
  - STOP if: API integrations fail (stock/address system downtime); quality gate becomes unreliable.
  - GO if: APIs working; quality-gate automation covers >50% of cases; manual portion scales.

**Week 43–44: Parallel Assessment & Deferred Commitment Launch**
- Pilot 200/week Premium high-value cases into parallel assessment:
  - Path A: Auto-screen starts at intake [0.8 hrs].
  - Path B: Evidence portal request sent immediately [1.5 hrs auto-request].
  - Path C: Fraud review started immediately [async, 18 hrs, may complete after decision gate].
- Decision gate runs at synchronization point (all three paths complete or fraud async-skipped).
- Metrics:
  - Time to decision gate (target: <24 hrs, driven by evidence SLA).
  - Time to fulfillment commitment (quality gate + rule result, target: <36 hrs).
  - Parallel benefit (time saved vs. serial assessment, target: >10 hrs compression for conditional cases).
  - Compliance (R1 violations  0 via decision gate rules).
- **Stop-go threshold:**
  - STOP if: Time to decision gate >30 hrs (parallel not delivering benefit), compliance violations re-emerge.
  - GO if: Time to decision gate <24 hrs, parallel saves >8 hrs vs. serial, compliance 100%.

**Week 45–46: Transparency & Status Updates**
- Implement hourly status updates for pilot cases (at each process gate: assessment complete, evidence received, fraud clear, decision made, quality passed, fulfillment in progress, shipped).
- Send automated email/SMS to customer at each milestone.
- Metrics: Customer satisfaction with status transparency (survey, target: >80% feel informed); complaint rate among transparent cases (target: <4% due to reduced surprise).
- **Stop-go threshold:**
  - STOP if: Status update infrastructure is unreliable (missed updates for >5% of cases), customer complaints about status increase.
  - GO if: >80% of customers receive all status updates; complaint rate <5%.

**Week 47–48: Quality Gate Bottleneck Assessment**
- All 200/week Premium high-value cases now go through quality gate (0.07 hrs each, ~14 hrs total labor/week).
- Expand to 300/week Standard high-value cases (21 hrs/week labor).
- Total quality-gate demand: 35 hrs/week for this segment. Current capacity: 2 inspectors × 36 hrs = 72 hrs/week.
- **Assessment:** 
  - If automation handles 50% of cases, manual load is ~17.5 hrs/week; easily handled.
  - If automation handles <40%, manual load is >21 hrs/week; approaching bottleneck.
- **Stop-go threshold:**
  - STOP if: Manual quality-gate cases build queue >50 cases (waiting >8 hrs for inspection).
  - GO if: Queue <20 cases; automation and manual inspection are balanced.

**Week 49–50: Unclear-Defect Parallel Evidence Path**
- 300/week unclear-defect cases enter parallel orchestration:
  - Path A: Auto-screen [0.8 hrs] (expected outcome: mostly auto-deny or auto-eligible with evidence).
  - Path B: Evidence portal [1.5 hrs request + 24 hr customer SLA].
  - Path C: Fraud [18 hrs, async].
- Decision gate runs at convergence (expected: 24 hrs after intake).
- Metrics: Cycle time (target:  140  85 hrs), auto-resolve rate (target: 30%), abandonment (target: <7%).
- **Stop-go threshold:**
  - STOP if: Cycle time >100 hrs (not improved), abandonment  >1 percentage point.
  - GO if: Cycle time 85–100 hrs, auto-resolve >25%, abandonment stable or .

**Week 51–52: Stabilization & Full Cohort Audit**
- Run 8,000-case sample through metrics.
- Compare Redesign C (pilot) vs. Redesigns A & B (operational controls) on cycle time, complaint, compliance, cost, resource utilization, customer satisfaction.
- Expected outcomes:
  - **Premium high-value:** cycle time 40–55 hrs ( from 120 hrs fast / 96 hrs manual), complaint 5% ( from 22% fast / 5% manual), compliance 100%.
  - **Standard high-value:** cycle time 50–65 hrs ( from 28–96 hrs), complaint 6% ( from 18% fast), cost –12% (parallel compression).
  - **Unclear-defect:** cycle time 85–100 hrs ( from 140 hrs), auto-resolve 25%, abandonment 5%.
  - **Resource utilization:** Manual reviewers ~75% ( from 82%+ due to parallel fraud/evidence), fraud review 85%, quality gate 50% (depends on automation success).

**Go-No-Go Threshold:**
- **GO:** Cycle time for high-value 60 hrs, complaint  >10 percentage points, compliance 100%, cost  >8%, resource pressure <85% for all teams, customer satisfaction  (transparency benefit).
- **NO-GO:** Cycle time >70 hrs, complaint  <5 percentage points, compliance violations, quality-gate bottleneck, status-update failure.

**Decision:** If GO, Redesign C is viable for high-value segment. Compare C vs. A+B on complexity/cost/benefit; decide on segmented rollout strategy.

**Decision Tree:**
- **If A & B both GO, C NO-GO:** Use A as operational baseline (low complexity, compliance fixed). Use B for specific segments (medium-risk, repeats). Recommended: A + partial B rollout by segment.

- **If none GO:** Root-cause analysis; identify broken assumptions (e.g., evidence portal adoption, auto-scorer accuracy, quality-gate automation). Design Redesign D (hybrid) or pursue different approach (e.g., hire manual reviewers, outsource low-risk triage).

### Learning Plan: How to Detect if Improvements Are Real vs. Shifted Downstream

**Concern:** Cycle time  in early stages (intake  dispatch) but complaints shift downstream (dispatch  complaint/rework). Cost is not actually reduced, just deferred.

**Approach:**

1. **Extended follow-up window:** Measure all 9.2% + baseline complaint rate for 30 days post-close, not just immediate issues. Use control group (Stage 1 parallel baseline) to compare.

2. **Root-cause breakdown:** Tag each complaint by root cause (design defect, wrong part, repair quality, eligibility error, etc.). Trend each category.
   - **If "wrong-part complaints"  50% in A/B/C:** Quality gate is working; improvement is real.
   - **If "repair-quality complaints"  20%:** Redesign is deferring rework; not real improvement.

3. **Lifetime cost per case:** Include labor (initial + rework) + material (replacement errors, reshipments) + service credits (Premium cases >24 hrs).
   - Expected: Current = EUR 25–30/case; Redesign A = EUR 24–28 (quality gate saves EUR 5, but manual review costs EUR 3). Net:  EUR 2–3.
   - If lifetime cost  >EUR 5, downstream costs are offsetting gains.

4. **Resource shift analysis:** Measure total labor hours (manual review, fraud, quality, rework, escalation) per case.
   - Expected: Current = ~3 hrs/case (many hours for repeats). Redesign A = ~2.8 hrs/case (fewer repeats, but more upfront quality).
   - If total hours  >0.5 hrs/case, improvement is illusory (labor just shifted).

5. **Cascading-failure detection:** Track cases that pass early gates but fail later in process (e.g., Quality check rejects 30% of pre-dispatch gates, creating hidden backlog).
   - **Risk metric:** "Cases waiting at any single gate >24 hrs" as % of in-process cases.
   - If this rises >10%, bottleneck is migrating, not being eliminated.

6. **Customer experience proxy:** Use CSAT + NPS trajectory, not just rate.
   - Current: CSAT ~70%, NPS ~20% (likely, given 9.2% complaint rate).
   - Expected: Redesign A = CSAT ~80%, NPS ~35% (fewer surprises, faster high-value resolution).
   - If CSAT  (e.g., 65%) or NPS  despite complaint rate , customers perceive slower service or reduced transparency; psychological cost.

7. **Segment divergence:** Compare redesign performance within each segment (A may help low-risk but hurt high-value). If high-value segment NPS  despite complaint , cycle-time increase is being resented.

8. **Audit compliance outcomes with real claims:**
   - Run 500 cases from Stages 1–2 through a *post-hoc* conformance check using domain expert (not just rule engine).
   - Expert review: "Did this case actually meet the intent of R1/R2/R3, or did the process technically comply but the outcome is risky?"
   - Example: Case was auto-approved as "eligible," but customer purchased device 5 years ago; warranty is likely expired. Rule was complied with, but intent violated.
   - If expert review finds >5% of cases where process is compliant but intent is violated, rule engine is incomplete.

## Conclusion

The three redesigns are **not simultaneously optimal**, but each is **locally optimal for different strategic priorities:**

- **Redesign A (Segment-Aware Triage):** Best for rapid compliance fix and low implementation risk. Trade: modest cycle-time improvement, must hire 1 FTE.
  
- **Redesign B (Risk-Tiered Evidence):** Best for medium-risk segment and repeat-complaint reduction. Trade: auto-scorer development risk, moderate complexity.

- **Redesign C (Parallel Processing):** Best for high-value devices and system integration maturity. Trade: highest complexity and cost, but best outcome for Premium/Standard high-value (cycle time 40–60 hrs, complaint 5%, compliance 100%).

**Recommended strategy:** Deploy **Redesign A first** (4–6 weeks, low risk, fixes immediate compliance violations). Then **pilot Redesign B or C** in parallel, targeting their respective sweet-spot segments. By Stage 3 (week 53), organization will have real data to choose operational strategy (A alone, A+B, A+C, or all three by segment).
