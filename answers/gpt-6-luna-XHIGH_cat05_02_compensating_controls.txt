## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 has a literal resource-separation requirement and a broader control purpose: keep one person from both initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent payment. Passing the literal check is evidence about identity separation, not proof that the second person was independent or reviewed the right evidence.

| Objective or threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stop one operator from initiating and authorizing the same transfer | Does not check human separation. Its covered checks may block some bad payments, but it cannot substitute for a second human. | An independent, substantive approval before release can prevent authorization by one person alone. Approval after release but before settlement can still stop the transfer at the settlement boundary. | Detects a same-user case after the event; it does not prevent the initial release. Recall may correct a defect if action is timely. | Can permit the exception only when all listed gates hold. It does not remove the objective or itself provide the independent review. |
| Catch duplicates, sanctions matches, account ownership problems, recent account changes, operator-limit breaches, or basic PO mismatch | Strong preventive coverage when fully operational and blocking. A warning detects a covered issue but is overrideable. A clean pass says nothing about checks outside this list. | Can independently scrutinize the flagged item and supporting records if the manager actually reviews them. | May discover a missed or overridden issue after release; recovery depends on the timeline. | Requires a full, unoverridden AV-2 pass for emergency use. |
| Verify receipt, service delivery, and commercial reasonableness | Explicitly cannot establish receipt or commercial reasonableness. | Can address these risks if the manager reviews reliable receipt/service evidence and challenges implausible terms. A rubber stamp does not. | Can detect fabricated or missing support retrospectively, but may be too late to prevent loss. | Provides no extra verification by itself; it relies on the quality of MG-4 and does not expand AV-2 coverage. |
| Resist collusion, credential sharing, or a compromised operator account | Does not establish who controlled an account or detect collusion. | Independence, conflict checks, separate credentials, and real review reduce this risk; a conflicted or credential-sharing manager does not. Even a sound approval cannot eliminate collusion risk. | Can identify patterns such as repeat pairings, overrides, or anomalies, but those patterns require investigation. | Requires an independent manager, but the token and other conditions do not prove the operator and manager are free of collusion. |
| Preserve a timely chance to stop or recover a bad transfer | A block before release is preventive. | Before settlement, an approver can stop settlement. After settlement, approval is only retrospective and any recovery depends on a separate recall path. | Detective by design; partly corrective only if a defect is found while stopping, recall, or recovery remains feasible. | Requires a one-business-day audit for eligible emergency use, but that deadline alone does not guarantee action before the bank's 24-hour recall window. |
| Ensure exceptions are justified and traceable | Records check outcomes, but a warning override can weaken the evidence. | Approval evidence must identify the manager, timing, independence, and records reviewed. | Creates a post-event record; a late or incomplete audit weakens assurance. | Makes emergency permission conditional on a prior token, amount, vendor tenure, account stability, clean AV-2, timely independent approval, and audit. ## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Assess each package against the payment's material threat scenarios and the point at which the transfer becomes effectively irreversible.

1. **Scope and coverage:** Identify which risks are addressed, which are outside the control's design, and whether the control covered this payment. A clean AV-2 pass is strong evidence only for its listed checks. Receipt, service quality, commercial reasonableness, collusion, and control of a compromised account need other evidence.
2. **Timing:** A control is strongest before release. A substantive independent approval after release but before bank settlement cannot undo the release event, but can prevent the irreversible transfer if settlement is still genuinely stoppable. An approval after settlement is retrospective. An audit is useful for correction only while a reliable stop, recall, or recovery route remains.
3. **Independence and collusion resistance:** Confirm distinct human actors, appropriate authority, no unresolved conflict, separate credentials, and evidence that the reviewer did not merely approve the operator's work. Distinct resource names alone do not establish independence. Repeated pairs and shared workstations are risk signals, not proof of collusion.
4. **Overrideability and failure behavior:** Distinguish a hard block from a warning. Record who overrode it, under what authority, and why. An unavailable validator is not a clean pass. An unsigned manual checklist is not an equivalent substitute without demonstrated coverage, independent review, and reliable evidence.
5. **Evidence quality:** Require timestamped approval and validation records, the documents actually reviewed, bank-detail provenance, token activation history, audit completion, and settlement/recall records. An event called “approve” is not enough to establish what was reviewed.
6. **Recoverability and response:** Compare detection and decision times to settlement and to the recall window. A one-business-day audit may satisfy an internal deadline while arriving too late to recall a payment. “Audit review” timestamps may indicate the start of work rather than its completion.
7. **Residual risk and classification:**
   - **Fully equivalent:** The package covers the material objectives with reliable evidence, independent and substantive review, suitable timing at or before the irreversible boundary, controlled overrides, and feasible response. Residual risks are understood and within the relevant control standard.
   - **Partially equivalent:** It replaces important functions of four-eyes for this payment but leaves material gaps, such as approval only after release, uncertain review quality, or incomplete evidence.
   - **Merely mitigating:** It lowers the likelihood or impact of some losses without providing a dependable independent authorization or timely opportunity to stop the transfer.
   - **Ineffective:** It is absent, overridden without a valid review, conflicted, too late to affect the transfer, or too poorly evidenced to rely on.
   - **Indeterminate:** A material fact needed to choose among these states is unresolved.

The classification is not based only on whether the package has the same expected loss as FEP-1. Expected-loss estimates can conceal rare but severe losses, correlated failures, and uncertainty about whether controls actually operated. The control objective also concerns independent authorization, dependable evidence, the ability to prevent irreversible action, applicable policy requirements, and assurance across repeated transactions. A package can appear to have similar average loss while providing weaker protection against a high-impact or systematic failure.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control/action | Preventive contribution | Detective contribution | Corrective contribution | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 hard block before release | Prevents release for a covered failure | Identifies the specific covered check that failed | None needed if the payment is canceled before release | Does not cover receipt, collusion, commercial reasonableness, or account control |
| AV-2 warning and override | May prompt an authorized reviewer before release | Signals a covered anomaly | Could support cancellation if caught in time | An override is not a pass; the reviewer and basis must be independent and evidenced |
| MG-4 before release | Prevents release without separate authorization when approval is a real gate | Can expose issues while reviewing evidence | Can stop release or order correction before payment instruction | A rubber stamp, conflict, or thin evidence review makes it weak |
| MG-4 after Release_Payment but before settlement | Cannot prevent the recorded release event. It can prevent the transfer from crossing the settlement boundary if it has authority and a reliable hold/stop path. | Detects and reviews the already-issued payment instruction | Can stop settlement or permit correction before funds become effectively irrevocable | This is a hybrid, stage-relative control. It is detective relative to release and preventive relative to settlement/loss. It is not equivalent if settlement already occurred or cannot be halted. |
| MG-4 after settlement | No preventive effect on the transfer already settled | Retrospective scrutiny | Only supports correction if a separate recovery route remains | Approval after settlement does not retroactively make the release independent |
| RA-7 audit within one business day | No initial preventive effect | Detects errors, anomalies, and evidence gaps | Partly corrective when timely action can stop, recall, or recover funds | One business day is not automatically within 24 hours of settlement; completion time and action matter |
| RA-7 after recall window | No preventive effect | May still detect control failure or misconduct indicators | Recovery is uncertain and often weak | Useful for remediation and accountability, but not a substitute for timely prevention |
| ED-1 permission conditions | Prevents an exception from being authorized unless its gates are met, if enforced before preparation | Produces auditable condition checks | Its timely manager and audit conditions support stopping or recovery | ED-1 is not a second releaser and cannot make unavailable, overridden, or conflicted controls effective |

For AP-201 and AP-207, manager review after release but before settlement is therefore a genuine hybrid. It can protect against loss at the irreversible-transfer boundary, but leaves a period in which a single operator has issued the instruction. AP-207 fits ED-1's explicit pre-settlement approval condition with a substantive review. AP-201 has similar timing but no emergency token or necessity, and the review evidence is less specific.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

- **Same-user rate:** Measure completed Prepare_Payment/Release_Payment cases where the human resource is identical, using completed payments as the denominator. Report by entity, payment channel, operator, amount band, and emergency status. Keep attempted but blocked releases separate. In this sample, six of eight completed cases are same-user.
- **Override rate:** Track AV-2 warnings, hard blocks, overrides, override credential owner, actual user, reason, approver, and downstream outcome. Alert on repeated overrides, override by the preparer, or use of another manager's credential. An alert is not proof that the override was improper or that credentials were shared.
- **Late manager approval:** Record approval relative to preparation, release, and bank settlement, not just whether a manager event exists. Distinguish approval before release from approval after release but before settlement and approval after settlement. Retain evidence of the manager's review and ability to stop settlement.
- **Audit before recall window:** Measure both audit start and completion against settlement time and the bank's recall deadline. Alert before the usable recall window closes, not merely at the one-business-day ED-1 deadline. Log whether a defect was found, who initiated the recall, when the bank received it, and the result.
- **Repeated resource pairs:** Calculate recurring preparer/releaser pairs and frequency, including the direction of the pair. Combine with payment count, value, exceptions, and defects. Repeated pairing can arise from a legitimate payment cell; investigate patterns rather than treating an alert as evidence of collusion.
- **Shared workstations and sessions:** Correlate workstation, device, session, credential, and authentication records. Alert when nominally distinct actors use the same session or workstation unusually often. Shared equipment alone does not prove one person controlled both accounts.
- **Bank-change proximity:** Preserve old/new bank details, ownership verification, who requested and confirmed the change, and exact timestamps. Flag payment release within seven days of a change, especially when combined with a warning override, new vendor, or high value. A recent legitimate change is a signal to verify, not proof of fraud.
- **Control outages:** Monitor AV-2 availability, individual check status, fail-open/fail-closed behavior, manual fallback use, checklist signatures, and restoration time. Do not record an outage as a clean validation. Require evidence of compensating verification and independent approval while the service is unavailable.
- **Emergency tokens:** Store immutable activation and expiry times, the associated incident, creator/approver, and whether activation preceded preparation. Alert on late creation and reconcile application, identity-provider, and clock logs to distinguish delayed logging from a genuinely late token.
- **Human and organizational independence:** Retain manager level, reporting relationships, conflicts, role assignments, credential use, and assignment-design documents. Preserve transaction-level review artifacts rather than relying on event names alone.
- **Outcomes and learning:** Link the payment to receipt/service evidence, audit findings, settlement, recall, recovery, and remediation. Sample clean payments as well as exceptions; an absence of detected defects is not proof that the control was effective.

These are triage and assurance signals. None of a same-user match, override, repeated pair, shared workstation, recent bank change, or control outage proves fraud or collusion by itself.
