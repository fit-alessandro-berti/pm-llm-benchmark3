## 1. Analytical assumptions

I treat a **case notion** as an analytical projection, not as a uniquely recoverable ground truth. An Order-based case is the chronological sequence of events directly related to an order; a Package-based case is the sequence directly related to a package, and similarly for the other types. Shared events are copied into the relevant cases. Events are ordered by timestamp; the equal timestamps of `e29` and `e30` concern different cases, so their relative ordering is immaterial.

**Convergence** means several business workstreams meeting through a shared event or object: `P3` brings together `O2` and `O3`, while `D2` carries packages associated with three orders. **Divergence** means one order splitting across resources, or one execution involving several orders: `O1` splits across `P1` and `P2`, and `P3` serves two orders.

Copied shared events are **both evidence and potential duplication**. The copies are valid evidence that, for example, `e16` directly involves `O1`, `O2`, and `O3`. They are not three independent dispatch executions. I retain each event’s identifier and complete related-object set outside the flattened case.

The baseline uses **only the supplied E2O relations**. I permit explicitly labelled transformations:

- Relation-specific endpoint back-projection, such as following `M3  settles  V2  bills  O2/O3` to recover endpoints of `e25`.
- A separate, unrestricted graph-closure diagnostic.

These transformations must not be mixed with direct E2O evidence. Closure can introduce misleading associations: from `O4`, one can reach `O3`, `I31`, and `P3`, and thereby `O2`. Adding `e02` to an `O4` case through that closure would not make `e02` an actual `O4` event.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Order flattening: apparent payment/physical-feedback loops — harmful if called rework.**  
   In `O2` and `O3`, `e22  e24` creates `Record Payment  Confirm Delivery`. Conversely, `e24  e25` creates `Confirm Delivery  Record Payment`; `O3` also has `e25  e27`. The latter is an instalment followed the next morning, not necessarily reopened physical work. These edges are **informative evidence of interleaved administrative and physical work**, but harmful if interpreted uniformly as exception correction.

2. **Order flattening: disappearance of `O1`’s physical branches — context-dependent.**  
   `O1` has `Pack Package` at `e11` and `e12`, then `Dispatch` at `e15` and `e16`, and confirmation at `e23/e24`. The resulting repeated activities conceal the meaningful split across `P1/D1` and `P2/D2`. Removing the split would be harmful; retaining the Order log while maintaining package sidecars is informative.

3. **Order and Package flattening: `P3` merges `O2` and `O3` — informative convergence, misleading independence.**  
   `e13` contains `O2/O3/I21/I31/P3`; `e16` and `e24` jointly involve the same orders through `D2`. Order cases correctly show both orders progressing, but cannot by themselves establish that the two orders share a physical dispatch. Package `P3` shows one coherent package lifecycle but not two independent package populations. The effect is harmful if event counts are treated as independent workloads.

4. **Order and Package flattening: `P4` joins `O3` and `O4` differently from `P3` — context-dependent.**  
   At `e14`, `P4` contains `I32` from `O3` and `I41` from `O4`; `e17` and `e27` subsequently involve `D3`. The Order log shows `O3` also travelling through `P3/D2`, whereas `O4` follows only this `P4/D3` branch. Identical package DFG paths can therefore conceal different order-level histories.

5. **Delivery flattening: `D2` looks like one simple case — harmful for end-to-end analysis.**  
   `e16` directly relates three orders, three items, two packages, and `D2`; `e24` repeats that shared membership. A `D2` trace contains only dispatch and confirmation. It is safe to conclude a `4:55` dispatch-to-confirmation interval, but not that the delivery represents a simple two-step fulfilment process or three independent shipments.

6. **Invoice flattening: `V2` has a payment loop but loses instalment meaning — harmful without O2O context.**  
   `V2` sees `Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Record Payment` through `e19/e22/e25`. However, `M2` is `partially_settles` and `M3` is `completes_settlement`; both labels flatten to `Record Payment`. The repeated activity is informative evidence of two recordings, but the self-loop alone cannot distinguish partial from final settlement.

7. **Payment flattening: four apparent instantaneous cases — harmful for performance inference.**  
   `M1`, `M2`, `M3`, and `M4` each participate in one event. They have no DFG edge or within-case elapsed time. That is a **near-singleton lifecycle caused by object choice**, not evidence that payments are instantaneous. The missing context includes `M2/M3  V2  O2/O3`.

8. **Delivery flattening: two-event lifecycles hide upstream and downstream time — context-dependent.**  
   The intervals are `D1=5:00`, `D2=4:55`, and `D3=22:45` between dispatch and confirmation. These are meaningful operational intervals, but orders were created on February 3 before `e15/e16/e17`, and invoices/payments/closures are outside these cases. A two-event skeleton is useful for this bounded question, not for total lead time.

9. **Item/Package flattening: equally short logistics traces hide different coverage — harmful in cross-view comparison.**  
   Every item and package has four direct events, but Item traces have four activity types while Package traces have only three. Neither includes invoice or closure. Moreover, equal-looking `I21/I31` cases correspond to shared `P3`, and equal-looking `I32/I41` cases correspond to shared `P4`. Equal trace length therefore does not establish equal process complexity.

10. **All frequency calculations: repeated labels and copied events are different — harmful if conflated.**  
    The 30 directly order-related events become 42 rows, but only 12 rows are surplus copies. The Item log similarly has 30 rows from 16 events. Conversely, `e29` and `e30` are distinct close events for `O2` and `O3`, despite sharing an activity and timestamp. Neither repeated labels nor valid cross-case copies should be deduplicated blindly.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

I would create **all six direct-E2O flattened logs**, but label them as views of one OCEL:

- **Order:** safe for per-order chronological coverage, activity presence, and order-level event timing.
- **Item:** safe for item handling intervals and same-event item bundles.
- **Package:** safe for package pack–dispatch–confirm sequences and mixed-order packaging when joined to O2O evidence.
- **Delivery:** safe for dispatch-to-confirmation duration.
- **Invoice:** safe for issue/payment-recording chronology.
- **Payment:** safe for locating payment-object events, not settlement completeness.

Each flattened log should retain a sidecar containing event identifiers, all directly related objects, and relevant O2O joins. Without that sidecar, a flattened log alone cannot reconstruct the nine-object membership of `e16` or the two-order membership of `V2`.

The **OC-DFG** is needed to inspect co-occurrence across activity and object types, especially `e16/e24` and the payment/confirmations. The **OTG** and **object-interaction summary** are needed for object-level relationships such as `P3–D2`, `P4–D3`, and the package-to-order associations. The **ET–OT graph** provides a more compact activity/object-type dependency view, but can obscure which particular `P3` or invoice caused an association. Equivalent-execution clustering is optional, not a prerequisite: Order is a declared business hierarchy, not a discovered equivalence relation.

Contradictions should be reconciled by returning to shared event identifiers and O2O relations. If an Order DFG suggests a feedback loop, inspect `e24/e25`; if a Delivery trace suggests simplicity, inspect all `D2` members; if Invoice repetition suggests instalments, inspect the `M2/M3` qualifiers. Neither projection should silently overwrite the other.

## 5. Lead-object decision

### B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.

**Decision criteria**

- Coverage of creation, physical fulfilment, financial recording, and closure.
- Interpretability of event frequencies and elapsed times.
- Preservation of cross-order and cross-resource relationships.
- Explicit accounting for shared events and truncated lifecycles.

**Strongest supporting evidence**

Order provides the broadest direct coverage: every `O1–O4` case contains all eight activity types, and all four reach `Close Order`. This supports Order as a useful reporting hierarchy for the stated end-to-end objective.

**Strongest counterargument**

Order is not a self-sufficient physical or financial explanation. Its cases hide `P3` versus `P4`, conceal the `P2/P3` consolidation in `D2`, and introduce mixed payment/confirmation loops. The missing direct order relations on `e25/e26` also show that static relationships cannot simply be assumed.

**Residual risk**

Users may still treat `O1–O4` as independent homogeneous cases, sum overlapping process times, or interpret DFG edges as causal. Those risks require shared-event denominators, sidecar relationships, and secondary resource views.

**Conditions for change**

I would make Package primary for a package-centric physical-throughput study, or Invoice primary for a strictly receivables question supported by payment-state data. I would reject Order as primary if order creation/closure were not mandatory lifecycle events, or if inferred endpoint propagation were the only way to obtain essential financial coverage. Automatic clustering would not itself justify a lead change.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

The block assumes a current PM4Py-style `ocel` with `events`, `activity_types`, `object_types`, `get_objects()`, and exploded E2O columns. It deliberately does not call `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`: the decision uses a declared Order hierarchy, not an automated equivalence claim.

```python
import collections
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Raw inspection: preserve event identity and complete E2O context.
print(ocel)
print("Activity types:", sorted(ocel.activity_types))
print("Object types:", sorted(ocel.object_types))
print("Extended events:")
print(ocel.get_extended_events())

events = ocel.events.copy()
activity_frequency = events["activity"].value_counts(dropna=False)
print("Activity frequencies:")
print(activity_frequency.sort_index())

object_inventory = ocel.get_objects()
object_type_frequency = (
    object_inventory.groupby("object_type")["object_id"].nunique()
)
print("Inventory objects by type:")
print(object_type_frequency.sort_index())

# A local event key avoids assuming a particular event-ID column name.
events["event_key"] = range(len(events))
event_objects = pd.DataFrame(
    events[["event_key", "activity", "object_types"]]
        .explode("object_types")
        .tolist(),
    columns=["event_key", "activity", "object_type", "object_id"],
)

# Direct-E2O incidence measures: events, unique objects, and references.
ocdfg_input_frequency = (
    event_objects.groupby(["activity", "object_type"])
    .agg(
        event_count=("event_key", "nunique"),
        unique_objects=("object_id", "nunique"),
        total_object_references=("event_key", "size"),
    )
)
print("Input incidence statistics for OC-DFG object-type relations:")
print(ocdfg_input_frequency)

object_types = [
    "Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"
]
case_attribute = "diagnostic_case_id"

flat_logs = {}
flat_dfgs = {}
flat_skeletons = {}
flattening_summary = []

for object_type in object_types:
    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flatting(
        ocel,
        object_type=object_type,
        case_id_attribute=case_attribute,
    )
    flat_logs[object_type] = flat_log
    flat_dfgs[object_type] = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    flat_skeletons[object_type] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_log)

    selected = event_objects[
        event_objects["object_type"] == object_type
    ]
    distinct_events = selected["event_key"].nunique()
    flattening_summary.append({
        "object_type": object_type,
        "cases": flat_log.attributes[case_attribute].nunique(),
        "distinct_direct_events": distinct_events,
        "flattened_event_rows": len(flat_log),
        "surplus_copies": len(flat_log) - distinct_events,
    })

flattening_summary = pd.DataFrame(flattening_summary)
print("Flattening comparison:")
print(flattening_summary)

for object_type in object_types:
    print("\nDFG:", object_type)
    print(flat_dfgs[object_type].graph)
    print("Skeleton:", object_type)
    print(flat_skeletons[object_type])

# Object-centric graphs preserve cross-type information lost by flattening.
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

print("OC-DFG:", ocdfg)
print("OTG:", otg)
print("ET-OT graph:", etot_graph)

# Audit native edge annotations rather than assuming one frequency convention.
ocdfg_edge_attributes = pd.DataFrame([
    {"source": source, "target": target, "attributes": attributes}
    for source, target, attributes in ocdfg.graph.edges(data=True)
])
print("OC-DFG native edge attributes:")
print(ocdfg_edge_attributes)

object_interactions_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
print("Object interaction summary:")
print(object_interactions_summary)

# These are semantic validation prompts, not conclusions from graph appearance.
semantic_review = {
    "order_payment_confirmation_loop": (
        "Inspect e22/e24/e25/e27 before calling this rework."
    ),
    "D2_consolidation": (
        "Verify O1/O2/O3, I12/I21/I31, and P2/P3 in e16/e24."
    ),
    "V2_instalments": (
        "Join M2/M3 qualifiers; repeated Record Payment alone is insufficient."
    ),
}
print("Required semantic reviews:", semantic_review)
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Copied-event sensitivity.**  
   Recompute flattened and DFG results with all shared events removed, then with shared-event weights \(1/k\), where `k` is the number of direct cases. For example, `e16/e24` have `k=3`; `e14/e17/e19/e22/e25/e27` have `k=2`. Separately report the number of distinct global events exposed to each case. Removing `e14` entirely may discard the real `P4` collaboration, so weighting is generally more informative than indiscriminate deletion.

2. **Direct versus propagated relations.**  
   Compare the baseline with a relation-specific endpoint-back-projection variant. Following `M3  V2  O2/O3` would expose `O2/O3` to `e25`, but the new membership must be marked inferred. Separately test unrestricted closure and reject assignments such as adding `e02` to `O4` merely because graph reachability exists. Report how many event memberships change.

3. **Change the lead object.**  
   Recalculate coverage, variants, frequencies, and performance under all six choices using a common business question. For example, Package is preferable for `P3`’s physical consolidation, while Invoice is preferable for `V2`’s billing chronology. Do not choose the longest traces: `O3`’s 13 events partly reflect participation in shared packages and events.

4. **Compare frequency units.**  
   For an OC-DFG relation, report distinct events, unique participating objects, and total object references. `Dispatch  Order` occurs in only `e16` and `e17`: **2 events, 4 unique orders, 5 order references**. `Record Payment  Order` has **4 events, 4 unique orders, 6 references**. These metrics answer different questions and must not be interchanged. Likewise, the six flattened logs contain 101 E2O rows, not 101 independent process events.

5. **Validate interpretations against the named business facts.**  
   Check that analyses can recover `P3: O2/O3`, `P4: O3/O4`, `D2: P2/P3`, `V2: O2/O3`, and the different settlement roles of `M2/M3`. Treat successful recovery as a consistency check—not proof that one flattened case definition is uniquely correct.

6. **Use time-window sensitivity.**  
   Compare dispatch-to-confirmation, package-to-confirmation, invoice-to-final-payment, and creation-to-closure only where their endpoints are directly available. This prevents `D3`’s 22:45 dispatch interval from being mistaken for its full fulfilment cycle, and prevents Package traces from being credited with financial completion time.
