## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped gap (0.32–0.40 fitness points from March onward) shows that 85–96% of the apparent conformance loss disappears after two provenance repairs: renaming `Eligibility Screen` and removing duplicated `Open Case`. It implies that the old model is being replayed against a new vocabulary, not that half of the behaviour became non-compliant. It does not imply that the repaired log equals the old process: mapped fitness never returns to 0.925, the gap itself moves (0.40 in April, 0.32 in December), and 140 March cases still carried the old label, so label share is a time-varying nuisance variable.

A mapping restores conformance by construction: any activity in the same position, given the old name, will replay. It cannot show that `Eligibility Screen` applies the same rules, is executed by the same roles, takes the same time, or rejects the same cases. That requires comparing attributes, outcomes, and durations across the cut-over, ideally with the old-label March cases as a concurrent control.

Duplicated technical events inflate variant counts, add spurious DFG edges, depress fitness, and may shift reconstructed case starts, but hardly touch operational cycle times. Missing resources leave control-flow analysis intact yet bias handover, workload, and per-assessor service analyses; because missingness concentrates in migrated activities, it is not ignorable.

The post-May changes survive normalization. `Assess -> Request Info` doubles on edges whose labels never changed; rework and cycle-time series come from operational tables; queue waiting grows while labels, duplicates, and missingness stay flat. Logging drift alone cannot produce that pattern.

What the table supports, in order of robustness:

- **March is invisible outside the logging metrics.** Median +0.2%, rework +0.5 pp, fast −2 pp are all inside one or two binomial standard errors (about 1.3–1.9 pp at n ≈ 620–640).
- **May is a different event.** Rework +11 pp against April (two-proportion z ≈ 5.2), fast −10 pp (z ≈ −3.7), median +14%, p90 +18%, while labels, duplicates, and missingness do not move.
- **Fast-to-rework transfer.** "Other" variants stay at 30–32% from January to September, so the May–August loss of fast cases went almost one-for-one into the rework variant. Only in October–November does a third path grow (other variants 36%), in step with `Senior Assessment`.
- **More cases loop, not deeper loops.** `Assess -> Request Info` per 100 cases equals the rework-variant share in every month where both are given (12/12, 24/24, 29/29, 20/20, 26/26, 21/21), i.e. about one loop per reworked case on average.
- **The Supplier Z exposure is odd.** Z cases grow 2.5-fold in May while non-Z cases fall by about 150 and total volume barely changes. A disruption that degrades bundle quality does not by itself explain a tripled share; rerouting, re-attribution, or a change in how the Z flag is set must be checked before any per-supplier rate is trusted.
- **Composition alone gets surprisingly far in May, but not beyond.** Fixed stratum rates of about 40% rework for Z and 7% for other cases reproduce January–April and predict about 21% for May (observed 24%), so May by itself does not prove that Z quality worsened. The same rates predict about 22% for July (observed 29%) and 15% for September (observed 20%), so within-stratum behaviour must also have changed; which stratum changed is not identifiable here.
- **Supplier Z alone cannot explain autumn rework.** If non-Z rework had stayed at 11.5%, the Z rework rate implied by the aggregate would be about 41% (May), 51% (July), 47% (September) and then 67–76% (October–November), after Z reported recovery. The last figures are implausible, so a second rework driver is required from October. A purely descriptive 12-point fit, rework ≈ −5.4 + 0.42·Z% + 0.52·HC%, reproduces every month within 1.9 pp; it is ecological, collinear, and has three parameters, so it shows only that Z share and complexity share together are *sufficient* to describe the series.
- **Assess workload.** Executions rise about 33% by July–August (35% and 31%); with median service at 5.4 instead of 4.8 h this is roughly +50% assessor-hours, before any capacity was lost.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG distance

Measure: for month m and reference January, **L1(m) = Σ over the seven supplied relations of the absolute rate difference**, in occurrences per 100 cases. For scale-free comparison I add the **Ruzicka distance 1 − Σmin/Σmax**, the share of L1 contributed by three edge blocks (rework: `Assess->RequestInfo`, `RequestInfo->CollectDocs`; senior: `Assess->SeniorAssessment`; mainline: the other four), and the **cosine between drift vectors** (month minus January) to compare direction rather than size.

Assumptions: omitted edges are treated as unchanged, so every distance is a lower bound; rates are intensities, not probabilities, so I do not renormalize them or use an entropy-based divergence; February is not available, so January alone is the reference; `Register->Eligibility` is constant at 100 and contributes nothing. With n ≈ 620–760 and integer rounding, the expected L1 between two samples of an unchanged process is roughly 5–9, so values below about 10 are not interpretable.

| month | L1 vs Jan | Ruzicka vs Jan | L1 vs previous listed month | rework share of L1 | senior share | mainline share | cosine with July drift vector |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-03 | 3 | 0.007 | 3 | 0% | 33% | 67% | not meaningful (noise) |
| 2026-05 | 40 | 0.093 | 37 | 60% | 8% | 33% | 1.00 |
| 2026-07 | 59 | 0.133 | 19 | 58% | 8% | 34% | 1.00 (reference) |
| 2026-09 | 28 | 0.066 | 31 | 57% | 11% | 32% | 0.99 |
| 2026-10 | 64 | 0.143 | 36 | 44% | 28% | 28% | 0.88 |
| 2026-12 | 36 | 0.084 | 28 | 50% | 28% | 22% | 0.90 |

Interpretation:

- March is not a control-flow change once labels are normalized.
- May, July, and September lie on one direction (cosine ≥ 0.99) with amplitude 40 → 59 → 28: one mechanism waxing and waning.
- October is as far from January as July (64 versus 59) but points elsewhere: the senior edge supplies 28% of the distance (4 → 22 per 100). Magnitude alone would call October "the same drift returning"; direction says it is a different mixture. December shrinks in amplitude but keeps the October direction.
- The fragment does not conserve flow, and the residual grows. Inflow to `Collect Documents` minus `CollectDocs->Assess` is 14 per 100 in January, 28 in May, 34 in July, 23 in September, 30 in October. The combined decision edge falls 83 → 72 → 79 → 73 → 78. If rates count occurrences, a growing share of re-collected cases reaches Assess or a decision through omitted edges (repeat collections, intermediate checks, decisions without a logged re-assessment). If they count cases, more cases exit or stall before assessment. The fragment cannot distinguish these, but either way some post-May drift sits on edges that were not supplied.
- This matters for conformance: the normative model already permits the `Request Info` loop, so legitimate rework should not lower fitness. Yet mapped fitness correlates −0.94 with the rework share. Whatever travels with rework (incomplete traces, skipped re-assessment, repeated collection) is non-conforming behaviour worth identifying with alignments.
- In the raw October snippet, 160 of 167 senior assessments lead directly to a decision, 34% of them rejections; without an earlier comparator this is a reference value, not a finding.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Medians are not additive and the mechanisms interact, so the split below is a set of ranges under an explicit convention: Supplier Z's effect is booked under rework (its mediator), complexity under case mix, and queueing means waiting not attributable to extra loops. Four pieces of evidence bound the ranges:

1. **Service.** +0.6 h per Assess execution, times about 1.3 executions per case, is about +0.8 h per case, against +13.5 h in the median. Service is small unless other activities slowed, which is unobserved.
2. **Supplier onset with staffing intact (April → May).** +11 pp rework coincided with +4.5 h median and +13 h p90, about 0.4 h of median per rework point. This includes re-queuing inside the loop.
3. **Matched-load contrast.** May, September, and December have nearly equal Assess execution indices (1.23, 1.18, 1.22) and similar volumes (680, 670, 690), but medians of 36.5, 41.5, and 42.3 h. The same offered load therefore costs 5–6 h more after mid-June. That difference bundles the staffing loss, the priority rule, and backlog carry-over; December also has more complex cases and overtime.
4. **Descriptive fit.** Median ≈ 20.7 + 0.45·rework% + 0.25·HC% + 4.5·(July onward) fits all twelve months within 1.3 h. With four parameters on twelve collinear points it is a consistency check, not an estimate.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| case mix (complexity, volume composition) | about 1–2.5 h (5–20%) | about 4–6 h (25–35%) | HC share +8 pp, then +21 to +25 pp; 0.25 h per pp in the descriptive fit | Stratum-specific cycle times are not supplied; complexity recording is unreliable |
| control-flow/rework | about 5–7.5 h (35–55%) | about 6–8 h (35–45%) | 16–17 pp excess rework at 0.4–0.45 h per pp | Overlaps with queueing because every loop re-enters the Assess queue; in autumn roughly two thirds of the excess rework is associated with complexity share rather than Z share |
| service | about 0.6–1 h (about 5%) | not observed; assumed at most 1–2 h | Assess service 4.8 → 5.4 h | Only one activity and only through August; part of it may be case mix |
| queueing (capacity, priority, backlog) | about 4–6.5 h (30–45%) | about 4.5–6.5 h (25–35%) | Matched-load contrast and July-onward step | Cannot separate staffing from the priority rule or carry-over; nonlinear in utilisation |

The tail deteriorates more than the median (p90/median 2.18 → 2.44–2.47). Rework loops and deprioritised standard cases are the natural candidates, but I do not attempt a p90 split from aggregates. The qualitative ordering I would defend is: queueing and rework together account for the large majority of the deterioration in both episodes, service for very little, and case mix moves from a minor contributor in summer to a major one in autumn.

### 3.5 One global change point or several?

Several mechanism-specific ones. Five observations make a single change point untenable:

1. Logging artefacts step in March while every process metric is flat.
2. Rework and performance step in May while every logging metric is flat.
3. Performance keeps deteriorating June–August after rework has plateaued, so performance has its own onset.
4. The September reversal is uneven: Z share retraces about two thirds of its excess over baseline (+29.5 → +9.5 pp), rework about half (+17.5 → +8.5 pp), and the median about a quarter (+13.9 → +10.4 h). Mechanisms with different persistence are at work.
5. October's DFG drift has a different direction from July's.

A global detector would return whichever artefact has the largest standardised jump: March on raw fitness, May or June on cycle time, October on complexity share. A composite index would lock onto March and mask the rest. Change-point analysis should therefore run per artefact family on label-normalized and stratified series, and the detected points should then be compared with the known timeline rather than fitted to it.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Whole log, dual labelling | Raw and normalized activity, registration cohort week, completion flag | Headline series; detect logging drift by contrasting labellings | At least 150 cases per week; only cohorts older than about 10 days for cycle-time and outcome metrics | Raw versus mapped; cohort versus completion view | Completion-based selection hides slow open cases; registration cohorts avoid it |
| L1 Regime windows | Eras bounded by the timeline: before 03-01; 03-01 to 05-04; 05-05 to 06-14; 06-15 to 06-30; 07-01 to 09-09; 09-10 to 09-30; 10-01 to 12-04; from 12-05 | Compare homogeneous periods; localise mid-month onsets | At least 300 cases per era; merge the two short eras (06-15 to 06-30, 09-10 to 09-30) with a neighbour for tail metrics | Before and after each event with others held fixed where possible | Era boundaries come from stakeholders' accounts; fitting to them invites confirmation, so run data-driven change points in parallel |
| L2 Supplier × complexity | Z or non-Z; high or standard complexity at registration | Separate within-stratum behaviour from mix | At least 100 cases per cell-window for rates, 200 for p90; the Z × high cell (about 25–40 cases per month before May) is pooled to 4-week or era windows | Supplier effect within complexity; seasonal effect within supplier; standardised whole-log index | Complexity may be affected by the priority rule; Z membership may have been redefined in May |
| L3 Priority class × era | Priority class, eras from 07-01 | Detect redistribution of delay | At least 100 cases per class-window; weekly for the standard class, 2-weekly for the priority class in summer | Class-specific waits before and after the rule and across the season | Class assignment is post-rule behaviour; up-classification would bias both classes |
| L4 Team and resource status | Team, resource known or missing, assessor tenure band | Capacity, handover, and service analyses | At least 30 Assess executions per assessor-window, 150 per team-window; otherwise aggregate to team | Staffing change within team; request propensity across assessors | Known-resource subset is selected by the migration; conclusions hold only where missingness is below about 2% |
| L5 Provenance strata | Label version of the eligibility event, duplicate flag, source system | Quantify logging artefacts and test equivalence of old and new recording | At least 100 cases per label version per window (available in March, later only if old labels persist) | Old-label versus new-label concurrent cases | Rollout order may correlate with unit, region, or case type |
| L6 Region and customer type within supplier | Region, customer type, channel | Check that supplier effects are not regional or customer effects | At least 100 cases per cell per era; regions below that are grouped by similarity | Z versus non-Z within region | Sparse cells tempt post-hoc grouping; fix the grouping in advance |

Rule for sparse cells: widen the time window first (week → 4 weeks → era), then coarsen the least informative variable, and never report a rate from fewer than 30 cases; where many small cells remain, use partially pooled (hierarchical) estimates rather than dropping them.

**Primary monitoring population:** label-normalized, non-Supplier-Z, standard-complexity cases (complexity fixed at registration), followed as weekly registration cohorts with a maturity lag. It is the largest stable stratum (an estimated 40–60% of volume, about 280–400 cases per month, if supplier and complexity are roughly independent), it is least exposed to the supplier disruption and to the seasonal complexity surge, so movement in it points to internal causes (capacity, policy, logging) rather than to composition, and it is the class the priority rule pushes back, which makes it the most sensitive early indicator of capacity shortage. It is not representative, so it is paired with a mix-standardised whole-log index and with the Z and high-complexity strata as sentinel populations, and its own share of volume is monitored to detect reclassification.

**Why thresholds differ.** Label and schema checks are deterministic set comparisons; proportions get binomial limits that scale with the stratum's sample size; quantile-based time metrics get relative margins plus persistence because their sampling error is larger; distances get an empirical null because their distribution depends on the number and frequency of edges; outcomes need long maturity lags. A single universal threshold would be simultaneously too loose for labels and too tight for small strata.

**Seasonality.** Every seasonal metric carries three references: a fixed pre-drift anchor (how far from the intended process), a lagged rolling window (what changed recently), and the same weeks of the previous year (is this the usual season). Until a second renewal season exists, October–December 2026 is stored as a provisional template with wide bands, explicitly marked as contaminated by the staffing gap and the priority rule. Alerts are evaluated on mix-standardised series so that expected composition changes do not fire, while the mix monitor reports the composition itself. A change calendar (releases, rule changes, campaigns, supplier notices) annotates alerts rather than suppressing them, and references are re-baselined only after a cause is signed off.

**Multiple testing.** Fourteen monitors over several strata and 52 weeks produce thousands of tests a year. I would (a) define a small first tier of about six series on the primary population with strict family-wise control (wider limits plus persistence over two of three windows), (b) evaluate stratified diagnostics as a second tier under a false-discovery-rate procedure, mainly when a first-tier alert fires, (c) require an effect-size floor in addition to statistical significance, (d) bundle co-occurring alerts into one incident labelled with the matching mechanism signature, and (e) review alert precision quarterly and adjust limits per monitor.

# Raw DFG snippets used only as a cross-check of the normalized rates.
RAW_SNIPPETS = [  # (month, normalized column, raw count)
    ("2026-01", "assess_reqinfo", 75), ("2026-01", "reqinfo_collect", 70),
    ("2026-05", "assess_reqinfo", 163), ("2026-05", "reqinfo_collect", 155),
    ("2026-07", "assess_reqinfo", 207), ("2026-07", "reqinfo_collect", 199),
    ("2026-10", "assess_senior", 167),

BASELINE_MONTHS = ["2026-01", "2026-02"]   # the only pre-migration months
PCT_COLS = ["high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
            "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct"]
EDGE_BLOCKS = {
    "mainline": ["reg_elig", "elig_collect", "collect_assess", "assess_or_senior_decision"],
    "rework": ["assess_reqinfo", "reqinfo_collect"],
    "senior": ["assess_senior"],
}
# Metric-specific step rules (deliberately NOT one universal threshold).
#   kind "prop": two-proportion z-test between consecutive months AND a minimum step in pp
#   kind "rel" : relative month-over-month change;  kind "abs": absolute change
STEP_RULES = {
    "cases": ("rel", 0.10), "median_cycle_h": ("rel", 0.075), "p90_cycle_h": ("rel", 0.10),
    "raw_model_fitness": ("abs", 0.05), "mapped_model_fitness": ("abs", 0.03),
    "distinct_activity_labels": ("abs", 1), "p90_to_median": ("abs", 0.08),
    "high_complexity_pct": ("prop", 3.0), "supplier_z_pct": ("prop", 3.0),
    "fast_variant_pct": ("prop", 3.0), "rework_variant_pct": ("prop", 3.0),
    "missing_resource_pct": ("prop", 2.0), "duplicate_open_case_pct": ("prop", 2.0),
}
Z_CRIT = 3.0


def load_and_validate():
    m = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV), dtype={"month": str}).set_index("month")
    d = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(DFG_CSV), dtype={"month": str}).set_index("month")
    problems = []
    expected = [str(p) for p in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-12", freq="M")]
    if list(m.index) != expected:
        problems.append("monthly table is not 12 consecutive months")
    if m.isna().any().any() or d.isna().any().any():
        problems.append("missing values present")
    if not ((m[PCT_COLS] >= 0) & (m[PCT_COLS] <= 100)).all().all():
        problems.append("percentage outside 0-100")
    if not m[["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]].stack().between(0, 1).all():
        problems.append("fitness outside 0-1")
    if (m["p90_cycle_h"] < m["median_cycle_h"]).any():
        problems.append("p90 below median")
    if ((m["fast_variant_pct"] + m["rework_variant_pct"]) > 100).any():
        problems.append("fast + rework variant share above 100")
    if (m["mapped_model_fitness"] + 1e-9 < m["raw_model_fitness"]).any():
        problems.append("mapped fitness below raw fitness")
    if (m["cases"] <= 0).any() or (d < 0).any().any():
        problems.append("non-positive cases or negative DFG rate")
    if not set(d.index).issubset(m.index):
        problems.append("DFG month absent from monthly table")
    for month, col, raw in RAW_SNIPPETS:      # raw counts vs normalized rates (rounding tolerance 1)
        implied = 100.0 * raw / m.loc[month, "cases"]
        if abs(implied - d.loc[month, col]) > 1.0:
            problems.append(f"{month} {col}: raw snippet implies {implied:.1f}, table says {d.loc[month, col]}")
    return m, d, problems


def add_derived(m):
    m = m.copy()
    m["p90_to_median"] = m["p90_cycle_h"] / m["median_cycle_h"]
    m["other_variant_pct"] = 100 - m["fast_variant_pct"] - m["rework_variant_pct"]
    m["supplier_z_cases"] = m["cases"] * m["supplier_z_pct"] / 100
    m["non_z_cases"] = m["cases"] - m["supplier_z_cases"]
    # Assess executions needed per month, relative to baseline (service time held fixed):
    load = m["cases"] * (1 + m["rework_variant_pct"] / 100)
    m["assess_load_index"] = load / load.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS].mean()
    return m


def baseline_relative(m):
    base = m.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS].mean()
    out = {}
    for col in m.columns:
        if col.endswith("_pct") or "fitness" in col or col in ("distinct_activity_labels", "p90_to_median"):
            out[col] = m[col] - base[col]                     # absolute change (pp or points)
        else:
            out[col] = 100 * (m[col] / base[col] - 1)         # percent change
    return base, pd.DataFrame(out)


def two_prop_z(p1, n1, p2, n2):
    p1, p2 = p1 / 100, p2 / 100
    pooled = (p1 * n1 + p2 * n2) / (n1 + n2)
    se = math.sqrt(max(pooled * (1 - pooled) * (1 / n1 + 1 / n2), 1e-12))
    return (p2 - p1) / se


def dfg_distances(d, ref_month="2026-01", direction_month="2026-07"):
    """L1 (per 100 cases), Ruzicka = 1 - sum(min)/sum(max), block shares, drift direction."""
    ref = d.loc[ref_month]
    ref_dir = d.loc[direction_month] - ref
    rows, prev = [], None
    for month, row in d.iterrows():
        delta = row - ref
        l1 = delta.abs().sum()
        rec = {"month": month, "L1_vs_ref": l1,
               "ruzicka_vs_ref": 1 - np.minimum(row, ref).sum() / np.maximum(row, ref).sum(),
               "max_edge": delta.abs().idxmax() if l1 > 0 else "-",
               "L1_vs_prev_table_row": np.nan if prev is None else (row - prev).abs().sum()}
        for block, cols in EDGE_BLOCKS.items():
            rec[f"{block}_share"] = delta[cols].abs().sum() / l1 if l1 > 0 else 0.0
        denom = np.linalg.norm(delta) * np.linalg.norm(ref_dir)
        rec[f"cosine_with_{direction_month}_drift"] = float(delta @ ref_dir / denom) if denom > 0 else np.nan
        rows.append(rec)
        prev = row
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("month")


def flow_gaps(d, m):
    """Flow-conservation residuals (traffic on omitted edges) and Senior Assessment recording density."""
    out = pd.DataFrame(index=d.index)
    out["collect_in_minus_collect_to_assess"] = d["elig_collect"] + d["reqinfo_collect"] - d["collect_assess"]
    out["loop_leak_reqinfo"] = d["assess_reqinfo"] - d["reqinfo_collect"]
    out["senior_per_100_high_complexity"] = 100 * d["assess_senior"] / m.loc[d.index, "high_complexity_pct"]
    return out


def fitness_recovery(m):
    base = m.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS, "raw_model_fitness"].mean()
    f = pd.DataFrame({"raw": m["raw_model_fitness"], "mapped": m["mapped_model_fitness"]})
    f["gap_mapped_minus_raw"] = f["mapped"] - f["raw"]
    lost = base - f["raw"]
    f["recovered_share_of_raw_loss"] = np.where(lost > 0.05, f["gap_mapped_minus_raw"] / lost, np.nan)
    f["residual_vs_baseline"] = base - f["mapped"]       # not explained by the mapping
    return f


def flag_change_points(m):
    """STEP: consecutive-month jump beyond a metric-specific rule.
    LEVEL: first month from which a proportion stays >= Z_CRIT binomial SEs away from the
    Jan-Feb baseline for two consecutive months without a STEP in that month (gradual drift)."""
    base = m.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS].mean()
    n_base = m.loc[BASELINE_MONTHS, "cases"].sum()
    flags, months = [], list(m.index)
    for col, (kind, thr) in STEP_RULES.items():
        step_months = set()
        for prev, cur in zip(months[:-1], months[1:]):
            a, b = m.loc[prev, col], m.loc[cur, col]
            if kind == "prop":
                z = two_prop_z(a, m.loc[prev, "cases"], b, m.loc[cur, "cases"])
                hit, size = abs(z) >= Z_CRIT and abs(b - a) >= thr, f"{b - a:+.1f} pp (z={z:+.1f})"
            elif kind == "rel":
                hit, size = abs(b / a - 1) >= thr, f"{100 * (b / a - 1):+.1f}%"
            else:
                hit, size = abs(b - a) >= thr - 1e-9, f"{b - a:+.2f}"
            if hit:
                step_months.add(cur)
                flags.append({"metric": col, "month": cur, "type": "STEP", "size": size})
        if kind == "prop":
            zs = {mo: two_prop_z(base[col], n_base, m.loc[mo, col], m.loc[mo, "cases"])
                  for mo in months if mo not in BASELINE_MONTHS}
            keys = list(zs)
            for i in range(len(keys) - 1):
                if abs(zs[keys[i]]) >= Z_CRIT and abs(zs[keys[i + 1]]) >= Z_CRIT:
                    if keys[i] not in step_months:
                        flags.append({"metric": col, "month": keys[i], "type": "LEVEL (gradual)",
                                      "size": f"{m.loc[keys[i], col] - base[col]:+.1f} pp vs baseline"})
                    break
    return pd.DataFrame(flags).sort_values(["month", "metric"]).reset_index(drop=True)


def run_part_a():
    pd.set_option("display.width", 220, "display.max_columns", 30)
    m, d, problems = load_and_validate()

                                    "p90_to_median", "assess_load_index"]].round(2).to_string())
    print("\nDFG FRAGMENT DISTANCES (reference 2026-01):\n", dfg_distances(d).round(3).to_string())
    print("\nFLOW RESIDUALS AND SENIOR-RECORDING DENSITY:\n", flow_gaps(d, m).round(1).to_string())
    print("\nRAW-TO-MAPPED FITNESS RECOVERY:\n", fitness_recovery(m).round(3).to_string())
    print("\nCANDIDATE CHANGE POINTS (screening only):\n", flag_change_points(m).to_string())
    print("""
CAVEATS
- The DFG fragment has 7 relations; omitted edges are treated as unchanged, so distances are lower bounds
  and flow residuals show that the omitted edges are not negligible after May.

def format_event_log(df, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col, resource_col=None):
    """Standardize column names with pm4py.format_dataframe; the raw label is kept in 'activity_raw'."""
    import pm4py
    df = df.copy()
    essential = [case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col]
    dropped = int(df[essential].isna().any(axis=1).sum())
    if dropped:
        print(f"warning: {dropped} events lack case/activity/timestamp and are removed")
    df = df.dropna(subset=essential)
    df[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df[timestamp_col], utc=True)
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=case_col, activity_key=activity_col, timestamp_key=timestamp_col)
    log["activity_raw"] = log[ACT]
    if resource_col is not None:
        log["org:resource"] = log[resource_col]
        log["resource_missing"] = log["org:resource"].isna()
    return log.sort_values([CASE, TS, "@@index"]).reset_index(drop=True)


def add_normalized_labels(log, label_map=LABEL_MAP, dedup_activities=TECHNICAL_DEDUP):
    """Add 'activity_norm' and provenance flags as NEW columns; nothing is overwritten or deleted."""
    out = log.copy()
    out["activity_norm"] = out["activity_raw"].replace(label_map)
    out["was_relabelled"] = out["activity_raw"].isin(list(label_map))
    repeat = out.groupby([CASE, "activity_raw"]).cumcount() > 0
    out["is_technical_duplicate"] = out["activity_raw"].isin(list(dedup_activities)) & repeat
    return out


def view(log, labels="normalized"):
    """Analysis copy whose 'concept:name' holds the raw or the normalized labelling."""
    if labels == "raw":
        v = log.copy()
        v[ACT] = v["activity_raw"]
    elif labels == "normalized":
        v = log.loc[~log["is_technical_duplicate"]].copy()
        v[ACT] = v["activity_norm"]
    else:
        raise ValueError("labels must be 'raw' or 'normalized'")
    return v


def slice_window(log, start, end, mode="traces_starting_in"):
    """start/end as 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS'. Use 'traces_starting_in' for registration cohorts,
    'traces_contained' for complete-case conformance, 'events' for workload and queue monitors."""
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode=mode, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def month_windows(first="2026-01", last="2026-12"):
    for p in pd.period_range(first, last, freq="M"):
        yield str(p), p.start_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), p.end_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")


def dfg_rates(window_log):
    """Directly-follows occurrences per 100 cases (same unit as the supplied table)."""
    import pm4py
    n = window_log[CASE].nunique()
    if n == 0:
        return pd.Series(dtype=float)
    dfg, _start, _end = pm4py.discover_dfg(window_log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    return pd.Series({f"{a} -> {b}": 100.0 * c / n for (a, b), c in dfg.items()}, dtype=float)


def variant_shares(window_log, top_k=20):
    import pm4py
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(window_log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    counts = pd.Series({" > ".join(k): (v if isinstance(v, int) else len(v)) for k, v in variants.items()},
                       dtype=float).sort_values(ascending=False)
    return (counts / counts.sum()).head(top_k)


def temporal_profile(window_log):
    """{(a, b): (mean_seconds, std_seconds)} over eventually-follows pairs."""
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window_log, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def declarative_models(window_log, skeleton_noise=0.05, min_support=0.10, min_confidence=0.90):
    """Optional: log skeleton and DECLARE constraints for ordering/co-occurrence monitoring."""
    import pm4py
    models = {"log_skeleton": pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        window_log, noise_threshold=skeleton_noise, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)}
    try:
        models["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(
            window_log, min_support_ratio=min_support, min_confidence_ratio=min_confidence,
            activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    except Exception as exc:                      # DECLARE discovery is absent in older PM4Py releases
        models["declare"] = None
        print("DECLARE discovery skipped:", exc)
    return models


def normative_fitness(window_log, tree_string=NORMATIVE_TREE, use_alignments=False):
    """Fitness against the old normative model; the model is passed explicitly so it is versioned."""
    import pm4py
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string))
    if use_alignments:
        return pm4py.fitness_alignments(window_log, net, im, fm,
                                        activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    return pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(window_log, net, im, fm,
                                            activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def l1_and_ruzicka(rates_a, rates_b):
    """Distances over the UNION of edges (a missing edge counts as rate 0)."""
    a, b = rates_a.align(rates_b, fill_value=0.0)
    return {"L1_per_100_cases": float((a - b).abs().sum()),
            "ruzicka": float(1 - np.minimum(a, b).sum() / max(np.maximum(a, b).sum(), 1e-12)),
            "largest_edge_changes": (b - a).reindex((b - a).abs().sort_values(ascending=False).index).head(8)}


def monthly_artifacts(log, labels="normalized", segment_filter=None, min_cases=100,
                      first="2026-01", last="2026-12", mode="traces_starting_in"):
    """Per-month DFG rates, variant shares and temporal profile for one labelling and one segment.
    segment_filter: optional function(DataFrame) -> boolean mask on case-level attributes; the value at
    the first event decides for the whole case, so traces are never truncated by the segmentation."""
    v = view(log, labels)
    if segment_filter is not None:
        mask = pd.Series(np.asarray(segment_filter(v), dtype=bool), index=v.index)
        v = v.loc[mask.groupby(v[CASE]).transform("first")]
    out = {}
    for month, start, end in month_windows(first, last):
        w = slice_window(v, start, end, mode=mode)
        n = w[CASE].nunique()
        if n < min_cases:                         # avoid tiny cells: caller should pool windows instead
            out[month] = {"cases": n, "skipped": f"support below {min_cases}"}
            continue
        out[month] = {"cases": n, "dfg_rates": dfg_rates(w), "variants": variant_shares(w),
                      "temporal_profile": temporal_profile(w)}
    return out


def compare_raw_and_normalized(log, start, end, mode="traces_contained"):
    """Same window under both labellings: what the mapping repairs and what it leaves unexplained."""
    result = {}
    for labels in ("raw", "normalized"):
        w = slice_window(view(log, labels), start, end, mode=mode)
        fit = normative_fitness(w)
        result[labels] = {"cases": w[CASE].nunique(), "events": len(w), "labels": sorted(w[ACT].unique()),
                          "n_variants": len(variant_shares(w, top_k=10**9)), "dfg_rates": dfg_rates(w),
                          "log_fitness": fit.get("log_fitness")}
    result["dfg_distance_raw_vs_normalized"] = l1_and_ruzicka(result["raw"]["dfg_rates"],
                                                              result["normalized"]["dfg_rates"])
    result["fitness_gap"] = result["normalized"]["log_fitness"] - result["raw"]["log_fitness"]
    scope = slice_window(log, start, end, mode=mode)
    result["relabelled_event_share"] = float(scope["was_relabelled"].mean())
    result["technical_duplicate_case_share"] = float(scope.groupby(CASE)["is_technical_duplicate"].any().mean())
    if "resource_missing" in scope:
        result["resource_missing_by_activity"] = scope.groupby("activity_norm")["resource_missing"].mean()
    return result


def waiting_before(window_log, activity="Assess", start_timestamp_col=None):
    """Hours between the previous event of the case and the start of each `activity` execution.
    Without a start timestamp this is waiting + service (sojourn), which must be stated in any report."""
    w = window_log.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
    w["prev_ts"] = w.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(1)
    begin = w[start_timestamp_col] if start_timestamp_col else w[TS]
    w["wait_h"] = (begin - w["prev_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    return w.loc[w[ACT] == activity, [CASE, TS, "wait_h"]].dropna()

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest (confidence 85% or more).** The March collapse of raw fitness is a provenance artefact of the migration: labels, duplicates, and resource gaps step together while rework, variants, cycle times, and the normalized DFG stay within noise, and the mapping recovers 93–96% of the loss. Equally strong: a real control-flow change begins in May on label-independent edges and in operational tables, so it is not a logging effect.

**Probable (60–80%).** Supplier Z's incomplete bundles are the main driver of the May–August rework rise, since rework follows Z share up in May and down in September. The June–August increase in cycle time is predominantly queueing before `Assess`, not slower assessment (service +0.6 h against +13 h in the median). October–November is mainly a seasonal volume-and-complexity shock hitting a process that was already capacity-constrained.

**Only plausible (30–55%).** That the staffing loss, rather than rework-inflated load, is the larger cause of queueing; that the priority rule lengthened the standard-case tail; that elevated rework after Z's recovery reflects lasting behavioural adaptation rather than residual Z backlog or complexity; that December's improvement is due to overtime and the quality campaign rather than seasonal relief; that the October jump in `Senior Assessment` is real routing rather than more complete recording.

**No clean attribution.** Supplier Z acts through composition and quality at once: its share triples and its cases were already more complex. Rework mediates between supplier or complexity and delay, so "rework" and "queueing" hours overlap. The staffing loss, the priority rule, and backlog carry-over begin within sixteen days of each other and persist together, and the season multiplies all of them. The hour shares in section 3 are therefore ranges under a stated convention, not estimates.

**What would change this account.** Non-Z or standard-complexity strata showing the same rework rise in May (an internal policy change); old-label and new-label March cases differing in outcomes or durations (the migration changed behaviour); queue waits rising before 15 June at unchanged capacity (load, not staffing); standard-case waits not diverging from high-complexity waits after 1 July (no priority effect); October–December 2025 showing the same mix-standardised deviation (recurrence, not new drift); or evidence that the Supplier Z flag was redefined in May.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops by more than 0.10 while mapped fitness, rework rate, and median cycle time stay within their limits and the label vocabulary or duplicate rate has changed, THEN open a data-provenance ticket, version the label mapping, and withhold process-drift conclusions for that artefact, BECAUSE a conformance loss confined to the raw labelling is the signature of logging or semantic drift rather than changed behaviour.
2. IF the `Assess -> Request Info` rate rises in Supplier Z cases but not in non-Z cases of the same complexity class, THEN escalate to supplier management and screen Z bundles for completeness before they enter assessment, BECAUSE rework that is specific to one supplier within fixed strata indicates input-quality drift, not population change or assessor behaviour.
3. IF the rework rate rises in Z and non-Z strata alike, or stays elevated for four weeks after Z bundle completeness has returned to its reference, THEN audit request-information decisions by team and assessor and review the guidance in force, BECAUSE rework no longer tied to the original exposure indicates persistent behavioural adaptation.
4. IF waiting before `Assess` exceeds its class reference for two consecutive weeks while `Assess` service time stays within limits, THEN compare offered assessor-hours (arrivals × Assess executions per case × service time) with rostered hours and add capacity or remove rework before changing how assessors work, BECAUSE waiting that grows without service-time growth is a utilisation effect of load against capacity.
5. IF standard-case waiting or p90 rises after a priority-rule change while high-complexity waiting is flat or lower and the pooled median barely moves, THEN report service levels per priority class and introduce an ageing limit for standard cases, BECAUSE a priority rule redistributes delay between classes and the aggregate conceals it.
6. IF volume and high-complexity share rise during the renewal window and the mix-standardised rework and cycle-time indices stay inside the band of the same weeks in the previous season, THEN classify the episode as recurring seasonal drift and activate the seasonal staffing plan, escalating to a root-cause review only if the band is exceeded, BECAUSE change that disappears under standardisation and repeats annually is recurrence, not new drift.
7. IF the missing-resource share of any activity exceeds 2% or differs significantly between teams, THEN mark handover, workload, and per-assessor analyses for that scope as unreliable and repair the source field before drawing staffing conclusions, BECAUSE non-random missingness concentrated in migrated activities biases exactly the analyses needed to test the staffing hypothesis.
