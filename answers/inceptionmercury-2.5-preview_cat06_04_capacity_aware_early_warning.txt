

## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility**
Cases qualify only if the current stage is strictly before `Diagnosis Complete` and time-to-deadline is under 24 hours. This ensures interventions can mechanically affect the process path.

**Probability Adjustment for Calibration Uncertainty**
Observed probabilities (`p_now`) are not taken as ground truth. We apply segment-specific offsets validated in backtests before calculating expected value.
*   **SME:** Add +0.07 to p (model underpredicts).
*   **Strategic Enterprise:** Subtract 0.08 from p (model overpredicts).
*   **Public Sector:** No adjustment (calibration acceptable).
*   **Non-Native-Language:** Apply 0.05 adjustment and reduce estimated risk-reduction effect by 50% to reflect data scarcity.

**Alert Persistence and Re-entry**
A repeated alert on the same case requires a "materially new event" (e.g., new logs, escalated ticket) to be actionable. Without new events, subsequent alerts are suppressed for the next cycle. This prevents noise from consuming scarce swarm capacity.

**Prioritization Logic**
We calculate a Prioritization Score (PS) for eligible cases.
1.  **Adjusted Probability of Breach:** $p_{adj} = p_{now} + \text{offset}$
2.  **Expected Direct Benefit:** $E[\text{Benefit}] = (p_{adj} \times \text{Value}) - \text{Swarm Cost} - \text{False Alarm Burden}$
3.  **Spillover Penalty:** Subtract estimated aggregate loss imposed on untreated cases per slot used (6 untreated cases $\times$ baseline risk increase $\times$ avg. case value).
4.  **Final Rank:** Select cases with positive net score in descending order, subject to capacity and diversity checks.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down Period:** 30 minutes (aligned with model refresh cycle).

**Suppression Logic:**
*   **I-05 (Suppressed):** The case generated three alerts without new data. We flag it as "Stale." Capacity is denied unless the ticket explicitly updates status or logs.
*   **I-07 (Re-Entry):** Despite being a repeat alert, a new diagnostic log was uploaded. The data quality signal overrides the suppression rule. This case is re-evaluated immediately.

**Escalation:** If a case receives three suppressions within 2 hours, it is flagged for manual review to determine if the threshold is too sensitive or if the case needs a different service path.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization:**
Prioritizing high-value cases (I-04, I-08) may systematically exclude lower-value SME cases (I-05, I-07), leading to higher breach rates for smaller clients.
*   *Safeguard:* Maintain a minimum 20% capacity quota for non-strategic segments regardless of score.

**Non-Native-Language Support:**
The sample size for this segment is too small for reliable calibration. Interventions may be ineffective or harmful here.
*   *Safeguard:* Exclude this segment from automated allocation until a minimum of 50 observed cases are accumulated. Route alerts manually.

**Differential Calibration:**
Strategic cases are overpredicted, which could lead to unnecessary swarm usage (false alarms) if uncorrected.
*   *Safeguard:* Apply the 0.08 offset strictly. Monitor "intervention yield" (breach avoided / intervention) by segment monthly.

**Selective Intervention:**
If we intervene only on high-risk cases, we lose data on what happens to those high-risk cases *without* intervention. This degrades future model training.
*   *Safeguard:* Randomize intervention for "borderline" cases (e.g., scores between 3rd and 4th priority) periodically to build counterfactual data.

**Spillover Harms:**
The spillover penalty (congestion) is paid by untreated cases, which are often lower-priority SME cases. This transfers harm to the very groups we risk under-servicing.
*   *Safeguard:* Track breach rates for untreated diagnosis-stage cases. If they rise >5% above baseline, reduce swarm usage regardless of selected case scores.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational Monitoring:**
*   **Slot Utilization:** Track fill rate vs. "Reserve" usage. Alert if reserve is breached without regulatory event.
*   **Spillover Metric:** Monitor average queue time for non-intervened diagnosis cases.

**Predictive & Causal Monitoring:**
*   **Calibration Check:** Compute Brier score by segment weekly. If Strategic error increases, re-tune the 0.08 offset.
*   **Intervention Effectiveness:** Compare observed breach rate of intervened cases vs. a randomized holdout of similar cases.

**Feedback Loop Triggers:**
*   **Policy Change:** If Net Impact (Adverse scenario) consistently shows loss, reduce daily slots by 1 or increase the threshold for intervention.
*   **Fairness Audit:** If SME breach rate exceeds Strategic breach rate by >10%, trigger quota override to force at least one SME case intervention per hour.

**Recalibration Cadence:**
*   Weekly: Adjust probability offsets based on error drift.
*   Monthly: Re-evaluate spillover cost estimates using queue data.
