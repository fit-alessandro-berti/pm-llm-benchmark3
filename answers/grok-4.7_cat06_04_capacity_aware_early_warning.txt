## 1. Decision rule

Treat the swarm as a scarce, congestion-generating action whose effect is an uncertain causal reduction in breach risk, not as a function of `p_now`. Observational scores are adjusted for calibration, then passed through hard gates. A case is swarmed only if it clears every gate.

**Eligibility.** Stage must be Triage or Diagnosis Started. At Diagnosis Complete or later the swarm is out of policy: route the case to resolution and customer-validation actions. A case already swarmed stays ineligible until a material new event occurs after that swarm. A load-bearing feature that is missing or imputed blocks slot consumption until the feature is restored and the case is re-scored. Time to deadline must cover the 45-minute swarm.

**Calibration layer (decision layer only; the model file is not edited).**
- SME: \(p_{adj}=p_{now}+0.06\), sensitivity \(+0.04\) to \(+0.09\).
- Strategic Enterprise: \(p_{adj}=p_{now}-0.08\), sensitivity \(-0.05\) to \(-0.12\), followed by a 10-minute artifact check for the known post-redesign overprediction before any swarm starts.
- Public Sector: \(p_{adj}=p_{now}\), informal uncertainty about \(\pm 0.03\).
- Non-Native-Language Support: keep \(p_{now}\) and display a wide band (\(\pm 0.15\)). Planning effect is shrunk: \(0.5\times\) stated midpoint \(+0.5\times 0.15\).

Cap any planned effect at \(p_{adj}\). Do not set effect equal to \(p_{now}\), \(p_{adj}\), or \(p_{now}-p_{prev}\).

**Alert persistence and minimum evidence change.** Build an evidence fingerprint from stage, external artifacts, complete (non-imputed) features, and \(p_{adj}\) rounded to 0.05. If the fingerprint is unchanged, suppress the alert for 90 minutes. Alert count and a sub-0.08 refresh do not raise priority. Re-entry requires a stage change, a new external artifact, restoration of a missing feature that moves \(p_{adj}\) by at least 0.08, an absolute \(p_{adj}\) move of at least 0.10 from complete features, or crossing a deadline band at 4h or 2h while still eligible.

**Value, externality, and rank.** For an effect level \(a\),

\[
B(a)=a\times V-450-\text{false-alarm burden}.
\]

Planning externality per slot, at reference value \(V_{ref}=€20{,}000\) for each of six untreated diagnosis-stage cases and \(\delta=0.02\):

\[
S=6\times 0.02\times 20{,}000=€2{,}400.
\]

\(V_{ref}\) is an explicit assumption, centered on the visible diagnosis-stage values, and is revised as spillover is measured. If the queue is already slow, multiply \(S\) by 1.5 and cut the daily cap by one. The delay minutes and the probability shift are one mechanism; they are not added twice.

A candidate must have \(B(a_{low})>0\). Rank by \(B(a_{mid})-S\). Diagnosis Started with 4h or less outranks a Triage case with 8h or more when surpluses are within 25%, because the eligibility window is closing. A Triage case with long slack is still eligible immediately when \(B(a_{low})-S>0\). Cases whose midpoint looks profitable and whose floor is negative stay on watch. While Strategic Enterprise bias remains at least 0.05, at most two Strategic Enterprise swarms are launched per day.

**Capacity and stop rule.** At most four slots. One stays in reserve until 15:00 for the possible regulatory arrival. Do not pull both seniors off a congested diagnosis queue; run swarms serially, tightest closing window first. If the first swarm already produces an adverse delay (about 15–30 minutes), cancel later non-regulatory launches. Regulatory use of the reserve can proceed on duty-manager sign-off when the penalty dominates the contractual values. High-\(p_{adj}\) cases that do not receive a swarm are shielded from the resulting queue delay and receive a non-swarm expedite when the deadline is inside 3 hours.

Contractual \(V\) is the default weight. A named owner may declare a non-monetary uplift for statutory or safety duty; until that declaration, the uplift is not invented case by case.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down and suppression.** After an alert, suppress further notifications for 90 minutes when the evidence fingerprint is unchanged. A 30-minute model refresh is not an event. Suppressed cases remain on a silent monitor. Priority is computed from calibrated risk, effect, value, stage, slack, data quality, and spillover. It is not computed from how many times the case has paged.

**Escalation.** Re-open a suppressed case only if the deadline crosses 4 hours or 2 hours while the case is still eligible, \(p_{adj}\) moves by at least 0.10 on complete features, a missing feature is restored and moves \(p_{adj}\) by at least 0.08, a new external artifact or activity arrives, or \(B(a_{low})\) moves from below \(S\) to above \(S\). Escalation is a re-score, not an automatic swarm.

**Re-entry after a swarm.** A second swarm requires a material event that post-dates the first swarm, and it must clear a higher bar, \(B(a_{low})>1.5\times S\), because a repeat is presumed to have a weaker marginal effect. A case that was deferred or suppressed re-enters on the same evidence rule with no penalty for the earlier deferral and no bonus for having been alerted before.

**I-05 versus I-07.** Both are third alerts on SME cases with no prior swarm, and both have their probabilities lifted for underprediction. The similarity ends at the evidence.

I-05 moved from 0.55 to 0.57 with no new event since the first alert. That is refresh noise. The alert is suppressed. The case stays eligible for a later first swarm only through the ordinary hurdle or the deadline-band rule. Its midpoint direct value, about €1,725, does not cover a €2,400 externality, so a quiet queue and an idle slot would still leave it unselected unless the equity rule in Section 5 is active.

I-07 moved from 0.75 to 0.72 and a new customer diagnostic log arrived. The fingerprint changed, so the third alert is scored as new information. Adjusted risk is about 0.76–0.81 with 1.5 hours left, still in Triage. The swarm’s own economics remain weak (about €500–€1,100 after cost and false-alarm burden). The response is an ordinary-priority expedite and protected-list shielding, not a 45-minute senior swarm. A swarm becomes admissible only as an expiring-slot, low-congestion, last-chance exception after higher claims are absent.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-weighted starvation.** \(a\times V\) promotes I-04 and I-08’s segment and leaves I-05 and I-07 behind even when their absolute risk and stated effects are larger. I-07 in particular can be breached with high probability while seniors are occupied on a €60,000 case that still has 8–10 hours. Safeguards: show dispatchers the surplus with contractual \(V\) and with \(V\) removed, so the value choice is visible. Give every eligible case with \(p_{adj}\ge 0.70\) and under 3 hours a non-swarm expedite and protected-list status; today that is I-07, and I-09 remains shielded unless it is swarmed. Cap an equity exception at one slot per day, usable only when total launches stay at or below three, congestion is not adverse, and the SME (or other low-value) candidate has \(B(a_{low})>0\). Track a four-week ratio: swarm share versus share of eligible cases with \(p_{adj}>0.60\). A gap above 15 percentage points arms the equity exception for the following week.

**Non-Native-Language Support.** I-09 can be skipped forever as “too uncertain,” or swarmed on an effect band that the sample cannot support. Safeguards: the shrinkage formula above; a hard requirement that language support is inside the swarm, with the effect planned at the floor when language support is absent; a guaranteed look at the 13:00 flexible slot rather than an automatic exclusion; and a collection rule that every case in this segment, swarmed or not, is followed until 50 completed outcomes or 20 swarms exist. Until then, a Non-Native-Language Support point score cannot outrank a better-calibrated case unless its lower planning effect still wins.

**Differential calibration.** SME cases are under-scored and would otherwise look too healthy to clear urgency bands. Strategic Enterprise cases are over-scored and, after the workflow redesign, can absorb the whole senior budget. Public Sector is the relatively usable reference, on a moderate sample. Safeguards: the segment shifts in Section 1 stay in the decision layer and are logged beside raw \(p\); weekly reliability diagrams by segment; a bias move of more than 0.03 updates the shift within five business days; the two-swarm Strategic Enterprise cap remains until measured bias is under 0.05. Today that cap is binding: I-02 and I-04 fill it, so I-08 cannot be added even if someone focuses on the €100,000 headline.

**Training-label feedback.** Swarmed cases will breach less often because seniors intervened. If those outcomes train the next model as natural history, the scored segments look safer, later swarms stop, and breaches return. If “swarm” is used as a model feature, untreated cases are scored as though they had received one. Safeguards: store \(Y_{observed}\) separately from the policy decision; forbid the swarm indicator as a feature in the alerting model; fit the baseline risk model on pre-decision prefixes; estimate effects with logged propensity and with randomization only at the margin (cases whose \(B(a_{mid})\) is within 20% of \(S\)). I-01 and I-09 are the natural margin. Keep a small shadow set when volume allows. Do not update effect ranges from fewer than about 30 swarms in a stage bucket.

**Spillovers landing on the untreated.** Each launch raises breach risk for about six diagnosis-stage cases that received nothing. If ranking always selects high \(V\), those six will disproportionately be SME, public-sector, and Non-Native-Language Support cases, so Strategic Enterprise breaches are bought with other people’s breaches. Safeguards: the hurdle already requires the treated case to clear \(S\) before launch. Within the diagnosis queue, push the delay onto low-risk, long-slack work and shield untreated cases with \(p_{adj}\ge 0.65\) and under 3 hours. Publish breach rate and wait by segment on days with 0, 1, 2, and 3+ swarms. If one segment absorbs more than 1.5 times its case share of spillover-linked breaches, raise that segment’s weight inside \(V_{ref}\) so the next week’s hurdle internalizes who is being harmed. The circuit breaker in Section 1 stops additional launches when delay is already adverse.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational.** For every swarm log start and end, stage at start, whether Diagnosis Complete occurred before the swarm finished, specialist IDs, queue length at start and at +2 hours, aggregate delay minutes, and the segment mix of delayed cases. Keep a slot ledger for used, reserved, released, and expired capacity, plus an exception log for artifact-check cancellations, equity launches, urgency overrides, and data holds. Audit eligibility at 100%: a swarm that starts at or after Diagnosis Complete is a policy breach. Review overrides weekly; more than three in a week means the rule is wrong and should be rewritten.

**Predictive.** Each week, on non-intervened cases only, report calibration slope, intercept, and error by segment, plus Brier score before and after the manual shift. Alert-stability rate (pages with an unchanged fingerprint) should fall once suppression is on; a high rate means cool-down is being bypassed. Track imputation frequency and how often an imputed score would have changed a slot decision, using I-06 as the template incident. Retire a segment shift after four consecutive weeks inside \(\pm 0.02\) of calibrated outcomes. Any new workflow change resets the Strategic Enterprise shift immediately, the same way the recent redesign did.

**Causal effect.** The estimand is absolute breach-risk reduction by stage (Triage versus Diagnosis Started) and by segment, plus the spillover estimand: change in untreated diagnosis-stage breach probability and wait as a function of slots used that day. Identify effects from margin randomization and logged propensity, not from raw before/after breach rates. Accidental post-Diagnosis Complete swarms are the negative control; a large apparent effect there means confounding, and the observational ranges should not be tightened. Keep the current wide ranges until about 30 swarms per stage bucket, or eight weeks, whichever is later. Refresh \(\delta\) and \(V_{ref}\) monthly from the queue study. Report Non-Native-Language Support effects as insufficient until its sample threshold is met.

**Capacity.** Daily: slots used, reserve consumed or released at 15:00, specialist-minutes removed, delay minutes, and eligible hurdle-clearing cases that hit Diagnosis Complete while waiting. More than two such forgone windows in a week means deferral is too strict; act earlier on closing windows. Idle slots alongside breaches on cases that had cleared \(B(a_{low})>0\) and \(B(a_{mid})>S\) mean the hurdle is too strict. A week of median delay above 20 minutes per slot cuts the next week’s cap by one and raises \(S\).

**Fairness.** Weekly swarm rate, expedite rate, breach rate, and spillover exposure by segment and by value quartile, restricted to eligible cases. A segment whose untreated eligible breach rate rises more than 5 points above its eight-week baseline is reviewed within a week. Lowest-quartile cases with \(p_{adj}>0.70\) that received neither a swarm nor an expedite are counted as a policy defect.

**When the rule changes.** Move probability shifts when a segment’s bias leaves its current band for two weeks or a workflow change lands. Move \(S\) and \(V_{ref}\) when the spillover interval updates; the hurdle moves with them. If a segment’s measured midpoint effect falls below the bottom of its planning range, stop routine swarms in that segment and leave only the equity path. If realized net benefit, after measured spillover, is at or below zero for two weeks, drop to a one-slot cap and require \(B(a_{low})>S_{high}\), with \(S_{high}\) at \(\delta=0.03\) and the current \(V_{ref}\). After 20 business days, set the reserve from the observed regulatory arrival rate: release earlier if arrivals are rare; make the reserve hard if they are frequent or the penalty is extreme. There is no single probability cutoff to tune. The quantity that changes is the comparison of calibrated, stage-eligible, evidence-qualified \(B(a)\) with measured spillover.
