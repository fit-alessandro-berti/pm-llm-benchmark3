6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

Redesign A
- Failure mode 1: The updated auto-screen increases manual-review inflow by 35% (upper end of the 15–35% range), pushing the 7 reviewers well past the 82% utilization threshold. Queues explode, Premium Track R cases face median delays exceeding the current 15-hour baseline (approaching p90 60+ hours), defeating the prioritization intent and triggering service-credit exposure.
- Failure mode 2: The evidence portal fails for customers with low digital literacy, increasing abandonment above the current 320 cases in the 22% unclear/missing cohort and shifting failure from “slow loop” to “silent exit,” which may not be visible until revenue or satisfaction metrics drop.
- Bottleneck migration: From fraud specialists and complaint loops into the manual eligibility review team; also, gate staffing can migrate pressure to inspectors if not separately resourced.

Redesign B
- Failure mode 1: Eliminating Fast approval for devices without expanding reviewer capacity directs ~2,850 cases (57% share: 14% Premium high-value + 21% Standard high-value + 22% Unclear) into Manual and Fraud review. The 7 reviewers and 2 specialists are overwhelmed; queue times exceed current 96–120-hour medians, and cycle-time targets collapse despite compliance gains.
- Failure mode 2: The visibility system for Standard customers is inadequate or not adopted; facing 120-hour median repair-track cycles without updates, Standard customers generate new complaints/reopens through the Escalation board, replacing fulfillment errors with experience failures.
- Bottleneck migration: From replacement errors and complaint/reopen loops directly onto manual reviewers and fraud specialists; the Escalation board remains a hard bottleneck for authorized replacements.

Redesign C
- Failure mode 1: Cross-trained inspectors apply R3 eligibility reviews inconsistently—approving replacements that violate policy or denying legitimate claims—because they lack the legal/policy depth of the manual-review team. Customer disputes and hidden compliance gaps emerge, especially for high-value devices.
- Failure mode 2: The universal pre-dispatch gate, applied even to legitimate Premium low-risk accessory claims, pushes median cycle times from 8 hours toward 20+ hours, breaching the 24-hour service-credit threshold and generating financial exposure that offsets the complaint-reduction benefit.
- Bottleneck migration: From manual reviewers (for reopened cases) and repair technicians (waiting for prices) onto the 2 quality inspectors, who now manage gate checks, embedded repair quality, and reopen reviews simultaneously—creating a critical bottleneck during repair campaigns.

7. Recommended experiment sequence

Phase 1 — First pilot: Redesign C downstream fixes (Weeks 1–6)
Scope: Apply the lightweight pre-dispatch gate to 100% of replacement dispatches within one fulfillment center or channel; implement Inspector-Led Reopen Review for all reopened claims entering Customer complaint/reopen; integrate part-price database access for the 3 repair-quote technicians.

Metrics to monitor:
- Gate catch rate (incorrect part selections intercepted) and added median delay (target 6–18h, not exceeding 18h median).
- Reopen loop duration: mean/median from Customer complaint/reopen to resolution (compare to baseline 40h/54h paths).
- Repair rework rate for high-value devices (baseline 12.5%) versus low-risk (4.0%).
- Complaint/reopen rate for cases passing through the gate versus matched control cohort running the old flow.
- Quality inspector utilization and manual-reviewer queue length (ensure inspectors do not exceed sustainable load).
- Service-credit trigger rate for Premium low-risk accessories (monitor 24-hour threshold).

Stop/go thresholds (qualitative/ranges):
- Stop if gate median delay exceeds 20 hours without reducing complaint/reopen by at least 25% relative to the 14.1% fast-path baseline.
- Stop if Inspector-Led Reopen Review does not reduce combined loop median below 24 hours (from ~31h + board time).
- Stop if manual-reviewer queue for non-reopen cases grows >10% (indicating organizational friction/role confusion).
- Go if catch rate is >5%, loop median falls below 24h, and inspector utilization stays below 90%.

Sequencing logic: Start with C because it requires no eligibility-rule rewrite, avoiding the 15–35% manual-inflow shock and the 82% queue risk. It uses the currently underused inspectors and addresses the contradiction where it hurts most—after dispatch and in the reopen loop—providing quick, observable feedback on whether downstream fixes can preserve the fast path’s speed advantage safely.

How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted:
Compare pilot cohort (treated) with a matched control cohort (same segments, same period, old process) using token-based replay and alignment diagnostics. Monitor the full path, not just endpoints: if Customer complaint/reopen drops but Quality check  Rework repair rises, the failure was shifted, not solved. Track resource utilization across all nodes—especially manual reviewers, fraud specialists, and inspectors—to detect bottleneck migration. If log fitness for the pilot cohort improves relative to the 0.91 baseline and alignment deviations (Fast approval before Evidence completeness, Dispatch without review) remain stable or fall only where intended, the improvement is structural.

Phase 2 — Pilot: Redesign A smart routing and portal (Weeks 7–14)
Scope: Deploy updated segment-aware auto-screen rules and the evidence portal for two cohorts in parallel: (a) the Unclear-defect / missing-evidence cohort (22% share, 140h median), and (b) Premium high-value device claims (14% share, 96h median reviewed path).

Metrics:
- Routing accuracy: percentage of Track R cases correctly diverted from Fast approval; R1/R2 conformance measured via replay alignment.
- Portal repetition rate: target <15% entering Request missing evidence twice versus 26% baseline.
- Manual review queue utilization (must stay <85% consistently).
- Abandonment rate via portal versus baseline 320.
- Median cycle time by track: Premium accessory must stay <24h; Premium high-value should improve from 96h; Standard high-value should not exceed 120h.

Stop/go thresholds:
- Stop if manual-review utilization exceeds 85% for more than two consecutive weeks (capacity failure).
- Stop if portal abandonment increases by >10% over baseline, particularly in Standard segments.
- Stop if Premium low-risk accessory median exceeds 30 hours (service-credit risk from gate + routing overhead).
- Go if R1/R2 deviation frequency drops >50% in pilot cohort and portal repetition is <15%.

Sequencing logic: Only after Phase 1 proves that downstream containment (gate, loop fix) works should we change front-end routing, because routing changes alter the volume entering the manual and fraud queues. Testing on the highest-pain cohorts—unclear evidence and Premium high-value—provides clear signal without overwhelming all 7 reviewers at once.

Phase 3 — Comparative evaluation and conditional B adoption (Weeks 15–24)
Scope: If Phase 2 succeeds, scale Redesign A to all segments. In a separate region or channel, apply Redesign B’s mandatory review-first device track (no Fast approval for devices) with full visibility support for Standard customers.

Metrics:
- End-to-end comparison: complaint/reopen rates (target <6% for devices), compliance fitness, labor cost per case, and customer-satisfaction scores by segment.
- Cycle-time distribution (median, p90) for Standard high-value (baseline 120h) versus A-treated cohort.
- Resource load on manual reviewers and fraud specialists under full-volume B conditions.

Stop/go thresholds:
- Stop if Standard high-value median exceeds 140h without satisfaction improvement.
- Stop if cost per device case exceeds 40% above current manual-path cost and quality gains are marginal.
- Go if B achieves <6% complaint/reopen for devices, compliance deviations near zero, and satisfaction scores rise despite longer median times (supported by visibility).

How to learn if Phase 3 is real versus shifted:
Use a hybrid control—run Redesign A in one region and B in a comparable region. Monitor upstream (Intake  Eligibility) and downstream (Repair  Quality check  End) together. If B improves quality but shifts failures into hidden Escalation board overflow (cases never resolved within the 45-minute slot) or into Customer complaint/reopen routed differently, the improvement is partial. Only adopt B for the device segments where A’s routing still allows unacceptable R1/R2 leakage; for low-risk accessories, retain A’s smart routing rather than B’s blanket restriction, preserving speed where risk is genuinely low. Do not claim exact ROI—report ranges (e.g., “quality improvement of 10–15 percentage points in complaint rate at a likely 25–40% labor-cost increase for devices”) based on observed cohort differences rather than modeled projections.
