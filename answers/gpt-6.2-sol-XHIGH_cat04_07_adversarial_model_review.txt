# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

I assess the model expressions as languages of complete, visible traces. In the process trees, `->` is sequence, `+` is parallel interleaving, `X` chooses exactly one child, and `tau` contributes no visible event. An alignment can give a high fitness score to a trace outside the language by paying for edits. Exact acceptance, alignment quality, and policy compliance are three distinct outcomes.

| Use | What matters most |
|---|---|
| Exploration | High coverage helps analysts inspect variation and recording gaps. A permissive model can be useful if users can see its exceptions and the reasons for alignment edits. |
| Compliance and release control | Mandatory controls and exclusions are hard gates. Legitimate variants need coverage to avoid unnecessary operational holds. Evidence gaps that affect the verdict require review. |
| Simulation | Feasible behavior must be established first. Frequencies, dependencies, service times, resources, and correction rates need separate calibration. Fitness and precision supply none of those parameters. |
| Stakeholder communication | A user should be able to explain the ordering, approval, and recheck rules and correctly predict what happens to a specific case. |

I treat P1–P7 and the probes as policy evidence subject to the stated ambiguities. For example, P3 imposes an obligation on external orders. An additional prohibition on Supplier Review for internal orders would require a policy decision. A missing event can represent a missing execution or a recording failure, and these explanations have different consequences for release.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case.** A small parallel block factors independent choices and lets analysts explore combinations absent from training. It accepts both orders of the mandatory checks and both approval types in their valid combinations. The silent skips make replay less brittle when an event goes unrecorded. That is a defensible exploratory design if skip-based matches are surfaced for investigation. For implementation, the model already gives a clear single registration, approval choice, and release.

**Strongest case against.** A silent skip in an executable model makes a check dispensable. Parallelism also makes Expedite Review eligible before its prerequisites even on complete data. The model lacks a correction path, so some noisy cases replay perfectly while legitimate corrections still incur edits.

The following shorthand is used throughout. `R` is Register Dossier, `C` is Credit Check, `T` is Technical Review, `S` is Supplier Review, `E` is Expedite Review, `D` is Correct Dossier, `M` is Manager Approval, `A` is Auto Approval, and `L` is Release Order.

| Admitted trace | Why it is undesirable or context-dependent |
|---|---|
| `R M L` | Both mandatory checks are absent. For a verified execution, P2 fails. |
| `R T M L` | Credit Check can be skipped in isolation. |
| `R C A L` | Technical Review can be skipped in isolation, including for Auto Approval. |
| `R E C T M L` | Expedite Review runs before either prerequisite despite both checks being present. |
| `R T E C A L` | Expedite Review runs with only one prerequisite complete. |
| `R C T A L` on a verified external order | Model S accepts a release without the required Supplier Review. The same event sequence is valid on an internal order. |

The three mandatory-check omissions and the missing Supplier Review can be intentional aids for searching incomplete records. Their implementation as ordinary accepted paths means the model itself cannot distinguish missing evidence from a skipped control. Early expedite is a separate ordering flaw. The observed checks are all present in those two examples, so missing-event tolerance offers no explanation.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case.** C is an explicit whitelist of eight complete sequences. Every listed sequence has both mandatory checks, approval and release in order, and exactly one approval. Every listed expedite follows both checks. The list can serve as a regression catalog or a tightly scoped manual-review trigger if the board formally approves those cases and routes unfamiliar cases for review. C’s narrower behavior prevents the direct bypasses in B3 and B4.

**Strongest case against.** Enumeration ties together choices the policy treats independently. For example, C knows the `T C` order, knows Auto Approval, and rejects their combination in B2. The external, expedited, auto-approved case in B5 needs a new branch despite using only familiar activities. C allows expedite with Supplier Review in just one relative order, and allows no Supplier Review branch with Auto Approval. A branch without Supplier Review could nevertheless be played by an external order because C lacks an attribute guard.

The correction branch hard-codes `C T D C T M`. It excludes a single affected check, multiple corrections, alternate recheck orders where both are affected, and correction combined with Auto Approval. It accepts that hard-coded sequence even when authoritative evidence says only Technical Review was affected. Thus a literal match can still violate P6.

Training precision of 0.941 is consistent with a whitelist fitted to frequently observed variants. The 0.229 fitness decline, F4, and B2/B5 make memorization a credible explanation. Establishing its share requires exact variant coverage, known-policy holdout labels, and per-case discrepancies. Unseen behavior may merit escalation. Novelty alone cannot determine a violation.

Repeated labels are separate activity occurrences in the tree, and may map to separate replay transitions. This fragments evidence about a single business control, introduces alignment choices, and can require edits across several branches for one policy change. A readable checklist for eight cases becomes a difficult policy definition as combinations accumulate. Any production use would need a documented mapping from model occurrences to the same business control and owner.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely effect on the board’s model choice. Lower-ranked effects can still determine operational feasibility.

1. **Fitness rewards making mandatory work optional.** S can give exact replay to `R M L`. On incomplete logs this raises apparent fit and conceals precisely the control failure a release model should expose.
2. **Positive-log-only precision cannot test policy exclusions.** C receives credit for excluding continuations absent from training, including compliant ones. Neither precision score tests unsafe acceptance. A labeled negative suite and explicit reachability queries address that gap.
3. **Missing-event bias changes both the target and the score.** Missing Supplier Review can favor S and penalize C. With missing sourcing, even a complete recorded sequence without Supplier Review has two possible policy verdicts. Missing correction or duplicate checks can likewise change the interpretation of P7. Obtain independent evidence and measure effects separately by defect and cohort.
4. **Split sensitivity or leakage can reverse the comparison.** Random case splits can share near-identical dossiers, variants, or upstream order groups. Preprocessing or tuning on all cases can leak holdout information. A time split may instead expose real drift. The reported gap warrants reproducibility checks. It is insufficient evidence of leakage by itself.
5. **Alignment costs can hide business severity.** Equal event-edit costs can make a missing Credit Check look as minor as an extra optional event. Cheap silent moves help models with skip routes, and alternative optimal alignments may tell different stories. Publish cost profiles and report visible discrepancies by type. Use exact membership and guards for enforcement.
6. **Token generalization can reward permissiveness.** Shared transitions in S can accumulate frequent visits while allowing invalid combinations. Splitting the same label across C’s branches can produce rarely visited transitions and a lower value. Test unseen but policy-valid combinations explicitly.
7. **Duplicate labels change replay and measurement.** C’s branch-specific transitions create multiple candidates for the same event. Token replay can assign visits differently depending on how ties are resolved. Alignment search, precision computation, and structural counts can also depend on the conversion and algorithm. Publish transition identities, label multiplicities, and the choice of variant.
8. **Silent transitions deserve their own audit.** S uses silent transitions as visible-work bypasses. Silent moves can also implement ordinary routing, as in the third model. Their count alone is ambiguous. Inspect reachable visible behavior, silent move costs, and silent closure during replay and precision computation.
9. **The stakeholder score can reflect the display or familiarity.** S’s compact drawing may be easier to read while making unsafe behavior harder to notice. Use equal presentation conditions, realistic decision tasks, objective scoring, and role-level results.
10. **Runtime is confounded by structure and measurement.** C’s larger net and repeated labels can increase branching, while parallelism and silent paths can raise costs in S or the third model. Trace length, fit, solver, caching, and failures also matter. Compare on identical cases and report tail latency, timeout rate, and model size.

# 7. Third Alternative

Use a workflow Petri net with an evidence-based policy guard. The net represents feasible control flow across possible sourcing categories and correction scopes. The guard determines whether the particular case satisfies the corresponding obligations. Keep an alignment of the recorded trace as diagnostic evidence alongside the policy verdict.

**Formal description.** After `R`, both mandatory checks are due. Executing a due check marks its result current. `D` may occur during preapproval and invalidates the current results in its affected set, which is `{C}`, `{T}`, or `{C,T}`. Invalidating a check already due leaves it due. A fresh result for an unaffected check remains current. Consecutive corrections can accumulate outstanding work. A check can run again only if invalidated by a correction since its last execution. `S` may run once at any point before approval. `E` may run once before approval and requires both mandatory results to be current when it runs. `M` and `A` also require both results current, and either consumes the shared permission to approve. Exactly one `L` follows. The sourcing guard requires `S` for external cases.

This version places Supplier Review before approval, as both submissions do, and permits it for internal orders. It treats a completed Supplier or Expedite Review as unaffected by correction. It allows correction before the first completion of both checks and after Expedite Review while approval is pending. F6 supports using `C` and `T` as the scope of rechecks. Ratify these choices, including how to handle corrections after approval and whether a correction can invalidate supplier or expedite work, before enabling release control.

The following code constructs a labeled workflow net. The silent transitions resolve the unknown correction scope in the structural model and clear optional-review bookkeeping. There is one visible transition per activity. Each silent correction resolution can invalidate a current check or leave it unchanged according to at least one admissible affected set. It cannot complete a check.

```python
from collections import deque
from itertools import product

from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking, PetriNet
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils.petri_utils import add_arc_from_to

REGISTER = "Register Dossier"
CREDIT = "Credit Check"
TECHNICAL = "Technical Review"
SUPPLIER = "Supplier Review"
EXPEDITE = "Expedite Review"
CORRECT = "Correct Dossier"
MANAGER = "Manager Approval"
AUTO = "Auto Approval"
RELEASE = "Release Order"

CHECKS = (CREDIT, TECHNICAL)
BOTH_CHECKS = frozenset(CHECKS)
AFFECTED_SETS = (frozenset({CREDIT}), frozenset({TECHNICAL}), BOTH_CHECKS)


def build_dossier_net():
    net = PetriNet("Dossier workflow")

    def place(name):
        node = PetriNet.Place(name)
        net.places.add(node)
        return node

    def step(name, inputs, outputs, label=None):
        transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
        net.transitions.add(transition)
        for node in inputs:
            add_arc_from_to(node, transition, net)
        for node in outputs:
            add_arc_from_to(transition, node, net)

    source, sink = place("source"), place("sink")
    active = place("preapproval")
    resolving = place("resolve_correction")
    approved = place("approved")
    check_status = {
        check: {False: place(check + " due"), True: place(check + " current")}
        for check in CHECKS
    }
    optional_status = {
        label: (place(label + " available"), place(label + " done"))
        for label in (SUPPLIER, EXPEDITE)
    }
    due = tuple(check_status[check][False] for check in CHECKS)
    current = tuple(check_status[check][True] for check in CHECKS)
    available = tuple(unused for unused, _ in optional_status.values())

    step("register", (source,), (active, *due, *available), REGISTER)
    for check, status in check_status.items():
        step(check, (active, status[False]), (active, status[True]), check)

    supplier_unused, supplier_done = optional_status[SUPPLIER]
    step("supplier", (active, supplier_unused), (active, supplier_done), SUPPLIER)
    expedite_unused, expedite_done = optional_status[EXPEDITE]
    step(
        "expedite",
        (active, expedite_unused, *current),
        (active, expedite_done, *current),
        EXPEDITE,
    )

    step("correct", (active,), (resolving,), CORRECT)
    for was_current in product((False, True), repeat=len(CHECKS)):
        possible_outcomes = {
            tuple(fresh and check not in affected
                  for check, fresh in zip(CHECKS, was_current))
            for affected in AFFECTED_SETS
        }
        before = tuple(check_status[check][fresh]
                       for check, fresh in zip(CHECKS, was_current))
        for remains_current in sorted(possible_outcomes):
            after = tuple(check_status[check][fresh]
                          for check, fresh in zip(CHECKS, remains_current))
            name = f"apply_correction_{was_current}_{remains_current}"
            step(name, (resolving, *before), (active, *after))

    for approval in (MANAGER, AUTO):
        step(approval, (active, *current), (approved,), approval)

    closing = approved
    for label, statuses in optional_status.items():
        cleared = place(label + " cleared")
        for status in statuses:
            step("clear " + status.name, (closing, status), (cleared,))
        closing = cleared
    step("release", (closing,), (sink,), RELEASE)
    return net, Marking({source: 1}), Marking({sink: 1})


model_third = build_dossier_net()


def accepts_exact(labels, marked_net):
    """Accept a complete visible trace, allowing silent moves only."""
    labels = tuple(labels)
    net, initial, final = marked_net
    queue = deque([(initial, 0)])
    seen = {(initial, 0)}
    while queue:
        marking, index = queue.popleft()
        if marking == final and index == len(labels):
            return True
        for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            label = transition.label
            if label is not None and (index == len(labels) or label != labels[index]):
                continue
            successor = semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)
            state = (successor, index + int(label is not None))
            if state not in seen:
                seen.add(state)
                queue.append(state)
    return False


def policy_verdict(labels, sourcing, affected_at):
    """Check a complete case. Scope keys are zero-based correction event indices.

    Sourcing is 'external', 'internal', or None. A missing or None scope
    represents unknown evidence. Supply verified values only.
    """
    labels = tuple(labels)
    if sourcing not in ("external", "internal", None):
        raise ValueError("Unrecognized sourcing category")
    if not accepts_exact(labels, model_third):
        return "FAIL", "Structural rule violated in the supplied sequence"
    if sourcing == "external" and SUPPLIER not in labels:
        return "FAIL", "Supplier Review required for external order"

    possible = {frozenset()}
    some_scope_fails = False
    for index, label in enumerate(labels):
        if label == CORRECT:
            scope = affected_at.get(index)
            scopes = AFFECTED_SETS if scope is None else (frozenset(scope),)
            if any(choice not in AFFECTED_SETS for choice in scopes):
                raise ValueError("Unsupported correction scope")
            possible = {fresh - choice for fresh in possible for choice in scopes}
        elif label in CHECKS:
            some_scope_fails |= any(label in fresh for fresh in possible)
            possible = {fresh | {label} for fresh in possible if label not in fresh}
        elif label in (EXPEDITE, MANAGER, AUTO):
            some_scope_fails |= any(fresh != BOTH_CHECKS for fresh in possible)
            possible &= {BOTH_CHECKS}

        if not possible:
            return "FAIL", "Recheck or current-check requirement violated"

    reasons = []
    if sourcing is None and SUPPLIER not in labels:
        reasons.append("Sourcing evidence needed to resolve Supplier Review obligation")
    if some_scope_fails:
        reasons.append("Affected-check evidence needed to resolve recheck obligation")
    if reasons:
        return "REVIEW", " / ".join(reasons)
    return "PASS", "Rules satisfied for all remaining attribute possibilities"
```

The structural check asks whether *some* hidden routing fits the events. The guard requires every evidence-consistent choice to satisfy the rules for a `PASS`. A mix of compliant and noncompliant interpretations yields `REVIEW`. For example, without scope evidence, `R C T D T M L` has a compliant interpretation under `{T}` and violations under the other masks. Neither the alignment nor the observed recheck supplies independent proof of the mask. If all interpretations violate a rule, the trace fails as supplied. Trusted evidence must establish the actual execution before a compliance finding is made against a potentially incomplete or duplicated record. Log ingestion should also validate metadata and quarantine schema errors, such as an affected-set field keyed to a noncorrection event.

Historical logs can support structural replay and conditional verdicts. The live workflow should collect sourcing and correction scope from authoritative systems and apply the same state rules at the time of approval and release. Those decisions need authenticated event and result evidence. A `REVIEW` cannot authorize an automatic release.

# 9. Validation Experiments

1. **Reproduce discovery and preserve independent evidence.** Version the event schema, extraction, event ordering and tie rules, activity mapping, deduplication, inclusion rules, discovery settings, PM4Py and solver versions, random seeds, and code. Publish reproducible case-ID manifests or privacy-preserving equivalents. Recreate an 80/20 split by case, grouping linked orders and copied dossiers together. Repeat with predetermined seeds and run a separate chronological evaluation. Fit discovery and preprocessing choices on training data and reserve a final locked test. Report case counts, variant overlap, exclusions, missingness, and paired uncertainty intervals with resampling by case or dossier group.
2. **Test by verified attributes.** Obtain sourcing from the order record and correction scope from the correction record or documented adjudication. Avoid inferring them from the very activities under test. Stratify by sourcing, approval type, mandatory-check order, expedites, correction count and scope, and event completeness. Report unknown attributes as their own cohort. Use hand-adjudicated examples or prospective collection where historical metadata is unavailable. Label legitimate cases, forbidden cases, and unresolved records separately, with reviewer disagreements recorded.
3. **Challenge positive and negative behavior.** Use B1–B8, A1–A5, and a board-approved suite with matched positive controls. Test no registration, early release, both or neither approval, skipped mandatory checks, early expedite, omitted Supplier Review on a verified external order, unjustified repeated checks, a stale mandatory check after correction, and a wrong known affected set. Include staging tests for premature approval, absent evidence, and protected release. For generalization, withhold complete combinations while retaining their constituent options in training. Include supplier with Auto Approval and reverse check order, both supplier/expedite orders, each single-check correction, both-check corrections in reverse recheck order, and multiple corrections.
4. **Perturb clean cases with known execution histories.** Delete single events and correlated groups, including Supplier Review, each mandatory check, and Correct Dossier. Inject duplicate events and retries, including distinct genuine repeated executions. Keep original truth labels and track changes in exact membership, alignment edits, and policy verdict. Test timestamp ties and delivery order against authoritative ordering. A corrupt record of a legitimate execution should lead to evidence recovery or review as applicable. Test that a genuinely skipped control can never yield automatic release.
5. **Inspect net reachability and guard coverage.** Check workflow soundness, including ability to finish, proper completion, and absence of dead transitions. Enumerate the reachable marking graph for this bounded net and check the place invariants for check and review status, terminal marking, and approval exclusivity. On the product of the net with the policy state, verify the state rules on all reachable transitions for each known sourcing/mask value, including a route to completion for external cases and all supported correction choices. Exercise races and retries in live integration. Tie logged evidence to the correct dossier revision and apply state changes and gates atomically. An unbounded number of corrections produces arbitrarily long traces while the control state remains finite.
6. **Measure separately and stress alignment settings.** Report training and holdout fitness, precision on each evaluation log with its cohort identified, token generalization and underlying visits, arc-degree simplicity, structural inventories, and exact policy test results. Show per-stratum exact positive coverage, critical negative acceptances, review rates, and edit categories. Compare standard costs, equal visible-edit costs, and higher costs for omitting critical controls, with a sensitivity check for silent costs. Keep silent costs and model normalization visible. Compare median, upper percentiles, timeout rate, and throughput on identical workloads with fixed resources. Use the sensitivity analysis to qualify metric conclusions. Policy verdicts remain tied to the rules and evidence.
7. **Measure comprehension through tasks.** Recruit compliance, operations, process owners, and IT. Present consistent materials with counterbalanced model order and a documented rubric. Ask participants to predict B3, B4, B5, and attribute-conditioned B6, explain an escalation, and identify the changes needed for a revised correction rule. Measure answer accuracy, critical misconceptions, time, and confidence by role. Have scorers use the same answer key and resolve disagreements. Keep self-rated ease as a separate result.
8. **Set decision gates before seeing results.** Any reachable critical policy violation in enforcement mode, authorization from unresolved consequential evidence, or unsound live workflow blocks deployment even with excellent metric scores. Require full passage of the agreed policy regression suite, coverage of adjudicated valid cases, and predeclared operational thresholds for false holds, review backlog, latency, and comprehension.                 transition: 0 for transition in net.transitions

            },
        },

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Keep S only as a clearly labeled exploratory view if its ease of use helps analysts. Show skipped mandatory events, early expedites, and omitted evidence as findings even when the trace fits. Retire C from consideration as a production compliance model. Preserve its eight sequences as replay fixtures and adjudicate the correction fixture against known scope. Neither original should authorize release or determine compliance on its own.

Advance the third alternative to a guarded shadow trial. First ratify correction scope and timing, preapproval Supplier Review, the internal-sourcing interpretation, and the validity of supplier and expedite results after corrections. In the trial, measure verdict accuracy, review burden and causes, and failure recovery on real cases with verifiable sourcing, correction scope, and activity evidence. Before enforcement, pass the hard policy and soundness gates, meet the predeclared operational and comprehension criteria, and verify that production approval and release actually consult the guard. Capture case evidence, configuration and rule versions, explanations for held cases, and the eventual adjudication. Limit automatic release to cases with verified rule satisfaction. # 11. Dissenting View

The board should retain Model C as a supervised release whitelist while it pilots the hybrid. Its eight sequences are reviewable as concrete operating procedures. Staff can match a dossier against a versioned checklist, verify sourcing and correction scope in the source systems, and send unfamiliar sequences to a supervisor. That arrangement would sacrifice throughput, but it could give auditors a consistent decision trail during the pilot. The reported holdout fitness drop identifies a population that needs triage. It gives us no validated estimate of the cost or volume of genuinely compliant cases that would be delayed.

The hybrid creates a different kind of audit burden. Its visible diagram no longer contains the full decision, and reviewing a release means reconstructing evidence, case state, and the rule version that was in force. The proposed treatment of early corrections, repeated corrections, and the continued validity of a previous expedite is plausible. Those choices still need explicit endorsement. Failing closed on missing evidence could leave the hybrid with much the same queue as C and more implementation risk. A short, governed whitelist and a staffed exception path may offer the safer interim service if the organization can prove that its staff consistently perform the evidence checks before release.

**Evidence that would change the decision.** I would support a time-limited, manually supervised whitelist if a controlled trial established correct sourcing and scope checks, reliable exception handling within agreed limits, and no forbidden releases in the challenge tests. I would withhold the hybrid from enforcement if its shadow trial exposed unavailable evidence, inconsistent verdicts across retries or revisions, or an unmanageable volume of legitimate cases held for review. Verified policy and independent holdout results would determine whether the extra routing flexibility justifies the operational cost.
