## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts taken from the materials

- GPIP-4 is a board-approved, enterprise-wide policy effective throughout the incident.
- DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled local SOP applicable to Aachen maintenance and production-support purchases.
- ECD-2 is a signed corporate incident directive with an exact purpose and effective window.
- The CFO email was sent after P-502 but before P-503 through P-508. It was not a controlled policy instrument.
- The Chief Risk Officer neither issued nor ratified ECD-2, and no written extension was issued after 18:00 on 18 July.
- Incident Command members have operational authority, but they are not automatically designated budget owners or Finance Controllers.
- ON-44 affects evidential weight, not normative requirements.
- An “unapproved supplier” is not necessarily a “new supplier.” The latter must be independently established for GPIP A3.
- No supplied norm creates a separate Receive_Goods control, so none is invented.

### Interpretive assumptions

1. **Authority is issue-specific, not a simple total ordering.** GPIP-4 controls enterprise integrity requirements. The local SOP controls Aachen workflow where consistent with GPIP. ECD-2 can displace identified local operational steps within its scope and period. The CFO can authoritatively interpret Finance execution, but cannot rewrite board-level segregation or Compliance requirements.

2. **ECD-2 is not, on the present record, an A7 exception.** A7 assigns exception authority to the Chief Risk Officer. Although GPIP does not expressly address jointly signed incident directives, the express reservation to the CRO, absence of CRO participation or ratification, and lack of a documented delegation defeat treating ECD-2 as a blanket GPIP exception. Prior overlay practice is relevant but not enough, especially for new-supplier Compliance.

3. **ECD-2 expressly overrides local PO sequencing and supplier-register requirements.** C1, C2, and C4 are sufficiently specific to displace B1 and B3 for purchases that meet C’s scope, time, and authorization conditions. It does not silently displace A1–A3.

4. **Combined effect of ECD-2 and the CFO email on local approvals.** For qualifying purchases after 15 July 07:20, C3 supplies the operational authorization and D can reasonably be treated as the CFO’s prospective interpretation that no separate local Finance event is necessary within D’s thresholds. This can streamline B2, but it does not establish that an Incident Command member was the independent budget owner or Finance Controller required by A2.

5. **B2 timing is textually ambiguous.** Unlike A2 and A3, B2 does not expressly say “before commitment.” The primary interpretation is:
   - in ordinary workflow, B1 normally places the PO and its approvals before commitment;
   - where B4 or C1 validly reverses the PO sequence, B2 approvals remain required but may be completed afterward unless another rule expressly makes them pre-commitment.
   
   This narrow interpretation is used for P-501. It does not permit later approval to cure A2 or A3, whose timing is explicit.

6. **The Shift Lead substitution in B4 is relevant only when the Shift Lead purports to provide Plant approval.** P-501 shows a later Plant_Approve event rather than a Shift Lead substitution. The Plant Manager’s availability therefore prevents treating SHIFT-1 as a substitute approver, but does not by itself defeat B4’s separate PO-sequencing relief.

7. **Emergency necessity is not a free-standing excuse.** It establishes B4 emergency status or helps determine C’s scope; it does not independently waive Finance, Compliance, segregation, or supplier controls.

8. **ECD scope is objective and purposive.** An Incident Command label is relevant but not conclusive. The purchase must be reasonably required to contain the current contamination event. The Internal Audit 24-hour limit is not adopted as a hard textual rule, but remoteness and existing stock are relevant. On that basis:
   - P-502, P-505, P-507, and P-508 are within scope;
   - P-504 is outside scope;
   - P-503’s scope is not demonstrated because stock lasted three days and the purchase was contingency inventory;
   - P-506 is outside the directive temporally even if its purpose remained related.

9. **Commitment occurs when Northbridge becomes bound or the supplier accepts the commitment, not necessarily when ERP is updated.** Supplier acknowledgments, calls, signed PDFs, and immutable metadata can outweigh delayed or inaccurate ERP timestamps.

10. **Burden of proof.**
    - Northbridge bears the burden of proving exception scope, timely authorization, actor qualifications, and pre-commitment gates.
    - A role-labeled controlled approval event is presumptive evidence of that approval unless contradicted.
    - An expressly absent event or missing actor qualification is not presumed into existence.
    - A late record can prove a timely act, but a later approval cannot be relabeled as a timely approval.
    - `INDETERMINATE` is reserved for cases where the result genuinely depends on a balanced unresolved fact. Where the record affirmatively shows a mandatory gate occurred late or not at all, the classification is `NONCOMPLIANT`.

### Classification method

- **COMPLIANT:** all triggered controls are satisfied without relying on an exception.
- **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** a prima facie workflow deviation is expressly permitted, its prerequisites are proven, and no other mandatory control remains breached.
- **NONCOMPLIANT:** at least one controlling requirement is proven unmet or a required pre-commitment gate is not demonstrated.
- **INDETERMINATE:** available evidence cannot resolve a dispositive factual or authority issue.

There is no single ordering for every issue. Authority, subject matter, scope, time, and expressness all matter.

### Evidential distinctions

| Evidential category | Meaning | Application |
|---|---|---|

| **Late act, not merely late evidence** | The underlying approval or clearance itself occurred after commitment | P-502 Finance and Compliance; P-505 Finance; P-506 Plant approval. These cannot be converted into timely acts by later ratification |
| **Inability to prove compliance** | The control might have been satisfied, but qualifying evidence is absent | A2 role qualifications for P-505, P-507, and P-508; P-504 new-supplier status; P-505 eventual PO; final approver identities in several cases |

### Monitoring actions

- Create an emergency-purchase register showing scope rationale, amount, supplier status, authorizers, actual commitment evidence, record deadline, and directive expiry.
- Reconcile ERP commitment times against supplier acknowledgments, calls, and signed artifacts after outages or clock incidents.
- Run automated conflicts for requester, committer, IC authorizer, budget owner, and final approver.
- Require role validation rather than accepting “Incident Command member” as evidence of budget or Finance authority.
- Implement hard alerts at EUR 25,000, EUR 50,000, EUR 100,000, and EUR 150,000.
- Block reliance on an expired directive unless a controlled written extension has been loaded.
- Review emergency usage by repeated supplier, buyer, purpose, and production line to detect normalization or avoidance of planning controls.
- Conduct an independent post-incident review within a fixed period, while stating expressly that retrospective review mitigates risk but does not cure missing pre-commitment controls.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement hierarchy and emergency-exception clause**

> **1. Hierarchy and authority.** Board-approved policy prevails over local procedures, incident directives, emails, and custom. Local procedures may be stricter. A lower-level or later instrument changes a higher-level control only when the higher-level policy expressly permits that change and the instrument identifies the affected clauses.
>
> **2. Emergency instrument.** An Emergency Exception Instrument must be signed before use by the Chief Risk Officer, or by a formally predesignated alternate, and by the responsible operational executive. A COO or Incident Commander directive without that signature is an operational instruction only and is not a policy exception.
>
> **3. Scope and duration.** The instrument must state the affected site, incident, objective purchase criteria, maximum amount, supplier category, start time, and exact expiry. A purchase qualifies only if contemporaneous evidence shows it is directly necessary to address the stated incident. No verbal extension is valid; an extension requires the same signatures and must be issued before expiry.
>
> **4. Permitted workflow relief.** The instrument may expressly permit commitment before PO creation, waive tender timing, allow provisional use of an unregistered supplier, and replace local Plant workflow. Controls not listed remain in force.
>
> **5. Nonwaivable and threshold controls.** The requester or committer may not serve as final business approver. At EUR 50,000 or more, pre-commitment approval must include a designated independent budget owner and a Finance Controller. Incident Command authorization counts only when the record identifies which member holds each role. Purchases above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier require pre-commitment Compliance clearance. Purchases above EUR 150,000 also require Legal clearance. Any reduced new-supplier review must be transaction-specific, signed by both the CRO and Chief Compliance Officer, and include sanctions, ownership, and product-quality screening.
>
> **6. Evidence and retrospective records.** Verbal Incident Command authorization is permitted only when documented within four hours. A timely call recording, signed immutable file, or access log may prove the act despite a later ERP posting. The record must distinguish the time of the act from the time of database entry. A later approval may mitigate consequences but does not cure a missed pre-commitment gate.
>
> **7. Expiry and normalization safeguard.** A commitment is covered only if the supplier accepted it before expiry; later receipt is permitted under that timely commitment. Repeated purchases for the same need, use caused by foreseeable planning failure, or emergency usage beyond a stated volume cap requires fresh CRO review. All emergency purchases must be independently reviewed within 48 hours and reported by trend to the board risk committee.
