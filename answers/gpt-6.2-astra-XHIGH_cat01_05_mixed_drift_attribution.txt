## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

Between February and March, raw fitness against the old model fell from .93 to .55. Mapping the eligibility alias and removing duplicate technical opens brings March fitness to .90, a gain of .35. The raw graph gives an independent check on the alias. Of 640 March registrations, 500 led to Eligibility Screen and 140 to Validate Eligibility. Together they yield 100 eligibility transitions per 100 cases, the January rate. The normalized eligibility-to-collection rate also holds at 98. Median cycle time moved only from 30.8 to 31.2 hours.

Fitness measures agreement with a specified model under a specified event interpretation. The correction can realign equivalent labels while hiding a change in what the screen checks. An allowed information-request loop can become frequent and still conform. Duplicate opens inflate event and edge counts, split otherwise identical variants and can create alignment penalties. Missing resource IDs chiefly undermine handover graphs, team workloads and resource-attributed service estimates. An activity-only alignment can survive a missing assignee. Dropping those rows could itself distort activity order.

After May, both legs of the normalized document-return loop strengthen, then partially recede in September. The operational rework series moves in the same direction, and the cycle-time tail and reconstructed waiting increase. A March label substitution alone cannot generate that sequence. The operational variant and timing tables need reconciliation to the same case cohorts, clocks and event definitions before treating their agreement as independent confirmation.

**Normalized DFG distance.** For each supplied month, form the vector of seven relation counts per 100 cases in the order given. Use the sum of absolute coordinate differences, with equal weight per occurrence. The two loop legs each count because they are distinct transitions. Marginal edge counts cannot show how often both belong to one complete loop in the same case. The vector need not sum to 100 and repeated traversals count. January is the DFG reference because February has no supplied vector.

| January | 0 | — |
| March | 3 | 3 |
| May | 40 | 37 |

In July the two loop legs each contribute 17 to the distance from January, and the pooled assessment-to-decision edge contributes 11. From September to October the largest single contribution is 15 on assessment-to-senior. Equal distances could thus reflect different routing changes. These are fragment distances on rounded rates. Unreported edges are unobserved and excluded consistently. Adding the remaining edges and resolving the pooled decision edge could increase the full-edge distance. A decline in the displayed decision relation does not establish fewer eventual decisions.

**Two composition checks.** If supplier mix alone accounted for the April–May rise in the rework variant, stable Z and non-Z rework rates would have to differ by about 44 pp (11 pp divided by a .25 change in Z weight). That is feasible and testable with pre-May strata. For senior assessment, suppose the rate within each of the two complexity strata stayed fixed. Even placing every September senior edge in the high-complexity stratum gives an October rate of only about 11 per 100 (7 × 41/26), against 22 observed. This bound assumes comparable cohorts and complexity coding. Finer changes in severity within strata, routing or recording are needed to bridge the gap.

**Cycle-time components.** By July the median had deteriorated by 13.05 h and p90 by 36 h. The available evidence supports a directional allocation.

For an event-level estimate, reconstruct a case timeline on a common clock. Assign supplier-pending time to document turnaround and start the assessment queue clock only when the case is ready. In a standardized simulation, replace intake mix, conditional path frequencies, and then service and wait distributions per visit with those of the comparison period. Recompute the median and p90 after each replacement, preserving within-case dependence. Reversing the replacement order tests sensitivity to interactions. Differences in medians of individual components do not add to the difference in the median total. These summaries cannot identify percentage shares.

**Candidate breaks.** For logging, March is the clear break. April is a weaker second candidate for migration stabilization (fitness recovery widened from .35 to .40), and June merits a missingness check (5.5% to 6.2%). Both could reflect changing activity weights. For behavior, test May for loop growth, September for partial reversal, and October separately for the senior branch. For performance, test May for the first material rise, mid-June through July for a queue build-up, September for relief, and October and December for opposite tail shifts. These are candidate windows. Fit change points to daily or weekly arrival, routing and waiting series with adequate support, allow lags from intake to assessment, and compare detected intervals with the intervention dates. One global break would conflate a March observation change with later changes in work and delay.

def read_numeric_table(source):
    rows = {}
    for row in csv.DictReader(StringIO(source)):
        month = row.pop("month")
        if month in rows:
            raise ValueError(f"Duplicate month {month}")
        values = {key: float(value) for key, value in row.items()}
        if not all(math.isfinite(v) and v >= 0 for v in values.values()):
            raise ValueError(f"Invalid value in {month}")
        rows[month] = values
    return rows


def l1(left, right):
    if left.keys() != right.keys():
        raise ValueError("Inconsistent coordinates")
    return sum(abs(left[key] - right[key]) for key in left)


def recovery(row):
    return row["mapped_model_fitness"] - row["raw_model_fitness"]


def analyze_summary():

    rates = read_numeric_table(RATES)

        raise ValueError("Unexpected month coverage or order")

    # Practical screens on adjacent months. Use absolute changes to detect relief too.
    # Percent columns are on a 0–100 scale, fitness on 0–1, and time in hours.
    for before, after in zip(months, months[1:]):
        a, b = monthly[before], monthly[after]
        delta = {k: round(b[k] - a[k], 6) for k in a}
        gap_change = round(recovery(b) - recovery(a), 6)
        flags = []
        if abs(gap_change) >= .04 or abs(delta["missing_resource_pct"]) >= .6:
            flags.append("logging/provenance")
        if abs(delta["rework_variant_pct"]) >= 5 or abs(delta["fast_variant_pct"]) >= 8:
            flags.append("behavior or mix")
        if abs(delta["median_cycle_h"]) >= 3 or abs(delta["p90_cycle_h"]) >= 10:
            flags.append("performance")
        if flags:
            print(f"{before} to {after}: {', '.join(flags)}")
    print("Omitted edges are unknown. Reported rates count occurrences per 100 cases.")
    print("Rounded monthly aggregates cannot identify effects, within-class waits or pooled quantiles.")
    print("Flags are candidate windows from practical cutoffs, without calibrated significance.")


# Part B — reusable event-level analysis (requires pandas and PM4Py)
CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIME = "time:timestamp"


def format_view(events, label_map=None, remove_verified_opens=False):
    """Input timestamps are UTC or offset-aware. Case IDs are stable strings."""
    import pandas as pd
    import pm4py

    frame = events.copy(deep=True)
    required = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "event_seq"]
    if frame[required].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Reconcile missing case, activity, timestamp or source order")
    if not frame["case_id"].map(lambda x: isinstance(x, str) and bool(x.strip())).all():
        raise ValueError("Supply canonical nonempty string case IDs")
    frame["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(frame["timestamp"], errors="raise", utc=True)
    frame["event_seq"] = pd.to_numeric(frame["event_seq"], errors="raise")
    order = ["case_id", "timestamp", "event_seq"]
    if frame[order].isna().any().any() or frame.duplicated(order).any():
        raise ValueError("Resolve ambiguous source order before constructing traces")
    if remove_verified_opens:
        flag = frame["verified_duplicate_open"]
        if not pd.api.types.is_bool_dtype(flag) or flag.isna().any():
            raise ValueError("verified_duplicate_open must be a known Boolean per row")
        if (flag & frame["activity"].ne("Open Case")).any():
            raise ValueError("Duplicate-open flag on a different activity")
        frame = frame.loc[~flag].copy()
    if label_map:
        frame["activity"] = frame["activity"].replace(label_map)
    frame = frame.sort_values(order, kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        frame, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )


def registration_window(first_registers, start, stop):
    """Filter anchors to [start, stop), then use their IDs to bring back whole histories."""
    import pm4py

    hits = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        first_registers, start.to_pydatetime(), stop.to_pydatetime(), mode="events"
    )
    return hits.loc[hits[TIME].ge(start) & hits[TIME].lt(stop)]


def completed_artifacts(traces, include_skeleton=False, reference_net=None):
    """reference_net, if supplied, is the frozen (net, initial_marking, final_marking)."""
    import pm4py

    if traces.empty:
        return None
    edges, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(traces)
    n = traces[CASE].nunique()
    result = {
        "cases": n,
        "dfg": edges,
        "edge_rates_per_100": {edge: 100 * count / n for edge, count in edges.items()},
        "starts": starts,
        "ends": ends,
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(traces),
        "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(traces),
    }
    if include_skeleton:
        result["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(traces)
    if reference_net is not None:
        result["fitness"] = pm4py.fitness_alignments(traces, *reference_net)
    return result


def compare_months(events, first_month, n_months, extract_end, followup_days,
                   include_skeleton=False, reference_net=None):
    """extract_end is an exclusive UTC cutoff. first_month is a YYYY-MM string."""
    import pandas as pd
    import pm4py

    if followup_days <= 0 or n_months < 1:
        raise ValueError("Use a positive follow-up duration and month count")
    cutoff = pd.to_datetime(extract_end, utc=True)
    age = pd.Timedelta(days=followup_days)
    views = {
        "raw": format_view(events),
        "normalized": format_view(
            events, {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}, remove_verified_opens=True
        ),
    }
    views = {name: df.loc[df[TIME].lt(cutoff)].copy() for name, df in views.items()}
    raw = views["raw"]
    anchors = raw.loc[raw[ACTIVITY].eq("Register")].drop_duplicates(CASE, keep="first")
    missing_register = raw.loc[~raw[CASE].isin(anchors[CASE]), CASE].nunique()
    all_months = {}
    start = pd.to_datetime(first_month + "-01", utc=True)
    for _ in range(n_months):
        stop = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        key = start.strftime("%Y-%m")
        cohort = registration_window(anchors, start, stop)
        entry = {"registrations_observed": len(cohort)}
        all_months[key] = entry
        start = stop
        if stop + age > cutoff:
            entry["status"] = "awaiting full cohort follow-up"
            continue
        if cohort.empty:
            entry.update(status="no registrations", unresolved_at_horizon=0)
            continue
        deadline = cohort.set_index(CASE)[TIME] + age
        # A Close is terminal for this case definition. The first Register anchors age.
        raw_close = raw.loc[raw[ACTIVITY].eq("Close")]
        closed_ids = raw_close.loc[raw_close[TIME].le(raw_close[CASE].map(deadline)), CASE].unique()
        entry["unresolved_at_horizon"] = len(cohort) - len(closed_ids)
        for name, df in views.items():
            observed = df.loc[df[TIME].le(df[CASE].map(deadline))].copy()
            complete = observed.loc[observed[CASE].isin(closed_ids)].copy()
            entry[name] = {
                "dfg_including_prefixes": pm4py.discover_dfg(observed)[0]
                if not observed.empty else {},
                "complete_by_horizon": completed_artifacts(
                    complete, include_skeleton=include_skeleton, reference_net=reference_net
                ),
            }
    return {"cases_without_registration": missing_register, "months": all_months}

    analyze_summary()

## 9. Provisional attribution

My highest-confidence attribution is the March collapse in raw model fitness. The alias accounts for the split after registration, and correcting it together with duplicate opens recovers .35 fitness units in March. The normalized fragment barely moved and cycle time stayed near baseline. Resource missingness is a distinct, persistent migration effect with high confidence. Its impact on any particular team's workload estimate depends on which assignments were lost.

I have moderately high confidence that incomplete supplier bundles contributed to the May increase in document rework. Requests and returns increased before staff left, and September brought partial relief near the reported recovery. Attribution of the full jump to bundle quality is weaker. Z's share rose sharply, its customers differed beforehand, and a case can generate several requests. I would revise the supplier account downward if matched cases showed stable first-pass defect and loop rates while changing supplier weights reproduced the aggregate.

I have moderately high confidence that reduced staffing amplified delays from June onward. The timing, the growing tail and the predominance of waiting over active-service growth support a capacity constraint. Rework also sends cases back to the same queue, mediating a supplier effect on other customers. The aggregate cannot assign those waiting hours uniquely to suppliers or staffing. A priority-induced shift of delay toward standard cases is plausible with low confidence until ready-time cohorts show differential waits or overtaking after July.

October's renewal-driven volume and mix shift has high confidence. Attribution of the senior branch to seasonality alone has low confidence. Detailed severity, actual referral decisions and historical coding coverage could change that assessment. December's improvement is consistent with lower seasonal load, overtime, better bundles and backlog clearance. Confidence in their separate effects is low. Verified assessor-hours, dated bundle defects, comparable prior renewal seasons and decision records linked to actual reviews would distinguish recovery in intake, execution and recording. Persistent excess rework in comparable off-season cases after capacity and supplier recovery would strengthen the case for lasting behavioral adaptation.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness falls while paired mapped fitness and normalized registration edges stay within reference bands, THEN audit emitter versions, eligibility semantics and duplicate-open evidence before changing the normative model, BECAUSE a representation change can create alignment penalties on otherwise equivalent traces.
2. IF resource missingness rises within migrated activities and apparent team throughput changes with the treatment of unknown IDs, THEN reconcile task assignments to rosters and publish workload bounds, BECAUSE selective loss of assignees can move measured work between teams.
3. IF first-bundle defects and subsequent return loops rise disproportionately within comparable Z cases after May 5, THEN trace request reasons to the affected bundle fields and track first-pass acceptance through recovery, BECAUSE a defect can trigger a document return and another assessment visit.
4. IF ready backlog age and ready-to-start waits rise after staff losses while service per comparable visit stays near baseline, THEN reconcile arrival work including repeat visits against staffed hours and review capacity in the affected queues, BECAUSE a shortfall accumulates time before work starts.
5. IF high-priority waits improve relative to standard waits after July 1 and dispatch records show older eligible standard cases being bypassed, THEN review priority ordering and age limits for waiting standard cases, BECAUSE advancing one class redistributes access to shared assessor capacity.
6. IF renewal weeks bring the expected mix and volume surge but a senior-referral excess survives severity standardization and seasonal comparison, THEN audit referral decisions and senior-event capture on matched cases, BECAUSE the residual can arise from changed practice, finer severity or coding coverage.
7. IF December queues and loops fall as demand eases and interventions begin, THEN compare dated overtime and campaign exposure at matched load and repeat the comparison after the season, BECAUSE simultaneous demand relief, added capacity and better documents can produce the same aggregate recovery.
