## 1. What is observed

Automation was **available** in Alpha and Beta from April 2026, in Gamma from July, and never in Delta or Epsilon. Availability is a binary branch-month flag, but **use** is lower and gradual. `automated_fast_path_pct` rose from 4% to 48–50% in Alpha and Beta by August and reached 32% in Gamma by August, one month after its pilot-based start. Treatment intensity is therefore time-varying and differs by branch.

**Selection** was non-random. Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical cycle times, and Gamma after a readiness pilot. Managers could also change local practices, so the adopters differ from the controls before rollout.

**Contemporaneous changes** are substantial. A central fraud desk began serving all branches in March. Routing sent about 10% more cases to Alpha and Beta from April and about 5% more to Gamma from July. Alpha lost two FTEs in May, Beta gained one in April, Gamma lost one in June, and Delta gained one in June. A May product change raised high-complexity shares in every branch, by +8 pp in Alpha and Epsilon, +5 pp in Gamma and Delta, and +3 pp in Beta.

**Outcome limits:** Medians and p90s are branch-month summaries that cannot be decomposed by path. Rework and escalation values repeat across consecutive months, which suggests carry-forward or coarser refresh. CSAT is a monthly mean with unknown response counts (roughly 20–45% response) and unknown nonresponse patterns. Fraud-desk waiting times are not recorded.

**Interference and treatment versions.** Treatment versions differ: pilot-phase Gamma (July–August), full-phase Alpha and Beta, and an automation rule that routes borderline cases to the shared fraud desk. Availability is not use, so E1 and E3 are different estimands. Interference is likely through the shared fraud desk and routing, which means Delta and Epsilon are not clean controls for Alpha and Beta after March. The decision-relevant estimand is a **package effect** (automation plus routing plus staffing as they actually occurred). It is identifiable only as a bundle, not as an automation-only effect.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary:** Staggered DiD / event study (group-time ATTs, Callaway–Sant'Anna-style, not TWFE) | Binary availability by cohort (Apr: Alpha, Beta; Jul: Gamma); event time k relative to onset | Never-treated (Delta, Epsilon); not-yet-treated (Gamma before July) | Calendar month FE; branch FE; complexity and FTE as sensitivity covariates (treated as co-interventions, not controls) | E1, E2, E5, E6, E9 | Parallel trends in log median and log p90 after branch and month FE; no anticipation before onset; no differential shocks other than the bundled package | Event-study leads; placebo onset in Jan–Mar; alternative baselines (Jan–Feb, Jan–Apr); randomization inference over cohort orderings | Five branches, so pre-trends are barely testable; Delta and Epsilon are not clean controls after March or after the May complexity shift |
| **Alt 1:** Comparative interrupted time series with Bayesian structural time series | Level and slope break at onset per adopter | Delta and Epsilon as covariates in a structural model | Control-branch series; complexity and FTE as regressors | E1, E2 for each adopter separately | Controls predict the adopter's counterfactual trajectory after the break; no concurrent adopter-specific shock | Posterior predictive checks on pre-period; sensitivity to prior; placebo breaks | Overfits a 3-month pre-period; ignores co-intervention timing |
| **Alt 2:** Fuzzy DiD / IV on dose | Availability × post as instrument for `automated_fast_path_pct` | Same controls as primary | Calendar FE; branch FE | E3 (dose-response, LATE-like) | Availability affects outcomes only through use; monotone dose response | First-stage strength (fast-path share jumps by about 425% at onset); Hausman-type comparison of OLS and IV | Weak exclusion: availability also changes routing and staffing practices |
| **Alt 3:** Synthetic control for Alpha and Beta | Single onset per adopter | Weighted combination of Gamma (pre-July), Delta, Epsilon | Pre-period outcome levels | E1 for each adopter | Donor-pool weights reproduce the pre-period; no donor shocks differ | Pre-fit RMSPE; leave-one-donor-out; in-space placebos | Donor pool of three; only three pre-months for Alpha and Beta; poor fit expected |

Primary strategy explanation: The primary design uses the staggered timing directly and compares each adopter with branches that have the same calendar shocks: growth in arrivals, the March fraud-desk shock, and the May complexity shift. Those shocks are common to all five branches, so they are differenced out. Two-way fixed effects with a single binary treatment would treat already-treated branches as controls for later adopters, and when effects change over time that comparison can have the wrong sign. The group-time design avoids those forbidden comparisons and makes the pre-period explicit. Using Gamma's pre-July months as not-yet-treated controls adds a third comparison. Because the design uses only five clusters, inference should rely on randomization inference or a wild cluster bootstrap rather than conventional clustered standard errors. Synthetic control and ITS are weaker here because the donor pool is three branches and the adopter pre-periods are short. Calendar shocks are not absorbed by a single-series model.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

Arithmetic facts are stated first. Interpretations follow. Medians and p90s are the arithmetic means of monthly branch values; these are not case-level medians.

**1. Branch-specific pre/post changes.** Windows: Alpha and Beta pre = Jan–Mar, post = Apr–Aug. Gamma pre = Jan–Jun, post = Jul–Aug. Controls (Delta and Epsilon) use matched calendar windows.

Control arithmetic: Delta and Epsilon median rose from 37.4 to 42.6 h (+13.1% in log terms). p90 rose from 77.2 to 93.2 h (+18.9%). Rework rose by 1.7 pp on average, and escalation by 1.8 pp. The March +2.0 pp escalation jump appears in every branch.

**Per-FTE load.** Alpha arrivals per FTE rose from 31.2 to 40.2 (+29%). Controls rose about 10%. Beta rose 19%.

**Late-window, single-month (August) contrasts.** Median: Alpha about 17%, Beta about 8%, Gamma about 10%. p90: Alpha about 12%, Beta about 8%, Gamma about 8%. These use one month and are noisy.

**2. Never-treated and not-yet-treated comparisons.** Delta and Epsilon (never-treated) show the same calendar drift. Gamma pre-July serves as a not-yet-treated comparison. Gamma's Jan–Jun median rose from 36.4 to 45.9 h, driven by its May complexity shift and rework spike (14.6%). Its Jan–Apr baseline (38.1 h) is therefore the cleaner comparison, which gives a DiD of about 9%.

**3. Treatment intensity.** Within Alpha, median fell about 4.6 h (after subtracting control drift) as fast-path share rose 23 pp from April to August. Within Beta, the adjusted change is about 1.9 h over the same 23 pp. Pooled, this is about 0.1 to 0.2 h per pp of fast-path share, or roughly 1 to 2 h per 10 pp (about 2% to 4%). This slope is confounded by time and by complexity. It is descriptive only.

**4. Pre-trends and regression to mean.**
- Alpha and Beta have only three pre-months, and the test has little power. Alpha's pre median is about 31% above the control level, and its pre p90 is about 28% above. Both are consistent with selection on poor historical performance.
- Regression to mean predicts decline for selected high-level branches. Alpha's median did not decline, while controls rose. This argues against pure regression to mean, but the controls' rise may reflect the shared complexity shock.
- Gamma is the most vulnerable. Its May–June spike (rework 14.6%, median about 45 h) coincided with its complexity shift and with the pilot period. Its July–August reversion to roughly its January–April level is consistent with regression to mean as well as with automation. Gamma's apparent effect should be treated as fragile.
- The March escalation jump appears in all five branches before any automation began for adopters. That is a common shock or measurement change, not an automation effect.

**5. Sensitivity bounds.**

Escalation and per-FTE specialist load are the most robust outcomes because they appear in every adopter. Rework and CSAT are the least robust.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Choice: `expand with an embedded evaluation`.**

**Next branch: Delta.** Delta has the lowest complexity (23%), stable staffing, and the cleanest pre-period. Epsilon is the highest-risk branch (38% complexity, p90 about 97 h) and is a poor first test. Epsilon remains untreated as the concurrent control.

**Assignment.** Randomize automation availability within Delta at the weekly-block level. For 12 weeks, alternate on and off blocks, stratified by week-of-month and by complexity class. Freeze routing campaigns and FTE changes during the test. Log every staffing change.

**Minimum data.** At least 12 weeks of pre-period case-level data and 12 weeks of randomized post-period data, followed by 8 weeks of full-on follow-up.

**Process-mining and queue data.** Case-level event logs with timestamps for registration, triage (automated or manual), fraud-desk enqueue and dequeue, specialist enqueue, start and end, rework loops, resolution, and closure. Queue-length snapshots every four hours. FTE hours by day. Risk class at registration. Survey response counts by branch-week and by path.

**Spillover monitoring.** Track fraud-desk volume and waiting time for Delta versus Epsilon. Track routing shares and specialist load in Alpha and Beta. Monitor staff transfers between branches.

**Guardrails.** Pause if the weekly p90 in on-blocks exceeds the concurrent control by more than 15% for two consecutive weeks. Pause if specialist wait rises more than 25%, specialist cases per FTE rise more than 20%, rework rises more than 2 pp, or CSAT falls more than 0.15 with at least 100 responses.

**Scale-up rule.** Expand to Epsilon and then to Alpha and Beta only if all of the following hold:
- the randomized median contrast is negative, with a 90% interval excluding zero or showing at least a 5% reduction;
- the upper bound of the p90 contrast is no more than +5% (non-inferiority);
- escalation and specialist-per-FTE load increase by no more than 10%;
- fast-path share reaches at least 30%;
- no guardrail is breached.

If these criteria fail, redesign triage: exclude borderline and fraud-adjacent cases from automation and test that variant.

req = ["automation", "arrivals", "fte", "high_complexity_pct", "automated_fast_path_pct",
       "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct", "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"]
assert df[req].notna().all().all(), "Missing values present"
assert not df.duplicated(["branch", "month_str"]).any(), "Duplicate branch-month"
assert df.groupby("branch").size().eq(8).all(), "Panel is not balanced"
assert (df["p90_cycle_h"] >= df["median_cycle_h"]).all(), "p90 below median somewhere"
assert df["csat_1to5"].between(1, 5).all(), "CSAT outside 1-5"

df["adopt"] = df["branch"].map(ADOPT)                      # NaT = never treated
expected_flag = ((df["month"] >= df["adopt"]) & df["adopt"].notna()).astype(int)
assert (df["automation"] == expected_flag).all(), "automation flag inconsistent with adoption dates"

adopter = df["adopt"].notna()
rel = (df["month"].dt.year - df["adopt"].dt.year) * 12 + (df["month"].dt.month - df["adopt"].dt.month)
df["rel"] = rel.where(adopter)                             # months since onset; NaN for controls

# Repeated-value check (suggests carry-forward or coarse refresh)
def longest_run(s):
    runs = (s != s.shift()).cumsum()
    return s.groupby(runs).transform("size").max()

for col in ["rework_pct", "escalation_pct"]:
    for b, sub in df.groupby("branch"):
        r = longest_run(sub[col])
        if r >= 2:
            print(f"WARNING: {b}.{col} has {r} identical consecutive months; "
                  "possible carry-forward or coarse refresh.")

# ---------- 2. Trends (one figure per outcome, no subplots) ----------
for col in OUT:
    print(f"\n{col} by branch-month")
    print(df.pivot(index="month_str", columns="branch", values=col)[BRANCHES].round(2))
    plt.figure(figsize=(8, 4.5))
    for b in BRANCHES:
        sub = df[df["branch"] == b]
        plt.plot(sub["month"], sub[col], marker="o", label=b)
    for b, t in ADOPT.items():
        plt.axvline(t, linestyle=":", alpha=0.4)
    plt.title(f"{col} by branch (dotted = adoption date)")
    plt.legend()
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.show()

# ---------- 3. Transparent adopter-specific pre/post contrasts ----------
def wmean(branch, start, end, col):
    m = (df["branch"] == branch) & (df["month_str"] >= start) & (df["month_str"] <= end)
    return df.loc[m, col].mean()

rows = []
for b, ((p0, p1), (q0, q1)) in WIN.items():
    rec = {"branch": b, "pre": f"{p0}..{p1}", "post": f"{q0}..{q1}"}
    for col in OUT:
        a_pre, a_post = wmean(b, p0, p1, col), wmean(b, q0, q1, col)
        c_pre = np.mean([wmean(c, p0, p1, col) for c in CONTROLS])
        c_post = np.mean([wmean(c, q0, q1, col) for c in CONTROLS])
        if col.endswith("_h"):
            rec[col + "_raw_%"] = round(100 * (a_post / a_pre - 1), 1)
            did = np.exp(np.log(a_post / a_pre) - np.log(c_post / c_pre)) - 1
            rec[col + "_DiD_%"] = round(100 * did, 1)
        else:
            rec[col + "_raw_diff"] = round(a_post - a_pre, 3)
            rec[col + "_DiD_diff"] = round((a_post - a_pre) - (c_post - c_pre), 3)
    load_pre = wmean(b, p0, p1, "arrivals") / wmean(b, p0, p1, "fte")
    load_post = wmean(b, q0, q1, "arrivals") / wmean(b, q0, q1, "fte")
    rec["arrivals_per_FTE_raw_%"] = round(100 * (load_post / load_pre - 1), 1)
    rows.append(rec)
print("\nAdopter-specific pre/post contrasts (DiD vs Delta/Epsilon matched windows):")
print(pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("branch").T)

# ---------- 4. Regression set-up ----------
df["branch_id"] = df["branch"].astype("category").cat.codes
df["ln_median"] = np.log(df["median_cycle_h"])
df["ln_p90"] = np.log(df["p90_cycle_h"])
df["fp_share"] = df["automated_fast_path_pct"] / 100.0

# ASSUMPTIONS (explicit, not proven by the regressions below):
# A1 Parallel trends: absent treatment, adopters would have followed controls' log trajectories.
# A2 No anticipation: outcomes do not move before the stated adoption dates.
# A3 Bundle: the adopter-specific change reflects automation plus concurrent routing,
#    staffing, complexity, and fraud-desk changes, not automation alone.
# A4 No interference: Delta/Epsilon are unaffected by Alpha/Beta through the fraud desk.
# A5 Descriptive models use TWFE and are NOT valid causal estimators under heterogeneous,
#    dynamic effects with staggered timing; they are reported only as summaries.

def fit_and_show(formula, label, keep_pattern):
    m = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(cov_type="cluster",
                                      cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch_id"]})
    tab = pd.DataFrame({"coef": m.params, "se_cluster": m.bse, "p": m.pvalues})
    print(f"\n=== {label}\n{formula}")
    print(tab.loc[tab.index.str.contains(keep_pattern)].round(4))
    return m

print("\nWARNING: only 5 branch clusters. Cluster-robust SEs are downward-biased here; "
      "p-values are descriptive. Use wild cluster bootstrap or randomization inference "
      "over cohort orderings for inference.")
print("WARNING: median_cycle_h and p90_cycle_h are monthly branch summaries. Averages of "
      "monthly medians are not case-level medians; path-level decomposition is impossible "
      "with these aggregates.")

# ---------- 5a. Descriptive panel models (binary availability) ----------
for y in ["ln_median", "ln_p90", "rework_pct", "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"]:
    fit_and_show(f"{y} ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month_str)",
                 f"Descriptive TWFE: availability -> {y}", r"^automation$")

# ---------- 5b. Relative-time (event-study) indicators ----------
es = df[df["rel"].isna() | df["rel"].between(-3, 4)].copy()   # restrict to comparable window
ev_map = {-3: "ev_m3", -2: "ev_m2", 0: "ev_p0", 1: "ev_p1", 2: "ev_p2", 3: "ev_p3", 4: "ev_p4"}
for k, name in ev_map.items():
    es[name] = (es["rel"] == k).astype(int)               # reference: rel = -1
ev_formula = ("ln_median ~ " + " + ".join(ev_map.values()) +
              " + C(branch) + C(month_str)")
fit_and_show(ev_formula, "Descriptive event study (reference rel=-1), log median",
             r"^ev_")
print("Event-time indicators are descriptive. With heterogeneous and staggered effects, "
      "these coefficients are not valid ATT(k) estimates; use a group-time estimator.")

# ---------- 6. Treatment-intensity model ----------
fit_and_show("ln_median ~ fp_share + C(branch) + C(month_str)",
             "Intensity (fast-path share) -> log median", r"^fp_share$")
fit_and_show("ln_p90 ~ fp_share + C(branch) + C(month_str)",
             "Intensity (fast-path share) -> log p90", r"^fp_share$")
# Sensitivity: complexity and FTE are post-treatment and may be mediators or
# co-interventions; including them changes the estimand and should be read cautiously.
fit_and_show("ln_median ~ fp_share + high_complexity_pct + fte + C(branch) + C(month_str)",
             "Intensity sensitivity with post-treatment covariates", r"^fp_share$")

# ---------- 7. Final caveats ----------
print("\nINTERPRETATION GUARDRAILS:")
print("- Regression coefficients here summarize associations under stated assumptions A1-A5.")
print("- Do not report a single 'cycle-time reduction %' from these models.")
print("- Escalation and specialist load per FTE are the most stable signals; CSAT and rework are not.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Automated fast path reached 48–50% of cases in Alpha and Beta by August, and manual fast path fell from roughly 88% to roughly 40%. Specialist cases per FTE rose more in all three adopters than in Delta and Epsilon (about 15–28 percentage points more).
- **Conditionally causal:** Under parallel trends and no fraud-desk spillover, offering automation was associated with about +0.9 pp (range +0.7 to +1.1) additional escalation relative to never-treated branches, and with a larger increase in specialist load per FTE.
- **Suggestive:** Adopters' median cycle time ended roughly 5–15% below the never-treated trajectory, and p90 roughly 5–12% below, but naive comparisons point the other way and the estimates depend on the baseline window.
- **Not supported:** "Automation reduces cycle time by X%," any tail-improvement claim, any CSAT improvement, and any transfer of these effects to Delta or Epsilon.
