# Reducing Environmental Impact of Spare-Parts Fulfillment: A Process-Grounded Redesign

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Boundary (gate-to-gate, as observable in the event log):** the order lifecycle from `Receive order` through `Close`, including all rework, packaging, and exception loops visible in the DFG (partial shipment  backorder wait, inspection  rework production  CNC, rework packaging loops, emergency production, cancellation).

**Inside the boundary:**
- Machine energy (`energy_kwh`) for CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell, including energy consumed in rework loops (attributed via `rework_flag`).
- Outbound transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`) by `route_mode` (parcel ground, air, dedicated van, consolidated truck), including second shipments caused by partial delivery.
- Packaging material (`package_material_kg`), including re-packaging after `rework packaging`.
- Process scrap (`material_scrap_kg`), including scrap generated by rework production.
- Obsolescence: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (a material + embodied-energy write-off visible in the batching evidence).
- Lost production from cancellations due to delay (energy spent on orders that never deliver).

**Outside the boundary (flagged as displacement risks, not counted):**
- Embodied carbon of purchased raw material except the scrapped fraction; inbound supplier transport; warehouse overheads not captured in `energy_kwh`.
- Customer use-phase and end-of-life of parts.
- **Customer-site consequences of slow critical deliveries** (idled production equipment at the customer) — excluded from our footprint but potentially dominating the true lifecycle effect; this is why the Critical-downtime tier is treated with restraint.

**Assumptions and uncertainties:**
- `energy_kwh` is machine-attributed per order; batch energy is allocated proportionally to orders — this attribution is approximate for batch sizes 12–20.
- Whether `transport_kgco2e` for dedicated vans already includes empty-return emissions is unknown; van load factor is not logged.
- Obsolescence disposition (scrap vs. eventual discount sale) is unknown.
- `promised_lead_time_hours` is taken as the contractual lateness reference; "Critical-downtime" labels are assumed mostly truthful (a risk revisited in Section 5).

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batchingexpedite rebound.** Larger batches cut kWh/item, but if batch wait (up to 38h median) causes a missed promise, the recovery is emergency production or an air leg (+15–20 kgCO2e per order) — one air recovery can erase the energy savings of many batched items. *Mitigation:* segment-specific waiting caps (S2) and slack-gated batching (S10).
2. **Obsolescence displacement.** 6% of batch-produced items go unshipped for 14+ days when forecasts are wrong; the "efficient" batch converts into written-off material and embodied energy. *Mitigation:* tie batch size to forecast confidence, cap WIP, define disposition rules; monitor 14-day unshipped rate.
3. **Packaging–returns trade-off displacement.** Reinforced packaging doubles material but cuts damage returns 40% for heavy MTO parts; a returned part triggers replacement production (18–24 kWh) plus return transport. Blanket de-packaging to save material, or blanket reinforcement to chase return KPIs, both shift impact rather than reduce it. *Mitigation:* keep Q1 risk-based (weight + certification), not cost-based; use S6 to eliminate only *incompatible* packaging choices.
4. **Inspection–throughput squeeze.** A new quality gate lengthens inspection_cell queues — the very queues that cause rushed inspections, missed defects, and returns. *Mitigation:* add inspection capacity before enforcing S7; monitor queue time as a leading indicator.
5. **Mode-shift resilience erosion.** Aggressive S4/S5 shifts shrink air and van volumes; carriers may be dropped and capacity atrophy, so that during a disruption no R1 alternative exists — and consolidation waits worsen exactly when carriers fail. *Mitigation:* retain contracted backup air for Service-contract accounts; audit route diversity per account.
6. **Urgency inflation (customer behavior).** If precautionary orders lose vans under S9, customers may relabel everything "true downtime" to keep the 14h service, restoring emissions and corrupting SP2. *Mitigation:* contract-based criticality definitions, sampled audits of true-downtime claims, chargeback or service-tier pricing for unjustified van use.
7. **Critical-downtime lifecycle displacement.** Cutting vans or air for genuinely critical parts saves ~35–45 kgCO2e per order within our boundary but can idle customer equipment whose energy and emissions losses are external and far larger. *Mitigation:* never apply emissions targets to the true-downtime tier; savings come from the ~190 unjustified vans and the precautionary tier only.
8. **Complete-order holding side effects (S11).** Holding for complete shipment raises stock aging/obsolescence and may push customers to place duplicate or precautionary orders, displacing footprint rather than removing it. *Mitigation:* per-line promised dates; monitor duplicate-order rates after the rule change.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a four-phase package, sequenced by confidence and by where the footprint actually lives:

**Phase 1 — Governance and clean wins (immediate):** S12 (exception log), S6 (packaging–route compatibility), S1 (shipping recommender), and S4 (airpriority-pick+ground for Standard-stock Service-contract where slack allows). These attack the clearest conformance failures (22% SP1 violations, ~600 packaging rework loops) with guardrails that keep lateness inside the 85% feasibility rule. Expected effect: roughly 5–6 tCO2e per 12,000 orders from S4 alone, plus packaging waste reduction, with negligible service risk.

**Phase 2 — Production system (3–9 months):** S2 (capped dynamic batching: tight caps for Service-contract MTO given the 1.7× heat-treat late multiplier; looser for Regular), S3 (CNC_BCNC_A shift gated on due-date slack), S7 (inspection gate with added inspection capacity), and an S11 pilot on availability rules. This targets the concentration of impact: MTO is ~87% of energy and ~86% of scrap, and the CNC_B rework variant is 5% of orders but ~a fifth of energy and a quarter of scrap. Halving CNC_B rework plus first-pass inspection improvements attacks both footprint and the worst service outcome in the log (32% late, 160h median).

**Phase 3 — Critical-tier redesign (6–12 months):** S9 (true vs. precautionary split with contractual definitions) plus S8 (shared emergency lanes only for the precautionary tier), with SP2 justification enforced through S12. True-downtime keeps emergency production + dedicated van untouched — this is the explicit refusal of the "lowest-emission" option where service and lifecycle consequences dominate. Savings come from the ~190 unjustified vans and precautionary air/van use.

**Phase 4 — Demand-side levers:** S10 (carbon-aware promise dates for Regular MTO) and S5 (twice-daily consolidated trucks for non-urgent Regular stock), both creating the slack that makes Phases 1–2 durable.

**Why this is balanced:** every environmental lever is paired with a service guardrail drawn from the stated policies — SP1's 85% feasibility threshold bounds mode shifts; S2's waiting caps bound batching; Q1 keeps reinforcement where damage risk is real; R1's alternative-route requirement is preserved by retaining air and exclusive van as backup tiers. Explicit non-goals: no blanket air ban, no maximum-batch policy, no removal of reinforced packaging from >8 kg MTO parts, no elimination of dedicated vans, and no consolidation of time-critical orders. Aggregate expectation: a meaningful reduction in transport CO2e (unjustified air, precautionary vans, parcel consolidation) and in MTO energy/scrap (rework elimination), while segment late rates stay within roughly 1–2 percentage points of baseline and Critical true-downtime service (15h median, 30h p90) is untouched.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Validation design:**
- Lock the current log as baseline; roll out in waves (by region or customer cohort) and compare matched segments pre/post (difference-in-differences on the Section 2 indicators), rather than before/after averages that absorb seasonality.
- Instrument what the log currently misses: part weight (for Q1 and per-kg transport intensity), route reliability at decision time, van occupancy/empty returns, forecast error linked to `batch_id`, and disposition of obsolete batch items.

**Conformance monitoring:**
- Model SP1 as a temporal rule: every air/van choice event must be preceded by a feasibility computation showing ground transit + buffer meets `promised_lead_time_hours` with >85% probability; alert when violations exceed ~5% in any segment (baseline: 22% for Standard-stock Service-contract).
- Model SP2 as a missing-activity check: `dedicated_van` requires a preceding justification event; target >95% completeness (baseline: 69%).
- Model Q1 as an attribute guard: parts >8 kg on MTO paths require `Package reinforced` or a certification event; track violations weekly, since they correlate with return/rework loops.
- Re-run process discovery after implementation: reduced emergency + partial-shipment chaos (from S9, S11, S12) should raise model precision, which itself becomes a measurable indicator that exception paths are being controlled rather than proliferating.

**Segment-level service monitoring:** monthly dashboards of median/p90 cycle time vs. promised lead, late rate, cancellation, and backorder waits per segment — including the new Critical subcategories. Stop-loss rules: if Standard-stock Service-contract late rate rises >2 points above the 5% baseline, or precautionary late rate exceeds ~10%, automatically re-widen air/van eligibility pending review.

**Rebound monitoring:** expedite_flag and emergency-production frequency after batching changes; air share after ground shifts; 14-day unshipped batch rate; migration of orders from precautionary to true-downtime labels; duplicate-order rate after S11; van load factor after S8.

**Governance:** a weekly exception-review board working from the S12 log (override taxonomy: customer demand, carrier disruption, quality hold, error); a monthly joint service–sustainability scorecard by segment so neither objective is silently traded away; quarterly recalibration of recommender thresholds and batch caps using observed data; and an R1 audit verifying every Service-contract account retains at least one certified alternative route before any carrier consolidation is approved.
