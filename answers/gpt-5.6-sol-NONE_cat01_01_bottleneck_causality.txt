## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 78/100.**

The strongest evidence is the reversal between the crude and complexity-stratified comparisons. Orion’s overall mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a crude difference of **+6.72 hours**. Yet Orion is faster within every recorded complexity stratum: low **7.50 versus 9.64** (2.14), medium **17.17 versus 19.38** (2.21), and high **31.57 versus 35.00** (3.43). Orion receives far more high-complexity cases (42.9% versus 12.5%), night work (92.9% versus 25%), compliance-required work (64.3% versus 25%), and rework cases. This is strong evidence that the aggregate difference largely reflects routing and operating conditions rather than a harmful Orion label.

Against a firm causal conclusion, overlap is weak: only two low-complexity Orion cases and two high-complexity Nova cases exist; Orion has only one day case. Complexity is coarse, unobserved case risk likely affects routing and duration, and shift/load/compliance are highly entangled. Moreover, completed cases alone condition the analysis on a potentially selective outcome.

“Orion” may combine reviewer skill, staffing, queue ownership, shift coverage, and routing policy. A total assignment effect could therefore operate through queueing, service, compliance coordination, or rework; a direct effect holding those mediators fixed is neither identified nor necessarily operationally meaningful.

Moving half of Orion’s work may succeed if Nova has spare capacity and Orion-specific queues are harmful. It may fail because the crude premise is confounded, or backfire by overloading Nova, moving difficult night/compliance cases away from certified reviewers, increasing rework, or delaying open cases.

Complexity-driven routing, Orion’s difficult-case role, night coverage, mandatory compliance performance, and the components of cycle time are supported by the prompt. Effects of reviewer skill, latent risk, group on quality, and completion selection are plausible assumptions. A static DAG suppresses time-varying feedback: queue length may alter later routing, staffing, and load; a Request Info event creates another review and can change queue state while the case is active.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orion’s crude disadvantage is mainly case-mix confounding | Routing sends difficult, compliance-heavy work to Orion | Crude +6.72 h reverses within all complexity strata; Orion has much more high complexity | Complexity may be too coarse; within-stratum routing still differs | Match or weight on pre-routing complexity, compliance requirement, shift, arrival load, claim attributes, and queue state; report overlap and sensitivity to latent risk | Strong attenuation or sign reversal after valid balancing favors this; persistence of a harmful effect in overlap weakens it |
| 2 | Orion is genuinely beneficial, masked by case mix | Certified reviewers process difficult cases more efficiently or avoid delay | Orion is faster in every complexity stratum, especially high; wait means are lower within strata | Tiny low/high comparison cells; reviewer skill and queue conditions are not observed | Within-route and complexity comparisons using reviewer IDs and certification; randomize eligible difficult cases under common queue conditions | Lower service time, queue delay, or rework for Orion/certified reviewers would favor benefit |
| 3 | Orion has a genuine harmful operational effect | Handoffs, local work practices, batching, or slower reviewer service lengthen cases | Orion’s unadjusted cycle time and rework rate are higher | Every complexity-stratified mean favors Orion; much of cycle time is waiting | Randomized pilot among mutually eligible cases, with queue state fixed/recorded; compare intention-to-treat cycle, service, and rework | Orion-routed cases remain slower under balance, with a specific DFG/service/wait mechanism |
| 4 | Shift and arrival load, not group, drive delay | Night staffing and high-load queues increase waiting; Orion inherits those conditions | Orion is 92.9% night and predominantly high-load | Crude day/night comparison is also composition-driven; within low Nova night is not slower | Reconstruct queue length, staffing, arrivals, and work-in-progress at classification; compare groups within narrow time/load blocks or exploit shift-boundary discontinuities | Group contrast shrinks within queue-state blocks; delay tracks WIP/staffing rather than group |
| 5 | Compliance coordination mediates a policy/group effect | Required cases wait before/around Compliance Check; certified-resource availability affects handoff | Compliance is 64.3% in Orion versus 25% in Nova; required cases have much longer crude duration | Within medium complexity, required and non-required means are similar; requirement is confounded by risk | Obtain compliance timestamps and team capacity; perform conformance and stage-duration analysis within required cases; pilot shared compliance capacity | Excess delay localizes between Classify, Compliance Check, and Review; capacity intervention lowers that interval |
| 6 | Rework mediates duration differences | Request Info creates a second review and external waiting/service | Orion has more observed rework overall; traces explicitly show repeated review | High-complexity rework rates are equal; review service aggregates both reviews | Compare initial review, external information wait, and repeat-review service within pre-treatment matched cases; analyze whether assignment changes Request Info probability | A group effect on rework incidence followed by longer repeat-cycle duration supports mediation; no incidence difference weakens it |
| 7 | Completed-case selection or measurement creates misleading contrasts | Congested, risky, or poor-quality cases remain open/abandon differentially; wait measure mixes stages | Only completed cases are present; no censoring/open-case information; quality is coarse | Direction and magnitude of selection are unknown | Build assignment-cohort data including open/abandoned cases; use survival/competing-risk analysis and inverse probability of observation sensitivity | Group contrast changes materially when censored/open cases are included or weighted; backlog age differs by group |
| 8 | Routing label is a proxy, not a portable treatment | Group encodes queue, reviewer certification, shift, and policy; moving the label changes congestion equilibrium | Operational facts explicitly tie Orion to difficult cases, certification, and night work | A controlled reassignment could still define a policy treatment | Interrupted or randomized routing with treatment-component logging; negative control outcomes measured before assignment; inspect spillovers on non-pilot queues | Effects follow queue/certification/load components rather than the nominal label; pre-assignment negative controls should remain balanced |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Instrument and diagnose | Preserve routing while capturing classification score, eligibility, timestamps, queue state, staffing, reviewer/certification, open cases, and abandonment | Confounding, selection, and stage-specific delay | All newly classified cases | No operational change; data-quality audit; common event definitions | Stage durations, DFGs, variants, queue WIP, compliance handoffs, repeat-review loops, active versus elapsed time | Establishes overlap, eligible pilot strata, backlog bias, and whether delay is queueing, compliance, or service | Pause pilot planning if treatment eligibility, timestamps, or completion coverage are unreliable |
| 2: Capacity/process intervention | Add or cross-train shared night/compliance capacity; use common priority rules and explicit handoff SLAs | Queueing and compliance waiting rather than group label | Night/compliance queue or shift-week clusters | Do not relax compliance; cap overtime; monitor downstream queues and quality | Classify-to-compliance, compliance-to-review, queue length, throughput, conformance violations, rework, aging WIP | Tests whether Orion’s apparent delay is caused by constrained stage capacity | Roll back if compliance breaches, appeal/rework control limits, or downstream WIP rises materially |
| 3: Small routing pilot | Randomize 10–20% of mutually eligible, overlapping cases between Orion and Nova, stratified by complexity, compliance, shift, load, and queue state | Total effect of assignment under defined operating conditions | Individual case, or time-block cluster if contamination is substantial | Exclude cases requiring unique certification; capacity caps; intention-to-treat; pre-specified quality non-inferiority; monitor spillovers | End-to-end cycle, queue and service components, variants, Request Info loops, reassignment/crossover, open-case survival, quality | Directly estimates a local policy effect and identifies mediated pathways | Stop for quality harm, certification violation, overload, worsening aged backlog, or predefined cycle-time futility/harm |
| 4: Adaptive scale-up | Expand only the winning mechanism: routing share, shared queue, or staffing change | Sustainable throughput and quality | Shift-week or routing-rule version | Staged rollout, holdout cells, periodic re-randomization, fairness checks | DFG drift, stage bottlenecks, workload balance, SLA tails, appeals, reopenings, censoring | Tests transportability and equilibrium effects at higher volume | Revert if gains disappear, spillovers offset benefits, or tail latency/quality worsens |

**Final recommendation:** Do not reassign 50% of Orion’s future portfolio based on the crude mean. The observed pattern is more consistent with selective routing than with Orion causing delay, and indiscriminate reassignment would move a disproportionate share of difficult, night, and compliance-required cases into a Nova system whose capacity and certification fit are unknown. It could also alter both queues, so historical case-level contrasts do not predict the new equilibrium.

First instrument the process at assignment and include all cases, not only completions. Reconstruct live queue state and split waiting into classification queue, compliance execution/wait, post-compliance queue, external information wait, and repeat-review time. In parallel, test shared night/compliance capacity because it targets a plausible bottleneck without assuming the group label is causal. Then conduct a capacity-capped randomized pilot only among cases for which both groups are genuinely eligible, stratified by major risk and operating variables. Estimate intention-to-treat effects on completed and still-open cases, with cycle-time components, rework, and quality safeguards. Scale only if the pilot demonstrates a time benefit without backlog spillovers or quality degradation.

    for idx, n in table.items():

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case-mix confounding:** Add the routing engine’s pre-assignment risk score and all input features used to choose a group.

2. **Queueing versus compliance versus external delay:** Add event timestamps for classification, compliance start/end, each review start/end, Request Info, information receipt, and closure.

3. **Capacity and congestion effects:** Add queue length, active staffing, reviewer availability, and work-in-progress at the exact routing time.

4. **Completed-case selection:** Change the extract to an inception cohort containing every assigned case, with open, abandoned, transferred, and censored status.

5. **Portable causal effect of reassignment:** Run a capacity-capped randomized routing pilot among cases certified as eligible for both groups.
