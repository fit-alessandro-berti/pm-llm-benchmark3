## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

I use an **event-time, evidence-aware, three-valued reconstruction**. The governing state at an event is not the final case state; it is the latest value known at or before that event under the source ordering. All timestamps are UTC. 

For case (c), attribute (a), and event (e):

[
State_a(c,e)=Value_a(u^*)
]

where (u^*) is the latest event in the same case, at or before (e), whose cell for (a) is nonblank.

* A blank cell means **no update**. It neither erases nor nulls the prior value.
* Values are forward-filled only within a case. They are never backward-filled.
* An attribute first observed after release cannot be copied into the release snapshot. Thus TX-305’s EUR 150,000 settlement amount is not automatically its release amount.
* An update is effective at its event timestamp unless separate evidence establishes another effective time.

Events are totally ordered as follows:

[
e_1 \prec e_2
\iff
ts(e_1)<ts(e_2)
;\lor;
\big(ts(e_1)=ts(e_2)\land index(e_1)<index(e_2)\big)
]

The `event_index` is therefore substantive, not cosmetic. For TX-311, `Enhanced_Review` at index 3 precedes `Update_Jurisdiction` at index 4, although both have timestamp 16:20. The review saw US; the subsequent update changed the case state to DE; the release snapshot is consequently DE.

### Material changes

I interpret materiality as **obligation-relative**:

* A change that activates or deactivates a rule is material, such as crossing the EUR 100,000 threshold, changing jurisdiction between DE and non-DE, or changing customer type to PEP.
* A change that alters exemption matching is material to the exception analysis.
* For R4, I compare every later amount with the amount captured at the latest review. A later amount greater than 120% of the reviewed amount invalidates that review for amount-dependent obligations.
* This review-baseline interpretation captures cumulative post-review growth and avoids missing two individually small increases whose cumulative effect exceeds 20%.
* A known decrease does not invalidate a review.
* A known increase of at most 20% does not invalidate a review unless it independently activates a rule or changes exemption scope.
* When the amount at the review is unknown, a later amount cannot safely be characterized as an increase, decrease, or percentage change relative to that review.

### Four distinct time concepts

| Time concept   | Meaning                                                                           | Treatment                                                                              |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Event time     | When the logged activity or update occurred                                       | Governs ordinary transaction-state updates                                             |
| Effective time | When a fact, exemption, or policy change begins to apply                          | Defaults to event time unless independently specified; exemptions use `effective_from` |
| Record time    | When the database received or stored the record                                   | May be later than effective time and does not alone prove late approval                |
| Evidence time  | When corroborating evidence came into existence or became cryptographically fixed | Used to determine whether claimed prior approval is credible                           |

A displayed approval field is a claim about approval time, not necessarily evidence that approval existed then. Conversely, a later database insertion can record an earlier valid approval when independently supported, as with EX-77’s qualified signature and documented outage.

### Formal pseudo-logic

Let (e \preceq f) mean (e\prec f) or (e=f). Attribute updates at event (e) are part of the state at (e).

```text
Order(e, f) :=
    timestamp(e) < timestamp(f)
    OR
    (timestamp(e) = timestamp(f) AND event_index(e) < event_index(f))

State(c, a, e) :=
    value of a on the Order-maximal event u
    such that Case(u)=c, u <= e, and value_a(u) is nonblank

Known(x) := x is not UNKNOWN
```

For a review (q) and release (r):

```text
AmountInvalid(q, r) :=
    exists amount-update u:
        q < u <= r
        AND Known(State(amount, q))
        AND Known(State(amount, u))
        AND State(amount, u) > 1.20 * State(amount, q)

AmountValidityUnknown(q, r) :=
    State(amount, q) is UNKNOWN
    AND an amount is first supplied or changed after q and by r
```

R1:

```text
Trigger_R1(c, r) :=
    Release_Funds(r)
    AND State(jurisdiction, r) != DE
    AND State(amount, r) >= 100000

Fresh_Enhanced_R1(c, h, r) :=
    Enhanced_Review(h)
    AND h < r
    AND review scope at h is known
    AND no R1-material state change m satisfies h < m <= r
    AND NOT AmountInvalid(h, r)

R1_OK(c, r) :=
    NOT Trigger_R1(c, r)
    OR exists h: Fresh_Enhanced_R1(c, h, r)
    OR exists x: Valid_R1_Exemption(x, c, r)
```

If the amount or jurisdiction prevents `Trigger_R1` from being established or excluded, `Trigger_R1=UNKNOWN`, not false.

R2:

```text
Trigger_R2(c, r) :=
    State(risk_level, r) = HIGH
    OR State(customer_type, r) = PEP

Relevant_Enhanced_R2(c, h, r) :=
    Enhanced_Review(h)
    AND latest relevant risk/customer-type update < h < r

R2_OK(c, r) :=
    NOT Trigger_R2(c, r)
    OR exists h, a:
         Relevant_Enhanced_R2(c, h, r)
         AND Compliance_Approve(a)
         AND h < a < r
         AND ApprovalPacket(a) covers EnhancedReview(h)
```

The disjunction uses three-valued logic: a known HIGH or PEP makes the trigger true even when the other field is unknown.

R3:

```text
Trigger_R3(c, s0, u, r) :=
    Sanctions_Screen(s0)
    AND Sanctions_List_Update(u)
    AND AddedJurisdiction(u) = State(jurisdiction, r)
    AND s0 is the latest screen before u
    AND s0 < u < r

R3_OK(c, r) :=
    for every triggered update u:
        exists s1:
            Sanctions_Screen(s1)
            AND u < s1 < r
```

R4:

```text
R4_Invalidates(c, q, r) := AmountInvalid(q, r)

ReplacementRequired(c, q, r) :=
    R4_Invalidates(c, q, r)
    AND an amount-dependent obligation is active at r
```

R5:

```text
Valid_R1_Exemption(x, c, r) :=
    ReferencedOrOtherwiseIdentified(x, c, r)
    AND ApprovedBy(x) = Head_Compliance
    AND EffectiveFrom(x) <= timestamp(r) < Expires(x)
    AND JurisdictionScope(x) matches State(jurisdiction, r)
    AND CustomerTypeScope(x) matches State(customer_type, r)
    AND AmountScope(x) matches State(amount, r)
    AND Waives(x, R1)
    AND EvidenceSupports(ApprovalExisted(x) no later than r)
```

R6 and R7:

```text
DecisiveUnknown(rule, c, r) AND not otherwise resolved
    => result(rule, c, r) = UNKNOWN

timestamp(e1) = timestamp(e2)
    => e1 < e2 exactly when event_index(e1) < event_index(e2)
```

For the case-level label, a definite unsatisfied triggered rule produces `NONCOMPLIANT`. If no definite failure exists but a decisive proposition remains unknown, the label is `INDETERMINATE`. I reserve `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` for a case whose otherwise-unmet obligation is affirmatively waived. A case that independently completed the required review remains `COMPLIANT` even if it also had a valid but redundant exemption.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-302.** I classify this as `COMPLIANT`, rather than `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`, because the Enhanced Review independently satisfies R1. A taxonomy that labels every release relying on a valid waiver as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` could use that label instead. Invalidating EX-77 would not change the substantive result unless the Enhanced Review were incomplete, did not cover the release state, or an unlogged material change occurred after it.

**TX-305.** An immutable release message containing the amount would be decisive. A review workpaper showing that the Enhanced Review considered a specific release amount would also help.

* If the release amount was below EUR 100,000, R1 was inactive.
* If it was at least EUR 100,000 and the review demonstrably covered that amount, the case could be compliant.
* If it was at least EUR 100,000 but changed materially after the review, or the review was not amount-aware, the case would be noncompliant.
* A purely syntactic reading under which the occurrence of any `Enhanced_Review` satisfies R1 could classify the case as compliant regardless of amount. I do not adopt that reading because the supplied note expressly says that the review document had no amount, while R1 and R4 are amount-dependent.

The EUR 150,000 settlement value is evidence about settlement time. It would become evidence about release time only if a stable payment identifier, immutable instruction, or bank protocol established that the settled instruction was identical to the released instruction.

**TX-310.** The result turns on what counts as sufficient evidence of pre-release approval.

* A contemporaneous Head Compliance call recording, independently timestamped approval message, signed decision record, or independent witness would support `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`.
* If policy requires a qualified signature before release, or if the requester’s testimony is rejected as insufficient, the case becomes `NONCOMPLIANT`.
* If oral approval is permissible but must later be documented, the evidence still needs to show that the oral approval actually occurred before 10:00. The PDF’s displayed 08:50 field does not establish that by itself.

**TX-311.** The immediate ordering consequence is not ambiguous: index 3 precedes index 4, so the Enhanced Review saw US and the release saw DE. Simply reversing the two equal-timestamp indexes would alter the review snapshot, but with DE still established before release it would not ordinarily change the release classification.

The result could change if:

* the DE update were unreliable, leaving release jurisdiction unknown;
* an immutable release payload showed a different non-DE jurisdiction introduced after the review; or
* the policy used a “sticky obligation” interpretation under which once R1 was activated under US at EUR 115,000, later deactivation did not remove the obligation and every subsequent material update demanded a new review.

The written phrase “At `Release_Funds`” favors the non-sticky, release-state interpretation used here.

**TX-306.** A stricter interpretation could treat every amount change after review as material, making the EUR 10,000 update require a renewed review and perhaps a renewed approval. That reading conflicts with R4’s explicit greater-than-20% invalidation threshold. A recorded tolerance decision or explicit “review remains valid up to EUR X” field would remove the ambiguity.

**TX-308.** A policy that demanded re-review after every amount change would fail the case because the decrease occurred after review. R4 explicitly says a decrease does not by itself invalidate a review, so the supplied policy supports compliance.

**TX-307.** The result would change if reliable evidence established that an Enhanced Review actually occurred but was mislabeled, and that the 13:12 approval referenced that enhanced packet. The present event log and note instead establish only a Standard Review and standard-packet approval.

    df["_prev_amount"].notna()
    & (
        (df["_prev_amount"] < 100000)
        != (df["amount"] < 100000)
    )
)
large_amount_step = (
    df["_prev_amount"].notna()
    & (df["amount"] > 1.20 * df["_prev_amount"])
)
unknown_amount_step = amount_update & df["_prev_amount"].isna()

other_material_change = pd.Series(False, index=df.index)
for col in ["risk_level", "jurisdiction", "customer_type"]:

    actually_changed = (
        df[f"_prev_{col}"].isna()
        | df[col].ne(df[f"_prev_{col}"])
    )
    other_material_change |= has_update & actually_changed

    other_material_change
    | (
        amount_update
        & (crosses_100k | large_amount_step | unknown_amount_step)
    )
)

def stamp(row):
    if row is None:
        return pd.NA
    return (
        f"{row['time:timestamp'].isoformat()}"
        f"#{int(row['event_index'])}"
    )


def latest(rows, activity):
    matching = rows[rows["concept:name"].eq(activity)]
    return None if matching.empty else matching.iloc[-1]


def tri_and(left, right):
    if left is False or right is False:
        return False
    if left is True and right is True:
        return True
    return pd.NA


def tri_or(left, right):
    if left is True or right is True:
        return True
    if left is False and right is False:
        return False
    return pd.NA


def amount_invalidation_after(rows_through_release, review_row):
    """
    Compare each later amount with the amount captured at review.
    Returns:
      invalidated,
      unknown-baseline-change,
      first-invalidating-row,
      maximum-ratio.
    """
    if review_row is None:
        return False, False, None, pd.NA

    reviewed_amount = review_row["amount"]
    later_updates = rows_through_release[
        (
            rows_through_release["_case_pos"]
            > review_row["_case_pos"]
        )
        & rows_through_release["amount__update"].notna()
    ]

    if pd.isna(reviewed_amount):
        return False, not later_updates.empty, None, pd.NA

    ratios = later_updates["amount"] / float(reviewed_amount)
    invalid = later_updates[ratios > 1.20]
    first_invalid = None if invalid.empty else invalid.iloc[0]
    max_ratio = pd.NA if ratios.empty else float(ratios.max())

    return (
        not invalid.empty,
        False,
        first_invalid,
        max_ratio,
    )

The code deliberately does not assign the final narrative labels through a case-ID lookup. It derives coarse outcomes from reconstructed release state and event order. EX-88’s `DISPUTED` status is an evidential input derived from the supplied register; consequently, TX-310 becomes `REVIEW_REQUIRED` rather than being automatically accepted or rejected. TX-305 also becomes `REVIEW_REQUIRED` because its amount and the amount scope of its Enhanced Review are unknown.

The final `coarse_trace_checks` dataframe demonstrates the limitation of a control-flow check: it can determine that an approval follows an Enhanced Review or that a screen follows an update, but not whether the rule was activated, whether the review covered the current amount, or whether an exemption was genuinely valid.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** A trace abstraction may attach the final known amount to every event. That would incorrectly assign TX-305’s 7 April settlement amount to its 6 April release and review.

2. **Post-hoc exemption records.** TX-302 and TX-310 both contain a release followed by a later `Record_Exemption`. Control flow cannot distinguish EX-77’s verified pre-release signature from EX-88’s post-release certificate creation and interested-witness account.

3. **Material changes after review.** TX-306, TX-308, and TX-309 all contain an Enhanced Review followed by an amount update and release. Their outcomes differ because the update is respectively a 4.55% increase, a decrease, and a 22.88% increase.

4. **Conditional activation.** The same absence of an Enhanced Review may be irrelevant for a low-value DE corporate case but a serious failure for a high-value foreign, HIGH-risk, or PEP case. Plain activity order does not evaluate the release-state predicates.

5. **Missing data and three-valued reasoning.** A conventional conformance checker often treats an absent predicate value as false or filters the case out. R6 instead requires `UNKNOWN`, as shown by TX-305.

6. **Same-timestamp ordering.** Timestamp-only sorting cannot tell whether TX-311’s review preceded or followed the jurisdiction update. The `event_index` establishes that the review was first and the DE update second.

7. **Review subtype and packet semantics.** A Standard Review and an Enhanced Review are different controls. Similarly, a generic `Compliance_Approve` does not satisfy R2 when it approved only a standard packet, as in TX-307.

8. **Review-state lineage.** The existence of an Enhanced Review event does not reveal the amount, jurisdiction, risk, customer type, sanctions-list version, or evidence bundle reviewed. This is decisive for TX-305 and after mutable updates.

9. **External reference-data changes.** R3 depends on sanctions-list state and list-version timing, not only the transaction trace. A DFG over transaction activities cannot represent which jurisdiction was added to which list version.

10. **Exemption scope.** An exemption may apply only to a particular jurisdiction, customer type, amount ceiling, time interval, and rule. A trace containing `Record_Exemption` cannot establish any of those matches.

11. **Evidence quality and provenance.** Control flow treats a displayed timestamp, database insert, qualified signature, and witness assertion as equivalent data fields unless an additional evidence model is applied.

12. **Deactivation versus sticky obligations.** Release-state semantics can deactivate R1 when jurisdiction becomes DE. A control-flow model does not determine whether obligations are evaluated only at release or persist once activated.

These failures arise from the supplied mutable attributes, late evidence, conditional rules, and ordering semantics rather than from unusual trace structure. 

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

1. **Create an immutable release payload.** Every `Release_Funds` event should contain or reference a cryptographically hashed payload with amount, currency, jurisdiction, risk level, customer type, exemption ID, sanctions-list version, transaction version, and payment-instruction ID. Later settlement events should reference the same instruction ID and payload hash.

2. **Record the exact review snapshot.** Each Standard and Enhanced Review should contain:

   * reviewed amount and currency;
   * reviewed jurisdiction, risk, and customer type;
   * sanctions-list version;
   * exemption considered;
   * applicable rule version;
   * review scope and outcome;
   * immutable packet or document hash.

   This would determine whether TX-305’s Enhanced Review was genuinely amount-aware.

3. **Link approvals to reviews.** `Compliance_Approve` should reference the exact `review_id` and packet hash it approves. Add fields such as `approved_review_type`, `approval_scope`, and `review_version`. This prevents a standard-packet approval from being mistaken for approval of an Enhanced Review.

4. **Separate event, effective, record, and evidence timestamps.** Use explicit fields:

   * `event_time`;
   * `effective_from`;
   * `effective_to`;
   * `recorded_at`;
   * `ingested_at`;
   * `signed_at`;
   * `certificate_created_at`;
   * `source_system_time`.

   A displayed approval timestamp should not be stored in the same field as a qualified signature timestamp.

5. **Model the exemption lifecycle as events.** Log `Exemption_Requested`, `Exemption_Approved`, `Exemption_Effective`, `Exemption_Revoked`, `Exemption_Expired`, and `Exemption_Recorded`. Include approver identity and role, rules waived, amount floor and ceiling, jurisdiction and customer-type scope, signature hash, certificate serial, approval channel, and whether oral approval is permitted.

6. **Capture old and new values with provenance.** Every mutable-data event should include:

   * `old_value`;
   * `new_value`;
   * source record ID;
   * actor and source system;
   * change reason;
   * effective time;
   * correction/backfill indicator;
   * prior version and new version;
   * supporting evidence reference.

   This makes “increase from unknown” visibly different from an ordinary increase.

7. **Use stable total ordering.** Retain `event_index`, but supplement it with a globally unique event ID, monotonic source sequence, causal predecessor ID, and ingestion sequence. Reject or quarantine duplicate timestamp/index combinations.

8. **Version sanctions data explicitly.** A `Sanctions_Screen` should record the list name, list version, list effective time, jurisdictions/entities checked, match outcome, and result hash. A list update should identify added and removed jurisdictions and its effective version.

9. **Log rule evaluation at release.** Create an immutable `Compliance_Decision_Evaluated` record containing:

   * rule-set version;
   * release-state snapshot hash;
   * each rule’s trigger result: true, false, or unknown;
   * review IDs used;
   * exemption ID and validation result;
   * stale-screen result;
   * unresolved-data flags;
   * final decision and reason codes.

10. **Use preventive release controls.** Block or escalate release when:

    * a decisive release attribute is unknown;
    * the release payload is not immutable;
    * an Enhanced Review is stale or has unknown scope;
    * R2 approval is not linked to the relevant Enhanced Review;
    * a sanctions-list update has made the latest screen stale;
    * an exemption has disputed approval timing or incomplete scope.

11. **Preserve correction semantics.** A late record should explicitly state whether it is:

    * a late insertion of an earlier event;
    * a retroactive correction with an independently established effective time;
    * a newly created post-hoc decision; or
    * merely a displayed backdated field.

12. **Monitor data quality as a compliance control.** Report rates of unknown release amounts, reviews without immutable snapshots, approvals without review linkage, exemption records inserted after release, equal-timestamp events without stable ordering, and screens lacking list-version identifiers.
