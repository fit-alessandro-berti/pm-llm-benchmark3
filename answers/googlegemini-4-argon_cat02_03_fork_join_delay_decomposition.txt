# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let $c \in \{1, \dots, 12\}$ index the cases (F01 through F12) and $i \in \{A, B, C\}$ index the three parallel branches released at `fork_release`. For each case $c$, let the observed timestamps be:
- $t_{c,\text{arr}} = \text{arrival}_c$
- $t_{c,\text{fork}} = \text{fork\_release}_c$
- $t_{c,i,\text{start}} = i\text{\_start}_c$ and $t_{c,i,\text{end}} = i\text{\_end}_c$ for $i \in \{A, B, C\}$
- $t_{c,F,\text{start}} = \text{final\_start}_c$ and $t_{c,F,\text{end}} = \text{final\_end}_c$

### Stage and Branch Formulas
1. **Upstream elapsed time before fork release ($U_c$):**
   $$U_c = t_{c,\text{fork}} - t_{c,\text{arr}}$$
   *(Note: Because upstream preparation start/end timestamps are not logged separately, $U_c$ combines any upstream queueing and active preparation service.)*

2. **Branch-specific queue waiting ($Q_{c,i}$):**
   $$Q_{c,i} = t_{c,i,\text{start}} - t_{c,\text{fork}}$$
   *(Assumes each branch becomes eligible for execution immediately at $t_{c,\text{fork}}$.)*

3. **Branch active service ($S_{c,i}$):**
   $$S_{c,i} = t_{c,i,\text{end}} - t_{c,i,\text{start}}$$
   *(Assumes uninterrupted work between logged start and end.)*

4. **Branch-specific elapsed time to completion ($E_{c,i}$):**
   $$E_{c,i} = t_{c,i,\text{end}} - t_{c,\text{fork}} = Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i}$$

5. **Join readiness timestamp ($t_{c,\text{join}}$) and critical branch ($i^*_c$):**
   Under the strict AND-join rule, the parallel block is ready for downstream release only when all three branches have finished:
   $$t_{c,\text{join}} = \max_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} t_{c,i,\text{end}}$$
   The **critical branch** set is $C^*_c = \arg\max_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} t_{c,i,\text{end}} = \arg\max_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} (Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i})$.

6. **Elapsed fork-join stage time ($FJ_c$):**
   $$FJ_c = t_{c,\text{join}} - t_{c,\text{fork}} = \max_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} (Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i})$$

7. **Branch-specific synchronization waiting after completion ($W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}$):**
   $$W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i} = t_{c,\text{join}} - t_{c,i,\text{end}} = FJ_c - (Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i}) \ge 0$$
   By definition, for the critical branch $i^*_c$, $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i^*_c} = 0$.

8. **Downstream queueing before F ($Q_{c,F}$):**
   $$Q_{c,F} = t_{c,F,\text{start}} - t_{c,\text{join}}$$

9. **Final service ($S_{c,F}$):**
   $$S_{c,F} = t_{c,F,\text{end}} - t_{c,F,\text{start}}$$

10. **Total case flow time ($TFT_c$):**
    $$TFT_c = t_{c,F,\text{end}} - t_{c,\text{arr}}$$

### Avoiding Double Counting Across Parallel Branches
For every single branch $i \in \{A, B, C\}$, the elapsed time of the fork-join stage is identically partitioned into three mutually exclusive components:
$$FJ_c = Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i} \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C\}$$
Because branches $A$, $B$, and $C$ execute concurrently across the same calendar interval $[t_{c,\text{fork}}, t_{c,\text{join}}]$, summing queue times, service times, or synchronization waits across $i \in \{A, B, C\}$ adds parallel clock durations together:
$$\sum_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} \left(Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}\right) = 3 \cdot FJ_c$$
Specifically, synchronization waiting $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}$ is simply the idle slack of branch $i$ while another branch $i^*_c$ is actively in queue or in service. That exact calendar time is already accounted for in $Q_{c,i^*_c} + S_{c,i^*_c}$. Therefore, an exact, non-double-counted serial decomposition of total case flow time must follow either the max-envelope (critical path) or any single branch $i$'s timeline:
$$TFT_c = U_c + FJ_c + Q_{c,F} + S_{c,F} = U_c + \underbrace{\left(Q_{c,i^*_c} + S_{c,i^*_c}\right)}_{FJ_c \text{ (since } W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i^*_c}=0\text{)}} + Q_{c,F} + S_{c,F}$$

### Why Mean Service Time Misleads—and Which Branch Truly Controls Completion
1. **Service Time vs. Total Branch Elapsed Time:**
   If an analyst looked only at active service time, **Branch A** would appear dominant: its mean service time is $50.00\text{ min}$ (deterministic), compared to $37.50\text{ min}$ for B and $20.17\text{ min}$ for C. However, Branch A never queues ($Q_A = 0$ across all 12 cases), so its total branch elapsed time is fixed at $E_A = 50\text{ min}$. Conversely, **Branch B** appears to operate as a single-server resource that falls behind the 15-minute inter-fork release cadence: whenever $S_{c,B} \ge 35\text{ min}$, the next case's B-branch queues behind the previous case's B-completion ($B_{\text{start},c} = B_{\text{end},c-1}$ for F02–F07 and F09–F11). Consequently, $Q_B$ averages $19.17\text{ min}$ (and reaches $30\text{ min}$ in F06, F07, F12), pushing B's mean elapsed time to $56.67\text{ min}$.

2. **Influence Ranking on `join_ready` and Total Flow Time:**
   - **Rank 1 (Primary Controller of Variance and Tail): Branch B (Machine Stress Test).**
     Branch B is the last-completing branch in **8 of 12 cases (66.7%)**, has the lowest mean synchronization wait ($2.67\text{ min}$), and controls **100% of the tail cases** ($FJ_c \ge 65\text{ min}$: F03, F06, F07, F09, F12). Across all 12 cases, B's completion time exceeds the next-latest branch (always A) by a total of $120\text{ min}$ ($10.00\text{ min/case}$ on average, and $15\text{ min/case}$ across the 8 cases where B is critical). Furthermore, $FJ_c$ correlates strongly with $E_{c,B}$ ($r = 0.906$), whereas $E_{c,A}$ is constant ($SD = 0$).
   - **Rank 2 (Hard Structural Floor / Second-Place Shadow Bottleneck): Branch A (Laboratory Assay).**
     Although Branch A is strictly critical in only **2 of 12 cases (16.7%: F01, F11)**—tied in frequency with Branch C—it is substantially more influential than C because A is the **second-latest branch in the remaining 10 of 12 cases (83.3%)**. Branch A establishes a rigid **50-minute lower bound** on `fork_join_elapsed` ($FJ_c \ge 50\text{ min}$ for every case). Any improvement to Branch B or C hits a hard wall at $E_A = 50\text{ min}$, meaning A limits how much B-acceleration can ever reduce `join_ready`.
   - **Rank 3 (Episodic Queue Disruption): Branch C (Documentation Review).**
     Branch C has the shortest mean service ($20.17\text{ min}$) and highest synchronization wait ($30.42\text{ min}$), sitting idle for over half the fork-join stage on average. However, in **2 cases (16.7%: F04, F08)**, sudden administrative queue spikes ($Q_C = 35\text{ min}$ and $25\text{ min}$) push $E_C$ to $57\text{ min}$ and $55\text{ min}$, briefly overtaking A's 50-minute floor while B experiences its shortest service times ($30\text{ min}$). Thus, C and A are tied on critical-branch frequency (2 cases each) and nearly tied on unique critical margin ($15\text{ min}$ total for A vs. $12\text{ min}$ total for C), but A dominates C in structural influence because A is always within $11\text{ min}$ (mean $W^{\text{sync}}_A$) of criticality.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

### Modeling Assumptions for Counterfactual Evaluation
- **Assumption CF-1 (Direct / Local Case-Level Effect):** Each target parameter ($S_{c,A}$, $Q_{c,B}$, or $Q_{c,F}$) is scaled directly per the counterfactual prompt while holding other branches' timestamps and `downstream_queue` ($Q_{c,F}$) fixed (for CF1 and CF2) so that any reduction in $t_{c,\text{join}}$ translates 1-for-1 into an earlier $t_{c,F,\text{start}}$ and $t_{c,F,\text{end}}$.
- **Assumption CF-2 (Downstream Masking / Fixed Release Schedule Bounds):** In reality, `final_start` occurs exclusively on 5- or 10-minute clock boundaries and shows a constant 15–20 minute lag after `join_ready` even though resources F1 and F2 have idle capacity (see Section 7). If `final_start` is partially fixed by scheduled release slots or upstream batching, improvements to `join_ready` may simply convert into extra `downstream_queue` (downstream masking). We therefore distinguish the **Direct Unmasked Benefit** from the **Fully Masked Bound** ($0\text{ min}$ if `final_start` cannot move earlier).

### Detailed Evaluation of Each Counterfactual

#### 1. Counterfactual 1: Reducing Branch A Service Time by 20% ($S_{c,A}: 50 \to 40\text{ min}$)
- **Direct Benefits:** Reducing $S_{c,A}$ by $10\text{ min}$ shifts $E_{c,A}$ from $50\text{ min}$ to $40\text{ min}$ in every case. However, total case flow time improves by only **15.0 minutes across all 12 cases (mean $1.25\text{ min/case}$, or 1.09% of TFT)**:
  - **F01:** $E_A$ drops from $50$ to $40\text{ min}$, but $E_B = 45\text{ min}$ ($Q_B=5, S_B=40$) now becomes the bottleneck. Thus, $FJ_{\text{F01}}$ drops from $50$ to $45\text{ min}$ (**5 min saved**, capped by B; A and B do not tie here—B takes over).
  - **F11:** $E_A$ drops from $50$ to $40\text{ min}$, tying $E_B = 40\text{ min}$ ($Q_B=10, S_B=30$). Thus, $FJ_{\text{F11}}$ drops from $50$ to $40\text{ min}$ (**10 min saved**, resulting in an A=B tie).
  -*(Note: F01's $E_B=45$, so only F11 ties at 40; let's check if any other case ties: in all other 10 cases, $\max(E_B, E_C) \ge 55\text{ min}$, so A is strictly non-critical).*
- **Cases with Zero Benefit:** **10 of 12 cases (83.3%: F02–F10, F12)** experience **zero reduction** in `join_ready` or `total_flow_time`. In these 10 cases, Branch B or C finishes between $55$ and $75\text{ min}$ after `fork_release`. Finishing A 10 minutes earlier simply increases Branch A's synchronization wait $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,A}$ by $+10\text{ min}$ (raising mean $W^{\text{sync}}_A$ from $11.00$ to $19.75\text{ min}$).
- **Secondary Effects:** While standalone CF1 yields almost no flow-time reduction, it lowers the **synchronization floor** from $50\text{ min}$ to $40\text{ min}$. This unlocks latent capacity if Branch B is subsequently improved (as shown in CF4, where combining CF1+CF2 saves $122.5\text{ min}$—$22.5\text{ min}$ more than the sum of CF1 [$15\text{ min}$] and CF2 [$85\text{ min}$] alone, demonstrating super-additive synergy across parallel branches).

#### 2. Counterfactual 2: Reducing Branch B Queue Delay by 50% ($Q'_{c,B} = 0.5 \cdot Q_{c,B}$)
- **Direct Benefits:** In the 8 cases where B is critical, $Q_{c,B}$ ranges from $20$ to $30\text{ min}$, so cutting $Q_{c,B}$ by 50% reduces $E_{c,B}$ by $10$ to $15\text{ min}$. However, **Branch A's 50-minute floor caps the savings** whenever $E'_{c,B}$ drops below $50\text{ min}$:
  - **Uncapped full savings ($E'_{c,B} \ge 50\text{ min}$):**
    - **F02:** $Q_B: 20 \to 10 \implies E'_B = 50\text{ min}$ (ties $E_A = 50$). Saves **10.0 min**.
    - **F03:** $Q_B: 20 \to 10 \implies E'_B = 55\text{ min}$ ($> E_A = 50$). Saves **10.0 min**.
    - **F06:** $Q_B: 30 \to 15 \implies E'_B = 50\text{ min}$ (ties $E_A = 50$). Saves **15.0 min**.
    - **F07:** $Q_B: 30 \to 15 \implies E'_B = 60\text{ min}$ ($> E_A = 50$). Saves **15.0 min**.
    - **F09:** $Q_B: 25 \to 12.5 \implies E'_B = 52.5\text{ min}$ ($> E_A = 50$). Saves **12.5 min**.
    - **F12:** $Q_B: 30 \to 15 \implies E'_B = 60\text{ min}$ ($> E_A = 50$). Saves **15.0 min**.
  - **Capped by Branch A ($E'_{c,B} < 50\text{ min}$):**
    - **F05:** $Q_B: 25 \to 12.5 \implies E'_B = 47.5\text{ min}$, but $E_A = 50\text{ min}$ becomes critical! Saving is capped at $60 - 50 =$ **10.0 min** ($2.5\text{ min}$ of B's improvement is absorbed by A).
    - **F10:** $Q_B: 20 \to 10 \implies E'_B = 45.0\text{ min}$, but $E_A = 50\text{ min}$ becomes critical! Saving is capped at $55 - 50 =$ **5.0 min** ($5.0\text{ min}$ of B's improvement is absorbed by A).
  - **Aggregate Direct Benefit:** **85.0 minutes saved across 12 cases (mean $7.08\text{ min/case}$, max $15.0\text{ min}$)**, and the worst-case fork-join time drops from $75\text{ min}$ to $60\text{ min}$.
- **Cases with Zero Benefit:** **4 of 12 cases (33.3%: F01, F04, F08, F11)** gain **zero benefit** because B is already non-critical in those cases; reducing $Q_B$ merely increases $W^{\text{sync}}_B$.
- **Secondary Effects (Queue Compounding):** Because $Q_B$ is generated by serial congestion at station B across consecutive cases, any operational change that reduces B's queue (such as adding a second B machine or trimming $S_B$ slightly) prevents queue build-up from compounding across F02–F07 and F09–F12. However, once $E_B$ is brought down to $\le 50\text{ min}$, **Branch A immediately becomes the new bottleneck** in 6 of the 12 cases (4 strictly critical + 2 ties with B).

#### 3. Counterfactual 3: Adding Final-Station Capacity or Reducing Downstream Queueing ($Q_{c,F}$)
- **Scrutinizing Final-Station Utilisation Before Modeling Capacity:**
  Inspection of the `final_resource` schedule reveals a crucial empirical anomaly:
  - **F1 handles odd cases:** F01 (`09:08–09:28`), F03 (`10:00–10:20`), F05 (`10:20–10:40`), F07 (`11:10–11:30`), F09 (`11:30–11:50`), F11 (`11:50–12:10`).
  - **F2 handles even cases:** F02 (`09:40–10:00`), F04 (`10:10–10:30`), F06 (`10:45–11:05`), F08 (`11:05–11:25`), F10 (`11:40–12:00`), F12 (`12:20–12:40`).
  In **11 of the 12 cases**, at least one of the two final resources (F1 or F2)—and in 7 cases, *both* F1 and F2—is completely idle at the exact moment `join_ready` is reached! The *only* case where both F1 and F2 are busy at `join_ready` is **F04** (`join_ready` = `09:52`, while F2 is busy on F02 until `10:00` and F1 is idle from `09:28` to `10:00`, though F1 starts F03 at `10:00`). Even for F04, F2 becomes free at `10:00`, yet `final_start` does not occur until `10:10`.
- **Direct Benefits Under Two Interpretations of CF3:**
  1. **Pure Capacity Addition (Adding Resource F3 without changing release/handoff policy):** If $Q_{c,F}$ is caused by a mandatory transfer delay, batch review window, or pre-assigned resource slotting rather than physical server unavailability, adding a third resource F3 yields **0 minutes saved** across all 12 cases.
  2. **Reducing Downstream Delay $Q_{c,F}$ by 50% (CF3a) or 100% (CF3b) via Handoff/Release Redesign:** Because final station F is **downstream of the AND-join**, any reduction in $Q_{c,F}$ bypasses parallel synchronization masking completely. Every minute removed from $Q_{c,F}$ reduces `total_flow_time` by a full minute across **all 12 cases (0 cases with zero benefit)**:
     - **CF3a (50% reduction in $Q_{c,F}$):** Saves **88.0 minutes total (mean $7.33\text{ min/case}$, range $4.0\text{ to }10.0\text{ min}$)**.
     - **CF3b (100% elimination of $Q_{c,F}$):** Saves **176.0 minutes total (mean $14.67\text{ min/case}$, range $8.0\text{ to }20.0\text{ min}$)**.
- **Secondary Effects (Downstream Masking of Branch Improvements):**
  Conversely, if $Q_{c,F}$ were caused by genuine downstream congestion or rigid clock-slot scheduling at F, earlier `join_ready` times from CF1/CF2 could be **masked** by increased waiting before F. In fact, in the baseline log, F04 (`join_ready` = `09:52`, `final_end` of F02 on F2 = `10:00`), F05 (`join_ready` = `10:10`, `final_end` of F03 on F1 = `10:20`), F08 (`join_ready` = `10:50`, `final_end` of F06 on F2 = `11:05`), F09 (`join_ready` = `11:15`, `final_end` of F07 on F1 = `11:30`), and F11 (`join_ready` = `11:30`, `final_end` of F09 on F1 = `11:50`) all reach `join_ready` **before** their assigned resource (F1 for odd, F2 for even) finishes the preceding case on that same resource! Specifically:
  - Under strict odd$\to$F1 / even$\to$F2 dedicated resource assignment, the previous case on the same resource finishes at `10:00` (for F04), `10:20` (for F05), `11:05` (for F08), `11:30` (for F09), and `11:50` (for F11). Notice that for **F05, F08, F09, and F11**, `final_start` (`10:20`, `11:05`, `11:30`, `11:50`) equals the **exact completion minute of the prior case on that dedicated resource**!
  - This is a major structural insight: if F03, F06, F07, and F09 finish earlier (via CF2), then F1 and F2 are freed earlier for F05, F08, F09, and F11, which *prevents* downstream blocking and allows CF2's branch savings to propagate through to `final_end`.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### Redesign 1: De-bottleneck Branch B (Machine Stress Test) via Parallel Capacity or Setup/Cycle Reduction
- **Intervention:** Reduce Branch B effective cycle time below the 30-minute threshold (e.g., by offloading pre-test calibration, automating stress-test setup to trim $S_B$ by 20–25%, or adding a second Stress Test rig during peak hours `08:30–12:00`).
- **Expected Impact:**
  - **Throughput & Queueing:** In the observed log, cases release every $15\text{ min}$ (`08:10` to `10:55`), while $S_B$ averages $37.5\text{ min}$. A single B machine has an implied traffic intensity $\rho_B = 37.5 / 15 = 2.50 > 1$ (or $\rho_B = 37.5 / 30 = 1.25$ if two B machines alternate odd/even cases), causing $Q_B$ to ratchet upward to $30\text{ min}$. Bringing B's effective processing rate in line with the 15-minute release cadence eliminates $Q_B$ growth.
  - **Flow-Time Tail:** Directly attacks the worst-case cases (F07 and F12 at $130$ and $125\text{ min}$ total flow time, where $FJ_c = 75\text{ min}$), compressing maximum `fork_join_elapsed` from $75\text{ min}$ down to the 50-minute floor set by Branch A (and $55–57\text{ min}$ in C-spike cases).
  - **Resource Utilization:** Lowers acute over-utilization on B and smooths downstream arrivals at Station F.
  - **Synchronization Effects & New Bottlenecks:** Immediately shifts the critical path onto **Branch A (50-min constant floor)** in ~75% of cases and exposes **Branch C's episodic queue spikes** (F04, F08) in the remaining cases. Branch A's mean synchronization wait drops from $11.0\text{ min}$ to near $0\text{ min}$, while B's synchronization wait rises from $2.67\text{ min}$ to $\sim 10\text{ min}$.
- **What to Pilot / Measure First:** Log resource IDs and sub-steps (setup vs. active machine run vs. teardown) at Branch B for 2 weeks to verify whether $Q_B$ is driven by single-machine capacity starvation or operator unavailability.

### Redesign 2: Replace Dedicated Odd/Even Final-Station Slotting with a Pooled Pull Queue & Immediate Handoff
- **Intervention:** Eliminate the rigid $8–20\text{ min}$ lag between `join_ready` and `final_start` ($Q_{c,F}$, averaging $14.67\text{ min/case}$) by (a) pooling F1 and F2 into a single first-ready, first-served queue rather than dedicating F1 to odd cases and F2 to even cases, and (b) automating the `join_ready` notification trigger so Station F begins immediately when all three branches complete.
- **Expected Impact:**
  - **Throughput & Mean Flow Time:** Because Station F service takes $S_F = 20\text{ min}$ and there are 2 servers (F1, F2), pooled station capacity is one case every $10\text{ min}$ ($\rho_F = 20 / (2 \times 15) = 66.7\%$). Under pooled immediate dispatch, a server is free at the exact instant of `join_ready` for **all 12 cases**, reducing $Q_{c,F}$ from a mean of $14.67\text{ min}$ to **$0.00\text{ min}$** and cutting mean total flow time from $115.25\text{ min}$ to **$100.58\text{ min}$** (a 12.7% reduction affecting 100% of cases).
  - **Flow-Time Tail:** Reduces every case's flow time by $8$ to $20\text{ min}$ regardless of which parallel branch is critical, lowering the 90th-percentile and maximum flow times by $10–15\text{ min}$.
  - **Resource Utilization:** F1 and F2 active utilization remains at $120\text{ min}$ of work over the $\sim 210\text{ min}$ active window ($\sim 57\%$), but eliminates wasted idle gaps where F1 sits idle (`09:28–10:00` and `10:40–11:10`) while even cases F02/F04/F06 wait for F2, or where both F1 and F2 sit idle after `join_ready` (e.g., F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F10, F12).
  - **Synchronization Effects & New Bottlenecks:** Does not alter internal fork-join synchronization waits ($W^{\text{sync}}_i$), but ensures that any upstream gains from Redesign 1 (Branch B) propagate 1-for-1 into final completion rather than being absorbed by rigid downstream slotting.
- **What to Pilot / Measure First:** Audit the transition between `join_ready` and `final_start` to determine how much of $Q_{c,F}$ is caused by dedicated F1/F2 case assignment (which accounts for exact start times on F05, F08, F09, F11), batch polling intervals, or unlogged physical transport/dossier compilation.

]
for col in time_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime(base_date + df[col])

# ==============================================================================
# 2. DELAY DECOMPOSITION & DETERMINISTIC CRITICAL-BRANCH IDENTIFICATION
# ==============================================================================
# NOTE ON PM4PY VS CUSTOM PANDAS FOR FORK-JOIN SYNCHRONIZATION:
# Standard directly-follows graph (DFG) performance discovery in PM4Py sequences
# events by timestamp within a case (e.g., C_end -> A_end -> B_end -> final_start)
# and computes inter-event lags between consecutive logged completions. That
# conflates parallel sibling completions with serial transitions and cannot
# recover common fork_release queues (i_start - fork_release), max-join readiness
# (max(A_end, B_end, C_end)), or per-branch synchronization waiting
# (join_ready - i_end). Hence, fork-join decomposition is computed explicitly in
# pandas below, while PM4Py is used for activity service times and interval logs.

def identify_critical_branches(row, col_map):
    """Deterministic tie-aware critical branch identifier."""
    vals = {b: row[col_map[b]] for b in ["A", "B", "C"]}
    max_val = max(vals.values())
    winners = [b for b in ["A", "B", "C"] if np.isclose(vals[b], max_val)]
    return "+".join(winners)

df["upstream_elapsed"] = (df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

for b in ["A", "B", "C"]:
    df[f"{b}_queue"] = (df[f"{b}_start"] - df["fork_release"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df[f"{b}_service"] = (df[f"{b}_end"] - df[f"{b}_start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df[f"{b}_elapsed"] = df[f"{b}_queue"] + df[f"{b}_service"]

df["join_ready"] = df[["A_end", "B_end", "C_end"]].max(axis=1)
df["fork_join_elapsed"] = (df["join_ready"] - df["fork_release"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

for b in ["A", "B", "C"]:
    df[f"{b}_sync"] = (df["join_ready"] - df[f"{b}_end"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

df["critical_branch"] = df.apply(
    identify_critical_branches, col_map={"A": "A_elapsed", "B": "B_elapsed", "C": "C_elapsed"}, axis=1
)
df["downstream_queue"] = (df["final_start"] - df["join_ready"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
df["final_service"] = (df["final_end"] - df["final_start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
df["total_flow_time"] = (df["final_end"] - df["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# Verify exact additive identity without double counting:
assert np.allclose(
    df["total_flow_time"],
    df["upstream_elapsed"] + df["fork_join_elapsed"] + df["downstream_queue"] + df["final_service"]
), "Decomposition identity failed!"

print("=== 1. CASE-LEVEL DECOMPOSITION TABLE (MINUTES) ===")
display_cols = [

    "B_queue", "B_service", "B_sync", "C_queue", "C_service", "C_sync",
    "join_ready_str", "fork_join_elapsed", "critical_branch",
    "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"
]
df["join_ready_str"] = df["join_ready"].dt.strftime("%H:%M")
print(df[display_cols].to_string(index=False))

# ==============================================================================
# 3. AGGREGATE CRITICAL-PATH SUMMARY TABLE
# ==============================================================================
summary_rows = []
for b in ["A", "B", "C"]:

    summary_rows.append({

        "Mean_Elapsed": df[f"{b}_elapsed"].mean(),

    })
summary_df = pd.DataFrame(summary_rows)
print("\n=== 2. AGGREGATE BRANCH COMPARISON ===")
print(summary_df.to_string(index=False))

# ==============================================================================
# 4. COUNTERFACTUAL REDESIGN ANALYSIS
# ==============================================================================
def evaluate_counterfactual(df_base, name, a_serv_mult=1.0, b_queue_mult=1.0, qf_mult=1.0):
    cf = pd.DataFrame()
    cf["case"] = df_base["case"]
    cf["A_el"] = df_base["A_queue"] + df_base["A_service"] * a_serv_mult
    cf["B_el"] = df_base["B_queue"] * b_queue_mult + df_base["B_service"]
    cf["C_el"] = df_base["C_elapsed"]
    cf["FJ_new"] = cf[["A_el", "B_el", "C_el"]].max(axis=1)
    cf["crit_new"] = cf.apply(
        identify_critical_branches, col_map={"A": "A_el", "B": "B_el", "C": "C_el"}, axis=1
    )
    cf["QF_new"] = df_base["downstream_queue"] * qf_mult
    cf["TFT_new"] = df_base["upstream_elapsed"] + cf["FJ_new"] + cf["QF_new"] + df_base["final_service"]
    cf["FJ_saved"] = df_base["fork_join_elapsed"] - cf["FJ_new"]
    cf["TFT_saved"] = df_base["total_flow_time"] - cf["TFT_new"]
    cf["scenario"] = name
    return cf

cf1 = evaluate_counterfactual(df, "CF1: -20% A_service", a_serv_mult=0.80)
cf2 = evaluate_counterfactual(df, "CF2: -50% B_queue", b_queue_mult=0.50)
cf3a = evaluate_counterfactual(df, "CF3a: -50% downstream_queue", qf_mult=0.50)
cf3b = evaluate_counterfactual(df, "CF3b: -100% downstream_queue", qf_mult=0.00)

# 7. Limits of inference

1. **Unlogged Branch Resource Identities and Capacities:**
   Unlike Station F (which logs `F1` and `F2`), Branches A, B, and C do not log resource identifiers. We can *hypothesize* from non-overlapping intervals that Branch B acts like a single server (since $B_{\text{start},c} = B_{\text{end},c-1}$ in 8 of 11 transitions) and that Branch A has at least 4 parallel servers or batch capacity (since up to 4 cases overlap simultaneously in `A_start`–`A_end` with zero queue delay), while C's spikes in F04 (`Q_C = 35`) and F08 (`Q_C = 25`) could reflect a single administrative reviewer taking breaks or handling non-logged external work. However, without resource IDs and availability calendars, we cannot distinguish capacity starvation from staff batching or external interrupts.

2. **Work Preemption, Batching, and Active vs. Elapsed Service:**
   The decomposition defines $S_{c,i} = t_{c,i,\text{end}} - t_{c,i,\text{start}}$ as active service. If an operator starts a branch, pauses it to work on another task (preemption), or leaves a completed test waiting to be signed off before logging `end`, that idle time is invisibly embedded inside $S_{c,i}$. In particular, Branch A's deterministic $50\text{ min}$ duration across all 12 cases strongly suggests an automated timer/assay cycle or standardized time-box rather than manual variable work.

3. **True Queueing vs. Release Policy at the Final Station ($Q_{c,F}$):**
   The $8$ to $20\text{ min}$ gap `final_start - join_ready` cannot be equated with classic resource-contention queueing without further evidence. Schedule reconstruction shows that in 11 of 12 cases, at least one of F1 or F2 is completely idle at `join_ready`, yet work does not start. Simultaneously, odd cases exclusively use F1 and even cases exclusively use F2, and in 4 cases (F05, F08, F09, F11) `final_start` matches the exact minute the assigned dedicated server finishes its prior case. In the remaining cases, `final_start` occurs on clean 5- or 10-minute boundaries (`09:08` excepted) 10–20 minutes after `join_ready`. Thus, $Q_{c,F}$ is an observational mixture of (a) dedicated odd/even server blocking, (b) unlogged handoff/preparation delay after `join_ready`, and/or (c) scheduled release windows.

4. **Observational vs. Causal Counterfactuals (Queue Propagation & Inter-Case Coupling):**
   Because cases share resources across time, reducing $S_B$ or $Q_B$ in case $c$ alters when the B-resource becomes free for case $c+1$, and shifting $t_{c,\text{join}}$ earlier alters when F1/F2 finish case $c$ and become available for case $c+2$. Without a validated structural queueing model (knowing exact server counts, dispatch rules, and external arrivals), counterfactual estimates must be interpreted as bounded local projections rather than guaranteed causal equilibria.
