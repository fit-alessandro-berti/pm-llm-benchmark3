## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model  

**Hard invariants**  
1. *Ordercreation uniqueness*: For each distinct `external_ref` appearing in an `Create Order` event, there must be exactly one `Order` with that `external_ref`.  
2. *Invoiceorder billing*: An `Issue Invoice` event ties exactly one `Invoice` to one or more `Order`s that share the same `customer` and whose aggregated `declared_value` is consistent with the `total` amount.  

**Strong defaults**  
3. *Itemorder containment*: Each `Add Item` event links one `Order` to one `Item`.  
4. *Packageitem packing*: Each `Pack Item` event relates one `Item` to one `Package`.  
5. *Deliverypackage dispatch*: Each `Dispatch Package` event links one `Package` to one `Delivery` (or a bulk dispatch may link one `Delivery` to multiple packages).  
6. *Invoicepayment settlement*: Each `Record Payment` event ties exactly one `Payment` to the `Invoice` that received it or to the sum of multiple invoices for the same customer.  

**Weak heuristics**  
7. *Timestamp proximity*: Events occurring within 5minutes on the same order are likely related (e.g., sequential `Add Item`s).  
8. *Identifier prefix matching*: Events sharing a prefix in their `external_ref` or `scanner_ref` suggest they belong to the same transaction thread.  
9. *Route alignment*: Packages dispatched on the same `route_hint` often share the same `Delivery`.  
10. *Amount consistency*: If two `Payment` amounts sum to an invoice total, it is plausible they settle that invoice, though not conclusive.  

**Evidence against a repair**  
11. *Missing carrier reference*: `ev10` lacks a carrier reference; asserting a direct link without corroborating evidence risks overfitting.  

**Combining evidence**  
- **Identifier similarity** (78) guides tentative linkages but is overridden by hard invariants.  
- **Time windows** (7) support plausible sequencing but do not force a relation if an invariant contradicts it.  
- **Amounts** (10) serve as corroborative, not decisive, evidence for invoicepayment linkage.  
- **Customer & route** (9) help align disparate events (e.g., linking `ORDA` to `DELX`).  
- **Lifecycle order** (hard invariants) is used to prune impossible configurations (e.g., an `Issue Invoice` cannot precede its related `Create Order`).  
- **Cardinality** checks are applied after heuristic suggestions to ensure no manytomany violations emerge.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles  

### Bundle A – Conservative / MinimumChange Repair  
- **ev08 & PK8**: Keep **R7** *provisional* (do **not** add; let `ITB1` remain unlinked to a package, assuming it will be reconciled later). This respects the weak heuristic without forcing a dubious relation.  
- **ev10 & DELX/DELY**: Add **R9** *provisional* linking `PK8` to a **new** `Delivery` object (e.g., `DELY`) whose `carrier_ref` is inferred from the missing value. This satisfies the dispatchpackage invariant while acknowledging uncertainty about the carrier reference.  
- **ev11 & delivery objects**: Accept **R10** linking `ORDA` to delivery `DELX` (customer alignment is strong). Do **not** link `ORDB` to any delivery; keep **R12** *unresolved*.  
- **INV44 & ORDB**: Keep **R11** (invoice bills `ORDA`) and **R12** unresolved. The invoice total (230) covers both orders, but we do not split the billing because the event attributes only mention `ORDA`.  
- **PAY2 & INV44**: Accept **R15** and **R16** (both high confidence).  

**Result**: The minimum set of additions respects strong defaults, acknowledges weak heuristics where evidence is marginal, and leaves only one ambiguity (whether `ORDB` is billed) unresolved.

### Bundle B – Plausible Alternative Interpretation  
- **ev08 & PK8**: **Accept** **R7** (full) – explicitly link `ITB1` to `PK8`. This assumes the scanner recorded the item correctly despite the missing event attribute, consistent with a possible reconciliation step.  
- **ev10 & DELX/DELY**: **Accept** **R9** *accept* (no provisional flag) linking `PK8` to **DELX** *instead* of creating a new `Delivery`. The reasoning: the timestamp of `ev10` coincides with the earlier dispatch of `PK7` to `DELX`; thus it is plausible both packages were dispatched in the same batch, even without a carrier reference.  
- **ev11 & delivery objects**: **Reject** **R10** *alternative* and add a new **R10alt**: link `ORDA` (via `portal_customer=C17`) to **DELY** using the `route=NORTH` and the missing carrier (`N-421`) inferred from the partial `external_ref=N-421?` pattern. This yields two deliveries for the same route, respecting that `ev11` may have lost the carrier ID.  
- **INV44 & ORDB**: **Accept** **R12** with **confidence 0.6** (partial billing) – assume the invoice partially covers both orders (e.g., a combined order). Thus `INV44` now has **two O2O edges**: to `ORDA` and to `ORDB`.  
- **PAY2 & INV44**: **Accept** **R15** and **R16** unchanged.  

**Result**: This bundle embraces a more active reconciliation of ambiguous evidence, producing a richer yet still plausible lifecycle with two deliveries and a shared invoice across both orders.

## 8. Overrepair warnings  

1. **Assuming a missing carrier reference** (e.g., `ev10` linking to `DELY`) may artificially create a delivery that never physically existed, inflating delivery statistics and misleading route analysis.  
2. **Full invoice billing of both orders** (strongly asserting `INV44` bills `ORDB`) can mask a genuine separation of charges, affecting downstream costallocation models.  
3. **Provisional itemtopackage link for `ITB1`** may introduce spurious weight considerations; later reconciliation could shift package grossweight calculations, impacting logistics optimization.  
4. **Treating `ev11` as delivery confirmation only for `ORDA`** (customer alignment) might hide the fact that the portal deliberately omitted the carrier ID, leading to incorrect attribution of delivery success metrics.  
5. **Merging two separate delivery paths into a single `DELX` for BundleB** could cause doublecounting in deliveryperformance dashboards (e.g., ontime delivery ratios) because both packages would appear as delivered under the same carrier, distorting latency analysis.  

These warnings advise exercising caution: keep provisional flags, monitor model outputs for anomalies, and validate reparations with domain experts or additional data sources before using them in critical decisionsupport systems.
