## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Admissibility requires information that was available when the first Coverage Check Completed event was observed, using the feature version then in force. An earlier event timestamp is insufficient if the record was received later or overwritten afterward. A later offline calculation can be valid if it reconstructs exactly what was knowable at t0. Events ordered after the coverage event are outside the prefix even if they share its timestamp.

Severity reflects the threat to the current explanation: critical means direct future-information contamination; high means an unresolved availability or lineage defect; medium means interpretation, fairness, or generalization concerns; low means no identified temporal defect. These are audit priorities, not measures of causal influence.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Reconstruct the prediction boundary and available prefix.** For each case, identify the first Coverage Check Completed event using reliable occurrence/order information. Materialize exactly the records available immediately afterward. Track event time, source availability time, version interval, and a reliable order key. Exclude records received after t0 even if backdated. With equal timestamps, include only events proven to precede or coincide with the prediction boundary in the correct sequence. Reject ambiguous required inputs rather than assigning them a convenient order. Test timestamp units, time zones, clock discrepancies, and duplicate events.

2. **Enforce point-in-time feature contracts.** Each input should declare its source, entity keys, aggregation window, availability rule, mutable-version rule, and missing-value behavior. Require every component of an aggregate to satisfy that contract. Validate backward queue joins, claimant-history cutoffs, current-claim exclusion, initial-value versions, and resource joins. A model-generated risk score must pass the same checks recursively for its own inputs. Offline reconstruction is permissible only from historical facts that could have been known then; filtering current warehouse rows by event date is insufficient.

3. **Detect suffix dependence.** Recompute all event-derived inputs from truncated prefixes, including counts, variants, patterns, and resource summaries. Use a suffix-invariance test: modifying, extending, or replacing events after an unchanged available prefix must not alter its features, prediction, or explanation. Changing extraction date must not silently change a historical feature. Repeated requests, appeals, later decisions, and closure should be explicit challenge cases. All five reported positive pattern occurrences must be absent from the supplied prefixes. A zero count is an observed prefix fact, not evidence that later work will never occur.

4. **Validate state and activity order against the normative process.** Encode coverage before the evidence/assessment stage; request–receipt–validation cycles with permitted renewed requests; an assessment before the initial settlement decision; a trigger before fraud opening; appeal only after Partial or Deny; and closure after a final decision or withdrawal. Permit fraud work to overlap evidence collection or assessment. Use a state machine, Petri-net/BPMN alignment, or declarative constraints to check these obligations and produce a localized diagnostic. At this t0, document requesting or fast-track assessment can be enabled, subject to branch eligibility. Fraud opening is conditionally possible if its trigger is satisfied. Decisions, appeals, and closure are future-possible but not enabled in the stated state. Assessment after genuine closure is impossible under this model. A forecast about a future activity must be labeled as a forecast, never described as an observed driver.

5. **Handle ties and concurrency explicitly.** Preserve a partial order where equal-timestamp order is unknown; consider all orders compatible with reliable evidence and process constraints. Distinguish a definitely conformant trace, a definite violation, and unresolved feasibility. For Z, reversing the tied pair is not proof of correctness: assessment, decision, and closure must all fit the full lifecycle. Seek a reliable source sequence or transaction/audit record; an arbitrary ingestion order is not necessarily occurrence order. Do not infer the “correct” order from the breach label or the model contribution. Later tied events remain inadmissible at t0 even if their retrospective order is recovered.

6. **Validate decision cycles semantically.** Store decision outcomes and link appeals to the decision being challenged. A second Settlement Decision Recorded event is legitimate after an eligible appeal; it need not imply a second damage assessment. A repeated decision without that link needs investigation, not automatic acceptance or rejection based on the coarse label alone. A shortened variant containing DEC–APP–DEC is insufficient to certify that the first decision was eligible for appeal.

Declare whether sequence patterns mean directly-follows, eventually-follows, or a projection onto selected activities. Concurrent investigations can interrupt raw adjacency without violating a legitimate evidence sequence. Prefix replay identifies what is currently enabled; alignment identifies departures from the reference model but cannot recover missing history. Footprints and declarative checks provide useful order and prerequisite diagnostics. A DFG describes observed adjacency and frequency, including logging errors; a frequent edge does not authorize a normative transition. None of these checks establishes causality or proves that an apparently compliant feature was available at t0.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

The classes overlap: a feature can be a sound predictor and still require fairness review. Availability is a necessary condition for all classes; predictive usefulness must then be demonstrated with leak-free validation.

| Class | Surviving or repairable signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | Frozen initial damage complexity; a verified initial reserve; repaired prior-claim history; a verified historical risk score with clean upstream inputs | These available measurements may improve prediction of later breach. Describe the learned association, uncertainty, and validation population. | Complexity, reserve, history, or a score is not a demonstrated cause merely because it receives attribution. Validity of the historical input does not guarantee useful importance after refitting. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | Prefix age and coverage-stage duration; a verified backward queue snapshot; historical team/role and experience; prefix state and prefix-only counts | They describe work already observed, elapsed slack, resource context, or congestion. Under a registration-based target, shorter elapsed age leaves more nominal slack. A queue can identify a plausible capacity concern. | The prefix does not reveal which future branch will occur. Queue length does not prove that removing a specified number of queued cases will prevent breach. Team attribution does not establish team performance or individual responsibility. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | Region; verified channel; potentially reserve, history, and routing/resource variables | They may be retained as predictors when their incremental performance, group behavior, and intended use are acceptable. Examine mechanisms, correlations, and population changes. | Region or channel is not itself a demonstrated delay mechanism. A proxy should not automatically become a reason to disadvantage a claimant, nor should all correlated variables automatically be deleted. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | Calendar week; available holiday schedules and the policy in force at t0 | A time variable can summarize a recurring pattern or a particular operational regime, subject to validation across periods. Separating policy and holiday indicators can improve interpretation when their effects are identifiable. | Week 52 is not intrinsically delay-producing. Strong alignment may prevent separating policy from holiday effects; calendar attribution does not identify a unique mechanism. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | Current risk score, nightly document count, extraction-time experience, unverified channel/reserve, current SQL history count, and unverified queue matches | State the missing evidence and specify an as-of replacement. Properly repaired versions may enter the preceding classes after validation. | Names such as “initial,” “before_t0,” or “snapshot” do not establish availability. Final handler, last complete-case role, and complete-trace summaries cannot be rescued merely by relabeling them as early features. |

For the supplied cases, completed coverage and age are legitimate prefix signals. The request count is zero and the coarse prefix variant is shared, so neither establishes observed rework or a distinguishing future path. A document count reconstructed from uploads known at t0 could describe initial evidence availability, but “more documents reduce uncertainty” is not established: count does not measure relevance, completeness, or validity. Any discrepancy between upload records and modeled document-receipt events needs a semantic explanation.

Predictive validity asks whether available information improves generalization and calibration. Causal interpretation asks what would happen under an intervention, requiring a defensible causal design and assumptions beyond attribution. Operational actionability additionally requires a feasible intervention, adequate timing, and evidence that its benefits justify its costs; even a causal factor may be impossible to change for this claim. Queue management is a candidate for operational evaluation, while region is contextual information rather than an intervention. Fairness review asks whether errors, service consequences, and proxy use are acceptable across relevant groups. Evaluate group calibration and error rates with uncertainty, network/channel differences, and potentially unequal outcome generation. Removing a sensitive proxy alone does not resolve these questions.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately mark the existing early explanations as unvalidated and establish the prediction/label contract.** Inventory every model input, not only the most important ones. Separate predictors from outcome tables. Specify the ten-day clock, its start, calendar versus business days, the settlement endpoint when decisions repeat, and treatment of withdrawal, open cases, and incomplete follow-up. These choices must be consistent across training, validation, and operational evaluation. An appeal's relationship to the label can depend on this endpoint definition.

2. **Create a historical feature store with enforceable availability rules.** Store immutable events, reliable order identifiers, historical assignments and mutable feature versions, event/effective time, and source availability time. Define entity keys, current-claim exclusion, deduplication, model versions for derived scores, and permissible backward staleness. Use bitemporal/as-of joins where necessary. Prohibit unrestricted extraction-date joins and future snapshots. Missing history must result in exclusion or a defensible missing-value treatment, not substitution of today's value. Historical facts reconstructed later must remain distinguishable from facts first learned later.

3. **Rebuild training and evaluation from prefix-only data and refit.** Implement the t0 boundary consistently for features, patterns, and explanations. Repair history SQL, queue joins, documents, reserves, resources, and scores; exclude complete-trace variables. Check suffix invariance and training–serving equivalence on representative cases. Train a new model and fit its explanation background from admissible inputs. Do not remove SHAP terms from the existing fit and assume its remaining parameters or probabilities are valid. Use temporally ordered evaluation with mature labels and prevent the same case or inappropriate overlapping claimant history from crossing folds.

4. **Make process and temporal conformance part of routine validation.** Test enabled activities, appeal outcome prerequisites, legitimate repeated decisions, evidence loops, concurrent investigations, closure ordering, tied timestamps, late-arriving records, and overwritten fields. Include conformant, violating, and genuinely ambiguous examples. Keep DFG diagnostics separate from normative constraints. Automatically flag explanations that cite an unobserved event as evidence or assert an impossible current state. Investigate source defects rather than silently sorting them away.

5. **Reassess discrimination, calibration, and explanation behavior after repair.** Measure predictive performance, probability calibration, and decision-threshold behavior on untouched temporal holdouts and relevant subgroups. Recalibrate with separate admissible data when needed; report uncertainty and compare against simple complexity/age/congestion baselines. Verify the explainer's output scale and additivity. Test explanation stability across refits, resampling, reasonable background sets, and correlated-feature substitutions. Consider grouped attributions where individual correlated-variable credit is unstable. Confirm that a stable numerical explanation also uses valid historical data.

6. **Review proxies and proposed actions separately.** Evaluate region, channel, history, reserve, and routing features for incremental predictive value, group calibration/error differences, operational consequences, and sensitivity to network and policy changes. Document the retention rationale and unresolved uncertainty. Prefer explanations tied to available measurements of service conditions where justified, without assuming that controlling for a correlated variable removes unfairness. Evaluate staffing, routing, or document-support interventions with a credible experimental or causal design before promising that they reduce breaches.

7. **Monitor both the data pipeline and the process.** Track missing historical versions, forward queue matches, snapshot age, ingestion delays, timestamp ties, feature-definition changes, and unexplained prefix-count changes. Monitor new activities, routing rules, appeal/decision semantics, calendar/policy regimes, subgroup performance, calibration, and explanation distributions after outcomes mature. Version the process model, feature definitions, and prediction landmark. Set review triggers for schema or process changes rather than continuing to present old importance rankings as stable operational reasons.
