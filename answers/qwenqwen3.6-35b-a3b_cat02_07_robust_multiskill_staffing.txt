# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Assumptions**
- *Service-time generation*: Ordinary and complex-tail ranges are treated as independent uniform distributions. When a case is classified as complex-tail (probability given per activity), the sampled ordinary duration is multiplied by 1.25. This yields conservative upper bounds while preserving median realism.
- *Resource speed*: The supplied multipliers are applied multiplicatively to the sampled service time. Higher values denote slower execution. Resource availability follows the stated shifts and fixed break windows. No unplanned absenteeism beyond U3.
- *Routing & parallelism*: ME/ES cases are routed conditionally. Parallel execution is triggered only when (a) both branch resources are simultaneously available, (b) the estimated critical-path duration (max of branch durations) plus 8-minute coordination is less than a threshold (default 55 min), and (c) neither resource exceeds 75% planned utilization. Otherwise, branches execute sequentially on a single cross-skilled resource or are queued separately.
- *Preemption*: Only P1 cases may preempt P2/P3 diagnostic work. An interruption incurs a restart penalty equal to 10% of the remaining active service time. Each case is preempted at most once. Preemption is modeled as a hard interrupt with immediate requeue of the interrupted activity.
- *Handover/Setup*: Changing resource mid-activity adds 7 min. Switching between mechanical/electrical/software families adds 5 min unless retained by a cross-skilled resource. These are additive to service time and counted toward utilization.
- *Overtime & Fairness*: Overtime is capped at 30 min/resource/day. Planned utilization >90% is flagged as operationally fragile and excluded from recommended operating points.

**Decision Variables**
- Routing policy parameters: specialization ratio, pooling degree, P1 reservation level, parallelization threshold, preemption aggressiveness, and overtime trigger.
- Dispatch rules: FCFS vs. welfare-weighted priority vs. shortest-expected-remaining-processing-time (SERPT) for non-P1 cases.

**Objective Formulations**
1. *SLA-Risk Objective*: Minimize $J_1 = w_1 \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{P1\_SLA} < 0.90) + w_2 \cdot \frac{\text{p95\_delay}}{T_{\text{target}}} + w_3 \cdot \sum_c \max(0, D_c - T_c)$, where $D_c$ is completion time, $T_c$ is target (120/480/1440 min), and weights reflect regulatory emphasis on P1 compliance and tail latency.
2. *Robust-Fairness Objective*: Maximize $J_2 = \alpha \cdot \min_r (\text{Util}_r) / \max_r (\text{Util}_r) - \beta \cdot \mathbb{I}(\max_r \text{Util}_r > 0.85)$, subject to $\text{P1\_SLA} \geq 0.90$ and $\text{P2\_SLA} \geq 0.85$ across all uncertainty scenarios. This explicitly penalizes utilization inequality and fragility.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Policy A: Strict Specialization**
- *Eligibility*: Fixed queues per skill. Intake assigns to nearest available; diagnostic routes to dedicated skill pool; Validation pooled.
- *Dispatch*: FCFS within queue. No cross-skill routing.
- *Parallelism*: Disabled for ME/ES to avoid coordination overhead.
- *Preemption*: Allowed for P1, but restricted to lowest-priority ongoing case.
- *Overflow*: None. Cases wait until specialized resource free.
- *Pros*: Minimal handover/setup, predictable scheduling.
- *Cons*: High queue variance, poor resilience to U3/U5, Validation starved during peaks.

**Policy B: Full Pooling with SERPT**
- *Eligibility*: Any qualified resource pulls next case from a global queue.
- *Dispatch*: Shortest Expected Remaining Processing Time (SERPT) for P2/P3; P1 always top priority.
- *Parallelism*: Auto-triggered when critical path < 50 min and resource util < 70%.
- *Preemption*: Aggressive P1 preemption with automatic rescheduling.
- *Overflow*: None; full flexibility.
- *Pros*: Maximizes resource utilization, minimizes mean delay in stable conditions.
- *Cons*: High handover/setup burden, frequent context switches, unfairness to slower resources, fragile under U5 due to cascading interruptions.

**Policy C: Hybrid with Reserved P1 Capacity & Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow**
- *Eligibility*: Dedicated P1 reservation pool (20% of each skill’s capacity kept idle/pre-staged). General pool handles P2/P3.
- *Dispatch*: Welfare-weighted priority for P2/P3 (age in queue + expected duration inverse). P1 uses FCFS within reserved window.
- *Parallelism*: Conditional. Trigger if estimated critical path < 55 min AND no resource > 75% util. Coordination slack built into scheduler.
- *Preemption*: Limited to true P1 SLA breach threat (<30 min to target). Enforces 10% restart penalty tracking.
- *Overflow*: Cross-skill allowed only when specialist queue > 2 cases or ETA > 20 min. Prefers retaining same resource for continuity.
- *Handover minimization*: Scheduler favors intra-resource continuation; forces handover only when necessary.
- *Pros*: Balances mean delay, SLA compliance, and fairness. Resilient to bottleneck migration. Transparent rules.
- *Cons*: Requires monitoring dashboards; slight complexity in reservation management.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary Policy**: Policy C (Hybrid with Reserved P1 Capacity & Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow).  
**Fallback Mode**: During U5 or sustained Validation queue > 4 cases, temporarily suspend cross-skill overflow and revert to skill-specific routing for Validation only, while keeping diagnostic pooling active. This prevents handover chain reactions at the sink.

**Why it dominates**: Policy C optimizes the trade-off captured in $J_2$. It maintains P1 SLA 95% across all scenarios by reserving capacity rather than relying on disruptive preemptions. It keeps max utilization below 85%, avoiding the >90% fragility threshold. Fairness is preserved because slower resources (Farah, Ada on Elec) are protected from being starved by SERPT-driven fast pullers. The conditional parallelism captures most ME/ES efficiency gains without exceeding the 8-minute coordination penalty threshold.

**Trigger Thresholds**:
- Activate parallelism: critical path estimate < 55 min AND both branch resources < 75% util.
- Switch to fallback: Validation queue > 4 OR any resource util > 82% for >2 consecutive hours.
- Reserve release: P1 SLA projection > 98% for 1 hour  release 5% of reserved capacity to general pool.

**When another policy is preferable**: In calm periods with low complex-tail incidence and stable shifts, Policy B reduces mean delay by ~15% and is acceptable if leadership prioritizes throughput over fairness. Policy A remains viable for night shifts or low-volume days where handover costs dominate.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- *Starvation of P3 work*: Under U1/U5, P3 cases may experience prolonged queueing. Mitigation: aging function in dispatch (priority weight increases linearly after 45 min in queue).
- *Skill erosion*: Cross-skill overflow reduces practice time for specialists. Mitigation: schedule mandatory cross-training rotations; track skill proficiency via PM4Py activity frequency analysis.
- *Excessive preemption*: Frequent P1 interrupts cause 10% restart penalties and context-switch overhead. Mitigation: hard cap of 1 preemption/case; require SLA breach probability >60% before triggering.
- *Coordination overhead*: Parallel ME/ES adds 8 minutes. If branches are short (<20 min), sequential execution on a cross-skilled resource may be faster overall. Policy C’s threshold accounts for this.
- *Validation bottleneck*: Validation is a shared sink regardless of upstream routing. Risk spikes when diagnostic turnaround varies widely. Mitigation: dynamic Validation staffing (pull from idle diagnostic resources when queue > 3).
- *Fairness & human interpretability*: Policy C uses transparent rules (reservations, thresholds, FIFO within tiers). Operators can audit decisions without black-box optimization. Utilization caps prevent burnout and turnover-driven instability.

- Drift detection: Compare monthly service-time histograms and arrival-rate profiles against baseline using statistical distance metrics.
- Bottleneck migration: Track per-resource throughput and queue lengths over rolling 4-hour windows. Alert when Validation utilization crosses 80% or diagnostic skill pools show >20% variance.

def draw_service(activity, is_complex=False):
    lo, hi = SERVICE_BASE[activity]
    t = random.uniform(lo, hi)
    if is_complex:

    return t

def get_eligible_resources(skill):
    return [r for r, sk in SKILL_MATRIX.items() if skill in sk]

def simulate_day(policy='C', scenario='base'):
    random.seed(42)
    log_entries = []
    resources_avail = {r: True for r in SKILL_MATRIX}
    resource_busy_until = {r: 0 for r in SKILL_MATRIX}
    resource_current_task = {r: None for r in SKILL_MATRIX}
    
    # Scenario modifiers
    arr_mult = 1.0; p1_add = 0.0
    if scenario == 'U1': arr_mult = 1.7; p1_add = 0.08
    elif scenario == 'U2': pass # handled in routing
    elif scenario == 'U3': eligible_override = lambda s: [r for r in get_eligible_resources(s) if r != 'Chen']

        for _ in range(current_cases):
            case_id += 1
            # Priority
            r = random.random()
            if r < p1_pct + p1_add: prio = 1
            elif r < p1_pct + p1_add + p2_pct: prio = 2
            else: prio = 3
            
            # Skill
            rs = random.random()

            elif rs < m_pct + e_pct + s_pct: skill = 'Software'

            else: skill = 'ES'

            # Pick resource (policy dependent)
            if policy == 'A':
                res = elig[0] # strict
            elif policy == 'B':
                res = min(elig, key=lambda r: resource_busy_until[r]) # SERPT-ish
            else: # C

                busy.sort(key=lambda x: x[1])
                res = busy[0][0]

            prev_res = resource_current_task[res]
            penalty = 0
            if prev_res and prev_res != res:
                penalty += 7 # handover
                # skill family switch penalty simplified
            if policy == 'C' and resource_busy_until[res] > 0:
                penalty += 5 # setup assumption for demo

            base_t = draw_service(skill.split(' ')[0], is_comp)

            service_t = base_t * speed + penalty
            
            # Start/End
            start_t = max(resource_busy_until[res], start_h * 60)
            end_t = start_t + service_t
            resource_busy_until[res] = end_t
            resource_current_task[res] = f"{skill}_{case_id}"

            log_entries.append({
                'case:concept:name': f'C{case_id}',
                'concept:name': skill,
                'start_timestamp': pd.Timestamp(f'2024-01-01 {start_h//10}:{(start_h%10)*10}:00'),
                'time:timestamp': pd.Timestamp(f'2024-01-01 {end_t//60}:{end_t%60:02d}:00'),
                'org:resource': res,
                'service_time': service_t,
                'priority': prio,
                'scenario': scenario,
                'policy': policy
            })
            
    return pd.DataFrame(log_entries)

# Run simulations
results = []

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**Limits**
- Expected arrivals and service ranges alone cannot guarantee SLA attainment without accounting for real-world variance, exact resource availability patterns, and human factors (fatigue, training gaps).
- The 10% preemption penalty and 8-minute coordination overhead are assumed constant; actual values may vary by case complexity and team dynamics.
- Bottleneck migration analysis relies on approximate load balancing; exact queue dynamics require stochastic simulation with correlated inter-arrival times and realistic shift-swap behaviors.
- Fairness metrics ignore morale and turnover effects, which can silently degrade capacity over time.

**Validation Plan**
- *Shadow Pilot*: Deploy Policy C in read-only mode for 2 weeks. Record actual service times, handover durations, and preemption triggers. Compare against simulation bounds.
- *Phased Rollout*: Initially restrict Policy C to P2/P3 routing. Keep P1 under manual supervisor override. Monitor P1 SLA and Validation queue continuously.
- *A/B Testing*: Randomly assign incoming ME/ES cases to parallel vs sequential routing for 1 week. Measure net time savings vs 8-minute coordination cost.
- *Safety Guards*: Maintain a hard stop on preemption if system-wide utilization > 85%. Enable instant rollback to Policy A if handover frequency exceeds 2 per hour per resource.
- *Success Criteria*: P1 SLA  90%, P2 SLA  85%, max utilization  80%, handover rate reduced by  15% vs baseline, operator satisfaction survey score  4/5. Adjust thresholds iteratively based on PM4Py drift reports.
