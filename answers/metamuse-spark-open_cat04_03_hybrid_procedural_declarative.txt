# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

### Material assumptions
1. **Case notion:** One case = one vendor onboarding request. `Receive Onboarding Request` is the unique `init`; `Close Onboarding` is the unique terminal event (exactly once per case). Cases do not interleave.
2. **Event semantics:** Activities are atomic, timestamped in hours relative to case start, totally ordered. No duplicate timestamps affect semantics.
3. **Resources:** `org:resource` is expected on every event. Missing resource means *incomplete data*, not compliance.
4. **Approval semantics:** `Activate Vendor` implies acceptance; `Reject Vendor` implies rejection. They are mutually exclusive terminal decisions (R1). `Manager Approval` = low-risk route, `Compliance Approval` after `Enhanced Due Diligence` = high-risk route (R2,R8).
5. **Scope of procedural backbone:** The backbone describes the *stable* control-flow: receipt  concurrent checks  classification  exclusive outcome  activation/release/close. Cross-cutting obligations (clarification cycles, sanctions/legal, SoD, post-activation `Change Bank Details`/`Suspend Vendor`) are orthogonal to that block structure.
6. **Clarification:** `Request Clarification` and `Receive Clarification` may appear anywhere before the terminal outcome, in zero or more cycles. No clarification after `Close Onboarding`.
7. **Sanctions:** `Sanctions Escalation` is an *internal* escalation decision occurring only after `Run Sanctions Screening` (R7). `Obtain Legal Opinion` is a document event that may be produced before, concurrent with, or after other checks.
8. **Post-activation:** `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and a second `Verify Bank Account` form a conditional, non-block-structured fragment that violates pure block structure if forced into a process tree. It is therefore excluded from the procedural tree.
9. **Temporal profile:** Means/standard deviations in R12 are *performance SLAs* measured on inter-event elapsed seconds. They are normative for flagging, not for automatic rejection.
10. **Missing attributes:** The log does **not** contain `emergency=true` nor `joint_authorization` (R6). Any decision that depends on them cannot be made from the activity sequence alone and requires an external, human-attested check.

### R5 vs R6 conflict
* **R5 (Legal):** `Sanctions Escalation`  `Obtain Legal Opinion` **must precede** `Activate Vendor` in *all* cases.
* **R6 (Operations):** Emergency activation *may* precede the opinion, provided joint authorization exists outside the log and the opinion arrives 24h after activation. The log has no field to distinguish emergency from non-emergency.

The two rules are logically incompatible on activity sequences alone: R5 is `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` conditioned on `Sanctions Escalation`; R6 is `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` + `response(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` with a 24h bound and an unobservable guard.

#### Two defensible resolutions
**Resolution A — Strict Legal (closed-world, conservative):** Enforce R5 as hard law. R6 is treated as an *out-of-band exception workflow* requiring documented approval stored in a separate GRC system and manual override. In the event log, every `Activate` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` after escalation is a **fail**, regardless of subsequent temporal proximity. The 24h SLA is monitored only as a secondary warning.

**Resolution B — Conditional Tolerance (open-world, operations-tolerant):** Relax R5 to R4 + temporal SLA: require only `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` and, *if* `Activate` precedes opinion, require `Obtain Legal Opinion` within 24h of activation. Violations become **warnings** pending attestation of joint authorization. This reduces friction but risks false negatives: an unauthorized early activation that meets the 24h window would incorrectly pass.

**Resolution C — Model extension (rejected):** Introduce `Emergency Activate Vendor` as a distinct activity type partitioned from normal `Activate Vendor`. Rejected because the historic log has no such label and would require relabeling with unavailable case attributes.

#### Implemented choice
**Resolution A is authoritative** for `declare_model` and `log_skeleton`. R5 is encoded as a *conservative* `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` in DECLARE with explicit documentation of over-approximation, and the *authoritative* conditional check is delegated to the resource/data-aware layer (Section 7) where both interpretations can be evaluated.

**Compliance risk of the choice:** Under A, true emergencies with timely opinion and valid joint authorization will be flagged as violations, creating operational friction and incentive to bypass logging. We mitigate by surfacing them as `policy-dependent` findings requiring attestation, not silent passes. Under B the risk is inverted: legal exposure — an attacker or negligent operator could activate a sanctioned vendor and still pass automated checks by obtaining a late opinion within 24h without verifiable authorization. Because legal risk dominates audit risk, A is preferred for automated rejection; B is offered only as a second-stage, human-in-the-loop downgrade.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

### 1. Compact formal description (Process Tree)

```
SEQ(
  "Receive Onboarding Request",
  AND( "Capture Master Data", "Run Sanctions Screening", "Verify Bank Account" ),
  "Classify Risk",
  XOR(
    "Reject Vendor",
    SEQ(
      XOR( "Manager Approval", SEQ("Enhanced Due Diligence","Compliance Approval") ),
      "Activate Vendor",
      "Release First Payment"
    )
  ),
  "Close Onboarding"
)
```

`SEQ`=sequence (``), `AND`=parallel (``), `XOR`=exclusive choice (`×`). The tree is block-structured by construction.

### 2. Self-contained PM4Py Python code assigning `procedural_model`

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# --- leaves ---
receive  = ProcessTree(label="Receive Onboarding Request")
capture  = ProcessTree(label="Capture Master Data")
run      = ProcessTree(label="Run Sanctions Screening")
verify   = ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account")
classify = ProcessTree(label="Classify Risk")
reject   = ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor")
manager  = ProcessTree(label="Manager Approval")
edd      = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence")
compliance = ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval")
activate = ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor")
release  = ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment")
close    = ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding")

# --- parallel checks after receipt ---
parallel_checks = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL)
parallel_checks.children = [capture, run, verify]
for c in parallel_checks.children:
    c.parent = parallel_checks

# --- low vs high risk ---
high_risk = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
high_risk.children = [edd, compliance]
for c in high_risk.children:
    c.parent = high_risk

approval_choice = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
approval_choice.children = [manager, high_risk]
for c in approval_choice.children:
    c.parent = approval_choice

# --- accepted path: approval -> activate -> release ---
accepted = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
accepted.children = [approval_choice, activate, release]
for c in accepted.children:
    c.parent = accepted

# --- outcome after classification ---
outcome = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
outcome.children = [reject, accepted]
for c in outcome.children:
    c.parent = outcome

# --- root sequence ---
procedural_model = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
procedural_model.children = [receive, parallel_checks, classify, outcome, close]
for c in procedural_model.children:
    c.parent = procedural_model

# Optional: convert to Petri net for alignment
# from pm4py.objects.conversion.process_tree import converter
# net, im, fm = converter.apply(procedural_model)
```

`procedural_model` is a `ProcessTree`. It can be used with `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments` or converted to an accepting Petri net.

### 3. Explanation

* **Concurrency:** `AND(Capture, Run, Verify)` allows any interleaving after `Receive Onboarding Request`, satisfying backbone point 2. `Classify Risk` is sequenced after the parallel block, enforcing join semantics (point 3).
* **Choices:** Outer `XOR` models the primary outcome (reject vs accept). Inner `XOR` models the low-risk vs high-risk route. This satisfies point 4 without introducing duplicate `Activate`/`Release` nodes.
* **Clarification loops:** Deliberately **excluded** from the procedural model. `Request Clarification`/`Receive Clarification` can occur in 0..n cycles *anywhere* before the terminal outcome; encoding them as a `LOOP` before `Classify` would forbid clarification after classification, while encoding them as a flexible loop around the entire post-classify fragment would break block structure and introduce unsound duplicates. The `altresponse` DECLARE rule is authoritative (Section 4) and the log is projected onto backbone activities before procedural alignment (extra activities are ignored, not penalized).
* **Terminal outcomes:** `Close Onboarding` is sequenced *after* the outer `XOR`, guaranteeing every case closes exactly once regardless of reject/accept branch, satisfying point 5. The tree forbids both `Reject` and `Activate` in the same case and forbids `Release` in the rejected branch.
* **Post-activation behavior:** `Change Bank Details`  `Suspend Vendor`  re-`Verify Bank Account`  `Release First Payment` is **excluded**. This fragment is non-block-structured (it creates a loop from after `Activate` back to `Verify` and introduces long-term dependency). It is handled declaratively (`response`/`precedence` + external guard). The procedural model remains sound and readable; forcing this fragment procedurally would require an approximate duplication of `Verify`/`Release` or an unstructured Petri net, which we disclose as an approximation we avoid.
* **Sanctions/legal:** `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` are also excluded from the control-flow backbone for the same reason: they are optional and conditionally ordered (R4/R5/R6). Their hard constraints live in DECLARE/external layers.

```python
    "always_after": {
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
        ("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding")
    },
    # unordered mutual exclusion
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Enhanced Due Diligence")
    },
    # allowed occurrence counts per case
    "activ_freq": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": {1},
        "Capture Master Data": {1},
        "Run Sanctions Screening": {1},
        "Verify Bank Account": {1, 2},  # 2 allowed for post-change re-verification (R11)
        "Classify Risk": {1},
        "Request Clarification": {0, 1, 2, 3, 4},
        "Receive Clarification": {0, 1, 2, 3, 4},
        "Sanctions Escalation": {0, 1},
        "Obtain Legal Opinion": {0, 1},
        "Enhanced Due Diligence": {0, 1},
        "Manager Approval": {0, 1},
        "Compliance Approval": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Change Bank Details": {0, 1},
        "Suspend Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Close Onboarding": {1}
    },
    "directly_follows": set()  # deliberately empty: concurrency makes DFG unreliable
}
# Usage: pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(log, log_skeleton)
```

**Orientation and overlap/disagreement:**

* `always_before` is **backward**: second element requires first before it. E.g., `("Run Sanctions Screening","Sanctions Escalation")` means any escalation must be preceded by screening — mirrors DECLARE `precedence` (R7) redundantly but from a frequentist/statistical perspective.
* `always_after` is **forward**: first requires second after it. E.g., `("Sanctions Escalation","Obtain Legal Opinion")` mirrors DECLARE `response` (R4). Having both provides defense-in-depth: DECLARE checks logical satisfaction per trace, Log Skeleton checks aggregate invariants.
* `never_together` mirrors DECLARE `noncoexistence` (R1,R8) but is unordered and counts-based, while DECLARE is sequence-based — they should agree; disagreement signals log noise or model misspecification.
* **Deliberate omissions (disagreements):** We **do not** include `("Obtain Legal Opinion","Activate Vendor")` in `always_before` as a hard invariant, because that would enforce strict R5 for *all* activates, contradicting the guarded external check and causing false positives for non-escalated cases. This is the documented conflict — Log Skeleton is kept permissive where DECLARE is conservative.
* We **do not** include `equivalence` (activities always co-occurring with same frequency) because no pair qualifies under both reject/accept branches.
* `directly_follows` is empty: with `AND(Capture,Run,Verify)` any of the three may directly follow `Receive`, and `directly_follows` would over-constrain and be brittle to concurrency.
* `activ_freq` is authoritative for R10 where DECLARE lacks cardinality expressivity (`Verify Bank Account` {1,2} accommodates R11 re-verification; others {0,1} enforce at-most-once).

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),   # 48h ±24h
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),     # 12h ±6h
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200, 129600)         # 72h ±36h
}
zeta = 2.0
# Usage: pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)
```

Seconds = hours × 3600. Allowed interval per pair is `mean ± zeta·stdev`:

* RequestReceive: 0 – 345600 s (0–96 h)
* EscalationOpinion: 0 – 86400 s (0–24 h)
* ActivateRelease: 0 – 518400 s (0–144 h)

**Why warnings, not automatic control-flow violations:**
R12 is explicitly described as "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject." Temporal deviations are performance/operational risks, not logical impossibilities; a late opinion (e.g., 34 h) may still be legally valid but indicates SLA breach and should trigger human review. Mixing statistical SLA checks with hard control-flow would conflate *conformance* (can the trace happen?) with *performance* (did it happen fast enough?), leading to false rejections of compliant but slow cases. The deployment therefore runs `conformance_temporal_profile` last and surfaces results as `warning` with `zeta` evidence, requiring triage rather than blocking closure.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required attributes: `case:concept:name` (or `case_id`), `concept:name` (activity), `time:timestamp` (seconds), `org:resource` (performer). Additional expected but missing: `joint_authorization` (boolean, outside log), `emergency` flag. If any required attribute is missing, the check returns `not evaluated` + data-quality alert, not `pass`.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

def to_case_traces(log_df):
    # log_df columns: case_id, activity, timestamp, resource
    return {cid: g.sort_values("timestamp") for cid,g in log_df.groupby("case_id")}

# --- R9 SoD 1: Verify Bank Account vs Approval ---
def check_sod_verify_approval(log):
    """
    R9a: resource(Verify Bank Account) != resource(Manager Approval)
         and != resource(Compliance Approval) if approval exists
    """
    # pm4py four-eyes as first-pass filter (activity-level)
    # Returns sublog of violating cases (same resource on both activities)
    viol_1 = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")
    viol_2 = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")
    # For fine-grained reporting we also compute via dataframe:
    # if log missing org:resource -> not evaluated
    return viol_1, viol_2

def check_sod_capture_activate(log_df):
    """R9b: resource(Capture Master Data) != resource(Activate Vendor)"""
    violations = []
    for case_id, trace in to_case_traces(log_df).items():
        cap = trace[trace.activity=="Capture Master Data"]
        act = trace[trace.activity=="Activate Vendor"]
        if cap.empty or act.empty:
            continue
        if cap.iloc[0].resource == act.iloc[0].resource:
            violations.append(case_id)
        if pd.isna(cap.iloc[0].resource) or pd.isna(act.iloc[0].resource):
            # missing resource -> data quality
            violations.append((case_id, "not evaluated: missing org:resource"))
    return violations

# --- R2 conditional exactly-one ---
def check_R2_approval_cardinality(log_df):
    violations=[]
    for case_id, trace in to_case_traces(log_df).items():
        has_activate = "Activate Vendor" in trace.activity.values
        has_reject   = "Reject Vendor" in trace.activity.values
        cnt_mgr = (trace.activity=="Manager Approval").sum()
        cnt_comp= (trace.activity=="Compliance Approval").sum()
        if has_activate and not has_reject:
            if cnt_mgr + cnt_comp != 1:
                violations.append((case_id, f"accepted but approvals={cnt_mgr+cnt_comp}"))
        elif has_reject and not has_activate:
            if cnt_mgr + cnt_comp != 0:
                violations.append((case_id, f"rejected but approvals present"))
        elif has_activate and has_reject:
            violations.append((case_id, "both Activate and Reject present (R1)"))
    return violations

# --- R3 pending clarification guard ---
def check_R3_pending_before_outcome(log_df):
    violations=[]
    for case_id, trace in to_case_traces(log_df).items():
        pending=0
        for _, row in trace.iterrows():
            if row.activity=="Request Clarification":
                pending+=1
            elif row.activity=="Receive Clarification":
                pending=max(0,pending-1)
            elif row.activity in ("Reject Vendor","Activate Vendor"):
                if pending>0:
                    violations.append((case_id, f"pending clarification before {row.activity}"))
    return violations

# --- R5 / R6 emergency policy (policy-dependent) ---
def check_R5_R6_escalation(log_df, policy="strict"):
    """
    policy="strict"  -> R5 hard precedence
    policy="tolerant"-> R6 SLA 24h + attestation
    Returns (failures, warnings)
    """
    failures, warnings = [], []
    for case_id, trace in to_case_traces(log_df).items():
        if "Sanctions Escalation" not in trace.activity.values:
            continue
        t_esc = trace[trace.activity=="Sanctions Escalation"].iloc[0].timestamp
        has_op = "Obtain Legal Opinion" in trace.activity.values
        has_act= "Activate Vendor" in trace.activity.values
        if not has_op:
            failures.append((case_id,"R4: escalation without opinion"))
            continue
        t_op = trace[trace.activity=="Obtain Legal Opinion"].iloc[0].timestamp
        if has_act:
            t_act = trace[trace.activity=="Activate Vendor"].iloc[0].timestamp
            if t_op > t_act:  # opinion after activation
                if policy=="strict":
                    failures.append((case_id,f"R5 strict: Activate@{t_act} before Opinion@{t_op}"))
                else: # tolerant
                    delta = (t_op - t_act).total_seconds() if hasattr(t_op,"total_seconds") else t_op - t_act
                    # delta in hours for our test data (hours are timestamps)
                    # convert: timestamps are hours relative, so delta_hours = t_op - t_act
                    delta_hours = t_op - t_act
                    if delta_hours > 24:
                        failures.append((case_id,f"R6 SLA breach: {delta_hours}h >24h"))
                    else:
                        # missing joint_authorization attribute -> cannot verify
                        warnings.append((case_id,f"R6 tolerant pass within 24h ({delta_hours}h) but missing joint_authorization attestation"))
            # temporal SLA Escalation->Opinion always checked separately via temporal_profile
    return failures, warnings

# --- R11 post-activation change ---
def check_R11_change(log_df):
    failures=[]
    for case_id, trace in to_case_traces(log_df).items():
        if "Change Bank Details" not in trace.activity.values:
            continue
        t_act = trace[trace.activity=="Activate Vendor"].iloc[0].timestamp if "Activate Vendor" in trace.activity.values else None
        for _, row in trace[trace.activity=="Change Bank Details"].iterrows():
            t_change=row.timestamp
            if t_act is not None and t_change <= t_act:
                continue # only after activation matters
            # must have Suspend after
            after = trace[trace.timestamp > t_change]
            if "Suspend Vendor" not in after.activity.values:
                failures.append((case_id,"R11: Change without later Suspend"))
            # if later Release exists, must have Verify between Change and Release
            releases = after[after.activity=="Release First Payment"]
            if not releases.empty:
                t_rel = releases.iloc[0].timestamp
                verifies = trace[(trace.activity=="Verify Bank Account") & (trace.timestamp > t_change) & (trace.timestamp < t_rel)]
                if verifies.empty:
                    failures.append((case_id,"R11: Release after Change without re-Verify"))
    return failures

# Missing attribute handler (wrapper)
def safe_check(check_fn, log):
    if "org:resource" not in log.columns or log["org:resource"].isna().all():
        return "not evaluated: org:resource missing, manual review required"
    return check_fn(log)
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement across layers:**
* R1 (`Reject`/`Activate` never together) and R8 (`Manager`/`Compliance`, `Manager`/`EDD`) are enforced in three places: procedural XOR, DECLARE `noncoexistence`, and Log Skeleton `never_together`. Redundancy is intentional for critical invariants — procedural catches structural impossibility, declarative catches log-level co-occurrence even if procedural projection is bypassed, Log Skeleton provides frequency-aggregated cross-check. Risk: contradictory findings if one layer is mis-configured (e.g., procedural allows extra branch via loop). Mitigation: declare DECLARE/Log Skeleton authoritative for co-occurrence, procedural for ordering.
* R4 (`EscalationOpinion`) duplicated as DECLARE `response` and Log Skeleton `always_after`. Agreement increases confidence; disagreement indicates data-quality issue (e.g., timestamp out of order).

**Constraints that become inconsistent under loops or post-activation changes:**
* Adding a loop for clarification or post-activation `ChangeSuspendre-Verify` breaks block structure. Forced into a process tree it requires duplicate `Verify`/`Release` nodes or an unstructured loop that makes `Classify Risk` no longer a strict join of the three checks. Our model avoids this by excluding those fragments procedurally. If they were forced procedurally, `always_before(Capture,Classify)` would be violated by a second `Verify` occurring after `Activate`.
* `altresponse(Request,Receive)` is inconsistent with a procedural `LOOP` that also allows `Close` without `Receive`: the procedural loop would need an exit condition tied to pending clarifications, which is data-dependent, not control-flow.

**Vacuity in declarative rules:**
* `response`, `precedence`, `noncoexistence` are vacuously true when antecedent never occurs. E.g., T1/T3 have no `Sanctions Escalation`, so R4/R5 vacuously pass — a trace with no escalation is not evidence of compliance, just absence of trigger. This inflates support/confidence if measured empirically. We set model `support=1.0` normatively, but warn that log-measured support would be misleadingly high. `noncoexistence` vacuously passes when neither activity occurs, masking that R2 (exactly-one in accepted cases) is violated.
* `altresponse` with 0 `Request` passes, correctly, but hides that a case *should* have had clarification but didn't — not detectable.

**Missing case attributes:**
* R6 requires `emergency` boolean and `joint_authorization` attestation outside the event log. No activity-only formalism can distinguish emergency from non-emergency. Our external check surfaces `policy-dependent`/`not evaluated` and forces manual attestation workflow. Ignoring this would silently collapse R5+R6 into a single temporal rule, creating false negatives.
* No vendor risk score attribute to decide low vs high route deterministically; procedural choice is non-deterministic, so R2's "exactly one approval in accepted" cannot be validated from activities alone without knowing intended risk class.

**Timestamp-only limitations:**
* Temporal profile uses only elapsed seconds between two events, ignoring business calendars, time zones, weekends, and case suspensions (e.g., waiting for vendor). A 34h EscalationOpinion (T7) flagged as warning may be 1 business day, not a breach. Conversely, a 10h delta that spans midnight may still violate 24h SLA if measured in business hours. No repair for clock skew or missing timestamps.
* Temporal profile cannot express conditional deadlines (R6's 24h after *activation* only if activation preceded opinion) nor deadlines with multiple occurrences (which `Verify` before which `Release`?).

**Risk of treating support/confidence metadata as normative truth:**
* PM4Py DECLARE dictionaries carry `support`/`confidence` as *discovered* statistics. Setting them to 1.0 in a *prescriptive* model does not make the log compliant; it declares expectation. Users may misinterpret `confidence=1.0` as empirical evidence. We explicitly set `support=1.0` to mean "required" not "observed", and require that conformance checking compares log against model, not vice versa. Low empirical support for a rule does not justify weakening the rule; it signals non-compliance.

**Other blind spots:**
* Resource checks assume stable `org:resource` mapping; they cannot detect delegation, proxy approval, or shared accounts. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is case-level, not activity-instance level, and misses SoD violations across sub-cases.
* No data attribute for bank account validity; R11's second `Verify` could be a dummy event with same data, undetectable from activity label alone.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation:** `ProcessTree` (Section 3) stored as the *reference* backbone in the process repository. Convert to accepting Petri net on demand for alignments (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`). POWL is not needed; its additional precedence edges would duplicate declarative constraints without benefit. Keep the procedural model minimal (10 backbone activities) and version it separately from policy packs.

**Authoritative policy layer:**

* **Hard control-flow invariants (R1,R7,R8):** DECLARE `noncoexistence`/`precedence`/`altresponse` is authoritative. Log Skeleton is secondary statistical confirmation.
* **Cardinality & conditional obligations (R2,R3-final guard,R5 conditional,R10,R11):** External validation layer (Section 7) is authoritative — it alone can evaluate counts conditioned on outcome and resource/time guards.
* **Resource/SoD (R9):** External layer authoritative; no other layer can see `org:resource`.
* **Temporal SLAs (R12) and emergency tolerance (R6):** Temporal profile is authoritative for *warnings*, never for automatic rejection.

**Order of conformance checks (fail-fast, cheapest first):**

1. **Data-quality gate:** Validate required attributes (`concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `case:concept:name`, `org:resource` when needed). If missing, mark `not evaluated` and route to data stewards — do not run other checks.
2. **Procedural alignment (backbone):** Project log onto backbone alphabet and run alignments. Failures here (e.g., T8) are structural and block further analysis.
3. **Resource/SoD & cardinality (R9,R2,R3-guard,R11, R5-strict):** Run dataframe checks. These are deterministic, low-cost, and catch segregation violations (T4) before expensive declarative queries.
4. **DECLARE conformance:** `pm4py.conformance_declare` for R1,R3,R4,R7,R8 (and conservative R5). Surface rule-level violations with explanation.
5. **Log Skeleton:** `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton` for frequency and always_before/after invariants (R10,R4,R7). Use to cross-validate DECLARE; raise data-quality alert on disagreement.
6. **Temporal profile:** `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(..., zeta=2.0)` last. Convert breaches to `warning` tickets with `mean±zeta·stdev` evidence (e.g., T7 34h > 24h).

**Surfacing contradictory findings:**
* Do not aggregate into a single red/green score. Emit a **layered finding object** per case: `{procedural: pass/fail, declare: [violations], log_skeleton: [violations], temporal: [warnings], resource: [violations], policy_dependent: [explanations]}`.
* For R5/R6, show **both** resolutions side-by-side in the case inspector: "Legal view: FAIL — Activate before Opinion (R5 strict). Operations view: WARNING — within 24h SLA but missing joint authorization (R6). Action: provide attestation or rollback activation." Require explicit override with dual approval to downgrade strict fail to warning.
* For vacuous passes, annotate "vacuously satisfied — trigger not present" to prevent misreading support/confidence as compliance evidence.
* Temporal warnings include sparkline of elapsed time vs SLA band and business-calendar toggle; they never auto-reject but block auto-closure until acknowledged.

This hybrid deployment respects that each formalism excels at what it was designed for — procedural for concurrency/choice, declarative for cross-cutting obligations, Log Skeleton for frequency invariants, temporal profile for performance, and an explicit external layer for data/resource conditions that cannot be recovered from activity sequences alone.
