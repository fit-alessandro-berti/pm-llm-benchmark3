# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

Automating isolated steps without respecting process dependencies will either shift queues downstream or automate compliance failures. Five dependency dimensions govern safe sequencing:

* **Upstream data quality and artifact dependencies:**
  * **Intake-to-Analysis chain (`A1`/`A2`  `A5`  `A6`  `A8`):** `A6` (financial-statement normalization) is highly vulnerable to incomplete or low-quality PDFs. Currently, `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` occurs 3,300 times (with 52% repeated loops and 2,800 returns). Deploying `A5` before or alongside `A6` raises first-pass document completeness so `A6` extracts clean statements rather than amplifying exceptions. Likewise, `A8` (Committee pack auto-assembly) cannot succeed until KYC status (`A4`/`A3`), normalized statements (`A6`), and valuations (`A7`) are reconciled; otherwise it automates garbage-in/garbage-out across the 3,900 committee cases.
  * **Routing accuracy (`A2`  downstream control paths):** Because `Classify product and exposure` has an 8% error rate and branches cases into concurrent `KYC check` (7,250) and `Financial document review` (3,940), misrouting cascades into missing collateral checks or bypassed High-exposure committee controls.
* **Legal and compliance control prerequisites:**
  * **Hard KYC-before-signing gate (`A4`/`A3`  `A9`  `A10`  `A11`):** Customer signing cannot legally precede KYC clearance, yet provisional signing occurs in 6.5% of cases (1.1% requiring remediation). Automating e-signature (`A10`) or loan activation (`A11`) before hard-gating KYC clearance via `A9` and clearing entity-mismatch stalls via `A4` would scale non-compliant bookings.
  * **Decision-to-contract reconciliation (`Committee decision`  `A9`  `A11`):** Contract-remediation loops hit 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewals (`Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue`: 650; return loop: 510). `A9` must ingest authoritative decision terms and verify four-eyes separation (preventing the 5.8% High-exposure month-end violations) before `A11` can ever be safely enabled.
* **Human escalation capacity and concurrency dependencies:**
  * **Concurrent branch synchronization (`KYC check` || `Financial document review`):** Although the system logs them sequentially, they run concurrently in practice and synchronize at `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging`. Speeding up only one branch yields minimal end-to-end cycle-time reduction because cases simply wait longer at the join point.
  * **Key-person specialist bottlenecks:** Compliance Specialists comprise only 3 FTE, with heavy handover concentration onto two senior specialists (`Resolve entity mismatch`: 31h wait, 38% rework). Any upstream automation (`A1`, `A2`, or overly sensitive `A3` false positives) that increases escalation volume will collapse specialist turnaround and starve Legal Ops (4 FTE) until month-end.
  * **Central analyst hub:** Credit Analysts (9 FTE) sit at the center of the working-together network (`Financial document review` 45m + `Normalize financial statements` 75m + `Credit analysis` 110m). Automating intake (`A1`) without relieving analyst effort (`A5`, `A6`) directly inflates the 18h–28h analyst queues.
* **External valuation dependency:**
  * `Wait for external valuation` (58.0h median wait, 19% late) is a major synchronization gate before `Committee packaging` (1,980 directly-follows transitions), affecting 71% of Equipment Finance and 18% of Working-Capital cases. Because `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` (2,400) currently runs *sequentially* after analysis, internal speedups before credit analysis are partially absorbed by external valuer latency unless valuation triage (`A7`)—or a policy trigger to request valuation in parallel at intake/classification—decouples it from the critical path.
* **Core-banking integration constraints:**
  * IT can support **at most two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months**. Deep integrations are required for state-changing or master-record modules (`A9`, `A4` if tightly coupled to KYC master status, `A1`, `A2`, `A3`, `A11`, `A12`). By contrast, `A5` (portal checklist/nudging), `A7` (external valuer triage), `A10` (e-sign provider orchestration), and initial phases of `A6` (document-extraction workbench outputting structured files before H2 LOS write-back) can deploy via lightweight API/workflow layers without consuming scarce H1 core-banking slots.

Also declines senior management's politically attractive `A1` quick win.

However, `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` (3,300; 52% repeated) persists and intermittently degrades `A6` extraction accuracy.
* **Handovers:** Dramatically eases the KYC Ops  2 senior Compliance Specialists bottleneck and reduces stalled handovers (`Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation`: 380) and late external-valuer chases (`Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging`: 1,980).
* **Resource demand:** Directly rebalances the two tightest internal pools—3-FTE Compliance Specialists (`A4`) and 9-FTE Credit Analysts (`A6`)—and smooths Legal Ops (4 FTE) inflow across the month instead of starving them early and overloading them at month-end. Slight shift in Collateral Team work toward exception/dispute resolution with external valuers.
* **Control violations:** Major reduction in provisional signing (6.5%) because `A4` removes the delay temptation and `A9` hard-blocks unverified contracts. Contract-remediation loops (8.5% / 14% renewals) drop sharply; four-eyes breaches (5.8%) decrease both via reduced month-end analyst panic and `A9` verification.

### Portfolio 3: Compliance-First (`A2, A3, A9, A12`)
* **Cycle time:** Minimal end-to-end cycle-time reduction and potential *short-term increase* as stricter automated control stops (`A2`, `A3`, `A9`) surface previously hidden exceptions and route them into `A12` before downstream processing stabilizes.
* **Waiting time:** Modest reduction before `KYC check` (4h) and `Contract preparation` (22h) once remediation stabilizes, but zero relief on `Resolve entity mismatch` (31h), `Normalize` (20h), `Credit analysis` (28h), `Wait for external valuation` (58h), or `Customer signing` (44h).
* **Rework:** Reduces misrouting rework from `Classify product and exposure` (8%) and prevents recurring `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` loops (650; 510 returns). Does not reduce the 3,300 missing-document loops or 1,160 entity-mismatch loops.
* **Handovers:** Formalizes and logs handovers between Compliance Ops, Legal Ops, and remediation teams via `A12`. Risk of *increased* escalation handovers to the 3-FTE Compliance Specialists if `A3` is conservatively tuned to avoid false negatives.
* **Resource demand:** Shifts Back Office and Compliance Ops from routine checking to exception handling; risks overloading Compliance Specialists and IT (four deep-integration modules competing for two H1 slots). Credit Analysts (9 FTE) receive no labor relief and may absorb extra clarification requests from stricter `A12` controls.
* **Control violations:** Highest theoretical control coverage—eliminates misrouted exposure tiers (`A2`), creates full KYC disposition audit trails (`A3`), blocks provisional signings and contract mismatches (`A9`), and resolves root-cause policy ambiguities (`A12`).

### Portfolio 4: Integrated Flow, Rework & Control Hybrid (`A4, A5, A6, A9`)
* **Cycle time:** Broad, consistent end-to-end cycle-time reduction across *all* segments (Micro, SME, High-exposure) and products because it simultaneously accelerates both concurrent upstream branches (`KYC check` / `Resolve entity mismatch` via `A4`; `Financial document review` / `Normalize` via `A5` + `A6`) before their synchronization at `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging`.
* **Waiting time:** Major cuts before `Resolve entity mismatch` (31h), `Financial document review` (18h), `Request missing documents` (8h + customer turnaround), `Normalize financial statements` (20h), `Credit analysis` (28h), `Committee packaging` (26h, via cleaner, synchronized inputs), and `Contract preparation` (22h). Remaining wait pockets are external (`Wait for external valuation` 58h, partially mitigated via parallel policy triggering) and customer-driven (`Customer signing` 44h, deferred to Year 2 `A10`).
* **Rework:** Eliminates the three highest-impact internal rework loops in the DFG: `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review` (3,300 / 2,800 loops, 52% repeat), `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` (1,750 / 1,160 loops, 38% rework), and `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` (650 / 510 loops, 8.5% overall / 14% renewals).
* **Handovers:** Breaks the single-point handover risk to the 2 senior Compliance Specialists (`A4`), reduces RM-triggered missing-doc ping-pong (`A5`), cuts late mismatch interruptions before Contract Prep (`Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation`: 380), and steadies work delivery to Legal Ops (4 FTE).
* **Resource demand:** Optimally matches automation to true constraint topology: frees substantial high-value capacity in the 9-FTE Credit Analyst hub (`A5` + `A6`) and 3-FTE Compliance Specialists (`A4`), eliminates Legal Ops month-end spikes (`A9`), and shifts analyst/specialist work from data wrangling toward complex judgment (High-exposure credit assessment and genuine entity-mismatch edge cases). Back Office (12 FTE) is intentionally left unautomated in Year 1, allowing afternoon slack to absorb transition support and complex customer document outreach.
* **Control violations:** Strongly reduces all three recorded control failures: hard-blocks provisional signing (6.5%) while removing the KYC delay driving RMs to bypass controls (`A4` + `A9`); eliminates committee-to-contract term mismatches (8.5% / 14% renewals) via `A9`; and cuts High-exposure four-eyes violations (5.8%) both by relieving month-end analyst congestion (`A6`) and enforcing a pre-contract four-eyes identity check inside `A9`.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

Automation does not transfer legal or regulatory accountability away from the bank; it changes failure modes from sporadic manual slips to fast, systemic errors. The following risks and compensating controls apply across the portfolio:

| Risk category | Specific failure mode in this process | Compensating controls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Auditability & accountability** | Automated recommendations in `A4` (entity matching), `A6` (financial ratios), or `A9` (contract clauses) obscure who made the credit/compliance decision, violating regulatory exam standards. | Immutable event-level audit log capturing: (1) model/rule version, (2) input artifact hash, (3) machine suggestion + confidence score, (4) human decision/override with mandatory reason code, and (5) authenticated approver ID and timestamp. |
| **False negatives (regulatory & credit loss)** | `A4` (or `A3`) mistakenly clears a genuine sanctions/entity mismatch; `A6` misreads a liability or decimal on a poor-quality PDF, inflating debt-service coverage; `A9` uses an outdated rule for Credit-line renewals. | Human-in-the-loop mandatory sign-off for all entity-mismatch clearances (`A4` assists evidence gathering, never auto-clears); automated balance-sheet reconciliation checks in `A6` (Assets = Liabilities + Equity, cross-check against tax/bank statements) with mandatory analyst review below confidence threshold or for High-exposure SMEs; formal Legal/Compliance change-control and dual sign-off on `A9` rule-library updates. |
| **False positives & exception overload** | Overly conservative thresholds in `A4`, `A6`, or `A9` flood the 3-FTE Compliance Specialists, 9-FTE Credit Analysts, or 4-FTE Legal Ops with 100% complex exception queues, increasing fatigue and wait times. | Tiered confidence routing (auto-pass only for deterministic validations, fast-track review for medium confidence, specialist queue for low confidence); dynamic queue monitoring with specialist WIP caps and cross-trained senior Compliance Ops backup; shadow-mode calibration prior to go-live to benchmark false-positive rates before turning on enforcement. |
| **Model & rule drift** | SME financial statement formats change, new loan products/clauses launch, or fraud/entity-mismatch patterns shift—silently degrading `A6` extraction accuracy or `A4` match precision. | Monthly champion-challenger and stratified sampling audits (e.g., 5% of auto-accepted `A6` extractions and 100% of High-exposure exceptions); automated drift alerts tracking override rates, reconciliation failure rates, and rule-trigger frequencies; quarterly model/rule recertification by Model Risk Management, Credit, and Compliance. |
| **Exception handling & bypass workarounds** | Rigid `A5` document checklists irritate customers and cause RMs to submit placeholder files or revert to email; hard `A9` KYC gates prompt RMs to seek manual off-system provisional signing. | Co-design `A5` with RMs using "soft-nudge + guided RM override with reason code" rather than hard submission blocks for non-mandatory docs; prohibit off-system contract issuance via core-banking activation check (operations cannot book loans lacking an `A9`-generated contract token); track RM bypass rates in monthly governance reviews. |
| **Four-eyes compliance (segregation of duties)** | During month-end rushes, the same Credit Analyst completes `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging` (currently 5.8% of High-exposure SME cases), or automation masks single-user self-approval. | System-enforced RBAC / segregation-of-duties block in workflow preventing the `Credit analysis` user ID from signing off `Committee packaging` on High-exposure cases, backed by an independent pre-contract user-ID verification check inside `A9` that halts contract generation and alerts Compliance if the same ID appears on both tasks. |
| **Automation bias & skill erosion** | Analysts rubber-stamp `A6` normalized figures or specialists uncritically accept `A4` match scores under month-end volume pressure. | Deliberate "friction on high risk": hide or de-emphasize machine confidence scores on High-exposure SME overrides, require explicit line-item confirmation on key credit ratios, and run blind QA spot checks comparing human overrides against independent senior review. |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Recommended Portfolio:** **Portfolio 4 (`A4, A5, A6, A9`)**, supplemented by a **zero-core-IT workflow policy change** to trigger `Collateral valuation request` in parallel with `KYC check` / `Financial document review` for Equipment Finance (71% required) rather than waiting for `Credit analysis` to complete.

This rollout strictly honors the **two deep core-banking integrations limit in Months 1–6** (`A9` and `A4`), deploys `A5` on the lightweight portal/workflow layer in H1, and schedules `A6`'s deep LOS/credit-model integration in H2 once `A5` improves upstream document quality.

### Phase 0: Governance, Data & Process Foundation (Months 1–2)
* **Actions:**
  * Lock legal/compliance rule library for `A9` (prioritizing Credit-line renewal terms, mandatory KYC-cleared status flag, and High-exposure four-eyes ID check).
  * Co-design `A5` document checklists and RM-friendly customer nudges with Relationship Managers and Credit Analysts.
  * Implement workflow policy change to trigger Equipment Finance collateral valuation early (parallelized with KYC/Doc review) without deep IT build.
  * Establish baseline process-mining KPIs and audit-log schema (suggestion, override, approver).
* **Gate 0 Criteria (Proceed to Wave 1):** Compliance sign-off on `A9` rule library and audit schema; RM leadership endorsement of `A5` UX prototypes; IT architecture confirms H1 uses exactly two deep core integrations (`A9`, `A4`).

### Phase 1 (Wave 1A): Control Anchor & Front-End Rework Reduction (Months 3–4)
* **Modules:**
  * **`A9` (Deep Core Integration #1 — Shadow Mode  Enforced Pilot):** Run contract generation + control-rule checks in shadow mode during Month 3 on all products; move to enforced gating in Month 4 starting with Credit-line renewals (14% remediation rate) and SME working-capital loans.
  * **`A5` (Light Portal/BPM Integration — Full Pilot):** Launch interactive missing-document checklists and automated customer reminders on portal-originated applications (72% of intake) with RM visibility dashboard and soft-override capability.
* **Monitoring Metrics:** `A9` rule-exception rate vs. actual legal defects; provisional-signing attempts blocked; `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` rate; RM override/friction complaints; Legal Ops turnaround time.
* **Gate 1 Criteria (Proceed to Wave 1B):** `A9` contract-remediation rate drops by at least half on pilot products with zero legal-rule defects; `A5` reduces repeated missing-doc loops without measurable drop in application completion or RM escalation spikes.

### Phase 2 (Wave 1B): Specialist Bottleneck Relief & Full Control Gating (Months 5–6)
* **Modules:**
  * **`A4` (Deep Core Integration #2 — Pilot  Full Rollout):** Deploy Entity-Mismatch Resolution Assistant for the 3-FTE Compliance Specialists (pre-fetching corporate registry/KYC evidence, scoring likely resolutions, and writing audited clearance directly to core KYC status).
  * **`A9` Scale-up:** Expand hard-gated contract generation (including automated four-eyes verification check) to 100% of products including High-exposure SMEs.
* **Monitoring Metrics:** `Resolve entity mismatch` median wait time (baseline 31h) and rework loop to KYC (baseline 1,160 / 38%); specialist utilization and override rates; provisional-signing incidence (baseline 6.5%); High-exposure four-eyes violations (baseline 5.8%); Legal Ops month-end queue variance.
* **Gate 2 Criteria (Proceed to Wave 2 / H2):** Zero unlogged or unauthorized KYC clearances in `A4` QA audit; measurable reduction in `Resolve entity mismatch` wait time and late blocks before Contract Prep; provisional signing below 1% (strictly documented emergency policy only); `A5` confirms higher first-pass document completeness feeding into Credit Analysts.

### Phase 3 (Wave 2A): Credit Analyst Relief — Standard Segments (Months 7–9)
* **Modules:**
  * **`A6` (Deep Core/LOS Integration #3 — H2 IT Capacity):** Deploy Financial-Statement Extraction & Normalization integrated with the credit model for Micro and standard SME applications first (where financial statements are more standardized), using confidence thresholds and mandatory automated balance-sheet reconciliation checks.
* **Monitoring Metrics:** `Normalize financial statements` service time (baseline 75m) and median wait (baseline 20h); extraction accuracy / analyst override rate; OCR failure rate on low-quality PDFs; `Credit analysis` median wait (baseline 28h).
* **Gate 3 Criteria (Proceed to Wave 2B):** Extraction accuracy meets credit-risk tolerance on audited sample; Credit Analyst throughput improves without increasing credit-model rework or downstream `Committee packaging` congestion.

### Phase 4 (Wave 2B): High-Exposure Scaling & Year-2 Readiness (Months 10–12)
* **Modules:**
  * **`A6` Expansion:** Extend assisted extraction to High-exposure SME financial statements with mandatory senior analyst line-item verification.
  * **Portfolio Stabilization & Year-2 Prep:** Re-run full process-mining discovery on post-rollout logs to verify shifted bottlenecks and validate prerequisites for Year-2 candidates—specifically `A10` (now safe behind `A9`'s proven KYC gate), `A7` (full valuer portal automation), `A8` (now fed by clean `A4`/`A6` artifacts), and potentially `A11` once control defects approach zero.
* **Monitoring Metrics:** End-to-end cycle time by segment/product; Credit Analyst & Compliance Specialist utilization balance; total rework rate; audit trail completeness (target: 100%).

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio 4: the Integrated Flow, Rework & Control Hybrid (`A4, A5, A6, A9`)**, paired with an immediate policy change to parallelize Equipment Finance collateral valuation requests.

### Why Portfolio 4 outperforms the alternatives
* **Beats Portfolio 1 (Quick-Win: `A1, A5, A9, A10`)** because Portfolio 1 optimizes slack and peripheral queues while leaving the bank's two true resource bottlenecks—Credit Analysts (9 FTE) and Compliance Specialists (3 FTE)—untouched. Automating intake (`A1`) and signing (`A10`) without relieving the middle of the process simply accelerates work into the 18h–31h internal bottlenecks.
* **Beats Portfolio 3 (Compliance-First: `A2, A3, A9, A12`)** because Portfolio 3 overloads IT's two-deep-integration H1 limit, deploys high-risk false-negative automation (`A3`), and invests in downstream symptom remediation (`A12`) instead of eliminating upstream rework and synchronization delays. Portfolio 4 achieves strong regulatory impact immediately via `A9` and `A4` while delivering tangible throughput gains.
* **Edges out Portfolio 2 (Bottleneck & Synchronization: `A4, A6, A7, A9`)** on Year-1 execution risk and dependency fit. While `A7` addresses the 58h external valuation wait, it primarily impacts Equipment Finance (71% required vs. 18% Working-Capital) and carries external partner dependency. By contrast, `A5` applies across *all* products, directly eliminates the 3,300-case missing-document loop (52% repeated), requires no deep core-banking integration in H1, and directly solves `A6`'s biggest vulnerability (incomplete/poor-quality documents). Meanwhile, a simple policy change to request collateral valuation earlier captures substantial parallelization benefit immediately, preserving `A7` and `A10` as clean, low-risk Year-2 additions.

### Why the highest-frequency activity (`A1`: Register application) should NOT be automated first
Senior management's instinct to automate `Register application` (11,880 cases, 8 min, very high feasibility) is a textbook **frequency-bias trap** contradicted by four pieces of process-mining evidence:
1. **It is not a bottleneck:** Median waiting time before `Register application` is only **1.5 hours** (versus 18h–58h downstream), and the Back Office pool (12 FTE) already drops **below 65% utilization after 15:00**. Automating it saves slack capacity that yields little end-to-end cycle-time reduction.
2. **It worsens downstream congestion:** Accelerating intake pushes uncleaned applications faster into `Classify product and exposure` (8% error rate), `Financial document review` (24% rework), and the heavily central 9-FTE Credit Analysts and 3-FTE Compliance Specialists—increasing work-in-process (WIP) and wait times at the true constraints.
3. **It crowds out high-value H1 IT capacity:** `A1` requires deep core-banking integration. Spending one of only two H1 deep-integration slots on `A1` forces the bank to delay either `A9` (which fixes 8.5%/14% contract remediation and 6.5% provisional-signing violations) or `A4` (which clears the 31h, 82%-critical-path entity-mismatch bottleneck).
4. **It fails Compliance's control mandate:** `A1` reduces low-complexity clerical effort without addressing any of the bank's regulatory exposures (provisional signing, four-eyes breaches, or contract-decision mismatches).

By contrast, prioritizing **`A9`, `A4`, `A5`, and `A6`** uses scarce IT and budget capacity where process-mining evidence shows end-to-end performance and compliance risk are actually governed—clearing synchronized bottlenecks, eliminating the largest rework loops, and establishing hard control gates that make downstream automation (`A10`, `A8`, `A11`) safe in Year 2.
