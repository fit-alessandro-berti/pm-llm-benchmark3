## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

I use an **issue-specific hierarchy**, not a total ordering of documents. A directive can validly change a local purchasing sequence without also having authority to remove segregation of duties or pre-commitment Compliance clearance. Likewise, a purchase can have a justified sequencing exception and an independently noncompliant approval.

**Authority: facts and interpretation.** GPIP-4 is board-approved; the COO has operational directive authority; the CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls. The Chief Risk Officer did not issue or subsequently ratify ECD-2. Previous acceptance of incident directives as temporary overlays supports some operational displacement, but the stated precedent does not extend to new-supplier Compliance clearance. I therefore presume that ECD-2 is an effective operational overlay, **not a blanket A7 exception**. Its possible equivalence to an A7 instrument remains an interpretive uncertainty, not an established fact.  

**Specificity and later-in-time rules.** ECD-2’s express permissions concerning PO timing and supplier-register status presumptively displace the corresponding local requirements within its scope. They do not, by implication, remove A1–A3. The CFO email has meaningful Finance interpretive weight despite being outside the controlled repository; its informal format does not make it irrelevant. Conversely, neither its later date nor “all operational approvals” establishes authority to waive Compliance or segregation of duties. I apply it prospectively from its sending, not as retrospective authorization for P-502.  

**Scope and necessity.** I interpret the local SOP’s “maintenance and production-support purchases” as including site maintenance, including the cafeteria repair. For ECD-2, I favor an incident-purpose interpretation that can include genuinely necessary backup-line capacity; Internal Audit’s proposed 24-hour limit is not written into the directive. I provisionally give P-503 the benefit of the broader interpretation, while requiring engineering substantiation of its contingency need. P-504’s stated purpose is outside ECD-2. Separately, B4 can operate before or after ECD-2, but requires evidence of imminent safety harm or a stoppage expected within six hours—not merely an emergency label.  

**Timing and approval semantics.** Rules apply at the actual relevant act. For commitment, I use the first externally binding purchasing undertaking, rather than automatically equating it with an ERP entry or the beginning of a telephone call. ECD eligibility is determined at commitment; recording obligations can mature after expiry. Crucially, **B2 does not expressly require pre-commitment approval**, whereas A2 does at EUR 50,000 and above. I do not import A2’s timing into a EUR 38,000 purchase. A6 prevents later approval from curing an actual pre-commitment requirement; it does not create such a requirement where none is stated.  

**Actor assumptions.** I provisionally treat an ordinary, valid `Plant_Approve` as the local final business/budget-owner approval and `Finance_Approve` as Controller approval. These mappings require verification, but named ordinary approvals are prima facie evidence of their usual functions. Generic Incident Command authorization is different: the supplied organizational facts expressly leave budget-owner authority unresolved. One event can evidence several approval functions, but only with evidence of the actors’ capacities. When joint Incident Command authorization supplies the final business approval, I treat both co-authorizers as participating final approvers. Two people being distinct from each other does not establish independence from the requester or committer.  

**Evidence and burden of proof.** Contemporaneous recordings, acknowledgments and signed records can outweigh ERP timestamps. ON-44 changes evidential weight, not permissions, and its stated outage is on **14 July**; it must not be generalized to the entire incident. P-505’s possible clock error and P-507’s document-system outage must be evaluated separately. “New supplier” and “unapproved supplier” are also distinct facts: neither implies the other.  

The party relying on an exception must substantiate its authority, scope and conditions. Nevertheless, a missing event in this incomplete record is not conclusive proof that the act never occurred. I treat an explicitly reported later approval as the operative approval unless there is a positively identified candidate earlier approval, such as joint authorization whose functional capacities need checking.

**Classification standard.** `COMPLIANT` means the ordinary applicable route is supported; `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` means an authorized departure and the remaining applicable controls are supported. `NONCOMPLIANT` means an established departure lacks a successful exception under the adopted interpretation. `INDETERMINATE` means a material unresolved issue could change the result. Severity concerns control exposure, not proven financial or contamination harm; confidence concerns the evidential and interpretive strength of the finding.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Policy breach** concerns what was done, by whom, under which effective rule. P-502’s reported post-commitment Compliance clearance is materially different from a database-order anomaly. P-503’s affirmative actor overlap is materially different from an absent event.

**Logging defect** concerns the representation of an act. P-505’s ERP clock may be wrong; P-507’s ERP entry may be late ingestion. Neither, alone, proves an untimely authorization or an out-of-window commitment.

**Late evidence of a timely act** must be separated into two questions: did authorization occur before commitment, and was its required durable record created by the recording deadline? P-507’s PDF is evidence of both an earlier authorization and a timely subsequent record. A document created after four hours but retrospectively dated to the authorization would not establish timely recording merely through its displayed event date.

**Inability to prove compliance** includes unknown actor capacities, missing form evidence and uncertain transaction times. A selected execution summary is not a complete inventory of telephone, paper and document-system evidence. Unlisted forms should be recorded as **unverified**, not automatically as late or absent. Likewise, a signed authorization document satisfies B6 only if its content and accepted evidence mapping make it an emergency form.

The four-hour C3 duty is an independent accountability requirement. A breach of that later duty should not automatically be transformed into proof that the earlier verbal authorization never occurred. For P-505, an authorization at 17:50 would ordinarily make its recording deadline **21:50**, even though the directive expires at 18:00.

**Immediate case-level actions.**

For **P-501**, retain the engineering forecast and ordinary approvers’ identities; establish whether SHIFT-1 was merely the committer. Do not manufacture retrospective delegation to fix a substitution that may never have occurred.

For **P-502**, preserve the supplier recording and obtain an independent review of the already-created supplier exposure, including the substance of the eventual Compliance decision. For **P-503**, obtain independent business/Risk review without PROC-3 supplying final approval, recover any actual earlier clearance, and substantiate the backup-line necessity. Later review should be labeled mitigation, not historical cure.

For **P-504**, correct the inappropriate ECD scope assertion, establish supplier history and route the expenditure through the applicable facilities-maintenance process. Do not infer a food-safety emergency from spoilage avoidance alone.

For **P-505**, preserve the call, full acknowledgment and clock evidence; reconstruct the actual commitment and the capacities exercised at 17:50. For **P-506**, cease reliance on the alleged verbal extension, review the missing pre-commitment business approval, and separately assess whether B4 genuinely applied. Any new authorization must be prospective for further commitments.

For **P-507**, preserve the original signed PDF, creation history and queue logs; resolve Finance/budget roles and the B6 mapping without raising a false late-recording finding from ERP ingestion alone. For **P-508**, preserve the evidenced Legal and Compliance clearances and determine whether the command authorization actually contained the required financial approvals.

Remediation affecting further orders or component use should be coordinated with Operations so that a control correction does not itself create an avoidable continuity or safety problem. The supplied record does not establish receipt, use, loss or actual contamination consequences.

**Monitoring design.** Preserve immutable raw events and separately store actual action time, durable-record creation time, ingestion time, clock uncertainty and evidence provenance. Resolve supplier status, actor delegations and applicable policy versions **as of commitment**, not from their current values.

Use data-conditional monitors for amount, newness, scope, effective windows and actor overlap. Report **satisfied, violated, pending and unverified** obligations separately, including the population requiring each control. Monitor emergency usage by purpose, issuer and supplier; expired-directive reliance; pre-commitment approval coverage; document-creation versus ingestion delays; and recurring requests for retrospective approval. A high eventual approval rate must not obscure a low pre-commitment approval rate.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

The following is a **prospective replacement clause**, not a reinterpretation that retrospectively determines these cases:

> **Authority and precedence.** GPIP integrity and approval requirements govern all purchases. Local procedures may add controls. A jointly signed COO–Incident Commander directive may expressly vary PO sequencing and approved-register requirements for purchases listed within its scope. Only the CRO or a formally predesignated Risk deputy may authorize other written, scoped and time-limited exceptions to controls not designated non-waivable below. CFO interpretations may define Finance delegations and evidence channels, but may not silently remove required approval functions. Ordinary emails and verbal instructions do not extend exception authority.
>
> **Scope.** Each directive must identify affected lines, eligible purposes and covered lots or a controlled eligibility register. Backup-line purchases are covered only when expressly listed and supported by Engineering’s documented contingency need and time-to-need assessment. A generic “site continuity” label is insufficient; cafeteria and other amenity purchases are excluded from this incident route. The local imminent-safety/six-hour-stoppage route remains separately available on documented facts.
>
> **Thresholds and independence.** At EUR 25,000 or above, Plant and Finance approvals must precede commitment; valid role delegation may substitute for the named office. At EUR 50,000 or above, these must include an independent budget owner and Finance Controller. Above EUR 100,000, or for any new supplier, Compliance clearance must precede commitment. Above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is additionally required. These pre-commitment functions and segregation of duties are non-waivable under emergency directives. Thresholds apply to aggregated purchase value, including related commitments and amendments; splitting purchases is prohibited.
>
> **Actors and suppliers.** Neither requester nor committer may participate as a final business approver, including as a joint co-approver. Two distinct Incident Command members must authorize each incident purchase before commitment. Their decision satisfies another approval requirement only when the record identifies the applicable authorized capacity. “New supplier” means no previous Northbridge purchase commitment under that supplier’s legal identity, irrespective of register status. Temporary register permission never substitutes for required Compliance clearance.
>
> **Time and evidence.** Commitment is the first externally binding purchasing undertaking, established from authenticated transaction evidence. Verbal command authorization must receive a durable record within four hours of authorization; the emergency form is due within 24 hours of commitment. An approved combined form may satisfy both. Authentic offline records count at creation, with ingestion separately recorded. Later approval mitigates but does not cure a missed prerequisite.
>
> **Expiry and safeguards.** Directives must state exact start and expiry times. Extensions require written approval by the competent issuers before expiry, renewed necessity evidence and independent Risk review. Verbal continuation is ineffective; accrued recording duties survive expiry. No automatic renewal is permitted. Recurring exceptions and emergency purchasing share must be reviewed weekly, with corrective actions to prevent emergency arrangements from becoming the ordinary purchasing process.
