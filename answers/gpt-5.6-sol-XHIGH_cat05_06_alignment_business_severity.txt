## 1. Materiality Framework

Alignment cost should be treated as a **structural diagnostic**, not a severity score. Business severity should consider the following dimensions together:

| Dimension | Materiality considerations |
|---|---|
| Control objective | Whether the deviation undermines coverage validation, loss assessment, fraud prevention, authorization, payment integrity, or claimant communication. Pre-payment fraud and authorization controls are generally more material than logging cleanliness. |
| Amount/exposure | Claim amount, potential recovery, aggregation across cases, and whether the beneficiary may be fraudulent. Amount increases exposure but does not alone determine severity. |
| Reversibility | Whether payment can still be stopped, recalled, or recovered. A bank-accepted payment is materially different from an unexecuted instruction. |
| Timing | Whether controls happened before payment, whether approval was merely recorded late, and whether notification met the 24-hour requirement. Sequence can be more important than event presence. |
| Customer/regulatory harm | Potential misdirected payment, delayed or duplicate communication, unfair denial, privacy concerns, audit failure, or breach of fraud-control obligations. |
| Recurrence | An isolated mapping defect is different from a connector affecting 74 cases or a rule defect producing four invalid auto-approvals. |
| Evidence reliability | Immutable system records and stable identifiers are stronger than verbal statements or mutable text links. Missing core-system evidence does not prove that an activity did not occur. |
| Model scope | Denied and withdrawn claims may legitimately follow paths absent from a model designed for payable claims. Such deviations may indicate model/population defects rather than operational failures. |

**Severity scale**

| Severity | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Non-material technical noise or fully fitting behavior with no identified policy issue. |
| 2 | Low business impact; evidence, mapping, correlation, or model-scope weakness requiring correction but no indication of an invalid payment. |
| 3 | Material but contained control, evidence, timing, or customer-service issue; potential harm is meaningful but limited or reversible. |
| 4 | Major policy or authorization failure associated with payment, meaningful exposure, recurrence, or elevated fraud/customer risk. |
| 5 | Critical pre-payment control failure involving high exposure, strong fraud indicators, or an irreversible payment made before mandatory controls. |

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Severity estimates the consequence if the interpretation is correct. Confidence estimates how strongly the evidence supports that interpretation and its cause. For example, CL-602 is severity 5 because of the exposure and missing mandatory fraud control, while confidence is high that evidence is absent but only medium that no fraud work occurred at all. A high-severity, medium-confidence case should be investigated urgently rather than automatically treated as either proven misconduct or harmless logging failure.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

1. **The cost function prices edits, not consequences.** Every visible log or model move costs 1. Thus CL-601 receives cost 3 for opening, autosaving, and closing a workbench, while CL-602 receives cost 1 for lacking evidence of a mandatory fraud screen on a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a recent bank change. The arithmetic is correct under the chosen cost function but has no inherent financial or compliance meaning.

2. **Synchronous control flow does not evaluate data conditions.** CL-609 and CL-610 both have cost 0 because the process tree permits `Auto Approve`. P1/P2 depend on amount, risk, and bank-change data that are not encoded in the tree. A zero-cost alignment therefore can contain a major or severe policy failure.

3. **Scope mismatch can generate many expensive model moves without a business failure.** CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 because the payable-claims model tries to complete approval, payment, and notification after denial or withdrawal. In business terms, not paying may be exactly correct. Their high cost diagnoses population/model incompleteness rather than four units of harm.

4. **Hidden moves are execution mechanics, not missing controls.** CL-605 contains `T(tau,c=0)`, a silent routing transition introduced in the Petri-net representation. It is fitting and should not be interpreted as an unlogged human or system activity. Its zero cost is appropriate for alignment semantics, but the same zero cost cannot validate policy data in CL-609 or CL-610.

5. **Activity granularity can turn one semantic action into two deviations.** In CL-611, `Assess Damage` is a log move and `Assess Loss` a model move, producing cost 2. If both labels refer to the same UI action and assessment artifact, the two edits represent one mapping defect, not an omitted assessment plus an unauthorized extra task.

6. **Duplicated telemetry inflates cost without duplicating business work.** CL-603’s second notification costs 1 even though the same message ID and retry evidence indicate a transport retry. The monthly set of 74 cases reinforces that interpretation. Conversely, if provider evidence showed two distinct deliveries, the same alignment shape could carry more customer impact. Cost alone cannot decide.

7. **Missing core events may coexist with substantive external evidence.** CL-606 costs 1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, yet a signed external-adjuster report exists before later controls. CL-608 costs 1 for missing core notification while a permitted CRM/portal message exists. The key issues are identifier stability, provenance, recipient, and retention—not merely whether the core emitted a matching label.

8. **Edit distance does not represent timing or irreversibility.** CL-604 costs 3, but the decisive business fact is that the bank accepted payment at 10:03 and approval was recorded at 10:20. Alignment cost does not increase because funds became difficult to recover, nor does it distinguish a late log entry from an actually unauthorized transfer.

9. **Alignment cost is not confidence.** A model move can mean nonperformance, off-system performance, failed correlation, a label mismatch, or an out-of-scope trace. CL-602 has strong evidence that fraud documentation is missing but weaker evidence about whether some fraud work happened. CL-606 has positive external evidence. Both have cost 1, but their evidentiary interpretations differ.

Raw cost remains useful for locating structural mismatches, comparing traces under a stable cost configuration, and identifying recurring event-quality patterns. It should be retained as a separate technical field—not converted directly into compliance risk, exposure, customer harm, or evidentiary confidence.

def parse_supplied_alignment(case_id, alignment_text):
    """Parse S/L/M/T moves from the supplied authoritative notation."""
    parsed = []

    for position, token in enumerate(alignment_text.split(""), start=1):
        token = token.strip()
        match = re.fullmatch(r"([SLMT])\((.*)\)", token)
        if not match:
            raise ValueError(f"Unrecognized alignment token: {token}")

        move_type = match.group(1)
        body = match.group(2).strip()

        if ",c=" in body:
            activity, cost_text = body.rsplit(",c=", 1)
            activity = activity.strip()
            move_cost = int(cost_text.strip())
        else:
            activity = body
            move_cost = 0

        parsed.append({
            "case": case_id,
            "position": position,
            "move_type": move_type,
            "activity": activity,
            "supplied_move_cost": move_cost,
            "move_pattern": f"{move_type}:{activity}",
        })

    return parsed
