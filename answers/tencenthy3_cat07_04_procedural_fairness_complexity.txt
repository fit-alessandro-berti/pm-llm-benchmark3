# 4. Plausible mechanisms

1. **Legitimate needs-based routing**  
   Minority-language status, accommodation requests, and higher risk scores genuinely require additional steps. This explains part of the variant mix and some cycle-time differences.

2. **Capacity shortages in rights-preserving steps**  
   A small bilingual workforce and a small accommodation specialist team create queues and forced handovers. This mechanism is not legitimate as a *permanent* condition; it converts protected needs into procedural disadvantage.

3. **Measurement bias in documentation and risk**  
   If forms, instructions, or completeness checks are not fully accessible, Group B may appear to have incomplete documentation. If `risk_score_band` reflects historical enforcement patterns, Group D may be over-routed to fraud review. Both would produce adjusted gaps that are not truly “explained by complexity.”

4. **Regional backlog and handover design**  
   Two regions have supervisor-review backlogs affecting B and C. The generalspecialistgeneral pattern adds transfer delay and may reduce decision consistency, increasing appeals.

5. **Decision opacity and inaccessible explanations**  
   Even if final decisions are similar, notifications that are not adequately translated or accommodated may lead to more appeals and rework, especially for B and D. This is a procedural fairness issue not visible in approval rates.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias**  
  `documentation_completeness` may be lower for Group B because of language barriers rather than missing evidence. `risk_score_band` may inherit prior systemic biases.

- **Missing applicant needs**  
  The log captures `disability_accommodation_requested` and `language_preference`, but not severity of disability, literacy, digital access, childcare, or transportation constraints. Dignity and comprehension are unobserved.

- **Unobserved resource workload**  
  Workload is only at daily granularity. Real-time queue length, caseworker skill mix, burnout, and concurrent assignments are missing, so waiting-time gaps cannot be fully attributed to individual behavior.

- **Appeal quality**  
  `appeal_flag` shows that an appeal occurred, but not its grounds, success rate, or whether the appeal corrected a procedural error. Higher appeal rates therefore cannot be interpreted as proof of wrong decisions or of mere confusion.

- **Historical bias in risk scores**  
  `risk_score_band` is treated as a control, yet it may be a proxy for prior contact with the system, neighborhood, or language group. Using it uncritically can launder historical disparity into “legitimate routing.”

- **Outcome narrowness**  
  `case_outcome` does not capture whether the applicant understood the decision, felt respected, or received correct benefit amount.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

- **Add capacity for language support or accommodation review**  
  *Expected effect:* reduced wait at V4/V5 and fewer handovers.  
  *Side effects:* hiring and training costs; if concentrated in few regions, temporary caseload imbalance; risk of superficial training if quality is not enforced.

- **Service-level targets for support steps**  
  Include `Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment` and `Accommodation Review  Eligibility Assessment` in timeliness SLAs.  
  *Side effects:* without added capacity, targets may pressure staff to rush support, reducing quality; metrics could be gamed by reclassifying activities.

- **Routing redesign to reduce handovers**  
  Allow bilingual or accommodation specialists to own the case from intake through eligibility rather than handing back to general workers.  
  *Side effects:* smaller specialist pool may become overloaded; need cross-training; possible loss of generalist redundancy.

- **Decision-explanation improvements**  
  Provide plain-language, translated, and accessible-format notifications with clear rationale.  
  *Side effects:* longer initial drafting time; need template governance to preserve consistency; but likely reduces avoidable appeals.

- **Monitoring adjusted disparities**  
  Track approval, cycle time, variant mix, and appeal rates by group after stratification.  
  *Side effects:* possible metric gaming (e.g., suppressing legitimate fraud review to equalize rates); requires careful interpretation to avoid punishing justified routing.

- **Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness**  
  Test for disparate impact, recalibrate risk model, and validate documentation standards across languages.  
  *Side effects:* if risk score has partial predictive value, removing it prematurely could change fraud detection; documentation standard changes may require policy and legal review.

*Crucially, none of these mitigations propose removing language support or accommodation review to speed up the process; those steps are rights-preserving and should be resourced, not eliminated.*
