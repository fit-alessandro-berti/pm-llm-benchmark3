# **OCEL 2.0 Flattening Analysis: Order-Fulfillment Process**

---

## **1. Analytical Assumptions**

### **Case Notion & Convergence/Divergence**
- **"Case notion"** refers to the unit of analysis that defines a coherent process trace. In this context, it must account for **shared events** (e.g., a dispatch event involving multiple orders) and **cross-order dependencies** (e.g., a package consolidating items from different orders).
- **"Convergence"** occurs when multiple process paths merge into a single event (e.g., multiple orders being invoiced together under `V2`). This can distort lifecycle analysis if the chosen lead object does not participate in the convergence.
- **"Divergence"** occurs when a single event splits into multiple branches (e.g., `D2` carrying `P2` and `P3`, which belong to different orders). Flattening on a single order may hide this divergence, while flattening on `Delivery` may artificially inflate event counts.

### **Copied Shared Events**
- Shared events (e.g., `e16` involving `O1, O2, O3`) are **both evidence and duplication**:
  - **Evidence**: They represent real-world consolidation (e.g., `D2` carrying multiple packages).
  - **Duplication**: If flattened on a single order, the same event appears multiple times, inflating event counts.
- **Treatment**: Shared events are **not silently propagated** through O2O links (per constraints). Instead, their presence must be explicitly accounted for in analysis.

### **Permitted Analytical Transformations**
- **Relation propagation** is **not** permitted unless explicitly justified (e.g., for synthetic interaction summaries).
- **Explicit transformations allowed**:
  - **Object interaction summaries** (e.g., counting how often `O2` and `O3` appear together in events).
  - **Equivalent-execution clustering** (grouping traces that represent the same logical process despite different lead objects).
  - **OC-DFG (Object-Centric DFG)** and **OTG (Object Transition Graph)** to visualize cross-object dependencies.

## **2. Object-Type Comparison Matrix**

| **Object type** | **Natural business question** | **What is preserved** | **What is lost** | **Convergence risk** | **Divergence risk** | **Expected event duplication** | **Expected DFG/performance distortion** | **Overall suitability** |
|----------------|-----------------------------|----------------------|----------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Order (O)** | *How long does it take to fulfill a single order?* | Full lifecycle for individual orders (e.g., `O1` from `e01` to `e28`). | Cross-order dependencies (e.g., `D2` consolidating `O1, O2, O3`) are fragmented. | High (e.g., `V2` consolidates `O2, O3` but appears as separate traces). | Low (each order has its own trace). | Moderate (shared events like `e16` appear multiple times). | Lifecycle may appear artificially short if shared events are excluded. | **Moderate** (good for per-order analysis but hides consolidation). |
| **Item (I)** | *How often are items delayed or mispacked?* | Fine-grained tracking of individual items (e.g., `I11` from `e05` to `e23`). | Loses order-level context (e.g., `O1` is split across `I11` and `I12`). | Low (items are not consolidated). | High (items from different orders may share events, e.g., `I21` and `I31` in `e13`). | High (e.g., `e13` appears for both `I21` and `I31`). | DFG may show spurious dependencies between unrelated orders. | **Low** (too granular; risks overfitting). |
| **Package (P)** | *How efficient is package consolidation?* | Captures cross-order consolidation (e.g., `P3` contains `I21` and `I31`). | Loses item-level details (e.g., `I11` vs. `I12` in `P1`). | Moderate (e.g., `D2` consolidates `P2` and `P3`). | Low (packages are discrete). | Low (each package has one trace). | May underrepresent item-level variability. | **High** (best for consolidation analysis). |
| **Delivery (D)** | *How reliable are delivery routes?* | Shows full delivery lifecycle (e.g., `D1` from `e15` to `e23`). | Loses order/item granularity (e.g., `D2` carries `O1, O2, O3` but no order-specific details). | High (e.g., `D2` involves multiple orders). | Low (deliveries are singular). | Low (each delivery has one trace). | May obscure order-specific issues. | **High** (good for logistics but loses order context). |
| **Invoice (V)** | *How are invoices split or consolidated?* | Captures invoice consolidation (e.g., `V2` for `O2, O3`). | Loses order-level details (e.g., `O1` and `O4` are separate). | High (e.g., `V2` merges two orders). | Low (invoices are singular). | Low (each invoice has one trace). | May hide payment discrepancies (e.g., `M2` and `M3` for `V2`). | **Moderate** (good for billing but loses order granularity). |
| **Payment (M)** | *How are payments distributed across invoices?* | Shows partial/complete payments (e.g., `M2` and `M3` for `V2`). | Loses invoice/order context (e.g., `M1` is for `O1` only). | Low (payments are singular). | High (e.g., `V2` is paid in two instalments). | Low (each payment has one trace). | May misrepresent payment timing if not linked to invoices. | **Low** (too narrow; risks missing context). |

## **3. Concrete Distortion Analysis**

### **1. Cross-Order Package Distortion (Flattening on Order)**
- **Affected flattening**: Order (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`, `O4`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e13` (packs `I21` and `I31` into `P3`), `e14` (packs `I32` and `I41` into `P4`).
- **Distortion**:
  - If flattened on `O2`, `e13` appears as a single event, but `O3`’s items are hidden.
  - If flattened on `O3`, `e13` and `e14` both appear, but the **consolidation logic** (why `I21` and `I31` are in the same package) is lost.
- **Effect**: **Harmful** for understanding cross-order efficiency.
- **DFG Impact**: Artificial divergence in `O3`’s trace due to shared events.

### **2. Consolidated Delivery Distortion (Flattening on Order)**
- **Affected flattening**: Order (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e16` (dispatches `D2` carrying `P2` and `P3`).
- **Distortion**:
  - `O1`’s trace includes `e16`, but `O2` and `O3`’s traces also include `e16`.
  - The **consolidation benefit** (single delivery for multiple orders) is invisible.
- **Effect**: **Informative if acknowledged** (shows that orders are co-delivered but obscures the logic).
- **DFG Impact**: Spurious "parallel" dispatch events in multiple traces.

### **3. Consolidated Invoicing Distortion (Flattening on Order)**
- **Affected flattening**: Order (`O2`, `O3`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e19` (issues `V2` for `O2` and `O3`).
- **Distortion**:
  - `O2`’s trace shows `e19`, but `O3`’s trace also shows `e19`.
  - The **consolidation reason** (shared customer, bulk discount) is lost.
- **Effect**: **Context-dependent** (useful for per-order billing but misleading for cross-order analysis).
- **DFG Impact**: Artificial convergence in billing events.

### **4. Partial Payment Distortion (Flattening on Invoice)**
- **Affected flattening**: Invoice (`V2`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e22` (`M2` partially settles `V2`), `e25` (`M3` completes settlement).
- **Distortion**:
  - If flattened on `V2`, the **partial payment logic** is clear.
  - If flattened on `O2` or `O3`, the payment appears as two separate events, obscuring the **invoice-level settlement**.
- **Effect**: **Harmful if payment timing is critical**.
- **DFG Impact**: Lifecycle appears incomplete if only one payment is visible.

### **5. Near-Singleton Lifecycle (Flattening on Package)**
- **Affected flattening**: Package (`P1`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e11` (packs `I11`), `e15` (dispatches `D1`), `e23` (confirms delivery).
- **Distortion**:
  - `P1` has a **short, linear lifecycle** (pack  dispatch  delivery).
  - If flattened on `Order`, `P1`’s events are mixed with `P2`’s, distorting **package-level efficiency**.
- **Effect**: **Informative for logistics but misleading for order-level analysis**.
- **DFG Impact**: Underrepresents complexity of multi-package orders.

### **6. Event Duplication in Cross-Order Flattening (Flattening on Item)**
- **Affected flattening**: Item (`I21`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e13` (packs `I21` into `P3`), `e16` (dispatches `D2` carrying `P3`).
- **Distortion**:
  - `I21`’s trace includes `e13` and `e16`, but so does `I31`’s trace.
  - The **shared dispatch** (`D2`) is duplicated, inflating event counts.
- **Effect**: **Harmful for performance metrics** (e.g., "items take 2 hours to dispatch" when some are co-delivered).
- **DFG Impact**: Artificial parallelism in item-level traces.

### **7. Performance Bias in Delivery Flattening**
- **Affected flattening**: Delivery (`D1`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e15` (dispatch), `e23` (confirm delivery).
- **Distortion**:
  - `D1`’s lifecycle is **short and clean**, but `D2`’s lifecycle includes **multiple orders**, distorting **delivery efficiency metrics**.
  - If flattened on `Order`, `D2`’s events are split, making it seem like orders take longer.
- **Effect**: **Context-dependent** (delivery-centric analysis may overlook order-specific delays).

### **8. Lifecycle Truncation in Payment Flattening**
- **Affected flattening**: Payment (`M1`).
- **Events/Objects**: `e21` (records `M1` for `V1`).
- **Distortion**:
  - `M1`’s trace is **too short** (only one event).
  - If flattened on `Order`, the **payment’s relation to invoicing** is lost.
- **Effect**: **Harmful for financial reconciliation**.
- **DFG Impact**: No meaningful process model for payments alone.

## **4. Traditional vs. Object-Centric Analysis Portfolio**

### **Flattened Logs to Retain**
| **Flattening** | **Use Case** | **Limitations** |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Order (O)** | Per-order performance, compliance checks. | Hides cross-order consolidation. |
| **Package (P)** | Logistics efficiency, consolidation analysis. | Loses item/order granularity. |
| **Delivery (D)** | Delivery route optimization. | Loses order-specific details. |

### **Questions Requiring Object-Centric Analysis**
1. **Cross-order consolidation analysis**:
   - Use **OC-DFG** to show how `O2` and `O3` interact via `P3` and `D2`.
   - Use **ET–OT graph** to visualize shared events (`e13`, `e16`).
2. **Payment-invoicing reconciliation**:
   - Use **OTG** to track how `V2` is paid in two instalments (`M2`, `M3`).
3. **Item-level mispacking**:
   - Use **object interactions summary** to count how often `I11` and `I12` are split across packages.
4. **Equivalent-execution clustering**:
   - Group traces where the **logical process** is the same (e.g., `O1` and `O4` both have single-item orders, despite different packages).

### **Reconciling Contradictory Findings**
- **Example**: Flattening on `Order` shows `O1` takes 12 hours, but flattening on `Delivery` shows `D2` (carrying `O1, O2, O3`) takes 5 hours.
  - **Resolution**: Use **OC-DFG** to show that `O1`’s actual time includes **shared dispatch** (`e16`), which is not visible in order-centric traces.
- **Example**: Flattening on `Invoice` shows `V2` is paid in two steps, but order-centric traces split payments.
  - **Resolution**: Use **ET–OT graph** to link `M2` and `M3` to `V2`, then propagate to orders if needed.

## **5. Lead-Object Decision**

### **Position: B. Use a Primary Lead Object Plus Explicitly Secondary Lifecycles**

#### **Decision Criteria**
1. **No single object captures all perspectives**:
   - `Order` is best for per-order analysis but hides consolidation.
   - `Package` is best for logistics but loses order context.
2. **Cross-object dependencies are critical**:
   - Shared events (`e13`, `e16`, `e19`) require **multiple views**.
3. **PM4Py supports hybrid analysis**:
   - Can flatten on `Order` for primary analysis, then use **OC-DFG** and **ET–OT** for cross-object insights.

#### **Strongest Supporting Evidence**
- **Consolidation is explicit in OCEL**:
  - `D2` carries `P2` and `P3`  **delivery-centric flattening** is useful.
  - `V2` bills `O2` and `O3`  **invoice-centric flattening** is useful.
- **PM4Py tools enable reconciliation**:
  - `pm4py.discover_ocdfg()` can show how `O2` and `O3` interact via `P3`.
  - `pm4py.discover_etot()` can show shared events (`e16`) linking multiple orders.

#### **Strongest Counterargument**
- **Overhead of managing multiple views**:
  - Requires **explicit transformation** (e.g., `ocel_objects_interactions_summary`).
  - May confuse stakeholders used to single-lead analysis.

#### **Residual Risk**
- **Misinterpretation of shared events**:
  - If analysts ignore `ET–OT` and assume `e16` is only for `O1`, they may misattribute delays.
- **Mitigation**: Document that **flattening is context-dependent** and use **equivalent-execution clustering** to group logically similar traces.

#### **Conditions to Change Decision**
- If the **primary question is strictly per-order** (e.g., "Why did O1 take longer?"), then **Order-centric flattening** may suffice.
- If the **primary question is logistics optimization**, then **Package/Delivery-centric flattening** is better.

## **7. Sensitivity and Validation Plan**

### **1. Remove/Copyweight Shared Events**
- **Test**: Run flattening on `Order` while **excluding shared events** (e.g., `e16`).
- **Expected**: Order lifecycles become **shorter and less realistic**.
- **Purpose**: Validate whether shared events are **artificially inflating metrics**.

### **2. Compare Direct vs. Propagated Relations**
- **Test**: Manually propagate relations (e.g., `O1`  `D2` via `P1`  `D2`).
- **Expected**: **OC-DFG becomes denser**, but may introduce **spurious dependencies**.
- **Purpose**: Check if **implicit relations** distort analysis.

### **3. Change Lead Object**
- **Test**: Flatten on `Item` instead of `Order` and compare **DFG complexity**.
- **Expected**: **Item-level traces will show more divergence** but lose order context.
- **Purpose**: Validate whether **granularity trade-offs** are acceptable.

### **4. Frequency Metrics Comparison**
- **Test**: Compare:
  - **Event counts** (e.g., `e16` appears 3 times in order-centric flattening).
  - **Unique object counts** (e.g., `D2` is one delivery but involves 3 orders).
  - **Total objects in OC-DFG** (e.g., `O2` and `O3` interact via `P3`).
- **Expected**: **Discrepancies will highlight where flattening fails**.
- **Purpose**: Ensure **metrics are not misleadingly aggregated**.

---

### **Final Recommendation**
**Use Order-centric flattening as primary, supplemented by OC-DFG, ET–OT, and object interaction summaries.**
This balances **practicality** (order-level analysis) with **depth** (cross-object insights). The residual risk of misinterpretation is mitigated by **explicit documentation** of shared events and **validation via sensitivity checks**.
