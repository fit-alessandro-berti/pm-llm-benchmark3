## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A credible validation suite should start by defining one prediction cut per claim: the first `Coverage Check Completed` event, with `t0` equal to immediately after that event. If multiple coverage events exist, only the first one is the coverage-time prediction point unless the business explicitly defines a later re-check model.

The first layer is a **point-in-time availability check**. Every feature should carry at least four timestamps where applicable: event time, effective business time, warehouse ingestion time, and feature availability time. A feature is admissible only if the value used by the model was available no later than `t0`. This catches obvious failures such as `total_case_duration_hours`, `final_decision`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `last_resource_role`, and nightly same-day document snapshots. It also catches subtler failures such as `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, where the name is correct but the SQL cutoff is wrong.

The second layer is a **complete-trace lineage check**. Any aggregation over a full case, terminal field, “final” attribute, “last” attribute, complete variant, or activity pattern mined from the whole trace should be treated as suspect until proven prefix-only. A useful automated test is to recompute the feature twice: once on the full trace and once on the prefix truncated at `t0`. If the value requires post-`t0` events, it is not admissible for this model.

The third layer is a **normative process-conformance check**. A declarative or Petri-net/process-tree representation of the simplified claim process can enforce rules such as:

* `Claim Registered` precedes the first `Coverage Check Completed`.
* Evidence cycles, if any, occur after coverage and before or alongside later assessment work.
* `Damage Assessment Completed` must occur before a normal `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
* `Fraud Investigation Opened` is optional and may run in parallel after a trigger.
* `Appeal` is enabled only after a `Settlement Decision Recorded` with Partial or Deny.
* A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` can be legitimate only when it follows a valid appeal.
* `Claim Closed` follows a final decision or withdrawal and should be terminal.

These checks distinguish an actually impossible pattern from a merely future-possible one. For example, `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically legitimate in an appeal-stage prefix, but impossible as an observed fact at coverage time. By contrast, `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is normatively suspicious even outside the prediction-time question, unless the edge is caused by timestamp ties or ingestion-order defects.

The fourth layer is a **same-timestamp ambiguity check**. Where two events have identical timestamps and no reliable sequence index, the validator should not infer a strict order. The pair should be represented as a partial order or flagged as ambiguous. A high-impact sequence explanation should not be allowed to rely on arbitrary ordering among equal-timestamp events. This is especially important for Case Z.

The fifth layer is an **activity-enabledness check at `t0`**. Immediately after coverage completion, `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Documents Requested`, or `Fraud Investigation Opened` may be future-possible depending on case state, but they have not yet happened. `Appeal` is not enabled because no Partial or Deny decision has yet occurred. `Claim Closed` is not enabled because no final decision or withdrawal has happened. Therefore, an explanation at `t0` may say “the model estimates higher risk because the prefix resembles claims that later required more work,” but it may not say “this claim breached because it had an appeal” or “because it followed a future full-trace path.”

DFG, footprint, and alignment checks are useful but have limits. A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule. It may reflect logging artifacts, timestamp ties, batch ingestion, duplicate events, or a coarse activity label. Conversely, a low-frequency edge can be normatively valid if it represents a rare appeal path. The audit should therefore compare discovered behavior against the declared process semantics rather than treating frequency as law.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal.**
Examples include `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and `initial_reserve` if its initial value is proven to be frozen before `t0`. A repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` may also belong here. The permissible claim is: “Historically, claims with this prefix-time property had different breach rates.” The impermissible claim is: “Changing this field would necessarily reduce breach risk.” Complexity, reserve, and prior-claim history may reflect underlying claim difficulty, but SHAP importance does not identify a causal mechanism.

**Process-state or congestion signal.**
`queue_length_at_t0` is the strongest surviving example. `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is also a process-state/slack feature. If historical team assignment at `t0` is rebuilt, current team workload or queue ownership may be process-state signals too. These features can support operational triage more credibly than future trace features, but even here actionability is not automatic. A high queue may indicate congestion, but moving one claim forward can delay others. The explanation should refer to “current workload and remaining slack,” not blame a final team or claim that the queue alone caused the breach.

**Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review.**
`claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and possibly `initial_reserve` can be predictive while also acting as proxies for repair-network access, language, claim type, socioeconomic status, documentation quality, or broker behavior. These features should not be deleted reflexively, because removing them can hide disparities rather than solve them. But they require subgroup calibration, error-rate analysis, counterfactual sensitivity checks, and a review of how predictions will be used. The allowed claim is a cautious association; the disallowed claim is that region, channel, or prior-claim history is a direct causal delay driver or a fair basis for worse service.

**Drift/calendar proxy.**
`calendar_week` is available at `t0`, but it is entangled with holiday effects and an operating-policy change. It may improve short-term prediction while harming generalization. The explanation should say that the model has learned a time-period association, not that the week itself is operationally worse. More credible alternatives are explicit policy-version, staffing-level, holiday, backlog, and vendor-capacity features. This feature needs out-of-time validation and monitoring after policy changes.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired.**
`risk_score_current`, employee experience joined as of extraction date, `final_handler_team`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` as currently implemented, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, and any latest/current/last/final warehouse attribute fall into this category. Some can be repaired by storing historical values as of `t0`. Others, such as `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, `full_trace_variant_id`, complete-trace sequence patterns, and `last_resource_role`, are not repairable as coverage-time facts; they must be removed or replaced with genuinely prefix-derived versions.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Rebuild the feature store around point-in-time semantics.** Every feature should have a source event time, business effective time, ingestion time, feature computation time, and availability time. Model training and scoring should use an as-of join with `available_at <= t0`, not extraction-date joins.

2. **Create immutable historical versions of mutable fields.** Risk scores, reserves, handler team, resource role, employee experience, assignment state, and queue state should be stored as time-versioned records. Avoid “current,” “latest,” “final,” and “last” fields unless they are explicitly converted into prefix-time versions.

3. **Recompute the training matrix from truncated prefixes.** For each claim, keep the label from the eventual 10-day settlement outcome, but compute features only from events and attributes available immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Run an automated diff between full-trace and prefix-trace feature values to detect hidden leakage.

4. **Remove or replace contaminated features before trusting explanations.** Drop `total_case_duration_hours`, complete-trace variants, future appeal flags, final decisions, full-case sequence patterns, final handler fields, and complete-case last-role fields. Repair features such as prior claim count, reserve, risk score, document snapshots, and employee experience only if historical timestamps support them.

5. **Recalibrate and re-threshold after feature removal.** The old probabilities are not reliable once strong leaked predictors are removed. Re-estimate discrimination, calibration, subgroup calibration, alert volume, and intervention thresholds using time-based validation.

6. **Add process-conformance tests to the explanation pipeline.** Explanations should be checked against the normative process before release. The validator should flag impossible orderings, future-only activities, same-timestamp ambiguity, appeal-before-decision violations, and invalid repeated settlement-decision semantics. Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events should be allowed only when justified by an intervening valid appeal.

7. **Use a prefix-aware explanation allowlist.** Local explanations should be allowed to mention only features certified as available at `t0`. Sequence explanations should be generated from the prefix, not the complete trace. Future activities may be described only as model-estimated risks, never as observed reasons.

8. **Test explanation stability.** Compare explanations across retraining windows, bootstrapped samples, feature-removal experiments, and policy/holiday periods. A feature whose importance collapses after removing leaked variables or shifting the validation period should be treated cautiously.

9. **Run proxy and fairness analysis without blindly deleting sensitive proxies.** Region, channel, prior claim count, reserve, and language/network-related mediators should be assessed for subgroup calibration, false-positive rates, false-negative rates, disparate impact, and operational use. Where possible, replace broad proxies with actionable operational mediators such as repair-network capacity, translation availability, document completeness, or queue ownership.

10. **Monitor schema, process, and lineage drift.** Add production checks for new activity labels, missing sequence indices, duplicate or same-timestamp events, changed warehouse refresh times, policy changes, holiday effects, and sudden importance spikes in terminal-looking features. Explanation validation should fail closed when a feature’s availability timestamp or lineage is missing.
