## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

### Smooth path

1. **Chemistry waiting and service**
   - At 15:30, the two analysts become free approximately at 15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30.
   - Under risk-priority selection, B-417 may enter service after the urgent sample, potentially around 16:30–17:30. Under FIFO, three ordinary samples may remain ahead, making a Friday start less likely.
   - Service is 1.5–4.5 h. A Friday start will often cross the 18:00 close, so unfinished work pauses for **62 calendar hours**, from Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00.
   - Consequently, a reasonable normal-result envelope is:
     - optimistic Friday completion: approximately 17:30–18:00;
     - more commonly, Monday 08:30–12:30;
     - unfavorable FIFO/long-service case: Monday afternoon.
   - Queue waiting and service are kept separate: the sample first waits for analyst selection, then consumes 1.5–4.5 laboratory working hours.

2. **Microbiology incubation and readout**
   - Incubation becomes ready Sunday 12:00–20:00.
   - With on-call authorization, dispatch plus readout gives a usable result roughly Sunday 13:30–Monday 00:30. QA is open only until 13:00 Saturday and not Sunday, so even an early Sunday result generally waits for Monday QA unless continuously available QA is separately authorized, which is not assumed.
   - Without authorization, the result is read Monday from 08:00, generally usable around 08:30–09:30.

3. **Parallel join**
   - The join is:
     \[
     T_{\text{join}}=\max(T_{\text{chem usable}},T_{\text{micro usable}})
     \]
   - It is **not** chemistry duration plus microbiology duration.
   - Even if microbiology is technically/readout-complete Sunday, QA cannot start routinely until Monday. Chemistry spillover can then determine whether the join is Monday morning or afternoon.
   - In the central smooth case, join is approximately Monday 09:00–13:00.

4. **QA waiting and service**
   - The five batches currently queued are not simply placed ahead of B-417 because QA has Friday and Saturday capacity, and new batches arrive before Monday.
   - For a Monday join, use about 1–8 open hours of waiting centrally, with 8–16+ hours under overdispersed congestion.
   - Service adds about 1–3.5 working hours. With two reviewers Monday, central completion of QA is Monday late morning through evening; congestion can push it into Tuesday.

5. **Optional rework/investigation**
   - None on the smooth scenario by definition.

6. **Final release**
   - Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 continuous hours.
   - Smooth release is centrally Monday 11:00–19:00, with Tuesday possible under QA congestion.

**Absolute earliest smooth exception:** If incubation is ready Sunday at 12:00, weekend authorization is immediate, dispatch/readout takes near its minimum, and QA exception coverage is assumed, release could be Sunday afternoon. Under the stated routine QA calendar, however, Monday morning is the defensible operational earliest.

### Chemistry rerun scenario

1. **Chemistry waiting and first service**
   - Same selection uncertainty as above. A Friday start may be interrupted at 18:00; otherwise it begins/resumes Monday.
   - The first usable analytical assessment is therefore likely Friday near close or Monday morning/afternoon.

2. **Rerun**
   - Borderline probability is 0.10–0.20 before conditioning on the excursion. If the excursion is confirmed, the upper part of this range or higher should be favored.
   - Rerun receives priority and consumes 1.0–3.0 laboratory working hours.
   - If borderline is identified Friday too late to finish, the rerun pauses at 18:00 and resumes Monday. If identified Monday morning, it may finish Monday late morning or afternoon.
   - Central usable chemistry result: Monday 10:00–16:00; unfavorable queue/long first test and rerun: Tuesday morning.

3. **Microbiology**
   - With no contamination indication, primary result is generally available Sunday with on-call or Monday 08:30–09:30 without it.

4. **Parallel join**
   - Here:
     \[
     T_{\text{join}}=\max(T_{\text{chem after rerun}},T_{\text{primary micro result}})
     \]
   - The chemistry branch often becomes the dominant clock, particularly if the rerun occurs Monday or the first run did not finish before the weekend.

5. **QA waiting and service**
   - A later Monday join meets a different, uncertain queue than an 08:30 join. Approximate wait plus service is 2–10 open hours centrally, potentially spilling into Tuesday.
   - A rerun also increases the probability of an investigation, so the no-investigation completion range should not be used unconditionally.

6. **Optional investigation**
   - Conditional investigation probability is in the 0.30–0.55 band because a rerun is an anomaly trigger.
   - Documentation-only: add 8–20 QA working hours, often moving the result to Wednesday–Friday.
   - Laboratory investigation: add 3–7 calendar days.

7. **Final event**
   - Release adds 0.25–0.75 h after acceptance.
   - Critical-defect rejection is possible only if investigation establishes such a defect; recording adds 0.5–1.0 h.

### Delayed microbiology readout or confirmation scenario

#### A. Primary result normal, but no weekend readout

1. **Chemistry**
   - Chemistry may finish Friday or Monday morning. Its elapsed time is not added to the microbiology elapsed time.

2. **Microbiology**
   - Incubation matures Sunday 12:00–20:00.
   - With probability roughly 0.55–0.75, authorization is not obtained. The technically ready result waits through Sunday night for staffing, then readout/recording finishes Monday about 08:30–09:30.

3. **Parallel join**
   - If chemistry is already usable, microbiology sets the join at Monday 08:30–09:30.
   - If chemistry was interrupted or queued, join instead occurs at the later chemistry time. Thus Monday morning is a floor, not a guaranteed join.

4. **QA**
   - Monday arrivals and backlog are uncertain. Two reviewers improve capacity, but overdispersion prevents assuming immediate service.
   - A central release remains Monday 12:00–20:00; Tuesday is plausible under congestion.

#### B. Contamination indication and confirmation

1. **First incubation and readout**
   - If an on-call microbiologist reads the indication Sunday, confirmation can start Sunday.
   - Without on-call, the indication is not observed until Monday 08:30–09:30, delaying confirmation initiation despite incubation already being technically ready.

2. **Confirmation incubation and second readout**
   - Add 20–30 continuous hours from the indication/readout point.
   - Sunday-start confirmation can mature Monday or early Tuesday; Monday-start confirmation matures Tuesday morning through Tuesday afternoon.
   - A second staffed readout adds 0.5–1.5 h. If maturation occurs outside 08:00–18:00, it waits until the next microbiology opening.

3. **Chemistry**
   - Usually complete by the time the confirmation is usable, although a correlated rerun may coexist if a temperature excursion is confirmed.

4. **Parallel join**
   - The join becomes:
     \[
     \max(T_{\text{chem, including any rerun}},T_{\text{confirmed micro result}})
     \]
   - Microbiology normally dominates, producing a Tuesday or Wednesday join.

5. **QA and investigation**
   - QA then adds uncertain queue waiting plus 1–3.5 h service.
   - An indication places investigation probability in the 0.30–0.55 range; a confirmed excursion strengthens this risk further.
   - Thus a contamination path can end Tuesday/Wednesday if resolved benignly, but frequently feeds the later investigation tail.

6. **Final event**
   - Benign confirmation leads toward release.
   - Confirmed critical contamination may ultimately lead to rejection; the 0.5–1.0 h rejection-recording duration is negligible compared with confirmation and investigation.

### QA query or investigation-tail scenario

1. **Pre-join branches**
   - Use the applicable normal, rerun, or contamination branch. Branch times remain parallel up to the join.

2. **QA waiting and initial service**
   - At the future join, queue length is latent. Approximate central waiting plus initial review is 2–10 open hours, but an overdispersed arrival burst can push service into the next QA day.

3. **Documentation Query**
   - Probability 0.15–0.30, consistent with the 0.82 completeness score.
   - Add 4–12 **QA working hours**, not plain elapsed hours.
   - Example: a query raised Monday 16:00 gets four QA hours by Monday 20:00, but a 12-hour resolution consumes eight additional hours Tuesday, returning around Tuesday 16:00 before resumed review.
   - A query late Friday may use Friday evening and Saturday morning, then pause until Monday.

4. **Documentation-only investigation**
   - If opened, about 60–80% are stated to be documentation-only, though this fraction is uncertain.
   - Add 8–20 QA working hours. From Monday afternoon, this usually implies Tuesday through Thursday calendar completion, followed by resumed/final review.

5. **Laboratory investigation**
   - The remaining investigation cases add 3–7 calendar days. This runs across nights/weekends as a calendar-duration assumption, but final QA still observes its calendar.
   - A Monday/Tuesday opening therefore produces a terminal event approximately Friday through the following Wednesday, with later outcomes if query and investigation are sequential.

6. **Dependence and final event**
   - Confirmed excursion makes chemistry rerun, microbiology indication, and investigation more likely together. Therefore the severe tail is a **clustered path**, not the product of independent low probabilities.
   - Most such cases still end in release after resolution; a confirmed critical defect leads to rejection recording in 0.5–1.0 h.

  - **Electronic Release** is the dominant terminal event, most likely on **Monday afternoon/evening or Tuesday**.
  - **Batch Rejected** is a plausible but unquantifiable minority outcome concentrated in confirmed contamination/critical-defect and laboratory-investigation paths. No supplied base rate supports a precise rejection probability.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Microbiology weekend authorization and staffed readout timing — calendar uncertainty**
   - This decides whether a Sunday-ready incubation is converted promptly into a usable result or waits until Monday. It also delays initiation of confirmation when an indication exists.

2. **Deviation path, especially laboratory investigation — path uncertainty**
   - Opening an investigation adds either 8–20 working hours or 3–7 calendar days and dominates the severe tail. Its probability jumps after rerun, indication, or excursion.

3. **Latent temperature excursion — dependence uncertainty**
   - It simultaneously raises chemistry-borderline, microbiology-indication, and investigation risks. It therefore shifts probability from isolated delays toward correlated, multi-stage slow paths.

4. **QA queue at the actual join time — queue uncertainty**
   - The present five-batch count is a weak predictor of Monday’s queue because service occurs before then, arrivals continue at 0.35/h during opening hours, arrivals are overdispersed, and Monday staffing doubles.

5. **Chemistry queue order and Friday spillover — queue/calendar uncertainty**
   - The FIFO versus risk-priority mixture determines whether B-417 starts Friday, while the 18:00 closure converts a modest remaining-work difference into a weekend pause. Service-time variation and rerun risk amplify this boundary effect.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most forecast-changing observation is **resolution of the temperature-excursion flag**.

- **If ruled out:** shift weight toward a normal chemistry result, normal primary microbiology result, and the Monday QA/release cluster; narrow the severe tail and favor the lower ends of rerun, indication, and investigation probabilities.
- **If confirmed:** increase the joint probability of chemistry rerun and microbiology indication rather than adjusting them independently; also move investigation probability toward its 0.30–0.55 anomalous-path band. The central forecast moves later, and substantially more weight shifts to Wednesday-through-following-week outcomes and possible rejection.
- **If still unresolved at QA:** even normal test results may attract additional scrutiny or an investigation, so uncertainty itself can delay acceptance.

A secondary update trigger is **the Sunday microbiology event comprising technical readiness plus on-call authorization/readout outcome**. A normal Sunday result sharply reduces branch uncertainty; no authorization anchors the join to Monday morning or later; an indication initiates the 20–30 h confirmation clock and materially moves the forecast into Tuesday/Wednesday and investigation-tail territory.
