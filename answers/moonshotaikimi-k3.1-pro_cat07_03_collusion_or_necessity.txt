# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Lena–Kim: fast bank-change/payment sequences, V-77x concentration, and loops**

1. **Legitimate mass maintenance.** The banking-platform change could have produced preauthorized, batch-processed bank-detail updates, with procurement-case linkage omitted during logging.  
   **Supports:** contemporaneous maintenance tickets covering the relevant vendor IDs and regions; independently approved change lists; callback or other bank verification; old/new bank values and effective dates consistent with the project; payments matching valid invoices.  
   **Weakens:** tickets absent or post-dated, changes outside project scope, no independent verification, payments released before verification, or repeated post-approval bank changes without documented corrections. Missing ticket linkage in the log is not itself proof that tickets were absent.

2. **Role specialization and risk-based routing.** Lena performs bank changes, while AP_Kim handles higher-risk suppliers; their repeated pairing could therefore be a product of work allocation.  
   **Supports:** documented routing rules, comparable Lena–Kim volumes in earlier periods, similar routing for other high-risk suppliers, and no manual queue overrides.  
   **Weakens:** ad hoc routing concentrated only on this pair, dispatcher overrides, or exception rates remaining anomalous after matching on vendor, amount, region, and contract type.

3. **Manual-upload timing and legitimate correction loops.** Seventeen minutes may represent upload or queue-processing time rather than actual review time, and loops may record corrections before release.  
   **Supports:** original source timestamps showing adequate review separation, correction reasons and attachments, and loops completed before payment release.  
   **Weakens:** source timestamps confirming very short independent review, missing correction artifacts, or further bank changes after payment release.

**Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo: fast emergency approvals and escalation loops**

1. **A genuine rush/on-call channel.** Emergency medical supplies and weekend facilities repairs can require immediate sourcing and approval; Gita and Hugo have relevant delegated roles.  
   **Supports:** independently timestamped emergency requisitions, clinical or operational demand, repair work orders, on-call logs, override preceding approval, delivery/service evidence, and reasonable prices.  
   **Weakens:** unsupported emergency flags, recurring nonurgent purchases routed through the channel, threshold-splitting, missing receipt evidence, or emergencies created only after approval.

2. **Clarification or correction loops.** Gita–Carla–Gita and Hugo–Dan–Hugo sequences may reflect requests for specifications, quotes, or repair details rather than rubber-stamping.  
   **Supports:** approval comments, changed quote/version identifiers, documented responses, and timely independent retrospective justification.  
   **Weakens:** no substantive changes or comments across repeated loops, persistent late justification, or source records showing that the same person supplied both sides of the purported review.

**Apparent four-eyes and same-person justification violations**

1. **Migration, shared-account, or mapping artifacts.** Batch entry can distort justification timing; Bot_PO mapping can make a bot and human appear identical; shared Gita attribution can conceal the actual approver.  
   **Supports:** original spreadsheet authors and timestamps, migration logs, corrected mapping tables, bot job IDs, and independent source approval IDs demonstrating different actors.  
   **Weakens:** source systems confirming that one human performed both actions, or inability to establish who approved. Batch migration may explain timestamps without explaining authorship.

2. **Authorized role design or emergency delegation.** The 31 vendor-check/bank-change pairs may be intentional role design, and some emergency actions may have authorized coverage arrangements.  
   **Supports:** approved role matrices, delegations effective at the event time, compensating review, and compliant independent justification.  
   **Weakens:** retrospective or undocumented delegation, absence of compensating checks, or justification outside three working days. The 31 role-design cases should not be reclassified as violations of a rule that was not stated.

**Routine repeated pairs, bot loops, and temporary-staff handovers**

- **Team allocation and ordinary correction:** Aline–Elena and Boris–Felix may reflect Team A/Team B assignment, while their loops may follow approval comments. Dispatch logs and stable peer-level routing rates would support this; manual routing overrides concentrated on exceptional vendors would weaken it.
- **Ordinary onboarding, automated exceptions, and backlog entry:** Marc–Iris may reflect onboarding specialization; Bot_Match–AP_Kim loops may correspond to match-exception codes; Temp_Nina–Carla may reflect end-of-quarter data entry. Exception logs, correction records, original request timestamps, and backlog tickets would support these explanations.
