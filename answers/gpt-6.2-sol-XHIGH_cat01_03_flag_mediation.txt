## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Freeze the intake scoring, complexity, and flagging rules across policies. Define **baseline flag eligibility** as the flag the current backend would assign from the intake record, even under a policy that hides the display. Apply each policy to the whole shared queue network over a specified operating period, including the cases that share staff and queues. Use the same target mix of incoming cases and starting system states for each regime. The reference population includes all arrivals, including cases that remain open at the end of an observation window.

- **P0** keeps current display, triage priority, routing, and staffing.
- **P1** hides the banner from staff. The score, case information, backend flag, and its automated priority and routing rules remain available to their existing users or systems. Staff may respond differently to the information they still see.
- **P2** keeps the banner and priority lane and removes the flag-only requirement for manual review. Triage staff use a prespecified discretionary review protocol, with independent safety and compliance requirements retained. Review and escalation otherwise follow current operating rules.
- **P3** keeps display and routing rules and adds specified, protected review slots staffed by qualified reviewers for baseline-flagged cases. When a slot opens, it takes the next eligible case in existing review order. These slots add to ordinary review staffing.
- **P4** acts at escalation while retaining review. In **P4-capacity**, add specified qualified senior shifts with current criteria and queue discipline. In **P4-criteria**, keep senior staffing and require a documented unresolved material issue at the end of review for discretionary escalation, with mandatory referrals and logged safety overrides preserved. Compare each version to P0.

Signs are intervention minus reference. The primary framework is **total policy effects and interventional analogues** defined by changing one operational rule and letting the network respond. E4 changes dispatch timing for a given reviewer after review entry. P3 can also change who serves cases and how the ordinary queue evolves. E8 controls a specified route and peer policy to isolate effects of display relative to that route. Examples include how thoroughly staff check a case, seek sign-off, or prepare its closing paperwork. This leaves those mechanisms free to change. Natural direct and indirect effects would require combining one exposure regime with a case’s individual mediator values under another. Review waiting is undefined on skipped routes, upstream treatment can change later confounders, and incompatible peer regimes can generate incompatible queues. No cross-world independence or additive decomposition is assumed.

The graphs show candidate links under current rules and the routes seen in this log. The two downstream queue states refer to the respective handoffs. Each case’s priority, routing, and staffing demand can affect the waits of peers sharing the triage, review, or senior pool. Faster triage can shift when a group enters review and create a backlog even at unchanged daily volume. Earlier triage can also change the shift or staff member handling the handoff. Those treatment-induced factors can affect both review waiting and cycle time or decision quality. Review findings are affected by routing and reviewer assignment and can determine both escalation and the decision. Backlogs can influence discretionary routing, and those routing decisions then change later backlogs for other arrivals. This feedback unfolds across time and cases. Adjusting only for `arrival_load` misses the later queue states, while conditioning indiscriminately on realized later states or staff assignments can block policy pathways or select incomparable cases.

**Limited P3 pilot**

- **Eligibility.** Baseline-flagged arrivals in the participating queue network qualify for added review slots whenever they enter review. Enroll all arrivals for spillover measurement.
- **Assignment.** Randomize matched whole-network operating blocks to P3 or P0. Match on shift and forecast load. Add one qualified reviewer per prespecified treatment shift for the next eligible flagged case in existing dispatch order. Use fixed warm-up and washout periods, track unfinished work across boundaries, and analyze cases by intake block.
- **Capacity guardrail.** Preserve baseline staffing and slots for ordinary review and senior work. Suspend treatment if the extra reviewer would have to be drawn from those pools.
- **Outcomes.** Primary is flagged mean time from arrival to close capped at seven days, with seven-day follow-up and open cases counted at the cap. Secondary outcomes include stage entry and ready-to-start times, unflagged cycle time, senior waits, and blinded audit of material decision errors or missed mandatory checks.
- **Minimum data.** Log intake score, eligibility and rule version, complexity, assignment, readiness and enqueue events, starts and completions, routing findings and exceptions, peer backlog, staffing, holds, close, and audit outcomes.
- **Stopping.** Pause for any serious compliance failure, or if unflagged review or senior 90th-percentile waiting exceeds matched baseline by 20% in two consecutive adequately populated monitoring windows. Set minimum window counts before launch.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. If an independent intake audit finds hidden severity accounts for the excess referrals and that the long handoffs largely reflect required evidence gathering, shift the intervention from extra review slots to earlier evidence collection and retain the existing safety routes.
2. If added review slots cut flagged review waits but increase senior and unflagged delays enough to worsen network cycle time, stop expanding P3 and test senior capacity or staggered handoffs.
3. If blinded adjudication shows a material increase in harmful errors or missed mandatory checks under P3, stop the rollout even if close times improve.
4. If timestamps mainly mark batch updates, deferred logging, or intervals when cases are on hold and ineligible for dispatch, withdraw the queue interpretation until readiness and work-start times are measured.
5. If qualified extra review coverage is feasible only on quiet shifts and high-load blocks cannot be assigned to both arms, withdraw the P3 pilot for the bottleneck population and test a component that can vary during those blocks.
6. If reviewers use the extra slots to make substantially more discretionary referrals at unchanged audited case mix, revisit the expectation that P3 leaves routing behavior stable. Pause expansion and test a prespecified referral protocol or senior staffing.

## 9. Final synthesis

In these 20 cases, flagged cases reached triage completion in 0.84 hours on average versus 3.43 hours for unflagged cases. They closed in 16.51 hours versus 8.17 hours. Review and escalation were more common among flagged cases, and the gap from triage completion to review start was longer among reviewed flagged cases. The two groups spent similar elapsed time in review once it started.

Priority triage plausibly accounts for part of the early advantage. Later, routing can send more cases to manual review and advance when they reach that shared pool. Some then enter a second queue for senior work. Those two handoffs account for substantial elapsed time, although the log does not show when cases were ready for dispatch. Staffing, shift boundaries, and preparation can all contribute.

The flag comparison cannot separate those mechanisms from severity, complexity, reviewer allocation, or behavior triggered by the visible banner. There is no score overlap in this sample, and the two observed rejection rates do not establish decision quality. The stage arithmetic does not identify a causal mediation split.

A randomized, limited P3 pilot would test whether additional qualified review slots shorten ready-to-start and close times under the same routing rules. Measuring unflagged and senior waits would show whether time is saved across the network or moved downstream. Independent decision audits would track material errors and show whether a larger safety assessment is needed before expansion.
