# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Core queueing quantities and units**

| Symbol | Definition here | Unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
|  (arrival intensity) | `arrivals` per opening-hours day (07:00–19:00, 12 h) | cases/day, or cases/hr = count/12 | Not a validated Poisson rate — just an observed count |
| throughput / completion intensity | `completed` per opening-hours day | cases/day or cases/hr | Realized completions, shaped jointly by demand, staffing, and backlog — **not** a clean service rate  |
| L (WIP) | `mean_wip`, averaged over 15-min snapshots (96/day) | cases (time-average) | A sampled estimator of the time-integral of WIP, not an exact integral; unclear whether snapshots exist only 07–19 or also overnight |
| service time | `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min` | minutes, **active handling only** | Excludes queueing/wait time — cannot substitute for Little's-Law flow time W |
| staff_fte | mean concurrent capacity | FTE | Supply-side variable; only loosely maps to a per-server rate |
| complex_pct | % of arrivals on multi-step variant | % | Case-mix proxy; may be a real shift, a coding change, or both (explicitly flagged) |
| outage_min | minutes of zero/near-zero capacity | minutes/day | Direct exogenous shock |
| peak_08_10_pct | share of daily arrivals in 08–10 | % | Intraday concentration / burstiness proxy |

**Opening-hours convention:** all rate normalizations below use 12 h/day (07:00–19:00). No service occurs outside this window, but cases can still be “in system” overnight, so day boundaries are **not** system boundaries.

**At least five threats to naive inference**

1. **Cohort mismatch**: `completed` on day *t* need not be the cases that arrived on day *t* (explicit caution). Arrivals  completions is a net flow, not a same-cohort service outcome.
2. **Snapshot vs. true integral**: `mean_wip` is a 15-min-sampled estimator; it has its own sampling variance and possibly excludes overnight WIP — unknown.
3. **Percentile non-additivity**: medians/p90s cannot be pooled by simple averaging across days to recover a combined distribution; the underlying spread and tail shape are unobserved.
4. **Single-instance events**: one outage, one staffing intervention — n=1 for each “treatment,” so their effects cannot be statistically separated from ordinary day-to-day variability.
5. **Serial dependence / non-independence**: WIP_t is mechanically linked to WIP_{t-1} + arrivals_t  completions_t; days are not exchangeable draws, so naive i.i.d. confidence intervals are too narrow.
6. **Unequal weekday/weekend sample sizes**: 20 weekdays vs 8 weekend days — pooled statistics are weekday-dominated even though weekend operating conditions (staffing, arrivals) differ by a factor of ~2.
7. **Ambiguous change-point timing**: outage window is only approximately known (“11:00–14:00”) and the staffing intervention start time within 2026-04-29 is unlogged — any regime boundary at day resolution has within-day uncertainty.
8. **Confounded case-mix signal**: `complex_pct` increase may be partly a coding/labeling artifact, which would inflate apparent service-time deterioration without a real demand change.

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

**Outage/recovery treatment**: the outage day is explicitly excluded from any “typical throughput” statistic — it is a capacity-zero shock, and its aftermath (04-16/04-17) is explicitly labeled transitional, not a new steady state, because completions there exceed arrivals only to clear the shock-induced backlog.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative: capacity/mix-tier segmentation (regime = function of `complex_pct` tercile and `staff_fte` level, independent of calendar date)**

Instead of chronological breakpoints, group days by:
- *Mix tier*: Low (complex_pct <25%), Mid (25–35%), High (>35%)
- *Staffing tier*: Low (2.5–3.0, weekend-type), Standard (5.5–6.0), Elevated (6.5–7.0)

This tells a different operational story: it treats the system as driven by two continuous covariates (demand composition, supply level) rather than by a sequence of discrete calendar events. It would classify, e.g., 04-16 (staff 7.0, mid mix) similarly to 04-29/04-30 (staff 7.0, high mix) even though these arose from different triggers (outage recovery vs. sustained mix shift).

**Strengths vs. primary segmentation**

| Aspect | Primary (chronological/event) | Alternative (mix/staffing tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Captures backlog carry-over and event causality (outage, intervention) | Yes, explicitly | No — loses the “why” |
| Generalizes to future days with similar mix/staffing regardless of date | Weak | Strong |
| Sample size per cell | Larger per calendar block | Smaller, more cells, sparser (e.g., “High mix + Low staff” may have very few days) |
| Risk of conflating distinct causal episodes | Low | Higher — pools recovery-driven high-completion days with intervention-driven high-completion days |
| Useful for staffing-policy rules (“if mix >35%, add X FTE”) | Indirect | Direct |

**Recommendation**: use the primary (chronological) segmentation for descriptive/diagnostic work on *this* dataset (it respects carry-over and event structure), but use the mix/staffing-tier alternative when trying to build a forward-looking staffing rule that should generalize beyond this specific four-week episode.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Three concrete distortions from pooling all 28 days:

**(a) Arrivals — pooled mean is a mixture, not a “typical day.”**
Pooled mean arrivals over all 28 days  2519/28  **90.0/day**. This number describes neither weekdays (102–121) nor weekends (36–53); it is an artifact of a 20:8 mixture. A staffing plan built on “90/day” would over-provision weekends and under-provision weekdays.

**(b) complex_pct — pooled mean hides a near step-change.**
Pooled mean complex_pct  920/28  **32.9%**. But the actual series is bimodal: ~18–27% for days 1–14, then ~39–47% for days 15–28 (with the jump concentrated between 04-19 and 04-20). No day in the dataset is actually near 33%. Reporting “33% complex mix” as a planning input would misrepresent both eras.

**(c) Deficit cancellation — a Simpson’s-paradox-like reversal.**
Pooled totals: arrivals 2519, completed 2409  net gap 110 over 4 weeks (4% shortfall), which looks like a roughly balanced system. But decomposing:
- Mix-shift weekdays+weekends (04-20 to 04-28, 9 days): arrivals 892, completed 786  **deficit 106 in just 9 days** (12/day).
- Outage day: deficit 47 (one day).
- Recovery days (04-16/17): surplus +30 total.
- Intervention days (04-29 to 05-01): surplus +18 total.

The pooled “near balance” is a coincidental cancellation of a large structural backlog build-up (mix-shift regime) against a large one-off shock deficit (outage) and two surplus-generating episodes (recovery, intervention). A manager reading only the pooled 4% gap would conclude the system is healthy, while the segmented view shows a 12/day structural deficit sustained for 9 days that only reversed because of a deliberate staffing intervention.

**Weekday/weekend composition caution**: with 20 weekdays vs 8 weekend days, any pooled percentile or mean is weekday-weighted by construction; medians of `median_service_min` (pooled 31.0 min) mask the baseline 24–26 min vs mix-shift 34–36 min split (a ~40% relative increase invisible in the pooled figure).

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Daily-level dispersion (arrivals within regime).** Using Fano factor = sample variance/mean and CV = sd/mean (see Section 8 code for exact values; order-of-magnitude here):
- Baseline weekday: Fano  0.15–0.20, CV  4%  arrivals are **sub-Poisson** (unusually regular day counts, plausibly capacity- or scheduling-censored).
- Mix-shift weekday: Fano  0.25–0.35, CV  5%  still sub-Poisson but more variable than baseline.

**Intraday (hourly) dispersion.** From the representative vectors:
- Ordinary weekday (04-07): Fano  3, moderate overdispersion, roughly unimodal peak at 10:00–11:00.
- Bursty post-mix weekday (04-21): Fano  10, **strongly overdispersed and visibly bimodal** (spikes at 10–11 and 13–14, arrivals 34 and 24 against a base of ~4–8) — consistent with `peak_08_10_pct` rising from ~30% to ~45–50%.

**Key reversal**: daily aggregation *smooths away* almost all of the intraday burstiness that actually stresses the queue. A model calibrated only on daily  would look only mildly overdispersed; the same days, viewed hourly, show an order-of-magnitude jump in dispersion. Any capacity model using daily rates would underestimate peak congestion.

**Serial dependence / lag structure.** Arrivals show strong weekly periodicity (lag-7 autocorrelation expected clearly positive; lag-1 expected to swing negative at weekdayweekend transitions) — see Section 8 output. The (arrivals  completed) gap series shows strong positive lag-1 autocorrelation during 04-2004-28 (persistent deficit) and during 04-2905-01 (persistent surplus), consistent with backlog carry-over rather than independent daily draws. Any naive i.i.d.-based variance estimate for daily WIP or gap would be too small.

**Change-point evidence.** Three distinct candidate breakpoints:
1. 04-15: isolated shock (outage) — not a regime start, a point event.
2. 04-1904-20: abrupt, sustained jump in complex_pct/service time/peak concentration — the clearest sustained regime change.
3. 04-2804-29: staff_fte increase coincident with the arrivals/completions ratio flipping from deficit to surplus.

**Limits of 4 representative days.** With one ordinary weekday, one outage day, one bursty post-shift weekday, and one weekend day, we cannot: estimate a full intraday rate function (only 4 point-in-time curves), assess whether the 04-21 bimodal pattern is typical of *all* mix-shift weekdays or a one-off, quantify day-to-day variance of the hourly shape, or validate stationarity within any regime. These vectors are illustrative, not a calibrated non-homogeneous arrival-process model.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law (L = W) requires a system in (approximate) steady state over the averaging window, with  and L measured consistently (same units, same population, matched over/under-counting of in-transit cases). It gives *mean sojourn time*, never a median or p90.

# 7. Operational implications

1. **Staffing**: keep FTE near the elevated 6.5–7.0 level while complex_pct remains materially above the ~18–24% baseline band; the baseline staffing (6.0 FTE) was apparently calibrated for the lower-complexity mix. (Hypothesis, not a validated formula: something like “FTE  baseline + k·(complex_pct  20)” could be tested with more data, but should not be treated as derived here.)

2. **Backlog recovery**: the mix-shift regime accumulated a 106-case deficit in 9 days (12/day). The observed drawdown surplus during 04-2905-01 was only +6 to +10/day — slower than the accumulation rate — and weekday WIP was still 32 on 05-01, well above the baseline 20. **Recovery was not complete by the end of the observation window**; recommend continued elevated staffing and monitoring past 05-03 rather than assuming the backlog has cleared.

3. **Peak smoothing**: peak_08_10_pct rose from ~30% to ~45–50% alongside the mix shift, and the 04-21 hourly vector shows a bimodal burst (10–11 and 13–14). This suggests investigating whether complex cases arrive in scheduled batches or are queued/handed off at specific times; staggered shift starts, a separate fast-lane for simple cases, or appointment smoothing for complex cases are candidate mitigations — but only 4 sampled hourly days support this, so pilot before committing.

4. **Data collection**: (a) log exact outage start/stop timestamps and the exact time-of-day the staffing intervention began on 04-29 — currently only day-level precision exists; (b) capture case-level timestamps (true event log) so that actual sojourn time (not just active service time) can be computed and Little's Law tested properly; (c) increase weekend sampling, since n=8 weekend-days is too sparse for regime-specific confidence.

5. **Policy that looks good pooled but is risky segmented**: *“Since 4-week pooled arrivals (2519) and completions (2409) are within ~4% of balance, revert staffing to the 6.0 FTE baseline now that the numbers look healthy.”* This is exactly the Simpson’s-paradox risk flagged in Section 4: the pooled near-balance is an artifact of the outage deficit, the mix-shift deficit, and the intervention surplus canceling out — not evidence of a stable, staffing-appropriate equilibrium. Complex_pct remained ~39–41% at the end of the series (versus 18–24% baseline), so the structural driver of the backlog has **not** reverted. Cutting staffing back to 6.0 FTE now would likely resume the 12/day deficit growth seen during 04-2004-28. The segmented view argues for holding elevated staffing until complex_pct itself normalizes, not until the pooled gap looks small.

def rolling_zscore_changepoints(series, window=5):
    s = series.reset_index(drop=True)

            continue
        past = s.iloc[i - window:i]

        z_out.append((s.iloc[i] - mu) / sd if sd > 0 else np.nan)
    return pd.Series(z_out)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Little's-Law implied flow time -- ONLY for explicitly chosen, defensible windows
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
OPENING_HOURS = 12.0  # 07:00-19:00

def littles_law_window(g, label, throughput_col):
    lam_per_day = g[throughput_col].mean()
    lam_per_hour = lam_per_day / OPENING_HOURS
    L = g["mean_wip"].mean()
    W_hours = L / lam_per_hour if lam_per_hour > 0 else np.nan
    return pd.Series({
        "label": label, "n_days": len(g), "throughput_proxy": throughput_col,
        "lambda_per_hour": lam_per_hour, "L_mean_wip": L,
        "implied_W_hours": W_hours,
        "implied_W_minutes": W_hours * 60 if pd.notna(W_hours) else np.nan
    })

ll_windows = [
    littles_law_window(df[df.regime_manual == "baseline_weekday"], "baseline_weekday", "completed"),
    littles_law_window(df[df.regime_manual == "baseline_weekend"], "baseline_weekend", "completed"),
    littles_law_window(df[df.regime_manual == "mix_shift_weekday"], "mix_shift_weekday (arrivals proxy)", "arrivals"),
    littles_law_window(df[df.regime_manual == "mix_shift_weekday"], "mix_shift_weekday (completed proxy)", "completed"),
    littles_law_window(df[df.regime_manual == "intervention_weekday"], "intervention_weekday", "completed"),
]
ll_df = pd.DataFrame(ll_windows)
print("\n=== Little's Law: L/lambda for explicitly chosen windows only ===")
print(ll_df.round(2))
print("Outage day and post-outage-recovery are deliberately EXCLUDED: single-day / transient,")
print("not stationary, so a point W estimate would misrepresent them (see Section 6).")
print("mix_shift_weekday is shown with BOTH throughput proxies on purpose: the two implied")
print("W values diverge because arrivals != completions there, itself evidence of non-stationarity.")
