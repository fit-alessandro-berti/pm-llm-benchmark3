# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy should optimize **effective throughput**—cases completed correctly and on time—not merely the number of assignments made or activities started. It is a safeguarded trade-off policy, not a claim that one weighting produces a universally optimal answer.

**Objectives**

- Reduce expected SLA lateness, giving urgent and high-priority cases priority for scarce capacity.
- Balance service time, waiting time, handover delay, and downstream rework.
- Keep case ownership stable when the owner can perform safely and effectively.
- Distribute effort-adjusted workload and overtime exposure fairly, rather than equalizing raw case counts.
- Provide supervised development linked to documented training priorities.
- Preserve specialist capacity and develop backups for Security, Approval, and other bottleneck skills.

**Hard safety and compliance constraints**

1. Skill 0 means no operational case work. Observation or classroom training must be logged separately.
2. High- or urgent-priority performers must have skill at least 2 unless genuinely supervised; activity-specific rules still apply.
3. Security Review requires:
   - a skill-3 performer; or
   - a skill-1–2 performer with explicit, available supervision from R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.
   - A case marked “needs specialist,” such as C001, requires a specialist to lead and sign off.
4. Final Approval requires a skill-2–3 finalizer. Skill 1 may prepare under supervision but cannot finalize; skill 0 cannot prepare or finalize.
5. Supervision must have reserved time and a named supervisor. Adding a supervisor’s name without capacity is not compliance.
6. No resource receives more than four new assignments in the batch. Supervisory and coaching work also consumes capacity.
7. Quality Checks may be batched only when all included cases are normal/low priority and have more than 24 hours remaining.

**Operational guardrails**

- High-overtime-risk resources are excluded by default. An exception requires an urgent SLA risk, documented absence of a safer alternative, and human approval.
- An assignment must not be presented as feasible just because the resource has some hours left today. Predicted effort, queue position, supervision, handover time, and capacity before the deadline must be considered.
- If no safe, timely candidate exists, the correct action is escalation—not silently violating a rule.

**Preference trade-offs**

- Continuity receives extra weight for high complexity, high handover cost, and cases already reassigned repeatedly.
- Competent resources should be fully acceptable for routine work; specialists should not automatically receive every case.
- The training target is at least 20% of normal/low assignments when this does not create unacceptable SLA or quality risk. There are nine such cases in the snapshot, so the target is two assignments.
- Training, raw assignment speed, and workload equalization may all be sacrificed temporarily for safety or urgent SLA risk.

# 2. Assignment policy

For each case, construct candidates consisting of a performer, an optional supervisor, and an ownership action: retain owner, owner-retained specialist intervention, or full ownership transfer.

```text
build_candidates(case):
    for resource r:
        reject if skill[r, case.required_skill] == 0
        reject if r cannot plausibly have capacity before the deadline

        if case.priority in {high, urgent} and skill[r] < 2:
            require a feasible supervised pair

        if case.activity == Security Review:
            accept skill 3
            accept skill 1 or 2 only with available Gita/Hugo supervision
            reject everything else
            if case requires a specialist:
                require a skill-3 lead and signatory

        if case.activity == Final Approval:
            skill 2 or 3: eligible to finalize
            skill 1: eligible only as preparer with a qualified finalizer
            skill 0: reject

        if r.overtime_risk == high:
            reject unless urgent_exception_is_documented_and_approved

    return candidates, including feasible supervised pairs
```

Each surviving candidate receives explainable component scores, normally on a 0–100 scale.

| Component | Operationalization |
|---|---|
| **SLA urgency and risk** | Order cases using priority, deadline pressure, customer waiting, and expected effort. For each candidate, estimate finish time as queue delay + handover/coordination time + service time + supervision buffer. Report expected slack and estimated miss probability. Urgent cases receive a higher SLA weight. |
| **Skill match** | Estimate quality and service-time fit using skill, complexity, and repeated-loop history. Skill 2 can score as highly as skill 3 on routine work. Skill 3 receives a clear advantage on complex or specialist-required work. Trainees are considered only in suitable supervised settings. |
| **Continuity and handover** | Retaining the owner receives a high score. An owner change is penalized by handover-cost band, complexity, and reassignment history. Its expected delay is also included in the SLA calculation. Thus the historical 2.7-hour owner-change delay and 3.3-hour weak-skill service penalty are alternatives to compare, not blanket rules. |
| **Workload and overtime fairness** | Use projected effort-adjusted open work, assignments already made in the batch, available hours, supervisory commitments, and overtime risk. Compare with similarly skilled capacity as well as the whole team; raw case counts alone are insufficient. |
| **Training and development** | Give a bonus when a normal/low case matches the trainee’s documented priority, has suitable complexity and slack, and includes real supervision. Skill-0 observation does not count as hands-on training. |
| **Resilience and bottleneck avoidance** | Penalize avoidable routine work sent to a congested specialist, reward use of qualified backups, and reserve capacity for remaining urgent cases. Consider Security and Approval waiting times and the effect of losing a specialist, not only current utilization. |

A possible combined score is:

```text
score(case, candidate) =
      w_sla       * SLA_score
    + w_skill     * skill_score
    + w_continuity* continuity_score
    + w_fairness  * workload_score
    + w_training  * training_score
    + w_resilience* resilience_score
```

The weights are contextual and reviewable: SLA dominates urgent cases, continuity receives more weight for costly high-complexity handovers, and training has little or no weight when deadlines are tight. They should not be presented as objectively optimal.

**Batch assignment with lookahead**

```text
snapshot skills, training plans, owners, overtime flags, workloads and deadlines
estimate effort and waiting-time ranges from historical data
build hard-rule candidate sets; escalate cases with no candidate

reserve capacity for urgent cases and necessary supervisors
process cases by urgency, but score candidates against the whole remaining batch

tentatively select the highest-scoring feasible candidate
after each selection:
    recheck the four-assignment cap and effort-adjusted capacity
    recheck whether remaining urgent cases still have a feasible resource
    recheck specialist bottleneck and supervision capacity
    repair by moving not-yet-started work to the next-best candidate if needed

form only permitted Quality Check batches
try to meet the normal/low training target without material SLA or quality loss
if the target cannot be met safely, record the shortfall and reason

log the selected assignment, main reasons, accepted trade-off, rejected hard-rule
candidates, policy version, owner before/after, and any human override
```

**Tie-breaking and exceptions**

- Break near-ties by greater SLA slack, then less ownership disruption, lower projected effort-adjusted load, lower overtime exposure, and lower use of scarce specialist capacity.
- Rotate among genuinely equivalent resources so deterministic tie-breaking does not repeatedly favor the same person; use resource ID only as the final reproducibility rule.
- Do not reassign started work for a small score improvement. Require a material benefit exceeding the handover and coordination cost.
- Human overrides require a reason. Hard skill rules cannot be overridden; workload or training preferences can.
- Absence, urgent-work overrun, or new SLA information triggers re-evaluation of work that has not started, rather than automatic ownership churn.

Also, literal first-available routing can produce hard-rule violations—for example, an unsupervised skill-1 resource on urgent work or an unqualified Approval finalizer. Such assignments should be marked **infeasible**, not credited with higher throughput.

# 5. Explanation to workers

> **How assignments are decided**
>
> We first check who is allowed to perform the work safely. Security and Approval have special rules, and a supervisor must genuinely have time to help. Urgent deadlines are then checked against the work already waiting for each qualified colleague.
>
> We usually try to keep a case with the person who knows it. That is why Celine keeps C007 and Eva keeps C016. Continuity is not absolute: sometimes waiting for the current owner, or asking them to work outside their strongest skill, would create a larger risk than a supported handover.
>
> Workload is not judged only by the number of cases. Complexity, expected effort, available hours, supervision, and overtime risk also matter. Bruno, David, and Iris receiving no new items in this snapshot is a capacity-protection decision, not a criticism of their performance.
>
> Specialists are reserved for work that needs them, while competent colleagues can handle suitable routine cases. Some normal and low-priority cases are deliberately used for supervised learning. Trainees are not left alone with urgent or high-risk work.
>
> Every assignment should have a short, visible explanation, such as “kept with current owner,” “qualified backup used to protect the deadline,” or “supervised development case.” If the underlying workload, skill, or availability information is wrong, please challenge it. A team lead can change an assignment, with the reason recorded, but cannot waive safety rules.
>
> Assignment changes and audit results are used to improve staffing, training, and the policy—not to rank or blame individuals.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Audit one row per **assignment decision**, not one row per event. In addition to the existing fields, retain `decision_batch_id`, `assigned_at`, `policy_version`, owner before/after, performer/finalizer, supervisor, skill and overtime values at decision time, predicted slack, and override reason.

| Indicator | Definition and interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Handover frequency** | Owner changes per 100 cases and handover edges per 100 activities. Report median waiting after handover, stratified by complexity and handover-cost band. Distinguish genuine owner transfer from owner-retained specialist help or collaboration. |
| **Reassignment count** | Maximum reassignment count per case, percentage exceeding 2, and increments attributable to the policy. Specifically monitor C003-like cases crossing that threshold rather than treating the threshold as a proven causal cutoff. |
| **Skill mismatch** | Percentage of assignments below the intended skill target, separated into supervised training and unsupervised mismatch. Hard violations—skill 0, unqualified Security, unqualified Approval finalization, or unsupervised high-priority skill below 2—should be zero. |
| **Workload distribution** | New assignments per resource per batch, effort-adjusted WIP/capacity, supervisory hours, p90/median load, and optionally a Gini coefficient within comparable skill groups. Report cap breaches separately. |
| **Overtime risk exposure** | Assignments and expected effort given to medium/high-overtime-risk resources; percentage of high-risk assignments using the urgent exception; documentation completeness and subsequent actual overtime where reliable data exist. |
| **SLA misses** | Completion after `sla_deadline`, median/p90 tardiness, and risk by priority, activity, assignment reason, and predicted slack. Handle still-open cases as censored rather than counting them as on-time completions. |
| **Rework/appeal after assignment** | Subsequent Rework Correction, repeated diagnosis/activity loops, or appeal events within a defined observation window. Compare owner-change, reassignment, mismatch, and training cohorts with case-mix adjustment; do not infer causation from simple correlations. |
| **Training assignment share** | Hands-on normal/low assignments matching a documented training priority divided by all normal/low assignments. Also report safe opportunities, target shortfalls, supervisor completion, trainee rework/SLA outcomes, and subsequent skill progression. Keep shadowing in a separate metric. |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | Specialist WIP and utilization, p50/p90 waiting before Security/Approval, percentage of work eligible only for skill 3, queue age, performance-DFG waiting edges, and qualified backup coverage. Test the operational effect of losing each critical specialist. |
| **Policy deviations** | Hard-rule violations, assignments outside the reconstructed candidate set, cap/batching violations, undocumented overtime exceptions, training-target shortfalls, and discretionary overrides by reason. A high override rate may indicate a bad policy or stale data, not bad worker behavior. |

Indicators should be reviewed by team and case mix, with workers able to inspect and contest the underlying data. Network centrality or assignment counts should not become individual performance rankings.

```python
    log, 24 * 3600, 366 * 24 * 3600       # illustrative duration bounds
)

# Case-attribute filtering is performed explicitly on assignment-time data.
high_complexity_df = df[df["case_complexity"] >= 0.75]

quality_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, ACT, ["Quality Check"], retain=True
)
observed_batches = pm4py.discover_batches(quality_log)

# Policy audit: these functions use the versioned snapshots and skill matrix.
decisions = extract_one_row_per_assignment_decision(df)

for d in decisions:
    candidates = rebuild_hard_candidate_set(
        d.queue_snapshot, d.resource_snapshot, d.versioned_skill_matrix
    )
    record_audit_row(
        decision=d,
        hard_violation=not hard_rules_satisfied(d),
        outside_candidate_set=d.assigned_resource not in candidates,
        owner_change=d.owner_after != d.owner_before,
        training_match=documented_hands_on_training_match(d),
        overtime_exception=valid_urgent_overtime_exception(d),
        quality_batch_violation=invalid_quality_batch(d, observed_batches)
    )
```

The outputs support, but do not replace, human judgment:

- Service/waiting-time results calibrate predicted finish times and quantify handover-associated delays by activity, complexity, and skill.
- Handover and working-together networks reveal disruptive handover paths and possible coaching relationships. Working together does **not** prove that supervision occurred.
- Resource similarity and discovered roles can suggest backup candidates, but cannot confer authorization or override the skill matrix.
- Performance DFGs and case-performance filtering locate queues and slow paths; variants help identify repeated diagnosis and rework loops.
- Discovered batches must be checked against priority and SLA-at-batch-formation, not accepted merely because events occurred together.
- Historical assignments may encode the weaknesses of first-available or specialist-only routing. They should therefore inform estimates and hypotheses, not be copied automatically as the future policy.
