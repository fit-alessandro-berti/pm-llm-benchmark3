## 2. Counterfactual continuations

These are feasible branching continuations, not identified causal forecasts. In particular, a process-valid trace does not establish its probability under intervention; prescriptive monitoring needs an intervention-effect estimate as well as an outcome prediction. ([arXiv][2])

**1 — D: screening plus a non-preemptive second-review route**

```text
Evidence Validated
  → [Alternate screening || dossier preparation || S4 allocation/ETA check]
  → Analyst Review Completed by R17, incorporating available screening findings
  → Second Officer Review Completed by S4, independently
  → {
       Valid approval → Payment Executed
       Documented rejection threshold met → Rejected
       Decision remains unsupported → Hold / targeted additional evidence
       Reporting warranted after required reviews → Hold + SAR filed → remains held
     }
```

The staffing check occurs **now**, not after the 15–30-minute screening wait. If S4 is too late, C becomes the candidate fallback, subject to the portfolio comparison.

A result supporting a false match may make approval more plausible; a corroborating adverse result may favor rejection or hold; conflicting or inconclusive results may require more evidence. None of these branches has an action-specific probability supplied. A favorable alternate screen does not itself approve the payment, and agreement between vendors is not assumed to be independent evidence.

**2 — C: expedited S2 review**

```text
Evidence Validated
  → Analyst Review Completed by R17
  → P-901 interruption recorded; S2 starts independent review
  → Second Officer Review Completed by S2
  → {
       Valid approval → Payment Executed
       Rejection justified → Rejected
       Insufficient basis for either decision → Hold / additional evidence
       Reporting warranted → Hold + SAR filed → remains held
     }
  → S2 returns to eligible work; P-901 impact reassessed
```

The direct benefit is earlier access to a mandatory reviewer, **not an increased entitlement to approval**. Main uncertainties are review duration, substantive findings, the amount of P-901 work displaced, and whether timely completion is still possible after analyst reassessment.

The nominal 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval prediction suggests approval is a substantial eventual possibility, but it does not provide an on-time execution probability or a rejection/hold/report split under C.

**3 — E: additional evidence**

```text
Evidence Validated
  → Analyst Review identifies a specific evidentiary gap
  → Evidence Requested
  → [6–24 hours]
  → Evidence Received → Evidence Validated → Analyst Review
  → Required independent second review
  → {Valid approval → later execution | justified rejection | continued hold/report}
```

This preserves a route to later approval but almost certainly sacrifices the current deadline. The key uncertainty is whether the requested evidence can actually resolve the gap. A generic repeat request for material already validated is not justified merely because it postpones a difficult decision.

If refreshed facts remove both second-review triggers, their effect on routing must be checked against the applicable workflow interpretation; the plan must not silently revert to Auto Clear.

**4 — F: conditional rejection**

```text
Evidence Validated
  → Post-evidence Analyst Review
  → Verify rejection threshold, authority, and required review completion
  → {
       All requirements established → Documented rejection → Rejected
       Requirements not established → Remain unexecuted; return to C/D/E
     }
```

The uncertainty is evidentiary and procedural: **does the current record justify rejection?** An unresolved fuzzy match is supplied; satisfaction of a rejection threshold is not. Neither the 0.46 match confidence nor the 0.55–0.75 approval prediction answers that question.

There is also a routing ambiguity: rule 6 explicitly requires second review before approval/execution, while rule 8 places dispositions after required reviews. A fast rejection therefore needs confirmation that it is authorized without an additional second review; otherwise F follows that review rather than bypassing it.

**5 — G: conditional exception**

```text
[Ordinary conformant processing continues]
  ||
Check for a genuine qualifying incident and contact Compliance Director
  → {
       Valid incident ID AND director authorization, both verified
         → Confirm authorized exception scope
         → Valid exception-authorized release → Payment Executed, if appropriate

       Either condition absent, authorization refused, or time budget exhausted
         → No override; ordinary review / hold continues
     }
```

The uncertain branches include incident eligibility, director reachability, authorization, and remaining execution time. Neither a promised response nor an incident draft is sufficient. Authorization obtained after execution would not make the earlier execution conformant.

**Across every branch, filing a SAR does not enable execution and must not be disclosed to the customer.**

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Use mandatory controls as feasibility constraints, then compare consequences among feasible plans.** Do not put A and B into an otherwise ordinary cost-minimization contest and allow sufficiently large deadline savings to “buy” their violations.

For an admissible plan, a useful objective is:

$$
\text{Expected lawful timely-execution value}
-\text{customer decision/delay harm}
-\text{residual compliance harm}
-\text{portfolio spillover}
-\text{resource opportunity cost}.
$$

This is a decision framework, not an identified numerical utility function. The given facts leave several necessary terms unknown.

**Deadline value.** For plan \(a\), the gross timely benefit lies between \(€20{,}000q_a\) and \(€35{,}000q_a\). Incremental benefit against normal processing depends on \(q_a-q_0\), not on \(q_a\) alone. Neither quantity is established for these interventions.

The observational 0.48–0.64 breach probability cannot simply be reduced by a guessed “expediting effect.” Its complement is not automatically the probability of valid on-time execution unless the prediction target treats approval, rejection, hold, and execution consistently with that outcome. Nor should it be multiplied by the approval probability without a justified joint model. Distinguishing predicted need from causal intervention benefit is central to resource-constrained prescriptive monitoring. ([arXiv][2])

**False rejection and prolonged hold.** The €15,000–€80,000 harm range makes both F and prolonged E consequential. However, predicted ultimate approval is not ground truth, so 0.55–0.75 is **not** an established false-rejection probability. A conformant review can correctly discover grounds for rejection that the predictor did not capture.

E has an important advantage over immediate F: it preserves this case’s route to approval while gathering missing information. That does not make E automatically preferable; unnecessary evidence requests can impose substantial avoidable delay.

**Regulatory and non-monetary exposure.** The supplied €100,000–€500,000 scale illustrates asymmetry but is not a calibrated expected penalty. For A and B, the process violation is known; the amount and probability of realized monetary consequences are not. For C, D, and E, procedural conformance reduces the bypass problem but does not prove that every substantive decision will be correct.

The constraints also protect independent judgment, accountability, and confidentiality. Those protections should not disappear because a narrow financial estimate assigns them a small value.

**Avoid double counting.** The €20,000–€35,000 avoided operational loss may overlap with the €15,000–€80,000 customer-harm estimate. Do not automatically sum them. Likewise, an objective should not count successful timely execution as a benefit and then separately charge the same forgone benefit as a second loss for the identical comparison.

The resulting comparisons are conditional:

* **C versus D:** neither universally dominates. C has the better supplied timing opportunity; D can protect P-901 and improve the evidentiary basis.
* **E versus further review:** E rises to first priority if a material evidence gap prevents a valid decision and cannot be resolved by screening or independent review.
* **F versus hold/review:** F becomes preferable only when its evidentiary and authorization conditions are established—not because it closes the case quickly.
* **G versus ordinary review:** G may become valuable once genuinely enabled, but its hoped-for availability is not equivalent to present authorization.

## 4. Conformance constraints

**The observed prefix is unfinished, not necessarily already nonconformant.** It still has valid extensions. The decision is which next continuation remains permitted.

The current second-review trigger is:

$$
\text{Second review required}
=(\text{risk}\geq70)\lor(\text{unresolved fuzzy match}).
$$

Both terms are presently true. Clearing only one leaves the other sufficient.

| Action | Relevant mandatory rules and implications                                                                                                                                                                                       |

| **A**  | Violates **6 and 9**: required independent review and valid approval are absent. It also cannot use **3**, because the supplied low-risk/no-alert Auto Clear condition is not met. **10** provides no currently enabled escape. |
| **B**  | Violates **7**. R17 cannot become an independent second officer by recording another review event. Any ensuing execution remains invalid under **6 and 9**.                                                                     |
| **C**  | Implements **5–9** when the post-evidence analyst review occurs, S2 is eligible and independent, and a valid disposition follows. Assignment alone is not completed review.                                                     |
| **D**  | Adds information, but must still implement **5–9**. Alternate screening is neither approval nor second review. A favorable result cannot automatically activate **3**.                                                          |
| **E**  | Uses the evidence cycle in **5**. After validation, return to Analyst Review and satisfy any continuing **6–7** obligations before approval/execution.                                                                          |
| **F**  | Requires a justified rejection disposition under **8**, after the applicable reviews. The prompt does not establish the rejection threshold or conclusively establish a fast-rejection route.                                   |
| **G**  | Requires both elements of **10** before using the exception. The authorization must cover the intended departure from the ordinary path; it does not turn a SAR into approval or remove **11**.                                 |

For **F**, the decision record needs the applicable rejection criterion, the verified evidence satisfying it, treatment of contradictory evidence, an authorized decision-maker, and the required review/sign-off history. “Unresolved match” is a reason to investigate or control execution; its sufficiency for rejection remains unproven here.

For **G**, require a genuine qualifying incident, a valid recorded identifier, attributable director authorization given before execution, and a clear record of the authorized exception scope. Neither an urgent customer deadline nor an invented identifier supplies the missing justification.

The **12-minute-old score deserves a freshness check**, but the prompt supplies no expiry rule that automatically invalidates it. Refreshing a score is not permission to edit risk downward. Neither risk score 72 nor match confidence 0.46 is established as a probability of unlawful payment.

Conformance monitoring should inspect **control flow, data predicates, resource identity/eligibility, and timestamps together**. A sequence-only checker could accept a “Second Officer Review” label while missing that R17 performed it; multi-perspective conformance checking addresses precisely this broader class of deviations. ([Eindhoven Tech Research Portal][3])

For auditability, record evidence and screening versions, score timestamps, actual review start/completion events, reviewer identities, approval basis, resource interruptions, exception records, and the rationale for each reranking. Keep SAR records access-controlled and customer communications neutral—for example, “additional verification is pending”—without disclosing a report.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4 is a portfolio allocation decision, not simply a race to finish P-884.** P-901 has the earlier deadline, and its loss severity is not supplied.

For the stated 45-minute interruption, a first-order expected spillover is:

$$
\Delta L_{901}
\in[0.12,0.28]\times L_{901},
$$

where \(L_{901}\) is the incremental loss associated with P-901 breaching. This is not yet a euro estimate.

A simplified preemption criterion is:

$$
\Delta q_{884}\,V_{884}
>
\Delta p_{901}\,L_{901}
+\text{handover and other incremental costs}.
$$

Here \(\Delta q_{884}\) compares S2 with the chosen non-preemptive alternative, not automatically with the supplied “normal processing” prediction. As an **equal-stakes sensitivity assumption**, ignoring other costs, P-884 would need a 12–28-percentage-point improvement in valid timely execution to offset the stated P-901 deterioration. Equal stakes and that improvement are not established.

The interruption estimate must also match the actual plan. A 35–75-minute review is not necessarily a 45-minute absence. A 75-minute diversion could span P-901’s entire remaining 70-minute window. Delayed preemption, handover overhead, or switching S2 back mid-review require revised estimates rather than linear extrapolation.

**Timing changes the ranking sharply.** With review duration \(r\in[35,75]\) and final disposition/execution \(f\in[5,15]\):

| **S4 starts at minute 35**                                |                   **75–125 min** | Retain D/S4 provisionally first: on-time execution remains possible while protecting P-901. It requires review plus finalization to fit within the remaining **55 min**.                                                                                   |
| **S4 starts at minute 65**                                |                  **105–155 min** | Under the stated duration proxy, S4 cannot meet the deadline. **C moves above waiting for S4 as a deadline-rescue option**, provided the portfolio gain justifies interruption. Otherwise accept delay rather than transfer greater harm blindly to P-901. |

These are planning ranges, not distributions or guaranteed bounds. Confirm S4’s own service estimate before treating the comparison as decisive.

Even at the fastest supplied review-plus-finalization time, the latest feasible review start is:

$$
90-(35+5)=50\text{ minutes from now}.
$$

That is an **optimistic feasibility limit, not a sensible time to begin looking for S2**. S2 may no longer be available then. At the slow end, \(75+15=90\), immediate start already consumes the entire window.

**API quota.** D uses one call and leaves two. Screening latency can fit inside even the shortest stated S4 wait, but result reconciliation can still add time. Its value depends on relevant information gained, vendor reliability, and competing cases’ need for the remaining quota. Use one justified call, not repeated screening until a favorable answer appears. If the alternate vendor is materially redundant or another case has demonstrably greater information value, retain the independent-review route without assuming D’s API component is worthwhile.

**Exception waiting.** Do not replace ordinary scheduling with a 20–60-minute wait for the director. Any genuine exception inquiry should run in parallel, have an owner, and have an explicit decision cutoff—for example, minute 60 as a chosen operating limit, not a permission rule. No authorization at the cutoff means no override. Waiting is itself consequential when scarce intervention opportunities may disappear; uncertainty-aware prescriptive monitoring explicitly considers that now-versus-later trade-off. ([arXiv][4])

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** begin **D as an adaptive parallel plan**. Use one alternate-screening call, prepare the evidence package, and obtain an authoritative S4 start-time and review-duration estimate immediately. Complete R17’s post-evidence analyst reassessment, incorporating material screening findings, then obtain an independent second review. Prefer S4 when early availability makes this a credible route and preserves P-901. Approval and execution remain contingent on the substantive decision and completed controls.

**Fallback:** use **C**, with S2’s independent expedited review, when updated staffing information makes S4 too late and the estimated marginal benefit to P-884 justifies P-901’s interruption. Make that staffing decision without unnecessarily waiting for screening to finish. Any material screening findings arriving during review must be incorporated before final approval.

If no valid approval or genuinely enabled exception is available, keep the payment unexecuted; the deadline does not activate A or B.

**Confidence: moderate overall.** Confidence is high in excluding A/B, requiring independent review under the present facts, and protecting SAR confidentiality. Confidence is lower in D-versus-C utility because intervention effects, S4’s actual service time, and P-901’s loss severity are missing.

**The next observation that should trigger immediate reranking is the authoritative S4 availability-and-duration update.** A credible minute-35 start supports the primary route; a minute-65 start moves S2 preemption to the front of the deadline-rescue assessment. Any material adverse screening result can instead make evidence gathering, hold, or properly justified rejection the correct next step.
