## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts (established by the record, not reasonably contestable):**

- M0's semantics are clear: Register_Supplier precedes the `checks` block; Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are truly concurrent (a StrictPartialOrder over the two nodes with no ordering edge); *both* checks must complete before the XOR decision — including before Reject_Supplier; on the approved branch Manual_Procurement_Approval strictly precedes Activate_Supplier; Notify_Requester follows the decision and Close_Case follows notification, in every variant.
- CS-9 (N1) is the only controlled, formally approved norm. It requires both checks before activation, manual approval before activation, and requester ≠ manual approver. It is silent on whether pre-screen-rejected leads are M0 cases and has no reactivation procedure.
- PD-3 (N2) existed only as minutes plus a follow-up email, was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and expired 2026-07-31. FD-7 (N3) was a bounded written directive that expired automatically 2026-07-12 23:59, with explicit compensating controls (sanctions before activation, EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, Quality_Review within two business days).
- The CRM migration (N4) degraded the onboarding log: notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%; only ~70% of notifications are mirrored; older CRM links are mutable text.
- Trace-level facts per case as tabulated, including: SUP-703 was HIGH-risk and ineligible yet Auto_Approved with a EUR 180,000 first purchase; SUP-708 was activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check with no overlay in force; SUP-711's duplicate Register events share one source transaction ID; SUP-712's requester and approver resolve to the same human via aliases; 312 June–July Auto_Approve cases split 289 eligible / 14 ineligible / 9 unknown; 6 Auto_Approve cases occurred in the first week of August; 47 Request_More_Info cases never activated before evidence arrived.

**Disputed propositions (turn on authority, scope, identity, or logging assumptions):**

- D1 — *PD-3's authority.* Whether a steering-committee minute plus email can create a valid exception to a controlled standard, especially given the Compliance observer's later disavowal ("monitored exception" vs. "I did not approve weakening CS-9").
- D2 — *Scope of M0 over rejected pre-screen leads* (SUP-709): process owner says in-scope, model owner says out-of-scope, the scope document is ambiguous. Unproved either way.
- D3 — *Scope of M0 over reactivations* (SUP-710): CS-9 is simply silent. Unproved.
- D4 — *Whether a "factual emergency" extends FD-7* past its written expiry (SUP-705). Operations asserts it; no written extension exists.
- D5 — *Whether missing Notify_Requester events are process skips or logging losses* (SUP-702, SUP-706, and the 29% aggregate). Case-dependent: a stable notification_id is strong evidence; a mutable text link entered two days later is weak.
- D6 — *Label equivalence* of QA_Clearance and Quality_Review (SUP-713): audit content supports equivalence; the mapping was approved only after the case.
- D7 — *Identity resolution* in SUP-712: "resolve to the same human through two aliases" is asserted; the resolution method's reliability is the remaining question.

**Burden of proof:**

- *To declare a case NONCOMPLIANT*, the accuser (prosecution/audit) must establish all three of: (a) the case is within the scope of the norm invoked; (b) the norm was in force at the time of the relevant events; and (c) the deviation is evidenced by reliable data and is not plausibly a logging or mapping artifact. Where the organization's own broken logging creates doubt (D5), that doubt is not resolved against the individual case — it is charged to the organization as a systemic data-quality finding.
- *To accept a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION*, the burden reverses and sits on whoever claims the exception: they must show (a) written authority from a body competent to grant it, in force at the event time; (b) that the case met every eligibility condition of that authority; and (c) that all compensating controls attached to the authority were actually executed. Desirability of the outcome, factual continuation of the triggering circumstances, or expectation of future ratification satisfy none of these.
- *To declare a MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT*, it must be shown that the behavior violates no substantive control objective and that the mismatch is attributable to M0's expressiveness, scope ambiguity, or label drift — not to the actor's choice to bypass a control.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially noncompliant, and the noncompliance is not an artifact of an old model. It is concentrated exactly where the risk is highest, and it exhibits the classic pathology of exception normalization.

**1. Formal authority was never obtained for the auto-approval practice, and it was exceeded even on its own terms.** CS-9 is the only controlled norm. PD-3 was a meeting minute and an email — never incorporated into CS-9 or M0 — and the Compliance observer explicitly denies having approved a weakening of CS-9 (D1). Even granting PD-3 arguendo, SUP-703 is indefensible: a HIGH-risk, new, foreign supplier was pushed through Auto_Approve on 2026-06-20 by an operator who "selected pilot despite ineligibility," followed by a EUR 180,000 first purchase. That is not a model problem; it is a manual override of the single most important gate in the process, with nine-times-the-emergency-cap money behind it. The aggregate confirms it is not isolated: 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and 9 with missing eligibility attributes — 23 of 312 cases (7.4%) where the pilot's own preconditions cannot be shown to hold.

**2. Expired authority was treated as living authority.** FD-7 expired automatically at 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-705 ran the identical activate-first pattern on 2026-07-13 with nothing in force. "The flood still factually existed" is precisely the argument written expiry clauses exist to foreclose: emergencies are defined by the directive, not by operations' judgment after the fact. The same disease appears at the pilot boundary: SUP-714 used Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 lapsed, and six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in the first week of August. Operations "believed permanent approval was imminent." Belief in future ratification is not authorization; if it were, expiry dates would be meaningless. This is normalization of deviance in textbook form: an exception becomes a habit, the habit outlives its authority, and each repetition makes the next one feel legitimate.

**3. The most severe deviation has no overlay to hide behind.** SUP-708: a HIGH-risk supplier activated 40 minutes *before* Sanctions_Check, with no emergency and no pilot. Sanctions screening before activation is the one invariant that every source — CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 alike — preserves. That "no transactions occurred before clearance" is luck and mitigation of impact, not compliance; the control failed at the moment of activation.

**4. M0-conformant traces hide violations, which cuts against the defense, not for it.** SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly, yet requester and approver resolve to the same human through aliases — a direct breach of CS-9's explicit segregation-of-duties rule. SUP-715 fits M0, yet the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in one reporting line and the required conflict-of-interest disclosure was never recorded. The prosecution concedes M0 does not encode these rules — but that concession is an argument for *more* enforcement infrastructure, not for leniency: the operation is noncompliant with CS-9 itself, and the control-flow model's blindness let it pass.

**5. The organization broke its own evidence chain.** The CRM migration drove notification missingness from 2% to 29% with mutable text links (SUP-706). An operation that cannot prove it notified requesters in nearly a third of cases has degraded its own auditability. Conformance that cannot be evidenced is, for assurance purposes, conformance that does not exist.

**6. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.** SUP-707's Request_More_Info loop may be good practice, and the 47-case aggregate shows discipline (no activation before evidence). SUP-702 hit every PD-3 condition. SUP-704 respected every FD-7 control. The prosecution does not dispute the *quality* of this behavior. But policy is amended by the policy process, not by accumulated good intentions — otherwise SUP-703 and SUP-714 are the inevitable next step, and the record shows they were. The remedy for a good practice missing from M0 is to amend M0, which the operation never did.

**Acknowledged defects (as the tribunal requires):** the prosecution accepts that M0 cannot express the Request_More_Info loop, encodes no resource or COI rules, has genuinely ambiguous scope for rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710), predates the QA_Clearance rename (SUP-713), and that SUP-711 is a proven migration artifact. None of these defects explains SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, or SUP-715.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0's headline "deviation rate" is a measurement of the model's obsolescence, not of the operation's conduct. Before a single case is condemned, the yardstick itself must be validated — and it fails validation on at least six independent grounds.

**1. M0 is expressively incomplete for behavior everyone agrees is correct.** M0 contains no loop construct at all. SUP-707 — obtaining missing certificates via Request_More_Info before approving — is behavior Compliance itself endorses, and the 47-case aggregate shows it operates as a genuine safeguard: not one case activated before the requested evidence arrived. A model that flags the process's *best* behavior as deviant has forfeited its claim to be the conformance oracle. Note also what M0 gets right and the prosecution should not obscure: `checks` is a true partial order, so SUP-701's sequential ordering of concurrent checks is conformant, not "lucky."

**2. M0 was never updated for two authorized overlays, so their traces deviate by construction.** SUP-702 satisfied every PD-3 condition: LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group, in-window, sanctions before activation, quality review the next business day, complete eligibility fields. SUP-704 satisfied every FD-7 condition: in-window, EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring, quality and retrospective approval the next business day. These are exceptions functioning exactly as designed — the deviation exists only because nobody performed the clerical act of merging the overlays into M0. On PD-3's authority (D1): a steering committee decided, minuted, and communicated it; the Compliance observer wrote "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception" *at the time*. Her later hedge is a dispute about characterization, not a revocation. Holding operators liable for executing a governance-approved pilot because governance filed it badly inverts responsibility. And the pilot's own numbers vindicate it: 289 of 312 cases (92.6%) demonstrably eligible.

**3. M0's scope was never defined, so "deviations" at the boundary are category errors.** SUP-709: M0 forces Quality_Review before Reject_Supplier — performing a quality review on a lead that already failed sanctions is wasteful and CS-9 itself contemplates an "abbreviated pre-screen" for rejected leads. Whether such leads are M0 cases at all is admittedly ambiguous (D2); an ambiguous scope document cannot ground a violation finding. SUP-710: CS-9 has no reactivation procedure whatsoever. A reactivation with valid prior certification that "lacks" Register_Supplier and Quality_Review is not a broken onboarding — it is a different process that the log forced into the wrong bucket.

**4. The data, not the process, deviates.** SUP-711 is conclusively a migration duplicate (same source transaction ID); after deduplication the trace is perfect. SUP-713 executed the identical control under the name QA_Clearance — the audit confirms equivalent content, and the mapping was formally approved a week later; the deviation is a dictionary problem. The CRM migration (N4) means roughly 30% of Notify_Requester events vanish from the onboarding log through no act of any case handler: SUP-702's notification is affirmatively proven by a stable notification_id at +2 hours, and SUP-706 more likely than not notified as well (CRM message at +3 hours). Traces cannot convict when the organization's own pipeline destroys the exculpatory events.

**5. Trace deviation is not business misconduct — and the distinction runs both ways.** SUP-704 and SUP-705 are byte-identical traces; only a date separates a textbook justified exception from an alleged violation. Conversely SUP-712 and SUP-715 are byte-identical to the compliant SUP-701. A conformance regime in which identical traces span the full spectrum from exemplary to severe proves that control-flow checking against M0, alone, measures nothing decision-relevant. Even the defense's hardest cases carry substance the prosecution undersells: SUP-705 kept spend at EUR 12,000, ran monitoring, and completed quality and approval next business day — every *substantive* FD-7 control was honored; only the paperwork of extension was missing. SUP-708, though indefensible as sequencing, produced zero transactions before sanctions clearance — the harm-prevention outcome the control exists for.

**Acknowledged risks and limits (as the tribunal requires):** the defense concedes SUP-703 is a genuine, severe violation no overlay covers; that SUP-708's sanctions-after-activation is unacceptable regardless of outcome; that informal practice cannot extend expired authority indefinitely (SUP-705, SUP-714 sit at or beyond the defensible line); that the undisclosed conflicts in SUP-712/SUP-715 are real CS-9/policy breaches; and that Operations' wish for a permanent auto-approval branch (N5) remains a wish until the policy process grants it.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The single lesson of this record is that no one layer can carry the whole normative load. The revision separates concerns into five layers, each with the formalism actually suited to it.

**Layer 1 — Control flow (POWL, model M1, Section 7).** POWL expresses what it is good at: ordering, concurrency, exclusive routing, and repetition. M1 provides four explicit routes under one XOR — standard manual, low-risk auto-approval, emergency/provisional, and rejection — plus a Request_More_Info loop and the invariant register → decision → notify → close spine. Crucially, the invariant *Sanctions_Check before any activation* is preserved inside every route. What POWL deliberately does **not** decide: *which* route a case may take. Route eligibility is data (risk, country, group, date, amount, authority), and encoding it in control flow is how M0 died; a control-flow model silently becomes wrong every time a threshold changes.

**Layer 2 — Data-aware eligibility/amount/time rules (DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints + pandas checks).** Declarative constraints over the enriched log, each carrying a rule ID and version:
- R-SANC-1 (hard, all routes, all times): `precedence(Sanctions_Check_completed, Activate_Supplier)`. Never waivable — this is what SUP-708 broke.
- R-AUTO-1: Auto_Approve permitted only if `risk == LOW ∧ country == DE ∧ corporate_group_status == APPROVED ∧ eligibility_snapshot complete ∧ an active overlay/policy authorizes the route at event time`. The gate must be *system-enforced* (the UI let SUP-703's operator pick "pilot"; a data check after the fact is too late).
- R-AUTO-2 / R-EMG-2: `response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)` within 2 business days on the auto and emergency routes.
- R-EMG-1: emergency route requires a Record_Emergency_Authority event referencing a directive ID whose validity window contains the activation timestamp; spend cap (EUR 20,000 or as the directive states) enforced against transaction data until Retrospective_Manual_Approval; `existence(Daily_Monitoring)` per calendar day of provisional status.
- R-NOTIFY-1: every Close_Case preceded by a Notify_Requester event bearing a stable notification_id (pandas join to the CRM mirror; alert on missingness > 2%).
- Pandas layer additionally runs dedup (unique source transaction ID per event — fixes SUP-711-class artifacts) and label-mapping application with mapping-version effective dates (fixes SUP-713-class drift).

**Layer 3 — Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (data checks + organizational controls).** These are invisible to control flow by nature:
- R-SOD-1: `resolved_actor_id(requester) ≠ resolved_actor_id(manual approver)` — evaluated on *resolved* identities via a maintained alias table (SUP-712's lesson: alias-level checks are worthless).
- R-COI-1: quality reviewer, approver, and requester screened pairwise against a declared-relationship register; any hit requires a recorded disclosure-and-independence decision *before* approval (SUP-715's lesson: the disclosure record, not the relationship, is the checkable object).
- R-DEL-1: any delegation of approval authority must produce a delegation-evidence event linking delegator, delegate, scope, and window.
- Organizational: periodic alias-table audit; HR-feed of reporting lines into the relationship register.

**Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance (organizational controls + machine-readable overlay registry).** The PD-3 and FD-7 failures were governance failures, so the fix is governance with teeth:
- Every pilot or emergency exists only as a signed entry in an **overlay registry**: overlay_id, issuing authority and their competence basis, scope predicate (machine-evaluable), compensating controls, effective_from, effective_to, and an owner for ratify-or-retire.
- Systems consume the registry: when an overlay expires, the corresponding route option is **automatically disabled** in the UI (this single control prevents SUP-705, SUP-714, and the entire August cluster).
- Post-overlay disposition is mandatory within 10 business days of expiry: either amend CS-9/M1 (as should have happened for the successful pilot core) or formally retire the practice. "Monitored exception" language without a registry entry has no normative force.

**Layer 5 — Explicit scope rules (documentation + case-typing at intake).** Every case is stamped at creation with a case_type that selects its yardstick:
- *Rejected pre-screen leads* (SUP-709): declared **in scope** as M1 cases taking the rejection route, in which Quality_Review is explicitly optional (a sanctions failure suffices to reject). This resolves D2 by decision rather than leaving it to litigation.
- *Reactivations* (SUP-710): a defined subprocess — no new Register_Supplier; Sanctions_Check always re-run; Quality_Review waivable only if prior certification is within a defined validity window (proposal: 12 months) with the waiver and certificate reference recorded; otherwise full re-review.
- *Withdrawals*: a Withdraw_Case activity permitted before the decision, followed by Notify_Requester and Close_Case, so abandoned cases stop looking like incomplete ones.

**Placement summary:** ordering/concurrency/routing/loops → POWL; temporal-existence rules and time-bounded responses → DECLARE/log-skeleton; eligibility, amounts, identity resolution, dedup, mapping → pandas/data checks over enriched events; authority, ratification, disclosure, alias hygiene → organizational controls backed by the overlay registry.

```python
# below, because POWL cannot and should not encode them.
# =====================================================================

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

def make_rmi_loop():
    # LOOP(do=tau, redo=Request_More_Info) => zero or more RMI events.
    # Placed unordered w.r.t. the checks and ordered before approval,
    # so RMI may interleave with checks (as in SUP-707) but approval
    # cannot occur until all requested evidence handling is done.
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                        children=[SilentTransition(),
                                  Transition(label="Request_More_Info")])

# ---------- Route 1: standard manual route ----------
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality   = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_checks    = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality])  # concurrent

std_rmi      = make_rmi_loop()
std_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[std_checks, std_rmi, std_approval, std_activate])
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_rmi, std_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_approval, std_activate)

# ---------- Route 2: low-risk auto-approval route ----------
# Sanctions + master-data validation strictly before Auto_Approve;
# Quality_Review deferred until after activation (<=2 business days,
# enforced in the constraint layer, not expressible in POWL).
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_mdv       = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_rmi       = make_rmi_loop()
auto_approve   = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate  = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality   = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_mdv, auto_rmi,
           auto_approve, auto_activate, auto_quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_sanctions, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_mdv, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_rmi, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_quality)

# ---------- Route 3: emergency / provisional route ----------
# Record_Emergency_Authority makes the invoked directive an explicit,
# checkable event BEFORE provisional activation (prevents SUP-705-style
# invocation of expired authority going unrecorded). Sanctions must
# still precede activation (the invariant SUP-708 broke). Quality
# review and retrospective manual approval follow activation; their
# 2-business-day deadline, the EUR 20,000 cap, and daily monitoring
# are data-layer rules.
emg_authority = Transition(label="Record_Emergency_Authority")
emg_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
emg_activate  = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
emg_quality   = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
emg_retro     = Transition(label="Retrospective_Manual_Approval")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[emg_authority, emg_sanctions, emg_activate, emg_quality, emg_retro])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_authority, emg_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_sanctions, emg_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_activate, emg_quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_activate, emg_retro)

# ---------- Route 4: rejection route ----------
# Sanctions always precedes rejection; Quality_Review is OPTIONAL
# (XOR with tau), legitimizing the abbreviated pre-screen of SUP-709
# while still admitting rejections that follow a full quality review.
rej_sanctions   = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_quality_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                               children=[Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
                                         SilentTransition()])
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_sanctions, rej_quality_opt, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_sanctions, reject)
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_quality_opt, reject)

# ---------- Decision, notification, closure ----------
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                        children=[standard_route, auto_route,
                                  emergency_route, reject_route])

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close  = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

print(M1)
```

**Stated limitations and approximations (not hidden):**
1. POWL cannot restrict *who may take which XOR branch*: nothing in M1 stops a HIGH-risk case from replaying the auto route (SUP-703's failure mode). This is intentionally delegated to system-enforced eligibility gates and rule R-AUTO-1; encoding thresholds in control flow is what made M0 rot.
2. The Request_More_Info loop is approximated as LOOP(tau, RMI), unordered with the checks and ordered before approval. This slightly over-permits (an RMI could in principle occur before any check starts) and under-permits (no RMI after approval); both are acceptable given the 47-case evidence that RMI functions as a pre-approval gate.
3. Duplicate labels across routes (e.g., four `Sanctions_Check` transitions) are distinct POWL objects by design; conformance checking will align an observed event to the route context. This is standard for label-duplicated models but should be borne in mind when interpreting alignments.
4. Reactivations and withdrawals are deliberately **not** in M1: reactivation (SUP-710) is a distinct subprocess (no Register_Supplier; sanctions re-run mandatory; Quality_Review waivable only under a recorded certificate-validity check), and cramming it into one POWL would either force fake Register events or make Register optional for everyone. It gets its own model M1-R plus scope rule SC-2 below.

With M1 as the control-flow spine, the constraint and data layers carrying the conditions POWL cannot, and the overlay registry making exception authority explicit and self-expiring, the specific failure modes in this record — eligibility override, expired-authority continuation, invisible resource breaches, and evidence loss — each acquire a concrete, checkable control.
