# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The warranty process exhibits a structural split between an over-permissive automated fast lane and congested, poorly coordinated manual and repair lanes. Attempting to optimize globally for average cycle time or front-end cost hides severe segment-level failures, compliance breaches, and downstream rework.

### The Fast-Path Paradox (Speed vs. Rework & Compliance)
The most frequent path—`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (2,550 cases entering `Fast approval` directly from auto-screen, plus 240 via `Fraud/risk review`)—is attractive on paper: `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` takes a median of 0.4 hours (mean 1.0h) and costs EUR 0.20 per screen, and `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` takes a median of 1.8 hours. Before rework, `Fast approval` cases are **31% cheaper**.

However, process-mining and outcome evidence exposes why this speed is illusory for complex claims:
* **Downstream Rework Compression:** `Customer complaint/reopen` hits **14.1%** of `Fast approval` cases (versus **9.2%** overall and only **3.3%** on the `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device` path). After factoring in complaints, reshipments (wrong-replacement expected cost is EUR 95 for low-risk accessories and **EUR 410** for high-value devices), and service credits, the fast path's cost advantage shrinks from **31% to just 9%**.
* **Hidden Long-Tail Cycle Time:** When the fast path fails, rework occurs far later in the lifecycle: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages **72.0 hours** (p90: 150.0h) and `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages **96.0 hours** (p90: 180.0h), followed by `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` (mean 40.0h) or `Escalation board` (mean 54.0h).
* **Segment-Specific Distortion:** The fast path works well for **Premium low-risk accessories** (18% of volume; 8h median, 6% reopen, avoiding >24h service-credit exposure) and acceptably for **Standard low-risk accessories** (25% of volume; 13h median, 8% reopen). It breaks down catastrophically for **Premium high-value devices** (14% of volume; **22% reopen** on the fast path vs. **5%** on the reviewed/repair path) and **Standard high-value devices** (21% of volume; **18% reopen** vs. **6%**).

### Evidence-Backed Performance, Quality, Compliance, and CX Issues
Drawing across the process-mining artifacts:
1. **Frequency Directly-Follows Graph (DFG):** Out of 5,000 cases, 3,600 (72%) enter `Auto eligibility screen` with outdated, segment-blind rules, bypassing upfront verification. Meanwhile, 1,430 transitions enter `Request missing evidence` (650 from Auto, 780 from Manual), feeding a loop back to `Manual eligibility review` (890 cases) and driving 320 abandonments (plus 70 abandonments after `Repair quote -> Customer approval`).
2. **Performance DFG:** Manual and evidence-waiting edges dominate tail latency: `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` averages **34.0 hours** (p90: 88.0h); `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` averages **22.0 hours** (p90: 60.0h, driven by waiting for part-price confirmation); and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` averages **30.0 hours** (p90: 80.0h). By contrast, `Quality check -> Quality/compliance close` (345 cases) uses 2 inspectors who remain largely underused outside repair campaigns.
3. **Token-Based Replay Diagnostics:** Against the normative Petri net, log fitness is **0.91**, but only **68% of traces are fitting**—meaning nearly one-third (32%) of all cases violate normative control-flow rules.
4. **Alignment Diagnostics & Low Model Precision:** Frequent move-on-log deviations show `Fast approval` occurring before evidence completeness (violating **R2**), `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices (violating **R1**), and `Customer complaint/reopen` occurring after close. Additionally, 120 reopened claims route from `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` and exit directly to `Dispatch replacement` (65), `Repair device` (45), or `Claim denied` (10) without passing `Manual eligibility review`, directly violating **R3**. Discovered model precision is low because ad-hoc combinations of auto-approval, risk review, and repair proliferate outside policy.
5. **Temporal Profile Deviations:** `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` routinely breaches Premium customer expectations (96h median on the reviewed/repair path vs. high service-credit sensitivity >24h on accessories and high expectations on high-value Premium), while `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` forms a long-tail pattern tied to incorrect replacement part selection.
6. **Rework & Segment Trap (`Unclear defect / missing evidence`):** Representing **22%** of volume, these cases suffer a **140-hour** median cycle time and the highest abandonment. Among all cases entering `Request missing evidence`, **26%** hit the activity at least twice. On the repair side, `Rework repair` hits **12.5%** of repaired high-value devices (vs. 4.0% for low-risk), signaling technical triage gaps.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Gated Conformance & Pre-Dispatch Verification

### Redesigned Process Flow
Replace the single, segment-blind `Auto eligibility screen` with an attribute-aware rules router (deployable in two weeks) and insert a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate staffed by reallocating the currently underused `Quality check` inspectors:
1. **At Intake:** Enforce mandatory capture of customer tier (Premium/Standard), device value (>EUR 400 vs. low-risk accessory), and evidence completeness flag.
2. **Low-Risk Accessories (43%):**
   * *Premium low-risk (18%):* Eligible for `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` immediately without evidence hold (explicitly permitted under **R2**), preserving the ~8h cycle time to avoid >24h service credits.
   * *Standard low-risk (25%):* Routed to `Fast approval` **only if** evidence is complete at intake (**R2** compliant); otherwise held in an automated evidence-wait state prior to `Fast approval`.
3. **High-Value Devices (>EUR 400, 35%):** Hard-block direct `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` (**R1**). All high-value claims route to `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` (prioritizing Premium high-value ahead of Standard). Any high-value case approved for replacement (rather than repair) must pass a **Pre-dispatch quality gate** (part/SKU verification executed by the 2 underused `Quality check` inspectors) before `Dispatch replacement`.
4. **Reopened Claims (R3 Wrapper):** Route `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` outputs (120 cases) through a rapid `Manual eligibility review` sign-off step before final dispatch, repair, or denial.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Targets:** High-value devices (Premium 14%, Standard 21%) currently leaking into `Fast approval`; `Auto eligibility screen` rules; `Dispatch replacement` part-selection errors; and non-conforming `Escalation board` exits.
* **Preserves:** The existing fast path for Premium low-risk accessories (18%).

### Mechanism of Improvement
* **Eliminates R1/R2/R3 Control Violations:** Hard-coding value (>EUR 400) and evidence-completeness checks into the two-week auto-screen update stops unauthorized fast approvals at the source (EUR 0.20/case) and raises fitting traces well above 68%.
* **Cuts EUR 410 Replacement Errors Using Idle Capacity:** Routing high-value replacements through the pre-dispatch quality gate adds 6–18 hours upfront but intercepts incorrect replacement parts before shipment, avoiding 96h-mean post-dispatch reopens and EUR 410 reshipment losses without hiring new inspectors (outside repair-campaign peaks).

### Deliberate Sacrifices & Unresolved Issues
* **Manual Bottleneck Pressure:** Updating auto-screen rules increases `Manual eligibility review` inflow by **15–35%**, threatening the **82% utilization cliff** across the 7 reviewers (252 productive hours/week).
* **Slower High-Value Cycle Times:** Premium high-value devices lose their 20h fast path and absorb both review latency and the 6–18h pre-dispatch gate.
* **Leaves Upstream Evidence & Repair Delays Unresolved:** Does not fix the 22% `Unclear defect / missing evidence` loop (26% repeat requests, 140h median, abandonment) or the part-price waiting delay inside `Repair quote`.

# 3. Redesign B: Front-Loaded Guided Evidence & Repair-First Triage

### Redesigned Process Flow
Instead of adding downstream gates to the replacement path, Redesign B restructures upstream intake and shifts repairable high-value devices out of replacement altogether:
1. **Guided Evidence Portal with Assisted Fallback:** Deploy the new guided evidence portal directly inside `Intake claim` (dynamically prompting for defect photos/serials by device type) to resolve evidence before eligibility routing. To counter abandonment among low digital-literacy customers, add a conditional opt-out/assisted callback trigger if a portal session stalls before submission.
2. **Pre-Priced Repair Catalog & Direct-to-Repair Track:** Eliminate the "waiting for part-price confirmation" bottleneck inside `Repair quote` (3 technicians, EUR 45/hr) by maintaining pre-negotiated part-price matrices for common high-value defects. Clear, repairable Standard high-value claims route directly from `Intake` / `Auto eligibility screen` (with asynchronous `Fraud/risk` validation running concurrently with quote generation) into `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`.
3. **Proactive Milestone Visibility for Standard Segments:** Add automated status notifications during `Request missing evidence`, `Repair quote`, and `Repair device` for Standard customers (low-risk and high-value), explicitly managing expectations around their 72-hour+ tolerance window to suppress status-driven `Customer complaint/reopen` events.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Targets:** `Unclear defect / missing evidence` (22% of volume; 140h median); `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loop (890 transitions, 34h mean); `Manual -> Repair quote` part-price delays (22h mean); and Standard high-value devices (21%) where repair yields a **3.3%** reopen rate vs. **18%** on fast replacement.

### Mechanism of Improvement
* **Defuses the Evidence Loop:** The evidence portal cuts repeated evidence requests by **30–45%**, shrinking the 890-case `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` feedback loop—which directly relieves `Manual eligibility review` utilization without cutting human review quality.
* **Shifts Volume to Lower-Reopen Repair:** Removing part-price waiting compresses `Manual -> Repair quote` (22h mean) and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` (30h mean), making the repair path (3.3% complaint/reopen) competitive with replacement on total net cost while avoiding EUR 410 wrong-replacement errors.
* **Protects the Escalation Board:** Proactive status visibility curbs impatient reopens from Standard customers, preserving the daily 45-minute `Escalation board` slot for complex disputes.

### Deliberate Sacrifices & Unresolved Issues
* **Short-Term Digital Abandonment Risk:** Even with an assisted fallback, moving evidence collection to intake risks higher upfront abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers compared to human follow-up.
* **Leaves Legacy Auto-Screen R1 Gaps Open (If Unpaired):** Does not inherently rewrite `Auto eligibility screen` R1/R2 blocks on clear high-value claims unless paired with rule changes, and leaves the **12.5% `Rework repair` rate** on high-value devices exposed to technician capacity limits (3 technicians).
* **Higher Direct Touch Cost on Repairs:** Increases technician labor utilization (`Repair quote` EUR 45/hr) and repair turnaround relative to automated fast replacements.

# 4. Redesign C: Economic Risk-Tiering, Accessory Self-Resolution & Parallel Surge Buffering

### Redesigned Process Flow
Redesign C restructures the process around **economic exposure** and **elastic capacity**, using financial substitution for low-value claims and parallelized, surge-buffered workflows for high-value claims:
1. **Low-Risk Accessory Self-Resolution & Voucher Option (43%):** For `Auto eligibility screen`-cleared low-risk accessories (especially repeat or ambiguous low-value SKU claims where a wrong replacement costs EUR 95), replace physical `Dispatch replacement` with an instant **automated service-credit / e-voucher** or **customer self-confirmed SKU selection** at intake. Premium accessory claims remain <8h, eliminating >24h service-credit penalties and part-mismatch reopens.
2. **Parallelized Premium High-Value Concierge Track (14%):** Instead of sequential `Intake -> Manual review (7.5h) -> Fraud/risk (18h) -> Repair quote (22h)`, run `Fraud/risk review` and technical/evidence validation **in parallel** immediately at intake. Cross-train the 2 underused `Quality check` inspectors to perform first-pass evidence and part-number verification for Premium high-value devices, keeping the 7 `Manual eligibility review` staff below the 82% utilization cliff.
3. **Monday/Post-Campaign Surge Buffer & Escalation Triage (21% Standard High-Value):** During Monday and post-marketing-campaign `Fraud/risk review` bottlenecks (2 specialists, EUR 52/hr), explicitly hold Standard high-value claims in a visible **48–72h surge buffer** with automated status updates rather than allowing rule-bypass into `Fast approval`. Restrict the daily 45-minute `Escalation board` strictly to Premium/high-value disputes, while routing routine reopen cases through a standardized R3-compliant `Manual eligibility review` resolution template.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Targets:** Low-risk accessories (43%) via economic substitution; Premium high-value devices (14%) via parallel processing; `Fraud/risk review` Monday/campaign peaks; `Manual eligibility review` >82% utilization risk; and the non-scalable `Escalation board`.

### Mechanism of Improvement
* **Eliminates Low-Value Fulfillment Friction:** Substituting vouchers/customer-confirmed SKUs removes warehouse dispatch latency, wrong-accessory costs (EUR 95), and Premium >24h SLA credit exposure in one stroke.
* **Compresses Premium High-Value Latency Without Sacrificing R1/R2:** Parallelizing `Fraud/risk` and technical/part checks cuts serial handoff waits (18h + 22h) while maintaining full risk and evidence conformance (**R1/R2**).
* **Matches Capacity to Demand Volatility:** Buffering SLA-tolerant Standard high-value claims smooths Monday/campaign spikes for the 2 Fraud specialists, while dedicating `Escalation board` minutes strictly to high-value retention maximizes CSAT return per minute.

### Deliberate Sacrifices & Unresolved Issues
* **Margin & Fraud Leakage on Accessories:** Instant vouchers or self-service credits on low-risk accessories reduce parts-mismatch rework but may increase total claim payout rate and invite mild opportunistic claiming.
* **Higher WIP & Coordination Cost on Parallel Claims:** Running `Fraud/risk` and technical/repair prep in parallel means technician or inspector effort is expended on claims that `Fraud/risk` ultimately denies (210 denied cases in baseline).
* **Standard High-Value Wait Penalty & QC Trade-Off:** Standard high-value devices absorb explicit queuing during Monday/campaign surges, and cross-training `Quality check` inspectors reduces repair-inspection slack during repair campaigns.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Segment-Gated Conformance & Pre-Dispatch Verification
* **Failure Mode 1 — Manual Review Queue Collapse:** Pushing +15–35% more cases from updated `Auto eligibility screen` rules into `Manual eligibility review` pushes the 7 reviewers past their **82% utilization cliff** (206.6 of 252 productive hours/week). Queue times explode beyond the current 7.5h mean / 18h p90, causing Premium cases to breach SLAs and triggering fresh `Customer complaint/reopen` waves—recreating the exact congestion the redesign tried to cure.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Pre-Dispatch Gate Latency Drift:** If the pre-dispatch quality gate operates at the upper bound of **18 hours** (rather than 6 hours) or encounters defective SKU data, high-value Premium customers experience multi-day fulfillment delays, shifting dissatisfaction from "wrong part shipped" to "slow fulfillment."
* **Bottleneck Migration:** Bottleneck shifts sharply from downstream `Customer complaint/reopen` / `Escalation board` upstream into **`Manual eligibility review`** and—during repair campaigns when the 2 `Quality check` inspectors are pulled back to post-repair inspection—into the **pre-dispatch quality gate** itself.

### Redesign B: Front-Loaded Guided Evidence & Repair-First Triage
* **Failure Mode 1 — Silent Abandonment & Channel Bypass:** If the guided evidence portal lacks frictionless assisted fallback, low-digital-literacy customers abandon at intake (worsening the 390 baseline abandonments) or deliberately misclassify defects as "clear/simple" to bypass the portal—re-introducing unverified claims downstream.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Unresolved Repair Quality Loop:** Shifting more high-value devices into `Repair quote -> Repair device` without addressing the **12.5% `Rework repair` rate** on high-value devices amplifies technician rework (`Quality check -> Rework repair -> Repair device`, 14h + 20h+ loops), eroding the 3.3% complaint advantage.
* **Bottleneck Migration:** Relieving the `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loop (890 transitions) migrates demand into **`Repair quote` (only 3 technicians)** and **`Quality check` (2 inspectors)**, turning post-repair inspection into a permanent bottleneck rather than a campaign-only one.

### Redesign C: Economic Risk-Tiering & Parallel Surge Buffering
* **Failure Mode 1 — Voucher Abuse & Margin Erosion:** Automated vouchers/credits on low-risk accessories incentivize repeat or borderline claims (lowering the effective cost of filing a claim) and increase direct payout costs if voucher redemption exceeds physical replacement cost.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Wasted Parallel Work & Inspector Contention:** Running `Fraud/risk review` in parallel with inspector-assisted part/evidence verification wastes EUR 38–45/hr technical effort on claims that `Fraud/risk` subsequently denies (210 denials in baseline). Additionally, if repair campaigns coincide with Premium high-value surges, cross-trained `Quality check` inspectors face conflicting priorities.
* **Bottleneck Migration:** Decongesting `Manual eligibility review` and `Escalation board` shifts pressure onto **cross-trained `Quality check` inspectors** (now serving dual intake-verification and post-repair roles) and creates Monday/post-campaign **WIP backlogs in the Standard high-value surge buffer**, testing customer patience if 72-hour commitments slip.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

Because exact queuing elasticities and behavioral responses (abandonment, voucher uptake, manual utilization above 82%) cannot be predicted from static logs, deploy modular, reversible experiments in three stages rather than a big-bang cutover.

### Stage 1 (Weeks 1–3): First Pilot — Segment-Aware Routing + Idle-Inspector Pre-Dispatch Gate on High-Value Devices (Core of Redesign A)
* **Why First:** Updating `Auto eligibility screen` rules takes only two weeks and requires no new IT portal or voucher accounting. Crucially, to prevent blowing through the 82% `Manual eligibility review` cliff on day one, scope the pilot first to **Premium and Standard high-value devices (>EUR 400)** via an A/B shadow/canary rollout (e.g., 25–30% of high-value inflow, prioritizing off-peak days) paired with the 2 underused `Quality check` inspectors running the pre-dispatch part check.
* **Metrics to Monitor:**
  * Conformance: R1/R2 violation rate and fitting-trace share (baseline: 68%).
  * Queue health: `Manual eligibility review` weekly utilization (vs. 82% threshold) and intake-to-review p90 wait time (baseline: 18h).
  * Quality & Speed: Pre-dispatch gate dwell time (target 6–18h window), caught wrong-SKU rate, and post-dispatch `Customer complaint/reopen` rate at 7–14 days post-dispatch (given 96h mean / 180h p90 reopen lag).
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * **Stop / Throttle:** Manual review utilization sustains **>80–85%** with p90 review wait rising >50% above baseline, OR pre-dispatch gate latency exceeds **18–24 hours**, OR Premium high-value complaint/reopen fails to drop materially below the 22% baseline.
  * **Go:** High-value R1/R2 violations fall sharply toward zero, manual utilization stays **<80%**, and caught wrong-part errors confirm net cost savings against EUR 410 reshipment cost.

### Stage 2 (Weeks 4–8): Upstream Evidence Portal (w/ Assisted Fallback) + R3 Escalation Fix (Elements of Redesign B & A)
* **Sequencing Logic:** Only *after* observing real manual-review load in Stage 1 should the organization expand strict R1/R2 auto-rules to 100% of high-value claims—and it must pair that expansion with the **guided evidence portal** so that the 30–45% reduction in repeat evidence requests offsets the +15–35% manual-review inflow. Simultaneously implement the mandatory **R3 `Manual eligibility review` sign-off** on `Escalation board` exits and automated status updates for Standard tiers.
* **Metrics to Monitor:**
  * Repeat `Request missing evidence` rate (baseline: 26% occurring $\ge 2\times$).
  * Intake abandonment rate segmented by age/channel proxy for digital literacy (baseline: 390 total abandoned cases).
  * `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` volume (baseline: 890) and p90 wait (baseline: 88h).
  * R3 compliance rate on reopened claims.
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * **Stop / Adjust Fallback:** Total abandonment rises **>2–4 percentage points** above baseline in low-digital-literacy cohorts, OR repeat evidence rate drops **<15%** (failing to offset Stage 1 manual inflow).
  * **Go:** Repeat evidence requests fall **25–45%**, net manual-review utilization stabilizes **<80%**, and abandonment remains flat or declines.

### Stage 3 (Weeks 9–14): Selective Economic Substitution, Pre-Priced Repair & Surge Buffering (Elements of Redesign B & C)
* **Sequencing Logic:** With conformance stabilized (Stage 1) and evidence loops shrunk (Stage 2), test higher-complexity structural levers in isolated cohorts:
  1. Test **instant SKU-confirmation / voucher option** on Standard low-risk accessories (25% of volume) to assess fraud/payout leakage vs. EUR 95 wrong-part savings.
  2. Pilot **pre-priced part catalogs** on the top 5 high-value repairable defects to compress `Manual -> Repair quote` (22h mean) and evaluate whether `Repair quote` (3 techs) can absorb redirected volume without triggering `Rework repair` >12.5%.
  3. Introduce **Monday/campaign surge buffering** with proactive 72h status messaging for Standard high-value claims while testing parallel `Fraud/risk` + technical check on Premium high-value claims.
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * **Stop:** Accessory claim payout/volume spikes **>10–15%** above baseline (indicating voucher abuse), OR `Repair quote` backlog grows >50% / `Rework repair` exceeds **12–15%** on high-value devices, OR Standard high-value reopens rise during Monday surge holds.
  * **Go:** Net cost per resolved claim (claims + labor + reshipments + credits) declines while Premium SLAs and overall fitting traces improve.

### How to Verify Improvements Are Real Rather Than Shifted Downstream
1. **Use Cohort-Matched Longitudinal Windows ($\ge$ 21–30 Days):** Because `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` has a **70-hour median and 180-hour p90**, evaluating a pilot after 48–72 hours will falsely declare victory. Track every cohort until at least 30 days post-close to capture delayed reopens, reshipments, and service credits.
2. **Measure Net Full-Lifecycle Cost, Not Activity Cost:** Explicitly track `auto + manual + fraud + repair + QC labor + wrong-part reshipments + service credits + voucher payouts` per resolved case so front-end savings cannot hide downstream losses (the exact trap that reduced the fast path's 31% upfront savings to 9% net).
3. **Track Cross-Resource Utilization & Abandonment Together:** Monitor utilization across all five constrained pools (`Manual`, `Fraud`, `Repair`, `QC`, `Escalation board`) alongside `Claim abandoned` rates. Faster cycle times paired with rising abandonment or spiking utilization in an adjacent pool indicate problem displacement rather than process improvement.
4. **Re-Run Process-Mining Conformance & Precision Checks Post-Pilot:** Recompute token-based replay fitness, fitting-trace percentage (vs. 68%), and R1–R3 alignment violations on pilot logs to confirm that improvements did not rely on undocumented policy workarounds.
