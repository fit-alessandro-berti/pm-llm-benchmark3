1. **Median vs. mean service time** – The table supplies `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min`, but queueinglaw claims (e.g., Little’s Law) require the *mean* service time. Median is robust to rightskew but is not a moment of the distribution; substituting it for mean can bias flowtime estimates.  
2. **Crossday carryover backlog** – Cases can remain in the system overnight (no service occurs while closed). The `mean_wip` snapshot does not distinguish sameday WIP from backlog carried from the previous day, so using it as `L` in Little’s Law may conflate acrossday state.  
3. **Outageinduced nonstationarity** – 20260415 has `outage_min = 180` (15% of opening hours). This day’s completions (58) collapse relative to arrivals (105), and `mean_wip` spikes to 47. Pooling it with normal days distorts all regimelevel statistics.  
4. **Complexvariant confound** – `complex_pct` rises from ~20% (early April) to ~45% (late April/weekends). The increase may reflect a real casemix shift, a codingclassification change, or both. Without loglevel variant tags, the operational driver is ambiguous, and pooling mixes potentially distinct states.  
5. **Simpson’sparadoxlike reversals from pooling** – Aggregate averages can mask opposite subgroup trends. For example, pooled `staff_fte  5.1` and `mean_wip  30` suggest moderate utilization, yet weekdays vs. weekends, and pre vs. postshift regimes, show markedly different demandcapacity balances. Pooling may therefore lead to the opposite of the correct operational conclusion.  

*Additional note*: Daily rows are not independent observations; they exhibit strong weekday/weekend seasonality, a staffing intervention, and an outage. Treating them as i.i.d. for parametric models is invalid.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative A: Lowcomplex vs. Highcomplex threshold at `complex_pct = 30%`**  
- **Lowcomplex** (complex_pct < 30%): comprises most earlyApril weekdays (Apr 6–14, where values range 18–25) and a few later days with 23–25. Groups across weekdays and weekends, mixing different staffing levels.  
- **Highcomplex** (complex_pct  30%): captures lateApril weekdays (Apr 20+) and all weekends where `complex_pct` is 40–44.  
- *Strength*: Isolates the casemix effect; highcomplex days show roughly double the `mean_wip` (44 vs. 20) and higher service times.  
- *Weakness*: Confounds the staffing intervention (postApr 29 `staff_fte` = 6.5–7.0) with the complex effect; weekends with high `complex_pct` are grouped with highcomplex weekdays but have `staff_fte` = 2.5–3.0, producing very different utilization ratios. The threshold also cuts through the outage day (complex_pct = 25), forcing it into lowcomplex and obscuring its disruptive nature.

**Alternative B: Staffingcapacity tiers**  
- **Reduced capacity** (`staff_fte  3.0`): all 8 weekend days.  
- **Standard capacity** (`5.5  staff_fte  6.5`): weekdays with `staff_fte` = 5.5 (Apr 10, Apr 24) or 6.5 (Apr 17, May 1) — about 5 days.  
- **Increased capacity** (`staff_fte  7.0`): postintervention Apr 29–30 (2 days).  
- *Strength*: Directly highlights capacity variation; makes clear that weekends operate with markedly less FTE.  
- *Weakness*: Ignores demand differences. Standardcapacity weekdays include both lowdemand early April (arrivals ~92–104) and highdemand late April (arrivals ~115+), so a single “standard” label masks the need for increased capacity during highcomplex periods. The tiers also do not account for the outage day’s capacity reduction.

**Comparison with the primary segmentation**  
The primary regimes respect the temporal ordering and known change points (complexvariant shift ~20260420, staffing intervention 20260429, outage 20260415). They avoid mixing states that have distinct demandcapacity dynamics, thereby preventing ecological fallacy and supporting actionable policy. The alternatives are simpler and useful for quick exploratory checks, but they risk attributing operational patterns to the wrong driver (e.g., labeling a staffingdriven recovery as a “complexmix” effect, or vice versa). The primary segmentation therefore offers a more defensible basis for operational decisions, while the alternatives serve as useful sensitivity checks.

**Why four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model**  
- They are **single realizations** under potentially nonidentical conditions (different `complex_pct`, staffing, outage status).  
- No interarrival time distributions are available; only binned hourly counts exist.  
- **No replication**: we cannot compute confidence intervals for arrival or service parameters.  
- The vectors capture only the intraday shape on specific days, not the full diurnal pattern under stationarity, nor the weekly seasonality beyond what is observed.  
- Consequently, any model fitted to these four vectors (e.g., arrival rate, servicetime distribution) would be highly tentative and contextspecific, not a general stochastic description.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing**  
- The regime analysis shows that a static 6.0FTE target matches baseline weekdays and postintervention recovery but is **insufficient** during highcomplex weekdays (where `mean_wip` reaches 50 and arrivals often exceed completions, producing backlog growth). Weekends are **overstaffed** at 6.0FTE; 2.5–3.0FTE would suffice.  
- **Recommendation**: implement dynamic FTE scheduling—6.0FTE during baseline periods, ramp to 7.0FTE during highcomplex windows (Apr20–28 and postintervention until backlog cleared), and reduce to 2.5–3.0FTE on weekends. Consider split shifts or staggered starts to cover the 08:00–10:00 peak, which accounts for 17–50% of daily arrivals.

**Backlog recovery**  
- The outage on 20260415 triggered a `mean_wip` spike to 47 (from a baseline ~20). Recovery required the next day’s elevated completions (129 vs. 105 average) and the staffing intervention bump to 7.0FTE.  
- **Policy**: after any outage exceeding 60min, trigger a 2day staffing increase and mandate a backlogclearance audit at 07:00 the following morning. Log the 07:00 backlog count going forward to quantify carryover and assess recovery dynamics.

**Peak smoothing**  
- The 08:00–09:59 peak fraction ranges from 17% (weekends) to 50% (highcomplex weekdays). Such concentration inflates p90 service times and WIP.  
- **Recommendation**: introduce earlyappointment slots or a staggered opening (e.g., 08:00 for highdemand days, 09:00 for lowdemand) to flatten the arrival curve. Even a 10% redistribution of the peak could reduce `mean_wip` by an estimated 15–20% (based on the difference between 50% and 30% peak fractions in the data).

**Datacollection upgrades**  
- Current aggregates lack the granularity for precise queueing analysis. Prioritize:  
  * Logging the number of cases waiting at 07:00 each day.  
  * Recording the exact timestamp of the staffing intervention (currently only datelevel knowledge).  
  * Tagging each arrival with its variant type (complex vs. simple) at entry, to disentangle codingmix from genuine casemix shifts.  
  * Capturing intraday WIP at a finer granularity (e.g., every 5min) or at least recording 07:00 and 19:00 counts.

**A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under a segmented view**  
> **“Maintain 6.0FTE staffing at all times.”**  
> *Pooledaverage view*: Average daily arrivals 90, pooled `staff_fte` 5.1, and the rounded 6.0FTE yields a utilization of ~15 cases/FTE, which appears moderate. The pooled `mean_wip` 30 also seems manageable, leading one to conclude that 6.0FTE is a safe, round number that covers most days.  
> *Segmented view*: During highcomplex weekdays (Apr20–28), 6.0FTE cannot absorb the elevated demand (arrivals 106–121, `mean_wip` 37–50, often completions < arrivals), leading to sustained backlog growth and delayed service. On weekends, 6.0FTE is excessive idle capacity, inflating operational costs without service benefit. The pooled mask therefore creates a “Goldilocks” illusion: the staffing level appears appropriate on average, but it is systematically inadequate during highrisk periods and wasteful during lowrisk periods. A dynamic, regimeaware staffing plan would reduce both backlog risk and idlecost waste.

    return float(x.var(ddof=0) / mu)  # population variance

def coefficient_of_variation(x):
    x = np.array(x, dtype=float)
    mu = x.mean()
    if mu == 0:
        return np.nan
    return float(x.std(ddof=0) / mu)

burstiness_results = []
for _, row in rep_df.iterrows():
    a_fano = fano_factor(row["arrivals_by_hour"])
    a_cv = coefficient_of_variation(row["arrivals_by_hour"])
    c_fano = fano_factor(row["completions_by_hour"])
    c_cv = coefficient_of_variation(row["completions_by_hour"])
    burstiness_results.append({
        "date": row["date"].strftime("%Y-%m-%d"),
        "note": row["note"],
        "arrivals_fano": round(a_fano, 2),
        "arrivals_cv": round(a_cv, 2),
        "completions_fano": round(c_fano, 2),
        "completions_cv": round(c_cv, 2)
    })
burst_df = pd.DataFrame(burstiness_results)

# Acrossday CV of daily arrivals
daily_cv_arrivals = df["arrivals"].std(ddof=0) / df["arrivals"].mean(ddof=0)
print(f"Acrossday CV of daily arrivals: {daily_cv_arrivals:.2f}")

# Simple lag1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals
arrivals_series = df["arrivals"].values
n = len(arrivals_series)
mean_a = arrivals_series.mean()
cov_lag1 = np.mean((arrivals_series[1:] - mean_a) * (arrivals_series[:-1] - mean_a))
var_a = ((arrivals_series - mean_a) ** 2).mean()
rho1 = cov_lag1 / var_a if var_a != 0 else np.nan
print(f"Lag1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals: {rho1:.2f}")

df["cusum"] = (df["mean_wip"] - overall_mean_wip).cumsum()
# Detect date where regime label flips (mean_wip < 30 vs >= 30)

change_dates = df[df["regime_simple"].diff().abs() > 0]["date"]

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Little'sLaw implied flowtime for chosen windows
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
lam_opening = 12  # hours

def little_implied(mean_wip, mean_arrivals_per_day, use_completions=False):
    if use_completions:
        lam = mean_arrivals_per_day / lam_opening  # using arrivals as base; note limitation
        # Here we simply reuse the arrivalsbased lambda for illustration;
        # in a full implementation one would use mean_completed.
        pass
    lam_h = mean_arrivals_per_day / lam_opening
    if lam_h == 0:
        return np.nan, np.nan
    W_h = mean_wip / lam_h
    W_min = W_h * 60
    return W_h, W_min

little_results = []
for regime, group in df.groupby("regime"):

        W_h_comp, W_min_comp = little_implied(mean_wip, mean_completed, use_completions=True)
