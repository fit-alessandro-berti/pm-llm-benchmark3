# 1. Fairness dimensions

The procedural question is whether applicants with comparable eligibility facts face consistent evidence demands and decisions, and whether applicants who need support receive it in time to participate on equal terms. Fair procedures can require different activities for different applicants.

| Dimension | What the assessment should measure |
|---|---|
| Outcome parity | Approval is 78% in A, 63% in B, 69% in C and 58% in D. These differences establish unequal observed outcomes. They cannot establish how similarly eligible applicants were treated. |
| Adjusted outcome disparity | After the preliminary adjustment, approval remains 5.5 percentage points lower for B, 2.2 lower for C and 3.1 lower for D than for A. The adjustment uses recorded case characteristics and lacks an independent determination of eligibility. |
| Procedural burden | Count document demands, requests for previously supplied evidence, applicant contacts, visits and transfers per case. An internal review and a demand that an applicant resubmit documents impose different costs. |
| Timeliness | Compare time to first decision, notification, activation for approved cases and appeal resolution. Examine the median, upper tail and deadline breaches. C’s 90th percentile cycle time is 38.8 days, against 21.4 for A. |
| Consistency of routing | For each policy trigger, measure missed required reviews and reviews imposed without documented grounds. Examine discretionary supervisor escalation among comparable cases. |
| Quality and accessibility of support | Among applicants who need support, assess whether it was offered, timely, in the requested language or format, and effective before decision. A logged contact establishes little about comprehension or accommodation fulfillment. |
| Appeal and rework burden | Separate agency corrections, applicant resubmissions, reopenings and appeals. Assess access to appeal, time to remedy and the share of decisions corrected. A high appeal rate can reflect a decision problem or an accessible remedy. |
| Resource allocation | Assess access to qualified staff, available hours relative to demand, queue priority and continuity of ownership. Specialist assignment can be appropriate even when too little capacity is allocated to the queue. |

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

1. **Define the cohort, clock and denominator.** Use one record per application for case-level comparisons. Anchor cohorts at submission and use baseline attributes as recorded then. Measure time to first decision separately from time through appeal or closure. Use a common follow-up window or time-to-event methods for pending cases. Compare appeals both per notified applicant and among applicants with comparable appealable decisions and time to file.
2. **Compare applicants on common ground.** Report raw differences, then match or stratify on submission month, region, channel, household size band, prior-case count, intake documentation and risk band. Standardize group-specific estimates to a common distribution of comparable cases. For durations, standardize distributions before estimating quantiles. Check balance, cell sizes, overlap and the share of each group excluded for lack of comparators. Report uncertainty intervals that preserve whole cases and account for office or worker clustering where feasible. Check group differences within region and channel, especially in the two backlog regions.
3. **Test routing at the point of decision.** Compare entrants to each routing point with the same documented trigger and similar evidence available then. Test whether required reviews occurred before decision and whether extra reviews had recorded grounds. Analyze actual language and accommodation requests separately from group membership. Neither appears among the preliminary model’s controls. Report overall burdens as well as comparisons within support-need strata. Where A has few comparable support users, compare users of the same service across offices or against an appropriate timeliness benchmark. Identify where overlap is insufficient for estimation.
4. **Distinguish route frequency from experience on a route.** Compare standardized probabilities of V1–V6, other variants, repeated activities and appeal branches. Then compare waits, handovers and rework among users of the same route. For a diagnostic decomposition, hold route shares fixed and estimate how much difference persists within routes. Routes, workers and appeals are assigned or realized during processing. Conditioning on them can hide inequity in queue entry or select cases with different unrecorded needs. Keep them out of the primary baseline adjustment.
5. **Locate delays and assignment effects.** For each stage, estimate the fraction exposed, visits per exposed case and time per visit. With suitable timestamps, split elapsed time into queue time, active service and time awaiting an applicant response. Distinguish time until a document request is issued from time until documents return. Stage medians cannot be added to recover a case median. Within region, day, task, need and priority, compare qualified-worker assignments, return transfers and time to service. Relate demand to staffed hours where rosters exist. If a gap shrinks within the same team, investigate the team’s capacity and the rules assigning cases to it.
6. **Challenge the controls.** Re-estimate with and without risk band and completeness, separately and jointly. Repeat on a stratified sample using independently reviewed eligibility facts and document requirements. Check risk classification against audited evidence across groups, including cases outside the fraud queue. Check whether incompleteness was recorded before accessible instructions were supplied and whether channel reflects an access constraint. Report how conclusions change. Residual disparities identify where to review matched files, reasons for exceptions and applicant accounts. They do not independently establish discrimination.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

- **Policy-based review.** Higher-risk cases must enter fraud review. More D cases could follow V3 even if workers apply the trigger consistently. Audit whether the subsequent supervisor referral also had documented grounds.
- **Required support under scarce capacity.** Bilingual and accommodation work may require extra skilled service time. Limited coverage can add idle days on top of that work. Comparable active service times but longer queues on low-coverage days would support the capacity explanation.
- **Transfer delays.** Returning a translated file to a generalist or forwarding an accommodation file to a supervisor can create a second queue or prompt a new owner to repeat a check. Compare return transfers with one-owner cases of similar complexity in the same office.
- **Access barriers recorded as complexity.** Instructions unavailable in the applicant’s preferred language or format can lead to incomplete submissions, repeated requests and denial. Review the instructions and documents actually available at the first request.
- **Local discretion and communication.** Officers may escalate similarly documented files at different thresholds. Notices may also leave applicants unsure how to correct an error. Compare escalation reasons within offices and distinguish misunderstood notices, later-arriving evidence and agency errors in appeal files.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

Risk scores can inherit disparities in past referrals, investigations and recorded findings. Broad bands can hide differences in underlying facts. Completeness can reflect inaccessible forms, staff coding or an update made after assistance. Prior-case count can include repeat applications after earlier administrative problems. Adjusting for such measures can absorb part of the burden under investigation.

Language preference does not establish a request, interpreter availability, literacy or comprehension. A recorded accommodation request omits unmet or undisclosed needs, type and urgency of accommodation, and whether it worked. Household bands and channel omit eligibility detail, digital access, travel and applicant effort. An outcome records neither decision correctness nor dignity of treatment. An appeal flag omits reasons, merits, representation, reversals, remedy quality and people discouraged from filing.

Daily workload cannot show the queue, priority, staffing hours or worker availability at assignment. A single event timestamp cannot distinguish work from queueing, parallel activity, delayed data entry or time with the applicant. The supplied summaries lack cross-tabulations, uncertainty intervals, censoring details and group-specific within-variant times. They do not identify the causal contribution of any single mechanism.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Action | Trade-off and check |
|---|---|
| Add bilingual and accommodation capacity in the regions and time periods with the longest waits. Provide qualified backup for specialists who also review decisions. | Training takes time and moving staff can displace delays into other queues. Track staffed hours, tail waits and quality across affected teams. |
| Set service-level targets for time to first substantive support and to the next processing step. Retain an end-to-end timeliness measure. | A contact-only target invites token contacts or rushed consultations. Measure fulfillment, comprehension and deadline breaches alongside speed. |
| Retain a named case owner who can consult a specialist directly. Reduce duplicate transfers while preserving assistance and required independent reviews. | Ownership can overload generalists or erode specialist oversight. Pilot the change and audit completed reviews, errors and repeat requests. |
| Provide accessible, translated requests and explanations identifying the missing evidence, decision reason, remedy and appeal deadline. | Translation and individualized review consume staff time. Better notice may initially increase appeals by improving access. Test comprehension and correction rates. |
| Monitor standardized routing, repeated requests, tail waits, appeals and rework by group, region and channel alongside raw burdens. | Small cells create noisy estimates and privacy risks. Pool periods where needed, show intervals and use alerts to trigger file review. |
| Audit risk scoring and completeness labels against independent file review, including low-risk cases and files submitted through different channels. Correct unsupported triggers or demands. | Historical fraud findings are selectively observed and independent review is costly. Validate changes against both missed legitimate referrals and unnecessary reviews. |

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The process shows credible signs of unequal procedural burden, strongest for B’s document requests and transfers and C’s specialist queue. D’s fraud routing has a plausible policy basis, while its risk classification and elevated appeal rate deserve separate scrutiny. Overall procedural fairness remains indeterminate. The evidence identifies where to investigate avoidable delays and inconsistent demands, but cannot establish how much additional time was necessary or whether similarly eligible applicants faced discrimination.
