## 1. Materiality Framework

**Purpose.** The purpose of this framework is to convert alignment moves into business consequences. Raw alignment cost is treated only as evidence that a trace diverged from the normative control-flow model under a particular cost function. It is not, by itself, a measure of compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or evidentiary confidence.

**Severity scale 1–5.**

| Severity | Business meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Harmless technical noise, expected silent move, or out-of-scope event with no control, financial, or customer impact. |
| 2 | Governance or data-quality issue: model scope mismatch, label mismatch, or minor evidence hygiene issue with limited direct exposure. |
| 3 | Material evidence or communication gap: an important control may have occurred, but evidence is incomplete, non-authoritative, or not reliably linked to the case. |
| 4 | Significant control failure: a required approval, screening, or notification control is bypassed or undocumented, with material exposure or customer/regulatory consequence. |
| 5 | Severe irreversible control failure: high-value payment, fraud-sensitive condition, payment before approval, or bypass of mandatory fraud/manual controls; potential fraud, financial loss, regulatory breach, or customer harm. |

**Severity factors.**

1. **Control objective:** Was the deviation about approval before payment, fraud screening, coverage validation, assessment evidence, notification, or scope integrity?
2. **Amount/exposure:** Larger paid amounts increase financial exposure, but small amounts can still be severe if fraud-sensitive, such as a recent bank-change bypass.
3. **Reversibility:** Payment is harder to reverse than notification. A payment before approval is more severe than a missing notification event if notification can be reconstructed.
4. **Timing:** Paying before approval, approving after payment, or notifying outside the required window matters more than the order of harmless UI events.
5. **Customer/regulatory harm:** Denied or withdrawn claims may be out of scope for a payable-claim model but still require correct customer communication and complaint handling.
6. **Recurrence:** Repeated retry duplicates may be individually harmless but can distort monitoring; repeated policy violations indicate systemic control failure.
7. **Evidence reliability:** Verbal checks, mutable text links, unstable document IDs, and external systems reduce confidence and may create compliance risk even when the substantive activity occurred.
8. **Model scope:** A high-cost alignment may be caused by a model that is intentionally limited to payable claims. In that case, raw cost reflects scope mismatch, not necessarily operational failure.

**Confidence is not severity.**  
Confidence measures how strongly the available evidence supports the interpretation. Severity measures business consequence. For example, CL-602 is high severity because a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a recent bank change lacks documented fraud screening, but confidence is moderate because the missing log event could reflect a logging gap rather than proof that no fraud check occurred. Conversely, CL-609 has zero alignment cost and high confidence of policy failure because the data context directly violates the auto-approval policy.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is a technical distance measure under a chosen cost function. In the supplied configuration, every visible log/model move costs 1, and hidden moves cost 0. That design makes the raw cost useful for detecting structural divergence, but it is insufficient for business prioritization.

1. **Zero-cost alignments can hide severe policy failures.**  
   CL-609 and CL-610 have raw cost 0 because the process tree permits the auto-approval branch. However, CL-609 violates P1 by auto-approving EUR 18,000 at medium risk, and CL-610 violates P1/P2 by auto-approving after a recent bank change. The alignment cost is blind to data conditions such as amount, risk level, and bank-change timing. A zero-cost trace can therefore be a high-risk control failure.

2. **Low-cost alignments can represent high-exposure control failures.**  
   CL-602 has raw cost 1 because only one model move, `Fraud Screen`, is missing. But the business context is a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a bank change one day earlier and no fraud evidence. The raw cost does not reflect the financial exposure or the fraud-control objective. Similarly, CL-608 has raw cost 1, but the missing notification evidence affects a EUR 250,000 high-risk claim and the external link is mutable.

3. **High-cost alignments can be harmless or out of scope.**  
   CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4, but the high cost is caused by applying a payable-claim model to a denied lead and a withdrawn claim. These cases may have no payment exposure. The raw cost is informative about model scope mismatch, but it should not be treated as severe operational risk.

4. **Hidden moves are technically fitting, not evidence of compliance.**  
   CL-605 contains a hidden routing transition `tau` with cost 0. This is expected from the converted Petri net and is not a business deviation. Hidden moves can make an alignment look cleaner than the underlying process design is, but they should not be escalated unless they conceal a meaningful control or data-policy issue.

5. **Uniform move costs ignore reversibility and timing.**  
   CL-604 has raw cost 3, while CL-601 also has raw cost 3. But CL-604 includes payment before manual approval and missing fraud screening, a severe and potentially irreversible control failure. CL-601 includes UI telemetry, which P5 explicitly says is not a business activity. The same numeric cost can therefore mean opposite things in business terms.

6. **Duplicated telemetry inflates cost without inflating risk.**  
   CL-603 has raw cost 1 because a duplicate `Notify Claimant` event appears. The additional pattern evidence shows 74 cases with the same message ID and a retrying connector. The business risk is low if only one notification was sent, but the technical cost creates false exceptions.

7. **Missing events are not proof that activities did not occur.**  
   CL-606 has a missing `Assess Loss` event, but an external adjuster signed an assessment report. The raw cost 1 does not capture whether the real issue is a logging gap, an evidence-linkage gap, or an actual skipped assessment. CL-608 is similar: the core log lacks notification, but CRM/portal evidence may exist. Alignment cost cannot distinguish missing activity from missing evidence.

8. **Activity granularity and label mapping affect cost but not necessarily control effectiveness.**  
   CL-611 has raw cost 2 because `Assess Damage` is logged instead of `Assess Loss`. If the substantive assessment occurred and fraud/manual controls were applied, the business risk is lower than the raw cost suggests. The issue is primarily event taxonomy and data governance.

9. **Scope decisions can dominate cost.**  
   CL-607 and CL-612 show that model scope can create large technical penalties for legitimate non-payable outcomes. If the process tree is designed only for payable claims, denied and withdrawn cases should not be treated as equally severe as payment-before-approval failures.

10. **Business severity requires judgment about control objectives.**  
   The most important question is not “how many moves cost 1?” but “which control objective failed, what was exposed, was it reversible, is the evidence reliable, and will it recur?” CL-604, CL-602, CL-610, and CL-609 should be prioritized above CL-607, CL-612, CL-601, and CL-603 despite lower or equal raw costs in some cases.

def classify_move_pattern(row):
    case_id = row["case_id"]
    move_type = row["move_type"]
    activity = row["activity"]

    if activity in {"Open Workbench", "Autosave", "Close Workbench"}:
        return "UI telemetry"

    if case_id == "CL-603" and move_type == "L" and activity == "Notify Claimant":
        return "Notification retry duplicate"

    if case_id == "CL-602" and move_type == "M" and activity == "Fraud Screen":
        return "Missing fraud evidence"

    if case_id == "CL-604" and (
        (move_type == "L" and activity == "Pay Claim") or
        (move_type == "M" and activity in {"Fraud Screen", "Pay Claim"})
    ):
        return "Payment before approval"

    if case_id == "CL-606" and move_type == "M" and activity == "Assess Loss":
        return "Assessment evidence gap"

    if case_id == "CL-608" and move_type == "M" and activity == "Notify Claimant":
        return "Notification evidence gap"

    if case_id in {"CL-609", "CL-610"} and activity == "Auto Approve":
        return "Data-aware auto-approval failure"

    if case_id == "CL-611" and (
        activity == "Assess Damage" or
        (move_type == "M" and activity == "Assess Loss")
    ):
        return "Event-label mismatch"

    if case_id in {"CL-607", "CL-612"} and move_type in {"L", "M"}:
        return "Out-of-scope terminal"

    .sort_values("technical_cost", ascending=False)
)

move_summary = (
    move_df
    .groupby("case_id")
    .agg(
        visible_moves=("move_type", lambda s: int((s.isin(["L", "M"])).sum())),
        computed_move_cost=("technical_cost", "sum"),
        patterns=("pattern", lambda s: "; ".join(sorted(set(s))))
    )
    .reset_index()
)

case_df = case_df.merge(move_summary, on="case_id", how="left")
case_df["raw_cost_authoritative"] = case_df["raw_cost"]
case_df["cost_matches_supplied"] = case_df["raw_cost"] == case_df["computed_move_cost"]

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Risk-prioritization evidence table.
#
#    This table keeps technical alignment cost separate from business severity.
#    The sort order uses manually assigned business severity as evidence, not a
#    formula that replaces narrative judgment.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
risk_priority = case_df[
    [
        "case_id",
        "raw_cost_authoritative",
        "computed_move_cost",
        "cost_matches_supplied",
        "amount_eur",
        "risk",
        "bank_change_7d",
        "policy_flags",
        "deviation_type",
        "business_severity",
        "confidence",
        "remediation_priority",
        "patterns"
    ]
].copy()

risk_priority = risk_priority.sort_values(
    by=["business_severity", "remediation_priority", "raw_cost_authoritative"],
    ascending=[False, True, False]
)

print("\nMove-pattern summary:")
print(pattern_summary.to_string(index=False))

print("\nRisk-prioritization evidence table:")
print("Note: business_severity is manually assigned and should not be replaced by a formula.")
print(risk_priority.to_string(index=False))
