## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Established Facts:
- **Suppliers undergo a standard onboarding process** governed by the normative POWL model M0, including Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review, Manual_Procurement_Approval, and Activate_Supplier.
- **Certain deviations** exist in practice, including the use of Auto_Approve, Request_More_Info cycles, and activation during emergency directives.
- **Incomplete data** exists in some cases, particularly regarding notification logs and actor aliases.
- **Emergency Directive FD-7** allows for provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval.
- **Pilot decision PD-3** permits low-risk suppliers in approved corporate groups to use Auto_Approve while still requiring Sanctions_Check.
- **Different stakeholder practices** exist, such as the desire for a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch and the acceptance of additional Request_More_Info cycles.

### Disputed Propositions:
- **Compliance vs. Informal Practice**: The normative model M0 enforces strict compliance with CS-9, but operational evidence shows widespread informal practices that bypass certain controls (e.g., Auto_Approve, Request_More_Info loops).
- **Scope and Authority**: M0’s scope does not explicitly cover scenarios like emergency activation, reactivation of closed cases, or the treatment of rejected leads.
- **Data Quality**: The CRM notification migration has led to missing or mutable notification logs, impacting traceability.
- **Model Limitations**: M0 lacks mechanisms to handle loops (e.g., Request_More_Info) and does not inherently enforce resource rules (e.g., different requesters and approvers).

### Burden of Proof:
- **Prosecution**: Must demonstrate that M0 is materially noncompliant with CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7, and that the existing deviations pose significant operational risks.
- **Defense**: Must show that M0 is obsolete, incomplete, or incorrectly scoped, and that the deviations are acceptable given the operational context and risk assessments.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### Compliance Breaches:
1. **Use of Auto_Approve**: Cases SUP-702, SUP-714, and SUP-715 show instances where Auto_Approve was used despite not being part of the normative M0. This bypasses critical checks (Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review) required by CS-9.
2. **Missing Notification Logs**: Cases SUP-706 and SUP-712 indicate missing notification events in the onboarding log, undermining traceability and accountability.
3. **Emergency Directive FD-7 Violations**: Case SUP-704 activates a supplier before Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval, violating FD-7 requirements.
4. **Reactivation Without Formal Process**: Case SUP-710 reactivates a supplier without following the defined M0 process, raising questions about scope and authority.
5. **Rejected Leads**: Case SUP-703 and SUP-703 show that rejected leads are processed with additional Request_More_Info cycles, which M0 does not account for.

### Aggregate Evidence:
- **Auto_Approve Usage**: 289 out of 312 cases used Auto_Approve, with 14 clearly ineligible cases. This suggests a high deviation rate from the normative model.
- **Request_More_Info Cycles**: 47 cases contained Request_More_Info cycles, indicating a deviation from the linear flow in M0.
- **Notification Missingness**: The rate of missing notifications increased from 2% to 29% after CRM migration, affecting auditability and accountability.
- **High-Risk Cases**: Cases like SUP-703 and SUP-708 involve high-risk suppliers, where deviations could lead to serious compliance breaches.

### Conclusion:
The prosecution argues that M0 is materially noncompliant with CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 due to the prevalence of deviations from the normative process. The evidence strongly suggests that the current model does not adequately capture the operational reality, leading to potential compliance risks.

## 3. Defense Brief

### Obsolescence and Incompleteness:
1. **Model Limitations**: M0 does not inherently support loops or parallel processes, which are necessary for handling Request_More_Info cycles and concurrent checks.
2. **Scope Gaps**: The model does not account for emergency directives (FD-7) or reactivation scenarios, indicating a scope limitation.
3. **Resource Rules**: M0 does not explicitly enforce rules regarding different requesters and approvers, which are critical for compliance with CS-9.

### Pilot and Emergency Overlays:
1. **PD-3 and FD-7**: These directives introduce deviations that are necessary for operational flexibility. While not formally part of M0, they are justified by the need to respond to external risks and emergencies.
2. **Pilot Decisions**: The PD-3 decision, although temporary, provides a structured exception that aligns with operational needs, albeit temporarily.

### CRM Migration and Data Quality:
1. **Notification Log Issues**: While the migration to a CRM service has introduced data quality issues, these are more about data integrity than process compliance. The core process remains intact, albeit with some data inconsistencies.
2. **Actor Aliases**: The use of aliases does not inherently violate M0, but it does affect traceability. This can be addressed through policy changes rather than model overhauls.

### Conclusion:
The defense argues that M0 is obsolete and incomplete but acknowledges that the deviations are largely due to operational realities and data quality issues. The model’s limitations can be addressed through enhancements rather than a complete overhaul.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Proposed Layered Architecture:
1. **Revised Control-Flow Model**:
   - **Concurrent Checks**: Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review should be concurrent for standard cases.
   - **Decision Nodes**: Introduce a decision node that considers low-risk auto-approval, emergency activation, and reactivation scenarios.
   - **Request_More_Info Loop**: Implement a loop mechanism to capture additional evidence before final approval.

2. **Data-Aware Rules**:
   - **Eligibility Checks**: Enforce eligibility rules based on PD-3, CS-9, and FD-7 criteria.
   - **Amount and Timing Rules**: Ensure spend caps and timing constraints are respected during emergency activations.
   - **Resource Rules**: Enforce resource constraints, such as different requesters and approvers.

3. **Resource and Conflict-of-Interest Rules**:
   - **Conflict-of-Interest Policy**: Enforce policy compliance through mandatory disclosures and approvals.
   - **Resource Assignment**: Ensure different humans are involved as requesters and approvers where required.

4. **Policy-Overlay Governance**:
   - **Pilot and Emergency Policies**: Define explicit policies for PD-3 and FD-7, including timing and approval criteria.
   - **Reactivation Policy**: Establish clear procedures for reactivating closed cases, including required documentation and approvals.

5. **Scope Rules for Special Cases**:
   - **Rejected Leads**: Define explicit rules for handling rejected leads, including any abbreviated processes.
   - **Reactivations**: Establish a reactivation protocol that aligns with M0 but includes necessary checks and approvals.
   - **Withdrawals**: Define clear procedures for withdrawing a supplier from the onboarding process.

### Implementation Proposal:
- **POWL Model**: Use POWL to define the core process flow, including concurrent checks and decision nodes.
- **Policy Overlay Table**: Create a table that maps policy rules to specific conditions (e.g., eligibility, emergency directives).
- **Event/Data-Collection Proposal**:
  - **Stable Notification IDs**: Ensure unique identifiers for notifications to maintain traceability.
  - **Rule/Version IDs**: Include versioning for policies and rules to track changes.
  - **Eligibility Snapshots**: Capture eligibility data at the time of onboarding.
  - **Actor Aliases**: Use unique identifiers to track actor relationships and resolve alias discrepancies.
  - **Emergency/Pilot Authority**: Encode authority levels for emergency and pilot scenarios.
  - **Immutable Timestamps**: Ensure all timestamps are immutable to maintain auditability.

### Python Code Proposal:

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Define transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Define POWL model
powl_model = StrictPartialOrder()

# Standard route
powl_model.add_node(register)
powl_model.add_node(sanctions)
powl_model.add_node(quality)
powl_model.add_node(manual_approval)
powl_model.add_node(auto_approve)
powl_model.add_node(activate)
powl_model.add_node(request_more_info)
powl_model.add_node(notify)
powl_model.add_node(close)

powl_model.add_edge(register, sanctions)
powl_model.add_edge(sanctions, quality)
powl_model.add_edge(quality, manual_approval)
powl_model.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)
powl_model.add_edge(activate, notify)
powl_model.add_edge(notify, close)

# Low-risk auto-approval route
powl_model.add_edge(register, auto_approve)
powl_model.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
powl_model.add_edge(activate, notify)
powl_model.add_edge(notify, close)

# Emergency/provisional route
powl_model.add_node(fd7_check)
powl_model.add_edge(register, fd7_check)
powl_model.add_edge(fd7_check, activate)
powl_model.add_edge(activate, notify)
powl_model.add_edge(notify, close)

# Request_More_Info loop
powl_model.add_edge(manual_approval, request_more_info)
powl_model.add_edge(request_more_info, quality)

# Define operators
decision_operator = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, children=[manual_approval, auto_approve])
decision_node = Transition(label="Decision_Node", operator=decision_operator)

# Integrate decision node
powl_model.add_node(decision_node)
powl_model.add_edge(quality, decision_node)
powl_model.add_edge(auto_approve, decision_node)

# Final judgment
powl_model.add_node(close)
powl_model.add_edge(decision_node, notify)
powl_model.add_edge(decision_node, close)
```
