Definitions to state in Section 1:
- Case notion: the object type whose instances become traces/cases in a flattened classical event log; each E2O relation between an event and an object of that type places a copy of the event into that object's trace.
- Convergence: one event relates to multiple objects of the lead type, so the event is copied into several cases (e16  O1, O2, O3). This inflates frequency, creates synchronization artifacts, and can fabricate loops.
- Divergence: one object of the lead type relates to multiple events/objects of another type (e.g., O1  two packages P1, P2 and deliveries D1, D2), so within a single case we see repeated activities that actually belong to distinct sub-instances; also one-to-many object relations (O1 contains I11, I12).
- Copied shared events: I treat them as both evidence and duplication — evidence of synchronization points (they carry genuine information about which concurrent lifecycles coincide) and duplication when used in frequency/performance aggregation (each physical event counted once per related case).
- Transformations permitted: I'll allow (a) explicit O2O propagation (e.g., projecting events onto Package via "packs Item" then Item "contained in Order"), clearly labeled as a derived variant, used only for sensitivity comparison, never merged silently with direct E2O; (b) enriching flattened logs with attributes (e.g., activity label suffixes with object identifiers like "Record Payment [M2: partial]") — again labeled; (c) de-duplication by counting unique event IDs per activity rather than trace occurrences.

## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** For a flattening on object type *T*, every event holding a *direct* E2O relation to an instance of *T* is copied into that instance's trace. The choice of *T* defines what "one process instance" means; I evaluate each choice by event coverage, duplication cost, and KPI bias — never by trace length.

**Convergence.** One physical event related to 2 objects of the lead type, so it is copied into several cases. Here: e16 and e24 (three orders O1/O2/O3), e13, e14, e17, e19, e22, e25, e27 (two orders each). Consequences: frequency inflation, identical timestamps across traces, possible spurious loops.

**Divergence.** One lead object fanning out to multiple sub-instances: O1  {P1, P2}  {D1, D2}; O3  {P3, P4}  {D2, D3}. Consequences: repeated activities inside one trace whose sub-instance identity the flattened log cannot express.

**Copied shared events: evidence or duplication?** Both, and I treat them as both simultaneously. e16 copied into O1, O2, O3 is *evidence* that three orders shared truck D2; it is *duplication* the moment Dispatch is counted six times for three physical dispatches. My reporting rule: every frequency claim is stated twice — event-unique and occurrence-based.

**Permitted transformations.** (i) O2O propagation (event  related object  O2O neighbours) as an explicitly labelled derived variant, never silently merged with direct E2O; (ii) activity-label enrichment from O2O qualifiers (e.g., "Record Payment [partial]" for M2 vs "[final]" for M3); (iii) de-duplication keyed on event IDs. All are reversible sensitivity variants, not replacements of the given relation set.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**T1 — Cross-order package erased (Package flattening).** P3's trace (e13e16e24) is activity-identical to P1's (e11e15e23) and P4's (e14e17e27). The log cannot distinguish order-pure P1 from cross-order P3 (I21 of O2 + I31 of O3) or P4 (I32 of O3 + I41 of O4). Four identical variants imply a homogeneity that is false at object level. *Harmful* for any compliance/risk question about order segregation; the trap is silent because the log is internally consistent.

**T2 — Cross-order package as frequency inflation (Order flattening).** e13 is copied into O2 and O3; e14 into O3 and O4. Pack Package appears 6 times for 4 physical packings; Dispatch 6 for 3; Confirm Delivery 6 for 3. O3's trace shows PackPack (e13e14) with two different partner orders, indistinguishable from O1's intra-order split (e11e12); and O2's "pack effort" timestamp e13 physically includes work on O3's item I31. *Context-dependent*: fine for "when were O2's items packed," wrong for capacity accounting.

**T3 — Consolidated delivery as weighted KPI bias (Order flattening).** e16/e24 are copied into O1, O2, O3; e17/e27 into O3, O4. D2's 4h55m lag is counted three times, D3's 22h45m twice. O4's 26h cycle (e04e31) is dominated by e27 (Feb 4, 10:00), a carrier-level event, although O4's own steps (e10, e14, e20, e26) finished early. O1's completion is ambiguous: first confirmation e23 (14:00) or last e24 (16:00)? *Harmful* as a KPI; the copies are simultaneously *evidence* of shared-truck fulfilment.

**T4 — Consolidated delivery as false equivalence (Delivery flattening).** D2's trace (e16e24) is identical to D1's and D3's, hiding that D2 carries two packages and three orders. D3's overnight 22h45m cannot be normalized against D1's 3h because load (1 vs 2 packages) is invisible. *Context-dependent*: safe for pure lag questions, misleading for cost/capacity.

**T5 — Consolidated invoicing as financial double counting (Order flattening).** e19 (Issue Invoice, V2) is copied into O2 and O3, and both instalment events e22 and e25 into both orders. Record Payment occurs 6 times for 4 physical payments, and each of O2, O3 appears individually "paid in two instalments," although there is one invoice (V2) settled in two instalments across both. Cash-flow aggregation from the order log double-counts V2. *Harmful*; corrective view is the Invoice log (V2: e19e22e25, zero copies).

**T6 — Partial payments disguised as rework (Invoice and Order flattenings).** e22 and e25 share the label "Record Payment"; the qualifiers distinguishing M2 (*partially_settles* V2) from M3 (*completes_settlement* V2) exist only in O2O. Even the clean Invoice log shows V2 as Issue InvoiceRecord PaymentRecord Payment, indistinguishable from a corrected/repeated settlement. A conformance rule "exactly one settlement per invoice" flags V2 as deviant. *Context-dependent*: harmless for cash timing; harmful for settlement-status analytics unless labels are qualifier-enriched.

**T7 — Near-singleton lifecycle (Payment flattening).** M1–M4 each appear in exactly one event (e21, e22, e25, e26). Four one-event traces; the DFG is one isolated node; the log skeleton is trivially "always Record Payment." Choosing Payment as lead — tempting for finance — deletes the process. Delivery is nearly as thin: D1–D3 have exactly DispatchConfirm Delivery. *Harmful* as a lead choice; legitimate as a lookup table.

**T8 — Phantom control flow (Order flattening).** O2 and O3 both contain Record Payment (e22, 12:40)  Confirm Delivery (e24, 16:00)  Record Payment (e25, Feb 4, 09:00), producing DFG edges Record PaymentConfirm Delivery (frequency 5) and Confirm DeliveryRecord Payment (frequency 2) — an apparent rework loop that is merely V2's second instalment landing after D2's confirmation. Conversely, the order DFG contains **no direct DispatchConfirm Delivery edge at all**: in all four traces, Issue Invoice (e18–e20) and Record Payment (e21/e22) fall chronologically between dispatch and confirmation, so the carrier path is forced through the billing thread, while the true DispatchConfirm chain with lags 3h/4h55m/22h45m exists only in the Delivery/Item/Package logs. *Context-dependent*: genuinely informative about billing–logistics concurrency; harmful if DFG edges are read as causal precedence.

**T9 — Longest-trace trap (Order flattening).** O3's 13-event trace is the longest, but 9 of its 13 events (e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27) are shared with other orders. Its length reflects O3's hub position across P3/P4, D2/D3 and shared invoice V2 — relational convergence, not behavioural complexity. Declaring the Order flattening "best" because O3's trace is rich, or flagging O3 as "the complex variant," confuses the two. The mirror trap is the Item log, where all six variants are identical, with I21/I31 sharing e13/e16/e24 timestamps exactly. *Harmful* if either is read as process characterisation.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Create, with safeguards:**
1. **Order log** — attach event IDs and related-object lists; report event-unique and occurrence counts side by side. Safe: order lifecycle variants (four distinct traces of 12/9/13/8 events), cycle times (all 26h), split detection (O1, O3 double dispatch), billing–logistics concurrency.
2. **Invoice log** — safe: instalment structure (V2: two instalments), settlement lags (V1 30 min; V2 20h55m to completion; V3 21h20m).
3. **Delivery log** — safe: dispatchconfirm lags (3h, 4h55m, 22h45m).
4. **Item log** — safe for stage lags (e.g., I11: Add 09:15Confirm 14:00) only with copy flags; unsafe for volume (6 pack occurrences, 4 physical).
5. **Package log** — optional; packdispatch lag only (53–60 min). **Payment log: do not create**; use as a timestamp table.

**Questions requiring object-centric views:** "Which packages mix orders, and which orders shared a truck or invoice?"  object interaction summary / OTG (P3–packsI21, I31; D2–carriesP2, P3; V2–billsO2, O3). "Which activities are invisible in which flattening?"  ET–OT graph (Create/Close Order touch only Order; Record Payment touches Order+Invoice+Payment). "Can any objects' reporting be merged?"  equivalent-execution clustering.

**Reconciling contradictions:** (a) classify each metric as *physical* (event-unique) or *stakeholder-facing* (occurrence-based) and report the classification; (b) verify that flattened-minus-OC differences equal the known copy factors (Dispatch 63 = 3 = two copies of e16 plus one of e17); (c) let qualifier-bearing O2O relations (M2/M3) override identical activity labels; (d) never declare one number wrong — record both with provenance.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: primary lead object Order, plus explicitly secondary Invoice and Delivery lifecycles (Package/Item as diagnostics, Payment as a fact table).**

**Decision criteria:** direct-E2O event coverage; existence of exclusive boundary activities; duplication cost; severity of KPI distortion; complementarity of the secondary logs; alignment with the dominant stakeholder question (order-to-cash).

**Strongest supporting evidence:** Order is the only object type related to all 31 events, and e01–e04 (Create Order) and e28–e31 (Close Order) are Order-exclusive, so only the Order log has a genuine start and end. Yet the truths that must not be distorted — V2's instalment structure (e19/e22/e25) and carrier lags (e15e23, e16e24, e17e27) — are clean only in the Invoice and Delivery logs (zero copies), which is precisely why they are kept as co-equal secondary lifecycles rather than absorbed.

**Strongest counterargument:** an OC-DFG with per-object-type frequencies already contains everything the six flattenings contain, without copying; anointing Order "primary" risks anchoring all KPIs on a log with a phantom payment loop (e24e25), no direct DispatchConfirm edge (T8), and 2× financial double counting of V2 across O2/O3. One could argue B is simply C in disguise, since five of six flattenings are trivial.

**Residual risk:** order-level dashboards silently embed carrier consolidation (D2, D3) and invoicing consolidation (V2); the 42/31 occurrence inflation (35%) leaks into capacity numbers; secondary logs may be ignored in practice.

**Conditions to change the decision:** if invoicing routinely consolidates orders (most invoices like V2), order-level financial reporting is structurally biased  switch to C; if the question becomes warehouse- or carrier-centric  Package/Delivery lead; if order-exclusive events (Close Order) disappear, Order's coverage advantage evaporates  C.
