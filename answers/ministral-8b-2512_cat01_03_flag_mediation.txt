## 8. Reversal Conditions

1. **Hidden risk**: If `risk_score` omits a critical risk factor (e.g., clinical severity), flagged cases may genuinely require longer review.
2. **Queue interference**: If unflagged cases arrive during peak load, shared queues may delay flagged cases even without the flag.
3. **Decision quality**: If flagged cases are rejected more often due to stricter review, the flag may improve compliance but reduce throughput.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If local clock time misaligns with resource availability (e.g., weekends), observed delays may not reflect policy.
5. **Positivity violation**: If no unflagged cases arrive during high load, stratification by `arrival_load` is impossible.
6. **Reviewer behavior change**: If reviewers adjust their pace when flagged cases are prioritized, service times may improve even without capacity changes.

## 9. Final Synthesis

### **Directly Observed**
- Flagged cases have **faster triage** but **longer total cycle times** due to mandatory manual review and escalation.
- **Shared queues** create interference: flagged cases delay unflagged cases and vice versa.
- **Complexity** correlates with both flag assignment and longer processing times, but it is not clear if this is causal.

### **Plausible Mechanism**
The flag acts as a **policy trigger** that:
1. **Accelerates triage** by routing cases to a priority lane.
2. **Induces mandatory manual review**, which increases manual queue waiting times (shared with unflagged cases).
3. **Triggers escalation** for a subset of cases, further delaying processing.
The net effect is **longer cycle times** because the downstream delays outweigh the triage acceleration.

### **Unidentified**
- **Unobserved risk factors**: `risk_score` may omit critical dimensions of risk.
- **Reviewer assignment**: Senior reviewers may be assigned to flagged cases, but we cannot observe their workload.
- **Behavioral feedback**: Reviewers may adjust their pace when flagged cases are prioritized.

### **Next Evidence**
A **limited pilot (P3)** testing **protected manual-review capacity** for flagged cases is the most actionable next step. It isolates the effect of capacity constraints while preserving the flag’s priority routing. If successful, it could reduce cycle times without compromising decision quality. If not, it would suggest that the flag’s primary issue is **shared-queue interference**, not inherent risk complexity.
