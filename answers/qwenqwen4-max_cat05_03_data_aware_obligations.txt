# Transaction-Review Compliance Assessment (event-time semantics)

## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

**State reconstruction.** For each case I sort events by `(time:timestamp, event_index)` ascending and fold them into a state vector `S = (amount, risk_level, jurisdiction, customer_type)`. A non-blank attribute cell is an *assignment*; a blank cell is a *no-op* (never a null assignment). State is therefore a **monotone forward fill (last-write-wins, causal order only)**: `S(e) = S(e)  payload(e)`. No backward fill, no interpolation, no use of the case's final row. `Create_Transaction` establishes the baseline; it is not itself a "change".

**Equal timestamps.** `event_index` is the authoritative intra-timestamp order. I materialise it as a per-case ordinal `ord` after sorting, so every temporal comparison in the analysis and in the code is a comparison of `ord`, not of raw timestamps. Consequence for TX-311: `Enhanced_Review (idx 3)` **precedes** `Update_JurisdictionDE (idx 4)`; the review was performed under `US/115,000` (R1 active at review time), and the jurisdiction became `DE` only afterwards, so R1 is *inactive at release*. Reversing the tie-break would not change the release-time verdict here, but it would change *whether the review was legally required when performed* — and in a variant where the post-review change were a >20 % amount increase, the tie-break would flip the verdict outright.

**Material change.** Per R4, materiality of an amount change is *strictly* `new > 1.20 × previous known amount`. Decreases are never invalidating. An increase from an unknown baseline is `UNKNOWN`, not `false`. I call this the **strict (R4) reading** and use it for the verdicts. I also compute an **extended reading** ("any change to a trigger attribute after the review makes the review stale") purely as a sensitivity analysis, because R1's phrase "latest material data change" is only defined by R4 for amounts. The two readings diverge for TX-306, TX-308, TX-311 and are reported explicitly.

**Final attributes.** The last observed value is *evidence about the end of the trace*, not about earlier events. TX-305's `150,000` is a **settlement-time** observation one day after release; it is admissible as *circumstantial evidence* about the release amount, never as the release state. TX-311's `DE` is post-review; TX-301's `125,000` is post-Standard_Review.

**Four time axes (kept separate everywhere).**

| Axis | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event time | when the logged business act occurred | `Release_Funds` TX-302 @ 09:40 |
| Effective time | when a fact became legally operative (may be stated explicitly) | EX-77 `effective_from 09:00`; EX-88 displayed `08:00` |
| Record time | when the system persisted the fact | `Record_Exemption` @ 11:00 (both cases) |
| Evidence time | when proof of the fact came into existence / became verifiable | EX-77 qualified signature 09:05 (before release); EX-88 signing certificate created 10:30 (**after** release) |

Rule default: an attribute update is effective at its event time unless evidence establishes another effective time. Exemptions are the one place where an explicit `effective_from` exists — but R5 makes *effective_from* insufficient without proof that the **approval existed** at the trigger. Hence EX-77 (record time late, evidence time early, outage documented) is accepted; EX-88 (effective field backdated, evidence time late, only interested-party testimony) is not.

**Burden of proof (used to separate NONCOMPLIANT from INDETERMINATE).** Missing *trigger* data  INDETERMINATE (R6). A fully established trigger plus an *unproven affirmative defence*  NONCOMPLIANT, because the exemption claim does not make the obligation itself uncertain. This is exactly the TX-305 / TX-310 split.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-305 (INDETERMINATE — required discussion).** Two independent unknowns: (a) *trigger* — release amount absent; (b) *satisfaction* — even if the amount were 100k, the ER document carries no amount, so its amount scope cannot be verified, and R4 forbids classifying an increase from an unknown baseline. The next-day settlement of 150,000 is reliable **at settlement time** only; without an immutable release payload it cannot exclude an amount change between 11:30 and settlement, nor fees/FX grossing. What would change the result: an immutable, signed release payload with amount 100,000 **plus** an ER document scoped to that amount  COMPLIANT; payload 100,000 with ER scope unverified or a >20 % post-review increase  NONCOMPLIANT; payload <100,000  COMPLIANT (R1 never activated). A monotone bound (e.g., an authoritative "amount  120,000 at creation" feed flag) would already establish the trigger and move the case from INDETERMINATE to a determinate verdict on the review-scope question. Note the trap: control-flow-only checking sees `ER  Release` and passes this case.

**TX-302 (required discussion).** The exemption is *valid* on strong evidence (qualified signature 09:05, hash valid, documented outage explaining the 11:00 insert), so late record time is harmless — R5 explicitly allows a late record as evidence of earlier approval. But R1 is *also* satisfied on its own facts (ER 09:20 after Create 09:00, before release 09:40, no material change). I therefore label COMPLIANT and treat the waiver as redundant; labelling it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is equally defensible and changes no remediation. What would change the result: evidence that the ER was void/scoped to a different amount, or a >20 % increase between 09:20 and 09:40 — then the exemption becomes load-bearing and the label becomes JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. If the outage documentation or signature verification were withdrawn, the exemption would degrade to UNPROVEN, yet the case would *still* be COMPLIANT via the ER — a useful robustness observation. Also note `effective_from 09:00` precedes the 09:05 approval: the *effective* field is backdated relative to proof of approval, which is harmless here only because the trigger (09:40) is later than both.

**TX-310 (required discussion).** NONCOMPLIANT is my verdict because the trigger is fully established (200,000, AE) and the only review is a Standard_Review; the exemption is an affirmative defence that fails R5's existence test (certificate created 10:30, after the 10:00 release; the 08:50/08:00 fields are display values; the sole witness is the requester). A defensible alternative is **INDETERMINATE**, on the view that R5 permits late records as evidence and the oral-approval claim is not formally excluded — this is the reading the code implements conservatively as `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. What flips it to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: an HSM/qualified-signature log or Head_Compliance attestation with a verifiable timestamp 10:00, an independent approver witness, or an outage record covering the AE case as for EX-77 — i.e., corroboration that is *not* self-interested and *not* created after release. What makes it worse: evidence the certificate creation time equals the actual approval time (then the exemption is affirmatively post-hoc, and the backdating is a integrity incident, not just an evidential gap).

**TX-311 (required discussion).** With `idx 3  idx 4`, the ER happened while the case was `US/115,000` — R1 was active *at review time* and was met — and the jurisdiction became DE before release, so R1 is inactive at the release trigger. Verdict COMPLIANT under either tie-break, but the *reason* differs (obligation satisfied vs. obligation never active), which matters for audit and for control design. Under the **extended materiality reading**, the post-review jurisdiction change would make the ER stale — irrelevant here only because R1 is inactive at release. What would change the result: a tie-break reversal *combined with* a >20 % amount increase at idx 4; a jurisdiction change to a non-DE country (R1 active at release with a stale review  NONCOMPLIANT); or evidence that the DE update was effective *before* 16:20 (explicit effective time), which would mean the ER was performed under DE and no R1 obligation ever arose. The pattern "amount crosses the threshold, review runs, jurisdiction flips to the exempt country minutes before release" also warrants a **gaming/anti-avoidance review** even though it is formally compliant; jurisdiction provenance (who requested the change, from which KYC source) is the missing evidence.

**TX-306 (additional).** COMPLIANT rests on reading "material data change" as R4's >20 % test: 230,000/220,000 = 1.0455. Under the extended reading ("any amount change"), ER 12:15 and CA 12:25 both precede the 12:30 change  NONCOMPLIANT for R1 (and arguably R2 freshness). R2's text only requires `ER  CA  release`, which is met; it does not require approval after the last data change. Evidence that would change the verdict: a policy definition stating that *any* amount change re-triggers review, or a rule-version record showing the operative policy version defined materiality differently.

**TX-308 (additional).** COMPLIANT because R4 explicitly says a decrease does not invalidate. Under the extended reading, ER 14:20 precedes the 14:25 change  deviation. Also note the boundary: had the decrease gone below 100,000, R1 would not have triggered at all; at exactly 100,000 it does. A policy clarification that reviews must reflect the released amount would flip this case.

**TX-307 (additional).** NONCOMPLIANT under the stated semantic fact that a standard-packet approval does not cure a missing ER. A contrary interpretation would need explicit policy text allowing Head_Compliance to substitute approval for enhanced review on low-value PEP cases; nothing in R2 or the register supports it (both exemptions waive R1 only, and none is asserted here). Evidence that could change it: an ER logged in a different system/case id, or an approval packet reference proving the packet actually contained an enhanced review despite the note.

**TX-304 (additional).** Depends on the list update's *effective* time. If the listing were effective only later (e.g., publication delay) and the evidence established that, R3 would not trigger at 08:45. As logged, the update is effective at its event time (default rule), so R3 is active and unmet.

def exemption_status(ex, rel_ts, jur, ctype, amount):
    """R5: scope + validity window + provable existence of approval at release."""
    if ex is None:
        return "NONE", {}
    prov = ((pd.notna(ex["qualified_sig"]) and ex["qualified_sig"] <= rel_ts)
            or (ex["documented_outage"] and ex["independent_corroboration"]))
    late_cert = pd.notna(ex["cert_created"]) and ex["cert_created"] > rel_ts
    facts = dict(exemption_effective_from=ex["effective_from"],
                 exemption_displayed_approval=ex["displayed_approval"],
                 exemption_qualified_sig=ex["qualified_sig"],
                 exemption_cert_created=ex["cert_created"],
                 exemption_provable_at_release=bool(prov and not late_cert),
                 exemption_backdate_only=bool(pd.notna(ex["displayed_approval"])
                                              and ex["displayed_approval"] <= rel_ts
                                              and not prov),
                 exemption_late_certificate=bool(late_cert),
                 exemption_witness_conflict=bool(ex["interested_party_only"]))
    if pd.isna(amount) or pd.isna(jur) or pd.isna(ctype):
        return "SCOPE_UNKNOWN", facts
    scope = (ex["scope_jur"] == jur and ex["scope_ctype"] == ctype and amount <= ex["amount_max"])
    facts["exemption_scope_match"] = bool(scope)
    if not scope:
        return "SCOPE_MISMATCH", facts
    if not (ex["effective_from"] <= rel_ts < ex["expires"]):
        return "OUT_OF_WINDOW", facts
    if ex["approver"] != "Head_Compliance":
        return "WRONG_APPROVER", facts
    return ("VALID_STRONG" if facts["exemption_provable_at_release"] else "UNPROVEN"), facts

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** Trace-level or case-attribute checks read the last row: TX-311 would be evaluated as "DE case, review present" and TX-305 as "150,000 case", copying a next-day settlement value backwards onto a release that had no amount. Both the *reason* and the *verdict* can be wrong (TX-301's 125,000 also post-dates its only review).
2. **Post-hoc exemptions / backdating.** Control flow sees `Record_Exemption` after `Release_Funds` and cannot distinguish TX-302 (qualified signature 09:05, documented outage  valid) from TX-310 (certificate created 10:30, interested witness  unproven). Identical activity traces, opposite verdicts. Conversely, a *tidy* trace with the exemption recorded before release could still carry a forged backdated field.
3. **Material change after review (staleness).** `Enhanced_Review  Release_Funds` is satisfied by TX-309, yet the 118k145k increase (+22.88 %) invalidated the review. Control flow has no notion of a review's *validity interval* or of the amount it covered; nor can it distinguish TX-308's non-invalidating decrease or TX-306's sub-threshold +4.55 %.
4. **Conditional activation.** R1/R2/R3 are guarded by data predicates (`jur  DE`, `amount  100,000`, `risk = HIGH`, `ctype = PEP`, list membership). An unconditional "ER precedes release" rule wrongly flags TX-307 (DE, 70k, no R1) and TX-311 (DE at release); an unconditional "approve before release" rule passes TX-303, which is missing approval on a HIGH-risk case. Vacuous satisfaction and false positives are both produced.
5. **Missing data.** Conformance is binary; R6 requires a third value. TX-305's unknown amount must yield INDETERMINATE, and an unknown review baseline must yield UNKNOWN staleness — a control-flow engine silently treats "no evidence of violation" as "compliant", which is exactly the unsafe default R6 forbids.
6. **Same-timestamp ordering.** Timestamp-only sorting makes TX-311's ER and jurisdiction change concurrent/ambiguous; the verdict's *justification* (and, in nearby variants, the verdict itself) depends on `event_index`. DECLARE's temporal templates over timestamps cannot express intra-timestamp causality.
7. **Semantic type and scope of activities.** TX-307 has `Standard_Review  Compliance_Approve  Release` — a textbook-compliant control flow — but the wrong review type and a standard-only approval packet. Label equality  obligation satisfaction; approval must *reference* the enhanced review it approves.
8. **Exogenous / cross-case facts and evidence time.** R3 depends on a global sanctions-list change; the "clear at screening time" screen was correct when performed and became stale because the world changed. Intra-trace conformance has no vocabulary for external state, nor for the four time axes (event/effective/record/evidence).

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

**Immutable release payload.** Emit a signed `Release_Precheck`/`Release_Payload` event containing the exact evaluated snapshot: `amount`, `currency`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, `customer_type`, `review_id(s)`, `exemption_id`, `policy_version`, and a SHA-256 hash of the payload signed by the release service. This alone would make TX-305 determinate and remove all reliance on next-day settlement inference.

**Four explicit timestamps on every fact.** `event_time`, `effective_time`, `record_time`, `evidence_time` (qualified signature / HSM / trusted-timestamp token), plus `clock_source` and `skew_bound`. Never let a displayed `effective_from` stand in for proof of approval; require `approval_evidence_time  trigger_time` to be machine-checkable (fixes TX-302 vs TX-310 without human archaeology).

**Exemption as a first-class, in-log object.** Log `Request_Exemption`, `Approve_Exemption` (approver identity + role assertion `Head_Compliance`, qualified signature), `Record_Exemption`, `Revoke_Exemption`, with structured scope: `jurisdiction_set`, `customer_type_set`, `amount_min/max`, `currency`, `waived_rules[]`, `effective_from/to`, `expires`, `outage_reference`, `corroboration_source` and an `interested_party` flag on any witness. Bind each exemption to the case and to the rule version it waives.

**Review scope and linkage.** Every `Standard_Review`/`Enhanced_Review` should carry `review_id`, `review_type`, `amount_reviewed`, `currency`, `jurisdiction_reviewed`, `risk_reviewed`, `customer_type_reviewed`, `state_hash`, and `invalidated_by` (backfilled pointer when a later material change occurs). `Compliance_Approve` must carry `approved_review_id` and `packet_type`, making "standard-only approval" a detectable data mismatch rather than a note field (TX-307).

**Change provenance and materiality metadata.** All `Update_*` events: `old_value`, `new_value`, `pct_change`, `source_system`, `actor`, `reason_code`, `requester_id`, `effective_time`, and `materiality_flag` computed against the policy version. Add `amount_feed_status` (`OK/UNAVAILABLE/ESTIMATED`) and `amount_lower_bound`/`amount_upper_bound` so unknown amounts can still be *bounded* (R6 resolution), and log jurisdiction changes with KYC provenance plus an anti-avoidance flag when a threshold-crossing or jurisdiction switch occurs within N minutes of release (TX-311).

**Sanctions-list versioning as a reference-data stream.** Publish `Sanctions_List_Update` with `list_version`, `added[]`/`removed[]`, `effective_time`, `publication_time`, and have screening events record `list_version_screened` and `jurisdiction_screened`. Then R3 becomes a version comparison (`list_version_at_release > list_version_screened`) rather than a timestamp guess (TX-304).

**Stable ordering and completeness.** A per-case monotonic `sequence_no` assigned by the system of record (not a display index), a global tie-break `(timestamp, sequence_no, source_id)`, idempotent event IDs, and an explicit `trace_complete` / `gap_detected` marker so missing events are distinguishable from missing obligations.

**Explicit unknowns and rule evaluation trace.** Replace blank cells with typed semantics (`NO_UPDATE` vs `UNKNOWN` vs `CLEARED`), and log the outcome of each rule evaluation at release (`rule_id`, `rule_version`, `trigger=TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN`, `verdict`, `evidence_refs[]`). This turns conformance checking from reconstruction into verification, makes INDETERMINATE a first-class, alertable state, and gives auditors the decisive evidence inline instead of in a PDF and a witness statement.
