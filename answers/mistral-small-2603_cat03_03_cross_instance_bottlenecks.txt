## 1. Interaction-graph description

The system can be modeled as a **typed interaction graph** where nodes represent objects (Jobs, Machines, Tools, Batches, ComponentLots, Shipments) and edges represent observed or inferred relationships. The graph is **multi-relational**, with edges qualified by activity types (e.g., "requires", "batches", "contains", "consolidates") and **higher-order motifs** involving more than two object types.

### Important node types:
- **Job**: J1–J6 (leaf nodes in the process flow)
- **Machine**: CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1 (processing resources)
- **Tool**: FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3 (shared fixtures/calibration tools)
- **Batch**: B1, B2 (synchronization units for oven processing)
- **ComponentLot**: L7, L8 (shared material lots with quarantine/release cycles)
- **Shipment**: S1–S3 (logistics consolidation points)

### Important edge types and qualifiers:
- **Job  Component**: `requires` (direct job needs)
- **ComponentLot  Component**: `contains` (material composition)
- **Job  Batch**: `batches` (grouping for oven processing)
- **Batch  Machine**: `processes` (inferred from "Batch Cure" events)
- **Job  Machine**: `uses` (via tool or direct assignment)
- **Job  Tool**: `acquires`/`releases` (tool contention)
- **Job  Shipment**: `consolidates` (logistics grouping)
- **Shipment  Job**: `dispatches` (final step)
- **ComponentLot  Job**: `quarantines`/`releases` (indirect job coupling)

### Higher-order motifs (3+ object types):
1. **Batch-Machine-Tool-Synchronization (B1 on OVEN-1 with RACK-7)**:
   - B1 batches J1, J2, J3  processed on OVEN-1 using RACK-7.
   - RACK-7 is shared between B1 and B2, causing idle time (e.g., OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 due to RACK-7 quarantine until 14:35).

2. **Tool-Machine-Job Cascading (FIX-A and CNC-2)**:
   - J3 holds FIX-A (09:00–11:40)  J4 waits for CNC-2 (idle 10:05–10:47) despite FIX-A release at 11:40, when J4 starts processing on CNC-2 (11:45).
   - Tool release (FIX-A) does not immediately enable downstream jobs due to machine scheduling.

3. **ComponentLot-Job-Machine-Shipment Coupling (L7 and S1)**:
   - L7 (contains C2 for J2, C5 for J5) is quarantined until 13:50  J5 waits despite J2 proceeding in B1 (supervisor override).
   - J1 (packed 13:30) and J4 (packed 19:10) consolidate in S1, dispatching at 19:25  J1 waits 5h55m for J4.

### Edge classification:
- **Direct observations**: All `requires`, `batches`, `consolidates`, and `contains` edges (explicit in O2O relations).
- **Inferred edges**:
  - `uses` (Job  Machine) via tool acquisition (e.g., J4 uses CNC-2 via FIX-A).
  - `processes` (Batch  Machine) from "Batch Cure" events.
  - `quarantines`/`releases` (ComponentLot  Job) from lot events.
  - `waits_for` (Job  Job) via synchronization barriers (e.g., J1 waits for B1 completion).

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

### Calculation 1: Avoidable waiting for J1 and J2 in B1
**Observed**:
- J1 ready at 08:10, B1 starts at 12:05  waiting time = 3h55m.
- J2 ready at 08:25, B1 starts at 12:05  waiting time = 3h40m.
- J3 ready at 11:55, B1 starts at 12:05  waiting time = 10m.

**Counterfactual**: If B1 started immediately when all jobs were ready (earliest readiness = 08:10 for J1), waiting time would be 0.

**Avoidable waiting**:
- J1: 3h55m (entirely avoidable if B1 started at 08:10).
- J2: 3h40m (avoidable).
- J3: 10m (minimal, likely due to setup).

**Interpretation**: Batch synchronization (B1) is a major delay source. OVEN-1 and RACK-7 are available earlier (OVEN-1 idle from 08:00–12:05), but RACK-7 may have been unavailable or B1 not prioritized.

---

### Calculation 2: Delay plausibly associated with FIX-A for J4
**Observed**:
- J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00, releases at 11:40.
- J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50, but `Machine Process` starts at 11:45.
- CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47.

**Counterfactual**: If FIX-A were available when J4 arrived at CNC-2 (09:50), J4 could have started processing immediately.

**Delay**:
- J4 waits from 09:50 to 11:45  1h55m.
- CNC-2 idle time (10:05–10:47) overlaps with this, suggesting tool contention as a root cause.

**Interpretation**: FIX-A is a shared tool causing downstream machine idle time. The delay is not just tool acquisition time but also scheduling misalignment.

---

### Calculation 3: Delay imposed on J1 by S1 consolidation
**Observed**:
- J1 packed at 13:30, S1 dispatches at 19:25  waiting time = 5h55m.
- J4 packed at 19:10 (last job in S1).

**Counterfactual**: If S1 dispatched immediately after J1 was packed (13:30), J1 would not wait.

**Delay**:
- J1: 5h55m (entirely due to shipment consolidation).
- S2 and S3 have shorter waits (25m, 35m), suggesting S1 is an outlier.

**Interpretation**: Shipment policy (waiting for all jobs) creates long waits for early-ready jobs. No single job is a bottleneck, but the consolidation rule couples jobs.

---

### Calculation 4: Delay plausibly associated with CAL-3 for J6
**Observed**:
- J5 and J6 ready for final test at 16:00 and 16:05.
- J5 tests 16:10–16:35, CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20.
- J6 tests 17:25–17:50.

**Counterfactual**: If CAL-3 were available immediately after J5’s test, J6 could have started at 16:35.

**Delay**:
- J6 waits from 16:05 to 17:25  1h20m.
- TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25, but CAL-3 is the limiting resource.

**Interpretation**: CAL-3 is a calibration tool causing idle time on TEST-1. The delay is tool-specific, not machine capacity.

---

### Calculation 5: ComponentLot L7 release delay for J5
**Observed**:
- L7 quarantined at 10:20, released at 13:50.
- J2 (requires C2 from L7) proceeds in B1 (supervisor override).
- J5 (requires C5 from L7) waits until 13:50.

**Counterfactual**: If L7 were released at quarantine time (10:20), J5 could have proceeded immediately.

**Delay**:
- J5 waits from job readiness (assume ~14:40, when J5 is ready for B2) to 13:50  but J5 cannot proceed until L7 is released, so delay = max(0, 14:40 – 13:50) = 50m (if J5 was ready by 13:50) or longer if J5 was ready later.

**Interpretation**: ComponentLot quarantine/release creates indirect job coupling. The delay is policy-driven (quarantine rules) rather than resource contention.

## 4. What flattening misses

### Job-flattened DFG:
- **Visible**: Direct job activities (component staging, machine process, inspection).
- **Hidden**:
  - Batch synchronization (B1/B2 waiting).
  - Tool contention (FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7).
  - ComponentLot quarantine delays.
  - Shipment consolidation waits.
- **Misleading**: No job has >1.8 duration, but jobs are coupled via hidden mechanisms. The DFG suggests "normal" performance, masking systemic delays.

### Machine-flattened summary:
- **Visible**: Machine utilization, activity sequences, idle gaps.
- **Hidden**:
  - Why machines are idle (tool unavailability, batch synchronization).
  - Tool-specific delays (e.g., CAL-3 recalibration).
- **Misleading**: Low utilization (44–58%) suggests no bottleneck, but idle time is caused by external factors (tools, batches). The "longest gap" (73m for OVEN-1) is due to RACK-7 quarantine, not machine capacity.

### Batch-flattened view:
- **Visible**: Batch start/end times, machine usage.
- **Hidden**:
  - Which jobs are batched (requires Job-flattened DFG).
  - Tool usage (RACK-7 is shared).
- **Misleading**: Batch DFG shows low frequency (2 batches), but impact is high (synchronization delays).

### Tool-flattened view (inferred):
- **Visible**: Tool acquisition/release events.
- **Hidden**:
  - Downstream effects (machine idle time, job delays).
- **Misleading**: Tool events are sparse; delays are inferred from gaps in other flattenings.

### ComponentLot-flattened view:
- **Visible**: Quarantine/release events.
- **Hidden**:
  - Which jobs are affected (L7 affects J2 and J5).
  - Link to machine/batch scheduling.
- **Misleading**: Lot events are not integrated with jobs, so their impact is invisible in Job/Machine flattenings.

### Shipment-flattened DFG:
- **Visible**: Shipment creation, load, dispatch times.
- **Hidden**:
  - How long packed jobs wait for consolidation.
  - Which jobs are consolidated (requires Job-flattened DFG).
- **Misleading**: Median load time is 0.8h, but J1 waits 5h55m. The DFG understates consolidation delays.

### Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting:
Machine utilization is low because:
1. **Tools are the bottleneck**: Machines wait for tools (FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7), not for their own capacity.
2. **Batches synchronize jobs**: OVEN-1 is idle because B1/B2 are not ready (RACK-7 unavailability, job readiness mismatches).
3. **Policy constraints**: ComponentLot quarantine and shipment consolidation force jobs to wait even when machines are free.

The flattenings hide these couplings by focusing on individual objects (jobs, machines) rather than interactions (tools, batches, lots).

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence: Multi-object causal narratives

### Narrative 1: FIX-A  CNC-2  J4 delay
1. **Temporal precedence**:
   - J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00.
   - J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 but cannot start.
   - J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40.
   - J4 starts CNC-2 at 11:45.
2. **Resource feasibility**:
   - FIX-A is required for CNC-2 processing (inferred from timing).
   - CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47, suggesting FIX-A unavailability.
3. **Policy constraints**:
   - No explicit policy for tool assignment; likely first-come-first-served.
4. **Causal speculation**:
   - FIX-A contention causes J4 to wait, leading to downstream delays (e.g., B2 readiness).

### Narrative 2: L7  J2/J5 coupling
1. **Temporal precedence**:
   - L7 quarantined at 10:20.
   - J2 proceeds in B1 at 12:05 (supervisor override).
   - L7 released at 13:50.
   - J5 waits until 13:50 to proceed.
2. **Resource feasibility**:
   - J2 requires C2 (from L7), J5 requires C5 (from L7).
3. **Policy constraints**:
   - Quarantine policy forces J5 to wait despite J2 proceeding.
4. **Causal speculation**:
   - ComponentLot coupling creates indirect job dependencies, violating "independent" jobs.

### Narrative 3: RACK-7  B1/B2  OVEN-1 idle
1. **Temporal precedence**:
   - B1 starts at 12:05 on OVEN-1 using RACK-7.
   - RACK-7 is "cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35.
   - B2 (J4, J5) cannot start until 14:50.
2. **Resource feasibility**:
   - OVEN-1 is free from 12:55–14:50, but RACK-7 is unavailable.
3. **Policy constraints**:
   - Batch scheduling does not account for tool availability.
4. **Causal speculation**:
   - Tool-sharing (RACK-7) creates a hidden bottleneck, causing OVEN-1 idle time.

3. **OCDFG**: Reveals batch synchronization (B1/B2) and tool usage (FIX-A, CAL-3) as high-frequency interactions.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (Batch synchronization):
1. B1/B2 start times align with job readiness (no 3h+ delays).
2. RACK-7 availability matches batch start times (no quarantine events).
3. OVEN-1 utilization >80% (suggesting machine capacity is the issue).
4. Jobs in B1 proceed without waiting (e.g., J1 starts immediately).
5. No idle time on OVEN-1 between batches.
6. Tool events (e.g., RACK-7 usage) are logged as available during B1/B2.

### Additional fields/event types to discriminate hypotheses:
1. **Tool availability logs**: Exact timestamps for tool acquisition/release.
2. **Batch scheduling policy**: Rules for when batches start (e.g., "start when all jobs ready").
3. **ComponentLot linkage**: Explicit events linking lots to jobs (not just quarantine/release).
4. **Shipment consolidation rules**: Whether shipments can dispatch partial loads.
5. **Machine setup times**: Separate setup events for OVEN-1/RACK-7.
6. **Job readiness events**: When jobs are truly ready (not just "released").
7. **Supervisor override logs**: Frequency and impact of overrides (e.g., J2 in B1).
8. **Tool calibration schedules**: Pre-planned CAL-3 recalibration times.
9. **Batch composition rules**: Why B1 includes J1/J2/J3 (e.g., material compatibility).
10. **Job priority data**: Whether some jobs are prioritized (e.g., J2 override).

These would help distinguish between synchronization delays, tool contention, policy constraints, and data artifacts.
