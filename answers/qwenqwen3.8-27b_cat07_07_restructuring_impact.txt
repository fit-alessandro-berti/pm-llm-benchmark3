# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency hidden by stable average**  
   Average cycle time can remain stable or improve while 90th, 95th, and 99th percentile waits worsen. Central queues create nonlinear waiting behavior: a small increase in arrival rate or capacity loss can produce large tail delays.  
   **PM signal:** 90th/95th percentile cycle time, waiting time after central handovers, queue length, performance DFG variance.

2. **High-complexity cases become slower even if simple cases improve**  
   Routing all high-complexity cases to the Technical Shared Service may improve average performance because simple cases move faster, while complex cases accumulate in a central queue.  
   **PM signal:** Filter by `case_complexity = high`; compare median, 90th percentile, rework, and waiting time before/after.

3. **Local knowledge loss is not visible in early handover counts**  
   Local specialists currently provide informal early advice that prevents unnecessary specialist referrals. This informal work may not appear as a formal handover, so the log may look cleaner while quality later degrades.  
   **PM signal:** Declining `local_expertise_flag`, reduced local working-together network density, increased rework in regional/convention-sensitive cases, more unnecessary specialist referrals.

4. **Fairness differences are masked by overall averages**  
   East may benefit from reduced E_Tech overload, while North and South may lose local expertise or face longer central waits. Customer segments needing language or regional accommodation may experience more handovers and lower satisfaction.  
   **PM signal:** Distributions by team, customer segment, language/regional flag, case complexity, and specialty; SLA miss and satisfaction gaps.

5. **Learning and skill atrophy are delayed effects**  
   If local staff stop handling difficult technical cases, their skills may atrophy. This may not appear immediately in cycle time but will reduce future resilience and increase dependence on central specialists.  
   **PM signal:** Local staff activity on high-complexity cases, resource similarity, local technical role stability, training completion, local expertise self-assessment.

6. **Accountability becomes diffuse in shared approval queues**  
   Shared approval may standardize decisions but reduce ownership. Cases may wait longer for clarification, be reassigned more often, or be reworked because no local owner feels responsible.  
   **PM signal:** Reassignment count, repeated local-to-shared approval transitions, role discovery instability, escalation rate, staff feedback on ownership.

7. **Exception handling degrades for legal/risk/security cases**  
   Legal and Risk specialists are already near capacity. Centralizing more legal/risk/security cases may create long exception-case waits that are hidden by the large volume of standard cases.  
   **PM signal:** Filter by `required_specialty in {legal, risk, security}`; monitor 90th percentile wait, rework after specialist handover, SLA misses.

8. **Resilience to absence becomes weaker even if nominal cross-team handovers fall**  
   Cross-team handovers may decrease, but the system may become more dependent on central queues. If a central specialist or approver is absent, delays may spread across all teams.  
   **PM signal:** Queue recovery time after absence, resource substitution patterns, DFG deviations during absence periods, internal reassignment count.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope**

- Run a **10-week pilot** before any full rollout.
- Keep **Intake** and **simple Eligibility Review** local.
- Create the **Technical Shared Service** and **shared approval queue** for:
  - **East**: all eligible high-complexity, legal, risk, security, and medium/high-value cases.
  - **North and South**: a randomized 50% of eligible high-complexity, legal, risk, security, and medium/high-value cases.
- Keep the remaining North/South eligible cases on the current local specialist and local supervisor path as the comparison group.
- Do not permanently remove local supervisors during the pilot. Use shared approval as a second-line or parallel path for the pilot cases, with a named local owner still assigned.
- Retain local technical capacity for early informal advice and simple technical tasks, even if difficult cases are routed centrally.

**Comparison group**

- Primary comparison: unchanged North/South control cases.
- Secondary comparison: pre-pilot baseline cases from the same teams.
- Matching controls: match pilot and control cases on `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, `priority`, and case age.
- If case-level randomization is not feasible, use a **stepped-wedge design**: East first, then North, then South, with team fixed effects and pre/post comparison.

**Pre/post observation window**

- **Baseline:** 8 weeks before pilot start.
- **Pilot:** 10 weeks.
- **Post-pilot stabilization:** 4 weeks after pilot end or after adjustment.
- Leading indicators reviewed weekly.
- Lagging indicators reviewed monthly and at pilot decision point.

**Segmentation**

Segment all metrics by:

- **Team:** North, South, East.
- **Case complexity:** low, medium, high.
- **Required specialty:** none, legal, risk, security.
- **Customer segment:** standard, high-value, regional/language accommodation, low-volume.
- **Priority:** standard, urgent, critical.

This is essential because East may benefit differently from North/South, and fairness effects may be concentrated in specific customer segments.

**Guardrail metrics**

| Guardrail | Suggested threshold | Action if breached |
|---|---:|---|
| Technical Shared Service 90th percentile wait |  8h for 2 consecutive weeks | Add capacity, reduce routing scope, or create local fast-track |
| Shared approval 90th percentile wait |  8h for 2 consecutive weeks | Add approvers, clarify ownership, or restore local approval for high-value cases |
| Legal/Risk 90th percentile wait |  24h for 2 consecutive weeks | Add legal/risk capacity, improve triage, or keep local pre-review |
| Overall 90th percentile cycle time increase |  1 day for any major segment | Pause expansion and investigate queueing |
| High-complexity 90th percentile cycle time increase |  2 days | Adjust routing or add high-complexity capacity |
| Overall rework increase |  0.5 percentage points | Investigate context loss and decision standardization |
| Complex-case rework increase |  1.0 percentage points | Retain local technical involvement for complex cases |
| SLA miss increase |  1.0 percentage points | Add capacity or adjust SLA routing |
| Reassignment count increase |  15% | Improve handover templates, clarify ownership, add queue owners |
| `local_expertise_flag` decline |  10% decline in pilot teams | Restore local technical development and local handling of some complex cases |
| Role clarity staff score |  3.5/5 average | Add named owners, RACI, and clear escalation rules |
| Fairness gap: East vs North/South 90th percentile cycle time | No adverse gap > 2 days for comparable segments | Rebalance routing or add capacity for disadvantaged teams |
| Language/regional segment satisfaction decline |  0.2/5 | Strengthen local accommodation handoff and local context capture |

**Staff feedback collection**

- Weekly 5-question pulse survey:
  - “I know who owns my case.”
  - “I can get technical advice when needed.”
  - “Shared queues are clear.”
  - “I have enough local context to make good decisions.”
  - “Workload is fair across teams.”
- Monthly focus groups by team and role: intake, caseworker, technical specialist, supervisor, legal/risk, quality.
- Shadowing of shared queues for 2–3 days per week during pilot.
- Coding of `handover_reason` free text for themes: missing context, unclear ownership, wrong queue, language/regional issue, absent specialist.
- Local expertise self-assessment before pilot and after pilot.

**Rollback or adjustment triggers**

**Rollback triggers:**

- Technical Shared Service 90th percentile wait exceeds 12h for 2 consecutive weeks.
- Legal/Risk 90th percentile wait exceeds 30h for 2 consecutive weeks.
- Complex-case rework increases by more than 2 percentage points.
- Overall SLA miss rate increases by more than 2 percentage points.
- Role clarity score falls below 3.0/5.
- A major customer segment experiences sustained satisfaction decline and SLA misses.
- Staff report severe accountability failure or unsafe decision-making.

**Adjustment triggers:**

- Technical Shared Service 90th percentile wait exceeds 8h: add capacity or limit routing to highest-complexity cases only.
- Shared approval 90th percentile wait exceeds 8h: add approvers, create fast-track for standard approvals, or keep local supervisor for high-value cases.
- Legal/Risk waits rise: improve pre-routing, add triage capacity, or retain local legal/risk pre-review.
- `local_expertise_flag` declines: require local technical involvement for a subset of complex cases and create local development cases.
- Reassignment count rises: improve handover templates, mandatory context fields, and named queue owners.
- Fairness gap emerges: rebalance routing thresholds, add team-specific capacity, or create local fast-tracks.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Modify and pilot first. Do not proceed to full rollout now.**

The proposed restructuring has a plausible operational logic: pooling scarce specialists, standardizing difficult reviews, and reducing ad hoc cross-team handovers. However, the current organization already shows signs that local expertise, informal technical advice, and local supervisor accountability are doing important work that is not fully visible in the event log. A full rollout could produce nominal handover reduction and higher specialist utilization while simultaneously creating central bottlenecks, tail latency, fairness gaps, and local skill atrophy.

Management should proceed only with a **modified hybrid pilot**:

- Centralize **high-complexity technical review**, **legal/risk/security review**, and **difficult quality check** in shared queues.
- Keep **Intake** and **simple Eligibility Review** local.
- Retain **local technical capacity** for early informal advice, simple technical tasks, and local-convention cases.
- Use the **shared approval queue** as a second-line or parallel approval path, not immediately as the sole approval authority.
- Keep at least one local supervisor accountable during the pilot, especially for high-value and complex cases.
- Give each shared queue a named owner, clear SLA, and clear escalation path.
- Protect East’s benefit while monitoring whether North and South are disadvantaged.
- Protect customer segments requiring language or regional accommodation.

**Conditions for expansion after pilot:**

Expand only if the pilot shows:

- Technical Shared Service and shared approval 90th percentile waits remain within guardrails.
- Legal/Risk waits do not deteriorate materially.
- Overall rework does not increase, and complex-case rework does not increase materially.
- SLA miss rate remains stable or improves by segment.
- `local_expertise_flag` and local technical activity do not decline materially.
- Role clarity and accountability scores remain acceptable.
- Fairness gaps between East, North, South, and customer segments are within acceptable limits.
- Staff feedback does not show severe ownership or workload fairness problems.

**What management should not infer from early averages:**

- Do not infer success from a stable or improved **average cycle time**. The average can hide worsening 90th and 95th percentile waits, especially for high-complexity and legal/risk cases.
- Do not infer reduced coordination from a lower **nominal handover count**. Central queues may replace local handovers with hidden queue waits and internal reassignments.
- Do not infer efficiency from higher **specialist utilization**. Utilization can rise while queue waits, rework, and fairness problems also rise.
- Do not infer improved quality from fewer visible local specialist loops. Informal local advice may disappear before any formal rework appears.
- Do not infer resilience from fewer cross-team handovers. A central queue may be more fragile during absence or peak load.
- Do not infer fairness from overall performance. East may improve while North/South or specific customer segments decline.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# Event log column names
CASE_ID = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"
RESOURCE = "org:resource"


def prepare_log(df, phase_start):
    """
    Format the raw dataframe for PM4Py and add a before/after phase column.
    phase_start should be the timestamp when the restructuring/pilot starts.
    """
    df = df.copy()
    df[TIMESTAMP] = pd.to_datetime(df[TIMESTAMP])

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )

    df["phase"] = np.where(
        df[TIMESTAMP] < pd.Timestamp(phase_start),
        "before",
        "after"
    )

    return df


def apply_filters(
    log,
    team=None,
    complexity=None,
    specialty=None,
    segment=None,
    priority=None
):
    """
    Filter the event log by organizational and case attributes.
    """
    if team is not None:
        values = [team] if isinstance(team, str) else team
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key="team",
            attribute_values=values
        )

    if complexity is not None:
        values = [complexity] if isinstance(complexity, str) else complexity
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key="case_complexity",
            attribute_values=values
        )

    if specialty is not None:
        values = [specialty] if isinstance(specialty, str) else specialty
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key="required_specialty",
            attribute_values=values
        )

    if segment is not None:
        values = [segment] if isinstance(segment, str) else segment
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key="customer_segment",
            attribute_values=values
        )

    if priority is not None:
        values = [priority] if isinstance(priority, str) else priority
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            attribute_key="priority",
            attribute_values=values
        )

    return log


def handover_network(log):
    """
    Discover who hands work to whom.
    Use to compare local specialist loops vs central shared-service handovers.
    """
    return pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
        log,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )


def working_together_network(log):
    """
    Discover which resources work on the same cases.
    Use to detect loss of local collaboration and informal advice.
    """
    return pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
        log,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )


def subcontracting_network(log):
    """
    Discover specialist/subcontracting-like dependencies.
    Useful for legal, risk, security, and difficult quality-check dependencies.
    """
    return pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
        log,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )


def resource_similarity_and_roles(log):
    """
    Discover resource similarity and organizational roles.
    Use to monitor role clarity, role overlap, and local expertise erosion.
    """
    similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        log,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )

    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(

        resource_key=RESOURCE
    )

def rework_and_sla_metrics(log):
    """
    Compute rework and SLA-related metrics.
    Assumes the log contains rework_flag and reassignment_count.
    For SLA, use an explicit sla_miss field if available;
    otherwise approximate from sla_due_timestamp or priority-based SLA rules.
    """
    rework_by_activity = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(
        log,
        case_id=CASE_ID,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY
    )

    case_level = log.groupby(CASE_ID).agg(
        rework_flag=("rework_flag", "max"),
        reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "sum"),
        local_expertise_flag=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
        case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        required_specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
        customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        team=("team", "first")
    )

    if "sla_miss" in log.columns:
        case_level["sla_miss"] = log.groupby(CASE_ID)["sla_miss"].max()
    elif "sla_due_timestamp" in log.columns:
        case_end = log.groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP].max()
        sla_due = (
            log.dropna(subset=["sla_due_timestamp"])
            .groupby(CASE_ID)["sla_due_timestamp"]
            .max()
        )
        case_level["sla_miss"] = (case_end > sla_due).astype(int)
    else:
        # Approximate SLA miss using priority-based SLA table.
        # Example: priority -> allowed hours.
        sla_hours_by_priority = {
            "low": 120,
            "medium": 72,
            "high": 48,
            "critical": 24
        }

        case_start = log.groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP].min()
        case_end = log.groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP].max()
        priority = log.groupby(CASE_ID)["priority"].first()

        cycle_hours = (case_end - case_start).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
        allowed_hours = priority.map(sla_hours_by_priority)
        case_level["sla_miss"] = (cycle_hours > allowed_hours).astype(int)

    return rework_by_activity, case_level

    """

    return {

    }


# Segments to compare before and after
segments = {
    "North_all": {"team": "North"},
    "South_all": {"team": "South"},
    "East_all": {"team": "East"},

    "High_complexity": {"complexity": "high"},
    "Medium_complexity": {"complexity": "medium"},
    "Low_complexity": {"complexity": "low"},

    "Legal": {"specialty": "legal"},
    "Risk": {"specialty": "risk"},
    "Security": {"specialty": "security"},

    "Regional_language_segment": {"segment": "regional_language"},
    "High_value_segment": {"segment": "high_value"},
    "Standard_segment": {"segment": "standard"}
}


def compare_before_after(log):
    """
    Compare before and after for each segment.
    """
    results = {}

    for segment_name, filters in segments.items():
        for phase in ["before", "after"]:
            phase_log = log[log["phase"] == phase]
            filtered_log = apply_filters(phase_log, **filters)

            if len(filtered_log) == 0:
                results[(segment_name, phase)] = None
            else:
                results[(segment_name, phase)] = summarize_log(filtered_log)

    return results
```

**How to use the outputs:**

- Use `handover_network` to compare local specialist loops against Technical Shared Service, Specialist Review, and shared approval handovers.
- Use `working_together_network` to detect whether local caseworker–local specialist collaboration is disappearing.
- Use `subcontracting_network` to monitor legal/risk/security dependency.
- Use `dfg_and_performance_dfg` to identify where central queues create waiting time.
- Use `waiting_and_service_time` to compare waiting after local handovers versus shared-queue handovers.
- Use `variants_analysis` to detect whether new central-queue variants are stable or fragmented.
- Use `resource_similarity_and_roles` to monitor role clarity, role overlap, and local expertise erosion.
- Use `rework_and_sla_metrics` to compare rework, reassignment, SLA misses, and `local_expertise_flag` by team, complexity, specialty, and customer segment.
- Use `temporal_features` to compare median, 90th, 95th, and 99th percentile cycle time rather than relying on average cycle time.
