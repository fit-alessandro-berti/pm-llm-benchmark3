### Methodological Framework Justification
Standard **Natural Direct and Indirect Effects (NDE / NIE)** rely on Pearl's cross-world counterfactual independence assumptions ($Y(a, m) \perp M(a^*) \mid C$), which are inherently untestable and fundamentally violated here due to:
1. **Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding**: Triage prioritization ($A$) accelerates triage completion, determining the arrival timestamp and queue depth at manual review ($L$), which confounds subsequent manual review durations and escalation decisions.
2. **SUTVA / Interference violations**: Waiting times in shared queues ($W_{\text{man}}, W_{\text{esc}}$) are not independent across cases; routing one case into review directly increases waiting times for all concurrent cases.
3. **Compound intervention structure**: The "flag" is not a monolithic point treatment but a bundle of triage prioritization, informational signaling, and mandatory routing rules.

Therefore, this analysis adopts **Separable Effects (Didelez, Stensrud et al.)** decomposing the intervention into $A = (A_{\text{prio}}, A_{\text{rout}}, A_{\text{info}})$, combined with **Interventional Direct and Indirect Effects (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt & Robins)** and **Policy-Interventional Queueing Estimands**. This framework replaces cross-world counterfactuals with population-level randomized draws from mediator distributions conditional on covariates and queue states, properly accommodating queue interference and exposure-induced confounding.

### Identification and Dynamic Feedback Explanation
Interference arises because manual review and escalation queues are shared, non-preemptive resources. A surge in flagged cases monopolizes reviewers (e.g., M1, M2, M3), inducing spillover delays on concurrent unflagged cases and violating SUTVA. Exposure-induced confounding occurs because the flag accelerates triage completion, altering the exact clock time and queue state at which a case enters the manual review backlog. This triage timing affects reviewer shift staffing, fatigue, and batching behavior, confounding downstream stage durations. Treatment-confounder feedback operates dynamically across arrivals: routing decisions for earlier cases create downstream backlog and resource starvation, which alters the congestion environment faced by subsequent arrivals.

### Empirical Contrasts and Causal Non-Equivalence
The empirical contrasts reveal a pronounced structural divergence between front-end and back-end processing. Flagged cases achieve dramatic acceleration at initial triage, entering service in a mean of 0.24h versus 2.48h for unflagged cases (a ~2.24h savings), reflecting effective priority lane operation. However, this front-end gain is overwhelmed downstream: 100% of flagged cases enter manual review, incurring an average queue wait of 6.50h (versus 1.88h for reviewed unflagged cases). Furthermore, 60% of flagged cases undergo senior escalation, adding 7.00h of queue waiting and 1.92h of service. Consequently, mean total cycle time for flagged cases is 16.51h compared to 8.17h for unflagged cases.

These contrasts are not automatically causal. Flag assignment is heavily confounded by underlying case complexity (100% of High-complexity cases vs 0% of Low-complexity cases enter review). The observed cycle-time inflation reflects a composite policy bundle—combining triage prioritization, mandatory manual routing, and shared-queue congestion—rather than an intrinsic delay caused purely by the risk flag itself.

### Structured Policy Pilot Proposal
**Eligibility**: All incoming cases with $\text{risk\_score} \ge 0.60$ and Medium/High complexity arriving during normal operational weekday shifts.

**Assignment Rule**: Stepped-wedge cluster randomization by operational shift (or 1:1 case-level randomized encouragement) assigning cases to Policy P2 (Priority triage + advisory review based on holistic triager judgment) versus Policy P0 (Priority triage + mandatory review).

**Queue-Capacity Guardrail**: If the manual review queue backlog exceeds 4 cases or waiting time exceeds 4.0 hours, automated load-shedding routes non-escalated low-complexity review cases to secondary standard pools.

**Primary Outcome**: Total case cycle time (arrival to close) and manual review queue waiting duration.

**Secondary Outcomes**: Approval/rejection accuracy, post-close quality audit defect rate, escalation proportion, and triage throughput.

**Minimum Process Data Needed**: Millisecond-accurate lifecycle timestamps (arrival, triage start/done, manual start/done, escalation start/done, close), reviewer IDs, instantaneous queue depth at every state transition, and blind expert re-audit quality scores.

**Stopping Conditions**: Terminate pilot immediately if (1) post-close audit shows a >2.0 percentage-point increase in high-risk defect/leakage rate relative to P0, or (2) unreviewed case volume creates severe liability breach.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk severity ($U_{\text{underlying\_risk}}$)**: If unobserved case risk strongly correlates with both flag generation and inherent case investigation difficulty, such that high-flag cases would require >6 hours of manual review and senior escalation even under an unflagged regime, then the flag is not creating artificial delay but merely surfacing non-discretionary case complexity.
2. **Queue interference and spillover dominance**: If cross-case queue interference is so extreme that the manual review backlog is primarily generated by unflagged cases or external surges rather than flagged volume, removing the mandatory flag review mandate will fail to reduce manual review waiting times.
3. **Decision quality and catastrophic risk leakage**: If eliminating mandatory manual review (Policy P2) leads to approving fraudulent or catastrophic claims that senior escalation currently intercepts (e.g., cases R03, R09, R13, R20), the organizational cost of quality defects will vastly outweigh the cycle-time savings, reversing the recommendation for P2 in favor of P0 or P3.
4. **Timestamp semantics and asynchronous logging**: If recorded timestamps represent batched end-of-day database commits or milestone clock-in events rather than true physical start and stop of cognitive labor, observed queue "waiting times" are measurement artifacts, invalidating the conclusion that manual review queues are the primary operational bottleneck.
5. **Positivity and lack of common support**: If case complexity and risk scores partition cases into deterministic clinical routing silos (e.g., zero Low-complexity cases can ever receive a flag, and zero High-complexity cases can ever bypass review), then counterfactual contrasts like $do(\text{Flag} = 1, M = 0)$ lack empirical support, rendering unconditioned mediation decompositions invalid.
6. **Reviewer behavioral compensation and risk-shifting**: If manual reviewers and senior staff, upon losing the explicit flag signal (Policy P1), become more anxious and defensively escalate a higher percentage of borderline cases based on heuristic suspicion, cycle times will paradoxically increase under flag suppression.

---

## 9. Final synthesis

**What is directly observed**: Flagged cases experience dramatic front-end triage acceleration (0.24h vs. 2.48h arrival-to-triage wait) but suffer double the overall completion time (16.51h vs. 8.17h). 100% of flagged cases enter manual review compared to 40% of unflagged cases, and 60% escalate to senior review, accumulating severe queue delays (6.50h manual wait, 7.00h escalation wait). All four rejected cases were escalated.

**What is a plausible mechanism**: The risk flag acts as an operational bottleneck trigger. Priority triage quickly funnels high-risk cases into downstream manual review and senior escalation stages that lack dedicated capacity. These shared, capacity-constrained queues suffer severe queuing congestion and cross-case interference, overwhelming the initial triage time savings.

**What remains unidentified**: The pure separable effect of the informational flag versus mandatory routing rules, the causal effect of manual review on decision accuracy versus baseline risk, the extent of unmeasured case severity confounding escalation, and the exact magnitude of queue spillover inflicted on unflagged cases.

**Which intervention produces the most useful next evidence**: A shift-randomized pilot of Policy P2 (priority triage with advisory, triager-discretionary review and queue guardrails). This separates triage acceleration from mandatory routing, directly measures reviewer discretion, relieves queue bottlenecks, and identifies whether mandatory review is clinically necessary or merely an operational bottleneck.
