## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**D1. Cross-order P3 inflates Order-level Pack frequency.**
Affected flattening: *Order*. Cited: e13 (Pack P3, I21, I31, O2, O3). e13 is copied to both O2 and O3 traces. The DFG edge AIPk gains an extra count. In O2's trace it is the only Pk; in O3's it is the first of two. The result is that cross-order packing is counted as if it were two independent order-level packing operations. **Harmful** — inflates Pk edge frequency and conceals that P3 is a cross-order artefact.

**D2. Cross-order P4 hides order-crossing in Item flattening.**
Affected flattening: *Item*. Cited: e14 (Pack P4, I32, I41, O3, O4). e14 is copied to I32 and I41. Both items receive identical traces AIPkDispCD. The fact that I32 belongs to O3 and I41 to O4 is entirely invisible. An analyst cannot detect that P4 crosses order boundaries from the Item DFG alone. **Harmful** — eliminates the order-crossing signal precisely where item-level analysis would be most natural to find it.

**D3. Cross-order P3 conflates orders in Package flattening.**
Affected flattening: *Package*. Cited: P3's trace (e13e16e24). P3 packs I21 (from O2) and I31 (from O3). In the Package trace, these are indistinguishable. ## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: Use a primary lead object (Order) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (Invoice for financial analysis; Package–Delivery for logistics analysis).**

### Decision criteria

1. **Lifecycle completeness** — how many distinct activities does the flattening cover?
2. **Trace interpretability** — how much distortion does the analyst face?
3. **Business naturalness** — does the object type map to a coherent business entity?
4. **Unique analytical value** — does it offer insights no other flattening provides?
5. **Manageability of known distortions** — can distortions be identified and corrected?

### Strongest supporting evidence

Order covers **8 distinct activities** (Create Order  Close Order) versus 2–4 for every other type. It produces **4 distinct variants** (O1's split-shipping variant, O2's instalment variant, O3's combined variant, O4's standard variant) versus 1 homogeneous variant for Item, Package, and Delivery. Even with 35% duplication inflation, the variant structure is the richest and reveals the most process-relevant distinctions (e.g., split shipping in O1, instalment billing in O2/O3). Invoice as a secondary lifecycle cleanly resolves the specific distortion of partial payments: the Invoice DFG's RPRP edge (V2) explains the Order-level CDRP interleaving (D6). The combination Order+Invoice covers both logistics and financial dimensions with complementary distortion profiles.

### Strongest counterargument

The data shows **pervasive cross-cutting** that defies single-object representation: P3 crosses O2/O3, P4 crosses O3/O4, D2 consolidates three orders, V2 consolidates two orders. O3's trace is 69% composed of shared events. Item, Package, and Delivery flattenings are trivially homogeneous (D10), suggesting the interesting behaviour lives in object interactions that no flattening preserves. This supports **Position C**—rejecting a single lead.

### Residual risk

Analysts using Order traces will encounter **false DFG edges** (CDCD from split shipping; CDRP from instalment interleaving) and **inflated frequencies** (e16/e24 triple-copied). Even with Invoice as a secondary view, the link between a specific payment and a specific order is mediated by the consolidated invoice V2 and requires manual correlation through V2 bills O2, V2 bills O3.

### Conditions for change

I would switch to **Position C** if:
- Additional orders introduced more cross-order packages or consolidated invoices (making O3-like traces the norm rather than an extreme case);
- Process-mining tools improved to the point where OC-DFG/OTG analysis is as accessible as traditional DFG, eliminating the practical need for a single lead.

I would switch to **Position A** (Order alone) if:
- Cross-order coupling were eliminated (no P3/P4 shared items, no V2 consolidated invoices);
- The financial dimension were secondary and instalment billing could be safely ignored.
