## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data-quality dependencies

- **A2 classification recommender** depends on accurate application intake data. If registration data is incomplete or duplicated, classification will misroute cases. A1 would help, but A2 can still be deployed with manual registration if data-quality checks are added.
- **A5 missing-document checklist** depends on A2 or equivalent product/exposure rules. The required document set differs by Micro/SME/High-exposure SME and by Working-capital, Equipment finance, and Credit-line renewal.
- **A4 entity-mismatch assistant** depends on KYC hit quality, entity hierarchies, and clear compliance ownership. It should not auto-close mismatches; it should recommend resolution and route to human approval.
- **A6 financial-statement normalization** depends on A5 reducing missing-document loops. Without A5, extraction automation will receive incomplete or inconsistent packets.
- **A7 valuation triage** depends on correct collateral requirement detection. This comes from A2/product classification and credit-analysis judgment. In the DFG, `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` occurs 2,400 times, so valuation demand is downstream of credit analysis.
- **A8 committee pack auto-assembly** depends on nearly everything upstream: KYC status, normalized financials, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. It should not be first.
- **A9 contract generation** depends on clean KYC clearance, committee decision data, and stable legal rules.
- **A11 loan activation bot** is the most downstream. It requires all control gates to be complete and auditable.

### Legal and compliance controls

- **KYC clearance must precede Customer signing.** Any e-signature automation, contract generation, or activation bot must enforce a hard stop unless KYC clearance is recorded. Provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases and should not be automated or normalized.
- **Four-eyes principle** for High-exposure SME cases requires separation between Credit analysis and Committee packaging. Automation should enforce role separation, not merely log it. Month-end violations at 5.8% suggest WIP pressure and weak manual controls.
- **Contract terms must match Committee decision.** Contract-remediation loops are 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal. A9 or A12 can help, but only after decision data is reliable.
- **Auditability** is mandatory: every model suggestion, human override, reason code, and final decision must be retained.

### Human escalation capacity

- **Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE** and the network shows high dependency on two senior specialists. A3 or A4 can easily overload them if escalations are not triaged.
- A4 should therefore be designed to **assist junior compliance staff**, reserve senior specialists for high-risk mismatches, and reduce repeated `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` loops.
- A5 shifts some work to customers and RMs. It needs an assisted exception path, or RMs will route around it.
- A6 can increase clean inflow to credit analysts. Unless analyst capacity or normalization throughput is managed, it can create a new bottleneck.
- A9 and A12 require legal-ops and control-owner capacity. Legal ops is underutilized when KYC is slow but overloaded at month end, so contract automation must be gated by upstream stability.

### External valuation dependency

- `Wait for external valuation` has a 58-hour median wait and 19% late rate. It is a synchronization point, especially for Equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases, and for Working-capital, where it is required in 18%.
- A7 cannot eliminate external dependency, but it can reduce avoidable delay through triage, valuer routing, SLA monitoring, and parallel initiation where risk policy allows.
- A7 should not be implemented as a black-box ordering engine. It needs valuer scorecards, quality-dispute handling, and clear escalation.

### Core-banking integration constraints

Only two modules should be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months. A sensible allocation is:

1. **A2 classification recommender** — deep integration with application data and routing rules.
2. **A4 entity-mismatch assistant** — deep integration with KYC/entity data and compliance workflow.

A5 can run through the document portal/case layer with lighter integration. A7 can run through a valuation gateway or SLA dashboard without requiring full core-banking transformation. A9, A10, and A11 should be deferred until upstream controls are stable because they are more legally sensitive and downstream.

|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### Quick-win portfolio: A1 + A2 + A5 + A10

**Cycle time**  
Expected to improve modestly at the front and back ends: `Receive application -> Register application` becomes faster, and `Contract preparation -> Customer signing -> Loan activation` can accelerate if e-signature adoption improves. However, the dominant exceptions remain: entity mismatch, external valuation, and normalization. End-to-end cycle time may improve less than expected because the critical-path variants are not addressed.

**Waiting time**  
- Registration waiting time, currently 1.5 hours, should fall.  
- Missing-document waiting and loops should fall because A5 attacks `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` (3,300 cases) and `Request missing documents -> Financial document review` (2,800 cases).  
- Customer signing wait, currently 44 hours, may fall with A10.  
- Entity mismatch wait, currently 31 hours, and external valuation wait, currently 58 hours, remain largely unchanged.

**Rework**  
- A5 should reduce the 52% repeated missing-document problem and the 24% financial document review rework rate.  
- A2 should reduce the 8% classification rework/misrouting rate.  
- A1 may reduce duplicate-related rework if duplicate detection is accurate.  
- A10 may reduce the 21% signing reminder loop, but only if customer identity and consent are handled well.

**Handovers**  
- Fewer manual handoffs in registration.  
- More customer/RM self-service in documents and signing.  
- Potentially more escalations into KYC and credit analysis if A1 increases throughput before downstream capacity is ready.

**Resource demand**  
- Back-office registration demand falls.  
- Compliance specialists may receive more KYC hits if throughput increases.  
- Credit analysts may receive more complete documents from A5, but normalization remains manual.  
- Legal ops may receive more contracts if signing accelerates, while month-end capacity remains tight.

**Control violations**  
- A2 improves routing control.  
- A10 is risky unless KYC clearance is a hard stop; otherwise provisional signing could increase.  
- Four-eyes and contract-decision matching are not directly strengthened.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A2 + A4 + A5 + A7

**Cycle time**  
This portfolio should produce the best end-to-end improvement among the three because it attacks synchronization points:

- A2 reduces misrouting from `Classify product and exposure -> KYC check` (7,250 cases) and `Classify product and exposure -> Financial document review` (3,940 cases).  
- A4 attacks `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch` (1,750 cases), `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` (1,160 cases), and `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` (380 cases). When present, entity mismatch is on the critical path in 82% of cases.  
- A5 reduces document loops feeding financial review and normalization.  
- A7 attacks `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` (2,400 cases), `Collateral valuation request -> Wait for external valuation` (2,350 cases), and `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` (1,980 cases).

**Waiting time**  
- Entity mismatch median wait of 31 hours should fall materially.  
- Missing-document wait of 8 hours and financial document review wait of 18 hours should fall.  
- External valuation wait of 58 hours should fall through triage, routing, SLA monitoring, and parallelization where allowed.  
- Committee packaging wait of 26 hours should fall because KYC, documents, and valuation status become more synchronized.

**Rework**  
- A5 reduces repeated missing documents and document-review loops.  
- A4 reduces the 38% rework/exception rate in entity mismatch.  
- A2 reduces misrouting and wrong-path rework.  
- A7 may reduce late valuations but could introduce quality disputes if valuer routing is too aggressive.

**Handovers**  
- A4 should reduce dependence on two senior compliance specialists by assisting junior staff with recommended resolutions.  
- A5 should reduce RM/back-office missing-document loops.  
- A7 should improve handoffs among collateral team, external valuers, and committee packaging.  
- The process should respect the practical concurrency between KYC check and financial document review, rather than forcing the sequential recording in the information system.

**Resource demand**  
- Back-office missing-document work falls.  
- Compliance specialists move from reactive firefighting to controlled exception resolution.  
- Credit analysts may see more complete files; because A6 is not included, normalization remains a manual pressure point. WIP caps and analyst queue monitoring are necessary.  
- Collateral team and external valuers need predictable routing and SLA management.  
- Legal ops should receive cleaner KYC and decision data over time.

**Control violations**  
- A2 strengthens correct product/exposure routing.  
- A4 strengthens KYC clearance before contract preparation and signing, reducing provisional-signing risk.  
- A7 must include controls to prevent inappropriate valuation ordering or quality shortcuts.  
- Four-eyes and contract-rule checks still need manual or lightweight workflow controls in year one.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3 + A4 + A9 + A12

**Cycle time**  
Cycle-time improvement is moderate and concentrated in control-heavy segments: KYC exceptions, entity mismatch, contract remediation, and legal rework. It will not materially reduce missing-document customer delay or external valuation delay.

**Waiting time**  
- KYC hit handling should improve, reducing pressure behind `KYC check`.  
- Entity mismatch wait should fall through A4.  
- Contract preparation wait and remediation loops should fall through A9/A12.  
- External valuation and missing-document waits remain largely unchanged.

**Rework**  
- KYC rework, currently 11%, should become more controlled.  
- Entity mismatch rework, currently 38%, should fall.  
- Contract remediation loops, represented by `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` (650 cases) and `Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` (510 cases), should become visible and manageable.  
- Contract-term mismatches, 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal, should decline if A9’s rule library is accurate.

**Handovers**  
- Escalations from KYC ops to compliance specialists become structured.  
- Legal ops shifts from rework to rule maintenance and exception approval.  
- Control remediation becomes an owned workflow rather than an informal loop.

**Resource demand**  
- Compliance specialists may receive more escalations from A3 unless A4 and triage rules reduce noise.  
- Legal ops may need more upfront rule governance but should see less later rework.  
- RMs and credit analysts receive less direct relief from document loops.

**Control violations**  
This portfolio is strongest for control risk:
- KYC-before-signing violations should decline.  
- Provisional signing should become harder and more visible.  
- Contract-decision mismatches should decline.  
- Four-eyes violations can be detected and remediated through A12, and prevented better if role checks are added to committee packaging.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or amplified by automation | Affected candidates | Compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| **False positives / false negatives** | A2 misclassification, A3 KYC hit misclassification, A4 wrong entity resolution, A6 poor extraction, A7 wrong valuer routing | Human-in-the-loop approval for high-risk cases; confidence thresholds; sampling QA; override reason codes; fallback to manual processing; periodic model/rule recalibration. |
| **Loss of auditability** | All model-assisted modules, especially A2, A3, A4, A9, A12 | Immutable log of model suggestion, human override, final decision, user ID, timestamp, rule/model version, and reason. Compliance should have read-only monitoring access. |
| **Exception-handling overload** | A3/A4 into compliance specialists, A5 into RMs/back office, A9/A12 into legal ops | Capacity planning, triage rules, WIP limits, SLA monitoring, senior-specialist escalation only for high-risk cases, cross-training, and exception dashboards. |
| **Four-eyes violations** | Credit analysis and Committee packaging, especially High-exposure SME and month-end | Role-based segregation; system prevents same user from completing both steps; month-end WIP caps; supervisor review; A12 or manual audit sampling. |
| **KYC clearance bypass / provisional signing** | A10, A9, A11, and any contract/signing acceleration | Hard stop: no signing or activation without recorded KYC clearance. Provisional signing only through documented exception approval and remediation workflow. |
| **Model drift and rule decay** | A2, A3, A4, A6, A9 | Scheduled recalibration, golden-sample testing, drift alerts, business rule versioning, legal rule library ownership, and change-management approval. |
| **Downstream bottleneck shift** | A1, A2, A5, A6 can push work into KYC, credit analysis, legal, or valuation | Throttled release, WIP caps, queue-length alerts, capacity buffers, phase gates, and explicit resource monitoring for compliance specialists, credit analysts, and legal ops. |
| **External partner dependency and quality disputes** | A7 | Valuer SLAs, scorecards, backup valuers, quality review sampling, dispute resolution workflow, and no automatic penalty/routing without human review for contested valuations. |
| **Customer friction and RM workarounds** | A5, A10 | Co-design checklists with RMs; plain-language requests; assisted channel for complex cases; consent management; monitor abandonment and RM escalation rates. |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out in downstream packs/contracts** | A8, A9, A11 | Do not automate downstream assembly until upstream KYC, documents, decision, and collateral artifacts are reconciled. Use readiness score before pack/contract generation. |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Recommended first-year portfolio: **A2, A4, A5, A7**.

This is the bottleneck/synchronization portfolio. It is phased to respect the two-deep-integration limit and to avoid accelerating downstream work before controls are stable.

| Phase | Timing | Modules and integration depth | Main actions | Monitoring metrics | Gate criteria to proceed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0: Guardrails and baseline** | Month 0-1 | No new deep integration | Establish baseline process-mining metrics; define control hard stops for KYC-before-signing and four-eyes; create data dictionary; co-design A5 checklist with RMs; baseline valuer SLAs. | Baseline waits, rework, control violations, RM friction indicators, valuer late rate. | Sponsor approval; control owners assigned; baseline metrics frozen; exception-handling capacity assessed. |
| **Phase 1: Routing control and document completeness** | Months 1-3 | **A2** = deep core integration #1. **A5** = portal/case-layer integration, not core-deep. | Deploy classification recommender with human override. Deploy missing-document checklist and nudging. Add WIP caps for credit analysts and compliance specialists. | Classification accuracy, override rate, misroute rate, missing-document repeat rate, financial document review wait, analyst WIP, RM feedback. | Classification sampled accuracy high; misrouting/rework trending down; missing-document repeat rate materially below 52%; no uncontrolled increase in analyst or compliance queues; RM friction acceptable. |
| **Phase 2: Entity-mismatch control** | Months 3-6 | **A4** = deep integration #2 with KYC/entity/compliance workflow. | Deploy entity-mismatch assistant. Route simple cases to junior compliance staff; reserve senior specialists for high-risk cases. Require human approval and audit trail. | Resolve entity mismatch wait, KYC loop count, provisional signing count, compliance specialist utilization, senior-specialist dependency, false-clearance rate. | Median entity-mismatch wait materially below 31 hours; repeated KYC loops decline; no unauthorized KYC bypass; audit trail complete; compliance specialist load stable. |
| **Phase 3: Valuation synchronization** | Months 6-9 | **A7** = lighter integration with valuation gateway/SLA dashboard; not core-deep. | Deploy valuation triage and valuer routing. Introduce parallel valuation initiation where risk policy allows, especially for Equipment finance. Add valuer scorecards and dispute workflow. | External valuation wait, late valuation rate, valuation quality disputes, committee packaging wait, Equipment finance cycle time, collateral team workload. | Valuer SLA adherence improves; late rate below 19% trend; quality disputes stable; committee packaging wait declines; no increase in credit rework due to premature valuation. |
| **Phase 4: Stabilization and next-wave decision** | Months 9-12 | No additional first-year modules | Stabilize A2/A4/A5/A7. Evaluate whether A6, A9, A12, or A1 should be next based on bottleneck evidence. | End-to-end cycle time, control violations, analyst normalization wait, contract remediation loops, registration queue, customer/RM satisfaction. | Process stable; control violations down; no new bottleneck exceeds pre-agreed threshold; business case for next wave approved. |

### Explicit treatment of A1

A1 is not the first module in this rollout. It remains attractive, but it should not be used to create a political quick win at the expense of flow stability. If senior management requires a visible early win, the safest early win is **A5**, because it reduces customer delay and repeated missing-document loops without creating as much uncontrolled downstream demand as A1.

A1 can be revisited in year two, or as a narrow duplicate-detection pilot after Phase 2, only if:

- classification accuracy is stable;
- KYC/entity exception queues are under control;
- credit analyst WIP is capped;
- legal and valuation queues are not deteriorating;
- duplicate detection does not auto-register high-risk cases without review.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend the **Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A2 + A4 + A5 + A7**.

This portfolio is superior to a frequency-first approach because the highest-frequency activity, **Register application**, is not the dominant end-to-end constraint. Registration is early in the process, appears in position 2 for 98% of cases, and has relatively low waiting time. Automating it first would save labor, but it would also push more cases into classification, KYC, financial document review, collateral valuation, and committee packaging. Without A2, A4, A5, and A7, the bank would likely convert back-office registration labor into downstream waiting, rework, and control exposure.

The recommended portfolio instead attacks the process-level constraints:

- **A2** prevents misrouting and ensures cases enter the correct KYC, document, collateral, and committee path.
- **A4** addresses a low-frequency but critical exception: entity mismatch. Although only 1,750 cases, it blocks KYC and signing and is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it appears.
- **A5** reduces the repeated missing-document loops that cause financial document review rework and customer delay.
- **A7** addresses the long external valuation synchronization point, especially important for Equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases.

The trade-off is clear: the bank gives up some immediate labor savings and the political simplicity of automating registration first. In exchange, it reduces critical-path delay, improves control integrity, lowers exception loops, and avoids overloading compliance specialists, credit analysts, legal ops, and external valuers.

The highest-frequency activity should therefore **not** be automated first as the portfolio anchor. A1 can be a future enhancement, but only after routing, KYC exception handling, document completeness, and valuation synchronization are stable. This is the more defensible process-level recommendation.
