## 1. Materiality Framework

Technical alignment cost measures syntactic edit distance against a control-flow skeleton under a chosen penalty table; **business materiality** measures the operational, financial, legal, and customer consequences of how a claim was actually handled. We evaluate every alignment using eight dimensions:

1. **Control Objective**: Whether mandatory risk-mitigation gates—coverage validation, independent loss assessment, fraud screening, segregation/level of approval, settlement authorization, and claimant notification—achieved their preventive or detective purpose. Bypassing a pre-disbursement gate (`P1`–`P3`) outweighs administrative logging flaws (`P4`–`P5`).
2. **Amount & Financial Exposure**: Both **direct case exposure** (e.g., EUR 480,000 in `CL-602`) and **systemic vector exposure** (e.g., an unguarded automated payout path in `CL-610` that attackers can exploit repeatedly below threshold).
3. **Reversibility**: External bank settlements (`Pay Claim`) are largely irreversible once accepted by the clearing network, especially in fraud or account-takeover scenarios. Conversely, ETL mapping gaps (`CL-611`), UI telemetry pollution (`CL-601`), and model scope omissions (`CL-607`, `CL-612`) are fully reversible in analytics pipelines.
4. **Timing & Sequence Integrity**: Sequence matters independently of presence. Executing `Pay Claim` at 10:03 before `Manual Approve` at 10:20 (`CL-604`) converts a preventive authorization control into a post-hoc rubber stamp (`P3`). Similarly, a beneficiary bank change 1 hour before auto-payment (`CL-610`) signals acute account-takeover velocity.
5. **Customer & Regulatory Harm**: Delayed or unprovable statutory notifications (`P4`), duplicate outreach (`CL-603`), or wrongful non-payment harm claimants and invite regulatory sanctions; improper disbursements breach prudential and anti-fraud obligations.
6. **Recurrence & Systemic Reach**: Isolated human workarounds require targeted coaching and recovery; recurring signatures (4 zero-cost policy breaches, 9 off-core adjuster reports, 74 retry duplicates) indicate structural defects in rule engines, integrations, or connectors.
7. **Evidence Reliability**: A control without durable, tamper-evident linkage fails audit standards. Signed external reports with stable IDs (`CL-606`) provide stronger corroboration than mutable free-text CRM links (`CL-608`) or uncorroborated verbal assurances (`CL-602`).
8. **Model Scope Fit**: Deviations against a payable-claims model (`P6`) caused by legitimate non-payable outcomes (`Deny Claim`, `Withdraw Claim`) represent **normative model incompleteness**, not operational failure.

### Severity Scale (1–5) vs. Epistemic Confidence
* **Severity (1–5)** rates the business impact of the case/control state:
  * **5 – Critical**: Severe preventive control bypass (`P1`–`P3`) involving irreversible unauthorized/pre-approval disbursement, high financial exposure, or acute fraud/account-takeover vectors.
  * **4 – High**: Substantive policy-overlay violation (`P1`/`P2`) causing unauthorized risk acceptance with moderate financial exposure, without acute fraud indicators.
  * **3 – Moderate**: Substantive business steps occurred, but cross-system gaps or mutable references create material auditability, lineage, or `P4` regulatory-proof exposure on high-value claims.
  * **2 – Low**: Data-hygiene, schema-mapping, or transport-retry defects that degrade conformance analytics or risk minor customer nuisance while core financial controls hold.
  * **1 – Minimal / Conforming**: Valid business execution (including fitting silent moves, benign UI telemetry under `P5`, or valid non-payable terminations out of model scope under `P6`).
* **How Confidence Differs from Severity**: Severity measures *how bad an outcome or control failure is*; confidence measures *how certain we are about what happened in reality* given log completeness, clock synchronization, and external corroboration. **Low confidence never discounts high severity**—instead, uncertainty on a high-exposure claim (e.g., whether `CL-602` or `CL-610` paid a fraudster, or whether `CL-608`'s mutable link was altered) triggers mandatory forensic verification.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

A missing event in the core event log (`M(A)`) is **never self-sufficient proof that business activity `A` did not occur**—under open-world enterprise logging, an activity may occur off-system (`CL-606`, `CL-608`), under an alias (`CL-611`), or genuinely be skipped (`CL-602`). Below are competing hypotheses and discriminating tests for seven cases/patterns:

1. **`CL-602` (`M(Fraud Screen)` on EUR 480k Bank-Swap Claim)**
   * *H1 (Informal Unrecorded Check)*: Approver genuinely consulted an SIU investigator verbally, who inspected fraud databases without opening a case ticket.
   * *H2 (Fabricated Post-Hoc Excuse / Negligent Bypass)*: Approver clicked `Manual Approve` without consulting SIU, or colluded to rush a fraudulent bank-change payout.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Query SIU investigator search/query audit logs (external fraud-bureau hits, internal watchlist lookups) for `CL-602`'s policy/IBAN prior to `Manual Approve`, check phone/Teams call metadata between approver and SIU, and verify beneficiary bank account ownership via bank confirmation-of-payee records.

2. **`CL-604` (`L(Pay Claim)` at 10:03 Before `S(Manual Approve)` at 10:20, plus `M(Fraud Screen)`)**
   * *H1 (True Pre-Approval Settlement Bypass)*: Payment instruction was released via unguarded batch/API trigger or manual treasury override at 10:03, followed by retroactive core-system approval at 10:20.
   * *H2 (Cross-System Clock Skew / Async Commit Delay)*: Approver clicked `Manual Approve` at 10:01, triggering real-time bank payment at 10:03, while a deadlocked core DB transaction or UTC/local-offset bug wrote the approval log timestamp as 10:20.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Inspect NTP clock-sync logs, timezone offsets, application-server request trace IDs, and DB transaction `commit_lsn` vs. UI click timestamps; verify who/what service principal signed the 10:03 bank payment payload; and audit SIU logs for any unlogged `Fraud Screen`.

3. **`CL-610` & `CL-609` (Zero-Cost `Auto Approve` Violating `P1`/`P2`)**
   * *H1 (Stale Read-Replica / Async Master-Data Lag)*: In `CL-610`, the bank change 1 hour earlier occurred in the customer portal DB, which replicates to the underwriting rules engine every 4 hours; in `CL-609`, risk tier upgraded from LOW to MEDIUM after rule evaluation cached.
   * *H2 (Defective Rule Logic / Missing Guard Clause)*: The decision table hardcodes an outdated EUR 25,000 cap (`CL-609`) and omits the `days_since_bank_change <= 7` predicate entirely (`CL-610`).
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Inspect rule-engine execution trace payloads (input JSON snapshot evaluated at `Auto Approve` decision time) for all 4 violating cases alongside git commit history of the DMN/rule table and master-data CDC replication lag metrics.

4. **`CL-606` Pattern (`M(Assess Loss)` Across 9 High-Value External-Adjuster Cases, 2 Without Stable IDs)**
   * *H1 (Pure Integration Omission with Valid Assessment)*: All 9 claims had valid, timely external adjuster reports completed before `Fraud Screen` and `Manual Approve`, but DMS intake lacks event emission and 2 reports had OCR/indexing typos on `claim_id`.
   * *H2 (Retroactive Document Backfilling or Borrowed Report)*: On the 2 unlinked high-value claims, adjusters never assessed the loss pre-payment, or staff attached unrelated/post-dated PDFs during audit prep.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Extract PDF cryptographic signature timestamps, DMS upload `created_at` immutable audit logs, adjuster invoice/assignment records, and property/claimant metadata inside the 2 unlinked PDFs against the `Fraud Screen` and `Manual Approve` timestamps.

5. **`CL-608` (`M(Notify Claimant)` in Core Log; Mutable CRM Link at +3h)**
   * *H1 (Valid Timely Portal Notification)*: Automated CRM workflow sent the secure-portal payout notice at +3h, and the mutable text field still points to the authentic, unaltered message.
   * *H2 (Post-Hoc Link Editing / Wrong Claimant Notice)*: Staff pasted an unrelated or backdated URL into the mutable CRM field after discovering a missed 24h SLA.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Query immutable secure-portal server logs and database primary keys by `claimant_id` and `timestamp` (+3h window) to verify message payload, recipient authentication, and CRM field audit-history (`old_value` -> `new_value` mutations).

6. **`CL-603` Pattern (74 Cases/Month with `S(Notify Claimant) -> L(Notify Claimant)` Sharing `message_id`)**
   * *H1 (Benign Transport-Layer Retry with Downstream Idempotency)*: Connector timed out waiting for HTTP 200 ACK and re-POSTed the same `message_id`; the notification gateway deduplicated on `message_id`, sending exactly one message to the claimant.
   * *H2 (Duplicate Customer Delivery)*: Downstream SMTP/SMS provider ignores `message_id` idempotency keys, delivering duplicate payment notifications to 74 claimants.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Cross-check connector retry logs against egress SMTP/SMS delivery provider receipts and portal inbox row counts per `message_id` for a sample of the 74 cases.

7. **`CL-607` & `CL-612` (`raw_cost = 4` Denials and Withdrawals)**
   * *H1 (Legitimate Non-Payable Outcomes Out of `M0` Scope)*: `CL-607` genuinely lacked policy coverage at intake; `CL-612` reflects an authentic, voluntary claimant withdrawal with zero payout.
   * *H2 (Improper Claim Suppression / Coerced Withdrawal)*: Valid covered claims were wrongfully denied (`CL-607`) or miscoded as "withdrawn" (`CL-612`) to manipulate loss-ratio or cycle-time KPIs, or a partial payment escaped off-ledger.
   * *Discriminating Evidence*: Check general-ledger/bank disbursement tables for zero outflow on `CL-607`/`CL-612`, verify signed claimant withdrawal correspondence (`CL-612`), and sample coverage-denial QA review logs and customer complaint/re-open rates (`CL-607`).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Standard trace alignment computes a minimum-cost path through the synchronous product of an observed trace net and a normative Petri net: $\text{raw\_cost} = \sum_{m \in \gamma} c(m)$. In our dataset, configuring uniform unit costs ($c=1$ for every visible log or model move) produces an **almost inverse relationship** between `raw_cost` and true business risk: Critical breaches score `0` (`CL-610`) or `1` (`CL-602`), whereas completely benign or desirable behaviors score `3` (`CL-601`) or `4` (`CL-607`, `CL-612`). Eight structural mechanisms explain this divergence:

1. **Cost-Function Design (Symmetric Unit Costs vs. Asymmetric Control Loss)**: Uniform $c=1$ weights treat `M(Fraud Screen)` on a EUR 480,000 claim (`CL-602`, `raw_cost=1`) identically to an extra `L(Notify Claimant)` retry (`CL-603`, `raw_cost=1`), and penalize a single synonym substitution (`CL-611`: `L(Assess Damage)` + `M(Assess Loss)`, `raw_cost=2`) twice as heavily as skipping fraud screening altogether. Edit distance counts *syntactic token edits*, blind to claim value and control criticality.
2. **Model Scope & Lifecycle Boundaries (`P6`)**: When a payable-only model (`M0`) aligns valid non-payable lifecycles like pre-screen coverage denials (`CL-607`, `raw_cost=4`) or claimant withdrawals (`CL-612`, `raw_cost=4`), the shortest path must "invent" missing model moves (`M(Assess Loss)`, `M(Auto Approve)`, `M(Pay Claim)`). Paradoxically, *had operations paid the uncovered or withdrawn claim*, `raw_cost` would have **decreased** while actual financial loss increased.
3. **Hidden / Silent Routing Moves (`tau`)**: Converting a process tree's XOR choice `X(->('Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve'), 'Auto Approve')` into a Petri net introduces silent `tau` transitions (`T(tau, c=0)`), explicitly visible in `CL-605` (`raw_cost=0`). Silent moves represent internal state-routing mechanics, not skipped business activities, and rightly carry zero cost—yet naive readers sometimes confuse `tau` moves with missing steps, or assume `raw_cost=0` guarantees policy compliance.
4. **Data Conditions & Guard Blindness**: Pure control-flow trees treat XOR branches as non-deterministic free choices. Consequently, `CL-609` (EUR 18k, MEDIUM risk) and `CL-610` (EUR 4.5k, bank changed 1 hour pre-payout) achieve `raw_cost=0` because `S(Auto Approve)` is structurally reachable, completely ignoring data predicates (`P1`, `P2`) that make that branch illegal.
5. **Activity Granularity & Synonym Drift**: Conformance assumes a 1-to-1 bijection between logged event strings and normative business activities. Sub-task UI telemetry in `CL-601` (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`) inflates `raw_cost` to `3` despite `P5` irrelevance, matching the `raw_cost=3` of `CL-604`'s illegal pre-approval EUR 90k wire. Likewise, unmapped schema synonyms (`CL-611`) manufacture `L+M` pairs (`raw_cost=2`) without any real-world deviation.
6. **Duplicated Transport Telemetry**: At-least-once delivery connectors emit technical retry logs at business-event granularity (`CL-603`, `raw_cost=1` across 74 cases/month). Alignment penalizes non-idempotent event logging identically to substantive workflow violations.
7. **Missing-in-Log vs. Missing-in-Reality (Epistemic Opacity)**: A model move `M(A, c=1)` merely proves *absence from the ingested event log*, not absence from reality. `CL-602` (`M(Fraud Screen)`), `CL-606` (`M(Assess Loss)`), and `CL-608` (`M(Notify Claimant)`) all have `raw_cost=1`. Yet `CL-606` has a signed external adjuster report in proper sequence, `CL-608` has a +3h portal message with a mutable CRM link, and `CL-602` has zero substantive fraud evidence on a EUR 480k payout.
8. **Irreversibility & Sequence Semantics**: In `CL-604`, alignment repairs an early 10:03 `Pay Claim` before a 10:20 `Manual Approve` via `L(Pay Claim, c=1)` + `M(Pay Claim, c=1)` (plus `M(Fraud Screen, c=1)` = `3`). Algebraically, moving `Pay Claim` across `Manual Approve` is a cheap two-step edit; operationally, once funds clear the external banking rail at 10:03 (`P3`), preventive authorization is irreversibly lost.
