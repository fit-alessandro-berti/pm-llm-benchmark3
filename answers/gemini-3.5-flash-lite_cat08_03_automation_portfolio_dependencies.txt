### 2. Dependency and Prerequisite Map

The small-business loan origination process is tightly coupled through data dependencies, compliance gates, and human resource bottlenecks. Automation cannot be deployed in isolation without addressing structural prerequisites:

*   **Upstream Data Quality Dependencies:** 
    *   Financial-statement extraction and normalization (**A6**) depends heavily on standardized document intake (**A5**). Feeding unstructured, messy PDFs into extraction models causes parsing errors that propagate downstream into Credit Analysis and Committee Packaging (**A8**), resulting in garbage-in/garbage-out loops.
    *   Contract generation (**A9**) and Committee Pack Assembly (**A8**) cannot succeed without fully reconciled KYC data, normalized financial statements, and collateral valuations. Upstream data errors directly trigger contract-remediation loops (which currently sit at 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewals).
*   **Legal and Compliance Controls:** 
    *   KYC clearance and four-eyes verification are absolute legal prerequisites. Contract preparation (**A9**) and Customer Signing must not occur before KYC clearance. Provisional signing (which occurs in 6.5% of cases) and month-end four-eyes violations (5.8% in High-exposure SME cases) indicate that automation must enforce hard stops rather than allowing bypasses.
    *   The Control Remediation Workbench (**A12**) and KYC pre-classifiers (**A3**) act as structural safeguards to ensure automation preserves auditable trails of model suggestions, human overrides, and final decisions.
*   **Human Escalation Capacity:** 
    *   Compliance operations are critically bottlenecked by a scarce resource pool: only 3 FTE Compliance Specialists handle entity mismatches and complex hits. Automating KYC hit pre-classification (**A3**) without expanding or optimizing the human escalation path will paralyze these 3 FTEs, especially given that entity mismatch resolution carries a 31-hour median wait and a 38% rework rate.
*   **External Valuation Dependency:** 
    *   Collateral valuation involves external partners, creating an external wait state with a 58-hour median waiting time and 19% late rates. Internal triage and valuer routing (**A7**) can optimize dispatch, but cannot eliminate the external synchronization delay. It must be decoupled from non-collateralized products (e.g., Working-capital and some renewals) via classification rules (**A2**).
*   **Core-Banking Integration Constraints:** 
    *   IT capacity strictly limits deep core-banking integrations to **two modules** in the first six months. Therefore, foundational workflow automations that require heavy legacy system hooks (such as Loan Activation bots **A11** or automated accounting ledger updates via **A9**) must be deferred to the second half of the year, prioritizing API-light workflow orchestrators and document AI tools in Phase 1.

### 3. Portfolio Alternatives

To meet strategic goals while respecting constraints, three distinct automation portfolios are defined below:

#### Portfolio 1: The Quick-Win Portfolio (Volume & Labor Focus)
*   **Modules Included:** A1 (Auto-registration), A5 (Missing-document checklist & portal), A9 (Contract generation), A10 (E-signature automation).
*   **What it optimizes:** Immediate direct labor reduction, reduction of simple manual data entry, and faster customer-facing interactions (targeting the 44-hour customer signing wait and high-frequency registration tasks).
*   **What it sacrifices:** It ignores structural process bottlenecks and synchronization delays. By pushing documents faster to Credit Analysts and Legal Ops without solving financial normalization (**A6**) or entity mismatches (**A4**), it risks exacerbating downstream queues, increasing rework loops, and causing compliance bypasses under peak loads.

#### Portfolio 2: The Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (Flow & Wait-Time Focus)
*   **Modules Included:** A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), A5 (Missing-document checklist & portal), A6 (Financial-statement extraction & normalization), A10 (E-signature automation).
*   **What it optimizes:** End-to-end cycle time, reduction of critical waiting times (targeting the 31-hour mismatch wait and 20-hour financial normalization wait), and smoothing out the handover-of-work network between Back Office, Compliance, and Credit Analysts.
*   **What it sacrifices:** It provides fewer direct administrative labor savings on high-frequency, low-complexity tasks (like basic application registration) and defers heavy structural control overhauls, requiring careful monitoring of model extraction accuracy.

#### Portfolio 3: The Compliance-First Portfolio (Risk & Control Focus)
*   **Modules Included:** A3 (KYC hit pre-classifier), A6 (Financial-statement extraction), A9 (Contract generation with control rules), A12 (Control remediation workbench).
*   **What it optimizes:** Strict adherence to regulatory compliance, elimination of four-eyes violations during month-end rushes, reduction of contract-remediation loops (currently at 8.5%–14%), and establishment of bulletproof audit trails.
*   **What it sacrifices:** Throughput speed and customer-facing ease. It introduces tighter validation gates that may temporarily increase initial cycle times for complex SME applications while compliance specialists adapt to automated pre-classification workflows.

### 5. Risks and Compensating Controls

*   **Auditability:** 
    *   *Risk:* Automated classification, KYC pre-screening, and contract generation may act as black boxes, failing regulatory requirements for explainability.
    *   *Compensating Control:* Implement a mandatory decision-logging layer across all modules that records model version, confidence scores, feature importance, human overrides, and timestamps.
*   **False Positives / False Negatives:** 
    *   *Risk:* KYC pre-classifiers (**A3**) or financial extraction tools (**A6**) may generate false negatives (missing true sanctions/risk hits or misinterpreting financial liabilities).
    *   *Compensating Control:* Establish hard confidence thresholds. Any extraction or classification falling below 90% confidence must be routed to human exception queues. Regular sample-based quality audits must be conducted on automated passes.
*   **Model Drift:** 
    *   *Risk:* Economic shifts, changing SME product lines, or evolving regulatory definitions can degrade the accuracy of classification and extraction models over time.
    *   *Compensating Control:* Establish a monthly model performance review board to track override rates, error rates, and drift metrics, with automated retraining triggers when error rates exceed 3%.
*   **Exception Handling:** 
    *   *Risk:* Automation may dump unhandled exceptions onto already strained human resources (such as the 3 Compliance Specialists), leading to abandoned applications.
    *   *Compensating Control:* Implement tiered exception routing and clear triage guidelines within the Control Remediation Workbench (**A12**), ensuring exceptions are prioritized by exposure level (High-exposure SME first).
*   **Four-Eyes Compliance:** 
    *   *Risk:* Month-end delivery pressure tempts staff to bypass segregation of duties, risking a recurrence of the 5.8% four-eyes violations in High-exposure SME cases.
    *   *Compensating Control:* Hardcode system-enforced segregation of duties in workflow routing (e.g., preventing a Credit Analyst from initiating Committee Packaging (**A8**) or approving terms on cases they analyzed) regardless of deadline pressure.

### 6. Recommended Phased Rollout

To respect the constraint of maximum four modules in the first year, deep core-banking integration limits (two modules in the first six months), and the compliance mandate for control strengthening, a phased rollout plan is established:

#### Phase 1 (Months 1–6): Foundation, Flow & Compliance Gating
*   **Modules Deployed:** 
    *   **A5 (Missing-document checklist & portal):** API-light deployment to improve customer document hygiene and cut back-and-forth loops.
    *   **A12 (Control remediation workbench):** Fulfills the compliance mandate immediately by establishing robust control error capture and four-eyes enforcement mechanisms.
*   **Core-Banking Integration:** Utilizes both available IT deep-integration slots (connecting document intake securely to core customer repositories and establishing rule hooks for control remediation).
*   **Monitoring Metrics:** Document collection cycle time, missing-document loop frequency (target: reduction from 52% to <20%), and four-eyes compliance violations (target: 0%).
*   **Gate Criteria for Phase 2:** Document-review rework rate must drop by at least 30%, and zero four-eyes violations must be recorded for two consecutive months.

#### Phase 2 (Months 7–12): Bottleneck Resolution & Document Normalization
*   **Modules Deployed:** 
    *   **A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant):** Relieves the 3 Compliance Specialists by streamlining entity resolution, tackling the 31-hour median wait.
    *   **A6 (Financial-statement extraction & normalization):** Standardizes financial data ingestion, protecting downstream Credit Analysts from raw PDF errors.
*   **Core-Banking Integration:** Leverages IT integration capacity freed up from Phase 1 for accounting system and document parser hooks.
*   **Monitoring Metrics:** Entity mismatch resolution time, financial normalization service time, and Credit Analyst throughput capacity.
*   **Gate Criteria for Year 2:** End-to-end cycle time for SME loans must decrease by 25%, and Credit Analyst utilization must stabilize below 80% during peak hours.

### 7. Final Recommendation

#### Portfolio Selection
We recommend adopting a hybrid path anchored primarily in **Portfolio 2 (Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio)**, explicitly augmented with **A12 (Control Remediation Workbench)** from **Portfolio 3** to satisfy the compliance prerequisite. 

#### The Trade-Off Argument: Why Frequency Alone is Misleading
Senior management’s initial instinct to automate the highest-frequency activity—**Receive Application (12,000 instances, 3 min service time)**—is fundamentally misleading. 
1. **Low Baseline Friction:** Application receipt is already digital for 72% of cases, with a negligible median waiting time (0.2 hours) and a 1% rework rate. Automating this further yields minimal end-to-end process acceleration.
2. **The Bullwhip Effect:** Pushing high volumes of applications faster into an upstream queue without fixing structural bottlenecks simply floods downstream resources. For instance, Financial Document Review already suffers from a 24% rework rate and an 18-hour wait; throwing more raw volume at it via high-frequency intake automation would bottleneck Credit Analysts and overwhelm Back Office FTEs.
3. **Targeting the Real Constraints:** True process performance is governed by synchronization delays and rework loops—specifically the **31-hour waiting time for Entity Mismatches (38% rework)**, the **58-hour external valuation wait**, and the **52% repeated missing-document requests**. 

By prioritizing **A5, A4, A6, and A12**, the bank targets the true operational constraints, slashes multi-day wait states, protects compliance controls, and establishes a scalable foundation for future growth without burning out scarce human specialists.
