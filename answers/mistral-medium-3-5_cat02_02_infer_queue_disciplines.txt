  - **D06:** Appointment honored despite D07 being eligible.
- **Contradicting:**

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### **Episode 1: QA at 08:52 (A02 vs. A04)**
- **Competing explanations:**
  1. **Appointment + Priority:** A04 (P1) preempts A02 (P3) because priority overrides non-appointment jobs.
  2. **SPT:** A04 (7 min) is shorter than A02 (remaining 8 min).
- **Discriminating observable:**
  - If a new job arrives at 08:50 with P2 and est_min=5, **priority** would select it over A02, while **SPT** would select it only if its est_min < 8.

### **Episode 2: QB at 10:00 (B01 vs. B05)**
- **Competing explanations:**
  1. **Preemptive Priority:** B05 (P1) preempts B01 (P3).
  2. **SPT:** B05 (14 min) is shorter than B01’s remaining time (~32 min).
- **Discriminating observable:**
  - If a new job arrives at 09:59 with P2 and est_min=10, **priority** would not preempt B01 (P3), while **SPT** would if 10 < 32.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable queue:** **QC** (Deadline-driven with priority tie-breaker). The deadline field is explicitly used, and the oldest job (C01) is consistently deprioritized in favor of tighter deadlines.
- **Least identifiable queue:** **QB**. Preemption, SPT, and priority all explain different episodes, and dispatcher overrides introduce noise.
- **Most dangerous inference:** Assuming **QD is purely skill-based**. The interaction with appointments (D06) and priority (D08) suggests a hybrid policy that could be misclassified if only skill constraints are observed.

**Cross-queue conclusion:**
- **QA and QC** show strong evidence of hybrid rules (appointment + priority, deadline + priority).
- **QB** is ambiguous due to preemption and manual overrides.
- **QD** is skill-dominated but with secondary appointment/priority influences.
- **No queue is purely FIFO or SPT**, and observational equivalence between priority, deadlines, and appointments is the biggest challenge.
