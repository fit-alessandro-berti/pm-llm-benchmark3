# Cyber-incident resilience review: pharmaceutical wholesaler order-to-delivery

## 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 – Baseline (Day -30 to Day -1).**
Operational state: fully system-supported flow at ~2,000 cases/business day (42,000 / 21). Dominant variant: the nine-step normative path (Receive → Validate → Reserve → Pick → Pack → Q/release → Dispatch → PoD → Invoice close) covers ~88% of cases end-to-end (36,800 of 42,000 closed by invoice). Legitimate side loops are small and controlled: compliance hold 1.7% of validations (700/41,700, 74% return to validation), backorder wait 2.2% (900/41,000, one third cancelled), repack/recheck 1.2% (450/38,900). Risk posture: strong — fitness 0.96, 91% fitting traces, traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain documentation missing 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control missing 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellations 2.1%. Segment service is differentiated by design (median 8h / 23h / 36h).

**T1 – Loss of WMS (Day 0 morning).**
Operational state: inventory reservation, scanning and release checks lose their system backbone; order intake also degraded. The organisation has no time-stamped electronic evidence for the rest of Day 0 morning. This is the moment when the real control decision was made implicitly: nobody had a pre-authorised "degraded mode", so each team improvised its own.

**T2 – Manual workaround (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2).**
Operational state: paper/spreadsheet operation. Intake did not collapse — 5,600 orders received over ~2.5 days (≈2,240/day, above baseline demand, probably including hospital stockpiling and re-submissions), but only ~2,530 reached an end state (2,100 via spreadsheet invoice hold, 430 cancelled); roughly 3,000 orders were still in flight at Day 2. Dominant variants (from the T2 DFG):

- *Controlled manual path* (~45%): Emergency triage → Manual authorization check (2,500) → Snapshot check → Manual pick list → Manual pack → Temporary release control (1,650) → Dispatch without scan → Phone PoD → Spreadsheet invoice hold.
- *Shortcut path* (~31% of triaged orders): Emergency triage → **Skip system authorization check (1,600)** → Snapshot check → Pick → Pack → **Dispatch without scan directly from pack (1,800 of 3,600 packs, 50%)** → Phone PoD.
- *Hospital fast lane* (~20%): Emergency triage → Fast lane (1,000) → Snapshot check (920) → …
- *Deferral path*: 1,080 orders deferred (500 at receipt, 580 after the snapshot check showed no stock), 960 queued, of which only 420 were picked in T2 and 320 were cancelled.

Risk posture: worst of the event. Fitness 0.71, only 44% fitting traces. Traceability exceptions 9.5% (12× baseline), cold-chain documentation missing 4.8% (12×), controlled-substance dual-control missing 2.9% (14.5×), rework 12.5%, cancellations 7.8%. The manual DFG contains **no temperature-check activity at all**, and half of packed orders had no release check of any kind (Manual pack → Dispatch without scan: 1,800). Mission outcome was protected: Hospital-critical median 10h (+25%), p90 21h, fill rate 91%. The cost was pushed onto pharmacies (median 54h, +135%; fill rate 61%) and bulk (median 96h, +167%; p90 180h).

**T3 – Partial recovery (Day 3 to Day 6).**
Operational state: intake system back, inventory sync and scanning unreliable — a hybrid mode that is arguably more dangerous than pure manual, because staff believe the system is authoritative when it is not. 11,400 cases in 4 days (2,850/day, i.e. new demand plus backlog drain). Hospital service back to near-normal (median 9h, p90 18h); pharmacy p90 still 90h, bulk p90 160h. Risk posture: improved but still far from baseline — fitness 0.82, 59% fitting traces, traceability exceptions 5.4%, cold-chain gaps 2.3%, controlled-substance gaps 1.4%, rework 9.0%. In absolute terms T3 produced *more* traceability exceptions than T2 (≈616 vs ≈532 cases) because volume was higher while controls were still degraded — the common "recovery relaxation" failure.

**T4 – Stabilization (Day 7 to Day 21).**
Operational state: normal systems mostly back; 35,000 cases in 15 days (2,333/day, ~17% above baseline demand as backlog was cleared). Dominant variant is the normative path again (28,800 of 29,600 releases dispatched normally), but with four recovery-specific loops: Backlog prioritization (1,500, of which 450 = 30% cancelled), Reconcile manual reservation (1,400; 300 cancelled, 200 unresolved), Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200 = 4.2% of dispatches; 400 unresolved), and Audit remediation (2,300 = 8.3% of invoice closes; 200 still open). Compliance holds ran at 3.9% of validations (1,300/33,500) versus 1.7% at baseline, and repack/recheck at 3.0% versus 1.2%. Risk posture: cycle-time dashboards say "recovered" for hospitals (median 8h) but not for pharmacies (p90 74h vs 49h) or bulk (p90 120h vs 72h), and the control metrics are still 2.2–2.75× baseline (traceability 2.2%, cold-chain 0.9%, controlled 0.5%). Fitness 0.90 with 76% fitting traces — 15 percentage points below baseline three weeks after the event. The audit-remediation queue outlived operational recovery: the hidden tail.

**Cumulative excess control gaps across T2–T4 (versus what baseline rates would have produced on the same volume):** roughly 1,500 additional traceability exceptions, ~640 cold-chain shipments without temperature documentation, and ~390 controlled-substance orders without dual-control evidence. These are regulatory-grade numbers, not rounding noise, and they are what management must weigh against the protected hospital fill rate.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this case the two look identical on the throughput dashboard and differ only in what happened to the *purpose* of a control. A useful test with four questions:

1. **Purpose preserved?** Did the adaptation achieve what the original control achieved (eligibility verified, custody proven, temperature within range, two people witnessed the controlled item), just by other means?
2. **Contemporaneous evidence?** Was the evidence created at the moment of the act, or reconstructed later from memory and spreadsheets?
3. **Declared and bounded?** Was the deviation visible to management at the time, scoped to specific products/customers, and time-limited?
4. **Reconcilable?** Can the case be fully brought back to the normative model afterwards, or is something permanently missing?

Resilience answers yes to all four; concealed noncompliance fails (1) or (2) while the throughput metric keeps looking fine. Note that T2 throughput preservation (hospital fill rate 91%) coexisted with a 14.5× rise in controlled-substance dual-control gaps: the same phase contains both true resilience and concealed noncompliance, and only decomposing by activity and product separates them.

**True resilience (purpose preserved, evidence equivalent or acceptable, declared):**
- **B Manual authorization check** — same decision, same decision-makers, offline evidence. The only defect is capacity.
- **F Temporary release control** for controlled substances — pharmacist review of 100% of controlled orders is the actual control, with or without a system.
- **I Defer noncritical order** and **J Backlog prioritization** as *mechanisms* — sacrificing low-risk service to protect controls and critical customers is the correct trade; their weakness is fairness, not compliance.
- **Spreadsheet invoice hold** — refusing to close cases without evidence is a control surviving the outage.
- **K Reconcile manual reservation** — necessary, honest clean-up; should simply have started earlier.
- **A Hospital-critical fast lane** as *prioritisation* — mission-aligned and the reason hospital p90 stayed at 21h.

**Risky shortcuts (control purpose removed, throughput preserved, gap invisible at the time):**
- **C Skip system authorization check** — 1,600 orders, ~31% of triaged volume, based on "assumed standing authorization". No decision record, no evidence; a purely capacity-driven bypass of a licensing control.
- **G Dispatch without scan** for controlled, cold-chain and recall-sensitive products — chain of custody broken at the handoff; the single largest driver of traceability exceptions and of the 1,200 PoD remediations.
- **The authorization-bypass component of A** — using the fast lane to avoid checks rather than to jump the queue.
- **F's blind spot** — the absence of any temperature check in the manual flow, so cold-chain product shipped with unknown thermal history. This is patient-safety exposure, not documentation.
- **E Manual pick list *with reconstructed* scans** for recall-sensitive and controlled items — the recall capability for everything shipped in T2 depends on inference.

**Ambiguous (depends on stated risk appetite and product scope):**
- **D Snapshot check** — acceptable on Day 0, negligent by Day 2; the risk is a function of staleness, which management must bound explicitly.
- **E** for ambient products — reconstructed lot data on ambient, non-recall-sensitive items is a tolerable data-quality cost if the pick ticket captured the lot at pick time.
- **H Phone PoD** — adequate for ambient pharmacy orders, inadequate for controlled products where the receiver's identity and quantity acceptance matter.
- **G** for ambient products with a signed courier manifest — arguably fine, since the scan is a data-capture convenience for those items.
- **L Audit remediation** — indispensable, but it is *not* equivalent to real-time compliance. Reconstructing a missing scan restores a record; "remediating" a missing dual-control event or temperature reading produces a deviation report. The case's stabilization data (controlled 0.5%, cold-chain 0.9% still above baseline in T4, 200 audit items still open) is the signature of gaps that cannot simply be closed.
- **I / J on fairness** — 30% of the prioritised backlog was cancelled and pharmacy p90 was still 1.5× baseline at Day 21. Whether that is acceptable is a policy choice about how much pharmacy (and therefore community patient) service may be sacrificed for hospital service; it should be made in advance, not by whoever runs the backlog queue.

The key abductive inference is that the shortcuts were *caused* by the resilient adaptations' capacity limits: B saturated at ~2,500 checks, so C absorbed the rest; F saturated at 1,650 releases, so G-from-pack absorbed the rest. The redesign must therefore not merely ban C and G — it must make B and F scalable or pre-compute their outputs, otherwise the next incident will recreate the same shortcuts under different names.

## 4. Recovery process model

### 4.1 Degraded-mode levels and explicit trigger conditions

| Level | Trigger (any one) | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| **L0 Normal** | — | — |
| **L1 System-degraded** | WMS or scanning unavailable > 60 min; scan success rate < 95% over 2h; inventory sync lag > 2h | All components available and reconciled for 4 consecutive hours |
| **L2 Manual operation** | WMS unavailable > 4h or declared unrecoverable within the shift; order intake also unavailable; cyber incident declared by IT security (isolation, ransomware, integrity uncertain) | Intake and WMS restored *and* inventory ledger reconciled to within a defined tolerance (e.g., 99% of A-class SKUs) |
| **L3 Hybrid recovery** | Systems partially restored but any of: sync lag > 2h, scan success < 95%, integrity of restored data not yet certified by IT security | Integrity certified; sync and scan KPIs inside tolerance for 24h |
| **L4 Reconciliation/remediation** | Entered automatically when leaving L2/L3; ends only when remediation queue = 0 and control-gap deviations dispositioned | Queue empty, CAPA opened for unrecoverable gaps |

Rules attached to triggers: the incident commander declares the level in writing (time-stamped); the declaration is itself an event in the log; every manual case created while a level is active carries the level as an attribute. This makes the deviation *declared* rather than concealed, and lets conformance checking later be run against the correct (degraded-mode) reference model instead of the normal one.

### 4.2 Triage rules (apply at "Receive order" in L2/L3)

Priority score = customer criticality × product risk × time sensitivity, with hard rules first:

1. **Hospital-critical, any product** → Fast lane, always. Fast lane means *queue position*, never *control omission*.
2. **Controlled substance, any customer** → never skipped, never deferred without customer contact, always through the full minimum-control set.
3. **Cold-chain, any customer** → ship only if temperature evidence can be captured at pack and handoff; otherwise hold in monitored storage and inform the customer. Deferral of cold-chain is preferred to blind shipment.
4. **Recall-sensitive** → ship only with lot/expiry captured on the manual pick ticket.
5. **Pharmacy-standard ambient** → whitelist-authorised path (see below); otherwise manual authorization queue.
6. **Wholesale-bulk ambient** → deferral candidate, with a maximum deferral age (e.g., 48h) and mandatory customer notification at deferral.
7. **Capacity reservation**: at least a fixed share (e.g., 25%) of pick capacity is reserved for non-hospital segments so protection of hospitals does not become abandonment of pharmacies.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product/customer risk (non-negotiable in any level)

| Product | Authorization | Inventory | Pick/pack evidence | Release | Dispatch | Proof of delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ambient, non-recall-sensitive | Whitelist (pre-signed) or manual check | Snapshot ≤ 8h old | Serialised manual pick ticket, lot optional | Sampled pharmacist review (≥10%) | Signed courier manifest | Phone PoD acceptable, entered within 24h |
| Recall-sensitive ambient | Whitelist or manual check | Snapshot ≤ 8h old | Lot and expiry mandatory on ticket | Sampled review (≥25%) | Signed courier manifest with lot list | Signed/photographed delivery note |
| Cold-chain | Manual check | Snapshot ≤ 4h old for cold storage | Lot, expiry, pack-out time, temperature at pack | 100% pharmacist review incl. temperature | Manifest + temperature at handoff + logger ID | Signed delivery note with receipt temperature |
| Controlled substance | Manual check by compliance staff, no whitelist | Physical count at pick | Dual initials at pick and pack, quantity reconciled to safe/cage log | 100% pharmacist release, dual signature | Manifest, dual signature, sealed tote ID | Signed receiver name, ID, quantity within 24h; escalation if missing |

The "Skip system authorization check" activity is deleted from the vocabulary. Its legitimate content becomes **"Whitelist authorization check"** — a pre-computed, QA-signed list of customer × ambient SKU combinations with standing authorization and recent order history, regenerated nightly and stored offline. Using it *is* a control; the list itself is the evidence.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture

- **Serialised Manual Case IDs (MC-IDs)** pre-printed in books/packs kept in the warehouse (offline). Every manual order gets one at triage; every downstream sheet, tote label and manifest references it. This alone eliminates most reconstruction ambiguity.
- **Offline-capable capture app** on ruggedised tablets (local storage, later sync) as the primary tool, with the paper kit as second fallback. Both record actor, timestamp, MC-ID, activity, lot/expiry, temperature, signatures.
- **Photo evidence** of signed manifests, delivery notes and temperature displays, filed by MC-ID.
- **Evidence-pending state**: a case cannot move to "Invoice close" from manual operation; it moves to *Invoice hold — evidence pending* (formalising the spreadsheet invoice hold), and is released to invoice only when the minimum-control checklist for its product class is complete or a deviation is opened.
- **Deviation at the point of failure**: if a minimum control cannot be performed (no thermometer, no second person), the operator records a deviation *then*, not in week three. This is the difference between declared and concealed noncompliance.

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization (L3/L4)

Backlog score, computed and logged per case = criticality weight (Hospital 3, Pharmacy 2, Bulk 1) × product-risk weight (Controlled/Cold-chain 2, Recall-sensitive 1.5, Ambient 1) × age factor (age / segment p90 target) × shelf-life urgency (cold-chain remaining shelf life). Rules: no case may exceed 2× its segment baseline p90 without customer contact; cancellation only after customer confirmation; a daily fairness report shows fill rate and backlog age by segment. The score is stored as an event attribute so "who was served first and why" is auditable — this answers the ambiguity flagged for J.

### 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

- A **reconciliation cell** starts at declaration of L2, not after recovery. It maintains the manual movements ledger (from pick tickets and manifests) so that when the WMS returns, the ledger is loaded before live transactions resume. This converts K from a 1,400-case clean-up into a controlled cut-over.
- **Remediation is split into two queues with different owners and SLAs:**
  - *Record reconstruction* (missing scan, missing PoD, invoice release): operations/data team, target ≤ 5 business days after restoration, tracked by age.
  - *Control-gap deviations* (missing dual control, missing temperature, unauthorised shipment): Quality/Responsible Person. Each requires product-disposition assessment (customer notification, quarantine/return for cold-chain, regulatory notification where applicable) and feeds CAPA. These are never marked "remediated" — they are "dispositioned".
- **Exit from L4** requires both queues at zero and a signed post-incident conformance report (fitness by product class, deviations dispositioned).

### 4.7 Process-tree notation of the degraded-mode model

Notation: `→(a, b)` sequence; `×(a, b)` exclusive choice; `∧(a, b)` parallel; `⟲(body, redo)` loop; `τ` silent step. Activities in brackets are level-gated.

```
Recovery_OTD =
→( Receive order,
   Declare level (L1/L2/L3),
   Triage & score,
   ×( Fast lane [Hospital-critical], τ ),
   ×( Whitelist authorization check [Ambient, non-controlled],
      Manual authorization check [all others] ),
   ×( Defer with notification (→ Backlog queue, max age) ,
      →( Snapshot check (TTL) / Physical count [Controlled],
         Manual pick ticket (lot/expiry; dual initials [Controlled]),
         Manual pack (temperature at pack [Cold-chain]),
         ×( Sampled release [Ambient],
            100% pharmacist release incl. temperature [Cold-chain, Controlled, Recall-sensitive] ),
         ∧( Manifest dispatch (courier signature; temp + dual sig [Cold-chain, Controlled]),
            Ledger entry (reconciliation cell) ),
         ×( Phone PoD [Ambient], Signed/photo PoD [Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive] ),
         Invoice hold — evidence pending,
         ×( Invoice close,
            →( Open deviation at point of failure, Disposition, Invoice close ) ) ) ) )

Backlog_Recovery (L3/L4) =
⟲( →( Backlog prioritization (logged score), Validate authorization, Reconcile ledger, normal path ),
   τ )
∧ →( Record-reconstruction queue (SLA 5d), Control-gap deviation queue (disposition + CAPA), Exit L4 )
```

The essential structural changes versus what happened in T2: no branch exists in which authorization or release is skipped; deferral carries a notification and an age limit; evidence capture is embedded in each activity rather than reconstructed; the ledger runs in parallel with dispatch; and remediation is two typed queues rather than one undifferentiated "Audit remediation" after invoice close.

## 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading indicators (monitored hourly/daily during L1–L3; thresholds trigger escalation):**

| Dimension | Indicator | Target / alert |
|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Manual packs per hour vs. baseline pick rate (baseline ≈ 2,000 cases/day) | Alert < 60% of baseline; T2 achieved ≈ 70% |
| Service protection | Hospital-critical fill rate and same-day dispatch rate; **and** pharmacy fill rate | Hospital ≥ 90% (T2: 91%); pharmacy ≥ 75% (T2: 61% — fails) |
| Traceability | Share of manual dispatches with MC-ID, lot, and signed manifest at the moment of handoff | ≥ 98%; "dispatch without manifest" count = 0 for controlled/cold-chain |
| Control completeness | Controlled-substance orders with dual signature at release; cold-chain orders with temperature recorded at pack and handoff; orders passing through any authorization step (target 100%, T2: 69% of triaged) | 100% for controlled/cold-chain; any miss = same-day deviation |
| Capacity of controls | Manual authorization queue length and wait time; pharmacist release queue length | Escalate additional trained staff before queue > 4h — this is the leading indicator that shortcuts (C, G) are about to appear |
| Snapshot staleness | Age of inventory snapshot in use; number of picks failing for stock not found | TTL 8h (4h cold); pick-fail rate < 5% |
| Backlog age | Oldest deferred order by segment; count over 2× segment p90 | 0 over threshold without customer contact |
| Fairness | Ratio of pharmacy to hospital fill rate; ratio of cycle-time inflation by segment (T2: hospital +25%, pharmacy +135%, bulk +167%) | Pharmacy/hospital fill ratio ≥ 0.8; inflation ratio ≤ 3× |
| Declaration hygiene | Share of manual cases carrying a declared level attribute; deviations opened at point of failure vs. found later | ≥ 99%; ratio > 1 |

**Lagging indicators (per incident, by phase and product class, plus 30/60/90-day follow-up):**

| Dimension | Indicator | Baseline reference / target |
|---|---|---|

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept, and pre-authorise, the deliberate sacrifice of pharmacy and bulk cycle time and fill rate to protect hospital-critical supply during an outage — the T2 outcome of hospital median 10h and 91% fill rate was the right objective and was achieved — provided that sacrifice is bounded by explicit deferral ages, customer communication and a reserved capacity share so it does not become the 61% fill rate and 30% backlog cancellation seen here. It should also accept slower, paper-based, evidence-heavy operation, reconstructed scans on ambient products, phone proof of delivery for ambient orders, and a longer visible reconciliation queue. It should not accept, under any throughput pressure, shipping controlled substances without dual control, cold-chain product without a temperature record, recall-sensitive product without lot capture, or any order without an authorization check of some kind; the ~390 controlled and ~640 cold-chain gaps accumulated across T2–T4 are patient-safety and licensing exposures that after-the-fact audit remediation documents but cannot cure, and they arose because the good controls (B, F) ran out of capacity and nobody had decided in advance what to do next. The recommendation would change if evidence showed that the skipped authorizations were in fact confined to whitelisted ambient customers with zero downstream compliance holds, or that the cold-chain documentation gaps were in shipments whose logger data was later retrieved and in range — in which case those items move from "concealed noncompliance" to "reconstructable record" and the minimum-control matrix could be relaxed for those classes; conversely, if remediation finds even a handful of controlled-substance shipments to unauthorised recipients, the fast lane's authorization bypass should be treated as a reportable incident and the recovery model made stricter still.
