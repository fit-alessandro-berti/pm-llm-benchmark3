## 1. Feasibility model
```text
For any case c:
Register_c < Triage_c
Triage_c < IdentityCheck_c

If originals_required(c)=no:
  Triage_c < DocumentCheck1_c
If originals_required(c)=yes:
  Triage_c < OriginalsReceipt_c < DocumentCheck1_c

IdentityCheck_c < Assessment1_c
DocumentCheckk_c < Assessmentk_c

If RequestMoreInfo_k_c occurs:
  Assessmentk_c < RequestMoreInfo_k_c < DocumentCheck(k+1)_c < Assessment(k+1)_c

If high_risk(c)=yes or high_value(c)=yes:
  LatestAssessment_c < FinalComplianceReview_c < Decision_c
Else:
  LatestAssessment_c < Decision_c

Decision_c < Notify_c

Optional concurrency:
  PreliminaryEscalationPacket_c may occur after Triage_c and before LatestAssessment_c
  PreliminaryCompliancePrep_c may occur before LatestAssessment_c, but does not replace FinalComplianceReview_c
  When originals are not required, IdentityCheck_c and DocumentCheck1_c may overlap
```

```text
Conditional and declarative constraints:
- Final Assessment requires completed Identity Check and the latest completed Document Check.
- When originals are required, Document Check cannot start before Originals Receipt; Identity Check may still start earlier.
- A preliminary compliance screen is advisory only; final compliance must still follow the latest relevant Assessment.
- After any Request More Info, a new Document Check and a new Assessment are mandatory before Decision.
- For high-risk/high-value cases, compliance cannot be skipped; if evidence changes after a request for more information, compliance must be new or explicitly reaffirmed.
- Four-eyes rule: the final Assessment and final Compliance Review cannot be performed by the same person.
- Resource exclusivity: one named resource cannot work two overlapping service intervals; C1 is a single-server compliance bottleneck.
- Queue snapshots are forecasts, so earlier readiness does not guarantee earlier service start.
- Controlled workarounds are only valid if timestamps reflect real start/finish times rather than later batch entry.
- Provisional reservation of scarce capacity consumes that capacity even when the early packet is incomplete or later superseded.
```

K-219 primarily changes routing from D1 to D2. K-411 primarily changes timing by enabling earlier parallel task creation and cleaner logging. K-104 changes timing and limited routing by adding early escalation/compliance preparation, but it does not change the requirement for post-assessment final compliance. K-337 is the only intervention that changes the allowed decision policy, because it forbids an otherwise permissible second evidence-gathering loop and forces a decision under residual uncertainty.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations
### K-104
Factual mechanism: The case was high-risk and high-value, so final compliance was unavoidable. The key factual delay was not only the long D1 queue, but also the late discovery-to-escalation pattern: the sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage, yet S1 advice and C1 reading were deferred until after A1's assessment. That stacked D1 waiting, A1 waiting, escalation, and then a long C1 wait/read-from-scratch interval.

Minimal intervention: The narrowest change is to create an early information packet at Triage, let S1 and possibly C1 do preliminary work while Identity and Document checks are still pending, and hold only a provisional C1 slot. Nothing else changes: D1 still performs the complex document review, A1 still performs the final assessment, and final compliance still occurs only after Assessment.

Temporal propagation: Early escalation does not move the case through D1 or A1 faster, so the readiness time for final compliance is still anchored by Document Check completion and then Assessment. What changes is the post-assessment segment: a reserved C1 slot may start sooner than the factual Wed 13:10, and a pre-read file may reduce final compliance service time by roughly 1-3 h. The local gain therefore comes from shortening the compliance queue plus read-in time, not from moving the earlier bottlenecks.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible because no-originals cases may run Identity and Document checks in parallel, preliminary compliance work does not substitute for the mandatory final compliance review, and A1 as final assessor remains different from C1 as final compliance reviewer. Resource exclusivity still applies: any preliminary C1 review and final C1 review must be separate, non-overlapping service intervals.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, S1 reviews on Monday, C1 pre-reads on Tuesday, and a reserved slot lets final compliance start soon after Tue 19:10, moving decision into late Tuesday or early Wednesday and saving roughly 12-20 h. Base case, the slot is only partially earlier, so decision moves forward about 4-10 h. Worst case, the early packet is too incomplete, the reservation is bumped by another urgent case, and the local gain collapses to near zero while other cases are delayed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If observed data showed that provisional C1 reservations can be released with very low waste and that pre-read work reliably cuts final review time by more than 3 h, I would view this intervention more favorably. If, instead, C1 already performs informal pre-reads without reservations, or if most triage packets are too incomplete to be useful, the intervention adds little and mostly reshuffles the queue.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: This was a low-risk, low-value case, so there was no compliance stage. The factual choice to wait for D1 reflected a quality-first routing rule: handwritten originals were treated as harder than ordinary standard documents, and D1's specialization reduced the chance of rework. The cost of that choice was a later start after originals arrived.

Minimal intervention: The intervention changes only the document-routing rule. As soon as originals arrive, the case is placed with D2 rather than waiting for D1. Identity timing, assessment logic, and decision authority all remain unchanged.

Temporal propagation: The expected gain comes from queue position, not faster service. D2 likely can start late Monday or early Tuesday because its queue was materially shorter than D1's, but D2 then works more slowly and with more interpretation risk on handwriting. If D2 finishes clearly enough, the case becomes assessment-ready several hours earlier and may enter A2's queue in time for a Tuesday daytime assessment rather than a late-evening one. If D2's reading is ambiguous, the earlier start is dominated by a formal rework loop after Assessment.

Process-feasibility check: Because originals are required, Document Check cannot start before Mon 17:30. Identity may remain earlier, which is feasible. For a low-risk case, Decision can follow the final Assessment directly. Any clarification loop still has to respect the formal rule that Request More Info is followed by a new Document Check and a new Assessment; D2 cannot bypass that structure.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, D2 starts near Monday night, completes by early Tuesday, and A2 assesses by Tuesday afternoon, saving roughly 12-18 h with the same approval outcome. Base case, D2 is earlier but slower, so the case is only 6-10 h faster. Worst case, ambiguous handwriting triggers an assessment-led rework loop, adding roughly 8-48 h and partially or fully reversing the speed gain.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If measured clarification and downstream correction rates for handwritten cases are nearly identical between D1 and D2, then the earlier D2 start is attractive. If D2's clarification rate is much higher than assumed, or if D2's queue inflates quickly once the policy is scaled, D1 remains the better default despite the slower initial start.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: The dominant factual delay was the second external rework loop, not internal service time. After Document Check 2 and Assessment 2, one discrepancy remained. Under the factual policy, A1 asked for one more confirmation, and because that request landed on Friday morning, the case absorbed a weekend delay before it could return through Document Check 3, Assessment 3, Compliance, and Decision.

Minimal intervention: The intervention is narrow in process placement but broad in consequence: after Assessment 2, no second Request More Info is allowed. The case must move directly to final compliance and then to a decision using the evidence already on file, with either rejection or an explicit conditional approval rather than a silent overlook.

Temporal propagation: Removing the second rework loop deletes the external wait, the third D1 review, the third A1 assessment, and the extra wait for C1 after Tuesday's factual Assessment 3. That is why the cycle-time gain can be measured in days rather than hours. But the saved time is not a pure efficiency gain; it is created by stopping evidence collection earlier, so the decision frontier itself moves.

Process-feasibility check: The counterfactual remains process-feasible if Compliance Review occurs after Assessment 2 and before Decision, and if compliance is not allowed to reopen evidence gathering under another label. Four-eyes still holds because A1 remains the final assessor and C1 remains the final compliance reviewer. What changes is not sequencing but the set of admissible decisions under unresolved evidence.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, the remaining discrepancy is immaterial, compliance supports a tightly conditioned approval on Friday or Monday, and the front-office cycle shortens by roughly 4-5 days. Base case, compliance or management rejects, or approves with stronger conditions, saving about 2.5-4 days but with a harsher decision. Worst case, the earlier decision triggers appeal, override, or later remediation, so apparent cycle-time gains are offset by poorer end-to-end resolution quality.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If historical data showed that second rework loops almost never change the final decision or only confirm what was already evident after Assessment 2, this intervention would look safer. If those loops frequently prevent bad approvals or salvage approvals that would otherwise be false rejects, then forcing earlier decisions is substantially more dangerous than the elapsed-time reduction suggests.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: The main issue was a system-induced handoff, not a missing prerequisite. Because originals were not required, Identity and Document checks were legally parallelizable, yet the legacy lock created only the identity task at Triage. Document work therefore joined the queue only after Identity completed, and by then the case was still headed into a growing Assessment queue.

Minimal intervention: The least-invasive change is to create both verification tasks at Triage, allow controlled parallel ownership by I2 and D2, and require real-time timestamps. No routing rule, decision rule, or evidentiary standard changes.

Temporal propagation: Earlier task creation moves the case's document-queue entry point forward. That can create some real overlap between Identity and Document work, but the overlap is constrained by D2's existing backlog, so the gain is probably measured in hours rather than half-days. Even if both checks finish earlier, the case then enters a congested assessment queue, so earlier readiness does not necessarily produce a much earlier Decision.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible because no originals are required, so concurrent verification is allowed. There is no compliance stage for this low-risk, low-value case. The critical control issue is evidentiary: timestamps must prove actual parallel execution rather than retrospective batch entry, otherwise the process log will overstate concurrency and understate queueing.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, D2 starts materially before Identity ends, the case becomes assessment-ready by early Tuesday evening, and Assessment is pulled slightly earlier on Wednesday, saving perhaps 4-6 h. Base case, actual overlap is small and Assessment remains the bottleneck, so the end-to-end gain is only 1-3 h. Worst case, the workaround introduces reconciliation overhead or poor logging, yielding little real speedup and an audit problem.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If event data showed that D2 could usually start several hours earlier than in the factual path and that A2 often has same-day slack for earlier-ready cases, this intervention would look better than my base view. If controls show frequent timestamp backfilling or manual rework caused by the workaround, then even the modest expected benefit is overstated.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis
| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation plus provisional C1 reservation | Adds early S1 review and shifts some C1 effort earlier; total C1 time is flat to slightly higher if reserved prep is wasted. | Post-assessment waiting for targeted high-risk cases. | C1 queue for other urgent cases; possibly S1 inbox. | Other high-risk/high-value cases needing C1 in the same window. | Mixed to negative beyond narrow targeting. | A single-server reserved-capacity system becomes fragmented; incomplete packets can consume scarce time with little payoff. |
| K-219 D2-on-arrival routing | Reduces D1 demand and increases D2 demand; may add extra D2/A2 work if handwriting causes rework. | D1 queue. | D2 queue; downstream Assessment queue if more cases become ready earlier. | Standard D2 cases and hard handwritten cases formerly protected by D1 specialization. | Mixed. | A small increase in rework probability can outweigh the benefit of earlier queue entry. |
| K-337 suppress second rework loop | Removes third-pass D1 and A1 work plus external waiting; pulls C1/M1 decision work earlier; may create off-path disputes and remediation. | D1 and A1 rework queues. | Exception, appeal, override, or post-decision remediation workloads; short-term C1 queue may also rise as cases surface earlier. | Similar incomplete-evidence cases and downstream resolution teams. | Ambiguous: throughput improves, risk may worsen. | Weekend avoidance creates large time savings, but rare bad decisions can have fat-tailed downstream cost. |
| K-411 parallel verification with real-time logging | Same total I/D service hours, but earlier queue entry and more overlap; adds logging/control overhead. | Identity-to-document handoff delay. | Assessment queue; possibly D2 queue if many cases join earlier. | Other cases awaiting A1/A2. | Small positive locally, mixed globally. | Logged concurrency can rise without real throughput gain if timestamps are backfilled. |

When each intervention is rolled out to 100 similar cases in one week, bottlenecks migrate. K-104 is the clearest example of a local counterfactual that does not scale cleanly. Reserving provisional C1 slots does not create compliance capacity; it converts a single scarce reviewer into a reserved-capacity system. A few targeted cases may finish earlier, but other urgent cases are displaced, and incomplete packets create stranded reservation time. Unless release rules are very aggressive, the net system effect is likely negative.

K-219 is a substitution policy, not a pure acceleration policy. Moving handwritten cases from D1 to D2 frees D1, but D2 is slower on this edge case and more likely to generate clarification. Across 100 cases, even a modest rework-rate increase can swamp the benefit of earlier starts. The bottleneck may simply migrate from D1 to D2 and then into Assessment as more cases become ready sooner.

K-337 removes the most elapsed time because it suppresses a loop that interacts badly with slow suppliers and weekends. At scale, that will reduce D1/A1 rework load materially. But the saved time is partly purchased by changing the decision standard. Throughput improves on the visible workflow while risk may reappear as appeals, overrides, tighter conditional approvals, or loss events outside the immediate path.

K-411 is the cleanest operational fix, but it mainly advances readiness into an already busy Assessment queue. On a 100-case rollout, the most likely result is that verification handoff delay shrinks while A1/A2 becomes the dominant bottleneck. Real-time timestamping matters because without it the organization may mistake better logging for true concurrency.

## 6. Pilot designs
| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411: low-risk, low-value, no-originals, standard digital cases that would otherwise hit the legacy case lock. | K-219: low-risk, low-value, originals-required, handwritten-but-standard cases arriving Monday-Thursday before close of business. |
| assignment rule | Randomize eligible cases at Triage by stable case-ID parity to controlled parallel-task creation versus legacy task creation. | Randomize at Originals Receipt to D2-first routing versus usual D1 routing, with a cap on treated share to avoid saturating D2. |
| sample/support logic | Run for at least two full backlog cycles so Tuesday and Wednesday queues are observed; include an audit observer in week 1. | Run until both first-pass completion rate and rework rate are estimated with useful precision; maintain D1 fallback for clearly illegible samples. |
| primary outcome | Triage-to-Notify cycle time and Triage-to-Assessment-ready time. | Originals Receipt-to-Decision cycle time and first-pass completion rate. |
| process-path outcome | Share of cases with true overlap between Identity and Document checks; delay from second verification completion to Assessment start. | Share of cases requiring formal rework after first Assessment; path mix of one-pass versus rework cases. |
| quality outcome | Audit exceptions, timestamp discrepancies, and any downstream correction of decisions. | Clarification/rework rate, downstream correction rate, and any shift in approval versus rejection for similar fact patterns. |
| queue-spillover outcome | Change in A1/A2 queue wait for non-pilot cases; any D2 queue inflation caused by earlier task creation. | Change in D1 and D2 queue waits plus any increase in A2 wait for non-pilot cases. |
| logging validation | Compare application timestamps with workstation or event-bus logs; discard cases with batch-entered timestamps from the causal estimate. | Validate D2 start/finish times against actual work logs and confirm that any rework is formally issued after Assessment, not informally hidden. |
| stopping rule | Stop if audit exceptions rise above a preset threshold or if A-queue wait worsens materially without a compensating cycle-time gain. | Stop if rework rate or D2 queue inflation exceeds preset bounds, or if median cycle-time gain is negligible after the planned sample. |

## 7. Final synthesis
K-411 appears most robust. Its expected gain is not dramatic, but it is a clean operational intervention: it changes task creation timing and timestamp discipline without changing evidentiary standards, compliance requirements, or the decision rule. That makes the downside limited and the learning value high. Even if the measured speedup is small, the pilot still reveals whether the true constraint is the legacy lock or the Assessment queue.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its speed. It can remove several elapsed days, especially when a second rework loop crosses a weekend, but those days are not saved by better flow alone. They are saved by deciding with unresolved evidence in a high-risk, high-value case. That shifts risk into false rejects, weakly defensible conditional approvals, appeals, overrides, and potential loss events.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104. In a single case, early escalation plus a provisional C1 reservation looks attractive because the case can avoid some post-assessment waiting and C1 can pre-read. At policy scale, that logic breaks: the same scarce reviewer cannot give everyone priority. The local improvement is therefore mostly queue displacement unless reservation release rules are extremely disciplined.

The most valuable additional data are queue-entry and queue-exit timestamps by resource, actual C1 reservation hold/cancel behavior, assessor and compliance calendars, timestamp provenance, and rework causes by document type and reviewer. For K-219 and K-411, the key missing fact is whether earlier readiness really converts into earlier Assessment. For K-337, the decisive missing outcomes are appeals, post-decision corrections, loss outcomes, and compliance exceptions.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. [structural] A provisional C1 reservation in K-104 does not waive the rule that final compliance must occur after the latest relevant Assessment.
2. [temporal] The queue snapshots are treated as approximate wait forecasts for nearby arrivals, with possible several-hour deviations in either direction.
3. [resource] C1 is the only effective compliance server in this operating window, so reservation effects cannot be absorbed by hidden backup capacity.
4. [behavioral] Early S1/C1 review in K-104 can shorten the later final compliance effort by roughly 1-3 h when the packet is informative enough.
5. [quality] D2 has materially lower first-pass reliability than D1 on handwritten originals, even though the underlying case is otherwise standard.
6. [behavioral] In K-337, once the second rework loop is prohibited, compliance and management will choose explicit rejection or explicit conditional approval rather than silently ignoring the discrepancy.
7. [interference] Earlier readiness in K-219 and K-411 spills primarily into Assessment queues rather than being fully absorbed by spare assessor capacity.
8. [interference] Logged overlap is not accepted as true concurrency unless supported by real-time event evidence rather than later batch entry.
