## 1. What is observed
Automation availability is a branch-month indicator: Alpha and Beta switch from `automation=0` to `1` in 2026-04, Gamma switches in 2026-07, and Delta/Epsilon remain `0` through 2026-08. Actual use is distinct from availability: `automated_fast_path_pct` sits at ~4–6% even pre-rollout, then jumps to ~25–27% at go-live and rises further (to ~48–50% by Aug in Alpha/Beta and to 32% in Gamma), consistent with gradual learning and implementation fidelity rather than a binary change.

Rollout was targeted and nonrandom: Alpha and Beta were selected because of high volume and poor historical cycle times, so treated branches differ systematically from non-adopters. Multiple operational changes co-occur in the same calendar window: a centralized fraud desk began in March; a routing campaign from April increased arrivals to Alpha/Beta (~10%) and increased Gamma’s arrivals after July (~5%); staffing changed (Alpha −2 FTE in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma −1 in June, Delta +1 in June); and a May product change increased `high_complexity_pct` in several branches. Managers could also adapt local practices during rollout.

Outcomes are branch-month aggregates (median and p90 cycle time, rework/escalation percentages, and a CSAT mean). Case-level paths, specialist-queue and fraud-desk waiting times, and CSAT response counts are unobserved, limiting precision and leaving open composition and queueing explanations.

## 3. Identification strategy
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: staggered-adoption event-study / stacked DiD (group-time ATT) with explicit dynamics** | Adoption month per branch; relative-time indicators; separate early vs later post | Not-yet-treated and never-treated branches (Delta/Epsilon; and Gamma pre-2026-07 when comparing Alpha/Beta) | Calendar month effects; baseline levels/trends; `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`; sensitivity excluding May–Jun | E1–E4, E8 (availability and dynamic effects) | Conditional parallel trends for treated vs controls; no spillovers (or spillovers are measured/adjusted); stable outcome definitions; no anticipation affecting pre-period | Pre-trend/event-time placebo coefficients; compare covariate trends; leave-one-branch-out; placebo “adoption” dates for controls; check for breaks in March/May | Time-varying confounding coincident with adoption (routing, staffing, product change) and interference via shared fraud desk/queues |
| **Alternative: augmented synthetic control / synthetic DiD per adopter** | Treat each adopter as its own intervention time series | Donor pool = non-adopters (Delta/Epsilon) and/or pre-adoption segments of others | Match on pre-period outcome levels and slopes; optionally on `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | E1–E2 (branch-specific ITT) | Donor pool approximates counterfactual trajectory; no unmeasured shocks differentially hitting treated at adoption | Pre-fit error; placebo in-time and in-space; robustness to donor reweighting | Donor pool is tiny (2 never-treated branches) and pre-period is short, making fits unstable |
| **Alternative: interrupted time series (segmented regression) within branch, focused on Gamma** | Single change-point at 2026-07 for Gamma | Gamma’s own pre-period (optionally with DE as covariate controls) | `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, calendar month indicators | E1–E2 for Gamma | No other concurrent shock at the change-point; model correctly captures secular trend | Check residual autocorrelation; placebo breakpoints; compare to DE trend shift at same time | Confounding by unmeasured July operational changes and learning dynamics mis-modeled as a step change |
| **Alternative: pre-trend matched DiD (level + slope matching)** | Pre-period matching then DiD at adoption | Match Alpha/Beta to Delta/Epsilon on Jan–Mar outcomes and covariates | Pre-period outcome mean and slope; `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | E1–E4 | Matched controls share counterfactual trend; matching variables capture selection drivers | Balance checks; sensitivity to matching set | With only 5 branches, matching is fragile and may mask rather than solve confounding |

The primary staggered-adoption/event-study approach is preferable because it explicitly uses calendar time to absorb shared shocks (e.g., fraud desk in March and a May product change) while leveraging the stagger (Alpha/Beta in April, Gamma in July) to separate “post” effects from general 2026 trends. It also lets us inspect dynamics (month 0–1 vs month 3–4) that are central here because use intensity increases over time and early rollout months are contaminated by learning. Compared with a single pre/post contrast, it provides visible diagnostics (pre-trend/placebo coefficients) and can be paired with sensitivity checks (exclude May–Jun; reweight controls) to see how much inference relies on specific months. Synthetic control is attractive for transparency but is severely constrained by the very small donor pool and short pre-period. Pure interrupted time series ignores cross-branch information and is vulnerable to any coincident operational changes at the breakpoint. None of these strategies fully solves interference through the shared fraud desk; that limitation should be treated as a first-order uncertainty driver rather than a footnote.

**Arithmetic reading (not causal):** controls (DE) see median and p90 worsen markedly after May, alongside rising complexity and rework. Treated branches also face higher demand and rising complexity, yet their median changes are smaller and (for Gamma, and later Alpha) improve.

**3) Treatment-intensity (dose) view (descriptive):**
- Within adopters, intensity ramps strongly; escalation rises with adoption and remains elevated. A descriptive fixed-effects regression (branch + month effects, clustered by branch) with intensity plus `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` yields roughly **−0.66 hours in median_cycle_h per +10 pp** fast-path intensity (very uncertain with only 5 branches) and roughly **+0.12 pp in escalation_pct per +10 pp** intensity.
- Simple correlations are unstable: within-branch, escalation is strongly positively correlated with intensity, while tail/median associations differ by branch (e.g., Gamma improves while Alpha/Beta tails worsen).

**4) Pre-trends and regression to the mean:** Alpha and Beta were selected for historically poor performance, so regression to the mean is a serious concern. In the observed pre-period (Jan–Mar), medians are not steadily improving (Alpha oscillates ~47–50h; Beta ~45–46h), which weakens—but does not eliminate—the “already improving” story. Calendar shocks are visible: escalation rises in March across branches (fraud desk start), and cycle times/rework worsen broadly after May (product change and complexity shift), suggesting strong common time effects that any design must absorb.

**5) Sensitivity / bounded conclusions (scenario intervals):**
- **Median cycle time (availability effect):** If parallel trends held and spillovers are negligible, DiD suggests ~4–5h median improvement for adopters relative to controls. If half of that is instead driven by selection/regression-to-mean or differential routing/case mix not captured by `high_complexity_pct`, a conservative scenario interval is **0 to −5 hours** on `median_cycle_h` in the first 4 months post-adoption.
- **CSAT:** DiD shifts are ~+0.08 to +0.10, but with unknown response counts and response-rate variation (20–45%), plausible nonresponse/composition shifts could easily dominate such small changes. A conservative interval is **−0.05 to +0.15** on `csat_1to5`, i.e., “direction unclear.”

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences
| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta chosen for poor history; Gamma after readiness | Often biases toward finding improvement (regression to mean), but could also bias against if rollout occurs during strain | Compare multiple pre-periods; use placebo breakpoints; incorporate historical (pre-2026) performance if available | A single “X% reduction” could be badly wrong when generalized |
| Nonparallel trends | Branches may have different secular trends unrelated to automation | Either direction; invalidates DiD/event-study | Pre-trend/event-study coefficients; branch-specific trends sensitivity | Apparent effects could be artifacts of trend differences |
| Time-varying demand (routing campaign) | Arrivals increase to Alpha/Beta (and later Gamma) coincident with adoption | Likely biases against automation (more load worsens outcomes), but could bias toward if routed cases are easier | Capture routing rules; compare case attributes pre/post; instrument using exogenous routing if any | Operational decision based on biased estimates could overload queues |
| Staffing changes | FTE shifts coincide with rollout (Alpha−2, Beta+1, Gamma−1, Delta+1) | Confounds outcomes; direction depends on branch | Collect role-level staffing (triage vs specialist); include shift schedules | Misattributing staffing effects to automation leads to wrong capacity planning |
| Case-mix changes (product change) | `high_complexity_pct` increases after May across branches | Likely biases against automation if complexity increases in treated; measurement drift could bias either way | Validate risk scoring stability; use additional severity features | Incorrectly blaming/crediting automation for product-driven changes |
| Treatment intensity / partial compliance | Availability ≠ use; intensity is endogenous and ramps with learning | Per-protocol estimates can be biased (better teams use more; or use more when overloaded) | Track eligibility and randomize encouragement; audit usage logs | Misleading dose-response claims and poor rollout guidance |
| Treatment-version heterogeneity | “Automation” may differ by configuration, overrides, local practice | Can create heterogeneous effects that invalidate a single average | Log model version, thresholds, override rates; stratify analyses | Expansion may fail if new branches implement a different “version” |
| Spillovers via shared fraud desk (interference) | Fraud desk serves all branches; automation sends more borderline cases | If controls are harmed by spillover, treated vs control contrast overstates benefit | Measure fraud-desk queue times, backlog, and inflow by branch | Wrong conclusion about branch-level benefit; hidden systemwide harm |
| Specialist-queue interference | Escalations increase; specialist capacity shared within branch/region | Could shift delays to tail (p90) and to other case types | Collect specialist WIP/queue times by branch; monitor SLA breaches | Customer harm via long-tail delays despite stable medians |

| Outcome aggregation (ecological bias) | Medians/p90s are nonlinear summaries; not additive across cases | Can mask subgroup harm (tail) even if median improves | Obtain case-level durations or at least quantile sketches; analyze distributional changes | Leadership may optimize median while harming worst-case customers |
| Survey nonresponse/selection | CSAT response rates vary; counts unknown | Any observed CSAT change may be dominated by composition | Collect response counts/weights; compare respondent mix | Overconfident claims about customer outcomes |
| Measurement changes in rework/escalation | Coding practices may change with new system | Bias can mimic improvement/deterioration | Audit definitions; cross-system reconciliation | Wrong quality conclusions and mis-specified guardrails |
| Small number of branches | Only 5 clusters; inference and diagnostics are fragile | Overconfident SEs/p-values; model instability | Use randomization inference where possible; report sensitivity ranges | Decisions based on “significance” are unreliable |
| Dynamic treatment effects | Learning means effects change over months | Collapsing to one average misleads | Estimate early vs late effects; minimum post-period before decision | Premature scale-up (or premature abandonment) |

## 8. Rollout recommendation
expand with an embedded evaluation

Expand next to **both Delta and Epsilon**, but do so with a design that creates credible counterfactuals and protects customers. First, **randomize the phase-in order** (e.g., one branch starts in Week 1, the other in Week 7) to avoid purely managerial selection of timing. Second, during the first 8–10 weeks in each new branch, implement a **case-level randomized “automation offer” (encouragement) design** for clearly eligible low/medium-risk cases: the system produces an automated triage recommendation for all, but a randomized fraction is routed to follow the automated fast path unless an explicit override is logged. This preserves safety while generating exogenous variation in **treatment intensity** that is otherwise endogenous.

Minimum measurement: collect **at least 3 months pre** and **4 months post** per branch (weekly reporting is even better) to capture learning and tail effects. Collect process-mining outcomes at **case level**: timestamps for `Register`, triage type (manual/automated), `Specialist Review`, each rework loop, `Resolve`, `Close`; explicit indicators for fraud-desk referral; and reason codes/override flags. Collect queue-state data: specialist and fraud-desk **waiting times**, WIP/backlog, service rates, and staffing by role (triage vs specialist vs fraud) rather than only branch FTE. For CSAT, record **response counts** and responder mix to enable weighting and uncertainty quantification.

Monitor spillovers by tracking fraud-desk inflow and wait time by source branch and by automation status. Guardrails: pause or rollback if **p90_cycle_h** rises >15% vs baseline for two consecutive weeks, **rework_pct** rises >2 pp, or **CSAT** drops >0.1 (after adjusting for response volume), or if fraud-desk wait times breach a predefined SLA. Decision rule for scale-up: proceed to wider rollout only if the embedded evaluation shows **median improves by ≥3 hours without p90 degradation (>+2 hours)** and without net increases in rework, while specialist/fraud capacity remains within SLA.

# 3) Adopter-specific transparent pre/post contrasts
adopt = {"Alpha": "2026-04", "Beta": "2026-04", "Gamma": "2026-07"}  # from the prompt

def window_mean(d, et_list, cols):
    w = d[d["event_time"].isin(et_list)]
    return w[cols].mean(numeric_only=True)

cols = ["automated_fast_path_pct", "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct", "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5",

    print("\n=== Treatment-intensity model (median_cycle_h) ===")
    # Causal assumptions for a dose-response interpretation are stronger: intensity must be exogenous conditional on controls.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- Observed: After go-live, `automated_fast_path_pct` increased sharply in Alpha/Beta (to ~25–50%) and in Gamma (to ~25–32%), while Delta/Epsilon stayed near ~4%, and `escalation_pct` rose in all adopters.
- Suggestive: Relative to Delta/Epsilon, adopters show smaller median-cycle deterioration (and sometimes improvement) during the same months despite higher routed demand and rising complexity, but tail behavior differs by branch.
- Conditionally causal: If Delta/Epsilon provide valid counterfactual trends and fraud-desk/specialist spillovers are negligible, automation availability plausibly reduced `median_cycle_h` by roughly 0–5 hours (branch- and time-varying) while increasing `escalation_pct` by about ~1 percentage point.
- Not supported: A single universal statement like “automation reduces cycle time by X%” that is stable across branches, months, and distributional tails.
