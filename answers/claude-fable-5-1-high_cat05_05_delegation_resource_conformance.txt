## 2. Rule Interpretation

RR1 (formal): for each case c, human(Draft_Change_Request, c) ≠ human(Approve_Change, c), where human() is the alias-normalized, session-attributed identity. Applies to all risk levels; no exemption for LOW risk or emergencies.

RR2: for each case c, human(Implement_Change, c) ≠ human(Verify_Implementation, c). An unattributed service-account verification cannot demonstrate compliance; I treat it as "not evidenced" (nonconformant for assurance purposes) rather than as a proven violation.

RR3: for each Approve_Change in a HIGH, non-emergency case at time t: the actor must hold QM as a standing role at t, or hold a delegation D with delegate = actor, role = QM, D.start ≤ t ≤ D.end, case.scope ∈ D.scope, principal formally absent at t if D requires it, D not a subdelegation where the parent prohibits it, and D evidenced by a record that existed before t (see below). Standing roles are read from the role intervals at t, never from the current user master (Eva is not QM on any date; Diego is IC only from 2026-07-01).

RR4: for a HIGH emergency case, Approve_Change may be by IC (standing) or a valid IC delegate under the same delegation tests as RR3, provided the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible; additionally a QM or valid QM delegate must perform Retrospective_QM_Review no later than the deadline. Business-day counting: business days are Monday–Friday on the site calendar (no holidays in the window). The trigger is the emergency Approve_Change (the moment QM authority was bypassed; using Implement_Change gives the same result here). The deadline is 23:59:59 UTC of the second business day after the trigger day. Trigger Tuesday 08-11 → business day 1 = Wed 08-12, business day 2 = Thu 08-13 → deadline 08-13 23:59:59. A stricter "48 business hours" reading gives 08-13 13:00/14:20; the most lenient reading still ends on 08-13. CHG-510's review (08-12 10:00) is timely under all readings; CHG-505's review (Fri 08-14 16:00) is late under all readings. The retrospective reviewer is judged with the same event-time role/delegation test; a delegation for "QM approval of production-equipment changes" does not literally cover a *retrospective review* of a *cooling-system* change, so I treat Ben's two reviews as scope-disputed rather than clearly valid.

RR5: svc_qms events are attributed only through session evidence; a WEAK attribution never satisfies a rule but may raise a hypothesis; a STRONG attribution is used as the human. Aliases are normalized before any test.

RR6: pair frequency, reciprocity, shared workstations and handover concentration are computed and reported as indicators; none of them is converted into a finding of collusion without independent evidence (statements, timing anomalies, benefit, communications).

Action by a principal while on leave: leave is an availability status, not a role revocation. If Asha herself performed CHG-504, it is authorized under RR3. Two consequences follow: (1) the identity burden rises, because leave is exactly the condition under which credential sharing is most likely and least detectable, so a leave-time approval needs corroboration (VPN source, MFA, device); (2) while she is demonstrably active (09:15–09:40 on 08-13) the "Asha absent" condition of D-QM-01 is not met, so a delegate approval inside that window would be conditionally invalid — none occurred; by 13:00 she was absent again, so Ben's delegation condition is satisfied for CHG-506.

Later database entry versus earlier delegation: a record created on 08-12 09:15 proves only that the delegation existed in the QMS from that moment. It cannot by itself prove authority on 08-11. Earlier authority can be established only by independent contemporaneous evidence: a cryptographically verifiable signature and trusted timestamp on the 08-09 PDF, notification to Ben before 08-10, or the migration source-system log showing the original creation date. If the PDF's "timestamp" is just document metadata or a typed date, CHG-501's approval rests on weak evidence (an evidential/data-quality gap, not a demonstrated authority failure). CHG-503 (08-12 11:30) post-dates the record and is not affected.

Verifier role after 08-12: Eva's Independent Verifier role ended 08-11 23:59; from 08-12 she verified as a Quality Engineer (CHG-502, 504, 506, 508 and, via svc_qms, 509). RR1–RR6 do not prescribe a verifier role, so these verifications are technically conformant; the control objective (verification by a designated independent verifier) is weakened and this is reported as an observation, not a violation.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Repeated pair Clara → Ben (18 of 23 HIGH production changes in 60 days; Asha only 3). Benign: same production cell, Ben is the designated QM delegate whenever Asha is away, no rotation policy exists, so concentration is structural. Adverse: a habitual rubber-stamp pair in which Ben approves outside any valid delegation and Asha has effectively ceded HIGH-risk authority. Discriminating evidence: the delegation register for the 60 days (were there valid QM delegations covering each of the 18 approvals? D-QM-01 covers only five days); draft-to-approve latency distribution (minutes vs. hours), rejection/return-for-rework rate for Clara's drafts versus other drafters, quality of approval comments, whether Asha was actually absent on those dates. This is the single most important systemic question in the file, because if most of the 18 lacked a delegation, RR3 has been failing routinely, not just in CHG-507.

Reciprocal pattern Ben drafts → Clara approves (4 LOW training changes). Benign: natural division of labour on low-risk documentation; no financial benefit known. Adverse: mutual accommodation that lowers scrutiny in both directions and, combined with CHG-508, a willingness to route around SoD. Discriminating evidence: approval latency and comment quality on those four, whether any were later found deficient, whether Clara holds any approval role for training documents, and whether the pair also appears together outside change control.

Shared workstation / service account (CHG-503 on WS-DM-04). Benign: a legitimate verifier used the nearest shared terminal already logged in as svc_qms — poor practice, but not self-verification. Adverse: Diego verified his own implementation, using the shared account to avoid leaving his name. Discriminating evidence: badge in/out for all persons in the room 12:00–12:45, svc_qms session start time and originating login, other svc_qms usage by Diego's station, QMS audit-trail metadata (e.g., client hostname, keyboard-language, form fill duration).

Absence conflicts. Asha (VPN during leave, CHG-504): benign — conscientious principal handling one urgent approval; adverse — shared credentials used by someone on site. Discriminate with VPN source location, MFA device, and who asked for the approval. Ben (account used at 08-13 13:00 and again at 08-14 16:00 during certified sick leave, from an office IP for the first): benign — he stayed a while after the leave nominally started or worked remotely/came in; adverse — a colleague used his session or password; Clara (drafter) and Diego (implementer) are the people who benefited from the 13:00 approval, and the 08-14 review closed an overdue RR4 obligation. Discriminate with badge-out time, workstation identity, fresh-login versus existing-session evidence, and Ben's statement about the 08-14 event (he has only been asked about 08-13).

Credential compromise as a general hypothesis. The cluster of 08-13 (Asha VPN 09:15–09:40, then Ben's account at 13:00 from an office machine) coincides with the day both QM-authority holders were unavailable. Benign reading: an under-resourced approval chain improvising under pressure. Adverse reading: a small group that knows how to obtain HIGH-risk approvals regardless of who is present. Discriminating evidence: authentication logs (MFA challenges, device IDs) for u_as and u_bk on 08-13/14, and whether the changes approved that day were later found technically justified.

Emergency credibility (RR4). Both emergency cases are cooling-system changes on 08-11 during the window of CI-44; that is coherent, but the log does not itself tie CHG-505/510 to CI-44, and CHG-510 also violates RR1. Benign: two urgent fixes in a live incident, with an independent verification and a next-day QM review. Adverse: the emergency flag as a route around QM approval. Discriminate with the incident record, deviation/safety assessment, and whether the retrospective reviews contain substantive challenge.

Handover network (this log): the dominant edges are Diego → Eva (implement → verify), Clara → Eva and Clara → Ben; a Clara → Clara self-edge (502, 510) and a Ben → Ben self-edge (508) appear once aliases are normalized. Working-together shows Clara–Eva and Clara–Diego as the most frequent pairs simply because Clara drafts and Diego implements nearly everything. None of this is evidence of collusion; it is evidence that the process has almost no resource diversity, so any single compromised or complicit actor has outsized effect.

## 6. Control Improvements

Delegation creation: delegations must be created in the validated QMS before their effective start, by the principal with an electronic signature, and must state role, activities covered (approval, retrospective review), scope categories using the same scope vocabulary as the change record (production_equipment, cooling_system, training_document), the absence condition, and an explicit subdelegation prohibition; the system should refuse an approval whose (role, scope, activity, time) tuple is not covered by an active delegation. Farid's email subdelegation should be technically impossible, not merely forbidden.

Effective-time recording: store both "delegation effective from" and "record created at" with an immutable audit trail; migrations must carry the source-system creation timestamp, and any approval that predates the record's existence should be auto-flagged for retrospective evidence review (this would have surfaced CHG-501).

Principal availability: link delegation activation to the HR absence register and to on-call rosters, so that "principal absent" is a recorded fact for weekdays and weekends alike; if a principal acts during recorded absence, require step-up authentication and a documented reason; if a delegate becomes unavailable (Ben's sick leave), automatically escalate to a named second delegate rather than leaving a HIGH-risk approval with no available authority.

Alias management: one human, one identity in the QMS; legacy IDs (b.koch) must be merged or disabled, and SoD checks must operate on the person identifier, not the login string. Periodically reconcile QMS accounts against HR records.

Individual authentication: retire shared/service accounts for GxP-relevant human actions; if svc_qms must exist, bind each action to a human via mandatory individual re-authentication (FIDO/badge) at the action, and enforce short screen-lock timeouts on shared workstations such as WS-DM-04. Deny approvals from sessions that show impossible presence (badge-out while account active; sick-leave status while approving from an office IP).

Emergency review: make the two-business-day Retrospective_QM_Review a system-tracked obligation with a due date computed from the emergency approval, escalation to the QM principal and Site Director on breach, mandatory linkage to the incident ID (CI-44), and a requirement that the reviewer hold QM authority for the *review* activity in the case's scope; the emergency flag should require a recorded safety rationale at approval time.

Pair rotation and approval diversity: define a rotation or a random second-reviewer sampling for HIGH-risk changes so that no drafter/approver pair exceeds a set share (for example, 50 percent of HIGH changes in a rolling quarter); require the QM principal to personally approve a minimum proportion; prohibit the same person approving and verifying a HIGH change; define who may verify (designated verifiers only).

Network monitoring: run the four-eyes, event-time authority and handover/working-together analyses monthly on alias-normalized data; trend pair concentration, self-loops, weekend and out-of-hours approvals, approvals during recorded absence, and service-account usage; treat threshold breaches as triggers for evidence review, not as findings.

Evidential retention: retain VPN, MFA, badge, workstation login and QMS audit-trail data for at least the record-retention period of the change record, time-synchronised to UTC, and make CCTV coverage or equivalent presence evidence available for shared GxP terminals; preserve signed delegation documents with verifiable timestamps.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Of the ten cases, four contain a genuine authority failure that no additional evidence can cure: CHG-507 (HIGH approval by someone with no role or delegation), CHG-510 (self-approval plus reliance on a void subdelegation), and the two SoD self-approvals CHG-502 and CHG-508 (the latter hidden behind an alias). CHG-505 adds a demonstrable RR4 failure (late retrospective review by a reviewer of disputed scope). Roughly half of the apparent nonconformance is therefore real authority or SoD failure, most of it low in sophistication: people approving what they should not, often under time pressure and with the principal QM structurally unavailable.

A second, comparable share is identity and data quality: an unattributable service-account verification of a HIGH-risk production change (CHG-503), a delegation whose system record post-dates its first use (CHG-501), a delegate account exercised twice during certified sick leave with a denial from its owner (CHG-506, and the unremarked 08-14 review in CHG-505), a principal approval from leave whose identity rests on self-attestation (CHG-504), and a Saturday delegation whose absence condition is unevidenced (CHG-509). These cases may be innocent, but the organisation currently cannot prove it, which in a GxP setting is itself the finding.

The third component is organizational-pattern risk: the Clara → Ben pair carrying 18 of 23 HIGH production approvals in 60 days, a reciprocal LOW-risk pattern, one implementer for every change, two weekend HIGH changes approved by the same non-QM, and the fact that the only QM delegation in evidence covers five days. This pattern is not proof of collusion, but it means the process has almost no resource redundancy and that RR3 may have been failing for months, not days. Uncertainty is highest on who actually operated u_bk on 08-13/14, who used svc_qms on WS-DM-04, and whether Asha's leave-time session was hers; it is lowest on CHG-502, 507, 508 and 510, where the facts are unambiguous.

Three highest-priority investigations: (1) the 60-day Clara → Ben approval history against the delegation register, to establish whether Ben's HIGH-risk approvals outside 08-10..08-14 had any valid authority; (2) the u_bk account activity on 08-13 13:00 and 08-14 16:00 (workstation, badge, session origin, Ben's statement on both events), since a compromised delegate credential would convert CHG-506 and the CHG-505 review from data problems into unauthorized acts and would indicate who benefited; (3) the CHG-503 svc_qms verification (room badge records, session origin, svc_qms credential holders), because an unverified HIGH-risk production change, possibly self-verified by its implementer, is the finding with the most direct product-quality consequence.
