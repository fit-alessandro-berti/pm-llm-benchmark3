# 1. Current-process diagnosis

### Performance problems

- The dominant route—`Intake -> Auto screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`—is genuinely fast: the first three transitions have medians of 0.5, 0.4, and 1.8 hours. It is therefore attractive for throughput and immediate cost.
- The alternatives contain severe waiting time. `Request missing evidence -> Manual review` has a median of 26 hours and p90 of 88; `Manual review -> Repair quote` reaches a p90 of 60 hours; `Repair quote -> Customer approval` reaches 80 hours. These tails explain why unclear/missing-evidence cases have a 140-hour median.
- Manual-review capacity is fragile: seven reviewers provide 252 productive hours per week, and queues rise sharply above 82% utilization. A rule update that diverts 15–35% more work there could turn a compliance correction into a cycle-time failure.
- Fraud/risk review is another constrained queue—only two specialists, with Monday and campaign-related peaks. It should not become a universal gate.
- Part-price confirmation, rather than technical work alone, is delaying repair quotes. Adding technicians without changing that dependency would have limited effect.

### Quality and rework problems

- The fast path externalizes work downstream. Complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of fast-approval cases versus 3.3% on the quote–approval–repair path. Its apparent 31% pre-rework cost advantage falls to 9% after complaints, reshipments, and service credits.
- The frequency DFG shows substantial downstream failure around replacement: 260 observed `Dispatch replacement -> Complaint/reopen` transitions, with mean 96 hours and p90 180 hours. Temporal profiling links this long tail to incorrect part selection.
- Wrong replacements are especially consequential for high-value devices: expected error cost is EUR 410 versus EUR 95 for accessories.
- Repair is not uniformly safe either. Rework repair reaches 12.5% for repaired high-value devices, so “send high-value claims to repair” is not by itself a complete quality redesign.
- Evidence collection is recursive: 26% of cases entering missing-evidence handling do so at least twice. The DFG also records 320 transitions from missing-evidence request to abandonment.

### Compliance problems

- Token replay fitness of 0.91 conceals that only 68% of traces fit. Thus aggregate fitness should not be interpreted as acceptable control.
- Alignments identify three important deviations: fast approval before evidence completeness, complaints/reopens after close, and high-value replacement dispatch without risk review.
- Those deviations directly threaten R1 and R2. Above-EUR-400 claims need manual or fraud/risk review before fast approval, and dispatch normally requires confirmed evidence completeness.
- R3 is also vulnerable because reopened claims can traverse escalation or operational resolution without a reliably enforced manual-review checkpoint before final close or denial.
- Low discovered-model precision indicates that infrequent combinations of auto approval, risk review, and repair are operationally possible even when policy forbids them. Training alone will not close this control gap; execution constraints are needed.

### Customer-experience problems

- Segment averages hide opposite needs. Premium low-risk accessories gain 38 median hours from the fast path—8 versus 46—and face service credits after 24 hours; their complaint trade-off is relatively modest, 6% versus 3%.
- Premium high-value devices show the opposite profile: 20 hours fast versus 96 hours reviewed/repair, but complaints rise from 5% to 22%, alongside high R1/R2 exposure.
- Standard accessory customers tolerate up to 72 hours when status visibility is good, creating room for a modest quality gate that would be unacceptable if indiscriminately imposed on Premium accessories.
- The one daily escalation-board slot has a strong satisfaction effect but cannot absorb routine complaints.
- Digital evidence collection may reduce repeated requests by 30–45%, but an exclusively digital route could increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy.

### The central contradiction

The most frequent path is not simply “good” or “bad.” It is fast, initially cheap, and appropriate for some Premium low-risk accessories. Yet its low apparent cost partly comes from deferring defects to complaints, reshipments, credits, and compliance exceptions. Conversely, the slower reviewed path has better complaint outcomes but imposes large queues and is not justified for every EUR 95 accessory risk.

The redesign decision should therefore not replace fast approval wholesale. It should separate **low-risk claims where speed is valuable** from **high-value, uncertain, or evidence-incomplete claims where downstream failure dominates**, while preventing the latter from leaking into the fast route.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-segmented lanes with hard compliance routing

### Redesigned process flow

1. At intake, classify by customer segment, claim value, product risk, defect type, evidence state, and basic fraud indicators.
2. Route into three executable lanes:
   - **Express lane:** Premium low-risk accessories with complete evidence and confident part identification: auto eligibility  fast approval  dispatch  close.
   - **Assured replacement lane:** Standard low-risk accessories and clear mid-risk devices: auto eligibility  evidence-completeness confirmation  automated part match  lightweight pre-dispatch gate  dispatch  close.
   - **Controlled lane:** Claims above EUR 400, high-value devices, unclear defects, or fraud signals: mandatory manual or fraud/risk review  repair/replacement decision  fulfillment  quality/compliance close.
3. Make R1 and R2 system-enforced prerequisites rather than reviewer reminders. No dispatch token is issued unless the required evidence and review events exist.
4. Route every reopen first to manual eligibility review before resolution or denial, enforcing R3.
5. Reserve the escalation board for Premium severe-impact cases and repeated failures, not ordinary complaints.

### Target

- Fast approvals that currently include high-value or evidence-incomplete claims.
- Premium and Standard low-risk accessories that should not be burdened with full manual review.
- High-value replacement dispatches with high wrong-part cost.
- The nonconforming paths exposed by alignment diagnostics.

### Mechanism of improvement

This design preserves the speed advantage where its economics are credible while removing unsuitable claims from the dominant path. Segment-aware auto rules can be deployed in two weeks, and hard routing reduces the discovered model’s low precision by making prohibited combinations unavailable.

The 6–18-hour pre-dispatch gate is applied selectively. That delay is defensible for Standard accessories, whose customers tolerate 72 hours with visibility, and for higher-risk devices where an incorrect replacement costs EUR 410. Premium low-risk accessories retain the R2 exception and avoid the gate when evidence and part match are highly confident.

Capacity is protected by sending only policy-required or uncertain work to humans. Above-EUR-400 claims can use either manual or fraud review under R1; simple policy validation should go to manual review, while specialists handle genuine fraud signals.

### Deliberate sacrifice or unresolved issue

- Some high-value cases become slower, especially if rule changes raise manual inflow by 15–35%.
- This design controls evidence state but does not fundamentally simplify evidence acquisition.
- A pre-dispatch gate may erode the Premium promise if classification is inaccurate.
- Repair-quote and repair-rework problems remain largely intact.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first case completion with assisted channels

### Redesigned process flow

1. Replace repeated ad hoc requests with a defect-specific evidence package at intake: serial number, entitlement proof, guided photos/video, symptoms, and accessibility needs.
2. Validate fields in real time and display exactly what remains missing. Do not repeatedly send the case around `Request evidence -> Manual review`.
3. Offer three completion modes:
   - self-service portal;
   - agent-assisted upload or phone intake for low-digital-literacy customers;
   - scheduled callback or retail/service-desk capture for complex cases.
4. Hold the eligibility decision until the evidence package is either complete or explicitly waived under the Premium low-risk accessory exception.
5. Once complete, route clear low-risk claims to fast approval and uncertain/high-value claims to the required review.
6. Give customers visible status, deadline, and a single consolidated request; use reminders before classifying a claim as abandoned.
7. Begin technical triage and part-price lookup in parallel where the submitted evidence is already sufficient.

### Target

- The 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment.
- Cases cycling repeatedly through missing-evidence handling.
- The 320 observed transitions from evidence request to abandonment.
- Quote delays where technical and part-pricing work can start before all administrative waiting ends.
- Standard customers who tolerate longer completion if status is visible.

### Mechanism of improvement

The portal is expected to reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%, directly attacking the 26% repeat-request rate and the 26-hour median/88-hour p90 return from evidence request to manual review. Better structured evidence should also improve defect diagnosis and part selection, reducing replacement complaints without adding a quality gate to every case.

Assisted channels make this more than portal automation: cases are segmented by digital capability so the intervention does not simply trade cycle time for abandonment. One complete evidence object also gives R2 a reliable control point and reduces reviewer clarification work.

Parallel part-price lookup addresses the specific cause of repair-quote delay. It is more targeted than adding quote technicians when the bottleneck is an external confirmation wait.

### Deliberate sacrifice or unresolved issue

- Intake becomes more demanding, and low-value simple claims may experience unnecessary friction if forms are not defect-specific.
- Assisted channels retain labor cost and cannot produce the full savings of digital-only intake.
- Better evidence does not itself satisfy R1 for above-EUR-400 claims.
- Fraud review and manual-review capacity remain constraints after evidence completion.
- Customers may still abandon when the requested evidence is inherently difficult to obtain.

# 4. Redesign C: Rapid provisional service with delayed final settlement

### Redesigned process flow

1. At intake, assign a provisional-service tier rather than making every customer wait for a final claim decision.
2. For Premium customers and selected high-impact device failures:
   - immediately provide a loaner, advance exchange under card hold, or temporary accessory;
   - collect the defective item and evidence concurrently;
   - complete mandatory manual/fraud review before converting provisional service into final warranty settlement.
3. For Standard high-value devices:
   - arrange rapid pickup and diagnosis;
   - provide proactive milestones and a conditional replacement only after review and part confirmation.
4. Route recovered devices to a centralized diagnostic cell using available quality inspectors during non-campaign periods.
5. Final disposition becomes repair, confirmed replacement, chargeable repair/return, or denial. Close only after evidence, review, fulfillment, and quality/compliance checks are complete.
6. Reopens automatically reopen the same case record and enter manual review, rather than appearing as a new post-close complaint.

### Target

- Premium high-value devices, where fast replacement yields a 20-hour median but 22% complaint rate, while reviewed/repair service yields 5% complaints but a 96-hour median.
- Standard high-value claims where wrong replacements cost EUR 410.
- Customers whose experience depends on restored product access more than immediate administrative closure.
- Reopens occurring after nominal close.

### Mechanism of improvement

This design decouples **time to restored service** from **time to final compliance decision**. It avoids the false choice between a fast but error-prone final replacement and a four-day reviewed path with no customer relief. Mandatory review still occurs before final settlement, preserving R1/R2, while loaners or conditional exchanges protect Premium experience.

Central diagnosis can improve part and repair decisions. Underused quality inspectors provide an initial staffing option, though their skills and campaign availability must be validated. Keeping cases open until final settlement also addresses the alignment deviation “complaint/reopen after close.”

### Deliberate sacrifice or unresolved issue

- Inventory, reverse logistics, deposits, and asset-loss exposure increase.
- Administrative cycle time may not fall; the improvement is primarily time to restored service, quality, and compliance.
- This is unsuitable for routine low-risk accessories, where logistics could cost more than the avoided error.
- Customers may dislike card holds or provisional terms.
- The diagnostic cell could inherit the repair campaign peaks and does not eliminate high-value repair rework.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A

**Failure modes**

1. Segment or value classification is wrong, allowing high-value claims into express service or forcing benign accessories into review.
2. Updated rules increase manual inflow by 15–35%, pushing utilization beyond 82% and creating long queues.
3. The lightweight gate becomes a ceremonial checkbox and fails to catch incorrect part selection.
4. Fraud specialists receive ordinary eligibility work and amplify Monday/campaign congestion.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

- From complaints and reshipments to manual eligibility and the pre-dispatch quality gate. If the gate is staffed by existing inspectors, pressure may spike after repair campaigns.

### Redesign B

**Failure modes**

1. Portal friction raises abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, masking poor performance as fewer completed claims.
2. Customers submit technically valid but diagnostically useless photos or descriptions.
3. Consolidated evidence requirements become over-engineered for accessories, damaging Premium cycle time.
4. Parallel price lookup creates wasted work where the claim is later denied.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

- From repeated evidence requests to assisted-intake support and technical validation. Once evidence arrives faster, manual review, fraud review, or part-price confirmation may become the dominant queue.

### Redesign C

**Failure modes**

1. Loaners or exchange stock are unavailable, lost, damaged, or incompatible.
2. Card holds and provisional terms generate disputes or discourage customers.
3. Temporary service is issued quickly, but final reviews accumulate in the background, creating aged open cases and hidden compliance debt.
4. Central diagnosis does not reduce the 12.5% high-value repair rework rate because repair capability, not diagnosis, is the true cause.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

- From customer waiting before service to reverse logistics, diagnostic capacity, inventory replenishment, and final-settlement review.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Stage 0: Instrumentation and baseline

Before changing routing, add reliable events and attributes for:

- evidence completeness and waiver reason;
- claim value, segment, device risk, defect type, and digital-assistance need;
- part-match confidence and gate result;
- provisional versus final decision;
- complaint cause, reshipment, service credit, and wrong-part cost;
- reviewer queue time versus touch time;
- case-linked reopening after close.

Create segment-level baselines for median and p90 cycle time, time to restored service, complaint/reopen rate, repeated evidence requests, abandonment, R1–R3 violations, fitting-trace percentage, total cost through at least the complaint horizon, and manual/fraud utilization. Means alone are insufficient because several edges have large p90 tails.

### First pilot: narrow Redesign A routing controls

Pilot updated auto rules and hard R1/R2 prerequisites on a bounded product family:

- retain express routing for Premium low-risk accessories with complete evidence;
- route high-value devices to mandatory review;
- apply the pre-dispatch gate to Standard high-value replacements;
- do not initially change all products or campaign traffic.

This goes first because the rules can be deployed in two weeks and address the highest-cost and highest-compliance leakage. It also tests whether manual capacity can tolerate corrected routing before larger changes accelerate inflow.

**Go indicators**

- Material reduction in high-value dispatches without required review and in dispatch before evidence completion.
- Wrong-part complaints and high-value reopens decline directionally, with no later rebound over the full 180-hour complaint tail.
- Premium accessory median remains near its current fast-path range and does not regularly cross the 24-hour service-credit boundary.
- Manual utilization remains below or only briefly near the 82% queue inflection.
- Overall fitting-trace percentage improves, not merely aggregate fitness.

**Stop or revise indicators**

- Manual inflow approaches the upper 15–35% estimate and causes sustained utilization above 82%.
- The quality gate adds time near 18 hours without detecting enough errors to justify the delay.
- Cases are relabeled to evade controls, or high-value exceptions persist.
- Complaints fall before close but rise later, indicating delayed rather than eliminated failure.

### Stage 2: Redesign B evidence pilot

Pilot defect-specific evidence packages for unclear claims in one region, randomizing eligible customers among:

1. current evidence request;
2. portal-only flow;
3. portal plus assisted channel.

Track repeat requests, abandonment, evidence-to-review median/p90, reviewer touches, diagnostic sufficiency, approval/denial mix, complaints, and channel usage by age or digital-support proxy.

**Go indicators**

- Repeat requests fall within or toward the expected 30–45% range.
- The 26-hour median and 88-hour p90 evidence-return delay improve materially.
- Total abandonment does not rise meaningfully; if portal abandonment rises, assisted completion offsets it.
- Reviewer touches decline without worsening complaint, denial-appeal, or compliance rates.

**Stop or revise indicators**

- Completion improves only by weakening evidence standards.
- Portal users appear faster because difficult customers abandon.
- Premium simple claims are slowed by requirements designed for unclear defects.
- Faster evidence merely moves the queue into manual or fraud review without lowering end-to-end p90.

If successful, use the released reviewer capacity to absorb Redesign A’s controlled-lane inflow.

### Stage 3: Redesign C high-value service experiment

After routing and evidence controls stabilize, pilot provisional service for a small Premium high-value cohort. Compare:

- advance exchange under controlled terms;
- loaner plus diagnosis;
- improved reviewed path without provisional hardware.

Monitor time to restored service separately from final-close time, complaint/reopen rate, R1/R2 conformance, loaner loss, reverse-logistics delay, diagnostic accuracy, repair rework, service credits, and total cost through final settlement.

**Go indicators**

- Time to restored service approaches the fast-path experience while complaints move directionally toward the reviewed-path range.
- No material increase in aged open cases or unresolved provisional settlements.
- Inventory loss and reverse-logistics cost remain operationally acceptable.
- Customer satisfaction improves without elevated disputes over holds or charges.

**Stop or revise indicators**

- Final reviews accumulate even though customers have temporary devices.
- Loaner availability is too low to deliver a reliable promise.
- Total failure cost remains high because diagnosis does not affect repair rework.
- Customers reject provisional terms or complaint volume changes form rather than falling.

### Sequencing and proof that value is not shifted downstream

Use concurrent control groups or stepped-wedge rollout by product/region rather than simple before/after comparison. Stratify results by segment, value, defect type, and campaign period.

Evaluate each cohort through a sufficiently long horizon covering the observed 150–180-hour complaint tails and subsequent reshipments, credits, and reopens. Reconcile:

- fast-path cost with complaint, reshipment, credit, and reverse-logistics cost;
- closure time with later reopening;
- lower abandonment with completed-claim outcomes;
- faster evidence completion with downstream review queues;
- faster restored service with final-settlement aging.

Promote a redesign only if it improves the intended segment without breaching compliance, moving utilization beyond stable capacity, or transferring defects to a later event that the initial dashboard excludes.
