# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

**Assumptions used in the reconstruction**

- **A1. Skill interpretation:** A job requiring multiple skills requires the resource to have all listed skills. This is needed to explain why D-M is not eligible for mechanical+electrical jobs and why D-X is not eligible for firmware+electrical D05.
- **A2. Visibility / arrival scan lag:** I treat the recorded arrival time as the documented eligibility time. Because arrival scans can lag by up to two minutes, decisions within two minutes of a recorded arrival are treated as having uncertain visibility. For decisions more than two minutes after arrival, I assume the job is visible unless an unobserved hold is hypothesized.
- **A3. Appointments and deadlines:** Appointment and deadline fields are treated as exact target times unless a tolerance window is explicitly considered. Deviations are evidence against a strict exact-time policy, but not necessarily against a soft-window policy.
- **A4. Preempted jobs:** A preempted job may be resumed. Whether a suspended job retains FIFO age is policy-dependent; where material, I show both interpretations.
- **A5. Availability exceptions:** Known availability exceptions are treated as hard constraints unless the policy accepts costly preemption. This is needed to interpret D-F’s idle period before its meeting.
- **A6. Small gaps:** Gaps of 0–2 minutes may be scan or setup lag rather than deliberate idleness.

Abbreviations: `P` = priority, `est` = est_min, `appt` = appointment, `dl` = deadline, `rem` = estimated remaining time.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable queue:** QD is the most identifiable. Skill requirements, resource availability, and the explicit note that D-F is the only fully qualified resource for D05 create strong, observable constraints. The appointment of D06 and the routing of mechanical jobs to D-M further support skill-based routing with FIFO-like behavior inside resource pools.

**Least identifiable queue:** QC is the least identifiable. The early sequence supports SPT, C05 supports priority/deadline escalation, and C01 supports FIFO/aging or initial-backlog precedence. The 10:32 and 11:40 decisions contradict several simple rules at once. Without queue-entry/hold data or deadline-tolerance data, QC remains deeply ambiguous.

**Most dangerous claimed policy to infer:** The most dangerous inference would be to label QB as a **preemptive priority** queue based only on the visible preemptions of B01 and the selection of B05. Those episodes are salient, but the broader pattern includes FIFO resumptions, lower-priority jobs starting before higher-priority jobs, an ignored appointment, and an explicit dispatcher reassignment. Treating QB as a stable preemptive-priority system would risk legitimizing frequent interruptions, obscuring manual dispatch behavior, and underestimating the importance of FIFO flow and appointment commitments.
