**Three temporal observations that anchor everything below:**

1. **Risk is time-dependent, not adaptation-dependent.** D (printed snapshot) is defensible at hour 2 and indefensible at hour 50: ~4,000 picks against a Day-0 snapshot make fast-mover error unbounded. The same adaptation migrates from resilience to concealed noncompliance without anyone deciding anything.
2. **The workaround had a *better* closure gate than the recovered process.** T2 ended in *Spreadsheet invoice hold* (commercial closure blocked pending evidence). T4 ends in *Invoice close  Audit remediation* (commercial closure first, evidence later). Recovery **removed** a control that improvisation had invented, and that inversion is precisely what makes noncompliance concealed: cycle-time clocks stop while compliance is still open.
3. **Reported exceptions understate structural exposure.** In T2, ~2,500–2,600 cases have no authorization event (1,600 Skip + ~920–1,000 fast lane), 1,800 have no release check, 650 dispatched cases have no POD in-window. Yet T4 audit remediation touches only 2,300 cases *in total*. Remediation is exception-triggered, so it cannot cover a gap that was never flagged. Also, only 1,500 cases were tagged "backlog" in T4 while T2 alone left ~3,000 open  **contingency cases lost their identity across phases**.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Definition used here.** Resilience = the *function* of a control is preserved by a different mechanism, at the moment it is needed, with evidence that a reviewer can later verify, and with the deviation declared. Concealed noncompliance = the control's function is not performed (or is performed too late to be meaningful), while the process record and the KPI dashboard still show a completed case.

Three discriminating tests, applied to this case:

1. **Substitution vs. omission.** B substitutes verification (offline list + phone) for verification (system)  resilience. C performs no verification  omission, regardless of how many orders it moved.
2. **Recoverable vs. irrecoverable evidence.** Authorization can be re-verified against master data; POD can be re-attested by the customer. But *temperature at handoff*, *two-person witness at pick/pack*, *which lot was physically picked*, and *who took custody* cannot be recreated later — only asserted. Adaptations that defer recoverable evidence (H for ambient, E with lot capture, L) are manageable debt. Adaptations that destroy irrecoverable evidence (G, F's cold-chain sampling, E without lot capture, H for cold-chain) are permanent loss dressed as delay.
3. **Declared vs. silent.** T2's *Spreadsheet invoice hold* was a declared, visible blocker. T4's *Invoice close  Audit remediation* is silent: 2,300 cases were commercially closed and cycle-time-complete while evidence was incomplete. Fitness recovering 0.71  0.90 while exceptions remain 2.75× baseline and 24% of traces still don't fit is the quantitative signature of concealment: **aggregate fitness recovers faster than assurance**.

**True resilience:** B; A1 (triage sequencing) extracted from A; I as a *mechanism*; J when explicit; K and L as *recovery* capabilities (valuable, but they are debt repayment, not resilience, and must never be scored as control performance).

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance:** C (no authorization); G (no custody evidence, and 1,800 cases with no release check at all); the packdispatch bypass edge itself; F's sampling where it covered cold-chain; H applied to controlled/cold-chain; L positioned after invoice close; reconstructed scans without provenance flags.

**Ambiguous — depends on declared risk appetite, and must be decided explicitly rather than by drift:**
- **A2 (release before authorization completes):** acceptable only as a *time-boxed conditional release* for ambient hospital-critical orders with a named approver, recipient notification, and a 24h evidence SLA. Never for controlled substances.
- **D:** resilient at hour 2, non-compliant at hour 50. Requires an expiry, not a judgement call.
- **E:** resilient if lot/expiry were captured on paper; concealed noncompliance if only "scans later reconstructed". The log cannot currently distinguish these — which is the real defect.
- **F sampling:** defensible for ambient with a defined sampling plan and escalation triggers; indefensible for cold-chain.
- **I / J:** resilient with published rules, aging floors and service minimums; unfair rationing without them. T4 shows the cost landed on pharmacies and wholesalers even after hospitals recovered.

**A specific contradiction worth escalating:** F is described as checking *all* controlled-substance orders, yet dual-control gaps rose to 2.9% and 1,800 packs bypassed release entirely. The most plausible abductive reading is that (a) the bypass path carried controlled orders outside F's view, and/or (b) a single pharmacist release was recorded as if it satisfied two-person dual control. Either way, **self-reported control coverage is not evidence of control execution**, and the reconciliation must be done from physical counts and custody artifacts, not from the release log.

# 4. Recovery process model

Notation: BPMN-style bullets. `` sequence, `XOR{}` exclusive gateway, `AND{}` parallel, `` message/escalation, `[guard]` condition. Every case carries a permanent `mode` attribute (`NORMAL` / `CONTINGENCY-L1/L2/L3`) and every event carries `provenance` (`observed` / `offline-captured` / `reconstructed-attested`).

### 4.1 Trigger conditions (entry, escalation, exit)

| Level | Objective trigger (any) | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 Degraded** | Scan success <99% for 30 min; inventory sync lag >15 min; authorization service latency >5 min | Shift supervisor, auto-declared by monitoring |
| **L2 Contingency** | WMS unavailable >30 min; scanning unavailable; authorization service unavailable; snapshot age > expiry | Incident commander + Responsible Pharmacist jointly |
| **L3 Restricted** | Cyber integrity doubt (data may be altered, not just unavailable); controlled-substance count variance >0; cold-chain logger unavailable | RP + QA head; regulator pre-notification  |

`XOR{mode}` is entered **automatically**, not by improvisation. Entry writes an incident record, activates the pre-approved contingency variant (a *versioned, published model*), and starts a **compliance clock** alongside the operational clock.

**Exit criteria (all must hold, per lane):** scan success 99.5% for 4h; inventory variance <0.5% on independent counts; sync lag <5 min; **100% of contingency-mode cases enumerated in the reconciliation census**; no open irrecoverable-evidence gap on controlled/cold-chain cases. Exit from *execution* contingency does **not** close the incident; the incident closes only when compliance debt = 0 or formally risk-accepted.

### 4.2 Triage rules

`Receive order  Assign contingency case ID (pre-numbered)  Score  XOR{lane}`

Score = `clinical urgency × customer criticality × product risk × age-boost`, published in advance:
- Customer criticality: Hospital-critical 3 / Pharmacy-standard 2 / Wholesale-bulk 1.
- Clinical urgency: patient-scheduled or stockout-within-24h = 3.
- Product risk: controlled 4, cold-chain 4, recall-sensitive 3, ambient 1 (**risk raises control intensity, not queue position**).
- Age-boost: +1 per 12h waiting; **mandatory escalation at 48h** (anti-starvation).

**Fairness guardrails (hard):** ring-fence 25% of pick/pack capacity for non-critical orders; pharmacy fill floor 85% and wholesale 80% during L2 (observed 61% is the failure to avoid); degradation ratio (non-critical median ÷ baseline) must not exceed 1.5× the hospital ratio. Every triage decision logs reason code + decider. Deferrals expire at 48h  ` customer contact  XOR{re-confirm | cancel with consent}` — **no silent deferral, no silent cancellation**.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product/customer risk (waiver authority in brackets)

| Product | Non-waivable at any level | Permitted substitution | Waivable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Controlled-substance** | Two-person dual control with recorded identities; independent physical count; numbered custody form signed at every transfer; POD with named recipient + ID; secure handoff | Pre-numbered duplicate custody book; offline scan store-and-forward | **No.** If unavailable  `Hold` and accept the throughput loss [RP only, never delegated] |
| **Cold-chain** | Temperature record at pack **and at handoff**; qualified shipper; excursion decision by pharmacist before release | Continuous logger download; validated passive indicator + pack-time reading + documented shipper qualification | Only *conditional release* for Hospital-critical with recipient notification + logged risk acceptance [RP + QA] |
| **Recall-sensitive** | Lot/batch + expiry captured **at pick** on peel-off duplicate label | Paper label + photo capture; later transcription with two-person check | **No** — without lot capture, recall capability is lost |
| **Ambient** | Authorization verified before dispatch; release check; custody evidence | Daily-signed offline authorization register + callback to the **registered** number (never a customer-supplied number); risk-based release sampling with defined plan | Sequencing only; **C-style assumption never permitted** |

`AND{}` note: authorization verification and physical picking may run **in parallel** for Hospital-critical ambient to protect speed, but `Dispatch` has a hard join: `[authorization verified] AND [release token] AND [custody artifact] AND [product-specific evidence]`. **No dispatch token is issuable without a release token** — this single structural rule eliminates the 1,800-case bypass.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture (the core architectural fix)

**Principle: the evidence plane must be independent of the execution plane.** WMS downtime must not imply evidence downtime.

- Offline-first handheld app: captures scan/photo/signature/timestamp locally, store-and-forward sync, tamper-evident local log. Turns **G "no scan" into "delayed scan"** and **H into timestamped attestation**.
- Pre-numbered contingency kits at every station: pick list (lot/expiry/qty fields), dual-control witness form, temperature log, custody manifest, POD form. Number ranges reconciled daily — missing numbers are detectable, unlike missing spreadsheet rows.
- One contingency case ID joins every artifact; IDs are issued at intake, never invented retrospectively.
- Transcription: two-person verification; **timestamps are never backdated**; reconstructed events keep `provenance=reconstructed-attested` permanently, with source artifact ID and attester.

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization

Single visible queue, all contingency cases flagged for life. `Backlog  Re-score (daily)  XOR{resume | customer contact | cancel with consent}`. WIP limits per lane; cold-chain and controlled items may **not** park in ambient staging; aging report published daily by segment; capacity ring-fence enforced. Target: burn backlog without extending non-critical p90 beyond 1.5× baseline (T4 observed 1.5–1.67× *with* invisible backlog — make it visible and bounded).

### 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

`Systems restored  AND{ Operational resume, MANDATORY CENSUS of every contingency case }`

Per case: `Three-way match (independent physical count  paper artifact  system record)  Evidence completeness checklist by product risk  XOR{`
- **(a) Complete**  `Compliance close`;
- **(b) Recoverable gap**  `Remediate with attestation` (SLA: controlled/cold-chain 24h, ambient 72h, financial 10 days)  `QA re-performance sample (10%)`  `Compliance close`;
- **(c) Irrecoverable gap**  `Risk assessment by RP/QA  XOR{documented risk acceptance | recipient notification | quarantine/withdrawal + recall | regulatory report}`  `Compliance close (deviation-accepted)`;
- **(d) Unexplained controlled variance**  `Diversion investigation  regulator` `}`

**Closure gates (restoring T2's own invention):** `AND{operational complete, compliance state  (complete, accepted-deviation)}  Invoice close  Case close`. Invoice hold is a *permanent* design element, not a workaround.

**Reporting:** dual cycle times — `T_op` (dispatch/POD) and `T_comp` (compliance close); true completion = `max(T_op, T_comp)`. Publish compliance-debt stock (cases × age × FTE-hours) as a first-class metric alongside throughput.

**Conformance checking:** two models — normative and published contingency. Report **declared-deviation rate** separately from **undeclared-deviation rate**; use alignment-based conformance with a cost function that penalises missing irrecoverable controls (temperature, dual control, lot, custody) far more heavily than a delayed invoice; compute conformance separately on `observed/offline-captured` events only, so reconstructed events cannot inflate fitness.

### 4.7 T3-specific rule (the phase with no DFG)

`[inventory sync unreliable]  Reconcile-before-promise`: no availability promise, no reservation, and no auto-release on unsynced stock. Controlled/cold-chain picks require a second-source physical verification. **Log the T3 variant graph explicitly** — partial recovery must be as observable as full outage, because that is where system-generated events look conformant while being semantically false.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** slower and explicitly rationed service for pharmacies and wholesalers, deferrals that are communicated and time-limited, declared control *substitutions* with named approvers and expiry, a visible dip in reported cycle time once compliance closure is gated, and a funded remediation tail with published debt aging — these are the legitimate price of protecting hospital supply, and the T2 evidence shows the mission goal was achievable (hospital median +25%, fill 91%). Management should **not accept** throughput purchased by deleting controls: assumed standing authorization (C), dispatch without custody evidence (G), the 1,800-case release bypass, single-signature "dual control", cold-chain shipments without temperature-at-handoff, picks without lot capture, reconstructed events indistinguishable from real-time records, or invoice/case closure before compliance closure. Hospital fill of 91% alongside pharmacy fill of 61% and 2.2% residual traceability exceptions is **not** recovery; it is transferred risk plus concealed debt. What would change this recommendation: evidence that a daily-signed offline register plus registered-number callback verifies authorization as reliably as the system (then a fast authorization path becomes permissible); validated continuous logging that makes handoff temperature genuinely recoverable (then phone POD becomes acceptable for cold-chain); store-and-forward scanning with proven integrity and bounded latency (then "no scan" becomes acceptable "delayed scan"); an independently counted reconciliation showing <0.5% inventory variance and full lot-level traceability from paper (then manual pick lists are keepable); and QA re-performance showing <1% attestation failure. Absent that evidence, the correct posture is: **protect speed by re-sequencing and substituting, never by omitting — and never let a case be called complete while its evidence is still missing.**
