## 1. Target definition

The warehouse label does not support “90-day default for every application.” It records default after actual activation, and activation is a selective process. A rejected application has no observed outcome under lending; assigning it zero would change the question.

**Primary target: an application-level competing-outcome distribution under the lender’s observed operating policy.** At Application Started, predict the following six mutually exclusive outcomes:

| Outcome | Definition |
|---|---|
| R | Rejected within the next 180 days, before activation, withdrawal, or transfer. |
| W | Withdrawn within 180 days, before another first disposition. |
| T | Transferred within 180 days, before another first disposition. |
| O | No activation, rejection, withdrawal, or transfer by day 180. |
| A-D | Activated within 180 days, before another first disposition, and default within 90 days after that activation. |
| A-N | Activated within 180 days, before another first disposition, and no default within 90 days after activation. |

Acceptance and contract signature are intermediate events. Transfer ends the origin-platform episode in this formulation. An activation after day 180 does not retrospectively change O. A-N does not imply freedom from default after the account’s 90-day horizon. R, W, T, and O are distinct outcomes, not “non-default” labels.

The primary prediction time is immediately after the Application Started event becomes available. The unit is an origin application episode, with one primary prediction per episode. The population is all 48,000 applications starting during the observation window, subject to documented identity and event-quality reconciliation. Secondary models at the third event and seven days after start apply only to episodes still awaiting a first disposition at those landmarks. Their horizons run 180 days from their respective prediction times, so their populations and estimands differ from the primary model.

For information X available at landmark t, the estimand is the conditional vector P(Y = j | X, eligible at t), for the six outcomes above. The 180-day disposition horizon is an explicit study choice requiring business acceptance, not a reinterpretation of `default_90d`. Activated branches require up to a further 90 days of exposure and subsequent feed arrival: this is not “default within 180 days of application.”

One coherent factorization estimates first-disposition cumulative incidences F_R, F_W, F_T, F_A and conditional default risk q90(X, u) among applications activated u days after prediction. Then:

    p_O = 1 − F_R(180) − F_W(180) − F_T(180) − F_A(180)
    p_A-D = integral from 0 to 180 of q90(X, u) dF_A(u | X)
    p_A-N = F_A(180) − p_A-D

Activation time u is integrated over at scoring time; the eventual observed activation time is not supplied as a prediction feature. This formulation supports operational routing and portfolio forecasting while preserving the distinction between credit exposure and non-exposure.

**Credible alternatives:** A conditional target, P(default_90d = 1 | early prefix, activation within 180 days), answers default risk among applications selected for activation under the observed policy. It is useful for portfolio risk monitoring but is neither an application-wide default probability nor the risk if any applicant were approved. Alternatively, time to default measured from activation supports account-survival analysis when reliable default dates and follow-up dates exist. The binary warehouse label alone does not identify event-time hazards. A counterfactual target such as default if every applicant received a specified loan requires a specified activation time, product and terms, consistency, conditional exchangeability, positivity, and outcomes beyond this selectively labeled sample; it is not identified here without strong additional assumptions or data.

## 2. Cohort construction

Construct the denominator from the application registry and complete origin event histories, not from the 21,600-case ML export. Include rejected, withdrawn, transferred, open, and immature activated cases. If the CSV contains only the existing export, the full study cannot be reconstructed from it; obtain the omitted histories before fitting the application-level model.

Use distinct clocks:

- Prediction time t governs feature availability.
- A historical fit cutoff B governs which events and labels a model could have learned from.
- The final event-observation cutoff is 31 December 2025, inclusive; the final label-knowledge cutoff is 15 February 2026. Extraction in February does not imply that application transitions in January or February were observed.

At cutoff B, use events observed by min(B, the event-observation cutoff), subject also to actual ingestion availability. Use labels only when their authoritative `label_available_at` is no later than B and their 90-day horizon has elapsed. Historical reconstruction from a February snapshot requires evidence that event records and label versions have not been retrospectively rewritten. Otherwise describe the exercise as retrospective reconstruction, not faithful deployment replay.

For process follow-up, censor at the verified coverage cutoff, not each case’s last event: inactivity can be real. An application is O only if coverage extends through its 180-day deadline without an earlier disposition. A recently open case is censored before that deadline. Once activation is observed, application-event censoring and external outcome-feed follow-up are separate clocks.

| Snapshot group | Count | Observed information | Role in the primary analysis |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Activated, labeled non-default | 18,900 | Observed negative 90-day account label, subject to availability audit | A-N if activation is the first disposition within the application horizon; otherwise classify by the horizon rule. |
| Activated, labeled default | 2,700 | Observed positive 90-day account label, subject to availability audit | A-D under the same activation rule. |
| Rejected | 9,100 | Observed origin rejection | Competing disposition R if within the horizon; no counterfactual default label. |
| Withdrawn | 5,700 | Observed withdrawal | Competing disposition W if within the horizon. |
| Transferred | 2,900 | Observed origin transfer; downstream linkage incomplete | Competing disposition T in the primary analysis; downstream credit outcome selectively unobserved. |
| Still open | 5,100 | No recorded disposition through event coverage | O if the deadline has been observed; otherwise right censored. Future activation and its outcome are unknown. |
| Activated, unlabeled | 3,600 | Activation observed, 90-day outcome/feed not mature in the supplied accounting | Separate unelapsed horizons, pending feeds, and overdue or potentially permanent missing feeds using dates and provenance. Never assign zero. |
| **Total** | **48,000** | **21,600 labeled activated accounts; 26,400 omitted cases** | **All retained in the cohort ledger.** |

The supplied totals describe snapshot status, not final counts in the six horizon-specific target classes. Dates are needed to derive those counts. In particular, the 3,600 cannot be numerically partitioned into censoring, ordinary delay, and permanent missingness from the aggregates. A feed marked “not mature” may actually be overdue. Reconcile any such discrepancy rather than manufacturing a breakdown. The 12.5% default rate in the labeled export is descriptive of that selected, ascertained subset only.

Exclude starts outside the defined window and genuine duplicate application records after identity reconciliation. Quarantine conflicting identities, missing starts, impossible chronology, or ambiguous event order for repair; report their counts and effects on coverage rather than silently changing the denominator. Do not exclude a case because it later defaults, stays open, or has a missing feed. A short trace is not an exclusion from the primary start-time model.

Use a stable origin-episode identifier across migration. Do not concatenate each event’s source system to its case identifier if that would split an episode when systems change. Linked downstream episodes remain separate operational episodes under the primary transfer definition, but known shared applicants/transfer chains must be grouped for validation. About 1,740 of the 2,900 transfers are linkable and about 1,160 are not; these are approximate, not additional applications in the ledger.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

**Ordinary right censoring:** An unresolved application whose 180-day deadline exceeds process coverage has unknown disposition by the horizon. An activated account whose activation plus 90 days exceeds available outcome follow-up has an incomplete default horizon. Neither is a known negative. Absence of a Default Recorded event is insufficient evidence of non-default. With only a terminal binary label, an incomplete account does not contribute fabricated default-free person-time; survival use requires a trustworthy “observed default-free through” date and event ascertainment.

**Delayed labels:** Exposure may have completed while its result is still in transit. Preserve both activation-plus-90 and `label_available_at`. For the main binary account-risk component, use an administratively mature training pool with activation plus 90 plus 35 days no later than the fit cutoff, then require an observed valid label for supervised fitting. Apply this eligibility gate to positives and negatives alike. The 35-day lag is a working maturity buffer, not a guarantee: late and permanently absent feeds remain possible. An earlier arriving label may be known at evaluation time, but it does not justify preferentially training on fast-arriving positives from an otherwise immature cohort.

**Selection through activation:** Activation is an observed competing disposition in the main analysis. The conditional account-default component is explicitly selected on activation. Do not use inverse activation propensity weights to manufacture outcomes for rejected or withdrawn applications. Weighting cannot recover an undefined or unobserved lending outcome without causal assumptions and support.

**Transfers:** Treat all origin transfers as T, including linked transfers; thus linkage success does not select the primary cohort. A secondary cross-platform study may stitch verified identities, but missing links and loss of downstream follow-up must then be modeled. The linked 60% are not automatically representative of all transfers.

**Permanently missing feeds:** Track feed source, partner origin, elapsed time since horizon completion, retry/status history if available, and resolved coverage incidents. “Overdue” does not prove permanent absence. Elevated partner missingness makes unconditional independent censoring implausible and can remain informative after adjustment.

**Primary analysis:** Fit four competing first-disposition hazards with administrative censoring, producing cumulative incidences and the open-state probability. These are a valid collapsed representation of the richer KYC/review/document process. Fit conditional 90-day default risk among lag-eligible activated accounts, using stabilized inverse observation-probability weights for missing feeds when justified. For each landmark, this component concerns first activations within its 180-day window; later activations do not convert O into an activated target. Estimate observation probability from the complete lag-eligible activation denominator for this population, including labeled and unlabeled accounts, with channel, source, region, prefix information, and relevant calendar/activation timing. Fit nuisance models within the training fold. Integrate the account-risk component over the predicted activation distribution to obtain A-D and A-N.

Report weight distributions, effective sample size, overlap, and any truncation. Truncation introduces a bias–variance tradeoff; it does not solve non-identification. Deterministically immature recent cohorts have zero probability of a mature label at that cutoff. Neither inverse weighting nor a survival model creates missing follow-up support. Estimation for those cohorts requires explicit transport assumptions, shorter supported horizons, or later data. If an important partner stratum has no outcome ascertainment, report bounds or abstain for its default component instead of a purportedly identified point probability.

**Sensitivity analyses:**

- Vary the lag buffer, for example 35 versus 60 days, and compare historically archived feed-arrival curves. Examine mature cohorts with stable coverage separately, while stating their narrower population.
- Use a pattern-mixture analysis for missing account outcomes: vary their default odds relative to comparable observed accounts, particularly for partner-originated accounts. Also give worst-case bounds: among n administratively eligible activated accounts with d known defaults and m missing outcomes, conditional default risk lies between d/n and (d+m)/n before further assumptions. A weighted analogue must use common base weights across that entire denominator, rather than first inflating observed cases with feed-ascertainment weights. Hold the disposition distribution fixed when propagating this uncertainty into A-D/A-N.
- Compare pooled, policy-stratified, and recent-period models. For a secondary cross-platform target, contrast verified-link results with explicit assumptions or bounds for unlinked transfers; do not carry the origin-transfer interpretation into that different target.

Schema validity, duplicate patterns, observed feed lags, missingness by source, covariate overlap, and observed calibration shifts are testable diagnostics. Completeness of ingestion and stable label versions are partially testable through independent reconciliations and archives. Conditional independent censoring, missing-at-random feeds, representativeness of linked transfers, and transport across policy regimes cannot be established from observed labels alone. Exchangeability for “default if approved” is fundamentally untestable here, with policy thresholds also threatening positivity.

## 4. Prefix extraction

Create each prefix from records available when its trigger fires, before attaching any outcome table. Event occurrence time and ingestion time should both precede prediction; where ingestion timestamps are unavailable, certify timely ingestion or acknowledge the replay limitation.

For fixed event counts, the primary k=1 prefix consists of Application Started. Additional k=3 and k=5 models trigger immediately after the kth valid event only if no activation, rejection, withdrawal, or transfer has occurred. A case with fewer than k events has not triggered that model; retain its start-time prediction and report coverage. Do not pad it, move its prediction to its future last event, or add a “will end before k” flag. Multiple prefixes from one application stay in one split, and a pooled training scheme must avoid giving long cases disproportionate weight.

For elapsed-time prefixes, take all available events through start plus, for example, one or seven days. The prediction timestamp is the scheduled landmark even if no event occurred then. Include idle ongoing cases; exclude cases already disposed by that time from that landmark’s at-risk population. Cases whose landmark has not yet been observed have no evaluation opportunity yet. Distinguish process age from time since the most recent event.

Preserve genuine document-request and review loops. Counts, returns to review, and inter-event delays are informative; collapsing each repeated activity to one occurrence destroys that information. Deduplicate only proven ingestion duplicates, preferably using immutable event IDs or migration reconciliation. Exact same-name/same-time rows can also be real events. Without reliable order for tied timestamps, quarantine the affected prefix or use a prespecified time-bucket approach; a row-number tie-breaker alone does not establish real order.

Map renamed activities to a verified canonical vocabulary while retaining raw name and source in the audit layer. At each historical backtest origin, use only mappings available or legitimately knowable then; unseen future activities map to an unknown category. A later canonicalized replay can be reported separately. Prefix eligibility and values must not change when events strictly after the prediction timestamp are appended.

## 5. Process-aware features

| Feature class | Examples available at prediction | Availability requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Static case attributes | Origin channel, region, original requested amount | Values recorded at application start, not subsequently revised values copied backward. |
| Prefix control flow | Last activity, activity counts, observed transitions, current review/document stage, seen-path encoding | Compute only on the prefix; fit vocabularies and rare-variant rules on training prefixes. |
| Prefix rework | Review visits, document requests, returns from document collection to review, repeated transition counts | Preserve legitimate loops; distinguish suspected duplicates. |
| Prefix timing | Application age, time since last event, gaps between observed events, elapsed time in the current observed stage | Use the landmark clock; an inter-event gap is not automatically employee service time. |
| Prefix resources | Distinct resources, observed handoffs, observed team or role, assignment changes | Use identities and roles as recorded then; handoff counts require ordered events and missing-resource handling. |
| Historical queue/reference data | Team backlog at t, prior throughput, historical activity-delay percentiles, resource experience at t | Reconstruct from events before t and staffing records valid then; no future completions or extraction-time summaries. |
| Calendar/cohort | Start month, weekday, policy version currently in force, migration era, origin source | Available at prediction; future policy decisions and eventual activation date are not baseline features. |

A training-prefix directly-follows graph or variant vocabulary can supply path rarity or deviations from previously observed transitions. Fit that reference only on histories available at the training cutoff. Full completed-case variants would reveal future routing. Queue lengths require a reconstructible assignment/queue state across all cases, not just the labeled export. The supplied schema lacks explicit lifecycle start/complete pairs, so it does not justify treating PM4Py service/waiting-time outputs as measured processing and waiting time.

At Application Started, many process features are constant or unavailable. That is an honest limitation of universal early prediction. Quantify the incremental value and reduced coverage of later landmarks rather than pretending every application has a long informative prefix.

Audit more than column names: compare early features before and after appending future events; test boundary timestamps and delayed labels; inspect point-in-time joins; verify that no encoder, imputer, calibration model, or reference graph uses held-out outcomes. The implementation uses a strict event-column allowlist and omits uncertain value-versioned attributes by default.

## 7. Validation design

Use historical deployment simulations with separate prediction, fitting, and evaluation vintages. Save each prediction when its landmark would have occurred. Fit on labels available at that fit cutoff, retain other histories as censored where appropriate, and evaluate the saved predictions as follow-up becomes available. Never refit an old prediction using the eventual February label table.

A concrete schedule compatible with this short dataset is:

1. Fit an initial, prespecified model on 1 May 2024 using applications started before that date and only information available then. Many training histories will be censored; only sufficiently early activations can inform the account-default component. Report whether that component has enough effective observations.
2. Generate untouched validation predictions for May 2024 starts. By 2 April 2025, those applications have had sufficient calendar time for the maximum 180 + 90 + 35-day window, subject to persistent feed missingness. Use only data available by 2 April to choose and calibrate the final specification.
3. Refit on eligible histories available by 2 April 2025 and test on applications starting from that date onward. The intervening June 2024–March 2025 starts can enter this final refit with their actual censoring and label status. The code names this interval the refit pool.
4. Keep the April model frozen for a shift analysis. Separately simulate prospective refits at later origins, including 1 July and 15 October, using only labels and completed validation information available at each origin. Do not tune a July model using outcomes that arrive in winter.

This deliberately gives up some apparent sample efficiency to preserve chronology. If the early fitting sample is inadequate, delay the study and accept a less mature final test; do not repair the shortage with future labels. Verified historical vintages are a prerequisite for claiming this was a faithful backtest.

**Follow-up feasibility matters.** At the final extraction, a 90-day exposure horizon has elapsed for activations on or before approximately 17 November 2025; the additional 35-day buffer moves the conservative activation date to 13 October. Observing a full 180-day application horizon through 31 December requires starts by approximately 4 July. Allowing activation at the end of that horizon, then 90 days plus 35 days for the feed, moves the conservative fully supported start date back to approximately 16 April 2025. Exact timestamps govern boundary cases. These are administrative eligibility dates, not guarantees against missing feeds.

Consequently, only a narrow early-April slice of the proposed final test has enough calendar time for every possible branch of the primary outcome. Some later cases resolve early, but evaluating only those would favor fast outcomes. Use censoring-aware analyses only where follow-up support exists. The complete primary six-outcome performance after the July policy change, and especially after the October migration, cannot be honestly established from this snapshot. Report supported shorter-horizon disposition results and mature account-outcome subsets separately, with their populations identified, and schedule later evaluation. Migration-era starts cannot even provide a full 180-day origin-process history here.

Compare pre/post-policy and pre/post-migration periods, including transition cohorts whose follow-up crosses a change. Check activation incidence and conditional account default separately: policy can alter both. Model inputs may include the policy known at prediction, but not an unannounced future policy change discovered retrospectively. Audit new activity names, duplicate rates, missing features, route frequencies, and score distributions around migration.

Split by case start, keep all prefixes of an episode together, and purge known linked applicant/transfer groups that span split boundaries for a stringent independence assessment. Report how many cases this removes and retain the original cohort ledger. Unknown links leave residual dependence; perform an origin-transfer-only or verified-identity sensitivity analysis rather than claiming perfect deduplication. Use case/group and calendar-block resampling for uncertainty intervals, not event-row resampling.

Evaluate:

- Disposition-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and time-dependent Brier scores at supported horizons; integrated Brier scores over a prespecified supported interval.
- The six-outcome vector using multiclass Brier score and log loss, with censoring/missing-observation adjustment or an appropriate observed-data likelihood. Do not score unknown outcomes as O or A-N. Weight definitions must match the process and account-feed observation mechanisms.
- Conditional 90-day default calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, reliability curves, Brier score, log loss, PR-AUC, and ROC-AUC on the correctly defined activated population, with ascertainment weights where justified. PR-AUC is meaningful alongside its selected-population prevalence.
- A-D calibration and observed-versus-predicted counts for operational portfolio forecasts; decision costs or utility only for the stated use and with unresolved outcomes accounted for.

Show confidence intervals, cohort sizes, numbers at risk, endpoint counts, label coverage, and effective weighted sample sizes. Report calibration and performance by channel, partner/origin source, region, requested-amount band, policy era, and migration era, plus prefix coverage and unsupported strata. Avoid unstable subgroup point estimates presented without uncertainty. Calibrators and censoring/observation models must be fitted within the temporal design.

A random case split mixes policy regimes, migration artifacts, label maturity and future process vocabulary. It can also place linked episodes in both sets and train on information unavailable at the nominal deployment date. It estimates interpolation within the reconstructed dataset, not prospective performance during the documented changes.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The primary probabilities describe routes and default after actual exposure under an observed policy and ascertainment model. They support early workload planning, routing, and qualified portfolio forecasts. They do not identify intrinsic borrower risk, the effect of approving an application, or the default risk of rejected applicants if lent to. A low A-D probability can result from a high rejection probability; it must not be read as evidence that an applicant is safe to approve.

Predictive associations reflect selection, economic conditions, processing behavior, product terms, and measurement. Conditional default risk among activated accounts remains policy-selected. Missing partner outcomes, incomplete transfer linkage, unverified historical values, and lack of long follow-up after the two changes limit identification and generalization. Censoring weights cannot resolve unmeasured informative missingness or zero observation probability. The simplified first-disposition design also requires exceptions such as reopened applications, rescinded decisions, and multiple activations to be assigned explicit episode rules.

Deployment can change the data-generating process in several ways:

- Using scores to change approval thresholds changes who activates and therefore the observed default-label population.
- Prioritizing review or document collection changes queues, event timing, rework, and time to activation.
- Changing limits, prices, contract terms, or collection interventions changes default outcomes after activation.
- Sending more cases to partners changes transfer incidence, linkage rates, and feed missingness.
- Staff reacting to scores or recording activities differently changes the prefix features themselves.

Begin with prospective shadow scoring and fixed outcome definitions. Monitor coverage, route incidence, feed delay, calibration and policy versions. Any decision policy based on the scores needs a separate causal evaluation or appropriately designed prospective study. The available data do not justify relabeling non-exposed applications or claiming universal counterfactual default estimates.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

The skeleton below implements cohort and landmark tables, leaving statistical fitting explicit. It assumes the full event file is available and flags ambiguous histories for repair. Populate the verified activity mapping and reconcile the printed cohort ledger to 48,000 before analysis. The historical replay guard intentionally prevents a February-only snapshot from silently posing as an earlier data vintage. Genuine early value snapshots can later extend the conservative feature allowlist. The final-cutoff target table is for assessment; historical fitting must rebuild targets at its own cutoff.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
UTC = lambda s: pd.Timestamp(s, tz="UTC")
EXTRACT = UTC("2026-02-16") - pd.Timedelta("1ns")  # Replace with the actual extraction instant.
EVENT_END = UTC("2026-01-01") - pd.Timedelta("1ns")
TRAIN_FIT, FINAL_FIT = UTC("2024-05-01"), UTC("2025-04-02")
H, D90, LAG = pd.Timedelta(days=180), pd.Timedelta(days=90), pd.Timedelta(days=35)
REPLAY_CERTIFIED = False  # Enable only after event/version/arrival-time audit.
ALIASES = {}  # Canonical aliases verified for this replay vintage; review unknown names.
DISPOSITION = {"Account Activated": "A", "Rejected": "R",
               "Withdrawn": "W", "Transferred": "T"}
raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv", dtype={C: "string"})
for col in [T, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
    raw[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col], utc=True, errors="raise", format="mixed")
assert raw[[C, A, T, "data_extract_time"]].notna().all().all()
assert raw["data_extract_time"].le(EXTRACT).all()
raw[A] = raw[A].replace(ALIASES)
raw["_row"] = np.arange(len(raw))
raw["default_90d"] = pd.to_numeric(raw["default_90d"], errors="raise")
assert raw["default_90d"].dropna().isin([0, 1]).all()
# Assumptions: globally stable origin-episode IDs, complete origin event capture,
# verified aliases, and authoritative label_available_at attached to this label version.
# Do not merge linked transfers selectively; link IDs are for split/group audits.

def event_view(cutoff):
    if cutoff < EXTRACT and not REPLAY_CERTIFIED:
        raise RuntimeError("Historical replay needs certified event and label versions")
    end = min(cutoff, EVENT_END)
    return raw.loc[raw[T].between(UTC("2024-01-01"), end)].copy(), end

def case_table(cutoff):
    ev, end = event_view(cutoff)
    ev = ev.sort_values([C, T, "_row"])
    g = ev.groupby(C, observed=True)
    m = g[T].agg(first_event="min", last_event="max")
    first = lambda name: ev.loc[ev[A].eq(name)].groupby(C)[T].min()
    m["start"], m["activation"] = first("Application Started"), first("Account Activated")
    d = ev.loc[ev[A].isin(DISPOSITION)].drop_duplicates(C).set_index(C)
    m["disposition_at"], m["cause"] = d[T], d[A].map(DISPOSITION)
    terminal = ev.loc[ev[A].isin(["Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"])]
    m["terminal_at"] = terminal.groupby(C)[T].min()
    # Without event IDs/sequence numbers, same-time events need review, not silent dedup.
    ties = ev.loc[ev.duplicated([C, T], keep=False), C].unique()
    m["quarantine"] = m.index.isin(ties) | m["start"].ne(m["first_event"])
    m["quarantine"] |= m["activation"].notna() & m["cause"].ne("A")
    m["process_cutoff"], m["outcome_cutoff"] = end, cutoff
    m["horizon_end"] = m["activation"] + D90
    m["horizon_elapsed"] = m["horizon_end"].le(cutoff)
    m["lag_window_elapsed"] = (m["horizon_end"] + LAG).le(cutoff)
    lab = ev.loc[ev["label_available_at"].le(cutoff) & ev["default_90d"].notna()]
    lg = lab.groupby(C)["default_90d"].agg(y="first", distinct="nunique")
    m = m.join(lg)
    m["available_at"] = lab.groupby(C)["label_available_at"].min()
    m["label_conflict"] = m["distinct"].gt(1)
    m["observed90"] = (m["horizon_elapsed"] & m["y"].notna()
                       & m["available_at"].ge(m["horizon_end"]) & ~m["label_conflict"])
    m["snapshot_group"] = m["cause"].map({"R": "rejected", "W": "withdrawn", "T": "transferred"})
    m["snapshot_group"] = m["snapshot_group"].fillna("open")
    m.loc[m["cause"].eq("A"), "snapshot_group"] = "activated_unobserved90"
    m.loc[m["cause"].eq("A") & m["observed90"], "snapshot_group"] = "activated_labeled90"
    m["label_status"] = "not_applicable"
    act = m["cause"].eq("A")
    m.loc[act & ~m["horizon_elapsed"], "label_status"] = "horizon_censored"
    m.loc[act & m["horizon_elapsed"], "label_status"] = "feed_pending_or_missing"
    m.loc[act & m["lag_window_elapsed"] & ~m["observed90"], "label_status"] = "overdue_unknown"
    m.loc[act & m["observed90"], "label_status"] = "observed"
    return ev, m

def fixed_prefix(ev, m, k):
    safe = ev[[C, A, T, "org:resource"]].copy()  # No warehouse outcome/decision fields.
    safe = safe.loc[safe[C].isin(m.index[m["start"].eq(m["first_event"])])]
    safe = safe.sort_values([C, T])
    fmt = pm4py.format_dataframe(safe, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(fmt)
    pref = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, length=k)
    p = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(pref)
    counts = p.groupby(C).size()
    p = p.loc[p[C].isin(counts.index[counts.eq(k)])].copy()  # No padding short cases.
    t = p.groupby(C)[T].max()
    live = m.reindex(t.index)["disposition_at"]
    t = t.loc[live.isna() | live.gt(t)]  # Predict strictly before any disposition.
    tied = ev.loc[ev.duplicated([C, T], keep=False)]
    bad = tied.loc[tied[T].le(tied[C].map(t)), C]
    t = t.loc[~t.index.isin(bad)]  # Future duplicates cannot change prefix eligibility.
    return p.loc[p[C].isin(t.index)], t

def elapsed_prefix(ev, m, days=7):
    t = m["start"] + pd.Timedelta(days=days)
    eligible = (t.le(m["process_cutoff"]) & m["start"].eq(m["first_event"])
                & (m["disposition_at"].isna() | m["disposition_at"].gt(t)))
    t = t.loc[eligible]
    tied = ev.loc[ev.duplicated([C, T], keep=False)]
    t = t.loc[~t.index.isin(tied.loc[tied[T].le(tied[C].map(t)), C])]
    p = ev[[C, A, T, "org:resource"]].merge(t.rename("prediction_at"), left_on=C, right_index=True)
    return p.loc[p[T].le(p["prediction_at"])].drop(columns="prediction_at"), t

def features(p, prediction_at):
    p = p.sort_values([C, T]).copy()
    assert p[T].le(p[C].map(prediction_at)).all()
    g = p.groupby(C, observed=True)
    x = g.size().to_frame("n_events")
    x["n_activities"] = g[A].nunique()
    x["repeated_activities"] = x["n_events"] - x["n_activities"]
    x["review_visits"] = p[A].eq("Credit Review").groupby(p[C]).sum()
    x["n_resources"] = g["org:resource"].nunique(dropna=True)
    x["age_hours"] = (prediction_at - g[T].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    x["idle_hours"] = (prediction_at - g[T].max()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    x["last_activity"] = g[A].last()
    x["policy_era"] = prediction_at.ge(UTC("2025-07-01")).astype(int)
    x["migration_era"] = prediction_at.ge(UTC("2025-10-15")).astype(int)
    return x

def targets(m, prediction_at):
    z = m.reindex(prediction_at.index).copy()
    z["prediction_at"], z["deadline"] = prediction_at, prediction_at + H
    z["process_observed_until"] = pd.concat([z["process_cutoff"], z["deadline"]], axis=1).min(axis=1)
    hit = z["disposition_at"].notna() & z["disposition_at"].le(z["process_observed_until"])
    z["stop"] = z["process_observed_until"].where(~hit, z["disposition_at"])
    z["followup_days"] = (z["stop"] - prediction_at).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    z["event_cause"] = z["cause"].where(hit, "censored")
    z["target"] = pd.Series(pd.NA, index=z.index, dtype="string")
    z.loc[~hit & z["deadline"].le(z["process_cutoff"]), "target"] = "open_at_180"
    for cause in ["R", "W", "T"]:
        z.loc[hit & z["cause"].eq(cause), "target"] = cause
    activated = hit & z["cause"].eq("A") & z["observed90"]
    z.loc[activated & z["y"].eq(1), "target"] = "activated_default90"
    z.loc[activated & z["y"].eq(0), "target"] = "activated_nondefault90"
    z["needs_outcome_review"] = z["quarantine"] | z["label_conflict"]
    return z  # Unknown targets remain NA; review flagged histories before fitting.
```
