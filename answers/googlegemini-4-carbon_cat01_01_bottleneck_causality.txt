## 1. Executive causal assessment
My provisional verdict is **`mainly confounded`**, with a confidence level of **70/100** (and secondary support for `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` due to extreme lack of positivity across shifts and sample-selection censoring).

The strongest evidence **for** confounding is a classic Simpson’s-paradox reversal: while Orion’s crude mean cycle time is 6.72 hours longer than Nova’s (21.96h vs. 15.24h), Orion is faster within *every* observed complexity stratum—Low (-2.14h), Medium (-2.21h), and High (-3.43h). Orion receives 42.9% high-complexity cases (vs. Nova’s 12.5%), 92.9% night work (vs. 25.0%), and 64.3% mandatory compliance checks (vs. 25.0%). Moreover, Orion exhibits shorter pre-review waiting times and slightly faster review service times within strata, consistent with its higher share of certified reviewers.

The strongest evidence **against** treating this stratified reversal as proof of Orion’s superiority is severe non-overlap and selection bias. Only 1 of 14 Orion cases occurs on Day shift (C02), and Nova has only 2 High-complexity cases. Furthermore, the dataset contains *only completed cases*; if Orion’s difficult night cases are more frequently still open or abandoned, Orion’s observed completion times could be artificially truncated. Unobserved case risk used by the post-`Classify` routing engine may also differ within coarse complexity labels.

Consequently, blindly reassigning 50% of Orion’s cases to Nova is likely to **backfire**. Shifting high-complexity, compliance-heavy, and night-shift claims to a group with fewer certified reviewers risks inflating review service times, increasing `Request Info` rework loops, creating night-queue bottlenecks for Nova, or degrading 30-day appeal outcomes—unless the intervention is accompanied by night-shift capacity realignment and certification-matched routing. Only if Orion currently suffers from unmeasured open-case backlog saturation would partial load-shedding temporarily help.

### Final Recommendation
Management should **reject the immediate 50% reassignment of Orion cases to Nova**. The proposal rests on an unadjusted ecological fallacy: Orion’s higher overall mean (21.96h vs. 15.24h) is driven by handling over three times the rate of High-complexity cases and nearly four times the rate of Night-shift cases. Within every observed complexity tier, completed Orion cases actually finish **2.1 to 3.4 hours faster** than Nova cases, primarily through shorter pre-review waits and slightly faster review execution. Dumping half of Orion’s complex, night-heavy workload onto Nova—which has fewer certified reviewers and minimal night coverage—risks severe night-queue congestion, longer review times, and higher appeal risk.

However, management should also avoid concluding that Orion is definitively faster until open/censored cases and sub-tier routing scores are audited. Adopt the three-phase learning plan: first instrument step-level timestamps and audit open claims (Weeks 1–2); second, attack the dominant delay driver—pre-review and compliance queueing—directly via capacity balancing (Weeks 3–6); and third, run a guarded 15–20% stratified randomized routing pilot on Medium-complexity claims (Weeks 7–10) to measure true causal effects safely.

    for _, row in strat.iterrows():
        if row["cases"] < 3:
            print(
                f"WARNING [Low Cell Support]: complexity={row['complexity']}, "
                f"resource_group={row['resource_group']} has n={int(row['cases'])} (< 3 cases)."
            )

    # 3. Complexity-standardized Orion-versus-Nova contrast
    print("\n" + "=" * 72)
    print("3. COMPLEXITY-STANDARDIZED CONTRAST (POOLED MARGINAL WEIGHTS)")
    print("=" * 72)
    # Common weighting distribution: empirical marginal complexity shares in the pooled 30-case sample
    weights = df["complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True).reindex(complexity_order)
    piv_means = strat.pivot(
        index="complexity", columns="resource_group", values="mean_cycle_h"
    ).reindex(complexity_order)

    std_orion = float((piv_means["Orion"] * weights).sum())
    std_nova = float((piv_means["Nova"] * weights).sum())
    std_diff = std_orion - std_nova

    print("Weighting distribution (pooled sample P(Complexity)):")
    for comp, w in weights.items():
        n_comp = int((df["complexity"] == comp).sum())
        print(
            f"  {comp:<6}: {n_comp:2d}/30 = {w:.3f} | "
            f"Orion={piv_means.loc[comp, 'Orion']:.2f}h vs Nova={piv_means.loc[comp, 'Nova']:.2f}h "
            f"(diff={piv_means.loc[comp, 'Orion'] - piv_means.loc[comp, 'Nova']:+.2f}h)"
        )
    print(f"\nStandardized Mean Cycle Time (Orion): {std_orion:.2f} hours")
    print(f"Standardized Mean Cycle Time (Nova) : {std_nova:.2f} hours")
    print(
        f"Standardized Contrast (Orion - Nova): {std_diff:+.2f} hours (descriptive, non-causal)"
    )

    # Also report target-population standardization (Orion's own complexity weights, ATT-like standardization)
    orion_weights = (
        df.loc[df["resource_group"] == "Orion", "complexity"]
        .value_counts(normalize=True)
        .reindex(complexity_order)
    )
    att_orion = float((piv_means["Orion"] * orion_weights).sum())
    att_nova = float((piv_means["Nova"] * orion_weights).sum())
    print(
        f"Orion-Weighted Contrast (if Nova faced Orion's complexity mix, Orion - Nova): "
        f"{att_orion - att_nova:+.2f} hours ({att_orion:.2f}h vs {att_nova:.2f}h)"
    )

    # 4. Overlap counts for every combination of complexity, shift, and resource_group
    print("\n" + "=" * 72)
    print("4. POSITIVITY & OVERLAP DIAGNOSTICS (COMPLEXITY x SHIFT x GROUP)")
    print("=" * 72)
    overlap = (
        df.groupby(["complexity", "shift", "resource_group"], observed=False)
        .size()
        .unstack("resource_group", fill_value=0)
        .reset_index()
    )
    overlap["total"] = overlap["Nova"] + overlap["Orion"]
    overlap["support_status"] = np.where(
        (overlap["Nova"] == 0) | (overlap["Orion"] == 0),
        "ZERO OVERLAP (Positivity Violation)",
        np.where(
            (overlap["Nova"] < 3) | (overlap["Orion"] < 3),
            "POOR SUPPORT (<3 in a cell)",
            "ADEQUATE (>=3 per group)",
        ),
    )
    print(overlap.to_string(index=False))

    print("\n--- Positivity & Small-Cell Warnings ---")
    for _, row in overlap.iterrows():
        for grp in ["Nova", "Orion"]:
            cnt = int(row[grp])
            if cnt == 0:
                print(
                    f"WARNING [Zero Positivity]: ({row['complexity']}, {row['shift']}, {grp}) "
                    f"has 0 cases; cross-group comparison in ({row['complexity']}, {row['shift']}) lacks support."
                )
            elif cnt < 3:
                print(
                    f"WARNING [Sparse Cell <3]: ({row['complexity']}, {row['shift']}, {grp}) "
                    f"has only {cnt} case(s)."
                )

    # 5. Process-mining variant and Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) comparisons using PM4Py
    print("\n" + "=" * 72)
    print("5. PM4PY TRACE VARIANTS AND DIRECTLY-FOLLOWS GRAPH (DFG) COMPARISON")
    print("=" * 72)

    variant_table = (
        df.groupby(["trace", "resource_group"])
        .size()
        .unstack("resource_group", fill_value=0)
    )
    variant_table["Total"] = variant_table.sum(axis=1)
    variant_table["Nova_pct"] = (
        100.0 * variant_table["Nova"] / variant_table["Nova"].sum()
    )
    variant_table["Orion_pct"] = (
        100.0 * variant_table["Orion"] / variant_table["Orion"].sum()
    )
    print("\n--- Trace Variant Counts & Group Shares ---")
    print(variant_table.to_string(float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.1f}"))

    for trace_str, row in variant_table.iterrows():
        if row["Nova"] < 3 or row["Orion"] < 3:
            print(
                f"WARNING [Variant Support <3]: Variant '{trace_str}' has "
                f"Nova={int(row['Nova'])}, Orion={int(row['Orion'])}."
            )

    # Parse group-specific event logs via pm4py.parse_event_log_string without fabricating timestamps
    dfg_records = {}
    start_records = {}
    end_records = {}

    for grp in ["Nova", "Orion"]:
        grp_traces = df.loc[df["resource_group"] == grp, "trace"].tolist()
        # parse_event_log_string parses '>' separated activity sequences per line
        event_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string("\n".join(grp_traces), sep=">")
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)
        dfg_records[grp] = dfg
        start_records[grp] = start_acts
        end_records[grp] = end_acts

    all_edges = sorted(set(dfg_records["Nova"].keys()) | set(dfg_records["Orion"].keys()))
    dfg_comp = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "from_activity": edge[0],
                "to_activity": edge[1],
                "Nova_freq": dfg_records["Nova"].get(edge, 0),
                "Orion_freq": dfg_records["Orion"].get(edge, 0),
                "Nova_per_case": dfg_records["Nova"].get(edge, 0) / 16.0,
                "Orion_per_case": dfg_records["Orion"].get(edge, 0) / 14.0,
            }
            for edge in all_edges
        ]
    )
    print("\n--- PM4Py Directly-Follows Relations by Resource Group ---")
    print(dfg_comp.to_string(index=False, float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.2f}"))
    print(f"\nStart activities -> Nova: {start_records['Nova']}, Orion: {start_records['Orion']}")
    print(f"End activities   -> Nova: {end_records['Nova']}, Orion: {end_records['Orion']}")


if __name__ == "__main__":

    run_diagnostics()

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Right-censoring and abandonment in the completed-case sample:** Because open and abandoned claims are omitted, we cannot rule out that Orion’s difficult night claims disproportionately stall in open queues. *Single most valuable addition:* **Complete cohort intake log from `Register` with current case status (`Completed`, `Open`, `Abandoned`) and censoring/cutoff timestamp.**
2. **Unmeasured sub-complexity risk in the routing engine:** Coarse `Low/Medium/High` labels may mask finer claim-severity features used by the post-`Classify` routing engine when selecting Orion vs. Nova. *Single most valuable addition:* **Exact routing-engine input feature vector / risk score (and deterministic vs. fallback rule ID) recorded at `Classify`.**
3. **Conflation of compliance delay and reviewer queue delay inside `wait_before_review_h`:** We cannot separate time spent waiting for/executing `Compliance Check` from time spent waiting for an Orion or Nova reviewer to pick up the file. *Single most valuable addition:* **Activity-level start and complete timestamps for `Classify`, `Compliance Check`, each `Review` pass, and `Request Info`.**
4. **Near-zero shift positivity between groups:** With only one Day-shift Orion case (Low complexity) and zero Day-shift Orion cases in Medium or High complexity, group performance cannot be separated from shift operating conditions non-parametrically. *Single most valuable addition:* **A stratified micro-randomized routing design change (e.g., 15–20% cross-routing within matched Day/Night shift windows for eligible Medium claims).**
5. **Reviewer certification vs. queue staffing bundling:** Even if Orion is genuinely faster, the data cannot distinguish individual reviewer certification efficiency from group-level shift staffing ratios (active WIP per available reviewer). *Single most valuable addition:* **Reviewer ID with certification tier plus real-time active queue length / on-duty headcount at the moment of case assignment.**
