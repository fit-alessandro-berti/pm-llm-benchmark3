## 1. Interaction-graph description

Model the process as a typed property graph, not as six independent job traces. Let **[O]** mean directly observed relation/event linkage and **[I]** mean inferred coupling from timing/resource feasibility.

**Important node types:** Job, Component, ComponentLot, Machine, Tool, Batch, Shipment.

**Important edge types and qualifiers:**

- **[O] Job  Component**: `requires`
- **[O] ComponentLot  Component**: `contains`
- **[O] Batch  Job**: `batches`
- **[O] Shipment  Job**: `consolidates`
- **[O] Job/Component/Batch  Machine/Tool**: use, process, cure, test, calibrate, acquire, release
- **[O] ComponentLot/Component  quarantine/release events**
- **[I] Job  Job/Resource**: `waits_for`, `synchronized_with`, `blocked_by` inferred from timestamps and resource states

**Higher-order motifs:**

1. **Lot–Component–Job–Batch–Machine/Tool motif:** L7 contains C2/C5; C2 belongs to J2 and C5 to J5; J2 is in B1 while J5 is in B2; both batches use OVEN-1 and RACK-7. Lot quarantine therefore couples jobs in different batches.
2. **Batch–Job–Component–Tool–Machine motif:** B1 bundles J1/C1, J2/C2, J3/C3; the single `Batch Cure` at 12:05 involves B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7 and all three jobs/components. One batch event hides three different readiness times.
3. **Tool–Machine–Job–Batch motif:** J3 holds FIX-A until 11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 but starts with FIX-A only at 11:45; J4 later becomes part of B2. A fixture hold propagates into batch readiness.
4. **Tool–Machine–Job–Shipment motif:** J5/J6 require TEST-1 and CAL-3; CAL-3 recalibration delays J6 after J5; J5/J6 are then both in S3. Test-tool sequencing propagates into shipment consolidation.
5. **Job–Batch–Shipment motif:** J1 is in B1 but ships in S1 with J4; J4 is delayed elsewhere and packed at 19:10, so J1’s completion is tied to a job from a different batch.

**Compact edge list:**

- **[O]** J1 `requires` C1; J2 `requires` C2; J3 `requires` C3; J4 `requires` C4; J5 `requires` C5; J6 `requires` C6.
- **[O]** L7 `contains` C2, C5; L8 `contains` C1, C3.
- **[O]** B1 `batches` J1, J2, J3; B2 `batches` J4, J5.
- **[O]** S1 `consolidates` J1, J4; S2 `consolidates` J2, J3; S3 `consolidates` J5, J6.
- **[O]** B1 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7 at 12:05; B2 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7 at 14:50.
- **[O]** J3 `acquires` FIX-A 09:00; J3 `releases` FIX-A 11:40; J4 `Machine Process` with CNC-2/FIX-A starts 11:45.
- **[O]** L7/C2/C5 `Quarantine Lot` 10:20; `Release Lot` 13:50. Job linkage is inconsistent: job flattening omits these; an alternate pipeline would attach them to J2/J5; J2 has a separate supervisor override.
- **[O]** J5 `Final Test` TEST-1/CAL-3 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 `Final Test` 17:25–17:50.
- **[O]** J1 packed 13:30; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatch 19:25.
- **[I]** J1/J2 `synchronized_with` J3 in B1; J4 `blocked_by` RACK-7 then J5 in B2; J4 `waits_for` FIX-A; J6 `blocked_by` CAL-3 recalibration; J1 `waits_for` J4 in S1.

Low interaction counts do not imply low impact: a single batch, lot, tool, or shipment edge can create hours of waiting.

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | What it makes visible | What it hides or duplicates | Misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Job stages, medians, rework loop, six similar total durations | Cross-job waits from batches, tools, lots, shipment partners; lot events omitted; tool waits have no activity | “No job is anomalous, so flow is stable.” J1’s 5 h 55 min S1 wait and J4’s FIX-A wait are not represented as dependencies. |
| Machine | Utilization, activity sequences, machine-event gaps | Why a job is absent from a machine: batch incomplete, RACK-7 cleaning, FIX-A held, lot quarantined | “Low utilization means no bottleneck.” OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 while RACK-7 is unavailable is not spare capacity. |
| Batch | Batch membership, cure start/end, oven/rack use at batch level | Different job readiness times; per-job waits; one batch event may be duplicated across members or collapsed into one cure | “Batch cure is normal”; hides J1/J2 waiting 3 h 55/3 h 40 for B1. |
| Tool | Acquire/release/recalibration/cleaning states | Which queued job was blocked; inferred holds may be mistaken for actual waits; recalibration may be missing | “Tools are lightly used”; FIX-A hold and CAL-3 recalibration can dominate job waiting despite few events. |
| ComponentLot | L7/L8 containment, quarantine/release events | Inconsistent source-to-job linkage; C2/J2 override; component-to-batch propagation | “Only four lot interactions, therefore low impact.” L7 quarantine lasts 3 h 30 min and affects different batches. |
| Shipment | CreateLoadDispatch per shipment | Already-packed job waiting for partners; J1’s wait for J4; different consolidation rules | “S1 dispatch took only 15 min after last pack.” J1 waited 5 h 55 min from pack to dispatch. |

Low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting because waiting often occurs **off-machine**. A job can be ready but not machine-eligible until a batch is complete, a fixture is released, a lot is released, or a shipment partner arrives. Machines then appear idle while the constraint is a synchronization rule or auxiliary resource state.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative A — B1 synchronization  
**Types:** Job, Component, ComponentLot, Batch, Tool, Machine.

- **Temporal precedence:** J1/C1 ready 08:10; J2/C2 ready 08:25; J3/C3 ready 11:55; B1 cures at 12:05 on OVEN-1/RACK-7.
- **Resource feasibility:** A single 12:05 cure event links B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3 and C1–C3, so all three jobs become coupled at that event.
- **Policy constraints:** Possible full-batch rule; L8 links C1/C3 and L7 links C2, adding lot genealogy to the batch.
- **Causal speculation:** J3’s late readiness may have created J1/J2 waiting; not proven because readiness trust and batch-release policy are not logged.

### Narrative B — FIX-A to CNC-2 to B2  
**Types:** Job, Component, Tool, Machine, Batch.

- **Temporal precedence:** J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4/C4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; J4 starts 11:45 with CNC-2/FIX-A; J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10.
- **Resource feasibility:** From 10:05 CNC-2 could plausibly process J4, but FIX-A remains unavailable until 11:40.
- **Policy constraints:** Tool ownership is non-preemptive; no `Wait for Tool` activity exists.
- **Causal speculation:** FIX-A contention plausibly delayed J4 and therefore downstream B2 readiness; alternative explanations include scheduling priority or unlogged setup.

### Narrative C — L7 quarantine with override  
**Types:** ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Machine, Tool.

- **Temporal precedence:** L7/C2/C5 quarantined 10:20; J2 enters B1 at 12:05; L7 released 13:50; J5 ready for B2 at 14:40; B2 starts 14:50 on OVEN-1/RACK-7.
- **Resource feasibility:** C5 availability after 13:50 is compatible with J5’s later readiness.
- **Policy constraints:** Lot quarantine should block C2/C5 use, but J2 used C2 in B1 before release due to supervisor override in another system.
- **Causal speculation:** The lot likely constrained J5, but J2 shows the rule can be bypassed; the job-level absence of lot events makes the graph convention decisive.

### Narrative D — CAL-3 to TEST-1 to S3  
**Types:** Job, Component, Tool, Machine, Shipment.

- **Temporal precedence:** J5/C5 and J6/C6 ready 16:00/16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 consolidates J5/J6.
- **Resource feasibility:** TEST-1 is free 16:35–17:25, but every Final Test requires CAL-3.
- **Policy constraints:** Mandatory recalibration creates tool-state unavailability; S3 waits for both jobs.
- **Causal speculation:** CAL-3 recalibration plausibly delayed J6; if safety rules require it, the issue is capacity/scheduling of calibration, not misbehavior.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
from collections import Counter

# ocel is assumed to be an existing PM4Py OCEL object.

# 1. Object interaction and object graph views
oi_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
objects_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# 2. Object-centric process graph
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)

# 3. Optional bipartite/network view
try:
    nx_ocel = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
except Exception:
    nx_ocel = None

# 4. Flattened baselines only; these are not used as proof of waiting.
job_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
machine_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Machine")

job_dfg, job_start, job_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(job_log)
machine_dfg, machine_start, machine_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(machine_log)

# 5. Feature extraction for downstream wait models
job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, object_type="Job")
tool_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, object_type="Tool")

# Helpers for comparing object-centric frequency metrics.
def graph_size(x):
    if x is None:
        return 0
    if hasattr(x, "number_of_edges"):
        return x.number_of_edges()
    if hasattr(x, "edges"):
        return len(x.edges)
    if isinstance(x, dict):
        return len(x)
    try:
        return len(x)
    except TypeError:
        return 0

def event_object_types(ocel, ev):
    # Accessor names vary slightly by PM4Py/OCEL version.
    objs = ocel.event_to_obj[ev] if hasattr(ocel, "event_to_obj") else ocel.events[ev].objects
    if hasattr(ocel, "obj_to_type"):
        return {ocel.obj_to_type[o] for o in objs}
    return {o.split(":")[0] for o in objs}

# Metric A: object-object interaction edges.
metric_object_edges = graph_size(objects_graph)

# Metric B: object transition graph edges.
metric_otg_edges = graph_size(otg)

# Metric C: multi-type event frequency, a higher-order object-centric metric.
multitype_counts = Counter()
for ev in ocel.events:
    tset = event_object_types(ocel, ev)
    if len(tset) >= 3:
        multitype_counts[tuple(sorted(tset))] += 1

metric_multitype_events = sum(multitype_counts.values())

comparison = pd.DataFrame([
    {"metric": "object_interaction_edges", "value": metric_object_edges},
    {"metric": "object_transition_graph_edges", "value": metric_otg_edges},
    {"metric": "events_with_3plus_object_types", "value": metric_multitype_events},
    {"metric": "ocdfg_size", "value": graph_size(ocdfg)},
]).sort_values("value", ascending=False)

print(comparison)
print("Top multi-type motifs:")
for motif, freq in multitype_counts.most_common(10):
    print(freq, motif)

# Important: machine_dfg gaps are treated only as flattened baselines.
# Waiting requires explicit readiness, batch, lot, tool-state, or shipment evidence.
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked **batch synchronization** explanation:

1. J1/J2 “ready for batch” timestamps are administrative, not true material/tool-cleared readiness.
2. B1 could legally cure as a partial batch, and no quality or recipe rule required J3.
3. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable from 08:10–12:05 for unlogged maintenance or another batch.
4. C1/C2 were not physically available until shortly before 12:05 despite job-level readiness.
5. J3’s 11:55 readiness is itself misrecorded; true readiness is 12:05 or later.
6. Similar readiness spreads in other batches produce no comparable waiting.
7. J1/J2 had no downstream need for earlier processing, so the “delay” had no operational effect.

Fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. `Job Batch Ready` timestamps with reason codes.
2. Batch recipe constraints: minimum/maximum batch size, compatible components, fixed cure windows.
3. Resource-state calendars for OVEN-1 and RACK-7: cleaning, quarantine, maintenance, unlogged use.
4. `Wait for Tool` events with requester Job, requested Tool, start/end and priority.
5. Tool acquisition/release events with intended versus actual job.
6. Lot event-object links emitted directly to Jobs and Components, not only to lots.
7. Supervisor override details: operator, reason, approved replacement component, timestamp.
8. Component inspection/release timestamps and alternate lot availability.
9. Shipment rules: customer promise, consolidation window, freight cutoff, pack-to-load policy.
10. CAL-3 calibration rules, expiry timestamps, and availability of alternate calibrators.
