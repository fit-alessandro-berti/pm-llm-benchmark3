# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## 2.1 Fast emergency approvals: Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo

### Benign explanation 1: Emergency handling is the real cause
These cases are concentrated in urgent medical supplies and weekend facility repairs. The `emergency_flag` is true in 79–88% of cases, and Manager_Hugo / Approver_Gita are designated delegates for urgent approvals. Fast approvals may reflect a pre-approved emergency channel.

- **Supporting evidence:** `emergency_flag=True`, emergency override forms, time-of-day / weekend timestamps, urgent purchase categories, and a corresponding Retrospective Justification.
- **Refuting evidence:** Fast approvals in cases without any emergency flag, missing emergency override documents, or approvals by people without delegated authority.

### Benign explanation 2: Informal specialization / fixed team routing
Buyers and approvers may be assigned to stable workflow queues. Buyer_Carla always routes to Approver_Gita because Gita covers the urgent medical-supply queue; Buyer_Dan routes to Manager_Hugo because Hugo handles weekend repair escalations. This is organizational routing, not personal collusion.

- **Supporting evidence:** An authorization matrix, team assignment list, or process model showing functional routing; similar routine pairs (AlineElena, BorisFelix) also have high counts.
- **Refuting evidence:** Evidence that cases are intentionally routed around controls, that approvals are made outside the assigned queue, or that the same individuals self-approve after mapping errors are corrected.

### Benign explanation 3: Shared-account data quality
Some emergency approvals use the shared account “Approver_Gita” while the detailed audit trail shows assistants in `entered_by`. If the shared account is not resolved to a person, “fast approvals” may reflect the assistant’s data entry, not the actual approver’s decision.

- **Supporting evidence:** Login/access logs showing multiple users on the Gita account; `entered_by` values distinct from `org:resource`.
- **Refuting evidence:** Unique accounts or access logs show the named approver alone performed and approved the action.

## 2.2 VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim: fast bank changes and payments

### Benign explanation 1: Legitimate mass maintenance from the banking-platform migration
Vendor family V-77x recently changed banking platforms. VendorMaster_Lena may have performed authorized mass-maintenance updates to bank details. AP_Kim may then process payments for those vendors as part of the normal post-update payment cycle. The 17-minute median time may reflect a single batch process, not a suspicious handover.

- **Supporting evidence:** Maintenance ticket numbers, batch IDs, bank confirmation letters, effective dates, and upload manifests showing bulk changes.
- **Refuting evidence:** No maintenance ticket or bank confirmation; changes are individual, unauthorized, or not part of the migration scope.

### Benign explanation 2: Automated exception routing to AP_Kim
Bot_Match is heavily connected to AP_Kim. Kim may be the AP resource who handles exception payments and higher-risk suppliers. Lena updates vendor master data; Kim pays after automated invoice match. The handover is therefore part of a legitimate operational workflow.

- **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Match exception records, invoice-matching audit trails, Kim’s role description as risk-specialist AP.
- **Refuting evidence:** Payments are released without a valid invoice match, approval, or supporting purchase order.

### Benign explanation 3: Missing linkage between maintenance ticket and procurement case
The event log may simply lack the link between the vendor-master maintenance ticket and the procurement case. The manual_upload source system may create a separate set of event timestamps, making legitimate changes look like same-day bank-to-payment sequences.

- **Supporting evidence:** Manual upload manifests, ticket IDs in source files, or a reconciliation table linking maintenance IDs to cases.
- **Refuting evidence:** No source file or ticket exists, or the upload manifest contains unsupported changes.

## 2.3 Same-person Retrospective Justification by Hugo/Gita

### Benign explanation 1: Batch data-entry / quarter-close cleanup
12 of 17 justifications have identical timestamps to the minute and came from `source_system="quarter_close_upload"`. This suggests a batch import from a spreadsheet, not 17 real-time decisions by the same person.

- **Supporting evidence:** Upload manifest, spreadsheet revision history, identical timestamps, and migration records.
- **Refuting evidence:** Evidence that each entry was made interactively by the named resource at the recorded time.

### Benign explanation 2: Shared credentials or assistant entry
If the “Manager_Hugo” or “Approver_Gita” accounts are shared, the same-resource flag may be an artifact of account sharing, not actual same-person action.

- **Supporting evidence:** Access logs, login records, and `entered_by` values showing different actual users.
- **Refuting evidence:** Unique credentials were used for both events.

### Benign explanation 3: Post-hoc administration of emergency approvals
Managers may have verbally approved urgent overrides during the quarter and only later entered the justifications. This is still a control weakness, but it is different from deliberate concealment.

- **Supporting evidence:** Emails, chat records, or contemporaneous notes showing approval before the official system timestamp.
- **Refuting evidence:** No contemporaneous evidence; justifications were created solely after quarter close with no underlying emergency record.

## 2.4 Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by the same person

### Benign explanation 1: Role design
VendorMaster_Lena and VendorMaster_Marc may both be expected to verify and maintain vendor master data. If the organization has not defined “Vendor Check” and “Change Vendor Bank Details” as separate makers/checkers, this is a design issue, not a fraudulent pattern.

- **Supporting evidence:** Role descriptions, segregation-of-duties matrix, and process documentation.
- **Refuting evidence:** Policy clearly requires different people for these activities.

### Benign explanation 2: Mapping or system artifact
In `legacy_erp`, the same `org:resource` may be recorded for different actual users because of a mapping error.

- **Supporting evidence:** Source-system mapping tables, audit trail fields showing different actual users.
- **Refuting evidence:** Corrected mappings still show the same person performing both activities.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- **Maintenance tickets or bank confirmations are missing** for V-77x bank-detail changes, especially where payments followed within minutes.
- **Unique-account access logs** show the same human actually created and approved purchase orders in the same case.
- **Retrospective justifications are absent** or were created only after an audit request, not after the emergency.
- **Manual upload manifests** show unauthorized vendor changes, changes outside the migration scope, or changes made by someone without vendor-master authority.
- **Approvals are not supported by emergency override forms** in emergency-flagged cases.
- **Vendor bank details are changed to accounts not appearing in the migration list**, or the same new account is used across unrelated vendors.
- **Payment approvals bypass the invoice-match step**, or Bot_Match exceptions are overridden without justification.
- **Source-system mapping corrections still leave real same-person four-eyes violations**.
- **Access logs show one person in both VendorMaster and AP roles**, or someone using another user’s credentials.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- **Emergency tickets, override forms, and contemporaneous approvals** exist for Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo cases.
- **Maintenance tickets and bank confirmations** account for all V-77x vendor-bank changes.
- **The mapping table error explains the bot/human same-resource flags**; after correction, different people perform Create PO and Approve PO.
- **Batch-entry documentation** explains the identical timestamps in Retrospective Justification cases.
- **Access logs show shared accounts were used by multiple data-entry staff**, and the named approver did not personally perform the recorded action.
- **The V-77x changes were all part of one sanctioned migration**, and no unauthorized vendor accounts were added.
- **Post-change reviews or reconciliations** were performed and cleared.
- **Vendor bank changes are linked to maintenance ticket IDs** in the full source system, even if the link is missing from the procurement log.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The procurement-to-payment event log contains anomalies that warrant follow-up, but the current evidence is not sufficient to conclude collusion or fraud. Most fast approvals and resource loops are concentrated in emergency medical-supply and weekend-repair channels, where delegated approvals and Emergency Override have plausible operational explanations. High co-working frequencies in routine buyer-approver pairs are also consistent with stable team routing. In addition, several four-eyes flags are weakened by data-quality problems: 23 emergency approvals used a shared “Approver_Gita” account, and bot/human mapping errors in `legacy_erp` can create false same-resource sequences. The overall evidence is therefore more consistent with emergency handling, informal specialization, and data-quality issues than with collusion.

The most concerning subgroup is VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim. It combines high-value vendor family V-77x concentration, a `manual_upload` source system, fast bank-detail-to-payment handovers, a correction loop, and no documented link to the banking-platform migration. This does not prove wrongdoing; it is a high-priority control review because the evidence trail is incomplete. The second priority is the 17 same-person Retrospective Justification cases, 12 of which were batch-entered after quarter close; these should be traced to underlying emergency justifications and access logs.

Lower-priority follow-up includes the Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo emergency approval channels and the 42 Create PO / Approve PO flags. We recommend targeted document review — bank-change tickets, vendor confirmations, emergency requests, uploaded spreadsheet manifests, and user access logs — before drawing conclusions. Until data-quality issues are resolved, control exception counts should be treated as upper bounds, not confirmed violations.
