## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree

Treat the raw event table and its E2O relations as immutable. Apply the following tree **before** ordering or process mining:

1. **Is this a repeated technical record from the same source?**
   - Same `source`, `source_event_key`, business timestamp, activity, payload, and other immutable business content: **drop the redundant delivery copy**, retaining its identifier and payload in the audit trail.
   - Same source key but conflicting content: **quarantine for review**. Do not choose a winner using ingestion time.
   - Equal activity/timestamp alone is insufficient. In this dataset, there are no strongly supported exact technical duplicates.

2. **Is there affirmative evidence of the same physical/business event?**
   - A unique domain identity, source reconciliation, or equivalent verified evidence: **merge attributes and relations**.
   - Use e03/e04’s reconciliation evidence and e11/e16’s invoice identity.
   - Different payload hashes do not disprove a verified identity; a WMS location enrichment can coexist with the scanner’s observation.

3. **Is one event an independently verified summary derived from another?**
   - Verified parent/derivation evidence, compatible attributes, and compatible relations: **merge**, retaining `detail` and `summary` roles.
   - A summary label or later generation alone is insufficient. Consequently, e06/e07 and e13/e15 remain separate in the conservative scenario.

4. **Do original source semantics distinguish the harmonized activities?**
   - Warehouse release and carrier acceptance are different observations: **retain both**, refining labels to `Warehouse Release` and `Carrier Accept`.
   - Sharing every modeled object does not establish identity.

5. **Are they same-label events involving different business objects or quantities?**
   - e03 versus e05, or e13 versus e14: **retain both unchanged**, optionally adding object-specific qualifiers.
   - Never infer deduplication from `have_common_object=True`.

6. **Is a candidate otherwise unresolved?**
   - **Quarantine the proposed identity linkage for review**, not the original events.
   - Original events remain available to a non-deduplicating working view; a validated analytical view may exclude quarantined records explicitly.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules

Construct a directed acyclic graph and take a deterministic topological sort.

Use these rules in precedence order:

1. **Verified domain constraints — semantic.** e12 precedes e13; e13 and e14 precede the ERP payment summary e15. Verified derivations also constrain e06 before e07.
2. **Within-source sequence — semantic only within that source.** For example, scanner e04 precedes e05; PSP e12, e13, e14 are sequential; WMS e03 precedes e06; carrier e09 precedes e10. Never compare ERP sequence 30 directly with carrier sequence 1.
3. **Lifecycle constraints on shared objects — conditional semantic assumptions.** If `Pick Item` means a completed pick, require both known picks before packing. This assumption yields e05  e06, but should be disabled or reviewed if the timestamps represent activity start rather than completion.
4. **Source-specific semantics — semantic distinctions, not universal ordering rules.** Release, acceptance, and handover are distinct. Only the supplied acceptance-before-handover constraint is imposed between them.
5. **Ingestion time — never a business-order rule.** It may appear in an audit display, but must not resolve a semantic tie.
6. **Stable fallback — deterministic only.** Here, use canonical `eid` ascending. An optional configured source priority must be followed by `eid`; it is not evidence.

Conflicts between verified constraints should stop the ordering or quarantine the affected records, not be silently broken by a tie-breaker.

| Time | Intended conservative order | Important alternatives/qualification |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00 | e03, e05, e06, e07 | e04 is represented by canonical e03. e05 may precede e03 if pick-before-pack is retained; e06 follows both. e07 is an update, not another confirmed physical packing step. |
| 10:15 | e08, e09, e10 | e09 must precede e10. No supplied fact requires e08 before e09. Thus e09, e10, e08 and e09, e08, e10 are admissible, provided the timestamp grouping is not itself treated as a proven lifecycle constraint. |
| 12:00 | e11, e12, e13, e14, e15 | e16 is represented by e11. e12 must precede e13; e13 precedes e14 by PSP sequence; both captures precede e15. Admissible examples include e11, e14, e12, e13, e15 and e12, e14, e13, e15, e11. No ordering between e11 and the payment events is established. |

Adding microseconds **before** duplicate decisions or constraint construction makes previously simultaneous observations appear causally ordered. That can falsely turn e13/e14 into retries, e06/e07 into rework, or e08/e09 into a warehouse-to-carrier sequence.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline

The minimum-change pipeline is:

1. Preserve the raw OCEL and E2O ledger unchanged.
2. Identify candidate groups, but merge only e03/e04 and e11/e16.
3. Refine e08 to `Warehouse Release` and e09 to `Carrier Accept`; retain e10 separately.
4. Attach observation qualifiers such as `WMS packing detail`, `ERP packing summary`, `M1 capture`, `M2 capture`, and `invoice-level ERP payment summary`.
5. Build the partial order and choose a deterministic linear extension.
6. Introduce synthetic ordering timestamps only after semantic decisions. Retain the original timestamp, tie status, constraint reason, and the original source event key.
7. Validate objects, relations, metadata, and preserved payment identities.

**Expected retained event count: 15.** Only e04 and e16 cease to be standalone canonical events. In particular, e05, e07, e08, e09, e10, e14, and e15 are not removed.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives

### Evidence-based semantic merging

Start with the two verified identities, then merge e06/e07 and e13/e15 **only if additional reconciliation establishes their derivation and abstraction relationship**. Preserve detail/summary roles and source-attributed attributes.

**Expected count: 13 events.** This is defensible only with new evidence; “summary” and “generated later” are not sufficient identity proofs.

### Mechanically aggressive PM4Py merging

Call `ocel_merge_duplicates` without custom identity verification, using the coarse `Order` scope. The same-label/time groups are:

- e03/e04/e05;
- e06/e07;
- e08/e09;
- e11/e16;
- e13/e14/e15.

**Expected count: 10 events.** The most likely damage is elimination of the I2 pick as an independent observation, collapse of M1 and M2 captures, conversion of logistics observations into one step, and loss of source-specific attributes or conflicting histories.

`have_common_object=True` cannot protect e13/e14: they share O1 and V1, so an “any common object” test returns true despite different Payment identities. Even requiring equality of all currently modeled objects would not distinguish e08/e09. A useful comparator must use the appropriate business identity, such as Payment M1 versus M2, and verified source semantics. `object_type="Payment"` is a useful restricted library scope, but is still not universal proof of event identity.

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation

The following targets the PM4Py 2.1 API and standard `_event_id`, `_object_type`, `_object_value` E2O columns. It deliberately validates the aggressive library result rather than assuming every PM4Py release has identical merge semantics. The guarded `ocel_drop_duplicates` call is a **no-op safety demonstration**: the supplied data contains no verified exact technical-retry group.

```python
import copy
import heapq
import json

import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel import OCEL

REL = ["_event_id", "_object_type", "_object_value"]
META = [
    "eid", "original_timestamp", "original_activity",
    "source", "source_event_key", "source_seq",
    "ingested_at", "payload_hash"
]
RULESET = "conservative-v1 / partial-order-v1"


def validate(o):
    e = o.events
    assert e["eid"].is_unique and e["_id"].is_unique
    assert e[META].notna().all().all()
    assert e["timestamp"].notna().all()

    r = o.e2o_relations
    assert r[REL].duplicated().sum() == 0
    assert set(r["_event_id"]) <= set(e["_id"])

    for typ, g in r.groupby("_object_type"):
        assert typ in o.object_types
        assert set(g["_object_value"]) <= set(o.object_types[typ])

    # Every supplied event has at least one object reference.
    assert set(r["_event_id"]) == set(e["_id"])
    return o


def rebuild(frame, relations, object_types):
    d = copy.deepcopy(frame).reset_index(drop=True)
    d["_id"] = d["eid"].astype(str)
    d["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(d["timestamp"], utc=True)
    o = OCEL(events=d, object_types=copy.deepcopy(object_types))
    o.e2o_relations = copy.deepcopy(relations[REL]).reset_index(drop=True)
    return validate(o)


def audit_rows(frame):
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "canonical_event_id": frame["eid"],
        "source_event_ids": frame["eid"].map(
            lambda x: json.dumps([str(x)])
        ),
        "source_event_id": frame["eid"],
        "source": frame["source"],
        "source_event_key": frame["source_event_key"],
        "original_timestamp": frame["original_timestamp"],
        "original_activity": frame["original_activity"],
        "observation_role": "independent",
        "merge_rule": "retained",
        "reviewer_or_rule_version": RULESET
    })


def verified_merge(o, groups, representatives, rule, confidence):
    """Merge only explicitly approved groups. No library-wide inference."""
    e = o.events.copy()
    rec = {x: row for x, row in zip(e["eid"], e.to_dict("records"))}
    keep_ids = set(e["eid"]) - set(groups)
    audit = audit_rows(e[e["eid"].isin(keep_ids)])
    output = e[e["eid"].isin(keep_ids)].copy()
    relations = o.e2o_relations.copy()

    excluded = {
        "eid", "_id", "activity", "related_objects",
        "timestamp", "original_timestamp", "original_activity"
    }
    value_fields = [c for c in e.columns if c not in excluded]

    for group in sorted(groups):
        ids = sorted(map(str, groups[group]))
        keep = representatives.get(group, min(ids))
        row = copy.deepcopy(rec[keep])
        conflicts = []

        for field in value_fields:
            candidates = {
                i: rec[i].get(field) for i in ids
                if pd.notna(rec[i].get(field))
            }
            distinct = {
                json.dumps(v, default=str) for v in candidates.values()
            }
            if len(distinct) > 1:
                row[field] = None       # Do not invent a conflict resolution.
                conflicts.append({
                    "field": field, "candidates": candidates
                })

        row["eid"] = keep
        row["_id"] = keep
        row["source_event_ids"] = json.dumps(ids)
        row["merge_rule"] = rule
        row["merge_confidence"] = confidence
        row["conflicts_not_selected"] = json.dumps(conflicts, default=str)
        output = pd.concat([output, pd.DataFrame([row])], ignore_index=True)

        # Union only within the approved identity group.
        relations = relations[~relations["_event_id"].isin(ids)]
        pairs = (
            o.e2o_relations[o.e2o_relations["_event_id"].isin(ids)]
            [["_object_type", "_object_value"]]
            .drop_duplicates()
        )
        for pair in pairs.to_dict("records"):
            relations.loc[len(relations)] = {
                "_event_id": keep,
                "_object_type": pair["_object_type"],
                "_object_value": pair["_object_value"],
            }

        audit.loc[len(audit)] = {
            "canonical_event_id": keep,
            "source_event_ids": json.dumps(ids),
            "source_event_id": keep,
            "source": row["source"],
            "source_event_key": row["source_event_key"],
            "original_timestamp": row["original_timestamp"],
            "original_activity": row["original_activity"],
            "observation_role": "merged",
            "merge_rule": rule,
            "reviewer_or_rule_version": RULESET,
        }

    return rebuild(output, relations, o.object_types), audit, relations


# Independent working copy; ocel_raw and its E2O ledger are never modified.
base = copy.deepcopy(ocel_raw)
relations = copy.deepcopy(base.e2o_relations[REL])
relations["_event_id"] = relations["_event_id"].astype(str)
relations["_object_value"] = relations["_object_value"].astype(str)
base = rebuild(base.events, relations, base.object_types)

# Materialize the displayed related-object field from the authoritative E2O copy.
object_description = base.e2o_relations.groupby("_event_id").apply(
    lambda g: ";".join(sorted(
        set(g["_object_type"] + ":" + g["_object_value"])
    ))
)
base.events["related_objects"] = base.events["eid"].map(object_description)

# PRE-CHECK: inspect, rather than automatically merge, same-label/time groups.
for (ts, act), g in base.events.groupby(
    ["timestamp", "activity"], sort=True
):
    print(ts, act, list(g["eid"]), list(g["source"]))

# This is a narrow library guard, not a semantic deduplication method.
exact_fields = [
    "source", "source_event_key", "timestamp",
    "activity", "payload_hash", "related_objects"
]
assert not base.events.duplicated(exact_fields, keep=False).any()

drop_scenario = pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(
    copy.deepcopy(base),
    activity_column="activity",
    timestamp_column="timestamp",
    object_type="Payment"
)
validate(drop_scenario)
# Fail closed if the supplied scenario unexpectedly drops an unapproved event.
assert set(drop_scenario.events["eid"]) == set(base.events["eid"])


def linearize(o):
    d = o.events.copy()
    alias = {"e04": "e03", "e16": "e11"}  # Verified canonical representatives.
    edges = {
        ("e03", "e06"), ("e05", "e06"),  # Pick-before-pack assumption
        ("e06", "e07"), ("e09", "e10"),
        ("e12", "e13"), ("e13", "e15"), ("e14", "e15")
    }
    linear_ids = []

    for _, g in d.groupby("timestamp", sort=True):
        adj = {alias.get(str(x), str(x)): set() for x in g["eid"]}
        indegree = {x: 0 for x in adj}

        # Source sequence is used only between adjacent events of one source.
        for _, sg in g.groupby("source", sort=False):
            ss = sg.sort_values(["source_seq", "eid"], kind="mergesort")
            for a, b in zip(ss["eid"], ss["eid"].iloc[1:]):
                a, b = alias.get(str(a), str(a)), alias.get(str(b), str(b))
                if (a, b) not in adj[a]:
                    adj[a].add(b)
                    indegree[b] += 1

        for a, b in edges:
            a, b = alias.get(a, a), alias.get(b, b)
            if a in adj and b in adj and (a, b) not in adj[a]:
                adj[a].add(b)
                indegree[b] += 1

        # Stable fallback: canonical eid. Never use ingested_at.
        heap = [(x, x) for x, dgree in indegree.items() if dgree == 0]
        heapq.heapify(heap)
        local = []
        while heap:
            _, a = heapq.heappop(heap)
            local.append(a)
            for b in sorted(adj[a]):
                indegree[b] -= 1
                if indegree[b] == 0:
                    heapq.heappush(heap, (b, b))

        if len(local) != len(adj):
            raise ValueError("Contradictory ordering constraints; review required.")
        linear_ids.extend(local)

    result = d.set_index("eid").loc[linear_ids].reset_index()
    result["order_index"] = range(1, len(result) + 1)

    ordered = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(
        rebuild(result, o.e2o_relations, o.object_types),
        "order_index",
        ascending=True
    )

    # Synthetic deltas are introduced only after the order is established.
    shifted = pm4py.ocel_add_index_based_timedelta(
        ordered,
        index_column="order_index",
        timestamp_column="timestamp"
    )
    validate(shifted)

    # Normalize the release-dependent library step to an explicit 1-us step.
    # This is a display key, never an observed event-time measurement.
    z = shifted.events.set_index("eid").loc[ordered.events["eid"]].reset_index()
    first = ordered.events.sort_values("order_index")["timestamp"].iloc[0]
    synthetic = [
        first + pd.Timedelta(microseconds=i)
        for i in range(1, len(z) + 1)
    ]
    z["timestamp"] = synthetic
    z["synthetic_step_us"] = z["order_index"].astype("int64") * 1000
    z["timestamp_is_synthetic"] = True
    return rebuild(z, shifted.e2o_relations, shifted.object_types)


ordered_raw = linearize(base)

conservative, conservative_audit, conservative_relations = verified_merge(
    copy.deepcopy(base),
    {"e03": ["e04"], "e11": ["e16"]},
    {"e03": "e03", "e11": "e11"},
    "verified physical/invoice identity",
    "high"
)
conservative = linearize(conservative)

# Hypothetical evidence-based scenario, not the minimum-change conclusion.
evidence, evidence_audit, evidence_relations = verified_merge(
    copy.deepcopy(conservative),
    {"e06": ["e07"], "e13": ["e15"]},
    {"e06": "e06", "e13": "e13"},
    "newly verified derivation evidence",
    "scenario-dependent"
)
evidence = linearize(evidence)

# WARNING: coarse identity rule, arbitrary conflicts, and potentially lost lineage.
aggressive = pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(
    copy.deepcopy(ordered_raw),
    activity_column="activity",
    timestamp_column="timestamp",
    object_type="Order"
)
validate(aggressive)
# Pin/verify the documented merge scenario; do not silently accept another.
assert len(aggressive.events) == 10
assert len(aggressive.e2o_relations) == 29
aggressive = linearize(aggressive)

for scenario in [drop_scenario, conservative, evidence, aggressive]:
    validate(scenario)
    assert {"e05", "e14"} <= set(scenario.events["eid"])
    print(len(scenario.events), len(scenario.e2o_relations), pm4py.__version__)

print(conservative.events[
    ["eid", "activity", "order_index", "original_timestamp"]
])
```

## 7. Sensitivity analysis

Counts below include the initial 17 events. E2O counts use **set-union within each merged event**; attributable, source-specific relation assertions remain in the provenance ledger.

| Dataset | Events / canonical E2O relations | Main flattened-DFG and metric changes |
|---|---:|---|
| Raw | 17 / 44 | Three Pick Item observations, two Dispatch Package observations, two invoice issuances, and three Capture Payment observations. Repeated scans, parallel logistics observations, and summary/detail pairs can inflate frequencies and weights. |
| Conservative | 15 / 39 | Removes two supported duplicate assertions, reducing Pick Item to two and Issue Invoice to one. Distinct payments, packing observations, release, acceptance, and handover remain. |
| Evidence-based merge | 13 / 35 | Additionally removes one packing and one capture observation by treating them as summaries. Event-transition weights fall, but abstraction-level execution is no longer directly visible. |
| Aggressive library merge | 10 / 29 | Leaves one observation per affected label/time group. This is the smallest log, but not necessarily the truest business history. |

Specific edge changes include:

1. **`Pick Item  Pick Item`:** raw 1, conservative/evidence/aggressive 0. The raw edge is a duplicate scan followed by the I2 pick, not necessarily rework.
2. **`Pick Item  Pack Package`:** raw 6, conservative 2, evidence 2, aggressive 0. These counts arise from flattening tied observations; they are not verified completion counts.
3. **`Dispatch Package  Dispatch Package`:** raw 1; zero thereafter when release and acceptance are distinguished or merged.
4. **`Dispatch Package  Handover to Carrier`:** raw 1, conservative/evidence 1, aggressive 0. Handover survives, but its incoming transition is damaged by aggregation.
5. **`Capture Payment  Capture Payment`:** raw 1, conservative 1, evidence/aggressive 0. The conservative self-loop represents M1 followed by M2 under an arbitrary total order—not a retry.
6. **`Authorize Payment  Capture Payment` for M1:** remains present in all four datasets.
7. **`Authorize Payment  Capture Payment` for M2:** absent in the first three, introduced by aggressive e13/e14/e15 merging. The merged row appears downstream of M1 authorization even though M2 has no supplied authorization event.
8. **`Pack Package  Pack Package`:** raw and conservative 1, evidence/aggressive 0. Suppression alone does not tell an analyst whether the second representation was detail or abstraction.

The **Order-flattened DFG** is particularly vulnerable to same-label ties and abstraction updates. The **Item-flattened DFG** can lose the duplicated I1 support, invent a pick-to-pick route, or erase the independent I2 pick under aggressive merging. The **Payment-flattened DFG** must filter on Payment identity; otherwise M1-to-M2 can look like retry/rework. In the aggressive result, Payment-object views expose a false M1-authorization-to-M2-capture path.

For the untyped **OC-DFG**, projection can create object-to-object paths such as M1  M2, Package  Delivery, or Item  Delivery without an explicitly observed transition between those object types. Edge frequencies become observation-support counts, not independent business occurrences.

**ET–OT/edge weights and OTG relations** change with every merge because transition support and E2O support are recomputed. Same-timestamp transitions should carry an `overlapping_time` flag, not be interpreted as measured waits. Synthetic timestamps must not be used for case-cycle-time or performance claims.

Apparent rework should be tested both unfiltered and object-filtered. e06/e07 can resemble packing rework; e13/e14 are not demonstrated rework. Lifecycle measurements should retain first/last observations per source and per object. For example, D1 has two raw/conservative dispatch-related observations but one aggressive observation; M1 still has authorization and capture, while M2 has only capture. The Order interval e01–e17 remains 24 hours, but fewer events must not be presented as improved productivity.

## 8. Audit trail and reversibility

Use an append-only event-lineage schema containing:

- canonical event ID;
- ordered list of source event IDs;
- source, source key, source sequence, ingestion time, and payload hash;
- merge rule, confidence, reason, reviewer, and rule version;
- original and refined activity;
- original timestamp, ordering timestamp, and synthetic-step value;
- attributes retained from each source, with source attribution;
- original and canonical E2O assertions;
- conflicts selected, deferred, or discarded, including their original values.

The raw view is reconstructed by restoring the immutable raw event rows and their original E2O relations, then using the lineage table to map source IDs to canonical IDs. Do **not** reconstruct the raw view by expanding a canonical event’s union relations: that would fabricate links absent from individual source records. Attribute conflicts must remain reconstructible even when no canonical winner was selected.

## 9. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Collapsing partial payments:** M1 and M2 are distinct amounts. Require matching payment identity before merging; test payment-object projections.
2. **Converting abstraction updates into false rework:** preserve detail/summary roles and causal-derivation evidence before aggregating.
3. **Ordering by ingestion time:** ingestion reflects receipt, not execution. Exclude it from business topological sorting.
4. **Treating shared objects as duplication proof:** common O1/V1 links cannot distinguish e13/e14, and common logistics objects cannot distinguish release/acceptance.
5. **Unioning incompatible E2O relations:** union only approved identity groups; retain per-source assertions and flag incompatible lifecycle membership.
6. **Changing durations through synthetic microseconds:** preserve original timestamps and tie bounds; exclude synthetic steps from duration statistics.
7. **Losing source-authoritative attributes:** merge by compatibility, not overwrite order; retain every conflicting candidate with attribution.
8. **Applying duplicate functions to the whole OCEL:** inspect candidate groups, constrain library scope, pin the PM4Py version, and fail closed on unexpected outputs.
9. **Losing identifiers during library merges:** maintain an external pre-merge lineage manifest; library metadata alone may not retain all source identities.
10. **Overinterpreting a total order:** report partial-order edges, alternative linear extensions, and unresolved ties separately.
11. **Comparing source sequence across systems:** source sequence is valid only within its own source.
12. **Letting object flattening impose causality:** an Order-flattened transition is not proof of a direct dependency between its participating objects.
