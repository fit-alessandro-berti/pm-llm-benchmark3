Abbreviations used below:

- `R` = Register Order
- `C` = Credit Check
- `E` = Engineering Review
- `S` = Supplier Confirmation
- `Q` = Expedite Review
- `A` = Approve Configuration
- `P` = Release Production
- `X` = Archive Case

The two DFG directions `C  E` and `E  C` establish only that both completion orders occurred in the log. They do **not** prove concurrency. The same pattern can arise from: genuinely parallel tasks with variable completion times; an XOR between two sequential policies; case-specific routing; rework not represented in the event labels; timestamp granularity; or ingestion ordering for equal timestamps. D7 and S7 specifically make a concurrency conclusion from the DFG alone unjustified.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. **Completion-event semantics.** Each event represents a recorded completion, not an activity start. Therefore, all models constrain observed completion order, not unobserved start-time overlap.

2. **Fixed envelope.** All candidates use `R` as the modeled start and `X` as the modeled end, with the high-confidence suffix `A  P  X`.

3. **Principal checks before approval.** Consistent with D3, `C` and `E` must both complete before `A` in the balanced and permissive candidates. The conservative candidate contains only observed variants, which also meet this condition.

4. **Supplier Confirmation interpretation.** Absence of `S` in an event trace is ambiguous:
   - a stocked-component case may legitimately have no confirmation, per S4; or
   - an external-component confirmation may have occurred but be absent from the event log, per S5.

   The models represent this ambiguity as a **control-flow alternative** containing either a recorded `S` or no recorded `S`. This is an observational abstraction. It does **not** claim that an external order is legally approvable without Supplier Confirmation.

5. **Absent case attribute.** Because external/stocked status was not exported, models cannot route deterministically based on that attribute. A future implementation should attach the branch to an explicit case attribute rather than treating it as a free behavioral choice.

6. **Optionality.** “Optional” means zero-or-one occurrence in a given modeled trace. It does not mean arbitrary repetition. `Q` occurs at most once; `S` occurs at most once in the modeled abstraction.

7. **Expedite Review.** `Q`, when present, follows both `C` and `E`, precedes `A`, and can occur before or after `S` once the prerequisite checks are complete.

8. **No loops or rework.** None of the candidates includes repeated checks, repeated approvals, cancellation, rejection, or rework because no evidence supports these behaviors.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The conservative candidate is an explicit finite-language hypothesis: it admits exactly the seven observed trace variants and no unobserved complete trace. It prioritizes trace-level precision over structural generalization, refuses to interpret reverse completion orders as concurrency, and treats the observed absence of Supplier Confirmation as a recorded-path fact without deciding whether it was true stocked-case optionality or logging loss. This is useful as a baseline and for strict conformance analysis, but it deliberately overfits sparse evidence.

**Compact formal expression.**

\[
M_c = \operatorname{XOR}\left(
R\,C\,E\,S\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,E\,C\,S\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,C\,E\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,E\,C\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,C\,S\,E\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,C\,E\,S\,Q\,A\,P\,X,\;
R\,E\,C\,Q\,S\,A\,P\,X
\right)
\]

**PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py

# ProcessTree parsed from a compact process-tree expression.
# Quoted labels avoid ambiguity for labels containing spaces.
conservative_tree_string = """
xor(
  ->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',
     'Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration',
     'Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',
     'Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration',
     'Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',
     'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',
     'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation',
     'Engineering Review','Approve Configuration',
     'Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',
     'Supplier Confirmation','Expedite Review',
     'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),
  ->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',
     'Expedite Review','Supplier Confirmation',
     'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case')
)
"""

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(conservative_tree_string)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- Any unobserved ordering, including `R,C,E,Q,A,P,X`.
- `Q` before `S` in the `C,E` completion order unless it is exactly `R,E,C,Q,S,A,P,X`.
- `S` after `E` in the `E,C` completion order but before `Q`, unless it is exactly the observed no-`Q` path.
- Any trace with `S` after both checks but followed by no `S` abstraction choice not already observed.
- All potential parallel interleavings that are not explicitly enumerated.
- Repetition of any activity.
- Any activity after `A` other than `P,X`.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- It assigns zero language support to legitimate but unobserved interleavings, despite D5, D6, S2, and S6.
- It cannot represent the likely distinction between external and stocked components.
- It treats low-frequency observed variants as separate alternatives rather than recognizing shared structure.
- Its XOR enumeration repeats labels across branches. Duplicate labels are acceptable in a ProcessTree because each leaf is a separate occurrence, but this can make alignments less interpretable and rapidly becomes unscalable: adding \(k\) independently reorderable activities can require factorially many branches.
- It may falsely flag valid future cases as nonconformant merely because they were not in the sample.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The balanced candidate treats `C` and `E` as unordered with respect to each other because both completion orders are common and S2 is consistent with overlap, while explicitly avoiding a claim that concurrency was proven. It uses a partial order rather than a parallel ProcessTree block because the relevant business rule is not simply “run all activities in parallel”: Supplier Confirmation may occur after either check, Expedite Review requires both checks, and both `S` and `Q` must precede approval when present. The external/stocked and missing-event ambiguity is represented as a recorded-`S` versus no-recorded-`S` branch. This is the most plausible structural generalization under the current evidence, while remaining bounded by D1–D6.

**Compact formal expression.**

Let `N` be a silent “no recorded Supplier Confirmation” alternative.

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_b =\;& R \cdot
\operatorname{SPO}\Big(
\{C,E,\operatorname{XOR}(S,\tau),\operatorname{XOR}(Q,\tau)\},\\
& C \prec Q,\ E \prec Q,\ C \prec A,\ E \prec A,\ S \prec A,\ Q \prec A
\Big)
\cdot A \cdot P \cdot X
\end{aligned}
\]

with the additional partial-order edges:

\[
C \prec S \quad \text{or} \quad E \prec S
\]

implemented as an XOR between two supplier-enable alternatives. This avoids allowing `S` before both principal checks while preserving D5’s “either may be sufficient” interpretation.

**PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Leaf constructors
R = Transition("Register Order")
C = Transition("Credit Check")
E = Transition("Engineering Review")
S = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
Q = Transition("Expedite Review")
A = Transition("Approve Configuration")
P = Transition("Release Production")
X = Transition("Archive Case")

tau_s = SilentTransition()
tau_q = SilentTransition()

# Recorded-S versus no-recorded-S abstraction.
supplier_or_absent = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [S, tau_s])

# Optional Expedite Review, at most once.
expedite_or_skip = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Q, tau_q])

# Balanced middle:
# - C and E have no ordering edge between them.
# - S must follow at least one check. This is modeled by choosing one
#   prerequisite alternative: C -> S OR E -> S.
# - Q must follow both C and E.
# - S and Q, when selected, must precede A.
#
# Because POWL partial orders do not natively express an OR-join prerequisite
# ("C OR E before S") as one edge set, we represent that uncertainty using
# an XOR between two otherwise equivalent partial-order fragments.

def balanced_middle(supplier_after):
    c = Transition("Credit Check")
    e = Transition("Engineering Review")
    s = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    q = Transition("Expedite Review")
    a = Transition("Approve Configuration")

    no_s = SilentTransition()
    no_q = SilentTransition()

    s_choice = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [s, no_s])
    q_choice = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [q, no_q])

    po = StrictPartialOrder([c, e, s_choice, q_choice, a])

    # Both checks required before approval.
    po.order.add_edge(c, a)
    po.order.add_edge(e, a)

    # If Supplier Confirmation is selected, the chosen enabling check precedes it.
    # The edge to XOR node applies to its visible S branch; tau can occur silently.
    po.order.add_edge(supplier_after, s_choice)

    # Expedite Review, if selected, requires both checks.
    po.order.add_edge(c, q_choice)
    po.order.add_edge(e, q_choice)

    # Both optional activities must be completed, if selected, before approval.
    po.order.add_edge(s_choice, a)
    po.order.add_edge(q_choice, a)

    return po

middle_after_credit = balanced_middle(Transition("Credit Check"))
middle_after_engineering = balanced_middle(Transition("Engineering Review"))

# Note:
# The two separately constructed fragments have duplicate visible labels.
# This is intentional: each branch represents a competing D5 interpretation
# about which check supplied sufficient information for S.
middle = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [middle_after_credit, middle_after_engineering]
)

model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [
        Transition("Register Order"),
        middle,
        Transition("Release Production"),  # A is inside the middle PO
        Transition("Archive Case"),
    ],
)
```

**Decision rule for sequence, concurrency, and partial order.**

- **Credit Check and Engineering Review:** no sequence edge is imposed. Both `C,E` and `E,C` are frequent, D7/S7 prevent causal inference from completion order, and S2 makes overlap plausible. This is modeled as **unordered/partially ordered**, not as proven physical concurrency.
- **Supplier Confirmation:** `S` is neither placed in a fixed sequence after both checks nor made fully unconstrained. V5 supports `C,S,E`; D5 says one principal check may provide enough information. Therefore the model uses an XOR between two causal hypotheses: `C  S` or `E  S`. It does not permit `S` before both checks.
- **Expedite Review:** `Q` is optional and requires both `C` and `E` before it, per D6/S6. Once enabled, it has no imposed order relative to `S`; both `Q,S` and `S,Q` are allowed before `A`.
- **Approve Configuration:** `A` follows `C`, `E`, `S` if recorded, and `Q` if selected. This encodes D2–D6 without inferring more ordering than warranted.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- `Q` before either `C` or `E`.
- `A` before either principal check.
- Recorded `S` before both `C` and `E`.
- `S` or `Q` after `A`.
- Repeated `S`, `Q`, approvals, or releases.
- Any direct `R  A` progression.
- Any post-release activity other than archive.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- The `C  S OR E  S` construction may be too restrictive if procurement can begin from registration data alone.
- It does not encode S1’s strict finance policy `C` before `E`; if S1 is authoritative and historical logs contain policy violations or different scope, this model is too permissive.
- It treats all no-recorded-`S` cases alike, despite the important distinction between stocked cases and missing integration events.
- It does not model case attributes, rework, approval rejection, or multiple supplier interactions.
- POWL XOR branches used to represent OR-style enablement introduce duplicate labels and may complicate conformance diagnostics.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** The permissive candidate treats the principal checks as a set of activities that may complete in either order and allows Supplier Confirmation to occur after either principal check, or after both, while maintaining the broad domain constraints around approval and expedite. It intentionally admits reasonable unobserved linearizations compatible with completion-only data and broad rules. It still prohibits behavior that conflicts with high-confidence constraints: no approval before both checks, no production before approval, no expedite before both checks, and no activity after release other than archive.

**Compact formal expression.**

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_p =\;& R \cdot
\operatorname{SPO}\left(
\{C,E,\operatorname{XOR}(S,\tau),\operatorname{XOR}(Q,\tau),A\},
\right.\\
& \left.
C \prec A,\;
E \prec A,\;
S \prec A,\;
C \prec Q,\;
E \prec Q,\;
Q \prec A
\right)
\cdot P \cdot X
\end{aligned}
\]

No precedence edge is imposed between `S` and either principal check. This is intentionally broader than D5: it treats the possibility that Supplier Confirmation can be initiated and completed from order-level information as a reasonable, but weakly supported, generalization.

**PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Leaves for permissive model
R = Transition("Register Order")
C = Transition("Credit Check")
E = Transition("Engineering Review")
S = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
Q = Transition("Expedite Review")
A = Transition("Approve Configuration")
P = Transition("Release Production")
X = Transition("Archive Case")

supplier_or_absent = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [S, SilentTransition()]
)

expedite_or_skip = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [Q, SilentTransition()]
)

middle = StrictPartialOrder([C, E, supplier_or_absent, expedite_or_skip, A])

# Principal checks are both required before approval.
middle.order.add_edge(C, A)
middle.order.add_edge(E, A)

# Supplier Confirmation, if recorded, must precede approval.
middle.order.add_edge(supplier_or_absent, A)

# Expedite Review, if selected, requires both checks and precedes approval.
middle.order.add_edge(C, expedite_or_skip)
middle.order.add_edge(E, expedite_or_skip)
middle.order.add_edge(expedite_or_skip, A)

# S may occur before, between, or after principal checks, but always before A.
# Q may occur before or after S, but only after both principal checks.

model_permissive = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [R, middle, P, X]
)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- `A` before `C` or `E`.
- `P` before `A`.
- `X` before `P`.
- `Q` before either principal check.
- `Q` after `A`.
- `S` after `A`.
- Repeated activities and loops.
- Any order in which `R` is not first or `X` is not last.

**Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behavior.**

Reasonable generalization includes unseen completion orders caused by variable work duration, unknown start-time overlap, unobserved component-routing conditions, and the uncertain availability of supplier information. Thus `S` may appear before either check, between them, or after both; `Q` may occur before or after `S` once `C` and `E` are complete.

Behavior remains forbidden when it contradicts high-confidence or medium-to-high-confidence rules: approval without both principal checks, release without approval, expedite without both checks, expedite after approval, supplier confirmation after approval, or any repeated/rework cycle not supported by evidence.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- It may still underfit if Supplier Confirmation can legitimately occur after approval in a post-approval procurement process, but D4 argues against that.
- It omits cancellation, rejection, resubmission, multiple suppliers, and other likely real-life exceptions.
- It remains unable to distinguish a true no-supplier stocked case from a missing supplier event.

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(conservative_tree_string)


def make_balanced_model():
    """
    Balanced POWL model.

    There are two XOR alternatives for the D5 ambiguity:
      1. recorded Supplier Confirmation is enabled by Credit Check;
      2. recorded Supplier Confirmation is enabled by Engineering Review.

    Both alternatives also allow a silent no-recorded-S path.
    """
    def fragment(enable_supplier_by):
        c = Transition("Credit Check")
        e = Transition("Engineering Review")
        s = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
        q = Transition("Expedite Review")
        a = Transition("Approve Configuration")

        supplier_choice = OperatorPOWL(
            Operator.XOR,
            [s, SilentTransition()]
        )
        expedite_choice = OperatorPOWL(
            Operator.XOR,
            [q, SilentTransition()]
        )

        po = StrictPartialOrder([c, e, supplier_choice, expedite_choice, a])

        # D3: C and E before approval.
        po.order.add_edge(c, a)
        po.order.add_edge(e, a)

        # D5 competing hypotheses: C -> S or E -> S.
        if enable_supplier_by == "credit":
            po.order.add_edge(c, supplier_choice)
        else:
            po.order.add_edge(e, supplier_choice)

        # D6/S6: Q after both checks.
        po.order.add_edge(c, expedite_choice)
        po.order.add_edge(e, expedite_choice)

        # D4/D6: selected S/Q before approval.
        po.order.add_edge(supplier_choice, a)
        po.order.add_edge(expedite_choice, a)

        return po

    middle = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [fragment("credit"), fragment("engineering")]
    )

    return OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.SEQUENCE,
        [
            Transition("Register Order"),
            middle,
            Transition("Release Production"),
            Transition("Archive Case"),
        ],
    )

model_balanced = make_balanced_model()


def make_permissive_model():
    c = Transition("Credit Check")
    e = Transition("Engineering Review")
    s = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    q = Transition("Expedite Review")
    a = Transition("Approve Configuration")

    supplier_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [s, SilentTransition()]
    )
    expedite_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [q, SilentTransition()]
    )

    middle = StrictPartialOrder([c, e, supplier_choice, expedite_choice, a])

    # D3: both principal checks before approval.
    middle.order.add_edge(c, a)
    middle.order.add_edge(e, a)

    # D4: S, if recorded, before approval.
    middle.order.add_edge(supplier_choice, a)

    # D6/S6: Q after both checks and before approval.
    middle.order.add_edge(c, expedite_choice)
    middle.order.add_edge(e, expedite_choice)
    middle.order.add_edge(expedite_choice, a)

    return OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.SEQUENCE,
        [
            Transition("Register Order"),
            middle,
            Transition("Release Production"),
            Transition("Archive Case"),
        ],
    )

model_permissive = make_permissive_model()

models = {
    "conservative": model_conservative,
    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}

        "fitness_alignments": lambda:
            pm4py.fitness_alignments(event_log, net, im, fm),

            print(metric_name, "=", value)

positive_suite = {

def trace_acceptance(net, im, fm, trace_string, tolerance=1e-9):
    one_trace_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([trace_string])
    fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(one_trace_log, net, im, fm)

    # PM4Py versions may return aggregate dicts with different key names.
    # Inspect and extract the most likely fitness value.
    if isinstance(fit, dict):
        for key in (
            "log_fitness",
            "average_trace_fitness",
            "perc_fit_traces",
        ):
            if key in fit:
                value = float(fit[key])
                if key == "perc_fit_traces":
                    return value >= 100.0 - tolerance, fit
                return value >= 1.0 - tolerance, fit

    # If API shape differs, return raw result for manual inspection.
    return None, fit

Implementation notes:

- PM4Py’s POWL API and conversion support can vary by release. If a constructor signature differs, inspect the installed `OperatorPOWL`, `StrictPartialOrder`, and `convert_to_petri_net` documentation and adapt only the construction syntax, not the stated constraints.
- Alignment fitness can show that a tested trace has a zero-cost fit under the converted net, but it is not a proof that sampled playout has exhaustively covered the model language.
- Precision and generalization values should be interpreted comparatively, especially for the conservative model: high precision can simply reflect memorization of the training variants.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate-use recommendation: `model_balanced`.** It best reflects the strongest combined evidence: both principal checks are required before approval; their completion order is not a reliable causal ordering; Supplier Confirmation has case-dependent and data-quality-dependent absence; and Expedite Review has explicit prerequisites but uncertain order relative to Supplier Confirmation. It is sufficiently constrained for operational discussion while avoiding the conservative model’s severe memorization bias.

**Sensitivity-analysis recommendation: `model_permissive`.** Use it to quantify how conclusions change if Supplier Confirmation can be completed from information available before either principal check finishes. It is appropriate for exploratory simulation, process redesign, and assessing whether apparent nonconformance is merely an artifact of overly rigid ordering assumptions.

A useful additional sensitivity model, not selected as one of the required three, would enforce S1 as a prospective policy: `R  C  E`, with Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review constrained around that sequence. Because it rejects many observed traces, it should be treated as a policy-compliance hypothesis rather than a historical discovery model.

Concrete data-collection plan:

1. **Export the external/stocked component attribute.**
   - Add a case-level `component_sourcing_type  {external, stocked, mixed, unknown}`.
   - Add an item-level representation if one order may contain both stocked and external components.
   - This directly distinguishes legitimate no-`S` cases from missing Supplier Confirmation events.

2. **Add lifecycle timestamps.**
   - Record at least `start`, `complete`, and preferably `scheduled/assigned` timestamps for `C`, `E`, `S`, and `Q`.
   - Store timestamp resolution and timezone consistently.
   - Preserve events with identical timestamps rather than resolving them through ingestion index alone.

3. **Capture Supplier Confirmation state and provenance.**
   - Record `supplier_confirmation_required`, `supplier_confirmation_received`, `integration_status`, source system identifier, and retry/error flags.
   - Add a reconciliation flag indicating whether `S` was reconstructed from external system evidence.
   - This separates true optionality from event loss.

4. **Record Expedite Review triggers.**
   - Add `expedite_requested`, `expedite_reason`, and `expedite_required_by_policy`.
   - This determines whether `Q` is merely optional or conditionally required for identifiable cases.

5. **Clarify S1 policy scope.**
   - Ask Finance whether “Credit Check must finish before Engineering Review starts” is:
     - an actual historic policy;
     - a future-state target;
     - applicable only to a risk category;
     - or a statement about authorization rather than physical work start.
   - Then connect that scope to a case attribute such as credit-risk class.

6. **Collect maximally informative future cases.**
   - To distinguish **sequence versus overlap** for `C` and `E`, collect cases with start and completion timestamps showing:
     - `C.start < E.start < C.complete < E.complete`;
     - `E.start < C.start < E.complete < C.complete`;
     - strict non-overlap in each direction.
   - To distinguish `S` enablement hypotheses, collect cases where:
     - `S.complete < C.complete` but `E.complete` has occurred;
     - `S.complete < E.complete` but `C.complete` has occurred;
     - `S.start` occurs before both checks finish;
     - `S` is absent while `supplier_confirmation_required = true`.
   - To distinguish `Q` optionality and ordering, collect cases with:
     - `Q` present before `S`;
     - `S` present before `Q`;
     - `Q` absent despite an explicit expedite request;
     - `Q` attempted before both checks, if such attempts can exist.
   - To distinguish true `S` optionality from missingness, sample no-`S` traces and reconcile them against procurement-system records.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Modeling decision | Confidence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `R` is start and `X` is end | High | D1 and all observed traces agree |
| `A  P  X` | High | D2 and all observed traces agree |
| `C` and `E` must complete before `A` | Medium-high | D3 and every observed trace agree |
| `C` and `E` are actually concurrent | Low | Reverse completion orders and S2 are suggestive, but D7/S7 prevent proof without lifecycle starts |
| Do not enforce `C  E` as a universal order | Medium-high for historical model discovery | S1 conflicts with many observed traces and S2; however, S1 could describe a scoped or future policy |
| `S` is conditionally required rather than globally optional | Medium | S3/S4 support conditionality; S5 prevents identifying the condition from the log |
| No recorded `S` is partly due to missing events | Medium | S5 reports 5–10% missingness, but individual cases cannot be classified |
| `S` may complete after only one principal check | Low-to-medium | V5 and D5 support it, but completion timestamps cannot establish actual enablement/start conditions |
| `Q` follows both principal checks and precedes approval | Medium-high | D6/S6 and observed expedite traces agree |
| `Q` may be before or after `S` | Medium | V6/V7 and S6 support both observed relative orders |
| No loops/rework should be modeled | Medium for this dataset, low for the real business process | No loop evidence exists, but absence in 30 cases is weak evidence of business impossibility |

The recommendation would materially change if S1 were confirmed as an enforceable, universally applicable historical rule and the reverse-order traces were shown to be logging defects or mislabeled activity semantics. In that case, the correct operational model would likely use a strict `C  E` sequence and treat `E  C` observed traces as data-quality exceptions rather than valid behavior.

A second material alternative is that Supplier Confirmation is truly mandatory for all orders in scope and every no-`S` trace is a missing-event artifact. If confirmed, the “no recorded `S`” branch should not be interpreted as a business path; it should instead be handled through event-log repair, probabilistic missing-event treatment, or a separate observation model.
