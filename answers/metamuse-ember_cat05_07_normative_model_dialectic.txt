## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts treated as established for this review**

- CS-9, effective 2025-01-01 and formally controlled, requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and requester different from manual approver. M0 represents the control-flow part but not the resource rule.
- PD-3 was a steering-committee decision in minutes and follow-up email, effective 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31. It allowed a LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group to use Auto_Approve instead of Manual_Procurement_Approval, required Sanctions_Check plus automated master-data validation before activation, and allowed Quality_Review up to two business days after provisional activation. The Compliance observer wrote that the pilot may proceed as a monitored exception and later stated that she had not approved weakening CS-9.
- FD-7 was effective 2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12 for critical relief suppliers. It allowed provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval, required Sanctions_Check before activation, capped spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, and required daily monitoring plus Quality_Review within two business days. It expired automatically.
- From 2026-07-01 Notify_Requester moved to a CRM service. About 70% is mirrored to the onboarding log. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. CRM links may be stable `notification_id`s or mutable text fields.
- M0 is: Register_Supplier -> (Sanctions_Check concurrent with Quality_Review) -> XOR(manual -> activation, rejection) -> Notify_Requester -> Close_Case.
- Stipulated case chronologies, eligibility context, dates, amounts, CRM findings, duplicate transaction ID, alias identity, label rename, and relationship facts are as stated in the materials. Aggregate facts are: 312 June-July Auto_Approve cases, 289 apparently PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility attributes; 47 Request_More_Info cases with none activating before requested evidence arrived; six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August; Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708.
- A trace order in the log is evidence of recorded order, not automatically proof of real-world occurrence when collection is incomplete, duplicated, renamed, or mutable.

**Disputed propositions**

- Whether PD-3, a non-CS-9 steering decision with a dissenting or non-concurring Compliance observer, temporarily derogates from CS-9 for eligible cases or is merely an unauthorized pilot that never amended the controlled standard.
- Whether PD-3 automated master-data validation is a separately logged activity, a gate inside Auto_Approve, or an unattributed data condition. The traces for SUP-702, SUP-703, and SUP-714 do not show a separate validation event.
- Whether a rejected pre-screen lead and a reactivation are in scope of M0 and CS-9. The scope documents are ambiguous for SUP-709 and SUP-710.
- Whether a CRM message is proof of Notify_Requester for a specific case when the link is a stable `notification_id` versus a mutable text field entered two days later.
- Whether QA_Clearance is equivalent to Quality_Review for all CS-9 purposes, particularly where the mapping was approved after SUP-713.
- Whether a factual continuation of flooding after 2026-07-12, normalized use of the pilot, or belief that permanent approval was imminent can extend FD-7 or PD-3. The materials contain no written extension or permanent amendment.
- Whether the spouse relationship in SUP-715 triggers disclosure and whether undisclosed relationship affects independence. No rule explicitly prohibits the relationship itself.
- Whether desirable conduct, especially Request_More_Info in SUP-707, is a policy amendment merely because stakeholders agree with it.

**Unproved propositions**

- That automated master-data validation passed for SUP-702, if it is not represented by Auto_Approve attributes.
- That the CRM message in SUP-706 was sent to the correct requester for that case at a time before Close_Case, given the mutable link.
- That aliases in SUP-712 reflect a valid delegation or an identity-resolution error rather than self-approval.
- That SUP-710 prior quality certification was valid on the reactivation date, beyond the statement that it remained valid, because no validity interval or prior-case link is supplied.
- The eligibility of the nine aggregate Auto_Approve cases with missing attributes.
- That no disclosure was made outside the case record in SUP-715.

**Burden of proof**

A declaration of NONCOMPLIANT requires the reviewer to establish four elements: the case is within the scope of the cited norm, the norm version was effective at the relevant time, reliable evidence proves the factual predicate, and the deviation is material to a preventive control rather than a mere label, duplication, or collection artifact. Absence of a log event under a known incomplete collection process does not by itself prove absence of the activity.

Acceptance of JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION places the burden on the operator invoking the exception. The operator must prove every predicate and condition of the derogation: eligibility, authority, dates, amount caps, monitoring, retrospective completion, and required pre-activation controls. Desirability, absence of harm, later ratification, or normalized practice does not discharge that burden.

MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT is appropriate where the literal model conflicts with a higher substantive rule, an agreed desirable practice, a renamed activity, or an undefined case type, and no material policy breach is proved. DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE is appropriate where defective collection prevents a reliable finding and neither side has discharged its burden. Trace deviation, policy violation, and personal misconduct are distinct findings.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Formal authority controls unless a competent amendment changes it**

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 cannot be treated as an approval to amend the standard. Meeting minutes and a follow-up email may authorize operations to run a monitored pilot, but they do not silently rewrite a controlled compliance standard. At most, they create a narrow, time-limited exception whose conditions must be proved. FD-7 is even clearer: it is a dated emergency directive with an automatic expiry. Neither factual emergency nor operational convenience extends it.

**Expiry is not a formality**

SUP-714 used Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 ended. Six further post-pilot cases in early August show repetition, not an isolated clerical slip. SUP-705 activated a supplier on 2026-07-13, one day after FD-7 expired. A rule that can be continued by the people it constrains is not an expiry rule. The later completion of approvals and low initial spend mitigate consequences but do not create authority retroactively.

**High-risk deviations are material even when transactions do not follow**

SUP-703 used Auto_Approve for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, inside the pilot calendar window but outside its eligibility predicate, followed by a EUR 180,000 first purchase. Timing inside the window is only one condition. SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check and before manual approval. The absence of transactions before clearance is luck and containment after the fact, not compliance with a preventive gate. Once Activate_Supplier executes, the supplier can transact, receive orders, or be relied upon by downstream systems. A control that only matters if a same-day transaction happens is not a preventive control.

**M0 hides some violations and the operation exploited that blindness**

SUP-712 fits M0 exactly but the requester and approver are the same human through two aliases, directly violating CS-9. SUP-715 fits M0 while a spouse in the same reporting line acts as quality reviewer and manual approver with no recorded disclosure. Conformance to control flow therefore cannot be the compliance criterion. The process allowed identity fragmentation and undisclosed relationships to pass as ordinary approvals.

**Exceptions normalized into a parallel process**

The pilot generated 312 Auto_Approve cases in June and July. Although 289 appear eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible and 9 lack eligibility attributes. A design that lets an operator select “pilot” despite ineligibility, as in SUP-703, is a control failure, not merely an individual error. The six post-pilot cases show that the exception became the default route. Normalization is evidenced by operations believing permanent approval was imminent when no amendment existed.

**Desirable behavior does not amend policy**

SUP-707 is substantively attractive, and the 47 aggregate Request_More_Info cases did not activate prematurely. That shows the loop should be modeled and governed, not that an unmodeled activity was authorized at the time. The same principle applies to the permanent low-risk auto-approval preference. A desirable future policy is an argument for amendment, not a retroactive defense.

**Concessions required by the evidence**

The prosecution cannot credibly claim that every M0 deviation is misconduct. M0 has no Request_More_Info loop, no resource, amount, date, identity, or eligibility rules, an outdated label for SUP-713, undefined scope for rejected leads and reactivations, and a notification log made incomplete by the CRM migration. SUP-704 satisfies the stated conditions of FD-7. SUP-702 satisfies the substantive conditions of PD-3 apart from the unshown master-data validation attribute. Counting literal M0 deviations without those overlays would produce a false noncompliance rate.

## 3. Defense Brief

**M0 is not a complete normative system**

M0 encodes only ordering, concurrency, and exclusive choice. It does not encode CS-9’s different-human rule, amount caps, risk predicates, pilot or emergency dates, business-day deadlines, spend limits, identity aliases, disclosure duties, provisional status, or automated validation. Fitting M0 therefore does not prove compliance, as SUP-712 and SUP-715 demonstrate. Deviating from M0 likewise does not prove business misconduct when a valid overlay or a scope rule governs the case.

**Concurrency is already recognized by M0**

Checks are a StrictPartialOrder with no edge between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review. In SUP-708, Quality_Review before Sanctions_Check is not itself a deviation. The violation is activation before both checks and before manual approval. In SUP-707, inserting Request_More_Info between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review also preserves the concurrency of the checks and every substantive precedence required before activation.

**Pilot and emergency overlays were real operational authorities, though narrow**

PD-3 was decided by a steering committee and implemented with a Compliance observer’s monitored-exception statement. For an eligible LOW-risk DE group supplier, requiring manual approval would defeat the pilot’s stated purpose. SUP-702 had complete eligibility fields, sanctions before activation, quality review the next business day, and a stable CRM notification identifier. SUP-704 was a critical pump supplier during FD-7, with sanctions first, EUR 18,000 spend below the EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, and quality plus manual approval the next business day. Those are the exact patterns the overlays were created to permit.

**The model lacks a desirable and safe loop**

Operations commonly uses Request_More_Info, Compliance agrees it is desirable, and in SUP-707 the missing certificates were obtained before approval. Aggregate evidence shows 47 such cases and no activation before requested evidence arrived. A model that penalizes evidence-gathering cycles necessary to make a safe decision is incomplete. The correct remedy is to add a governed loop or a declared repetition mechanism, not to classify evidence-driven caution as misconduct.

**CRM migration, renaming, and duplication explain part of the deviation rate**

After migration only about 70% of notifications are mirrored, and missingness rose to 29%, almost exactly the expected unmirrored share. SUP-702 has a stable CRM notification_id. SUP-711 has the same source transaction ID on both Register_Supplier rows, which is a deterministic migration duplicate rather than evidence of two registrations. SUP-713 uses QA_Clearance after a procedure and UI rename, and audit documents show equivalent content. A literal label match would manufacture a deviation from terminology drift.

**Scope for rejected leads and reactivations is genuinely undefined**

SUP-709 was rejected after failing sanctions pre-screen. Requiring a full Quality_Review before rejecting a sanction-failed lead would delay a protective decision and contradict the abbreviated pre-screen concept in CS-9. SUP-710 reactivated a supplier after six months with a still-valid quality certification, repeated Sanctions_Check, and repeated manual approval. CS-9 contains no reactivation procedure. Treating every reactivation as a new onboarding disregards valid prior assurance and the absence of a normative procedure.

**Deviation is not the same as harm or misconduct**

SUP-708 had no transactions before sanctions clearance. SUP-705 had low spend and completed retrospective approval. SUP-714 concerned an eligible low-risk supplier. Those facts affect severity and remedy. They do not convert a preventive-control breach into a compliant act, but they distinguish a governance failure from fraud, deliberate evasion, or realized loss.

**Concessions required by the evidence**

The defense cannot establish authority from facts alone. SUP-703 was ineligible and high-risk. SUP-708 had no pilot or emergency authority. SUP-705 lacked a written FD-7 extension. SUP-714 lacked a permanent amendment. Normalized practice and belief in imminent approval are not authority. The mutable CRM link in SUP-706 does not prove notification for that case. Desirable Request_More_Info behavior does not amend CS-9 or M0 by itself. A system that permits an operator to select an ineligible pilot route, activate before sanctions, or close cases without reliable notification evidence is materially defective even if many individual outcomes are benign.

**System-level judgment**

Operationally, the system is materially noncompliant in specific and consequential respects. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are severe preventive-control failures. SUP-705 and SUP-714 are authority failures caused by expiry and normalization. SUP-712 defeats segregation of duties. SUP-715 shows that undisclosed relationships can pass unchallenged. The aggregate 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and 9 cases with missing eligibility data show that SUP-703 was not wholly isolated. That is enough to reject the claim that the operation is generally compliant.

The system is not, however, pervasively noncompliant in the sense that every M0 deviation is misconduct. SUP-701 is clean. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are justified exceptions. SUP-707 is good practice penalized by an incomplete model. SUP-711 is a duplicate log artifact. Notification missingness is substantially explained by CRM migration. Conformance to literal M0 is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for compliance.

M0 remains valid for the standard new-onboarding core represented by SUP-701. It is invalid as a universal normative model for all observed case types. It lacks pilot and emergency routes, deferred quality review, a Request_More_Info loop, rejected-lead and reactivation scope, provisional status, resource rules, amount and date rules, label versioning, and reliable notification evidence. M0 should be superseded, not merely supplemented by informal explanations.

**Where the prosecution argument is rejected**

The prosecution overstates the case when it treats every M0 deviation as a policy violation, applies M0 to ambiguous rejected-lead and reactivation cases without a scope ruling, treats a missing CRM-mirrored event as proof of no notification, requires Quality_Review before rejecting a sanctions-failed lead, and infers intent or misconduct from trace deviation alone. It also overstates the authority dispute if it denies that PD-3 gave at least a narrow operational basis for eligible cases during its window. The better reading is that PD-3 was a time-limited exception, not an amendment to CS-9.

**Where the defense argument is rejected**

The defense overstates the case when it treats factual emergency as legal extension, normalized pilot use as permanent policy, stakeholder desirability as authorization, harmless outcomes as cures for preventive-control breaches, a mutable CRM text link as proof of notification, and later label mapping as a substitute for versioned equivalence. It also fails to explain why a system allowed an operator to select an ineligible pilot, allowed activation before sanctions, and allowed closure without immutable notification linkage. Those are design and governance defects, not merely model defects.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Layer 0: Scope and case typing**

Every case must carry an immutable `case_type` and `case_id` before control-flow evaluation. The types are NEW_ONBOARDING, PRES_SCREEN_LEAD, REACTIVATION, and WITHDRAWAL. A case may transition from PRES_SCREEN_LEAD to NEW_ONBOARDING after passing sanctions pre-screen, retaining the same case_id and adding an entry event that records the transition. Reactivation requires `prior_case_id`, the prior supplier identifiers, certification validity interval, and a material-change assessment. Withdrawal requires a Withdraw_Case event and is distinct from rejection. This layer prevents SUP-709 and SUP-710 from being forced into a single new-onboarding model.

**Layer 1: Revised control flow in POWL**

POWL should represent ordering, concurrency, exclusive route choice, and mandatory milestones: registration or reactivation entry, sanctions, quality, approval or auto-approval, activation or rejection, notification, and closure. Provisional activation must be distinguishable by status and overlay attributes, not by the Activate_Supplier label alone. The standard route keeps Sanctions_Check concurrent with Quality_Review and both before manual approval and activation. The auto route keeps sanctions before auto-approval and activation, with quality after provisional activation. The emergency route keeps sanctions before provisional activation and places quality and manual approval after activation but before closure. Rejection after pre-screen does not require quality.

**Layer 2: Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules**

These rules do not belong in POWL. They belong in DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints for precedence and response patterns, and in pandas or database checks for dates, amounts, and predicates. Examples include: Sanctions_Check always precedes Activate_Supplier; Auto_Approve only when risk equals LOW, country equals DE, corporate-group approval is valid, and the overlay date predicate holds; Quality_Review within two business days after provisional activation under PD-3 or FD-7; cumulative spend at or below EUR 20,000 before retrospective approval under FD-7; Request_More_Info precedes activation and requested evidence is recorded before manual approval; Notify_Requester precedes Close_Case. Business-day calculation, amount aggregation, and predicate evaluation require data checks.

**Layer 3: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules**

Identity must resolve to a canonical `person_id` before segregation checks. The data rule is requester canonical ID different from manual approver canonical ID. Delegation requires a delegation record naming delegator, delegate, scope, amount limit, and validity interval; a delegate may not be the requester for that case. Quality reviewer and manual approver relationships must be checked against a disclosure registry. A spouse or same-reporting-line relationship should trigger disclosure and, above a defined risk threshold, recusal or independent review. These are pandas, identity-registry, and organizational controls, not POWL constraints.

**Layer 4: Policy-overlay governance**

Pilots and emergencies must be first-class versioned overlay objects. Each overlay needs an ID, version, authorizing body, compliance concurrence, predicate, permitted deviations, non-derogable controls, effective interval, expiry behavior, monitoring duties, evaluation criteria, and termination rule. An extension must be issued before expiry and must state whether it is prospective only. Ratification after expiry may mitigate sanctions or validate already-completed retrospective controls, but it must not backdate eligibility. User interfaces should compute eligibility from the snapshot and disable Auto_Approve when the predicate is false, rather than allowing operator selection of “pilot.”

**Layer 5: Scope rules for special cases**

A PRES_SCREEN_LEAD that fails sanctions is rejected without Quality_Review, then notified and closed. A PRES_SCREEN_LEAD that passes becomes a NEW_ONBOARDING case without a second Register_Supplier event. A REACTIVATION with valid certification, no material change, fresh Sanctions_Check, and manual approval does not require a new Register_Supplier or Quality_Review. Expired certification or material change requires renewed Quality_Review. A withdrawal before decision requires Withdraw_Case, notification, and closure, with no activation or rejection. These scope rules should be encoded as case-type predicates and separate entry models, not hidden in trace interpretation.

**Allocation of mechanisms**

POWL belongs to control-flow structure. DECLARE and log skeletons belong to precedence, non-co-occurrence, response, and repetition patterns that do not depend on complex attributes. Pandas and database checks belong to eligibility snapshots, dates, amounts, business days, identity resolution, duplicates, cumulative spend, and CRM reconciliation. Organizational controls belong to authority approval, training, recusal, audit, and overlay issuance. The revised system should report separate results for control-flow conformance, overlay validity, data-rule compliance, resource compliance, and data-quality sufficiency.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**POWL limitation and approximation**

The PM4Py POWL classes used in M0 provide StrictPartialOrder and OperatorPOWL but no loop operator. A repeatable Request_More_Info cycle therefore cannot be represented faithfully as an unbounded loop in this construction. The defensible approximation below models zero or one Request_More_Info occurrence before the route choice and relies on a DECLARE repetition constraint plus data checks for additional cycles and evidence receipt. Distinct Transition objects with identical labels are used for different routes so that route-specific order edges do not leak across branches. Automated master-data validation is treated as a required data condition on Auto_Approve rather than a separate event because no separate validation activity is observed. Reactivation and withdrawal are excluded from this single new-onboarding and pre-screen model and require companion scope models.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def T(label):
    """Create a distinct visible transition instance for a route."""
    return Transition(label=label)


def S():
    """Create a silent transition used for an optional skip."""
    return SilentTransition()


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry and Request_More_Info approximation
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
register = T("Register_Supplier")

# Approximation: zero or one Request_More_Info before route choice.
# PM4Py POWL as used here has no loop operator; additional repetitions are
# governed by a DECLARE repetition constraint and data checks requiring
# recorded evidence before activation.
request_more_info = T("Request_More_Info")
info_gate = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[S(), request_more_info],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard route: concurrent checks, then manual approval, then activation
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_std = T("Sanctions_Check")
quality_std = T("Quality_Review")
checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_std, quality_std])
# No edge between sanctions_std and quality_std: they are concurrent.

manual_std = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = T("Activate_Supplier")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_std, manual_std, activate_std]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(checks_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_std, activate_std)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route: sanctions -> auto-approval -> provisional
# activation -> quality review. Eligibility, risk, country, group status,
# and automated master-data validation result are data overlay conditions.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_auto = T("Sanctions_Check")
auto_approve = T("Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = T("Activate_Supplier")
quality_auto = T("Quality_Review")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_auto, auto_approve, activate_auto, quality_auto]
)
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_auto, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate_auto, quality_auto)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency route: sanctions -> provisional activation -> retrospective
# quality review and manual approval in parallel -> notification/closure.
# Amount cap, monitoring, deadline, and authority are data overlay rules.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_em = T("Sanctions_Check")
activate_em = T("Activate_Supplier")
quality_em = T("Quality_Review")
manual_em = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_em, activate_em, quality_em, manual_em]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_em, activate_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, quality_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, manual_em)
# No edge between quality_em and manual_em: retrospective work is concurrent.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Rejection route: sanctions pre-screen, optional quality review for a
# late rejection, then rejection. Early pre-screen rejection skips quality
# through the silent branch.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_rej = T("Sanctions_Check")
quality_rej_or_skip = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[S(), T("Quality_Review")],
)
reject = T("Reject_Supplier")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_rej, quality_rej_or_skip, reject]
)
reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_rej, reject)
reject_route.order.add_edge(quality_rej_or_skip, reject)
# No edge between sanctions_rej and quality_rej_or_skip.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route choice and common completion
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route],
)

notify = T("Notify_Requester")
close = T("Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, info_gate, route_choice, notify, close]
)
M1.order.add_edge(register, info_gate)
M1.order.add_edge(info_gate, route_choice)
M1.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Event and data-collection proposal**

Every case and event should carry: `case_id`, `case_type`, `supplier_id`, `event_id`, `source_transaction_id`, `activity_label_original`, `activity_label_canonical`, `activity_mapping_version`, `event_time_utc`, `recorded_time_utc`, `rule_set_version`, `overlay_id`, `overlay_version`, `eligibility_snapshot_id`, `actor_alias_id`, `actor_canonical_person_id`, `actor_role`, and `delegation_id` where applicable. Effective time and recorded time must both be immutable server-controlled timestamps; corrections must be new superseding events, never overwrites.

The eligibility snapshot must be captured at the decision point and stored immutably with risk tier, country, corporate-group status, supplier identifiers, prior-case link for reactivation, certification validity interval, and source-system timestamps. Later recomputation must not change the snapshot used to judge the decision.

Sanctions, quality, auto-approval, activation, and rejection events must carry structured results rather than free text: check result, criteria version, validation result for Auto_Approve, provisional or full activation status, cumulative pre-approval spend, monitoring confirmation for emergency cases, and retrospective approval identifier. Notification requires a stable UUID `notification_id` generated by the CRM, a case-notification link table, recipient canonical ID, channel, template version, and delivery status. Mutable text fields must not be used as the authoritative case link.

Identity data must use a versioned alias registry mapping alias IDs to canonical person IDs. Delegation evidence must name the delegator, delegate, scope, amount limit, start and end times, and approving record. Conflict data must include disclosure ID, relationship type, reporting-line flag, and recusal or independent-review decision. Emergency and pilot events must carry the authority document ID, authorizing role, compliance-concurrence record, and the predicate values that made the overlay applicable.

With those fields, conformance reporting can separate four judgments: whether the trace fits M1, whether the cited overlay was valid and its predicates were satisfied, whether resource and amount rules were satisfied, and whether the evidence is sufficient to support any of those conclusions.
