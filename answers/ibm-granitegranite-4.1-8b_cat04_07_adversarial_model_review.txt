# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Intended Use Cases**
- **Exploration**: Early-stage process discovery where slight deviations and noise are tolerated to surface potential new paths.
- **Conformance Enforcement**: Production-level process monitoring requiring strict adherence to policy; any deviation must be flagged.
- **Simulation**: Forecasting the impact of policy changes or new activities (e.g., external sourcing) without real-world execution.
- **Stakeholder Communication**: Providing a model that can be easily understood by non-technical stakeholders for governance discussions.

**Tradeoff Considerations**
- **Exploration** values **permissiveness** (high fitness on noisy data) but tolerates lower precision, allowing novel traces to slip through.
- **Conformance** demands **high precision** and **low fitness on violations**, even if it reduces training alignment (i.e., the model may fit less smoothly on known data).
- **Simulation** balances **generalization** (captures unseen but plausible behavior) against **soundness** (ensures only allowed traces are accepted).
- **Stakeholder Communication** prioritizes **comprehensibility** (high comprehension scores) over raw metric scores, ensuring the model can be explained in meetings.

Given these contexts, a model suitable for **exploration** could justify a slightly lower holdout fitness, while a model for **compliance** must demonstrably reject unseen but policyviolating traces, irrespective of training fitness.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest Case for Model S**
- **Robustness to Missing Events**: By allowing optional activities (e.g., Supplier Review, Expedite Review) and permissive ordering, Model S can process logs with partially observed traces without crashing alignment.
- **Ease of Use**: Simple linear process tree structure makes it digestible for business analysts (high comprehension score 4.4).

**Strongest Case Against Model S**
- **Policy Ambiguities**:
  1. **Skipping Mandatory Checks** (`Credit Check` and `Technical Review` can be omitted if not explicitly present).  
     *Trace*: `Register Dossier  Manager Approval  Release Order` (forbidden by P2). Model S would accept it.
  2. **Incorrect Expedite Ordering**: Expedite Review can precede mandatory checks.  
     *Trace*: `Register Dossier  Expedite Review  Credit Check  Technical Review  Manager Approval  Release Order` (forbidden by operational policy). Model S accepts it.
  3. **Silent Duplicate Labels**: No explicit prohibition of repeated checks without correction; silent repetition could be interpreted as noise tolerance.  
     *Trace*: `Register Dossier  Credit Check  Credit Check  Technical Review  Manager Approval  Release Order` (policyambiguous; F7 expects correction). Model S treats it as permissible.
  4. **Lack of External/Supplier Distinction**: Supplier Review is optional but not conditioned on an external flag, violating P3.  
     *Trace*: `Register Dossier  Credit Check  Technical Review  Supplier Review  Manager Approval  Release Order` (valid per Model S but may apply to an internal order that shouldn’t need Supplier Review). Model S cannot enforce P3.
  5. **Optional ExactlyOne Approval**: Model S simply chooses between ‘Manager Approval’ and ‘Auto Approval’ without guaranteeing exclusivity; a trace could contain both, causing logical inconsistency.  
     *Trace*: `Register Dossier  Credit Check  Technical Review  Manager Approval  Auto Approval  Release Order` (Model S would accept, violating P5).  

**Semantic vs. Noise Tolerance**
- Permissiveness (e.g., forgiving missing `Credit Check`) is **intentional noise tolerance** for exploratory use, but the above traces reveal **accidental semantic flaws** that directly contradict policy requirements.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest Case for Model C**
- **AuditReady Branch Coverage**: Explicit enumerations of all observed permutations (creditcheck order, presence of Supplier Review, Expedite Review) ensure that known valid traces are accepted with high precision (0.941).  
- **Correction Handling**: Includes a dedicated `Correct Dossier  Credit Check  Technical Review` loop, theoretically allowing selective rechecking (P6 compliance).  

**Strongest Case Against Model C**
1. **OverEngineering with Branch Enumeration**: The model lists 8 atomic sequences plus a correction loop, leading to **branch bloat** (F3). Future policy changes (e.g., adding a new optional step) would require extensive edits.  
2. **Duplicate Labels and Complexity**: Reporting 33 duplicate labels (unexplained) indicates **inconsistent naming**, complicating impact analysis and ownership (F3).  
3. **Unseen Combination Suppression**: Probe B5 (`Register Dossier  Technical Review  Credit Check  Supplier Review  Expedite Review  Auto Approval  Release Order`) is a plausible external/urgent case but **not explicitly enumerated**, causing Model C to likely reject it (holdout fitness 0.744). This reflects **limited generalization** (F4).  
4. **Correction SemiTransparency**: The correction loop repeats *both* checks regardless of which was actually affected, violating P6 (“only the checks affected by the correction should repeat”). Consequently, the model may **rerun unnecessary checks**, inflating runtime and risking doublecounting.  
5. **High Holdout Degradation**: Low holdout fitness (0.744) implies the model fits training data tightly but **fails to generalize** to unseen legitimate behavior, which is unacceptable for **conformance** (P2).  
6. **Maintainability Burden**: Maintainers must track each atomic branch; missing event detection (e.g., external sourcing flag not present) forces manual extension, increasing operational risk.  

**Possible Artifact of Memorization**  
The spike in training precision (0.941) paired with a sharp drop in holdout fitness suggests **overfitting to specific training traces** rather than capturing underlying process semantics.

# 7. Third Alternative

## 7.1 Compact Formal Description
We propose a **ProceduralConstraintWeighted (PCW) model** that combines a lightweight procedural backbone with attributeconditioned constraints and uncertainty handling.

- **Procedural Backbone** (linear):
  ```
  Register Dossier
   Credit Check
   Technical Review
   ConditionalSupplier?   // optional, gated by external flag (missing if unknown)
   ConditionalExpedite?   // optional, may follow Supplier Review
   ApprovalChoice        // exactly one of (Manager Approval, Auto Approval)
   Release Order
  ```
- **Constraints** (declarative, enforced by preprocessing):
  1. **Mandatory Checks**: `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` must always occur.
  2. **Supplier Review Condition**: `Supplier Review` only allowed if `external = true`. If `external` is missing (NA), treat as *internal*  skip Supplier Review; log a warning.
  3. **Expedite Order**: `Expedite Review` must appear **after** `Technical Review` **and** may appear **before or after** `Supplier Review` but **must be preceded** by both mandatory checks.
  4. **ExactlyOne Approval**: Enforce that exactly one of `Manager Approval` or `Auto Approval` appears; reject traces with both.
  5. **Correction Loop**: If a trace contains `Correct Dossier`, rerun **only** the checks that were affected (determined via a posthoc mapping):
     - `Correct Dossier  [Credit Check] if credit missing/incorrect`
     - `Correct Dossier  [Technical Review] if technical missing/incorrect`
     - `Correct Dossier  [Supplier Review] if external flag changed`
     - `Correct Dossier  [Expedite Review] if expedite timing changed`
  6. **Soundness**: All transitions must be reachable from `Register Dossier` and terminate at `Release Order`.

- **Uncertainty / Posthoc Policy Check**: After alignment, run a **policy validator** that inspects the execution trace for any prohibited permutations (e.g., Expedite before mandatory checks). If found, mark as *nonconforming* despite high alignment fitness.

## 7.2 PM4Py Code (main model)

```python
import pm4py  # noqa

# ---- Define the process as a POWL (Partial Order Workflow Language) ----
from pm4py.objects.powl import factory as powl_factory
from pm4py.objects.powl.variants import imperative  # linear backbone
from pm4py.objects.powl.decorators import (
    conditional as cond,
    ordered as ord
)
from pm4py.serializers.powl import petri

# Create basic sequential transition
seq = powl_factory.create_process_imperative()

# 1. Register Dossier
start = seq.add_transition("Register Dossier")

# 2. Credit Check
credit = seq.add_transition("Credit Check", source=start)

# 3. Technical Review
tech = seq.add_transition("Technical Review", source=credit)

# 4. Conditional Supplier Review (optional)
supplier_cond = cond.create_conditional_transition(
    source=tech,
    true_branch=seq.add_transition("Supplier Review", name="External Supplier Review"),
    false_branch=tech,  # skip if not external
    condition=lambda trace, idx: trace.attributes.get("external", False)
)

# Note: If `external` flag missing  treated as False, thus condition evaluates to False.
# 5. Conditional Expedite Review (optional, ordersensitive)
expedite_cond = cond.create_conditional_transition(
    source=tech,
    true_branch=seq.add_transition("Expedite Review", name="Expedite Review"),
    false_branch=tech,
    condition=lambda trace, idx: trace.attributes.get("expedite_requested", False)
)

# Ensure Expedite comes after Tech (already true because of conditional placement)
# 6. Approval Choice – exactly one of Manager or Auto Approval
approval_choice = ord.create_ordered_transition(
    input_transitions=[seq.add_transition("Manager Approval"),
                       seq.add_transition("Auto Approval")],
    ordered=True,
    condition=lambda trace, idx: (trace.get_event(idx-1) == "Manager Approval") ^ 
                                 (trace.get_event(idx-1) == "Auto Approval")
)

# 7. Release Order
release = seq.add_transition("Release Order", source=approval_choice)

# ---- Add Correction Loop (posthoc handled, but modeled as optional transition) ----
correct = seq.add_transition("Correct Dossier")
tech.add_predecessor(correct)
credit.add_predecessor(correct)

# ---- Export to Petri Net for evaluation ----
net, im, fm = petri.apply_powl_to_petri_net(seq)

# Assign to variable for later use
model_third = net
```

## 7.5 Limitations Due to Missing Attributes
- **External/Internal Flag**: Not present in logs  must rely on **default handling** (`external=False` when missing) and issue a **warning** for ambiguous cases.
- **AffectedCheck Attribute**: Not available, but the correction mechanism uses **tracelevel event context** (e.g., presence of a preceding `Credit Check` failure marker) to infer which check to rerun. This introduces **heuristic uncertainty** that must be validated with domain experts.

## 9.8 Decision Rule
- **Reject** any model that:
  - Has **training precision < 0.85** *or* **holdout precision < 0.80** (indicating memorization or poor generalization).
  - Shows **fitness on negative suite > 0.05** (accepts violations).
  - Fails **soundness check** (`pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)`) or exhibits **suspicious duplicate mandatory checks**.
- **Accept** a model if it satisfies **all**:
  - Training fitness  0.95, holdout fitness  0.80,
  - Training precision  0.90, holdout precision  0.85,
  - Median stakeholder comprehension  4,
  - No violations on curated negative suite,
  - Soundness = True.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Exploratory Use**:  
- **Model S** may be retained **only** for rapid, noisetolerant discovery of new path patterns, **but** it must be accompanied by **postprocessing filters** that enforce P2P5 (e.g., a rule engine that blocks traces lacking mandatory checks).  
- **Model Third** is **preferred** for exploratory mining because its structured backbone still permits permissive extensions (e.g., silent optional steps) while retaining a clear separation between mandatory and conditionally optional activities.

**Compliance Enforcement**:  
- **Model S** is **unacceptable** due to its inability to enforce mandatory checks and policyambiguous ordering. It should be **retired** for any production monitoring role.  
- **Model C** is **auditready** in terms of precision but suffers from bloat, runtime, and poor generalization. It should **not** be used for enforcement of unseen scenarios.  
- **Model Third** is **recommended** for compliance monitoring. Its explicit constraints, correction handling, and soundness verification make it robust against policy violations. It must undergo the validation program (Section9) before production deployment.

**Hybrid Strategy (if needed)**:  
- Deploy **Model Third** as the **primary compliance engine**.  
- Run **Model S** in parallel in a **sandbox environment** to discover novel process variations; any novel patterns that align with policy should be **reviewed** and **ingested** into Model Third as additional optional branches.

**Conditions for Deployment of Model Third**:  
1. **All validation experiments** (Section9) must meet the decision rule thresholds.  
2. **Stakeholder comprehension** survey must yield a median 4 with low interquartile range (<1).  
3. **External flag handling** must be documented and tested with both present and missing cases.  
4. **Correction logic** must be reviewed by process owners and updated if realworld feedback shows missing affected checks.

# 11. Dissenting View

While the third alternative appears a balanced compromise, one could argue that **Model C**, despite its apparent overengineering, offers unprecedented **precision** on the training data and a **discrete enumeration** that aligns with audit requirements. Retaining Model C could be justified if the organization values **absolute certainty** for highrisk transactions, even at the cost of higher maintenance overhead. Moreover, the **negativetrace suite** used for validation might itself be incomplete; unseen policy violations could still slip through Model Third if the suite fails to capture edge cases. Finally, the **external/internal flag** assumption (internal when missing) could be undesirable—if a missing flag actually indicates an **unknown external source**, the model would incorrectly skip Supplier Review, potentially compromising compliance.

**Additional Evidence Required**:  
- A **goldstandard audit log** annotated with groundtruth labels for *external* sourcing and *expedite* urgency to measure target metrics against true compliance.  
- **Longitudinal studies** demonstrating how the third alternative performs under realtime operational traffic, especially for **bursty expedite requests**.  
- **Costbenefit analysis** comparing maintenance effort (duplicatelabel resolution, correction logic updates) versus the reduction in policyviolation incidents.  

Only with such evidence can we definitively choose between the third alternative and retaining Model C for highstakes compliance contexts.
