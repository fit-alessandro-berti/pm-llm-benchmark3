### 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current warranty process exhibits a **structural mismatch between volume-driven efficiency and risk-adjusted quality**, creating a false economy. The process-mining artifacts reveal that the dominant path is not universally optimal; it is a high-volume, low-precision path that externalizes costs into rework, compliance exceptions, and customer dissatisfaction.

*   **The "Fast Approval" Trap and Hidden Costs:** The DFG shows `Intake -> Auto eligibility -> Fast approval` handles 3,600 cases (72% of volume). While the mean cycle time is low (performance DFG: mean 1.0h, median 0.4h, p90 4.0h), the rework statistics reveal a critical flaw: `Fast approval` cases have a 14.1% complaint/reopen rate and a 9% cost reduction only after accounting for rework (vs. 31% before). The performance DFG shows `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` has a mean of 72 hours and p90 of 150 hours. The aggregate "fast" path is dragging down overall quality and eroding cost savings, particularly for high-value devices where wrong replacements cost EUR 410.
*   **Segment Blindness in Auto-Screening:** The auto-screen applies uniform logic, ignoring the `Customer segment outcomes`. Premium high-value cases routed to Fast approval suffer a 22% complaint rate, while Standard high-value cases suffer 18%. The auto-screen is likely misclassifying risk for these segments. Conversely, Premium low-risk cases tolerate the fast path well (6% complaint rate), indicating the fast path is viable for low-risk/low-value segments.
*   **Compliance Violations and Precision Loss:** Conformance findings highlight `Fast approval before Evidence completeness` and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. Normative rule R1 requires manual/fraud review for claims >EUR 400, but the fast path likely bypasses this for high-value items. Rule R3 is violated by `Customer complaint/reopen` cases that reopen without mandatory manual review, creating a loop of `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` (890 cases) that is slow (mean 34h) and expensive.
*   **Evidence Friction and Abandonment:** The `Request missing evidence` activity is a major bottleneck. It occurs in 26% of cases with multiple requests, has a mean duration of 14.0h (p90 36.0h), and feeds back into manual review. The performance DFG shows `Request missing evidence` adds significant delay. This friction drives abandonment (320 cases) and increases cycle time for 22% of cases classified as "Unclear defect / missing evidence."
*   **Resource Underutilization and Bottlenecks:** Fraud/risk specialists are a bottleneck (2 specialists, high utilization), yet the auto-screen pushes cases that should be reviewed into the fast path, causing downstream complaints that require manual intervention later. The `Escalation board` is underused for proactive risk management and only activated reactively. The process lacks a mechanism to filter low-risk claims from high-risk ones before decisioning, forcing all claims through a single funnel.

**Synthesis:** The contradiction between speed/cost and quality is resolved by segmenting the process. The fast path is efficient *only* for low-risk, low-value claims. For high-value or premium claims, the fast path is a compliance risk and a quality trap. The redesign must decouple these flows to prevent the "fast" path from contaminating high-risk segments while preserving speed for safe segments.

### 2. Redesign A: Segment-Adaptive Dynamic Routing with Risk-Weighted Auto-Approval

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
1.  **Intake Claim:** Capture segment, device value, and defect type.
2.  **Segment & Risk Classifier:** A lightweight decision engine applies R1/R2 rules immediately.
    *   *High-Value (>EUR 400) OR Premium High-Value:* Routes to `Expedited Manual Review` or `Fraud/Pre-check`.
    *   *Standard Low-Value / Premium Low-Risk Accessory:* Routes to `Auto Eligibility` with enhanced rules.
    *   *Unclear Evidence:* Routes to `Evidence Capture` (see Redesign B integration).
3.  **Segmented Decisioning:**
    *   *Fast Path Segment:* `Auto Eligibility` -> `Dispatch` (with parallel part-matching validation).
    *   *Manual Segment:* `Manual Eligibility` -> `Repair Quote` or `Dispatch` (with compliance close).
4.  **Compliance Gate:** `Dispatch` only if `Part-Match Score` > threshold; otherwise `Rework Selection`.

**Targeted Segments/Paths:**
*   Targets the `Intake -> Auto eligibility` divergence.
*   Targets `Fast approval` complaints on high-value devices.
*   Targets `Request missing evidence` loop by routing evidence-heavy cases to appropriate queues earlier.

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Compliance by Design:** Enforces R1/R2 at intake. High-value claims cannot take the fast path, eliminating the 22% complaint rate for Premium high-value and 18% for Standard high-value. This reduces rework costs and wrong-replacement costs (EUR 410).
*   **Speed Preservation for Safe Segments:** Low-risk claims retain the fast path. By removing high-value cases from the auto-screen, the auto-screen can be tuned more aggressively for speed without compliance risk.
*   **Resource Optimization:** Redirects manual reviewers from low-value claims to high-value claims where their expertise adds value. Reduces `Fraud/risk review` load by filtering obvious low-risk cases via classifier.
*   **Quality Gain:** Reduces `Customer complaint/reopen` by ensuring high-value claims receive appropriate scrutiny. Aligns process with the "Normative Petri net" by preventing R1/R2 violations.

**Deliberate Sacrifices/Unresolved:**
*   **Classifier Accuracy Risk:** If the classifier misclassifies a high-value claim as low-risk, compliance risk returns. Requires robust validation.
*   **Intake Friction:** Requires richer data at intake (e.g., serial number validation, purchase date). May slow initial intake if data is missing.
*   **Unresolved:** Does not address the `Request missing evidence` loop directly; evidence issues can still occur in the manual path. Does not optimize parallelism for the manual path.

### 3. Redesign B: Evidence-First Intelligent Capture with Adaptive Abandonment Mitigation

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
1.  **Intake Claim:** Capture claim details.
2.  **Intelligent Evidence Gate:**
    *   System predicts evidence completeness based on claim type and history.
    *   *High Confidence Complete:* Routes to `Auto Eligibility`.
    *   *Low Confidence / Missing:* Routes to `Evidence Portal/Chatbot` with guided capture.
3.  **Evidence Validation:**
    *   `Evidence Portal` -> `Auto-Validation` (OCR/Image analysis).
    *   *Valid:* Routes to `Eligibility Assessment`.
    *   *Invalid/Missing:* `Targeted Request` with clear deadline and status visibility.
4.  **Adaptive Routing:**
    *   If evidence remains missing after validation, routes to `Manual Eligibility` with full context.
    *   Abandonment triggers `Retention Offer` (e.g., partial credit) rather than hard denial.

**Targeted Segments/Paths:**
*   Targets `Request missing evidence` loop (26% rework rate).
*   Targets `Claim abandoned` (320 cases) and `Customer complaint/reopen` due to poor evidence experience.
*   Targets `Unclear defect / missing evidence` segment (22% of cases).

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Cycle Time Reduction:** Moves evidence collection upstream. Reduces `Request missing evidence` occurrences by 30-45% (per prompt). Reduces rework by ensuring evidence is complete before decisioning.
*   **Cost Reduction:** Eliminates multiple evidence requests per case. Reduces manual effort in `Manual eligibility review` by providing validated evidence upfront.
*   **Compliance Support:** Ensures `Evidence completeness` is confirmed before `Fast approval`, supporting R2. Reduces `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` caused by missing proof.
*   **Customer Experience:** Guided capture reduces effort for customers. Status visibility reduces anxiety. Adaptive retention offers may recover abandoned claims that are actually eligible.

**Deliberate Sacrifices/Unresolved:**
*   **Digital Literacy Risk:** Evidence portal may increase abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers. Mitigated by `Adaptive Abandonment Mitigation` (offers).
*   **Implementation Complexity:** Requires integration with evidence portal, OCR, and chatbot. Higher dev cost.
*   **Unresolved:** Does not address high-value compliance risks; evidence gate must integrate with segment data to ensure high-value claims still get manual review. Does not reduce cycle time for claims that are truly eligible but routed to slow manual paths.

### 4. Redesign C: Parallel Risk Synchronization with Embedded Quality Gates

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
1.  **Intake Claim:** Standard intake.
2.  **Parallel Processing Engine:**
    *   Trigger `Auto Eligibility` AND `Parallel Risk/Compliance Check` simultaneously.
    *   `Parallel Risk/Compliance Check` evaluates: R1/R2 rules, `Fraud/Risk` indicators, `Part-Match` prediction, and `Customer Segment` risk profile.
3.  **Decision Orchestration:**
    *   If `Auto Eligibility` = Pass AND `Parallel Risk` = Low: -> `Fast Approval`.
    *   If `Auto Eligibility` = Pass AND `Parallel Risk` = Medium/High: -> `Expedited Manual Review` (with pre-filled data).
    *   If `Auto Eligibility` = Fail: -> `Request missing evidence` or `Denial`.
4.  **Embedded Quality Gate:**
    *   `Fast Approval` cases include `Pre-Dispatch Validation` for part selection based on device attributes.
    *   `Dispatch` includes `Proof of Delivery` capture to close compliance loop.

**Targeted Segments/Paths:**
*   Targets `Auto eligibility screen -> Fraud/risk review` sequential delay.
*   Targets `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` due to wrong parts.
*   Targets `Quality/compliance close` latency.

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Cycle Time Reduction via Concurrency:** `Auto eligibility` and `Fraud/risk` run in parallel. Reduces p90 cycle time for cases that would otherwise go to `Fraud/risk review`. Performance DFG shows `Fraud/risk review` has high variance (p90 28.0h); parallelization reduces this tail.
*   **Resource Efficiency:** `Parallel Risk/Compliance Check` can be lightweight automation for low-risk cases, reserving human `Fraud/risk review` for true exceptions. Reduces `Fraud/risk review` load by filtering false positives.
*   **Quality Improvement:** `Pre-Dispatch Validation` catches wrong part selection before dispatch, reducing `Rework repair` and `Customer complaint/reopen`.
*   **Compliance:** Embedded R1/R2 checks in parallel engine ensure compliance is not bypassed. `Parallel Risk` flags high-value claims for manual review even if auto-screen passes.

**Deliberate Sacrifices/Unresolved:**
*   **Resource Contention:** Parallel checks may spike demand on `Fraud/risk` or `Quality check` resources if not scaled.
*   **Orchestration Complexity:** Requires robust system integration. Risk of "decision paralysis" if parallel checks take too long.
*   **Unresolved:** Does not address evidence capture; if evidence is missing, parallel checks may still pass, leading to errors. Requires `Intelligent Evidence Gate` to be effective.

### 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A: Segment-Adaptive Dynamic Routing**
*   *Failure Mode 1:* **Segment Misclassification.** If the classifier incorrectly routes a high-value claim to the fast path, compliance risk (R1) and complaint rates spike. This could be mitigated by a "confidence threshold" that routes uncertain cases to manual review.
*   *Failure Mode 2:* **Intake Data Quality.** If segment/value data is missing or inaccurate at intake, the classifier fails. This displaces the bottleneck to intake data validation, increasing `Intake claim` duration.
*   *Displaced Bottleneck:* **Manual Eligibility Queue.** By filtering low-risk cases, manual load may shift to high-value cases, potentially increasing wait times for manual reviewers if capacity is not adjusted. However, high-value cases may have higher priority, improving CX.

**Redesign B: Evidence-First Intelligent Capture**
*   *Failure Mode 1:* **Portal Abandonment.** Customers with low digital literacy may abandon claims when presented with an evidence portal. This increases `Claim abandoned` rate, hurting volume and revenue.
*   *Failure Mode 2:* **False Evidence Validation.** If OCR/Image analysis incorrectly validates poor evidence, claims proceed to `Fast approval` with insufficient proof, leading to downstream complaints and compliance issues.
*   *Displaced Bottleneck:* **Evidence Validation Queue.** If auto-validation fails, cases may pile up in `Targeted Request` or `Manual Eligibility` with rich context, potentially slowing resolution for edge cases.

**Redesign C: Parallel Risk Synchronization**
*   *Failure Mode 1:* **Parallel Check Latency.** If `Parallel Risk/Compliance Check` takes too long (e.g., due to system load or complex rules), it negates the speed benefit of parallelism and may exceed SLAs for `Fast approval`.
*   *Failure Mode 2:* **Resource Starvation.** Parallel execution increases concurrent resource demand. If `Fraud/risk` or `Quality check` resources are constrained, parallel checks may queue, causing system-wide slowdowns.
*   *Displaced Bottleneck:* **Decision Orchestration Layer.** The system managing parallel flows may become a bottleneck if not designed for high throughput. Also, `Pre-Dispatch Validation` may create a bottleneck if part-matching data is stale or requires manual intervention.

    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:*
        *   *Go:* Compliance violations reduced to <1%. `Fast approval` complaints on high-value reduced by >50%. Cost savings >5% after rework.
        *   *Stop:* `Claim abandoned` increases by >10% (indicating intake friction). Manual reviewer utilization exceeds 90% (indicating resource strain).

2.  **Second Pilot: Redesign B (Evidence-First Capture) on "Unclear Defect" Segment**
    *   *Rationale:* Once routing is stable, address evidence friction. Pilot B only on cases flagged as `Unclear defect / missing evidence` to limit risk. Test evidence portal vs. current process.
    *   *Metrics:* `Request missing evidence` frequency, Cycle time for evidence cases, Abandonment rate, Customer satisfaction (CSAT).
    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:*
        *   *Go:* `Request missing evidence` reduced by >30%. Cycle time reduced by >20%. Abandonment increase <5%.
        *   *Stop:* Abandonment increases by >10%. Portal usage <50% of target cases.

3.  **Third Pilot: Redesign C (Parallel Risk Synchronization) on Low-Risk Segment**
    *   *Rationale:* Parallelism is complex and resource-intensive. Pilot C only on low-risk claims where compliance risk is low. Test parallel checks vs. sequential process.
    *   *Metrics:* Cycle time p90, `Fraud/risk` utilization, System latency, `Fast approval` accuracy.
    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:*
        *   *Go:* Cycle time p90 reduced by >15%. `Fraud/risk` utilization reduced by >20%. No increase in system errors.
        *   *Stop:* Parallel check latency >2 hours. Resource contention causes delays in other processes.

**Learning Logic:**
*   **Isolating Improvements:** Use A/B testing within segments. Ensure that improvements in Redesign A are not just shifting complaints to later stages by monitoring `Customer complaint/reopen` rates over time.
*   **Downstream Shift Detection:** Monitor `Quality/compliance close` and `Escalation board` volumes. If Redesign A reduces complaints but increases `Escalation board` load, the improvement may be superficial.
*   **Cost Validation:** Track `Cost per claim` including rework and service credits. If Redesign C reduces cycle time but increases `Rework repair` due to parallel check errors, the cost benefit is negated.
*   **Sequence Rationale:** A ensures compliance and segment safety. B addresses evidence friction which impacts all segments. C optimizes concurrency only after A and B are stable, as parallelism requires clean inputs and routing. This sequence minimizes risk and builds organizational capability.
