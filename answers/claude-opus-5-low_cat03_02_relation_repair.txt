## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants** (violating these means the repair is wrong, not merely unlikely):

- H1. No new events or objects may be created; repair only adds relations among the twelve listed objects and fourteen listed events.
- H2. Every E2O and O2O row must reference an existing event id and existing object id (referential integrity).
- H3. O2O edges are directional and typed: `Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, `Payment settles Invoice`. A repair may not invert a qualifier to make a pattern fit.
- H4. Relations must respect lifecycle order in the sense that an object cannot be *packed* before it is *added*, dispatched before packed, or invoiced before ordered. All candidate repairs here are checked against the timeline 08:00 → 16:00 and none require a backwards edge.
- H5. No duplicate (event, object, qualifier) or (source, target, qualifier) tuples.

**Strong defaults** (domain semantics 1–7; deviations need positive evidence):

- S1. `Pack Item` has exactly one Item and one Package. ev08 has an Item only, and rule 3 explicitly names scanner outage as the expected cause — so the *missing* side is a Package, not an intentional zero.
- S2. `Dispatch Package` has at least one Package and one Delivery. ev10 has a Package only.
- S3. `Record Payment` has exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice. ev14 has a Payment only; unapplied cash is the sanctioned exception.
- S4. E2O and O2O should be mutually consistent: if event *e* relates an Item and a Package under `Pack Item`, the corresponding `Package packs Item` O2O edge is expected, because the three existing pack rows (ev06/PK-7/IT-A1, ev07/PK-8/IT-A2) show exactly that pairing convention.
- S5. An Item belongs to exactly one Order (each Item here carries an `order_hint` and exactly one `contains` edge).

**Weak heuristics** (informative, never decisive — rule 9):

- W1. Identifier similarity: `scanner_ref=PK8-2` resembles `PK8-1`; `external_ref=N-421?` resembles `carrier_ref=N-421`; `N-42*` matches both `N-420` and `N-421`.
- W2. Temporal proximity: ev07 (09:04) and ev08 (09:05) are one minute apart, consistent with the same packing station; ev09 and ev10 share a timestamp (10:00), consistent with either a shared dispatch wave or two independent dispatches.
- W3. Route and carrier: PK-7, PK-8, DEL-X, DEL-Y all sit on route NORTH, so route agreement discriminates nothing.
- W4. Customer identity: ORD-A, ORD-B and INV-44 all reference C17, which makes a consolidated invoice possible but also makes customer matching non-discriminating between the two orders.
- W5. Amount arithmetic: 130 + 100 = 230 and PAY-1 + PAY-2 = 230. Rule 6 is explicit that this is evidence, not proof; fees, taxes, partial shipments and coincidence can all produce equal sums.

**Evidence against repairs:**

- A1. The question mark in `external_ref=N-421?` is itself a data-quality marker: the source system was unsure. Treating it as a clean key is over-reading.
- A2. DEL-Y is otherwise completely unreferenced. Attaching ev10 to DEL-Y is the only thing that gives DEL-Y a life — which is a motive to be suspicious of the inference (rule: do not repair merely to complete lifecycles).
- A3. PAY-1 = 100 = ORD-B's declared value and PAY-2 = 130 = ORD-A's declared value. This is a *trap*: it tempts an order-level payment allocation that has no counterpart in the qualifier vocabulary (`settles` targets Invoices, not Orders) and no support from any event.
- A4. ev11 lists ORD-A only. Any delivery attachment is inference from a wildcard string.
- A5. INV-44 total = 230 while its only `bills` edge points at ORD-A (130). Either the invoice is consolidated, or it includes 100 of fees/tax/shipping on a single order. Both are live.

**Combination policy.** I treat cardinality gaps (S1–S3) as the *trigger* for repair, and identifier/time/amount similarity only as the *selector* among candidate targets. Where a cardinality gap has a single type-compatible candidate that is also supported by two independent weak signals, I accept (confidence ≥ 0.7). Where two candidates remain type-compatible, I mark provisional and never let confidence exceed ~0.6. Where the only support is arithmetic or customer identity — signals shared by both competing hypotheses — I mark unresolved or reject. Existing O2O structure is used as a *template* (the pack/carry/bill/settle patterns already realised), not as independent confirmation of an edge I just inferred.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative / minimum-change.** Add only what a cardinality gap forces and where a single type-compatible target exists.

- *ev08 / PK-8*: add E2O ev08→PK-8 and O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` (P01, P02). Rule 3 names scanner outage; PK-8's scanner sequence PK8-1/PK8-2 is the strongest same-station signal in the log. Accepted, with the explicit note that PK-8 becomes a mixed-order package.
- *ev10 / DEL-X vs DEL-Y*: add E2O ev10→DEL-Y and O2O `DEL-Y carries PK-8` as **provisional**, flagged rather than silently merged. The distinct external ref is the discriminating evidence; the `?` keeps confidence below 0.6.
- *ev11*: leave the delivery attachment **unresolved**. `N-42*` matches both carriers, and under Bundle A ORD-A's goods are split across PK-7/DEL-X and PK-8/DEL-Y, so a single-delivery confirmation would be internally inconsistent. ev11 keeps ORD-A only; no ORD-B is added.
- *INV-44 / ORD-B*: left **unresolved**. The only support is arithmetic, which rule 6 disqualifies as proof and which the fee/tax story explains equally well.
- *PAY-2 / INV-44*: added **provisionally** (P12, P13) because rule 7's "normally one Invoice" plus a single existing Invoice makes unapplied cash the less likely of two readings — but it is marked, not asserted.

Lifecycle coherence: Orders — ORD-A is created, itemised, packed (through its items), dispatched, confirmed, invoiced, paid; ORD-B is created, itemised, packed, dispatched, and then stops. That truncation is *honest*: ORD-B's downstream status genuinely is not recorded. Packages both have pack and dispatch steps. DEL-X is complete; DEL-Y is dispatched but never confirmed. INV-44 is issued and receives two payments totalling its face value while formally billing one order — a residual inconsistency Bundle A deliberately preserves rather than smooths.

**Bundle B — consolidated-customer interpretation.** Hypothesis: C17's two orders were handled as one commercial and physical flow.

- *ev08 / PK-8*: same as A (P01, P02) — this bundle depends on it, since a mixed package is what makes consolidation physical.
- *ev10*: same as A (DEL-Y), but confidence rises slightly because a two-delivery NORTH run is consistent with two packages leaving simultaneously on different carrier refs.
- *ev11*: attach **both** DEL-X and DEL-Y (P07, P08) and additionally ORD-B (P09). Reading: the portal issued one confirmation for the whole customer consignment and lost the carrier suffix, which is exactly the failure mode rule 5 describes; `N-42*` as a wildcard over both refs is the natural reading.
- *INV-44 / ORD-B*: add E2O ev12→ORD-B and O2O `INV-44 bills ORD-B` (P10, P11). Rule 6 explicitly permits multi-order invoices, and the invoice's customer attribute matches.
- *PAY-2 / INV-44*: add (P12, P13), now as a two-instalment settlement of a consolidated invoice.

Lifecycle coherence: both Orders acquire full create→item→pack→dispatch→confirm→invoice→pay lifecycles; both Deliveries are dispatched and confirmed; INV-44's 230 is reconciled to two orders and two receipts. The bundle is internally consistent — that is precisely its danger. Its coherence is *produced* by the repair, not observed, and it rests on one arithmetic coincidence plus one wildcard.

**Bundle C — bulk-dispatch, single-delivery variant.** ev10 is a second row of a bulk dispatch: PK-8 also goes on DEL-X (P05, P06), the `N-421?` being a mis-transcribed line item. ev11 then attaches to DEL-X alone with much higher confidence, and DEL-Y is accepted as a genuinely unused master-data object. Invoice and payment handling follow Bundle A. This bundle is coherent and has the virtue of explaining the identical 10:00 timestamps, but it must dismiss the one concrete discriminating field in the log (the differing external ref), so I rank it below A and B.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** Both orders are created and itemised through explicit events, so their early lifecycle is E2O-supported and O2O-supported independently (`contains` edges exist and `Add Item` events exist — these are two genuinely separate records, though they were almost certainly produced by the same source transaction, so their independence is weaker than it looks). ORD-A continues through confirmation, invoicing and one payment. ORD-B's trace ends at packing/dispatch under Bundle A. ORD-B is therefore a *partially orphaned* object: it is not relation-less, but it has no downstream financial edge. I regard this as a faithful representation, not a defect.

**Item.** All three items have exactly one Order and, after P01/P02, exactly one Package. No item is orphaned. Cardinality is clean 1:1 on both sides.

**Package.** PK-7 holds one item; PK-8 holds two, one from each Order. That one-to-many Item relation is the most *suspicious* structure the repair introduces: it is the only cross-order aggregation in the dataset and it rests on a device-identifier substring. Gross weights (3.1 vs 2.9) do not adjudicate it — a two-item package being lighter than a one-item package is possible with GLASS-1 vs CABLE-2/CABLE-3 but is mild counter-evidence worth recording.

**Delivery.** DEL-X is dispatch-linked and (unresolved) possibly confirmation-linked. DEL-Y under Bundle A becomes dispatch-linked only — it acquires a beginning but no confirmation, an incomplete but plausible lifecycle. Under Bundle C, DEL-Y stays a genuine orphan object, which is acceptable: master data referenced by nothing is a normal occurrence.

**Invoice.** INV-44 is issued once, bills one order (Bundle A), and receives two payments. The 130-vs-230 mismatch is a live inconsistency that Bundle A does not resolve.

**Payment.** Both payments now settle INV-44. No payment is orphaned. The 2:1 Payment→Invoice cardinality is normal instalment behaviour and does not require explanation.

**Orphan events.** After Bundle A, no event lacks at least one object. ev11 remains under-specified (one Order, no Delivery) — an *under-connected* rather than orphan event.

**Circular reasoning check.** Three of my additions are explicitly non-independent pairs: (ev08→PK-8, `PK-8 packs IT-B1`), (ev10→DEL-Y, `DEL-Y carries PK-8`), and (ev14→INV-44, `PAY-2 settles INV-44`). In each case the O2O edge is *derived* from the E2O edge by the log's own pack/carry/settle convention. It would be circular to then cite the O2O edge as corroboration of the E2O edge, and I do not: each pair carries a single joint confidence, and the O2O member is always scored slightly lower than its E2O parent. The only relations in this dataset with genuinely dual support are the pre-existing ones (ORD-A contains IT-A1 with ev03; PK-7 packs IT-A1 with ev06; DEL-X carries PK-7 with ev09; INV-44 bills ORD-A with ev12; PAY-1 settles INV-44 with ev13) — and even there, both records likely originate from one system write.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired, the Item object type produces `Add Item → Pack Item` for all three items, but IT-B1's Pack Item event carries no Package, so the Package type shows only two `Pack Item` occurrences and one `Dispatch Package` reaching a Delivery. Bundle A adds a Package-typed `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` arc for PK-8 and, via ev10→DEL-Y, a Delivery-typed dispatch fragment for DEL-Y. Bundle B additionally creates Order-typed arcs `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery → Issue Invoice → Record Payment` for ORD-B, i.e. a second complete order-to-cash path where the unrepaired log has none, and it doubles the Delivery-typed `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` arc (once for DEL-X, once for DEL-Y). Bundle B also makes `Record Payment` appear twice on the Invoice-typed path, turning a single settlement into a visible instalment loop.

**OTG (object-type graph).** Unrepaired: Order–Item, Item–Package, Package–Delivery, Invoice–Order, Payment–Invoice. Bundle A adds no *new type pair* — it only thickens Item–Package, Package–Delivery and Payment–Invoice. This is a good property of a conservative repair: it changes weights, not topology. Bundle B likewise adds no new type pair but substantially raises Invoice–Order (2 instead of 1) and Order–Delivery co-occurrence via ev11, which can flip an edge from below to above a frequency threshold and make the OTG look denser.

**ET–OT graph.** Unrepaired, `Pack Item` relates to Item and Package for two of three events and Item only for the third; `Dispatch Package` relates to Package always and Delivery only once; `Record Payment` relates to Payment always and Invoice once. Bundle A regularises all three activity/type cells to full coverage, which is exactly what makes the repaired log look "cleaner" than the evidence warrants. Bundle B further adds an (Confirm Delivery, Delivery) cell that the raw log does not contain at all — the raw ev11 touches only Order — and strengthens (Issue Invoice, Order) to two objects.

**Flattenings.**
- *Order*: unrepaired, ORD-A yields Create Order, Add Item ×2, Confirm Delivery, Issue Invoice; ORD-B yields Create Order, Add Item. Bundle A leaves this unchanged (no Order-side additions). Bundle B gives ORD-B the trace Create Order, Add Item, Confirm Delivery, Issue Invoice — a fabricated variant that would, in a variant analysis, appear as a legitimate second behaviour.
- *Package*: unrepaired, PK-8 has Pack Item ×1 and Dispatch Package; Bundle A makes it Pack Item ×2 then Dispatch Package, changing PK-8's variant and introducing a repeated-activity pattern.
- *Delivery*: unrepaired, DEL-Y has no trace at all. Bundle A gives it a single Dispatch Package. Bundle B gives it Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery, duplicating DEL-X's variant and doubling the apparent number of completed deliveries.
- *Invoice*: unrepaired INV-44 shows Issue Invoice → Record Payment; both bundles show Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment.
- *Payment*: PAY-2 is trace-less in the unrepaired log and gains a one-event trace in both bundles.

**Activity frequency measures.** Event-count frequencies never change — the repair adds no events. Unique-object and total-object counts change substantially: `Dispatch Package` goes from 3 related objects (PK-7, DEL-X, PK-8) to 4; `Pack Item` from 5 to 6; `Record Payment` from 3 to 4 under Bundle A, and `Issue Invoice` from 2 to 3 and `Confirm Delivery` from 1 to 4 under Bundle B. Any dashboard ranking activities by object involvement will re-order under Bundle B while an event-count ranking stays flat — a good diagnostic for detecting that the repair, not the process, moved the numbers.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, ORD-A and ORD-B fall into clearly distinct clusters: one full order-to-cash execution, one truncated. Bundle A preserves that separation (ORD-B gains only indirect package/delivery context). Bundle B collapses them into a single equivalence class, which would be reported as "the process is homogeneous across orders" — the exact conclusion the missing data cannot license. If a downstream analysis is about order-completion variance, the choice between bundles determines the answer, so it must be surfaced, not buried.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Fabricating a cross-order package.** Accepting ev08→PK-8 makes PK-8 the only object joining ORD-A and ORD-B physically. Every downstream convergence metric, every Package-flattened variant, and any "orders are consolidated" narrative then hangs on one scanner-reference substring. If the true package was PK-7 (or a package not in this extract), the analysis reports a consolidation behaviour that never occurred.

2. **Turning an arithmetic coincidence into an order-to-cash branch.** Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130+100=230 gives ORD-B a full lifecycle, collapses the Order equivalence classes into one, and makes the process look uniform. Fees, taxes and shipping on ORD-A produce the same total; rule 6 warns about precisely this.

3. **Allocating payments to orders by amount.** PAY-1=100 and PAY-2=130 mirror the two orders' declared values so neatly that an aggressive repair will pair them. Doing so invents an Order–Payment type edge that appears nowhere in the observed O2O vocabulary and would show `Record Payment` inside Order-flattened traces, corrupting cycle-time-to-cash calculations.

4. **Resolving the ev11 wildcard to force a confirmation step.** Assigning `N-42*` to a single delivery creates a `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` arc for that delivery and denies it to the other. Under a mixed-package reading, ORD-A is not fully delivered by either delivery alone, so either single assignment is provably incomplete, while assigning both may invent a confirmation for a delivery that failed.

5. **Reviving DEL-Y just to avoid an orphan.** Master-data objects legitimately exist without activity. Attaching ev10 to DEL-Y because DEL-Y "needs" a lifecycle inverts the direction of inference; if ev10 was really the second line of a bulk dispatch onto DEL-X, the repaired log reports two consignments where one shipped, doubling delivery counts and halving average packages-per-delivery.

6. **Treating derived O2O edges as independent corroboration.** Adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` right after adding ev08→PK-8, then citing the O2O edge as a second source, manufactures apparent confidence. Any enrichment step that materialises O2O edges from E2O co-occurrence compounds this and can make a 0.7-confidence guess look like a verified fact.

7. **Suppressing genuine data-quality signal.** The `?` in `N-421?` and the `*` in `N-42*` are the log telling us it is unsure. A clean repaired export silently deletes that signal, so nobody downstream can re-open the question — the most damaging over-repair of all, because it is invisible.
