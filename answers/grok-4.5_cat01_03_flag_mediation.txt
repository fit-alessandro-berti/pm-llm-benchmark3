## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework: **interventional direct/indirect effects** (and policy contrasts), not natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects require cross-world counterfactuals (mediators under one flag world, outcomes under another) that are poorly defined here: shared queues create interference (SUTVA failure), flag is a **policy bundle** (priority lane + review routing + escalation propensity), and post-flag human actions respond to the same case features that would remain if the bit were flipped. Interventional effects randomize or set mediator *distributions* under explicit policies P0–P4; separable-effects ideas are used when disabling a downstream pathway (e.g., mandatory review) while leaving triage priority intact.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P1 vs P0 (suppress flag display; score & charts unchanged) | case-level; all arrivals in steady state | total cycle time | total policy effect (interventional) | Partially: display suppression is operationally clear | Interference via shared queues; behavior change when staff know score still exists |
| E2 | P0 flagged vs P0 unflagged, within risk_score band | case-level; cases with overlapping scores | arrivaltriage_done | descriptive / crude association, not pure total effect | Weak as causal total effect | Confounding by complexity, U_risk, arrival_load; flag not randomly assigned |
| E3 | P2 vs P0 (keep priority triage; remove automatic mandatory MR) | case-level; would-be-flagged under current scorer | cycle time; P(manual_review=1) | interventional indirect via MR *occurrence* (pathway disabled) | Yes as policy contrast | Exposure-induced confounding: triage findings affect discretionary MR |
| E4 | P3 vs P0 (protected MR capacity for flagged) | case-level; flagged; system-level for spillovers | manual_queue_wait; cycle time | interventional effect on waiting-time mediation | Yes if capacity rule is explicit | Spillover to unflagged; need system-level estimand too |
| E5 | P4 vs P0 (change escalation criteria/capacity) | case-level among reviewed; system-level seniors | escalation occurrence; esc wait; cycle time | interventional indirect via escalation pathway | Yes as protocol change | Scarce senior team; selection into escalation confounds quality |
| E6 | P2 vs P0, mediator stochastic intervention on MR wait distribution | would-be-flagged | cycle time | interventional indirect via *waiting* (not occurrence) | Conceptually yes; hard in data | MR wait is queue-equilibrium outcome, not unit treatment |
| E7 | P1 vs P0 | cases near flag threshold | decision_outcome (Approve/Reject) & proxy error if labels exist | total effect on decision quality | Partial | No ground-truth label; risk_score imperfect; confounding |
| E8 | Controlled direct: force priority_lane=0, hold MR/escalation rules fixed | hypothetical unit intervention on lane only | triage_wait + triage_service | controlled direct analogue on triage completion | Only if lane can be set independently of flag bit | In actual system lane is bundled with flag; positivity thin |
| E9 | P0 vs “no priority, same MR rules” (synthetic bundle split) | flagged population | triage_done timestamp relative to arrival | interventional direct (triage path) | Requires ability to unbundle | Historical co-occurrence of flag and lane |

Interference arises because flagged volume loads shared manual and senior queues, so one case’s flag alters others’ waiting times (not a unit-treatment world). Exposure-induced confounding appears when triage content and intermediate delays, themselves affected by the flag/priority bundle, influence discretionary review and escalation—mediators then confound later mediator–outcome paths. Treatment–confounder feedback occurs along the timeline: queue state is shaped by prior flags and then confounds later cases’ waits; conditioning on post-flag queue or service times opens colliders with U_risk and U_queue_state. Cross-world natural-effect contrasts that set “MR as if unflagged” while “keeping priority as if flagged” are not organizationally meaningful without an explicit unbundling policy.

Durations in minutes. **Absent stages are not coded as 0**; subgroup stage means use only cases that traverse the stage (`NA` if structural absence or n=0). Values are arithmetic means (small n; medians similar for triage).

**Handling:** means over cases with non-missing stage timestamps only; `NA` = stage not in path for that subgroup (or universal absence). Do not impute zero wait for skipped MR/escalation when interpreting “delay.”

**Interpretation (observational only):** Flagged cases show much shorter arrivaltriage_start (~14 vs ~149 min) and somewhat shorter triage service (~36 vs ~57), consistent with a priority lane and faster handling. Downstream, **all** flagged cases enter manual review with long triagemanual waits (~390 min vs ~113 among unflagged who are reviewed), and 6/10 escalate with multi-hour senior queues—so longer total cycle (~991 vs ~490) is concentrated in MR occurrence, MR queueing, and escalation, not triage. Escalated flagged cases average ~1285 min vs ~549 without escalation. These contrasts are **not automatically causal**: flagged cases have higher risk_score and complexity; unflagged MR is selectively applied; shared queues mean flagged volume may lengthen waits for everyone; arrival_load is coarse; residual close times look administrative and similar across groups.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is longer cycle caused by flag policy or by risk mix? | Local threshold / within risk_score band (e.g., 0.55–0.75) comparing flag 0/1 or discontinuity if sharp cutoff | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, time-of-day | Local overlap; no precise manipulation of score around cutoff; stable measurement | Density test on score; balance of complexity either side; placebo outcome pre-triage | Smooth cycle-time rise with score and **no** jump at flag  confounding dominates policy |
| How much delay is MR wait vs service vs occurrence? | Queue-state-aware survival models for wait; competing paths for skip MR | queue length at entry (from timestamps), TOD, complexity, flag, resource | Correct queue reconstruction; limited unmeasured priority within queue | Predicted vs actual wait calibration; independence of service time from queue after complexity | If conditional on queue state flag adds no wait, “flag penalty” is congestion, not case stigma |
| What does suppressing display do? | Randomized encouragement / staggered P1 pilot: hide flag bit, keep score | site or case random arm; block on load | Compliance known; no contamination across boards | Manipulation check (did staff see bit?); first-stage on MR rate | If MR rate & cycle unchanged under hide-flag, automatic UI bit is inert vs score-driven judgment |
| MR occurrence vs wait mediation | Interventional mediation: stochastic intervention on P(MR) and separately on wait distribution under P2 vs P3 | pre-flag confounders; avoid post-treatment colliders | No unmeasured MR–outcome confounding **or** use interventional (not natural) effects; partial interference modeling | Path-specific G-formula/sim queue model vs observed; E-value for U_risk | If killing auto-MR (P2) removes most cycle gap, occurrence—not triage—dominates |
| Negative control | Outcome: residual-to-close admin gap; or exposure: flag effect on pre-register delay (should be null) | same strata | Admin close not affected by flag path | Non-null “effect” on pre-arrival or pure admin residual | Suggests timestamp artifacts or selection coding bias |
| Sensitivity to U_underlying_risk | Rosenbaum/E-value bounds; simulate omitted risk correlated with flag and service times | observed score, complexity | Bounded confounding strength | How large (U,flag) must be to explain away triage or cycle gaps | Small confounding suffices to explain cycle gap  do not claim large direct flag harm |
| Interference-aware | Case-weighted exposure: fraction flagged in prior window; cluster RCT of P3 capacity | own flag + recent flagged volume + load | Partial interference within time windows | Own-flag effect shrinks after peer-flag control; cluster-level cycle moves with flagged share | Strong peer effects  unit-level mediation estimates misleading; prefer system policies |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 current | Fast for flagged | Heavy MR + senior load on flagged | Long for flagged; mixed system | Higher scrutiny on high score | Unflagged may wait behind flagged MR | Observational only; confounded | Baseline; do not expand flag rate blindly |
| P1 hide flag | May lose some priority if lane tied to bit | MR rate may fall if auto-rule needs bit | Ambiguous: slower triage, less MR | Under-review of high risk if staff ignore score | Less MR congestion | Weak without RCT | Test only with quality audit |
| P2 priority, no auto-MR | Keep fast triage | Large drop in MR entries; remaining MR harder mix | Likely shorter mean cycle for ex-flagged | **Highest** miss risk if score imperfect | Frees MR for judgment cases | Medium conceptual; untested | Pilot candidate with safety monitors |
| P3 + protected MR capacity | Unchanged triage | Shorter flagged MR wait; unflagged depends on additive vs reallocated staff | Shorter flagged cycle if capacity **additive** | Maintains scrutiny | If reallocated, unflagged hurt | Medium; needs capacity cost | Best performance–quality trade if budget allows |
| P4 escalation criteria/capacity | None direct | Senior queue  if criteria tighter or staff  | Large gain for escalated tail (~1285 min group) | Too-tight criteria  bad Approve; too-loose  queue | Senior scarcity is binding | Tail pattern clear in data | Pair with P2/P3; fix senior bottleneck |

**Limited pilot (prefer P2 + light P4 guardrails, or P3 if headcount exists):** Choose **P2** as primary knowledge-generating pilot (unbundles priority from mandatory MR).

- **Eligibility:** new cases with risk_score  current flag threshold; exclude legally mandatory review categories if any.  
- **Assignment:** case-level Bernoulli (p=0.5) after score: Arm A = P0 bundle; Arm B = priority lane retained, **no** automatic MR (discretionary MR allowed using score + complexity + triage notes). Stratify by complexity and arrival_load.  
- **Queue guardrail:** if discretionary MR backlog > pre-specified percentile or senior queue > T hours, temporarily restore auto-MR for High complexity (safety valve). Cap concurrent Arm B rate if triage outpaces MR.  
- **Primary outcomes:** cycle time; triage completion time. **Secondary:** MR rate, MR wait, escalation rate, decision distribution, 30-day adverse quality proxy (appeals, losses, rework), unflagged spillover waits.  
- **Minimum data:** full stage start/done timestamps, resources, score, complexity, load, queue length at each entry, arm id, discretionary reason codes.  
- **Stopping:** harm rule if adverse quality proxy exceeds bound; futility if cycle time  <  with no quality change; sample for 95% CI width on cycle difference.  
- **Do not** interpret as natural DE/IE; report intention-to-treat policy contrasts and pre-specified mediation by realized MR occurrence/wait.

TIME_COLS = [
    "arrival",
    "triage_start",
    "triage_done",
    "manual_start",
    "manual_done",
    "escalation_start",
    "escalation_done",
    "close",
]


def parse_cases(csv_text: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(csv_text))
    for c in TIME_COLS:
        df[c] = pd.to_datetime(df[c], errors="coerce")
    df["flag"] = df["flag"].astype(int)
    # treat literal "-" resources as missing
    for c in ["triage_resource", "review_resource"]:
        df[c] = df[c].replace({"-": np.nan})
    return df


def minutes_between(a: pd.Series, b: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    """Minutes from a -> b; NaN if either missing (absent stage)."""
    return (b - a).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0


def stage_intervals(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Explicit waits/services; absent stages stay NaN (not zero)."""
    out = df[["case", "flag", "complexity", "arrival_load", "risk_score", "outcome"]].copy()
    out["arrival_to_triage_start"] = minutes_between(df["arrival"], df["triage_start"])
    out["triage_service"] = minutes_between(df["triage_start"], df["triage_done"])
    out["triage_done_to_manual_start"] = minutes_between(df["triage_done"], df["manual_start"])
    out["manual_service"] = minutes_between(df["manual_start"], df["manual_done"])
    out["manual_done_to_escalation_start"] = minutes_between(
        df["manual_done"], df["escalation_start"]
    )
    out["escalation_service"] = minutes_between(
        df["escalation_start"], df["escalation_done"]
    )
    out["has_manual"] = df["manual_start"].notna()
    out["has_escalation"] = df["escalation_start"].notna()
    # residual: close - last real stage end
    last = df["triage_done"].copy()
    last = last.where(~df["manual_done"].notna(), df["manual_done"])
    last = last.where(~df["escalation_done"].notna(), df["escalation_done"])
    out["residual_to_close"] = minutes_between(last, df["close"])
    out["total_cycle"] = minutes_between(df["arrival"], df["close"])
    return out


def wide_to_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Only emit events for stages that actually occurred. No fabricated skips."""
    records = []
    stage_specs = [
        # (concept, start_col, end_col, resource_col)
        ("Register", "arrival", "arrival", None),  # instantaneous anchor
        ("Triage", "triage_start", "triage_done", "triage_resource"),
        ("Manual Review", "manual_start", "manual_done", "review_resource"),
        ("Escalation", "escalation_start", "escalation_done", None),
        ("Close", "close", "close", None),
    ]
    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        for concept, sc, ec, rc in stage_specs:
            start_ts = row[sc]
            end_ts = row[ec]
            if pd.isna(start_ts) or pd.isna(end_ts):
                continue
            rec = {
                "case:concept:name": row["case"],
                "concept:name": concept,
                "time:timestamp": end_ts,
                "start_timestamp": start_ts,
                "org:resource": row[rc] if rc else np.nan,
                "flag": row["flag"],
                "complexity": row["complexity"],
                "arrival_load": row["arrival_load"],
                "risk_score": row["risk_score"],
                "outcome": row["outcome"],
            }
            records.append(rec)
    event_df = pd.DataFrame.from_records(records)
    event_df = event_df.sort_values(
        ["case:concept:name", "start_timestamp", "time:timestamp"]
    ).reset_index(drop=True)
    return event_df

    sub = intervals.loc[mask]
    n = len(sub)
    print(f"\n=== {label} (n={n}) ===")
    if n < 5:

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** Conditional on a richer risk label (audits, losses), residual cycle gaps and Reject rates are fully explained by U_underlying_risk; the flag bundle adds no operational delay beyond warranted scrutiny—then “flag causes delay” is mostly confounding, and cutting auto-MR (P2) is unsafe.  
2. **Queue interference:** System models show most flagged MR wait is caused by peer flagged volume, not own-case routing; unit-level mediation is misleading and **P3 additive capacity** or flag-rate caps dominate P2.  
3. **Decision quality:** Pilot or retrospective labels show P2 raises false Approve / compliance failures beyond tolerance—then retain mandatory MR (P0/P3) despite longer cycles.  
4. **Timestamp semantics:** “Start” times are when staff open a workitem after offline delay, or clocks differ by resource; apparent triage acceleration is recording artifact—then priority-lane direct effect is overstated.  
5. **Positivity / overlap:** Essentially no unflagged cases resemble flagged High/complexity/score support; within-score contrasts are empty—then observational DE/IE analogues are unidentified and only prospective randomized unbundling is credible.  
6. **Reviewer behavior change:** Under P1/P2, reviewers substitute even heavier discretionary work or informal escalation (“workarounds”), erasing cycle gains or biasing quality—then UI/policy changes fail and incentive/training redesign is required before capacity changes.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** In these 20 cases, flagged work reaches triage far sooner and with shorter triage service, yet every flagged case enters manual review, most of the extra cycle sits in long post-triage manual waits and, for a high-complexity subset, multi-hour escalation queues; mean cycle is roughly double that of unflagged cases. Unflagged low-complexity cases often skip review entirely. Rejects cluster on high complexity, not the flag bit alone. Resources and arrival_load co-vary with paths.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag implements a **bundle**—priority triage plus near-mandatory review plus elevated escalation propensity—on imperfect scores. Acceleration at triage is real as policy; net slowdown is mediated mainly by **review occurrence and shared-queue waiting**, then scarce senior time, not by slower fine-motor service in review.

**Unidentified:** Natural direct/indirect effects of “setting flag=0,” pure causal shares of occurrence vs wait vs confounding by U_risk, and equilibrium spillovers under interference. Cross-world “MR as if unflagged while priority as if flagged” is not a defined organizational act without unbundling. Decision quality lacks ground truth.

**Best next evidence:** A limited **P2** randomized unbundling pilot (priority retained, auto-MR removed, safety valve for High complexity), with queue-length logging and quality proxies—or **P3** if additive reviewer capacity is funded. That estimates interventional policy contrasts the business can actually run, separates triage from downstream mediation, and stresses quality before scaling.
