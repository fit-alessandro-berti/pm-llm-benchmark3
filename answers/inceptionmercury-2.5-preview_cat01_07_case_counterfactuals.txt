

## 1. Feasibility model
1. Partial-order relation set:
```text
Register < Triage
Triage < {Identity Check, Document Check}
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Decision
Decision < Notify
Identity Check  Document Check < Compliance Review (if high risk)
Assessment < Compliance Review (final)
Decision > Compliance Review (final)
```

2. Conditional and declarative constraints:
- High-risk/value cases require a final Compliance Review after the latest Assessment.
- Same person cannot perform both final Assessment and final Compliance Review.
- Resources cannot overlap service intervals.
- Original Receipt precedes Document Check only if originals required.
- Rework loops require returning to Document Check/Assessment.

3. Intervention classifications:
- K-104 changes timing and resource coordination (parallel work).
- K-219 changes routing (resource substitution).
- K-337 changes decision policy (suppressing information gathering).
- K-411 changes concurrency and task creation.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
- **Factual mechanism:** Final compliance review waited until assessment completion and C1 became available after 20h backlog.
- **Minimal intervention:** S1 engages at Triage; C1 slot reserved; compliance work starts early without blocking final sign-off.
- **Temporal propagation:** Parallel work reduces total elapsed time. However, final review still waits for assessment.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Compliant with rules; only timing changes.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: -10h cycle time; Base: -6h; Worst: slot reserved but work insufficient, no gain.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If preliminary compliance flags new missing docs, early work becomes wasted.

### K-219
- **Factual mechanism:** D1 held due to queue; D2 bypassed despite availability.
- **Minimal intervention:** Route to D2 immediately upon receipt.
- **Temporal propagation:** D2 starts sooner but may finish slower.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Permitted routing change.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 handles cleanly, -3h; Base: no change; Worst: handwriting clarification needed, +14h.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If D2 rejects task due to handwriting complexity, routing fails.

### K-337
- **Factual mechanism:** External inconsistencies caused repeated RMI loops.
- **Minimal intervention:** Enforce decision policy stopping info gathering after second assessment.
- **Temporal propagation:** Eliminates weekend delay loop.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Allowed via policy override; still requires Compliance sign-off.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: -48h; Base: -50h; Worst: compliance rejection if risk deemed too high.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If unresolved discrepancy violates risk policy, approval impossible.

### K-411
- **Factual mechanism:** System lock delayed document task creation.
- **Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks; allow concurrent work.
- **Temporal propagation:** Both checks finish earlier.
- **Process-feasibility check:** Concurrency allowed per rule 3.
- **Best/base/worst case:** Best: -4h; Base: -4h; Worst: assessment bottleneck adds 4h wait.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If system prevents logging concurrent timestamps, audit fails.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early compliance prep | C1 demand shifted earlier | Identity | Compliance | Urgent cases needing C1 | High | C1 single point of failure |
| D1D2 substitution | D1 queue decreases | Assessment | Documents (D2) | Cases needing D1 speed | Medium | D2 rework probability increases |
| Suppress RMI loop | External wait removed | External | Decision | Cases needing external confirm | High | Decision accuracy drops nonlinearly |
| Parallel checks | Assessment arrival earlier | Assessment | None | Cases arriving earlier | Medium | Queue congestion spikes on arrival |

Rolling out these policies to 100 cases weekly shifts critical bottlenecks. K-104's early compliance prep reserves C1 capacity before readiness, potentially blocking other urgent high-risk cases waiting in queue. K-219's D1D2 routing reduces D1 wait but increases D2 load; given D2's slower handling of complex inputs, rework rates may spike, pushing work back into Assessment queues. K-337's policy change removes external delays but exposes the system to higher compliance risk and potential decision reversals, which are costly downstream. K-411's parallelization finishes checks faster, causing "rush hour" arrival at the assessment stage. Since Assessment queue is already growing (e.g., Wed 08:00), this accelerates congestion. Weekend and external-response effects remain unpredictable. Real-time timestamps required by K-411 must be validated; otherwise, false concurrency could lead to audit failures. The system-wide effects are nonlinear: capacity reservation and queue spikes interact, where earlier readiness without downstream capacity leads to blocked pipelines rather than speed gains.

## 7. Final synthesis

The "Suppress RMI loop" intervention appears most robust because it fundamentally alters the decision policy to prioritize throughput when evidence stagnation is high. It avoids resource contention and yields immediate time savings. However, the "Early compliance prep" intervention is the most dangerous despite potential speed gains. Because compliance capacity is a single point of failure (C1), reserving slots early can starve other urgent cases, causing system-wide gridlock if capacity remains scarce. The least transportable counterfactual is K-411's parallelization; system locks and timestamp requirements vary across environments, making concurrent workflows hard to replicate without specific technical integration. The most valuable additional data is C1 workload distribution and the specific triggers for document rejection during assessment. Understanding where bottlenecks truly form is essential to ensuring that faster upstream processing does not simply pile up inventory downstream where it cannot be processed efficiently. Monitoring queue displacement will reveal whether "speed" translates to throughput or merely to congestion.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. Resource pools (C1, D2) availability remains constant during the window (resource).
2. External supplier response times remain consistent during pilot (temporal).
3. Compliance rules do not change during observation (structural).
4. Document complexity distribution is stable across cases (behavioral).
5. Assessment capacity can absorb increased upstream throughput (interference).
6. "Early" compliance work does not require re-reading evidence (quality).
7. System workaround for parallel tasks is approved by IT (structural).
8. Decision policy for suppressed RMI is approved by risk committee (behavioral).
