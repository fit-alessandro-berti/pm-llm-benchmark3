# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

**Reading the tables.** The waiting sets below contain jobs whose scans have occurred by the stated decision time, which are unfinished and not already in service; a suspended job is shown as waiting for resumption. Unless noted, all listed jobs are qualified for the indicated resource. We assume scans correspond to entry into the dispatchable queue and use the recorded arrival order as a working FIFO order. Neither is guaranteed: the true arrival can precede a scan by up to two minutes, so an unscanned job may already be physically present at a boundary, and arrivals whose scans are within two minutes can reverse order. No job with a scan more than two minutes after a decision can have arrived by that decision under the stated bound. Scan time alone also does not establish when dispatch could see or select a job.

Priority predictions use smallest priority number; SPT uses the arrival-time estimate for a new job. For a suspended job, its current expected remaining time is unobserved, so SPT predictions involving it are conditional. Appointment predictions assume preferential service at or near the appointment, but the width and direction of any tolerance window and whether early appointments are eligible are unknown. A dash means that the rule alone does not choose among these jobs; a skill rule filters candidates but does not define an ordering. Deadlines are discussed separately, since earliest deadline first is not among the requested columns. All apparent selection errors are conditional on the listed waiting jobs actually being eligible and on the resource being available.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

- **QA:** A02, A04, A05 and A07 follow arrival order at their respective decisions, while A03 supports an appointment override. A03 over A02 requires appointment preference or another undocumented reason under pure FIFO. A06 at 09:20 supports both FIFO and appointment preference; starting A05 at 09:05 despite A06's 09:15 slot requires a sufficiently tolerant appointment window, acceptance of lateness, or an exception. A08 repeatedly waiting behind lower-priority jobs challenges strict priority, not FIFO. Calibration explains the 08:52–08:58 absence of service but does not explain the earlier 08:30–08:32 gap.
- **QB:** B01's resumptions and B04 ahead of B06 support FIFO with retained position; B02 supports an urgent preemption override. B05 ahead of B01 and B04 supports an urgent override but contradicts unconditional FIFO. B03 ahead of B05 at 09:48, and B01 ahead of B06 at 10:14, contradict strict priority if all are eligible. The documented reassignment explains the B01 stop, but not the ranking of the available jobs. B07's late start challenges a rule guaranteeing the appointment. Restart costs or constraints could explain continuation or resumption preferences, but their amounts and effect are not recorded.
- **QC:** C02 and C03, and later C06, support short-estimate preference. C04 over C06 and C01 over C07 contradict global SPT if both members of each pair are eligible. Neither is explained by the recorded arrival timing; a batch restriction, readiness constraint, aging rule or manual intervention would be an undocumented exception. C05 supports priority, earliest deadline, or an endangered-SLA override equally at 10:54. Its earlier non-selection at 10:18 and 10:32 challenges a fixed strict priority or earliest-deadline rule, assuming eligibility.
- **QD:** D05 assigned to D-F and D07 to D-X support skill constraints; D06 supports appointment-based assignment, although FIFO also selects it at 11:30. D04 ahead of D08 and D07 ahead of D10 challenge strict priority and SPT. The scheduled meeting can explain deferring D05 at 11:36, but not by itself D-F's failure to start D10. An unrecorded resource state, reservation, readiness restriction or other exception is needed if the records otherwise represent the full dispatchable workload. Skill compatibility is a constraint, not evidence of a unique ranking rule.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

1. **QA, 08:20: appointment preference versus FIFO.** With the observed waiting set, appointment preference predicts A03 and FIFO predicts A02; A03 was chosen. Capture the dispatcher's appointment tolerance and whether A03 was marked protected or reserved at the decision. This would tell whether the documented appointment mechanism actually applied, rather than merely coinciding with a manual choice. A repeat decision where an appointment is near its window but is not also the oldest or shortest would provide a stronger behavioral test.
2. **QB, 10:14: retained-position resumption versus strict priority.** Retained-position FIFO predicts B01; priority predicts B06. B01 resumed. Record whether B01 retained its queue position and was eligible to resume, together with the applied policy and any resumption cost. That would distinguish a deliberate resumption rule from an override or an eligibility exclusion of B06.
3. **QC, 10:54: priority versus deadline/SLA escalation.** Both explanations predict C05 in this actual set, so this episode alone cannot discriminate. In a comparable counterfactual set where C05 is priority 1 but a different eligible job has an earlier deadline or more endangered SLA, priority predicts C05 while earliest-deadline or SLA escalation predicts that other job. Record the dispatcher-visible SLA status and the rule or reason code used for selection; observe actual decisions with such conflicting rankings before claiming a policy.
4. **QD, 11:48: FIFO versus priority.** FIFO predicts D07 and strict priority predicts D10; D07 was selected. Record the eligible resource-job pairs and whether D07 had a reservation or unique-skill assignment commitment. This would separate FIFO ranking from a joint assignment that deliberately allocates D-X to D07 while expecting another resource to handle D10.

These are predictions under explicitly stated competing rules, not claims that the hypothetical jobs or policy states were observed.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

The following ranking is qualitative: no frequencies or exception logs are supplied.

1. **Skill and calendar constraints, plus possible resource reservations.** The skill sets, A1's calibration, D-F's meeting and C1's prior job are recorded, so these constraints are highly plausible. They make several apparent queue-order violations infeasible comparisons. A D-X reservation for D06 or a decision not to start D05 before a meeting is plausible but unrecorded; the latter still leaves D10 at 11:36 unexplained. Verified reservations would strengthen a skill-and-appointment hybrid and reduce the evidence for unexplained idleness.
2. **Urgent interventions and interruption/resumption costs.** B01's two stops have recorded reasons, making an exception-based QB hybrid plausible. A cost of stopping or restarting, partially completed work, or a dispatcher reassignment can affect when and whether to resume, but no cost or eligibility restriction is documented. Evidence of a priority threshold and resumption policy could turn the QB diagnosis into a more precise conditional priority rule; without it, strict priority is contradicted.
3. **Unrecorded readiness, batching, or dispatch overrides.** A scanned job might need parts, approval, preparation, or a batch release before it is dispatchable, or a manager might manually choose another job. Such states could explain QC's C04 over C06 or C01 over C07 and QD's idle capacity. They are hypotheses, not observed facts. If confirmed, the appropriate comparisons would use the smaller true eligibility sets, potentially making SPT or FIFO much more consistent.
4. **Changing urgency or SLA policy.** C05's late deadline and priority 1 make a switch to urgent handling plausible at 10:54, but those signals coincide. C07 later misses its 12:00 deadline, which prevents inferring a universal deadline-first rule. A logged threshold or policy-version change could distinguish escalation from a fixed mixed score or manual choice.
5. **Data timing and measurement issues.** Scan lag of up to two minutes can change apparent order or physical presence around a decision, for example A05 at 08:20 or C05 at 10:10. It cannot make C06 arrive after 10:32 or B05 after 09:48. Minute precision limits fine-grained sequencing, and missing or inaccurate decision, eligibility, resource-state or interruption records could create further apparent violations, though no such error is established. Corrected event times could change borderline comparisons, not the well-separated ones.
6. **Noisy or strategically inaccurate estimates.** SPT uses estimates available at dispatch, not realized duration. Different estimates visible at decision time or updated remaining-time forecasts could change the predicted ranking. Here the recorded complete segment durations equal the initial estimates for all jobs, including B01's total; that agreement does not prove forecasts were accurate ex ante or rule out unrecorded updates. A strategic estimate hypothesis needs evidence and should not be used to erase observed SPT contradictions by itself.

Appointment windows are another material uncertainty: an exact booking timestamp does not say whether early starts are allowed, how much lateness is tolerated, or whether a booking reserves a particular resource. A06's five-minute delay and B07's 26-minute delay therefore have different potential interpretations, but neither proves a specific tolerance.

# 6. Additional event data needed

1. **At every dispatch decision, capture the decision timestamp, trigger, selected job and resource, the complete eligible job–resource pairs, and exclusion or hold reasons.** Also record queue-entry and physical-arrival times separately from scan time, with scan latency and timestamp precision. This directly tests whether QC's C06/C07, QB's B05/B06, and QD's D10 were selectable when bypassed.
2. **Record resource state over time:** busy, idle, unavailable, meeting, calibration, setup and reservation, including planned versus actual start and end. Capture the simultaneous assignment decision across resources. This is essential for D-X's 11:20–11:30 gap and D-F's 11:36–12:00 gap, and for deciding whether D07 was reserved for D-X.
3. **Capture appointment rules and state:** booked time, permitted early/late tolerance, whether early arrival makes the job eligible, resource reservation, and the reason for any missed slot. Use A03/A06 and D06 to separate appointment preference from FIFO; use B07 to test whether its appointment had dispatch authority.
4. **Log the active policy version and actual decision rationale**, including manual overrides, priority, deadline, SLA risk, escalation threshold and whether the rule changes during a shift. Preserve what the dispatcher saw at the time. This distinguishes C05's priority from its overdue deadline and tests whether C07's later bypass was intentional.
5. **For each interruption and resumption, record reason, preempting job, eligibility and queue position after stopping, setup or restart cost, and current estimated remaining time.** These fields are particularly important for B01 and for separating preemptive priority, SPT, and resumption preference.
6. **Retain estimate history and outcomes:** the arrival-time estimate, all revisions with timestamps and authors, work completed, remaining-work estimate, and realized active duration. Audit systematic incentives or errors in estimates before interpreting SPT deviations. Collect repeated decisions where FIFO, priority, short estimates, appointments and deadlines predict different eligible jobs, rather than relying only on this small sample.

# 7. Final synthesis

QD is the most identifiable: the observed assignments obey the stated skill combinations and availability constraints, although its ordering within eligible work remains uncertain. QC is the least identifiable: several choices favor short estimates, but later decisions contradict global SPT and could reflect batching, eligibility, urgency or overrides. The most dangerous claim would be that QB implements **strict preemptive priority** merely because B02 interrupted B01: B03 precedes the already-arrived priority-1 B05 at 09:48, and B01 resumes ahead of priority-2 B06 at 10:14. The evidence supports a conditional hybrid, not that unconditional rule.
