# 1. Fairness dimensions

**Outcome parity (raw).** Raw approval rates range from 78% (A) to 58% (D), with B at 63% and C at 69%. These gaps are real and decision-relevant for applicants, but they are not, by themselves, evidence of discrimination: Group D has 51% high-risk scores (vs 18% for A) and Group B has 46% incomplete documentation (vs 21%). Conversely, the raw gaps cannot be dismissed as "just case mix" either, because two key case-mix variables — `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` — may themselves be produced by the process (see §5). Raw parity is a starting descriptive fact, not a verdict in either direction.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** After controlling for risk band, documentation completeness, channel, household size, prior cases, region, and month, gaps shrink but do not vanish: approval is 5.5 pp for B, 3.1 pp for D, 2.2 pp for C; cycle time is +2.6 d (B), +4.1 d (C), +0.9 d (D). The interpretation is bounded: these residuals are disparities *conditional on measured covariates*. They are consistent with (a) genuine differential treatment, (b) unmeasured confounding (severity, urgency, language proficiency beyond a binary flag), or (c) bias baked into the control variables themselves. The evidence does not discriminate among these.

**Procedural burden.** Burden is not only the decision but the path to it. Group A reaches the fast lane (V1, 5.8 d) in 42% of cases; B, C, D reach it in only 18–22%. Groups B and C are concentrated in the longest support-step variants (V4 = 24% of B at 19.2 d; V5 = 31% of C at 21.6 d). Even where each step is individually justified, the *distribution* of burden — extra steps, extra document demands, extra handovers — falls systematically on minority-language and accommodation-requesting applicants. Adjusted models sharpen this: B is +9.5 pp more likely to receive Request Missing Documents *even at the same recorded documentation completeness*, and +8.0 pp more likely to receive Supervisor Review. That pattern is not explained by measured complexity and warrants scrutiny.

**Timeliness.** Median cycle times differ (8.1 d A vs 13.7–15.9 d for B/C), and the tail is worse: 90th-percentile is 21.4 d (A) vs 34.2 (B), 38.8 (C), 29.5 (D). After adjustment, D's time gap nearly disappears (+0.9 d), suggesting D's slowness is mostly routing; B (+2.6 d) and C (+4.1 d) retain adjusted penalties that align suspiciously well with measured queue waits at rights-preserving steps (3.8 d at Language Support Contact  Eligibility; 4.7 d at Accommodation Review  Eligibility). For a benefits program, tail delays are hardship, not noise.

**Consistency of routing.** Routing is partially consistent with policy: D's 28% share of V3 (Risk Screening  Fraud Review  Supervisor Review) tracks its 51% high-risk share. But consistency must be tested *within* strata, not assumed: do equally documented, equally risky applicants receive Supervisor Review at equal rates across groups? The adjusted +8.0 pp supervisor-review uplift for B suggests possible inconsistency; the variant table alone cannot confirm it.

**Quality/accessibility of support.** Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review are rights-preserving; their existence is a fairness strength. But support that is nominally available and practically queued (only 7 bilingual caseworkers; 5 accommodation specialists) is accessibility with a tax: applicants who exercise a right receive a slower, more handoff-heavy procedure, and no service-level target exists to trigger escalation. A further test of quality — whether support steps *work* (do they reduce downstream document requests and rework?) — is available in the log but not yet answered.

**Appeal/rework burden.** Appeals are 6% (A) vs 13% (B), 10% (C), 16% (D), and remain elevated after adjustment (+4.4 pp B, +5.7 pp D). Reopen/rework is 11% (A) vs 20–24% (others). Appeals are ambiguous evidence: they may signal worse initial decisions, or better-informed applicants exercising voice. Rework is less ambiguous as a process-health signal and correlates with the high-handover routing patterns for B and C.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** Group B's cases concentrate on 7 bilingual caseworkers and 3 overloaded regional officers; Group C's on 5 specialists, two of whom also perform Supervisor Review (a bottleneck coupling and a potential separation-of-duties concern). Skill-based assignment is legitimate; *capacity under-provisioning* of the skills that minority groups need is a structural allocation choice that converts rights into delays. Because workload is observed only at daily granularity, true utilization cannot yet be computed — allocation unfairness is suggested, not proven.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The analysis must separate four quantities that the raw tables conflate:

| Quantity | Definition | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Disparity | Group difference, cause unspecified | Raw and adjusted gaps |
| Legitimate routing | Difference explained by policy-mandated triggers (risk  fraud review; accommodation request  accommodation review) | Trigger-to-path conformance checks |
| Discrimination | Differential handling of otherwise-similar cases | Within-stratum, within-variant comparisons |
| Uncertainty | Sampling noise, measurement error, unobserved confounding | CIs, sensitivity bounds, overlap diagnostics |

**Matching/stratification variables.** Stratify or match (coarsened exact matching or propensity weighting) on `risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`, `channel`, `household_size_band`, `prior_case_count`, `region`, and month derived from `time:timestamp`. Report overlap/common-support diagnostics — Group D's high-risk stratum has few Group A comparators, so some adjusted contrasts rest on extrapolation and should be flagged rather than pooled silently.

**Mediator-aware adjustment.** `language_preference`, accommodation requests, and — critically — `documentation_completeness` and `risk_score_band` may lie *between* group membership and outcome (a minority-language applicant may be "incomplete" *because* intake was not accessible). Report three model tiers: (1) unadjusted, (2) adjusted for pre-treatment attributes only (channel, household size, region, month, prior count), (3) fully adjusted. Divergence between (2) and (3) localizes how much "explanation" comes from possibly endogenous variables rather than exogenous case mix.

**Path/variant comparison.** Decompose each group's cycle-time gap into (i) a *composition* term (different variant shares: V1 access, V4/V5 exposure) and (ii) a *within-path* term (same variant, different duration), using an Oaxaca–Blinder-style decomposition on log cycle time. Then test: within V2 and within matched strata, does B still wait longer? Within high-risk strata, is D's fraud-review share proportional to A's?

**Resource-assignment comparison.** Build handover-of-work and working-together networks per group; compute per-resource case loads, group concentration (share of each resource's caseload by group), and handover counts per case. Test whether handover count predicts rework and whether bilingual/specialist queues explain the support-edge waits.

**Waiting-time decomposition.** Separate service time from waiting time per event and per directly-follows edge, per group. A gap concentrated in *waiting* on support-step edges implicates capacity and queueing; a gap in *service* time implicates case difficulty or handling. These have different remedies and different fairness meanings.

**Sensitivity analysis for biased covariates.** Re-estimate all models (a) excluding `risk_score_band`, (b) replacing it with its constituent inputs if obtainable, (c) treating `documentation_completeness` as measured pre- vs post-language-support. Report sensitivity bounds (e.g., E-values or Rosenbaum-style bounds for matched analyses) showing how strong an unmeasured confounder would need to be to erase the adjusted approval gap for B (5.5 pp) and the adjusted appeal gaps (+4.4 pp B, +5.7 pp D).

**Subgroup checks.** Interact group with `region` and `channel`: the two regions with Supervisor Review  Decision Draft backlog (1.9 d for B vs 1.0 d for A) should be analyzed separately, since pooled estimates may hide localized inequity. Check in-person vs online applicants (B is 38% in-person, and in-person intake waits longer). Use partial pooling for small cells; pre-register hypotheses; control multiplicity.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

**Most important disparities, ranked:**

1. **Support-step queues (highest concern).** Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment waits 3.8 d (B); Accommodation Review  Eligibility Assessment waits 4.7 d (C). These are *queue* waits behind tiny teams (7 bilingual staff; 5 specialists), with no SLA. A queue is a provisioning decision, not a property of the applicant's complexity — this is the least justifiable disparity in the log, and it plausibly accounts for most of the adjusted cycle-time penalties (+2.6 d B, +4.1 d C).

2. **Fast-lane access.** V1 carries 42% of A but only 18–22% of others. Part of this is genuinely explained (B's 46% incompleteness, D's 51% high-risk). But the adjusted +9.5 pp uplift in Request Missing Documents for B *at equal recorded completeness* means part of V1's inaccessibility is generated inside the process, not imported into it.

3. **Document-request behavior for B.** If recorded completeness is equal, differential requesting implies either subjective completeness standards applied unevenly, or completeness recorded coarsely enough to hide real differences, or requests used to manage language-communication difficulty. Each explanation has a different remedy; none is visible in current attributes.

4. **Supervisor Review uplift for B (+8.0 pp adjusted).** No policy trigger in the scenario explains extra supervisor review for minority-language cases at equal risk. Combined with the backlog in two regions, this both burdens B and degrades the shared queue for everyone.

5. **Handover structure.** B experiences generalbilingualgeneral chains; C experiences generalspecialistsupervisor chains, with two specialists holding both roles. Handovers are a known error/rework vector and match the elevated rework rates (22–24% vs 11%).

**Plausibly justified by case complexity (conditional on caveats):** D's concentration in V3 (28%) is consistent with the policy rule that high risk requires Fraud Review, and D's adjusted cycle-time penalty is only +0.9 d — its raw slowness is mostly routing, not queueing. D's wait at Risk Screening  Fraud Review (2.8 d vs 2.2 d for A) is a modest, risk-adjacent difference. Caveat: this justification inherits the validity of `risk_score_band` itself (§5), and D's +5.7 pp adjusted appeal gap — the largest in the table — hints at decision-quality or communication problems that routing legitimacy does not explain.

**Not justified and structurally concerning:** queue waits on rights-preserving steps; absence of timeliness targets for those steps; concentration of B/C caseloads on small overloaded teams; dual-role specialists coupling Accommodation Review to Supervisor Review.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**Legitimate mechanisms:**

1. **True complexity differences.** B's 46% incomplete documentation genuinely requires document requests; D's 51% high-risk share genuinely requires fraud review. These generate real, policy-compliant variant and duration differences.
2. **Skill-based routing.** Language and accommodation needs correctly route to specialists; specialist capacity is finite, so some queuing is unavoidable even under good faith.
3. **Regional backlog.** Two regions' supervisor backlogs inflate waits for whoever is routed there — a place-based, not group-based, mechanism that nonetheless lands unevenly because of where B applicants live and how they are routed.

**Concerning mechanisms:**

4. **Rights-as-friction.** Support steps lack SLAs, so congestion there never triggers escalation; the system silently taxes the exercise of rights, converting legal entitlement into delay and extra handovers.
5. **Mediated documentation bias.** If intake is not linguistically accessible, "incomplete documentation" is partly *produced* by the process; treating it as a neutral control then "explains away" a disparity the agency itself caused. The adjusted +9.5 pp request uplift for B is consistent with this loop.
6. **Risk-score feedback loop.** Historical enforcement patterns  elevated `prior_case_count` and risk scores for D  more fraud review  more denial/delay  more appeals and re-contacts  higher future scores. Controlling for the score then certifies as "legitimate" a disparity with discriminatory ancestry.
7. **Handover-induced rework.** Multi-resource chains (generalbilingualgeneral) lose context, producing the 2× rework rates for B/C, which feeds back into longer cycles and more supervisor scrutiny — a self-confirming perception that these cases are "difficult."
8. **Defensive scrutiny.** Extra Supervisor Review for B (+8.0 pp adjusted) may reflect reviewer uncertainty about minority-language files — a subtle differential-treatment channel invisible in approval rates.

These mechanisms are not mutually exclusive, and the current data cannot fully separate them; several are testable with the plan in §7.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias in key covariates.** `documentation_completeness` is likely assessed by the same staff whose behavior is under review, at a point in the process that may post-date language-access failures; `language_preference` understates need (stigma, form defaults). Both biases push adjusted gaps toward zero for B.
- **`risk_score_band` is not neutral.** Scores trained on historical data encode historical enforcement; controlling for them conditions on a possible consequence of discrimination. All D-related conclusions are conditional on a score whose fairness is itself unaudited.
- **Missing applicant needs.** No literacy, digital access, disability type/severity, interpreter quality, or urgency/severity measures. Residual confounding in both directions is guaranteed; bands (e.g., household size) guarantee further within-band confounding.
- **Unobserved resource workload.** Daily-granularity workload cannot distinguish idle queueing from overload, cannot compute utilization, and cannot detect whether "overloaded regional officers" are overloaded *because of* group-mix assignment.
- **Appeal quality unknown.** `appeal_flag` is binary: no grounds, no outcome, no representation, no reversal rate. Appeals can indicate error (bad) or access to redress (good); current data support neither reading. "Reopen/rework" is similarly undefined.
- **Outcome quality unmeasured.** `case_outcome` omits correctness (no re-adjudication gold standard), explanation quality, and dignity of treatment — dimensions where procedural unfairness commonly hides.
- **Process-data caveats.** Timestamps at event granularity may mis-separate service vs waiting (especially with the daily workload data); concurrency and batch processing (notifications, closures) distort performance DFGs; missing or out-of-order events bias variant and path-performance statistics; no statutory deadline attribute exists to test *legal* timeliness.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Mitigation | Rationale | Side effects / trade-offs | Safeguards |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Add capacity** for bilingual casework and accommodation review (hiring, cross-training, workload-based staffing) | Directly attacks the 3.8 d / 4.7 d queues; the only remedy that addresses a provisioning deficit | Cost and hiring lag; cross-training dilutes specialization; small teams remain brittle; concentrating all minority-language cases on few staff risks burnout and siloing | Model staffing from arrival rates and SLA targets; rotate; maintain minimum redundancy per skill |

No mitigation removes support steps; the goal is to make exercising rights cost no more time than not exercising them.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The evidence supports a **calibrated finding of procedural unevenness, with unresolved questions about discrimination**.

What can be said with reasonable confidence: (1) the large raw approval gaps (78% vs 58–69%) are substantially, though not fully, attenuated by measured case mix — raw parity is not the right benchmark; (2) after adjustment, Group B still shows a 5.5 pp approval gap, a +9.5 pp excess in document requests at equal recorded completeness, and a +8.0 pp excess in supervisor review, none of which is explained by the policy's stated routing rules; (3) Groups B and C experience systematically greater procedural burden and delay, and the largest, clearest component is queueing at rights-preserving support steps that lack any timeliness standard — a structural provisioning failure rather than a property of applicant complexity; (4) Group D's pathway is broadly consistent with legitimate risk-based routing *if* the risk score is valid, which has not been established, and its +5.7 pp adjusted appeal gap is unexplained.

What cannot be said: that any individual or unit discriminated; that approvals are incorrect; that disparities are wholly benign. The process is therefore best characterized as **not demonstrably discriminatory, but demonstrably inequitable in timeliness and burden distribution, and indeterminate on differential treatment pending the risk-score audit, mediator-aware adjustment, and within-stratum routing tests**. The appropriate response is remediation of the identified structural deficits (capacity, SLAs, handovers, explanations) alongside the deeper audits — not a verdict of either "fair" or "discriminatory," neither of which the current evidence can sustain.
