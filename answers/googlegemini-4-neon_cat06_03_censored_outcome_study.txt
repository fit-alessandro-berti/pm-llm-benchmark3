## 1. Target definition

The business request—“predict 90-day default from the first few events of every credit application”—conflates two distinct quantities: **whether an application reaches activation under the lender’s current underwriting policy**, and **whether an activated account defaults within 90 days**. Rejected, withdrawn, and unlinked transferred applications are never exposed to credit on the lender’s books; treating them as “non-default” manufactures false certainty and severely understates risk.

### Primary formulation: Decomposed multi-state early-warning target
To score *every* active application honestly without equating non-activation with non-default, we adopt a **two-stage multi-state (competing-outcome + conditional credit-risk) target**:

- **Unit of analysis:** An active, pre-decision application prefix $(i, t_p)$, where application $i$ has reached prediction point $t_p$ without yet reaching any terminal or post-decision state (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`, `Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`).
- **Prediction time ($t_p$):** Operationalized at early process checkpoints—primarily fixed canonical event count $k \in \{3, 4\}$ (typically after `Application Started`, `Identity/KYC Check`, and initial `Credit Review`) and, in parallel, fixed elapsed time $\Delta t \in \{24\text{h}, 72\text{h}\}$ from `Application Started`—using only the event history $\mathcal{F}_{t_p}$ observable at or before $t_p$.
- **Population:** All applications started within the observation window that remain open and pre-decision at $t_p$.
- **Horizons:**
  1. *Disposition horizon:* Time from $t_p$ to terminal application disposition $D_i \in \{\text{Activated}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}\}$, subject to administrative right censoring at the observation cutoff (or fixed horizon $H_{\text{disp}} = 60\text{ days}$).
  2. *Credit outcome horizon:* $90$ calendar days following `Account Activated` ($t_{\text{act}, i} + 90\text{d}$), observable only after external feed reporting lag ($t_{\text{act}, i} + 90\text{d} + L_i$, with $L_i \in [7, 35]\text{ days}$).
- **Primary estimands:** Given $\mathcal{F}_{t_p}$ under the prevailing credit policy $\pi$, we estimate:
  1. **Competing disposition probabilities:** $p_d(t_p) = P_\pi(D_i = d \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$ for $d \in \{\text{Activated}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}\}$.
  2. **Conditional 90-day default risk among activated accounts:** $q(t_p) = P_\pi(Y_{90, i} = 1 \mid D_i = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$, adjusted for reporting maturity and permanently missing partner feeds.
  3. **Joint realized default incidence (early-warning portfolio risk):**
     $$P_\pi(D_i = \text{Activated}, Y_{90, i} = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p}) = p_{\text{Activated}}(t_p) \times q(t_p)$$

**Why this primary formulation is defensible:** It produces a well-defined, empirically identifiable risk profile for *all* incoming applications; preserves the exact semantics of `default_90d`; separates underwriting selection ($p_{\text{Activated}}$) from post-activation credit risk ($q$); handles open applications via right censoring rather than dropping or mislabeling them; and prevents downstream users from mistaking low activation probability for low credit risk.

### Credible alternative targets (and why they answer different questions)

1. **Conditional time-to-default after activation with right censoring:**
   - *Estimand:* $S(t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p}, D_i = \text{Activated}) = P(T_{\text{def}} - t_{\text{act}} > t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p}, D_i = \text{Activated})$ for $t \in (0, 90]$.
   - *Different question:* Answers *"Assuming this account activates, what is its daily default hazard over the first 90 days?"* It exploits partial follow-up for recently activated accounts (if exact default timestamps exist in `Default Recorded`), but applies only to the selected activated subpopulation and cannot by itself project application-funnel risk.
2. **Policy-specific counterfactual default target (reject-inference estimand):**
   - *Estimand:* $P(Y_{90}(\text{do}(\text{Activate})) = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$—the 90-day default probability if the lender *intervened* to approve and activate applicant $i$ regardless of current policy.
   - *Different question:* Answers *"Should we change our credit policy to approve applicants we currently reject?"* While strategically attractive, it is **not point-identified** from these observational data without strong, untestable ignorability assumptions (no unmeasured underwriter judgment) or a quasi-experimental instrument (e.g., exploiting the 1 July 2025 threshold shift strictly for marginal cases). It is therefore relegated to sensitivity analysis rather than the primary target.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

### Five distinct mechanisms

| Mechanism | Affected Cases | Statistical Nature & Bias Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Ordinary right censoring** | 5,100 still-open applications (Stage 1); accounts activated after `2025-11-17` (Stage 2) | Event has not yet occurred by cutoff. Dropping open cases over-represents fast-resolving cases (e.g., instant auto-rejects or clean auto-approvals) and biases against slow document-loop cases. |
| **2. Delayed labels (reporting lag)** | Accounts activated roughly between `2025-10-13` and `2025-11-17` (within the 90d + 7–35d lag window) | **Outcome-dependent ascertainment bias:** An account defaulting at day 25 may report at day 45, whereas a confirmed non-default cannot be finalized until day 97–125. Filtering on `default_90d.notna()` inflates the apparent default rate in recent months; imputing missing as `0` artificially depresses it. |
| **3. Selection through activation** | 9,100 rejected + 5,700 withdrawn applications | Non-random truncation driven by both observed covariates and unobserved underwriter judgment. Conditioning on activation induces collider bias (Berkson’s paradox) if coefficients are misinterpreted as population-wide risk effects. |
| **4. Transfer (competing event / missing follow-up)** | 2,900 transferred applications (~60% linkable, ~40% unlinked) | Operationally terminates the local workflow; statistically acts as informative loss-to-follow-up if transfers systematically involve high-complexity or specialist-risk applicants. Partial linkage (60%) is likely non-random across partners. |
| **5. Permanently missing outcome feeds** | Subset of the 3,600 unlabeled activated accounts with $t_{\text{act}} \le \text{2025-10-13}$, concentrated in partner-originated channels | Missing outcome despite full maturity. Because partner channels differ in underwriting and applicant mix, complete-case analysis is biased unless weighted or bounded. |

### Primary analysis strategy
1. **Stage 1 (Disposition modeling):** Fit cause-specific hazard models (or discrete-time competing-risks hazard models / Fine-Gray cumulative incidence functions) over `{Activated, Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred}`, treating the 5,100 open cases as right-censored at `2025-12-31`. This uses every application without discarding slow, rework-heavy cases.
2. **Stage 2 (Conditional 90-day default):**
   - **Strict activation-date maturity filter:** For any training or evaluation cutoff $T_c$, include an activated cohort if and only if $t_{\text{act}} \le T_c - 125\text{ days}$. Never filter by `label_available_at <= T_c` alone; this eliminates differential reporting-lag bias.
   - **Inverse Probability of Observation Weighting (IPW) for permanently missing feeds:** Among activation-mature cases ($t_{\text{act}} \le T_c - 125\text{d}$), define $R_i = \mathbb{I}(\text{default\_90d observed by } T_c)$. Model $e(X_i) = P(R_i = 1 \mid \text{case:channel}, \text{source\_system}, \text{case:region}, \text{prefix features}, t_{\text{act}})$ using stabilized, truncated weights $w_i = P(R=1)/e(X_i)$ (with overlap checks) to reweight observed mature accounts when estimating $q(t_p)$.
   - **Optional time-to-default extension:** Where `Default Recorded` timestamps are reliable, supplement the binary IPW model with an IPCW-weighted survival model over days $[0, 90]$ post-activation, censoring only at $T_{\text{extract}} - 35\text{d}$ (conservative lag buffer).

### Sensitivity analyses
1. **Permanently missing feed bounds (tipping-point / Manski bounds):** Among mature unlabeled activated cases ($t_{\text{act}} \le \text{2025-10-13}$), re-estimate overall default calibration and partner-channel risk under MNAR scenarios where missing-feed accounts have $0.5\times$, $1.5\times$, and $2.5\times$ the baseline odds of default (plus worst-case $[0, 1]$ Manski intervals).
2. **Linked-transfer sensitivity:** For the ~60% of transferred cases with cross-platform linkage, verify whether transferred applicants who activate externally exhibit higher default rates than domestic activations, quantifying how much total credit risk is exported via transfers.
3. **Policy-discontinuity selection sensitivity (1 July 2025):** Use the exogenous threshold change on 1 July 2025 in a narrow window around the pre/post policy cutpoint (regression discontinuity / Heckman-style selection sensitivity) to bound how default risk behaves for marginal applicants shifted between `Rejected` and `Activated`.

### Hierarchy of assumption testability
- **Testable from these data:** Whether reporting lag (`label_available_at - (t_act + 90d)`) differs between defaults and non-defaults on mature 2024 cohorts; whether missing feeds concentrate in specific `case:channel` or `source_system` strata; whether acceptance rates shifted discontinuously on 1 July 2025; whether canonicalized activity frequencies remain stable across 15 October 2025.
- **Partially testable:** Non-informative administrative censoring for open cases (can verify whether case-start date alone explains censoring, but cannot observe post-Dec-31 resolutions); Missing-At-Random (MAR) for permanently missing partner feeds (can check covariate balance and positivity overlap, not unmeasured risk differences); whether the 60% linked transfers match the 40% unlinked transfers on observed prefix covariates.
- **Fundamentally untestable from these data:** Counterfactual default outcomes for rejected and withdrawn applicants (unobserved underwriter private information implies MNAR); whether permanently missing partner feeds have higher unobserved default propensity conditional on all logged features; outcomes of the 40% unlinked transferred cases.

## 4. Prefix extraction

### Strict as-of truncation principle
For any prediction point $t_p$ of case $i$:
1. Retain **only** events with `time:timestamp <= t_p`.
2. Exclude any event belonging to the post-decision/post-activation set $\mathcal{E}_{\text{post}} = \{\text{Accepted}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}, \text{Contract Signed}, \text{Account Activated}, \text{Collections Contact}, \text{Default Recorded}\}$.
3. Verify that case $i$ has **not** experienced any event in $\mathcal{E}_{\text{post}}$ at or before $t_p$. If it has, case $i$ is already resolved and is excluded from the active prediction cohort at $t_p$.

### Fixed event-count vs. elapsed-time prefixes
We extract both representations because they answer complementary operational triggers:

- **Fixed event-count prefixes ($k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$):**
  - *Trigger:* Fired immediately upon completion of the $k$-th canonical event (e.g., $k=3$ typically coincides with initial `Credit Review`).
  - *Strengths:* Aligns cleanly with workflow handoffs and underwriter decision queues; uniform control-flow depth.
  - *Watch-out:* Because document loops consume events, $k=5$ represents a very different process stage for a looping case than for a straight-through case.
- **Elapsed-time prefixes ($\Delta t \in \{24\text{h}, 72\text{h}, 7\text{d}\}$ from `Application Started`):**
  - *Trigger:* Fired at fixed SLA clocks after intake, setting $t_p = t_{\text{start}, i} + \Delta t$.
  - *Strengths:* Captures **inactivity / stalling** (e.g., an application still stuck in KYC at 72h is informative even though no new event occurred) and aligns directly with daily portfolio monitoring batches.
  - *Watch-out:* Produces variable event counts per case, requiring aggregation/encoding that handles lengths $1$ through $n$.

### Handling short cases, rework loops, and migration artifacts
- **Short cases:** Cases that terminate before reaching $k$ events (e.g., instant rejection at $k=2$ after KYC failure) or before $\Delta t$ are **not padded** and **not carried forward** into longer-prefix cohorts. Instead, they are scored only at earlier checkpoints where they were still open, and a prefix-attrition table reports how many cases drop out at each $k$ or $\Delta t$.
- **Repeated document loops (`Document Requested` $\leftrightarrow$ `Credit Review`):** Rather than collapsing loops (which destroys rework signal) or letting long loops explode feature dimensionality, we preserve the full truncated trace and explicitly summarize loops via rework counts (`n_doc_requests`, `n_credit_reviews`, `rework_ratio`, `time_in_doc_loop`). For milestone-specific underwriting use, an optional milestone prefix ("immediately after the 1st `Credit Review`") can supplement fixed-$k$ prefixes.
- **Activity renaming & duplicate events (15 October 2025 migration):**
  1. *Canonicalization:* Apply a deterministic versioned mapping table `(source_system, raw_activity, time:timestamp >= 2025-10-15) -> canonical_activity` **before** prefix extraction, DFG discovery, or feature computation.
  2. *Deduplication:* Remove exact and near-duplicate migration artifacts (identical `case_id`, `canonical_activity`, `org:resource`, and timestamp delta $\le 1\text{ second}$, or flagged duplicate system writes) **before** counting prefix length $k$. Without pre-prefix deduplication, duplicate events post-Oct-15 artificially shorten the effective process horizon captured by a fixed-$k$ prefix.

## 5. Process-aware features

All features must be strictly measurable with respect to the filtration $\mathcal{F}_{t_p}$ (events and system states observable at or before $t_p$).

### A. Static case attributes (fixed at `Application Started`)
- **Application metadata:** `case:channel` (direct, broker, partner), `case:region`, `case:requested_amount` (raw and log-transformed), initial `source_system`.
- **Cohort & calendar features:** Application start day-of-week, hour-of-day, month/seasonality (cyclical encodings), policy regime indicator (`case_start >= 2025-07-01`), and post-migration indicator (`case_start >= 2025-10-15` for drift diagnostics, used cautiously so models do not memorize calendar time).

### B. Prefix-derived dynamic features (computed solely from events of case $i$ with `timestamp <= t_p`)
- **Control-flow:** Current (last) canonical activity at $t_p$; binary indicators for milestones reached (`has_kyc`, `has_credit_review`); canonical activity frequency counts; bigram / directly-follows transition counts learned strictly from training prefixes.
- **Timing & velocity:** Elapsed time $t_p - t_{\text{start}, i}$; time since last event $t_p - t_{\text{last}, i}$ (crucial for elapsed-time prefixes to measure current stall duration); min, median, max, and standard deviation of inter-event sojourn times within the prefix; share of events occurring outside business hours.
- **Rework & complexity:** Count of `Document Requested` events; count of `Credit Review` entries; loop ratio ($1 - \frac{\text{unique canonical activities}}{\text{total prefix events}}$); cumulative time spent waiting between `Document Requested` and the subsequent `Credit Review`.
- **Dynamic risk trajectory:** Initial `risk_score`, latest `risk_score` observed at $\le t_p$, $\Delta\text{risk\_score} = \text{latest} - \text{initial}$, number of `risk_score` updates in the prefix, missingness indicator for `risk_score`.
- **Within-prefix resource dynamics:** Number of distinct `org:resource` actors touching the case; handoff count (consecutive events with different resources); indicator of whether the latest resource belongs to a specialist/escalation pool (defined historically).

### C. Historical / reference-data features (computed strictly from past events $< t_p$)
- **Inter-case queue & congestion (point-in-time):** Total active Work-In-Progress (WIP) applications in `Credit Review` at $t_p$; intake volume in the same `case:region` and `case:channel` over the trailing 7 days; rolling 14-day median waiting time from `Identity/KYC Check` to `Credit Review` ending strictly before $t_p$.
- **Reconstructed point-in-time resource experience:** Cumulative number of prior applications processed by the current `org:resource` strictly before $t_p$ (or true tenure $t_p - \text{hire\_date}$ from an HR snapshot, **never** extraction-time experience); rolling 30-day workload of that resource.
- **Historical target/transition encodings (strictly lagged):** Smoothed historical `Activated` rate by `case:channel` $\times$ `case:region` computed only over applications resolved prior to $t_p$ (and for default-rate priors, strictly over accounts reaching $t_{\text{act}} + 125\text{d} \le t_p$).

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | **Severe post-decision leakage.** Populated only after final credit decision; non-nullness directly reveals `Accepted` and magnitude reflects final underwriting risk. | Never available at pre-decision $t_p$. | **Drop** | Cannot exist at prediction time; leaks both Stage 1 disposition and Stage 2 credit risk. |
| `decision_reason` | **Severe direct target/disposition leakage.** Populated at or after `Accept`/`Reject`. | Never available at pre-decision $t_p$. | **Drop** | Directly encodes the Stage 1 outcome and underwriting judgment made after $t_p$. |
| `collection_status` | **Severe post-outcome leakage.** Updated after activation and directly reveals delinquency or default. | Never available prior to activation. | **Drop** | Directly reveals `default_90d` and occurs months after $t_p$. |
| Future events (`Accepted`, `Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact`, `Default Recorded`, any event $> t_p$) | **Severe temporal & label leakage.** Reveals case trajectory, disposition, and post-activation behavior after prediction time. | Strictly filter `time:timestamp <= t_p` and exclude cases with any decision/terminal event $\le t_p$. | **Repair (truncate) / Drop post-prefix** | Prefix must mirror exact information state at operational scoring time. |
| `documents_total` | **Same-day lookahead leakage.** Nightly batch snapshot includes documents uploaded *later* on the same calendar day than $t_p$, leaking subsequent applicant engagement/rework. | Either reconstruct cumulative uploads from timestamped events strictly $\le t_p$, or lag the nightly table by a full day (`snapshot_date < date(t_p)`). | **Repair (reconstruct or lag) or Drop** | Prevents intra-day future information from leaking into early-day predictions. |
| Updated `risk_score` | **Temporal lookahead & backfill leakage.** Aggregating max/last score over the full case—or using a warehouse column overwritten in-place on later events—leaks post-$t_p$ credit bureau refreshes. | Extract only `risk_score` values recorded on events with `time:timestamp <= t_p`, and audit event logs to confirm values are immutable per event rather than retroactively overwritten. | **Repair (as-of extraction + immutability audit)** | Early risk score is legitimately available and highly predictive, provided later refreshes are excluded. |
| Resource experience (at extraction time) | **Lookahead & survivorship leakage.** Value as of `2026-02-15` leaks future tenure and calendar time (staff still employed in 2026 appear more experienced in 2024). | Recompute point-in-time experience as `count(prior cases by org:resource with timestamp < t_p)` or `t_p - employee_hire_date`. | **Drop raw / Repair via point-in-time reconstruction** | Preserves legitimate handler-experience signal without leaking future calendar/employment info. |
| Label availability (`default_90d`, `label_available_at`, `data_extract_time`) | **Direct target leakage (if used as features) & ascertainment bias (if used to filter cohorts).** Filtering training rows on `default_90d.notna()` or `label_available_at <= cutoff` over-samples fast-reporting defaults relative to mature non-defaults. | Never include as features. Define Stage 2 eligibility strictly via activation-time maturity (`t_act + 125d <= T_cutoff`). | **Drop as features / Repair cohort filter** | Eliminates both label leakage and reporting-lag selection bias. |
| Post-migration renamed activities & duplicate events (`concept:name`, `source_system`) | **Covariate/schema shift & prefix-truncation bias.** Post-`2025-10-15` renames break control-flow features; duplicate events prematurely exhaust fixed-$k$ prefixes. | Apply canonical activity map and deduplicate near-identical events prior to prefix slicing. | **Repair** | Ensures semantic continuity across the October 2025 migration. |
| `case:channel` & partner missingness | **Selective missing-outcome bias (not leakage).** Partner accounts have elevated permanently missing outcome feeds. | Available at `Application Started`; include as feature and in IPW observation model. | **Keep + adjust via IPW** | Required both for predictive signal and to correct partner-channel missing-feed bias. |

## 7. Validation design

### Why a random case split is misleading here
A random split (such as naive case-level shuffling via `pm4py.split_train_test`) fails on four structural grounds:
1. **Regime leakage:** Randomly mixing pre- and post-`2025-07-01` cases allows the model to learn post-policy selection rules while evaluating on pre-policy data (and vice versa), hiding calibration collapse under policy shift.
2. **Migration leakage:** Mixing pre- and post-`2025-10-15` traces masks schema/activity-rename fragility.
3. **Inter-case & macro leakage:** Concurrent applications share queue congestion, underwriter pools, and macroeconomic conditions; splitting them randomly leaks contemporaneous state across train and test.
4. **Unrealistic label maturity:** In real deployment on date $T$, labels for the prior ~125 days of activations are not yet mature; random splits grant access to future mature labels that would never exist in production.

### Temporal validation and backtesting scheme
The exact calendar geometry of this snapshot imposes an essential constraint: because $T_{\text{extract}} = \text{2026-02-15}$ and full reporting maturity requires $90 + 35 = 125\text{ days}$, the latest activation date with guaranteed mature labels is **`2025-10-13`**—two days *before* the `2025-10-15` system migration. Our validation design explicitly confronts this boundary:

1. **Expanding-window temporal split by `case_start` (with strict as-of maturity embargoes):**
   - **Train (Pre-policy historical core):** Applications started `2024-01-01` to `2025-03-31`. When simulating deployment as of `2025-04-01`, Stage 2 training uses only activated accounts mature by that cutoff (or for the final retrospective model, mature by the validation start).
   - **Validation 1 (Pre-policy temporal holdout):** Applications started `2025-04-01` to `2025-06-30`. Tests temporal stability and hyperparameter/calibration tuning *before* the policy break; activations are fully mature by `2026-02-15`.
   - **Test 1 (Post-policy, pre-migration credit test):** Applications started `2025-07-01` to `2025-09-30` (evaluating Stage 2 strictly on activations occurring by `2025-10-13`, with a secondary survival check through `2025-11-17`). Directly measures **concept drift, selection shift, and recalibration need** caused by the 1 July 2025 credit-policy change.
   - **Test 2 (Post-migration operational & drift test):** Applications started `2025-10-15` to `2025-12-31`. Evaluates Stage 1 competing-risks disposition (with right censoring for open cases), canonical activity mapping stability, duplicate filtering, and score-distribution shift. **Explicitly does not claim validated 90-day default accuracy**, because post-migration activations have not yet reached 125-day maturity; they must be evaluated prospectively in shadow mode as feeds mature in Q2 2026.
2. **Rolling-origin backtesting (within 2024–2025):** Run quarterly expanding origins ($T_0 \in \{\text{2024-10-01}, \text{2025-01-01}, \text{2025-04-01}, \text{2025-07-01}\}$), enforcing at every origin $T_0$ that training uses only Stage 1 resolutions $\le T_0$ and Stage 2 labels with $t_{\text{act}} + 125\text{d} \le T_0$. This mirrors true production retraining lag.

### Evaluation metrics & diagnostic checks
- **Stage 1 (Competing-risks disposition):** Cause-specific time-dependent ROC-AUC / C-index; IPCW-weighted multi-state Brier score (accounting for the 5,100 censored open cases); per-disposition calibration curves (`Activated`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`).
- **Stage 2 (Conditional 90-day default):** IPW-weighted PR-AUC and ROC-AUC (prioritizing PR-AUC given the ~12.5% base rate); IPW-weighted Brier score; **Calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/intercept, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**—crucial because policy changes often preserve ranking while breaking probability calibration. If time-to-default is modeled, report IPCW Concordance Index and Integrated Brier Score (IBS).
- **Joint risk ($p_{\text{Activated}} \times q$):** Overall Brier score, decile lift on realized defaults across all applications, and **uncertainty intervals** reflecting the missing-feed sensitivity bounds.
- **Subgroup & shift audits:** Disaggregate all metrics by `case:channel` (especially partner vs. direct), `case:region`, `source_system`, prefix length $k$, and rework depth (`n_doc_requests = 0` vs. $\ge 1$). Track Population Stability Index (PSI) and Jensen-Shannon divergence on canonical activity distributions and predicted risk scores across both the `2025-07-01` and `2025-10-15` boundaries.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### What this study can and cannot claim
- **Permitted claims:**
  - Well-calibrated, uncertainty-aware early predictions of (a) application disposition probabilities and (b) conditional 90-day default risk *under the lender’s current underwriting policy*, strictly for applications that remain open at checkpoint $t_p$.
  - Transparent quantification of partner-channel missing-feed uncertainty and empirical measurement of drift across the July 2025 policy change.
- **Prohibited claims:**
  - Cannot claim to know the true default probability of rejected, withdrawn, or unlinked transferred applicants if they were activated; therefore, **cannot** directly automate approval-threshold expansion without controlled experimentation (e.g., randomized marginal approvals / champion-challenger testing).
  - Cannot claim validated 90-day credit calibration on the post-`2025-10-15` migrated platform until Q2 2026 label maturation.
  - Cannot rule out unmeasured confounding (MNAR) within permanently missing partner feeds.

### Predictive association vs. selection effects vs. counterfactual default risk
- **Predictive association:** Measures correlation within the observed workflow (e.g., multiple document loops correlate with higher observed default among activated accounts). Useful for triage and operational prioritization, not causal attribution.
- **Selection effects:** Conditioning on `Account Activated` creates collider bias. An applicant with a weak initial `risk_score` who still passes underwriting likely possessed strong unlogged compensating factors (e.g., verified collateral or manual underwriter vouching). Consequently, feature effects learned on activated accounts can attenuate or even reverse relative to the full applicant pool.
- **Counterfactual default risk:** $P(Y_{90}(\text{do}(\text{Activate})) = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$ asks what would happen under an *intervention* that overrides the underwriting policy. Identifying it requires randomization, an exogenous policy instrument, or untestable no-unmeasured-confounding assumptions. Operational dashboards must label our Stage 2 output explicitly as **"Predicted 90d Default Risk (Given Activation Under Current Policy)"**—never as unconditional applicant creditworthiness.

### How deployment can alter the data-generating process (performative feedback loops)
1. **Underwriting triage feedback:** If early high-risk flags cause underwriters to scrutinize or reject flagged applications more aggressively, those cases shift from `Activated` to `Rejected`. Future training data will contain fewer defaults from that region of feature space, degrading calibration and amplifying selection bias.
2. **Queue & resource reallocation:** Routing high-risk prefixes to senior specialists or expedited queues changes waiting times, rework loops, and handler-experience profiles—breaking the historical relationship between process-timing features and risk.
3. **Applicant and broker behavioral adaptation:** If applicants or partner brokers infer that certain submission patterns (e.g., requested amounts, channel selection, or document timing) trigger heightened friction, they may game inputs or shift channels, inducing covariate and concept drift.
4. **Strategic transfer leakage:** If operations route borderline high-risk cases to partner platforms to reduce local default rates, the `Transferred` rate rises and credit risk migrates into the 40% unlinked / missing-feed blind spot.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# --- Configuration & domain constants ---
EXTRACT_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15")
OBS_END = pd.Timestamp("2025-12-31 23:59:59")
POLICY_CHANGE_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01")
MIGRATION_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15")
MATURITY_DAYS = 90 + 35  # 90d exposure + 35d max external feed lag

TERMINAL_ACTS = {"Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred", "Account Activated"}
POST_DECISION_ACTS = {
    "Accepted", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred",
    "Contract Signed", "Account Activated", "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"
}
FORBIDDEN_FEATURES = {
    "final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d",
    "label_available_at", "data_extract_time", "documents_total", "resource_experience"
}
# Example mapping for post-2025-10-15 renamed activities (expand from schema diff)
CANONICAL_ACTIVITY_MAP = {
    "App_Init_V2": "Application Started",
    "KYC_Verify_V2": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Underwriting_Review_V2": "Credit Review",
    "Doc_Req_V2": "Document Requested",
    "Acct_Active_V2": "Account Activated",
}


def load_and_canonicalize(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Load event log, normalize post-migration activity names, deduplicate, and format for PM4Py."""
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    for col in ["time:timestamp", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
        if col in df.columns:
            df[col] = pd.to_datetime(df[col], errors="coerce")

    df = df[df["time:timestamp"] <= OBS_END].copy()
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(CANONICAL_ACTIVITY_MAP)

    # Deduplicate migration double-writes (same case, activity, resource within 1 second)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"])
    df["ts_sec"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.floor("s")
    df = df.drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "org:resource", "ts_sec"],
        keep="first",
    ).drop(columns=["ts_sec"])

    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )


def build_case_outcomes(log_df: pd.DataFrame, as_of_date: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Construct case-level disposition, censoring, and maturity status as of `as_of_date`."""
    g = log_df.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=False)
    cases = pd.DataFrame({
        "case_start": g["time:timestamp"].min(),
        "case_end": g["time:timestamp"].max(),
        "channel": g["case:channel"].first(),
        "region": g["case:region"].first(),
        "requested_amount": g["case:requested_amount"].first(),
        "default_90d": g["default_90d"].first(),
        "label_available_at": g["label_available_at"].first(),
    })

    # Identify first terminal disposition & activation timestamp
    term_ev = log_df[log_df["concept:name"].isin(TERMINAL_ACTS)].sort_values("time:timestamp")
    first_term = term_ev.groupby("case:concept:name").first()
    cases["disposition"] = first_term["concept:name"].reindex(cases.index).fillna("Open")
    cases["disposition_time"] = first_term["time:timestamp"].reindex(cases.index)

    act_ev = log_df[log_df["concept:name"] == "Account Activated"].groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    cases["activation_time"] = act_ev.reindex(cases.index)

    # Maturity classification strictly by activation_time (NOT label_available_at alone)
    mature_cutoff = as_of_date - pd.Timedelta(days=MATURITY_DAYS)
    is_act = cases["disposition"] == "Account Activated"
    is_mature = is_act & (cases["activation_time"] <= mature_cutoff)
    has_label = cases["default_90d"].notna() & (
        cases["label_available_at"].isna() | (cases["label_available_at"] <= as_of_date)
    )

    cases["stage2_status"] = np.select(
        [
            cases["disposition"] == "Open",
            cases["disposition"].isin(["Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]),
            is_mature & has_label,
            is_mature & (~has_label),
            is_act & (~is_mature),
        ],
        [
            "right_censored_open",
            "selectively_unobserved",
            "mature_labeled",
            "suspected_permanent_missing",  # Partner-feed IPW target
            "immature_or_delayed",
        ],
        default="audit_required",
    )
    cases["is_censored_stage1"] = cases["disposition"] == "Open"
    return cases.reset_index()


def extract_safe_prefixes(log_df: pd.DataFrame, prefix_len: int = 3) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Extract fixed-length prefixes strictly before any decision/post-decision event."""
    # Exclude events from the first post-decision event onward per case
    is_post = log_df["concept:name"].isin(POST_DECISION_ACTS)
    post_seen = is_post.groupby(log_df["case:concept:name"]).cumsum() > 0
    pre_decision_log = log_df[~post_seen].copy()

    # Keep only cases that actually reach `prefix_len` pre-decision events (no padding short cases)
    counts = pre_decision_log.groupby("case:concept:name").size()
    eligible_cases = counts[counts >= prefix_len].index
    pre_decision_log = pre_decision_log[pre_decision_log["case:concept:name"].isin(eligible_cases)]

    # PM4Py fixed-length prefix extraction on strictly pre-decision traces
    prefix_df = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(pre_decision_log, length=prefix_len)
    drop_cols = [c for c in FORBIDDEN_FEATURES if c in prefix_df.columns]
    return prefix_df.drop(columns=drop_cols)


def build_prefix_features(prefix_df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Compute leak-free process-aware features solely from truncated prefixes."""
    g = prefix_df.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=False)
    feats = pd.DataFrame({
        "prefix_end_time": g["time:timestamp"].max(),
        "elapsed_hours": (g["time:timestamp"].max() - g["time:timestamp"].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0,
        "last_activity": g["concept:name"].last(),
        "n_unique_activities": g["concept:name"].nunique(),
        "n_doc_requests": g["concept:name"].apply(lambda s: (s == "Document Requested").sum()),
        "n_resources": g["org:resource"].nunique(),
        "first_risk_score": g["risk_score"].first(),
        "latest_risk_score": g["risk_score"].last(),
        "channel": g["case:channel"].first(),
        "requested_amount": g["case:requested_amount"].first(),
    })
    feats["risk_score_delta"] = feats["latest_risk_score"] - feats["first_risk_score"]
    # NOTE: Historical queue/WIP & point-in-time resource experience should be joined here
    # using strictly prior events (timestamp < prefix_end_time) without lookahead.
    return feats.reset_index()


def leakage_audit_check(prefix_df: pd.DataFrame, feature_df: pd.DataFrame, cases_df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    """Fail-fast audit verifying absence of forbidden fields, post-decision events, and time travel."""
    leaked_cols = FORBIDDEN_FEATURES.intersection(set(feature_df.columns))
    assert not leaked_cols, f"Forbidden columns found in features: {leaked_cols}"
    assert not prefix_df["concept:name"].isin(POST_DECISION_ACTS).any(), "Post-decision event leaked into prefix!"

    merged = feature_df.merge(cases_df[["case:concept:name", "disposition_time"]], on="case:concept:name")
    resolved = merged["disposition_time"].notna()
    assert (merged.loc[resolved, "prefix_end_time"] < merged.loc[resolved, "disposition_time"]).all(), (
        "Prefix timestamp reaches or exceeds case disposition time!"
    )


def run_study_pipeline(csv_path: str, prefix_len: int = 3) -> dict:
    log_df = load_and_canonicalize(csv_path)
    cases = build_case_outcomes(log_df, as_of_date=EXTRACT_DATE)

    # Temporal split by case_start (preferred over random pm4py.split_train_test due to policy/migration shift)
    cases["split"] = np.select(
        [
            cases["case_start"] < pd.Timestamp("2025-04-01"),
            cases["case_start"] < POLICY_CHANGE_DATE,
            cases["case_start"] < MIGRATION_DATE,
        ],
        ["train_pre_policy", "val_pre_policy", "test1_post_policy"],
        default="test2_post_migration_stage1_only",
    )

    prefix_df = extract_safe_prefixes(log_df, prefix_len=prefix_len)
    feature_df = build_prefix_features(prefix_df)
    leakage_audit_check(prefix_df, feature_df, cases)

    # Justified PM4Py discovery on TRUNCATED prefixes only (audits migration continuity without future leakage)
    train_ids = set(cases.loc[cases["split"] == "train_pre_policy", "case:concept:name"])
    post_mig_ids = set(cases.loc[cases["split"] == "test2_post_migration_stage1_only", "case:concept:name"])
    dfg_train, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(prefix_df[prefix_df["case:concept:name"].isin(train_ids)])
    variants_post_mig = pm4py.get_variants(prefix_df[prefix_df["case:concept:name"].isin(post_mig_ids)])

    # Modeling hooks (external survival/competing-risk packages; not fabricated in PM4Py):
    # - Stage 1: Fit cause-specific Cox / Fine-Gray competing-risks model on all `split == train_pre_policy`
    #   cases using (disposition, disposition_time - prefix_end_time, is_censored_stage1).
    # - Stage 2: Fit IPW-weighted 90d default model ONLY on `stage2_status == 'mature_labeled'` cases,
    #   with IPW weights estimated from P(mature_labeled | mature_labeled or suspected_permanent_missing, X).
    # - Sensitivity: Re-evaluate Stage 2 under MNAR tipping-point assumptions for `suspected_permanent_missing`.
    return {
        "cohort_counts": cases.groupby(["disposition", "stage2_status"]).size(),
        "split_counts": cases.groupby(["split", "stage2_status"]).size(),
        "n_train_dfg_edges": len(dfg_train),
        "n_post_migration_prefix_variants": len(variants_post_mig),
        "features": feature_df,
        "cases": cases,
    }
```
