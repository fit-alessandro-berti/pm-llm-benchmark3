## 4.1 Prepare two actor views rather than silently “fixing” the log

`entered_by` must not automatically be treated as the approver: it may identify only the person who keyed the record. Construct an event-level actor-resolution table from source audit records with separate fields for the credential user, data-entry actor, and business decision actor. Retain the raw value alongside the resolved value.

```python

TS = "time:timestamp"

df["dq_missing_core"] = df[[CASE, ACT, TS]].isna().any(axis=1)

    analysis_df,

#

analysis_df = analysis_df.merge(

    .astype("string")
    .fillna(analysis_df["raw_actor"])

raw_human_log = analysis_df.loc[

resolved_human_log = analysis_df.loc[
```

Run every actor-sensitive analysis in at least three forms:

1. raw `org:resource`;
2. identity-resolved actor;
3. unresolved cases reported separately.

An exception that persists after reliable actor reconstruction carries more evidential weight than one present only in the raw extraction.

Do **not** rank edges by raw frequency alone. For every pair calculate:

* co-case count divided by the number of cases in which the source resource could have handed over;
* the target resource’s baseline share among eligible approvers;
* lift relative to that baseline;
* median and percentile handover time;
* results within `purchase_category`, `region`, `amount_band`, `emergency_flag`, `contract_type`, source system, and weekend/weekday strata;
* the proportion of pair cases with a formal control exception.

This should make Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix useful high-volume baselines rather than presumptively suspicious pairs.

```python
def policy_deadline(ts: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.Timestamp:
    # Confirm whether policy means the same clock time after three business
    # days or the end of the third business day.
    return ts + 3 * business_day

def test_retro_case(
    case_events: pd.DataFrame,
    timestamp_col: str,
    actor_col: str,
) -> list[dict]:
    g = case_events.sort_values([timestamp_col, "_raw_row"])
    overrides = g[g[ACT] == "Emergency Override"]
    retros = g[g[ACT] == "Retrospective Justification"]

    unused_retro_indices = set(retros.index)
    results: list[dict] = []

    for override_idx, override in overrides.iterrows():
        candidates = retros.loc[
            retros.index.isin(unused_retro_indices)
            & (retros[timestamp_col] >= override[timestamp_col])
        ].sort_values(timestamp_col)

        if candidates.empty:
            results.append({
                CASE: override[CASE],
                "override_index": override_idx,
                "status": "missing_justification",
            })
            continue

        retro_idx = candidates.index[0]
        retro = candidates.loc[retro_idx]
        unused_retro_indices.remove(retro_idx)

        identity_unresolved = (
            bool(override.get("actor_unresolved", True))
            or bool(retro.get("actor_unresolved", True))
        )

        results.append({
            CASE: override[CASE],
            "override_index": override_idx,
            "retro_index": retro_idx,
            "override_actor": override[actor_col],
            "retro_actor": retro[actor_col],
            "identity_unresolved": identity_unresolved,
            "same_actor": (
                np.nan if identity_unresolved
                else override[actor_col] == retro[actor_col]
            ),
            "deadline": policy_deadline(override[timestamp_col]),
            "late": retro[timestamp_col] > policy_deadline(
                override[timestamp_col]
            ),
            "elapsed": retro[timestamp_col] - override[timestamp_col],
            "retro_source_system": retro["source_system"],
        })

    return results

retro_results = pd.DataFrame(
    result
    for _, case_events in analysis_df.groupby(CASE, sort=False)
    for result in test_retro_case(case_events, TS, ACTOR)
)
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

| Evidence dimension                    | Evidence that would **increase concern**                                                                                                                                                    | Evidence that would **reduce concern**                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Actor identity**                    | Source-level credential, SSO/MFA, and decision records confirm that the same natural person performed both restricted actions after shared-account and mapping correction.                  | Reliable source audit records identify different authorized humans, and the apparent same-resource exception disappears under a documented, reproducible mapping correction. |
| **V‑77x migration authorization**     | No approved migration ticket or authorized vendor list exists; reviewed cases fall outside the project dates or scope; links are created only after the audit request.                      | A pre-existing approved project, case-level ticket crosswalk, authorized implementation window, and complete V‑77x population explain the concentration.                     |
| **Bank-account legitimacy**           | New accounts are not owned by the relevant vendors, multiple unrelated vendors share a beneficiary, account data conflicts with vendor confirmations, or payments are disputed or recalled. | Independent bank-owner verification, authenticated vendor callback, and matching contractual and invoice records confirm each beneficiary.                                   |
| **Temporal proximity**                | Bank details change immediately before a high-value payment with no operational reason, especially where the new account receives the first payment and is changed again afterward.         | Changes follow a scheduled migration sequence, payments occur according to normal terms, and peer migration cases show comparable timing.                                    |
| **Manual upload provenance**          | Missing source file, mutable spreadsheet with unexplained edits, no hash or run ID, uploader also performed restricted actions, or business timestamps were overwritten by ingestion time.  | Immutable source file and hash, controlled uploader, reconciliation report, preserved original timestamps, and an audit trail showing that upload was purely administrative. |
| **Approval substance**                | Blank or copied approval comments, no reviewed attachment, no change after a return loop, approvals outside authority, or manual queue bypass targeted to selected vendors.                 | Documented review checklist, substantive comments, changed fields or attachments after clarification, proper queue routing, and authority within delegated thresholds.       |
| **Emergency basis**                   | `emergency_flag` added after approval, no incident or operational need, non-emergency cases use the rush route, or the approver was not on duty or delegated.                               | Contemporaneous clinical shortage, outage or safety incident, on-call roster, valid delegation, framework contract, and subsequent receipt of required goods or services.    |
| **Retrospective justification**       | Original justification does not predate quarter close, the same person authored and approved it, independent reviewer denies involvement, or it was completed outside the policy deadline.  | Independently signed contemporaneous justification, original document metadata, email or repository history, and a verified review within three working days.                |
| **Network concentration**             | Pair concentration remains exceptional after normalization for workload, role, category, region, amount, emergency status, queue, availability, and source system.                          | Pair frequencies match formal team routing and eligible workload; comparable specialist pairs show similar handover and loop rates.                                          |
| **Commercial substance**              | Duplicate invoices, purchase splitting near approval thresholds, absent goods receipts, unusual pricing, unsupported contract changes, related-party links, or repeat payment failures.     | Valid quote comparison, contract terms, goods receipt, invoice match, market-consistent pricing, and no beneficiary, duplicate, or related-party anomalies.                  |
| **Legacy mapping**                    | Raw source records themselves use one human identity for bot creation and human approval, rather than the anomaly arising only in ETL.                                                      | Raw bot and human identities are distinct, the transformation defect is reproduced, and corrected mappings reconcile to source totals without unexplained residuals.         |
| **Communication and access evidence** | Case-specific communications show instructions to bypass controls, share credentials, conceal a bank change, or route transactions away from the normal queue.                              | Communications document legitimate urgency, requests for clarification, escalation, vendor verification, and ordinary queue-assignment decisions.                            |

The assessment should be materially updated only after this evidence is linked to individual **case IDs and source event IDs**. General explanations such as “emergency” or “migration” are insufficient unless they reconcile transaction by transaction.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

> Internal Audit analyzed procurement-to-payment event-log indicators covering segregation-of-duties exceptions, approval timing, recurring resource interactions, and case attributes. The results do not establish fraud or collusion. Overall, the evidence is more consistent with a combination of routine specialization, emergency processing, control-execution weaknesses, and data-quality limitations.
>
> The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cluster involving vendor family V‑77x. This cluster combines nine apparent bank-change/payment-approval four-eyes exceptions, unusually short bank-change-to-approval intervals, high-value cases, strong vendor concentration, extensive use of manual uploads, and several tight correction loops. Those combined indicators justify prompt review, but they are not proof of misconduct. The documented banking-platform migration and missing maintenance-ticket links remain plausible explanations.
>
> The Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo patterns appear substantially associated with emergency medical-supply and weekend-repair workflows. Their speed and repeated pairing therefore have lower evidential weight than the V‑77x cluster. Nevertheless, late or non-independent retrospective justifications represent control exceptions that should be tested independently of anyone’s intent.
>
> Before drawing person-level conclusions, Audit should reconstruct the responsible human actor and the business-time sequence. Shared-account use, batch-loaded quarter-close records, and legacy-ERP mapping errors may create or inflate apparent violations. Targeted follow-up should begin with vendor-master change tickets, independent vendor and bank-account verification, payment-beneficiary records, source-file and version history, SSO/MFA and access logs, delegation records, and contemporaneous emergency justifications. Findings should be reclassified after identity and timestamp reconciliation, with unresolved control exceptions reported separately from any evidence bearing on misconduct.
