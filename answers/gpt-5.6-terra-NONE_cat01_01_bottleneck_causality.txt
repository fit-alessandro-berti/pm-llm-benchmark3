## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: partly causal through mechanisms. Confidence: 58/100.**

Orion’s higher crude mean cycle time (21.96h versus 15.24h; +6.72h) is not persuasive evidence that Orion itself is slower. Orion receives a much more adverse observed mix: 42.9% high-complexity versus 12.5% for Nova, 92.9% night work versus 25.0%, and 64.3% compliance-required versus 25.0%. Within every observed complexity stratum, Orion is faster: Low 7.50 versus 9.64h, Medium 17.17 versus 19.38h, High 31.57 versus 35.00h. Orion also has lower mean waits in each stratum. This reversal strongly suggests routing/case mix and operating conditions drive the crude difference.

That said, stratification on complexity alone does not identify a causal Orion effect: shift, load, latent case risk, routing criteria, reviewer skill, and compliance details remain imbalanced. Group may also represent a bundle—certified reviewers, night coverage, queue configuration, and routing rules—rather than an independently manipulable treatment. The data suggest a plausible beneficial Orion effect through lower queue wait and possibly service efficiency, but not a clean direct “group-label” effect.

Reassigning 50% of Orion work to Nova may fail because Nova may inherit difficult, night, compliance-heavy cases without equivalent staffing or capacity. It could backfire by increasing Nova queues and displacing its current work. It may succeed only if Nova receives matched capability, compliance capacity, and protected queue capacity, or if Orion’s apparent advantage is caused by selective routing rather than operational capability.

Supported facts include routing after classification, Orion’s difficult-case/night concentration, required compliance, queueing included in wait, repeated review after request information, and completed-only sampling. Arrows from group to queue/service/rework are causal hypotheses, not established facts. Unobserved reviewer skill and case risk are assumptions representing omitted selection factors. A static DAG suppresses queue feedback: sending work to Nova changes its future queue length, which changes later waits, routing behavior, and perhaps staffing; reviewer fatigue and nightly workload can similarly evolve over time.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Beneficial Orion capability masked by case mix** | Certified reviewers and/or Orion queue management reduce waits and service time for comparable cases | Orion is faster in every complexity stratum; mean wait is lower in every stratum; Orion handles more difficult/night cases | Nonrandom routing, sparse cells, and unobserved risk may create apparent advantage | Exact/propensity matching on pre-routing complexity, shift, arrival load, compliance requirement, calendar period, and richer intake attributes; inspect balance and discard non-overlap | A persistent negative Orion-minus-Nova cycle-time contrast, especially in queue wait, after good balance would favor this explanation |
| 2 | **Genuine harmful Orion effect** | Night staffing, handoffs, overloaded Orion queues, or group-specific practices add delay | Orion’s crude mean is +6.72h; Orion’s rework percentage is higher | Within every complexity stratum Orion is faster; its waits are lower | Within shared shift/load/compliance cells and calendar windows, compare randomized or quasi-random assignments; reconstruct queue state at assignment | If Orion remains slower after common-support matching and equivalent queue state, especially through wait/rework, harmful group operations become credible |
| 3 | **Shift/load confounding and capacity imbalance** | Orion’s night coverage and high-load work generate longer queues independent of reviewer group | Orion is 92.9% night versus Nova 25.0%; many Orion cases are night/high-load; wait is large part of elapsed time | Orion is still faster within complexity despite observed adverse shift/load mix | Build time-indexed queue-state measures: arrivals, staffing, work-in-progress, age, compliance backlog. | 4 | **Compliance/rework mediation, not a direct group-label effect** | Difficult cases require compliance; compliance and Request Info create waiting and repeated review | Required cases are much slower; rework adds a second Review; Orion has more required and rework cases | Required cases appear similar in average delay across groups; no evidence Orion causes requirement | Decompose trace variants and timestamps into pre-compliance, compliance, queue, first-review, request-info, second-review segments; compare variant-specific outcomes under overlap | If group gaps are concentrated in compliance/rework variants and vanish within identical variants and queue states, mediation/process-path explanation is favored |
| 5 | **Routing selection on unobserved case risk** | Routing engine sends cases with latent difficulty, documentation gaps, fraud/compliance concern, or customer complexity to Orion | Prompt explicitly says Orion is usually selected for difficult cases; complexity is coarse | Orion’s favorable within-complexity pattern could indicate capability rather than selection | Add intake score, reason codes, claimant/channel, document completeness, product, jurisdiction, and predicted complexity; conduct sensitivity analysis for unmeasured confounding | If richer pre-routing risk features eliminate Orion’s apparent advantage, latent-risk selection is the likely explanation |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

**Final recommendation**

Do not move 50% of Orion work to Nova based on the crude mean. The observed reversal is important: Orion is faster within every complexity stratum and has lower stratum-specific waiting, while carrying disproportionately high-complexity, night, and compliance-required work. That makes a blanket transfer more likely to test Nova’s capacity limits than to reveal an Orion delay.

First make the intervention explicit: “assign an eligible case to Nova with equivalent reviewer certification, night coverage, compliance access, and protected queue capacity.” Then run a constrained randomized or quasi-random pilot in overlap-supported strata, initially excluding the highest-risk cases and monitoring both transferred and incumbent Nova workloads. Measure assignment-to-close survival, queue state, compliance turnaround, service time, rework, and quality—not merely completed-case mean cycle time.

In parallel, target night/compliance queue capacity. If this reduces waits across both groups, the primary issue is system congestion rather than Orion. Scale reassignment only where the pilot demonstrates no deterioration in tail cycle time, compliance performance, backlog, or quality. Retain Orion for strata lacking overlap or showing a credible Orion capability advantage.

        for edge, count in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda x: (x[0][0], x[0][1])):
            print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {count}")
    except Exception as exc:
        print(f"\nPM4Py parsing/discovery failed for {group}: {exc}")
        print("Fallback directly-follows frequencies from trace strings:")
        dfg = {}
        for trace in trace_strings:
            activities = trace.split(">")
            for a, b in zip(activities, activities[1:]):
                dfg[(a, b)] = dfg.get((a, b), 0) + 1
        for edge, count in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda x: (x[0][0], x[0][1])):
            print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {count}")

print("\n=== Additional diagnostic warnings ===")
if df["case_id"].nunique() != len(df):
    warnings.warn("Duplicate case IDs detected.")
if not set(df["group"]).issuperset({"Orion", "Nova"}):
    warnings.warn("Expected both Orion and Nova groups.")
if (df["cycle_time_h"] < df["wait_before_review_h"] + df["review_service_h"]).any():
    warnings.warn(
        "At least one case has cycle time below wait + review service; inspect "
        "measurement definitions before decomposing cycle time."
    )

print("\nReminder: These diagnostics describe the completed cases only. "
      "They cannot estimate effects on still-open or abandoned cases.")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unmeasured case difficulty may remain after Low/Medium/High classification.**  
   Most valuable addition: a pre-routing intake risk score with reason codes, document completeness, product, jurisdiction, and customer/channel attributes.

2. **The assignment mechanism is not observed well enough to distinguish routing selection from group performance.**  
   Most valuable addition: a routing-engine decision log containing eligible groups, assignment scores, overrides, and the exact assignment timestamp.

3. **Queue wait cannot be separated cleanly from compliance-related delay or other pre-review waiting.**  
   Most valuable addition: event timestamps for queue entry/exit, compliance start/end, first review start/end, request-info send/receive, and second review start/end.

4. **Completed-case-only data may create survivorship or censoring bias.**  
   Most valuable design change: retain a cohort of all assigned cases, including open, abandoned, reassigned, and censored cases, with status timestamps.

5. **The transportability of Orion capability to Nova is unknown.**  
   Most valuable design change: a stratified randomized routing pilot with documented reviewer certification, staffing level, workload, and capacity protection in both groups.
