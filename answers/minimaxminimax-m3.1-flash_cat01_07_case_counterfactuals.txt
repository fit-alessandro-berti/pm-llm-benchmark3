Process-feasibility check: Register and Triage remain first. Both checks complete before A1's Assessment. The preliminary C1 screen does not count as the mandatory final review: C1 conducts the final review only after Assessment, and C1 is different from A1, satisfying four-eyes. No resource intervals overlap, and Decision still precedes Notify.

Best/base/worst case: In the best case, the packet resolves the ambiguity, final review takes about 3 h, and M1 signs late Tuesday, producing a cycle near 39–43 h, approximately 16–20 h faster. The base is about 49 h, 9.5 h faster, with approval under conditions. In the worst case, the slot is unavailable when Assessment finishes or the preliminary packet creates additional compliance questions; completion can remain near the factual 58.5 h or become later. Quality should improve through earlier scrutiny, but a material sanctions issue could still lead to rejection rather than the factual conditional approval.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Actual C1 reservation and no-show data would show whether a Tuesday hold was genuinely available or merely displaced another urgent case. Evidence that the preliminary packet was too incomplete to save final-review effort, or that A1's Assessment overran materially, would weaken the expected gain. A fixed M1 decision schedule that excludes Tuesday evening would also eliminate part of the best case.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: D1 was retained because of handwriting difficulty, but it did not begin until Tuesday 09:30, despite receipt of originals on Monday. Document Check then ended at 13:30, A2 still did not start until 20:00, and M1 did not sign until Wednesday. The delay was therefore a combination of D1 queueing, assessment queueing, and decision batching—not document service alone.

Minimal intervention: D2 becomes eligible when the originals arrive. Identity Check remains unchanged, and D2 still observes ordinary queueing; assignment on arrival does not guarantee immediate service. In the base, the Monday 16:00 five-hour D2 forecast is used only to construct a plausible release around 22:30, followed by about five hours of standard-document service.

Temporal propagation: With D2, document completion moves from Tuesday 13:30 to approximately Tuesday 02:30, about 11 h earlier. A2 still encounters a queue, but an assessment around 14:00 can finish by 16:30, allowing a same-day M1 decision. If M1 only acts the next morning, the earlier document and assessment completion produces no end-to-end gain. If D2 identifies unclear handwriting, the first Assessment must raise a formal clarification request. An 8–36 h response, another four-to-eight-hour document check, and another assessment consume the apparent early gain.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes D2's Document Check. Identity and the document check both complete before Assessment. The rework branch explicitly includes a new Document Check and new Assessment after Request More Info. Because the case is neither high-risk nor high-value, no mandatory Compliance Review is introduced. D2, I2, A2, and M1 have non-overlapping intervals.

Best/base/worst case: The best no-rework case is approximately 25–32 h if A2 is served promptly and M1 can act the same day, versus 72 h factually. A more conservative base is about 32.25 h, approximately 39.75 h faster, because M1 signs Tuesday afternoon. If M1 does not, the cycle remains 72 h despite readiness being about 10–15 h earlier. The worst case combines a 36 h clarification response, another D2 check and A2 assessment, and the next available manager session, reaching roughly 120 h. Approval remains reasonable if the handwriting is interpreted correctly, but conditional approval or rejection is more plausible than with D1.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Measured D2 handwriting error and clarification rates, actual rather than forecast D2 queue releases, and evidence that A2's Tuesday slot is unavailable would reverse the apparent advantage. If D2's service is materially slower and M1's schedule is fixed, only readiness improves and end-to-end cycle time may not.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The first supplier response produced a corrected schedule but left one inconsistency. The second response crossed the weekend, after which Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 again delayed final compliance. The long delay was not caused solely by document or assessment service: it arose because an unresolved discrepancy was routed back into a slow external-response cycle.

Minimal intervention: The policy stops after Assessment 2. The first loop remains intact—Request More Info 1 is followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2—but Request More Info 2, Document Check 3, and Assessment 3 are removed. Final Compliance Review still follows the latest relevant Assessment. The unresolved discrepancy is made explicit in C1's opinion and M1's decision rather than silently accepted.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 ends Friday at 10:30. A new final compliance review on Friday can therefore support a Friday decision, avoiding the 46.5 h weekend response and the subsequent document and assessment intervals. That advances decision readiness by roughly four days. If C1 cannot start Friday, the gain migrates to C1's queue. If M1 does not act until its next regular session, the case can wait despite being decision-ready, so endpoint improvement is not guaranteed merely by deleting work.

Process-feasibility check: RMI 1 is followed by the required new Document Check and Assessment. No prohibited second request is issued. The final Compliance Review follows Assessment 2, C1 is distinct from A1, and Decision precedes Notify. Either conditional approval with explicit controls or rejection is allowed; approving while ignoring the discrepancy is not.

Best/base/worst case: Operationally, prompt rejection after C1 concurrence or conditional approval with strong controls can finish around 100–103 h, about 111 h faster than factual. Commercially, conditional approval may be preferable only if the controls work; otherwise rejection is safer despite possible appeal. If C1 and M1 retain factual batching, the cycle may remain about 214 h despite readiness being much earlier. If an approval is later challenged, the total recovery time can extend by weeks. Quality is therefore more uncertain than speed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that the remaining discrepancy affects sanctions, identity, legal eligibility, or material value would support rejection and weaken the approval-with-conditions scenario. C1's refusal to concur, failure of compensating controls, or comparable appeal and loss rates in comparable cases would reverse any claim that speed was obtained safely.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The legacy workflow imposed a task-creation dependency, not a legal verification dependency. Because only the identity task existed at Triage, D2's document work could not begin until I2 had completed. The document check ended Tuesday at 19:30, after which the already congested assessment queue pushed service to Wednesday morning.

Minimal intervention: Both verification tasks are created at Triage, and a controlled override allows I2 and D2 to work concurrently. Real event timestamps must show task ownership, resource acquisition, service start, and service completion; simply backdating the document start would not constitute a valid counterfactual.

Temporal propagation: A plausible release has I2 starting around 11:30 and D2 around noon. Their intervals overlap, and D2 completes around 16:30 rather than 19:30. Assessment readiness advances by about three hours, but readiness is not service. In the base, A2 starts at 20:00 and finishes at 22:30, much earlier than the factual Wednesday 09:30–12:00 assessment, but M1's next-day session keeps the endpoint unchanged. If A2 is available immediately and M1 can decide Tuesday, the cycle falls to roughly 10–14 h.

Process-feasibility check: No originals are required, so concurrent Identity Check and Document Check are permissible. I2 and D2 are different resources, so there is no resource conflict. Both checks complete before A2 starts Assessment, and Decision precedes Notify. The workaround changes task release, not the substantive decision standard.

Best/base/worst case: The best case is about 10–14 h, approximately 16–19 h faster, with unchanged approval quality because the same evidence is completed before assessment. The base is 29.5 h, unchanged end to end: checks and assessment finish earlier, but M1's session and assessment capacity dominate. The worst case can slightly exceed 29.5 h if earlier document demand and assessment arrivals displace other work. If apparent concurrency is not real, document evidence may be incomplete at Assessment, increasing rework, rejection, or audit findings.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Source-event logs showing no actual overlap, hidden D2 queueing, or backdated task ownership would invalidate the apparent concurrency benefit. A1/A2 queue-arrival and service-start data showing that earlier completions cannot obtain an earlier assessment slot would show that the intervention mainly shifts congestion downstream.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 earlier escalation and compliance preparation | Adds S1 advisory effort and a C1 preliminary interval; reserves scarce C1 capacity; may reduce final rereading by 1–3 h. | The target's compliance waiting time and possibly final-review effort. | C1's backlog for other cases; downstream Decision demand if holds are released together. | Urgent compliance cases, other final-review cases, and cases whose decisions depend on C1. | Positive locally; ambiguous and potentially negative if reservations are universal. | A hold consumes capacity even when unused; later release can create a decision burst. Packet completeness and assessment overruns determine whether the hold pays off. |
| K-219 D1-to-D2 routing | Moves one standard document check from D1 to slower D2; possible additional document, response, and assessment work. | D1's immediate queue. | D2's queue; assessment and external-response queues if clarification occurs. | Other standard D2 cases and complex cases vulnerable to D2 displacement. | Locally faster if no rework; potentially negative at portfolio scale. | Slower service and a nonlinear rise in clarification/rework can consume the capacity initially released from D1. |
| K-337 no second rework loop | Removes an external response, a document check, an assessment, and associated waiting; retains final C1 and M1 work. | Document, assessment, and external-response queues. | Monitoring, dispute, appeal, or risk-acceptance queues if decisions are conditional. | Other document, assessment, and supplier-response cases benefit from released capacity. | Positive only if residual decisions remain defensible. | Time savings are large, but loss and appeal consequences are nonlinear in discrepancy materiality. |
| K-411 concurrent verification | Pulls identity and document demand earlier without adding service capacity; shifts completion toward the assessment queue. | The target's identity/document waiting time. | Assessment queue and possibly identity/document queues for displaced cases. | Other cases waiting for D2, I2, or A2. | Locally positive, system-wide ambiguous because the bottleneck migrates. | Queue arrivals cluster before capacity becomes available; false concurrency produces apparent speed without actual throughput. |

Treating 100 cases as independent would be misleading: each policy changes release times or priorities, and queues turn local gains into displacements. In K-104, early packets and provisional C1 reservations consume the only certified reviewer's capacity. Even when preliminary work saves 1–3 hours at final review, a held slot occupies capacity that urgent cases cannot use; if assessments slip, reservations become idle and their release produces a later C1/manager burst. At portfolio scale, selective reservation is more defensible than universal holding.

For K-219, moving standard work from D1 to D2 frees D1 but loads D2, whose standard service is typically slower than D1's. As D2's queue and handwriting clarification rate grow, second document checks and assessments, plus external clarification waits, consume the capacity released initially. D1 should absorb the freed work rather than become idle.

For K-337, suppressing the second request removes a 46.5-hour weekend supplier interval, a document check, and an assessment, freeing D1, A1, and external-response capacity. This is the clearest throughput gain, but conditional approvals can transfer delay into monitoring, disputes, appeals, and loss investigation; a conservative rejection transfers it to appeal handling. C1 and M1 remain bottlenecks because final compliance and decision cannot be skipped.

For K-411, simultaneous task creation raises early identity/document arrivals without adding service capacity. True concurrency can shorten this case's readiness, but the assessment queue may simply move downstream; 100 completions can arrive together before other assessment slots exist. Real-time task, resource-acquisition, and completion events are essential: backdated timestamps can create apparent concurrency, premature assessments, and invalid audit evidence without delivering any capacity. Queue forecasts should therefore be tested against arrival/departure counts and waiting reasons, not treated as guaranteed slots.

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the four, K-411 appears most robust because it changes task creation and scheduling while leaving decision rights and compliance requirements intact. It is feasible for digital, no-original cases, uses distinct resources for genuine concurrency, and is highly reversible. Its benefit is conditional, however: earlier readiness does not create assessment capacity, so a pilot should measure queue delay rather than infer speed from check overlap.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potentially largest saving. Prohibiting the second information request is a change in decision policy, not an acceleration. A conditional approval can leave the discrepancy unresolved; a rejection can be safe but invite appeal. Compliance defensibility depends on C1's explicit concurrence, four-eyes separation, and effective compensating controls. Ending a long loop is valuable only if the residual risk is consciously accepted or refused.

The K-104 reservation counterfactual is least transportable from one case to a system-wide policy. C1 is a single scarce reviewer, and a reservation that helps one case is an opportunity cost for another. Pilot selectively, pool preliminary and final work, and make holds conditional and cancellable. K-219 is less attractive because D2's handwriting clarification risk may simply convert document delay into assessment rework.

The most valuable additional data are not another queue forecast. They are event-level task creation, resource acquisition, service start, service end, and waiting-reason timestamps; queue arrivals and departures by resource and priority; reservation and no-show histories; assessment-to-compliance handoffs; and rework triggers with external-response times. For K-337, decision rationales, imposed conditions, subsequent disputes, reversals, losses, and compliance findings are essential. A 100-case pilot should distinguish true concurrency from backdated completion and measure non-target queue displacement before scaling.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. structural — The normative prerequisites, mandatory final reviews, four-eyes rule, and Decision-before-Notify order remain binding; only the explicitly prohibited K-337 second information loop is removed.
2. temporal — Queue snapshots are treated as uncertain forecasts rather than guaranteed start times, although the illustrative releases are consistent with the stated estimates.
3. resource — Resource capabilities and capacities remain fixed as specified: C1 is the sole certified reviewer, M1 signs decisions, and I2 and D2 can work concurrently without a resource conflict.
4. temporal — External responses remain subject to the 8–36 h range and weekend timing; K-337 avoids the second response rather than assuming suppliers suddenly respond faster.
5. behavioral — D2's handwriting clarification risk is not assumed away, and clients or suppliers do not provide missing information instantaneously merely because routing or scheduling changes.
6. quality — Preliminary compliance work can save roughly 1–3 h at final review, but it cannot eliminate the need to read complete evidence or prevent a material discrepancy from requiring rejection.
7. interference — The single-case counterfactuals hold other system behavior approximately fixed; queue displacement and bottleneck migration are therefore treated separately in the 100-case analysis.
8. resource — Earlier task release does not create additional service capacity; it changes queue arrival times and can displace other work unless capacity is explicitly rebalanced.
