## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is an explicit projection, not a discovered truth: choosing a type makes one object of that type the case and its trace the events directly related to it through E2O relations. Order therefore means the trace of O1, O2, O3 or O4; Package means P1–P4; and so on. No type is the “real” case merely because its traces are long. Order happens to cover all eight activities, but that breadth is a reason to inspect it, not to externalise its distortions.

**Convergence** means several predecessor objects merge into one object or shared event, e.g. I21+I31 into P3 in e13, or P2+P3 into D2 in e16. **Divergence** means one object branches into several successors over time, e.g. O3 into P3 and P4, P4 and D3, or V2 into M2 and M3. Flattening creates two opposite risks: convergence is copied as if each merged case progressed independently, while divergence is rewritten as repeated sequential activities, such as PackPack for O3.

**Copied shared events are both evidence and duplication.** They are evidence that e16 genuinely involved O1, O2 and O3, so sequence position is real. They are duplication for counts and performance: summing trace frequencies counts e16 three times.

**Transformations.** The baseline is strict direct E2O only; O2O relations are not silently propagated. A labelled sensitivity variant may propagate one named O2O step, for example from O1 through *contains* to I11/I12, but provenance must be retained and results must not be pooled with direct-E2O results.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**1. Cross-order package homogeneity — Package flattening.** P1–P4 all produce PackDispatchConfirm, so the Package DFG reports one variant for four cases. That hides that P3 in e13 merges I21/O2 with I31/O3 and P4 in e14 merges I32/O3 with I41/O4, while P1 is a single-order package for I11/O1. The homogeneity is **informative** about a standard packing sequence but **harmful** if read as absence of consolidation complexity.

**2. Cross-order package item equivalence — Item flattening.** I21 belonging to O2 and I31 belonging to O3 share e13, e16 and e24; I32/O3 and I41/O4 share e14, e17 and e27. All six items therefore have identical traces and cluster as one variant. This is useful for physical item handling but **harmful** for order accountability, because O2 and O3 later share invoice V2 while O4 has V3.

**3. Same PackPack edge, opposite semantics — Order flattening.** O3 has PackPack through e13 with O2/P3 and e14 with O4/P4, which is a merge of foreign items. O1 also has PackPack through e11/P1 and e12/P2, which is a split of its own I11 and I12. The merged Order DFG cannot distinguish partition from convergence. Reading the edge as rework is **harmful**; annotating it with P1–P4 makes it **informative**.

**4. Consolidated delivery copied as synchrony — Order and Delivery flattenings.** D2 carries P2 and P3 in e16 and e24, involving O1, O2 and O3. In the Order log, one Dispatch and one Confirm become three case copies, and DispatchIssue Invoice appears synchronously for three orders at 11:05. In the Delivery log, D2 still looks like D1: just DispatchConfirm, despite serving three orders. Counting copies **inflates throughput**; the coupling itself is **informative** only through ET–OT or OTG views.

**5. Consolidated invoicing duplicated per order — Order versus Invoice flattening.** e19 issues V2 for both O2 and O3. The Order log produces four Issue Invoice incidences for three real issue events, making billing look order-specific. The Invoice log correctly has one V2 case, but then cannot show whether O2 or O3 drives timing; both close at 11:05 in e29/e30. This is **context-dependent**: shared billing is real, per-order billing performance is not.

**6. Partial payments as a PayPay loop — Invoice, Payment and Order flattenings.** V2 is settled by M2 in e22, qualified as *partially_settles*, and M3 in e25, qualified as *completes_settlement*. The Invoice DFG therefore contains a Record Payment self-loop, easily misread as duplicate payment processing. Payment flattening makes M2 and M3 two identical singleton Record Payment traces and discards the qualifiers. In the Order log this also creates PayConfirm for O4 through e26e27 and ConfirmPay for O2/O3 through e24e25. The loop is **harmful** as evidence of rework; it is an instalment schedule when O2O qualifiers are retained.

**7. Singleton Payment lifecycle — Payment flattening.** M1–M4 each contain exactly one event: e21, e22, e25 or e26. The resulting DFG has nodes but no edges, no meaningful skeleton and zero apparent duration. This is the clearest artificial truncation: it cannot distinguish M2 partial settlement from M3 completion and cannot answer any end-to-end question. It is useful only as a timestamp and count inventory.

**8. Near-singleton Delivery lifecycle — Delivery flattening.** D1–D3 each contain only Dispatch and Confirm. No Pack event e11–e14 directly mentions a delivery, so waiting such as e13 at 10:12 to e16 at 11:05 for D2 is invisible. D3’s 22h45 from e17 to e27 may be a confirmation delay rather than transit slowness. The trace is **informative** for confirmed transit only and **harmful** for lead-time or SLA analysis.

**9. Multi-item self-repetition — Order flattening.** O1 and O3 show AddAdd, PackPack, DispatchDispatch and ConfirmConfirm, whereas O2 and O4, with one item each, do not. The repetition pattern therefore encodes order size—O1 has I11/I12 and O3 has I31/I32—rather than process failure. Treating self-loops as inefficiency would be **harmful**; treating them as evidence of split handling is **informative**.

**10. Item lifecycle truncation — Item flattening.** Items begin at Add e05–e10 and end at Confirm e23/e24/e27, omitting Create e01–e04 and Close e28–e31. An item lead time such as I11 from 09:15 to 14:00 understates O1’s order-to-close span from 09:00 on 3 February to 11:00 on 4 February. This is **harmful** for customer SLA questions and defensible only for pack-to-dispatch physics.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

All six flattened logs may still be created, but with different evidential status. **Order** is the primary log for end-to-end sequence and closure. **Item** may answer pack and ship handling for I11–I41. **Package** may answer whether P1–P4 follow the same physical sequence. **Delivery** may answer confirmed DispatchConfirm durations for D1–D3, with consolidation caveats. **Invoice** may answer issue-to-first-payment and issue-to-completion for V1–V3, separating M2 from M3. **Payment** should be used only for counts and timestamps for M1–M4, not for a DFG.

Questions about cross-type ordering—Add Item to Pack Package to Dispatch to Issue Invoice to Record Payment—require an **OC-DFG**, because no single flattening contains all counterpart transitions without truncation or copying. Questions about who contains, packs, carries, bills or settles whom require the **OTG**: *contains* for O1–I11/I12, *packs* for P3–I21/I31 and P4–I32/I41, *carries* for D2–P2/P3, *bills* for V2–O2/O3, and *settles* for M2/M3–V2. Questions about why e13, e16, e19 or e22 affect several cases require an **ET–OT graph**. An **object-interaction summary** should quantify convergence: how many orders share a package, delivery or invoice. **Equivalent-execution clustering** is useful mainly as a warning: all items and all packages look equivalent sequentially, despite different relational positions.

Contradictions should be reconciled by precedence and accounting, not by voting. Direct E2O participation outranks propagated inference. Shared events must be counted once as events and separately as case incidences. If the Order DFG suggests a payment loop but the OTG shows M2 and M3 as distinct payments for V2, the loop interpretation loses. If the Item DFG is homogeneous but P3 is cross-order in the OTG, the heterogeneity is relational rather than sequential.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Decision: B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria.** I prioritised business accountability, coverage of the process from creation to closure, ability to audit duplication, and avoidance of absorbing every other object’s semantics into one trace. Trace length alone was explicitly not a criterion.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Order is the only type present in all eight activities. O1–O4 trace from Create e01–e04 to Close e28–e31, include Add, Pack, Dispatch, Issue Invoice, Record Payment and Confirm Delivery, and connect through O2O to all items via *contains* and all invoices via *bills*. That makes Order the least incomplete primary lens for customer-facing end-to-end analysis.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order is also the most convergence- and divergence-exposed type: 42 case incidences from 31 events, cross-order P3/P4, three-order D2 and two-order V2. A sole Order lead would therefore manufacture synchrony for e16/e24 and obscure the package, delivery and instalment mechanisms that explain the sequence.

**Residual risk.** Even under B, stakeholders may read Order KPIs as independent case measurements and double-count shared dispatch, confirmation, invoicing and payment events. Secondary Package, Delivery, Invoice and Item lifecycles mitigate but do not eliminate that presentational risk.

**Conditions for change.** I would move to A if contractual accountability required one case definition and shared-event prevalence fell sharply after process redesign. I would move to C if consolidation like P3/P4, D2 and V2 became dominant, or if the governing question became logistics cost, transit reliability or billing rather than order fulfilment. I would elevate Delivery or Invoice only for a explicitly operational or financial decision, not for end-to-end fulfilment.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
from collections import Counter
import pm4py

# Assumes: ocel already exists
print(ocel)

# --- Basic inspection: activities and object types ---
activity_counts = dict(ocel.events["ocel:activity"].value_counts())
object_type_counts = dict(ocel.objects["ocel:type"].value_counts())
object_types = sorted(object_type_counts)

# --- Flatten on every candidate object type ---
flattened_logs = {
    object_type: pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    for object_type in object_types
}

# Store DFGs and skeletons per flattening; do not rely on visual inspection alone.
dfg_by_type = {
    object_type: pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    for object_type, log in flattened_logs.items()
}

skeleton_by_type = {
    object_type: pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
    for object_type, log in flattened_logs.items()
}

# Numeric variant summary per flattening: exposes trivial homogeneity
# for Item, Package, Delivery and Payment singleton traces.
variant_counts_by_type = {
    object_type: Counter(
        tuple(event["concept:name"] for event in trace)
        for trace in log
    )
    for object_type, log in flattened_logs.items()
}

# --- Object-centric views ---
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
objects_interactions_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

# Under Decision B, equivalent-execution clustering is used only to test
# whether Order traces are genuinely homogeneous. Equivalent Item or
# Package traces must not be interpreted as business equivalence, because
# e.g. P3 and P1 remain relationally different through O2O links.
equivalent_execution_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel)

# Print numeric summaries alongside stored graph objects.
print("activity_counts:", activity_counts)
print("object_type_counts:", object_type_counts)
print("variant_counts_by_type:", variant_counts_by_type)
print("objects_interactions_summary:", objects_interactions_summary)
print("equivalent_execution_clusters:", equivalent_execution_clusters)
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**1. Removing or down-weighting copied shared events.** Recompute each flattened DFG after deleting shared events such as e16 and e24, and again after weighting each copy by 1/k, where k is the number of cases sharing it. For Order flattening, e16 contributes three Dispatch copies in the baseline but one weighted event in the sensitivity version. If DispatchIssue Invoice or Confirm frequencies collapse, the original conclusion was duplication-driven.

**2. Direct E2O versus propagated O2O relations.** Build a second OCEL variant in which events propagate along one explicit O2O step, for example Create Order e01 for O1 propagates to I11/I12 through *contains*, or Pack e13 for P3 propagates to its packed items. Compare trace lengths and DFG edges with the baseline. Any new Create… edge for Items or Packages must be reported as propagated, not observed, and must not be used for performance claims.

**3. Changing the lead object.** Rerun variant counts, DFGs and skeletons for all six types. The expected instability is diagnostic: Items, Packages and Deliveries each have one sequential variant, Orders have distinct O1–O4 patterns, Invoices split into V1/V3 versus V2, and Payments have no edges. A conclusion that reverses when the lead changes cannot be presented as process truth without naming the projection.

**4. Events versus unique objects versus total objects in the OC-DFG.** For each activity–object-type relation, report three denominators: number of distinct events, number of unique objects, and total object incidences. Dispatch e16 is one event, involves three orders in the Order view, and has nine related objects overall. If these metrics disagree, the event-based and incidence-based conclusions must be separated rather than averaged.

**5. Payment timing and qualifier sensitivity.** Split V2’s settlement into first payment e22/M2 and completion e25/M3, and retain the O2O qualifiers *partially_settles* and *completes_settlement*. Compare issue-to-first-payment with issue-to-completion and check whether the PayPay self-loop disappears once M2 and M3 are treated as distinct instalments. Also audit the e29/e30 timestamp tie for O2 and O3 closures so that ordering artefacts are not interpreted as sequence evidence.
