## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts established directly by the materials

1. **M0's control-flow semantics.** Registration precedes checks; `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are concurrent inside `checks`; the decision is an exclusive choice between the approved branch — `Manual_Procurement_Approval` strictly before `Activate_Supplier` — and `Reject_Supplier`; `Notify_Requester` follows the decision and `Close_Case` follows notification. M0 therefore imposes checks-before-decision but no ordering between sanctions and quality, no `Auto_Approve`, no `Request_More_Info`, no provisional activation, and no loop.
2. **CS-9 is the only controlled, formally approved standard** (effective 2025-01-01). It requires sanctions and quality before activation, manual approval before activation, and separation of requester and manual approver — the last of which M0 does not encode.
3. **PD-3 is a steering-committee pilot, not incorporated into CS-9 or M0**, effective only 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31, restricted to LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, requiring sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation, and permitting quality within two business days after provisional activation. The Compliance observer's position is ambiguous ("monitored exception," later denying approval of weakening CS-9).
4. **FD-7 is a time-boxed emergency directive**, 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59, for critical relief suppliers, requiring sanctions before activation, a EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days. It expired automatically; no written extension exists.
5. **From 2026-07-01, notification moved to CRM**; only ~70% of notifications are mirrored in the onboarding log, notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%, and older CRM-to-case links are mutable text fields (SUP-706), while stable `notification_id` links exist elsewhere (SUP-702).
6. **Observed facts per case** (trace order, dates, spend, eligibility fields, alias identity in SUP-712, duplicate transaction ID in SUP-711, post-hoc label mapping in SUP-713) are as tabulated; aggregate counts (312 auto-approvals: 289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 missing attributes; 47 `Request_More_Info` cases with no premature activation; six post-pilot auto-approvals in early August) are given.

### Disputed propositions

- **D1 — Authority of PD-3.** Does a steering-committee minute plus a compliance observer's "monitored exception" remark authorize deviation from a controlled standard, and for whom — only those who can prove eligibility, or everyone who used the pilot flow? This is an authority question, not a trace question.
- **D2 — Scope of M0.** Does M0 govern rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710)? CS-9 is silent on the first, the scope document is ambiguous, and there is no reactivation procedure at all.
- **D3 — Identity and resource facts.** SUP-712's two aliases resolve to one human; SUP-715 involves spouses. Whether these breach rules depends on identity resolution and on whether a disclosure duty (715) equals a prohibition.
- **D4 — Logging vs. behavior.** SUP-706 (missing notification event, mutable CRM link), SUP-711 (duplicated registration with identical transaction ID), and SUP-713 (renamed label, mapping approved after the fact) turn on whether log absence/duplication/naming is evidence about the world or about the recording pipeline.
- **D5 — Factual emergency vs. legal authority.** SUP-705 turns on whether a persisting factual flood substitutes for an expired directive.
- **D6 — Desirability vs. normativity.** `Request_More_Info` (SUP-707, 47 cases) is endorsed by both Operations and Compliance but absent from M0 and CS-9's activity list.

### Burden-of-proof approach

- **Declaring noncompliance** requires the accuser to show, by preponderance of traceable evidence: (a) the case is in scope of an identified, in-force norm; (b) the norm's conditions (risk class, dates, amounts, identities) are established by reliable, preferably immutable evidence; (c) the observed behavior violates it; and (d) no valid overlay (pilot, emergency) covers the case *at the time of the act*. Where an element depends on missing eligibility attributes (9 of 312 auto-approvals), mutable links, or ambiguous scope, the correct verdict is indeterminate/defect, not guilt.
- **Accepting an exception** requires the party invoking it to prove eligibility affirmatively and contemporaneously: risk class, group membership, dates within the window, spend under cap, monitoring performed. An exception claimed retrospectively, after expiry, or on the basis that approval was "imminent" (SUP-714) fails this burden.
- **Severity** scales with risk exposure actually created (sanctions bypass, high-risk ineligible auto-approval, uncapped spend), not with the raw count of model deviations.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is not "the log deviates from M0" — that would be circular where M0 is defective — but that **a controlled standard was displaced in practice by instruments that were narrower, weaker, or expired, and that the displacement produced real, identifiable risk in specific cases.**

**Formal authority.** CS-9 is controlled and formally approved; PD-3 is meeting minutes and an email, expressly *not* incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The only Compliance voice on record qualified the pilot as a "monitored exception" and later denied approving any weakening of CS-9. A pilot can, at most, create a bounded, conditional carve-out; it cannot silently amend a controlled standard for whoever chooses to invoke it. Yet the aggregate shows 312 invocations, of which 14 were clearly ineligible and 9 cannot even be shown eligible. SUP-703 is the paradigm: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, an operator who simply selected "pilot" despite ineligibility, auto-approval instead of manual approval, quality deferred, and a first purchase of EUR 180,000 — an order of magnitude beyond even the emergency cap. Internal Audit independently flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

**Sanctions before activation is non-negotiable in every instrument.** CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 all agree on this floor. SUP-708 breached it: a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance, with no pilot or emergency cover. That no transaction occurred before clearance mitigates realized harm; it does not cure the breach, because the rule exists precisely for the window in which the organization is exposed. If "nothing bad happened" excused control bypass, no preventive control could ever be enforced.

**Expiry and normalization of deviance.** FD-7 expired automatically on 2026-07-12; SUP-705 used the identical provisional pattern on 2026-07-13 on the plea that the flood "still factually existed." Emergencies are governed by directives, not by weather: without a written extension, post-expiry provisional activation is unauthorized, however sympathetic. The same pathology appears with PD-3: six cases used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August, including SUP-714 two days after expiry, justified by Operations' belief that permanent approval was "imminent." Belief is not an amendment. No permanent low-risk auto-approval policy exists (N5), yet Operations wants one and has behaved as if it has one. This is textbook normalization: a monitored exception became the default workflow, outliving its legal basis. The June–July statistics cut both ways, but the prosecution reads them as showing the exception swallowed the rule — 312 auto-approvals against a standard that permits zero.

**Deviations hidden by M0.** M0-conformance is not compliance. SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly while the requester and manual approver are the same human behind two aliases — a direct violation of CS-9's separation-of-duties rule that M0 cannot even express. A purely model-based audit would have certified this case. Conversely, the prosecution insists the correct unit of analysis is CS-9 plus overlays, with M0 as one (incomplete) encoding; on that fuller standard, the violation list grows, not shrinks.

**Desirable outcomes do not amend policy retroactively.** SUP-707's information requests obtained missing certificates and harmed no one; the prosecution concedes this below. But a control framework in which any deviation later judged beneficial is ratified is no framework at all: SUP-703's operator presumably also believed the pilot flow was fine. Ratification must be prospective, through amendment. Where the organization tolerated 47 unmodeled `Request_More_Info` cases and hundreds of pilot cases without updating CS-9 or M0, the failure is governance failure — and governance failure by the standard's owners does not retroactively legalize each act done under the stale standard; it means both the deviations *and* the failure to amend require remedy.

**Concessions the prosecution must make.** M0 genuinely lacks the desirable information loop; notification evidence after the CRM migration is unreliable (29% missingness proves pipeline failure, not 29% misconduct); SUP-711 is a migration duplicate with one transaction ID; SUP-709 and SUP-710 expose real scope ambiguity; SUP-713 is a documented label rename. The prosecution does not ask to punish those. It asks that they not be used as solvent to dissolve SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, and SUP-712, where dates, risk classes, amounts, and identities are established and no valid cover existed.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that **M0 is being used as a measuring instrument it was never capable of being**: it encodes only part of CS-9, none of the legitimate overlays, none of the desirable practice, and it is fed by a log whose collection pipeline changed mid-stream. A high deviation rate against such a model is evidence about the model–log–organization system, not, without more, about misconduct.

**Concurrency and what M0 does say.** Within `checks`, sanctions and quality are concurrent. Any auditor who treats "quality before sanctions" or vice versa as an ordering violation misreads the POWL partial order. Several apparent anomalies dissolve once M0 is read as a partial order rather than a sequence template.

**Missing loop, missing activities.** M0 has no `Request_More_Info` and no loop. Forty-seven cases used it; in none did activation precede the requested evidence; Compliance itself calls the behavior desirable (N5). SUP-707 obtained missing certificates before approval. To classify SUP-707 as "noncompliant" would be to prefer a model that forbids due diligence over the diligence itself. The deviation is in the model, full stop — and the same applies to `Auto_Approve` as an activity: M0 cannot represent *any* auto-approval, legitimate or not, so "activity not in model" carries zero information about legitimacy.

**Legitimate overlays exist and fit.** SUP-702 satisfies every PD-3 condition on complete evidence: LOW-risk, DE, approved group, in-window (2026-06-15), sanctions before activation, quality next business day, CRM notification linked by stable ID. SUP-704 satisfies every FD-7 condition: in-window (2026-07-11), critical supplier, sanctions first, EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring, quality and approval next business day. Judging these against M0 alone double-counts: the organization *decided* to run a pilot and *issued* an emergency directive; the normative model simply was never updated to depict decisions the organization actually took. A model that is not maintained cannot convict the reality it failed to track. Operations' claim that M0's deviation rate "mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope" is, for June–July, substantially supported: 289 of 312 auto-approvals appear PD-3-eligible.

**CRM migration destroyed the notification evidence.** After 2026-07-01, ~70% mirroring and a jump in missingness from 2% to 29% mean that absence of `Notify_Requester` in the onboarding log is the *expected* state for compliant cases. SUP-702's CRM record proves notification occurred invisibly to the log. SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours; its link is a mutable text field entered two days later, which weakens proof but does not prove non-notification — the direction of inference from a broken sensor to a breached duty is exactly backwards. SUP-711's doubled `Register_Supplier` carries a single transaction ID: one act, recorded twice by migration. SUP-713's `QA_Clearance` has audit-documented equivalent content to `Quality_Review`; only the mapping's approval postdates the case. These are data-engineering findings, not supplier-onboarding misconduct.

**Scope is genuinely undefined at both ends.** SUP-709: a sanctions-failed lead received no quality review. If rejected leads are intake cases outside M0 (model owner's position), there is no deviation at all; if they are M0 cases, the model demands a quality review of a lead already failed on sanctions — a requirement no one defends on the merits, and CS-9 expressly contemplates an abbreviated pre-screen without saying whether M0 governs it. SUP-710 is a reactivation with a still-valid prior certification; CS-9 has no reactivation procedure, so the missing `Register_Supplier`/`Quality_Review` may reflect a case-identity decision in the log (a new case ID for an old supplier) rather than skipped work. A model cannot be violated by cases it may not govern.

**Trace deviation  business misconduct.** SUP-715 fits M0; the spouses issue invokes a policy that requires *disclosure*, not abstention, and no rule prohibits the relationship. SUP-712 shows the converse: perfect trace, real problem — proving that the interesting compliance questions (identity, eligibility, amounts, dates) live entirely outside M0's vocabulary. The defense therefore asks that conformance reporting be rebuilt around data-aware rules rather than trace fitness.

**Concessions the defense must make.** The defense cannot explain SUP-703 (ineligible, high-risk, EUR 180,000) or SUP-708 (sanctions after activation) by any model defect: every instrument, including the most permissive directive, required sanctions before activation and eligibility for auto-approval. It cannot extend PD-3 past 2026-07-31 or FD-7 past 2026-07-12 by factual sympathy: SUP-714 and SUP-705 occurred under expired authority, and "imminent approval" is not approval. It cannot convert the Compliance observer's ambiguous remark into a standard amendment. And it must accept that 23 of 312 pilot cases are ineligible or unprovably eligible — an exception regime with a ~7% proof failure is not being operated as a "monitored" exception at all. Model obsolescence explains the *rate*; it does not acquit the *tail*.

### System-level judgment

**On operational compliance:** The operation is *selectively* noncompliant, not systemically lawless. The bulk of June–July pilot use (289/312 apparently eligible; SUP-702, SUP-704 exemplary) was disciplined. But there is a hard core of genuine violations — SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712 — plus an authority-tail problem (SUP-705, SUP-714, and the six August cases) showing that exceptions continued after their legal basis lapsed. The gravest systemic failure is governance: a controlled standard, a minutes-level pilot, and an expiring directive were allowed to coexist with no versioned overlay, no eligibility gating, and no model update for months.

**On model validity:** M0 is *valid as a depiction of the CS-9 standard route and invalid as the organization's conformance instrument*. It omits a CS-9 rule (separation of duties), omits endorsed behavior (information loop), omits both authorized overlays, has undefined scope at intake and reactivation, and is fed by a log whose notification channel broke on 2026-07-01. Deviation rates computed against M0 alone are not a compliance metric.

**Where each side is rejected.** The prosecution is rejected insofar as it would read raw M0 deviation rates, missing notifications, duplicate registrations, label renames, and the information loop as misconduct — those inferences fail on the evidence. The defense is rejected insofar as it would let model obsolescence launder ineligible high-risk auto-approval, sanctions-after-activation, self-approval, and post-expiry exception use; expired or never-granted authority is not cured by operational habit or good outcomes. The correct synthesis: **enforce CS-9's substance (sanctions-first, eligibility, separation of duties, time-boxed authority) rigorously; retire M0 as the sole test immediately.**

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A layered architecture, because no single formalism carries control flow, data conditions, time, identity, and authority at once.

**Layer 1 — Revised control-flow model (POWL).** Route-based XOR after registration: standard route (concurrent sanctions  quality, repeatable information requests, manual approval  activation), low-risk auto route (sanctions + master-data validation  auto-approval  provisional activation  deferred quality), emergency route (sanctions  provisional activation  retrospective quality  manual approval), and rejection route. Notification strictly before closure in all routes. POWL owns *ordering and concurrency only*.

**Layer 2 — Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules (DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints + pandas checks over the event log and case attributes).** These are inherently data- and clock-dependent and must *not* be baked into POWL:
- Auto route permitted only if `risk=LOW  country=DE  group_approved=true  case_date  PD-window  rule_version = PD-3` — evaluated on an eligibility snapshot frozen at decision time.
- Emergency route only if `directive_id` valid at activation timestamp, `initial_spend  20000`, monitoring events daily, quality and retrospective approval within 2 business days.
- Everywhere: `timestamp(Sanctions_Check) < timestamp(first activation)`; standard route: quality and manual approval before activation; deferred quality deadline = activation + 2 business days.
- Log-skeleton/DECLARE form: e.g., `Response(Auto_Approve, Quality_Review)` with deadline attribute; `NotSuccession(Activate, Sanctions)` negated as a precedence constraint.

**Layer 3 — Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (organizational controls + pandas identity checks).** Canonical actor identity resolved through an alias table (SUP-712); `requester_id  approver_id` as a hard gate at approval time (system-enforced workflow control, not merely audited); COI disclosure as a mandatory recorded attribute before quality review/approval where a declared relationship exists, with disclosure — not automatic prohibition — as the rule (SUP-715), escalating to recusal only under a future explicit prohibition.

**Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance.** Pilots and emergency directives become first-class, versioned objects: unique `rule_version_id`, issuing authority, effective/record timestamps (immutable, distinct — *valid time* vs *transaction time*), eligibility predicate, expiry, and a named owner obliged to either amend CS-9/M0 or let the overlay lapse. System configuration must hard-stop overlay routes at expiry (SUP-705, SUP-714 become technically impossible rather than merely forbidden). Expansion of an exception beyond ~5% of volume triggers mandatory standard amendment.

**Layer 5 — Explicit scope rules.** Rejected leads: a separate intake/pre-screen process model ending in `Reject_Supplier`, with M0 proper beginning at pre-screen acceptance — quality review not required for sanctions-failed leads (resolves SUP-709 by *deciding* it). Reactivations: a defined route requiring sanctions re-check, certification-validity verification in lieu of full quality review where unexpired, and a `Reactivate_Supplier` registration event (resolves SUP-710). Withdrawals: an explicit terminal branch (registration  withdrawal  notification  closure) so withdrawn cases stop masquerading as incomplete onboardings.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**Limitation stated plainly:** POWL cannot enforce risk class, dates, amounts, identity, or whether a pilot/emergency authorization is in force, and a POWL LOOP repeats its "do" part — so the `Request_More_Info` loop below conservatively *repeats the concurrent checks* after each information request (safe: re-verification, though stricter than practice). Eligibility, deadlines, caps, and authority are enforced by the overlay table and data checks beneath the code, not hidden inside the model. Distinct `Transition` objects with identical labels are used per route, since a POWL node cannot be shared across XOR branches.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def T(label):
    return Transition(label=label)

# ---------- Standard route: (Sanctions || Quality) with repeatable Request_More_Info,
# ---------- then Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier
s_std, q_std = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Quality_Review")
checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_std, q_std])          # concurrent: no edge
info_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                         children=[checks_std, T("Request_More_Info")])
man_std, act_std = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), T("Activate_Supplier")
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[info_loop, man_std, act_std])
standard_route.order.add_edge(info_loop, man_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(man_std, act_std)

# ---------- Low-risk auto route (PD-3 style): Sanctions + master-data validation
# ---------- -> Auto_Approve -> provisional Activate -> deferred Quality_Review
s_auto, mdv = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
pre_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_auto, mdv])             # concurrent preconditions
auto_appr, act_auto, q_auto = T("Auto_Approve"), T("Activate_Supplier"), T("Quality_Review")
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[pre_auto, auto_appr, act_auto, q_auto])
auto_route.order.add_edge(pre_auto, auto_appr)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_appr, act_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(act_auto, q_auto)                    # quality after provisional activation

# ---------- Emergency route (FD-7 style): Sanctions -> provisional Activate
# ---------- -> (Quality_Review || retrospective Manual_Procurement_Approval)
s_em, act_em = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Activate_Supplier")
q_em, man_em = T("Quality_Review"), T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
retro = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[q_em, man_em])               # concurrent retrospective duties
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_em, act_em, retro])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(s_em, act_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_em, retro)

# ---------- Rejection route: sanctions pre-screen -> Reject_Supplier
s_rej, rej = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Reject_Supplier")
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_rej, rej])
reject_route.order.add_edge(s_rej, rej)

decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                        children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route])

register, notify, close = T("Register_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester"), T("Close_Case")
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# SilentTransition is available for future optional/skippable steps (e.g., an
# optional co-approval placeholder) without polluting the visible alphabet.
_optional_placeholder = SilentTransition()

print(M1)
```

### Event/data-collection proposal

- **Stable notification identity:** CRM emits `Notify_Requester` events into the onboarding log via an immutable, system-generated `notification_id` foreign key (replacing mutable text links); closure is system-blocked until a linked notification exists or a documented notification-failure exception is recorded. This converts SUP-706 from indeterminate to decidable.
- **Rule/version stamping:** every approval/activation event carries `rule_version_id`, the `eligibility_snapshot` (risk, country, group, spend-to-date) frozen at decision time, and the `overlay_id` invoked — making the 9 "missing eligibility" cases impossible by construction (route selection disabled without a complete snapshot).
- **Dual timestamps:** `effective_time` (when the act occurred) and `record_time` (when logged, immutable, append-only) on every event, so late-entered links (SUP-706) and post-hoc mappings (SUP-713) are visible as recording lag rather than silent edits. Label changes require a versioned label-mapping table effective-dated *prospectively*.
- **Actor identity:** a governed alias-resolution table mapping all user IDs to one canonical `person_id`; approval workflow rejects `requester_person_id = approver_person_id` at runtime; COI disclosure is a mandatory structured field on quality/approval events.
- **Authority evidence:** pilot/emergency invocations require a `delegation_evidence_ref` pointing to the versioned directive; the route is technically unavailable outside its window.
- **Deduplication:** ingestion keys on `source_transaction_id` (SUP-711), with duplicate arrivals quarantined, never silently double-logged.
- **Case scoping:** `case_type  {intake_lead, onboarding, reactivation, withdrawal}` recorded at case creation, binding each case to exactly one governing model version.

With these layers, conformance checking becomes three honest questions — does the trace fit the route's POWL, do the data satisfy the overlay in force at that timestamp, and do the identities satisfy the resource rules — instead of one dishonest question asked of M0 alone.
