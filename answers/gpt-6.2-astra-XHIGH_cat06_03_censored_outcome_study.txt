## 1. Target definition

Predict a **six-outcome distribution for each application under the operating policy in force**. The unit is the original application. Score it when the lender receives its Application Started event, using the start payload. This one-event landmark gives even a case that closes immediately a prediction opportunity. Three-event and 48-hour updates are separate predictions for cases still eligible at those landmarks.

Let S be start time and H = S + 30 days. Take the first of rejection, withdrawal, outward transfer, and account activation on the originating platform through H, inclusive. If none occurs, the process state at H is pending, which includes accepted or signed cases awaiting activation. For activation at time A by H, split the outcome by the warehouse definition of default in the 90 days following A. The six outcomes are **rejected, withdrawn, transferred, pending, activated with default, and activated without default**. An account activated on day 29 gets the full subsequent 90 days of outcome follow-up. An account activated on day 31 was pending at H. Feed delay affects when the answer is known.

The estimand is the vector of probabilities of those outcomes conditional on information available at the landmark, for the lender's application-start population and policy environment. Thirty days is a proposed operational window for near-term activations. The probability of “activated with default” measures how often an application like this will both lead to credit exposure within that window and default on that exposure. Showing the other five probabilities makes a low joint risk interpretable when activation itself is unlikely.

A credible account-monitoring alternative is to score at activation and estimate the probability of default by A + 90 days among activated accounts, possibly using an earlier stored application prefix. This supports servicing decisions and has a different denominator. “Would this applicant default if offered and drawn on a specified loan?” is a third estimand. It requires specifying terms, take-up and policy, and evidence or assumptions about outcomes under exposures the historical policy did not generate.

## 2. Cohort construction

Build the application spine from all starts from 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2025, inclusive. Reconcile original identifiers, account-to-application joins and outbound handoffs against the source registers. Count a linked partner continuation once under its original transfer. Include genuinely new applications as separate units. Audit events without an in-window start, cases with conflicting first dispositions, unmatched accounts and ambiguous identifiers. Record counts and reasons for any unscorable or unresolved cases against the original denominator.

These are snapshot counts. Day-30 class sizes require disposition timestamps. A rejection, withdrawal or transfer after H also leaves the application pending at H. The 12.5% observed default fraction (2,700/21,600) describes the labeled activated subset. The snapshot gives no separate count of permanently missing feeds.

At any analysis cutoff c, use origin events observed by the earlier of c and the end of 31 December 2025. Advance the feed clock through c, at most the 15 February 2026 extraction. Require a dated, authoritative account label available by c and compatible with the activation and outcome horizon. Absence of January process events in this file provides no evidence that a December case stayed open in January. For the primary joint-outcome analysis require full process coverage through H and c ≥ H + 90 + 35 days for **every** included start. This common calendar rule also applies to quickly closed cases. It leaves application intake and scoring unrestricted, and defines a buffered, older population for fitting and evaluation. Report coverage by start month.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

| Mechanism | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Administrative right censoring | If the event watermark precedes H and no disposition has occurred, the final process state is unresolved. If c precedes A + 90, follow-up for the binary endpoint is incomplete. Keep these cases on the spine. For the primary analysis, wait for the common cohort window to elapse, including for early positives. |
| Reporting delay | Once A + 90 passes, an absent result can still be in transit. A dated final result may be used at a freeze after its arrival. Use 35 days as a reporting allowance. Estimate the arrival-lag distribution from older feeds and retain an overdue category. |
| Selection through activation | Historical decisions and customer take-up determine exposure. Model activation as an outcome for all starts. Estimate account default conditional on activation by H. Weighting for feed receipt addresses a different selection step. |
| Transfer | Outward transfer ends origin follow-up by design. For a cross-platform analysis, audit the roughly 60% linkage rate by source, applicant mix and period. Treat unlinked downstream paths as unknown and bound their contribution. |
| Persistent feed loss | For activated cases otherwise mature, retain a missing ascertainment indicator. Investigate partner-specific nonreceipt against feed delivery records. A missing result at one freeze cannot distinguish permanent loss from an unusually long delay. |

Fit a five-class process model for probabilities g of rejection, withdrawal, transfer, pending and activation by H. Fit a second model q for default conditional on activation by H and the prediction prefix. The six probabilities are the four nonactivation components of g, plus g_activation × q and g_activation × (1 − q). A missing account label still allows the case to train the process model.

Within buffered day-30 activations, estimate the receipt probability r(Z) by the analysis freeze, using variables recorded for *all* such accounts, including channel, partner/source, prefix history, activation vintage and time since the outcome horizon. Fit the conditional default model on reported labels with inverse observation weights 1/r(Z). Extra variables known at activation can inform the receipt model without entering the early-warning score. Estimate nuisance functions within training data, cross-fit if flexible, and audit weight tails, balance, effective sample size and coverage. For evaluation, estimate ascertainment for the evaluation vintage using a prespecified procedure and receipt indicators available at that freeze. No weight for approval is needed for this observed-policy estimand.

This correction requires that, conditional on Z (including relevant prediction features), receipt is independent of default, that receipt has positive probability in the target strata, and that receipt probabilities can be estimated adequately. Timestamp order, binary-label consistency, observed arrival lags and differential receipt rates can be checked. Covariate balance, overlap and historical calibration can be assessed only on observed support. Dependence on unreported defaults, eventual arrival of overdue records, and counterfactual defaults for unexposed applicants are untestable from this snapshot. In a partner stratum with no reports, show bounds for the default component.

Repeat the analysis with longer reporting allowances on common vintages. For a missing-not-at-random sensitivity analysis, vary the odds of default among nonreporters relative to comparable reporters, for example by factors of 0.5, 1 and 2 within partner strata. Also show extremes assigning all missing *eligible activated* labels first to 0 and then to 1. Propagate these assumptions into joint risk and calibration, and report whether alert decisions change. They leave observed competing dispositions intact.

## 4. Prefix extraction

Replay each origin episode in event-availability order. At start, read only the triggering start message. For the three-event update, trigger immediately after the third canonical event, counting the start; issue it only before acceptance, another disposition or the end of the 30-day window. For the 48-hour update, include events available through S + 48 hours and score at that clock time if the case is still in the predecision process. A case with only its start event can receive the 48-hour update. Time since the last event is then 48 hours. Later reviews never reset either clock.

Keep separate risk sets and report how many starts reach each landmark, close earlier, lack sufficient observation, or have ambiguous ordering. A short case retains its start score. Do not pad traces using their ultimate length. Keep genuine document requests, uploads and review returns as distinct events. Canonicalize migration aliases by source and effective date using a verified dictionary. Remove a duplicate only with evidence of a replay, such as a stable upstream event identifier. Use a source sequence for equal timestamps if available. Otherwise withhold the affected sequence prefix or use a prespecified order-insensitive representation. A tie later in the trace cannot invalidate an earlier prefix.

A valid replay needs evidence that each field was observable at the nominal scoring time. If event time and arrival differ, use an ingestion history and discard messages that had not arrived by the landmark. A final CSV extraction timestamp alone cannot reconstruct that history.

## 5. Process-aware features

| Source | Examples and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Immutable start payload | Intake channel, region and requested amount as submitted. Preserve original versions and missingness. |
| Prefix control flow and rework | Current activity, recent activity transitions, number of reviews, document requests, completed request-to-review returns and distinct activities. A count of prior returns distinguishes a first review from a later review at the same activity. |
| Prefix timing and resources | Age at scoring, last transition duration, time since last activity, distinct resources and observed handoffs. Inter-event elapsed time can include both service and waiting. Separate them only if lifecycle timestamps support it. |
| Event-time risk and calendar | Last score actually published by the landmark, its age or missingness, intake weekday and policy version known at scoring. Audit cases spanning a policy change using actual decision dates offline. |
| Historical reference data | Number of cases already waiting in the same queue at the landmark, trailing review times from completed transitions, or staff tenure from effective-dated records. Reconstruct queue membership from prior arrivals and exits, with a warm-up period for initial backlog. Historical default rates, if used, require labels already available then and exclusion of the current case. |

At the start landmark, many path features are constant. Their predictive use begins with later updates. Learn frequency encodings, vocabularies derived from observations, imputations and scaling parameters within each training fold.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Reveals the final decision or terms | Only set after decision | Drop | Outside every specified scoring landmark. |
| `decision_reason` | Encodes acceptance or rejection | Only set at or after decision | Drop | Direct information about a target branch. |
| `collection_status` | Reveals repayment or default | Servicing data after activation | Drop | Outcome proxy. |
| Future events, trace length, eventual variant | Reveal closure, activation, collections or ultimate rework | Slice at the landmark before computing aggregates | Keep prefix only | Even a count of future events leaks the path. |
| `documents_total` | Nightly snapshot sees later same-day uploads | Count timestamped uploads actually received through scoring | Repair or drop | Calendar date alone gives insufficient ordering. |
| Updated `risk_score` | Final or backfilled value can replace the historical score | Use the last version published by scoring time, forward within the observed prefix only | Keep verified versions | Preserves score changes and genuine missingness. |
| Resource experience | Extraction-time tenure includes subsequent employment | Effective-dated staff record joined at event time | Repair or drop | Prevents using future tenure or role. |
| `default_90d`, `label_available_at`, feed receipt | Repeated values leak the endpoint, and receipt selects accounts | Isolate for outcome construction, maturity checks and ascertainment modeling | Drop from score inputs | Neither label presence nor arrival lag is an intake predictor. |
| `source_system`, `data_extract_time`, identifiers | Later platform, extract age or reused IDs can reveal the path or permit memorization | Use verified intake source only as a predictor; retain clocks and IDs for joins and audits | Restrict | Preserves point-in-time provenance and origin boundaries. |

Audit a sample of prefixes against contemporaneous messages. Recompute features after deleting or altering every later event and label in a test fixture; an already issued feature vector must be identical. Check maximum contributing availability time, allowed columns and one vector per eligible origin and landmark.

## 7. Validation design

Use expanding, forward-in-time backtests grouped by original application start, keeping all its prefixes together. In one concrete split, train on starts before 1 November 2024 using information available through 31 October. Generate forward validation scores for starts from November 2024 through June 2025. Tune model complexity, calibration and any alert threshold using validation outcomes buffered by 30 June 2025. For example, 26 January is the latest start date that can meet the 155-day rule at that freeze. Refit on eligible pre-July data and lock the model before scoring starts from 1 July through 31 December 2025. Keep preprocessing, feature lookups and label availability aligned to each historical fit or prediction time. Repeat at additional prespecified origins to show variability across training windows.

At the February 2026 freeze, **13 September 2025 is the latest start date eligible for the fully buffered joint test**. Report pre-policy validation and July-to-September post-policy test results separately. Score the October migration and later starts prospectively in replay. The process-only evaluation can extend through starts on 1 December with full day-30 coverage. Compare periods on each side of 15 October, including schema coverage, alias frequencies, ambiguous duplicates, trace lengths at each landmark and measured alert rates. Fully buffered post-migration joint calibration requires a later outcome freeze, and evaluation for late December starts also requires extended origin-event coverage. Receiving a few early labels cannot establish performance for that vintage.

Report multiclass log loss and Brier score, calibration by predicted-probability bin for each state, and observed versus predicted defaults per 1,000 starts. Score g on every process-evaluable case. For joint metrics on buffered cohorts, use weight 1 on the four known nonactivation states and 1/r(Z) on reported activated outcomes, with unreported activated cases contributing no observed loss term. For example, sum weighted six-class Brier losses and divide by the number of buffered, scorable starts. Also assess q within day-30 activations using weighted calibration and precision–recall performance. Where an alert budget is specified, report precision and recall for activated-with-default at that budget. Compare against training-period state frequencies and an intake-only model on the same evaluation risk sets.

Break out counts, coverage, receipt rates, calibration and error by partner/channel, region, requested-amount band, calendar regime and landmark. Show uncertainty intervals, for example by resampling start-week blocks and refitting ascertainment weights, keeping each origin's predictions together. Flag small or unsupported groups. Track feature missingness, score distributions and changes in disposition rates conditional on similar prefixes. Random splitting mixes policies and logging regimes, can place later-known labels in the simulation of an earlier fit, and can separate repeated prefixes or linked continuations. Its apparent calibration may describe no actual deployment period.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

Use the score for portfolio forecasting and prioritizing human review under a comparable lending and follow-up policy. Display the process probabilities alongside conditional account risk and record the score time and model version. A low joint default probability driven by frequent rejection supplies no evidence that issuing a loan to such an applicant would be safe. A causal estimate for a changed approval policy would require a specified intervention, consistency of the credit exposure, exchangeability or a credible experimental design, adequate overlap, and ascertainment under that policy. Historical selection can leave whole applicant groups without exposed counterparts.

Deployment can change at least four mechanisms. Reviewers may reject more flagged applicants, changing who receives credit. Additional document requests may alter rework, withdrawal and activation times. Outreach, pricing or changed limits may affect take-up and default after activation. Rerouting flagged cases to partners may change both transfer rates and feed completeness. Log actions and reporting coverage, monitor calibration by vintage, and evaluate the effect of interventions separately from the ability to forecast outcomes.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

The skeleton assumes UTC, verified origin-episode IDs, immutable historical event payloads and event times that coincide with availability. Where these conditions fail, reconstruct the point-in-time view before running it. The canonical document names are examples to bind to the source dictionary. The code fails on unmapped activities. It keeps labels separate even when they are physically repeated on prefix rows.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
Y, L, SRC = "default_90d", "label_available_at", "source_system"
CH, RG, AM = "case:channel", "case:region", "case:requested_amount"
D = pd.Timedelta(days=1)
def end(day):
    return pd.Timestamp(day, tz="UTC") + D - pd.Timedelta(1, "ns")

BEGIN = pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01", tz="UTC")
EVENT_END, SNAP = end("2025-12-31"), end("2026-02-15")
CHANGE = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15", tz="UTC")
VAL, TEST = (pd.Timestamp(s, tz="UTC") for s in ["2024-11-01", "2025-07-01"])
START, ACT, REV, DOC = "Application Started", "Account Activated", "Credit Review", "Documents Requested"
exits = [ACT, "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]
stops = exits + ["Accepted", "Contract Signed", "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"]
vocab = set(stops + [START, "Identity/KYC Check", REV, DOC, "Documents Received"])
raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv", dtype={C: "string"})
for col in [T, L, "data_extract_time"]:
    raw[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col], utc=True, errors="raise")
assert raw[[C, A, T, "data_extract_time"]].notna().all().all()
assert raw["data_extract_time"].max() <= SNAP
dated = raw[L].notna()
assert raw.loc[dated, L].le(raw.loc[dated, "data_extract_time"]).all()
raw[C] = "origin/" + raw[C]  # preserve opaque IDs in PM4Py, including leading zeros
# Supply the governed (source, raw activity) aliases effective from CHANGE before use.
aliases = {}
m = raw[T].ge(CHANGE)
raw.loc[m, A] = [aliases.get((s, a), a) for s, a in zip(raw.loc[m, SRC], raw.loc[m, A])]
assert set(raw[A]) <= vocab, "Resolve unknown activities against the source dictionary"
# Input contract: source-audited origin IDs, immutable event-time payloads and ordered delivery.
# Remove only confirmed replay copies upstream. Keep unresolved multiplicity for audit below.
def snapshot(asof):
    watermark = min(asof, EVENT_END)
    e = raw.loc[raw[T].between(BEGIN, watermark)].sort_values([C, T], kind="stable")
    starts = e.loc[e[A].eq(START)].groupby(C)[T].min()
    c = starts.to_frame("start")
    print("IDs with events but no in-window start", e[C].nunique() - len(c))
    e = e.loc[e[C].isin(c.index) & e[T].ge(e[C].map(starts))]
    disp = e.loc[e[A].isin(exits), [C, T, A]]
    first = disp.loc[disp[T].eq(disp.groupby(C)[T].transform("min"))]
    assert first.groupby(C)[A].nunique().le(1).all(), "Reconcile simultaneous dispositions"
    c = c.join(first.drop_duplicates(C).set_index(C).rename(columns={T:"exit_at", A:"exit"}))
    c["activation"] = c.exit_at.where(c.exit.eq(ACT))
    c["process_until"] = c.exit_at.fillna(watermark)
    h, due = c.start + 30*D, c.activation + 90*D
    c["g"] = c.exit.where(c.exit_at.le(h), "Pending").where(c.exit_at.le(h) | h.le(watermark))
    c["buffered"] = h.le(watermark) & (h + 125*D).le(asof)
    # A dated final feed can be attached to an old event. Its reporting clock is L.
    obs = e.loc[e[Y].notna() & e[L].le(asof), [C, Y, L]].copy()
    assert obs[Y].isin([0, 1]).all() and obs.groupby(C)[Y].nunique().le(1).all()
    assert obs[L].ge(obs[C].map(c.activation)).all()
    neg = obs.loc[obs[Y].eq(0)]
    assert neg[L].ge(neg[C].map(due)).all()
    c["y"] = obs.groupby(C)[Y].first().reindex(c.index).where(due.le(asof))
    c["feed"] = np.select([c.activation.isna(), due.gt(asof), c.y.eq(1), c.y.eq(0),
                           (due + 35*D).ge(asof)],
                          ["inapplicable", "before day 90", "default", "non-default",
                           "awaiting feed"], default="overdue/unknown")
    c["outcome"] = c.g.where(c.g.ne(ACT), c.feed.map({"default":"A1", "non-default":"A0"}))
    c["split"] = np.select([c.start.lt(VAL), c.start.lt(TEST)], ["train", "validation"], "test")
    return e, c

# These are event-time views, including the score version visible on each event.
inputs = [C, A, T, "org:resource", CH, RG, AM, "risk_score"]
def features(e, c, asof, mode="start", k=3):
    x = e.loc[e[C].isin(c.index), inputs].copy()
    if mode == "start":
        x = x.loc[x[A].eq(START) & x[T].eq(x[C].map(c.start))]
        at = c.start
    else:
        gate = x.loc[x[A].isin(stops)].groupby(C)[T].min().reindex(c.index)
        stop_at = x[C].map(gate)
        before_stop = stop_at.isna() | x[T].lt(stop_at)
        x = x.loc[before_stop & x[T].lt(x[C].map(c.start) + 30*D)]
        if mode == "48h":
            at = c.start + 2*D
            elig = at.le(min(asof, EVENT_END)) & (gate.isna() | gate.gt(at))
            x = x.loc[x[C].isin(c.index[elig]) & x[T].le(x[C].map(at))]
        elif mode == "k":
            # Do not break a timestamp tie by arbitrary CSV row order.
            x = x.sort_values([C, T], kind="stable")
            at = x.loc[x.groupby(C).cumcount().eq(k-1)].set_index(C)[T]
            x = x.loc[x[C].isin(at.index)]
        else:
            raise ValueError(mode)
    payload = x.loc[x[T].le(x[C].map(at))]
    ambiguous = payload.loc[payload.duplicated([C, T], keep=False), C].unique()
    print(mode, "landmarks reached", payload[C].nunique(), "ambiguous", len(ambiguous))
    payload = payload.loc[~payload[C].isin(ambiguous)]
    keys = x.loc[x[C].isin(payload[C].unique()), [C, A, T]].copy()
    if payload.empty:
        return pd.DataFrame(index=pd.Index([], name=C))
    p = pm4py.format_dataframe(keys, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)
    if mode == "k":
        p = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(p, k)
    p = p[[C, A, T]].merge(payload, on=[C, A, T], validate="one_to_one").sort_values([C, T])
    assert len(p) == len(payload) and (mode != "k" or p.groupby(C).size().eq(k).all())
    assert p[T].le(p[C].map(at)).all() and not p[A].isin(stops).any()
    assert not {Y, L, "final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status"}.intersection(p)
    g = p.groupby(C, sort=False)
    f = p.drop_duplicates(C).set_index(C)[[CH, RG, AM]].copy()
    f["events"] = g.size()
    f["reviews"] = p[A].eq(REV).groupby(p[C]).sum()
    f["doc_requests"] = p[A].eq(DOC).groupby(p[C]).sum()
    f["last_activity"], f["last_known_score"] = g[A].last(), g["risk_score"].last()
    f["staff_seen"] = g["org:resource"].nunique()
    f["age_hours"] = (at.reindex(f.index) - c.start.reindex(f.index)).dt.total_seconds()/3600
    f["idle_hours"] = (at.reindex(f.index) - g[T].max()).dt.total_seconds()/3600
    f["new_policy"] = at.reindex(f.index).ge(TEST).astype(int)
    return f

# For the primary landmark only. Tune using validation, refit at TEST, score locked test.
def panel(which, cutoff):
    e, c = snapshot(cutoff)
    print(pd.crosstab(c.exit.fillna("Open"), c.feed), "starts", len(c))
    mask = c.split.isin(which) & c.buffered
    b = c.loc[mask]
    X = features(e, b, cutoff)
    allowed = {CH, RG, AM, "events", "reviews", "doc_requests", "last_activity",
               "last_known_score", "staff_seen", "age_hours", "idle_hours", "new_policy"}
    assert set(X) <= allowed and X.index.is_unique
    print(which, "buffered", len(b), "scorable", len(X))
    return X, b.loc[X.index, ["g", "y", "outcome", "feed"]]  # labels kept separate

train_X, train_y = panel(["train"], end("2024-10-31"))
val_X, val_y = panel(["validation"], end("2025-06-30"))
refit_X, refit_y = panel(["train", "validation"], end("2025-06-30"))
test_X, test_y = panel(["test"], SNAP)
events, cases = snapshot(SNAP)
print(cases.groupby(["split", "g", "feed"], dropna=False).size())
shadow = cases.loc[cases.split.eq("test")]
score_X = features(events, shadow, SNAP)  # all scorable test starts, including December
complete30 = shadow.start.add(30*D).le(EVENT_END)
process_test = shadow.loc[complete30 & shadow.index.isin(score_X.index), "g"]
# Separate landmark datasets. Join targets by origin ID; fit/evaluate with the same fold rules.
for mode in ["k", "48h"]:
    print(mode, "scorable at freeze", len(features(events, cases, SNAP, mode)))
# Fit a process classifier for g, then an ascertainment-weighted y model on day-30 activations.
# A competing-risk/time-to-event extension would also use start, exit_at, process_until.
```
