# INDEPENDENT PROCESS-COMPLIANCE ASSESSMENT
## Northbridge Sterile Systems – July 2026 Emergency Procurement

---

## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Core Assumptions

**1.1 Authority and Norm Hierarchy Premises**

- **Statutory/Policy Precedence:** GPIP-4 (board-approved, global, effective 2026-01-01) establishes baseline mandatory controls. It is not automatically superseded by later instruments unless they explicitly invoke the specific waiver authority it names (A7: Chief Risk Officer) or unless a specifically authorized emergency directive coherently displaces it within a narrow temporal scope.

- **Incident Directives as Temporary Overlays:** ECD-2 (COO + Incident Commander co-signed, 2026-07-14 09:00–18:00, 96-hour window) is treated as a legitimate operational instrument that can narrow process timing (PO may follow commitment; approved-supplier register may be bypassed). However, ECD-2 does not explicitly invoke GPIP-4's A7 exception mechanism and does not mention GPIP-4 at all. It therefore cannot be presumed to waive controls that GPIP-4 designates as core (segregation of duties, Compliance clearance thresholds). **Scope ambiguity** in ECD-2 ("critical sterilization-line lots") requires evidence-based interpretation at decision time, not retroactive loose construction.

- **Local Procedures as Stricter Baseline:** SOP B (Aachen, effective 2025-03-01) is stricter than GPIP-4 on some dimensions (EUR 25,000 vs. EUR 50,000 thresholds for Plant/Finance approval). GPIP-4 A5 permits local stricter rules. Where B conflicts with A, A governs unless a coherently scoped emergency directive displaces it. B's approval sequencing (B1: PO before commitment) is "ordinary" local workflow that ECD-2 C4 can override for timing; segregation of duties, however, are integrity controls that ECD-2 does not explicitly address.

- **CFO Email (D):** Ordinary email, not in controlled policy repository, sent within ECD-2 window. D1 and D2 are CFO interpretations of Finance controls within D's purview. D3 is correct: the CFO has no documented authority to waive Compliance clearance (a distinct control) or GPIP-4 segregation of duties. D4 ("use judgment; not meant to remove essential integrity controls") creates a clear ceiling: D cannot be read to excuse deviations that core policy forbids. D is *supportive* of timely Finance approval during the emergency but is not itself a policy exception.

**1.2 Scope and Temporal Application**

- **At-the-Time Assessment:** Compliance is assessed against the rules *in force at the moment each act occurred*. ECD-2's 09:00–18:00 CEST window is hard. Acts purporting to occur before 09:00 or after 18:00 are assessed under baseline rules unless contemporaneous evidence (call recordings, supplier acknowledgments, signed documents with hard timestamps) proves otherwise.

- **System Outage (Note EV-44):** The ERP outage (08:30–13:00, timestamps up to 12 minutes late) affects database records' evidential weight. Phone recordings, signed PDFs with creation timestamps, supplier acknowledgment emails, and access logs are privileged as stronger evidence of *when acts truly occurred*. This is not a normative exception but a corrective lens on evidence.

- **Post-Hoc Approval and Cure:** GPIP-4 A6 is explicit: later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval. *Sequence matters.* However, if (1) the commitment was authorized at the time under an applicable emergency rule, and (2) supporting approval follows within a defined grace period, the deviation is mitigated, not cured. I will classify such cases as **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** (not *compliant* ex ante) only if the underlying authorization was sound and the time gap is bounded and reasonable.

**1.3 Evidence and Burden**

- **Defensible Reconstruction:** For timing, supplier recordings and signed documents are stronger than ERP timestamps during/near outages. If multiple evidence streams conflict, the analyst chooses the most contemporaneous, least-easily-fabricated source. Absence of a required record (e.g., no Finance_Approve event) is a defect unless (a) the act fell outside scope, (b) the emergency waived it explicitly, or (c) contemporaneous evidence shows it was waived by someone with actual authority.

- **Burden of Proof:** Compliance is presumed unless a deviation is shown by clear evidence. *Exceptions* are presumed not to apply unless someone with explicit authority invoked them. The CFO email does not constitute an A7 exception; it is an internal interpretation of Finance authority.

**1.4 Rule of Interpretation for Ambiguity**

- **Segregation of Duties (GPIP-4 A1):** Applies to *final business approver* of a purchase. The question "who is the final approver?" depends on the process: if plant approval is mandatory, the Plant Manager is final approver unless procedure specifies otherwise. Incident Command authorization of a supplier commitment is a *different* act from Plant/Finance approval. A person may authorize a supplier commitment and then approve the PO, provided they are not the "final" approver in the approval chain. Ambiguity on "final" is treated strictly: if approval order is unclear, segregation is not waived.

- **"Ordinary" vs. "Integrity" Controls:** ECD-2 C4 overrides "ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding." Timing (PO before/after commitment) is ordinary. Supplier onboarding (approved-supplier check) is procedural. Finance and Compliance clearance are *integrity* controls tied to financial exposure and supply-chain risk; they are not pure procedural steps. Unless ECD-2 expressly waives them, they remain. Segregation of duties is an *integrity* control. This distinction is not in the sources; I assume it is defensible because it aligns with internal control theory (segregation prevents fraud, timeliness is process hygiene).

**1.5 Scope of "Critical Sterilization-Line Lots" (ECD-2 C6)**

- **Operations Interpretation:** Components for the affected sterilization line and the backup line are "critical."  
- **Internal Audit Interpretation:** Only parts needed within 24 hours are covered.

I treat **Operations' reading as presumptively correct** for cases marked by Incident Command as critical (P-503, P-507, P-508); however, **P-503 (backup-line pumps expected to last three days) is borderline** and Audit's narrower reading has force. Ambiguity is noted; Operations has day-to-day authority to define "critical" during an incident, but if a purchase's criticality is questionable in retrospect, its exception status becomes **INDETERMINATE**.

**1.6 Proof of Authorization**

- **Verbal + Recorded:** ECD-2 C3 permits verbal authorization recorded within four hours. This is sound for an incident.  
- **Verbal + No Recording:** If verbal authorization is alleged post-hoc with no contemporaneous recording, phone logs, or witness statement, it is presumed not to have occurred. (P-506 is a case in point.)

### **Alternative 2: P-503 – Incident Command Membership and Requester Role**

**Standard Interpretation (above):** PROC-3 is requester + committer + one of two authorizers. R1 segregation violation. Result: **NONCOMPLIANT.**

**Strongest Alternative:** "Incident Command member" and "procurement requester" are not mutually exclusive. In a contamination crisis, the procurement person may be part of the Incident Command (cross-functional team) and also the requester of parts. The segregation rule (R1) prohibits a single person from being *requester/committer* **and** the *final business approver.* If the Incident Command's authorization is *not* a "business approval" in GPIP-4 A1's sense (which is narrower: Finance approval, Plant approval, budget owner approval), then PROC-3 can be a requester + IC member without violating segregation. **The two distinct Incident Command authorizers may include PROC-3 (as one of two) and another person (IC-2, unnamed in case).** Under this reading, segregation is satisfied: PROC-3 + IC-2 jointly authorize; neither is the sole final approver. R4 (ECD-2 C3, two IC members) is satisfied. **Controlling premises to change:** (1) Interpret GPIP-4 A1 "final business approver" narrowly, excluding Incident Command operational authorization (which is a crisis decision, not a business approval in the accounting sense). (2) Treat PROC-3's role as both requester and one of two IC authorizers as operationally necessary during an incident, not a violation, as long as there is a second, independent authorizer. **Credibility:** **Weak-to-moderate.** The rebuttal is that Incident Command members are typically senior ops/legal/risk personnel, not the requester's peer. If PROC-3 is a buyer and holds no other formal role in Incident Command, the dual role is suspicious. However, if the incident created an all-hands structure where PROC-3 was added to IC for supply-chain expertise, the alternative is credible. The case does not clarify IC membership structure. **R3 (Compliance clearance)** and **R2 (Finance approval)** still fail even under this alternative, so P-503 remains at least **NONCOMPLIANT** on those grounds (likely more serious than segregation alone).

(2) ERP clock skew is presumed post-hoc logging delay, not the real action time. **Credibility:** **Moderate-to-strong.** Supply-chain practice treats supplier's email acknowledgment as binding; the Commit_to_Supplier database entry is clerical. ### **Alternative 4: P-507 – Finance Approval as Deferred/Audited Post-Commitment**

**Standard Interpretation (above):** EUR 110k requires R2 dual approval before commitment; Finance_Approve absent; classified **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** with assumption of deferred Finance approval. Result: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** (assumption-dependent).

**Strongest Alternative:** ECD-2 C4 says it "takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements." **Finance approval *timing* is an "ordinary" process requirement**, not a core integrity control like segregation of duties (R1). Under ECD-2, R2's *timing* (approval before commit) is overridden; Finance approval may follow commitment. CFO Email D1 says "Incident Command authorization is sufficient for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000," which can be read as: *Incident Command authorization at T1 satisfies the operational commitment decision; Finance approval becomes a post-commitment audit function, not a pre-commitment gate*. This is a **process inversion**: instead of gating (Finance  Commit), it becomes confirmation (Commit  Finance audit). Under this reading, absence of Finance_Approve at commitment time is not a breach; it is expected timing under ECD-2. **Finance_Approve must follow within grace period (suggest 72 hrs); if none is issued, then reclassify to NONCOMPLIANT. But if issued post-hoc, even days later, the purchase is COMPLIANT-AS-DEFERRED.** **Controlling premises to change:** (1) Interpret "ordinary purchasing timing" broadly to include all process sequencing, including Finance approval timing (not just PO before/after commitment). (2) Treat ECD-2 C4 ("takes precedence") as a temporal override, not a control existence waiver. (3) Presume CFO D1 is a legitimate operational interpretation, not a policy exception (hence it can override *ordinary* timing without requiring CRO A7 signature). **Credibility:** **Moderate.** The language of C4 and D1 supports this reading, but it requires a generous interpretation of "ordinary." GPIP-4 A2 (dual approval "before") is stated as a hard temporal requirement, not a process flexibility. However, in a crisis with trusted IC authorization and Compliance clearance, deferring Finance to post-commitment audit is operationally reasonable. **The risk:** If Finance_Approve never issues (organization deems it unnecessary because Compliance + IC sufficient), then the waiver becomes de facto, not justified as deferred. Recommend mandatory Finance_Approve follow-up within 72 hours to support this alternative.

### **Alternative 5: P-508 – CFO D1 Threshold Tolerance and Legal Substitution**

**Standard Interpretation (above):** CFO D1 says "sufficient up to EUR 150,000"; P-508 is EUR 155k (over threshold); Finance_Approve absent; classified **COMPLIANT** (deferred Finance assumption). Result: **COMPLIANT** (assumption-dependent).

**Strongest Alternative:** CFO D1–D2 form a **graduated control structure:**
- **EUR 0–150k:** Incident Command authorization (R4) sufficient for operational commitment; Finance may follow.
- **EUR 150k–X:** Incident Command authorization (R4) + Legal clearance (D2) sufficient; Finance may follow.

P-508 (EUR 155k) fits the EUR 150k–X band. **Compliance (R3) + Legal (D2) + IC authorization (R4) = stronger scrutiny than Finance alone.** Legal reviews contract terms, IP, liability; Finance reviews budget availability. In a crisis, Legal scrutiny is *higher* integrity than Finance budget review (legal issues are harder to reverse). Under this reading, **P-508 is COMPLIANT as a graduated-control design**, not merely a deferred-approval assumption. **Finance approval may follow as a post-hoc confirmation, but its absence at commitment time is not a breach; Legal substitutes.**

**Controlling premises to change:** (1) Read CFO D1–D2 as a **control matrix**, not a serial checklist (Compliance, then IC auth, then Finance). (2) Treat Legal clearance as a high-integrity control capable of substituting Finance approval in a narrowly defined band (EUR 150k+). (3) Presume CFO has authority to design emergency Finance controls (D says CFO "has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls"), including control substitution within a defined scope. **Credibility:** **Moderate-to-weak.** The CFO email does not explicitly state that Legal substitutes Finance; it says Legal is required "as well" (implying both). However, if the organization has historically treated Legal and Finance as complementary (not redundant), the substitution reading is plausible. **The risk:** D3 notes "no document states CFO may waive Compliance controls or segregation," implying CFO *can* modify Finance controls but within limits. If Finance is a "segregation" of duties (Finance as independent approver, per A1), then it cannot be waived; if Finance is a "process gate," it can be deferred/substituted. This hinges on whether GPIP-4 A2's Finance approval is a segregation control or a procedural gate—likely the former (independent approver), which limits substitution. **Likelihood:** If strict reading of A2 (Finance as segregation) controls, NONCOMPLIANT. If CFO's authority to modify Finance timing is broad, COMPLIANT with deferred approval is defensible. The D4 constraint ("not meant to remove essential integrity controls") cuts both ways: if Finance is essential, no substitution; if Finance timing is procedural, it can be adjusted.

### **Monitoring Actions (Preventive)**

1. **Incident Authorization Logging:** Immediately after any incident directive (COO/Incident Commander co-signed), create a **master authorization log** in a shared, read-only repository. Log all exceptions granted, scope boundaries, and temporal limits. Require Incident Commander to **close the log in writing** (e.g., "As of [date/time], ECD-2 exceptions are no longer in effect; all future purchases return to baseline GPIP-4/SOP B").

2. **Real-Time Compliance Monitoring:** During an incident, assign a **Compliance Officer or Finance Controller on-call** to provide same-day review (within 4–8 hours) for purchases >EUR 50k. Shift from post-hoc audit to concurrent review.

3. **Segregation Audits:** Monthly, run a query: "Purchases where requester = committer = final approver." Flag for human review. If flagged, investigate whether authorization delegation was in force but not documented.

4. **Supplier Evidence Trail:** Require procurement to log **supplier commitment evidence** (phone call, email, signed PO) with timestamp. Cross-check against internal Commit_to_Supplier ERP entry. If timestamps diverge by >15 minutes, escalate for explanation.

5. **Post-Incident Reconciliation:** Within 30 days of incident close, re-audit all emergency purchases using baseline rules + exception criteria. For any purchase with deferred approval (Finance, Compliance, Plant approval), confirm that deferred approval was issued. If not, issue retroactive approval or variance report.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

### **REVISED EMERGENCY PROCUREMENT AUTHORITY AND CONTROLS FRAMEWORK**
**Effective [Date]; Supersedes ECD-2, CFO Email D, SOP B4 (emergency sections)**

#### **1. AUTHORITY STRUCTURE**

**1.1 Incident Emergency Directive Issuance**
- **Authority:** Chief Operating Officer (COO) + Incident Commander (joint signature).
- **Scope Definition (Required):** Every incident directive must specify:
  - **(a) Triggering Event:** Production line stoppage, contamination event, safety risk (cite specific asset or line).
  - **(b) In-Scope Purchases:** Explicit list or decision tree (e.g., "spare parts for affected sterilization lines and identified backup lines; parts needed within [X] hours of directive issuance").
  - **(c) Temporal Boundary:** Start date/time and end date/time (hard stop; no verbal extensions accepted).
  - **(d) Financial Thresholds:** EUR amount and control adjustments by tier (see Section 3 below).
  - **(e) Authorized Approvers:** Name(s) of Incident Command members authorized to approve commitments (must be 2 per commitment).
- **Authorization Mechanics:** Joint signature by COO and Incident Commander (electronic signature acceptable; must be timestamped and placed in shared incident repository).
- **Escalation Clause:** If directive duration exceeds 7 days or EUR threshold exceeds EUR 500k, Chief Risk Officer (CRO) must review and formally ratify by Day 3 (or directive expires).

**1.2 Chief Risk Officer Authority (Formal Exception)**
- **CRO reserves right** to issue written, scoped exception to any control under GPIP-4, with rationale and time limit.
- **Incident Emergency Directives do NOT automatically constitute CRO exceptions.** Directives are operational overlays (temporary process changes) and require CRO ratification if they materially waive Compliance clearance, segregation of duties, or Finance approval for >72-hour windows.
- **Expedited CRO Review:** If COO/IC directive is issued and CRO is unavailable, deputy CRO may provisionally ratify for 48 hours; CRO must confirm or revoke within 48 hours.

#### **2. SEGREGATION OF DUTIES (NON-WAIVABLE)**

**2.1 Baseline Rule**
- **Requester, Supplier Committer, and Final Business Approver must be three distinct persons.**
- **Final Business Approver = last mandatory approval (Finance, Plant Manager, or IC authorization where delegated as final).**

**2.2 Emergency Incident Directive Modification**
- Incident Command may authorize supplier commitment (committer role) and may provide the *first* approval (operational commit decision).
- Incident Command authorization does NOT replace segregation.
  - If IC member (A) is also the requester or committer, a *different* IC member (B) must also authorize the commitment. Both (A) and (B) together = the "approval" (shared responsibility).
  - If IC member (A) is the sole authorizer, A may not be the committer or requester.
- **In no case** may one person be all three (requester + committer + sole final approver).
- **Post-commitment Approval Sequence:** Finance approval and Plant approval (if required by baseline rules) must follow, provided by persons other than requester/committer.

**2.3 Operational Guidance**
- Incident Command may include procurement personnel (e.g., buyer, procurement manager).
- For segregation purposes, the buyer's "Incident Command member" role is **operationally distinct** from their "procurement requester/committer" role.
- If a buyer wears both hats, another Incident Command member must co-authorize any purchase the buyer commits to. This avoids the perception of self-approval.

#### **4. INCIDENT AUTHORIZATION MECHANICS (MANDATORY)**

**4.1 Two-Member Joint Authorization (Tier 2+)**
- **In-Scope Commitment:** Before any commitment EUR 50k within an incident directive, two distinct Incident Command members must jointly authorize.
- **Authorization Method (Tier 2):**
  - **(a) Verbal:** IC-A and IC-B jointly discuss on call/video, agree to proceed. One of them (usually the IC-A) documents via email within 4 hours: "I, [IC-A], and [IC-B] jointly authorized commitment to [Supplier] for [Part] on [Date/Time] under [Incident Directive Name]. Amount: EUR [X]. Expected delivery: [Date]. Justification: [brief rationale]."
  - **(b) Signed Document:** IC-A and IC-B both sign (electronic or paper PDF) authorization form within 2 hours of commitment, time-stamped.
- **Authorization Method (Tier 3):**
  - **(a) Pre-signed:** IC-A and IC-B sign blank authorization template at start of incident (Day 0) authorizing the Incident Commander to execute commitments on their behalf within defined scope and amount. Each daily commitment is logged (supplier name, amount, date/time).
  - **(b) Spot Check:** Incident Commander (or CFO delegate) confirms budget and risk daily; IC-A and IC-B review log weekly.
- **Recording Repository:** All authorizations recorded in shared, read-only incident repository (wiki, shared drive, Slack channel pinned message) with timestamps. No retroactive edits allowed.

**4.2 Supplier Commitment Evidence (Mandatory)**
- **At time of commitment (call, email, PO issuance):** Procurement records:
  - Supplier name, phone/email contact, part number, amount, expected delivery.
  - Confirmation of approval (authorization names, time IC authorized).
  - Time commitment was made (call start, email send, PO creation).
- **Supplier Acknowledgment:** Within 4 hours of commitment, obtain supplier's written confirmation (email reply, PO acceptance, phone call recording transcript) timestamped.
- **Mismatch Protocol:** If ERP timestamp diverges from supplier email/call evidence by >15 minutes, flag for review (system clock skew, late logging). Use external evidence as ground truth.

**4.3 Emergency Form (Mandatory)**
- **Due:** Within 24 hours of commitment.
- **Contents:** Supplier, amount, part, commitment time, IC authorizers (names), rationale (scope: "affected line repair," "backup line maintenance," etc.), estimated delivery date.
- **Approver:** Procurement Manager or Incident Commander (not the requester/committer).
- **Missing Form  Reclassification:** If emergency form is not filed within 24 hours, purchase is presumed routine, not emergency-authorized, and must meet baseline rules retroactively.

#### **5. TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES AND EXTENSIONS**

**5.1 Hard Deadline for Incident Directive**
- Every incident directive has a fixed end date/time (e.g., "ECD-2 expires 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST").
- **At expiry:** All uncommitted purchases return to baseline GPIP-4/SOP B rules. No oral extensions accepted.
- **Verbal Claim of Extension:** If buyer alleges verbal extension ("Incident Command extended ECD-2 overnight"), **zero weight without written amendment.** Purchase is assessed under baseline rules. If baseline rules are violated, purchase is **NONCOMPLIANT**.

**5.2 Formal Extension (If Needed)**
- **Amendment Process:** COO and Incident Commander jointly issue written amendment (email or signed memo) with new end date/time, filed in incident repository within 1 hour of extension decision.
- **Scope Adjustment:** Amendment may narrow or expand in-scope purchases (e.g., "Extension applies only to sterilization-line parts, not backup-line contingency").
- **Finance Adjustment:** If extension adds cost >5% of original incident budget, CFO must approve extension.

**5.3 Purchases Straddling Boundary (Close to Expiry)**
- **Commitment within window, but late approvals after expiry:** Post-commitment approvals (Finance, Plant, Compliance) occurring after directive expiry remain valid *if* commitment was made before expiry and approval follows within grace period (see Section 3.5).
- **Commitment after expiry, with evidence of supplier contact before expiry:** If supplier acknowledges commitment email/call **before expiry**, and ERP Commit_to_Supplier timestamp is after expiry due to system delay, use supplier acknowledgment time as ground truth (commit = within window, **COMPLIANT**). See Section 6 (Evidential Sufficiency).

#### **6. SEGREGATION, COMPLIANCE, AND NEW SUPPLIERS (HARD RULES)**

**6.1 Segregation (R1) – Non-Waivable**
- Applies to all purchases, at all times. Emergency directive does not waive; it may adjust implementation (see Section 2.2).
- **Non-compliance cannot be cured by post-hoc approval.** If one person is requester + committer + sole final approver, the purchase is **permanently non-compliant**, regardless of subsequent approvals.
- **Audit Trail:** Monthly segregation audit required; any flagged purchase triggers variance investigation.

**6.2 Compliance Clearance (GPIP-4 A3) – Waive Timing, Not Existence (Tier 3+)**
- **Baseline:** EUR >100k or new supplier  Compliance clearance pre-commitment.
- **Emergency (Tier 3+):** Timing waived; Compliance clearance must follow within grace period (24 hrs). If not obtained, purchase is **out-of-compliance** and supplier relationship halted (reversal/credit required).
- **New Supplier (Tier 2+):** Legal pre-qualification substitutes for formal Compliance clearance (Legal reviews contract, supplier credentials, delivery capability). Compliance clearance still required within grace period; Legal pre-qual is a gate, not a replacement.

**6.3 Approved-Supplier Register – Waivable for Emergency (Tier 1+)**
- Supplier outside register allowed if:
  - **(a) IC authorization obtained** (per Section 4.1).
  - **(b) Legal pre-qualification completed within grace period** (confirms supplier credibility, contract viability).
  - **(c) Supplier added to register post-incident or marked "emergency-only"** (non-indefinite status).

---

#### **7. MONITORING, AUDIT, AND VARIANCE**

**7.1 Incident Compliance Officer**
- During any incident >EUR 500k total exposure, designate a **Compliance Officer on-call** to:
  - Daily (Day 0–7) audit all commitments against tier rules (random sample: 20% of commitments).
  - Flag missing approvals, segregation issues, scope drift.
  - Issue daily compliance summary to Incident Commander and CFO.

**7.2 Post-Incident Reconciliation (Mandatory)**
- Within 30 days of incident closure:
  - Re-audit 100% of emergency purchases.
  - Confirm all grace-period approvals were obtained (Finance, Compliance, Plant).
  - For any missing post-hoc approval, issue variance report: classify as **NONCOMPLIANT** or **OUT-OF-COMPLIANCE VARIANCE** (require retrospective approval or credit reversal).
  - Summarize to Audit Committee; escalate policy breaches to CFO + CRO.

**7.3 Variances and Consequences**
- **In-Scope Variance (Approved Exception):** Purchase is within incident directive scope and tier rules; minor timing gap (e.g., Finance 48-hr deadline missed by 4 hours) is logged as **variance-noted** (not re-approval-requiring).
- **Out-of-Scope Variance (Unauthorized):** Purchase is outside incident directive scope (e.g., cafeteria freezer, P-504) or violates segregation (R1). Require post-hoc CRO exception or supplier reversal.
- **Material Breach (Irreversible):** Segregation violation (one person approver), or >30-day gap in post-commitment approval, or commitment >EUR 5M outside baseline rules. Escalate to Audit Committee; may require board-level disclosure.

---

#### **8. TRAINING AND POLICY REFRESH**

**8.1 Annual Training**
- All Incident Command members (CFO, COO, CRO, Plant Managers, Procurement Managers, Compliance) complete annual training on this framework.
- Simulation exercise (once per year): mock incident, group practices authorization, approval sequence, grace-period management.

**8.2 Incident Directive Template**
- COO and Incident Commander use mandatory template (see Appendix A) when issuing any emergency directive >EUR 100k or >48-hr window.
- Template requires: triggering event, in-scope purchases (with decision tree), temporal boundaries, authorized approvers (named), financial thresholds (tier assignment), CRO escalation triggers.

**8.3 Policy Review**
- This framework reviewed annually by CFO, CRO, Chief Procurement Officer, and Audit Committee.
- Update thresholds (EUR values) based on inflation (every 2–3 years).
- Incident post-mortems trigger framework review (e.g., if an incident uncovers a gap, framework is amended within 90 days).

---

#### **9. SAFEGUARD AGAINST EMERGENCY NORMALIZATION**

**9.1 Sunset Clause**
- Every incident emergency directive is **time-limited and non-renewable.** If the incident extends beyond the initially stated end date, a *new* incident directive must be issued (with COO + IC signature + CRO escalation).
- **Prevents informal perpetuation:** Organizations often let emergency procedures drift into routine because they are convenient. This rule forces periodic re-authorization, maintaining conscious control.

**9.2 Escalation to Board (Exceptional)**
- If a single incident generates >EUR 5M in emergency spending or >7-day incident response, Audit Committee and Board must be notified within 5 business days of incident closure.
- Board review includes: scope of emergency purchases, controls invoked, variances found, and recommendations for policy adjustment.

**9.3 Retroactive Authority Review**
- Every incident directive is subject to **retroactive CRO review** within 30 days of expiry.
- **CRO reviews:** Did the directive waive necessary controls? Were waivers proportionate to the crisis? Should this directive type be formalized in policy (e.g., "contamination incident directive," "production stoppage directive")?
- **CRO may retroactively ratify, conditionally accept with recommendations, or reject.** Rejection does not void the directive (incident is over) but flags for policy remediation.

**9.4 Policy Normalization Trigger**
- If the same incident type repeats within 3 years (e.g., second sterilization-line contamination), the Board + CRO assess whether a *standing operational plan* should replace ad-hoc incident directives.
- Standing plans are pre-approved emergency procedures (e.g., "Contamination Incident Procurement Protocol," signed by CRO, valid for 2 years). They prevent re-inventing authorization on each incident.

### **Policy Strengths**

1. **Authority Clarity:** CRO formal exception mechanism is preserved; incident directives are operational overlays, not policy waivers.
2. **Segregation Protection:** R1 is non-waivable; emergency procedures must work *within* segregation, not around it.
3. **Tiered Control Reduction:** Controls are waived by *tier* (amount and risk), not by blanket emergency claim. EUR 50k emergency is different from EUR 500k emergency.
4. **Grace Periods, Not Deferrals:** Post-commitment approvals have hard deadlines (24–48 hrs); deferred approvals are not indefinite.
5. **Safeguard Against Normalization:** Sunset clause, CRO retroactive review, and standing-plan triggers prevent emergency procedures from becoming the new normal.
6. **Evidential Hierarchy:** Supplier evidence (email, phone) is preferred over ERP timestamps during outages; reduces disputes over timing.
7. **Dual Authorization (Tier 2+):** Requires joint IC decision-making, reducing solo-actor risk.
8. **Mandatory Compliance Officer:** On-call audit during incident maintains contemporaneous control vigilance.
