## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not a single objective. It bundles several threats, and the compensating controls cover different subsets.

| Objective / threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1. One actor must not both initiate and authorise | Prevents indirectly by blocking some releases before Release_Payment | Prevents if independent, level-appropriate and substantive, before release or before settlement; detects if later | Detects after the fact; corrects only inside recall window | Does not itself prevent; it is a conditional permission to use one operator |
| O2. Payee, bank ownership, sanctions, bank-change and limit integrity | Strong preventive control for the checks actually performed: sanctions, bank ownership, 7-day bank changes, operator limit, duplicate, PO match | Prevents or detects only if the manager actually inspects bank evidence | Detects some failures retrospectively | Requires AV-2 pass and no recent bank change as preconditions; adds no new bank validation |
| O3. Duplicate, PO and amount accuracy | Prevents duplicate and basic PO/limit errors; failed by warning override | Can prevent if manager checks amount, PO and invoice | Detects duplicates or errors after release | Requires AV-2 pass without override |
| O4. Commercial substance: goods/services received, reasonableness, fabricated receipt | Fails to address. A pass is not evidence of receipt or reasonableness | Can prevent if manager reviews invoice, receipt/service evidence and bank details; weak if rubber-stamped | Detects fabricated or unsupported payment, often too late to prevent loss | Does not create receipt evidence; it only requires a manager approval |
| O5. Collusion, shared credentials, compromised account, conflict of interest | Fails to address collusion and who controlled an account. Override may worsen the risk | Can resist collusion only if truly independent, not subordinate, not conflicted, and not sharing credentials or workstation | Detects patterns, not proof of collusion by itself | Fails if the “independent” manager is the supervisor, credential owner or otherwise conflicted |
| O6. Intervention before irrevocable settlement and recoverability | Prevents when it blocks or stops release before settlement | Prevents if before settlement; corrective if after settlement but inside recall; detective only after recall | Detective, and partly corrective only within the usual 24-hour recall period | Requires manager before settlement and audit within one business day |
| O7. Auditability, emergency governance and accountability | Produces validation evidence, but not complete accountability | Produces approval evidence only if review artefacts exist | Produces retrospective accountability and pattern evidence | Is the governance gate: token before preparation, amount/vendor/bank limits, AV pass, manager before settlement, timely audit |

The key distinction is coverage. AV-2 is strong for O2/O3, weak or absent for O4/O5. MG-4 can cover O1/O4/O5, but only with independence and substantive review. RA-7 is mainly detective. ED-1 is not a control activity; it is an exception framework that is valid only when the other controls actually satisfy its conditions.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should be tested on six dimensions, not on the mere presence of an activity.

1. **Timing.** When did the control act relative to Prepare_Payment, Release_Payment, bank settlement and the 24-hour recall window. A manager before Release_Payment is stronger than a manager after release but before settlement; a manager after settlement is not preventive.
2. **Independence.** Was the checker a different human, with a different credential, not subordinate to the preparer, not conflicted, and not merely the same workstation or shared session. A supervisor who sets the preparer’s rating is not independent for this purpose.
3. **Coverage.** Which threats were actually checked. AV-2 covers bank, sanctions, duplicate, PO and limit checks. It does not cover receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion or credential control.
4. **Overrideability.** A hard block is different from a warning. A warning overridden with another person’s credential is evidence of control failure, not compensating control.
5. **Recoverability.** Could the defect still be stopped, recalled or recovered. Audit after the recall window is detective only.
6. **Evidence quality and collusion resistance.** Was there a substantive review artefact, signed checklist, token log, bank-ownership evidence and receipt verification. Repeated pairing, shared workstations and missing organisational design weaken collusion resistance.

Classification rubric:

- **Fully equivalent / OBJECTIVE_MET:** every material threat is covered by at least one independent preventive control before settlement, or ED-1 is fully satisfied with AV-2 pass, independent pre-settlement manager review and audit within one business day. Residual risk is low.
- **Partially equivalent / OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET:** most material threats are covered before settlement, but there is a formal defect, a narrow coverage gap or an evidence gap. The payment may be technically nonconforming but tolerable for this case if exposure is low and the gap is not the realised loss mechanism.
- **Merely mitigating / OBJECTIVE_FAILED in substance:** controls reduce some risk but a material threat remains uncovered, is overridden, is conflicted, or is checked only after settlement/recall expiry. This is not equivalence.
- **Ineffective:** no independent preventive control before settlement, AV-2 absent or overridden, manager late or conflicted, audit after recall, especially with high amount, new vendor or recent bank change.

“Same expected loss” is not the only or best standard. Expected loss averages probability and amount, but it can hide tail risk, correlation, repeated small breaches, fraud adaptivity and loss of authorisation integrity. A low-amount same-user payment can have low expected loss yet still breach the control objective and create a scalable pattern. A high-amount legitimate payment can have no realised loss yet still have high unrecoverable exposure at release. Detection after loss is also not equivalent to prevention even if recovery sometimes occurs, because recovery is uncertain, delayed and may not restore trust, liquidity or regulatory compliance.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

Chosen semantics: a control is **preventive** if it can stop the commitment before irreversible bank settlement; **detective** if it discovers a defect after the internal release step; **corrective** if it can still stop, recall or recover the payment. Under those semantics, the relevant irreversibility point is settlement, not the internal Release_Payment click.

| Control | Primary function | Timing that makes it preventive | Strength | Main limitation | Illustrative failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 automated validation | Preventive, with detective logging | Before release; can block or warn | Strong for duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank change, limit and basic PO match | Does not prove receipt, reasonableness, collusion or account control; warning can be overridden | AP-205 block worked; AP-202 warning was overridden; AP-204 fabricated receipt passed |
| MG-4 independent manager approval | Preventive if before settlement; hybrid preventive-detective if after Release_Payment but before settlement; detective-corrective if after settlement but inside recall; detective only thereafter | Before release is strongest. After release but before settlement is the last preventive gate | Can cover substance, receipt and bank evidence that AV-2 misses | Fails with rubber-stamping, supervisor conflict, shared credential or shared workstation | AP-203 and AP-206 manager before release; AP-201/AP-207 manager before settlement; AP-202 supervisor ratified late; AP-208 manager after settlement |
| RA-7 retrospective audit and recall | Detective, conditionally corrective | Within 24 hours and before recall expires it can support correction | Can detect fabricated receipts, duplicates, patterns and segregation failures | Does not prevent the initial transfer; value collapses after recall window | AP-204 audit found fabrication but late; AP-202 audit after recall; AP-201/AP-203/AP-206/AP-207/AP-209 timely |
| ED-1 emergency single-operator permission | Governance permission, not a standalone control | Token must be active before preparation; all other conditions must hold | Allows urgent operation only where AV-2, manager and audit compensate for single-operator handling | Any failed condition removes the permission; token alone does not validate bank details or receipt | AP-203/AP-207 satisfy the framework; AP-206 has late token; AP-208 has token but fails amount, bank-change, AV-2, manager and audit conditions |

A manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement should therefore be labelled **hybrid preventive-detective**: detective relative to the release step, preventive relative to irreversible loss. That is the correct label for AP-201 and AP-207. It is preventively meaningful, but weaker than a manager before release because the internal authorisation has already been completed by the same user.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Qualitative residual-risk bands are used. No precise probabilities are asserted.

- **AP-201.** Most plausible loss mechanism: single-operator convenience allows an erroneous duplicate, wrong bank detail or unsupported office-supply payment to pass without a second human. AV-2 breaks the duplicate/bank chain and m04 breaks the single-actor chain before settlement. What remains exposed: receipt/substance, formal emergency governance and normalisation of same-user handling. A truly independent second releaser would probably remove the formal deviation and add a receipt check, but the marginal risk reduction for this payment is small because exposure was low and a pre-settlement manager already intervened.
- **AP-202.** Most plausible loss mechanism: diversion to a new vendor whose bank account changed shortly before payment and was linked to an employee relative, facilitated by warning override. AV-2 actually broke the chain by warning, but override reconnected it. What remains exposed: bank ownership, receipt, credential control, supervisor conflict and recovery. A truly independent second releaser, not using the supervisor credential and requiring bank-change evidence, would likely have blocked or escalated the payment; if that releaser were colluding, the reduction would be smaller.
- **AP-204.** Most plausible loss mechanism: fabricated service receipt submitted and accepted by the second user, either through collusion, shared access, rubber-stamping or deception. AV-2 does not break this chain. Audit breaks it only after several days. What remains exposed: commercial substance, pair independence and the wider population of joint payments. A genuinely independent releaser with separate workstation, rotation and receipt verification would likely reduce the risk materially; merely replacing the user ID while preserving the same pair, workstation and checking practice would not.
- **AP-205.** Most plausible loss mechanism: duplicate invoice payment to an established vendor. AV-2 breaks the chain before release. What remains exposed: the reason for submission and possible repeat attempts. An independent releaser would add a second layer, but for this payment AV-2 already prevented completion, so the incremental loss reduction is limited.
- **AP-206.** Most plausible loss mechanism: urgent outage repair paid quickly with an incorrect amount, bank detail or unsupported service. AV-2 and the pre-release manager break the single-actor and amount/bank chains. What remains exposed: formal ED-1 token timing and timestamp reliability. A truly independent second releaser would change little substantively because m11 already acted before release; the main benefit would be cleaner formal compliance.
- **AP-208.** Most plausible loss mechanism: bank diversion during an AV-2 outage, with a bank change three days earlier and no reliable ownership evidence. No control breaks the chain before settlement. What remains exposed: bank ownership, amount limit, vendor onboarding and recovery. A truly independent second releaser would likely demand bank-ownership evidence and could stop the payment, giving a material risk reduction, although a releaser who merely accepted an unsigned checklist could still fail.
- **AP-209.** Most plausible loss mechanism: the same pair-based fabrication risk seen in AP-204, but no defect was found in this payment. AV-2 and timely audit partially break the chain, and audit was inside the recall window. What remains exposed: collusion resistance and portfolio risk from repeated pairing. A rotating, demonstrably independent releaser would increase the cost of collusion and reduce pattern risk moderately; it would not eliminate deception by fabricated documents.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Monitoring should produce alerts for inquiry, not automatic findings of misconduct. A repeated pair may be a designed payment cell; a shared workstation may be a pooled terminal; a late manager approval may reflect time-zone or settlement-cutoff issues; a bank change may be legitimate after callback verification.

| Metric or evidence rule | Suggested alert | Required follow-up | Alert versus proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-user rate | Any completed same-user payment outside ED-1; rising rate in a team; same user repeatedly using emergency route | Token log, amount, vendor tenure, bank-change status, manager timing, business justification | A high rate proves process deviation, not fraud |
| Override rate | Any AV-2 warning override; override on bank change, sanctions or operator limit; override using another person’s credential | Override credential owner, session/IP/device, approver identity, bank evidence, vendor-master change history | Override is a control failure signal; credential sharing needs session evidence |
| Late manager approval | Manager after settlement; manager more than a short defined period after release; manager before settlement but after release for non-emergency payments | Settlement timestamp, review artefacts, documents viewed, manager independence and level | Late approval weakens prevention; it does not prove the manager did not review |
| Audit before recall window | Emergency same-user payments not audited within 24 hours; audit start after settlement plus recall expiry | Audit start/completion times, recall attempts, settlement status, escalation record | Late audit proves loss of corrective capacity, not that the payment was wrong |
| Repeated resource pairs | Pair concentration, for example the same preparer-releaser pair exceeding a defined share of team payments or a defined count in 30 days | Organisational design, rotation, peer comparison, joint-payment outcomes, independent check artefacts | Repetition is a collusion-risk alert, not proof of collusion |
| Shared workstations | Same device, session or room for preparer and releaser; sequential logins without break; shared credentials | Device/session logs, badge or login separation, terminal allocation policy | Shared facilities may be legitimate; shared identity is the serious issue |
| Bank-change proximity | Bank change within seven days; new vendor under 180 days; bank change plus AV warning or outage | Callback to known contact, bank-ownership evidence, vendor-master dual approval, sanctions and duplicate checks | Recent bank change is a high-risk alert, not proof of diversion |
| Control outages | Any release while AV-2 is unavailable, degraded or in warning-only mode; unsigned manual checklist | Outage ticket, fallback procedure, incident approval, manual evidence, post-outage reconciliation | Outage releases should be treated as unvalidated unless equivalent evidence exists |
| Emergency token governance | Token created after preparation; token reused; token without outage or safety justification; amount near limit | Token issuance log, clock synchronisation, emergency ticket, retrospective approval | Late token may be logging delay or permission failure; logs decide |

Evidence to collect and retain: immutable event log with UTC timestamps; AV-2 rule version, inputs and outputs; override session and credential issuance; emergency token creation and approval; manager identity, level, conflict check and documents viewed; receipt/service evidence; vendor-master and bank-change history; settlement and recall logs; device, workstation and IP session data; organisational chart for payment cells; rotation records; audit findings and recovery outcomes. Each completed payment should have a control-objective memo linking the threats present to the control that addressed them before settlement.

    manager_time = manager_rows.min() if len(manager_rows) else pd.NaT

    activities = set(grp["concept:name"])
    if "Automated_Validate" in activities:
        av_coverage = "FULL_PASS"
    elif "Automated_Validate_Warning" in activities:
        av_coverage = "WARNING"
    elif "Automated_Validate_Block" in activities:
        av_coverage = "BLOCKED"
    elif "Validation_Service_Unavailable" in activities:
        av_coverage = "UNAVAILABLE"
    else:
        av_coverage = "NONE_IN_LOG"

    if pd.isna(manager_time):
        manager_timing_log = "NO_MANAGER"
    elif not pd.isna(release_time) and manager_time < release_time:
        manager_timing_log = "BEFORE_RELEASE"
    elif not pd.isna(settlement_time) and manager_time < settlement_time:
        manager_timing_log = "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT"
    elif not pd.isna(settlement_time) and manager_time >= settlement_time:
        manager_timing_log = "AFTER_SETTLEMENT"
    else:
        manager_timing_log = "NO_SETTLEMENT_IN_LOG_AFTER_MANAGER_OR_RELEASE"

    audit_hours = None
    if not pd.isna(audit_time) and not pd.isna(release_time):
        audit_hours = (audit_time - release_time).total_seconds() / 3600.0

    prepare_resource = first_resource("Prepare_Payment")
    release_resource = first_resource("Release_Payment")
    pair = f"{prepare_resource}->{release_resource}" if prepare_resource and release_resource else None

    summaries.append(
        {
            "case_id": case_id,
            "prepare_resource": prepare_resource,
            "release_resource": release_resource,
            "pair": pair,
            "av_coverage": av_coverage,
            "manager_timing_log": manager_timing_log,
            "audit_hours_after_release": audit_hours,
        }
    )

summary_df = pd.DataFrame(summaries)
pair_counts = summary_df.dropna(subset=["pair"]).groupby("pair").size().rename("pair_count_completed")
summary_df = summary_df.merge(pair_counts, left_on="pair", right_index=True, how="left")
summary_df["pair_count_completed"] = summary_df["pair_count_completed"].fillna(0).astype(int)

merged = case_facts.merge(summary_df, on="case_id", how="left")
merged["nominal_same_user_flag"] = merged["prepare_resource"] == merged["release_resource"]
merged["av_full_pass"] = merged["av_coverage"] == "FULL_PASS"
merged["audit_within_24h_strict"] = merged["audit_hours_after_release"].notna() & (merged["audit_hours_after_release"] <= 24)
merged["repeated_pair_flag"] = merged["pair_count_completed"] >= 2
merged["emergency_eligibility_prelim"] = (
    merged["emergency_token_before_prepare"]
    & (merged["amount_eur"] < 100000)
    & merged["vendor_active_at_least_180d"]
    & (~merged["bank_change_within_7d"])
    & merged["av_full_pass"]
    & merged["manager_before_settlement_from_facts"]
    & merged["audit_within_24h_strict"]
)

print("EVIDENCE FOR JUDGMENT - NOT FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT")
print("Nominal violating completed cases from PM4Py filter:", violating_cases)
print("Attempted or blocked but not completed:", attempted_blocked_not_completed)
print(
    merged[
        [
            "case_id",
            "amount_eur",
            "nominal_same_user_flag",
            "pair",

            "repeated_pair_flag",
            "av_coverage",
            "manager_timing_log",
            "audit_hours_after_release",
            "audit_within_24h_strict",
            "bank_change_within_7d",
            "emergency_eligibility_prelim",
        ]
    ].to_string(index=False)
)

PM4Py behaviour that cannot be guaranteed across versions: `format_dataframe` expects an EventLog-compatible dataframe and the column names supplied; `filter_four_eyes_principle` is documented to return cases violating the principle for the two activities, but the exact return type, handling of cases missing one activity, and conversion back to a dataframe can differ between PM4Py 2.x and later releases. The code therefore treats AP-205 separately by searching for block and attempt events without a completed release, uses a strict 24-hour audit proxy rather than a business-day calendar, and labels all flags as preliminary evidence.
