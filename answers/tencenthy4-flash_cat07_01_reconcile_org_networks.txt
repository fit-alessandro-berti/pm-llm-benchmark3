# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Overall, the evidence is most consistent with Mira_Coordinator being a **selective exception-routing and coordination node**, not a conventional line manager and not merely “unimportant.” The apparent contradiction comes from the fact that the networks measure different things.

  - **What it implies:** Mira sits on many directed transitions: Bot_Rules  Mira, Mira  specialists/adjusters, specialists/temps  Mira, and team-lead payment-release paths involving Vendor_Pay. Her handover out-degree, in-degree, and especially betweenness are very high. This means she is frequently a transfer point between automation, exception handling, specialists, adjusters, and temporary staff.
  - **What it does not imply:** High handover centrality does not mean she performs most of the substantive work, has formal authority, or is necessarily a bottleneck in all cases. # 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. The evidence may support a hybrid interpretation.

- **Hypothesis 1: Formal coordinator / exception desk**
  - **Mechanism:** Bot_Rules routes flagged cases to Mira as an exception desk. She clears missing-information queues, reopens cases, escalates complaints, and assigns hard cases to specialists or adjusters.
  - **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Rules  Mira is the largest non-routine automation handover at 920 events. Mira touches 22% of all claims but 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints. Her activity profile is exception-oriented. During her absence, exception queues accumulated while ordinary claims were unaffected.   - **Limiting evidence:** She does not show the same working-together pattern as formal team leads. Her exception share is high, 48%, but specialists have even higher exception shares. The log does not prove a formal job description or explicit authority.
  - **Future-log predictions:** Mira events should concentrate in cases with exception_flag=True, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, or Fraud Screen updates. Queue-entry and queue-exit timestamps should show backlog before her actions. Non-exception cases should mostly bypass her.

- **Hypothesis 2: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency**
  - **Mechanism:** Mira has tacit knowledge and discretionary control over how hard cases are routed. Many exception cases effectively wait for her judgment, even though her individual handling time is short.

  - **Future-log predictions:** Waiting time before Mira’s assignment events should be high and volatile. Cases awaiting her routing should age faster than cases awaiting comparable team-lead actions. Absences or high exception volume should produce queue spikes that recover after she returns. - **Hypothesis 3: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker**
  - **Mechanism:** Mira bridges structural holes between automation, Team North, Team South, specialists, temporary staff, and external vendors. She does not co-work continuously with one team; she translates exceptions into the next action across boundaries.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk:** The risk is concentrated in exception handling, not routine claims. Mira’s absence did not change ordinary low-value claims, but p90 exception cycle time rose sharply. That pattern suggests a tail-risk bottleneck: most cases flow normally, while difficult cases wait for a scarce coordination point.

- **Coordination risk:** Mira often appears after Bot_Rules and then disappears from the case for several events. That can be efficient specialization, but it can also create context loss. Low working-together rates mean she is not embedded in the sustained production pairs that carry routine claims. If assignment rationale is not logged, downstream workers may receive work without enough context.

- **Key-person risk:** The combination of high betweenness, high subcontracting centrality, and subjective statements about knowing where hard cases should go indicates dependency on individual knowledge. If Mira is absent, leaves, or is overloaded, exception routing may degrade. The risk is not merely workload volume; it is undistributed decision capability.

- **Compliance/control risk:** In a regulated claims process, detecting a problem and selecting the next specialist can create a segregation-of-duties concern. This is a control-design risk, not evidence of wrongdoing. The log shows concentration of discretion; it does not show abuse. Auditability would improve with decision reasons, routing rules, approvals for high-risk cases, and periodic review.

- **Workload fairness risk:** Mira’s work may be undercounted because short routing events do not look like substantive collaboration. Specialists have high service times and high exception shares; Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay contribute waiting-time components; temporary staff have low co-work and limited routing centrality. Removing or bypassing Mira could shift hidden coordination burden onto adjusters, specialists, or temps.

- **Data-quality risk:** Network conclusions depend on activity granularity, lifecycle semantics, timestamp precision,_case attributes, and inclusion of bots/vendors. Co-case rates are denominator-sensitive. Normalized network statistics are relative measures. The absence period should be checked against seasonality, staffing levels, case mix, priority, amount band, region, and channel before being interpreted causally.
