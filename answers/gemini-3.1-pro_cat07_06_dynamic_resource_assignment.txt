# 1. Objectives and constraints

**Objectives:**
- **SLA & Throughput:** Meet strict deadlines (especially for urgent/high-priority cases) while maintaining overall system throughput.
- **Fairness & Wellbeing:** Prevent burnout by actively managing workload and protecting resources with high overtime risk.
- **Skill Development:** Continuously build team resilience by hitting the 20% training target for normal/low priority work.
- **Continuity:** Minimize handover costs and errors by keeping high-complexity cases with their current owners when safe.

**Hard Constraints (Safety/Compliance):**
1. Maximum 4 new assignments per resource per batch.
2. High-priority cases cannot be assigned to skill < 2 unless explicitly supervised.
3. Security Review must be skill 3, or skill 1-2 explicitly supervised by R7 or R8. (Skill 0 is strictly forbidden).
4. Final Approval must be skill 2-3 to finalize (skill 1 can only prepare).

**Soft Constraints (Preference Trade-offs):**
1. Target >= 20% of normal/low-priority cases for training.
2. Avoid assigning more work to `overtime_risk="high"` resources unless SLA is urgent and no alternative exists.
3. Prefer continuity for high-complexity/high-handover cases unless compliance rules force a handover.
4. Batch Quality Checks for normal/low priority if SLA > 24h.

# 2. Assignment policy

The proposed Explainable Dynamic Policy operates in sequential scoring passes:

1. **Eligibility Filter:**
   - Filter out resources with 4 new assignments in the current batch.
   - Enforce hard compliance rules (Rule 6 for Security, Rule 7 for Approval, Rule 2 for High Priority). Note: If a resource has skill 0 for a regulated task, they are excluded even if notes suggest them as a trainee.

2. **Urgency & SLA Scoring:**
   - Base score = `(1 / SLA_time_remaining) * priority_multiplier` (Urgent = 10, High = 5, Normal = 2, Low = 1).

3. **Overtime Risk Penalty:**
   - If a resource has `overtime_risk == "high"`, apply a massive negative penalty score. This penalty can only be overridden if the case SLA is urgent and *no* other eligible resource is available.

4. **Continuity & Handover Scoring:**
   - If `current_owner` is eligible, grant a strong bonus, scaled by `case_complexity` and `reassignment_count`.
   - Penalize handovers that push `reassignment_count` > 2 (historical risk of 1.8x rework).

5. **Training Bonus:**
   - If case priority is normal/low, grant a bonus to eligible resources where `required_skill` matches their `training_priority`. 

6. **Batching Execution:**
   - Pull Quality Check cases with normal/low priority and SLA > 24h into a separate batch queue, assigning them identically to the lowest-utilized eligible resource (e.g., skill >= 1, low OT risk) for grouped execution.

7. **Tie-breaking:**
   - Lowest `current_open_cases`, followed by highest `hours_remaining_today`.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

- **Expected throughput:** *First-available* routing creates a short-term spike but ultimately drags down throughput due to massive rework rates (1.8x) and skill mismatches. *Specialist-only* routing throttles throughput by creating artificial bottlenecks at Security and Approval. The *Proposed Policy* balances throughput by batching low-priority tasks and reassigning cases away from fatigued workers.
- **SLA risk:** The proposed policy directly penalizes SLA risk, whereas *specialist-only* causes SLA misses on standard cases stuck in queue, and *first-available* fails urgent complex cases through poor execution quality.
- **Error/rework risk:** Significantly lower in the proposed policy than *first-available*, as it limits `reassignment_count` (which drives a 1.4x appeal rate) and enforces skill floors for high-priority items.
- **Fairness of workload:** *First-available* treats all humans as identical machines, while *specialist-only* punishes the most competent workers. The proposed policy explicitly incorporates the `overtime_risk` flag to dynamically shed load from burned-out employees (e.g., reassigning David and Iris's work).
- **Skill development:** *Specialist-only* guarantees 0% skill development. The proposed policy mathematically guarantees >= 20% of safe volume is routed toward documented training goals.
- **Continuity of case ownership:** *First-available* destroys continuity, adding a median 2.7h delay per complex case. The proposed policy protects continuity using handover-cost scoring, breaking it only for strict compliance or severe overtime risks.
- **Resilience to absence/bottlenecks:** By consistently training resources on normal/low priority tasks, the proposed policy actively widens the skill pool, unlike *specialist-only* which creates single points of failure (a bus factor of 1).

"Our goal is to ensure you have a manageable workload while delivering the best service to our customers. This assignment policy looks at a few things: First, it prioritizes keeping you with the cases you already know well, especially complex ones, so you don't have to start from scratch. Second, it monitors how much you have on your plate today to protect you from unexpected overtime; if you're overloaded, we'll route new standard cases to teammates who have more capacity. Finally, it ensures we are consistently matching cases to the right skill levels while carving out safe, low-stress opportunities for everyone to train on new skills. Assignments aren't just about who is free right now, but about setting the whole team up for success without burning anyone out."

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

To ensure the policy acts as intended, we will audit the event log for the following indicators:
- **Handover frequency:** Edges in the handover-of-work network; median handovers per case.
- **Reassignment count:** The percentage of cases reaching >2 reassignments (which historically drives a 1.8x rework rate).
- **Skill mismatch:** Percentage of urgent/high-priority cases executed by resources with a skill level < 2 without a documented supervisor.
- **Workload distribution:** Variance in `current_open_cases` and working hours across the organization.
- **Overtime risk exposure:** Volume of new case assignments given to resources while their status was flagged as `overtime_risk="high"`.
- **SLA misses:** Percentage of cases where `service_time + waiting_time` exceeds `sla_deadline`.
- **Rework/appeal after assignment:** Frequency of `Rework Correction` activities immediately following a handover event.
- **Training assignment share:** Percentage of normal/low-priority cases completed by resources where the activity matched their `training_priority`.
- **Specialist bottleneck indicators:** Average waiting time specifically concentrated in `Security Review` and `Final Approval` activities.
- **Deviations from assignment policy:** Explicit instances where hard constraints were breached (e.g., Skill 0 executing Security Review).

import pm4py
import pandas as pd

def audit_assignment_policy(log_df):
    # 1. Format the dataframe for PM4Py
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(log_df, case_id='case:concept:name', 
                                 activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
    
    # 2. Audit handovers and reassignment counts
    hw_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(hw_network, "handover_network.png")
    
    # 3. Discover bottlenecks via Performance DFG
    perf_dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    
    # 4. Calculate service and waiting times to identify SLA misses
    enriched_log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
    
    # 5. Identify rework/appeal occurrences using attribute filtering
    rework_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log, "concept:name", ["Rework Correction"], level="case", retain=True
    )
    
    # 6. Check for SLA deviations via performance filtering
    # Example: Filter cases taking longer than 24 hours (86400 seconds)
    sla_missed_log = pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, min_performance=86400)
    
    # 7. Audit batching behavior (e.g., for Quality Check constraints)
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)
    
    # 8. Discover roles/resource similarity to ensure specialists aren't siloed
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
    
    return {
        "handover_network": hw_network,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "rework_cases": len(rework_log),
        "batches_detected": batches,
        "roles": roles
    }
