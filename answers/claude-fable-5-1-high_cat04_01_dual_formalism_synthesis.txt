# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

Abbreviations used throughout (full labels are used in all code):

RD = Register Deviation, CM = Contain Material, ARC = Analyze Root Cause, PV = Plan Verification, DCA = Design Corrective Action, SR = Supervisor Review, QR = Quality Review, RR = Regulatory Review, CS = Consult Supplier, ET = Execute Trial, IT = Inspect Trial, AP = Adjust Parameters, RM = Release Material, AC = Archive Case.

1. **Completion-time semantics.** Each event marks the completion of an activity. "X may start only after Y is complete" is therefore modeled as the ordering constraint "Y occurs before X in the trace". Starts are not observable, so "overlap" cannot be observed either (see item 6).
2. **Low-risk / high-risk choice.** The risk level is case data, not an event. It is represented as an exclusive choice `XOR(SR, AND(QR, RR))` whose selection is left to the environment. Exactly one of the two branches is executed in every case; a trace containing SR together with QR or RR is forbidden, as is a trace containing only one of QR/RR. The choice is placed so that it can only be resolved after ARC (D5 below); in the process tree it is additionally delayed until CM has completed (approximation, Section 3).
3. **Optional supplier consultation.** Represented as `XOR(CS, tau)`: a silent transition skips it. At most one CS per case follows from the fact that the choice is executed once and contains no loop. The data condition deciding the choice is not modeled.
4. **Passing inspection.** The inspection result is not a label. A *failing* inspection is represented by the decision to execute the redo part of a loop (AP followed by another ET–IT cycle); a *passing* inspection is represented by the silent loop exit. The loop body is `ET → IT`, the redo part is `AP`. Consequently the last IT before RM is by construction the passing one, and AP can never follow it. The number of iterations is unbounded.
5. **Duplicate visible labels.** Not permitted in either model. Each of the fourteen labels appears exactly once. This keeps alignments, token replay and any model-to-log projection unambiguous; a duplication-based approximation was considered and rejected (Section 3, paragraph on approximation).
6. **"May overlap" = absence of an order constraint.** Both PM4Py process trees and POWL have interleaving semantics: `AND` / an unconnected pair in a partial order means "any interleaving of the completion events", not true simultaneity. Because only completion events exist, this is the strongest statement about overlap that a completion-only model can make. Every "may overlap" in the narrative is therefore realized as "no edge / no sequence between the two submodels".
7. **Approximation policy for the process tree: conservative under-approximation.** The required dependency structure contains an N-shaped sub-order (CM→PV→ET, ARC→DCA→ET, ARC→Approval→RM, with CM and Approval unrelated) which is not series-parallel and therefore has no exact block-structured (process-tree) representation. The tree adds a single synchronization point after CM and ARC (both initial work streams must be complete before PV, DCA, the approval route or CS may occur). Every trace accepted by the tree is intended; some intended traces (e.g., PV completing before ARC) are rejected. Nothing unsafe is admitted.
8. **POWL is exact** with respect to the intended completion-event language as specified in Section 2. Cross-branch dependencies are expressed directly as partial-order edges.
9. **Release synchronization.** RM occurs only after the trial loop has exited (passing IT), the approval route has completed, and the CS-or-skip choice has completed. AC follows RM. Nothing may occur after AC and nothing may precede RD.
10. **No other activities** (no rework of RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, approvals, or a second CS) are permitted; each non-loop activity occurs exactly once (CS, SR, QR, RR at most once, governed by the choices).
11. **Transitive edges** in the POWL model are implied (e.g., RD before RM) and are not added explicitly; the transitive reduction is stated. `powl.order.add_transitive_edges()` can be called to obtain the closure for checking.

# 2. Intended Dependency Specification

| ID | Source activity or submodel | Target activity or submodel | Relation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | RD | every other activity | precedence (RD is unique start) | Req. 1: every case starts with RD |
| D2 | RD | CM, ARC | precedence; CM and ARC mutually concurrent | Req. 2: the two work streams start after registration and may overlap |
| D3 | CM | PV | precedence (only this dependency for PV) | Req. 3: PV waits for containment, not for ARC |
| D4 | ARC | DCA | precedence (only this dependency for DCA) | Req. 4: DCA waits for root cause, not for CM |
| D5 | ARC | Approval = XOR(SR, AND(QR, RR)) | precedence + exclusive choice | Req. 5: route selected after ARC; low risk = SR, high risk = QR and RR |
| D6 | QR | RR | concurrency (no order) | Req. 5: either completion order in high-risk cases |
| D7 | ARC | Supplier = XOR(CS, tau) | precedence + optionality (at most once) | Req. 6: CS may start only after ARC, is optional, occurs at most once |
| D8 | Supplier | PV, DCA, Approval, Trial loop | concurrency (no order) | Req. 6: CS may overlap design, planning, approvals and trials |
| D9 | PV and DCA | Trial loop (first ET) | synchronization (AND-join) | Req. 7: trial needs both work products |
| D10 | Approval | Trial loop | concurrency (no order) | Req. 7: approvals may overlap the trial cycle |
| D11 | ET | IT | precedence inside the loop body | Req. 8: a cycle is ET followed by IT |
| D12 | IT (failed) | AP → ET → IT | loop (repeat-until) | Req. 8: failed inspection triggers AP and a new cycle |
| D13 | IT (passed) | loop exit | silent exit; AP never after the final IT | Req. 8: repetition stops at a passing inspection |
| D14 | Trial loop, Approval, Supplier | RM | synchronization (AND-join of three independent obligations) | Req. 9: release requires passing inspection, completed approval route, and CS if selected |
| D15 | RM | AC | precedence; AC is unique end | Req. 1, 10 |
| D16 | Approval, Supplier | CM | *no* dependency intended | Req. 5–6: approvals and CS depend on ARC only (this is exactly what the tree cannot express) |

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

Compact expression (PM4Py string syntax: `->` sequence, `+` parallel, `X` exclusive choice, `*` loop with children (do, redo), `tau` silent):

```text
-> ( 'Register Deviation',
     + ( 'Contain Material', 'Analyze Root Cause' ),
     + ( -> ( + ( 'Plan Verification', 'Design Corrective Action' ),
              * ( -> ( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ) ),
         X ( 'Supervisor Review', + ( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ),
         X ( 'Consult Supplier', tau ) ),
     'Release Material',
     'Archive Case' )
```

Mapping to the narrative: the outer sequence enforces start with RD and end with RM → AC (D1, D14, D15). The first `+` block is the two initial work streams (D2). The second `+` block contains three concurrent obligations that are joined at RM: (a) the work stream `PV || DCA` followed by the trial loop (D9, D11–D13), (b) the risk route (D5, D6), and (c) the optional supplier consultation (D7, D8). Because the second block starts only after the first block completes, the tree realizes D3, D4, D5, D7 (all targets do occur after their sources) but *also* imposes the non-intended orders discussed below.

Self-contained construction code with explicit parent/child handling (every child is appended to `parent.children` and its `parent` attribute is set):

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def add_leaf(label, parent):
    node = ProcessTree(label=label, parent=parent)
    parent.children.append(node)
    return node


def add_operator(operator, parent):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, parent=parent)
    if parent is not None:
        parent.children.append(node)
    return node


def add_tau(parent):
    node = ProcessTree(parent=parent)          # label=None, operator=None -> silent step
    parent.children.append(node)
    return node


tree = add_operator(Operator.SEQUENCE, None)                       # root

add_leaf("Register Deviation", tree)

initial = add_operator(Operator.PARALLEL, tree)                    # CM || ARC
add_leaf("Contain Material", initial)
add_leaf("Analyze Root Cause", initial)

core = add_operator(Operator.PARALLEL, tree)                       # three obligations joined at release

work = add_operator(Operator.SEQUENCE, core)                       # (PV || DCA) -> trial loop
prep = add_operator(Operator.PARALLEL, work)
add_leaf("Plan Verification", prep)
add_leaf("Design Corrective Action", prep)
trial_loop = add_operator(Operator.LOOP, work)                     # *(do, redo)
cycle = add_operator(Operator.SEQUENCE, trial_loop)               # do: ET -> IT
add_leaf("Execute Trial", cycle)
add_leaf("Inspect Trial", cycle)
add_leaf("Adjust Parameters", trial_loop)                          # redo: AP

approval = add_operator(Operator.XOR, core)                       # SR  xor  (QR || RR)
add_leaf("Supervisor Review", approval)
high_risk = add_operator(Operator.PARALLEL, approval)
add_leaf("Quality Review", high_risk)
add_leaf("Regulatory Review", high_risk)

supplier = add_operator(Operator.XOR, core)                       # CS  xor  tau
add_leaf("Consult Supplier", supplier)
add_tau(supplier)

add_leaf("Release Material", tree)
add_leaf("Archive Case", tree)

# Equivalent one-liner (same tree, same string representation):

#     "X ( 'Supervisor Review', + ( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ), "
#     "X ( 'Consult Supplier', tau ) ), 'Release Material', 'Archive Case' )")
```

Loop semantics used: PM4Py's `*(do, redo)` executes `do`, then zero or more times `redo` followed by `do`, and exits only after a `do`. Hence every case has the shape `ET IT (AP ET IT)*` (interleaved with concurrent approvals/CS), and AP can never be the last loop activity (D13).

**What the tree cannot represent exactly.** The intended order is not series-parallel: CM→PV→ET, ARC→DCA→ET, ARC→Approval→RM with CM and Approval unrelated form an N-shape, so no block-structured tree exists for D3, D4, D5, D7, D9, D10 and D16 simultaneously. The tree resolves this with a conservative under-approximation: it inserts a synchronization point after `CM || ARC`, which adds four non-intended orderings — ARC before PV, CM before DCA, CM before the approval route, CM before CS. Concrete intended traces the tree rejects: `RD CM PV ARC DCA SR ET IT RM AC` (PV completes while root-cause analysis is still running), `RD ARC DCA CM PV SR ET IT RM AC` (design finished before containment), and `RD ARC SR CM PV DCA ET IT RM AC` (supervisor approves before containment is recorded). An exhaustive playout bounded to length 15 (which covers all traces with at most two trial iterations) yields 4,588 tree variants versus 7,896 POWL variants; the tree language is a strict subset, and every one of the 3,308 missing traces violates at least one of the four added orderings, none violates anything else — so the loss is exactly the described gate and nothing more. Rejected alternatives: (i) placing the approval and supplier choices in the ARC branch (`+( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC, +(DCA, Approval, Supplier)) )`) would force approvals and CS to finish before the first ET, contradicting D8 and D10; (ii) placing them next to the trial loop after `+( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC,DCA) )` would forbid approvals during design/planning (adds PV/DCA before Approval); (iii) a permissive variant `+( ->( +( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC,DCA) ), loop ), Approval, Supplier )` preserves every intended trace but admits SR/QR/RR/CS before ARC — an approval of an unknown root cause — which was judged unsafe; (iv) duplicating labels (e.g., two copies of the approval subtree in different positions) cannot remove the N-shape because the join at ET and the join at RM would still need to be block-nested, and it would destroy label uniqueness for conformance checking. Everything else — D1, D2, D6, D9, D11–D15, exclusivity of the risk route, at-most-once CS, no AP after the passing inspection — is represented exactly.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

Compact description (nodes of the root partial order and its transitive reduction; unlisted pairs are unordered, i.e., concurrent):

```text
Root = PO(nodes = { RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, Approval, Supplier, Trial, RM, AC },
          order = { RD->CM, RD->ARC,
                    CM->PV, ARC->DCA, ARC->Approval, ARC->Supplier,
                    PV->Trial, DCA->Trial,
                    Trial->RM, Approval->RM, Supplier->RM,
                    RM->AC })

Approval = XOR( SR, HighRisk )
HighRisk = PO(nodes = { QR, RR }, order = {} )            # concurrent reviews
Supplier = XOR( CS, tau )                                 # optional, at most once
Trial    = LOOP( do = Cycle, redo = AP )                  # ET IT (AP ET IT)*
Cycle    = PO(nodes = { ET, IT }, order = { ET->IT } )
```

Self-contained construction code:

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)

# visible activities (each label exactly once)
register   = Transition(label="Register Deviation")
contain    = Transition(label="Contain Material")
analyze    = Transition(label="Analyze Root Cause")
plan       = Transition(label="Plan Verification")
design     = Transition(label="Design Corrective Action")
supervisor = Transition(label="Supervisor Review")
quality    = Transition(label="Quality Review")
regulatory = Transition(label="Regulatory Review")
supplier   = Transition(label="Consult Supplier")
execute    = Transition(label="Execute Trial")
inspect    = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
adjust     = Transition(label="Adjust Parameters")
release    = Transition(label="Release Material")
archive    = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# risk route: SR  xor  (QR || RR)
high_risk = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality, regulatory])      # no edge -> concurrent
approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supervisor, high_risk])

# optional supplier consultation: CS  xor  tau
skip_supplier = SilentTransition()
supplier_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supplier, skip_supplier])

# trial cycle with repeat-until: do = (ET -> IT), redo = AP
cycle = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[execute, inspect])
cycle.order.add_edge(execute, inspect)
trial_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[cycle, adjust])

# root partial order
powl = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[
    register, contain, analyze, plan, design,
    approval, supplier_opt, trial_loop, release, archive,
])
powl.order.add_edge(register, contain)        # D1/D2
powl.order.add_edge(register, analyze)        # D1/D2
powl.order.add_edge(contain, plan)            # D3
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, design)          # D4
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, approval)        # D5
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, supplier_opt)    # D7
powl.order.add_edge(plan, trial_loop)         # D9
powl.order.add_edge(design, trial_loop)       # D9
powl.order.add_edge(trial_loop, release)      # D14
powl.order.add_edge(approval, release)        # D14
powl.order.add_edge(supplier_opt, release)    # D14
powl.order.add_edge(release, archive)         # D15
```

Semantics of an edge `a -> b`: the entire behavior of submodel `a` completes before any activity of submodel `b` starts. The twelve edges are the transitive reduction; RD before everything and everything before AC follow transitively.

Why every intentionally unconnected pair may remain concurrent:

- **CM vs ARC** (D2): independent initial work streams; the narrative says they may overlap.
- **PV vs ARC, PV vs DCA** (D3, D4): PV depends on containment only; DCA depends on root cause only. This is the N-shape the tree could not express; here it is just the absence of two edges.
- **DCA vs CM**: same reason, symmetric case.
- **Approval vs CM, PV, DCA, Trial** (D5, D10, D16): the route is selected after ARC and may run in parallel with design, planning and the whole trial cycle; it is only synchronized at RM.
- **QR vs RR** (D6): either completion order is legal.
- **Supplier vs CM, PV, DCA, Approval, Trial** (D7, D8): CS may overlap all of them; it is only bounded by ARC (before) and RM (after). The silent alternative makes the node vanish from the trace without affecting any other node.
- **Trial vs Approval / Supplier**: the loop, including any number of AP iterations, may be interleaved with approval activities and CS (as in witness T2: `ET QR IT RR`). RM waits for all three.

No further edges are needed; adding any would exclude intended behavior, and removing any of the twelve would admit a trace violating Section 2.

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

**Canonical representation: the POWL model.** It is exact for every relation in Section 2, expresses the N-shaped cross-branch dependencies as plain edges instead of an artificial gate, has no duplicate labels, and converts to a sound workflow net of 20 places and 18 transitions. The process tree is kept as a documented, conservative companion (useful where a tool requires a tree, e.g., for tree-based alignments or inductive-miner comparison), with the explicit caveat that it rejects intended early-planning/early-approval behavior; its role is "safe subset", never "reference".

Validation plan (all steps executable with the code above; the numbers in parentheses are what the steps produced when run on PM4Py 2.7.x):

1. **Structural checks on the POWL object.** `powl.order.is_irreflexive()` must hold; then `powl.order.add_transitive_edges()` followed by `powl.order.is_strict_partial_order()` must return True (acyclicity). Verify each nested `StrictPartialOrder` the same way and that every `OperatorPOWL` with `Operator.LOOP` has exactly two children in (do, redo) order.
2. **Conversion to accepting Petri nets.** `net_t, im_t, fm_t = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)` and `net_p, im_p, fm_p = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(powl)`. Inspect (not just accept) the result: count places/transitions (tree: 22/19; POWL: 20/18), confirm exactly 14 labeled transitions and no repeated label in either net, and count silent transitions (tree 5, POWL 4).
3. **Soundness.** `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)` (deprecated in 2.7 but functional; equivalent call: `from pm4py.algo.analysis.woflan import algorithm as woflan; woflan.apply(net, im, fm)`). Both nets must be sound (both were). Soundness rules out deadlocks and dead transitions but says nothing about fidelity — it is a prerequisite, not evidence of correctness.
4. **Bounded exhaustive playout and language comparison.** `from pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.petri_net import algorithm as po; po.apply(net, im, fm, variant=po.Variants.EXTENSIVE, parameters={"maxTraceLength": 15})` for both nets. Length 15 is exactly the longest trace with two trial iterations (high risk + CS), so this covers all traces with ≤ 2 iterations. Check (a) tree language ⊆ POWL language (true: 4,588 ⊆ 7,896), and (b) that every trace in the difference violates one of the four documented added orderings and nothing else (true for all 3,308). Sampled playout (`pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={"noTraces": 1000})`) is additionally used to exercise ≥ 3 iterations.
5. **Invariant checks over the playout languages** (POWL, and tree as sanity): every trace starts with RD and ends with AC; ET occurs only after both PV and DCA; SR is present iff neither QR nor RR is present, and QR and RR are present together; ET and IT alternate starting with ET and ending with IT; AP is never the activity immediately before RM; CS occurs at most once and, when present, after ARC and before RM; RM occurs after the last IT, after the last approval activity and after CS. All held for the POWL language; the tree language additionally satisfies the four non-intended orderings, which is the expected signature of its approximation.
6. **Explicit positive and negative trace tests.** Build one `Trace` per witness of Section 5 and run `pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net.algorithm.apply_trace(trace, net, im, fm)`; assert `fitness == 1.0` for T1–T4 on both nets, `fitness == 1.0` on POWL and `< 1.0` on the tree for T5, and `< 1.0` on both nets for N1–N3. Inspect the alignment of each negative trace to confirm the first model/log move is at the activity named as "earliest violation" (AP for N1, ET for N2, RM for N3). Beware of trace-order pitfalls when aligning a whole log (results are returned in the log's internal case order, not insertion order) — align per trace or carry case identifiers.
7. **Silent-behavior and duplicate-label checks.** (a) Assert `len({t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label}) == 14` and that the multiset of labels has no repeats. (b) For the loop exit: assert that no accepted playout trace has AP as the last loop activity and that the trace `... ET IT AP RM AC` (N1) is rejected — this specifically tests that the silent exit is reachable only after IT. (c) For the supplier skip: assert both `... ARC PV DCA SR ET IT RM AC` (no CS) and the same with one CS are accepted and the same with two CS occurrences is rejected. (d) Visualize the nets (`pm4py.view_petri_net`) and confirm every silent transition is either a loop entry/exit, an XOR skip or a pure routing split/join, not a hidden activity.
8. **Limitations.** Playout enumeration is bounded (length ≤ 15 exhaustively, longer only sampled), so properties for ≥ 3 iterations are checked statistically, not proven. Alignments prove membership only relative to the converted nets, so a conversion bug would be invisible to steps 4–7 unless caught by step 2's inspection. None of the checks can validate data-dependent correctness (a low-risk case wrongly taking the high-risk route is accepted by construction), nor can they observe true concurrency, because the models and any completion-only log are interleaving-based. Soundness and successful conversion are treated as necessary conditions, not as proof of fidelity; fidelity rests on Sections 2, 5 and 6.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **Semantics — the passing inspection is inferred, not modeled.** Both models encode "pass" as a silent loop exit. Any log that records the inspection result as an attribute (or as distinct labels such as "Inspect Trial – fail") cannot be checked against the models for the crucial property "AP occurs only after a failed inspection": a trace `ET IT(pass) AP ET IT(pass)` is accepted because the models cannot see the result. The abstraction is faithful to the stated activity-based scope but hides the one business rule that most needs auditing.
2. **Semantics — the tree's approximation policy trades one intended behavior for another without empirical weighting.** I chose to gate PV, DCA, approvals and CS behind both CM and ARC because containment is usually fast, so the lost traces are arguably rare in practice. That is an assumption about operational data; if verification planning routinely finishes before root-cause analysis, the alternative placement (gating approvals behind PV and DCA instead) would lose fewer real traces. The quantitative comparison (3,308 of 7,896 variants) counts variants, not case frequencies, and therefore overstates or understates the practical loss.
3. **Syntax/robustness — reliance on library details that may drift.** The construction assumes PM4Py's `*(do, redo)` two-child loop semantics and that `convert_to_petri_net` accepts a POWL whose order is given as a transitive reduction (verified on 2.7.x, where `pm4py.check_soundness` is already deprecated). Other versions may require `add_transitive_edges()` before conversion, expose a differently named soundness API, or change the number of silent transitions generated, which would alter the concrete counts quoted in Sections 3 and 7 even though the behavioral claims would remain unchanged.
