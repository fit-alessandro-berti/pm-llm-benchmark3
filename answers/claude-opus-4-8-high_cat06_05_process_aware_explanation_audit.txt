## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

The prediction time is `t0` = immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. At `t0` the only admissible prefix is `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`. Everything below is judged against that prefix.

| Item | Finding type | Severity | Why | Required remediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `total_case_duration_hours` (G1) | Temporal leakage (post-hoc target-adjacent) | critical | Computed after case closes; it is essentially the label restated as a feature (duration vs. a duration threshold). Not available at `t0` at all. | Remove from model. If a duration signal is wanted, replace with `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (prefix-only) as a partial, non-target proxy. |
| `appeal_seen_anywhere` (G2) | Temporal leakage + process impossibility at `t0` | critical | An appeal can occur only after a Partial/Deny decision, which is far downstream of `t0`. Knowing it at `t0` is impossible; the value is derived from the complete trace. | Remove from prefix model. Only usable in a post-decision re-prediction stage, not at `t0`. |
| `final_handler_team` (G3) | Mutable-lineage leakage (no availability timestamp) | high | Warehouse field populated at closure. It coincides with the `t0` team in ~65% of cases, but there is no historical availability timestamp, so the value used is the closure value, not the `t0` value. | Replace with `handler_team_at_t0` captured from the queue/assignment snapshot at `t0`. Do not use closure field. |
| `num_document_requests_total` (G4) | Temporal leakage (complete-trace count) | critical | Total document requests are only known when evidence cycles finish; at `t0` zero document cycles have occurred. | Remove. Optionally replace with `document_requests_before_t0` (which is 0 for the standard prefix and therefore carries no signal here). |
| `queue_length_at_t0` (G5) | Legitimate signal (with tolerance caveat) | low | Joined from a timestamped queue snapshot valid within ±5 min of `t0`. Genuinely a `t0` process-state/congestion signal. | Keep. Verify snapshot timestamp is within tolerance per case; flag joins outside ±5 min. |
| `initial_damage_complexity` (G6) | Legitimate signal | low | Entered at registration and frozen before `t0`. Fully admissible. | Keep. Present as predictive, not as an actionable cause. |
| `full_trace_variant_id` (G7) | Temporal leakage (whole-path identity) | critical | The variant id encodes the entire realized path (including DOC cycles, appeal, close). It is the trace outcome encoded as an input. | Remove. Replace with `prefix_variant_id` computed only from events at/before `t0`. |
| `calendar_week` (G8) | Drift/calendar proxy (temporally available) | medium | Known at `t0`, so temporally admissible, but strongly confounded with a policy change and holiday period; predictive but not causal and prone to drift. | Keep only as a drift/seasonality control; do not interpret as "bad weeks." | `claimant_region` (G9) | Proxy requiring fairness review (temporally available) | medium | Known at registration; admissible in time, but correlated with repair-network coverage, language, channel, socioeconomics. | `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (G10) | Legitimate signal | low | Derived only from past timestamps (registration to `t0`). A clean prefix-age feature. | Keep. Good "slack already consumed" signal. |
| `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (G11) | Repairable lineage defect | high | Intended to count earlier claims, but SQL uses `claim_created_at <= extraction_date` instead of `< t0`, so it can include claims created after `t0`. | Repair predicate to `claim_created_at < t0`; recompute before use. |
| `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (G12) | Repairable / temporal leakage (same-day contamination) | high | Refreshed nightly at 23:00; can include documents uploaded after `t0` on the same day. Also conceptually documents are a post-`t0` activity for this prefix. | Repair to count only documents with upload timestamp `< t0` (≈0 here). As currently built, remove. |
| Employee experience (lineage note, not in top-12) | Repairable lineage defect | high | Joined as of extraction date, not event date, so it reflects experience at extraction, not at `t0`. | Recompute as-of `t0`; then reassess importance. |
| Seq: `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` | Process impossibility / same-timestamp artifact | critical | Assessment cannot legitimately follow closure; this is an ordering artifact from identical timestamps and a missing sequence index. It is not a real behavioral pattern. | Exclude as a feature; fix ingestion ordering with a reliable sequence index; treat as data-quality flag, not signal. |
| Seq: `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` | Temporal leakage but semantically legitimate | high | Semantically valid (appeal legitimately triggers a second decision), but both events are far after `t0`; using it at `t0` is leakage of the future. | Remove from the `t0` model. Valid only for a post-appeal re-scoring model. Do NOT treat like the impossible closed→assessment pattern. |
| Seq: `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` | Temporal leakage (post-`t0` behavior) | high | Describes evidence-cycle behavior that has not happened at `t0`. | Seq: `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` | Future-possible, not enabled at `t0` | medium | At `t0` a fraud investigation is future-possible but has not occurred and is not guaranteed. | Local Y: `initial_reserve = €25,000` | Legitimate signal (verify timestamp) | low | Reserve is typically set at/near registration; if set before `t0`, admissible and a plausible complexity proxy. | Keep if reserve timestamp `< t0` is confirmed; otherwise treat as repairable. |
| Local Z: `pattern Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` | Process impossibility / timestamp artifact | critical | Same as the sequence-pattern item; impossible ordering caused by identical timestamps and missing sequence index. | Exclude; fix ordering. |
| Local Z: `risk_score_current` | Mutable feature, no timestamp | high | Mutable and possibly recalculated after `t0`; no feature timestamp stored, so the value may reflect post-`t0` information. | Recompute as `risk_score_at_t0` with a stored feature timestamp; until then unusable. |
| Local Z: `last_resource_role = Special Investigation Unit` | Temporal leakage (complete-case role) | critical | "Last role in the complete case," i.e., end-of-case state; also implies an SIU/fraud path that is only known later. | Replace with `resource_role_at_t0`; remove closure-based field. |

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should sit between the event log/feature store and the explanation layer, and run per feature, per pattern, and per case.

1. Prefix-time availability check.
   - For every feature, require a recorded `feature_valid_from` (availability timestamp) and assert `feature_valid_from <= t0`. Features with no stored timestamp (e.g., `risk_score_current`, `final_handler_team`, `last_resource_role`, employee experience) fail closed and are quarantined rather than silently trusted.
   - For counts/aggregations, assert the SQL time predicate is strictly `< t0` (this catches `prior_claim_count_before_t0`'s `<= extraction_date` bug and the nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot` refresh).

2. Activity-order feasibility (footprint/DFG check).
   - Build a directly-follows graph (DFG) and a footprint matrix from the normative model, and validate observed patterns against them. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` violates the normative footprint (assessment precedes settlement/closure), so it is flagged as infeasible.
   - Limitation: a frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule. The DFG describes observed behavior (including data-quality artifacts). Feasibility must be judged against the normative model / declarative constraints, not raw edge frequency. Use the DFG to *surface* candidates, and alignment/declarative checks to *decide*.

3. Same-timestamp ambiguity check.
   - Detect events sharing a timestamp within a case and verify a reliable, monotonic ingestion sequence index exists to break ties. Where the index is missing (Case Z's `Claim Closed`/`Damage Assessment Completed`), mark the ordering as ambiguous and forbid any order-dependent feature or pattern derived from that pair. This distinguishes true impossibility from mere tie-break failure.

4. Complete-trace detection.
   - Flag any feature whose definition references terminal or aggregate-over-whole-trace information: suffixes like `_total`, `_anywhere`, `full_trace_*`, `final_*`, `last_*`, `total_*_duration`. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role` all trip this check. Require a prefix-only reformulation before use.

5. Explanation-referent enablement check.
   - Using alignment/token-replay on the prefix and the model's enabled-set, classify each activity an explanation refers to as: (a) already occurred (enabled and realized before `t0`) — admissible; (b) impossible given the prefix/normative model — reject; (c) future-possible but not yet occurred — reject as leakage if the explanation uses its occurrence, allow only if it uses a genuine `t0`-observable trigger.
   - This is what separates `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` (future-possible, legitimate later) from `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` (impossible ordering).

6. Repeated-activity legitimacy check.
   - `Settlement Decision Recorded` may legitimately appear twice when an appeal causes reconsideration. The suite must not treat the second decision as a duplicate/error. Validate with a declarative rule: a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate iff it is preceded by `Appeal`, which is itself preceded by a Partial/Deny decision. If that chain is absent, flag as anomaly. This is a semantic legitimacy test, distinct from the same-timestamp artifact test.

Role and limits of the techniques: alignment checks give per-event conformance and enablement (best for the enablement/impossibility questions but need a trustworthy model); footprint/DFG checks are cheap for order feasibility but conflate frequency with normativity; prefix checks enforce temporal admissibility but not semantics; declarative (Declare-style) constraints capture "appeal-before-second-decision" style rules that a DFG cannot express cleanly. Use them in combination and fail closed on missing timestamps.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Classifying only the signals that survive or can be repaired:

- Defensible predictive signal (admissible at `t0`, usable as-is).
  - `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and (subject to timestamp verification) `initial_reserve`.
  - May claim: these are associated with higher/lower breach risk and are available at `t0`. `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` legitimately captures "slack already consumed."
  - May NOT claim: that they *cause* breach or that acting on them changes outcomes; complexity/reserve are severity proxies, not levers.

- Process-state / congestion signal.
  - `queue_length_at_t0` (and, after repair, `handler_team_at_t0`).
  - May claim: reflects real operational load at prediction time; a plausible congestion indicator and the most operationally *actionable* of the set (staffing/routing can move queue length).
  - May NOT claim: a precise causal magnitude without a controlled/uplift study; validity depends on the ±5-min snapshot join being correct.

- Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.
  - `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and calendar-adjacent socioeconomic correlates.
  - May claim: predictive lift only, after review.
  - May NOT claim: causal or actionable interpretation. These are proxies for repair-network coverage, language, channel, and socioeconomic status; using them can encode disparate treatment. Prefer replacing region/channel with the underlying mechanistic driver (e.g., repair-network coverage) and run subgroup performance/fairness diagnostics before shipping.

- Drift / calendar proxy.
  - `calendar_week`.
  - May claim: captures seasonality/regime effects (holiday, policy change) useful for calibration and drift control.
  - May NOT claim: that a week is intrinsically "operationally worse." It is confounded with a policy change and holiday; the mapping is unstable and must be monitored, not interpreted as a fixed cause.

- Unusable until lineage is repaired.
  - `final_handler_team` (needs `t0` team), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (fix `< t0` predicate), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (restrict to pre-`t0` uploads), `risk_score_current` (needs `risk_score_at_t0` with stored timestamp), `last_resource_role` (needs `resource_role_at_t0`), employee experience (as-of `t0`).
  - May claim: nothing yet. After repair and re-fit, re-evaluate importance and calibration before any interpretation.

Standards to keep distinct throughout: predictive validity = temporally admissible and improves out-of-sample accuracy; causal interpretation = supported by design/intervention evidence, not SHAP magnitude; operational actionability = there exists a lever a case handler can pull at/after `t0` to change the outcome (only queue/staffing/routing qualify here); fairness review = required for any feature that proxies protected or sensitive attributes even if predictive.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Prioritized (P1 = do first):

- P1 — Feature-store design with temporal contracts. Make every feature a point-in-time record keyed by entity + event time, with a mandatory `feature_valid_from` and immutable snapshots. Serving must perform strict as-of `t0` lookups. This structurally eliminates the closure-field and mutable-score leaks (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, employee experience).
- P1 — Historical feature timestamps / point-in-time correctness. Backfill availability timestamps and forbid any feature lacking one from entering the model (fail closed). Convert warehouse "final/last/total/anywhere" fields into explicit prefix-aggregations.
- P1 — Prefix-only recomputation and re-fit. Rebuild the training set so every feature is computed strictly from events with sequence index at/before `t0`; fix the `prior_claim_count` predicate (`< t0`) and the nightly-refresh contamination (`same_day_document_count`). Re-fit the model on the clean feature set.
- P1 — Process-conformance tests in CI. Automate the Section-2 suite: prefix availability, footprint/DFG feasibility, same-timestamp/sequence-index integrity, complete-trace detection, enablement classification, and appeal→second-decision legitimacy. Block release on any critical finding; require a reliable ingestion sequence index so identical-timestamp cases are resolvable.
- P2 — Calibration after feature removal. Removing leaked features (esp. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`) will lower AUC and shift score distribution; re-calibrate (Platt/isotonic), re-select thresholds against the 10-day target, and report the honest, non-leaked performance rather than the inflated leaked baseline.
- P2 — Explanation stability. After re-fit, test that local explanations are stable across seeds, background sets, and small prefix perturbations; flag features whose attribution flips sign or rank as low-confidence and do not surface them in operator-facing narratives.
- P2 — Proxy / fairness analysis. Run subgroup performance and disparity diagnostics for `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and calendar-linked correlates; where possible substitute mechanistic drivers (repair-network coverage) and document any retained proxy with its justification and monitoring.
- P3 — Drift and schema monitoring. Monitor `calendar_week`/seasonality regimes, policy-change effects, feature-distribution drift, and schema changes (new activities, renamed warehouse fields). Alert when the DFG or footprint diverges from the normative model, when as-of joins fall outside tolerance (e.g., queue snapshot beyond ±5 min), or when timestamp-missing rates rise — any of which should trigger re-validation before explanations are trusted.
