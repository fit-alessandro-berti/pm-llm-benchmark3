## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

For each case \(c\):

\[
Human(Draft\_Change\_Request,c) \ne Human(Approve\_Change,c)
\]

The comparison is performed after alias normalization and any reliable service-account attribution. If attribution is unresolved, the technical record may show distinct IDs, but human-level compliance is **indeterminate**, not proven.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

For each case \(c\):

\[
Human(Implement\_Change,c) \ne Human(Verify\_Implementation,c)
\]

Again, compare resolved humans, not raw IDs. A service account without reliable mapping cannot establish the required independent verification.

### RR3 — High-risk approval authority

For every HIGH-risk `Approve_Change` event at timestamp \(t\), the resolved human must be either:

1. the standing QM at \(t\), or
2. a valid QM delegate at \(t\).

A QM delegation is valid only where all are true:

\[
t \in [effective\_start, effective\_end]
\]

\[
scope(change) \subseteq scope(delegation)
\]

\[
principal\ absent/formally\ unavailable\ if\ required
\]

\[
no\ prohibited\ subdelegation
\]

The control must use the evidence available for the event-time delegation, not merely a later current-state record.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution and retrospective review

For a HIGH-risk emergency change:

* `Approve_Change` may be performed by the IC or a valid IC delegate.
* The emergency flag and safety rationale must be credible. The supplied log contains the flag but does not itself provide the safety rationale, so full exception validity is not conclusively established.
* `Retrospective_QM_Review` must be completed by a QM or valid QM delegate within two business days after the emergency implementation/approval. A conservative operational rule is to measure from `Implement_Change`; the same outcome arises here.

**Business-day convention used here:**

* A business day is Monday–Friday in the site calendar, excluding stated holidays.
* The site has no stated holidays during the period.
* The event day is day zero.
* The next qualifying business day is business day one; the following qualifying business day is business day two.
* For an implementation on Tuesday 2026-08-11, the deadline is close of business Thursday 2026-08-13 under a calendar-day operational cutoff, or the equivalent timestamp-based deadline if the SOP specifies one. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is late under either practical interpretation.

Weekend 2026-08-15/16 does not count as business days.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

A service account is not a human actor. It can support a rule only if reliable session evidence identifies an authorized human at the event time. Reliability should consider:

* individual authentication or cryptographic token evidence;
* temporal proximity;
* workstation exclusivity;
* badge/access evidence;
* whether another person could use the endpoint or credential;
* audit-log integrity and action-to-session linkage.

Alias normalization is mandatory before SoD checks. The user ID cannot be treated as the person.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

Repeated pairings, reciprocal approvals, shared devices, unusual handovers, and off-pattern activity are risk indicators. They may justify investigation, increased sampling, or control redesign. They do not alone establish collusion, fraud, or intentional circumvention.

### Principal acting while on leave

Leave is evidence relevant to delegation conditions, but it is not an automatic revocation of Asha’s standing QM authority. If Asha personally performed CHG-504 approval, she had standing authority. However:

* the VPN session supports the claimed activity;
* it does not conclusively prove she personally acted;
* a leave-period action should be reviewed for authentication integrity, governance compliance, and whether an appropriate delegate should instead have acted.

### Later database entry and earlier delegation

A later database creation date does **not**, by itself, prove that the delegation did not exist earlier, nor does it independently prove it did. For D-QM-01, the signed PDF timestamp of 2026-08-09 18:00 is contemporaneous evidence that can support validity from 2026-08-10. The database entry on 2026-08-12 is a migration/auditability weakness and should be reconciled, but it does not negate the signed delegation absent contrary evidence.

# Add two business days from implementation date; deadline shown at 23:59:59.
def add_business_days(date_value, n):
    current = pd.Timestamp(date_value).normalize()
    added = 0

        if current.weekday() < 5:  # Monday=0 ... Friday=4

    return current

## 6. Control Improvements

### Delegation creation and effective-time recording

1. Require delegations to be entered in the controlled QMS before use, with immutable:
   * creation time;
   * approval/signature time;
   * effective start/end;
   * scope taxonomy;
   * principal-absence condition;
   * subdelegation setting;
   * revocation history.
2. Preserve the original signed delegation artifact and cryptographically link it to the controlled record.
3. Prohibit back-dated delegation activation except through a formally approved exception process with independent QA review.
4. Implement validation that rejects attempted subdelegation where the parent delegation says “no subdelegation.”

### Principal availability and absence controls

1. Integrate leave, sick leave, on-call status, and formal unavailability with QMS routing.
2. Distinguish:
   * leave as an HR status;
   * formal delegation-triggering absence;
   * emergency availability/on-call override.
3. Require a documented principal-availability attestation where delegation requires principal absence, especially for weekend or out-of-hours approvals.
4. Trigger security review when approval credentials are used during certified absence or when location/device data materially conflict with the user’s statement.

### Alias management

1. Maintain a controlled identity-resolution table linking all aliases to a unique human identifier.
2. Use the unique human identifier, not mutable login name, for SoD enforcement.
3. Block self-approval across aliases at workflow-engine level.
4. Reconcile IAM, HR, QMS, training, and electronic-signature masters routinely and after migrations.

### Individual authentication and service accounts

1. Eliminate service-account execution of GMP-relevant approval or verification steps where technically feasible.
2. If legacy service accounts remain temporarily necessary:
   * require named-user re-authentication immediately before the controlled action;
   * bind the signed action to a specific person, MFA event, and device/session ID;
   * prohibit shared terminal sessions;
   * retain endpoint and application audit trails.
3. Require FIDO/MFA or equivalent individual electronic signature for verification and approval.

### Emergency review controls

1. Require an emergency safety-rationale field, incident reference, and IC authority source before emergency implementation can proceed.
2. Automatically calculate and display the two-business-day retrospective-QM-review deadline.
3. Escalate overdue reviews to QM leadership and site quality management before closure.
4. Validate review authority independently from emergency approval authority; a valid IC authority does not create QM review authority.
5. Prevent closure while retrospective review is missing, late without deviation approval, or performed by an unauthorized person.

### Pair rotation and independent review

1. Establish risk-based reviewer rotation for recurring HIGH-risk production changes.
2. Require an additional independent QA sample or second-level review when a drafter–approver pair exceeds a defined concentration threshold.
3. Monitor reciprocal approvals, not as presumptive misconduct, but as a trigger for independence review.
4. Maintain documented staffing and expertise exceptions so operational concentration can be distinguished from routing manipulation.

### Network monitoring and evidential retention

1. Use PM4Py/pandas or equivalent scheduled analytics to flag:
   * self-approval after alias normalization;
   * implementation/verification overlap;
   * service-account actions;
   * approvals during leave/sick leave;
   * prohibited delegation chains;
   * late emergency reviews;
   * concentrated and reciprocal working patterns.
2. Treat network findings as triage evidence only; require case-specific investigation before alleging misconduct.
3. Retain QMS audit trails, MFA records, workstation/EDR logs, badge records, VPN logs, delegation artifacts, and workflow-routing history for a validated retention period.
4. Ensure timestamps use synchronized UTC sources and preserve source-system timestamp precision.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is mixed rather than attributable to a single cause.

* **Likely genuine authority/control failures:** CHG-502 and CHG-508 contain clear human-level drafter/approver SoD failures; CHG-507 lacks demonstrated HIGH-risk QM approval authority; CHG-510 has an objectively invalid emergency authority chain and a direct RR1 failure. CHG-505 also has a likely late and unsupported retrospective QM review.
* **Likely identity/data-quality or authentication-control failures:** CHG-503 cannot establish independent verification because `svc_qms` is not reliably attributable; CHG-506 is a high-priority suspected credential/identity issue; CHG-504 is authorized if Asha acted but requires authentication confirmation; CHG-509 has strong service-account attribution to Eva but unresolved principal-absence and verifier-authorization evidence.
* **Organizational-pattern risk:** Clara–Ben concentration and reciprocal LOW-risk approvals are meaningful independence indicators, but they do not prove collusion. They justify targeted review, rotation controls, and broader audit sampling.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **CHG-506:** preserve and reconcile QMS, workstation, badge, MFA, VPN, and HR leave evidence to determine who used Ben’s account after sick leave began.
2. **CHG-503:** determine the actual verifier behind `svc_qms` and assess whether Diego could have verified his own implementation.
3. **Emergency governance for CHG-505 and CHG-510:** verify incident rationale, IC authority chain, QM-review authority, and retrospective-review timing; remediate the prohibited-subdelegation and scope-control defects.

Overall, there is substantial evidence of control-design and identity-assurance weakness, several confirmed authorization/SoD failures, and suspicious collaboration patterns requiring investigation—but the available facts do not support a definitive collusion finding.
