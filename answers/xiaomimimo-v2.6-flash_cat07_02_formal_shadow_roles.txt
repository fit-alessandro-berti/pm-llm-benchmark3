# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Definition used throughout.** A *core* activity is one that (i) takes a large share of that resource's own events, (ii) recurs in every observation week, (iii) is matched by a certification or formal title, and (iv) is performed without needing rechecking by another resource. A *peripheral* activity is lowshare, episodic, contingent on absence or incident load, or performed **without** the corresponding formal authority. Case complexity is used as a second axis: an activity can be frequent yet peripheral if it is only ever executed on trivial cases.

**R1 — Automated Intake (Nora).**
*Core:* A1 (760) and A2 (610); top4 concentration = 100%. *Peripheral:* none — there is no tail at all, which is itself diagnostic of an automation boundary rather than an organisational role. *Complexity:* median 0.11, zero critical. *Substitution:* n/a, but it is the **first stage in fragment 6**, where bot/human A2 output is what the 17 reopen events point back to. *Authority:* execution only; it cannot commit the organisation, reopen, or approve. **Naïve reading would treat its 1,370 events as the largest human workload in the process.**

**R2 — FrontOffice Service Agents (Amira, Ben).**
*Core:* A1/A2/A4/A12 = 85.8% (Amira) and 90.8% (Ben) of events; every week present. *Peripheral:* A7 (18/5), A8 (42/25), A11 (6/3), A13 (8/4), A14 (4/2). These tail events are what differentiates Amira from Ben: her complexcase load is 67 cases (58 high + 9 critical) against his 34 — a **casemix difference, not necessarily a competence difference**. *Complexity:* 0.34 vs 0.29; both sit in the low/medium band, consistent with a *firstline* role whose job is to keep highvolume traffic off the specialists. *Substitution:* they are the ones who absorb rework from the temps (fragment 6: "Reopen  R01/R02/R13 rework"). *Authority:* execution + triage. Amira's 18 A7 and 42 A8 events are **peripheral because they are unmatched by a commercial title in the same way Giorgio's are (certified pricing) — the deciding evidence is whether those events require Fatima's countersignature.**

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

A shadow role is a defacto way of working that is not (or only partly) reflected in titles and org charts; a bridging role connects two formal clusters. **None of the following is improper** — each exists for a reason — but each carries a benefit *and* a risk that the process owner must weigh.

**A. R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow role / dualstranded senior agent.**
*Basis:* 0.71 similarity to Fatima and 0.84 to Amira; 72 A7 and 88 A8 events; 41 A7 events outside any recorded absence; top4 activity concentration of only 68.3%; 36 critical cases — more than the designated commercial specialist.
*Benefits:* eliminates the bottleneck that produced 38  74 min pricing latency when Fatima was out; a frontoffice senior who can price and negotiate removes one handover (A7  A8  A9) and shortens endtoend time on complex frontoffice cases; it is institutional knowledge that survives a single absence.
*Risks:* (i) **silent authority** — 37% of all A9 events and 50% of A7 events sit outside the formally titled holders, so audit trails and commitment controls are fuzzy; (ii) concentration risk — losing Giorgio removes both frontoffice depth *and* commercial backup at once; (iii) uneven service — his 72event pricing practice vs Fatima's 122 means two defacto pricing standards; the substitution episode already showed a 95% latency increase, so throughput  equivalence.
*Recommended treatment:* formalise as a **certified secondary holder** of the pricing competency with a defined delegation trigger, rather than leaving it as an implicit practice.

**B. R13_Mateo — crossfunctional escalation broker.**
*Basis:* the only resource active in all 14 activities; top4 concentration 46% (lowest); median complexity 0.77; **76 critical cases (highest)**; similarity 0.54 to Hana and 0.39 to Erik; present as the bridge in fragments 3 and 5; performs 44 A9 events of which 21 fall in Hana's absence window — i.e. **23 preliminary approvals happen outside any substitution period**.
*Benefits:* triage that measurably reduced false escalation complaints in weeks 7–8; routes reopened cases (fragment 5) to the right specialist instead of blanketescalating; his indegree/outdegree in the handover network is what makes the process navigable under incident load.
*Risks:* (i) **he can become an unlogged gate** — filtering security referrals removed work from Erik, and two critical cases then waited >2 days: volumebased metrics would show "improved efficiency" while tail latency worsened; (ii) his 62% preliminaryapproval recheck rate means decision authority is genuinely ambiguous; (iii) singleperson broker dependency — if he is unavailable, fragments 3 and 5 have no alternative spine.
*Recommended treatment:* keep the brokering, but put a **timebound service level on critical security referrals** and distinguish "preliminary approval" from "approval" explicitly in the log (separate concept:name or an attribute), so that mined A9 events are not doublecounted as authority.

**C. R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist.**
*Basis:* 170 events (lowest in the log), 56.5% concentrated in A6, median complexity 0.86, 55 critical cases, 6.8 years tenure, sole security certification.
*Benefits:* the role exists to absorb the lowfrequency/highconsequence tail; his rarity is a feature (restricted, auditable risk acceptance), not a defect.
*Risks:* (i) **utilisation blindness** — any costcutting rule based on activity counts would target him first, which is precisely backwards; (ii) uncovered overlap — 50 of 146 A6 events (**34.2%**) are performed by Chen (24), Mateo (16), Dina (6) and Ivan (4), none of whom hold the security certification, so the "rare specialist" boundary is already leaking; (iii) single point of failure on the late/day boundary (he is day shift only).
*Recommended treatment:* measure him by **criticalcase coverage and timetoA6**, not by event count, and either certify a second holder (Dina is the natural candidate — she already performs 6 A6 events) or log uncertified A6 events as exceptions.

**D. R14_Nora — automation boundary role.**
*Basis:* 1,370 events, 100% in A1/A2, median complexity 0.11, system contract, 24/7.
*Benefits:* absorbs 45.6% of registration and 27.1% of entitlement validation, freeing credentialed staff.
*Risks:* it inflates apparent FrontOffice capacity if counted as staff; and it is upstream of the temprelated rework loop (fragment 6) — 50.2% of all A2 events are produced by Nora + the two temps, which is exactly where the 17 incompletenote reopens originated. **Automated and provisional A2 output should be treated as one quality cohort.**

**E. R11_Kai / R12_Lina — temporaryworkforce shadow role.**
*Basis:* 0.98 mutual similarity, 98% of events in four activities, 0 critical cases, 17 reopens traced to their A2 notes.
*Benefits:* elastic capacity that prevented frontoffice queues in weeks 8–10.
*Risks:* they create **downstream** load (reopens  rework by R01/R02/R13) that is invisible in their own utilisation figures; their activity profile is indistinguishable from permanent staff to any cosinebased method, so they can be mistaken for fullfunction agents in capacity planning.

**F. R09_Ivan / R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping shadow role.**
*Basis:* 0.97 similarity; 88.9% of A11 and 76.6% of A14; but also 73 A10 and 48 A5 events between them.
*Benefits:* consistent gate and a maintained knowledge base; strong rework learning loop.
*Risks:* (i) **independence** — implementing (A10/A5) and checking (A11) on overlapping cases can produce selfreview; (ii) shift asymmetry — the merge hides that lateshift quality is one person; (iii) gate power without commitment authority means quality holds can be overridden, so their influence is real but formally weak.

# 4. Ambiguous assignments and alternative interpretations

**4.1 R07_Giorgio — permanent dual role vs. habitual substitute.**
- *Interpretation 1 (dual role):* a certified senior frontoffice agent with standing pricing/negotiation competence; his A7/A8 and criticalcase load are part of normal design.
- *Interpretation 2 (habitual substitute):* a frontoffice senior who drifted into commercial work and is being read by the log as a specialist because he covered weeks 3–4 and continued afterwards out of convenience.
- *Deciding evidence:* (a) **time decomposition of A7** — 41 of 72 A7 events occur outside the weeks 3–4 window, which already favours interpretation 1; check whether they cluster in Fatima's other absences or spread evenly; (b) **duration distribution** — his inwindow A7 median 74 min vs outofwindow median (if 38–45 min, he is genuinely practised; if still 70 min, he is a substitute with a slower routine); (c) **certification and delegation records** for pricing; (d) whether his A7 events require a Fatima/Hana countersignature (cooccurrence of A7 by Giorgio followed by A9 or a second A7 in the same case); (e) outcome comparison — reopen/rework rate of cases where he performed A7 vs Fatima's.

**4.2 R13_Mateo — authorised coordinator vs. defacto approver.**
- *Interpretation 1:* a legitimate crossfunctional router who issues *preliminary* approvals that are advisory only.
- *Interpretation 2:* an acting supervisor whose approvals substitute for Hana's, with real commitment authority.
- *Deciding evidence:* **recheck/reversal rate** — 13/21 (62%) of his week5 approvals were rechecked by Hana, which supports interpretation 1; check whether the 23 A9 events outside week 5 were also rechecked; compare A9 durations (preliminary approvals should be short); check `escalation_level` and reopen rate of cases closed under his A9; check whether his A9 is followed by Hana's A9 in the same case (log the distinction explicitly).

**4.3 R05_Erik — genuinely rare specialist vs. underutilised / defensibly filtered.**
- *Interpretation 1:* the correct design — most security referrals are lowrisk and should never reach him; Mateo's filtering in weeks 7–8 was good triage (false escalation complaints fell).
- *Interpretation 2:* he is being bypassed — 50/146 A6 events (34.2%) are done by uncertified resources, and two critical cases waited >2 days, indicating an access problem rather than a workload problem.
- *Deciding evidence:* **conversion rate of security referrals** (referrals  A6) before/after Mateo's filtering; **timetoA6 conditioned on `escalation_level` and on `product_line="Enterprise Security"`**; a sample audit of *filtered* referrals to estimate false negatives; whether the 2 delayed critical cases were filtered, queued, or simply out of shift; `customer_segment` breakdown of complaints.

**4.4 R03_Chen vs R04_Dina — interchangeable peers vs. senior/junior ladder.**
- *Interpretation 1:* two equivalent technical analysts differing only by tenure and shift.
- *Interpretation 2:* Chen is the defacto senior handling high/critical and securityadjacent work; Dina is still ramping (1.2y).
- *Deciding evidence:* **withinactivity complexity** — compare the median `case_complexity` of A3/A5/A10 events *performed by each resource* (this controls for case mix; if Chen's withinactivity medians are higher for the same activity, interpretation 2 holds); handover direction ChenDina (does Dina hand complex cases to Chen?); refusal/escalation after Dina's diagnosis; distribution of A6 (24 vs 6) — uncertified securityadjacent work concentrated on one person.

**4.5 R11_Kai / R12_Lina — a distinct role vs. scaleddown instances of R2.**
- *Interpretation 1:* a legitimate "triage/closure" subrole for simple requests.
- *Interpretation 2:* no distinct role exists — they are undifferentiated overflow, and the 17 reopens show they are performing A2 beyond their competence.
- *Deciding evidence:* **reopen rate per A2 performer** (Kai/Lina vs Amira/Ben/Nora) over a full cycle; whether the "incomplete note" cause is a training artefact (fixable) or scope creep; whether they ever receive medium cases (their 76/70 medium cases suggest some); certification status before allowing independent A2.

**4.6 R14_Nora — part of the Front Office cluster vs. a nonorganisational automation layer.**
- *Interpretation 1:* treat it as a member of the frontoffice role for capacity purposes.
- *Interpretation 2:* exclude it from organisational role discovery entirely; it has no title, shift, tenure, or authority.
- *Deciding evidence:* check `contract="system"` and `usual shift="24/7"` — decisive for interpretation 2 for *authority*, but its A1/A2 volume is real *capacity*; the practical rule is: include in capacity models, exclude from authority and staffing models. Also check reopen/rework rates downstream of Nora's A2 vs human A2 (fragment 6 ties A2 quality to reopens).

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
# =====================================================================
# Order-to-resolution: organisational role discovery with guardrails
# =====================================================================
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import networkx as nx
import pm4py
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans   # only for the *baseline to argue against*

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. LOAD + FORMAT  (event-level log, one row per event)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_log.csv")

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Extra event/case attributes used below (kept as-is by format_dataframe):
#   case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
#   temporary_substitution, escalation_level
log = df                      # PM4Py >= 2.3 works directly on dataframes

# Sanity: no resource row without a role-bearing resource string
assert df["org:resource"].notna().all()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. ORGANISATIONAL ROLE DISCOVERY
#    Returns {role_id: {"resources": [...], "activities": [...],
#                       "activities_number": n, "resources_number": m}}
#    NOTE (version-tolerant): older PM4Py returns the same dict shape;
#    if your build returns a ClusterAnalysisResult, unwrap .clusters / .labels.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)

role_rows = []
res2role, res2activities = {}, {}
for rid, info in roles.items():
    members = sorted(info.get("resources", []))
    acts    = sorted(info.get("activities", []))
    for r in members:
        res2role[r] = rid
        res2activities[r] = acts
    role_rows.append({
        "role_id": rid,
        "n_resources": len(members),
        "n_activities": len(acts),
        "resources": ", ".join(members),
        "activities": ", ".join(acts),
    })
role_df = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. ACTIVITY-BASED RESOURCE SIMILARITY  (cosine over normalised counts)
#    -> dict of dicts; convert to a matrix for inspection/heatmap
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
sim_df = pd.DataFrame(sim).astype(float)

def top_similar(resource, k=3, exclude_self=True):
    s = sim_df[resource].drop(resource) if exclude_self else sim_df[resource]
    return s.sort_values(ascending=False).head(k)

# Selected pairs used in the report
for a, b in [("R03_Chen","R04_Dina"), ("R09_Ivan","R10_Julia"),
             ("R11_Kai","R12_Lina"),  ("R01_Amira","R02_Ben"),
             ("R06_Fatima","R07_Giorgio"), ("R08_Hana","R13_Mateo"),
             ("R05_Erik","R13_Mateo"), ("R01_Amira","R07_Giorgio")]:
    print(f"{a:>10} ~ {b:<10} = {sim_df.loc[a, b]:.2f}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. NETWORKS: handover, working together, subcontracting
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, weight=1)   # DataFrame
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)             # DataFrame
subcontr = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log, n_size=2)     # DataFrame

def net_metrics(adj, name):
    G = nx.from_pandas_adjacency(adj.astype(float), create_using=nx.DiGraph)
    m = pd.DataFrame({
        f"{name}_out":   pd.Series(nx.out_degree_centrality(G)),
        f"{name}_in":    pd.Series(nx.in_degree_centrality(G)),
        f"{name}_betw":  pd.Series(nx.betweenness_centrality(G)),
    })
    return m.sort_values(f"{name}_betw", ascending=False)

hm = net_metrics(handover, "handover")
print(hm.head(10))
# Expectation: R13_Mateo high betweenness (broker), R01/R02 -> R03/R04 strong
# out-edges (fragment 2), R13 -> R05_Erik edge present only in incident cases.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. RESOURCE x ACTIVITY PROFILE + CASE COMPLEXITY + SUBSTITUTION JOIN
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5a. event-level profile
act_res = (df.groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"])
             .agg(events        = ("concept:name", "size"),
                  median_complexity = ("case_complexity", "median"),
                  n_critical     = ("case_complexity", lambda s: int((s >= 0.80).sum())),
                  n_substitution = ("temporary_substitution", lambda s: int(s.astype(bool).sum())),
                  median_escalation = ("escalation_level", "median"))
             .reset_index())

# 5b. resource totals -> role membership, concentration ("role sharpness")
res_tot = (df.groupby("org:resource")
             .agg(total_events = ("concept:name", "size"),
                  median_case_complexity = ("case_complexity", "median"),
                  critical_cases = ("case_complexity", lambda s: int((s >= 0.80).sum())))
             .reset_index())
res_tot["role_id"] = res_tot["org:resource"].map(res2role)

top4 = (act_res.sort_values(["org:resource", "events"], ascending=[True, False])
                .groupby("org:resource")["events"].head(4)
                .groupby("org:resource").sum())

                      share_tol=0.20, complexity_tol=0.25):
```

**Reading rule to accompany the code.** `discover_organizational_roles` and `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` return *behavioural* groupings derived from shared activity profiles. They are evidence about **what people do**, never about **what people are allowed to decide**. Therefore, before any cluster is promoted to a "role", it must be joined against (i) `case_complexity` / criticalcase share, (ii) the authority activity set above, (iii) `temporary_substitution` windows, and (iv) recheck/reopen outcomes. The `authority_warning` function exists precisely to make that failure visible instead of silent.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**How to use the discovered structure.**

*Staffing and coverage.* The role map converts into a shift and span plan rather than a headcount plan. Three structural facts stand out: **(a)** lateshift capability is thin and concentrated — Chen (highcomplexity technical, 42 critical), Ivan (quality gate) and Lina (temp) are the only lateshift resources, so a late incident is handled by exactly one senior technical person; **(b)** the security function is dayshift only and singleperson (Erik), which is how two critical cases came to wait more than 2 days when Mateo filtered referrals; **(c)** 50.2% of all entitlement validations are produced by the bot plus two temps, i.e. the leastexperienced producers of the activity most strongly associated with rework. Staffing actions that follow: give the late shift a second securitycapable resource or an explicit oncall rule; treat Giorgio as a **named, certified secondary** for pricing rather than an informal habit; and staff the front office by *complexity bands* (who can absorb a medium case), not by raw event volume.

*Training and certification paths.* Use the role table as a competency ladder: temps  entitlement certification before independent A2 (fixes the 17reopen failure at source); Dina  security certification (she already performs 6 A6 events without it); Ivan/Julia  an explicit implementervschecker rule; Giorgio  formal pricing delegation with documented triggers. Chen/Dina should be managed as a deliberate senior/junior ladder with knowledge transfer on A3/A5 rather than as an interchangeable pair.

*Risk control.* Make **authority explicit in the log**: A9 should distinguish "preliminary" from "approved", A6 should record whether the certified specialist performed or endorsed it, and A7 should record who may commit pricing. Monitor three ratios continuously: uncertified A6 share (currently 34.2%), nonsupervisor A9 share (currently 36.7%), and recheck rate by approver (Mateo 62%). Add a latency service level keyed to `escalation_level` and `product_line="Enterprise Security"`, because volume metrics will keep reporting Mateo's triage as an improvement while tail latency degrades.

*Resilience.* Broker and specialist roles need named deputies, not nominal ones: Mateo's brokering has no documented alternative path for fragments 3 and 5; Erik has none; Ivan alone covers late quality. Build runbooks for those three positions and rehearse them. Keep the automation boundary measurable — track reopen rate downstream of bot A2 versus human A2 so automation growth does not silently move quality problems forward.

**Cautions about changing staffing on mined roles alone.**

1. **A cluster is not a job description, and frequency is not authority.** R08_Hana has the thirdlowest event count (245) but 63.3% of all approval events; R05_Erik has the lowest (170) yet the secondhighest criticalcase load. Any staffing decision driven by utilisation or cluster size — cutting the "small" cluster, merging the "lowvolume" resource into a bigger team — removes exactly the controls that carry the risk. Require an authority and complexity join (as in §5) before any role is redefined, contracted, or eliminated.

2. **Do not convert substitution episodes or low volume into claims of permanent competence — or incompetence.** Giorgio's A7 duration doubled (38  74 min) during Fatima's absence: that demonstrates *capacity to cover*, not equivalence of *throughput or judgement*. Conversely, Erik's low volume does not mean he is underworked — it means most referrals should not reach him, and Mateo's filtering already showed how easily that distinction is lost. Validate any reclassification against multicycle outcome data (reopen rate, rework, recheck rate, criticalcase latency), certification records, and the delegation rules — never against a twoweek substitution window or a similarity score.

3. **Similarity profiles are not interchangeable profiles.** Chen–Dina (0.94) and Ivan–Julia (0.97) look like safe merge targets for crosstraining or headcount reduction, but the underlying case mixes (42 vs 9 critical) and shift assignments (late vs day) differ materially. Merging on similarity alone would leave highcomplexity lateshift work and quality coverage each with a single point of failure.

4. **Rerun discovery over several windows before institutionalising anything.** The 10week window contains two anomalies — an Enterprise Security incident spike (weeks 7–8) and a seasonal temp surge (weeks 8–10). Roles inferred from such a window (especially Mateo's filtering behaviour and the temps' apparent capacity) may reflect the window rather than the organisation. Confirm stability of role membership, similarity rankings, and network centrality across at least three comparable periods before embedding them in organisation design, performance targets, or job descriptions.
