## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants** (violations block a repair):
- H1. `Create Order` relates exactly one Order.
- H2. A stored E2O or O2O edge must reference existing objects and events. Self-loops are not allowed.
- H3. O2O qualifiers are directional. `DEL-X carries PK-7` is not the same as `PK-7 carries DEL-X`.
- H4. An item already packed into PK-7 (IT-A1) cannot also be packed into another package without a split or re-pack event, which is absent. So IT-A1 must not be added to any other package.
- H5. Orders are not merged or split by repair. ORD-A and ORD-B remain distinct objects.

**Strong defaults** (violations need explicit justification):
- S1. A Dispatch Package event covers one package and one delivery. Bulk dispatch is possible but is a deviation.
- S2. Pack Item normally relates one item and one package. An item-only row is legitimate during a scanner outage (domain rule 3), so it is evidence of an outage, not necessarily an error.
- S3. Lifecycle order: create  add item  pack  dispatch  confirm  invoice  payment. Observed timestamps respect this order for every existing chain.
- S4. Record Payment relates one payment and normally one invoice. Unapplied cash is allowed.
- S5. An O2O edge should be justified by E2O evidence that does not depend on the O2O edge itself.

**Weak heuristics** (can support a candidate, cannot establish it alone):
- W1. Identifier similarity. `N-421?` is close to `DEL-Y carrier_ref=N-421`. `scan=PK8-2` suggests PK-8. `N-42*` matches both N-420 and N-421.
- W2. Timestamp proximity. ev08 (09:05) follows ev07 (09:04). ev09 and ev10 both occur at 10:00.
- W3. Customer and route. Both orders are C17. Both packages and both deliveries are NORTH. Both facts are shared by many objects and so are weakly discriminating.
- W4. Amount arithmetic. 130 + 100 = 230 (ORD-A + ORD-B = INV-44). 230  100 = 130 (PAY-2 equals the residual). PAY-1 = 100 = ORD-B declared value. PAY-2 = 130 = ORD-A declared value. These coincidences are real, but invoices carry fees, discounts and taxes, and payments can be partial or misallocated. Arithmetic is evidence, not proof.

**Evidence against a repair:**
- E1. Existing exclusive edges (PK-7 dispatched via ev09 to DEL-X; IT-A1 already packed).
- E2. Conflicting identifiers (N-420 vs N-421).
- E3. Missing timeline support (no event places PAY-2 against INV-44).
- E4. Customer-only evidence is insufficient to pick an order, since C17 owns both orders.

**Combination method.** First filter candidates by hard invariants and strong defaults. Then score the survivors by the number of independent evidence families (identifier, time, amount, customer, route, existing structure). A candidate supported only by a weak heuristic is capped at about 0.35. Arithmetic alone is capped at about 0.6. Confidence above 0.6 requires at least two independent families with no E1–E2 violation. O2O edges derived from E2O additions are not counted as independent confirmation of those additions.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum change.**
- ev08 and PK-8: leave ev08 as an item-only row (P18). It is a legitimate outage pattern and reconciliation is possible later. Do not link IT-B1 to PK-8.
- ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y: add P02 (ev10  DEL-Y) and P03 (DEL-Y carries PK-8), both provisional. DEL-X remains tied only to PK-7 and ev09. Reject P10.
- ev11 and the delivery objects: keep ev11 attached only to ORD-A. Leave P04–P06 unresolved. The wildcard is not allowed to pick a delivery.
- INV-44 and ORD-B: keep INV-44 billing ORD-A only. Do not add P11/P12. Report the 230 vs 130 mismatch as an anomaly to review.
- PAY-2 and INV-44: leave PAY-2 without an invoice link (unapplied cash candidate). Reject P15.

Coherence check: ORD-B becomes an order with two events and one item-only pack, with no package, dispatch, confirmation or invoice. That is an incomplete but honest lifecycle. ORD-A gets packages, one dispatch (ev09 for PK-7), and a confirm/invoice that are only partly consistent. The package-delivery layer is nearly complete. The invoice-payment layer is partial. Bundle A adds only one new delivery edge and one new carrier mapping, so its lifecycles are internally consistent even where they are incomplete.

**Bundle B: plausible alternative interpretation (one consolidated C17 shipment and invoice).**
- ev08 and PK-8: ev08 is an outage row whose scanner ref names PK-8, so it is linked to PK-8 (P07), and PK-8 packs IT-B1 (P08). The package now carries items from two orders, which is allowed here only because both belong to one customer and one route.
- ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y: same as Bundle A (P02, P03).
- ev11 and the delivery objects: ev11 is treated as a bulk acknowledgment covering both deliveries (P04 and P05, each at 0.30). This is the bulk-confirm reading. The portal identifier loss is the alternative reading.
- INV-44 and ORD-B: ev12 is linked to ORD-B (P11), and INV-44 bills ORD-B (P12). The invoice then covers both orders, matching its 230 total.
- PAY-2 and INV-44: ev14 is linked to INV-44 (P13), and PAY-2 settles INV-44 (P14). The residual after PAY-1 is paid.

Coherence check: the full bundle makes ORD-B flow through PK-8 and DEL-Y, and it makes ORD-A flow through PK-7, PK-8, DEL-X and DEL-Y. The lifecycles are complete on paper, but the shared PK-8 and the bulk ev11 generate two-order objects whose behavior is entirely determined by weak evidence. P07 and P11 are the critical edges. If either is wrong, the bundle propagates the error across several objects. Bundle B is coherent as a story, but the story depends on several provisional links.

**Bundle C: variant of B with a single delivery confirmation.** Use Bundle B except ev11  DEL-X only (P04 at 0.30), reflecting the N-420 prefix preference. ev11 is not linked to DEL-Y. This makes DEL-Y confirmation-free. Its lifecycle is shorter than Bundle B but still ties the wildcard to one delivery arbitrarily. Use it only as a sensitivity check.

**Caveat on Bundle B and C.** Both assume that ORD-B has been invoiced before any evidence of its delivery. Under B, ORD-B receives an invoice whose only delivery evidence is the shared PK-8 dispatch. This is possible under consolidated billing, but it is a lifecycle order concern: the invoice may be issued against unconfirmed goods.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A has a complete create-add-pack-dispatch-confirm-invoice-pay shape under Bundle B, and a partial shape under Bundle A. Under A, ORD-A's invoice relation is unchanged but its total (230) exceeds its declared value (130). ORD-B is an order with two events and an item-only pack. Under A it is incomplete. Under B it gains invoice and payment links that depend on weak evidence. ORD-B has no order-level dispatch or confirmation in either bundle, so an unshipped order is invoiced in B.

**Item.** IT-A1 is packed into PK-7 only. IT-A2 is packed into PK-8 only. IT-B1 is unpacked in A and packed into PK-8 in B. In B, PK-8 is many-to-many with items (IT-A2, IT-B1) and orders (ORD-A, ORD-B). That is the most suspicious cardinality in the repair.

**Package.** PK-7 has one item and one dispatch. PK-8 has one item in A and two items in B. Under A, PK-8 gains a DEL-Y carrier. Under B, PK-8 has two order contexts.

**Delivery.** DEL-X has one dispatch and one carried package. DEL-Y has no events in the observed log. Under A it gains one dispatch and one carried package. Under B it additionally receives a bulk confirmation, which makes ev11 many-to-many with deliveries (one event, two objects). No delivery is confirmed by a unique event in either bundle.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has one invoice event (ev12) and one or two orders. One invoice relating to two orders is allowed by the semantic rules (one invoice, one or more orders), so B is semantically valid. Its arithmetic is not proof.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is linked to INV-44. PAY-2 has no invoice in A (unapplied cash candidate) and settles INV-44 in B. Under B, ev14 is the second payment for the same invoice, which is normal for partial payment.

**Independent support check.**
- DEL-Y/ev10: supported by the carrier reference and the package, which is E2O evidence. The O2O "DEL-Y carries PK-8" is not independent.
- PK-8/ev08: supported by the scanner reference (E2O). The O2O "PK-8 packs IT-B1" is not independent.
- ORD-B/INV-44: supported by the invoice arithmetic and customer. Not independent of the amount.
- ev11/DEL-X and DEL-Y: supported only by a wildcard. Not independently confirmed.

**Circular reasoning to avoid.** If we first assume ev08  PK-8, then infer PK-8 packs IT-B1 (O2O), and then cite "PK-8 packs IT-B1" as confirmation that ev08 belongs to PK-8, the argument is circular. The O2O edge is a restatement of the E2O assumption. The same pattern appears for DEL-Y carries PK-8 and for INV-44 bills ORD-B. Each O2O must be justified by independent E2O or attribute evidence, and each E2O by identifiers or timestamps, not by its counterpart.

**Orphans.** Under A: ev08 is partial (item-only pack event); ev14 is not an orphan (it relates PAY-2) but has no invoice; PAY-2 is an object without an invoice link (unapplied cash). Under B: no orphan events remain, but ORD-B and DEL-Y lifecycles rest on provisional links.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log.** The OC-DFG contains Create Order  Add Item  Pack Item (ORD-A items, PK-7/PK-8)  Dispatch Package (PK-7  DEL-X)  Confirm Delivery (ORD-A)  Issue Invoice  Record Payment (PAY-1). Pack Item for IT-B1 appears with only an item object, so its outgoing package-type edge is missing. Dispatch of PK-8 has no delivery object. Record Payment for PAY-2 has no invoice object.

**Bundle A.**
- OC-DFG: Dispatch Package gains a Delivery-object edge to DEL-Y (from ev10). No new activity edges appear. Pack Item for IT-B1 remains a dead-end item fragment.
- OTG: a new DEL-Y  PK-8 edge. DEL-Y becomes a node connected only through ev10. No ORD-B edges are added.
- ET–OT graph: ev10 gains a DEL-Y connection. ev08 stays connected only to IT-B1.
- Flattening on Order: direct E2O flattening is unchanged (ev08 and ev10 are not directly related to any order). Order-level flattening does not change.
- Flattening on Delivery: DEL-X: ev09; DEL-Y: ev10 (new single row). No duplication.
- Payment: PAY-2 remains an unapplied payment; Record Payment appears as an event with no invoice object.
- Activity frequencies: events count unchanged (14 events). Unique objects for Dispatch Package increase by one (DEL-Y). Total object-event pairs increase by 2 (ev10–DEL-Y, ev10–PK-8 already present).
- Equivalent-execution clustering around Order: ORD-A and ORD-B remain structurally different; ORD-B stays a short fragment, so it is not clustered with ORD-A.

**Bundle B.**
- OC-DFG: Pack Item (ORD-B item)  Pack Item (PK-8) appears, linking ORD-B's item lifecycle to ORD-A's package lifecycle. Dispatch Package PK-8  DEL-Y appears. Confirm Delivery now feeds two delivery objects. Issue Invoice follows Create Order (ORD-B) as a new edge from ORD-B's create to the invoice. Record Payment connects to INV-44 with two payments.
- OTG: a PK-8 node bridging ORD-A and ORD-B, and an ev11 node connected to DEL-X and DEL-Y, producing a new cycle through ev11 (ORD-A  ev11  DEL-X/DEL-Y). Cycles through shared events inflate path counts.
- ET–OT graph: ev08 now connects to PK-8 and IT-B1; ev11 has three object connections; ev12 has three connections (ORD-A, ORD-B, INV-44). ev12 is a bridge between two order objects.
- Flattening on Order: ev12 appears in both the ORD-A and ORD-B flattenings, so invoice counts or amounts summed over orders double-count 230. ev11 is absent from order flattenings if confirmation is linked only to deliveries, which is a flattening artifact.
- Flattening on Delivery: ev11 appears in both DEL-X and DEL-Y flattenings. Confirmation counts double.
- Invoice: flattening on Invoice shows two orders per invoice; the invoice lifecycle now starts with an unshipped-looking order (ORD-B has no dispatch evidence).
- Payment: PAY-2 is attached to INV-44; the invoice's payment total becomes 230, which is paid in full. This changes receivables analysis.
- Activity frequencies: events are unchanged, but unique objects for Confirm Delivery rise to three (ORD-A, DEL-X, DEL-Y), and Issue Invoice to two orders.
- Equivalent-execution clustering around Order: ORD-B now has a full-looking lifecycle, so it clusters with ORD-A. Both share an invoice event (ev12), which merges their executions in invoice-based clustering. This produces a single equivalence class containing two orders when the true business processes may differ.

**Edges that may appear:** DEL-Y dispatch (A and B); PK-8  DEL-Y (A and B); Pack Item  PK-8 for ORD-B items (B); Confirm Delivery  DEL-Y (B); Create Order (ORD-B)  Issue Invoice (B). **Edges that may disappear or be duplicated:** the item-only Pack Item for IT-B1 (A stays as a dead end; B replaces it with a PK-8 edge); ev11 as a single confirmation (duplicated in B); ev12 duplicated across two orders (B).

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Assigning ev11 to one delivery.** The wildcard N-42* matches both carriers. Assigning it to DEL-X creates a confirmed delivery that the evidence cannot support.
2. **Linking ev12 to ORD-B and duplicating invoice totals.** Flattening on Order then counts 230 under both orders. Revenue summed by order is inflated, and ORD-B appears invoiced before any shipment.
3. **Accepting PAY-2  INV-44 on the residual.** The residual match is arithmetic, and PAY-2 may be unapplied cash. Marking INV-44 as fully paid creates a false receivables closure.
4. **Linking ev08 to PK-8 and deriving "PK-8 packs IT-B1".** This creates a shared package across two orders and makes ORD-B's items look shipped. The O2O edge is circular support for the E2O assumption.
5. **Adding DEL-Y carries PK-8 as independent confirmation.** Its only support is the ev10 addition, so the log now appears to confirm its own inference.
6. **Merging ORD-A and ORD-B.** Customer identity (C17) does not justify merging orders. A merged order collapses two execution histories into one equivalence class.
7. **Forcing every object to a complete lifecycle.** ORD-B can look complete only by inventing shipment and payment links. Incomplete lifecycles are honest signals of missing data, and removing them hides a real reconciliation task.
8. **Creating a second dispatch for DEL-X.** Reusing N-420 for PK-8 (P10) would make DEL-X dispatch two packages in violation of the dispatch rule, and this would inflate the Dispatch Package frequency.
