## 1. Decision rule

### Eligibility & Intervening Conditions
- **Process Stage**: The intervention is only eligible if the case is in the "Diagnosis Started" stage.
- **Prediction Reliability Adjustment**: For the Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise, and SME segments, apply a bias correction to the `p_now` predictions by subtracting the respective calibration offsets (0.04–0.09, 0.05–0.12, and none respectively). For Non-Native-Language Support cases, apply no adjustment due to tiny validation sample size.
- **Effect Heterogeneity**: Only cases where the estimated absolute risk reduction (ARR) lies within the top 50% of the range are eligible. This captures cases with the highest potential benefit.
- **False Alarm Burden**: Cases with a false-alarm burden exceeding €200 are ineligible to avoid excessive customer disruption.
- **Reserved Slot**: One slot is reserved for a potentially incoming regulatory incident, regardless of the above criteria.
- **Repeated Alerts**: 
  - I-05 is excluded from intervention due to repeated alerts without new evidence.
  - I-07 is eligible because its alert introduces new customer diagnostic information.
- **Data Quality Note**: Cases with missing or incomplete data (e.g., I-06) are excluded from intervention unless corrected.

### Prioritization & Scoring
- **Priority Score**: Calculated as (ARR × Value of Avoiding Breach) - (Swarm Cost + Expected False Alarm Burden).
- **Tiers**:
  - **Tier 1**: Highest priority score, guaranteed intervention if eligible.
  - **Tier 2**: High priority score, intervention based on remaining slots.
  - **Tier 3**: Lower priority score, intervention if slots remain.
  - **Tier 4**: Not eligible for intervention.

### Robustification
- **Calibration Uncertainty**: For cases in segments with known calibration issues (Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise), apply a 10% adjustment downward to the ARR.
- **Segment-Specific Bias**: Adjust `p_now` for Non-Native-Language Support cases by applying a neutral bias due to small validation sample size.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

### I-05 vs I-07
- **I-05**: Despite repeated alerts, no new evidence has emerged for 30 minutes. Therefore, it is excluded from intervention to prevent unnecessary disruption and ensure resources are allocated to cases with genuine new information.
- **I-07**: The new customer diagnostic log introduces genuine new information, justifying its intervention despite the repeated alert. The fresh data justifies the allocation of a swarm slot, enhancing the likelihood of a successful diagnosis before the SLA deadline.

### Cool-Down, Suppression, Escalation, Re-entry Logic
- **Cool-Down**: A 30-minute period post-intervention before a case can be re-evaluated for another intervention. This prevents unnecessary repeat interventions.
- **Suppression**: If a case receives three consecutive alerts without new evidence, it is suppressed for 24 hours to prevent false-alarm burden.
- **Escalation**: If a case’s breach probability exceeds a threshold (e.g., 0.75) and interventions fail, escalate to senior management for potential additional resources or external expertise.
- **Re-entry**: If new evidence emerges after suppression or escalation, the case re-enters the intervention eligibility criteria.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

### Value-Based Prioritization
- **Risk**: Higher-value cases may be prioritized over lower-value ones, potentially leaving critical but less valuable cases unattended.
- **Mitigation**: Include a fairness weighting factor in the priority score, ensuring a minimum intervention rate for lower-value cases to prevent systemic bias.

### Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support
- **Risk**: The small validation sample size introduces uncertainty in risk predictions for cases requiring Non-Native-Language Support.
- **Mitigation**: Allocate a buffer slot for these cases and implement a manual review process for decisions involving this segment.

### Differential Calibration Across Segments
- **Risk**: Different segments (Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise, SME) have varying calibration biases, leading to inconsistent intervention effectiveness.
- **Mitigation**: Continuously monitor calibration errors and adjust the ARR estimates accordingly. Regularly retrain the model with updated data to improve accuracy across all segments.

### Selective Intervention and Training Labels
- **Risk**: Interventions may alter the data distribution, affecting future model performance and leading to a feedback loop.
- **Mitigation**: Use a holdout validation set to monitor changes in model performance over time and recalibrate the model periodically to mitigate drift.

### Spillover Harm to Untreated Cases
- **Risk**: Intervention may increase breach risk for untreated cases due to congestion.
- **Mitigation**: Implement a spillover adjustment in the decision rule, limiting the intervention’s impact on non-intervened cases. Monitor spillover effects and adjust slot allocation accordingly.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

### Operational Monitoring
- **Real-time Dashboard**: Track intervention usage, breach probabilities, and spillover effects in real-time. Highlight cases with high spillover risk for proactive management.
- **Queue Performance Metrics**: Monitor queue length and average diagnosis time to detect congestion patterns and adjust swarm allocation.

### Predictive Monitoring
- **Calibration Drift Detection**: Regularly compare observed breach probabilities with predicted probabilities. Use statistical tests (e.g., chi-square test) to detect drift.
- **Model Retraining**: Schedule quarterly model retraining using the latest data to improve prediction accuracy and reduce calibration bias.

### Causal-Effect Monitoring
- **Effect Size Analysis**: Periodically analyze the actual impact of interventions on breach probabilities. Compare predicted versus realized ARR to refine the intervention effect estimates.
- **A/B Testing**: Implement A/B testing for new intervention strategies or segment-specific adjustments to validate their effectiveness before full rollout.

### Capacity Monitoring
- **Slot Utilization**: Track the number of swarm slots used and remaining. Adjust the reserved slot policy based on the probability of incoming regulatory incidents.
- **Resource Allocation**: Monitor specialist availability and adjust the number of swarm slots allocated based on workload forecasts.

### Fairness Monitoring
- **Prioritization Audit**: Regularly audit the prioritization process to ensure fairness across case segments and value levels. Adjust weighting factors as needed to balance value-based and fairness considerations.
- **Feedback Loop Analysis**: Monitor the impact of interventions on future model training data. Ensure that interventions do not skew the training data in a way that diminishes model performance for specific segments.

### Recalibration Trigger
- **Decision Threshold**: If the cumulative calibration error exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.1) or if the net impact of interventions over a month is consistently negative, trigger a model recalibration.
- **Data Collection**: Collect detailed logs of interventions, outcomes, and case attributes to feed into the recalibration process.

By following this structured decision rule, capacity allocation, expected-impact analysis, temporal policy, fairness considerations, and monitoring plan, the intervention policy aims to maximize the expected benefit while minimizing unintended consequences and ensuring operational realism across diverse case segments.
