## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Notation: `Y` = cycle time (arrival to close, hours); `M` = manual-review occurrence; `W` = manual-review queue wait (triage_done to manual_start); `E` = escalation occurrence; `S` = risk score; `C` = complexity; `Q` = queue state at the relevant decision time. The flag is a deterministic function of `S` in these data (every score ≤ 0.61 is unflagged and every score ≥ 0.64 is flagged, so the cutoff `c` lies in (0.61, 0.64)). The flag is therefore not a manipulable property of the case. What can be manipulated is the bundle of organisational responses triggered by it: (a) the priority-lane component (a dedicated triage lane, which in these data is triagers T1/T2), (b) the review-mandate component, (c) the display/label component shown to humans, and (d) the downstream escalation rule and capacity. Each policy P0–P4 switches a different component.

**Framework.** I use three frameworks, each where it fits, and do not use natural direct/indirect effects:

- **Policy-level total effects (E1, E8, E9).** These are defined at block level so that interference is part of the estimand rather than a violation of it.
- **Separable effects of the flag bundle (E2, E3, E5, E10).** The flag acts only through components that can each be switched on or off (lane, mandate, label, escalation rule). Each policy P1–P4 is literally a component switch, so a "direct effect" is simply the effect of the components that remain. This avoids the fiction of do(flag=0) while S and the human information stay unchanged.
- **Interventional (randomized) analogues for queue-type mediators (E3, E4, E5)**, plus a **controlled direct effect** where routing can be forced (E6).

Natural direct/indirect effects are rejected for four reasons. They need cross-world independence, which no experiment can check. Review occurrence changes reviewer assignment and queue state, which then confound escalation and later waits (exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding, the "recanting witness" problem). Queue waits depend on other cases' treatment (no SUTVA). And the flag has no positivity given S away from c. Setting the flag to zero is also not the same as P1 or P2. A high-score case with the flag set to zero still shows its score to humans, who review 100% of unflagged High cases anyway. Its counterfactual is therefore not the observed unflagged population, which has lower scores and different complexity.

**Where the graph breaks standard assumptions.** *Interference*: review and escalation queues are shared. The mandate adds flagged Medium cases to M1/M2/M3 and escalations add to the senior queue, so one case's flag changes others' `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait`. Case-level potential outcomes are then not defined without fixing everyone else's assignment. *Exposure-induced confounding*: `manual_review` (caused by the flag) sets `review_resource` and `U_manual_queue_state`. Both affect escalation and cycle time, so they confound the escalation-to-outcome relation while being consequences of the exposure. This blocks natural indirect effects through escalation. *Treatment-confounder feedback*: over time, long review waits raise WIP, WIP raises `arrival_load` for later arrivals, and load changes triage waits and human referral thresholds. If the score is recalibrated using outcomes of reviewed cases, the flag itself becomes a function of past routing.

Descriptive accounting of the +8.34 h gap in mean cycle time (flag 1 minus flag 0). Each stage's per-case contribution is its occurrence rate times its mean duration given occurrence. Triage wait contributes −2.24 h and triage service −0.35 h. Review wait contributes +5.75 h: +1.1 to +3.9 h from higher review occurrence and +1.9 to +4.6 h from longer waits given review; the range depends on which group's weights serve as reference. Review service contributes +1.60 h (almost all from occurrence). Escalation wait contributes +2.90 h (+2.6 to +2.8 h from occurrence). Senior service contributes +0.75 h and residual −0.07 h. This is bookkeeping, not mediation.

Several contrasts fit the pattern "faster at triage, slower downstream". Flagged cases start triage about 2.3 h sooner and finish triage service faster, and their triage wait barely changes with load (0.20 vs 0.25 h), unlike unflagged cases. After triage, the same cases wait a median 6.75 h for review versus 1.75 h for reviewed unflagged cases. Within High complexity the gap is 8.0 vs 2.0 h, largely at the same reviewer, M2. Escalation adds hours mainly through occurrence (60% vs 20%), not through longer escalation waits. None of this is automatically causal. The lane is confounded with triager identity (T1/T2 only). Flagged cases have more High complexity and higher unobserved risk. Reviewer M2 carries 7 of 8 High cases. Waits depend on shifts and on other cases' queue position; for example, no escalation started before 00:00 even though five cases were ready from 18:42. Cells contain 1–6 cases. Timestamps may record pickup rather than work start. Finally, all flagged cases were reviewed, so there is no within-flag contrast for review.

Rosenbaum-style bounds for matched near-cutoff pairs | Complexity strata, score band | The bias model's structure (U_risk raises escalation and duration and correlates with flag given score and complexity) | Benchmark U_risk against the observed strength of complexity (High vs Medium differs by about 10 h) | If an unobserved risk weaker than complexity explains away the within-stratum +4.6 to +7.0 h, no causal claim about flag handling survives |
| What are the direct and spillover effects under shared queues? | Switchback (time-block) cluster randomization of P0 vs P2 (or P3), possibly crossed with reviewer pools. Identifies block-level total effects and spillovers onto non-eligible cases (E1, E9) | Block strata: weekday, shift, forecast load | Partial interference confined within blocks after washout; stable demand | Carry-over test (early-block outcomes depend on the previous block's arm); compare cases arriving early vs late in a block | Negative spillover (worse waits for flagged High or unflagged cases) would reverse P2's net value. No change in others' waits would mean waits are not load-driven |
| Do timestamps mean what the intervals assume? | Semantics audit: compare workflow/system logs (assign, claim, open, save, submit) with stage timestamps; check M2's overlapping intervals and the midnight escalation start window | Resource, stage, hour | The audit logs exist and are reliable | Share of stage "start" events that match first user activity | If manual_start records the second reviewer's join or a batch release, "review wait" is partly service or calendar time, and the mechanism story must change |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0: current system | Flagged triage fast (≈0.2 h wait); unflagged ≈2.5 h | Flagged review wait ≈6.75 h; escalation ≈7 h for 60% of flagged | Flagged ≈16.5 h mean vs ≈8.2 h unflagged (crude) | Unknown: no ground truth; current controls maximal | Mandatory reviews load shared reviewers; lane partitions triage | Observed, descriptive | Baseline and comparator in all pilots |
| P1: hide label, keep score, human info and automated routing | About 0 if the lane is automated. If humans trigger the lane, flagged triage wait rises by about 1–2.5 h | Could shorten review pickup if deprioritisation is label-driven; otherwise about 0 | 0 to −3 h for flagged (highly uncertain) | Low–moderate: score still visible, but reduced vigilance possible | Minimal | Weak (label effect not separable from triager identity) | Cheap secondary arm to isolate the label component; not the primary lever |
| P2: keep lane, remove mandatory review (discretion kept) | Unchanged | Fewer reviews: WIP falls at M1/M3 (Medium cases) and somewhat at M2; shorter waits for remaining reviews if waits are load-driven | Waived cases −4 to −8 h; others 0 to −2 h through reduced load; High cases largely unaffected (reviewed anyway) | Moderate–high for missed risk if the score omits risk; possible regulatory conflict if the mandate is required | Positive (frees capacity) | Moderate for the occurrence mechanism; weak for quality | Pilot in a bounded risk band with retrospective audit (selected) |
| P3: current routing plus protected review capacity for flagged | Unchanged | Flagged review wait falls if waits are capacity-driven. The observed idle gaps and overtaking suggest part of the wait is not capacity-driven, so gains may be small. Escalations reach seniors sooner | Flagged 0 to −5 h; senior queue may absorb part of the gain | Low | Negative for unflagged if carved from the shared pool; costly if new staff | Weak–moderate (depends on the queue-state analysis) | Conditional: adopt only if the queue-state analysis shows congestion-driven waits |
| P4: change escalation criteria (P4a) or senior capacity/timing (P4b) | Unchanged | P4a: fewer escalations. P4b: shorter senior waits (no escalation started before 00:00, so shift alignment may help) | Would-be escalated cases: P4a −3 to −9 h; P4b −2 to −6 h | P4a high (all four rejections passed through escalation); P4b low | P4b benefits all groups sharing the senior team | Weak (escalation confounded by complexity and risk) | Prefer P4b as an operations fix after a timestamp audit; avoid P4a without quality labels |

**Pilot: restricted P2 ("risk-tiered review mandate").**
- **Eligibility:** flagged cases with score from the cutoff to cutoff + 0.12, Medium complexity, no triage red flag, and no regulatory review requirement. The priority lane stays. Staff may still request review, with the reason logged.
- **Assignment:** switchback randomization of 6-hour blocks (P0 vs P2), stratified by weekday and shift. The first 2 hours after each switch are a washout excluded from analysis. All eligible cases in a block get that block's arm. A random 30% of waived cases get a non-blocking retrospective audit by off-queue staff.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail:** a block reverts to P0 if reviewer or senior WIP exceeds the P0 90th percentile, or if the flagged-High review wait exceeds the baseline median + 2 h.
- **Primary outcomes:** block-level mean cycle time of all arrivals; audited material-miss rate among waived cases against a pre-registered non-inferiority margin.
- **Secondary outcomes:** cycle time of eligible cases; stage waits for non-eligible flagged and unflagged cases (spillover); discretionary review rate; escalation and rejection rates; 90-day reversals, losses and complaints.
- **Minimum process data:** case ID, score version, flag, complexity, block and arm; assignment, claim, start and completion timestamps per stage, with resource IDs; shift rosters; queue snapshots; override reasons; audit findings; follow-up outcomes.
- **Stopping conditions:** the upper confidence bound of the material-miss rate exceeds the margin; any severe loss linked to a waiver; the guardrail triggers in three consecutive blocks; a compliance objection; or the planned 40 blocks are completed.

## 7. Reproducible PM4Py-oriented Python

The code below builds an event log in which absent stages produce no events. It uses `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the start-timestamp key. It discovers frequency and performance DFGs per flag group; with start timestamps set, a performance-DFG edge measures the completion of one activity to the start of the next. It also computes explicit stage intervals. `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` is used only as a cross-check. It gives case-level totals: service = the sum over a case's events of (complete − start), sojourn = last completion − first start, and waiting = sojourn − service. It cannot attribute waiting to a specific stage, and it mixes queueing with calendar time.

MIN_SUPPORT = 5
TS_COLS = ["arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "manual_start", "manual_done",
           "escalation_start", "escalation_done", "close"]

# 1. Parse; "-" means the stage did not occur and becomes NaT (never zero).
cases = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV), na_values=["-"], keep_default_na=False)
for c in TS_COLS:
    cases[c] = pd.to_datetime(cases[c], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M", errors="raise")
assert cases["triage_start"].notna().all() and cases["close"].notna().all()

# 2. Wide -> event-level. Register and Close are instantaneous (start == complete).
#    Absent Manual Review / Escalation stages produce NO event rows.
stage_specs = [
    ("Register", "arrival", "arrival", None),
    ("Triage", "triage_start", "triage_done", "triage_resource"),
    ("Manual Review", "manual_start", "manual_done", "review_resource"),
    ("Escalation", "escalation_start", "escalation_done", None),  # senior resource not logged
    ("Close", "close", "close", None),
]
case_attrs = ["risk_score", "flag", "complexity", "arrival_load", "outcome"]
rows = []
for _, r in cases.iterrows():
    for act, s_col, e_col, res_col in stage_specs:
        if pd.isna(r[s_col]) or pd.isna(r[e_col]):
            continue
        if r[e_col] < r[s_col]:
            raise ValueError(f"{r['case']} {act}: completion before start")
        rows.append({
            "case:concept:name": r["case"],
            "concept:name": act,
            "start_timestamp": r[s_col],
            "time:timestamp": r[e_col],
            "org:resource": (r[res_col] if res_col and pd.notna(r[res_col]) else pd.NA),
            **{f"case:{a}": r[a] for a in case_attrs},
        })
events = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "start_timestamp"])

# 3. PM4Py formatting with explicit start timestamp key.
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)
print(f"Event rows: {len(events)} (no events fabricated for absent stages)")
print(events.groupby("concept:name").size().rename("event_count").to_string(), "\n")

# 4. Frequency and performance DFGs per flag group.
#    Performance-DFG edge values are elapsed times between consecutive events in a case
#    and mix queueing, calendar effects and (depending on timestamp semantics) service.
for flag_value, grp in events.groupby("case:flag"):
    n_cases = grp["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    freq_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        grp, activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name")
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        grp, activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name")
    print(f"=== flag={flag_value} (cases={n_cases}) ===")
    print("start activities:", dict(sa), "| end activities:", dict(ea))
    for edge, cnt in sorted(freq_dfg.items()):
        perf = perf_dfg.get(edge, {})
        med_h = perf.get("median", float("nan")) / 3600
        mean_h = perf.get("mean", float("nan")) / 3600
        print(f"  {edge[0]:>13} -> {edge[1]:<13} freq={cnt:2d}  median={med_h:5.2f}h  mean={mean_h:5.2f}h")
    if n_cases < 30:
        warnings.warn(f"flag={flag_value}: only {n_cases} cases; DFG frequencies/performances are unstable.")
    print()
warnings.warn("DFG performance differences between flag groups are descriptive: they are confounded by "
              "complexity, unobserved risk, reviewer assignment, queue state and time of day, and must not "
              "be read as direct/indirect effects of the flag.")


# 5. Explicit stage intervals (hours). Conditional stages are NaN when absent.
def hours(a, b):
    return (cases[b] - cases[a]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600


st = cases[["case", "flag", "complexity", "arrival_load", "risk_score", "review_resource", "outcome"]].copy()
st["has_review"] = cases["manual_start"].notna()
st["has_escalation"] = cases["escalation_start"].notna()
st["triage_wait"] = hours("arrival", "triage_start")
st["triage_service"] = hours("triage_start", "triage_done")
st["review_wait"] = hours("triage_done", "manual_start")          # NaN if no review
st["review_service"] = hours("manual_start", "manual_done")
st["escalation_wait"] = hours("manual_done", "escalation_start")  # NaN if no escalation

st["cycle_time"] = hours("arrival", "close")

interval_cols = ["triage_wait", "triage_service", "review_wait", "review_service",
                 "escalation_wait", "senior_service", "residual_to_close", "cycle_time"]
assert (st[interval_cols].min() >= 0).all(), "negative interval: check timestamp semantics/timezone"

# Cross-check case-level PM4Py service/waiting (sum of event service; waiting = sojourn - service).
cs = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    events.copy(), activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
cs = cs.groupby("case:concept:name")[["@@service_time", "@@waiting_time", "@@sojourn_time"]].first() / 3600
st = st.merge(cs, left_on="case", right_index=True)
explicit_service = st[["triage_service", "review_service", "senior_service"]].sum(axis=1, min_count=1)
assert (abs(st["@@service_time"] - explicit_service) < 1e-9).all()
assert (abs(st["@@sojourn_time"] - st["cycle_time"]) < 1e-9).all()


def summarize(df, label):
    out = {"subgroup": label, "n": len(df),
           "n_review": int(df["has_review"].sum()), "n_escalation": int(df["has_escalation"].sum())}
    for c in interval_cols:
        vals = df[c].dropna()
        out[c] = f"{vals.median():.2f} (n={len(vals)})" if len(vals) else "NA"
    for c, n_col in [("review_wait", "n_review"), ("escalation_wait", "n_escalation")]:
        if 0 < out[n_col] < MIN_SUPPORT:
            warnings.warn(f"[{label}] {c}: only {out[n_col]} cases observed that stage.")
    if len(df) < MIN_SUPPORT:
        warnings.warn(f"[{label}] subgroup has only {len(df)} cases.")
    return out

groups += [(f"complexity={c}", g) for c, g in st.groupby("complexity")]

flagged = st[st.flag == 1]
groups += [(f"flag=1, load={l}", g) for l, g in flagged.groupby("arrival_load")]
groups += [(f"flag=1, escalated={e}", g) for e, g in flagged.groupby("has_escalation")]

               if len(st[(st.flag == f) & (st.complexity == c)]) == 0]

    warnings.warn(f"Positivity violation: no cases in (flag, complexity) cells {empty_cells}.")
if (flagged["has_review"]).all():
    warnings.warn("All flagged cases were reviewed: effects of flag with review absent are not identified.")

# 6. Descriptive (non-causal) accounting of the flagged-unflagged gap in mean cycle time.
#    Per-case contribution = P(stage occurs) * mean duration given occurrence.
print("Per-case mean contribution (h) = occurrence rate x conditional mean duration:")
contrib = {}
for f, g in st.groupby("flag"):
    contrib[f] = {c: g[c].fillna(0).mean() for c in interval_cols}
contrib = pd.DataFrame(contrib).rename(columns={0: "flag0", 1: "flag1"})
contrib["gap"] = contrib["flag1"] - contrib["flag0"]
print(contrib.round(2).to_string(), "\n")
for stage, occ in [("review_wait", "has_review"), ("escalation_wait", "has_escalation")]:
    p0, p1 = st.loc[st.flag == 0, occ].mean(), st.loc[st.flag == 1, occ].mean()
    d0 = st.loc[(st.flag == 0) & st[occ], stage].mean()
    d1 = st.loc[(st.flag == 1) & st[occ], stage].mean()
    print(f"{stage}: occurrence part {(p1 - p0) * d0:.2f}-{(p1 - p0) * d1:.2f} h, "
          f"duration-given-occurrence part {p0 * (d1 - d0):.2f}-{p1 * (d1 - d0):.2f} h "
          f"(range depends on reference weights; not a natural indirect effect)")

# Queue-state signal: later-ready cases that started earlier at the same reviewer.
print("\nReviewer overtaking (later-ready case started earlier at the same reviewer):")
rv = st[st.has_review].merge(cases[["case", "triage_done", "manual_start"]], on="case")
for m, g in rv.groupby("review_resource"):
    g = g.sort_values("triage_done")
    for i, a in g.iterrows():
        for j, b in g.iterrows():
            if b["triage_done"] > a["triage_done"] and b["manual_start"] < a["manual_start"]:
                print(f"  {m}: {b['case']} (flag={b['flag']}) overtook {a['case']} (flag={a['flag']})")
warnings.warn("Only reviewers appearing in this 20-case extract are visible; 'idle' gaps may hide "
              "work on cases outside the extract, shift calendars, or assignment-vs-pickup semantics.")

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** audits or follow-up may show that near-cutoff flagged Medium cases carry materially higher unobserved risk that mandatory review catches (for example, review finds decision-changing issues far more often than discretionary review would). In that case the mandate is doing real protective work, and I would switch from P2 to P3 or a dispatch fix.
2. **Queue interference:** a full-log queue reconstruction may show that the apparent idle gaps were filled by work outside the extract and that flagged waits scale with WIP ahead in the queue. That would replace the "flag-specific hold/deprioritisation" interpretation with plain congestion. It would also mean removing reviews mainly speeds up other cases through spillover, so switchback designs and protected capacity become the primary lever.
3. **Decision quality:** the long pre-review interval for flagged cases may turn out to be evidence collection (documents, third-party checks) whose results change decisions. Escalated cases may also show lower 90-day losses or reversals than comparable non-escalated cases. Then the delay is part of quality, and shortening it (P2, P4a) would be a false economy.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** manual_start may record a claim, a second reviewer joining, or a batch release rather than the start of work. Or priority-lane triage_done may be logged at hand-off before triage work is actually complete. Then "faster triage, slower review" is partly a relabelling of the same work, and the stage decomposition must be redone on audit-log events.
5. **Positivity / overlap:** with more data near the cutoff, and flagged/unflagged cases within the same complexity and score band, the regression discontinuity or matched analysis may show no cycle-time jump after accounting for complexity. Then the gap is case mix, and no change to flag handling is warranted; capacity planning for High-complexity cases would be the target instead.
6. **Reviewer behaviour change:** under P2, reviewers may pull most eligible cases into discretionary review (the review rate stays above about 80%), or become less careful once the mandate is lifted. The observed overtaking may also prove to be cherry-picking of easier unflagged cases. In each case, a dispatch rule (priority-consistent pickup, WIP caps) would be preferred over removing the mandate.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** In 20 cases, flagged cases start triage about 2.3 h sooner, via a lane staffed only by T1/T2. They still close about 8.3 h later on average (median 16.45 vs 5.75 h). Every flagged case was reviewed, compared with 4/10 unflagged. Flagged reviews waited a median 6.75 h, versus 1.75 h for unflagged reviews, even at the same reviewer, and unflagged cases were sometimes picked up first. Escalation is 60% vs 20%, almost entirely in High complexity. Escalation service and residual-to-close do not differ.

**Plausible mechanism.** The triage gain is real but absorbed downstream. The mandate adds reviews for Medium cases (occurrence mediation). A hold, dispatch order or shift timing at the review queue delays flagged cases beyond congestion (waiting mediation). Escalation adds time mainly through complexity-driven occurrence. Case mix explains part of the gap: within-stratum differences are about +4.6 to +7 h.

**Unidentified.** Natural direct and indirect effects: they need cross-world assumptions, and exposure-induced confounding (via the assigned reviewer and queue state) and shared-queue interference break them. Also unknown: the effect on flagged cases without review (positivity), the role of hidden risk, the decision quality of any regime, and whether timestamps mark actual work.

**Most useful next evidence.** A switchback-randomized, risk-tiered P2 pilot with retrospective audits, paired with a timestamp audit and a queue-state reconstruction. It separates occurrence effects from waiting effects, measures spillover, and produces quality labels. The question then shifts from what the flag does to which part of the organisation's response to it is worth its delay.
