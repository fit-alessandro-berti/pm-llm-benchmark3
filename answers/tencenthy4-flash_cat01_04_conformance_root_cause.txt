The cleanest abductive story is stratified. UI release 4.2 changed the emitted activity name from `Classify` to `Risk Screen` without changing the underlying screening step; that alone turns many otherwise fitting traces into simultaneous log/model moves. From 3 June the gateway added `Queue Routed`, a mostly technical routing marker with missing resource data; feeding it to a control-flow model adds log moves. From 15 June, warehouse-ingestion timestamps made `Notify` appear before `Decision` for a subset of cases, adding order moves. Each mechanism is supported by the monotonic repair sequence: fitness rises from 32% raw to 58% after label mapping, 81% after technical-event removal, and 89% after source-sequence ordering. In August-September a genuine policy change added `Supervisor Sign-off`, but the model remained control-flow-only and high-value status was omitted, so sign-off looks deviant even when policy-compliant; adding an optional sign-off raises fitness to 86% but is overpermissive because it cannot enforce the EUR 50,000 guard. Real noncompliance remains possible: enhanced reviews without compliance checks and overrides may be true events, yet they may also be missing events or legitimate corrections. Stable approval and appeal rates modestly weaken a large violation wave, but they cannot prove data error: detection is incomplete, low-frequency violations can be diluted, and outcomes may lag or be insensitive. Equally, they do not rule out localized noncompliance requiring audit.

def dominant_alignment_moves(diagnostics, top_n=8):
    counts = Counter()
    for item in diagnostics:
        for log_activity, model_activity in item.get("alignment", []):
            if log_activity == model_activity:
                continue
            if not is_skip(log_activity) and is_skip(model_activity):
                counts[f"log move: {log_activity}"] += 1
            elif is_skip(log_activity) and not is_skip(model_activity):
                counts[f"model move: {model_activity}"] += 1
            else:
                counts[
                    f"other: {log_activity} / {model_activity}"
                ] += 1
    return counts.most_common(top_n)

## 7. Provisional conclusion

Primary explanation. The measured collapse is most plausibly a provenance and model-governance problem, not evidence that most reviewers stopped following the process. The June-July deterioration begins exactly with UI relabeling, gateway technical events, and the ETL timestamp change; staged repairs recover fitness from 32% to about 89%. `Risk Screen` substituting for `Classify`, `Queue Routed` being inserted after screening, and `Decision`/`Notify` reordered by ingestion time account for most alignment moves. In August-September, the policy-created `Supervisor Sign-off` adds a second, different problem: the normative tree is obsolete and non-data-aware. Optional sign-off improves fit, but because the model cannot enforce EUR 50,000, that improvement is partly an overfit to control flow rather than a faithful policy model.

Separate operational assessment. A real compliance concern may remain. The 25 and 30 enhanced-review-without-check traces, Manual Override cases, and incomplete incident detection are genuine signals. However, the small scale, stable approval/appeal rates, lack of confirmed incident increase, and availability of legitimate override explanations mean noncompliance is currently a localized risk, not the primary explanation for the aggregate fitness drop.

Confidence: high, around 90%, that label, technical, and order artifacts dominate the measured June-July collapse; high, around 80%, that model obsolescence dominates the incremental August-September gap; low-to-moderate, around 35-45%, that true noncompliance materially contributes.

Immediate actions: IT should freeze and document event contracts, reprocess source-sequence timestamps, tag technical events, and recover missing source events. Process owners should update the model to include high-value sign-off as a data-aware constraint and decide how Manual Override is represented. Compliance should audit a stratified sample of enhanced-review-no-check, high-value/no-signoff, and override cases, plus reviewer-level outliers.

Do not yet claim that reviewers became broadly noncompliant, that the optional sign-off model is policy-correct, or that unchanged appeal rates prove no compliance issue exists.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. Version every normative model with effective dates, owner approval, policy references, and explicit links to release or policy changes.

2. Define event-semantic contracts that separate display labels from canonical activity IDs, with backward-compatible mappings for renamed UI labels.

3. Maintain a technical-event policy that classifies system, routing, and infrastructure events separately from business activities and controls their inclusion in conformance logs.

4. Preserve timestamp provenance by recording source-system time, ingestion time, sequence number, and timezone for every event, with an authoritative ordering rule.

5. Encode data-aware constraints such as amount thresholds and authorization rules; do not approximate policy guards with overpermissive optional control-flow activities.

6. Require post-release validation that compares variants, DFGs, alignments, fitness, precision, and business outcomes before and after each UI, ETL, gateway, or policy change.
