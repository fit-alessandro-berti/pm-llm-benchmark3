## 1. Interaction-graph description

Represent Jobs, Components, ComponentLots, Machines, Tools, Batches, and Shipments as typed object nodes. Add event nodes with timestamped event-to-object incidence; a shared event is a hyperedge, not several independent executions. Preserve relationship qualifiers and, where available, their validity intervals and provenance.

Compact edge list (`D` = supplied relation/event observation; `I` = inferred dependency):

```text
D  Ji --requires--> Ci                         i = 1,...,6
D  L7 --contains--> {C2,C5}; L8 --contains--> {C1,C3}
D  B1 --batches--> {J1,J2,J3}; B2 --batches--> {J4,J5}
D  S1 --consolidates--> {J1,J4}
D  S2 --consolidates--> {J2,J3}; S3 --consolidates--> {J5,J6}
D  Cure@12:05 --related-to--> {B1,OVEN-1,RACK-7,J1,J2,J3,C1,C2,C3}
D  Process(J4)@11:45 --related-to--> {J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A}
D  Quarantine@10:20,Release@13:50 --related-to--> {L7,C2,C5,J2,J5}*
I  J3 --may-block-via-FIX-A--> J4
I  B1 --shares-OVEN-1-and-RACK-7-with--> B2
I  J4 --may-hold-shipment-partner--> J1
```

The starred lot incidence is supplied in the integrated evidence but absent from the source-only Job extraction. Its Job links require provenance labels; they cannot all be called direct source observations. FIX-A acquisition/release are observed; continuous exclusive possession between them is an inference requiring resource semantics. “May-block” edges express hypotheses, not causality established by graph reachability.

Higher-order motifs include (1) Job–Component–Batch–Machine–Tool synchronization in B1; (2) Job–Component–ComponentLot–Component–Job coupling between J2 and J5; (3) Job–Tool–Machine–Job contention connecting J3 and J4; and (4) Batch–Job–Shipment–Job convergence connecting B2, J4, S1, and J1. These overlapping memberships connect all six jobs. L8 provides structural connectivity between J1 and J3, but no observed L8 delay.

Interaction frequency is not bottleneck severity: 15 Job–Tool interactions cannot outrank two Batch–Machine interactions by count alone. Job–Machine counts omit waiting. The two directional Job–ComponentLot rows must not automatically be summed into eight distinct interactions.

Visibility abbreviations: J = Job, M = Machine, B = Batch, T = Tool, L = ComponentLot, S = Shipment; visibility depends on retained events and links.

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | Visible | Hidden or duplicated; misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Individual activity order and elapsed transitions. | Shared cure can appear in three traces; lot events are omitted here. Medians hide partner dependence. “Normal DFG and no duration beyond 1.8 standard deviations means no serious waiting” is unsupported with six jobs and common delays. |
| Machine | Machine sequences, recorded activity and inter-event gaps. | Eligibility, tools, and partner readiness disappear. Point-event gaps need not equal idle intervals. “54% CNC utilization proves J4 could start immediately” ignores FIX-A and other prerequisites. |
| Batch | Shared cure and batch-level load/start/unload. | Member readiness disappears unless explicitly linked; the same rack appears across separate batch cases. “Ten minutes after final readiness means negligible batch delay” overlooks early members' hours. |
| Tool | Acquisitions, releases, cleaning and calibration if retained. | Hold intervals may include inactive possession; cross-resource feasibility disappears. “Long holds prove useful utilization” confuses possession with productive use. Shared events can recur in multiple tool traces. |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine and release, shared eligibility state. | Per-component demand, exceptions and downstream joins disappear. “Both L7 jobs were blocked until 13:50” contradicts J2's override. Expanding the lot event across jobs can inflate counts. |
| Shipment | Create, Load, Dispatch and final handling. | Packing before shipment creation and partner asymmetry are absent. “Median Load→Dispatch of 0.3 h means shipping causes little delay” misses J1's 355-minute packed wait. |

Low machine utilization is compatible with long waiting because processing needs a conjunction of machine, tool, material, policy, and membership conditions. A free oven cannot cure an ineligible group; a free tester lacks CAL-3 during recalibration. Averaged utilization also conceals peaks and calendar mismatches. Shared events must retain their original identities across projections.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Fixture-to-shipment chain: Job–Tool–Machine–Batch–Shipment.** Temporal precedence: J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40; J4 starts CNC-2 at 11:45, becomes B2-ready at 13:10, joins its 14:50 start, packs at 19:10, and S1 dispatches at 19:25. Resource feasibility: exclusive FIX-A use would prevent J4 overlapping J3's hold. Policy constraints: B2 waits for its members; S1 consolidates J4 with J1. Causal speculation: shortening J3's hold might advance J4 and J1's dispatch. However, J5's 14:40 readiness could absorb that advance, and later J4 operations remain unexplained.

**Lot-to-batch chain: ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch–Tool–Machine.** Temporal precedence: L7 is released at 13:50, RACK-7 at 14:35, J5 becomes ready at 14:40, and B2 starts on OVEN-1 at 14:50. Resource feasibility: a ready rack and oven are insufficient while C5/J5 remains ineligible or unprepared. Policy constraints: lot release binds J5, while J2's override permits B1 at 12:05. Causal speculation: faster valid disposition could advance J5 and thus J4, but rack readiness limits the gain. Uniformly enforcing the hold instead could delay J2 and B1's J1/J3.

**Calibration-to-consolidation chain: Job–Tool–Machine–Shipment.** Temporal precedence: J5 finishes TEST-1 at 16:35; CAL-3 recalibration ends at 17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50. Resource feasibility: every final test requires CAL-3 despite TEST-1 being idle. Policy constraints: S3 contains J5/J6; propagation additionally assumes final testing precedes packing and dispatch requires both partners. Causal speculation: calibration delays J6 and possibly S3, which departs 35 minutes after its last packing event. Missing packing times prevent identifying the last partner, and succession does not prove J5 triggered recalibration.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

Evaluate complete interacting groups or comparable time blocks, accounting for carryover on shared tools. Independent job randomization would contaminate controls: nearly every treatment can affect partners. Compare workload, staffing, quality, and due dates, measuring job lateness, readiness-to-start tails, throughput, packed waiting, costs, and quality outcomes together.

| Intervention and target | Expected benefit; helped objects | Potentially harmed objects; new cost or risk | Perverse metric | Object-centric evaluation design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flexible batch membership, capped early-member waits, or smaller validated batches | Reduce synchronization; advance eligible J1/J2 when J3 is late, potentially J4 when J5 is late. | J3/J5 or B2 may lose oven slots; extra cycles, setups, energy and rack cleaning; cure quality needs validation. | Average batch wait encourages premature readiness declarations or tiny batches. | Compare matched oven schedules with stable readiness definitions; track each member, original event IDs, rack cycles, throughput and quality. |
| Shorten FIX-A possession; consider qualified spare fixtures/CAL-3 and scheduled rack cleaning | Reduce feasible tool blocking for J4/J6 and later batches. | J3/J5 may lose preferred slots; additional tools require qualification, maintenance, storage, and traceability. | Tool utilization rewards unnecessary holding or deferred calibration. | Separately pilot release rules and added capacity; join tool holds to eligible-job demand, machine availability, test quality and downstream shipments. |
| Permit partial dispatch or regroup shipments by readiness | Reduce J1's consolidation wait, potentially S2/S3 asymmetry. | J4 and other customers may lose freight capacity or consolidation economies; more shipments, handling, emissions and customer complexity. | Dispatch lead time rewards delaying shipment creation. | Compare agreed eligible shipment groups using packing-based waits, customer receipt dates, partner lateness, freight cost and partial-delivery acceptance. |
| Faster valid L7 disposition and explicit component-level release/override governance | Help C5/J5, B2/J4, and potentially S1/S3; clarify J2 eligibility. | Other lots may lose quality-team attention; selective release requires evidence and can increase testing or traceability costs. | Hold duration rewards premature release or unrecorded overrides. | Compare similar quality incidents with effective hold intervals, demand times, authorized overrides, defects and downstream gate times. |
| Preserve source and enriched OCEL links; log readiness, blocked reasons, holds and overrides | Improve attribution for all jobs, especially J2/J5; does not itself remove physical delay. | Operators and integration teams bear capture burden; erroneous enrichment can contaminate downstream decisions. | Completeness scores reward speculative links or fabricated wait labels. | Replay identical immutable events through both pipelines, audit provenance and timestamps, and reconcile unique event counts before comparing operational performance. |

Buying machine capacity should follow evidence that eligible work queues while the required machine is genuinely busy. Otherwise it can increase upstream work-in-progress and downstream congestion without advancing shipment completion.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The block assumes a PM4Py installation exposing the requested APIs and standard OCEL attributes. Graphs show interactions, not causal direction. Compare distinct event-pair counts with distinct-object counts; neither measures duration or proves congestion. Supplemental readiness below comes explicitly from the question, not reconstructed machine gaps. Lifecycle features support investigation, not a six-job causal classifier.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve the supplied OCEL: do not silently enrich or overwrite its links.
interaction_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
interaction_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    ocel, graph_type="object_interaction"
)
object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
event_type_object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
print(interaction_summary)

# OC-DFG edge metrics count different units. Never add them together.
edge_metrics = ocdfg["edges"]
rows = []
for object_type in sorted(set(edge_metrics["event_couples"]) |
                          set(edge_metrics["unique_objects"])):
    event_pairs = edge_metrics["event_couples"].get(object_type, {})
    unique_objects = edge_metrics["unique_objects"].get(object_type, {})
    for edge in set(event_pairs) | set(unique_objects):
        rows.append({
            "object_type": object_type,
            "source_activity": edge[0],
            "target_activity": edge[1],
            "distinct_event_pairs": len(event_pairs.get(edge, set())),
            "distinct_objects": len(unique_objects.get(edge, set())),
        })
frequency_comparison = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print(frequency_comparison.to_string(index=False))

# Six projections are diagnostic comparators, not interchangeable case notions.
types = ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]
present_types = set(ocel.objects[ocel.object_type_column])
flat_logs, performance = {}, {}
for object_type in types:
    if object_type not in present_types:
        continue
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    flat_logs[object_type] = flat
    performance[object_type] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        flat,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )  # Returns performance DFG plus start/end activity summaries.
    print(object_type, performance[object_type][0])

job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")

# Audit event identity, observed incidence and separate O2O qualifiers.
# Presence in this OCEL does NOT establish that a link existed at source.
eid = ocel.event_id_column
oid = ocel.object_id_column
activity = ocel.event_activity
timestamp = ocel.event_timestamp
incidence = ocel.relations[[eid, oid]].drop_duplicates()
event_objects = incidence.merge(
    ocel.events[[eid, activity, timestamp]], on=eid, validate="many_to_one"
)
lot_audit = event_objects[
    event_objects[activity].isin(["Quarantine Lot", "Release Lot"])
].sort_values([timestamp, eid, oid])
print(lot_audit.to_string(index=False))
qualified_o2o = getattr(ocel, "o2o", None)
print("Explicit O2O relations:", qualified_o2o)
# Inspect source/enrichment provenance and the separate override system before
# interpreting missing Job links or propagating holds through O2O relations.

# Supplied same-day times; timedeltas avoid inventing a calendar date/timezone.
readiness = pd.DataFrame([
    ("B1", "J1", "08:10:00", "12:05:00"),
    ("B1", "J2", "08:25:00", "12:05:00"),
    ("B1", "J3", "11:55:00", "12:05:00"),
    ("B2", "J4", "13:10:00", "14:50:00"),
    ("B2", "J5", "14:40:00", "14:50:00"),
], columns=["batch", "job", "reported_ready", "batch_start"])
for col in ["reported_ready", "batch_start"]:
    readiness[col] = pd.to_timedelta(readiness[col])
readiness["latest_member_ready"] = readiness.groupby("batch")[
    "reported_ready"
].transform("max")
for name, later, earlier in [
    ("wait_min", "batch_start", "reported_ready"),
    ("readiness_skew_min", "latest_member_ready", "reported_ready"),
    ("launch_residual_min", "batch_start", "latest_member_ready"),
]:
    readiness[name] = (
        readiness[later] - readiness[earlier]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60
readiness["eligibility_caveat"] = readiness["job"].map(
    {"J2": "Lot hold and override: verify effective eligibility intervals"}
).fillna("Readiness still requires validation")
print(readiness.to_string(index=False))

# Conditional gate model: changing one gate may produce no start-time change.
t = pd.to_timedelta
b2_gates = {"J4": t("13:10:00"), "J5": t("14:40:00"),
            "RACK-7": t("14:35:00"), "OVEN-1": t("12:55:00")}
baseline_gate = max(b2_gates.values())
no_rack_gate = max(v for k, v in b2_gates.items() if k != "RACK-7")
print("B2 rack-only saving, fixed other gates (min):",
      (baseline_gate - no_rack_gate).total_seconds() / 60)
# A constant ten-minute launch allowance cancels in that comparison.
# For actual blocked-time estimation, additionally join valid readiness,
# resource calendars, exclusive holds, quality eligibility, and policy state.
# Intersect simultaneous restrictions; do not sum them or label every gap wait.
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken batch synchronization as the leading explanation:

1. Audits show J1/J2's recorded readiness omitted unfinished prerequisites until near 12:05.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was independently unavailable throughout B1's apparent synchronization interval.
3. Batch membership was retrospective bookkeeping and full membership was never required to start.
4. Controlled earlier J3 readiness or feasible batch splitting fails to advance J1/J2 starts under comparable conditions.
5. A larger matched history shows little early-member waiting, with most lateness arising in nonbatched operations.
6. Earlier cures consistently fail to advance packing or dispatch because later constraints absorb gains. This weakens intervention priority, not the observed pre-cure waiting itself.

Additional discriminating evidence:

1. Readiness creation, revocation and effective times, prerequisite definitions, route stage, and responsible source.
2. Resource request, grant, acquire/release and actual use intervals; tool capacities, compatibility, cleaning/quarantine and calibration triggers.
3. Machine processing start/end, setup, breakdown, staffing calendars, reservations and eligible queues.
4. Effective lot/component hold intervals, demand times, scope, inspection outcomes, and J2's override authorization and validity.
5. Time-valid batch/shipment memberships, minimum-fill rules, planned departures, priorities and permitted splits.
6. Complete packing, loading, dispatch, rework and test lifecycle times, plus due dates and customer acceptance constraints.
7. Immutable event IDs, original versus enriched E2O/O2O qualifiers, clock synchronization, missing-event indicators and extraction rules.
