- **Peripheral:** None.
- **Complexity, substitution & authority:** Strictly bounded to deterministic, low-complexity intake (median 0.11). Nora has zero exception-handling or closure authority; her `A2 Validate` acts as an automated precheck rather than binding entitlement adjudication on complex cases.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow and bridging roles are not inherently improper; they frequently emerge to absorb handoff friction, cover absences, and connect functional silos. However, because they sit outside formal org charts, they introduce distinct operational trade-offs that management must make visible.

### 1. R07_Giorgio — Commercial Shadow Role & Front-Office/Commercial Bridge
- **Behavioral evidence:** Formally titled *Senior Service Agent* in Front Office, yet holds *pricing* certification and performs 72 `A7` and 88 `A8` events (41 `A7` and 70 `A8` outside Weeks 3–4 substitution), driving his 0.71 similarity to Fatima and 0.58 median complexity.
- **Benefits:**
  - Provides immediate operational continuity when Fatima is absent (preventing a complete commercial standstill in Weeks 3–4).
  - Eliminates handoff latency on moderately complex front-office cases that require quick pricing adjustments or customer negotiation (fragment 4).
  - Preserves institutional knowledge across both customer service and commercial policy (7.4y tenure).
- **Risks:**
  - **Efficiency and quality gap under full load:** Median pricing review duration nearly doubled (38  74 minutes) during Fatima's absence, showing he is not a drop-in equivalent at scale.
  - **Hidden capacity drain:** Shadowing commercial work pulls senior capacity out of Front Office without formal workload accounting.
  - **Authority ambiguity:** His 20 `A9 Approval` events risk blurring pricing execution with supervisory sign-off (segregation-of-duties concern).

### 2. R13_Mateo — Cross-Functional Escalation Broker & Informal Triage Filter
- **Behavioral evidence:** Touches all 14 activities, mediates fragments 3, 5, and 6, leads the organization in `A13 Reopen` (42) and critical cases (76), substituted on `A9` in Week 5, and informally pre-screened `A6` security referrals in Weeks 7–8.
- **Benefits:**
  - Bridges silos across Front Office, Technical, Commercial, Risk, and Management during complex incidents and reopens.
  - Protects scarce specialist bandwidth—his Weeks 7–8 triage reduced false security escalation complaints to Erik.
  - Absorbs rework fallout from temporary agents (fragment 6), preventing customer-facing breakdowns.
- **Risks:**
  - **False-negative filtering risk:** Without security certification, Mateo's informal triage delayed two critical Enterprise Security cases by >2 days.
  - **Phantom authority and rework:** Performing 21 preliminary `A9` approvals during Hana's absence resulted in 13 rechecks (62% rework rate), creating duplicate effort and unclear accountability.
  - **Key-person bottleneck:** Acting as universal organizational "glue" concentrates tacit routing knowledge in a single coordinator.

### 3. R05_Erik — Rare-but-Critical Security Specialist & Risk Gatekeeper
- **Behavioral evidence:** Accounts for only 170 events (~1.6% of log events) yet holds the highest case complexity (median 0.86) and anchors security review in fragment 3.
- **Benefits:**
  - Provides specialized, high-assurance risk control that prevents catastrophic compliance or enterprise security failures.
  - Contributes expert input into complex diagnostics (`A3:16`), configuration (`A5:10`), implementation (`A10:18`), and security knowledge capture (`A14:9`).
- **Risks:**
  - **Single point of failure (bus factor = 1):** No other resource holds *security* certification (Chen's 24 `A6` events are adjacent/partial, not certified coverage).
  - **Visibility trap:** Low event volume makes Erik vulnerable to under-resourcing or queuing bottlenecks during surges (Weeks 7–8).
  - **Unsafe informal offloading:** When overloaded, workarounds (like Mateo filtering referrals) degrade risk control rather than safely expanding capacity.

### 4. R14_Nora — Automation Boundary Role
- **Behavioral evidence:** Executes 1,370 events strictly in `A1` and `A2` at 0.11 median complexity, feeding human agents in fragments 1 and 2.
- **Benefits:** Absorbs massive 24/7 routine intake load, freeing human agents for judgment-heavy responses and complex validation.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The Python script below demonstrates an end-to-end PM4Py workflow that discovers roles on both the full log and a non-substitution baseline, computes similarity and social networks, joins organizational metrics with `case_complexity` and `temporary_substitution`, and programmatically flags cases where activity-frequency clusters would misrepresent complexity or formal authority.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load event log & resource metadata, and format for PM4Py
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
raw_df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_events.csv")
resource_meta = pd.read_csv("resource_attributes.csv")
# Expected resource_meta columns:
# ['org:resource', 'formal_title', 'home_team', 'contract', 'certifications', 'usual_shift']

# Format dataframe to standard PM4Py event log schema
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
)

# Ensure boolean / numeric types for contextual attributes
log_df["temporary_substitution"] = log_df["temporary_substitution"].astype(bool)
log_df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(log_df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")

# Separate baseline (routine) log from substitution episodes
baseline_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log_df,
    attribute_key="temporary_substitution",
    values=[False],
    level="event",
    retain=True
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover organizational roles (Full log vs. Non-substitution baseline)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
roles_full = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log_df)
roles_baseline = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(baseline_df)

def roles_to_dataframe(roles, label):
    """Flatten PM4Py Role objects into a resource-to-role assignment table."""
    rows = []
    for idx, role in enumerate(roles, start=1):
        activities = list(role.activities) if hasattr(role, "activities") else []
        originators = role.originator_importance if hasattr(role, "originator_importance") else {}
        for res, weight in originators.items():
            rows.append({
                "org:resource": res,
                f"role_id_{label}": f"{label}_R{idx}",
                f"role_weight_{label}": weight,
                f"role_activities_{label}": ", ".join(sorted(activities))
            })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

role_df_full = roles_to_dataframe(roles_full, "full")
role_df_base = roles_to_dataframe(roles_baseline, "base")

# Keep primary (highest weight) discovered cluster per resource for comparison,
# while retaining secondary memberships to detect hybrid/bridging roles.
primary_full = (
    role_df_full.sort_values("role_weight_full", ascending=False)
    .groupby("org:resource", as_index=False)
    .first()
)
multi_role_counts = (
    role_df_base.groupby("org:resource")["role_id_base"]
    .nunique()
    .rename("n_baseline_roles")
    .reset_index()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
sim_metric = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(baseline_df)

# Extract pairwise similarities from PM4Py SNA/metric object
sim_rows = [
    {"res_a": r1, "res_b": r2, "activity_similarity": val}
    for (r1, r2), val in sim_metric.connections.items()
    if r1 < r2
]
sim_df = pd.DataFrame(sim_rows).sort_values("activity_similarity", ascending=False)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover-of-work, Working-together, and Subcontracting networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
how_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log_df, beta=0)
wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log_df)
sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log_df)

def sna_to_df(sna_obj, metric_name):
    return pd.DataFrame([
        {"source": u, "target": v, metric_name: w}
        for (u, v), w in sna_obj.connections.items()
        if w > 0
    ]).sort_values(metric_name, ascending=False)

how_df = sna_to_df(how_net, "handover_score")
wt_df = sna_to_df(wt_net, "working_together_score")
sub_df = sna_to_df(sub_net, "subcontracting_score")

# Inspect directed escalations & rework loops (e.g., R04->R03, Temp->R01/R02/R13, R13->R08)
print("Top Handover-of-Work Links:\n", how_df.head(15))
print("Top Subcontracting Loops (potential rechecks/bounces):\n", sub_df.head(10))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Service & waiting time diagnostics (substitution & bottleneck impact)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
enriched_df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log_df)
# Compare duration of A7 Pricing Exception Review across normal vs. substitution
a7_perf = (
    enriched_df[enriched_df["concept:name"] == "A7. Pricing Exception Review"]
    .groupby(["org:resource", "temporary_substitution"])
    .agg(
        event_count=("concept:name", "count"),
        median_case_waiting_sec=("@@case_waiting_time", "median"),
        median_case_service_sec=("@@case_service_time", "median")
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Join role discovery with complexity, substitution, and metadata
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Compute case-level complexity per resource (deduplicated by case)
case_res = (
    log_df[["case:concept:name", "org:resource", "case_complexity", "escalation_level"]]
    .drop_duplicates(subset=["case:concept:name", "org:resource"])
)
complexity_stats = (
    case_res.groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        unique_cases=("case:concept:name", "nunique"),
        median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        p90_complexity=("case_complexity", lambda s: np.percentile(s.dropna(), 90)),
        high_escalation_share=("escalation_level", lambda s: (s >= 2).mean())
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# Compute substitution exposure & sensitive-activity exposure
SENSITIVE_ACTS = {
    "A6. Security Review": "security",
    "A7. Pricing Exception Review": "pricing",
    "A9. Supervisor Approval": "approval"
}

sub_stats = (
    log_df.groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        total_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        distinct_activities=("concept:name", "nunique"),
        substitution_events=("temporary_substitution", "sum"),
        substitution_rate=("temporary_substitution", "mean")
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# Combine into unified organizational profile
org_profile = (
    resource_meta
    .merge(primary_full, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(multi_role_counts, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(complexity_stats, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(sub_stats, on="org:resource", how="left")
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Guardrails: Avoid over-interpreting frequency clusters as authority
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Guardrail A: Flag high-similarity pairs with large complexity divergence
# (Catches pairs like R03_Chen vs. R04_Dina or R01_Amira vs. R11_Kai)
comp_lookup = complexity_stats.set_index("org:resource")["median_complexity"].to_dict()
sim_df["complexity_gap"] = (
    sim_df["res_a"].map(comp_lookup) - sim_df["res_b"].map(comp_lookup)
).abs()

misclassification_risk_pairs = sim_df[
    (sim_df["activity_similarity"] >= 0.85) & (sim_df["complexity_gap"] >= 0.15)
]

# Guardrail B: Flag uncertified execution of authority-sensitive activities
# (Catches R13_Mateo on A9/A6, R07_Giorgio on A9, R03_Chen on A6)
cert_lookup = resource_meta.set_index("org:resource")["certifications"].fillna("").to_dict()

authority_audit_rows = []
for act, req_cert in SENSITIVE_ACTS.items():
    act_df = log_df[log_df["concept:name"] == act]
    grouped = act_df.groupby("org:resource").agg(
        total_act_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        sub_act_events=("temporary_substitution", "sum")
    ).reset_index()
    for _, row in grouped.iterrows():
        res = row["org:resource"]
        has_cert = req_cert in str(cert_lookup.get(res, "")).lower()
        authority_audit_rows.append({
            "org:resource": res,
            "activity": act,
            "required_cert": req_cert,
            "has_formal_cert": has_cert,
            "total_events": row["total_act_events"],
            "substitution_events": row["sub_act_events"],
            "non_sub_events": row["total_act_events"] - row["sub_act_events"],
            "flag": "OK" if has_cert else "SHADOW_OR_PROVISIONAL_AUTHORITY"
        })

authority_audit_df = pd.DataFrame(authority_audit_rows)

# Guardrail C: Detect shadow/bridging roles via role multiplicity & activity entropy
org_profile["is_potential_bridge"] = (
    (org_profile["n_baseline_roles"].fillna(1) > 1)
    | (org_profile["distinct_activities"] >= 10)
    | (org_profile["substitution_rate"] >= 0.05)
)

print("\nHigh-Similarity Pairs with Dangerous Complexity Gaps:\n", misclassification_risk_pairs)
print("\nAuthority & Certification Alignment Audit:\n", authority_audit_df[authority_audit_df["flag"] != "OK"])
print("\nUnified Organizational Role Profile:\n", org_profile)
```

**Why this pipeline avoids naïve misclassification:**
1. **Baseline vs. full comparison:** Filtering `temporary_substitution == False` before running `discover_organizational_roles` prevents short-term absences (Giorgio in Weeks 3–4, Mateo in Week 5) from permanently warping discovered role boundaries.
2. **Complexity divergence audit (Guardrail A):** Pairs with high cosine similarity (`>= 0.85`) but wide complexity gaps (`>= 0.15`) are explicitly surfaced so analysts do not merge Tier-2/Tier-3 technicians (Chen vs. Dina) or permanent agents vs. temps (Amira/Ben vs. Kai/Lina).
3. **Authority alignment check (Guardrail B):** Cross-checking sensitive activities (`A6`, `A7`, `A9`) against formal `certifications` and `temporary_substitution` distinguishes formal authority from provisional substitution and shadow execution.
4. **Network & performance overlay:** Combining `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network`, and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` exposes hidden bottlenecks (Erik's >2-day waits), rework loops (Hana rechecking Mateo; Front Office reworking temp closures), and efficiency penalties (Giorgio's 3874 min substitution jump).

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should treat the discovered role structure as a **diagnostic map of how work actually flows**, not as an automatic template for headcount reallocation.

### Staffing and Shift Design
- **Address late-shift asymmetry:** Currently, the late shift is staffed by only three human resources—`R03_Chen` (Senior Technical), `R09_Ivan` (Quality), and `R12_Lina` (Temp Front Office)—plus `R14_Nora`. There is no late-shift Commercial, Security, Supervisory, or permanent Front-Office generalist coverage. Management should either define explicit after-hours SLA deferrals for pricing/security/approvals or establish an on-call escalation rotation rather than relying on Chen to absorb late-shift complexity informally.
- **Right-size temporary labor boundaries:** Keep `R11_Kai` and `R12_Lina` strictly on well-defined, low-complexity queues (simple standard responses and closures) and route entitlement validation (`A2`) on non-trivial cases away from temps until certified, eliminating the 17-case rework loop hitting `R01_Amira`, `R02_Ben`, and `R13_Mateo`.
- **Recognize Giorgio's dual allocation explicitly:** Rather than treating Giorgio as 100% Front-Office headcount while expecting him to absorb commercial spillover, formally allocate a split capacity budget (e.g., 70% Senior Front Office / 30% Commercial Support) so neither team suffers invisible staffing shortfalls.

### Targeted Training and Capability Building
- **Structured Tier-2  Tier-3 technical progression:** Use `R03_Chen` to mentor `R04_Dina` on high-complexity diagnostics and security-adjacent cases (`A3`/`A5`/`A6`), narrowing the 0.71 vs. 0.48 complexity gap and reducing late-shift key-person risk.
- **Close the commercial substitution performance gap:** Provide `R07_Giorgio` with standardized pricing exception playbooks, pricing decision tools, and peer reviews with `R06_Fatima` to bring his substitution cycle time closer to Fatima's 38-minute baseline (from 74 minutes).
- **Tighten temp validation quality:** Deploy a mandatory `A2 Validate Entitlement` checklist (co-designed by QA stewards `R09_Ivan`/`R10_Julia` via `A14 KB Update`) and short-cycle QA sampling for temporary hires during their first 60 days.

### Risk Control and Decision Governance
- **Clarify `A9 Supervisor Approval` delegation rules:** The fact that `R08_Hana` rechecked 13 of `R13_Mateo`'s 21 Week 5 approvals—while Mateo (23 non-sub), Giorgio (20), and Fatima (16) also logged `A9` events—signals ambiguous delegation thresholds. Establish explicit, tiered approval limits (e.g., pre-authorized low-risk thresholds that do not require Hana's recheck) and enforce hard system controls preventing self-approval on high-value pricing exceptions (`A7` + `A9` by the same person).
- **Replace informal security filtering with structured triage criteria:** Mateo's Weeks 7–8 filtering reduced false escalations to `R05_Erik` but caused >2-day delays on two critical Enterprise Security cases. Co-author an explicit risk-scoring triage rubric owned by Erik so low-risk referrals are filtered safely while high-severity triggers bypass manual screening and alert Erik immediately.

### Organizational Resilience
- **Eliminate single points of failure (bus factor = 1):** Four critical functions currently hinge on single individuals: Security (`R05_Erik`), Commercial (`R06_Fatima`), Approval (`R08_Hana`), and Coordination (`R13_Mateo`).
  - Cross-train `R03_Chen` (already handling 24 `A6` events at 0.71 complexity) for formal backup security certification/triage under Erik.
  - Formalize deputy approval authority for Hana's planned absences so audit workshops do not create 62% rework.
  - Document Mateo's escalation routing rules into the KB (`A14`) and pair him with `R01_Amira` or `R07_Giorgio` as a trained backup coordinator.

### Cautions Against Changing Staffing Based Only on Mined Roles
1. **Do not equate activity similarity with resource interchangeability:** A naïve reading of the 0.94 cosine similarity between `R03_Chen` and `R04_Dina` might tempt management to replace senior technical capacity with junior hires or flatten pay grades. Doing so would destroy the hidden high-complexity and security-adjacent capability that Chen provides (median complexity 0.71 vs. 0.48).
2. **Do not equate low event volume with low utilization or excess capacity:** `R05_Erik` (170 events) and `R08_Hana` (245 events) appear tiny next to `R01_Amira` (1,375 events) or `R11_Kai` (871 events). Reducing headcount or adding routine tasks to low-volume specialists based on mined activity frequency would trigger severe risk-control failures and multi-day critical bottlenecks.

Substitution masked a 95% duration penalty for Giorgio (38  74 min) and a 62% recheck rate for Mateo (13/21)—proving that shadow coverage is a fragile resilience buffer, not a permanent staffing substitute.

Staffing decisions must pair mined role profiles with complexity, quality/reopen metrics, and formal accountability structures.
