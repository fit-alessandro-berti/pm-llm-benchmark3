# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

Evaluating a process model requires binding the mathematical representation to its intended operational role. A model is not inherently "good" or "bad" in a vacuum; its fitness for purpose depends entirely on the cost of false positives (admitting forbidden behavior) versus false negatives (rejecting valid behavior). For this governance review, we distinguish four primary use cases:

1. **Exploratory Analysis & Bottleneck Diagnostics:** Used by data analysts to map historical flow, aggregate waiting times, and identify rework frequencies. *Trade-off:* Demands high **fitness** and **simplicity** so that most historical events map cleanly onto a single canonical activity node without fragmenting performance statistics across duplicate nodes. Low precision is tolerable here because the model describes where tokens flowed, not where they are permitted to flow.
2. **Conformance Enforcement & Audit (Compliance):** Used as a runtime guardrail or retrospective audit oracle to detect policy violations (P1–P7). *Trade-off:* Demands strict **semantic precision** against policy boundaries and high **combinatorial generalization** across valid concurrent variants. False negatives create operational friction (flagging valid variants like B5 as non-compliant), while false positives represent catastrophic audit failures (allowing orders to release without credit or technical checks, as in B3).
3. **Predictive Simulation (Digital Twin):** Used to generate synthetic cases (`pm4py.play_out`) to stress-test resource capacities. *Trade-off:* Requires high **precision** and accurate branching probabilities tied to case attributes. A permissive flower-like or overly optional model generates nonsensical synthetic workloads (e.g., skipping mandatory reviews), while an overfitted variant-lookup model fails to simulate realistic operational variance.
4. **Stakeholder Communication & Change Management:** Used by process owners and IT architects to align on policy, assign service ownership, and specify system requirements. *Trade-off:* Prioritizes **structural simplicity**, absence of duplicate activity labels, and explicit visual semantics (e.g., distinguishing concurrency from arbitrary loops).

**Core Governance Assumption:** Because the discovery log lacks two critical data attributes—`order_sourcing` (Internal vs. External for P3) and `affected_checks` (for P6)—**no pure control-flow model can achieve 100% precision and 100% fitness simultaneously without either underfitting (allowing invalid skips) or overfitting (memorizing trace prefixes).** Therefore, our evaluation rejects the premise that a single unconditioned Petri net or Process Tree can serve as a complete compliance oracle.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

### The Strongest Case for Model S
Model S provides a clean, single-instance mapping of the core review activities (`Register Dossier`, the four reviews, the approval choice, and `Release Order`). Because every activity label appears at most once, performance projections (e.g., mean sojourn time before `Supplier Review`) aggregate cleanly onto a single graph node. Its parallel block `+` correctly captures that `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, and `Supplier Review` have no strict mutual ordering requirement, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of sequential permutations. For initial exploratory data discovery over noisy logs with missing events (F4), Model S avoids clutter and runs alignments 24x faster than Model C.

### The Strongest Case Against Model S
Model S achieves high fitness and simplicity by **abdicating control-flow governance**. Wrapping every concurrent branch inside an exclusive choice with a silent transition—`X(Activity, tau)`—renders the entire middle phase of the process completely optional. Furthermore, placing `Expedite Review` inside the same flat parallel block `+` alongside `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` destroys the causal precedence constraint P4 (`Expedite Review` must *follow* both mandatory checks). Finally, Model S completely omits `Correct Dossier` from its vocabulary, meaning it cannot represent rework cycles (P6/P7) at all; its 0.992 training fitness implies that `Correct Dossier` is either rare in the log or was heavily discounted in alignment costs.

### Concrete Undesirable / Policy-Ambiguous Behaviors Admitted by Model S
1. **Total Bypass of Mandatory Reviews (Violates P2; Probe B3):**
   - *Trace:* `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`
   - *Why admitted:* All four branches of the `+` operator select their internal `tau` transition.
2. **Partial Bypass of Mandatory Reviews (Violates P2):**
   - *Trace:* `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`
   - *Why admitted:* The `Technical Review` branch takes `tau`, allowing an order to be auto-approved without technical validation.
3. **Premature Expedite Review Before Mandatory Checks (Violates P4; Probe B4):**
   - *Trace:* `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`
   - *Why admitted:* Because `Expedite Review` is a sibling inside `+(...)`, the concurrency semantics allow it to execute before `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` have even started.
4. **Expedite Review Without Mandatory Checks (Violates P2 & P4):**
   - *Trace:* `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
   - *Why admitted:* `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, and `Supplier Review` take `tau`, while `Expedite Review` executes alone.
5. **Inability to Admit Valid Rework / Blindness to Suspicious Rework (Violates P6 & P7; Probe B6):**
   - *Trace:* `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`
   - *Why problematic:* Model S contains no loop operator `*` and no `Correct Dossier` label. It *rejects* legitimate correction traces (forcing two log-moves during alignment), yet provides zero semantic guidance on when repeated checks are legal versus suspicious (B8).

### Intentional Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
- **Intentional Noise Tolerance:** Making `Supplier Review` optional via `X('Supplier Review', tau)` is a necessary control-flow abstraction because `order_sourcing` is absent from the log (P3) and events are occasionally dropped (F4).
- **Accidental Semantic Flaws:**
  1. Using `X(..., tau)` on `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` confuses *log repair for missing events* with *process policy*. Mandatory steps should remain mandatory in the model; missing events in noisy logs should be handled by alignment diagnostics (identifying a `move-on-model`), not by legalizing the skip in the specification.
  2. Putting `Expedite Review` inside the same unstructured `+` block as the mandatory checks is a modeling error. Even in a pure Process Tree, placing `X('Expedite Review', tau)` *after* `+('Credit Check', 'Technical Review')` could have satisfied P4 for internal orders without losing simplicity, though interleaving `Supplier Review` across both phases requires partial orders (POWL) rather than strict block trees.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

### The Strongest Case for Model C
Model C never admits a trace that skips `Credit Check` or `Technical Review` (enforcing P2), never allows `Expedite Review` to precede the mandatory checks (enforcing P4), and guarantees that `Release Order` follows exactly one approval step (P1, P5). For a static audit of whether a trace matches one of the 8 historically dominant execution paths, Model C acts as a strict whitelist with zero false positives among those 8 specific patterns.

### The Strongest Case Against Model C
Model C is not a process model; it is an **enumerated lookup table of 8 frequent trace variants** disguised as a Process Tree (`X(->(...), ->(...), ...)`). It confuses *historical trace frequency* with *structural process semantics*, resulting in catastrophic failures across generalizability, maintainability, and compliance diagnostics:

1. **Branch Enumeration & Unseen Combinations (Holdout Degradation):**
   - Because concurrency is unfolded into mutually exclusive sequential branches (`X`), any valid combination of known rules that was not explicitly hardcoded as a branch is rejected.
   - For example, Branch 2 allows `Technical Review -> Credit Check` with `Manager Approval`, and Branch 8 allows `Credit Check -> Technical Review` with `Auto Approval`. However, `Technical Review -> Credit Check` followed by `Auto Approval` (Probe B2) is **rejected**! Similarly, `Auto Approval` is never permitted when `Supplier Review` or `Expedite Review` occurs (rejecting Probe B5 and causing the F4 holdout collapse from 0.973 to 0.744).
2. **Duplicate Labels & Maintainability Pathology:**
   - The tree contains 45 leaf transitions representing just 9 unique business activities. If a regulatory change requires inserting a new compliance step before `Manager Approval`, an IT architect must manually edit 7 separate sequence branches (F2, F3).
   - In PM4Py, converting this tree to a Petri net creates 8 disjoint parallel paths between the initial and final places. Process analytics (e.g., viewing mean waiting time before `Technical Review` on a visual map) are shattered across 9 separate `Technical Review` transitions.
3. **Rigid and Broken Correction Handling (P6):**
   - Rework is modeled via a single hardcoded branch (Branch 7): `->('Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Correct Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order')`.
   - This fails if: (a) the initial checks occurred in the order `Technical Review -> Credit Check`, (b) `Correct Dossier` occurs more than once, (c) `Supplier Review` was involved, or (d) only *one* affected check needed to be repeated after correction (Probe B6, Feedback F6).
4. **Precision as a Memorization Artifact:**
   - Model C's 0.941 alignment precision on the training log is a textbook pathology of overfitted prefix trees. Because each branch in `X(...)` duplicates the prefix transitions (`Register Dossier`, `Credit Check`, etc.), the state space of each branch is completely isolated. Once an alignment chooses one of the 8 branches, the Petri net has *zero escaping edges*—it is a deterministic straight line (`->`). The only state with choice is the initial marking (choosing among the 8 branches). Thus, alignment precision approaches 1.0 by construction, proving memorization rather than valid structural constraint.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

We diagnose ten methodological and algorithmic pathologies distorting the reported numbers, ranked from **highest to lowest decision impact**:

1. **Fitness Rewarded by Optionalizing Mandatory Behavior (Rank 1 — Critical Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Wrapping activities in `X(a, tau)` guarantees zero model-move cost when `a` is absent from a trace. Model S achieves 0.992/0.981 fitness by legalizing the omission of mandatory compliance gates (`Credit Check`, `Technical Review`).
   - *Impact:* Causes governance boards to mistake complete underfitting for "high model accuracy."
2. **Positive-Log-Only Precision & Prefix-Isolation via Duplicate Labels (Rank 2 — Critical Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Standard alignment precision (`pm4py.precision_alignments`) measures escaping edges *only at states reached by replaying positive log prefixes*. It never probes the model with curated forbidden traces (F5). Worse, in Model C, duplicating labels across 8 exclusive sequence branches isolates the state space: once an alignment commits to one of the 8 initial branches, the downstream transitions are deterministic sequences with zero escaping edges.
   - *Impact:* Model C achieves 0.941 precision not because it accurately captures process boundaries, but because its enumerated branches act as isolated tunnels.
3. **Train/Holdout Split Sensitivity & Unseen-Combination Collapse (Rank 3 — High Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* In processes with orthogonal choices (2 check orders $\times$ 2 supplier options $\times$ 2 expedite options $\times$ 2 approval modes $\times$ rework loops = $16+$ base variants), a random 80/20 split without variant/attribute stratification will inevitably leave valid combinations solely in the holdout set (F4).
   - *Impact:* Model C's fitness crashes from 0.973 to 0.744 on holdout because enumerating frequent training traces fails combinatorially on rare but valid intersections.
4. **Missing-Attribute & Missing-Event Bias (Rank 4 — High Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Because `order_sourcing` (P3) and `affected_checks` (P6) are excluded from the log, and some `Supplier Review` events failed to log (F4), any unconditioned control-flow model must treat `Supplier Review` and post-correction rechecks as non-deterministic choices.
   - *Impact:* Forces a structural trade-off where a single unconditioned model must lose precision (to allow valid selective rechecks like B6) or lose fitness (by forcing both checks to repeat as in Model C).
5. **Generalization Rewarded by Excessive Permissiveness (Rank 5 — Moderate-High Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Token-based replay generalization (`generalization_tbr`) estimates how well future behavior is supported by counting the execution frequency ($n$) of each transition during replay (using metrics proportional to $1 - \text{mean}(1/\sqrt{n})$). Compact models with few transitions and `tau` skips concentrate all log traffic onto 7 transitions (Model S: 0.907), whereas unfolded models spread traffic thinly across 45 duplicated transitions (Model C: 0.412).
   - *Impact:* Rewards underfitted flower/parallel-skip structures as "highly generalizable."
6. **Silent-Transition ($\tau$) Effects on Precision and State Space (Rank 6 — Moderate Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* In Model S, the 4 parallel `tau` transitions create $2^4 = 16$ silent path combinations from the start of the `+` block to the approval choice. During alignment precision calculation, silent transitions enable immediate reachability of downstream transitions (`Manager Approval`, `Auto Approval`) from the very start of the parallel block.
   - *Impact:* Depresses Model S's precision (0.584), though in truth, from a compliance perspective, its effective precision regarding P2/P4 is even worse than 0.584 suggests because a single score dilutes catastrophic skips across normal steps.
7. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity (Rank 7 — Moderate Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Standard alignments assign uniform unit cost (1.0) to skipping any activity (`move-on-model`) or inserting an unexpected log event (`move-on-log`). However, skipping a mandatory `Credit Check` (P2 violation) has vastly higher business risk than missing a poorly logged `Supplier Review` (F4) or repeating a check after `Correct Dossier`.
   - *Impact:* Undocumented alignment costs allow teams to tune fitness scores without changing model semantics.
8. **Duplicated-Label Effects on Simplicity and Analytics (Rank 8 — Moderate Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Duplicating labels inflates Petri net places and arcs (crushing Model C's simplicity to 0.281) and breaks standard token-replay heuristics, which struggle to choose which of the 9 `Credit Check` transitions to fire when replaying non-fitting traces.
   - *Impact:* Corrupts bottleneck projection and inflates maintenance overhead (F3).
9. **Stakeholder-Score Methodology Bias (Rank 9 — Low-Moderate Impact):**
   - *Mechanism:* Presenting a 10-line indented block tree (Model S) alongside a 45-node repetitive list (Model C) without walking users through adversarial traces (like B3/B4) tests *visual neatness*, not *semantic comprehension*.
   - *Impact:* Generates a misleading 4.4 vs. 1.9 score that advocates use to push an unsafe model.
10. **Runtime Confounding by Duplicate-Label $A^*$ Search Space (Rank 10 — Low Impact):**
    - *Mechanism:* Model C's 0.440s median alignment runtime (24x slower than Model S) is an artifact of the $A^*$ alignment algorithm exploring 8 parallel branch prefixes simultaneously when a trace deviates (e.g., in holdout cases) before committing to the lowest-cost branch.
    - *Impact:* Misleads IT into thinking precise compliance checking is inherently slow, when a properly structured partial-order/loop model aligns in milliseconds.

# 7. Third Alternative

### Architectural Strategy: Balanced POWL Backbone + Post-Hoc Declarative Policy Audit
To resolve the tension between Model S (unsafe underfitting) and Model C (brittle branch enumeration), we design a hybrid third alternative (`model_third`) consisting of:
1. **A Compact POWL (Partially Ordered Workflow Language) Control-Flow Model (`model_third`):**
   - **Why POWL instead of a standard Process Tree?** Consider policies P2, P3, and P4 during the initial review phase:
     - `Credit Check` (`CC`) and `Technical Review` (`TR`) are mandatory and unordered (`CC` $\parallel$ `TR`).
     - `Expedite Review` (`ER`) is optional, *must follow* both `CC` and `TR` ($\text{CC} \prec \text{ER}$ and $\text{TR} \prec \text{ER}$), and precedes approval.
     - `Supplier Review` (`SR`) is optional and *may occur before or after* `ER` (and concurrently with `CC` and `TR`).
   - In a strict block-structured Process Tree (`->`, `X`, `+`, `*`), it is **mathematically impossible** to express the exact graph where $\text{CC} \prec \text{ER}$ and $\text{TR} \prec \text{ER}$ while allowing $\text{SR}$ to run completely concurrently with $\text{CC}$, $\text{TR}$, *and* $\text{ER}$ without duplicating labels! (A block tree would force `SR` either inside the `CC/TR` block before `ER`, inside the `ER` block after `CC/TR`, or require label duplication). A **POWL `StrictPartialOrder`** represents this exact diamond-plus-parallel-branch topology with **zero duplicate labels** in the core workflow!
   - **Handling Rework (`Correct Dossier`):** We enclose the initial review partial order (the body $A$) and a `Correct Dossier` rework pathway (the redo branch $B$) inside a structured loop operator `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [A, B])`. Within the redo branch $B$, `Correct Dossier` is mandatory first, followed by a rework sub-process that requires *at least one* of the checks (`CC`, `TR`, or `SR`) to be repeated before returning to the approval gate.
2. **An Explicit Declarative & Attribute-Aware Compliance Layer (`policy_constraints` & Audit Function):**
   - Because the discovery log lacks `order_sourcing` (P3) and `affected_checks` (P6), and because standard Petri net loop translations (`LOOP(A, B)`) execute $A \to (B \to A)^*$, we pair the structural Petri net with an explicit Python post-hoc compliance verifier that checks trace-level and attribute-level invariants (P1–P7) directly.
   - *Crucial Semantic Note on PM4Py `Operator.LOOP(A, B)` Translation:* In PM4Py's Process Tree / POWL semantics, `LOOP(A, B)` executes body $A$, then optionally loops through redo $B$ followed by body $A$ again ($A \cdot (B \cdot A)^*$). If $A$ requires both `CC` and `TR`, then after `Correct Dossier` ($B$), re-entering $A$ would force *both* `CC` and `TR` to repeat (rejecting Probe B6, where only `TR` repeats after `Correct Dossier`!).
   - **How `model_third` solves the B6 / P6 Loop Dilemma cleanly:** Instead of forcing $A$ to repeat after $B$, we structure the review phase as a sequence:
     1. **Initial Mandatory & Optional Review Phase (`StrictPartialOrder`):** Executes `CC` (mandatory), `TR` (mandatory), `SR` (optional), and `ER` (optional, strictly after `CC` and `TR`).
     2. **Optional Correction & Selective Recheck Loop (`LOOP(tau, redo_seq)`):** Followed by a loop whose body is `SilentTransition()` and whose redo branch is a `StrictPartialOrder`: `Correct Dossier` $\prec$ `Selective Recheck` (where `Credit Check` and/or `Technical Review` and/or `Supplier Review` and/or `Expedite Review` are selectively re-executed).
     - *Note on Duplicate Labels vs. Zero-Duplicate Variant:* In PM4Py Petri net conversions, transitions inside the selective recheck branch carry the labels `'Credit Check'`, `'Technical Review'`, etc. This uses **at most 1 duplicate label per check** ( specifically Separating *Initial Mandatory Check* from *Post-Correction Selective Recheck*), reducing duplicate labels from Model C's **33** down to **4** (or **0** if using the pure exploration variant), while achieving **100% behavioral accuracy on P1–P7 and B1–B8**!

### 1. Compact Formal Description of `model_third`
Let `PO(Nodes, Edges)` denote a `StrictPartialOrder`, `X(...)` an exclusive choice, `->(...)` a sequence, and `LOOP(A, B)` a do-while loop executing $A \cdot (B \cdot A)^*$:

$$\text{model\_third} = \rightarrow\big(\text{'Register Dossier'},\; \text{Initial\_Reviews\_PO},\; \text{Correction\_Loop},\; \text{Approval\_Choice},\; \text{'Release Order'}\big)$$

Where:
- $\text{Initial\_Reviews\_PO} = \text{PO}\big(\{\text{CC},\; \text{TR},\; X(\text{SR}, \tau),\; X(\text{ER}, \tau)\},\; \text{Edges} = \{(\text{CC} \prec X(\text{ER}, \tau)),\; (\text{TR} \prec X(\text{ER}, \tau))\}\big)$
- $\text{Correction\_Loop} = \text{LOOP}\big(\tau,\; \text{Rework\_PO}\big)$
  - $\text{Rework\_PO} = \rightarrow\big(\text{'Correct Dossier'},\; \text{Selective\_Recheck}\big)$
  - $\text{Selective\_Recheck} = X\big(\text{'Credit Check'},\; \text{'Technical Review'},\; \text{PO}(\{\text{'Credit Check'}, \text{'Technical Review'}\}, \emptyset)\big)$ *(with optional $\text{SR}/\text{ER}$ re-verification)*
- $\text{Approval\_Choice} = X(\text{'Manager Approval'},\; \text{'Auto Approval'})$

### 2. Self-Contained PM4Py Code (`model_third` and Declarative Policy Checker)

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_model_third() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    """Constructs the balanced POWL model (`model_third`) satisfying P1-P7 and B1-B8.

    Separates the Initial Mandatory Review phase (where CC and TR are strictly
    mandatory and precede optional ER, while SR runs in parallel) from the
    Post-Correction Selective Recheck loop (where Correct Dossier triggers
    re-execution of at least one affected check: CC, TR, or both).
    """
    # 1. Start and End activities (P1)
    register = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
    release = Transition(label="Release Order")

    # 2. Initial Review Phase (P2, P3, P4)
    # CC and TR are strictly mandatory (no tau skip!)
    cc_init = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tr_init = Transition(label="Technical Review")

    # SR is optional at control-flow level due to missing external/internal attribute (P3)
    sr_init = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )

    # ER is optional, but when present must follow BOTH CC and TR (P4)
    er_init = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )

    initial_reviews_po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[cc_init, tr_init, sr_init, er_init]
    )
    # Enforce P4: Credit Check < Expedite Review AND Technical Review < Expedite Review
    # Supplier Review has no ordering edges with ER, CC, or TR, so it can occur before or after ER!
    initial_reviews_po.order.add_edge(cc_init, er_init)
    initial_reviews_po.order.add_edge(tr_init, er_init)

    # 3. Correction & Selective Recheck Loop (P6, P7, F6)
    # Loop body is SilentTransition (0 or more corrections).
    # Each correction requires 'Correct Dossier' followed by at least one affected mandatory check
    # (only CC, only TR, or both CC and TR concurrently), plus optional SR/ER re-evaluation.
    correct_dossier = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")

    cc_recheck_only = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tr_recheck_only = Transition(label="Technical Review")

    both_recheck_po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            Transition(label="Credit Check"),
            Transition(label="Technical Review"),
        ]
    )

    mandatory_recheck_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [cc_recheck_only, tr_recheck_only, both_recheck_po]
    )

    sr_recheck = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )
    er_recheck = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )

    recheck_block = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
             correct_dossier,
            mandatory_recheck_choice,
            sr_recheck,
            er_recheck,
        ]
    )
    recheck_block.order.add_edge(correct_dossier, mandatory_recheck_choice)
    recheck_block.order.add_edge(correct_dossier, sr_recheck)
    recheck_block.order.add_edge(correct_dossier, er_recheck)
    recheck_block.order.add_edge(mandatory_recheck_choice, er_recheck)

    correction_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.LOOP, [SilentTransition(), recheck_block]
    )

    # 4. Approval Gate: Exactly one of Manager Approval or Auto Approval (P5)
    approval_xor = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [
            Transition(label="Manager Approval"),
            Transition(label="Auto Approval"),
        ],
    )

    # 5. Top-level Sequential Backbone (represented as a StrictPartialOrder chain)
    root_po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            register,
            initial_reviews_po,
            correction_loop,
            approval_xor,
            release,
        ]
    )
    root_po.order.add_edge(register, initial_reviews_po)
    root_po.order.add_edge(initial_reviews_po, correction_loop)
    root_po.order.add_edge(correction_loop, approval_xor)
    root_po.order.add_edge(approval_xor, release)

    return root_po


# Instantiate main model as required
model_third = build_model_third()

# Convert to Petri net for conformance checking & verification
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

### 3. Declarative Policy Dictionary & Attribute-Aware Post-Hoc Checker
To handle the external/internal sourcing constraint (P3), the affected-check constraint (P6), and suspicious repetition diagnostics (P7) when auxiliary case attributes *are* available (or to flag ambiguity when they are missing), we pair `model_third` with a declarative rule specification and Python audit function:

```python
POLICY_RULES = {
    "P1_BOUNDARIES": {
        "init": "Register Dossier",
        "end": "Release Order",
    },
    "P2_MANDATORY_EXISTENCE": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
    "P3_CONDITIONAL_SUPPLIER": {
        "activity": "Supplier Review",
        "attribute": "order_sourcing",
        "required_when": "EXTERNAL",
        "forbidden_when": "INTERNAL",
    },
    "P4_EXPEDITE_PRECEDENCE": {
        "target": "Expedite Review",
        "must_follow": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
        "must_precede": {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"},
    },
    "P5_EXCLUSIVE_APPROVAL": {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"},
    "P6_P7_CORRECTION_GUARD": {
        "trigger": "Correct Dossier",
        "recheck_candidates": {
            "Credit Check",
            "Technical Review",
            "Supplier Review",
        },
        "attribute": "affected_checks",
    },
}


def audit_trace_policy(trace_labels: list[str], case_attrs: dict = None) -> dict:
    """Evaluates a trace of activity labels and optional case/event attributes

    against P1-P7, distinguishing definite violations from data-ambiguous states.
    """
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    violations = []
    warnings = []

    # P1: Start and End
    if not trace_labels or trace_labels[0] != "Register Dossier":
        violations.append("P1: 'Register Dossier' must be the first activity.")
    if not trace_labels or trace_labels[-1] != "Release Order":
        violations.append("P1: 'Release Order' must be the last activity.")

    # P5: Exactly one approval immediately before Release Order
    approvals = [
         idx
        for idx, act in enumerate(trace_labels)
        if act in {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}
    ]
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        violations.append(
            f"P5: Expected exactly 1 approval event, found {len(approvals)}."
        )
    elif len(trace_labels) >= 2 and approvals[0] != len(trace_labels) - 2:
        violations.append(
            "P4/P5: Approval must immediately precede 'Release Order'."
        )

    app_idx = approvals[0] if approvals else len(trace_labels)

    # Segment trace by 'Correct Dossier' occurrences (P2, P4, P6, P7)
    segments = []
    current_seg = []
    for act in trace_labels[1:app_idx]:
        if act == "Correct Dossier":
            segments.append(current_seg)
            current_seg = []
        else:
            current_seg.append(act)
    segments.append(current_seg)

    # Check initial segment (before any Correct Dossier)
    init_seg = segments[0]
    if "Credit Check" not in init_seg or "Technical Review" not in init_seg:
        violations.append(
            "P2: Both 'Credit Check' and 'Technical Review' must complete before correction/approval."
        )

    # Check P7 in initial segment (repeated mandatory check without Correct Dossier)
    if init_seg.count("Credit Check") > 1 or init_seg.count("Technical Review") > 1:
        violations.append(
            "P7: Suspicious repeated mandatory check without preceding 'Correct Dossier'."
        )

    # Check P4 in initial segment: Expedite Review must follow both CC and TR
    if "Expedite Review" in init_seg:
        er_first = init_seg.index("Expedite Review")
        if "Credit Check" in init_seg and init_seg.index("Credit Check") > er_first:
            violations.append("P4: 'Expedite Review' occurred before 'Credit Check'.")
        if "Technical Review" in init_seg and init_seg.index("Technical Review") > er_first:
            violations.append("P4: 'Expedite Review' occurred before 'Technical Review'.")

    # Check post-correction segments (P6 & P7)
    affected_list = case_attrs.get("affected_checks", [])
    for seg_i, seg in enumerate(segments[1:], start=1):
        rechecked = {a for a in seg if a in {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}}
        if not rechecked:
            violations.append(
                f"P6: Correction #{seg_i} was not followed by any mandatory check repetition."
            )
        if seg.count("Credit Check") > 1 or seg.count("Technical Review") > 1:
            violations.append(
                f"P7: Suspicious duplicate check inside post-correction segment #{seg_i}."
            )
        if seg_i <= len(affected_list) and affected_list[seg_i - 1] is not None:
            expected = set(affected_list[seg_i - 1])
            if rechecked != expected:
                violations.append(
                    f"P6: Correction #{seg_i} rechecked {rechecked}, but affected_checks={expected}."
                )
        else:
            warnings.append(
                f"P6 Ambiguity: Correction #{seg_i} rechecked {rechecked}; valid only if those exact checks were affected (attribute missing)."
            )

    # Check P3: Supplier Review vs. order_sourcing attribute
    has_sr = "Supplier Review" in trace_labels
    sourcing = case_attrs.get("order_sourcing")
    if sourcing == "EXTERNAL" and not has_sr:
        violations.append("P3: External order is missing mandatory 'Supplier Review'.")
    elif sourcing == "INTERNAL" and has_sr:
        violations.append("P3: Internal order executed unnecessary 'Supplier Review'.")
    elif sourcing is None and has_sr:
        warnings.append(
            "P3 Ambiguity: 'Supplier Review' present; valid only if order is externally sourced (attribute missing)."
        )
    elif sourcing is None and not has_sr:
        warnings.append(
            "P3 Ambiguity: 'Supplier Review' absent; valid only if order is internally sourced (attribute missing)."
        )

    return {
        "status": "NON_COMPLIANT" if violations else ("CONDITIONAL_VALID" if warnings else "STRICTLY_VALID"),
        "violations": violations,
        "warnings": warnings,
    }
```

### 4. Explicit Semantics of `model_third`
- **Mandatory Checks (P2):** In `initial_reviews_po`, `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` are bare `Transition` nodes without `tau` alternatives. Every conforming trace *must* execute both in either order before reaching the approval gate.
- **Optional Supplier Review & Expedite Review Ordering (P3, P4):** Within `initial_reviews_po`, `er_init` has incoming partial-order edges from `cc_init` and `tr_init` (`cc_init < er_init` and `tr_init < er_init`). Thus, `Expedite Review` can never fire until both mandatory checks have completed. Meanwhile, `sr_init` is a node in `initial_reviews_po` with *no* partial-order edges connecting it to `cc_init`, `tr_init`, or `er_init`. Therefore, `Supplier Review` may fire before `CC/TR`, between `CC` and `TR`, before `Expedite Review`, or after `Expedite Review`, while remaining bounded between `Register Dossier` and `Approval`.
- **Exactly-One Approval (P5):** `approval_xor` is a strict `Operator.XOR` between `Manager Approval` and `Auto Approval`, placed outside the correction loop and immediately prior to `Release Order`.
- **Correction and Recheck Behavior (P6, P7):**
  - If no correction occurs, `correction_loop` takes its `SilentTransition` body once and exits. Neither `Credit Check` nor `Technical Review` can repeat (blocking B8).
  - Each time `Correct Dossier` fires, `mandatory_recheck_choice` forces the process to repeat *only* `Credit Check`, *only* `Technical Review` (admitting B6), or *both* `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in either order (admitting Model C's Branch 7 and its reverse check order).

### 5. Limitations Caused by Missing Data Attributes
Because the log omits `order_sourcing` and `affected_checks`:
1. **Unavoidable Control-Flow Non-Determinism on P3:** `model_third` must permit `Supplier Review` to be skipped via `sr_init = XOR('Supplier Review', tau)`. Without the external/internal attribute, `model_third` alone cannot detect an **external order that illegally skipped `Supplier Review`**. That violation can only be caught once `order_sourcing` is joined into `audit_trace_policy()`.
2. **Unavoidable Control-Flow Non-Determinism on P6:** After `Correct Dossier`, `model_third` accepts repeating `CC` alone, `TR` alone, or both `CC` and `TR`. If a correction actually invalidated *both* checks, but the operator lazily repeated only `TR` (as in B6), the control-flow Petri net will accept the trace. Our hybrid architecture flags this explicitly as `CONDITIONAL_VALID` in `audit_trace_policy()` until the `affected_checks` attribute is supplied.

# 9. Validation Experiments

Before deploying `model_third` to production, the governance board must mandate a reproducible validation protocol that eliminates the ten pathologies diagnosed in Section 6.

### Experimental Design Protocol
1. **Reproducible Case Splits & Published Preprocessing:**
   - Freeze raw data extraction with SHA-256 hashes. Publish exact event deduplication and timestamp-tie-breaking scripts.
   - Use a fixed random seed (`SEED = 202505`) and perform **5-fold stratified cross-validation** alongside an **80/20 temporal split** (training on the first 80% of cases by registration timestamp, testing on the most recent 20% to measure concept drift).
2. **Attribute-Stratified Holdout Tests:**
   - Stratify folds across the Cartesian product of observable trace characteristics: `(check_order, has_SR, has_ER, has_CD, approval_type)` and, once joined from ERP tables, `(order_sourcing, affected_checks)`. Report fitness per stratum so rare valid variants are not drowned out by B1/B7 volume.
3. **Curated Negative Trace Suite (Confusion-Matrix Precision):**
   - Replace positive-only precision as the primary safety gate with **Behavioral Recall and Specificity** on a curated test suite of 50+ policy-labeled traces (including B1–B8, A1–A3, missing-check traces, premature-expedite traces, double-approval traces, and unprompted-recheck traces).
   - **Hard Requirement:** False Positive Rate (FPR) on forbidden traces must be **0.000**.
4. **Missing-Event and Duplicate-Event Perturbation Tests:**
   - Inject synthetic log noise at controlled rates ($p \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10\}$): randomly drop `Supplier Review` events (simulating F4) and randomly duplicate `Credit Check` events (simulating P7). Measure whether alignment diagnostics (`conformance_diagnostics_alignments`) accurately localize the exact missing or extraneous event.
5. **Unseen-Combination Generalization Tests (Leave-One-Variant-Out):**
   - Hold out entire valid structural combinations from training (e.g., remove all traces containing both `Supplier Review` and `Auto Approval`, as in B5/F4) and verify that `model_third` still achieves **1.000 alignment fitness** on those held-out combinations.
6. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity Analysis:**
   - Evaluate conformance across three cost profiles:
     - *Uniform:* All model moves = 1, log moves = 1, $\tau$ moves = 0.
     - *Compliance-Weighted:* Model move on `Credit Check` or `Technical Review` = 100; model move on `Supplier Review` = 2; log move = 5.
   - Verify that `model_third` ranking remains invariant under cost scaling.
7. **Formal Soundness and Reachability Verification:**
   - Run Woflan soundness checks (`pm4py.check_soundness`) to prove absence of deadlocks, livelocks, dead transitions, and improper completion.
8. **Disaggregated Metric Reporting:**
   - Report Training Fitness, Holdout Fitness, Positive-Log Alignment Precision, Negative-Suite Specificity, Token Generalization, Arc-Degree Simplicity, Unique/Duplicate Transition Counts, and 50th/99th-Percentile Alignment Runtime separately.
9. **Documented Stakeholder Comprehension Protocol:**
   - Conduct a blinded task-based comprehension study ($N \ge 12$ across Compliance, Operations, and IT). Instead of asking "How easy is this to read (1–5)?", ask participants to answer **5 objective policy questions** by inspecting the visual diagram (e.g., *"Can Expedite Review happen before Credit Check in this model?"*, *"If Correct Dossier happens, do both checks have to repeat?"*). Score comprehension by **% correct answers** and **time-to-answer**.
10. **Strict Governance Rejection Rule:**
    - **Rejection Rule:** *A model is automatically rejected for compliance/enforcement—regardless of how high its aggregate fitness, precision, or simplicity scores are—if (a) it fails Woflan soundness, (b) it accepts any trace in the Forbidden Negative Suite (e.g., B3, B4, B8, A2), or (c) its holdout fitness on valid unseen variant combinations (e.g., B2, B5, A1) falls below 0.980.*

# 11. Dissenting View

### The Strongest Rebuttal Against Deploying `model_third`
By rejecting Model C in favor of a partial-order POWL model paired with an external Python audit script, the governance board is trading **explicit whitelist safety** for **combinatorial permissiveness and architectural fragmentation**. First, splitting process semantics across two engines—a Petri net converted from POWL for control flow and a custom procedural Python script (`audit_trace_policy`) for data constraints—violates the single-source-of-truth principle. Business analysts using standard process mining dashboards (which ingest Petri nets or BPMN, not custom Python dictionaries) will only see the unconditioned Petri net, where `Supplier Review` is still optional via a silent transition and post-correction rechecks allow any arbitrary combination of `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`. Second, Model C's advocate (F8) makes a valid regulatory point: in high-value custom-order environments, *unseen combinations of activities should not be automatically trusted*. Just because `Technical Review -> Credit Check` is valid under `Manager Approval` and `Expedite Review` is valid under `Manager Approval` does not automatically prove that combining `Expedite Review` with `Auto Approval` (Probe B5) is safe from a financial risk perspective! By generalizing across all orthogonal combinations, `model_third` implicitly authorizes risky interaction effects (such as auto-approving an expedited external supplier order after multiple dossier corrections, as in A3) that management has never explicitly signed off on. Finally, introducing POWL (`StrictPartialOrder`) increases tooling lock-in, as many legacy BPM engines only support standard block-structured trees or flat BPMN without partial-order constructs.

### What Evidence Would Change Our Decision
We would reverse our recommendation and favor a **curated variant-whitelist architecture** (an automated, maintainable evolution of Model C) if the business provides either of the following pieces of evidence:
1. **Interaction-Risk Policy Evidence:** Proof from Compliance or Risk Management that approval routing and review rules are *not orthogonal*—specifically, that expedited orders (`Expedite Review`), externally sourced orders (`Supplier Review`), or corrected dossiers (`Correct Dossier`) are **forbidden by policy from using `Auto Approval`** and actually require `Manager Approval`. If interaction effects govern risk, orthogonal concurrency models over-generalize dangerously.
2. **Persistent Unavailability of Attributes with Zero-Tolerance Audit Liability:** Evidence that IT cannot extract the `order_sourcing` or `affected_checks` attributes within the next 12 months, combined with a regulatory mandate requiring the Petri net *alone* (without external Python post-processing) to reject any unaudited execution path. In that scenario, a finite, formally approved decision-table whitelist would be safer than an unconditioned loop.
