# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Estimands and units**

| Symbol | Meaning | How it is obtained here | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ | arrival intensity | `arrivals` / 12 | cases per opening hour |
| δ | completion intensity (throughput) | `completed` / 12 | cases per opening hour |
| S | active handling time per case | only median and p90 are given; the mean is not observed | minutes |
| L | time-average number in system | `mean_wip` (15-minute snapshots) | cases |
| c | concurrent capacity | `staff_fte` | FTE |
| ρ | capacity pressure = λ·E[S] / (c·60) | needs E[S], so it is an estimate under assumption A5 | dimensionless |
| net | recorded inflow minus recorded completions | `arrivals − completed` | cases per day |
| W | mean time in system (flow time) | not observed; L/λ is used as a proxy only where section 6 allows it | opening hours |

**Opening-hours convention.** One day is one 12-hour window, 07:00–19:00. All rates are per opening hour and all implied times are in opening hours: the clock stops at 19:00 and restarts at 07:00. A case carried overnight therefore has a calendar flow time at least 12 hours longer than its opening-hours flow time.

**Labelled assumptions**

- A1: weekends use the same 07:00–19:00 window. Needed to convert weekend counts into rates.
- A2: the WIP snapshots are taken only during opening hours and are equally spaced, so `mean_wip` is a 12-hour time average. If closed hours were included, L would be weighted towards the overnight stock.
- A3: "in the system" covers waiting plus in-service cases of any arrival date.
- A4: no arrivals or completions occur outside opening hours. The table counts only opening-hours events, so anything outside them is invisible.
- A5: handling time is roughly lognormal, so E[S] ≈ median·exp(σ²/2) with σ = ln(p90/median)/1.2816. This is needed because capacity pressure depends on the mean, which is not supplied. It is used only for ρ and utilisation, never as a flow time.
- A6: all `staff_fte` works this queue, and the outage removes all capacity for `outage_min`.
- A7: Apr 29 is treated as a transition day because the intervention start time is unknown.

A partial check on A5 (estimate): on days where staff were plausibly never idle, staffed minutes divided by completions bounds E[S] from above. This gives about 42 min for Apr 20–28 weekdays (4,270/100.6) and 39 min for Apr 16 (5,040/129). The lognormal estimates are 44 and 40 min, so A5 is not far off in the saturated regimes. It is untested in the baseline.

**Threats to naive inference**

1. **Mixture of day types.** Weekdays (92–121 arrivals, 5.5–7 FTE) and weekends (36–53 arrivals, 2.5–3 FTE) are two different systems. No day has between 54 and 91 arrivals, so the pooled mean of 90 describes no real day.
2. **Several drivers change at once on Apr 20.** Complex share, intraday concentration, volume and handling time all step up on the same Monday, and the regime starts with backlog left over from the outage. Their effects cannot be separated from these aggregates.
3. **Stock and flow do not reconcile (observation).** Over the four weeks recorded arrivals exceed completions by 110 (2,519 vs 2,409). Over Apr 21–28 the recorded net inflow is +92, but daily mean WIP rises only from 37 (Apr 20) to 50 (Apr 28). WIP also drops by 10–20 from each Friday to Saturday without matching completions (Apr 17→18: 35→16; Apr 24→25: 43→23). Hypotheses: exits that are not counted as `completed` (abandonment, cancellation, transfer), a narrower WIP scope on weekends, or out-of-hours events. Under the first hypothesis about 10 cases per day (roughly 10% of arrivals) left unrecorded during Apr 21–28. The gap closes in other windows (Apr 14→17: +17 vs +12; Apr 28→May 1: −18 vs −18).
4. **Service statistics are medians and p90s of handling time.** They are not means and not flow times. It is also not stated whether they describe cases arriving or completing that day. A median of daily medians is not the pooled median.
5. **Staffing is endogenous.** Extra FTE appears when backlog is already high (Apr 16–17, Apr 29 onward), so staff and WIP are positively correlated in pooled data for reasons unrelated to staff causing WIP.
6. **Unknown intervention time.** A daily mean of 7.0 FTE on Apr 29 with a mid-day start implies either an early start or more than 7 FTE afterwards.
7. **Serial dependence and small samples.** WIP is a stock, so consecutive days share state. Regimes have 1–7 days each, and effective sample sizes are smaller than the row counts.
8. **The hourly vectors and `peak_08_10_pct` disagree (observation).** The 08:00–09:59 share computed from the vectors is 16.8%, 18.1%, 9.6% and 11.8%, against table values of 31, 32, 47 and 22. A 09:00–10:59 window reproduces three of them (31.7, 33.3, 21.6) but not Apr 21 (36.5 vs 47). Either the column definition, the bin labels or the vectors are offset or stylised, so I use the column only as a relative concentration index.
9. **The complex flag may have changed meaning.** A coding change would alter `complex_pct` without altering work content.
10. **Handling time may depend on system state.** It rose on Apr 15–17 (median 29–33) while complex share was still 25–27%, so later increases cannot be attributed to mix alone.
11. **Edge censoring.** The 07:00 backlog is missing, nothing is known before Apr 6 or after May 3, and the four hourly days were chosen as "representative", not sampled.

**Outage and recovery treatment.** B1 and B2 are kept out of every "normal" estimate. The 129 completions on Apr 16 are the highest daily count and among the highest per-FTE outputs of the month (18.4 per FTE-day). That figure was reached with a queue that never ran dry and extra staff, so it is a ceiling under ideal feeding and not a planning capacity. Recovery was incomplete by the recorded counts (30 of 47), which means C1 inherits backlog and its WIP level is not a clean mix effect. The outage day itself shows no catch-up: throughput after 14:00 (28 in five hours) equals an ordinary afternoon (29 on Apr 7) despite roughly 70 cases in the system. Hypotheses are a staggered restart, lower afternoon staffing than the daily FTE mean suggests, or rework.

**What caused C1 (hypotheses compared).**

- A pure coding change would leave handling time, volume and completions unchanged. They all moved on the same day, so relabelling alone is not sufficient.
- A pure composition shift with fixed per-class handling times fits poorly. Solving the two-regime mean equations under A5 gives about 14 min for simple and about 81 min for complex cases. With 21% complex cases at that level, the baseline p90 would have to sit well above the observed 47.
- The most defensible reading is a real change in work content, possibly combined with a redefinition of "complex" and some congestion-driven inflation. On weekdays median handling time correlates as strongly with WIP (0.91) as with complex share (0.92).
- An abrupt overnight step on a Monday is more typical of an administrative event, such as a new intake stream or rule, than of organic drift. Per-variant handling times before and after Apr 20 would settle this.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative A: state-based regimes.** Days are grouped by what the backlog is doing, regardless of date. The rule is transparent but its margins are thin: shock if `outage_min` > 0, accumulating if net ≥ +4, draining if net ≤ −5, otherwise balanced. A one-case change would reclassify Apr 11 (+3) or Apr 26 (+4).

| State | Days | Arrivals/day | Completions/day | Median service | Mean WIP | ρ (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 balanced, weekday | Apr 6–10, 13–14 | 100 | 100 | 25 | 20.3 | 0.66 |
| S1 balanced, weekend | Apr 11–12, 18–19, May 2–3 | 44 | 44 | 29.5 | 16.0 | 0.83 |
| S2 accumulating, weekday | Apr 20–24, 27–28 | 114 | 101 | 35 | 44.3 | 1.17 |
| S2 accumulating, weekend | Apr 25–26 | 47 | 41 | 35.5 | 21.5 | 1.16 |
| S3 shock | Apr 15 | 105 | 58 | 31 | 47 | 1.21 |
| S4 draining | Apr 16–17, Apr 29–May 1 | 111 | 121 | 33 | 39.6 | 0.88 |

The operational story is one queue that moves between balanced, accumulating and draining states according to the sign of capacity minus demand, with extra staff as the control action. Mix, outage and intervention are causes of state changes, not regimes themselves.

| | Primary (calendar eras × day type) | Alternative A (backlog state) |
|---|---|---|
| Strengths | Boundaries come from exogenous events; usable for forecasting and before/after evaluation; keeps mix eras apart | Matches queueing mechanics and Little's-Law applicability; more days per state (five draining days instead of two plus two) |
| Weaknesses | Many small cells; assumes the mix step is the dominant driver; C1 is contaminated by leftover outage backlog | Defined on the outcome (net flow), so it is partly circular; S4 pools 26% and 44% complex days; S1 weekend pools ρ 0.77 and 0.97; not contiguous, so it cannot date a change |

The two agree on A1, C1, C2 and the outage. They disagree on whether Apr 16–17 and Apr 29–May 1 belong together, and on whether May 2–3 is "baseline-like". Alternative A says it is, because flows balance. The primary view says it is not, because that balance holds at ρ about 0.97 instead of 0.77.

**Alternative B: intraday regimes.** On the four hourly days the queue also has within-day regimes: a quiet opening (07:00–09:00), a ramp and peak (09:00–12:00) where even the baseline briefly runs above capacity (20 arrivals in 10:00–11:00 against a 12.8/h estimated capacity), and an afternoon decay. On Apr 21 this is replaced by three spikes. This segmentation is the right one for rostering, but it rests on four selected days and cannot be estimated for the other 24.

The three views together show a confounded design. High mix is observed at 6 FTE for seven weekdays and at 7 FTE for two or three. Low mix at 7 FTE is observed once, during recovery. The staffing effect under low mix and the mix effect free of backlog are therefore not identifiable.

Further Simpson-like patterns:

- **WIP and delay rank in opposite order across day types.** Baseline weekends have lower WIP than weekdays (13.8 vs 20.3) but a longer implied time in system (about 4.0 h vs 2.4 h), because λ is 2.4 times lower.

Interpretation:

- **A Fano factor above 1 across hours is not, by itself, overdispersion.** A smooth time-of-day profile produces it under a perfectly Poisson process. On Apr 7, 15 and 25 the high lag-1 autocorrelation (+0.6 to +0.7) shows that the dispersion is mostly a single diurnal hump.
- **Apr 21 is structurally different.** The lag-1 autocorrelation is negative and lag-3 is positive, because arrivals come as three spikes at 10:00–11:00, 13:00–14:00 and 16:00–17:00 (34, 24, 14) over a floor of about 5 per hour. The regular three-hour spacing and declining sizes suggest batch releases or scheduled feeds (hypothesis). If so, the recorded arrival time may be a registration time and true waiting started earlier.
- **Arrivals are bursty but completions are flat on Apr 21** (7–13 per hour from 09:00, CV 0.34). That is the signature of a saturated server whose output is clipped at capacity. On Apr 7 completions follow arrivals with a short lag, which fits a demand-limited system.
- **The outage was a supply shock.** The Apr 15 arrival profile is almost identical to Apr 7.
- **Carry-over is large (estimate).** The implied 07:00 backlog is `mean_wip` minus the time-average of cumulative arrivals minus completions. It assumes no unrecorded exits during the day. On Apr 7 about 18 of the mean WIP of 20 is stock carried in, so roughly 18% of a day's cases cross a night.
- **Burstiness is not the cause of the C1 deficit.** Even perfectly smoothed, Apr 21 demand exceeds daily capacity (ρ about 1.16). Bursts add intraday queueing on top of a daily shortfall.

**Daily sequence**

- **Dispersion.** Daily arrivals have Fano 10.0 pooled, 0.66 on weekdays and 0.73 on weekends, and 0.26 (A1) and 0.29 (C1) within regime, with a CV of about 5%. For seven Poisson days with mean 100 the simulated 95% range of the Fano factor is 0.20–2.43, and a value of 0.29 or lower occurs about 6% of the time. Within-regime counts are therefore suggestively underdispersed (scheduled or capped demand, or stylised data) but this is not established. The pooled overdispersion is entirely a mixture effect.
- **Lag dependence.** Arrivals have lag-1 autocorrelation +0.39 and lag-7 +0.72 over all days, which is weekly seasonality. On the weekday sequence, lag-1 is +0.51 for arrivals and +0.75 for WIP. After subtracting primary-regime means these fall to −0.20 and +0.09. Most of the measured dependence is level shifts between regimes. The residual estimates are biased downward by demeaning short segments and are too noisy to rule out dependence.
- **Runs.** Net inflow is positive on nine consecutive days (Apr 20–28). Under independent, symmetric signs that has probability of about 0.2%. This is persistence in the regime, not independent daily noise.
- **Change points (heuristic, not ground truth).** A mean-shift binary segmentation on the 20 weekdays gives:

| Variable | First split (new segment starts) | Share of variance removed | Second-level split of note |
|---|---|---|---|
| complex_pct | Apr 20 | 0.96 | — |
| peak_08_10_pct | Apr 20 | 0.85 | — |
| median_service_min | Apr 15 | 0.86 | Apr 20 (0.51 of the later segment) |
| p90_service_min | Apr 15 | 0.84 | Apr 20 (0.57) |

  The variables do not share one change date. Mix and concentration break on Apr 20, handling time and WIP break at the outage, and capacity breaks on Apr 29. That is direct evidence that demand, service, capacity and backlog are non-stationary on different schedules. The weekend rows narrow the mix step to between close on Apr 19 and open on Apr 20. The heuristic also splits the baseline at Apr 9 on a one- or two-minute drift, which shows it will always find a split, and its squared-error criterion is dominated by the outage day for `net` and `completed`.

- **Drift inside the baseline.** Complex share creeps from 19% to 24% and arrivals from about 98 to 105 before any step, so A1 is only approximately stationary.

**Why four days cannot support a stochastic model.** There is one realisation per day type. Rate profile and random variation cannot be separated without replicates, and hourly counts give no inter-arrival distribution or within-hour clustering. The days were selected as "representative", one of them is an outage, and their bins do not reconcile with the daily concentration column. They support qualitative shape statements only.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

**Definitions used.** L is the mean of `mean_wip` over the days in the window. Each day contributes the same 12 hours, so this is time-weighted. λ is total arrivals divided by 12 hours times the number of days. W = L/λ is an implied mean time in system in opening hours. It is not a measured flow time, not a handling time, and not calendar time.

**Boundary condition.** Over a finite window the identity is exact only if the system is empty at both ends. Otherwise it is approximate, to the extent that the stock carried in matches the stock carried out in size and age. Here the system is never empty (about 18 cases at 07:00 on an ordinary day), so every use depends on that balance.

**Throughput proxy.** Where arrivals and completions agree within about 1%, either can be used and I report arrivals. Where they differ, the two ratios bracket the answer and neither is a point estimate. Under accumulation L/λ with arrivals tends to understate the eventual time of the arriving cohort, because unfinished cases are truncated. If unrecorded exits explain the stock-flow gap, then arrivals are the better proxy for all exits and the result is a time to any exit, not to completion.

Two further limits apply to every row:

- **Calendar time is longer.** For the baseline a rough estimate is 2.4 h plus 0.18 × 12 h, or about 4.6 calendar hours, assuming each carried case crosses one night.
- **The baseline queue is not explained by staff capacity alone (estimate).** Roughly 4 of the 20 cases in system are in active handling (8.35/h × 28 min). At ρ of about 0.66 with six servers a simple multi-server model predicts almost no queue. Most baseline time in system is therefore waiting between steps or on other parties (hypothesis), and a capacity-only queueing model would be misleading even within A1.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing (recommendations; FTE figures are estimates under A5, with roughly ±10–15% uncertainty)**

- Plan by regime, not by the pooled day. At the current mix, weekday work content is about 4,600–5,000 staff-minutes per day. That needs 6.4–7.0 FTE to break even and about 7.5–8.2 FTE for ρ near 0.85. The baseline needed about 3.9 FTE to break even and had 5.9.
- Treat the intervention level of 7 FTE as the minimum for the high-mix regime. It gives ρ of about 0.93 on weekdays and 0.97 on weekends, which leaves little room for the bursts.
- Keep the extra weekend half-FTE. Before it, weekends added 12 cases to the backlog; with it they are flat.
- Remove the Friday dip (5.5 or 6.5 FTE) while WIP is above target.
- If the three-hourly spikes recur, roster to them (10:00–11:00, 13:00–14:00, 16:00–17:00). A single morning peak is the baseline pattern only.

**Backlog recovery**

- A three-hour outage cost about 47 cases. With one extra FTE the drain rate was 13–17 cases per day, so recovery needs three or more weekdays. Two were used before the weekend, and the recorded residual was 17.
- Start the surge on the outage afternoon. On Apr 15 the post-outage rate was 5.6 per hour against an estimated 9.6.
- Use a trigger on net flow and opening backlog, for example two consecutive days with net inflow above +8, or 07:00 backlog above about 30. The thresholds are illustrative and need calibrating on event data. Daily mean WIP lags the true state and should not be the trigger.
- Do not adopt recovery-day throughput (129) as a capacity target.

**Peak smoothing**

- Find the source of the Apr 21 spikes. If they are upstream batch releases, moving to hourly or continuous release would cut the intraday backlog peak (+27 on that day) at little cost.
- Smoothing does not close the daily gap. It should accompany added capacity or reduced work content, not replace them.

**Data collection**

- A case-level event log with arrival, step start and end, and exit type. This would resolve the stock-flow gap, which amounts to roughly 10% of arrivals over Apr 21–28.
- WIP at 07:00 and 19:00, and the snapshot scope on weekends.
- Intraday roster and the timestamp of the Apr 29 change.
- The version of the complex-variant coding rule, and per-variant handling times.
- Outage start and end, and whether handling clocks kept running.
- Out-of-hours submissions.
- The definition behind `peak_08_10_pct`.

**Policies that look beneficial under pooled averages but are risky under the segmented view**

1. **Ending the temporary staffing.** The pooled evidence is that utilisation is about 0.87–0.92, completions per FTE are flat at 16.7, and weekday WIP is no lower after the intervention than before (37.0 vs 34.5). Under the segmented view, 6 FTE at the current mix gives ρ of about 1.17 and a backlog growing by 13 or more cases per weekday, as in Apr 20–28.
2. **Levelling staff across the week** at the pooled 5.1 FTE. This would raise weekday pressure to roughly 1.25–1.35 in the high-mix regime and leave weekends heavily overstaffed.
3. **A single morning-peak roster** built from a pooled hourly profile. It would miss the 13:00 and 16:00 spikes that carry about a third of the arrivals on the one post-change day observed.

def best_split(x, min_len=2):
    x = np.asarray(x, float)
    sse0 = ((x - x.mean()) ** 2).sum()
    best = (None, 0.0)
    for k in range(min_len, len(x) - min_len + 1):
        sse = ((x[:k] - x[:k].mean()) ** 2).sum() + ((x[k:] - x[k:].mean()) ** 2).sum()
        gain = 1 - sse / sse0 if sse0 > 0 else 0.0
        if gain > best[1]:
            best = (k, gain)
    return best


def binseg(x, dates, depth=2, lo=0, out=None):
    out = [] if out is None else out
    k, gain = best_split(x)
    if k is None or depth == 0 or len(x) < 4:
        return out
    out.append((dates[lo + k], round(float(gain), 2), len(x)))
    binseg(x[:k], dates, depth - 1, lo, out)
    binseg(x[k:], dates, depth - 1, lo + k, out)
    return out
