# 1. Data audit and estimands

I use a 12-hour opening day (07:00–19:00). All rates below are cases per opening hour unless stated otherwise.

| Estimand | Definition | Units / convention |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival intensity `lambda` | arrivals in the day / 12 | cases per open hour |
| Completion intensity `mu` | completed in the day / 12 | cases per open hour |
| WIP `L` | daily `mean_wip` | mean cases in system, 15-minute snapshot basis |
| Service behavior | daily `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min` | active handling minutes, excluding waiting |
| Capacity | daily `staff_fte` | mean concurrent FTE available |
| Case mix | `complex_pct` | percentage of arrivals on a multi-step complex variant |
| Outage | `outage_min` | minutes of service interruption logged on that date |

Important limitations:

1. **Carry-over and missing backlog.** Cases can arrive on one day and complete on another; the 07:00 backlog is unknown. Therefore `completed - arrivals` on a single day is not necessarily the daily WIP change.
2. **Open-hours convention.** The center is closed at night, but cases remain in the system. If `mean_wip` is an opening-hours average rather than a 24-hour average, Little’s-Law calculations omit closed-hours sojourn time or use a different time basis.
3. **Non-stationarity.** The outage, recovery period, complex-mix shift, staffing intervention, and weekday/weekend pattern all change the operating regime. A daily mean can hide these shifts.
4. **Aggregation and burstiness.** Daily totals erase within-day peaks. The representative hourly vectors show extreme burstiness, especially on 2026-04-21, and hourly completion zeroes during the outage.
5. **Confounded attributes.** `complex_pct` may be a real case-mix change, a coding change, or both. `staff_fte` changed around the intervention, but exact intervention time is unlogged. Service medians/p90s are not mean service times, so no service-time distribution can be fitted.
6. **Internal inconsistency.** The `peak_08_10_pct` column does not match the representative hourly vectors. For example, 2026-04-21 has 3 arrivals in 08-09 and 8 in 09-10, or 11/115  9.6%, while the table reports 47%. I therefore treat that column as suspect and use the hourly vectors for burstiness evidence instead.
7. **Non-independence.** Daily rows are not independent: same-weekday autocorrelation, weekend effects, and carry-over backlog create serial dependence.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different segmentation is an all-days “operational wave” segmentation, ignoring weekday/weekend differences:

| Segment | Dates | Story | Arr/h | Comp/h | WIP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 2026-04-06..14 | stable, low complexity, no outage | 7.2 | 7.2 | 18.7 |
| Outage/recovery | 2026-04-15..19 | outage plus catch-up and weekend recovery | 6.7 | 6.4 | 32.6 |
| Mix shift | 2026-04-20..28 | high complexity, no added staff | 8.3 | 7.3 | 39.2 |
| Intervention | 2026-04-29..05-03 | staffing intervention, including weekend | 7.4 | 7.7 | 30.4 |

**Strengths:** it is simple, maps directly to management events, and has fewer cells. It correctly shows that the post-intervention period is the first time completions exceed arrivals overall.

**Weaknesses:** it mixes very different demand levels. The 04-20..28 segment contains both 9.5 arrivals/h weekdays and about 4 arrivals/h weekends. The post-intervention segment is diluted by the 05-02/03 weekend, so it understates the weekday improvement and makes the intervention look less dramatic. It also places 04-18/19 into recovery, although those days look more like ordinary low-mix weekends.

A second alternative would be a complexity-threshold segmentation: low-complex days versus high-complex days. That captures the service-time shift, but it fails on the recovery days: 04-16/17 have low `complex_pct` yet very high WIP, because they are driven by the outage backlog. It would also obscure the staffing intervention because both pre- and post-intervention high-complex days are common. The primary segmentation is therefore preferable for queueing analysis, while the wave segmentation is acceptable for a simple executive narrative.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

## 4.1 Weekday/weekend composition

Pooled over all 28 days:

- arrivals: 2519 / (28 × 12) = 7.50/h
- completions: 2409 / (28 × 12) = 7.17/h
- mean WIP: 836 / 28 = 29.9

Segmented by day type:

- weekdays: 9.00 arrivals/h, 8.60 completions/h, WIP 34.9
- weekends: 3.74 arrivals/h, 3.60 completions/h, WIP 17.4

The pooled average hides a weekday load that is roughly 2.4 times the weekend load. Any single-queue model using the pooled arrival rate will understate weekday congestion and overstate weekend workload.

## 4.2 Complex-mix confound

Pooled complexity comparison:

- days with `complex_pct < 30`: mean WIP  23.6
- days with `complex_pct >= 40`: mean WIP  37.4

A naive conclusion would be that complexity alone explains the WIP increase. Segmented evidence says otherwise:

- pre-shift low-mix weekdays: WIP  20.3
- pre-intervention high-mix weekdays: WIP  44.3
- pre-intervention high-mix weekends: WIP  21.5

High complexity occurs on weekends too, but weekend WIP remains low because demand and staffing are very different. Thus pooling by complexity alone confounds case mix with calendar demand and capacity.

## 4.3 Outage, recovery, and staffing intervention

Pooled before/after the 2026-04-29 intervention:

- before 04-29: WIP  29.7, net completion-minus-arrival  0.46/h
- from 04-29 onward: WIP  30.4, net  +0.30/h

The pooled comparison shows little change. The segmented weekday comparison is sharper:

- high-mix weekdays before intervention: arrivals 9.5/h, completions 8.4/h, WIP rising from 37 to 50
- weekdays after intervention: arrivals 9.6/h, completions 10.1/h, WIP falling from 43 to 32

The sign of the net flow reverses once weekends and the ordinary pre-shift period are removed. Similarly, the recovery days 04-16/17 look like high-throughput days, but they are not sustainable capacity; after the backlog was absorbed, completion rates fell back to about 8.4/h in the pre-intervention high-mix period.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

The hourly vectors are only four representative days, so they cannot define a complete stochastic model. Still, they show clear non-Poisson behavior. I use the sample Fano factor = sample variance / mean, and coefficient of variation = standard deviation / mean.

| Representative day | Arrival Fano | Arrival CV | Completion Fano | Completion CV | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-04-07 | 3.2 | 0.62 | 1.7 | 0.45 | Ordinary weekday; arrivals more variable than a Poisson process |
| 2026-04-15 | 4.1 | 0.69 | 2.3 | 0.68 | Outage creates zero-completion bins and extra dispersion |
| 2026-04-21 | 10.1 | 1.03 | 1.0 | 0.34 | Bursty arrivals; completions are relatively smooth |
| 2026-04-25 | 1.3 | 0.55 | 0.8 | 0.47 | Weekend; lower volume and less burstiness |

The 04-21 vector is particularly important: 34 arrivals in the 10:00–11:00 bin and 24 in the 13:00–14:00 bin create a bimodal intraday profile. A daily Poisson assumption would severely understate queue-length variance and the risk of waiting-time spikes.

On the daily scale, serial dependence is dominated by the day-of-week rhythm. The lag-7 autocorrelation of arrivals is clearly positive because the same weekday repeats, while lag-1 is distorted by the weekend/weekday alternation. I treat these as descriptive indicators only; 28 daily observations are too few for reliable autocorrelation inference.

Change-point evidence is visible in the daily series:

- 2026-04-15: large negative net count and a WIP spike.
- 2026-04-20: `complex_pct` jumps from 25–27% to 41%+, and service p90 jumps to 78–88.
- 2026-04-29: net completion-minus-arrival becomes positive on weekdays and WIP starts falling.

The code below implements a transparent rolling 3-day net-count heuristic; it is a diagnostic, not a definitive change-point test. Four representative hourly vectors cannot establish a full stochastic model because they are not a random sample, do not cover all regimes, and cannot provide confidence intervals for diurnal shape.

# 6. Little’s-Law applicability matrix

| Window | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow time if defensible | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: pre-shift normal weekdays | Moderate; arrivals  completions | Arrivals or completions, nearly equal | Daily snapshot means, open-hours convention | Moderate; opening backlog unknown but stable on average | Approximately stationary over the 7 weekdays | L/ = 2.43 h; L/ = 2.45 h | Approximate only |
| B: normal weekends | Moderate; short sample | Arrivals or completions | Same | Low-to-moderate | Approximate; only 4 days | L/  3.95 h; L/  3.97 h | Approximate only |
| C: outage day | None | Neither | Not comparable; WIP spikes during zero service | High | Not stationary | Not calculated | Not defensible |
| D: recovery weekdays | Unstable; backlog being drained | Completions are catch-up, not sustainable throughput | WIP high but falling | High | Not stationary | Not calculated | Not defensible |
| E: high-mix weekdays, pre-intervention | Unstable; WIP accumulating | Neither; arrivals exceed completions and WIP rises | WIP not stationary | High | Not stationary; upward WIP trend | Not defensible; diagnostic L/ = 4.66 h, L/ = 5.29 h | Not defensible |
| G: post-intervention weekdays | Transitional; WIP decreasing | Completions exceed arrivals; no steady state yet | Same, but not stationary | Medium | Transitional | 3-day nominal: L/ = 3.86 h, L/ = 3.67 h; excluding 04-29: L/ = 3.56 h, L/ = 3.37 h | Approximate only |
| H: post-intervention weekends | Stable but n = 2 | Arrivals = completions | Same | Low-to-moderate | Insufficient data | L/ = L/  5.02 h | Approximate only |
| All 28 days pooled | Not stable | Neither | Mixed regimes | High | Multiple shifts and nonstationarity | Diagnostic only: L/ = 3.98 h, L/ = 4.16 h | Not defensible |

The main principle: Little’s Law requires a stationary, time-homogeneous system. Recovery days and high-mix accumulation periods violate this, so the implied flow times computed there are diagnostics, not steady-state estimates.

# 7. Operational implications

- **Staff by day type and regime, not by pooled average.** Weekday demand is about 9.0–9.6 arrivals/h; weekend demand is about 3.5–4.1 arrivals/h. A capacity plan based on the pooled 7.5 arrivals/h is wrong for both.
- **Do not treat recovery as normal capacity.** The 04-16/17 completion surge was backlog drainage. If that rate is used for staffing, the center will appear more capable than it actually is during sustained high-mix periods.
- **Manage backlog explicitly.** The pre-intervention high-mix period accumulated WIP from 37 to 50 over two weeks. A backlog cap or trigger for surge capacity would have intervened earlier.
- **Smooth intraday bursts.** The 04-21 hourly vector shows a severe peak around 10:00–11:00 and another around 13:00–14:00. Appointment throttling, self-service options, or dynamic staff breaks could reduce the queue tail.
- **Collect process-mining-grade data.** Record case IDs, timestamps for arrival/service start/completion, variant IDs, queue length at 07:00 and 19:00, and the exact staffing-intervention time. The daily aggregates in this prompt are not a PM4Py event log; they cannot support full temporal process mining.
- **Cautious policy example:** A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages is “use the average 7.5 arrivals/h to staff 6 FTE every day.” It appears adequate because pooled WIP is only about 30. Under the segmented view, weekday high-mix demand reaches 9.5 arrivals/h, WIP grows, and service p90 rises above 80 minutes. Moving staff from weekdays to weekends based on the pooled average would make the weekday backlog worse while adding capacity where it is not needed. A safer policy is day-type-specific staffing with explicit weekday high-mix surge capacity.
