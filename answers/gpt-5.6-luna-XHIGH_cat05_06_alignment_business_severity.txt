## 1. Materiality Framework

I use severity as an assessment of business consequence, not as a transformation of `raw_cost`. The assessment starts with the control objective and then considers exposure, timing, reversibility, customer and regulatory impact, recurrence, evidence quality, and whether the case is inside the model's scope.

| Severity | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 — Informational | No meaningful control or customer impact. The observation is harmless telemetry, a fitting silent transition, or a correctly handled out-of-scope lifecycle variant. Track it for model quality or housekeeping. |
| 2 — Minor | A localized data, mapping, notification, or auditability defect with little plausible financial harm and no demonstrated payment-control bypass. It should be corrected, but is not normally an incident. |
| 3 — Moderate | A material control or evidence weakness, or a policy breach with limited or contained exposure. Payment may have been made under a less reliable decision record, although no loss or prohibited sequence is shown. |
| 4 — Major | A credible breach of a high-value or fraud-sensitive control, a recent-bank-change control failure, or a material evidence failure that could impair recovery, investigation, or regulatory response. Exposure may be bounded, but the failure is operationally significant. |
| 5 — Critical | Payment was authorized, accepted, or made before an applicable approval/control, or a high-value/high-risk case lacks a required fraud control, with substantial or potentially irreversible exposure. Immediate containment and case-level investigation are warranted. |

The dimensions are applied as follows:

- Control objective: a missing fraud screen, missing manual approval, or payment-before-approval is more serious than an absent UI event.
- Amount and exposure: EUR 480,000 and EUR 90,000 increase the consequence of a control failure; amount does not make a required control optional.
- Reversibility and timing: a payment already accepted by the bank, or a payment sent after a beneficiary-bank change, may be difficult to recover. A notification can usually be corrected, although a missed statutory or contractual window matters.
- Customer and regulatory harm: fraud, misdirected funds, denial of due process, or inability to demonstrate a required decision raises severity.
- Recurrence: the 74 retry cases and four data-aware auto-approval cases require systemic remediation even when any one case has modest harm.
- Evidence reliability: a signed external document, an immutable delivery receipt, and a mutable CRM text link do not provide the same assurance. Weak evidence can be a serious audit/control problem without proving that the underlying activity did not occur.
- Model scope: a payable-claims model should not be penalized for denied or withdrawn variants unless those variants are intended to be in scope. Scope ambiguity is itself a governance issue.

Confidence is separate from severity. Severity asks, “How bad would this be if the indicated business condition is true, and how much control exposure is present?” Confidence asks, “How well do the alignment, timestamps, attributes, and evidence establish the interpretation?” A high-severity case can have medium confidence when an external activity may have occurred but is poorly linked. Conversely, a low-severity model defect can have high confidence. Missing log evidence is not proof that the activity never occurred; it is proof only of an evidence or observability gap unless other evidence resolves it.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied cost is an edit distance under a uniform cost function: every visible log/model move costs 1, while every synchronous or silent move costs 0. That is useful for diagnosing fitness under a particular model, but it omits the dimensions that determine business risk.

1. Cost does not encode amount, fraud sensitivity, or irreversibility. CL-602 costs only 1 despite a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a recent beneficiary-bank change and no retained Fraud Screen. CL-604 costs 3 despite a EUR 90,000 payment accepted before approval. The number of edits is not a monetary exposure measure.

2. Scope can dominate the score. CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 largely because the payable model requires future activities after a denial or withdrawal. If those are legitimate terminal outcomes, the business process is behaving correctly and the model is wrong or incomplete. Their high costs are useful evidence of scope mismatch, not evidence of four operational failures.

3. Hidden moves are free by design, not proof of low risk. CL-605 has a `T(tau)` routing step and cost 0. That is technically fitting, but zero cost says only that the silent route can be absorbed by the model. It does not validate amount/risk data or prove that a control was performed.

4. Data conditions are outside the structural tree. CL-609 and CL-610 both have cost 0 because Auto Approve is a permitted structural XOR branch. CL-609 violates the EUR 5,000 ceiling; CL-610 violates both the recent-bank-change restriction and the mandatory Fraud Screen/Manual Approve rule. A cost based only on event order cannot see attributes or seven-day history.

5. Activity granularity changes the edit count. CL-601 costs 3 because UI workbench events were logged, even though they are not business activities under P5. Conversely, CL-611 costs 2 because one business assessment is split into a log label and a model label. The same underlying work can create zero, one, or several edits depending on naming and event granularity.

6. Duplicate telemetry is not the same as duplicate business effect. CL-603 costs 1 and is strongly associated with a connector retry. It could represent one logical notification with two transport attempts, or two customer-visible messages. Cost does not distinguish those outcomes; provider delivery evidence does.

7. Missing core evidence is not equivalent to missing work. CL-606 costs 1, but a licensed external adjuster signed an assessment. CL-608 costs 1, but a CRM-linked portal message occurred three hours after payment. The business questions are whether the external artifact is genuine, correctly linked, delivered, retained, and timely. Alignment cost sees only the absent core event.

8. Sequencing and control applicability matter more than the count of edits. In CL-604, the critical fact is that payment was accepted before the applicable approval. In CL-602, the critical fact is that a required fraud control cannot be evidenced. A cost of 1 or 3 is not a substitute for checking whether an irreversible action occurred before its gate.

9. A single cost function cannot express confidence. CL-611 has relatively strong evidence of a naming mismatch; CL-608 has weaker evidence because the CRM link is mutable. Both have cost 1 or 2, but the confidence and remediation differ. Confidence should be stored separately from severity, and both should be supported by provenance.

Therefore raw cost should be retained as a technical diagnostic column, not discarded, but triage should combine it with policy predicates, event timing, amount, evidence reliability, model scope, recurrence, and reversibility. The resulting risk-prioritization evidence table is an aid to review; it must not replace the narrative judgment about what happened.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


# This is the supplied normative process-tree expression. The parser's exact
# rendering of the resulting tree may differ by PM4Py version.
tree_string = (
    "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
    "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
    "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
)

process_tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(process_tree)


# A small event log is enough to exercise the conversion and alignment API.
# The diagnostic input intentionally contains a missing Fraud Screen and an
# out-of-model Deny Claim. UI/retry details are omitted here because the
# supplied alignment table, rather than this reduced log, is authoritative.
trace_definitions = {
    "CL-601": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve",
        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-602": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Manual Approve",
        "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-604": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Pay Claim",
        "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant",
    ],
    "CL-607": [
        "Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim",
        "Notify Claimant",
    ],
}

event_rows = []
base_time = pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01 00:00:00", tz="UTC")
for case_id, activities in trace_definitions.items():
    for position, activity in enumerate(activities):
        event_rows.append(
            {
                "case:concept:name": case_id,
                "concept:name": activity,
                "time:timestamp": base_time + pd.Timedelta(
                    minutes=position
                ),
            }
        )

event_df = pd.DataFrame(event_rows)
event_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    event_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(event_df)

# PM4Py computes its own optimal alignments under its defaults. They are a
# reproduction aid, not a replacement for the authoritative supplied moves:
# alternative optimal alignments and PM4Py's move formatting/costs can differ.
pm4py_alignments = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
    event_log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
)
print("PM4Py alignments:")
for case_id, alignment in zip(trace_definitions, pm4py_alignments):
    print(case_id, alignment)


# One row per visible non-synchronous move in the supplied authoritative
# alignment notation. Synchronous and silent moves have technical cost zero
# and are not needed for the move-pattern aggregation.
move_rows = [
    # case, move kind, activity, cost
    ("CL-601", "L", "Open Workbench", 1),
    ("CL-601", "L", "Autosave", 1),
    ("CL-601", "L", "Close Workbench", 1),
    ("CL-602", "M", "Fraud Screen", 1),
    ("CL-603", "L", "Notify Claimant", 1),
    ("CL-604", "L", "Pay Claim", 1),
    ("CL-604", "M", "Fraud Screen", 1),
    ("CL-604", "M", "Pay Claim", 1),
    ("CL-606", "M", "Assess Loss", 1),
    ("CL-607", "L", "Deny Claim", 1),
    ("CL-607", "M", "Assess Loss", 1),
    ("CL-607", "M", "Auto Approve", 1),
    ("CL-607", "M", "Pay Claim", 1),
    ("CL-608", "M", "Notify Claimant", 1),
    ("CL-611", "L", "Assess Damage", 1),
    ("CL-611", "M", "Assess Loss", 1),
    ("CL-612", "L", "Withdraw Claim", 1),
    ("CL-612", "M", "Auto Approve", 1),
    ("CL-612", "M", "Pay Claim", 1),
    ("CL-612", "M", "Notify Claimant", 1),
]
move_df = pd.DataFrame(
    move_rows,
    columns=["case_id", "move_kind", "activity", "technical_cost"],
)
move_df["move_pattern"] = (
    move_df["move_kind"] + ":" + move_df["activity"]
)


# These fields are deliberately separate from technical alignment cost.
case_rows = [
    ("CL-601", 1200, "LOW", 3, 1, True, "P1 satisfied"),
    ("CL-602", 480000, "HIGH", 1, 5, True, "P2 fraud evidence missing"),
    ("CL-603", 3200, "LOW", 1, 2, False, "P4 retry/duplicate review"),
    ("CL-604", 90000, "MEDIUM", 3, 5, True, "P2/P3 payment-before-approval"),
    ("CL-605", 800, "LOW", 0, 1, False, "P1 satisfied"),
    ("CL-606", 75000, "HIGH", 1, 3, True, "external assessment linkage"),
    ("CL-607", 0, "N/A", 4, 1, False, "scope disputed; denied"),
    ("CL-608", 250000, "HIGH", 1, 3, True, "P4 alternate-channel evidence"),
    ("CL-609", 18000, "MEDIUM", 0, 3, True, "P1 amount ceiling"),
    ("CL-610", 4500, "LOW", 0, 4, True, "P1/P2 recent bank change"),
    ("CL-611", 62000, "HIGH", 2, 2, False, "label mapping"),
    ("CL-612", 2000, "LOW", 4, 1, False, "scope disputed; withdrawn"),
]
case_df = pd.DataFrame(
    case_rows,
    columns=[
        "case_id", "amount_eur", "risk_level", "raw_cost",
        "business_severity", "policy_overlay_issue", "policy_overlay_flag",
    ],
)

move_df = move_df.merge(
    case_df[
        [
            "case_id", "amount_eur", "risk_level",
            "policy_overlay_issue", "policy_overlay_flag",
        ]
    ],
    on="case_id",
    how="left",
    validate="many_to_one",
)


def unique_values(values):
    return ",".join(sorted({str(value) for value in values}))


# Aggregation by technical move pattern. Amount is shown as exposure context,
# not multiplied into technical cost or used as an automatic severity score.
move_pattern_summary = (
    move_df.groupby("move_pattern", as_index=False)
    .agg(
        case_count=("case_id", "nunique"),
        occurrence_count=("case_id", "size"),
        technical_cost_sum=("technical_cost", "sum"),
        amount_total_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        risk_levels=("risk_level", unique_values),
        policy_overlay_issue_moves=("policy_overlay_issue", "sum"),
    )
    .sort_values(["technical_cost_sum", "case_count"], ascending=False)
)

# A case-level roll-up keeps raw cost and the policy overlay distinct.
case_rollup = (
    case_df.groupby("policy_overlay_flag", as_index=False)
    .agg(
        case_count=("case_id", "nunique"),
        raw_cost_sum=("raw_cost", "sum"),
        amount_total_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        risk_levels=("risk_level", unique_values),
        policy_issue_case_count=("policy_overlay_issue", "sum"),
    )
)

# Evidence table for human prioritization. The severity values are the
# narrative judgments above; this table does not calculate or replace them.
risk_evidence = case_df[
    [
        "case_id", "raw_cost", "amount_eur", "risk_level",
        "business_severity", "policy_overlay_issue", "policy_overlay_flag",
    ]
].sort_values(
    ["business_severity", "amount_eur", "raw_cost"],
    ascending=[False, False, False],
)

print("\nMove-pattern summary:")
print(move_pattern_summary.to_string(index=False))
print("\nCase roll-up by policy overlay:")
print(case_rollup.to_string(index=False))
print("\nRisk-prioritization evidence (narrative severity retained separately):")
print(risk_evidence.to_string(index=False))
```
