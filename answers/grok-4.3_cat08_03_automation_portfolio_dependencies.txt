**2. Dependency and prerequisite map**  
A6 (normalization) and A5 (missing documents) must precede A8 and A9; poor upstream data creates GIGO for committee packs and contracts. A3 and A4 are prerequisites for A9 and A10 because KYC clearance and entity resolution are legal gates before signing. A2 depends on A1 for clean classification input. A7 has external valuation dependency that cannot be removed internally. A11 requires reliable four-eyes and KYC status; it cannot be deployed until A3/A9 are live. Human escalation capacity (3 compliance specialists, 9 credit analysts) limits A3 and A6; back-office under-utilization after 15:00 offers buffer only if handovers are reduced. Core-banking integration is limited to two modules in the first six months, so A9 and A11 compete for that slot. A12 is needed to preserve four-eyes in High-exposure cases.

**3. Portfolio alternatives**  
- Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A6. Optimizes visible labor reduction and portal throughput; sacrifices synchronization points (valuation, committee) and control strengthening.  
- Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A8. Optimizes critical-path waits (entity mismatch, missing docs, normalization) and committee packaging; sacrifices pure frequency wins and immediate legal-rule automation.  
- Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12. Optimizes auditability, four-eyes enforcement, and KYC clearance; sacrifices direct analyst time savings and external valuation improvement.

**4. Expected process-level effects**  
Quick-win: Reduces Register application and Classify queues but increases inflow to KYC and Credit analysis, raising median waiting time before Credit analysis (28 h) and Committee packaging (26 h) unless A6 is included. Rework on Request missing documents falls modestly.  
Bottleneck/synchronization: Cuts Resolve entity mismatch (31 h wait) and Request missing documents loops, lowering Committee packaging wait by ~15 h and reducing Contract preparation  Remediate loops. Credit-analyst demand rises initially but stabilizes once A6 is active. Four-eyes violations may increase if A12 is absent.  
Compliance-first: Lowers provisional signing (6.5 %) and contract-remediation (8.5–14 %) rates; increases handovers to compliance specialists but reduces downstream rework. Cycle time for High-exposure SME cases improves most because KYC gate is enforced earlier.

**5. Risks and compensating controls**  
Risks: false-negative KYC hits (A3), model drift on financial PDFs (A6), GIGO in committee packs (A8), outdated control rules in contracts (A9), and four-eyes bypass during month-end (A12). Compensating controls: mandatory human-override logging with timestamp and rationale for every model suggestion; daily exception queue sampling by compliance; periodic rule-library review by legal; automated four-eyes check before Committee packaging for High-exposure cases; staged rollout with rollback trigger if control-violation rate exceeds 3 %.

**6. Recommended phased rollout**  
Phase 1 (months 1–3): Deploy A5 and A6 (no deep core-banking integration). Monitor missing-document loop rate and normalization throughput. Gate: >25 % reduction in Request missing documents rework before next phase.  
Phase 2 (months 4–6): Add A4 (still no deep integration). Monitor entity-mismatch resolution time and KYC-to-signing critical path. Gate: entity-mismatch wait <20 h median.  
Phase 3 (months 7–12): Select one deep-integration module (A9) plus A12. Monitor contract-remediation rate and four-eyes compliance. Monthly dashboard: cycle time, waiting time before Committee packaging, control-violation %, analyst utilization.

**7. Final recommendation**  
Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A8) is recommended. Highest-frequency automation (A1) should not be first because it merely accelerates inflow into already-constrained KYC, financial-review, and committee stages, increasing waiting time and analyst overload. The selected modules directly attack the longest external and exception waits (Resolve entity mismatch, Request missing documents, Wait for external valuation via better triage data) while preserving control integrity and respecting the two-deep-integration limit. This yields the largest end-to-end cycle-time reduction with acceptable risk.
