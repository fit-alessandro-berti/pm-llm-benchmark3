# Warranty-Process Redesign: Reconciling the Fast-Path Paradox

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The frequency DFG shows a heavily skewed process: 3,600 of 5,000 cases (72%) go through `Intake  Auto eligibility screen`, and 2,550 of those (51% of all cases, ~71% of auto-screen output) reach `Fast approval`. The dominant happy path is therefore `Intake  Auto screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close`, traversed by **~2,300 cases (46% of volume)**. This is the "fast and cheap" path the executive sees in dashboards.

The contradiction is that this same path concentrates the bulk of the process's failure modes:

- **Rework statistics reveal a 14.1% complaint/reopen rate on Fast approval versus 3.3% on the Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device path** — a ~4× penalty for speed. Applied to ~2,300 fast approvals, this produces ~324 complaint-driven reopens, vs ~14 on the equivalent repair volume.
- **Performance DFG shows the long tail of these complaints is severe:** `Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen` has mean 72 h, p90 150 h; `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` has mean 96 h, p90 180 h. The temporal profile annotation links this to incorrect replacement-part selection. By contrast, the repair path's complaint edge has mean not shown but only 30 cases and a 3.3% rate.
- **Conformance diagnostics expose the structural failure:** token-based replay gives a **log fitness of 0.91 but only 68% of traces fit the normative Petri net**, meaning 32% of cases deviate. The named move-on-log deviations — *Fast approval before Evidence completeness*, *Customer complaint/reopen after close*, and *Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices* — map directly onto the dominant fast path and explain both the rework and the compliance exposure.
- **Normative rule violations R1 (claims >EUR 400 must pass Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval) and R2 (evidence completeness before dispatch, except Premium low-risk) concentrate in fast path output**, especially the 14% Premium high-value and 21% Standard high-value segments. Compliance exposure is therefore not uniform; it is segment-coupled.
- **Customer-segment outcomes resolve the paradox by segment**: Premium high-value devices have **22% complaint/reopen on fast path vs 5% on reviewed/repair**, while Premium low-risk accessories have only 6% vs 3%. The fast path is genuinely good for low-risk accessories and genuinely catastrophic for high-value devices. The current process cannot tell them apart because the auto-screen rules are described as "outdated and not segment-aware."
- **The Request-missing-evidence loop is a parallel quiet crisis:** mean dwell time `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` is **34 h (p90 88 h)**, and 26% of cases that enter it do so at least twice. The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases) shows median cycle time **140 h** and the highest abandonment rate.
- **Resource pressure is misaligned**: Fraud/risk review (2 specialists, EUR 52/h) is the binding bottleneck on Mondays and after marketing campaigns; Manual eligibility review queue grows non-linearly above 82% utilization; Quality check (2 inspectors) is *underused except after repair campaigns* — meaning the spare capacity to absorb a pre-dispatch verification exists today.

**Synthesis of the contradiction:** The fast path is "cheap" only because the current cost accounting stops at labor before dispatch and ignores (a) EUR 410 average wrong-replacement cost on high-value devices, (b) service-credit exposure on Premium cases exceeding 24 h, (c) compliance exception risk on R1/R2, and (d) churn from complaints. **Fast  efficient** in a process where downstream correction cost is larger than upstream savings.

## 2. Redesign A — "Segment-Stratified Triage with Selective Risk-Gating"

**Redesigned flow:**
1. Intake captures customer segment (Premium/Standard) and defect/device class (low-risk accessory / mid-risk device / high-value device) at the same time as the claim is registered.
2. The auto-screen is replaced with a **segment-and-value-aware ruleset** that emits one of four explicit dispositions: `Express low-risk`, `Standard fast`, `Verified fast`, `Reviewer queue`.
   - `Express low-risk`: Premium or Standard low-risk accessories below the EUR 400 threshold  straight to Fast approval (compliant with R2 exception, R1 not triggered).
   - `Standard fast`: Standard low-risk accessories above EUR 400, mid-risk devices  Fast approval **only after evidence-completeness token is present**.
   - `Verified fast`: any high-value device  mandatory light Fraud/risk review (target 2–4 h, not the current 11 h) before Fast approval. The Fraud/risk review is reduced to a model-assisted scoring + 5-minute human confirm for the 60% of cases scoring below threshold; only the top-band goes to full manual.
   - `Reviewer queue`: routed to Manual eligibility review when the auto-screen cannot reach a confidence threshold.
3. Dispatch requires an evidence-completeness token. The exception for Premium low-risk is preserved and explicit (R2 compliance).
4. Reopened claims still pass Manual eligibility review (R3 unchanged), but with the segment already attached, the reviewer starts with context.

**Segments/paths targeted:** High-value Premium and Standard segments, the EUR 400+ boundary where R1 currently fails, and the auto-screen as the chokepoint that misroutes ~14% × 22% = ~150 cases into the wrong fast path today.

**Mechanism of improvement:**
- Routes the ~35% of cases (Premium high-value 14% + Standard high-value 21%) through a *fast-but-verified* lane that removes the dominant R1/R2 violation and the 410 EUR wrong-replacement exposure on the ~324 cases that today generate most of the rework cost.
- Forces the auto-screen to discriminate, eliminating the move-on-log deviation "Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices."
- Keeps the genuine speed advantage for the 43% of cases that *should* be fast (both low-risk accessory tiers).

**What it deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:**
- **Increases load on the Fraud/risk review bottleneck** (only 2 specialists). Mitigated by model-assisted triage but still a net add. The prompt warns that rule updates can increase manual inflow by 15–35%; this redesign consciously pushes some of that into Fraud/risk instead.
- Does **not** shorten the 34 h evidence-to-review loop — that is left for Redesign B.
- Does **not** add a pre-dispatch quality gate — that is Redesign C. Wrong replacement for the residual ~5% of cases still slipping through remains an exposure.

## 3. Redesign B — "Front-Loaded Evidence Portal with Parallelized Risk Triage"

**Redesigned flow:**
1. Intake is replaced (or augmented) by a **guided evidence portal**: at claim submission the customer uploads photos, receipts, and a defect self-classification. The portal uses device-image recognition and a structured checklist to mark the claim as `Evidence-complete` or `Evidence-incomplete` on the spot.
2. `Evidence-complete` cases flow to the segment-aware auto-screen of Redesign A's disposition logic (lightweight re-use).
3. `Evidence-incomplete` cases do **not** go straight to Manual eligibility review. Instead they enter a **dual-track triage**: (a) automated evidence-gap detection enriches the case, and (b) a parallelized risk-signal check (model-assisted Fraud/risk, capacity borrowed from Quality check idle time) runs concurrently. Only cases that remain ambiguous after both tracks land in Manual review.
4. The single `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` edge (mean 34 h, p90 88 h) is broken: customers with low digital literacy receive a **fallback assisted-upload channel** (e.g., one-call photo capture by an agent), preventing abandonment.
5. Abandonment is monitored as a first-class metric; cases silent for >72 h receive a single outbound call rather than auto-abandon.

**Segments/paths targeted:** The 22% "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (140 h median cycle, highest abandonment), and the 26% of cases that re-enter `Request missing evidence` at least twice. Also targets the Fraud/risk review Monday bottleneck by spreading risk-signal work across the week.

**Mechanism of improvement:**
- Removes the 34 h evidence loop dwell by collapsing evidence gathering into a single front-loaded interaction. Even a 30% reduction (the lower bound stated in the prompt) on the ~1,100 cases/year that hit evidence-incomplete frees ~11,200 h of cycle time.
- Parallelizes risk triage with evidence completion instead of serializing them; this directly addresses the "Fraud/risk review  Fast approval" mean of 16 h and the "Manual  Fraud/risk" mean of 18 h, by letting both run after intake instead of stacking.
- Addresses abandonment — the segment with the worst customer experience — by replacing auto-abandon with assisted outreach.

**What it deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:**
- **Does not fix wrong-replacement selection on high-value fast-path cases.** Those still go through the same Fast approval  Dispatch edge once evidence is present.
- Carries an explicit abandonment risk for low-digital-literacy customers (the prompt warns of this). The assisted-upload fallback is the mitigation; without it the redesign is worse on customer experience.
- **Longest implementation lead time** (portal build, image-recognition training, fallback channel). Cannot be piloted on Premium-low-risk without redesigning intake itself.
- Increases initial intake cost per case (portal UX, model inference), partially offset by removing downstream evidence-loop labor.

## 4. Redesign C — "Confidence-Gated Fast Approval with Pre-Dispatch Verification"

**Redesigned flow:**
1. Fast approval is split into two sub-decisions: `Fast approval (low confidence)` and `Fast approval (high confidence)`, both of which still precede dispatch.
2. The auto-screen (segment-aware per Redesign A) emits a confidence score. High-confidence cases go straight to dispatch (current behavior). Low-confidence cases — defined by either (a) high-value device, (b) ambiguous defect code, or (c) replacement-part selection ambiguity — enter a **lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate** staffed by the currently underused Quality check inspectors.
3. The pre-dispatch gate is a **soft gate**, not a re-review: it confirms part selection against a device-serial lookup, runs a 5-minute visual/photo check, and either confirms dispatch or escalates to Manual eligibility review. The prompt states this gate costs 6–18 h; this redesign commits to the lower end (target 8 h) by limiting scope to part selection, not full re-review.
4. Dispatch without part-confirmation is blocked at the system level for high-value devices; the R2 evidence-completeness token is still required.

**Segments/paths targeted:** The ~324 complaint/reopen cases that originate in the fast path, especially the high-value subset where wrong-replacement cost is EUR 410 (vs EUR 95 for low-risk). Also targets the temporal-profile deviation "Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen is a long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection" — directly.

**Mechanism of improvement:**
- Eliminates a large fraction of the 14.1% fast-path complaint rate (target: bring it to 6%, approaching the repair-path baseline of 3.3%). At EUR 410 per wrong-replacement on high-value, even a halving of the complaint rate on the ~340 high-value cases pays back the gate cost multiple times over.
- **Reuses Quality check idle capacity** (2 inspectors, EUR 38/h, described as "underused except after repair campaigns") — turning an underused resource into the gate's staffing source, so cost per case rises only marginally.
- Preserves the speed advantage for high-confidence low-risk claims (43% of cases).

**What it deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:**
- **Adds 6–18 h cycle time to a subset of fast-path cases**, partially eroding the speed advantage that made the fast path attractive. The trade is intentional: it exchanges ~8 h of predictable verification for a 70–80% reduction in the 96 h mean / 180 h p90 tail of `Dispatch  Customer complaint/reopen`.
- **Does not address the evidence loop** (Redesign B's domain).
- **Does not solve R1/R2 for the high-value fast path on its own** — it reduces the *consequence* of a wrong fast approval but does not prevent the rule violation. Best combined with the routing logic of Redesign A.
- Creates a new potential bottleneck on Quality check inspectors if fast-path volume grows or if the gate is over-applied.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- *Failure 1:* The auto-screen rule update could push 15–35% more cases into Manual review (as the prompt warns), and Manual queue already grows non-linearly above 82% utilization. Result: cycle time on the reviewed path deteriorates, negating the segment-stratification benefit.
- *Failure 2:* The Fraud/risk light-touch review (2 specialists, EUR 52/h) becomes the new binding bottleneck. The Monday spike and post-campaign spike get *worse*, not better, because high-value volume is now serialized through them. Without model-assisted triage actually shipping, the 2–4 h target slips to 8+ h.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Fraud/risk review queue, and possibly Manual eligibility review if rule updates misclassify.

**Redesign B**
- *Failure 1:* The evidence portal increases abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers (the prompt explicitly flags this). The 22% unclear/evidence segment already has the highest abandonment; making self-service the default without a robust fallback *worsens* CX on the worst-CX segment.
- *Failure 2:* Parallelized risk triage reuses Quality check inspectors' idle time, but Quality check is "underused except after repair campaigns." A campaign spike (marketing event, product recall) would simultaneously *increase* repair-campaign workload *and* gate work, collapsing the spare capacity assumption.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Customer-service assisted-upload channel, or Quality check inspectors during campaign overlap.

**Redesign C**
- *Failure 1:* Over-application of the gate — if "low confidence" is defined too broadly, the 6–18 h cost is paid on the majority of fast-path cases, eroding the speed advantage and pushing total volume back toward the reviewed path's 96–120 h median.
- *Failure 2:* The system-enforced part-confirmation token could create a hard stop on legitimate dispatches (e.g., genuine new SKUs not in the device-serial lookup), generating a new failure mode not present today.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Quality check inspectors if gate scope expands; otherwise the bottleneck largely stays where it is.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Sequencing logic:** Pilot the redesign with the lowest implementation risk and clearest metric first (C), then layer the structural change (A), then the heaviest investment (B). Each pilot includes an explicit learning test for whether the improvement is real or merely shifted downstream.

### Stage 1 — Pilot Redesign C on Standard high-value device (21% of cases)

- *Why first:* Reuses existing underused Quality check capacity; system change is a gate + token rule; cycle-time effect is bounded (18 h). Fastest signal on whether wrong-replacement exposure actually drops.
- *Metrics to monitor:* complaint/reopen rate at 30/60/90 days post-dispatch; replacement error cost per claim (track EUR 410 incidents); median cycle time for gated cases; Quality check utilization; R1/R2 exception count.
- *Stop/go thresholds (qualitative):* **Go** if fast-path complaint rate on Standard high-value drops below ~10% (from 18%) AND replacement error cost per claim falls 40%. **Adjust** if complaint rate drops <30% relative to baseline. **Halt** if median cycle time on gated cases exceeds 24 h OR if Quality check utilization exceeds 80% (gate is creating its own bottleneck).
- *Downstream-shift test:* monitor 60-day post-dispatch NPS, service-credit issuance, and total cost per claim (not just per-edge cost). A drop in complaint rate *with* flat or rising service-credit issuance means the rework cost has merely moved categories.

### Stage 2 — Pilot Redesign A on Premium high-value device (14% of cases)

- *Why second:* Builds on the gate from Stage 1; tests whether segment-aware routing eliminates R1/R2 violations and whether the Fraud/risk light-touch review is feasible at scale.
- *Metrics to monitor:* R1/R2 exception rate per claim; Fraud/risk review cycle time (target 2–4 h); Manual review queue depth; Premium high-value complaint rate (baseline 22% on fast path); cycle time.
- *Stop/go thresholds:* **Go** if R1/R2 exceptions drop 70% AND Premium high-value complaint rate approaches 8–10%. **Adjust** if Fraud/risk cycle time drifts above 6 h. **Halt** if Manual review inflow rises >20% without compensating cycle-time improvement.
- *Downstream-shift test:* monitor compliance-audit exception count and customer-segment NPS for Premium specifically (where service-credit exposure on >24 h cycle time is high). If Premium NPS falls despite compliance improvements, the redesign has sacrificed the segment's strict service expectations.

### Stage 3 — Pilot Redesign B on the Unclear defect / missing evidence segment (22% of cases)

- *Why third:* Heaviest implementation; highest residual CX damage if it fails; benefits the segment where current process is worst. Doing it last lets Stages 1–2 absorb the easier wins and reveal whether the Fraud/risk and Manual bottlenecks are still the binding constraint (if they are, B's parallelization is more valuable).
- *Metrics to monitor:* abandonment rate (currently highest in this segment); median cycle time (baseline 140 h); repeat-evidence-request rate (currently 26% of evidence-entry cases re-enter); Manual review queue depth; low-digital-literacy customer subgroup abandonment separately.
- *Stop/go thresholds:* **Go** if median cycle time falls below ~90 h AND repeat-evidence-request rate drops 30% AND overall abandonment does not rise >3 percentage points. **Adjust** (expand assisted-upload channel) if low-digital-literacy abandonment rises >5 pp. **Halt** if abandonment rises >8 pp regardless of cycle-time gains — the segment is already the worst CX.
- *Downstream-shift test:* monitor 90-day customer retention for the segment and total cost per claim inclusive of assisted-channel labor. A cycle-time improvement with a retention drop means the front-loading has merely shifted effort from the company to the customer.

### Cross-stage learning discipline

At every stage, the executive sponsor should require evidence that improvement is not *shifting downstream*: monitor 30-, 60-, and 90-day post-process outcomes (complaints, service credits, churn signals, compliance exceptions) alongside process-mining metrics on the in-process edges. A redesign that improves median cycle time while worsening p90 complaint tail is a net loss.
