## 1. What is observed

The supplied panel contains **40 branch-month observations: five branches from January through August 2026**. Alpha and Beta obtained automation availability in April; Gamma followed in July. Delta and Epsilon remained without the new system through August. Availability is a branch-month status, not an indication that every case received automated processing.  

Automated fast-path completion increased after adoption, reaching 48% in Alpha, 50% in Beta, and 32% in Gamma in August. However, the panel also reports 4–6% automated fast-path completion when availability equals zero. This requires clarification of legacy automation, pilot activity, or coding conventions. Moreover, fast-path completion does not measure automated triage followed by escalation, so it is not a complete measure of system use.  

Rollout was targeted rather than random: Alpha and Beta had high volumes and poor historical cycle times. The period also included a shared fraud desk, additional routing to adopters, staffing changes, a product-driven complexity shift, and evolving local practices. 

Outcomes are monthly aggregates. Averaging monthly medians does not recover a pooled case median. Rework and escalation rates lack case-level denominators and pathway detail; customer satisfaction lacks respondent counts despite varying response rates. Fraud-desk waiting times are absent. The supplied data do not establish whether monthly cycle summaries describe registration cohorts, completion cohorts, or another population.  

## 2. Causal estimands

**The intervention must be defined before estimating its effect.** The most defensible historical target is an *as-implemented rollout policy*, not an isolated software effect. Rollout-linked routing and local practices may be components of that policy; independently scheduled staffing or product changes remain potential confounders.

**Interference:** a branch’s outcome can depend on other branches’ adoption because they share the fraud desk. A branch-specific effect therefore needs a specified *other-branch rollout schedule*. A system-wide effect instead permits shared queues to respond to the entire policy.

**Treatment versions:** availability with 25% fast-path completion is not operationally identical to availability with 50%, different staffing, or different referral thresholds. Conditioning on *observed successful fast-path completion* would select a post-treatment subgroup, not identify E2. The supplied definitions and rollout narrative make these distinctions material.

For the supplied historical data, cohort-time comparisons are preferable because they preserve the distinction between April and July adoption and expose which untreated branches support each estimate. They also allow separate median, tail, and quality contrasts without imposing one constant effect across implementation stages. This follows the group-time approach to staggered adoption rather than treating a single regression coefficient as the policy effect. ([arXiv][1])

I would initially use Delta and Epsilon as fixed controls, display their results separately, and add Gamma only after dating its readiness pilot. Baseline covariates should guide comparability checks, not support a high-dimensional propensity model fitted to five branches. Contemporaneous arrivals, staffing, complexity, and fast-path share require a causal-role audit: some may be confounders, others mediators or components of the rollout package.

This is preferable to a conventional pooled event study because heterogeneous cohort effects can contaminate its lead and lag coefficients. It does **not** solve targeted adoption, shared queues, or simultaneous routing changes. ([arXiv][2])

With only five branches, I would emphasize branch contrasts and sensitivity scenarios over significance declarations. A controlled time series needs substantially more history; randomized phase-in offers stronger future identification but cannot retrospectively repair this panel. Small-cluster inference remains a substantive problem even after clustering standard errors. ([JHR][3])

The primary arithmetic comparison is:

$$
D_{g,t}
=
(\bar Y_{g,t}-\bar Y_{g,g-1})
-
(\bar Y_{C,t}-\bar Y_{C,g-1}),
$$

where \(C=\{\text{Delta, Epsilon}\}\). It becomes a causal estimate only under the stated assumptions. No already-treated branch is used as an untreated control.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean**

January–March median changes are **−1.0 hours in Alpha, +1.0 in Beta, +3.2 in Gamma, +1.8 in Delta, and −1.2 in Epsilon**. The early-adopter average is flat; the control average rises 0.3 hours. Two pretreatment changes cannot establish parallel trends.

Selection for historically poor performance creates a regression-to-the-mean concern, although the supplied pre-period does not show one uniform adopter trend. Gamma’s large raw improvement from May–June to July–August—**−8.45 median hours and −15.60 p90 hours**—also coincides with improving controls. After the same control adjustment, those changes become **−4.18 and −7.65 hours**. The short, unusually difficult immediate pre-period matters.  

**Sensitivity and scenario ranges**

First, replacing the control average with Delta or Epsilon individually gives an envelope across adopter-specific contrasts of **−6.39 to −2.58 median hours**, **−8.80 to −4.77 p90 hours**, **−2.64 to −1.22 pp rework**, **+0.62 to +1.18 pp escalation**, and **+0.062 to +0.111 respondent CSAT**. This tests donor dependence, not unmeasured confounding.

Second, consider an explicitly assumed trend-stress scenario. Let \(K\) be each adopter’s largest absolute pretreatment monthly change in its outcome gap relative to Delta/Epsilon. Assume its untreated gap could subsequently drift by up to \(K\) per month in either direction. The mean pre/post separation is four months for all three windows, giving the conservative scenario:

$$
\tau \in [D-4K,\;D+4K].
$$

| Adopter | Median \(K\), hours/month | Median scenario, hours | p90 \(K\), hours/month | p90 scenario, hours |
| ------- | ------------------------: | ---------------------: | ---------------------: | ------------------: |
| Alpha   |                      1.65 |        −12.22 to +0.98 |                   3.05 |     −18.59 to +5.81 |
| Beta    |                      2.35 |        −12.76 to +6.04 |                   4.35 |    −23.64 to +11.16 |
| Gamma   |                      2.05 |        −12.09 to +4.31 |                   3.75 |     −22.13 to +7.87 |

These are **assumption-based sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals or guaranteed effect bounds**. They admit benefit, no effect, and harm for both cycle outcomes. New shocks or spillovers could exceed the assumed drift.

Finally, satisfaction is especially weakly bounded. Taking 20% as a hard minimum response rate *only illustratively*, and allowing nonrespondent satisfaction anywhere from 1 to 5, a respondent mean near 4 permits an all-customer mean from roughly **1.6 to 4.8**. The small respondent-score contrasts cannot establish population satisfaction gains.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Next, enroll **pretreatment-defined low-risk queues in both Delta and Epsilon**, rather than their entire caseloads. Stratify by branch and registration risk; initially retain existing controls for high-complexity and borderline-fraud cases. Epsilon’s August high-complexity share is 38%, compared with Delta’s 23%, so eligibility and residual-manual-queue effects must be assessed separately. 

Before enabling, reconcile the pre-availability fast-path figures and require at least **12 weeks of audited case and queue measurements before and after assignment**, supplemented where possible by 6–12 months of historical operating data. Twelve weeks is a measurement minimum, not a power guarantee; sample size must be based on independent assignment units and meaningful harm margins.

Randomize genuinely distinct queue–team units within each branch to immediate or delayed availability, matched on baseline workload and capacity. Keep eligibility, marketing routing, and planned staffing stable during comparison. Analyze original assignment, including overrides and escalations—not just successful fast-path completions. Audit queue separation before counting teams as independent units. If only two shared operational units exist, treat this as a limited operational pilot and obtain more independent units before making precise causal claims.

Collect immutable registration risk, assignment, attempted automation, overrides, software version, activity start/completion times, queue entry/exit, productive hours, completed throughput, backlog age, rework loops, and survey invitations/responses. Follow registration cohorts through closure or explicitly record censoring. Use genuine event logs for process discovery and pathway analysis.

Monitor fraud-desk arrivals, waiting, backlog, staffing, and outcomes from **all five branches**, including manual controls. Establish immediate manual fallback for serious mis-triage and prespecified sequential harm checks.

Proposed scale-up criteria are a supported **at least 5% median improvement**, with one-sided uncertainty bounds excluding more than **5% p90 deterioration** or **1 pp additional rework**, alongside satisfactory safety audits and no material harm to untreated queues. These are proposed business thresholds, not facts inferred from the panel. Failure to demonstrate non-harm is not evidence of safety; retain limited exposure until the prespecified decision criteria are met.

def require(condition, message):
    if not condition:
        raise ValueError(message)

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(CSV))
require(not df.isna().any().any(), "Missing input values")
require(not df.duplicated(["branch", "month"]).any(), "Duplicate branch-month")
require(set(df.branch) == set(EXPECTED) | set(NEVER), "Unexpected branches")
df["month"] = pd.to_datetime(df["month"], format="%Y-%m")
df = df.sort_values(["branch", "month"]).reset_index(drop=True)
expected_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
    [sorted(df.branch.unique()), pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-08-01", freq="MS")],
    names=["branch", "month"])
require(df.set_index(["branch", "month"]).index.equals(expected_index),
        "Expected a balanced five-branch, eight-month panel")
numeric = df.select_dtypes(include="number")
require(np.isfinite(numeric.to_numpy()).all(), "Nonfinite numeric values")
require(df.automation.isin([0, 1]).all(), "Availability must be binary")
require((df.fte > 0).all() and (df.arrivals >= 0).all(), "Invalid load inputs")
require((df.arrivals % 1 == 0).all(), "Arrivals must be counts")
require((df.median_cycle_h >= 0).all()
        and (df.p90_cycle_h >= df.median_cycle_h).all(), "Invalid quantiles")
for col in ["high_complexity_pct", "automated_fast_path_pct", "rework_pct", "escalation_pct"]:
    require(df[col].between(0, 100).all(), f"Invalid percentage: {col}")
require(df.csat_1to5.between(1, 5).all(), "Invalid CSAT")
require(df.groupby("branch").automation.diff().fillna(0).ge(0).all(),
        "Availability is not absorbing")
adoption = df.loc[df.automation.eq(1)].groupby("branch").month.min()
require(adoption.dt.strftime("%Y-%m").to_dict() == EXPECTED, "Wrong adoption dates")

warnings.warn("Only FIVE branch clusters: clustered SEs and intervals are fragile; "
              "the shared fraud desk also threatens cross-branch independence.")
warnings.warn("Averages/regressions of monthly medians or p90s are NOT pooled "
              "case quantiles or average individual treatment effects.")
warnings.warn("Availability=0 has positive automated fast-path shares. Audit "
              "legacy use, pilots, and coding; do not silently recode them.")

      "Statsmodels:", statsmodels.__version__)

    base = pd.Timestamp(g) - pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    for month in treated.index[treated.index >= g]:
        estimate = treated.loc[month] - treated.loc[base] - control.loc[month] + control.loc[base]

# Regressions below are DESCRIPTIVE. Availability timing was endogenous.
# TWFE can mix treatment cohorts and exposure durations under heterogeneous effects;
# these coefficients are NOT the primary group-time estimands above.
# Fast-path completion share is post-treatment and mixes routing, selection, learning,
# and successful completion. Its coefficient is NOT a case-level treatment effect.
# Contemporaneous load/complexity adjustment may block mediators or induce selection;
# it is a specification sensitivity, NOT a controlled direct-effect identification.
G = df.branch.nunique()
models = [
    ("Descriptive availability TWFE", "automation", "automation"),
    ("Descriptive intensity TWFE", "fast10", "fast10"),
    ("Intensity + contemporaneous controls (sensitivity)",
     "fast10 + arrivals_per_fte + high_complexity_pct", "fast10")
]
reg_rows = []
for label, rhs, term in models:
    for y in OUTCOMES:
        model = smf.ols(f"{y} ~ {rhs} + C(branch) + C(month_fe)", data=df)
        require(np.linalg.matrix_rank(model.exog) == model.exog.shape[1],
                "Rank-deficient regression design")
        fit = model.fit(cov_type="cluster", use_t=True,
                        cov_kwds={"groups": df.branch, "use_correction": True,
                                  "df_correction": True})
        beta = fit.params[term]
        variance = fit.cov_params().loc[term, term]
        require(variance >= -1e-10, "Negative target coefficient variance")
        se = np.sqrt(max(variance, 0.0))
        critical = student_t.ppf(0.975, G - 1)
        reg_rows.append({"model": label, "outcome": y, "coefficient": beta,
                         "branch_cluster_se": se,
                         "formal_95_low": beta - critical*se,
                         "formal_95_high": beta + critical*se})
print("\nDESCRIPTIVE REGRESSIONS (formal intervals are not causal uncertainty bounds)\n",
      pd.DataFrame(reg_rows).round(4).to_string(index=False))

# Survey-selection illustration only: treat the approximate 20% response rate

df["population_csat_lower"] = 1 + r_min*(df.csat_1to5 - 1)
df["population_csat_upper"] = 5 - r_min*(5 - df.csat_1to5)
print("\nILLUSTRATIVE ALL-CUSTOMER CSAT LEVEL ENVELOPE:",
      df.population_csat_lower.min(), df.population_csat_upper.max())
print("No raw event discovery is possible from this panel; collect real event logs.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

* Observed: Automated fast-path completion increased after rollout, but outcomes were mixed: Alpha and Beta’s average monthly p90 rose by approximately 9.7 and 9.8 hours, respectively. Higher fast-path share did not guarantee absolute tail improvement. 
* Suggestive: Adopters generally performed better relative to Delta/Epsilon on monthly median, p90, and rework summaries, while recording more escalation. This is consistent with useful process substitution, but does not establish additional system capacity or customer benefit.
* Conditionally causal: Under parallel untreated trends, valid exposure definitions, no unaccounted anticipation, and absent or adequately modeled spillovers, cohort-time comparisons could identify effects of the specified rollout policy on branch-month outcomes. Those assumptions are not established here.
* Not supported: “Automation reduces cycle time by X% across branches,” “a higher fast-path share causes proportional improvement,” or “automation improves all-customer satisfaction and is safe to expand without evaluation.”
