## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**State machine (per case).** `ALERTED  (material evidence OR milestone gate)  ELIGIBLE  (slot allocated)  SWARMED  POST-EVALUATED`, with side-states `WATCHED` (suppressed, monitored by milestone triggers) and `DECLINED` (with mandatory reason code + alternative action + owner).

- **Cool-down.** Minimum 90 min between two alerts on the same case *unless* a material event occurs; minimum 24 h and supervisor sign-off before any second swarm on the same case, and only if a **posterior** re-estimate of remaining ARR (using the first swarm’s observed effect) still clears G4. A second swarm without a materially new event is forbidden by rule and by cost: at €450 + €1.2–3.2k of portfolio spillover, repeat swarms on unchanged cases are almost never justified.
- **Suppression.** Refresh-only re-alerts are auto-suppressed with reason code `REFRESH_NO_NEW_EVIDENCE`. They consume no capacity, create no queue ordering, and increase no urgency score. A suppression never silently closes a case: it converts it to `WATCHED` with a named owner and a scheduled gate.
- **Escalation.** Escalation is by **state/time**, never by alert count: (i) any case with `p_adj  0.70` and no response within 90 min escalates to the duty manager; (ii) a case in `WATCHED` whose milestone gate fires escalates straight into the slot queue without waiting for a model refresh; (iii) a case that breaches after being declined triggers a 24-hour retrospective with a reason-code review (was it an evidence failure, a value-density failure, a capacity failure?); (iv) an unresolved diagnostic backlog > 2 h escalates to the queue lead, who decides whether to add the case to the swarm queue as a *capacity-driven* selection (documented, and booked against the spillover ledger).
- **Re-entry.** A suppressed case re-enters eligibility on: (a) a material event; (b) a corroborated change of  0.05 in `p_now` **plus** a case fact; (c) a milestone gate; (d) the max-wait rule (no high-risk case may exceed 2 h without either a swarm or a documented no-regret response). Re-entry re-scores with current calibration and re-enters the queue at its new rank — it does not inherit priority from earlier alerts.

**I-05 vs I-07 — the comparison that matters.**

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**1. Value-based prioritisation starves low-value cases.** Ranking by `×V` systematically favours large contracts; I-05, I-07, I-10 (and I-01) fail the externality-inclusive gate even though their corrected risks are 0.52–0.78. These are mostly SME and Public-Sector cases, so value-density ranking **can become a segment proxy**. Safeguards:
- **Two-tier response law:** a slot is scarce; attention is not. Every eligible case with `p_adj  0.70` must receive a documented no-regret response within 60 min and **never wait more than 2 h** without either a swarm or a documented alternative.
- **Freeze the value weights for a defined period** (e.g. 90 days) and add a **small non-monetary uplift** `V_adj = V × (1 + )` with   [0, 0.15] for churn/reputation/reference-value effects, estimated from SME/Public-sector post-incident reviews — the margin for this uplift is unknown, so  is conservative and re-estimated quarterly.
- **Equity floor on the allocation metric:** no segment may receive more than 1.5× its share of *post-hoc breach incidence* of swarm-window cases for two consecutive months without a written exception; conversely, no segment may receive less than 0.67× its incidence share without an equity-impact note.
- **Publish the externality ledger:** expected spillover harm borne per segment per day (p × V, split by who is untreated). If a group absorbs disproportionate harm, that is an equity event even if the portfolio average improves.
- Audit “not-swarmed, high-risk” cases weekly for decay: a case can be repeatedly off-scored *precisely because* it is low value — that is the mechanism by which fairness gaps grow silently.

**2. Non-Native-Language Support (I-09).** Sparse calibration *and* sparse effect evidence means both errors are likely (over- and under-alerting), and our uncertainty is not neutral: if we skip I-09 because “we don’t know”, we systematically under-serve a group the model already mis-serves. Safeguards:
- **Never convert “insufficient data” into exclusion.** For NNLS cases, unknown-direction calibration is handled by **widening** the interval, using the lower bound for “must act now” decisions, and the midpoint for EV; a discretionary penalty is not permitted.
- **Mandatory cheap human confirmation** before any NNLS swarm (5–15 min case-owner check, no specialist capacity), which both prevents wasted slots and prevents “data-poor” drift into neglect.
- **Balanced effect logging:** log ARR-relevant covariates for *all* NNLS cases, swarmed or not, so that untreated cases contribute to effect estimation and I-09’s ARR can be estimated from segment-specific data rather than borrowed from others.
- **Explicit service standard:** minimum sample size and time window (e.g. re-estimate NNLS calibration quarterly with bootstrap CIs; relax any conservatism as soon as the CI width falls below a set threshold), and a published “NNLS service-level report” (alerts, slots, response times, breaches) co-signed by the segment owner.

**3. Differential calibration.** SE over-predicts post-redesign (0.05–0.12) while SME under-predicts (0.04–0.09); because SE also has high V, uncalibrated ranking would allocate slots to SE over its true need and away from SME under its true need — the two biases compound. Safeguards: calibration-corrected inputs to *every* magnitude and urgency calculation (§1); per-segment calibration monitoring with bootstrap CIs and a standing correction term; ARR transferability haircuts for redesigned segments; and a monthly **incidence-share vs slot-share** audit computed on *post-hoc breaches*, not on model predictions (predicted-incidence audits would reproduce the same bias).

**4. Selective intervention corrupts future labels.** Intervened cases are a policy-chosen, non-random subset; “successful diagnosis” and “no breach” labels after a swarm are treatment-affected. Training naively on them inflates the apparent value of swarms and can also teach the model that “cases like the ones we swarm are swarm-worthy”, producing self-fulfilling alerts and eventual alert fatigue — the very mechanism behind I-05’s refresh storm. Safeguards:
- **Intervention ledger as a first-class data object:** every alert (with the features/prefix that fired), every suppression, every decline (reason code), every swarm (start/end, `Diagnosis Complete` timestamp, delivered effect) — immutable and linked to the case record.
- **Propensity-corrected estimation:** the policy’s own scoring function is the propensity; use inverse-propensity weighting or doubly robust estimators for ARR, so that treated cases are not read as if randomly assigned.
- **Embedded randomisation:** randomise the *marginal* slot among near-ties (e.g. within 0.05 of the cut-off) on a fixed fraction (15%) of days, and randomise the *within-day order* where deadlines are equivalent. This is the cheapest way to get an honest effect estimate without extra capacity.
- **Holdout for unbiased benchmarking:** keep a small (5%) capability-share randomisation so that a genuinely untreated benchmark exists for calibration and ARR, protected from drift.
- **Rule against contaminated labels:** never use post-swarm outcomes to update the *baseline* breach model without propensity correction; record `swarm_attributed` reasons in case closure data and maintain a separate "would-be" outcome field.
- **Model-health tripwires:** monitor the share of alerts that are pure refreshes (target: near zero under the new rule), alerts per case per day, and precision *on untreated cases* (if the model’s untreated-case precision decays, the alert stream is being trained on its own interventions — retrain and recalibrate).

**5. Spillover falls on the untreated, systematically.** Intervening on the highest-value cases means the delay lands on the remainder, concentrated in lower-value segments and cases with less slack: average portfolio harm falls while within-portfolio inequality in *who absorbs harm* rises; and untreated cases that then breach get re-labelled higher-risk, so the policy may become a treadmill that eventually “justifies” intervening on them. Safeguards: (i) expose and publish the treatment of externality (§3) and include it in the allocation objective, not as a footnote; (ii) **per-case cumulative spillover cap** — no untreated case may absorb more than  +0.05 expected breach probability per day from swarm delays; if it would, that case must be prioritised in the next window or the swarm moved into its slack; (iii) **spillover-aware scheduling** — place swarm windows where the high-value exposed cases have slack, and re-sequence on the queue lead’s live delay data; (iv) **compensation**: fund cheap interventions (diagnostician time, queue re-prioritisation) for the cases absorbing the most spillover, so the harm is partly offset; (v) a **supply-side review**: if measured spillover cost per slot exceeds the measured value of prevented breaches for a quarter, cut daily slots from four to three and redeploy the freed specialist-hours to general diagnosis — the tool must be judged at portfolio level, not case level.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational (per swarm, same day).** Log: slot start/end, dead time per specialist, waiting cases at swarm start, the six exposed cases and their slack, `Diagnosis Complete` timestamp (to catch “missed window”: case diagnosed before the swarm — the swarm is then released back to the pool, not spent), intervention completion q, actual cost vs €450, customer-disruption events, and re-routing. KPIs: % of swarms delivered before `Diagnosis Complete`; specialist-hours displaced per swarm; queue-delay minutes attributable to swarm windows (structured *interference codes* on affected incidents — the same “interference reason” discipline used in queueing measurement); % of swarms resolving to a documented new diagnosis step within 24 h. Alerts: alerts/case/day, % of alerts that are refresh-only (target  0), time from alert to decision, % of alerts closed as `WATCHED` without a documented response (target 0).

**Predictive.** Weekly: rolling per-segment calibration (bias, ECE, Brier, reliability curves) with bootstrap CIs and PSI feature drift; alert precision/PPV at the operating point, computed **separately for treated and untreated cases** (this is the tripwire for label contamination); alert volume and recurrence statistics; distribution of `p_now  p_prev` to detect imputation artefacts like I-06 (a >0.05 jump without a corroborating case fact is flagged as suspected artefact); per-value-decile and per-segment allocation shares vs post-hoc breach incidence.

**Causal-effect (ARR estimation).** Priority order: (1) **regression discontinuity** at the day’s allocation cut-off — cases just above vs just below the rank threshold, with the rank score as the running variable; (2) **embedded randomisation** of the marginal slot among near-ties (15% of days) and of within-day order where deadlines are equivalent; (3) **staggered rollout** — different archetypes on different days/weeks to separate archetype effects from day effects; (4) **difference-in-differences** for the SE workflow redesign (treated = SE post-redesign; control = comparable non-SE cases, with pre-trends checked); (5) **spillover estimation**: interrupted time-series on daily queue delay around swarm windows, plus the six exposed cases’ realised p, to replace the assumed 0.01–0.03 with a measured distribution. Publish a **posterior ARR with credible interval per archetype monthly**, plus a per-case posterior update after each swarm (feeding §4’s re-swarm rule). Success criterion: the high bound of the credible interval must stay above the assumed midpoint before the assumption is used for capacity decisions.

**Capacity.** Daily: slots used vs slots released unused vs missed windows; value density of the marginal slot (`PS_base  opportunity cost`); exposure map (the six cases) vs assumed _E V; cumulative spillover per untreated case vs the +0.05 cap; specialist-hours displaced; % of high-risk cases meeting the 2-hour response rule. Weekly: rate of slot exhaustion before 14:00 (a leading indicator of value-based under-allocation, i.e. that the queue, not the policy, is the constraint); time between swarm and `Diagnosis Complete` for treated cases (the proximal effect measure); mitigated spillover actually delivered by catch-up windows.

**Fairness.** Monthly dashboard: (i) slot share by segment and value decile vs *post-hoc breach incidence* share (the only fair denominator); (ii) disparity ratios for time-to-first-touch, time-to-first-intervention, and unresolved-with-no-response; (iii) “never-touched” ageing report — cases with `p_adj  0.70` still without a documented response; (iv) externality ledger by segment (expected harm borne vs caused); (v) I-07/I-05-type cases (high risk, low absolute value) — a tracked cohort with their own response-time standard, since the swarm will structurally never serve them. Quarterly: qualitative review of churn/reference harm for SME and Public-Sector breaches to re-estimate ; co-signature of segment owners.

**Pre-committed change triggers (thresholds, not intentions).**
- If the realised ARR posterior median for the top archetype falls **below the lower bound** of the assumed range  raise the evidence bar (G3) and reduce daily slots to 3 pending re-estimation.
- If measured spillover per slot implies average p **> 0.02** on exposed cases, or the spillover harm to exposure ratio **> 1.0** (harm exceeds prevented-breach value in expectation)  reduce to 3 slots, reschedule swarms into slack windows, and enforce the cumulative cap.
- If **> 20%** of swarms are missed windows (case reached `Diagnosis Complete` first)  tighten G1/G3 to require a live diagnosis-stage blocker with a named addressable bottleneck; stop swarming Triage-only cases without a stall.
- If the marginal slot’s `PS_base` is **negative for 4 consecutive weeks**  cut capacity; if the 25th-percentile marginal value is positive for 6 weeks and the exposure map is verified  propose adding a fifth slot (with the same externality ledger).
- If a segment’s calibration bias CI excludes zero for 3 consecutive months  hard-code the correction; if segment ARR is unavailable after 2 quarters  commission a targeted effect study before further allocation to that segment.
- If the slot-share/incidence-share ratio exceeds 1.5 for any segment for 2 months  temporary cap with documented exception handling (and a rights-impact note).
- **Governance:** weekly operational review (slot ledger, exposure map, fairness tripwires); monthly effect/calibration review with the ten-business-day evidence production; **quarterly formal re-approval** of the rule set by risk, compliance and segment owners, with the versioned decision log, the ARR posterior, and each threshold change recorded with its trigger evidence. Any change to G1–G5 or to the externality price requires written justification: the standard for changing the rule is *measured* benefit and harm, never observed pressure from a single loud case.
