## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw fitness collapse (0.925 to 0.55 in March, and stuck near 0.5 through June) is mainly a provenance signal. The old model cannot replay `Eligibility Screen` or the duplicated `Open Case` events, so the raw fitness is measuring label mismatch. The mapped-minus-raw gap of about 0.35 recovers 85–96% of the raw drop in every month, so most of the March collapse is label-attributable. This does not show that the business process was unchanged. Mapping recovers conformance only for relations that were aliased. If the migrated screen performs a different check, or sequences work differently, the alias absorbs a real change by construction. The mapped drop that remains (0.025 in March, growing to 0.065 in July) is not explained by labels, and it persists after the labels are reconciled.

Duplicated `Open Case` events mainly distort start-of-case edges, case start times, and throughput, so they are removed in the normalized view. Missing resource identifiers invalidate resource- and team-level analyses (queue attribution, workload, handovers) but leave control-flow analyses largely intact. Because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, team comparisons are biased in exactly the period when staffing changed.

The post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift. The rework edge rises from 12 to 29 per 100 cases, AssessSenior rises in October, and median cycle time rises about 40%. None of these depends on the Eligibility label, and duplicate events (at most 6%) cannot generate a rework loop. Logging artifacts can inflate the magnitude of the cycle-time rise, but they do not explain its direction, its timing, or its link to supplier share.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG distance

Two versions are reported. The full L1 double-counts the rework loop, because AssessRequest Info and Request InfoCollect Docs move together. The grouped L1 averages the two loop edges into one dimension.

Assumptions and omissions: the fragment omits Close, direct AssessDecision edges, Validate-stage rejections, and Approve/Reject edges outside the senior path. Decision reach (73–83 per 100) therefore does not equal completion; the remainder is either open cases at month end (censoring) or non-decision exits. The 4–9 AssessSenior values before October conflict with the raw fragment, which shows no Senior edges before October, so the pre-October values may reflect the normalization or undercounted recording.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Ranges are reasoned from explicit assumptions. Medians are not additive, so these are not exact shares.

Assumptions: high-complexity cases take 1.6× standard time (sensitivity 1.3–2.0×); each rework loop adds one Collect and Assess cycle plus waiting, with an average effect of 8–15 h; Assess service rose 0.6 h, with about 1.2 Assess executions per case.

### 3.5 Change points

Logging drift:
- LCP1, 2026-03-01 (sudden): label replacement, duplicate Open Case, raw-fitness collapse, distinct labels 9  11.
- LCP2, mid-June to July (gradual): missing resources peak at 6.5% in July, duplicates reach 6% in July, coinciding with the staffing change. This may be a mediator (reassigned work recorded without a resource).

Behavioral drift:
- BCP1, 2026-05-05 (sudden): supplier onset; AssessRequest Info and rework roughly double.
- BCP2, 2026-10-01 (seasonal, with a new edge): AssessSenior from 7 to 22; senior per high-complexity case from about 0.27 to 0.54.
- Candidate BCP3, 2026-07-01 (priority rule): fast variant dips and Request Info plateaus; weak evidence because the aggregate cannot separate queue redistribution.

Performance drift:
- PCP1, May 2026 (gradual onset): median 32.0  36.5  40.8 (June), so the rise starts before the staffing and priority changes.
- PCP2, September–October (recovery then re-acceleration): median 41.5 in September, 47.8 in October.

Single global change point or multiple? The data support multiple, mechanism-specific change points. A single global break at 1 March would be dominated by logging and would miss the May behavioral break. A break at 5 May would misdate the logging and seasonal effects. Monthly resolution cannot separate 5 May, 15 June, and 1 July, so the onsets of supplier, staffing, and priority effects remain confounded.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 global monthly | month | headline trend and dashboard | 300 cases per month | month vs month; baseline vs peak | hides mechanism-specific onsets |
| L1 registration cohort × migration status | registration month; pre/post 1 Mar; mapped vs raw label | separate semantic from behavioral change | 100 cases per cohort-month | pre/post mapping; cohort vs cohort | completion-based cohorts are censored; use registration cohort |
| L2 supplier × complexity | Z vs non-Z; HC vs standard | separate supplier effect from mix | 40 cases per cell per month; else pool two months | supplier effect within complexity; complexity within supplier | supplier non-random; complexity may be post-assessment |
| L3 priority class × complexity × queue stage | class; complexity; waiting interval | measure redistribution of delay | 30 waiting intervals per cell per two weeks | difference-in-differences around 1 Jul | priority tied to complexity; selection into queue after rework |
| L4 team/resource | team (non-missing only); "missing" as its own cell | staffing and workload effects | 30 non-missing events per team-month; missing 10% | team pre/post 15 Jun; team vs others | missingness and reassignment bias team attribution |
| L5 case-level path | variant class (fast, rework, senior, other) | behavioral shifts | 20 cases per variant-month; else "other" | variant share shifts with mix standardization | rework and senior are mediators, not exogenous |

Primary monitoring population: standard-complexity, non-Supplier-Z, registration-cohort cases, with mapped labels, reported alongside a mix-standardized view of all cases.

Rationale: this population has stable composition (about 68% of January cases, about 44% by October), which makes it a clean behavioral baseline. Supplier and renewal mix are removed from the primary signal. Standard cases are the group most exposed to priority-rule delay and staffing loss, so they are the most sensitive canary for those mechanisms.

Risks: complexity is probably assigned during assessment, so conditioning on it introduces post-treatment bias. Use complexity recorded at intake where possible. Non-Z is not random either (regional correlation), so report regions as a sensitivity check.

Seasonality: until two annual cycles are available, use mix-standardized expectations (monitors M7, M9, M14) and treat October–December as provisional. Use same-month prior-year baselines where available, or STL or Holt-Winters on weekly series. Do not alert on expected October volume increases; alert on deviation from the seasonal expectation.

Thresholds: do not use one universal threshold. Use per-monitor thresholds scaled to segment size and historic variance, and log-scale cycle time before thresholding.

Multiple testing: with about 17 monitors across many segments, hundreds of tests run per week. Use Benjamini–Hochberg (q=0.10) within each monitor family per reporting period. Designate a small set of primary monitors (M5, M7, M9, M11, M14) that page; others are diagnostic only. Require persistence over two or three windows before alerting, and use always-valid sequential methods (CUSUM or EWMA) rather than repeated fixed-threshold tests.

def format_event_log(raw_df, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col):
    """Return a PM4Py-standardized copy; the input dataframe is not modified."""
    df = raw_df.copy()
    df[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df[timestamp_col])
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=case_col,
                                  activity_key=activity_col,
                                  timestamp_key=timestamp_col)


def apply_label_mapping(formatted_df, mapping, activity_key="concept:name"):
    """Return a mapped copy, e.g. mapping={'Eligibility Screen': 'Validate Eligibility'}."""
    out = formatted_df.copy()
    out[activity_key] = out[activity_key].replace(mapping)
    return out


def drop_repeated_technical_events(formatted_df, activity="Open Case",
                                   case_key="case:concept:name",
                                   activity_key="concept:name",
                                   ts_key="time:timestamp"):
    """Drop Open Case events that immediately repeat within a case (assumes consecutive duplicates)."""
    out = formatted_df.sort_values([case_key, ts_key], kind="mergesort")
    prev = out.groupby(case_key)[activity_key].shift()
    dup = (out[activity_key] == activity) & (prev == activity)
    return out.loc[~dup].copy()


def time_window(formatted_df, month, mode="traces_contained"):
    """Slice one calendar month. Use 'traces_contained' for complete cases only,
    or filter by registration time for a registration cohort."""
    start = pd.Timestamp(f"{month}-01")
    end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_df, start, end, mode=mode)


def monthly_dfgs(formatted_df, months):
    out = {}
    for mo in months:
        log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(time_window(formatted_df, mo))
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        out[mo] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_acts, "end": end_acts}
    return out


def variant_counts(formatted_df):
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    rows = []
    for v, traces in variants.items():
        key = v if isinstance(v, str) else ",".join(v)
        n = len(traces) if isinstance(traces, (list, tuple)) else int(traces)
        rows.append((key, n))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["variant", "cases"]).sort_values("cases", ascending=False)


def temporal_profile(formatted_df):
    """Returns {(activity_a, activity_b): (mean_seconds, std_seconds)}."""
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df)

def compare_label_mappings(formatted_raw, mapping, months, baseline_months):
    """Compare raw and mapped views without overwriting the original log.
    Fitness uses a Petri net discovered on the mapped baseline window; this is a
    proxy for the old normative model, which is not supplied."""
    mapped = drop_repeated_technical_events(apply_label_mapping(formatted_raw, mapping))
    base_df = pd.concat([time_window(mapped, mo) for mo in baseline_months])

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions (high confidence).** The March raw-fitness collapse is primarily semantic and provenance-related. Label replacement, duplicated Open Case events, and a raw-minus-mapped gap of about 0.35 (85–96% of the raw drop recovered each month) support this. The May–July rise in AssessRequest Info and rework is a control-flow change that label mapping cannot explain. It coincides with Supplier Z share rising from 14% to 46%, and Z-implied rework of 41–51% with non-Z rework near baseline fits the data. The October rise in high-complexity share (20% to 41–45%) is seasonal population drift and accounts for roughly 3–5 h of the 17 h median rise.

**Plausible (medium confidence).** Staffing-driven queueing from mid-June is the most likely driver of the June–August median rise beyond supplier rework; the queue component is roughly 40–70% of the July rise. The priority rule may have redistributed delay toward standard cases, but no class-level data isolate this. The October jump in senior routing (senior per high-complexity case from about 0.27 to 0.54) may partly reflect recording practice.

**Mediation and interaction.** Supplier Z cases were already more complex, and rework returns cases to the queue, so supplier, staffing, and priority effects are entangled. Queue waiting is partly a consequence of rework. Missing resources are concentrated in migrated activities and rose during the staffing window, so team-level attribution is compromised. Monthly resolution cannot separate 5 May, 15 June, and 1 July. The largest unexplained residual is the Q4 rework rise after Supplier Z share fell.

**What would make me revise the account.** I would revise if:
- event-level data show non-Z rework rising before 5 May (supplier onset is then not the trigger);
- senior-coded October cases have the same resources and timestamps as earlier senior-like events (recording change, not behavior);
- standard-case queue waits do not rise after 1 July, or rise before 15 June (priority or staffing attribution weakens);
- December improvement persists at matched high-complexity mix and supplier share (intervention effect confirmed);
- prior-year renewal seasons show the same Request Info pattern (October becomes recurring rather than new drift).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF a new activity label appears in more than 0.5% of events for two consecutive weeks, or the raw-minus-mapped fitness gap exceeds 0.10 outside a planned release window, THEN freeze the normative model, review the alias table with the data owner, and report mapped and raw metrics separately, BECAUSE label or schema changes can collapse raw conformance without any change in business behavior.

2. IF duplicate technical events exceed 1% of cases or missing resource share rises more than 2 pp above its reference for two consecutive weeks, THEN suspend resource- and team-level conclusions and audit the logging of the affected activity, BECAUSE duplicates and missing resources corrupt start-of-case and workload analyses but leave control-flow measures largely intact, so they must be separated from behavioral signals.

3. IF the complexity-standardized AssessRequest Info rate exceeds its reference by more than two robust SDs on a CUSUM for three consecutive weeks AND the excess is concentrated in Supplier Z cases, THEN escalate a bundle-completeness review with Request Info reason codes and supplier management, BECAUSE incomplete bundles generate re-collection loops that cascade into rework.

4. IF AssessRequest Info rises in non-Z cases while Supplier Z share falls, THEN investigate assessor practice and queue pressure before concluding that the supplier has recovered, BECAUSE rework that persists after supplier recovery indicates behavioral adaptation or queue-driven shortcuts.
