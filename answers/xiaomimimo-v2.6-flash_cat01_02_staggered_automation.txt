def window_means(data, branches, pre, post, cols):
    m = {}
    for lab, (s, e) in [("pre", pre), ("post", post)]:
        sub = data[data.branch.isin(branches) & (data.month >= pd.Timestamp(s)) & (data.month <= pd.Timestamp(e))]
        for c in cols:
            m[(lab, c)] = sub[c].mean()
        m[(lab, "n_months")] = sub["month"].nunique()
    return m

**Interference and treatment-version notes.** SUTVA fails on two counts: (i) a *common* centralized fraud desk from March, onto which automated triage routes somewhat more borderline cases, so the "control" outcomes partly reflect treated branches' behaviour; (ii) a routing campaign that moves ~10% of volume toward Alpha/Beta, changing controls' load. The treatment is also multi-version: two branches receive the same `automation=1` at very different afp trajectories (Alpha 2548, Gamma 2532), so E1 averages over non-comparable doses. Any scalar "the effect of automation" therefore requires specifying *which* estimand, *which* version, and *whose* outcome.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: interaction-weighted group–time ATT / event study** (Callaway–Sant'Anna; Sun–Abraham) | Explicit adoption date per branch (Alpha/Beta 2026-04, Gamma 2026-07) mapped to relative month   [6, +4]; availability treated as absorbing; afp reported alongside as a *fidelity* measure, not as treatment | Never-treated Delta + Epsilon as the base control pool; not-yet-treated Gamma used for Alpha/Beta only through 2026-06 | Branch FE; calendar-month FE; **pre-determined** covariates only (pre-period median, p90, complexity, FTE, volume). Post-treatment fte/arrivals/complexity are *excluded* from the headline spec — they are mediators of the rollout package | ATT(g,t) and dynamic ATT() for E1, E2, E4, E5, E6 | Parallel untreated trends between each cohort and the control pool; no anticipation before =0 beyond the readiness pilot; consistent availability treatment; SUTVA (violated via fraud desk + routing) | Pre-period event coefficients for  = 3,2,1 jointly  0; placebo adoption dates assigned to Delta/Epsilon; leave-one-branch-out; never-treated vs. not-yet-treated control sets compared; cohort-specific (n=2, n=1) estimates shown separately rather than pooled | Unverifiable parallel trends with 3 pre-periods for the April cohort and a single-unit July cohort; any mean reversion in the below-average controls is read as treatment benefit |
| Alternative 1: synthetic control / convex-combination donor pool | Separate donor weights fitted on the pre-period for each adopter at its own adoption date | Convex combination of the other four branches (excluding the same cohort) | None beyond weighting; optionally ridge-penalised weights balancing baseline complexity and arrivals/FTE | Branch-specific ATT on `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` (three separate estimates) | Pre-period fit proxies the counterfactual; no structural break common to all donors in Apr/May | Pre-treatment RMSPE; in-space placebo ranks; leave-one-donor-out weight stability; in-time placebo (fake treatment in 2026-02) | Only 3–6 pre-periods  unstable weights; the **May product change and seasonal load wave hit every donor**, so the post-period gap mainly captures the branch-specific load of a common shock |
| Alternative 2: controlled interrupted time series / local projections | Level and slope breaks at adoption month, plus explicit breaks at 2026-03 (fraud desk) and 2026-05 (product change) | Within-branch pre-trend extrapolation, scaled by the contemporaneous change in Delta/Epsilon | `high_complexity_pct`, arrivals/FTE, FTE, month dummies | Immediate level change and slope change per adopter at  = 0 | Pre-period trend would have continued; covariates measured without error and unaffected by treatment; no unmeasured branch-specific shock at the break date | Residual autocorrelation; placebo break dates; out-of-sample forecast back into the post-period; Durbin–Watson | Routing campaign, staffing loss/gain and product change all coincide with the break; a 3-month pre-trend cannot support 5-month extrapolation |
| Alternative 3: treatment-intensity dose–response panel | No discrete break; afp enters as a continuous branch-month level (and lagged one month) | Within-branch deviations from the branch mean, net of calendar-month means | Branch FE + month FE; optionally lagged load and complexity | E3: semi-elasticity per 10 pp of automated fast-path share | afp as-if exogenous given branch and month effects (**false** — afp responds to load and case difficulty); correct functional form; unaggregated outcomes | First stage: afp on adoption × time-since-availability; compare full-panel FE slope with within-adopter post-period slope; check sign stability across specifications | Endogenous intensity; with 5 clusters the coefficient is unidentified separately from time trends, and the FE version is essentially a rescaled DiD rather than an independent dose–response |

**Why the primary strategy is preferable.** It matches the data-generating structure: adoption occurs at exactly two known dates, is staggered, and two branches never adopt, while the effect plausibly evolves as afp ramps from 25% to 50%. It keeps Delta/Epsilon as a clean base, permits Gamma to serve as a not-yet-treated control for Alpha/Beta only through 2026-06 (and refuses it afterwards), estimates a distinct ATT(g,t) per cohort instead of one number, and exposes dynamics as event-time coefficients rather than averaging them away. Decisively, it avoids the negative and ad-hoc weights that two-way fixed effects imposes when already-treated units serve as controls and effects differ across cohorts and event months — exactly the pattern here, where April and July cohorts have different pre-periods, different doses and different concurrent shocks. Synthetic control is attractive with five units but has too few pre-periods and cannot absorb the nationwide May shock; controlled ITS isolates level breaks but has no credible 5-month extrapolation; the intensity regression speaks to dose but is descriptive. With five clusters, all uncertainty must come from wild cluster bootstrap or randomization inference, never asymptotics.

**(b) Within-adopter, post-period only, branch-demeaned OLS (n = 12 branch-months; afp range  2550):**

| outcome | slope per +1 pp afp | implied over the observed 25 pp within-post range |

**Arithmetic fact:** specification (a) and (b) **disagree in sign on the tail.** Specification (a) is essentially a rescaled DiD — it identifies from "adopters are below the cross-branch month average after adoption" — so it adds no independent dose information. ### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

**Event-study pre-coefficients** (gap between treated and control, normalised to the mean of  = 3,2,1; Alpha+Beta pooled):

|  | 3 (Jan) | 2 (Feb) | 1 (Mar) | 0 (Apr) | 1 (May) | 2 (Jun) | 3 (Jul) | 4 (Aug) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median (h) | +0.15 | +0.50 | 0.15 | 2.57 | 5.37 | 4.77 | 3.97 | 5.77 |
| p90 (h) | 0.02 | +0.63 | 0.62 | 4.72 | 7.42 | 6.42 | 4.92 | 8.12 |

**Arithmetic fact:** pre-coefficients stay within ±0.6 h on a 40–115 h scale, versus post-coefficients of 2.6 to 5.8 h (median) and 4.7 to 8.1 h (p90). No visible divergence — but with 3 pre-periods and 5 units, the test has almost no power (a ±3 h pre-trend would go undetected). Gamma's own event study is noisier: pre-gaps range 1.4 to +1.5 h, then break to 3.7 h (=0) and 3.8 h (=1) on the median, and to 6.7/6.9 h on the p90.

**Regression to the mean.** Alpha and Beta were selected for poor *historical* cycle times. Two distinct RTM stories point in opposite directions:

1. **Adopter-side RTM** would predict absolute improvement in Alpha/Beta. It did not occur (0.4 h, +1.9 h), so RTM does **not** inflate the before–after estimate here — because there is essentially no before–after estimate to inflate.
2. **Control-side mean reversion** is plausible and would inflate the DiD. Pre-period medians: Alpha 48.9, Beta 45.9, Gamma 40.6, Epsilon 40.3, Delta 34.5 (5-branch mean 42.0). The two never-treated controls were *below* the panel mean and then rose +4.4 and +6.0 h. Some of that is the genuine May product shock (complexity +5 to +8 pp, arrivals +8–12%), but part may be convergence. Because selection period is unknown, the share attributable to reversion cannot be bounded from these data — hence the -sensitivity in 5.6.

### 5.5 Sensitivity: crediting the control trend only partially

Let   [0,1] be the fraction of the observed control-group change credited to the counterfactual ( = 0 is pure before–after;  = 1 is full parallel trends). Pooled across the three adopters (Gamma May–Jun window):

|  | pooled  median | pooled  p90 | pooled  rework | pooled  escalation | pooled  CSAT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 (before–after) | 2.33 h | **+1.29 h** | 0.67 pp | +2.15 pp | +0.060 |
| 0.5 | 3.36 h | 2.74 h | 1.33 pp | +1.53 pp | +0.076 |
| 1.0 (full DiD) | 4.38 h | **6.76 h** | 1.99 pp | +0.91 pp | +0.092 |

Repeating with the alternative Gamma window (Jan–Jun) moves the endpoints to median **0.64 h** (=0) and **4.29 h** (=1); p90 **+6.19 h** (=0) and **6.59 h** (=1).

**Bounded conclusions (arithmetic, not causal):**
- **`median_cycle_h`:** across all designs and windows the pooled estimate lies between **0.6 h and 4.4 h**, i.e. about **1% to 10%** of a ~45 h counterfactual median. Branch-specific ranges are wider and include zero (Beta: +1.9 h to 3.4 h).
- **`p90_cycle_h`:** the range is **6.8 h to +6.2 h** — it spans zero with large magnitude on both sides. The tail effect is **not identified** by these data; only its *sign relative to the control group* is stable.
- **`csat_1to5`:** point estimates span **+0.03 to +0.09** across all designs, but with 20–45% response rates, unknown n (a branch-month mean with n  100 on a 1–5 scale has SE  0.05–0.08), and unknown nonresponse composition, the entire **0.10 to +0.10** interval remains compatible with the data. The effect on customer satisfaction is effectively unestimated.
