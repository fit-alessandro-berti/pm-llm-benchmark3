# Warranty-claim process: diagnosis and three redesigns

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

**The contradiction, resolved by disaggregation.** The dominant variant (Intake -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Close) carries roughly 2,300 of 5,000 cases and is fast (Intake->Auto 0.8h, Auto->Fast approval 1.0h, Fast approval->Dispatch 3.0h, Dispatch->Close 5.0h mean; ~10h end to end). It is also the path with the worst downstream behaviour: 14.1% of Fast-approval cases reopen versus 3.3% on the quote/approval/repair path, and the two complaint edges attached to it (Fast approval->Complaint 140, Dispatch->Complaint 260) account for about 400 of 510 reopen events. The apparent cheapness mostly evaporates once rework is priced in (31% cheaper pre-rework, 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments and service credits). The data do not say "fast is bad", though; they say the aggregate hides a segment split:

- For **low-risk accessories** (43% of cases) the fast path is genuinely good: 6-8% reopen, EUR 95 expected wrong-replacement cost, and for Premium accessories any path longer than 24h triggers service credits (fast path median 8h vs 46h manual). Premium low-risk is also the only R2 exception. Here the slow path is worse on every axis that matters.
- For **high-value devices** (35% of cases) the fast path is the problem: 22% (Premium) and 18% (Standard) reopen versus 5-6% on the reviewed/repair path, EUR 410 expected wrong-replacement cost, and it violates R1 whenever the claim exceeds EUR 400 without Manual or Fraud/risk review. The alignment diagnostics name exactly this pattern ("Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices", "Fast approval before Evidence completeness").

So the frequency DFG shows the average of a population that should never have been averaged: the auto-screen rules are "not segment-aware", so the same gate approves a Premium cable and a Standard EUR 900 device on the same 0.4h median edge. The contradiction is a Simpson's-paradox artefact of a segment-blind gate, not a real speed-versus-quality dilemma for most cases.

**Main problems, by artefact:**

1. *Frequency DFG - the evidence loop.* Request missing evidence has 1,430 entries; 890 go back to Manual eligibility review and 780 of Manual review's 1,400 outgoing arcs go to Request missing evidence (56% of manual reviews end in an evidence request). Combined with the rework statistic (a second evidence request in 26% of entering cases) and the "unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases, 140h median, highest abandonment), this loop is the single largest cycle-time consumer.
2. *Performance DFG - waiting, not work.* Request missing evidence -> Manual review has mean 34h / p90 88h; Manual review -> Repair quote 22h / p90 60h (flagged as too slow for Premium); Repair quote -> Customer approval 30h / p90 80h; Customer approval -> Repair device 20h / p90 52h. The repair path's 96-120h medians are mostly queueing and customer/part waiting, not touch time. Resource notes confirm it: quote delay is "mostly waiting for part-price confirmation". This matters because the "good quality" path is slow for removable reasons.
3. *Conformance - fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces, low precision.* Deviations are small and widespread rather than concentrated in a few variants; roughly a third of traces have at least one policy breach. R1/R2 breaches sit on the fast path; R3 is structurally bypassed by Escalation board -> Dispatch/Repair/Denied (120 cases decided without Manual review), and "Complaint/reopen after close" shows reopen events that do not even appear in the DFG.
4. *Rework statistics - rework concentrates where the money is.* Rework repair is 12.5% on high-value devices vs 4.0% on low-risk; the Dispatch -> Complaint long tail (mean 96h, p90 180h) is tied to wrong replacement part selection, at EUR 410 per error for devices.
5. *Resource information - the manual queue is half rework.* Manual eligibility review's inflow is 1,400 first-pass + 890 evidence-loop returns + 310 reopens = 2,600 arrivals; 46% of the workload of a 7-person team with a hard knee at 82% utilisation is self-inflicted rework. Fraud/risk review (2 specialists, 980 arrivals, Monday and post-campaign spikes) shows queueing in its inbound edges (11h and 18h means). Quality check (2 inspectors) is underused - slack capacity sitting next to the wrong-part problem.
6. *Customer outcomes - tolerance differs by segment.* Standard accessory customers accept 72h given status visibility; Premium accessory customers penalise anything over 24h; high-value customers on the fast path are the least satisfied group in the process despite being served fastest.

**Data-quality caveat that should shape decisions.** The DFG arc counts do not reconcile at several nodes: Manual eligibility review has 2,600 inbound but only 1,400 outbound arcs; Fraud/risk review 980 in vs 760 out; only 3,625 END arcs for 5,000 cases; Dispatch replacement has more outbound (2,580) than inbound (2,365). Either ~1,300 cases were open at extraction (right-censored - which would understate manual-path cycle times and complaint rates) or edges such as Manual review -> Fast approval / -> Close were filtered out. Any redesign business case must first close this gap; the segment table, not the DFG, is currently the more reliable evidence for manual-path outcomes.

## 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered gate with a Premium express-review lane

**Redesigned flow.** Intake -> *segment- and value-aware auto screen* (uses segment, declared value, defect type, device class) with three outcomes:
- Tier 0 (low-risk accessories, both segments, no risk flags): Fast approval -> Dispatch -> Close, unchanged. Premium accessories keep the R2 exemption; Standard accessories get a lightweight evidence-completeness confirmation at intake (photo/serial) so R2 is formally satisfied without a queue.
- Tier 1 (high-value devices, no fraud flags): mandatory *Express eligibility review* - a time-boxed (target < 8h touch-to-decision) manual review lane, prioritised for Premium, with the reviewer required to make a technical decision (replace/repair) and confirm evidence in one touch. Then Dispatch or Repair quote.
- Tier 2 (fraud/risk flags regardless of value): Fraud/risk review as today, but Monday/post-campaign surge handled by pre-trained manual reviewers acting as first-line risk screeners.
- Reopens: Escalation board may recommend but cannot decide; every reopen passes Manual review (R3), with the board's slot reserved for Premium high-value.

**Targets.** The 35% high-value population currently flowing Auto -> Fast approval -> Dispatch; R1/R2/R3 conformance; Premium high-value cycle time (temporal-profile deviation on Manual review -> Repair quote).

**Mechanism.** Removes the segment-blind gate that manufactures the contradiction. High-value fast-path reopen rates (18-22%) should move toward the reviewed-path rates (5-6%), eliminating most of the 260 Dispatch -> Complaint events and the EUR 410 wrong-replacement exposure. R1 violations disappear by construction; the escalation-board change closes the R3 bypass. Deployment is fast (auto-screen rule change in two weeks). The express lane is what keeps this from being "route everything slow": Premium high-value should land around a 24-40h median rather than the current 96h reviewed path, because the lane skips the Manual review -> Repair quote hand-off wait.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** High-value cycle time gets worse than 20-28h; the redesign accepts this because those customers are already the least satisfied. Manual-review inflow rises 15-35% and a 56% share of manual reviews currently spawn evidence requests, so without a companion change the evidence loop grows. Standard high-value customers get a longer path without an express guarantee. The evidence loop, the unclear-defect segment, and part-price waiting on the repair path are left untouched. Fraud coverage on accessories is deliberately kept light (accepting EUR 95 errors).

## 3. Redesign B: Keep the fast path, fix the two rework engines (pre-dispatch part gate + one-shot evidence completion)

**Redesigned flow.** Routing stays as today. Two insertions and one rule:
- *Pre-dispatch quality gate* between Fast approval and Dispatch replacement, applied only when device class = high-value: an inspector verifies part/SKU selection against the defect type and serial, and records evidence completeness (satisfying R2's "completeness confirmed" for that case). Staffed by the two underused Quality check inspectors, designed for the low end of the 6-18h range (same-day batch, target 6-8h), with a bypass when the queue exceeds a threshold rather than letting Premium cases age.
- *Evidence completion redesign*: structured evidence portal with defect-type-specific checklists so the first request lists everything at once; a rule of "maximum two requests, then a proactive call"; an assisted channel (phone/chat-guided upload, retail drop-off) for customers who do not respond within 48h, to protect low-digital-literacy customers.
- *Status visibility* pushed at every state change for Standard accessory customers, who tolerate 72h if informed.

**Targets.** Dispatch -> Complaint (260, mean 96h, wrong-part long tail); Request missing evidence loop (26% repeats, 22% segment, 140h median, highest abandonment); manual-review capacity consumed by 890 evidence-loop returns.

**Mechanism.** Attacks rework at its two sources instead of re-routing. The gate converts idle inspector time into avoided EUR 410 errors and reshipments; if it catches even half of the wrong-part cases, the fast path's net cost advantage widens well beyond 9%. The evidence redesign cuts repeated requests 30-45% (prompt estimate), which shrinks the 34h/88h return edge, reduces abandonment in the worst segment, and frees a large share of manual-review capacity without hiring - capacity that later makes Redesign A affordable.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** R1 remains violated: a part-selection gate is not a risk review, so claims above EUR 400 still get Fast approval without Manual/Fraud review, and the compliance exposure for Premium high-value stays open. Fraud exposure is unchanged. High-value replacements get 6-18h slower; Premium high-value at 20h today may cross the 24h service-credit line unless the gate is genuinely fast. The portal may raise abandonment in the low-literacy sub-population even with the assisted channel. The escalation board's R3 bypass is not addressed.

## 4. Redesign C: Split into two value streams - "Accessory express with sampled audit" and "Device diagnose-then-remedy" with repair-first and pre-priced parts

**Redesigned flow.** Two separate processes with different control philosophies, selected at intake by device class:
- *Accessory express* (43% of cases): claim -> instant approval -> ship -> auto-close. No pre-approval controls at all; instead a *post-hoc sampled audit* (e.g. 5-10% of closed accessory claims, higher after marketing campaigns) by the fraud specialists, feeding rule updates. Evidence is requested only in audit or on repeat claims per customer.
- *Device diagnose-then-remedy* (35% + most of the unclear-defect segment): Intake -> *Technical triage* (technician or reviewer classifies defect type from a structured intake, requests all evidence once) -> *Technical decision*: repair-first for repairable defect types, replacement only where repair is not viable or where the defect type has high repair-rework history -> Fulfillment -> Close with quality check. Two structural changes make repair fast enough to be the default: (i) a *pre-negotiated part-price catalogue* plus *pre-consent at intake* ("repairs up to EUR X proceed without a separate quote"), collapsing Repair quote -> Customer approval (30h/p90 80h) and Customer approval -> Repair device (20h) into near-zero waiting for most cases; (ii) Premium high-value gets a committed diagnosis SLA and a loaner/advance option only where R2 permits (it does not permit advance dispatch for devices, so the SLA is on diagnosis, not shipping). Reopens go to Manual review by rule; the escalation board is replaced by a Premium recovery owner inside the device stream.

**Targets.** The whole repair path (currently 3.3% reopen but 96-120h median); the Manual review -> Repair quote and quote-to-repair waits; high-value wrong-replacement cost; fraud-specialist capacity (moved from queue-based to sample-based for accessories); the unclear-defect segment via technical triage instead of eligibility ping-pong.

**Mechanism.** Rather than choosing between the fast bad path and the slow good path, it makes the good path fast by removing waiting (part-price confirmation, quote approval) and making it the default for devices. The reviewed/repair path already shows 5-6% reopen and 3.3% on the repair variant; if its 96-120h median can be cut to roughly 40-60h by removing two waiting edges and the Manual review -> Repair quote hand-off, it becomes attractive on all axes for devices. For accessories it goes further than A or B: the control cost (EUR 0.20 screen, evidence requests, 6-8% reopen) is replaced with sampling, because the expected error is EUR 95 and customers value speed.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** Accepts a higher tolerated fraud/error rate on accessories and relies on audit feedback that lags. Repair-first increases load on three technicians and exposes the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate; some customers prefer replacement and will complain differently. Two processes mean two normative models, two sets of KPIs, and more governance. Parts-catalogue negotiation and pre-consent terms need legal/commercial work, so this is the slowest to deploy. Fraud specialists' Monday spikes are relieved for accessories but not for devices.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- Failure 1: manual review crosses the 82% utilisation knee. Inflow rises 15-35% while 56% of manual reviews already generate an evidence request, so the express lane's promised 24-40h collapses toward the current 96h and Premium high-value credits increase rather than decrease.
- Failure 2: value-threshold gaming and misclassification. Rules keyed on EUR 400 or device class create a cliff; declared values cluster just below the threshold, or accessories bundled with devices are misrouted, recreating R1 breaches under a different label.
- Failure 3: the express lane becomes rubber-stamping under SLA pressure, reproducing fast-path error rates with a compliant-looking trace (fitness improves, outcomes do not).
- Bottleneck migration: from Dispatch -> Complaint to the Manual-review queue, then to Request missing evidence (more reviews, more requests, more abandonment) and to Fraud/risk review on Mondays when reviewers are pulled into surge screening.

**Redesign B**
- Failure 1: the gate adds the high end (18h) rather than 6h, pushing Premium high-value replacements past 24h; service-credit cost replaces reshipment cost.
- Failure 2: portal-driven abandonment. Abandonment is already highest in the unclear-defect segment; a portal may lower repeated requests by 30-45% while raising drop-out among low-literacy customers, and abandonment silently improves cycle-time and complaint metrics, hiding the failure.
- Failure 3: compliance remains exposed. Auditors see the same R1 breaches; a later forced fix may have to be done in a hurry.
- Bottleneck migration: to the two QC inspectors precisely after repair campaigns, when they are already busy; to the assisted-channel support desk; and to Manual review if the gate rejects parts and routes cases back for re-decision.

**Redesign C**
- Failure 1: repair capacity and parts. Three technicians absorb most device volume; high-value repair rework (12.5%) and parts lead times reintroduce waiting that the pre-priced catalogue was supposed to remove.
- Failure 2: fraud displacement. Removing pre-controls on accessories shifts opportunistic claims into the express stream; a lagging sampled audit detects it late, and post-campaign spikes hit exactly when the sample rate should rise.
- Failure 3: customer preference mismatch. Premium device customers expecting replacement perceive repair-first as a downgrade; complaints move from "wrong part" to "unwanted repair", so reopen statistics may not improve even though quality does.
- Bottleneck migration: from eligibility review and fraud review to repair technicians, parts procurement, and the technical triage step; escalation-board CSAT effect is lost unless the recovery-owner role is staffed.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 - fix the evidence (2-4 weeks, no process change).** Reconcile the DFG imbalances (1,200 missing Manual-review outbound arcs, 1,375 missing END arcs, Dispatch out > in). Add segment, declared value, defect type and device class as case attributes so every metric below can be cut by segment. Re-run conformance per segment to size the actual R1/R2 breach population. Establish a per-segment baseline of: median/p90 cycle time, reopen rate at 30 days after close, abandonment rate, evidence-request count per case, cost-to-serve including credits and reshipments, resource utilisation.

**Stage 1 - first pilot: Standard high-value devices, A-routing plus B-gate.** This segment has the highest wrong-replacement cost (EUR 410), an 18% fast-path reopen rate, and no 24h service-credit cliff, so it can absorb added hours safely. Implement the segment-aware rule for this segment only and the pre-dispatch part gate staffed by QC inspectors; run as staged rollout (alternate weeks or regions) against a control group.
- Metrics: reopen rate at 30 days, wrong-part rate, median/p90 cycle time, manual-review utilisation and queue age, gate dwell time, evidence requests per case, abandonment.
- Go: reopen rate falls by at least a third relative to control, gate dwell stays in the 6-10h band, manual utilisation stays below ~78-80%, abandonment does not rise by more than 1-2 points. Stop: manual utilisation above 82% for two consecutive weeks, or median cycle time more than doubles without a matching reopen reduction.

**Stage 2 - evidence-loop redesign (B) for the unclear-defect segment, A/B by channel.** Run portal-with-checklist versus current process, with the assisted channel active in both arms so the test isolates the portal effect.
- Metrics: repeat-request rate (target: down 20%+ relative, consistent with the 30-45% claim), Request missing evidence -> Manual review edge time, abandonment split by digital-proficiency proxy (age band, channel of intake), manual-review capacity freed.
- Go: repeat requests down and abandonment flat or down in the low-proficiency stratum. Stop: abandonment up more than 2-3 points in any stratum, even if aggregate cycle time improves - abandonment must be treated as a failure outcome, not a completion.

**Stage 3 - Premium high-value express lane (A), only after Stage 2 has freed manual capacity.** Requires headroom, because this lane's promise is time-boxed review.
- Metrics: median cycle time (target band 24-40h), p90 under ~72h, reopen rate moving from 22% toward the 5-8% range, service-credit spend, R1/R2 violation count for the segment.
- Go/stop: stop if p90 exceeds roughly 72h for two weeks or if rubber-stamping is detected (reviewed cases with reopen rates statistically indistinguishable from the old fast path).

**Stage 4 - repair-first with pre-priced parts (C) on a defect-type slice.** Pick two or three high-volume, low-rework-history defect types for high-value devices; negotiate the parts catalogue for those only; offer pre-consent at intake.
- Metrics: Repair quote -> Customer approval and Customer approval -> Repair device edge times (expect large falls), repair rework rate versus 12.5% baseline, customer choice rate (repair vs replace), reopen rate, technician utilisation.
- Go: device-stream median in the 40-60h range with reopen at or below the reviewed-path 5-6%. Stop: repair rework above ~15% or technician utilisation saturating.

**Stage 5 - accessory express with sampled audit (C) for Standard accessories.** Only after fraud specialists have been relieved by Stages 1-4; start at a 10% sample, raise after campaigns.

**Sequencing logic.** Start where the wrong-replacement cost is highest and the CX cliff is absent (Standard high-value); free manual capacity (evidence loop) before demanding time-boxed manual reviews (Premium express); prove that the repair path can be made fast before making it the default; and remove pre-controls on accessories only once audit capacity exists. Each stage funds the next in capacity rather than headcount.

**How to know improvements are real rather than displaced.**
- Measure outcomes on a case cohort with a fixed post-close window of at least three weeks: the complaint edges have p90 delays of 150-180h, so early reads will look better than reality.
- Always report the triple "cycle time, reopen at 30 days, abandonment" together; any option that improves the first two while raising the third is shifting failure onto customers who quit.
- Track cost-to-serve end to end (labour, parts, reshipments, credits, audit), not activity cost; the current 31%-to-9% collapse is the template for how a local saving disappears.
- Watch queue age at every downstream resource (Manual review, Fraud/risk, QC, technicians, parts) as leading indicators of bottleneck migration; a rising queue somewhere is usually where the "saved" time went.
- Re-run conformance per segment: fitness rising while segment reopen rates do not fall indicates compliant-looking rubber-stamping; look specifically for reopen-after-close events that today escape the DFG.
- Check the distribution of declared values around EUR 400 and device-class labels for drift, to detect gaming of the new routing rules.
