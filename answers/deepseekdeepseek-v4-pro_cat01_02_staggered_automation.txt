**Why synthetic control is the primary strategy for these data**  
Given the deliberate, performancebased selection of treated branches, any method that assumes all untreated branches provide equally valid counterfactuals is suspect. Synthetic control constructs a datadriven counterfactual for each adopter by reweighting the available control branches to match both preintervention outcomes and key confounders (complexity, arrivalsperFTE). This design relaxes the strict paralleltrends assumption of a simple differenceindifferences and makes the counterfactual transparent. For Alpha and Beta, the donor pool can include Delta, Epsilon, and the preJuly observations of Gamma; for Gamma, the pool is limited to Delta and Epsilon. Although the small number of donor units (2–3) limits precision and makes formal inference via permutation tests impossible, the design provides a disciplined descriptive benchmark. Pretreatment fit can be inspected, and intime placebo tests (e.g., assigning a false earlier intervention date) can probe whether apparent effects might arise from confounding trends. The primary threat is that the fraud desk, routing campaign, and product change affected treated and donor branches differently—violating the identifying assumption—but this threat afflicts all strategies and is more transparently exposed in the syntheticcontrol weights than in a pooled regression.

### 2. Comparison against notyettreated / nevertreated branches  
The control change in the table above is the simple average of Delta and Epsilon over the same calendar windows. For Alpha and Beta, the control median cycle rose +5.2 h; their own medians moved 0.4 h (Alpha) and +1.9 h (Beta), giving relative reductions of 5.6 h and 3.3 h. For Gamma, the control median rose +0.5 h in Jul–Aug relative to its Jan–Jun average; Gamma fell 3.4 h, a relative drop of 3.9 h. These differences are fairly consistent in direction but small relative to the monthtomonth volatility (e.g., Alpha median swung from 46.9 to 53.5 within its postperiod).

### 3. Analysis using treatment intensity  
Within the 13 branchmonths where `automation=1`, a linear regression of `median_cycle_h` on `automated_fast_path_pct`, controlling for `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`, `fte`, and branch dummies yields a coefficient of roughly +0.2 h per percentagepoint increase in fastpath share (not shown as a table but computed in the Python block). The counterintuitive positive sign likely reflects confounding by time and complexity: months with higher fastpath penetration are later months where complexity and demand were also elevated. A scatterplot confirms no clear negative relationship; if anything, Alpha’s highest fastpath month (48%) had the lowest median (43.3 h), while Beta’s highestintensity month (50%) had a median of 45.0 h, hinting at a possible nonlinear effect only at saturation.

### 4. Pretrends and regression to the mean  
Alpha’s preadoption median: 50.4 (Jan)  46.9 (Feb)  49.4 (Mar). The dip in February suggests some volatility but no clear downward trend; there is no strong evidence that a high January value was meanreverting. Beta’s pretrend: 45.4  45.9  46.4, slowly rising. Gamma’s pretrend: 36.4  36.5  39.6  39.7  45.4  45.9 – a marked upward trajectory before adoption. Selection into the programme was based on historical cycle times (preJanuary 2026), not on the preperiod we observe. Gamma’s worsening pretrend argues against regression to the mean; if anything, one would have expected further deterioration. The sharp reversal in July could therefore be partially attributable to automation, but the coincident drop in arrivals (878 in Jul, 814 in Aug vs. 859 in Jun) and the loss of one FTE in June confound the interpretation.

### 5. Sensitivity analysis and bounded conclusions  
- **Median cycle time for Gamma**: Assume the counterfactual without automation would have followed the control branches’ July–August trend (+0.5 h) plus an additional rise of 2–3 h due to the pretrend. The observed 3.4 h change then translates to a plausible effect between 6% and 12% of premedian. If instead the control trend is believed to be a perfect counterfactual, the effect is 3.9 h (9.6%). My conservative range: median reduction between 0% and 10%.  
- **p90 cycle time for Alpha/Beta**: The control p90 increased by +12.4 h; treated p90 increased by ~9.3–9.7 h. The relative “benefit” of about 3 h could vanish if the complexity increase (Alpha +8 pp, Beta +3 pp) added roughly that amount to p90 independently. Plausible range: from 5% (beneficial) to +5% (harmful), leaning toward no effect or slight worsening.  
- **Escalation**: The DiD estimates for escalation are +2.0 pp (Alpha), +1.5 pp (Beta), +1.7 pp (Gamma). Even under varying assumptions about casemix and frauddesk referrals, the lower bound remains positive (+1 pp). Thus escalation most likely increased.

## 8. Rollout recommendation  
**Expand with an embedded evaluation.**

### Design
Select **Delta** as the next branch for a controlled, datarich pilot, while **Epsilon** serves as a concurrent holdout control. Start automation in Delta from **2026October01** after a minimum of 9 preintervention months (Jan–Sep 2026) are available. Observe both branches for at least **6 postimplementation months** (Oct2026 through Mar2027) before judging scaleup. To avoid the “availablebutunused” conflation, mandate a target `automated_fast_path_pct` of 40% by month two via process design and training, while still measuring actual usage.  
### Data collection
Move from branchmonth aggregates to **caselevel event logs** that capture timestamps for each activity (`Register`, `Triage`, `Specialist`, `Rework`, `Resolve`, `Close`), case complexity, and final CSAT. At the branchmonth level, additionally collect:
- Queue lengths at triage and specialists at the start of each day;
- Specialist utilisation (hours worked/hours available);
- Frauddesk referral counts and waittimes per case;
- Weekly CSAT response counts and raw scores to model nonresponse.
### Spillover monitoring
Track the frauddesk queue and processing times for all branches, and record any manual rerouting of cases to Delta/Epsilon. If the fraud desk is shared, model its capacity to ensure automation’s increased referrals do not simply shift delays elsewhere.
### Guardrails
- If `p90_cycle_h` for Delta exceeds its preperiod maximum by >20% in any postlaunch month, suspend automation and investigate.
- If CSAT (adjusted for response patterns) drops by 0.2 points below the preintervention lower 95% prediction bound, halt and remediate.
### Decision rule for full scaleup
After 6 postmonths, estimate the conditional average treatment effect on `median_cycle_h` using a syntheticcontrol comparison of Delta vs. Epsilon (and possibly other branches as donors). If the central estimate of the relative reduction exceeds **5%** with a consistent pretrend fit and placebointime tests that fail to produce similar drops, expand to Epsilon using a staggered design (Epsilon starts 2027April01). If the estimate is smaller or ambiguous, commission a redesigned triage algorithm before further rollout.

print("\nPanel model (binary automation, branch+month FE):")
print(rob.summary().tables[1])
print("Warning: only 5 clusters; standard errors are unreliable.\n")

df_auto = df[df['automation']==1].copy()
model2 = smf.ols('median_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + high_complexity_pct + arrivals + fte + C(branch)', data=df_auto).fit()

## 10. Final claim ladder  

- **Observed:** All three branches that adopted automation experienced an increase in escalation percentage of at least 1.5 percentage points relative to nevertreated branches, a pattern visible even after accounting for casemix and frauddesk timing.  
- **Suggestive:** In Gamma, the combination of automation and reduced arrivals was accompanied by a marked drop in median cycle time (from ~46 to 36–38h) that is larger than what control branches experienced in the same months, hinting at a beneficial fastpath effect when capacity and complexity are stable.  
- **Conditionally causal:** Under the assumption that a synthetic control built from Delta and Epsilon provides a valid counterfactual, automation reduced Gamma’s median cycle time by roughly 5–10%, but this estimate is fragile because of the small donor pool and coincident staffing and demand changes.  
- **Not supported:** A single, crossbranch claim such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible; the data show heterogeneous, contextdependent effects that make a uniform statement misleading.
