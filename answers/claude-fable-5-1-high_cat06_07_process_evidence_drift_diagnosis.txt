## 1. Drift diagnosis table

Reading conventions used below. "Observed" = a number that is in the tables; "inferred" = a conclusion drawn from several numbers; "hypothesis" = plausible but untested. Label-maturity direction: a non-breach label is known when a case closes before its promised date; a breach label cannot be known until the promised date has passed (or until a late closure). Breaches therefore mature later than non-breaches, so any immature cohort under-states its breach rate. This matters for Aug (93%), Sep (82%) and above all Oct (55%).

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January-March (stable reference). AUROC 0.81-0.82, Brier 0.130-0.132, ECE 0.022-0.025, mean prediction equals observed breach (0.18-0.19), 100% maturity, variant mix 45/35/20, parts edge 28-35 h, unknown activity 0.2-0.3%. This is the only period where every layer (data, process, labels, population, model) is simultaneously clean; it defines the reference for all later comparisons. Note the parts edge already drifts 28 -> 35 h across the quarter - small, but the earliest sign of what becomes the July-October problem.

April (schema/pipeline incident, outcome side unaffected). The rename of the anchor activity broke prediction-prefix construction: 17% of events unknown, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped variants. The model over-predicted (0.28 vs 0.19) - the signature of prefixes built at the wrong (usually later, longer) event, imputed missing features, and duplicated events inflating count features. Critically, the world did not change: overall breach 0.19, per-variant rates within ~0.03 of baseline, parts edge 34 h, remote edge normal, labels fully mature. Competing explanation - "April was a real concept shift" - is contradicted by the unchanged outcome side and by the recovery in May once the mapping patch landed. April deterioration is a measurement failure, not a model or process failure. It still has a lasting consequence: the cached prefix-length feature was never recomputed for April and new-source duplicates before 20 April were never removed, so April as training data remains corrupted even though the dashboard looks repaired.

May (residual pipeline effects, otherwise normal). Metrics recover but not fully (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.05, mean 0.21 vs 0.18, unknown 2%, missing 3%, unmapped 1%). The residual is proportionate to the residual data-quality defects, which is the expected pattern under the pipeline hypothesis. The parts edge is 38 h.

June (real process change in routing, not a change in the outcome mechanism). The remote-routing policy moves V2 from 34% to 52%. Observed breach falls to 0.15, which is exactly what the baseline per-variant rates applied to the new mix predict (0.158). Per-variant rates are unchanged (0.16 / 0.09 / 0.35) and the diagnosis -> Remote Fix edge is stable. The model over-predicts (0.20) because, predicting at diagnosis before the routing branch, it embeds the old probability that a case becomes a technician visit. This is a control-flow process drift that produces a calibration offset and a mild AUROC dip (0.79); the mapping from path to outcome has not moved. Treating this as concept drift and retraining would be unnecessary; a recalibration or a routing-eligibility feature suffices. It must be kept separate from August-September because its sign is opposite (over- rather than under-prediction) and its mechanism is benign.

July (onset of a performance drift that becomes concept drift for the model). Parts edge 42 -> 65 h; V3 matured breach 0.35 -> 0.46; V1 and V2 unchanged. Observed overall breach 0.19 with mean prediction 0.18 - the aggregate hides the V3 problem because V3 is only 22% of cases. AUROC 0.76. Labels 98% mature, so July is trustworthy evidence that the supplier problem started in July (possibly earlier, given the Mar-Jun creep).

August (severe concept drift plus a secondary control-flow swing; Partner Z absent). Parts edge 130 h; V3 breach 0.64; V3 share 35%, V2 down to 40%. Observed breach 0.28 (93% mature, so biased slightly low) against mean prediction 0.19; AUROC 0.68, ECE 0.12. Decomposition: about +0.12 of the rise is within-V3 rate change and about +0.02 is mix. Because Partner Z is 0% in August, population shift cannot explain August; the pipeline is clean (unknown 0.7%, missing 2%); labels are 93% mature. The competing explanation for the V3 share increase (backorders converting would-be standard visits into part orders vs. fault-mix change vs. remote-routing saturation) is open, but it is secondary to the within-variant effect.

September (three overlapping shifts: performance/concept, population, and the start of label immaturity). Parts edge 148 h; V3 share 42%, of which a large part is Partner Z (24% of cases, 58% V3). Observed breach 0.31 at 82% maturity; predicted 0.21; AUROC 0.66; ECE 0.14. Decomposition: +0.135 within-variant (mostly the supplier-driven V3 rate), +0.035 mix (mostly Partner Z's V3 routing). Partner Z's 0.45 vs 0.27 is largely reproduced by its V3 share alone, leaving a small residual that could reflect rural travel distance and device age (unobserved by the model) or the asymmetric label maturation. Within-V1 and within-V2 rates tick up slightly (0.19, 0.12), consistent with a modest Partner Z effect not mediated by variant. September should not be read as "Partner Z broke the model": the model was already at AUROC 0.68 in August without Partner Z.

October (label-maturity artefact dominates; underlying drift almost certainly continues). 55% maturity, median label delay 52 d, outcome API backlog concentrated on long-path (V3) cases. Observed breach 0.16 is not comparable to earlier months: immature cohorts under-state breaches, and the least-labelled cases are the ones most likely to breach. The parts edge (155 h) and V3 share (41%) say the true October rate should be at or above September's. The apparent flip to over-prediction (0.23 vs 0.16) and the AUROC of 0.64 are computed on an outcome-selected subsample and carry no information about model quality yet. October is evidence of a label-pipeline problem and nothing else until it matures.

Why the four episodes are not one failure: April is measurement (input side) with a healthy process; June is a benign control-flow change with unchanged outcome mechanics and over-prediction; August-September is a real change in outcome given path (under-prediction) compounded by mix and a new sub-population; October is missing labels. They differ in layer, in sign of miscalibration, in the remedy (recompute / recalibrate / retrain-with-new-features-plus-business-fix / wait), and in which months can be trusted for training.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.
Procedure: for each case-start month, plot cumulative share labelled vs days since case start, split by variant and source; recompute Aug/Sep metrics using only labels that would have been available at the same maturity as Oct currently has (e.g. 55%), and recompute Oct metrics as labels arrive.
Expected if censoring explains Oct: Aug and Sep at 55% simulated maturity show a similarly depressed breach rate (~0.15-0.18), apparent over-prediction, and AUROC in the mid-0.60s; Oct's breach rate rises toward or above 0.30 as maturity approaches 95%.
Expected if Oct is a real improvement/real model collapse: Aug/Sep at 55% maturity already look like their mature values; Oct's breach rate stays near 0.16 and AUROC stays ~0.64 after maturation.
Also: Partner Z vs non-Z maturity curves; if Partner Z's curve lags, the Sep 0.45 vs 0.27 gap must be re-estimated at equal maturity.

2. Recomputation after activity-label normalisation and duplicate cleaning.
Procedure: rebuild April-May prefixes and features from raw events with `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` mapped to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, deduplication applied to all sources from 1 Jan, and the cached prefix-length feature recomputed; re-score with the frozen production model.
Expected under the pipeline hypothesis: April AUROC returns to ~0.80, ECE to ~0.03, mean prediction to ~0.19; May residual disappears; per-variant April rates stay ~baseline.
Expected under "April was real drift": metrics remain degraded after recomputation; unknown-activity share falls but calibration does not recover.
Additionally compare the recomputed April features to the cached ones (distribution and per-case diff) to quantify training-data contamination.

3. Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.
Procedure: reliability diagrams, ECE, AUROC, Brier per (month x eventual variant x source), on cohorts >= 95% mature only.
Expected under supplier-driven concept drift: within-V3 calibration collapses from Jul (predicted ~0.32, observed 0.46-0.64) while V1/V2 calibration stays intact; AUROC within V3 falls because the model has no feature separating early vs late parts delivery.
Expected under June routing drift: all variants remain well calibrated within-variant in June; the miscalibration exists only in aggregate.
Expected under Partner Z population shift as the driver: Sep non-Partner-Z metrics are close to Aug's, and Partner Z shows within-variant miscalibration; if instead non-Partner-Z Sep metrics are as bad as Aug's, Partner Z is not the primary driver.

4. Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges.
Procedure: monthly variant shares by source; frequency and performance DFGs by case-start month; edge medians and p90 for `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Part Received -> Dispatch Technician`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`, `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`; count of `Order Part` events per case; share of cases with repeated `Order Part`.
Expected under supplier hypothesis: only edges downstream of `Order Part` degrade, starting July, worsening in Aug; other edges flat; the increase in V3 share in Aug comes with more repeat part orders or with part types that were previously in van stock.
Expected under a general capacity/operations problem: `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed` and V1 breach rates also degrade.
Expected under Partner Z effect: `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed` lengthens for Partner Z cases only (travel distance) from September.

5. Test whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed.
Procedure: within V3 only, fit breach ~ month indicators + diagnosis-time features + realised parts lead time; compare (a) month effects with and without lead time; (b) breach probability within lead-time bins (e.g. <48 h, 48-96 h, >96 h) across months.
Expected under lead-time-mediated concept drift: the month effects for Jul-Sep vanish once lead time is included, and breach within each lead-time bin is stable across months.
Expected under a broader concept change (e.g. tighter promised dates, changed breach definition, technician shortage): month effects persist after conditioning on lead time, or breach rises within the same lead-time bin.
Also check the promised-resolution-date policy: if promised durations shortened, that is an outcome-definition change the audit said did not happen - verify it from the data, not the documentation.

6. Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z.
Procedure: (a) reweight Sep non-Partner-Z cases to the baseline variant mix and to the Jan-Mar covariate distribution; (b) train a source classifier (Partner Z vs other) on diagnosis-time features to measure separability; (c) compare Partner Z vs non-Z breach within variant and within strata of shared features; (d) evaluate the model on Partner Z only.
Expected if Partner Z's difference is mix-only: within-variant Partner Z rates ~ non-Z rates; source classifier AUC modest; reweighting Partner Z to the non-Z variant mix reproduces ~0.27.
Expected if Partner Z is a genuinely different population: within-variant gaps persist (especially V1, via travel distance), source classifier AUC high on observed features, and the model is miscalibrated within Partner Z even after mix adjustment.
Expected if the gap is a maturation artefact: the gap shrinks as Partner Z labels catch up.

7. Feature missingness and lineage audits.
Procedure: per month and per source, missingness of every feature; feature-version hashes; confirm the diagnosis-anchor event used for each prefix; compare cached vs recomputed features for April; check that dedupe ran for all sources.
Expected under the pipeline hypothesis: April (and 1-20 April in particular) shows anchor mismatches, duplicated counts and unrecomputed features; other months clean.
Expected under "Partner Z ingestion is also broken": Sep-Oct show elevated missingness or new raw activity labels specifically for Partner Z's source system; if clean, Partner Z's effect is population, not pipeline.

8. Distribution monitoring of diagnosis-time covariates (covariate drift test).
Procedure: monthly PSI / KS per feature and adversarial-validation AUC (month vs baseline).
Expected under pure concept drift (Jul-Aug): features stable, adversarial AUC ~0.5-0.55, yet outcomes change.
Expected under covariate drift (Sep): shift concentrated in Partner Z cases and in features correlated with region/device.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Stage 0 - immediate containment (this week).
- Declare the model "degraded, under-predicting for parts-dependent cases" to consumers; disable any fully automated actions that depend on the risk score being calibrated.
- Add a transparent business rule alongside the score: if the diagnosis code implies a part order and the trailing 14-day median `Order Part -> Part Received` exceeds 2x baseline, escalate regardless of score. This addresses the dominant, well-evidenced failure without touching the model.
- Freeze automated retraining and any retraining that would include Oct (55% mature) or Sep (82%) labels or April's unrecomputed features.
- Escalate two non-model incidents: the supplier lead-time problem (business) and the outcome API backlog (data engineering). Most of the observed deterioration is not fixable inside the model.

Stage 1 - short-term recalibration (2-4 weeks, reversible).
- Fit a calibration layer on matured Jul-Aug labels (Aug reaching ~100% by now) using a small, interpretable set of conditioning variables: predicted score, probability/indicator of part need at diagnosis, trailing parts lead time, source (Partner Z), remote-eligibility flag. Isotonic or logistic recalibration with these terms is enough; a pure intercept shift is not, because the shift is concentrated in V3-prone cases and would over-correct V1/V2.
- Do not fit calibration on April or May raw features (pipeline residuals) or on Oct (immature). Jun is safe for calibration purposes but reflects a regime with normal parts lead times; include it only with the lead-time covariate so the layer does not assume the crisis is permanent.
- Recognise the limit: recalibration cannot recover lost discrimination (AUROC 0.68). It fixes the level, not the ranking.

Stage 2 - feature and pipeline repair (parallel, 4-8 weeks).
- Canonical activity vocabulary with alias mapping applied at ingestion; dedupe for all sources for the whole history; recompute all cached features for Jan-Oct from the cleaned log; store feature lineage.
- New prediction-time features: trailing parts lead time (supplier-level if available), part-need indicator from diagnosis code, remote-eligibility flag under the current routing policy, source/partner, rural travel distance, device age, current open-order backlog.
- Label pipeline: maturity tracking per case; a censoring-aware evaluation dataset that records the label vintage.

Stage 3 - full retraining (after Sep-Oct labels reach >= 95% maturity and Stage 2 is complete; realistically late Dec-Jan).
- Training months: Jan-Mar safe. April unsafe until recomputed from raw events and validated against test 2; if it fails validation, exclude it. May usable after recomputation. Jun-Aug safe (labels mature; Jun carries the routing regime, Jul-Aug the supplier regime, which is desirable variety if lead time is a feature). Sep conditional on maturity >= 95% and on the Partner Z maturation lag closing. Oct unsafe until mature; never train on Oct's currently available labels because the labelled subset is selected on short resolution paths.
- Use time-ordered validation (train through Jul, validate Aug-Sep) to confirm that the lead-time feature makes the model regime-robust rather than crisis-fitted.
- Architecture: a two-stage or path-aware model (P(path | X) x P(breach | path, X, lead time)) is justified because the two real drifts act on different components: June moved P(path | X); July-September moved P(breach | V3, lead time). This is better than a monolithic model because each component can be recalibrated independently when routing policy or supplier performance changes.
- Segment-specific model for Partner Z: not justified on current evidence. Partner Z's elevated breach is mostly explained by variant mix; the correct fix is to add the missing covariates (source, travel distance, device age) and check within-Partner-Z calibration. Build a separate model only if, after those features are added and labels are mature, Partner Z residuals remain large and stable. Variant-specific treatment (the path-aware architecture above) is justified.

Stage 4 - rollback and regime handling.
- Keep the pre-crisis model as a fallback; if the supplier recovers (parts edge returns to < 50 h), a model retrained on Aug-Sep without a lead-time feature would over-predict as badly as it under-predicts now. The lead-time feature and the calibration layer must both be reviewed when the performance-DFG monitor returns to baseline.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Monitors (each computed monthly and weekly, by case-start cohort, stratified by variant-so-far, source, and prediction-time month):

1. Data-quality monitor (gate). Activity vocabulary vs canonical set (new/unknown labels, share of `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`), duplicate rate by source, feature missingness per feature, anchor-event hit rate (share of cases whose prefix was cut at the canonical diagnosis event), feature-version/lineage hashes, event-count per case vs baseline. Thresholds from Jan-Mar (e.g. unknown share > 1%, missingness > 3%, anchor hit rate < 98%).
2. Label-maturity monitor (gate). Share of cases labelled by days since start, per variant and source; median and p90 label delay; outcome API lag; expected vs actual maturity given historical curves. Marks each cohort as "mature (>= 95%)", "provisional", or "not evaluable".
3. Process-variant monitor (leading, label-free). Variant shares, new/unmapped variants, share of cases with repeated `Order Part`, routing-policy version. Alerts on mix shifts (e.g. any variant share moving > 8 points).
4. Performance-DFG monitor (leading, label-free). Median and p90 elapsed time on key edges (`Order Part -> Part Received`, `Part Received -> Dispatch Technician`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`, `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`), by supplier and source. Alert when a key edge exceeds 1.5x baseline for two consecutive weeks. Emits a "process performance regime" flag consumed by the calibration monitor and the containment rule.
5. Population monitor (leading, label-free). Source/partner shares, covariate PSI and adversarial-validation AUC on diagnosis-time features, unobserved-proxy checks (region, device age where available).
6. Predictive monitor (lagging). AUROC, PR-AUC, and score distribution (mean predicted risk; this part is label-free) on mature cohorts only; fixed-vintage comparisons for provisional cohorts.
7. Calibration monitor (lagging). ECE, reliability by decile, mean predicted vs observed, and the mix/within-variant decomposition (expected rate at baseline per-variant rates vs observed). Reports "mix-driven" vs "within-variant" miscalibration explicitly.
8. Intervention/deployment monitor. Model version, feature-set version, threshold changes, calibration-layer changes, business-rule activations, routing-policy and partner-onboarding events from a change register; intervention rate on high-risk cases (interventions that prevent breaches make a correct model look over-predicting).

Alert dependencies (so a label-delay alarm does not trigger retraining):
- Data-quality red -> suppress predictive and calibration alarms for the affected cohorts, tag them "metrics untrustworthy", open a pipeline incident. Retraining is blocked.
- Label-maturity below threshold for a cohort -> predictive/calibration metrics for that cohort are computed only as fixed-vintage comparisons and are not eligible to raise alarms; a label-delay alarm routes to data engineering and freezes training-set assembly; it never triggers retraining.
- Performance-DFG or process-variant alarm alone (labels not yet mature) -> "process regime change" notice to the business owner and to the calibration monitor; enables the containment rule; requests a recalibration review, not retraining.
- Population alarm alone -> stratified evaluation request; no model action until mature labels exist for the new population.
- Retraining trigger requires all of: (a) predictive or calibration alarm on a cohort with maturity >= 95% and data-quality green; (b) corroboration from a leading monitor (performance-DFG, variant, or population) or persistence over two consecutive mature months; (c) sign-off that the driver is in-scope for modelling (a supplier crisis mainly needs a business fix plus a lead-time feature, not a new concept).
- Recalibration trigger: calibration alarm with predictive discrimination intact (AUROC within 0.03 of baseline) and decomposition showing a mix-driven offset -> recalibrate. If discrimination is also degraded within a variant -> feature repair/retraining path.
- Intervention monitor changes -> annotate all downstream metrics with the change window before interpretation.

Process-evidence extraction sketch (auditable summaries, no model fitting; PM4Py has no built-in drift detector, so change detection is done on these monthly summaries outside PM4Py):

```python
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].str.strip().replace(CANON)
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "source_system"]).reset_index(drop=True)
    # Duplicate strategy: exact repeats across sources, plus same case+activity within 60 s (replay artefact).
    exact = df.duplicated(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    same_prev = df["case_id"].eq(df["case_id"].shift()) & df["activity"].eq(df["activity"].shift())
    near = same_prev & (df["timestamp"] - df["timestamp"].shift()).le(pd.Timedelta("60s"))
    df["is_dup"] = exact | near
    df["is_unknown"] = df["activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")
    audit = (df.assign(month=df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str))
               .groupby("month")
               .agg(events=("case_id", "size"), dup_share=("is_dup", "mean"),
                    unknown_share=("is_unknown", "mean"),
                    renamed_share=("activity_raw", lambda s: s.isin(CANON).mean())))
    clean = df.loc[~df["is_dup"] & ~df["is_unknown"]]
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
                                 timestamp_key="timestamp")
    return log, audit


def variant_family(trace):
    if "Remote Fix" in trace:
        return "V2"
    if "Order Part" in trace:
        return "V3"
    if "Dispatch Technician" in trace:
        return "V1"
    return "other"


def _naive(ts):
    return ts.dt.tz_convert(None) if ts.dt.tz is not None else ts


def monthly_summary(log, start, end):
    # Event-calendar cohort: everything that HAPPENED in the month -> throughput / edge-performance view.
    ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, f"{start} 00:00:00", f"{end} 00:00:00", mode="events")
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev)
    freq, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(ev)
    part_h = perf.get(PART_EDGE, {}).get("median", float("nan")) / 3600
    # Case-start cohort: cases that STARTED in the month -> matches the prediction cohort.
    # Open cases are included with partial traces, so variant and closure shares are censored at the cutoff.
    starts = _naive(log.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min())
    ids = starts[(starts >= pd.Timestamp(start)) & (starts < pd.Timestamp(end))].index
    cs = log[log["case:concept:name"].isin(ids)]
    fam = {}
    for variant, cnt in pm4py.get_variants(cs).items():
        n = cnt if isinstance(cnt, int) else len(cnt)
        fam[variant_family(variant)] = fam.get(variant_family(variant), 0) + n
    n_cases = max(sum(fam.values()), 1)
    per_case = cs.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].agg(set)
    return {"month": start[:7], "cases_started": n_cases,
            "share_closed_at_cutoff": per_case.map(lambda s: "Case Closed" in s).mean(),
            "share_with_diag_anchor": per_case.map(lambda s: DIAG in s).mean(),
            **{f"share_{k}": v / n_cases for k, v in fam.items()},
            "part_edge_median_h": part_h, "part_edge_n": freq.get(PART_EDGE, 0)}


if __name__ == "__main__":
    log, audit = load_and_clean()
    months = pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M")
    rows = [monthly_summary(log, str(p.start_time.date()), str((p + 1).start_time.date()))
            for p in months]
    summary = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("month").join(audit)
    # Complementary system-dynamics view (event-calendar grouping, not case-start).
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log, grouper_freq="M")
    summary.to_csv("monthly_process_evidence.csv")
    temporal.to_csv("monthly_temporal_features.csv")
    print(summary.round(3))
```

Interpretation note for the two cohorts: the event-calendar cohort answers "how did the process perform this month" (e.g. parts lead time realised in August, whichever month the case started) and is the right basis for the performance-DFG monitor; the case-start cohort answers "what happened to the cases we scored this month" and is the right basis for predictive, calibration and label-maturity monitors. Do not use `mode="traces_contained"` for the case-start cohort: it keeps only cases fully inside the window and reproduces exactly the short-case selection bias that distorts October.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. Retraining on immature labels (October, and September for Partner Z). The labelled subset is selected on short resolution paths, so the model would learn that long-path/V3-prone and Partner Z cases are low risk, and would under-predict even more once labels mature; validation on the same immature data would falsely confirm the improvement. Mitigation: maturity gate (>= 95%) on every training and evaluation cohort, label-vintage stamping, fixed-vintage backtests, and censoring-aware evaluation (e.g. report metrics on cases whose promised date has passed).

2. Calibrating or retraining around the April pipeline bug. Fitting on April's cached features (wrong anchor event, duplicates, unrecomputed prefix length) teaches the model that inflated event counts and missing queue features indicate risk, degrading it on clean months; a calibration layer fitted on April-May would carry a spurious downward offset. Mitigation: quarantine April until recomputed from normalised, de-duplicated events and validated (test 2); lineage checks that refuse features produced under an unrecognised activity vocabulary.

3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift (dismissing August as noise, as Partner Z, or as label delay). Under-prediction for parts-dependent cases persists at roughly 2x the predicted rate, escalation resources are misallocated, and promised dates keep being breached silently. Mitigation: within-variant calibration monitoring and performance-DFG edges as leading indicators; the containment rule tied to trailing lead time; formal business escalation because the root cause is outside the model.

4. Attributing Partner Z's higher breach rate to unfair or broken model behaviour without stratified evidence. Reactions such as suppressing Partner Z cases, adding a penalising partner indicator, or building a Partner Z model on 82%-mature labels would encode a mix effect (V3 routing under a supplier crisis) as a partner attribute, and could be discriminatory toward rural customers. Mitigation: within-variant and within-stratum comparisons at equal label maturity; add the missing covariates (travel distance, device age) rather than a partner penalty; evaluate calibration within Partner Z; involve the partner in the parts-lead-time discussion.

5. Over-fitting to the crisis regime. Retraining on Aug-Sep without a lead-time feature bakes in a 0.6 breach rate for V3; when the supplier recovers, the model over-predicts, triggering unnecessary escalations and eroding trust. Mitigation: make lead time and routing policy explicit features, keep the pre-crisis model as fallback, and tie recalibration reviews to the performance-DFG monitor returning to baseline.

6. Treating June as concept drift and retraining. The outcome mechanism did not change; retraining would spend weeks and risk pulling in April contamination to fix what a recalibration or a remote-eligibility feature fixes in days. Mitigation: mix/within-variant decomposition in the calibration monitor before any retraining decision; recalibration path when discrimination is intact.

7. Intervention feedback masquerading as miscalibration. If the containment rule or the risk score triggers expediting for high-risk cases, breaches among high scores fall and the model appears to over-predict; "correcting" it removes the protection. Mitigation: log interventions per case, evaluate on non-intervened or counterfactually adjusted cohorts, and annotate metrics with intervention windows via the deployment monitor.

8. Consolidating all months into one drift label. Choosing "concept drift" for the whole period retrains on corrupted April features and immature October labels; choosing "pipeline failure" for the whole period ignores a real doubling of V3 breach risk. Mitigation: the layered monitoring dependencies in Section 5, and per-month drift attribution recorded alongside every model decision.
