3. **Local knowledge loss**:  
   Specialists can now only see the case online after it has been interpreted by a local caseworker. Many decisions depend on unwritten local context (language, local regulations, known customers). This leads to either slow requests for reinterpretation or shortterm quality mistakes. This is not a PM4 log attribute; it appears as rework rates for cases with high-value features over time.

4. **Fairness / cross segment differences**:  
   The shared service gives preference to queue categories, but not to geographic, language, or regional preferences. Customers that need accommodation may be handled by a generic worker, creating extra handoffs and it may be invisible in overall average KPI. Evaluate `customer_segment` separately.

5. **Learning job: no body grows**:
   Local junior caseworkers no longer see how a specialist solves difficult problems. Within 1–2 years, they will be slower, less able to work in an informal advice mode. Hard to quantify but `discover_8_activity_based_resource_similarity` may show that local resources become more similar to schedulers, not deeper.

6. **Lack of responsibility/accountability**:
   In a shared queue, advice can come from different resources each time; no one remembers the case giving advice. When a mistake is spotted, the chain of whom did it is broken. Cycle time may look stable because errors are repaired, but costs escalate.

7. **Exception handling rises**:  
   Nonstandard cases (unusual regional law, unexpected vendor issue) will be continuously returned from the queue because they are not recognized by rules. They then bounce between queue and local case manager. Each bounce adds waiting time; since the average remains low, these exceptions leverage rework.

---

# 5. Pilot design

- **Pilot scope**:  
  Route only highcomplexity cases and required specialty cases that are *new* for one month in **East and North** teams through the proposed sharedservice queue.  
  “Shared queue” service for priority tech and legal/risk only; do **not** consolidate approval queue, and **do not** remove specialist positions yet. The current local specialists remain to help unprocessed cases (or serve as a shadow resource).

- **Comparison group**:  
  - Southern region serve as control (legacy process).  
  - East also acts as an internal before–after comparison because E_Tech is overloaded; North acts as “prob” team with strong local knowledge.  
  - Within East, randomly split 50% of highcomplexity cases using shared-route vs remaining legacylocal; concerning details.

- **Pre/post observation window**:  
  - Set, before: 4 weeks of current baseline, we need ~400 cases in allocated region.  
  - Post: 6–8 weeks of piloting.

- **Segmentation**:  
  - by team: East and North, South as compare;  
  - by complexity: high/medium/low use `case_complexity`;  
  - by specialty: `legal`, `risk`, `security` groups in `required_specialty`;  
  - by customer segment: e.g. “regional/local” customers vs “national/global”;  
  - Record string log: `team`, `location`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`.

- **Guardrail metrics** (staged):  
  - 10% no claims against waiting:  90th waiting time for each shared queue must not be > 8h for 3 days in a week.  
  - 20% tail latency: 95th percentile cycle time for highcomplexity cases in pilot must not rise > +15% in current pilot relative preperiod / control.  
  - 30% workload balance: highest resource utilization in shared queue  80% and lowest  45%; else consider prioritizing or accelerating staffing.  
  - Minimal turnover: the portion of “case returns from shared queue” that produce rework within 5 days must be < 10 p.p.

- **Staff feedback collection**:  
  - Weekly 2-question pull survey to all involved: (1) “Do you know who is accountable when a shared queue fails?” (2) “This week, did the shared service add value?”  
  - Interview each embedded specialist about value loss of their job.

- **Rollback / adjustment triggers**:  
  - If any guardrail is broken for 3 consecutive working days, rollback to local routing for two weeks. After stabilization, attempts can restart with a corrected de-queue mechanism.  
  - If East306 East positive but North/South negative; stops the crosspollination effect.

---

# 6. Recommendation

**Modified pilot first** — do not abrogate full rollout. Approve a controlled pilot confined to highcomplexity and specialty cases in **East** and **North** for 46 weeks of pre and 68 weeks of post and while maintaining full local capacity. **Do not** remove local supervisors yet, because the approval queue risk has not been validated. All local specialists remain employed to keep a fallback and to learn, while the shared queue is used as a advisory resource rather than a hard replacement.

Executive management **must not conclude from pilot averages that** the change is working. The evaluation should be based on:

- 90th/99th cycles, not the mean;
- segmentspecific rework for highcomplexity, legal, and regional customer;
- queue depth / waittime percentiles, not only utilization;
- line staff feedback, not only throughput.

The pilot itself must be treated as a “tooknowabout-shared-widget” experiment — incorporate knowhow from local specialists into new standard process. If `5 percentage point` improvement tail is achieved while fairness metrics remain, then scale one step further (e.g., legal, then risk). If fail, do not expand until effective compensating taken (hire more shared staff, documentation).
