# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Committee & Chief Operating Officer  
**From:** Lead Process Mining & Operations Strategist  
**Date:** August 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Executive Decision Memo: Strategic Direction for Fiber Installation Redesign (Pilot R)  

### Executive Summary & Decision
Management should **reject both an unconstrained national rollout (Alternative A)** and an **unselective full rollback (Alternative B)**. Instead, management should immediately approve **Alternative E: A Segmented Hybrid Deployment with Conformance Guardrails**, combined with targeted operational experiments and measurement instrumentation.

Pilot R is neither an unqualified breakthrough nor an outright failure. It produced an **asymmetric, bifurcated operational outcome**:
1. **The Fast-Track Success:** For standard, single-family homes with low building risk (34% of volume), Redesign R delivered significant efficiency gains—reducing median cycle time by 6.0 days, compressing p90 duration by 8.0 days, lowering visible upfront costs by €23/order, and improving first-time-right (FTR) installations by +3 percentage points.
2. **The Complex-Case Breakdown:** For apartments, older permit-sensitive buildings, and business customers (55% of volume), aggressive survey skipping, bypassed municipal permit checks, and premature billing created severe operational bottlenecks. Consequently, tail cycle times exploded (p95 worsened from 45.0 to 62.0 days), rework surged (second visits rose from 11% to 18%), and 30-day customer complaints increased by 60% (from 6.1% to 9.8%).

### Core Operational Trade-Offs
Management must address three critical trade-offs exposed by process-mining diagnostics:
- **Upfront Velocity vs. Downstream Rework Costs:** Skipping field surveys reduced upfront delivery costs from €184 to €161 and dropped survey team utilization to 52%. However, this shifted work into expensive rework loops—surging second-visit utilization from 66% to 92% and pushing total estimated cost to €207/case (with downstream complaint costs truncated after week 4).
- **Process Simplicity vs. Organizational Resilience:** Merging validation with scheduling and centralizing case ownership reduced handovers (cases with 3+ handovers dropped from 34% to 12%). However, single-point case ownership created severe bottlenecking (coordinator utilization reached 93%) and destroyed operational resilience during coordinator absences.
- **Invoicing Acceleration vs. Customer Trust:** Shifting activation checks after billing accelerated revenue recognition on paper, but created a toxic customer failure mode ("billing for non-working fiber"), degrading post-installation NPS from +21 to +17 and generating move-on-log compliance deviations.

### Recommended Action Plan
Management must transition from a uniform process to an **intelligent, gated operating model**:
1. **Scale Selectively (Track 1):** Deploy merged validation/scheduling and coordinator case ownership exclusively for verified **Simple House / Low Building Risk** properties across all regions.
2. **Halt Toxic Failure Modes Immediately:**
   - Suspend field-survey skipping for all apartments, older structures, and business clients.
   - Enforce automated hard conformance gates blocking `Billing start` until automated `Activation test` is verified.
   - Enforce mandatory `Permit check` gates prior to dispatch in older properties.
3. **Restructure Coordinator Capacity:** Transition isolated coordinators into 3-person cross-functional pods to eliminate single-point-of-failure bottlenecks and balance the 93% utilization load.
4. **Deploy Value-of-Information Program:** Implement structured failure reason codes (M3), automated conformance monitoring (M7), full 60-day downstream cost tracking (M4), and predictive survey-rule validation (M6) before expanding automation to complex multi-dwelling units.

This hybrid approach captures proven financial and speed gains on one-third of the portfolio while immediately halting the tail degradation, rework costs, and compliance risks plaguing complex installations.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

The pilot data exhibits five apparent paradoxes. Process-mining mechanics, queueing theory, and organizational design explain why these metrics diverge simultaneously.

### 3.1 High Fitness (0.94) vs. Low Precision (0.51)
- **Mechanisms:** Replay fitness ($0.94$) measures how much of the observed log can be reproduced by the discovered process model. Precision ($0.51$) measures how much behavior allowed by the model is actually observed in compliant practice. 
- **Explanation:** In the baseline, rigid sequential handovers constrained execution to a structured pipeline ($P=0.79$). In Redesign R, eliminating hard gates (allowing skipped surveys, optional permit checks, manual activations, and post-billing activation checks) resulted in an under-constrained execution environment. The discovered process model had to insert multiple parallel bypasses, unconstrained loops, and loose interleavings to replay the chaotic log traces. While the model replays the pilot data easily (high fitness), it also allows vast spaces of unapproved, invalid behavior—such as billing customers who never received working service or repeating installations indefinitely without surveys (low precision).

### 3.2 Improved Median (14.2d -> 10.8d) vs. Worsened Tail (p90: 31.0d -> 38.5d, p95: 45.0d -> 62.0d)
- **Mechanisms:** The median reflects the 50th percentile, heavily weighted by the fast happy path of standard cases. The p90 and p95 tails reflect the cumulative delay experienced by exception cases.
- **Explanation:** For simple single-family homes (34% of volume), merging address validation with appointment scheduling and eliminating field surveys removed days of administrative waiting, driving the median down by 3.4 days overall (-6.0 days in simple houses). However, when a complex case failed on site due to an unaddressed permit or blocked riser (FTR dropped to 76%), it entered a rework loop. Because coordinator utilization reached 93% and second-visit teams hit 92%, these exception queues operated near saturation. According to Kingman’s formula for queueing systems ($W_q \approx \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$), waiting times explode non-linearly as utilization $\rho \to 1.0$. Consequently, rework cases stalled for weeks, pushing p95 duration out by 17.0 days to 62.0 days.

### 3.3 Fewer Handovers (34% -> 12%) vs. Lower Organizational Resilience
- **Mechanisms:** The handover-of-work network measures transfers of control between roles. Organizational resilience measures the system's ability to maintain throughput when individual resources are unavailable.
- **Explanation:** Replacing functional handoffs (validation $\to$ scheduling $\to$ survey $\to$ install $\to$ activation $\to$ billing) with a single installation coordinator successfully eliminated handoffs during standard execution. However, this transformed a distributed network with shared departmental queues into a star network centered on isolated individuals. Case context was trapped in individual coordinators' heads. When a coordinator was absent or overwhelmed (93% load), their assigned cases had no backup handler or shared buffer, causing those cases to halt completely. The baseline process had higher handover friction but greater operational redundancy.

### 3.4 Lower Visible Cost (€184 -> €161) vs. Higher Downstream / Rework Cost (€198 -> €207, Incomplete)
- **Mechanisms:** Visible cost captures upfront pre-complaint process activities. Total lifecycle cost incorporates failure loops, repeat truck rolls, manual activations, and customer dispute resolution.
- **Explanation:** Visible costs dropped by €23 because field surveys were slashed from 31% to 18% of cases (driving survey utilization down from 78% to 52%) and intake tasks were merged. However, this upfront saving created massive downstream expenses: failed visits rose from 13% to 19%, second-visit truck rolls rose from 11% to 18% (driving utilization to 92%), manual activations jumped from 7% to 15%, and 30-day complaints surged to 9.8%. A second truck roll and manual dispute handling cost significantly more than an upfront survey. Furthermore, because pilot cost tracking truncated complaint handling after week 4, the €207 figure substantially underestimates the true financial loss.

### 3.5 Improvement for Simple Houses vs. Deterioration for Apartments / Old Buildings
- **Mechanisms:** Process sensitivity to physical infrastructure variance.
- **Explanation:** Single-family homes feature standard drop connections, clear demarcation points, and minimal municipal permitting. Fast-tracking them via predictive rules and merged scheduling eliminates pure administrative waste. In contrast, apartments (MDUs) and older buildings require internal vertical riser access, shared conduit coordination, historical building approvals, and municipal street permits. Applying a predictive skip rule to these properties resulted in blind technician dispatches who encountered physical and legal blockages on site, causing FTR to plunge by 8pp in apartments and 11pp in old buildings, while p90 cycle times increased by +12.0 and +25.0 days.

Management should formally adopt **Alternative E (Segmented Hybrid Deployment with Conformance Guardrails)**, supported by immediate measurement deployment.

### 6.1 Immediate Action (Weeks 1–2)
1. **Deploy Conformance Gates (M7):** Implement automated workflow locks in the CRM/ERP:
   - *Gate A (Billing Integrity):* Prohibit `Billing start` execution until an automated `Activation test` returns a verified successful signal.
   - *Gate B (Compliance Integrity):* Prohibit `Installation visit` dispatch for older/permit-sensitive buildings without a verified `Permit check` completion timestamp.
2. **Implement Bifurcated Intake Routing:** Triage all new orders at Address Validation into two distinct operational tracks:
   - **Track 1 (Fast-Track R):** Simple Single-Family Houses $\to$ Merged Validation/Scheduling $\to$ Predictive Survey Skip $\to$ Coordinator Pod.
   - **Track 2 (Comprehensive Gated):** Apartments, Old Buildings, Business, and Rural $\to$ Mandatory Upfront Field Survey $\to$ Mandatory Permit Check $\to$ Pre-Activation Quality Gate.
3. **Transition to Coordinator Pods (M5):** Group installation coordinators into 3-person pods with shared digital work queues to eliminate single-point-of-failure case stalling and lower utilization from 93% to $\sim 78\%$.

### 6.2 What to Pause
- **Pause all survey skipping** on medium-risk apartments, older buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance orders.
- **Pause post-billing activation checks** across all customer segments without exception.
- **Pause isolated solo-coordinator assignments** in favor of cross-covering pod structures.

### 6.3 What to Scale
- **Scale Track 1 (Redesign R)** nationally for all verified Simple House / Low Building Risk orders (capturing €23 savings and 6-day cycle time reduction on 34%+ of national volume).
- **Scale telemetry instrumentation:** Implement structured failure reason codes (M3) and 60-day full lifecycle cost tracking (M4) across all operational regions.

### 6.4 What to Measure Next
- **M7 (Real-time Conformance Tracking):** Monitor move-on-log and move-on-model violations weekly.
- **M3 (Structured Failure Codes):** Track specific failure reasons for all second visits and failed installations.
- **M6 (Predictive Skip Calibration):** Back-test the building-risk algorithm against 2,000 completed Track 2 field surveys to evaluate if specific apartment sub-types can safely transition to Track 1.
- **M4 (60-Day Cost Accounting):** Measure total cost of ownership per segment through 60 days post-activation.

### 6.5 Thresholds and Evidence Justifying Broader Rollout
Before expanding survey skipping or fast-track scheduling to medium-risk apartments or rural segments, the operations team must achieve all of the following empirical thresholds over a rolling 6-week evaluation window:

1. **Quality Gate:** Track 2 First-Time-Right (FTR) installation rate reaches $\ge 85\%$ (up from 76%).
2. **Tail Latency Gate:** p90 cycle time in complex segments drops to $\le 25.0$ days, and p95 drops to $\le 40.0$ days (reversing the 62-day tail blowout).
3. **Customer Integrity Gate:** 30-day complaint rate drops to $\le 5.0\%$ overall, with exactly $0.0\%$ complaints regarding "billing before working service."
4. **Algorithmic Accuracy Gate (M6):** The predictive survey-skip model demonstrates a False Negative Rate (FNR) $\le 4.0\%$ on audit surveys.
5. **Capacity & Resilience Gate (M5):** Coordinator pod utilization stabilizes between $75\% - 82\%$ with zero cases stalling due to staff absence.
6. **Financial Gate (M4):** Fully loaded 60-day lifecycle cost per case is empirically verified to be $\le \text{EUR } 180$ on Track 1 and $\le \text{EUR } 195$ on Track 2.
