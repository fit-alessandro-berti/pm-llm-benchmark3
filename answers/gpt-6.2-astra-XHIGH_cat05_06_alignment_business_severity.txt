## 1. Materiality Framework

**Investigate CL-604 and CL-602 first.** In CL-604 the bank accepted EUR 90,000 before the recorded approval. In CL-602, EUR 480,000 was released one day after a beneficiary-bank change without substantiated fraud screening. CL-610 also warrants immediate beneficiary verification, despite a raw cost of zero. None of these amounts is an established loss.

| Severity | Business meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Critical. Funds have been released with strong evidence of a preventive control failure, or a high-consequence payment cannot be shown to have passed a required control. | Trace the funds and investigate while recovery is possible. |
| 4 | High. A paid claim used a prohibited approval route. Diversion risk, the missing applicable authorization, or recurrence warrants containment. | Contain the route and review affected payments promptly. |
| 3 | Material. A credible unresolved gap could affect entitlement, customer rights, or a wider population and needs a time-bound investigation. | Assign an owner and establish which outcomes were affected. |
| 2 | Limited. Independent evidence supports the business outcome, or a specific evidence, mapping, or communication question remains. | Verify the evidence and repair the record or process as appropriate. |
| 1 | Minimal on current evidence. Routing, technical events, or an appropriate terminal outcome explains the trace. | Preserve the underlying evidence and correct reporting if necessary. |

For each claim, identify the control objective and the decision it should have changed. Consider the amount at risk, beneficiary integrity, whether intervention was still possible at the time, prospects for recovery, and the effect on the claimant. An unauthorized transfer creates different financial and regulatory exposure from an unprovable notice that could impair a claimant's ability to respond. Recurrence raises the priority of a systemic fix. Model scope determines whether a missing step was owed in the first place.

**Confidence** describes the evidence for a particular interpretation. High confidence comes from an explicit rule applied to known facts or corroborating transaction records. Medium confidence means the interpretation is supported but linkage, meaning, or timing needs confirmation. Low confidence means the available evidence cannot resolve competing explanations. Poor evidence can itself be a control issue. A large paid claim can warrant urgent investigation even when confidence that screening was actually skipped is low.

Except where stated, those times were not supplied.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

With the supplied costs, raw cost is the number of visible L and M moves in the supplied alignment. S and T contribute zero. This is an edit distance conditional on the chosen model, the log abstraction, and the move prices. It has no term for euros, elapsed time, valid authority, evidence quality, customer harm, or recovery prospects.

- **Irreversibility and granularity.** CL-604 and CL-601 both cost 3. For CL-604, replay consumes the single early payment as L and supplies the expected later payment as M. A third edit accounts for the unmatched screen. The bank's acceptance before recorded approval matters more than the edit count. For CL-601, three UI records surround an eligible payment without altering its controls.
- **Identical price, different evidentiary weight.** CL-602 and CL-606 each cost 1. The first has a verbal account but no corroborating screen on a EUR 480,000 transfer to changed bank details. The second has a signed external assessment predating the checks. CL-608 also costs 1, yet the +3-hour portal record shifts the question from lateness to durable attribution. M means the aligned log lacks a match. External evidence determines what can be asserted about performance.
- **Invisible data conditions.** CL-605, CL-609, and CL-610 all cost 0. The hidden move in CL-605 is normal routing and the case meets P1. The same visible auto route passes replay for an over-limit, MEDIUM-risk claim and for a recent bank change. Eligibility requires historical attributes and a release-time decision test.
- **Event identity.** CL-603's extra notification is a transport attempt with the same message ID. In CL-611 a label substitution costs two edits even if the employee completed one full assessment. Correlation and semantic mapping change the representation on which distance is computed.
- **Scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 because the payable model insists on a continuation after a terminal event. The model's inserted payment has no evidential value as a debt to the claimant. Audit the legitimacy of closure and its communication under the correct scope.

In the twelve supplied cases, three payments have a P2 trigger and lack core evidence of the full required path before payment. Their face values sum to EUR 574,500 (CL-602, CL-604, and CL-610). Count each payment once even when several controls flag it. The four monthly auto exceptions have no supplied aggregate amount.

Business-specific move prices could help direct attention within a fixed model, but would still miss a fully synchronous prohibited decision. Retain raw cost for diagnosing structural fit. Prioritize action using the breached objective, case-specific exposure and timing, corroboration, and scope.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The replay below uses the observed events from the twelve examples. A fresh conversion can omit a routing tau or allow a different diagnostic path. For example, PM4Py can align CL-604 through the auto branch and consume the recorded payment there. All business findings and unit-cost aggregates below use the supplied alignments. The monthly pattern counts are separate observations.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

TREE = (
    "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
    "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
    "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
)
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
net, initial, final = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# Each token is move:activity. Expand the supplied alignment once so that
# observed traces contain only log-consuming moves (S and L).
labels = {
    "R": "Register Claim", "V": "Validate Coverage", "A": "Assess Loss",
    "F": "Fraud Screen", "M": "Manual Approve", "U": "Auto Approve",
    "P": "Pay Claim", "N": "Notify Claimant", "O": "Open Workbench",
    "B": "Autosave", "C": "Close Workbench", "D": "Deny Claim",
    "W": "Withdraw Claim", "G": "Assess Damage", "t": "tau",
}
specs = {
    "CL-601": "S:R S:V L:O S:A L:B S:U S:P S:N L:C",
    "CL-602": "S:R S:V S:A M:F S:M S:P S:N",
    "CL-603": "S:R S:V S:A S:U S:P S:N L:N",
    "CL-604": "S:R S:V S:A L:P M:F S:M M:P S:N",
    "CL-605": "S:R S:V S:A T:t S:U S:P S:N",
    "CL-606": "S:R S:V M:A S:F S:M S:P S:N",
    "CL-607": "S:R S:V L:D M:A M:U M:P S:N",
    "CL-608": "S:R S:V S:A S:F S:M S:P M:N",
    "CL-609": "S:R S:V S:A S:U S:P S:N",
    "CL-610": "S:R S:V S:A S:U S:P S:N",
    "CL-611": "S:R S:V L:G M:A S:F S:M S:P S:N",
    "CL-612": "S:R S:V S:A L:W M:U M:P M:N",
}
unit_cost = {"S": 0, "L": 1, "M": 1, "T": 0}
rows = []
for case, spec in specs.items():
    for position, token in enumerate(spec.split()):
        move, symbol = token.split(":")
        rows.append((case, position, move, labels[symbol], unit_cost[move]))
moves = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["case", "position", "move", "activity", "cost"])
observed = moves[moves["move"].isin(["S", "L"])].copy()
# Synthetic times preserve order only. Do not use these times to assess P2–P4.
observed["time"] = (pd.Timestamp("2025-01-01", tz="UTC")
                    + pd.to_timedelta(observed["position"], unit="s"))
formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    observed[["case", "activity", "time"]].copy(),
    case_id="case", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="time",
)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted)
diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, initial, final)
assert len(log) == len(diagnostics) == len(specs)
# PM4Py may select another optimal alignment, format moves differently, or
# scale move costs. Keep its native output independent of the supplied replay.
native = pd.DataFrame([
    {"case": trace.attributes["concept:name"], "pm4py_native_cost": result["cost"],
     "pm4py_alignment": result["alignment"]}
    for trace, result in zip(log, diagnostics)
])
print(native.to_string(index=False))

# None means unspecified. Severity and confidence are analyst judgments from
# section 2. Amount is claim face value, including for the withdrawn claim.
metadata = [
    ("CL-601", 1200, "LOW", False, 3, 1, "high", "UI telemetry"),
    ("CL-602", 480000, "HIGH", True, 1, 5, "high gap / low omission", "screen unverified"),
    ("CL-603", 3200, "LOW", False, 1, 1, "high retry", "transport retry"),
    ("CL-604", 90000, "MEDIUM", False, 3, 5, "high recorded order", "early payment"),
    ("CL-605", 800, "LOW", False, 0, 1, "high", "silent routing"),
    ("CL-606", 75000, "HIGH", None, 1, 2, "medium linkage", "external assessment"),
    ("CL-607", None, "UNKNOWN", None, 4, 1, "high denial / scope open", "closure scope"),
    ("CL-608", 250000, "HIGH", None, 1, 2, "medium linkage", "portal notice"),
    ("CL-609", 18000, "MEDIUM", False, 0, 4, "high", "ineligible auto"),
    ("CL-610", 4500, "LOW", True, 0, 4, "high exception", "ineligible auto"),
    ("CL-611", 62000, "HIGH", None, 2, 2, "medium equivalence", "label mapping"),
    ("CL-612", 2000, "LOW", None, 4, 2, "high withdrawal / notice open", "closure scope"),
]
cases = pd.DataFrame(metadata, columns=[
    "case", "amount_eur", "risk", "recent_bank_change", "listed_raw_cost",
    "business_severity", "confidence", "review_reason",
]).set_index("case")
cases["amount_eur"] = cases["amount_eur"].astype("Int64")
cases["recent_bank_change"] = cases["recent_bank_change"].astype("boolean")
cases["raw_cost"] = moves.groupby("case")["cost"].sum()
assert cases["raw_cost"].eq(cases["listed_raw_cost"]).all()

# One line per supplied move pattern; both distinct cases and multiplicity matter.
print(moves.groupby(["move", "activity"]).agg(
    occurrences=("case", "size"), distinct_cases=("case", "nunique"),
    cost_contribution=("cost", "sum"),
).to_string())

# Use known eligibility attributes and observed steps only. Unknown input
# values stay unknown unless another predicate settles the rule.
seen = observed.pivot(index="case", columns="position", values="activity")
auto = seen.eq("Auto Approve").any(axis=1)
paid = seen.eq("Pay Claim").any(axis=1)
risk = cases["risk"].astype("string").replace("UNKNOWN", pd.NA)
eligible = (cases["amount_eur"].le(5000) & risk.eq("LOW")
            & ~cases["recent_bank_change"])
manual_required = (cases["amount_eur"].gt(50000) | risk.eq("HIGH")
                   | cases["recent_bank_change"])

# First observed positions suffice for these twelve traces, each of which has
# at most one observed payment. Missing control positions fail the order test.
def first_position(activity):
    selected = observed.loc[observed["activity"].eq(activity)]
    return selected.groupby("case")["position"].min().reindex(cases.index)

screen = first_position("Fraud Screen")
approval = first_position("Manual Approve")
payment = first_position("Pay Claim")
manual_path_evidenced = screen.lt(approval) & approval.lt(payment)
cases["P1_ineligible_auto"] = auto & ~eligible
cases["P2_pre_payment_path_unverified"] = paid & manual_required & ~manual_path_evidenced
cases["P3_paid_before_recorded_manual"] = paid & payment.lt(approval)
# A prohibited auto decision can precede payment chronologically and still
# fail to authorize it. Keep that distinct from the observed CL-604 inversion.
cases["P3_paid_on_ineligible_auto"] = paid & cases["P1_ineligible_auto"]
# Qualitative flags below use the supplied cross-system and outcome evidence.
cases["P4_mutable_notice_link"] = cases.index.isin(["CL-608"])
cases["P4_closure_notice_duty_open"] = cases.index.isin(["CL-612"])

flag_cols = [c for c in cases.columns if c.startswith(("P1_", "P2_", "P3_", "P4_"))]
# Absence of a flag means no such finding on these data, not verified compliance.
flag_summary = []
for flag in flag_cols:
    affected = cases.loc[cases[flag].fillna(False)]
    flag_summary.append({
        "flag": flag, "flagged_cases": len(affected),
        "unknown_flags": cases[flag].isna().sum(),
        "cases_with_known_amount": affected["amount_eur"].count(),
        "known_face_value_eur": affected["amount_eur"].sum(min_count=1),
    })
print(pd.DataFrame(flag_summary).to_string(index=False))

# Aggregate from one row per claim to avoid multiplying euros by move count.
# These descriptive sums are neither losses nor additive across policy flags.
for grouping in ["review_reason", "risk", "raw_cost"]:
    print(cases.groupby(grouping).agg(
        cases=("business_severity", "size"),
        known_amounts=("amount_eur", "count"),
        known_face_value_eur=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.sum(min_count=1)),
        total_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
    ).to_string())

evidence = cases.reset_index().sort_values(
    ["business_severity", "case"], ascending=[False, True],
)
print(evidence[["case", "business_severity", "confidence", "review_reason",
                "raw_cost", "amount_eur", "risk", "recent_bank_change"]
               + flag_cols].to_string(index=False))
```
