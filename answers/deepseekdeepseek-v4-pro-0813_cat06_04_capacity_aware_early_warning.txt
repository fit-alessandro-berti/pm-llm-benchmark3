## 1. Decision rule

No single breach-probability threshold is sufficient. The deployable rule is a multi-stage gate:

1. **Stage eligibility**  
   Intervene only in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. Once `Diagnosis Complete` occurs, swarm is not used regardless of risk.

2. **Alert validity / persistence**  
   - First alert is eligible.  
   - Repeated alerts are eligible only with materially new information: stage change, new diagnostic log, new customer contact, repaired missing data, or an absolute adjusted-risk increase of at least 0.08 since the last alert.  
   - Unchanged repeated alerts are suppressed into cooldown; they are not themselves a reason to swarm.

3. **Calibration robustification**  
   Convert `p_now` into a planning interval, not a point:
   - SME: add 0.04–0.09 to reported risk.
   - Strategic Enterprise: subtract 0.05–0.12 due overprediction.
   - Public Sector: use reported risk with normal monitoring uncertainty.
   - Non-Native-Language Support: treat as sparse; widen intervals and use conservative effect assumptions.

4. **Direct benefit gate**  
   Compute:
   ```text
   direct benefit range  ARR_range × value of avoiding breach  swarm cost  false-alarm burden
   ```
   A case is priority-eligible only if the lower-bound direct benefit is positive, unless a fairness/safety override is approved by a named human.

5. **Spillover-aware prioritization**  
   Estimate spillover harm per used slot:
   ```text
   spillover per slot  6 × [0.01, 0.03] × average value of untreated diagnosis-stage case
   ```
   Using a base average value of €15,000, this gives approximately €900–€2,700 per slot. Rank eligible cases by midpoint direct benefit minus midpoint spillover charge, with additional penalty for:
   - imputed or missing data,
   - sparse calibration segments,
   - repeated unchanged alerts,
   - high false-alarm burden relative to expected benefit.

6. **Capacity and reserve policy**  
   Reserve one slot today for the possible regulatory incident before 15:00 unless the current portfolio deterioration is severe and a named owner overrides.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

### Cool-down and suppression
- After an alert, the case enters a **120-minute cooldown** unless a material event occurs earlier.
- During cooldown, automated re-alerts are suppressed unless:
  - adjusted risk rises by 0.08 absolute,
  - stage changes,
  - new diagnostic/customer data arrive,
  - missing data are repaired,
  - a human reviewer confirms a material change.
- A second swarm on the same case is normally forbidden unless a materially new event occurred after the first swarm.

### Escalation
- Repeated unchanged alerts do not automatically escalate to swarm.
- After two unchanged alerts, the case is routed to human review.
- If the case still appears high-risk after review and capacity exists, it may be placed on a watchlist and re-evaluated at the next refresh.

### Re-entry logic
A new material event resets the alert count and cooldown. Examples:
- new customer log,
- stage change,
- new contact from customer,
- repaired live-queue or other missing feature,
- large adjusted-risk increase.

### Explicit comparison: I-05 vs I-07
- **I-05**: third alert, no new event since prior alert, `p_prev` 0.55  `p_now` 0.57. This is the canonical case for suppression. The risk may be somewhat higher after SME adjustment, but there is no new information and the expected net is not strong enough to justify swarm. It enters cooldown and human review.
- **I-07**: third alert, but a new customer diagnostic log arrived. This is materially new information even though the point estimate fell slightly from 0.75 to 0.72. The alert is valid and it may re-enter the priority queue. However, with current capacity and spillover, it is not selected because expected net is negative. It should be monitored closely and reconsidered if a slot frees or risk climbs.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

### Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases
Risk: a pure expected-monetary-value rule will systematically favor Strategic Enterprise and high-value cases.

Safeguards:
- Track intervention rates by value band and risk band.
- If low-value/high-risk cases receive zero interventions while similarly high-risk high-value cases receive repeated interventions, conduct a fairness review.
- Use a fairness override that allows a lower-value case to receive a slot if:
  - lower-bound direct benefit is positive, and
  - exclusion is likely to create non-monetary harm or contractual/regulatory impact.
- In the current portfolio, I-01 and I-07 are not ignored permanently; they are waitlisted and reviewed at the next material change.

### Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support
Risk: small sample size means both calibration and intervention-effect estimates are unstable.

Safeguards:
- Widen confidence intervals.
- Require language-aware human review before final intervention.
- Maintain a separate monitoring subsegment for Non-Native-Language Support.
- Do not extrapolate intervention effects from the majority-language population.
- I-09 is selected despite this uncertainty, but with conservative gating and specific review.

### Differential calibration across segments
Risk: SME underprediction, Strategic overprediction, and Public Sector moderate sample size could bias decisions.

Safeguards:
- Use segment-specific risk intervals in all dashboards and decision rules.
- Recalibrate segment models monthly or when drift is detected.
- Do not use raw `p_now` as a single threshold.

### Selective intervention changing future training labels
Risk: if swarms prevent breaches in selected cases, future models may learn that those high-risk cases “do not breach,” leading to underprediction.

Safeguards:
- Log every intervention flag with outcome data.
- Include `intervention_received` in training data.
- Prefer uplift models or causal-effect models over naive outcome models.
- Maintain untreated comparison groups where ethically and operationally possible, or use natural experiments from capacity constraints.
- Retrain on features available at alert time, not post-intervention outcomes.

### Spillover harms falling on untreated groups
Risk: using slots on high-value cases may delay lower-value, SME, Public Sector, or already-congested diagnosis-stage cases.

Safeguards:
- Charge each slot an explicit spillover cost before prioritization.
- Track untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates by segment after intervention-heavy windows.
- If spillover harm is consistently concentrated on lower-value/public/SME cases, reduce maximum slots or create protected capacity.
- Cap same-segment slot concentration unless a named override is granted. For example, no more than two of four slots per window should normally go to Strategic Enterprise.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

### Operational monitoring
- Track slot utilization, reserved-slot availability, time from alert to intervention, repeat-alert counts, cooldown breaches, and stage at intervention.
- Track false-alarm burden incurred by selected cases.
- Track time from swarm to diagnosis completion or next stage change.

### Predictive monitoring
- Compare predicted risk intervals with realized breach outcomes by segment.
- Monitor whether SME remains underpredicted by 0.04–0.09, Strategic overpredicted by 0.05–0.12, and Public Sector calibration remains acceptable.
- Recalibrate when segment bias drifts beyond these bounds or when a segment contains enough new cases for stable estimates.

### Causal-effect monitoring
- Maintain an intervention log containing:
  - segment,
  - stage,
  - risk prediction,
  - data-quality flags,
  - intervention given or not,
  - outcome.
- Estimate intervention effects using:
  - randomized or cluster-randomized slot allocation where ethical,
  - natural experiments from capacity constraints,
  - propensity score matching,
  - uplift modeling.
- Update ARR ranges by stage, segment, risk band, and data-quality status.
- If observed ARR falls below the lower bound currently used for gating, revise the selection rule.

### Capacity and spillover monitoring
- Measure aggregate diagnosis-queue delay per used slot.
- Estimate empirical spillover: compare untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates in intervention-heavy versus intervention-light windows.
- If observed spillover per slot exceeds roughly €2,500 or 0.03 additional breach probability for each of six untreated cases, reduce the number of slots allocated per window.

### Fairness monitoring
- Track intervention rate by segment, value band, language support, and sector.
- Track breach outcomes and spillover harm by segment.
- Track false-alarm burden distribution.
- Trigger a fairness review if:
  - low-value/high-risk cases are systematically excluded,
  - Non-Native-Language Support outcomes are materially worse or more volatile,
  - untreated groups experience disproportionate spillover harm.

### When to change the threshold or allocation rule
Trigger a policy review if:
- base-scenario net portfolio impact turns negative,
- adverse scenarios occur repeatedly,
- calibration drifts beyond known bounds,
- empirical spillover is higher than the current charge,
- fairness triggers fire,
- reserved capacity is consistently unused or insufficient,
- operational false-alarm rate exceeds roughly 30%.

At that point, update:
- segment-specific probability adjustments,
- ARR ranges,
- spillover charge per slot,
- maximum slots per window,
- reserve policy,
- data-quality gates,
- fairness overrides.
