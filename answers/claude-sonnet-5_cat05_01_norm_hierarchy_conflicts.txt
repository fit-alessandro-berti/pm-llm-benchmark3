## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts vs. interpretation.** The amounts, timestamps, actors, and document text in the scenario are treated as stipulated facts. Everything else below — which source governs which issue, whether ECD2 is an A7 exception, whether an ERP timestamp or external evidence controls a given case — is my interpretation, clearly labeled, not a given fact.

**Source authority.** I reject a single total ranking ("global beats local beats directive beats email") in favor of an *issue-specific* hierarchy. GPIP4 is board-approved and global, but it is not self-executing on operational detail; the SOP and ECD2 are more specific in time, place, and factual trigger. My working rule: **a lower-tier instrument may govern an issue only within the scope it is textually granted, and never an issue GPIP4 reserves to a named authority (the CRO) unless that authority acted.** Recency alone does not confer authority — a later, more urgent, more emphatically worded document (the CFO email) does not out-rank a control it has no delegated power to touch (D3 explicitly limits the CFO to Finance-control interpretation).

**ECD-2's status.** GPIP4 leaves open whether a COO/IC-signed directive counts as an A7 exception (A7 last sentence). I resolve this using: (a) the organizational fact that formal GPIP exceptions are "reserved to the CRO"; (b) the CRO's non-availability and non-ratification; (c) ECD2's own self-limiting language (C4: precedence over "ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements" only); (d) C5's explicit silence on segregation of duties, Finance, Compliance, the €100k threshold, and the CRO. **Interpretation adopted: ECD2 is a valid, real, but narrow-scope operational directive — it overrides PO/commit sequencing and the approved-supplier register, but it is not an A7 exception and does not touch segregation of duties (A1) or Compliance_Clearance (A3).** This is a contestable interpretive choice, not a fact; the alternative (broad overlay) is discussed in Section 5 where relevant.

**The CFO email.** D1–D4 are treated as a legitimate, narrow delegation *within the Finance domain only* (D3), time-bound to the ECD2 window, capped at €150,000, and expressly disclaiming any effect on "essential integrity controls" (D4). It can substitute for the *substance* of Finance-controller sign-off but cannot substitute for Compliance_Clearance or cure a segregation-of-duties problem.

**Emergency necessity.** Necessity is treated as a *factual predicate* that must independently be shown to exist at the time of the specific act (SOP's six-hour/imminent-harm test; ECD2's "critical … lot" test), not as a general atmosphere that legitimizes any deviation once one emergency exists somewhere in the plant. Necessity can justify the specific things a rule enumerates (e.g., commit-before-PO); it does not create authority for an undelegated actor or waive a control no source purports to waive.
