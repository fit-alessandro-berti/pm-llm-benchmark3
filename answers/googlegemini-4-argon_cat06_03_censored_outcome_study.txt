## 1. Target definition

The fundamental statistical obstacle in this request is **sample selection and counterfactual missingness** (often termed *reject inference* in credit risk). The business asks to predict 90-day default from the first few events of *every* application. However, `default_90d` is physically impossible to observe—and conceptually undefined without an intervention—unless an application is eventually accepted, signed, and activated. Treating rejected or withdrawn applications as "non-default" conflates creditworthiness with credit denial or applicant self-selection, inducing severe selection bias.

### Primary Formulation: Competing-Risk / Multi-State Operational Target for All Applications
To provide a valid early-warning model at the start of the process for *every* application without relying on untestable counterfactual assumptions right out of the gate, we decompose the process into an observable two-stage or multi-state operational target:

*   **Unit of analysis:** An individual credit application (`case:concept:name`), evaluated at a specific prefix landmark $t_p$.
*   **Population:** All started applications that reach the landmark event (e.g., first arrival at `Credit Review`) and remain active at $t_p$.
*   **Prediction time ($t_p$):** Immediately upon completion of the first `Credit Review` event (typically prefix length $k=3$: `Application Started` $\to$ `Identity/KYC Check` $\to$ `Credit Review`).
*   **Horizon:** 
    *   *Stage 1 (Process Disposition):* Time from $t_p$ to operational resolution within $\tau_{\text{proc}} = 60\text{ days}$, categorized into mutually exclusive competing states: $S \in \{\text{Activated}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}\}$, subject to right censoring if still open.
    *   *Stage 2 (Conditional Default):* Exactly $\tau_{\text{def}} = 90\text{ days}$ post-`Account Activated` for the subset where $S = \text{Activated}$.
*   **Primary Estimand:** We jointly estimate two observable quantities given the prefix history $\mathcal{H}(t_p)$:
    1.  **The Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF) of process disposition:** $\Pr(T_{\text{proc}} \le \tau_{\text{proc}}, S = s \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p))$ for each disposition $s$.
    2.  **The Factual Conditional Default Risk:** $\Pr(Y_{90} = 1 \mid S = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{H}(t_p))$, adjusted for right-censoring and missing outcome feeds via Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW).
    
    *Operational Synthesis:* Combining these yields the **factual joint probability of an application activating and defaulting under the prevailing policy**: 
    $$\Pr(S = \text{Activated}, Y_{90} = 1 \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)) = \Pr(S = \text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)) \times \Pr(Y_{90} = 1 \mid S = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{H}(t_p))$$
    This gives operations both a funnel forecast (who will activate vs. drop out) and a conditional risk score for those on track to activate.

### Alternative Formulations and Why They Answer Different Questions

1.  **Alternative A: Counterfactual Potential Outcome under Mandatory Activation, $\Pr(Y_{90}^{\text{do}(S=\text{Activated})} = 1 \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p))$**
    *   *What it answers:* "If we were to override the underwriting policy and force-activate this specific application, what is the probability it would default within 90 days?"
    *   *Why it differs:* This is the true causal estimand required if the model is used to *replace* or *modify* the acceptance policy (underwriting). However, because credit officers reject applicants based on unobserved factors (e.g., manual document inspection notes not captured in the event log) and applicants self-withdraw based on external offers, activation is non-ignorable (Missing Not At Random / MNAR). Estimating this requires Inverse Probability of Selection Weighting (IPSW) or Heckman-style selection models, which fail if deterministic policy cutoffs create regions of zero positivity (where $\Pr(S=\text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)) = 0$).
2.  **Alternative B: Continuous Time-to-Default After Activation, $S(t \mid \mathcal{H}(t_{\text{act}})) = \Pr(T_{\text{def}} > t \mid S=\text{Activated}, \mathcal{H}(t_{\text{act}}))$**
    *   *What it answers:* "Once an account is activated, what is its hazard rate of default over time?"
    *   *Why it differs:* This moves the prediction point $t_p$ forward to `Account Activated` ($t_{\text{act}}$). It is highly useful for post-origination portfolio provisioning and early collections routing, and naturally incorporates accounts with $<90$ days of follow-up via survival analysis (Cox PH or Random Survival Forests). However, it fails the business request to act as an early warning during the *first few events* of the application.

## 2. Cohort construction

To prevent look-ahead bias, cohort membership and label maturity must be constructed strictly **as of** a specific evaluation cutoff date $t_{\text{eval}}$ (for the full extract, $t_{\text{eval}} = \text{2026-02-15}$).

### Explicit Rules
*   **Entry (Inclusion) Rule:** All unique applications (`case:concept:name`) with an `Application Started` timestamp between `2024-01-01 00:00:00` and `2025-12-31 23:59:59`.
*   **As-Of Observation Rule:** When evaluating at cutoff $t_{\text{eval}}$, any event with `time:timestamp` $> t_{\text{eval}}$ is masked. A label `default_90d` is treated as known at $t_{\text{eval}}$ **if and only if** both $t_{\text{act}} + 90\text{ days} \le t_{\text{eval}}$ AND `label_available_at` $\le t_{\text{eval}}$.
*   **Exclusion Rules:** No applications are excluded from the *process* cohort ($N=48,000$). However, for the *Stage 2 Conditional Default* model, non-activated cases (Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, Open) are structurally excluded from the outcome denominator (or modeled only to compute selection weights), while activated cases without mature labels are treated as right-censored or missing-data records rather than silently dropped.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

The 26,400 omitted cases represent five distinct missingness mechanisms that require tailored statistical handling:

1.  **Ordinary Pre-Activation Right Censoring (5,100 still-open cases):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Applications started late in 2025 (or stuck in long document loops) have not reached a terminal decision by `2026-02-15`.
    *   *Handling:* In Stage 1, these are standard right-censored observations at censoring time $C_i = t_{\text{eval}} - t_{p,i}$. We estimate Stage 1 probabilities using an Aalen-Johansen estimator or fine-and-gray competing risks regression, which naturally accounts for right censoring under independent censoring conditional on covariates.
2.  **Post-Activation Administrative Censoring & Delayed Labels (Subset of the 3,600 activated cases):**
    *   *Mechanism:* An account activated on `2025-12-01` reaches its 90-day horizon on `2026-03-01` (after the `2026-02-15` extraction). Furthermore, an account activated on `2025-11-01` reaches day 90 on `2026-01-30`, but because the external outcome feed takes 7–35 days (`label_available_at`), its outcome has not arrived by `2026-02-15`.
    *   *Handling:* Because the maximum feed delay is 35 days, any account activated after $\text{2026-02-15} - (90 + 35)\text{ days} = \text{2025-10-13}$ is subject to administrative horizon/feed censoring. Dropping them biases the sample toward fast-activating cohorts.
3.  **Permanently Missing Outcome Feeds (Remaining subset of the 3,600 activated cases):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Partner-originated (`case:channel == 'Partner'`) activated accounts older than `2025-10-13` that still lack `default_90d` have experienced a permanent feed failure.
    *   *Handling:* This is Missing At Random (MAR) if missingness depends only on observed `case:channel`, `source_system`, and activation timing, or MNAR if partners selectively fail to report defaults.
4.  **Selection Through Activation (9,100 Rejected + 5,700 Withdrawn):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Underwriting policy filters out high-risk cases (`Rejected`), while market competition filters out price-sensitive/low-risk cases (`Withdrawn`).
    *   *Handling:* Treated as competing risks in Stage 1. In Stage 2, our primary target conditions on $S=\text{Activated}$ explicitly.
5.  **Transfer as a Competing Event / Partial Linkage (2,900 Transferred cases):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Cases routed to a specialist platform. A linkage table exists for ~60% ($\approx 1,740$ cases), while 40% ($\approx 1,160$ cases) vanish.
    *   *Handling:* In the primary analysis, `Transferred` is treated as a distinct Stage 1 competing operational outcome on the main platform, because specialist platforms have different contract terms, credit limits, and servicing rules (violating consistency if pooled naively).

### Primary Analysis Strategy: IPCW-Adjusted Two-Stage Modeling
For Stage 2 (predicting $Y_{90}$ among the $21,600 + 3,600 = 25,200$ activated accounts), let $R_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether `default_90d` is observed by $t_{\text{eval}}$ (`label_available_at` $\le t_{\text{eval}}$).
1.  Estimate the observation probability $\hat{\pi}_i = \Pr(R_i = 1 \mid S_i = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{H}_i(t_p), t_{\text{act}, i}, \text{channel}_i, \text{source\_system}_i)$ across all 25,200 activated cases using a regularized logistic or survival model.
2.  Fit the Stage 2 classifier on the 21,600 complete cases weighted by stabilized Inverse Probability of Censoring Weights:
    $$w_i = \frac{\Pr(R_i = 1 \mid S_i = \text{Activated})}{\hat{\pi}_i}$$
    (Alternatively, enforce an administrative buffer excluding activations after `2025-10-13` from training, and apply IPCW specifically to correct for the partner-channel missing feed among pre-October activations).

### Sensitivity Analyses
*   **Sensitivity 1 (Counterfactual / Reject Inference via IPSW):** Estimate $\hat{p}_{\text{act}}(\mathcal{H}(t_p)) = \Pr(S = \text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p))$ using the Stage 1 model. Re-weight the Stage 2 model by $w_i^{\text{total}} = (\hat{p}_{\text{act}, i} \cdot \hat{\pi}_i)^{-1}$ with weight trimming at the 99th percentile. Compare the resulting risk shifts to assess how severely underwriting selection distorts raw prefix associations.
*   **Sensitivity 2 (Transfer Linkage & Manski Bounds):** For the 60% of linked transfers that activated on the specialist platform, incorporate their outcomes and re-weight by $1 / 0.60$ conditional on transfer covariates. For the permanently missing partner feeds, compute **Manski worst-case/best-case bounds** by imputing all missing partner outcomes as $Y_{90}=0$ (lower bound) and $Y_{90}=1$ (upper bound).

### Testability of Assumptions
*   **Testable:** *Positivity / Overlap* ($\Pr(S=\text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)) > 0$ and $\Pr(R=1 \mid \dots) > 0$) is directly testable by inspecting empirical propensity distributions across `risk_score` and `case:channel`. *Feed delay distribution* ($P(\text{label\_available\_at} - (t_{\text{act}} + 90\text{d}))$) is directly observable among the 21,600 labeled cases.
*   **Partially Testable:** *Independent pre-activation censoring* and *MAR for partner feeds* can be stress-tested by checking whether observable early delinquency indicators (if any exist prior to feed cutoff) or prefix risk scores differ between partner cases with and without missing feeds, or by tracking late-arriving feeds in subsequent data extracts.
*   **Fundamentally Untestable:** *Unconfounded Selection (Ignorability of Activation)*—i.e., $Y_{90}^{\text{do}(\text{Act})} \perp S \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)$—and *MNAR non-response* (that missing partner feeds have identical default rates to observed partner feeds with identical covariates) cannot be verified without outcomes on rejected/unreported accounts (e.g., via a randomized "approve-all" holdout sample).

## 4. Prefix extraction

To generate features without look-ahead leakage, prefixes must be truncated strictly prior to any terminal decision (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`) or post-decision event.

### 1. Activity Canonicalization (Handling the 15 October 2025 Migration)
Before extracting prefixes, we must standardize the schema across the `2025-10-15` migration boundary:
*   **Activity Mapping:** Apply a deterministic dictionary mapping post-migration `concept:name` values back to canonical pre-migration names (e.g., `'KYC_Verification_v2' -> 'Identity/KYC Check'`, `'Underwriting_Review' -> 'Credit Review'`, `'Doc_Request' -> 'Request Documents'`).
*   **Deduplication:** Migration bugs caused occasional duplicate events. We sort events by `(case:concept:name, time:timestamp)` and collapse consecutive identical canonical activities occurring within a short technical threshold (e.g., $\Delta t \le 5\text{ seconds}$) when resource and payload attributes are identical, retaining the first timestamp.

### 2. Fixed Event-Count Prefixes vs. Elapsed-Time Prefixes
*   **Fixed Event-Count ($k$-prefixes):** Truncates a trace to its first $k$ canonical events: $\sigma_{\le k} = \langle e_1, \dots, e_k \rangle$, with prediction time $t_p = t(e_k)$.
    *   *Pros/Cons:* Aligns all cases at the same logical control-flow stage (e.g., $k=3$ aligns with the completion of the first `Credit Review`). However, calendar time elapsed varies wildly across cases.
*   **Elapsed-Time ($d$-prefixes):** Truncates a trace to all events occurring within $d$ hours/days of `Application Started`: $\sigma_{\le d} = \{e_j \in \sigma : t(e_j) \le t(e_1) + d\}$, with prediction time $t_p = t(e_1) + d$.
    *   *Pros/Cons:* Provides a uniform temporal horizon (e.g., "predict risk 24 hours after application start") and naturally captures stalls/waiting time even when no event fires.
*   **Study Choice:** We use **landmark/event-count prefixes** at $k \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ (anchored on `Identity/KYC Check` and subsequent `Credit Review` iterations) as our primary design for operational routing, supplemented by a **24-hour elapsed-time prefix** for SLA monitoring.

### 3. Handling Short Cases and Repeated Document Loops
*   **Short Cases (Early Termination):** If a case terminates *at or before* prefix length $k$ (e.g., rejected at $e_2$ during `Identity/KYC Check`, or withdrawn before $k=3$), it **must not** be padded or included in the prediction cohort at length $k$. At prediction time $t_p = t(e_k)$, that case is already closed. In multi-prefix training, a case contributes a sample at length $k$ *if and only if* it has $\ge k$ events and $e_1, \dots, e_k$ contain no terminal/post-decision activities.
*   **Repeated Document Loops:** A case looping `Credit Review -> Request Documents -> Credit Review` 4 times will reach $k=5$ while still in underwriting, whereas a fast-tracked case would hit `Accepted` at $e_4$. By filtering out cases that have already reached a decision prior to $k$, longer prefixes ($k=5, 7$) naturally specialize in evaluating complex, looping applications. Rather than flattening loops, we preserve them to extract loop-count and rework duration features.

## 5. Process-aware features

All features are computed strictly as of the prefix timestamp $t_p = t(e_k)$. We categorize them into three distinct scopes:

### 1. Static Case Attributes (Available at $e_1$: `Application Started`)
*   `case:channel`: Categorical (Direct, Partner, Broker, Online).
*   `case:region`: Geographic region (one-hot or target-encoded using out-of-fold historical data).
*   `case:requested_amount`: Numeric requested loan/credit limit; also log-transformed $\log(1 + \text{amount})$.

### 2. Prefix-Derived Intra-Case Features (Computed from $\sigma_{\le k}$ up to $t_p$)
*   **Control-Flow & Rework Features:**
    *   `current_activity`: Canonical activity name at $e_k$.
    *   `doc_loop_count`: Number of occurrences of `Request Documents` in $\sigma_{\le k}$.
    *   `credit_review_count`: Number of occurrences of `Credit Review` in $\sigma_{\le k}$.
    *   `is_rework`: Binary indicator of whether any activity has appeared $>1$ time in $\sigma_{\le k}$.
*   **Timing & Bottleneck Features:**
    *   `elapsed_time_total`: $t(e_k) - t(e_1)$ in hours.
    *   `time_to_kyc`: $t(\text{first KYC}) - t(e_1)$.
    *   `last_step_duration`: $t(e_k) - t(e_{k-1})$.
    *   `max_inter_event_gap`: $\max_{2 \le j \le k} (t(e_j) - t(e_{j-1}))$, capturing applicant hesitation or document gathering delays.
    *   `doc_turnaround_time_mean`: Average time elapsed between `Request Documents` and the subsequent `Credit Review` within $\sigma_{\le k}$.
*   **Dynamic Risk & Resource Handover Features:**
    *   `risk_score_initial`: Value of `risk_score` at $e_1$ (or first non-null in $\sigma_{\le k}$).
    *   `risk_score_latest`: Value of `risk_score` at $e_k$ (strictly last known value at or before $t_p$).
    *   `risk_score_delta`: $\text{risk\_score\_latest} - \text{risk\_score\_initial}$ (captures deterioration discovered during KYC/review).
    *   `unique_resources_count`: Number of distinct `org:resource` IDs in $\sigma_{\le k}$.
    *   `resource_handover_ratio`: $\text{unique\_resources\_count} / k$.

### 3. Inter-Case, Queue & Historical Reference Features (Computed using strictly prior history $\le t_p$)
*   **Cohort & Calendar Features:**
    *   `hour_of_day`, `day_of_week`, `is_weekend` of $t(e_1)$ and $t_p$.
    *   `post_policy_change_flag`: Binary indicator $\mathbb{I}(t(e_1) \ge \text{2025-07-01})$ to allow the model to adapt its intercept/interactions across the policy shift.
*   **Resource & Queue Congestion (Reconstructed As-Of $t_p$):**
    *   `active_wip_at_tp`: Count of *other* cases in the event log that started before $t_p$ and had not yet reached a terminal state before $t_p$ (measures system-wide queue congestion).
    *   `resource_cumulative_cases_at_tp`: Count of historical events handled by `org:resource(e_k)` strictly within $[t_{\text{start of log}}, t_p)$ (replaces the leaked extraction-time resource experience).
    *   `resource_recent_workload_24h`: Count of events processed by `org:resource(e_k)` in $[t_p - 24\text{h}, t_p)$.

| **Resource experience** (static warehouse field) | **Retrospective / Survival Leakage:** Reflects tenure/volume at `2026-02-15`. An employee who stayed through 2026 or processed many cases *after* $t_p$ looks artificially experienced in 2024. | Cannot use the static extraction-time value. Must reconstruct dynamically using events with $t(e) < t_p$. | **Repair (Dynamic cumulative count)** | Drop the static field; replace with `resource_cumulative_cases_at_tp` computed strictly from historical events prior to $t_p$. |
| `default_90d` & `label_available_at` | **Hindsight & Label Maturity Bias:** `default_90d` is repeated across early events in the raw CSV and reflects knowledge that only arrived at `label_available_at`. | In backtesting at cutoff $t_{\text{eval}}$, `default_90d` is observable iff $t_{\text{act}} + 90\text{d} \le t_{\text{eval}}$ AND `label_available_at` $\le t_{\text{eval}}$. | **Repair (Mask on events; enforce maturity gate)** | Strip `default_90d` from all event rows before prefixing; gate training/validation targets strictly by `label_available_at` $\le t_{\text{eval}}$. |

## 7. Validation design

### Why a Random Case Split is Misleading Here
A random train/test split (such as a naive call to `pm4py.split_train_test`) is statistically invalid for this study for four reasons:
1.  **Temporal Regime & Migration Leakage:** It places cases from late 2025 (post-`2025-07-01` policy change and post-`2025-10-15` system migration) into the training set to predict early 2024/2025 cases, leaking future base rates, underwriting rules, and activity distributions.
2.  **Inter-Case Feature Leakage:** Queue congestion (`active_wip_at_tp`) and dynamic resource experience (`resource_cumulative_cases_at_tp`) link concurrent cases. A random split leaks test-set trajectory information into training features.
3.  **Hindsight Label Leakage:** A random split evaluates models on cases whose 90-day window and 7–35 day feed delay matured after the nominal training cutoff, ignoring the operational reality of delayed feedback.
4.  **Optimistic Variance:** It masks temporal concept drift, yielding artificially tight confidence intervals and overconfident probability calibration.

### Realistic Temporal Validation & Rolling Backtesting Scheme
We implement an **as-of temporal split with a label-maturity purge buffer** based on case start time ($t_0$) and evaluation cutoff dates ($t_{\text{eval}}$). Note that for an activated account's label to be fully mature without feed-delay bias, approximately $90\text{d (horizon)} + 35\text{d (max feed delay)} + 15\text{d (activation time)} \approx 140\text{ days}$ must elapse from application start.

*   **Split 1: Pre-Policy vs. Post-Policy Regime Backtest (Stress-Testing the `2025-07-01` Shift)**
    *   *Train Window:* Cases starting `2024-01-01` to `2025-02-10`, evaluated strictly **as of $t_{\text{eval, train}} = \text{2025-06-30}$**. (All labels observed only after `2025-06-30` are masked/censored during training).
    *   *Purge / Buffer Zone:* Cases starting `2025-02-11` to `2025-06-30` whose outcomes mature *after* `2025-06-30` are treated as right-censored at `2025-06-30` for IPCW estimation (preventing look-ahead across the policy boundary).
    *   *Validation Window (Post-Policy):* Cases starting `2025-07-01` to `2025-08-15`, evaluated as of `2025-12-31`. This tests out-of-time generalization across the credit-policy change.
*   **Split 2: Final Out-of-Time Test Set (Covering Post-Migration Regime)**
    *   *Retrain / Validation Update:* Expand training/tuning up to `2025-07-01`–`2025-08-31` ( incorporate early post-policy data with a higher sample weight or domain-adaptation layer).
    *   *Test Window:* Cases starting `2025-09-01` to `2025-10-31` (spanning the `2025-10-15` system migration), evaluated as of extraction date `2026-02-15`. Cases starting in November–December 2025 cannot have matured 90-day default labels by `2026-02-15`; they are evaluated **only on Stage 1 (disposition / activation within 30 days)** and used to estimate IPCW weights.

### Evaluation Metrics, Calibration, and Subgroup Auditing
Because of right-censoring and missing partner feeds, standard unweighted metrics on complete cases are biased. All Stage 2 metrics on the test set must be **IPCW-adjusted**:
*   **Discrimination:**
    *   *Stage 2 Conditional Default:* **IPCW-weighted ROC-AUC** and **IPCW-weighted PR-AUC** (critical given the 12.5% base default rate: $2,700 / 21,600$).
    *   *Stage 1 Multi-State:* Cause-specific **Time-dependent Concordance Index ($C$-index)** at $\tau_{\text{proc}} = 30\text{ and }60\text{ days}$.
*   **Proper Scoring & Calibration:**
    *   **IPCW-adjusted Brier Score:** $\text{BS}_{\text{IPCW}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{act}}} \sum_{i \in \text{Act}} R_i \hat{w}_i (Y_{90, i} - \hat{p}_i)^2$.
    *   **Expected Calibration Error (ECE) & Reliability Diagrams:** Plotted separately pre- and post-`2025-07-01`. Post-policy shifts require **recalibration** (e.g., Platt scaling or isotonic regression on the most recent matured rolling window) before deployment.
*   **Subgroup & Distribution Shift Audits:**
    *   Report metrics stratified by `case:channel` (especially `Partner` vs. `Direct`), `case:region`, prefix length $k \in \{2, 3, 5\}$, and pre/post-`2025-10-15` migration to verify that activity canonicalization eliminated migration drift (monitored via Population Stability Index, PSI, on prefix features).

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### What This Study Can and Cannot Claim
*   **Predictive Association vs. Selection Effects:** The Stage 2 model estimates $\Pr(Y_{90} = 1 \mid S = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{H}(t_p))$—the risk of default **given that an application passes the existing underwriting policy and the applicant signs the contract**. If a feature (e.g., high `doc_loop_count`) is associated with lower default among activated accounts, that may be a *selection effect*: credit officers only approve multi-loop applicants when they provide exceptional collateral documentation.
*   **Absence of Counterfactual Validity for Policy Replacement:** The study **cannot** claim that rejected applications with low predicted conditional risk $\hat{p}$ would have had low default rates had they been approved. Where the historical policy enforced hard cutoffs (deterministic rejection), positivity is violated ($\Pr(S=\text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{H}(t_p)) = 0$), making counterfactual risk fundamentally unidentifiable from observational data alone.
*   **Valid Operational Decision-Use Boundary:**
    *   *Safe Uses:* Early operational triage (routing complex high-risk prefixes to senior underwriters earlier), prioritizing document verification queues, forecasting portfolio activation volume and expected loss reserves for the pipeline, and triggering post-activation early monitoring.
    *   *Unsafe Uses:* Automatically rejecting applications at event $k=2$ or $k=3$, or loosening credit policy thresholds for historically rejected segments without running a controlled randomized exploration trial (e.g., approving a small stratified random sample of borderline cases to gather counterfactual labels).

### Three Ways Deployment Could Change the Data-Generating Process (Performative Feedback Loops)
1.  **Early Rejection Censoring Loop (Truncating the Funnel):** If underwriters use the early-warning score at $k=3$ to reject high-score applications immediately, future cohorts of activated accounts will no longer contain those prefixes. This shrinks the overlap region, degrades future model retraining (selective label problem), and renders the model self-fulfilling but un-auditable on high-risk profiles.
2.  **Process-Timing & Operational Intervention Shift:** If a high early-warning score triggers mandatory senior review or additional `Request Documents` loops, the meaning of process features changes. Historically, `doc_loop_count` reflected organic applicant risk/complexity; post-deployment, it becomes an endogenous byproduct of the model's own prediction, breaking feature-target calibration in future retraining.
3.  **Applicant & Broker Behavioral Gaming (Goodhart's Law):** If partner channels or brokers learn that slow document uploads (`max_inter_event_gap`) or specific `case:requested_amount` brackets trigger high early-warning flags, they will coach applicants to submit placeholder documents rapidly or split loan requests, shifting the covariate distribution ($P(\mathcal{H}(t_p))$) without reducing underlying credit risk.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ==============================================================================
# 1. CONFIGURATION & CANONICAL SCHEMA DEFINITIONS
# ==============================================================================
CSV_PATH = "applications_events.csv"
EXTRACT_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15 00:00:00", tz="UTC")
TRAIN_START_MAX = pd.Timestamp("2025-02-10 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
VAL_START_MAX   = pd.Timestamp("2025-08-31 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
POLICY_CHANGE_DT = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01 00:00:00", tz="UTC")
PREFIX_LEN = 3  # e.g., Application Started -> Identity/KYC Check -> Credit Review

# Map post-2025-10-15 migration names back to canonical process activities
ACTIVITY_MAP = {
    "App_Initiated_v2": "Application Started",
    "KYC_Verification_v2": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Underwriting_Review": "Credit Review",
    "Doc_Request_v2": "Request Documents",
    "Decision_Approved": "Accepted",
    "Decision_Declined": "Rejected",
}
TERMINAL_AND_POST_EVENTS = {
    "Accepted", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred",
    "Contract Signed", "Account Activated", "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"
}
FORBIDDEN_LEAKAGE_COLS = {
    "final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status",
    "documents_total", "default_90d", "label_available_at"
}

def build_as_of_case_table(df_raw: pd.DataFrame, eval_cutoff: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Constructs case-level disposition, activation, censoring, and label maturity
    strictly AS OF `eval_cutoff` to prevent hindsight leakage.
    """
    df = df_raw[df_raw["time:timestamp"] <= eval_cutoff].copy()
    
    # Group by case to extract milestone timestamps
    case_starts = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("start_time")
    case_last   = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].max().rename("last_event_time")
    static_cols = df.groupby("case:concept:name")[["case:channel", "case:region", "case:requested_amount"]].first()
    
    def get_first_ts(act_name):
        sub = df[df["concept:name"] == act_name]
        return sub.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()

    act_ts   = get_first_ts("Account Activated").rename("activated_time")
    rej_ts   = get_first_ts("Rejected").rename("rejected_time")
    with_ts  = get_first_ts("Withdrawn").rename("withdrawn_time")
    trans_ts = get_first_ts("Transferred").rename("transferred_time")
    
    # Extract raw outcome & availability timestamp (evaluated against eval_cutoff)
    lbl_df = df.groupby("case:concept:name")[["default_90d", "label_available_at"]].first()
    
    cases = pd.concat([case_starts, case_last, static_cols, act_ts, rej_ts, with_ts, trans_ts, lbl_df], axis=1)
    
    # Assign mutually exclusive Stage 1 state as of eval_cutoff
    conditions = [
        cases["activated_time"].notna(),
        cases["rejected_time"].notna(),
        cases["withdrawn_time"].notna(),
        cases["transferred_time"].notna(),
    ]
    choices = ["Activated", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]
    cases["stage1_state"] = np.select(conditions, choices, default="Open_Censored")
    
    # Enforce 90-day horizon AND delayed outcome feed maturity as of eval_cutoff
    horizon_matured = (cases["activated_time"] + pd.Timedelta(days=90)) <= eval_cutoff
    feed_arrived    = cases["label_available_at"].notna() & (cases["label_available_at"] <= eval_cutoff)
    cases["label_matured"] = (cases["stage1_state"] == "Activated") & horizon_matured & feed_arrived
    
    # Strictly mask default_90d unless matured by eval_cutoff (never impute 0 for Rejected/Open!)
    cases["target_default_90d"] = np.where(cases["label_matured"], cases["default_90d"], np.nan)
    
    # Differentiate post-activation administrative/feed delay from permanent missing feed (>125d old)
    days_since_act = (eval_cutoff - cases["activated_time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    cases["cohort_bucket"] = np.select(
        [
            cases["label_matured"] & (cases["target_default_90d"] == 0),
            cases["label_matured"] & (cases["target_default_90d"] == 1),
            (cases["stage1_state"] == "Activated") & (~cases["label_matured"]) & (days_since_act <= 125),
            (cases["stage1_state"] == "Activated") & (~cases["label_matured"]) & (days_since_act > 125),
        ],
        [
            "Activated_Matured_NonDefault",
            "Activated_Matured_Default",
            "Activated_Censored_ImmatureOrDelayed",
            "Activated_MissingFeed_Permanent",
        ],
        default=cases["stage1_state"]
    )
    return cases.reset_index()

def engineer_safe_prefix_features(df_clean: pd.DataFrame, k: int) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Extracts length-k prefixes using PM4Py, removes cases already decided at/before k,
    and computes leak-free process, rework, and dynamic resource experience features.
    """
    # 1. Compute dynamic resource experience strictly from prior historical events (fixes static leakage)
    df_sorted = df_clean.sort_values(["time:timestamp", "case:concept:name"]).copy()
    df_sorted["resource_Dynamic_exp"] = df_sorted.groupby("org:resource").cumcount()
    
    # 2. Drop all post-prefix/outcome columns before slicing prefixes
    safe_cols = [c for c in df_sorted.columns if c not in FORBIDDEN_LEAKAGE_COLS]
    df_safe = df_sorted[safe_cols].copy()
    
    # 3. Extract fixed-length k prefixes via PM4Py
    df_pref = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(df_safe, length=k)
    
    # 4. Filter to valid active prefixes: must have exactly k events AND no terminal/post events in prefix
    ev_counts = df_pref.groupby("case:concept:name").size()
    has_terminal = df_pref[df_pref["concept:name"].isin(TERMINAL_AND_POST_EVENTS)]["case:concept:name"].unique()
    valid_cases = ev_counts[ev_counts == k].index.difference(has_terminal)
    df_pref = df_pref[df_pref["case:concept:name"].isin(valid_cases)].copy()
    
    # 5. Annotate cumulative waiting/service times strictly within the truncated k-prefix log
    df_pref = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df_pref)
    
    # 6. Aggregate prefix-level control-flow, timing, rework, and risk trajectory features
    grp = df_pref.groupby("case:concept:name")
    feats = pd.DataFrame(index=valid_cases)
    feats["prefix_end_time"]    = grp["time:timestamp"].max()
    feats["elapsed_hours"]      = (grp["time:timestamp"].max() - grp["time:timestamp"].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    feats["doc_loop_count"]     = df_pref[df_pref["concept:name"] == "Request Documents"].groupby("case:concept:name").size().reindex(valid_cases, fill_value=0)
    feats["review_count"]       = df_pref[df_pref["concept:name"] == "Credit Review"].groupby("case:concept:name").size().reindex(valid_cases, fill_value=0)
    feats["unique_resources"]   = grp["org:resource"].nunique()
    feats["risk_score_initial"] = grp["risk_score"].first()
    feats["risk_score_latest"]  = grp["risk_score"].last()
    feats["risk_score_delta"]   = feats["risk_score_latest"] - feats["risk_score_initial"]
    feats["res_exp_at_tp"]      = grp["resource_Dynamic_exp"].last()
    feats["post_policy_regime"] = (feats["prefix_end_time"] >= POLICY_CHANGE_DT).astype(int)
    
    return feats.reset_index(), df_pref

def run_pipeline():
    # --- Load & Canonicalize ---
    df = pd.read_csv(
        CSV_PATH,
        parse_dates=["time:timestamp", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]
    )
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)
    
    # Format with PM4Py standard event log columns
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    # Deduplicate migration glitches (identical case, activity, resource within 5 seconds)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    dt_diff = df.groupby(["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "org:resource"])["time:timestamp"].diff()
    df = df[(dt_diff.isna()) | (dt_diff > pd.Timedelta(seconds=5))].copy()

    # --- Build Cohort Table & Print Flow Accounting ---
    case_table = build_as_of_case_table(df, eval_cutoff=EXTRACT_CUTOFF)
    print("=== COHORT FLOW ACCOUNTING (AS OF 2026-02-15) ===")
    print(case_table["cohort_bucket"].value_counts(dropna=False))
    
    # --- Extract Safe Prefix Features (k=3) ---
    feats_df, df_pref_log = engineer_safe_prefix_features(df, k=PREFIX_LEN)
    dataset = pd.merge(case_table, feats_df, on="case:concept:name", how="inner")
    
    # --- Automated Leakage Audit Assertions ---
    leaked_cols = FORBIDDEN_LEAKAGE_COLS.intersection(set(feats_df.columns))
    assert len(leaked_cols) == 0, f"LEAKAGE DETECTED in feature matrix: {leaked_cols}"
    assert (dataset["prefix_end_time"] < dataset["activated_time"].fillna(pd.Timestamp.max.tz_localize("UTC"))).all(), \
        "LOOK-AHEAD LEAKAGE: Prefix timestamp exceeds or equals Account Activated timestamp!"
    
    # --- Temporal Train / Validation / Test Split (Replacing random pm4py.split_train_test) ---
    train_df = dataset[dataset["start_time"] <= TRAIN_START_MAX].copy()
    val_df   = dataset[(dataset["start_time"] > TRAIN_START_MAX) & (dataset["start_time"] <= VAL_START_MAX)].copy()
    test_df  = dataset[dataset["start_time"] > VAL_START_MAX].copy()
    
    # Inspect DFG / variants strictly on training prefixes to prevent test structure leakage
    train_pref_log = df_pref_log[df_pref_log["case:concept:name"].isin(train_df["case:concept:name"])]
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(train_pref_log)
    train_variants = pm4py.get_variants(train_pref_log)
    print(f"Train cases: {len(train_df)} | Val: {len(val_df)} | Test: {len(test_df)} | Train k-variants: {len(train_variants)}")

    # ==========================================================================
    # DOWNSTREAM MODELING HOOKS (Competing Risks & IPCW Stage 2 Default)
    # ==========================================================================
    # 1. Stage 1 (All active prefixes in train_df): Fit Fine-and-Gray / Aalen-Johansen
    #    competing-risk model on (duration = last_event_time - prefix_end_time, event = stage1_state).
    # 2. Stage 2 IPCW Weights (train_df[stage1_state == 'Activated']): Fit logistic/survival model
    #    predicting P(label_matured == 1 | channel, elapsed_hours, start_time) -> compute w_ipcw = 1 / P_hat.
    # 3. Stage 2 Classifier (train_df[label_matured == True]): Fit gradient boosted trees on
    #    target_default_90d with sample_weight = w_ipcw; calibrate on val_df; evaluate IPCW-Brier on test_df.
    return train_df, val_df, test_df

if __name__ == "__main__":
    run_pipeline()
```
