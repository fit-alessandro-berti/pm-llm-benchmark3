## 1. Conformance semantics

Interpreting object-centric deviations against an incomplete normative specification requires separating syntactic graph differences from genuine business noncompliance. Because this norm is **closed-world** only for explicitly `forbidden` relations and for `mandatory` relations within their stated scopes—and **open-world** everywhere else—absence from the normative graph never implies a violation by itself.

The seven diagnostic classes are defined as follows:

- **Forbidden behavior**: An observed relation explicitly disallowed under a closed-world prohibition (e.g., `N3`, `T6`, `E10`). Any occurrence is a presumptive violation unless disproven as a logging or identity artifact.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: An absent or deficit relation whose stated scope applies under closed-world expectations (e.g., `N1`, `N4`, `E2`, `E6`, `E8`). Confirmation requires verifying both scope applicability and representational validity.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: An observed relation absent from the norm or marked `unspecified` (e.g., `N6`, `T4`, `T5`, `E9`). Under open-world semantics, it is non-violating and informs future modeling rather than escalation.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: Missing occurrences of a `typical` relation (e.g., `N2`, `N5`, `T3`) where business exceptions are explicitly tolerated. Without normative thresholds, lower frequencies are informational rather than noncompliant.
- **Likely data-quality error**: A syntactic violation probably produced by ETL, schema, timestamp, or identifier-join defects rather than real operational misbehavior (e.g., `E10`, missing object instantiation in `E6`).
- **Abstraction mismatch**: A gap created because a summary graph (such as directly-follows edges in an OC-DFG) is stricter than the underlying business rule (such as an eventually-follows rule in `N1`).
- **Insufficient evidence**: An ambiguity that aggregate counts cannot resolve without instance-level traces, timestamps, and attributes (e.g., distinguishing whether two packages missed packing or underwent an intermediate step).

Under **closed-world** rules, observed forbidden edges or missing in-scope mandatory edges trigger mandatory investigation. Under **open-world** rules, unmodeled edges and typical-pattern exceptions remain benign unless corroborated by external risk evidence.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An object-centric directly-follows graph (OC-DFG) establishes **local, object-typed sequential adjacency**—whether activity $B$ immediately follows activity $A$ within an object's projection—along with aggregate transition counts. It reliably detects stark local sequence breaches such as `Order: Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (2 Orders, `N3`), where no legitimate direct step should connect cancellation to dispatch.

However, an OC-DFG **cannot establish** eventual reachability, multi-step paths, causality across different object instances, or case-level identity alignment. Counts and scopes heavily distort naive readings: `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) and `Validate Order -> Close Order` (83) sum to 155 despite only 100 newly created Orders, indicating pre-window Orders or repeated validations. Similarly, `Invoice: Issue Invoice` with no successor (14 of 110) reflects snapshot right-censoring as readily as nonpayment.

Crucially, **interpreting an "eventually expected" business rule as a directly-follows rule creates systematic false positives**. Norm `N1` expects every standard Order to *eventually* reach validation, yet the OC-DFG checks only immediate adjacency (`98 of 100`). Because 3 Orders follow `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`, an intermediate compliance review naturally breaks the directly-follows edge without violating the business rule—and the 98 + 3 = 101 overlap further hints at timestamp ties or non-unique transitions. The same risk applies to `N4` (`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, 198 of 200): ET–OT shows 200 `Pack Package` and 200 `Dispatch Package` links to Packages, meaning an intermediate relabeling or inspection step could make the packages conforming at an eventual-response abstraction while suspicious in an OC-DFG.

### OTG

An object-type graph (OTG) establishes **macro-level structural relationships between object types**—specifically co-occurrence (`object_interaction`), simultaneous creation (`object_cobirth`), and simultaneous termination (`object_codeath`). It excels at catching forbidden lifecycle coupling, such as `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` (`1` edge, `T6`), which signals either a broken business boundary or an event-bundling ETL fault.

Yet an OTG **cannot establish** which event type produced an interaction, in what order interactions occurred, or whether mandatory links were present at the *required* lifecycle stage. Because OTG weights aggregate across all event types, a conforming OTG edge can conceal a localized event-level violation:
- `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` has weight `1450` (`T1`), appearing fully compliant alongside `1450` `Add Item – Item` links, yet ET–OT reveals only `1448` `Add Item – Order` links—meaning other events supplied the missing Order–Item pairings.
- `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight `200` (`T2`), masking the `2` `Dispatch Package` events lacking a Delivery link in ET–OT (`198`), because a later event (such as delivery confirmation) linked Package and Delivery.

Conversely, an OTG edge absent from the norm—such as `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (`7`)—looks "extra" syntactically while being operationally benign under open-world rules.

### ET–OT

An event-type to object-type (ET–OT) graph establishes **per-event-type object participation requirements**, making it the sharpest tool for auditing mandatory relational completeness (`E1`–`E8`) and forbidden domain cross-contamination (`E10`). Comparing paired weights for the same event type exposes precise relational deficits that OTG hides: `Add Item` links to `1450` Items but `1448` Orders (`E2` deficit of 2); `Dispatch Package` links to `200` Packages but `198` Deliveries (`E6` deficit of 2); `Record Payment` links to `93` Payments but `92` Invoices (`E8` deficit of 1).

However, ET–OT **cannot establish** temporal order, object multiplicity per individual event (e.g., whether one event linked two objects while another linked zero), or attribute-level qualifiers. Thus, a single event linking `Sensor Heartbeat` to `Invoice` (`E10`) is immediately flagged in ET–OT, yet ET–OT alone cannot prove whether it is a physical system integration breach or a reused-ID join error. Likewise, `Record Payment – Invoice` (`92` of `93`) appears noncompliant against `E8`, even though Finance permits unapplied cash (`T3`), because ET–OT lacks the semantic nuance to separate missing event-subtype labels from unallocated funds. Meanwhile, `Manual Compliance Review – Customer` (`3`) is syntactically unmodeled in ET–OT but entirely benign given open-world semantics and the observed `RiskCase` context (`E9`).

The sketch below preserves raw PM4Py structural diagnostics intact and layers normative status, scope, and temporal semantics externally, since bare graph objects cannot encode open-world/closed-world rules or legal intent.

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Any, Dict, List, Set, Tuple
import pm4py

# Assumes `ocel`, `norm_ocdfg` (dict), `norm_otg` (tuple), and `norm_etot` (tuple) are loaded.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Discover observed summaries and compute raw PM4Py conformance diagnostics
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Preserve raw PM4Py outputs unmodified; these reflect syntactic graph diffs only,
# NOT business or legal compliance decisions.
raw_diagnostics: Dict[str, Any] = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(obs_ocdfg, norm_ocdfg),
    "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(obs_otg, norm_otg),
    "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(obs_etot, norm_etot),
}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. External normative metadata registry
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# IMPORTANT: Bare OC-DFG dicts and OTG/ET-OT tuples only store edges/weights.
# Normative status (mandatory/forbidden/typical/unspecified), scope predicates,
# temporal modality (directly_follows vs eventually_follows), and known DQ
# hypotheses MUST be maintained in an external policy table outside the graphs.
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class NormRuleMeta:
    norm_id: str
    status: str  # "mandatory" | "forbidden" | "typical" | "unspecified"
    scope: str
    modality: str = "exact"  # "exact" | "eventually_follows" | "per_event"
    dq_suspect_hint: str = ""


NORM_METADATA: Dict[str, Dict[Tuple[str, ...], NormRuleMeta]] = {
    "ocdfg": {
        ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N1", "mandatory", "newly created standard Orders", "eventually_follows"
        ),
        ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N2", "typical", "Orders with optional intermediate steps"
        ),
        ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N3", "forbidden", "same Order after cancellation"
        ),
        ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N4", "mandatory", "externally shipped Packages", "eventually_follows"
        ),
        ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N5", "typical", "excludes write-offs, credit notes, open credit"
        ),
        ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"): NormRuleMeta(
            "N6", "unspecified", "reopening outside norm governance"
        ),
    },
    "otg": {
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"): NormRuleMeta(
            "T1", "mandatory", "required for Add Item", "per_event"
        ),
        ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"): NormRuleMeta(
            "T2", "mandatory", "required for Dispatch Package", "per_event"
        ),
        ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"): NormRuleMeta(

        ),

        ),
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): NormRuleMeta(
            "T5", "unspecified", "risk review ungoverned here"
        ),
        ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"): NormRuleMeta(
            "T6", "forbidden", "cannot be born in same business event"
        ),
    },
    "etot": {
        ("Add Item", "Order"): NormRuleMeta("E2", "mandatory", "every Add Item"),
        ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): NormRuleMeta(
            "E6", "mandatory", "every Dispatch Package",
            dq_suspect_hint="check carrier attribute ETL extraction",
        ),
        ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): NormRuleMeta(
            "E8", "mandatory", "except explicitly typed unapplied cash",
            dq_suspect_hint="check missing unapplied-cash subtype label",
        ),
        ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): NormRuleMeta(
            "E9", "unspecified", "legitimate rare interaction"
        ),
        ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): NormRuleMeta(

            dq_suspect_hint="suspected reused numeric ID join error",
        ),
    },
}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Separate semantic-classification layer (open-world by default)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def extract_observed_edges(model_family: str, discovered_obj: Any) -> Set[Tuple[str, ...]]:
    """Normalize PM4Py discovered structures into comparable edge tuples."""
    if model_family == "ocdfg":
        edges: Set[Tuple[str, ...]] = set()
        for ot, dfg in discovered_obj.get("edges", {}).get("event_couples", {}).items():
            for (src, tgt), val in dfg.items():
                if val:
                    edges.add((ot, src, tgt))
        return edges
    # OTG and ET-OT tuples typically expose edge sets/dicts in element 0
    edge_container = discovered_obj[0] if isinstance(discovered_obj, tuple) else discovered_obj
    return set(edge_container.keys()) if isinstance(edge_container, dict) else set(edge_container)


def classify_semantic_deviations(
    model_family: str,
    observed_edges: Set[Tuple[str, ...]],
    partial_deficits: Dict[Tuple[str, ...], str],
    raw_diag: Any,
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Map observed edges and count deficits to open/closed-world semantic classes."""
    rules = NORM_METADATA[model_family]
    classified: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []

    # A. Evaluate all observed edges
    for edge in sorted(observed_edges):
        meta = rules.get(edge)
        if meta is None or meta.status == "unspecified":
            classified.append({
                "edge": edge,
                "norm_id": meta.norm_id if meta else "UNMODELED",
                "classification": "unexpected_but_unregulated",
                "requires_escalation": False,
            })
        elif meta.status == "forbidden":
            cls = "likely_data_quality_error" if meta.dq_suspect_hint else "forbidden_behavior"
            classified.append({
                "edge": edge,
                "norm_id": meta.norm_id,
                "classification": cls,
                "note": meta.dq_suspect_hint or "Closed-world prohibition breached",
                "requires_escalation": True,
            })

    # B. Evaluate absent or partially deficient normative edges (e.g., 98/100, 198/200)
    for edge, meta in rules.items():
        missing_completely = edge not in observed_edges
        has_deficit = edge in partial_deficits
        if not (missing_completely or has_deficit):
            continue

        if meta.status == "mandatory":
            if meta.modality == "eventually_follows":
                cls = "abstraction_mismatch_or_insufficient_evidence"
            elif meta.dq_suspect_hint:
                cls = "likely_data_quality_error_or_missing_mandatory"
            else:
                cls = "missing_mandatory_behavior"
            classified.append({
                "edge": edge,
                "norm_id": meta.norm_id,
                "classification": cls,
                "deficit_context": partial_deficits.get(edge, "absent from graph"),
                "requires_escalation": True,
            })
        elif meta.status == "typical":
            classified.append({
                "edge": edge,
                "norm_id": meta.norm_id,
                "classification": "typical_pattern_absence",
                "deficit_context": partial_deficits.get(edge, "absent from graph"),
                "requires_escalation": False,
            })

    return {
        "model_family": model_family,
        "raw_pm4py_diagnostics": raw_diag,  # Preserved untouched
        "semantic_findings": classified,
    }


# Example deficits derived from comparing paired event/object counts outside bare graphs
count_deficits = {
    "ocdfg": {
        ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): "98 of 100 newly created Orders",
        ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): "198 of 200 externally shipped Packages",
        ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): "92 of 110 Invoices",
    },
    "otg": {},
    "etot": {

        ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): "198 vs 200 Dispatch Package-Package edges",
        ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): "92 vs 93 Record Payment-Payment edges",

}

semantic_report = {
    fam: classify_semantic_deviations(
        fam,

    )
    for fam, obs in [("ocdfg", obs_ocdfg), ("otg", obs_otg), ("etot", obs_etot)]
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **Temporal modality mismatch (`N1`, `N4`)**: `N1` states an *eventually-follows* requirement but is evaluated via a directly-follows summary, and `N4` does not clarify whether intermediate warehouse steps (e.g., inspection or relabeling) are allowed between packing and dispatch.
   - *Operational risk*: False alarms on compliant exception paths (like compliance reviews) causing alert fatigue and misdirected audits.
   - *Clarification*: Redefine `N1` and `N4` as eventually-follows response constraints with explicit allowed intermediate states.
   - *Target status*: `mandatory`.

2. **Interaction between cancellation (`N3`) and reopening (`N6`)**: The norm forbids `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (`N3`) while leaving `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (`N6`) ungoverned, leaving post-reopen fulfilment undefined.
   - *Operational risk*: Ambiguity over whether a cancelled-then-reopened order may legally ship, creating either control bypasses or blocked legitimate orders.
   - *Clarification*: Prohibit dispatch after cancellation *unless* preceded by an authorized `Reopen Order` and fresh `Validate Order`.
   - *Target status*: `forbidden` (for dispatch without authorized reopen/re-validation).

3. **Contradiction on unapplied cash (`T3` vs `E8`)**: `T3` marks `Invoice–Payment` interaction as `typical` to allow unapplied cash, while `E8` makes `Record Payment – Invoice` `mandatory` unless explicitly typed, without specifying the required typing mechanism.
   - *Operational risk*: Legitimate finance postings trigger false compliance breaches, or genuine misapplied funds hide behind informal "unapplied cash" claims.
   - *Clarification*: Require an explicit `is_unapplied_cash` attribute or `Record Unapplied Payment` event type, keeping invoice linkage conditional on standard payments.
   - *Target status*: `mandatory` (for explicit classification) alongside `typical` (for overall Invoice–Payment pairing).

4. **Event-scoped vs lifecycle-scoped OTG rules (`T1`, `T2`)**: `T1` and `T2` claim to be mandatory for specific events (`Add Item`, `Dispatch Package`), yet OTG aggregates across the entire object lifecycle and cannot enforce event-specific binding.
   - *Operational risk*: Passing OTG checks (`1450` and `200`) create false assurance while event-level gaps (`1448` and `198`) go undetected.
   - *Clarification*: Anchor event-specific cardinality rules in ET–OT (`E2`, `E6`) and define OTG interaction edges as lifecycle-level summaries.
   - *Target status*: `mandatory` (at the ET–OT event level).

5. **Absence of windowing, right-censoring, and statistical tolerances (`N5`, `N2`)**: The norm defines no aging threshold or snapshot-boundary rules for open invoices (`14` with no successor) or pre-window Orders (`72` + `83` validation successors).
   - *Operational risk*: Genuine overdue collections or stalled orders blend invisibly into benign snapshot censoring.
   - *Clarification*: Specify open-case grace periods (e.g., standard net-30 credit terms) and separate right-censored cases from overdue exceptions.
   - *Target status*: `typical` (with explicit monitoring thresholds outside binary violation flags).

6. **Unaddressed `object_codeath` semantics and compliance-review customer linkage**: Neither `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (`7`) nor `Manual Compliance Review – Customer` (`3`) is referenced in the norm.
   - *Operational risk*: Automated conformance scripts lacking open-world guards will repeatedly flag benign lifecycle endings and customer-linked reviews as structural errors.
   - *Clarification*: Explicitly record shared Package–Delivery confirmation endings and Customer linkage during compliance reviews as outside this control scope (deferred to Customer policy `T4`).
   - *Target status*: `explicitly unspecified`.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

Prioritization focuses on the two highest-severity behavioral prohibitions—`N3` (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`) and `T6` (`(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`)—plus immediate data quarantine for `E10` (`Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice`), without escalating rare benign behavior (`1` `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `3` `Manual Compliance Review` cases, or `7` `Package–Delivery` codeath edges).

- **Immediate containment**:
  - *Priority 1 (`N3`)*: Pull the 2 Orders showing `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`; verify physical shipment status, halt carrier delivery or initiate return/recovery if already shipped, and freeze automated billing reversals until reconciled.
  - *Priority 2 (`T6`)*: Locate the 1 event creating an Order and Payment simultaneously; verify payment authenticity, authorization, and whether fraud or validation bypass occurred.
  - *Data-integrity containment (`E10`)*: Exclude the 1 `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` edge from financial reporting and audit views immediately.
- **Data repair**:
  - Fix the OCEL extraction join behind `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` (`E10`) by scoping joins with system-prefixed composite keys rather than raw numeric IDs.
  - Update the `Dispatch Package` extractor (`E6`) to instantiate/link Delivery objects from the carrier reference attribute and backfill the 2 affected events.
  - Investigate ETL foreign-key drops for the 2 `Add Item` events missing `Order` links (`E2`) and implement explicit unapplied-cash typing for `Record Payment` (`E8`).
- **Process investigation**:
  - Trace the 2 `N3` Orders end-to-end across OMS and WMS to distinguish true cancellation-propagation lag from timestamp skew or unlogged reopening.
  - Inspect the 2 `N4` Packages lacking `Pack -> Dispatch` DF edges and the 2 `N1` Orders lacking `Create -> Validate` DF edges to verify whether intermediate steps occurred or controls were bypassed.
  - Confirm with Finance that the 1 unlinked `Record Payment` (`93` vs `92`) is valid unapplied cash.
- **Norm revision**:
  - Upgrade `N1` and `N4` to eventually-follows rules, reconcile `T3`/`E8` unapplied-cash typing, define `Reopen Order` interaction with `N3`, and explicitly register benign open-world patterns (`object_codeath`, `Manual Compliance Review – Customer`) as `unspecified`.
- **Monitoring**:
  - Deploy automated zero-tolerance alerts *only* for closed-world prohibitions (`N3`, `T6`, `E10`) and event-level mandatory cardinality deficits (`E2`, `E6`, `E8`).
  - Track `typical` exception rates (`N5` write-offs and aged open invoices) via trend dashboards rather than binary violation tickets, and exclude explicitly `unspecified` rare events from alert queues.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

- **Robust conclusions**: The `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` occurrences (`2`, `N3`) are unambiguous high-priority control alerts; `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (`72`), `Manual Compliance Review – Customer` (`3`), `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (`7`), and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (`1`, `N6`) are non-violating under open-world semantics; and ET–OT reliably proves localized relational deficits (`E2`, `E6`, `E8`) that OTG masks.
- **Conclusions dependent on norm incompleteness**: Whether the `1` unlinked `Record Payment` is compliant hinges on reconciling `T3` with `E8`; whether the `2` missing `Create -> Validate` (`N1`) and `2` missing `Pack -> Dispatch` (`N4`) DF edges are violations depends on formalizing eventually-follows semantics; and whether `14` unclosed invoices (`N5`) warrant action depends on absent aging tolerances.
- **Conclusions dependent on suspected data-quality issues**: `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` (`E10`), the `2` `Dispatch Package – Delivery` gaps (`E6`), and potentially the `1` `Order–Payment` cobirth (`T6`) and `2` `Add Item – Order` gaps (`E2`) may be ETL/join artifacts rather than operational noncompliance.
- **Undecidable from graph summaries alone**: Aggregate OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT counts cannot establish instance-level eventually-follows satisfaction, distinguish timestamp ties from parallel execution (as in `98 + 3 = 101` vs `100` Orders), verify whether paired event counts belong to the exact same object IDs (`200` Pack vs `200` Dispatch), or separate snapshot censoring from stalled processes.
