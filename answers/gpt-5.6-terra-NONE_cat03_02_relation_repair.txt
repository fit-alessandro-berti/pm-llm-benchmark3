## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

| Rule / evidence | Classification | Use in this repair |
|---|---|---|
| Every E2O target must exist in the object table; every O2O endpoint must exist. | hard invariant | No new object identifiers are introduced. |
| O2O qualifier direction is meaningful. | hard invariant | `DEL-X carries PK-7` does not imply storing `PK-7 carries DEL-X`. |
| A `Create Order` event normally has one Order. | strong default | Existing relations satisfy this; no additions are proposed. |
| `Add Item` normally has one Order and one Item. | strong default | Existing rows already support all three item memberships. |
| `Pack Item` normally has one Item and one Package. | strong default | Supports adding `PK-8` to ev08, but scanner-outage semantics make this provisional rather than certain. |
| `Dispatch Package` normally has one Package and one Delivery. | strong default | Supports linking ev10 to `PK-8` and likely `DEL-Y`; bulk dispatch remains possible. |
| `Record Payment` normally has one Payment and one Invoice. | strong default | Supports a candidate `PAY-2`–`INV-44` relationship, but unapplied cash remains possible. |
| Invoice normally covers one or more orders. | strong default | Existing `INV-44 bills ORD-A` is insufficient to explain the invoice total, but not necessarily wrong. |
| Matching carrier prefix/reference (`N-420`, `N-421?`, `N-42*`) | weak heuristic | Strongly suggests ev09/DEL-X and ev10/DEL-Y; ev11 remains ambiguous because `N-42*` could denote either or both. |
| Same route (`NORTH`) | weak heuristic | Supports a shared delivery/confirmation context but cannot distinguish `DEL-X` from `DEL-Y`. |
| Amounts 100 and 130 summing to invoice total 230 | weak heuristic | Supports, but does not prove, that both payments settle INV-44 and that both orders are billed. Fees, taxes, discounts, partial payment, and coincidental values remain possible. |
| Same customer C17 | weak heuristic | Supports grouping ORD-A, ORD-B, INV-44, and ev11; it is not order-level identification. |
| Lifecycle order: packing before dispatch before confirmation; invoice/payment after order creation | strong default | Favors `IT-B1  PK-8  DEL-Y` and later payment allocation, but timestamp order alone does not determine relations. |
| Existing O2O structure | strong default when directly observed | `PK-8 packs IT-A2` makes a second package membership for IT-A2 unlikely; it does not prove that ev08 belongs to PK-8. |
| Candidate creates duplicate exclusive relation without contrary evidence | evidence against a repair | For example, adding `PK-7 packs IT-B1` would conflict with temporal/scanner evidence and route/package structure. |
| Candidate only “completes” a lifecycle, without independent evidence | evidence against a repair | Completeness is not treated as proof. |

Evidence is combined abductively rather than mechanically. Identifier and scanner-reference similarity are more specific than route or customer. Lifecycle sequence and cardinality constrain plausible candidates. Amount equality is treated as corroboration only. Observed O2O edges are stronger than inferred O2O edges. An inferred E2O relation is not allowed to become “independent” confirmation merely by adding a corresponding O2O relation.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A accepts only the high-confidence structural repair: add ev08–PK-8 and ev10–DEL-Y, plus the corresponding inferred O2O edges as provisional annotations. It leaves confirmation, invoicing of ORD-B, and PAY-2 allocation unresolved.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Add E2O `ev08  PK-8`; optionally add provisional `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. This yields a plausible PK-8 package containing IT-A2 and IT-B1.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Keep observed PK-8 and add DEL-Y to ev10; add provisional `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. DEL-X remains associated only with PK-7 through ev09 and observed O2O.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** Leave both DEL-X and DEL-Y unresolved. The wildcard carrier reference is too weak to uniquely identify either delivery.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Leave ORD-B unresolved. The 230 amount is suggestive but not dispositive.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave unresolved/unapplied. It may settle INV-44, but amount complementarity alone is insufficient.

This bundle repairs likely scanner and carrier-reference omissions while preserving genuine uncertainty in customer-level confirmation and finance.

### Bundle B: consolidated customer-level fulfillment and invoice interpretation

Bundle B retains Bundle A’s package/delivery repairs and additionally interprets INV-44 as a consolidated invoice for both orders, with both payments settling it.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Same as Bundle A: IT-B1 is packed into PK-8.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Same as Bundle A: PK-8 is dispatched with DEL-Y.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** Keep ev11 related only to ORD-A and do not add a delivery relation. The portal record may be order/customer-level confirmation with lost carrier identity. This avoids falsely claiming delivery-specific confirmation.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Add `INV-44 bills ORD-B` and ev12–ORD-B. Bundle B treats 230 as a plausible combined invoice total for 130 and 100, supported by common customer and close timing.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Add ev14–INV-44 and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, interpreting 100+130 as two allocations against the consolidated invoice.

The financial lifecycle becomes coherent: both orders contribute to a single invoice, and both payments settle it. However, this coherence remains abductive; the invoice amount could include adjustments, and PAY-2 could be unrelated cash.

### Bundle C: shared dispatch/portal ambiguity retained

A more cautious alternative keeps only A1 and A4: ev08 is connected to PK-8 and ev10 remains package-only. DEL-Y, the confirmation mapping, ORD-B billing, and PAY-2 allocation all remain unresolved. This is appropriate if `N-421?` is considered too unreliable to create a delivery relation.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A has creation, item addition, invoice issuance, and an order-level delivery confirmation. ORD-B has creation and item addition, and—under Bundle A—its later financial and confirmation lifecycle remains incomplete. That is acceptable. ORD-B is not treated as orphaned because it has observed lifecycle activity, although it lacks an observed invoice/payment outcome.

**Item.** Each item has one observed `contains` relation and one Add Item event. IT-A1 is packed into PK-7. IT-A2 is packed into PK-8. Bundle A provisionally assigns IT-B1 to PK-8, yielding one package per item, not a conflicting many-to-many packing pattern.

**Package.** PK-7 has packing, dispatch, and delivery association through DEL-X. PK-8 has two items under Bundle A, which is plausible. It has a dispatch event and provisional delivery association with DEL-Y. No package-confirmation relation is forced.

**Delivery.** DEL-X has a coherent dispatch/package lifecycle. DEL-Y becomes non-orphaned under Bundle A through ev10 and PK-8. Neither delivery has a unique confirmation because ev11 is ambiguous.

**Invoice.** INV-44 is issued and bills ORD-A; it receives PAY-1. Under Bundle A, it may be partially settled, fully settled with unresolved PAY-2, or represent an adjusted invoice. Bundle B instead makes it a consolidated invoice for both orders.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is linked to INV-44. PAY-2 is an intentionally unresolved payment object under Bundle A; this is preferable to inventing an allocation.

Independent support is limited. The ev08–PK-8 relation has scanner-reference and lifecycle support; the O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is derived from it and therefore is not independent corroboration. Likewise, ev10–DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` are mutually coherent but not independent. Treating either inferred O2O edge as proof of its E2O source would be circular.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log.** The OC-DFG contains fragmented object behavior. IT-B1 has `Add Item  Pack Item`, but its pack event lacks package participation. PK-8 has `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`, but ev08 is absent from its lifecycle. DEL-Y is an orphan delivery object. PAY-2 is an orphan payment object. Order flattening likely gives ORD-A a richer trace than ORD-B; Delivery flattening may omit DEL-Y entirely.

**Bundle A.** The OC-DFG gains package-centered continuity for PK-8: `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`, now including ev08 as a PK-8 event. Delivery DEL-Y gains `Dispatch Package`; its trace remains short because confirmation is unresolved. The OTG gains object-type interactions implied by events: Item–Package at ev08 and Package–Delivery at ev10. The ET–OT graph gains ev08–Package and ev10–Delivery links. No new Order–Invoice or Payment–Invoice edge is added, so financial uncertainty remains visible.

Order flattening changes only indirectly: if O2O-aware analyses traverse package/item structure, ORD-B may appear more connected to shipment operations. Package flattening becomes materially less fragmented. Delivery flattening gains a DEL-Y dispatch case but no fabricated confirmation. Invoice and Payment flattenings remain conservative: PAY-2 may appear as a single-event payment case.

**Bundle B.** Bundle B adds `Issue Invoice` to ORD-B’s flattened trace and `Record Payment` to INV-44/PAY-2 financial traces. The OC-DFG may show ORD-B progression toward `Issue Invoice`, and invoice/payment views may gain `Issue Invoice  Record Payment` paths involving both payment events. Depending on flattening implementation, INV-44 can produce duplicated or branching payment behavior because two payments relate to one invoice. OTG gains Order–Invoice and Payment–Invoice interaction strength; ET–OT gains ev12–Order(B) and ev14–Invoice edges.

Activity frequencies need interpretation. Event-based frequency never changes: there remain three Pack Item, two Dispatch Package, one Issue Invoice, and two Record Payment events. Unique-object participation changes: Bundle A raises Package participation in Pack Item and Delivery participation in Dispatch Package; Bundle B raises ORD-B participation in Issue Invoice and INV-44 participation in Record Payment. Total object counts remain unchanged. Equivalent-execution clustering around Order may merge ORD-A and ORD-B more strongly under Bundle B because both share the same invoice/payment suffix; Bundle A preserves their operational asymmetry.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Linking ev11 uniquely to DEL-X because `N-42*` resembles `N-420` would falsely create a delivery-confirmation lifecycle and suppress the equally plausible DEL-Y interpretation.

2. Linking ev11 to both deliveries merely because both are on route NORTH would fabricate a consolidated confirmation event and create misleading Delivery flattening cases.

3. Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` solely because 130 + 100 = 230 would turn weak arithmetic evidence into a false many-order invoice and alter order-equivalence clustering.

4. Allocating PAY-2 to INV-44 solely because it equals ORD-A’s declared value could falsely represent a fully settled invoice and hide unapplied cash.

5. Adding `DEL-X carries PK-8` because both packages use NORTH would collapse two carrier references into one delivery and generate false package co-shipment behavior.

6. Inferring that IT-B1 belongs to PK-8 and then treating the inferred O2O edge as independent support for ev08–PK-8 would double-count circular evidence.

7. Reversing O2O qualifiers, such as storing `IT-B1 packs PK-8` or `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`, would corrupt directional object semantics even if the underlying association were plausible.

8. Adding ORD-B to ev11 merely because both orders belong to C17 would create an artificial order-delivery confirmation path and overstate customer-level portal data.
