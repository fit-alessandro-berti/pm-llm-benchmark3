# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **Intake and routing.** A1 can supply consistent case and entity identifiers to A2, A4 and the document workflow. Use the existing intake feed when it meets that standard. A2 can supply verified product and exposure to A5's checklist, A7's collateral routing, and the committee and four-eyes rules. A human-verified classification can supply those same inputs. Re-evaluate downstream requirements when product, exposure or entity changes. Use a stable application ID and version source records, approvals and documents.
- **Parallel branches and committee readiness.** The directly-follows graph (DFG) records Classification → KYC 7,250 times and Classification → Financial document review 3,940 times. Use work-item timestamps to recover overlap that sequential logging obscures. Run authorized KYC and document tasks concurrently. On the compliance branch, A3 can route hits and A4 can prepare mismatch evidence for specialist disposition. On the financial branch, A5 helps obtain a complete file before A6 produces reviewed inputs for credit analysis. Committee packaging is a join requiring current KYC status, normalized financials, credit notes and an approved valuation when required. A8 can assemble a draft, with release contingent on these inputs and an independent pack owner where required. Measure which prerequisite arrives last and whether it causes a missed committee cutoff.
- **External valuation.** The observed path runs from credit analysis to valuation request to external wait and onward to packaging. A7 can route cases using confirmed collateral requirements, partner availability, expertise and service commitments. Check which request steps bank policy permits before final credit notes, and start them early only when eligibility, scope and customer authorization are established. Keep internal acceptance of appraisals. Valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Working-capital cases, so measure the effect separately for collateralized variants.
- **Decision, signing and activation.** A9 uses the versioned Committee decision, or an authoritative approval where the bank permits a different route. It checks generated terms against that record. A8 is optional for A9. A10 requires the approved contract, verified signer and consent, and current KYC clearance before signing in any channel. A11 requires valid signing and completion of all activation controls. A12 can route failures at these checkpoints to accountable owners, record a disposition and return corrected cases for revalidation. Holds remain in force pending documented resolution.
- **People and integration.** Compliance owns screening and clearance. Reserve review time and backup coverage among the three specialists, given the handover dependency on two seniors. Assign analyst time to validation and credit exceptions, and reserve separate qualified owners for High-exposure credit analysis and packaging. Budget first-half deep core integration for A4's authoritative KYC status and disposition interface. Stage A6 through analyst-reviewed imports and schedule A9's decision-to-contract integration after month six. This uses one of the two permitted first-half slots and leaves room if A6's validated import requires a core interface. A5 can use the existing portal and document status feed.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick-win**

- **Cycle time and waiting.** Registration occurs at position two in 98% of cases, and Receive → Register occurs 11,880 times. Eight minutes of mean service and a 1.5-hour median queue make a visible local improvement plausible. The 31-hour mismatch wait and 28-hour credit-analysis wait can still dominate. On eligible cases, A10 can reduce part of the 44-hour median signing wait and the 21% reminder loop. Track activation's 8-hour median queue as signed cases arrive sooner.
- **Rework and handovers.** A1, A2 and A5 can reduce duplicate handling, incorrect routes and repeated Review → Request missing documents → Review handovers. Those two edges occur 3,300 and 2,800 times, respectively. Give the RM and customer a shared, consolidated list and a way to correct a request. KYC referrals and contract remediation still rely on their existing teams.
- **Resource demand and controls.** Back office and RMs gain time, while better routed and more complete files can increase demand for compliance, credit and collateral work. Pace releases against those teams' capacity. A2 can reduce wrong-control-path errors with verified classifications and holds on uncertain exposure. A10 can reduce opportunities for provisional signing when the gate covers all channels. Track attempted and completed violations against the reported 6.5% provisional signing and 1.1% remediation figures. Contract term mismatches and High-exposure staffing conflicts still need direct checks.

**Bottleneck/synchronization**

- **Cycle time and waiting.** Entity mismatch has a 31-hour median pre-activity wait and is on the critical path in 82% of the cases where it occurs. A4 can shorten the time to an actionable specialist review. A5 and A6 address delays on the parallel financial branch, including the 18-hour median document-review queue and 20-hour normalization queue. The effect at packaging depends on the last prerequisite and whether earlier readiness catches a committee cutoff. The 2,350 Valuation request → External wait transitions, 58-hour median external wait and weekly High-exposure committee schedule limit gains in those variants. Estimate savings from case timelines and branch overlap because activity medians describe different sets of cases.
- **Rework and handovers.** The 1,160 Mismatch resolution → KYC transitions identify where a complete evidence handoff can prevent repeat investigation. For the 380 Mismatch resolution → Contract preparation transitions, check actual clearance timestamps and case state to establish whether the gate was met. A5 targets repeated document requests. A6 delivers validated inputs through the Normalize financial statements → Credit analysis path, observed 5,000 times. A9 targets the Contract preparation → Remediate control issue and return edges, observed 650 and 510 times. Start with eligible Credit-line renewal contracts, given 14% contract remediation versus 8.5% overall.
- **Resource demand and controls.** A4 reduces specialist preparation work, while better detection and routing can initially increase referrals. A6 frees time across nine analysts if extraction saves more work than validation adds. The 110-minute mean credit-analysis step still demands capacity. Expect earlier and potentially lumpier releases to the four-person Legal ops team and to signing. A4 strengthens evidence for clearance, and A9 blocks contract releases with mismatched terms. Keep separate High-exposure pack ownership and the signing gate in force.

**Compliance-first**

- **Cycle time and waiting.** A3 and A4 can reduce time spent awaiting triage or evidence. Use the KYC → Mismatch resolution edge, observed 1,750 times, and the return to KYC to track referral age and clearance time. A9 and A12 can shorten Contract preparation → Remediation → Contract preparation loops. Early detection can increase waiting for held files. The normalization and credit queues, required appraisal and committee cadence still govern many cases.
- **Rework and handovers.** Send a referral or control failure with its reason, evidence, accountable owner and required disposition. This can reduce duplicate investigation by KYC ops, specialists, Legal and operations. Route recurring policy or data defects to their owners. Track deliberate handovers for independent review separately from avoidable returns for missing evidence.
- **Resource demand and controls.** Expect a near-term rise in screening reviews, overrides requiring verification and remediation work. Staff backups for the senior specialists and reserve independent pack owners for month end, when High-exposure four-eyes violations reached 5.8%. The portfolio offers the broadest tracking of attempted prohibited releases, wrong terms and recurring exceptions. Measure actual violations and missed issues from audit samples as well as recorded exceptions, since better detection can raise the latter.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **False negatives and false positives.** Test suggestions in shadow mode against independently reviewed outcomes. For KYC and entity matching, send ambiguous or material cases to qualified reviewers and preserve authorized human adjudication of hits and mismatches. Sample negative and no-action recommendations as well as referrals, including known difficult cases. Measure missed issues, referral precision and reviewer load before changing thresholds. Keep records distinct when duplicate matching is uncertain.
- **Data quality and model drift.** Reconcile extracted financials against source pages, periods, currencies, units and totals before use in credit analysis. Send unreadable or unsupported files to manual extraction. Track errors, overrides and shifts in inputs or model scores by segment, product, format and channel. Test updates against representative and adverse cases, version releases, and suspend affected rules or models when performance falls outside agreed limits.
- **Auditability and responsibility.** Record the case ID, evidence references, rule or model version, suggestion and rationale, actors, timestamps, any override and its reason, and the final accountable decision. Keep an access-controlled, tamper-evident history linked to decision and contract versions. Compliance, Credit and Legal retain their approval responsibilities. Assign Legal ownership of rule changes, templates and effective dates. Test mappings for approved terms such as amount, rate, tenor and covenants, including amended approvals.
- **Signing and four-eyes.** Verify current KYC clearance, approved contract version, signer authority and consent at signing. Enforce the hold on portal, RM and manual routes. For High-exposure SME, compare the authenticated identities of the accountable credit analyst and pack completer before pack release. Send conflicts to a qualified alternate and record both actors. Test shared-account and service-account cases. Reserve separate coverage for month end, and revalidate affected artifacts and approvals after material changes.
- **Exceptions and retries.** Give each exception a severity, reason code, owner, turnaround target and escalation path. Provide a staffed manual fallback subject to the same release checks. Forecast peak demand using arrivals, referral and repeat rates, review minutes and backup coverage. Throttle automated releases if queue aging or workload breaches agreed limits. Reconcile retries and use idempotent writes to prevent duplicate instructions, contracts or activations.
- **Customer and partner exceptions.** Let RMs correct checklists, offer assisted submission or signing, and cap and stop reminders once the customer responds. Track complaints and abandonment alongside completion. For valuers, agree on quality and turnaround standards, share only required data, and give late or disputed cases a named owner. Require an accepted valuation at the committee readiness check when applicable.

# 7. Final recommendation

Choose **A4, A5, A6 and A9**, in that order, with A4 and A5 in the first wave. A4 addresses a concentrated critical-path delay and strengthens evidence for KYC clearance. A5 reduces repeat document requests, and A6 frees analyst capacity to absorb more complete files while improving committee readiness. A9 then addresses a material late-stage control problem, starting with eligible Credit-line renewals. For collateral-heavy Equipment finance, measure how often external valuation is the last prerequisite after the pilot. That evidence should inform an investment in A7, especially where faster dispatch or turnaround would help cases catch a committee cutoff.

Defer A1 as the first investment. Receipt is the highest-frequency activity and is already digital for 72% of cases. Registration offers a visible win at 11,880 occurrences, eight minutes of mean service and a 1.5-hour median queue. The 12-person back office has spare capacity after 15:00, while entity mismatches wait a median of 31 hours and depend on a three-person specialist pool, and credit analysis waits a median of 28 hours. Faster registration would save back-office work and send cases sooner into those constrained queues. Revisit A1 when case-level evidence shows registration or duplicate handling materially affects total cycle time, or when downstream teams can absorb the additional inflow.
