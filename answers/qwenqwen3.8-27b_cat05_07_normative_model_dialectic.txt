**Burden-of-proof approach**

For a finding of **noncompliance**, the following should be established:

1. A normative rule applied to the case type.
2. The case was in scope of that rule.
3. Reliable event, attribute, or documentary evidence shows a violation.
4. No valid exception, pilot, emergency, or scope rule applies.
5. Any data gap is not material to the conclusion, or the gap is resolved by independent evidence.

If a material fact depends on unreliable logging, ambiguous scope, unresolved identity, or missing eligibility evidence, the case should generally be classified as **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE** or **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, not automatically noncompliant.

For acceptance of an **exception**, the exception must be affirmatively proven:

1. Valid written or formally recognized authority.
2. Effective date/time window.
3. Scope matching the case.
4. Eligibility snapshot at the relevant time.
5. Required conditions met, such as sanctions before activation, spend caps, monitoring, retrospective approval, or quality SLA.
6. Sufficient evidence of those conditions.

Informal belief, operational convenience, imminent amendment, or desirable outcomes do not satisfy the burden for an exception.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest process-aware prosecution is that the operation has material noncompliance in exception governance, high-risk activation controls, resource segregation, and notification evidence, even though M0 is not a complete model.

**1. Formal authority favors CS-9/M0 unless an exception is proven.**  
CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. M0 is the normative control-flow model. PD-3 is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer’s later statement that she did not approve weakening CS-9 undermines the view that PD-3 is a clean, standing exception. Even if PD-3 is treated as a valid temporary overlay, it only covers eligible LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups during 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. The aggregate evidence shows 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases, 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes, and 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in early August. SUP-703 is the most serious example: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier was processed under the pilot despite ineligibility, had no manual approval, and made a first purchase of EUR 180,000.

**2. Core activation controls were violated in high-risk cases.**  
CS-9 requires sanctions and quality before activation, and manual approval before activation, unless a valid overlay applies. SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check, with no emergency or pilot. The absence of transactions before sanctions clearance does not remove the control violation; the activation itself occurred before the required risk gate. SUP-705 followed the emergency pattern after FD-7 expired, with no written extension. The factual continuation of flood recovery does not prove formal authority. SUP-703 similarly activated a high-risk supplier under an inapplicable pilot.

**3. M0 hides resource and conflict-of-interest violations.**  
M0 is control-flow only. CS-9 requires requester and manual approver to be different humans. SUP-712 fits M0 but the requester and approver resolve to the same human through aliases. No explicit rule prohibits the relationship, but COI policy requires disclosure, and none was recorded. These are not merely model defects; they are governance failures that M0 cannot see.

**4. Notification evidence is weakened by the CRM migration.**  
M0 requires Notify_Requester before Close_Case. After the CRM migration, notification missingness in the onboarding log rose from 2% to 29%. SUP-706 has no notification event in the onboarding log. CRM contains a message, but the case link is a mutable text field entered two days later. That is weak evidence. The prosecution should not assume notification occurred merely because a CRM message exists. Stable `notification_id` linkage and system timestamps are required.

**5. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.**  
Request_More_Info is desirable, and the 47 observed RMI cases did not activate before requested evidence arrived. But M0 has no RMI loop, and no formal policy amendment exists. Operations also wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch, but no permanent amendment exists. SUP-714 occurred after PD-3 expired, based on belief that permanent approval was imminent. Belief in imminent policy change is not authority. The six post-pilot auto cases show normalization of a temporary exception.

**6. Genuine model/data defects exist, but they do not erase the violations.**  
The prosecution must acknowledge that M0 is incomplete: it lacks RMI, auto-approval, emergency provisional activation, reactivation, rejected-lead scope, label evolution, and CRM notification linkage. SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are better explained by model, scope, or data defects than by misconduct. SUP-702 and SUP-706 also have data-quality dimensions. However, those defects do not explain SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-712, SUP-714, SUP-715, the ineligible auto cases, or the post-pilot auto cases. The operation is therefore materially noncompliant in important respects, even if M0 should not be used as the sole conformance oracle.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest process-aware defense is that M0 is obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, or applied to inadequate data, and that many apparent deviations are not business misconduct.

**1. M0 does not represent the actual controlled process.**  
M0 only models a standard manual route. It does not model:
- Request_More_Info loops;
- PD-3 low-risk auto-approval;
- FD-7 emergency provisional activation;
- reactivation;
- rejected-lead pre-screen scope;
- label evolution such as `QA_Clearance`;
- CRM notification evidence;
- resource or conflict-of-interest rules.

Therefore, a high M0 deviation rate is expected and should not be read as a high misconduct rate.

**2. Concurrency and overlays are misrepresented by M0.**  
M0 requires Quality_Review before the decision and before activation. But PD-3 permits quality up to two business days after provisional activation for eligible low-risk cases. FD-7 permits provisional activation before quality and manual approval for critical relief suppliers during a defined emergency window. SUP-702 is within the PD-3 window, appears eligible, has complete pilot eligibility fields, quality review the next business day, and a stable CRM notification. SUP-704 is within FD-7, has sanctions before activation, spend below EUR 20,000, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality and approval the next business day. These are not ordinary M0 deviations; they are policy overlays that M0 cannot express.

**3. Request_More_Info is a model defect, not a control failure.**  
Operations uses RMI cycles before decision, and compliance agrees this is desirable. The aggregate evidence shows 47 RMI cases and none activated before requested evidence arrived. SUP-707 obtained missing certificates before approval. M0 cannot express the loop, so the trace appears nonconformant even though the behavior is safer than M0. Treating SUP-707 as noncompliant would punish a desirable control.

**4. Scope is ambiguous for rejected leads and reactivations.**  
SUP-709 is a rejected lead that failed sanctions pre-screen and did not receive Quality_Review. CS-9 allows abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads but does not say whether those leads are M0 cases. The process owner says rejected leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance. Until scope is clarified, SUP-709 should not be treated as a clear CS-9 violation.

SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier. Prior quality certification remained valid. CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. The case lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review, but that may be correct for reactivation if prior quality is still valid and sanctions are current.

**5. Data collection changed and is not fully reliable.**  
From 2026-07-01, notifications moved to CRM. Only about 70% are mirrored into the onboarding log. SUP-702 has a CRM notification with a stable `notification_id`. SUP-706 has a CRM message but a mutable text link entered later. SUP-711 has a duplicated Register_Supplier event with the same source transaction ID, likely a migration artifact. SUP-713 uses `QA_Clearance` instead of `Quality_Review`, with audit evidence of equivalent content and a mapping approved shortly afterward. These are data-quality and model-alignment issues, not necessarily process violations.

**6. Trace deviation is not the same as business misconduct.**  
A trace can deviate from M0 because:
- the model is too narrow;
- the case is out of scope;
- the event label changed;
- the event was not mirrored;
- the event was duplicated;
- an overlay applied.

The defense should not be forced to prove misconduct for every M0 mismatch.

**7. The defense must acknowledge genuine risk and limits on informal practice.**  
The defense cannot excuse:
- SUP-703, an ineligible high-risk supplier under the pilot with a EUR 180,000 first purchase;
- SUP-708, activation before sanctions clearance with no overlay;
- SUP-705, emergency-style activation after FD-7 expired without written extension;
- SUP-714, auto-approval after PD-3 expired with no permanent amendment;
- SUP-712, same requester/approver through aliases;
- SUP-715, missing COI disclosure;
- the 14 clearly ineligible auto cases, 9 missing-eligibility cases, and 6 post-pilot auto cases.

Informal practice, operational belief, or desirable outcomes cannot override formal authority, expiry, eligibility, or evidence requirements.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should separate control flow from data rules, resource rules, policy overlays, and scope rules.

**Layer 0: Scope and case type**

Define explicit case types:

- **Onboarding**: new supplier entering the onboarding process after intake/pre-screen acceptance.
- **Rejected lead / pre-screen**: abbreviated intake case where a lead fails early screening, such as sanctions pre-screen.
- **Reactivation**: supplier previously onboarded and closed, now being reactivated.
- **Withdrawal / suspension**: active supplier being suspended, withdrawn, or closed.

Scope rules:

- M1 onboarding model applies to onboarding cases.
- Rejected leads use an abbreviated route: intake/register, sanctions pre-screen, reject, notify, close. Quality_Review is not required unless policy says otherwise.
- Reactivations do not necessarily require Register_Supplier. They require current Sanctions_Check, quality revalidation only if prior certification is expired or invalid, and approval according to risk.
- Withdrawals are separate from onboarding and require notification and closure, but not new activation controls unless reactivation is requested.

**Layer 1: Revised control-flow model**

The control-flow model should express:

- Standard route:
  - Register;
  - concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review;
  - optional repeatable Request_More_Info before decision/activation;
  - Manual_Procurement_Approval;
  - Activate_Supplier;
  - Notify_Requester;
  - Close_Case.

- Low-risk auto-approval route:
  - Register;
  - Sanctions_Check and Master_Data_Validation before activation;
  - optional Request_More_Info;
  - Auto_Approve;
  - Activate_Supplier;
  - Quality_Review after activation within policy SLA;
  - Notify_Requester;
  - Close_Case.

- Emergency/provisional route:
  - Register;
  - Sanctions_Check before activation;
  - optional Request_More_Info;
  - provisional Activate_Supplier;
  - Quality_Review and retrospective Manual_Procurement_Approval within two business days;
  - Notify_Requester;
  - Close_Case.

- Rejection route:
  - Register/intake;
  - Sanctions_Check or pre-screen;
  - Reject_Supplier;
  - Notify_Requester;
  - Close_Case.

**Layer 2: DECLARE / log-skeleton-style constraints**

Use temporal/order constraints for rules that are easier to express outside POWL:

- `Sanctions_Check` before `Activate_Supplier` for all activation cases.
- Standard route: `Quality_Review` before `Activate_Supplier`.
- Standard route: `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
- Auto route: `Master_Data_Validation` before `Activate_Supplier`.
- Auto route: `Quality_Review` within two business days after `Activate_Supplier`.
- Emergency route: `Quality_Review` within two business days after `Activate_Supplier`.
- Emergency route: `Manual_Procurement_Approval` within two business days after `Activate_Supplier`.
- `Notify_Requester` before `Close_Case`.
- If `Request_More_Info` is issued, activation must not occur before requested evidence is received.

**Layer 3: pandas / data checks**

Use data checks for attributes that POWL cannot enforce:

- Deduplicate events by `case_id + source_transaction_id`.
- Canonicalize labels using a versioned label mapping table.
- Validate eligibility snapshots:
  - risk level;
  - country;
  - approved corporate group;
  - pilot eligibility;
  - emergency eligibility;
  - critical relief flag.
- Validate date windows:
  - PD-3 window: 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31;
  - FD-7 window: 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12.
- Validate spend:
  - emergency spend before retrospective approval  EUR 20,000;
  - no unexplained high spend before approval.
- Validate monitoring:
  - daily transaction monitoring flag for emergency cases.
- Validate notification:
  - `notification_id` present;
  - CRM case foreign key or stable link;
  - notification timestamp before close timestamp.
- Validate resource rules:
  - requester ID  approver ID after alias resolution;
  - COI disclosure present where required;
  - delegation evidence present where used.

**Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules**

Organizational controls should enforce:

- requester and manual approver are different humans;
- alias resolution is versioned and auditable;
- delegation of approval is explicitly recorded;
- COI disclosures are captured for related parties, including spouses or same reporting line;
- quality reviewer and approver relationships are disclosed where policy requires.

**Layer 5: Policy-overlay governance**

Pilots and emergencies should be governed by explicit overlays:

- Each overlay has:
  - rule ID;
  - authority;
  - effective start/end;
  - scope;
  - eligibility conditions;
  - required controls;
  - monitoring requirements;
  - evidence requirements;
  - expiry behavior.
- Pilots cannot be extended by operational belief.
- Emergencies cannot be extended by factual continuation alone.
- Permanent changes require formal amendment to CS-9/M1.
- Overlays must be attached to cases by rule/version ID.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**Limitation and approximation**

POWL is not suitable for enforcing all amount, date, identity, authority, eligibility, or evidence conditions. The code below therefore constructs a revised POWL for the main control-flow skeleton and includes a normalized policy-overlay table and event/data-collection proposal as Python data structures.

The Request_More_Info mechanism is modeled as an optional zero-or-more loop using `Operator.LOOP`. This is the most defensible POWL approximation, but it does not by itself enforce that requested evidence arrived before activation. That condition should be enforced by a DECLARE/log-skeleton or pandas check.

Route-specific transitions intentionally reuse the same business labels, such as `Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, and `Activate_Supplier`, while being separate POWL nodes. The policy overlay and case-type data should be used to select the correct route during conformance checking.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def optional_rmi(suffix: str) -> OperatorPOWL:
    """
    Approximate a repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism.

    This creates:
      XOR(
        LOOP(Request_More_Info),
        SilentTransition(No_Request_More_Info_<suffix>)
      )

    The silent branch allows zero RMI events.
    The loop branch allows repeated RMI events.
    This does not enforce that requested evidence arrived before activation;
    that is a data/DECLARE rule.
    """
    rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
    rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[rmi])
    no_rmi = SilentTransition(label=f"No_Request_More_Info_{suffix}")
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[rmi_loop, no_rmi])


# Root intake
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard manual route
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_rmi = optional_rmi("standard")

# Standard route requires concurrent sanctions and quality checks.
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality, std_rmi])

std_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

std_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_approval, std_activate])
std_branch.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_approval)
std_branch.order.add_edge(std_approval, std_activate)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_master = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_rmi = optional_rmi("auto")

# PD-3-style route: sanctions and master-data validation before activation.
auto_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_master, auto_rmi])

auto_approval = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_pre, auto_approval, auto_activate, auto_quality]
)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_pre, auto_approval)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_approval, auto_activate)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_quality)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency / provisional route
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_rmi = optional_rmi("emergency")

# FD-7-style route: sanctions before provisional activation.
em_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[em_sanctions, em_rmi])

em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

# Retrospective quality and approval may occur after provisional activation.
em_post = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[em_quality, em_approval])

em_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[em_pre, em_activate, em_post])
em_branch.order.add_edge(em_pre, em_activate)
em_branch.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_post)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Rejection route
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_supplier = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

rej_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_sanctions, rej_supplier])
rej_branch.order.add_edge(rej_sanctions, rej_supplier)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route decision and closure
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[std_branch, auto_branch, em_branch, rej_branch]
)

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```
