# 1. System boundary and assumptions

### Inside the system boundary (Gate-to-Customer Delivery)
The analysis covers operational impacts directly captured or proxied in the enriched event log across the 12,000-order baseline:
* **In-house manufacturing energy (`energy_kwh`):** Electricity and thermal energy consumed across `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, `inspection_cell`, and warehouse handling/picking.
* **Direct process scrap and packaging mass (`material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`):** Machining swarf, rejected parts from `Rework production` (470 cases), discarded materials from `Rework packaging` (620 cases), standard vs. reinforced dunnage, and parts rendered obsolete when large batches remain unshipped beyond 14 days.
* **Outbound and exception transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`):** Well-to-wheel/tank-to-wheel emissions logged across `parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van` (including empty backhauls where logged), `consolidated_truck`, and secondary shipments from `Partial shipment` (1,010 cases).

### Outside the system boundary
* **Upstream embodied carbon of raw materials:** Extraction, smelting, and forging of metal blanks prior to `CNC production`, as well as upstream manufacturing of corrugated/reinforced packaging materials.
* **Customer-side downtime and end-of-life impacts:** Environmental or safety consequences at the industrial customer’s facility if a `Critical-downtime` part arrives late (e.g., flaring, idling plants), and end-of-life recycling of replaced spare parts.
* **Facility overhead and capital goods:** HVAC, lighting, machine-tool manufacturing, and carrier fleet manufacturing.

### Key assumptions and remaining uncertainties
* **Order-level flattening distortion:** Because multi-line orders are flattened to single cases, attributes like `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, and `material_scrap_kg` aggregate heterogeneous items. A single missing line item can trigger `Partial shipment` or `Batch wait` for an entire order.
* **Unlogged reverse-logistics impacts:** Customer damage returns (correlated with Quality Policy Q1 violations) generate reverse transport and replacement production that may appear as new orders rather than within the original case’s `transport_kgco2e` or `Close` boundary.
* **Grid and carrier factor staticness:** `energy_kwh` does not reflect time-of-day grid carbon intensity, and `transport_kgco2e` uses route-mode averages that may understate partially empty `consolidated_truck` runs or overstate belly-cargo `parcel_air` variations.
* **Hidden Embodied Carbon Multiplier:** Although upstream metal production is outside the direct log, avoiding 1 kg of `material_scrap_kg` or obsolete inventory carries a high unlogged lifecycle benefit, meaning scrap and obsolescence must be weighted more heavily than raw mass alone suggests.

# 3. Intervention options

### 1. Priority Picking + Ground Default for Standard-Stock Service-Contract (S4 + S1)
* **Target path/segment:** `Standard-stock Service-contract` (16% of volume; 1,920 orders), specifically targeting the 22% SP1 non-conformance rate contributing to `Stock -> standard pack -> air` (620 `Package standard -> Ship air` transitions).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Replaces `parcel_air` (18.5 kgCO2e/order) with `parcel_ground` (3.6 kgCO2e/order) by prioritizing warehouse picking and `Availability check` queue jump, saving up to 24h internally so ground shipping still meets `promised_lead_time_hours` with >85% probability.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Maintains the 5% late-delivery baseline by trading warehouse idle time for transit time; preserves `parcel_air` as an R1 fallback during ground carrier disruptions.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Timestamp delta `Receive order -> Package standard`, SP1 violation rate on `Standard-stock Service-contract`, and late-delivery % on shifted cases.

### 2. Twice-Daily Consolidated Truck Departures for Non-Urgent Stock (S5)
* **Target path/segment:** `Standard-stock Regular` (39% of volume; 31h median cycle time) currently flowing through `Package standard -> Ship ground` (5,100 cases) vs. `Consolidated truck` (850 cases).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Shifts eligible `parcel_ground` orders with >30h slack into `consolidated_truck`, cutting outbound `transport_kgco2e` by 35–55% per shifted order without altering `energy_kwh` or packaging.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Adds a 12–24h consolidation wait, pushing median cycle time up from 31h toward ~45–50h, which remains safely below `Regular` promised lead times if gated by slack; improves multi-carrier resilience (R1) by maintaining active LTL/truck lanes alongside parcel carriers.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Transition ratio `Package standard -> Consolidated truck` vs. `Ship ground`, dwell time prior to truck departure, and `Standard-stock Regular` late-delivery rate (keep <=7%).

### 3. Slack-Gated Dynamic Batching on CNC_A and Heat Treatment (S2)
* **Target path/segment:** `Made-to-order` cases entering `Plan production -> Batch wait` (3,020 cases) and `CNC production -> Heat treatment` (1,820 cases).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Groups orders dynamically to approach efficient thresholds (`batch_size` >=12 on `CNC_A`, dropping unit energy from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh; `batch_size` >10 on `heat_treat`) *only* for actual demand or high-certainty short-horizon demand, while enforcing strict segment-specific maximum wait caps (e.g., max 16h wait for `Service-contract`, 28h for `Regular`) so batches release early rather than forcing downstream air expediting or speculative overproduction.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Prevents the 38h median `Batch wait` tail and mitigates the 1.7x late-delivery multiplier at `heat_treat`, while curbing the 6% 14-day batch obsolescence rate.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Batch wait` duration distribution, `batch_size` histograms on `CNC_A` and `heat_treat`, downstream `expedite_flag` activation after `Batch wait`, and 14-day unshipped batch inventory %.

### 4. Slack-Aware Routing from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3)
* **Target path/segment:** `Made-to-order` cases routed to `CNC_B`—especially the pathological path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` (5% of all orders; 600 cases, 32% late, 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Redirects `Made-to-order` jobs with sufficient due-date slack from `CNC_B` (18% rework rate) to `CNC_A` (9% rework rate), halving `Rework production` loops on shifted orders and eliminating double machining energy and 0.9–1.8 kg scrap spikes.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Increases initial machining queue/processing time slightly, but cuts p90 tail delays (160h on the `CNC_B` rework path) and improves product quality; keeps `CNC_B` free as surge/emergency capacity (`Emergency production -> CNC production`, 720 cases) for resilience.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** `machine_group` split by `case_type`, queue wait at `CNC_A`, `rework_flag` rate per machine, and `CNC_B` utilization during emergency spikes.

### 5. Dedicated Inspection Quality Gate for High-Rework Combinations (S7)
* **Target path/segment:** `CNC production -> Inspection` (1,710) and `Heat treatment -> Inspection` (1,780), specifically targeting `CNC_B` outputs and heavy/complex `Made-to-order` parts before `Package reinforced`.
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** De-rushes `inspection_cell` queues via workload leveling and mandatory automated/structured checks on high-risk part-machine pairs, preventing escaped defects that trigger customer returns (avoiding full round-trip `transport_kgco2e` + replacement manufacturing) and catching out-of-spec drift early on `CNC_B` before a full batch is scrapped.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Adds minor inspection cycle time (~1–3h) but substantially reduces field failures, Q1-related return loops, and downstream packaging rework.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** `inspection_cell` queue wait vs. active service duration (spotting rushed inspections < threshold time), `Inspection -> Rework production` rate, Q1 conformance, and post-delivery return flags.

### 6. Pre-Pack Compatibility Rules & Q1 Automated Verification (S6)
* **Target path/segment:** `Package standard -> Rework packaging` (260 cases) and `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging` (360 cases)—620 total packaging rework loops (5.2% of all orders).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Embeds part-geometry, weight (>8 kg Q1 rule), and `route_mode` check into WMS/MES *before* packing begins, eliminating discarded `package_material_kg` from incompatible first-attempt packing while preventing under-packed heavy MTO parts (preserving the 40% reduction in damage returns).
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Eliminates rework delay at the shipping dock, reduces damage returns, and avoids missed carrier cutoffs that otherwise force `Ship air` upgrades.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Transition counts into `Rework packaging`, `package_material_kg` per order, Q1 violation rate, and damage-return rate.

### 7. Two-Tier Triage of Critical-Downtime Orders (S9)
* **Target path/segment:** `Critical-downtime Service-contract` (11% of volume; 1,320 orders, 38.0 kgCO2e/order) and `Availability check -> Emergency production` (760 cases), addressing the 31% of `Dedicated van` trips (out of 620) lacking SP2 justification.
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Splits intake into **Tier-1 True Line-Stop** (verified active plant stoppage; retains `Emergency production` and `Dedicated van` / `parcel_air`) and **Tier-2 Precautionary Urgent** (low customer safety stock, 24–36h actual tolerance; routed via priority stock/CNC queue + fast `parcel_ground` or regional express courier).
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Protects the 15h median cycle time and 4% late rate on true downtime events while freeing `Emergency production` and van capacity from false emergencies, directly strengthening shop-floor and fleet resilience.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Share of Tier-1 vs. Tier-2 sub-classifications, SP2 justification completeness (target 100%), and Tier-1 SLA adherence.

### 8. Shared Emergency Courier Lanes & Backhaul Matching (S8)
* **Target path/segment:** `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` (620 cases; 52.0 kgCO2e on the emergency path due to empty return legs).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Replaces single-tenant `dedicated_van` dispatches on dense industrial corridors with co-loaded/shared time-critical courier networks and schedules vendor/return pickups on backhauls, cutting empty-mile `transport_kgco2e`.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Minimal transit-time penalty (+1–2h) when restricted to Tier-2 urgent or multi-drop corridors; increases R1 resilience by onboarding vetted regional time-critical carriers rather than relying solely on dedicated vans.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** `transport_kgco2e` per emergency km, empty-return rate on `dedicated_van`, and `Dedicated van -> Confirm delivery` cycle time.

### 9. Availability-Check Hold Window to Curb Partial Shipments (S11)
* **Target path/segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010 cases; 7% share path shows 79h median cycle time, 24% late delivery, 7.2 kgCO2e—double `Stock -> ground` at 3.6 kgCO2e—plus 60 `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` cases).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Modifies `Availability check` rules so multi-line `Regular` orders missing an item due for replenishment within 24–36h are held for single complete shipment (`Package standard -> Ship ground`/`Consolidated truck`), halving `transport_kgco2e` and eliminating duplicate `package_material_kg`.
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Eliminates double customer handling and lowers administrative noise; for backorders exceeding the lead-time slack threshold, proactively offers line-level split or date negotiation to prevent the 60 backorder cancellations.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` frequency, `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` count, shipments per order, and complete-order OTD.

### 10. Carbon-Aware Promise-Date Negotiation for Regular MTO (S10)
* **Target path/segment:** `Made-to-order Regular` (22% of volume; 2,640 orders, currently suffering 18% late delivery and 410 upfront `Availability check -> Cancel due to delay` events across the system).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** At order intake, offers `Regular` MTO customers a realistic +24–48h `promised_lead_time_hours` option (paired with minor commercial/priority certainty incentives), creating the scheduling slack needed to batch cleanly on `CNC_A`, avoid `CNC_B`, and ship via `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` instead of `MTO -> CNC immediately -> air` (24.0 kWh, 23.0 kgCO2e).
* **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Cuts the 18% late-delivery rate by setting achievable promises, lowers `Cancel due to delay` events, and smooths shop-floor peak loads.
* **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Opt-in rate, `promised_lead_time_hours` distribution for `MTO Regular`, `MTO -> air` share, and `Cancel due to delay` rate.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching Energy Savings Displaced into Air Freight Expediting (Batching -> Transport):**
   Pushing `CNC_A` batch sizes to 12–20 saves ~1.5 kWh per item (2.8 down to 1.3 kWh), and batching `heat_treat` >10 saves thermal energy. However, letting `Batch wait` stretch to 38h consumes due-date slack (1.7x late risk at heat treat). If planners compensate at the shipping dock by upgrading a single delayed order from `parcel_ground` (5.8 kgCO2e) to `parcel_air` (23.0 kgCO2e), the +17.2 kgCO2e transport penalty wipes out the grid-electricity carbon savings of an entire machining batch.
2. **Over-Batching Driven Forecast Obsolescence (Batching -> Obsolescence & Scrap):**
   Padding MTO or hybrid production batches with forecasted stock items to hit `batch_size >= 12` triggers the documented 6% obsolescence rate (items unshipped after 14 days). Scrapping just 1 item out of a 16-item batch wastes 100% of its machining/heat-treat energy plus its high upstream embodied metal carbon, neutralizing the per-unit energy efficiency of the remaining 15 items.
3. **Packaging Light-Weighting Triggering Damage Returns & Remakes (Packaging -> Rework & Transport):**
   Attempting to cut `package_material_kg` by relaxing `Package reinforced` (which uses 2x packaging material) on heavy (>8 kg) `Made-to-order` parts violates Q1 and forfeits the 40% reduction in damage-related returns. A single damaged heavy MTO part incurs reverse shipping + replacement `CNC production` (16–32 kWh, 0.7–1.8 kg metal scrap) + second outbound shipping—far exceeding the saved cardboard/foam.
4. **Aggressive Ground/Consolidation Shifting Causing Buffer Erosion & Emergency Rebounds (Transport -> Service & Resilience):**
   Forcing `consolidated_truck` (+12–24h wait) or `parcel_ground` (+24h transit) onto borderline-slack orders leaves zero buffer for minor warehouse or carrier hiccups. When a truck is delayed, `Service-contract` customers experience stockouts and subsequently escalate replacement orders as `Critical-downtime` (`Dedicated van` at 52.0 kgCO2e + `Emergency production` at 7.0 kWh), creating an emissions spike larger than the original ground savings.
5. **CNC_A Queue Overload Triggering Rushed Inspections or Cancellations (Rework Avoidance -> Service & Quality):**
   Shifting too much `Made-to-order` volume from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` without slack gating saturates `CNC_A` queues. Downstream, late batches hit `inspection_cell` in waves, inducing rushed inspections that miss defects (causing packaging rework or field returns) or increasing `Cancel due to delay` (already 530 cases), where work-in-progress energy and materials already expended become pure waste.
6. **Customer SLA Gaming and Order Splitting (Process Rules -> Customer Behavior):**
   If `Regular` or `Precautionary Urgent` tiers become noticeably slower due to consolidation waits (S5), partial-shipment holds (S11), or Tier-2 triage (S9), customers and sales reps learn to game intake rules—either misclassifying routine orders as Tier-1 `Critical-downtime` to unlock `Dedicated van` shipping, or splitting multi-line orders into separate single-line orders to bypass the `Partial shipment` hold rule (S11), rebounding parcel counts and emissions.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Rather than pursuing lowest-emission extremes that break SLAs, implement an integrated **Slack-Synchronized Flow Redesign** structured into three operational streams plus a governance backbone. The core principle is **internal time harvesting**: eliminating internal waste loops (picking delays, rework, dock repacking) creates time slack that safely funds low-carbon batching and surface transport.

### Stream 1: Fulfill-from-Stock Synchronization (Interventions S4+S1, S5, S11)
* **For `Standard-stock Service-contract` (16%):** Implement **S4+S1**—grant immediate priority warehouse picking at `Availability check` and default to `parcel_ground` whenever `promised_lead_time_hours` can be met with >85% probability (enforcing SP1). Retain `parcel_air` strictly for genuine <24h slack cases and R1 carrier-disruption backups.
* **For `Standard-stock Regular` (39%):** Pair **S11** (hold multi-line orders up to 24–36h when backordered items have confirmed inbound receipts to cut the 1,010 `Partial shipment` split paths) with **S5** (route complete orders with >36h slack into twice-daily `consolidated_truck` departures). Add an automated multi-order merge rule by customer address/day to prevent order-splitting gaming.

### Stream 2: First-Time-Right MTO Manufacturing & Slack Creation (Interventions S10, S2, S3, S7, S6)
* **At Intake (`MTO Regular`, 22%):** Deploy **S10** (carbon-aware promise-date negotiation offering +24–48h lead time for reliability/commercial credits) to reduce the 18% late rate and build upfront scheduling slack.
* **In Shop-Floor Scheduling:** Pair **S3** (route MTO jobs to low-rework `CNC_A` [9% rework] whenever slack permits, reserving `CNC_B` for true fast-track/emergency surges) with **S2** (dynamic batching targeting `batch_size >= 12` on `CNC_A` and `>10` on `heat_treat`, strictly bounded by **hard segment wait-time caps**—max 16h wait for `Service-contract`, 28h for `Regular`—and a **zero-speculative-padding rule** barring unconfirmed forecast items to eliminate the 6% obsolescence waste).
* **At Quality & Packing:** Implement **S7** (protected inspection time + mandatory checks on remaining `CNC_B` and complex parts) and **S6** (automated WMS pre-pack compatibility and Q1 >8 kg `Package reinforced` enforcement). Cutting the 470 production-rework and 620 packaging-rework loops recovers days of cycle time on tail orders (currently 156–180h p90) and protects the 40% reduction in heavy-part damage returns.

### Stream 3: Tiered Emergency Response & Resilient Transport (Interventions S9, S8, S12)
* **At Emergency Intake (`Critical-downtime`, 11%):** Deploy **S9** to separate verified **Tier-1 Line-Stop** orders (which keep unrestricted `Emergency production` on freed-up `CNC_B` capacity and immediate dispatch, preserving the 15h median cycle time and 4% late rate) from **Tier-2 Precautionary Urgent** orders (closing the 31% SP2 justification gap and shifting them to priority `parcel_ground` or **S8** shared time-critical regional couriers with backhaul matching).
* **Resilience Preservation (R1):** Maintaining active contracts across `consolidated_truck`, `parcel_ground`, shared emergency couriers, `parcel_air`, and Tier-1 `dedicated_van` guarantees >=2 viable routes per `Service-contract` lane during disruptions.

2. **A/B Lane & Machine Cell Pilot (Weeks 5–12):** Roll out S5 (`consolidated_truck`) on two regional corridors and S2/S3/S7 on half of MTO part families. Use process mining to compare variant distributions, DFG loop frequencies, and outcome metrics before full scale-up:
   * **DFG Path Shifts:** Verify statistically significant drops in `Package standard -> Ship air` (from 620), `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (from 1,010), `Inspection -> Rework production` (from 470), `Package -> Rework packaging` (from 620), and `Cancel due to delay` (from 530).

### Continuous Conformance & Segment Service Monitoring
Embed automated process-mining conformance checks refreshed daily:

### Exception Governance (Intervention S12)
Deploy the **Sustainability Exception Log (S12)** integrated into order management and shipping workstations:
* Whenever an operator overrides SP1 (forces air), SP2 (dispatches a dedicated van without a customer line-stop ticket), or Q1 (overrides packaging spec), the system logs `override_actor`, `override_reason_code` (e.g., *Carrier Strike/Weather [R1]*, *Machine Breakdown*, *Sales Escalation*, *Cutoff Missed*), and the incremental `transport_kgco2e` / `package_material_kg` delta.
* **Bi-Weekly Governance Review:** Operations, Quality, and Sales review Pareto charts of S12 overrides and model precision. High *Cutoff Missed* overrides trigger warehouse/inspection bottleneck fixes (rather than blaming shipping); high *Sales Escalation* or repeated Tier-1 claims by specific accounts trigger commercial SLA audits to prevent customer gaming.
