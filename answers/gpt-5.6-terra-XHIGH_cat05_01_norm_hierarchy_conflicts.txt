## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts accepted as given.** GPIP-4 is board-approved and global; SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled Aachen procedure; ECD-2 is a signed, time-limited incident directive; the CFO email is an ordinary email with stated Finance-interpretation authority but no stated Compliance or segregation-of-duties authority. No CRO exception or later CRO ratification exists.

**Rebuttable interpretive assumptions used for this assessment.**

1. **No single total hierarchy applies.** Authority, subject matter, scope, and time determine priority by issue:
   - GPIP-4 is the substantive floor for global integrity, Finance, and Compliance controls.
   - ECD-2 is a later, specific operational overlay for its defined incident scope and window.
   - The SOP remains effective where ECD-2 does not clearly displace it and may remain stricter.
   - The CFO email can have limited interpretive weight for Finance workflow, but not automatically for Board-reserved integrity or Compliance requirements.

2. **ECD-2 is not treated as an A7 CRO exception.** This is an interpretation, not a stated fact. It follows from: (i) A7 naming the CRO; (ii) the known fact that the COO may issue operational directives but GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to the CRO; and (iii) no CRO signature, delegation, or ratification. The prior practice of treating signed directives as temporary overlays supports ECD-2 as an operational/local overlay, but does not establish authority to waive GPIP-4, especially Compliance clearance for a new supplier.

3. **ECD-2 clearly displaces only the local matters it expressly addresses.** During its effective window and within its scope, it overrides:
   - B1’s normal PO-before-commit sequence through C1;
   - B3’s approved-supplier restriction through C2; and
   - ordinary local onboarding/timing requirements to the extent covered by C4 and B7.
   
   ECD-2 does **not**, on its text, expressly displace GPIP-4 A1–A3, nor clearly replace B2’s Plant and Finance approval requirements. C3 adds a two-person Incident Command authorization; it is not automatically an independent budget-owner approval or Finance Controller approval.

4. **A sequencing exception does not remove the underlying activity.** “Commitment may precede Create_PO” permits reversed order; it does not mean that a PO never needs to be created. Likewise, an emergency authorization record may substitute for a local emergency-form timing requirement only if it captures equivalent core facts. It does not silently remove all recordkeeping obligations.

5. **The CFO email is given limited, not zero, effect.** After it was sent, D1 can reasonably be read as a temporary Finance-workflow instruction, potentially allowing Incident Command authorization to substitute for a separate Finance routing step. It does not, without more:
   - designate Incident Command members as independent budget owners;
   - waive A1 segregation of duties;
   - waive A3 Compliance clearance;
   - create a CRO A7 exception; or
   - apply retroactively to an act completed before the email was sent.
   
   For amounts above EUR 150,000, “as well” in D2 is read as adding Legal clearance to the Incident Command process, not as silently eliminating GPIP-4 controls.

6. **Emergency necessity is rule-specific.** B4 requires imminent safety harm or a production stoppage expected within six hours. ECD-2 has a different, narrower test: critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the contamination event. Operational urgency alone does not create an unstated necessity defense to A1–A3.

7. **Scope must be affirmatively supported.** The party relying on ECD-2 bears the burden of showing both:
   - that the purchase was within the 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 window; and
   - that it was a critical sterilization-line lot required for containment.
   
   “Critical contingency” labeling, without a documented containment nexus, is insufficient where the facts point the other way.

8. **Timing is assessed at the actual act, not merely at later data entry.** A supplier commitment occurs when the company becomes externally committed, normally shown by a supplier acceptance, telephone recording, or equivalent contemporaneous evidence. ERP timestamps are not conclusive where outage or clock-error evidence exists.

9. **Evidential burden.** A claimed exception, delegation, substitute approver role, or timely verbal authorization must be supported by reliable contemporaneous evidence. Later approvals can mitigate risk and consequences, but A6 prevents them from automatically curing a missing pre-commitment approval. Where a required preventive control is neither recorded nor otherwise evidenced, the result is treated as noncompliant on the available record, while identifying the distinction between a proven breach and an inability to prove compliance.

10. **Severity is qualitative.** “Critical,” “High,” and “Moderate” reflect integrity, supplier, regulatory, and operational exposure, not a numerical score.

**Issue-specific result.**

- **Integrity, independent budget approval, Finance Controller approval, and Compliance clearance:** GPIP-4 governs unless a valid A7 exception is proven.
- **PO timing and approved-supplier register:** ECD-2 governs over the SOP for an in-scope, in-window incident purchase.
- **Local Plant/Finance workflow:** SOP B2 presumptively survives; ECD-2/CFO-email displacement is arguable but not clear enough to override GPIP-4.
- **Evidence timing:** contemporaneous external evidence can prevail over an ERP timestamp; it cannot create an approval that did not occur.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinguishing the defect types**

| Category | Cases and treatment |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed policy breach** | P-502 has objectively late Finance and Compliance actions against pre-commit A2/A3 requirements. P-503 lacks Compliance and required preventive approvals and has a serious A1 conflict. P-504 used an unapproved supplier outside ECD scope. P-506 relied on an expired directive and has no valid written extension. |
| **Control failure evidenced primarily by absence of proof** | P-505, P-507, and P-508 lack evidence that the required independent budget-owner and Finance Controller controls existed before commitment. This is noncompliance on the supplied record, but supporting delegations could materially refine the finding. |
| **Logging defect, not necessarily an approval breach** | P-507’s 14:30 ERP entry is not proof of late C3 recording if the signed 12:40 PDF is authentic and contains the authorization. P-505’s 18:06 ERP time is not conclusive if the system was nine minutes fast and the supplier acceptance was before 18:00. |
| **Late evidence of a timely act** | P-502’s verbal IC authorization was recorded at 12:55, still within C3’s four-hour period. P-507’s signed PDF, if verified, is timely evidence even though the ERP entry was later. |

**Monitoring and systemic actions**

- Create an incident-purchase register with actual commitment time, supplier acceptance evidence, scope basis, approval identities, and each exception relied upon.
- Require automated pre-commit blocks at EUR 50,000 and EUR 100,000 unless a named exception ID is entered.
- Require supplier-register status and “new supplier” status as distinct fields; unapproved does not always mean new, but both require explicit handling.
- Preserve immutable telephone, PDF, email-header, and access-log evidence during system outages.
- Issue daily Incident Command/CFO/CRO exception reports during an event, including open authorizations not yet converted into POs.
- Measure repeat emergency usage by buyer, supplier, cost center, and incident; repeated reliance on emergency controls should trigger a root-cause review and ordinary supplier onboarding.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement hierarchy and emergency-exception clause**

> **Emergency Procurement Hierarchy and Controls.**  
> 1. Board procurement policy is the minimum control standard. Local procedures may impose stricter controls. An incident directive may alter a local workflow only where it expressly identifies the local clause altered.  
> 2. No incident directive, executive email, verbal Incident Command instruction, or Finance interpretation may waive segregation of duties, independent budget approval, Finance approval, or Compliance clearance unless it cites a written exception approved by the Chief Risk Officer or a formally documented CRO delegate. COO and Incident Commander signatures alone are not a CRO exception.  
> 3. An emergency exception must state: incident ID; site; affected line; purchase class; supplier status; controls waived or substituted; named substitute approvers; start and end time; value cap; and risk owner. It expires automatically at the stated time and cannot be orally extended.  
> 4. For purchases of at least EUR 50,000, before supplier commitment there must be both: (a) an independent budget approval; and (b) Finance Controller approval. A valid emergency exception may name an Emergency Budget Authority and a CFO-designated Finance delegate, but neither may be the requester or committer and they must be distinct persons.  
> 5. For a purchase above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier, Compliance clearance is required before commitment. A new supplier may be used without ordinary onboarding only if the CRO exception expressly permits it and specifies interim due diligence, sanctions screening, quality checks, and a post-event onboarding deadline.  
> 6. For an emergency purchase above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is also required before commitment unless the CRO exception expressly states why that is impracticable and names a compensating control.  
> 7. A commitment may precede PO creation only where the applicable emergency exception or directive expressly says so. The PO must be created within one business day, linked to the exception ID, and marked with the actual commitment time. A post-event PO does not convert a missing pre-commit approval into a timely approval.  
> 8. Verbal emergency authorization is permitted only where the exception allows it. It must be recorded within four hours in an immutable incident record showing approvers, actual authorization time, supplier, amount, purpose, line/containment nexus, and actual commitment time. Later ERP entry may corroborate but does not replace this record.  
> 9. Emergency scope is limited to components or services demonstrably necessary to protect safety or contain the identified incident on the affected line. Convenience purchases, general site-continuity purchases, and stock held for ordinary contingency are excluded unless separately approved.  
> 10. To prevent emergency normalization, no exception may be renewed for the same supplier or purchase category beyond 72 hours without CRO review, and every emergency purchase must receive an independent post-event review within five business days.
