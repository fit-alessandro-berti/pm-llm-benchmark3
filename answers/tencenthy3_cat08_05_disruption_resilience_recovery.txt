# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience** is the ability to maintain the organization’s core mission—patient-critical supply and regulatory control—under stress, using conscious, documented trade-offs and minimum controls. In this case, true resilience includes:

- **A + B:** Hospital-critical fast lane only works as resilience because trained staff performed manual authorization checks.
- **I:** Deferring noncritical bulk orders protected hospital throughput; this is a transparent capacity trade-off, not a control failure.
- **J:** Backlog prioritization by criticality and risk is resilient if the rules are explicit.
- **F (for controlled substances):** Pharmacist review of all controlled orders retained a key control during outage.
- **D and E (early/modified):** Time-boxed snapshot and manual pick lists kept physical flow; they are resilient only with expiration and captured evidence.

**Concealed noncompliance** is a deviation that preserves throughput or cycle-time appearance while silently removing mandatory regulatory controls. It is not resilience even if service levels look acceptable. Clear examples:

- **C:** Skipping system authorization for ambient products created an authorization gap with no compensating control.
- **G:** Dispatch without scan deleted real-time traceability, especially serious for controlled and cold-chain products.
- **Missing Quality/release check, missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature record** (move-on-model deviations in T2) are concealed noncompliance, not acceptable variation.
- **H for controlled products** and **stale D** become concealed noncompliance when used beyond the emergency bridge.

**Ambiguous depending on risk appetite:**

- **A alone (fast lane before auth):** Resilient if B is applied; risky shortcut if authorization is assumed.
- **I / J:** Resilient if communicated and fair; risky if informal and unfairly penalize pharmacies.
- **L:** Acceptable as a limited break-glass reconstruction, but if routine it masks real-time control failure and violates the principle that after-the-fact remediation is not real-time compliance.

# 4. Recovery process model

## Trigger conditions
- **Severity 1 – Full WMS/scan/validation outage >30 minutes:** Activate Emergency Order Fulfilment Plan (manual mode).
- **Severity 2 – Partial recovery (intake up, inventory sync unreliable):** Activate Hybrid Mode with reconciliation sprints.

## Triage rules
- Tag every order with **customer class** (Hospital-critical, Pharmacy-standard, Wholesale-bulk) and **product risk** (Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive, Ambient).
- Priority tiers:
  1. Hospital-critical + any product, or any customer + Controlled/Cold-chain urgent.
  2. Pharmacy-standard urgent + medium-risk product.
  3. Wholesale-bulk / non-urgent ambient.

## Minimum controls by product/customer risk
- **High-risk (Controlled, Cold-chain, Hospital-critical):** Mandatory manual authorization (B); physical count or time-boxed snapshot (D modified); 100% temperature log and dual-control sign (F extended); no uncontrolled dispatch-without-scan—use paper manifest + later scan; enhanced POD (signed image or two-person phone) for controlled.
- **Medium-risk (Recall-sensitive):** Manual or verified standing authorization; lot/expiry capture; temp log if applicable.
- **Low-risk (Ambient non-controlled):** Fast lane allowed, but still documented authorization or verified standing list; paper manifest; scan within 24h.

## Manual evidence capture
- Offline/rugged-device form per order: order ID, customer verification, product/lot/expiry, pick time, temp record, checker signature, courier manifest, POD evidence.
- Forms uploaded immediately when systems return; no “memory-only” confirmation.

## Backlog prioritization
- After system return, sort backlog by `(customer criticality, product risk, order age)`.
- Explicit weights: Hospital-critical controlled > Hospital-critical other > Pharmacy urgent high-risk > Wholesale-bulk deferred.
- Communicate deferrals to pharmacy/bulk customers to preserve fairness.

## Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow
- **Daily reconciliation sprint:** Match manual picks/reservations to inventory (K) within 24h; close scans/POD within 72h.
- **Exception-only audit remediation (L):** Only unresolved cases enter `Audit remediation`; this queue is an operational KPI, not a hidden tail.
- **Close or regulatory exception:** Cases either reach normal `Invoice close` or generate a compliance exception report.

## Process notation (POWL-style, optional)
```
-> (seq[
     Trigger,
     Triage,
     ->(xor[Normal validation, Emergency triage]),
     ->(xor[Manual auth (B), Skip auth (C) -- retired]),
     ->(xor[Timeboxed snapshot/physical count (D), Manual pick (E)]),
     ->(xor[Temporary release extended (F), Standard pack]),
     DispatchModified(G'),
     PODmodified(H'),
     Reconcile(K),
     ->(xor[Invoice close, Audit remediation (L) -- exception only])
   ])
```
*Notation: `->` = sequence; `xor` = exclusive choice. This shows that skip-auth and uncontrolled dispatch are removed from the allowed emergency path; remediation is the exception branch, not the default.*

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** deliberate, transparent load-shedding and manual controls that preserve life-critical supply and core compliance—specifically the hospital-critical fast lane when paired with manual authorization, deferral of noncritical bulk, formalized backlog prioritization, and pharmacist review of controlled substances. These protect patients and regulatory integrity even if noncritical cycle times and cancellations rise. Management should **not accept** throughput-preserving shortcuts that omit mandatory authorization, scans, temperature, or dual-control records—such as skip-system-authorization for ambient products, uncontrolled dispatch-without-scan for high-risk products, or stale inventory reliance beyond the emergency bridge—because they create concealed noncompliance and a later audit tail. The recommendation would change if evidence from a controlled drill shows that offline authorization can be scaled with near-zero error and that point-of-activity mobile capture can achieve <1% missing scan/temperature/POD; under those conditions, some ambient fast-lane dispatch without immediate system scan could be reclassified as modified resilience rather than retired noncompliance.
