### Causal-mechanism interpretation

**Shared mechanisms:**
- Parallelizing independent verification tasks *reduces sequential delay* only when both tasks can start and execute concurrently without blocking. At Site A, identity and document queries use independent external data; this is likely true at Site B as well.
- Overlapping intervals compress waiting time before downstream Assessment, provided downstream capacity is sufficient.

**Site-specific mechanisms:**
- **Originals receipt delay (Site B only):** The 18-hour median originals delay is a hard gate for 62% of Site B's volume. Parallelizing identity and document checks does not reduce this delay. If originals arrive *after* identity completes, document check still cannot start; parallelization buys nothing.
- **Legal review and supplement loop (Site B only):** 40% of Site B cases enter legal review; 10.7% loop back for supplementary document checks. This rework serializes benefit gains. Site A measured only 11.8% rework and had no legal review; this is a major structural difference.
- **Role specialization (Site B stronger):** Site B has four separate role pools; Site A reports cross-training. Specialized pools reduce load-balancing flexibility. If identity finishes but document reviewers are saturated, parallelization gains vanish.
- **Case locking and timestamp integrity (Site B weaker):** Site B's legacy lock and post-hoc workarounds undermine concurrent-event measurement. The baseline 6% overlap may reflect lock enforcement, not process reality. This makes it impossible to validate true concurrency without system changes.

**Effect modifiers:**
- **Document channel:** Digital-only claims (38%) have no originals gate; these can benefit from parallelization. Originals-required claims (62%) are bottlenecked by external receipt and gain little.
- **Capacity ratios:** If identity reviewers are idle but document reviewers are saturated, parallelization increases queuing. Site B's 4:7 identity-to-document ratio differs from Site A's 18 cross-trained reviewers.
- **Rework and legal loops:** Supplement requests bypass Assessment back to Document Check. If parallelization increases parallel work, rework loops may amplify queuing costs.

**Why process diagrams alone do not guarantee transportability:**
- A control-flow diagram shows *logical* sequencing but not *resource contention*, *external delays*, *data quality*, or *feedback loops*. Site A's simpler originals-handling and absence of legal review mean the parallelization benefit is not offset by rework serialization. Site B's legal loop and originals delay are orthogonal to the process tree and will interact adversely with parallelization if not controlled.
- Site A's before/after evidence conflates treatment (parallelization), co-interventions (API latency reduction, staffing, queue huddles, incoming mix shift), and measurement artifact (improved timestamp recording). Without a randomized control, we cannot isolate the treatment effect. Exporting a confounded effect to Site B risks failure.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

### Process Specification

**Eligibility:**
- All cases where identity verification and document checking can start independently after registration.
- **Segmentation:**
  - **Cohort A (Primary, Phase 1):** Digital-only cases (no originals required) AND non-complex or routine public-sector cases. **Target: 22–28% of volume.**
  - **Cohort B (Secondary, Phase 2):** Originals-required cases; parallelization applies to identity check only, while document check waits for originals receipt. **Target: 62% of volume, but document parallelization deferred.**
  - **Cohort C (Excluded initially):** High-complexity cases and those with known legal-review flags; these remain sequential until bottleneck analysis is complete.

**Concurrent task creation:**
- **For Cohort A:** After `Register` completes, the case system creates two tasks simultaneously: `Identity Check` (assigned to identity pool) and `Document Check` (assigned to document pool). Both tasks enter the respective queue; execution order depends on reviewer availability, not task creation order.
- **For Cohort B (originals-required):** After `Register` completes, the case system creates `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` tasks simultaneously. `Document Check` is created only when `Originals Receipt` is marked complete. Thus, document work is not truly parallelized with identity (it is gated by originals arrival), but identity and originals tracking are parallel.
- **For Cohort C:** Sequential flow unchanged; `Identity Check`  `Document Check` (after originals receipt, if required)  `Assessment`.

**Mandatory prerequisites:**
1. `Register` must complete before any verification task starts.
2. `Originals Receipt` must complete before `Document Check` can start for any originals-required case (legal/statutory constraint, immutable).
3. Both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` (where applicable) must be marked complete before `Assessment` can start.
4. `Assessment` must complete before `Legal Review` (if applicable) or `Approve/Reject` can start.
5. If `Legal Review` issues `Request Supplement`, `Document Check` and `Assessment` tasks are re-opened. The case loops back to document rework; no assessment action is final until legal review is satisfied.

**Queue and WIP controls:**
- **Per-reviewer WIP limit:** Maximum 8 open verification tasks (identity or document) per reviewer. If limit is reached, new task creation is deferred until an open task is marked complete.
- **Assessment queue limit:** Maximum 10 cases awaiting Assessment. If limit is reached, verification task completion is paused (tokens are held in the verification pool until Assessment queue drains to <6).
- **Legal-review queue limit:** Maximum 5 cases awaiting Legal Review. If exceeded, Assessment output is held until legal queue drains.
- **Queue monitoring:** Daily huddles report current queue length, oldest case in queue, and estimated wait time. If wait time exceeds SLA (18 hours before Assessment, 12 hours before legal review), escalation is triggered.

**Resource responsibilities:**
- **Identity pool (4 reviewers):** Performs identity verification. When `Identity Check` task is created, it is assigned to an available identity reviewer (round-robin). Identity reviewers must log task start and completion timestamps (system-generated if possible, manual if legacy system enforces post-hoc entry, but with strict validation).
- **Document pool (7 reviewers):** Performs document verification only for originals that have been received. For Cohort A (digital-only), document task is created in parallel with identity. For Cohort B (originals-required), document task is created only after `Originals Receipt` is complete. Document reviewers must coordinate with originals receipt team to ensure documents are physically or digitally available before task starts. Timestamps must be logged.
- **Assessment pool (3 reviewers):** Performs assessment after both identity and document checks are complete. Assessment takes cases in FIFO order, with priority override for cases that have been waiting >12 hours. Assessment task includes a gate: cannot start until both identity and document tasks are marked complete.
- **Legal-review pool (2 reviewers):** Reviews cases flagged as requiring legal review (public-sector, high-value, or identified by assessor). Legal review may issue `Approve`, `Reject`, or `Request Supplement`. If supplement is issued, a new `Document Check` task is created (with priority flag). Legal reviewers work in close coordination with document and assessment reviewers to manage supplement loops.
- **Originals receipt / logistics (functional role, not a person pool):** Tracks external originals. For originals-required cases, creates an `Originals Receipt` task that is marked complete when documents are physically received (or digitally confirmed). This task is independent of identity and document verification.

**Legal-review and supplement-loop handling:**
- `Legal Review` is triggered when `Assessment` marks a case as "legal review required" (automatic flag for public-sector or high-value; optional flag for complex cases).
- If `Legal Review` issues `Approve` or `Reject`, the case moves to `Notify`.
- If `Legal Review` issues `Request Supplement`, a new `Document Check` task is created with **priority = HIGH** and **original case ID = loop parent**. The document reviewer is notified to prioritize this task. After document recheck, an `Assessment` task is re-opened (not a new instance, but a reactivation of the prior assessment task). After re-assessment, `Legal Review` is re-triggered.
- To minimize rework cycle time, the system pre-allocates an assessment slot: when a supplement is issued, a "follow-up assessment" task is scheduled to start 2 hours after the document recheck is expected to complete, reducing waiting time.

**Logging requirements (IT system upgrade necessary):**
1. **Task creation events:** System-generated timestamp (not manual) when a task is created. Includes task type (Identity Check, Document Check, etc.), case ID, reviewer pool assignment, and task ID.
2. **Task start events:** When a reviewer begins work on a task, the system records the start time and reviewer ID (manual entry or system auto-detection, depending on system design).
3. **Task completion events:** When a reviewer marks a task complete, system records end time and outcome (e.g., "identity verified", "documents incomplete").
4. **Parallel execution flag:** If multiple tasks for the same case have overlapping [start, end] intervals, the case is flagged as "parallelized = true". This flag is used for post-pilot analysis.
5. **Gate events:** When Assessment or Legal Review tasks are gated (delayed because prerequisites are not complete), the system logs the reason (e.g., "awaiting document check") and duration.
6. **Rework cycle tracking:** When a supplement loop occurs, the case is labeled with loop count and total rework duration.

### Partial-order process specification (POWL-style):

```
PROCESS Site_B_intervention:
   Register
   Assign_cohort (based on originals requirement, case complexity)
   CHOICE:
       IF Cohort A (digital-only, simple):
          PARALLEL_START identity_check, document_check
          PARALLEL_END   joint_completion = (identity_check AND document_check)
       ELIF Cohort B (originals-required):
          PARALLEL_START identity_check, originals_receipt
          identity_check   (completes, awaits assessment gate)
          originals_receipt  document_check (originals gate lifted)
       ELIF Cohort C (complex, excluded):
          identity_check
          WAIT originals_receipt (if required)
          document_check
       END_CHOICE
   Assessment (gate: joint_completion OR (identity AND document) completed)
   CHOICE:
       IF legal_review_required:
          Legal_review
          CHOICE:
              IF supplement_requested:
                 Supplement_document_check (priority = HIGH)
                 Assessment (reactivated; re-assessment)
                 Legal_review (loopback; recursive until approved/rejected)
              ELSE:
                 SKIP
              END_CHOICE
          Decision = Legal_review.outcome
       ELSE:
          Decision = Assessment.outcome
       END_CHOICE
   Notify (based on Decision)
END_PROCESS

CONSTRAINTS:
  - Register before all others.
  - Identity_check AND Document_check can start after Register (Cohort A).
  - Document_check cannot start before Originals_receipt (Cohort B).
  - Assessment cannot start before (Identity_check AND Document_check are complete).
  - Legal_review cannot start before Assessment is complete.
  - WIP_limit_identity_pool: 8 open tasks per reviewer.
  - WIP_limit_document_pool: 8 open tasks per reviewer.
  - WIP_limit_assessment_queue: 10 cases.
  - WIP_limit_legal_queue: 5 cases.
  - Supplement loop repeats until Legal_review approves or rejects (no infinite loops possible if outcome is always decided).
```

### Separation of mechanism failure from implementation failure (220 words)

The pilot isolates mechanism failure from implementation failure through stratification, process mining, and qualitative triangulation.

**Mechanism failure** occurs when parallelizing identity and document checks does not reduce waiting time *even if the system executes the process correctly*. This would indicate that true concurrency is not possible (e.g., both checks are actually sequential despite task-level parallelization) or that downstream bottlenecks prevent benefit (e.g., assessment queue is already saturated). *Signature:* Overlap measure is low (<15% in Stratum 1 treatment arm) despite parallel task creation, or waiting-before-assessment time does not decrease despite higher parallelization rate.

**Implementation failure** occurs when the designed process is not executed as intended. Examples: case-management system rejects concurrent task creation due to legacy locks; teams ignore the new process and continue sequential work; timestamps are incorrect due to post-hoc entry; or communication breaks down. *Signature:* Treatment cases show low overlap (but for observable reasons: system errors, log analysis reveals tasks are created sequentially despite randomization to parallel arm, or team interviews reveal non-compliance).

The pilot distinguishes these through:
1. **Process mining reconstruction:** For each treatment case, extract exact task creation, start, and completion times from system logs (not manual reports). If overlap is low, examine logs to determine whether tasks were created concurrently (design was followed) or sequentially (implementation failed).
2. **Team interviews:** Ask reviewers whether they attempted parallelization and encountered technical barriers (mechanism failure) or organizational friction (implementation failure).
3. **Subgroup analysis:** If overlap is high in one team but low in another, implementation failure is likely. If overlap is high in all teams but waiting-time improvement is small, mechanism failure is likely (bottleneck is downstream).

## 9. Recommendation

**CONDITIONAL GO**

### Summary

I recommend a **conditional go** for Site B's parallelization pilot, with the following rationale, design, and contingencies.

**What should be transferred:**
- The *mechanism* of parallelizing independent verification tasks (identity and document checks) to reduce waiting time before downstream Assessment, **only for digital-only cases (38% of volume) that do not require external originals**. This cohort is most analogous to Site A: identity and document checks are independent external queries, neither is gated by external receipt, and assessment capacity is sufficient to absorb concurrent input. Median waiting-time reduction of 6–9 hours is plausible for this cohort.
- **The operational discipline:** Queue huddles (daily visibility of queue length and oldest case), WIP limits (prevent unbounded queuing), and concurrent event logging (system-generated timestamps to validate true concurrency) are co-interventions at Site A that amplified the effect. These should be implemented at Site B *regardless of parallelization*, as they are operational best practices. The pilot should combine parallelization + these operational controls, not just parallelization alone.

**What should not be copied:**
- **Do not apply parallelization to originals-required cases (62% of volume).** The 18-hour median originals delay is orthogonal to verification parallelization. Parallelizing identity and document checks does not accelerate external document receipt. For these cases, the bottleneck is logistics, not process sequencing. Parallelizing adds complexity (WIP tracking, concurrent ownership) without reducing cycle time. Instead, prioritize originals expediting (e.g., weekend/holiday processing, electronic receipt).
- **Do not assume Site A's full 9-hour median improvement (22% reduction) will transport to Site B.** Site A's observed effect is confounded by four co-interventions: API latency upgrade, staffing increase, queue huddles, and incoming mix shift toward simpler cases. At Site B, the mix shift is unlikely (it was deliberate marketing at Site A), the API upgrade is not confirmed, and staffing is constrained. A realistic expectation for digital-only cases is 6–9 hours (15–28% reduction), not 9 hours.
- **Do not ignore Site B's legal-review and supplement-loop structure (40% of cases).** Legal review introduces a serialization bottleneck orthogonal to verification parallelization. Supplement requests loop back to document check, and rework cycle times are significantly longer. For legal-review cases, parallelization may backfire if legal queue grows and assessment input floods the system. Exclude high-complexity and legal-flagged cases from the initial pilot; they are a secondary cohort.

**Which subgroup should enter first:**
1. **Phase 1 (weeks 4–8): Digital-only, routine/simple cases only.** Target 25% of volume (~200–250 cases in pilot). These are the closest match to Site A and have the highest parallelization benefit potential. Randomize 1:1 to parallel vs. sequential within this cohort.
2. **Phase 2 (weeks 9–13, conditional on Phase 1 results): Originals-required cases.** If Phase 1 shows positive waiting-time reduction (+5 hours) with stable quality, expand to originals-required cohort. For originals-required cases, apply a modified intervention: parallelize identity + originals-receipt tracking (not document + identity, because document is gated by originals). This allows identity verification to proceed independently of slow external originals arrival.
3. **Deferred: High-complexity and legal-review cases.** These remain sequential until bottleneck analysis is complete. If Assessment or legal-review capacity is saturated (>85% utilization), capacity must be increased before parallelizing complex cases.

**Expected benefits and risks:**

*Benefits:*
- **Median cycle time for digital-only cohort:** 6–9 hours reduction (from ~38 h to 29–32 h), representing 15–24% improvement. This translates to faster first-decision and better customer perception.
- **Waiting-before-assessment time:** 3–6 hours reduction. This is the direct mechanism of parallelization; evidence of mechanism.
- **Throughput:** 5–8% increase in cases completed per day for digital cohort, if downstream Assessment capacity is available.
- **Operational learning:** The pilot provides evidence on true concurrency, bottleneck locations, and IT system reliability at Site B. This learning is valuable even if the effect is modest.

*Risks:*
- **Quality regression:** Rework or appeal rates increase by >2 percentage points if parallel work is not coordinated well, or if rushed assessment skips checks. Risk is moderate if WIP limits and case gates are implemented.
- **Bottleneck shift:** If Assessment or legal-review capacity is already saturated, parallelization floods the queue. Cycle time for downstream tasks worsens. Risk is moderate; mitigated by WIP limits and queue monitoring.
- **Implementation failure:** IT system does not support concurrent task ownership after the proposed upgrade, or teams do not comply with the new process. Risk is low if IT fix is validated before pilot; moderate if IT upgrade is inadequate.
- **No effect or worse effect for originals-required cases:** 62% of volume may see no benefit or worsening if originals delay is the dominant bottleneck. Risk is high if parallelization is extended to originals-required cases without proper stratification. Mitigated by Phase 1 focus on digital-only.
- **Pilot fatigue:** Site B teams may resist process change after experiencing failures or confusion. Risk is low if communication, training, and early wins (Phase 1 successes) are prioritized.

### Evidence-driven decision criteria for expansion, modification, or abandonment

**Expand parallelization (Phase 2 and beyond):**
- Phase 1 results (digital-only cohort, weeks 9–10 interim analysis): Median waiting-before-assessment time decreases by 5 hours; rework rate stable or increases by <2 percentage points; overlap measure increases to 40% (true concurrency validated); no safety stops triggered.
- Assessment and legal-review queue lengths remain <5 cases on average during Phase 1.
- Team qualitative feedback is positive (parallelization is feasible, no major technical barriers).
- *Action:* Proceed to Phase 2 (originals-required cases, modified intervention). Enroll 350–400 cases. Repeat interim analysis after 12 weeks total. If Phase 2 results are similarly positive, expand to all digital-only and originals-required cases (60% of volume) by week 16.

**Modify intervention:**
- Phase 1 interim analysis shows waiting-before-assessment reduction of 2–4 hours (modest but positive) and assessment queue length is rising (>8 cases average). Assessment or legal capacity is the bottleneck, not verification sequencing.
- *Action:* Do not expand parallelization yet. Instead, increase Assessment capacity by 0.5–1 FTE. Re-run Phase 1 (adjusted) with added capacity. If waiting-time reduction improves to 5 hours and queues remain stable, continue to Phase 2.
- Or, if queue huddles and WIP limits were insufficient to prevent Assessment queue growth, implement a more aggressive prioritization rule (e.g., priority override for cases waiting >8 hours before Assessment). Re-test Phase 1.

**Abandon or suspend parallelization:**
- Phase 1 interim analysis shows: waiting-before-assessment reduction is <1 hour (no meaningful effect); rework rate increases by >2 percentage points; overlap measure remains <15% (true concurrency not achieved, likely due to IT lock or process non-compliance); Assessment queue grows to >12 cases on average; IT system fails or undergoes emergency patching.
- Any safety stop is triggered: quality regression (rework +8%), queue saturation (20+ cases awaiting Assessment for >2 days), or data quality loss (>20% missing timestamps).
- *Action:* Pause parallelization immediately. Conduct root-cause analysis (IT? Capacity? Process design?). Engage IT and operations teams to remediate. Document findings. If remediation is feasible (e.g., IT fix takes 2–4 weeks), plan a Phase 2 restart. If barriers are insurmountable (e.g., legacy IT system cannot be upgraded, Assessment is permanently understaffed), abandon parallelization and pursue orthogonal improvements (originals expediting, legal-review staffing, API latency reduction).

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Assumption: Identity and document checks use independent external data sources.**
   - *Why needed:* Parallelization requires that the two checks are not serially dependent. If document check requires identity data (e.g., identity check must confirm an identity before document eligibility is determined), parallelization is impossible. Site A reports this independence; assumed to hold at Site B but not verified.
   - *How to test:* Interview identity and document reviewers: "Do you ever wait for identity check results before starting document check?" Review 20–30 cases (identity and document task logs) to determine actual dependency rate. If >10% of document checks are delayed by identity results, the assumption is false.
   - *Consequence if false:* Parallelization is infeasible. Return to sequential process. Pursue orthogonal improvements (API latency, staff capacity, queue management).

2. **Assumption: Site B's IT case-management system can be upgraded to support concurrent task ownership and system-generated timestamps before the pilot begins.**
   - *Why needed:* Without concurrent task support, the legacy case lock enforces sequential task ownership; the system cannot create two tasks for the same case simultaneously. Without system-generated timestamps, measured concurrency is unreliable (post-hoc manual entry hides true concurrency). The pilot depends on reliable concurrent execution and measurement.
   - *How to test:* IT scoping phase (weeks 1–3): Vendor assessment or in-house technical review. Prototype concurrent task creation in a test environment. Validate that system generates timestamps on task creation (not closure). Conduct a short (50-case) pre-pilot to measure overlap after IT fix. If pre-pilot overlap remains <15%, timestamp generation is not working.
   - *Consequence if false:* Pilot results will be inconclusive (measured overlap remains low even if real parallelization occurs). Recommendation: defer parallelization until IT upgrade is complete; pursue queue huddles and WIP limits as interim improvements.

3. **Assumption: Downstream Assessment and legal-review capacity is sufficient (not saturated) to absorb concurrent verification output without major queue buildup.**
   - *Why needed:* Parallelization compresses verification waiting time and increases the rate of Assessment input. If Assessment is already 80%+ utilized, queuing shifts upstream (waiting before Assessment stays high or increases). Parallelization gains are negated.
   - *How to test:* Baseline capacity analysis (week 1–2): Measure current Assessment service time, number of assessors, and current utilization rate. If utilization >75%, conduct a capacity expansion (hire 0.5–1 FTE assessor) *before* pilot. Re-measure utilization. For legal review, measure legal-review queue length during typical days and peak days. If queue >5 cases, legal is likely bottlenecked.
   - *Consequence if false:* Parallelization increases Assessment/legal-review queue lengths without reducing overall cycle time. Pilot appears ineffective. Response: increase Assessment/legal capacity, then re-run pilot with larger sample size to isolate the real effect.

4. **Assumption: Originals receipt delays are largely independent of verification-task sequencing and external to the case-processing system.**
   - *Why needed:* For 62% of Site B's volume, external originals arrival time (median 18 hours, potentially up to 40+ hours) is the dominant bottleneck. Parallelizing verification does not accelerate external document delivery. If originals arrival is assumed to be quick or controllable, the transport model is wrong.
   - *How to test:* Baseline metrics (week 1–2): Measure originals receipt time by case type (government, third-party, etc.). Calculate the fraction of variance in overall cycle time explained by originals delay vs. verification queueing. If originals delay accounts for >50% of variance, assumption is valid. If <30%, assume verification queueing is the main bottleneck.
   - *Consequence if false:* Parallelization has minimal benefit for originals-required cases (62% of volume). Median cycle-time improvement is capped at ~6–8 hours (digital-only cohort only). Effort should focus on originals expediting (external logistics), not process parallelization.

5. **Assumption: Site B's case complexity and legal-review burden (40%) will not materially change during the pilot.**
   - *Why needed:* At Site A, incoming case mix shifted toward simpler digital claims (marketing co-intervention), confounding the treatment effect. If Site B's mix also shifts during the pilot (e.g., marketing pushes digital claims, or complex claims decrease due to external factors), cycle-time improvements may be attributed to parallelization when they are actually due to mix shift. Similarly, if legal-review requirements increase (e.g., new regulation), legal queue congestion may counteract parallelization benefits.
   - *How to test:* Track incoming case-complexity distribution weekly during baseline (4 weeks pre-pilot) and pilot (10 weeks). Plot case-complexity % over time. If pre-pilot and pilot distributions are stable (±5%), assumption is valid. If distribution changes by >10%, adjust analysis to include mix as a covariate.
   - *Consequence if false:* Pilot results are confounded with mix shift. Causal attribution to parallelization is invalid. Response: stratified analysis by case type and sensitivity analysis (results with vs. without mix adjustment).

6. **Assumption: The legal-review and supplement-loop structure will not be materially disrupted by parallelization (e.g., no catastrophic increase in supplement-request rates due to rushed assessment).**
   - *Why needed:* Parallelization may increase assessment speed and reduce wait times for legal review, but if Assessment quality suffers (rushed work), supplement-request rates may increase, offsetting any cycle-time gain. Rework cycles become longer because legal queue is also congested. Assumption: Assessment quality remains stable; supplement-request rate is driven by case complexity, not assessment speed.
   - *How to test:* Track supplement-request rate (Request Supplement outcomes from Legal Review) weekly during pilot. Compare to baseline (estimated at 10% of legal-review cases, or ~40 of 1,360 legal cases per 6 months = 0.9% of total volume). If supplement rate increases by >2 percentage points during pilot, quality may be declining.
   - *Consequence if false:* Rework cycle times increase; overall cycle-time improvement is negated for legal cases. Response: adjust assessment workload or add pre-assessment quality gates to slow down and improve accuracy before legal review.

7. **Assumption: Site B teams will comply with the new parallel process design and adopt queue discipline (WIP limits, daily huddles) as intended.**
   - *Why needed:* Implementation failure (teams continue sequential work despite parallelization enablement, WIP limits are ignored, huddles are deprioritized) undermines the intervention. Compliance depends on team buy-in, training, and perceived benefit. Without compliance, measured concurrency remains low and pilot results are inconclusive.
   - *How to test:* Weekly compliance audits (weeks 4–13): Sample 10 treatment cases per week; verify that assigned-to-parallel cases are actually executed in parallel (check task creation timestamps). Observe at least one daily huddle per team per week; confirm queue-length reporting and escalation protocol. Conduct team survey (week 8) on perceived parallelization benefit and barriers.
   - *Consequence if false:* Pilot appears ineffective, even if parallelization has intrinsic causal value. Response: retraining, process clarification, management escalation to enforce compliance. Consider a "per-protocol" analysis (analysis of only fully-compliant cases) vs. intent-to-treat (all randomized cases) to isolate implementation failure from mechanism failure.

8. **Assumption: Process-mining tools and event-log extraction will be accurate and available during the pilot.**
   - *Why needed:* Verification of true concurrency, bottleneck identification, and mechanistic confirmation depend on detailed event logs with reliable timestamps. If logs are incomplete, inaccurate, or inaccessible, process-mining analysis is not possible. The pilot's causal interpretation rests on this assumption.
   - *How to test:* IT system upgrade (weeks 1–3): implement system-generated task-creation and completion timestamps, and export logs in standard format (XES, CSV). Conduct a 50-case pre-pilot data extraction and validation. Verify that 100% of tasks have start/end times and case IDs. Spot-check 10 logs against manual case notes to validate accuracy.
   - *Consequence if false:* Event logs are too sparse or inaccurate for process mining. Analysis is limited to summary statistics (throughput, cycle time) without mechanistic insight. Conclusion: "parallelization improved cycle time" is valid, but "waiting-before-assessment was the mechanism" cannot be confirmed. Confidence in transportability is reduced.
