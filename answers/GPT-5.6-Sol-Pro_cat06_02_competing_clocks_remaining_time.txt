## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

The governing decomposition is:

[
\text{terminal time}
====================

\underbrace{\max(C,M)}_{\text{parallel join}}
+
\text{QA waiting}
+
\text{QA service}
+
\text{optional rework}
+
\text{final recording}.
]

Chemistry and microbiology are therefore compared as competing clocks. Their durations are not added.

**Common chemistry queue arithmetic.** At 15:30, the two current analysts become available at approximately 15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30. Under risk-priority, the urgent sample plausibly takes the first slot and B-417 takes the second, so B-417 starts around 16:42–17:30. It receives only approximately 0.5–1.3 h of service before the Friday 18:00 closure. With a 1.5–4.5 h service requirement, its usable result is then approximately Monday 08:15–12:00, with Monday 09:00–11:00 the more central range.

Under FIFO, the four samples remain ahead. The first two—and possibly part of a third—are processed Friday; the remaining queued work resumes Monday. B-417 then plausibly starts around Monday 10:00–13:30 and finishes around Monday 12:00–17:30. A combination of long preceding jobs and a long B-417 service time can push the final part into Tuesday morning, but that is a tail rather than the central FIFO outcome.

**Common microbiology arithmetic.** From the forecast origin, 44.5–52.5 h of primary incubation remain, making the culture technically ready Sunday 20 September, 12:00–20:00. With weekend authorization, the result is usable roughly Sunday 13:00–23:00. Without authorization—the more likely 55–75% staffing outcome—the result waits for Monday staffing and is recorded around Monday 08:30–09:30. The on-call decision therefore changes the microbiology timestamp substantially, but it usually does not permit a pre-Monday join because routine chemistry has already stopped.

**Common QA queue arithmetic.** Between Friday 15:30 and Monday 08:00, QA has 8.5 open hours and one reviewer. About three additional arrivals are expected in that interval, but the count and their service demands are overdispersed. With five batches initially waiting and a right-skewed service distribution, a plausible Monday-opening backlog is approximately 3–7 batches, with a wider tail. Two reviewers from Monday 08:00 begin draining that queue. A batch joining early Monday may therefore wait longer than one joining Monday afternoon after some backlog has cleared. Centrally, I use 2–6 working hours of queue waiting plus 1–3.5 h review service; the congestion tail is considerably wider.

**Smooth path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** Risk-priority produces a Monday morning result; FIFO produces a Monday afternoon result. The chemistry result is approximately Monday 09:00–17:30 across the central queue-discipline mixture.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Usually Monday 08:30–09:30 without on-call; possibly Sunday if on-call is authorized.
* **Parallel join:** (J=\max(C,M)). Under risk-priority, microbiology and chemistry compete around Monday morning. Under FIFO, chemistry normally overtakes microbiology and sets a Monday afternoon join. The central join is approximately Monday 10:00–16:30.
* **QA waiting and service:** Approximately 2–6 h waiting and 1–3.5 h service centrally. A review starting near 20:00 is paused to Tuesday.
* **Optional rework:** None in this scenario.
* **Final step:** Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 h continuously. This produces a Monday late-afternoon/evening or Tuesday morning terminal event.

**Chemistry-rerun path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** The primary result follows the smooth chemistry clocks. A borderline result then receives a priority rerun lasting 1–3 h. Under risk-priority, the usable chemistry result is typically Monday 10:00–15:00. Under FIFO it is more often Monday 14:00–18:00; a late primary result plus rerun crosses the 18:00 closure and resumes Tuesday around 08:00–10:00.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Assuming a normal primary result, it is generally usable by Monday 09:30 and is no longer the dominant branch.
* **Parallel join:** Usually equal to the rerun-completion timestamp, not the sum of primary chemistry, rerun and microbiology elapsed times.
* **QA waiting and service:** The same future-queue uncertainty applies. A late Monday join has the advantage of a partially drained queue but the disadvantage of proximity to the 20:00 closure.
* **Optional rework:** The table’s central range assumes no laboratory investigation. A rerun is nevertheless an anomaly trigger, so an investigation is in the 30–55% conditional band; query or documentation-only investigation overlap accounts for the wider tail.
* **Final step:** A clean rerun is followed by Electronic Release. A rerun alone does not establish a critical defect.

**Microbiology-confirmation path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** Usually complete Monday morning or afternoon and waits at the join.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** With an early Sunday on-call readout, confirmation becomes technically ready approximately Monday 09:00 to Tuesday 05:00. Depending on weekday staffing, its final readout is roughly Monday 09:30 to Tuesday 09:30. Without weekend on-call, the primary indication is found around Monday 08:30–09:30; adding 20–30 continuous hours and another staffed readout yields a usable confirmation around Tuesday 08:30–17:00.
* **Parallel join:** Microbiology normally dominates. The branch join moves from Monday on the smooth path to predominantly Tuesday.
* **QA waiting and service:** Tuesday has two reviewers, so the residual queue may be smaller than Monday morning, but new arrivals and overdispersion remain relevant. Use approximately 1–5 h waiting centrally, with a longer burst-arrival tail, plus review service.
* **Optional rework:** A contamination indication places the case in the elevated 30–55% investigation band. A Documentation Query can also occur independently of the test anomaly.
* **Final step:** If confirmation is acceptable, the case returns to QA and likely ends in Electronic Release. A confirmed critical defect can instead be recorded as Batch Rejected in another 0.5–1.0 h, potentially earlier than a release path that must queue for QA.

**Documentation-Query path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** Usually follows one of the smooth branch clocks, although a rerun can overlap.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Usually the primary Monday result, although confirmation can overlap.
* **Parallel join:** Often Monday, with the usual (\max(C,M)) rule.
* **QA waiting and service:** B-417 first waits and begins QA review. The query is raised during that review rather than before it.
* **Optional rework:** Missing information adds 4–12 QA working hours. A query raised Monday at 18:00 receives only two working hours before closing, so even the four-hour lower bound finishes no earlier than Tuesday 10:00. After information is supplied, the case returns to QA and may incur another queue wait and resumed service.
* **Final step:** Electronic Release follows acceptance. This is why a nominal “4-hour query” can produce a much larger elapsed delay when raised near closing.

**Documentation-only-investigation path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** May be normal or anomalous; branch completion still precedes the investigation.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** May be the Monday primary result or Tuesday confirmation result.
* **Parallel join:** The slower test branch establishes when QA can first discover or open the deviation.
* **QA waiting and service:** Initial queue waiting and at least part of a review occur before investigation.
* **Optional rework:** The 8–20 working hours can span one to several QA openings. For example, an investigation opened Monday at 16:00 receives four hours Monday; an eight-hour total completes around Tuesday 12:00, while a 20-hour total reaches approximately Wednesday 12:00. Re-entry waiting and final review are additional.
* **Final step:** Most such paths still end in release if the documentation resolves the concern. Rejection remains possible if the investigation confirms a critical defect.

**Laboratory-investigation path.**

* **Chemistry waiting and service:** The initial branch may already contain a rerun, but the subsequent 3–7-day investigation dominates that earlier difference.
* **Microbiology incubation and readout:** A contamination confirmation or confirmed excursion is a plausible trigger, though a laboratory investigation is not restricted to microbiology.
* **Parallel join:** Normally Monday or Tuesday, followed by the initial QA review.
* **QA waiting and service:** Initial QA processing occurs before the investigation; another queue and review occur after laboratory findings return.
* **Optional rework:** Add 3–7 calendar days from investigation opening. If opened Monday or Tuesday, findings return from Thursday through the following Tuesday. A result arriving after QA closes, on Sunday, or near Saturday 13:00 creates another calendar step before resumed review.
* **Final step:** Electronic Release follows a satisfactory investigation. A confirmed critical defect instead produces Batch Rejected in 0.5–1.0 h after confirmation. This path drives the severe tail.

The **central operational forecast** is therefore release or rejection **late Monday through Tuesday**, with **Tuesday daytime** a defensible planning anchor but not a precise prediction.

The 90th percentile is structurally uncertain. The estimated laboratory-investigation probability of roughly 2–12% straddles the 10% threshold. If its true probability is near 2–4%, the actual P90 may remain around Wednesday or Thursday in a documentation-only tail. If it is near 10–12%, the P90 enters the multi-day laboratory-investigation path.

**Electronic Release is by far the most plausible terminal event.** Batch Rejected is a lower-probability alternative concentrated in confirmed critical contamination or deviation outcomes. Its probability cannot be responsibly quantified because the indication-to-confirmed-critical conversion rate is absent. A critical rejection could occur comparatively early—after Monday or Tuesday confirmation—even though most severe elapsed-time tails arise from investigations that eventually permit release.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most forecast-changing new event is the **primary microbiology readout**, specifically whether it records a normal result or a contamination indication.

* **Normal primary result:** The microbiology clock ends Sunday with on-call authorization or around Monday 08:30–09:30 without it. The parallel join then becomes a competition with chemistry, and under FIFO chemistry probably dominates. The 20–30 h confirmation path disappears, the possibility of an immediate contamination-based rejection recedes, and the investigation probability is lower unless the temperature excursion or chemistry rerun supplies another trigger. The forecast contracts toward **Monday late afternoon through Tuesday**.
* **Contamination indication:** The microbiology branch remains open for another 20–30 continuous hours plus a staffed readout. Without weekend on-call, the usable confirmation shifts mainly to **Tuesday 08:30–17:00**. Microbiology becomes the dominant branch, the investigation probability moves to the **30–55%** band, and Batch Rejected becomes a live terminal alternative. The central forecast moves roughly a day later and the upper tail widens materially.
* **No weekend readout:** Even before the result value is known, failure to obtain authorization confirms that the primary result cannot be usable before Monday morning. This removes the Sunday-result branch, although chemistry still prevents a Sunday release.

The secondary update trigger is **engineering resolution of the temperature-excursion flag**. A cleared flag would justify using the lower ends of the rerun, contamination and investigation ranges and would narrow the P80/P90 tail. A confirmed excursion would move those risks jointly upward rather than as independent adjustments, reduce the smooth-path probability, and make stacked chemistry, microbiology and QA rework substantially more credible.
