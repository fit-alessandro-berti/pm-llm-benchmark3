# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Times below are minute differences on 2026-05-19. For case \(c\) and branch \(i \in \{A,B,C\}\):

\[
\begin{align*}
T^{up}(c) &= t(c,\text{fork_release}) - t(c,\text{arrival}) \\
Q(c,i) &= t(c,i_{\text{start}}) - t(c,\text{fork_release}) \\
S(c,i) &= t(c,i_{\text{end}}) - t(c,i_{\text{start}}) \\
O(c,i) &= Q(c,i)+S(c,i) = t(c,i_{\text{end}}) - t(c,\text{fork_release}) \\
t(c,\text{join_ready}) &= \max_i t(c,i_{\text{end}}) \\
W(c,i) &= t(c,\text{join_ready}) - t(c,i_{\text{end}}) \\
T^{fj}(c) &= t(c,\text{join_ready}) - t(c,\text{fork_release}) = \max_i O(c,i) \\
T^{down}(c) &= t(c,\text{final_start}) - t(c,\text{join_ready}) \\
S_F(c) &= t(c,\text{final_end}) - t(c,\text{final_start}) \\
T^{flow}(c) &= t(c,\text{final_end}) - t(c,\text{arrival})
\end{align*}
\]

The serial identity, which counts the fork–join block once, is

\[
T^{flow}(c) = T^{up}(c) + T^{fj}(c) + T^{down}(c) + S_F(c).
\]

It holds for every case in this log (checked by reconstruction).

The critical set is \(K(c)=\{i:t(c,i_{\text{end}})=t(c,\text{join_ready})\}\). A tie is kept as a tie: every branch in \(K(c)\) is critical, and the case label lists them in order A, B, C joined by `+`. In this log every critical set is a singleton, so \(W(c,k)=0\) for the critical branch \(k\), and

\[
T^{fj}(c)=Q(c,k)+S(c,k).
\]

Along that branch the same flow time is

\[
T^{flow}(c)=T^{up}(c)+Q(c,k)+S(c,k)+T^{down}(c)+S_F(c).
\]

Synchronization on the other branches is slack inside that same window. It is reported per branch and is excluded from the sum.

**Why summing synchronization waits double-counts.** For every branch, queue, service, and sync telescope:

\[
Q(c,i)+S(c,i)+W(c,i)=T^{fj}(c).
\]

Summing over the three branches therefore equals \(3\,T^{fj}(c)\), not \(T^{fj}(c)\). The non-critical sync waits are calendar time already contained in the critical branch’s queue plus service. Adding \(\sum_i W(c,i)\) on top of \(T^{fj}(c)\) counts that overlapping slack again. Adding all three branches’ queue, service, and sync counts the whole parallel stage three times and overstates flow by \(2\,T^{fj}(c)\).

Numeric illustration, F02: \(T^{fj}=60\). Branch sums are \(0+50+10\), \(20+40+0\), and \(5+20+35\), each 60. Their total is 180. Syncs alone sum to 45. True flow is \(20+60+15+20=115\). The 180-minute branch total plus upstream, downstream, and final service produces 235, which exceeds the true flow by \(2\times 60\).

**Assumption-based split of the downstream gap (A5).** The logged final resource is treated as sticky. Let \(f(c)\) be the previous `final_end` on that same resource, or \(-\infty\) if the resource has not yet worked. Then

\[
\begin{align*}
C^{wait}(c) &= \max\bigl(0,\ f(c)-t(c,\text{join_ready})\bigr) \\
R^{wait}(c) &= t(c,\text{final_start})-\max\bigl(t(c,\text{join_ready}), f(c)\bigr) \\
T^{down}(c) &= C^{wait}(c)+R^{wait}(c).
\end{align*}
\]

\(C^{wait}\) is time the assigned server is still busy. \(R^{wait}\) is time after both the case is join-ready and that server is free. This split is an assumption about assignment, not a logged queue discipline. The assumption-free quantity used in the flow identity remains \(T^{down}\).

Other assumptions used later:

- **A1.** `join_ready` is the latest branch completion, as defined by the process rule. It is not a logged event.
- **A2.** A branch may start at `fork_release`. The start gap is that branch’s queue. The log cannot show whether the gap is congestion, transport, setup, or a hold.
- **A3.** Service is end minus start. Internal pauses are invisible, so service is elapsed occupancy of the activity, not necessarily touch time.
- **A4.** \(T^{down}\) is called downstream queueing only while A5 holds. Section 7 gives the evidence against reading the whole gap as a capacity queue.
- **A6.** Counterfactual shifts of one branch are applied independently on each case. Shared-resource spillover is not resimulated inside A or B.
- **A7.** Model P, used for final-station reactions: the case keeps its logged resource and its original residual \(R^{wait}\); the new start is \(\max(t'_{join}, f')+R^{wait}\), and final service stays 20 minutes. Predecessor end times are recomputed in case order per resource.
- **A8.** Timestamps are exact at one-minute resolution, as stated. A margin of 5 minutes is only a few ticks.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

**Level of the flow.** Mean flow is \(1383/12 = 115.25\) minutes (median 115, minimum 93 on F01, maximum 130 on F07). Nearest-rank 90th percentile, rank \(\lceil 0.9 \times 12 \rceil = 11\) on the sorted sample, is 125. Mean composition:

| component | total minutes | mean | share of flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Upstream, arrival to fork | 235 | 19.58 | 17.0% |
| Fork–join stage, \(\max_i O_i\) | 732 | 61.00 | 52.9% |
| Downstream gap, final start − join | 176 | 14.67 | 12.7% |
| Final service | 240 | 20.00 | 17.4% |
| Case flow | 1383 | 115.25 | 100% |

The fork–join stage is the majority of elapsed time. On the critical path inside it, queue sums to 260 minutes (mean 21.67) and service to 472 (mean 39.33). Service is still the larger critical-path piece. B’s queue is what makes B overtake A’s longer pure service.

Five definitions agree on the order B, then A, then C: critical frequency, share of fork–join minutes, unique margin, mean completion offset, and mean sync (smaller sync means the branch finishes closer to the join). Mean service has the different order A, then B, then C. A’s 50-minute service is the longest activity body and is also the least variable. It is not the strongest control on completion, because A always starts at the fork and B’s mean queue of 19.17 minutes more than closes B’s 12.5-minute service disadvantage.

There is no aggregate near-tie between branches. B accounts for about four fifths of the minutes by which the leader sits ahead of the runner-up. Case-level near-ties do exist and should not be smoothed away:

- Margin of 5 minutes: F01 (A over B), F08 (C over A), F10 (B over A).
- Margin of 7: F04 (C over A).
- Margin of 10: F02, F05, F11.
- Margins above 15, both B over A: F07 and F12, 25 minutes each.

Median margin is 10. A one-minute recording grid makes the three 5-minute classifications fragile. Flipping all three would move B’s critical count among 7, 8, and 9. B would remain first. The unique-margin totals (120 vs 15 vs 12) would still rank B first.

**How each branch contributes to the join.**

- A sets a hard floor. Offset is exactly 50 on every case, so \(T^{fj}(c) \ge 50\). The two shortest fork–join stages, F01 and F11, sit on that floor. A is critical or runner-up on all 12 cases and is runner-up on all 8 B-critical cases. A is the truncation point for B improvements.
- B sets the typical level and the tail. Mean offset 56.67 is already above A’s floor. Sorted B offsets run from 35 to 75; the two fork–join maxima of 75 are F07 and F12, both B, both with queue 30 and service 45. Eight of twelve joins are exactly B’s end. Mean sync is only 4.33, because on the four cases where B is early the slack is modest (5, 17, 20, 10).
- C is usually early and occasionally decisive. Median C queue is 5, while the mean 10.42 is pulled by 15, 25, and 35. C is critical only on F04 (offset 57, margin 7) and F08 (offset 55, margin 5). On the other 10 cases C has the largest sync. C is never the runner-up. Mean sync of 30.42 is parallel slack, so it is real waiting of completed administrative work and is not serial delay.

Mean flow when B is critical is \(955/8 = 119.4\), against 101.5 on the two A-critical cases and 112.5 on the two C-critical cases. The flow-time tail (125 and 130) is the B tail plus a downstream gap, not the long assay.

**Observed overlap, used only as capacity context.** Concurrent in-progress intervals reach 4 for A, 3 for B, and 3 for C. None of the three branches is a single exclusive server. A’s peak of 4 with a queue of 0 on every case is what a 50-minute activity started every 15 minutes looks like when at least four slots exist. C is also not one FCFS queue: F05 starts C at 09:15 while F04, released earlier, waits until 09:30; F09 starts C at 10:15 while F08 waits until 10:20. The two overtaken cases are exactly the two C-critical cases, and they also have the two longest C services (22 and 30). That cluster is descriptive. A case-specific documentation hold is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause.

**Downstream is only partly server busy-time.** Under A5, 73 of 176 downstream minutes (41.5%, mean 6.08) occur while the assigned final server is still busy, and 103 minutes (58.5%, mean 8.58) occur while that server is already free. Six cases have contention of 0. Four cases start the moment the assigned server frees (F05, F08, F09, F11). The residual is large enough that final-station congestion can mask a branch improvement, and it is also large enough that adding a third server addresses the smaller slice of the gap.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

Rules held fixed unless a counterfactual says otherwise: other branches stay on their logged timestamps; a non-critical branch that finishes still earlier leaves `join_ready` unchanged; if the critical branch is pulled forward by \(\delta\), the join falls by \(\min(\delta,\text{margin})\) and then the runner-up binds. Flow effects are reported under two readings. The direct reading moves `final_start` one-for-one with the join (residual interpreted as a join-relative handoff, no capacity coupling). Model P applies A5 and A7, so predecessor completion can mask or transmit the change. Absolute appointment times for F would push realized branch savings toward zero; that reading is noted and not used as the main figure.

### Counterfactual 1 — A service reduced by 20%

A service is 50 on every case, so 20% is exactly 10 minutes. New A end = logged A end − 10. A queue remains 0.

| case | A was critical? | margin | join reduction | Model P flow reduction |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| F01 | yes | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| F02–F10 except none of these are A-critical | no | — | 0 | 0 |
| F11 | yes | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| F12 | no | — | 0 | 0 |

Join reductions are 5 on F01 and 10 on F11 only. Total join saving 15 minutes, mean 1.25 per case. Ten cases have zero join benefit because A already had positive sync, and making A faster increases that slack. F01 is partial: after 5 minutes B becomes critical (B ends 08:55), and the other 5 minutes of the assay cut are absorbed. F11 captures the full 10 minutes at the join and then ties B at 11:20.

Model P realized flow saving is only F01’s 5 minutes (mean 0.42). F11’s join moves from 11:30 to 11:20, but F11’s start is F09’s end at 11:50, and F09 is untouched, so F11’s downstream gap grows from 20 to 30 and the case flow does not change. No further cascade: F01 ending 5 minutes earlier does not bind F03, whose join is 09:45 and whose start is 10:00.

Direct benefit: at most 1.25 minutes of mean flow, and only on two cases. Zero benefit on the other ten at the join, and on F11 at the flow time as well under Model P. Secondary effect: the intervention does not relieve F, and it does not change B or C. A shorter assay is a method change on a perfectly regular 50-minute activity that already does not queue. After the cut, A’s floor is 40 minutes, which would matter only once B is no longer the leader. Doing this first leaves the sample maximum, F07 at 130, untouched.

The transmission path on F1 is the useful example. F07’s join falls 15 minutes, from 10:55 to 10:40. F04 and F08 similarly gain 8 minutes of flow with no join change, inherited from F02 and F06. In the other direction, F06’s 15-minute join gain is partly masked: F04’s end moves only 8 minutes, and F06 becomes predecessor-bound, so flow falls by 8 rather than 15.

Critical control splits: A alone on F01, F05, F10, F11; B alone on F03, F07, F09, F12; ties A+B on F02 and F06; C still alone on F04 and F08. A second B-only cut has much less room. The A floor is the secondary bottleneck this intervention reveals.

If final starts are fixed appointments, flow savings are about 0. If residuals are join-relative and cases do not couple through F, flow savings equal the join savings, mean 7.71. Model P is the high reading, mean 10.17. The range is the result. A6 also makes 92.5–122 optimistic if pulling one B job forward delays another B job on a shared pool.

### Counterfactual 3 — final-station capacity or a smaller downstream gap

Branch timestamps and `join_ready` stay fixed. Three nested interventions:

| variant | what changes | total minutes saved | mean | cases with zero saving |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3a. Extra final capacity | contention removed, residual kept; cases decouple | 73 | 6.08 | F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F12 (six cases) |
| 3b. Start when the assigned server is free | residual set to 0, two sticky servers, cascade | 171 | 14.25 | none; F11 keeps 5 minutes of contention |
| 3c. Start at `join_ready` | entire downstream gap removed | 176 | 14.67 | none; every case has a positive gap (8 to 20) |

Per-case contention savings for 3a are 0, 0, 0, 8, 10, 0, 0, 15, 15, 5, 20, 0. The 20-minute F11 wait and the 15-minute waits on F08 and F09 are the capacity prizes. Six cases gain nothing from another server because their assigned server was already free at the anchor \(\max(\text{join}, f)\).

Per-case savings for 3b are 8, 15, 15, 18, 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 20, 15, 10. These sum to 171, which equals total downstream 176 minus the 5 minutes F11 still waits for F09. Removing the residual also removes the contention that the residual was pushing onto the next case. With immediate start on the existing two servers, the reconstructed F1 chain is F01 09:00–09:20, F03 09:45–10:05, F05 10:10–10:30, F07 10:55–11:15, F09 11:15–11:35, F11 11:35–11:55. Only F11 overlaps its predecessor. The F2 chain has no remaining overlap. Two servers are enough for this arrival rhythm once residual lag is gone: offered load is about \((20/15)/2 = 2/3\) of the two-server pool.

Direct benefit of 3c is the full observed gap, mean 14.67, including F01’s 8 minutes. That bound is achievable only if every minute of `final_start − join_ready` is removable delay. 3b is the portion that looks removable while still respecting the logged assignment and the 20-minute service. 3a is the smaller portion attributable to the assigned server being busy.

Secondary effects differ from the branch counterfactuals. The join, the critical branch, and synchronization waits do not change. Quality risk sits in the residual: if those minutes are unlogged reading or approval work, deleting them either moves the work into `final_service` or drops it. Throughput does not rise at the observed arrival spacing. Twelve final services occupy 240 minutes. From the first final start at 09:08 to the last end at 12:40 is 212 minutes on two servers, 424 server-minutes, so occupancy in that window is about 57%. The admission rhythm, one case every 15 minutes, is slower than two 20-minute servers.

**Stacking is sub-additive.** Applying counterfactual 2 and residual removal together, under Model P with residuals set to 0, saves 268.5 minutes total (mean 22.4), not \(122+171\). Examples of the overlap: F07’s start can move only once; part of F11’s gain is the same F1 chain viewed from two interventions. Combined new mean flow would be about 92.9 minutes if both mechanisms are real. That joint figure is a second-order estimate under A6 and A7, reported so the two levers are not added naively.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

Two redesigns follow the influence ranking and the masking result. Cutting A’s protocol by 20% is not one of them: mean service is the wrong ranking, ten cases would feel no join effect, and the one full join gain (F11) is absorbed by F1 still finishing F09.

### Redesign 1 — reduce B start latency, then stop at the A floor

Pilot a capacity or release change on the machine stress test aimed at about half of today’s B queue (the counterfactual 2 probe: roughly 8–10 minutes of mean flow if the final station tracks readiness, 7.7 minutes if only the join is counted). Operationally this means earlier access to a stress-test slot: more parallel fixtures, shorter changeover, or releasing the slot at `fork_release` rather than after a 20–30 minute wait. It does not mean shortening A.

- **Throughput.** Inside this morning’s demand, cases already complete and arrivals are slower than the final station. B concurrency peaks at 3, so some parallel capacity already exists. A faster B raises throughput only if B slots are what currently refuse extra arrivals. The arrival-to-fork timestamps themselves fit a stronger throughput constraint, stated as hypothesis H-U below.
- **Flow-time tail.** F07 and F12 are the tail (flow 130 and 125, fork–join 75, B queue 30, B service 45). They keep a 10-minute B lead even after a 50% queue cut, so they improve and they remain B-critical. The sample maximum falls toward about 115 minutes under Model P, conditional on final starts moving.
- **Utilization.** B busy time stays 450 minutes if service is unchanged; waiting falls. A stays at a deterministic 50 minutes of occupancy per case with no queue. Final-server busy time stays 240 minutes. Apparent B utilization falls if slots are added and demand is held fixed.
- **New bottleneck.** After a 50% queue cut, remaining unique B lead in this sample is 27.5 minutes, and A becomes critical or tied on half the cases. Further B waiting reduction is truncated by the 50-minute assay. C’s two spike cases remain C-critical; a B program does not touch them.
- **Synchronization.** C’s mean sync of 30 minutes remains, and A’s sync shrinks on the cases B currently wins. Completed C work will still sit at the join. That slack is not a reason to add administrative staff.
- **Measure first.** On each B start, log the fixture id, whether a fixture was idle, and the reason code (fixture busy, setup, transport, hold for A/C, maintenance). The pilot should publish, per case, the margin of B’s end over A’s end. Stop adding B capacity once that margin is near zero on most cases. Also track whether an earlier B start delays a different case’s B start; A6 will not hold in the live pool.

Hypothesis H-U, not part of the measured decomposition: a single upstream server with a 15-minute service, started at F01’s arrival, reproduces every `fork_release` exactly. F01 queues 0; every later case queues 5 because it arrives 5 minutes before the server frees. The server is then busy without a gap from 07:55 to 10:55. If that mechanism is real, upstream is the throughput ceiling at one case per 15 minutes, while its flow-time contribution above the 15-minute service is only the 5-minute queue. Confirm with an upstream start event before buying prep capacity for flow-time reasons.

### Redesign 2 — remove residual lag at F before adding an F3 server

Pilot an immediate-start rule: when `join_ready` has passed and the assigned reviewer is free, F starts. Keep two reviewers. Counterfactual 3b estimates a mean flow reduction of 14.25 minutes, against 6.08 from adding capacity while leaving the residual in place. The existing pair of servers leaves only 5 minutes of contention in this sample once residual lag is removed.

- **Throughput.** Unchanged at one arrival per 15 minutes. Two servers at 20 minutes of service can accept a case every 10 minutes. A third server would sit at still lower occupancy.
- **Flow-time tail.** Every case has a downstream gap, so the whole distribution moves, by 8 to 20 minutes under 3b. The new maximum is still the long B cases, about 115 minutes (F07 and F12). Residual removal does not touch the fork–join tail.
- **Utilization.** Busy minutes stay 240. Idle-while-a-case-is-waiting shrinks, so work shifts into gaps that are idle today (09:28–09:40, 11:05–11:10, and the post-ready residuals generally). Measured occupancy over the same wall-clock window can look slightly higher because the same 240 minutes finish earlier.
- **New bottleneck.** After the gap is gone, fork–join is about 61 of the remaining ~101 minutes of mean flow. B becomes a larger share of what customers still feel. F itself is then a small residual queue (the 5 minutes on the F1 chain in this sample), not the constraint.
- **Synchronization.** Branch sync is unchanged, because the join timestamp is unchanged. The redesign does not harvest C’s 30 minutes of sync. It does make branch improvements more visible in flow time: counterfactual 1’s F11 gain and counterfactual 2’s cascade both depend on F actually starting earlier.
- **Risk and the first measurement.** The residual may be necessary unlogged work. If reviewers use those minutes to read the three branch packs, the pilot will either inflate `final_service` or weaken the release decision. Before changing the rule, stamp three instants the log does not contain: all three branch artifacts actually in the reviewer’s hands, the reviewer marked free, and `final_start`. If artifacts are late, the gap is a handoff problem and `join_ready` is an early estimate of readiness. If the reviewer is free and the artifacts are present, the gap is a release habit and the immediate-start pilot is the right test. Add F3 only if, after residuals fall, `contention_wait` remains large. In this sample it would not.

C should be handled as an exception path rather than a third redesign. The two critical C cases are the two that were overtaken and the two with elevated C service. A checklist for missing documents on the case, triggered when C has not started within a few minutes of the fork, matches that pattern better than a permanent increase in administrative headcount. Mean C sync is a poor staffing signal.

Shared risks of both redesigns: n = 12 on a single morning, and cases share servers, so these are descriptions of this window rather than stable treatment effects. Margins of 5 minutes can flip under ordinary timestamp noise. Speeding a non-critical branch has a zero case-level join effect until it becomes critical. Quality of the assay and of the release decision is not in the data.

    midnight = datetime.strptime(DAY, "%Y-%m-%d")
    return (clock - midnight).total_seconds() / 60.0


def clock(minute: float) -> str:
    base = datetime.strptime(DAY, "%Y-%m-%d") + timedelta(minutes=float(minute))
    return base.strftime("%H:%M")

def decompose(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    out = frame.copy()
    out["upstream_elapsed"] = out["fork_release"] - out["arrival"]
    for i in BRANCHES:
        out[f"{i}_queue"] = out[f"{i}_start"] - out["fork_release"]
        out[f"{i}_service"] = out[f"{i}_end"] - out[f"{i}_start"]
        out[f"{i}_offset"] = out[f"{i}_queue"] + out[f"{i}_service"]
    end_cols = [f"{i}_end" for i in BRANCHES]
    out["join_ready_min"] = out[end_cols].max(axis=1)
    # Deterministic ties: every branch that attains the max is critical.
    # Label uses fixed order A, B, C. No alphabetical coin-flip and no hidden winner.
    labels = []
    for _, row in out.iterrows():
        winners = [i for i in BRANCHES if row[f"{i}_end"] == row["join_ready_min"]]
        labels.append("+".join(winners))
    out["critical_branch"] = labels
    for i in BRANCHES:
        out[f"{i}_sync"] = out["join_ready_min"] - out[f"{i}_end"]
    ranked = out[end_cols].apply(lambda r: sorted(r, reverse=True), axis=1)
    out["margin"] = ranked.apply(lambda ends: ends[0] - ends[1])
    out["fork_join"] = out["join_ready_min"] - out["fork_release"]
    out["downstream_queue"] = out["final_start"] - out["join_ready_min"]
    out["final_service"] = out["final_end"] - out["final_start"]
    out["total_flow_time"] = out["final_end"] - out["arrival"]
    out["reconstructed"] = (
        out["upstream_elapsed"] + out["fork_join"] + out["downstream_queue"] + out["final_service"]
    )
    return out

def resource_anchor(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Split downstream gap using the logged sticky resource (assumption A5)."""
    out = frame.copy()
    prev_end = {}
    contention, residual, anchor = [], [], []
    for case, row in out.iterrows():
        res = row["final_resource"]
        join = row["join_ready_min"]
        if res not in prev_end:
            free = float("-inf")
        else:
            free = prev_end[res]
        bound = max(join, free)
        contention.append(max(0.0, free - join) if free != float("-inf") else 0.0)
        residual.append(row["final_start"] - bound)
        anchor.append(bound if free != float("-inf") else join)
        prev_end[res] = row["final_end"]
    out["contention_wait"] = contention
    out["residual_wait"] = residual
    out["anchor"] = anchor
    return out

def model_p_starts(frame: pd.DataFrame, join: pd.Series, residual: pd.Series) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """A7: sticky resource, preserved residual, final service unchanged.

    start = max(join, previous end on the same resource) + residual.
    Processing in case order matches the logged F1/F2 alternation.
    """
    prev_end = {}
    starts, ends = {}, {}
    for case, row in frame.iterrows():
        res = row["final_resource"]

        start = earliest + residual.loc[case]
        end = start + row["final_service"]
        starts[case] = start
        ends[case] = end
        prev_end[res] = end
    sched = pd.DataFrame({"model_start": pd.Series(starts), "model_end": pd.Series(ends)})

# 7. Limits of inference

The accounting identity is identified. The causes are not.

**Causality.** The 12 cases are one morning and they share servers, so they are serially dependent. Critical-branch counts and means describe this window. They are not an i.i.d. sample, and an independence-based standard error would overstate precision. Counterfactuals are algebraic probes under A1–A8. They are not estimated treatment effects. A6 in particular ignores the possibility that moving one B start earlier delays another case’s B start.

**`join_ready` is constructed.** No event records the moment both technical results and the document pack were actually usable together. If a branch end is “result posted” and the artifact arrives later, synchronization is understated and the downstream gap contains hidden branch time. If a branch end includes private wrap-up after the result was usable, synchronization is overstated.

**Branch resource capacity is not in the log.** Only start and end are known. Overlap shows that A, B, and C each run several at once (peaks 4, 3, and 3), so a single-server queueing formula does not apply. The number of fixtures, the queue discipline, reneging, and setup-versus-wait cannot be separated. A’s zero queue and flat 50-minute duration are compatible with ample assay slots and a fixed protocol clock; they are also compatible with a timer that includes unlogged waiting inside the activity. C’s overtakes (F05 before F04, F09 before F08) rule out one global FCFS documentation queue and do not by themselves prove case-specific defects. B’s 5–30 minute start gaps are compatible with fixture contention, changeover, or a required settle time. A “queue” reduction is infeasible if the gap is required settle time. That distinction is the measurement redesign 1 asks for, and it cannot be closed from these columns.

**Preemption and rework.** A single start and a single end cannot show interruption, multitasking, or a hidden rework loop. Service is elapsed time. Constant A service of 50 minutes means every assay occupied 50 recorded minutes, not that a technician was touching the sample for 50 minutes.

**Final-station waiting is only partly a queue.** Under the logged assignment, 103 of 176 downstream minutes occur while the assigned server is already free, and there are intervals when both F1 and F2 are idle and a join-ready case has not started (09:28–09:40 for F02; 11:05–11:10 for F07). Those minutes fit a release habit, a handoff, or unlogged review work as well as they fit a queue. The 73 contention minutes fit a sticky-server queue and would shrink under pooling only if the other server was eligible. Eligibility is not logged: the column records who did the work, not who was allowed to do it. Model P is therefore a reading that reproduces the timestamps, not a tested dispatching rule. If the residual is an absolute appointment book, the cascade that lifts counterfactual 2 from 92.5 to 122 minutes does not occur.

**Upstream is a fitting hypothesis, not a logged activity.** Arrival and `fork_release` alone are in the table. A single 15-minute prep server busy from 07:55 to 10:55 fits every fork time exactly, with a 5-minute queue after F01. A rule that simply sets fork delay to 15 minutes for F01 and 20 minutes otherwise also fits. Throughput conclusions that depend on a saturated prep server should wait for an upstream start timestamp.

**What the timestamps do identify.** Given the stated AND-join, each case’s upstream time, branch queue, branch service, sync slack, fork–join length, downstream gap, final service, and flow time are determined, and they add without a remainder. A is the 50-minute floor and the usual runner-up. B is the usual and the tail-controlling branch because of queue plus service. C controls two near-tied cases and otherwise waits. Speeding a branch with positive sync does not move that case’s join. Part of any join improvement on F1/F2 is masked when the next final start is pinned to a predecessor, and part is amplified when that predecessor moves. Those are structural features of this log under the formulas in section 1, and they survive the causal gaps above.
