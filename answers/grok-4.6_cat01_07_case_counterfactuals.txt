## 1. Feasibility model

```
UNIVERSAL
  Register ≺ Triage
  Triage ≺ IdentityCheck
  IdentityCheck ≺ Assessment*
  DocumentCheck* ≺ Assessment*
  Assessment* ≺ Decision
  Decision ≺ Notify
  Assessment*  := latest Assessment after the last completed DocumentCheck
  DocumentCheck* := DocumentCheck that feeds Assessment*

ORIGINALS REQUIRED (K-219, K-337)
  Triage ≺ OriginalsReceipt
  OriginalsReceipt ≺ DocumentCheck
  IdentityCheck may overlap waiting for originals, but not DocumentCheck

ORIGINALS NOT REQUIRED (K-104, K-411)
  after Triage: IdentityCheck ∥ DocumentCheck is allowed

HIGH-RISK OR HIGH-VALUE (K-104, K-337)
  Assessment* ≺ ComplianceReview*
  ComplianceReview* ≺ Decision
  PreliminaryComplianceScreen ⊀̸ Decision
  resource(Assessment*) ≠ resource(ComplianceReview*)

REQUEST-MORE-INFO LOOP (enabled unless explicitly forbidden)
  (Assessment_i ∨ Compliance_i) ≺ RMI_i
  RMI_i ≺ DocumentCheck_{i+1}
  DocumentCheck_{i+1} ≺ Assessment_{i+1}
  if high-risk/high-value: Assessment_{i+1} ≺ ComplianceReview* (new or reaffirmed)

K-104 INTERVENTION-ENABLED
  Triage ≺ PreliminaryEscalation(S1)
  Triage ≺ optional PreliminaryComplianceScreen(C1)
  PreliminaryEscalation may overlap IdentityCheck and DocumentCheck
  Assessment* ≺ FinalComplianceReview(C1)
  a reserved C1 interval does not waive Assessment* ≺ FinalComplianceReview

K-337 INTERVENTION-ENABLED
  Assessment_2 ≺ ComplianceReview* ≺ Decision
  Assessment_2 ⊀ RMI_2   (second loop forbidden)
  Decision ∈ {approve-with-conditions, reject}
```

```
DECLARATIVE / CONDITIONAL CONSTRAINTS
  C1: Register is first; Triage is second.
  C2: Assessment cannot start until both IdentityCheck and the relevant DocumentCheck have completed.
  C3: If originals are required, OriginalsReceipt ≺ DocumentCheck; IdentityCheck may proceed earlier.
  C4: High-risk or high-value cases require ComplianceReview* after Assessment* and before Decision.
  C5: A preliminary compliance or escalation screen never replaces ComplianceReview*.
  C6: After any allowed RMI, a new DocumentCheck and a new Assessment are mandatory; high-risk/high-value cases then need a new or explicitly reaffirmed compliance review.
  C7: Four-eyes: the person who performs Assessment* may not perform ComplianceReview*.
  C8: No resource performs two service intervals at the same clock time.
  C9: Queue snapshots are remaining-work forecasts, not guaranteed start times.
  C10: S1 advice may overlap other checks; S1 cannot sign ComplianceReview*.
  C11: K-337 may not convert an unresolved discrepancy into a silent approve.
  C12: K-411 concurrency is valid only if both tasks actually exist after Triage and timestamps are real start/complete times.
  C13: Holding a provisional C1 slot does not create extra C1 hours; it reorders other compliance work.
```

K-411 is primarily a **timing / concurrency** change: the same activities, same resources, earlier joint enablement. K-104 is **timing plus scarce-capacity reservation** and adds optional early activities; it does not change the allowed decision rule. K-219 is a **routing** change (D1 → D2) that can stochastically add a rework loop. K-337 is the only intervention that **changes the allowed decision policy and deletes a legal control-flow loop**; any time saving is a consequence of that policy change, not a pure schedule improvement.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Triage already saw the sanctions ambiguity but did not open an escalation or compliance path. Identity and the complex D1 check therefore ran as an ordinary high-risk file. Assessment (A1) rediscovered the same issue, S1 advised only after that, and C1 started Wednesday afternoon with almost no reusable work. The long pole before Assessment is D1 (Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40) plus A1’s queue; the long pole after Assessment is C1’s backlog, not the 2 h of S1 advice.

Minimal intervention: At Mon 08:50, emit an S1 packet and a provisional C1 hold. Permit S1, and a limited C1 screen, to run beside IdentityCheck and DocumentCheck. Do not start Decision, and do not treat any Monday/Tuesday C1 reading as ComplianceReview*. Keep A1 as assessor and C1 as final reviewer so four-eyes still holds.

Temporal propagation: Identity, D1, and A1 are untouched, so Assessment still cannot start before both checks finish and A1 is free — factually Tue 15:10–19:10, and that window is the right base. What changes is the post-Assessment wait. Factual C1 starts Wed 13:10 after S1 Tue 19:20–21:20. If S1 has already advised on Monday, that 2 h block leaves the critical path. A provisional Wednesday-morning C1 slot, against Wednesday’s 8 h forecast, can pull the final review from 13:10 toward 08:00–10:00. Guidance says complete preliminary work may cut 1–3 h from a 3–6 h review; the factual review was 4.5 h, so a prepared final review is roughly 1.5–3.5 h. Combining an earlier start with a shorter service gives a base Decision late Wednesday morning rather than 18:00. A more aggressive reserved Tuesday-evening slot is only feasible if C1 is not still working the two urgent Monday additions; that is a best-case branch, not the base. Queue figures remain forecasts: the reserved interval can sit idle if Assessment overruns, in which case this case does not finish earlier and other cases have already been pushed.

Process-feasibility check: Register ≺ Triage ≺ parallel checks is legal because originals are not required. High-risk/high-value still has Assessment* ≺ Final C1 ≺ Decision. Preliminary C1 does not replace that. A1 ≠ C1. S1 may overlap checks. C1 cannot overlap its own preliminary and any other compliance interval. The intervention is feasible as a local rewrite; it is not free of interference.

Best/base/worst case: Best — usable packet, C1 does a short Tuesday-evening final after Assessment, Decision Tue night or first thing Wednesday (~10–22 h saved), decision still approve-with-conditions. Base — Assessment unchanged, S1 off the critical path, Wednesday-morning reserved C1, 1–3 h shorter review, Decision ~Wed 10:30–12:30 (~6–12 h saved). Worst — packet too thin, C1 rereads from scratch, reservation misplaced or consumed by the urgent pair, this case finishes with you or later, and non-target compliance SLAs slip.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 actual remaining-work traces showing zero movable Wednesday slack; timestamps showing C1 already double-booked when the reservation would fire; a rule that preliminary notes cannot be reused, wiping the 1–3 h saving; or Assessment discovering a new issue that forces RMI and a new compliance pass, putting the case behind the factual path.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Originals arrived Mon 17:30. Triage had already routed handwriting to D1. D1 did not start until Tue 09:30 (overnight plus D1’s long Monday queue), then finished a clean 4 h check. A2 started Tue 20:00 after its own queue and M1 decided Wednesday morning. There is no compliance stage. The factual design traded start delay for specialist accuracy.

Minimal intervention: At OriginalsReceipt Mon 17:30, enqueue (or assign) D2 instead of waiting for D1. Leave Identity, Assessment routing, and the decision rule unchanged. Do not invent a compliance step.

Temporal propagation: D2’s Mon 16:00 forecast is 5 h, so D2 is not free at 17:30. If evening document work is allowed — K-104 shows D1 working overnight — D2 can start late Monday and finish in the 4–8 h standard band, stretched toward 6–10 h for handwriting. That can put DocumentCheck complete early Tuesday, several hours before factual 13:30. Assessment then joins A2’s Tuesday queue (6 h at Tue 08:00) earlier and may start mid-afternoon rather than 20:00. M1 may still wait until Wednesday morning, so calendar saving can collapse to less than an hour even when verification finished earlier. If D2 does not work Monday night, the case waits for Tuesday’s D2 queue (4 h at 08:00), starts ~Tue 12:00, and can finish **later** than D1’s 13:30 because D2 is slower. The rework branch dominates uncertainty: any D2 clarification opens RMI (8–36 h external) plus a new DocumentCheck and Assessment, which the factual path never paid.

Process-feasibility check: OriginalsReceipt ≺ DocumentCheck(D2) holds. Identity may run earlier and in parallel with the wait for originals. No compliance or four-eyes issue. D2 cannot start while still serving its residual Monday queue (C8). A second DocumentCheck after RMI is the only legal way to recover from a bad handwriting read.

Best/base/worst case: Best — D2 starts Monday evening, no RMI, A2 Tuesday afternoon, M1 Tuesday evening (~12–16 h saved), still approve. Base — D2 after residual queue, slower service offsets earlier enablement, Decision Wednesday morning ± a few hours (about −8 to +4 h). Worst — D2 requests clarification, weekend or multi-day supplier wait, second check and assessment, Notify Thursday–Friday, and a weaker or delayed decision.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 timestamped clarification rate on handwritten standard files near zero, and evening D2 capacity actually idle Monday — then the optimistic branch becomes the base. Conversely, D2 complex-like 8–14 h service on handwriting, or a policy that D2 cannot work evenings, makes the intervention slower even without rework.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: After a first, justified RMI, Assessment 2 on Friday still lacked one confirmation. The process opened a second external loop that sat over the weekend (Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00). Only after DocumentCheck 3 and Assessment 3 did C1 review on Tuesday and M1 approve Wednesday. Most calendar time after Friday is **waiting**, not skilled work. The factual Decision is an approve after the discrepancy was cleared.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), prohibit RMI_2. Treat Assessment 2 as Assessment*. Require C1 then M1. M1 must either reject or approve with conditions that name the remaining inconsistency. Do not keep the factual approve-as-cleared label.

Temporal propagation: Cutting RMI_2 removes the weekend from the critical path and also removes Mon DocumentCheck 3, Tue Assessment 3, and the Tue 15:30 C1 start that depended on them. The new critical path is Assessment 2 → C1 → M1. Thursday’s C1 forecast is only 5 h, so a Friday-ready high-risk file can plausibly reach C1 Friday afternoon or Monday morning — not the following Tuesday. C1 on an incomplete file is more likely 4–6 h than a short rubber stamp. If C1 and M1 still operate Friday evening, Notify can move to Friday close of business (~5 calendar days saved). If M1 does not decide Friday night, the base is Monday Decision (~2 days saved). Work hours saved (DocumentCheck 3 + Assessment 3 + some wait) are much smaller than calendar hours saved; that is why the local gain looks huge.

Process-feasibility check: High-risk/high-value still requires ComplianceReview* after the last Assessment and before Decision. A1 performed Assessment 2; C1 performs compliance — four-eyes holds. Rule 7 is not triggered because no second RMI is issued. The intervention is feasible only because the decision **policy** is relaxed: the unresolved item must appear in the Decision. A silent approve would be non-compliant.

Best/base/worst case: Best — C1 Friday afternoon, M1 Friday evening, explicit conditions, no appeal (~5 days faster). Base — C1 Friday/Monday, M1 Monday, approve-with-conditions or reject (~2 days faster, different decision). Worst — C1 or M1 refuses the incomplete file and forces the same RMI anyway; or a reject/weak approve generates appeal and later loss. Time then matches the factual path or the organization pays the cost outside this process.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The residual inconsistency is shown to be immaterial, so a conditional approve is outcome-equivalent to the factual approve. Or C1 has no Friday/Monday slack, so ComplianceReview* cannot start until the factual Tuesday anyway. Or subsequent loss data show the second confirmation was necessary to avoid a large payout — then the “faster” path is not better.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: After Triage the legacy system created only the identity task. The case lock blocked document ownership until IdentityCheck completed (Tue 13:20). D2 was created at 15:00 and finished 19:30. Assessment waited overnight in a grown A2 queue (Wed 09:30–12:00). Serialisation, not service time, is what kept the checks from overlapping. Originals are not required, so the rules already allow concurrency.

Minimal intervention: At Triage (Tue 09:35) create both verification tasks and apply a controlled lock workaround so I2 and D2 can own work at the same time. Record actual start and complete times, not a later batch.

Temporal propagation: I2’s Tuesday morning remaining work is about 2 h at 08:00, so IdentityCheck still starts late morning and still lasts ~1.5–2 h — close to factual. D2’s remaining work is about 4 h at 08:00, so D2 can start around midday rather than 15:00 and, at the factual 4.5 h service, can finish mid-to-late afternoon instead of 19:30. Both predecessors of Assessment are then ready 2–3 h earlier. That does **not** move Decision by 2–3 h. A2 already had a 6 h forecast at Tue 08:00; a case becoming ready Tue 16:30–17:30 typically waits overnight and starts Wednesday morning only slightly before 09:30. Earlier readiness is real; earlier **completion** is mostly eaten by Assessment. Only if A2 takes the case Tuesday evening does Decision move into Wednesday late morning.

Process-feasibility check: No originals, so Identity ∥ Document is legal. Both must still complete before Assessment. No compliance/four-eyes constraint. I2 and D2 must not be double-booked with other work. The workaround is process-feasible only if the log times are the times the resources actually worked.

Best/base/worst case: Best — D2 finishes mid-afternoon, A2 works Tuesday evening, Decision Wednesday morning (~4–6 h saved). Base — checks finish earlier, overnight Assessment queue absorbs most slack, Decision ~1–3 h earlier. Worst — Assessment congestion leaves Notify at Wed 14:30; simultaneous I+D load slows other cases; batch-entered timestamps show illegal or fictitious overlap.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A2 (or A1) idle at Tue 16:00–20:00, in which case upstream saving transmits one-for-one. Or the workaround cannot release the lock without corrupting the case record, in which case the factual serial path is the only feasible one.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation + C1 reservation | S1 a few hours earlier (overlap OK); C1 often **more** total hours (preliminary + final) and a blocked calendar interval | This case’s post-Assessment wait; possibly later C1 if preliminary substitutes | C1 now, especially Monday–Wednesday; urgent compliance | The two urgent Monday compliance files and any case behind C1 | Locally faster; system-wide likely slower or zero-sum | Reservation is a reorder of a single server. Two-touch review increases C1 demand unless the packet is reusable. Idle reserved slots destroy utilisation |
| K-219 assign D2 at originals | Moves 4 h+ of document work from D1 to slower D2; expected extra RMI adds a second Document+Assessment cycle with positive probability | D1 (handwriting no longer waits for the specialist) | D2 immediately; Assessment if rework appears | Other D2-served standard files; later D1-served corporate files gain | Ambiguous; flips sign if rework rate is material | Start-time gain versus service-time loss plus a heavy-tailed external RMI. Skill mismatch turns a wait substitution into demand creation |
| K-337 suppress second RMI | Removes DocumentCheck 3, Assessment 3, and weekend-wait occupancy; pulls C1/M1 forward to Fri/Mon | D1 and A1 late-week / next Monday; this case’s calendar wait | C1 and M1 Friday–Monday; possible appeal/rework **outside** the process | Other Friday compliance/decision files; Monday D1/A1 may go idle | Faster locally; system quality and Friday congestion worsen | Calendar time ≫ work time because of the weekend. Deleting a loop changes the decision distribution, not just occupancy |
| K-411 parallel I+D | Same service minutes, higher **simultaneous** I and D utilisation; Assessment arrivals bunch | Identity/document idle time caused by the lock | Assessment (Tue night–Wed); peak Identity and Document | Other Tuesday assessments and any case sharing I2/D2 at the new peak | Small local gain; near-zero system gain once Assessment is saturated | Bottleneck migration: harvested verification slack reappears as Assessment wait. Batch timestamps hide the peak |

A 100-case weekly rollout turns each local slack harvest into contention. C1 is a single certified reviewer. Monday snapshots already show 18–20 h of compliance work, with two urgent files added by 16:00. If even 20 high-risk arrivals take a 2 h preliminary screen, that is 40 h of displaced C1 time — more than two reviewer-days — before any reserved final slot is used. Reservations then fail in the usual way: Assessment finishes later than the forecast used to place the hold, the slot goes idle, and non-target urgent cases have already waited. The local story “start compliance earlier” does not add C1 hours.

D1–D2 substitution moves wait from the specialist to the generalist. D2 is slower on difficult handwriting and more likely to open RMI. At a 20–30% rework rate, 100 cases create 20–30 extra document-assessment cycles, enough to rebuild a queue on D2 and lengthen A2. The same shift frees D1 for complex corporate files, so spillover is heterogeneous: K-104-like cases can benefit while handwritten standard cases and other D2 work suffer.

Suppressing the second RMI recovers real D1/A1 time after Friday and removes weekend-bound external waits. Applied at volume it also advances Compliance and Decision into Friday, just as the mid-week C1 backlog is clearing. Some risk leaves the process as appeals or losses rather than as visible cycle time. The first RMI, and slow suppliers, remain.

Parallel verification does not add much work per case, but it synchronises Identity and Document completions. A week of such cases arrives at Assessment as a tighter wave. Tuesday–Wednesday A1/A2 queues grow and absorb most of the local saving. If the workaround is batch-logged, measured concurrency diverges from actual occupancy, hiding no-overlap breaches and corrupting the queue forecasts the next intervention will trust.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411-like: not high-risk, not high-value, no originals, digital standard documents, legacy lock would otherwise serialise checks | K-104-like: high-risk or high-value, sanctions/ambiguity flag at Triage, no originals required, complex documents allowed |
| assignment rule | At Triage, create Identity and Document tasks together; unlock concurrent ownership; no change to who assesses or decides | At Triage, send S1 packet and place a **soft** C1 hold after the forecast Assessment window; hard preemption of already-urgent C1 work forbidden |
| sample/support logic | Randomise eligible Tuesday–Thursday arrivals for 2 weeks or until ~40 cases per arm. Power for a 2–4 h cycle-time shift, not for rare quality events | Randomise eligible Monday–Tuesday high-risk arrivals for 2 weeks or ~20–30 cases. Pair with C1 remaining-work snapshots every 1–2 h |
| primary outcome | Case cycle time Register→Notify, reported as a range with Assessment-wait mediation | Cycle time Register→Notify **and** C1 start lag after Assessment* |
| process-path outcome | Share of cases with true overlapping I and D intervals; time from Triage to both-checks-complete | Share with S1 before Assessment; share whose Final C1 actually used preliminary notes; reservation idle rate |
| quality outcome | Decision unchanged rate; audit exceptions on the workaround; identity/document error or reopen rate | Four-eyes breaches (target 0); C1 “read from scratch” rate; decision/condition mix vs control |
| queue-spillover outcome | A2/A1 queue at the hour both checks complete; I2/D2 wait of non-pilot cases the same afternoon | C1 wait of non-pilot and urgent compliance cases; count of displaced C1 starts |

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 is the most robust intervention. It only enables parallelism the rules already allow when originals are not required, leaves the decision policy untouched, and fails softly: if Assessment is congested, the case waits in the same place as in the factual path. Residual risks — logging integrity and a modest Assessment peak — are visible in a small pilot.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite the largest calendar saving. Removing the second Request More Info is not a schedule tweak. It changes the evidence that may support a high-risk, high-value Decision. Cycle time falls because a weekend-sized external wait disappears, but the unresolved discrepancy must become conditions or a rejection. That shift is easy to miss if the scoreboard is throughput.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104’s reserved C1 slot. For one case, borrowing a few hours from a 16–20 h compliance queue looks cheap. As policy, reservation plus two-touch preliminary review exhausts the only certified reviewer and delays other urgent compliance work. “Start compliance earlier” does not survive a single-server bottleneck.

K-219 sits between: one file may start sooner on D2, but Triage’s factual D1 routing was already a handwriting quality control. At volume that becomes systematic rework and a D2/Assessment bulge.

The most valuable extra data are hourly C1 remaining-work traces, not four daily snapshots; D2 clarification rates on handwritten versus typed standard documents; Assessment queue length at the minute a verification pair becomes ready, to test bottleneck migration; and paired actual-start versus recorded-start times on workaround cases, so true concurrency is not confused with batch entry.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — The normative partial order, including Assessment* ≺ ComplianceReview* on high-risk/high-value work and the four-eyes rule, remains binding in every counterfactual except the explicit K-337 ban on RMI_2.
2. **temporal** — Snapshot queue times are unbiased but noisy forecasts of remaining work at that clock time, not guaranteed offsets to the next start.
3. **resource** — C1 is the only person who can complete ComplianceReview* in this window; S1 cannot substitute.
4. **temporal** — Some document work can run outside 08:00–18:00 (K-104’s overnight D1 interval), but Assessment, Decision, and most Compliance finishes cluster on extended weekdays; weekend Decision/Compliance capacity is limited.
5. **behavioral** — D2 has a materially higher chance than D1 of requesting clarification on difficult handwriting; K-219’s clean D1 check is evidence of that skill gap.
6. **quality** — A K-337 Decision taken without the second confirmation is not outcome-equivalent to the factual approve; at least the condition set or accept/reject label can change.
7. **interference** — A reserved C1 interval or extra D2 occupancy displaces non-target work one-for-one while that resource is busy; single-case ceteris paribus does not survive identical policy at n = 100.
8. **behavioral** — Staff will run the K-411 workaround as intended if live timestamps are required; without that control they may backfill overlapping times, producing recorded concurrency that did not occur.
