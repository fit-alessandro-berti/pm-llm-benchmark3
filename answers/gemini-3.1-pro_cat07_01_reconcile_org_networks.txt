# 2. Competing role hypotheses

- **Mechanism**: Mira serves as the designated, officially recognized queue manager for exceptions flagged by the rules engine or escalated by temporary staff.
- **Evidence supporting it**: She holds the title "Coordinator," handles 48% of exception cases, and receives 920 direct handovers from Bot_Rules for exception routing. Removing her is predicted to increase formal escalations.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**: The process owner views her as "just a queue cleaner," suggesting a lack of formal authority. Adjusters rely on her personal knowledge rather than standard operating procedures.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**: Strict, rules-based routing patterns should emerge from her node. Most exception flags raised by Bot_Rules will route directly to her before reaching any human adjuster.

### Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency
- **Mechanism**: Mira possesses critical implicit knowledge of the organization, making her a single point of failure for routing complex or anomalous cases.
- **Evidence supporting it**: During her 6-day absence, the 90th percentile cycle time for exception cases spiked from 8.4 to 13.9 days. She has a massive handover betweenness (0.77), indicating most complex paths must traverse her node.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**: Her high processing speed (4.2 min service time) contradicts the idea that she is a capacity bottleneck when present. Normal claims were unaffected by her absence.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**: During future absences or volume spikes, wait times (queue times) *immediately preceding* her typical activities will increase dramatically, while her individual service time remains low.

### Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker
- **Mechanism**: Mira acts as the relational "glue" connecting siloed departments (Team North, Team South, Medical, Legal) and translating outputs from automated bots to human workers.
- **Evidence supporting it**: She frequently subcontracts to highly specialized silos (Omar_Medical, Priya_Legal) and routinely hands off to adjusters across both North and South teams. Adjusters trust that she "knows where hard cases should go."
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**: Her low working-together centrality indicates she passes cases across boundaries rather than actively spanning and synthesizing knowledge across them in a collaborative way.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**: Cross-functional handovers (e.g., from Legal back to an Adjuster, or North to Medical) will almost always be intermediated by Mira rather than occurring as direct peer-to-peer handovers.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk**: Low during normal operations due to her fast service time, but highly vulnerable to availability shocks. She acts as a structural choke point for exceptions.
- **Coordination risk**: High. The organization relies on her implicit knowledge to coordinate complex claims. If she leaves, the informal routing rules break down.
- **Key-person risk**: Critical. As evidenced by the severe degradation in exception processing during her 6-day absence, the process is highly dependent on her presence to function efficiently.
- **Compliance/control risk**: Notable. She detects issues and routes them to specialists, giving her wide discretion over exception handling. Without formal oversight, this unchecked authority could bypass standard quality gates, though there is no direct evidence of wrongdoing.
- **Workload fairness risk**: Moderate. Because she controls the flow of reassigned and missing-info claims to adjusters (Carla, Diego, Elise, Farid), she could inadvertently skew workloads, though current data shows relatively balanced handovers between senior and junior staff.
- **Data-quality risk**: High. If her implicit triage decisions are logged only as generic activities (e.g., "Assign Specialist"), the underlying business logic remains hidden from the event log, hindering future automation efforts.
