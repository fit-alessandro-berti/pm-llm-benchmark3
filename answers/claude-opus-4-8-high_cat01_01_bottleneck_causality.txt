## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded** (with a plausible sliver of *beneficial* Orion effect masked by case mix). Confidence: **68/100**.

The decisive signal is a Simpson-style reversal. Crude cycle time is higher for Orion (mean 21.96 vs 15.24; median 18.15 vs 10.60), which superficially supports management's proposal. But within *every* observed complexity stratum, Orion is equal or faster: High 31.57 vs 35.00, Medium 17.17 vs 19.38, Low 7.50 vs 9.64. The crude gap is manufactured by routing: Orion carries 42.9% high-complexity, 92.9% night, and 64.3% compliance-required work, versus Nova's 12.5%, 25.0%, 25.0%. So the *total* association is upward while the *within-stratum* contrast is flat-to-favorable.

Strongest evidence for the verdict: the reversal plus the explicit routing rule ("Orion usually selected for difficult cases") and lopsided covariate balance. Strongest evidence against: stratification is coarse (Orion has only 2 Low cases, Nova only 2 High), night and compliance load confound Orion almost perfectly, and unobserved case risk/reviewer skill are uncontrolled—so I cannot rule out a small genuine effect in either direction.

Why the 50% reassignment may backfire: moving Orion's *hard, night, compliance* cases to Nova removes them from the faster-per-stratum group, plausibly increasing their cycle time, while overloading Nova's day-oriented capacity and night coverage. The crude mean would fall only through relabeling, not real improvement—an aggregation artifact, not a mechanism.

## 2. Causal graph

```text
complexity            -> routing_rule, compliance_required, queue_wait, review_service, rework, unobserved_case_risk
unobserved_case_risk  -> complexity, compliance_required, rework, quality_outcome, review_service
routing_rule          -> resource_group
shift                 -> routing_rule, arrival_load, queue_wait
arrival_load          -> queue_wait
resource_group        -> queue_wait, review_service, rework, unobserved_reviewer_skill
unobserved_reviewer_skill -> review_service, rework, quality_outcome
compliance_required   -> compliance_performed
compliance_performed  -> queue_wait, cycle_time
queue_wait            -> cycle_time
review_service        -> cycle_time
rework                -> review_service, queue_wait, cycle_time
cycle_time            -> case_completed
quality_outcome       -> case_completed
```

Supported by supplied facts: `complexity/shift -> routing_rule -> resource_group` (stated routing engine, difficulty and night coverage); `compliance_required -> compliance_performed` (performed iff required); `arrival_load -> queue_wait`; `queue_wait + review_service + rework -> cycle_time` (definitional). Assumptions: `resource_group -> unobserved_reviewer_skill/review_service` (skill-mix bundle), `unobserved_case_risk` arrows, and `cycle_time/quality -> case_completed` (only completed cases observed → selection). Suppressed dynamic: `arrival_load` and `queue_wait` co-evolve over the shift (a busy night raises queue, which delays reviews, which lengthens future queues)—a feedback loop a static DAG cannot represent; likewise rework re-enters the review queue in real time.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Case mix confounding (crude gap is complexity/shift/compliance) | Routing sends hard/night/compliance cases to Orion; those cost more wait+service | Reversal in all 3 strata; Orion 42.9% High/92.9% Night/64.3% compliance | Only 2-case cells at extremes; residual confounding possible | Complexity-standardized contrast + exact matching on complexity×shift×compliance; positivity check | Standardized Orion−Nova ≈ 0 or slightly negative; matched pairs show Orion ≤ Nova |
| 2 | Beneficial Orion effect masked by mix (skill) | More certified reviewers → faster/cleaner review per case | Orion ≤ Nova in every stratum; High −3.43 h | Effect within noise; unobserved skill not measured | Within-reviewer / within-route comparison; front-door via review_service | On matched hard cases Orion review_service ≤ Nova and cycle ≤ Nova |
| 3 | Shift/load is the real driver | Night + high arrival_load lengthen queue_wait regardless of group | Night longer; Orion 92.9% night; wait dominates cycle | Group and shift nearly collinear → hard to separate | Compare Nova-night vs Orion-night, Nova-day vs Orion-day; queue-state reconstruction | Within same shift, group gap shrinks toward 0; wait explained by load not group |
| 4 | Compliance/rework mediation | Required compliance adds queue+service; rework re-enters review loop | wait_before_review includes compliance wait; Orion higher compliance% & rework% | Mediators, not confounders → don't prove Orion harm | Mediation/front-door decomposition of cycle into wait vs service vs rework | Most of Orion's excess raw wait attributable to compliance_performed + rework, not group |
| 5 | Selection/measurement on completed cases | Only completed cases observed; abandoned/open censored; 30d outcome post-process | "Only completed cases present"; "post-process factors" | Cannot test with given data alone | Recover open/abandoned cases; survival/censoring model; negative-control outcome | If Nova abandons/queues more hard cases, its completed sample looks artificially fast |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Diagnostic (no change) | Freeze routing; standardize + match on complexity×shift×compliance; positivity audit | Confounding structure | Case (analysis only) | No operational change; committee sign-off | DFG per group, variant frequencies, wait vs service split, overlap counts | Establishes whether any group gap survives adjustment | Halt rollout if standardized gap ≈ 0 |
| 1 — Small randomized routing pilot | Randomize the *eligible-either-way* cases (medium complexity, both shifts) between Orion/Nova | Isolate group effect free of routing confounding | Case, within positivity region | Cap at ~15–20% volume; exclude cases only one group can safely handle; monitor SLA | Per-arm cycle, wait, service, rework, conformance to standard variant | Clean within-stratum causal contrast where overlap exists | Stop if pilot arm cycle > control +1 SD or SLA breach |
| 2 — Capacity intervention (not relabeling) | Add compliance/queue capacity on night shift (extra compliance slots, night reviewer buffer) | queue_wait / compliance bottleneck | Shift-day (time block) | Staffing floor; night coverage preserved | wait_before_review distribution, queue length reconstruction, compliance wait share | Tests whether wait (dominant term) is capacity-driven, not group-driven | Roll back if night wait not reduced after 2 weeks |
| 3 — Targeted, gated reassignment | Reassign only *low/medium day* cases (positivity-safe) in modest share, informed by Phase 1 | Load balancing without harming hard-case handling | Case cohort | Never reassign high-complexity night cases blindly; keep Orion for difficult work | Per-cohort cycle, appeal rate, Nova utilization/overload | Confirms marginal reassignment helps only where overlap exists | Reverse if Nova cycle rises or appeals climb |

Final recommendation (120–200 words): Do not implement the blanket 50% reassignment. The crude Orion penalty is an aggregation artifact: within every complexity level Orion is at least as fast as Nova, and Orion's higher raw mean reflects its assignment to hard, night, compliance-heavy work. Moving half of that work—especially difficult night cases—to Nova would likely *raise* those cases' cycle times (Nova is slower per stratum), strain Nova's day-oriented capacity, and jeopardize night coverage, while the headline mean improves only through relabeling. Instead, run Phase 0 diagnostics to confirm the reversal survives matching and positivity checks, then a small randomized pilot restricted to the overlap region (medium-complexity cases either group can take) to obtain a clean group contrast. In parallel, attack the actual bottleneck—queue_wait, which dominates cycle time and is inflated by night load and compliance steps—by adding night compliance and reviewer capacity rather than changing labels. Only after Phase 1 evidence should any reassignment proceed, limited to positivity-safe low/medium day cases, with SLA and appeal-rate stop rules.

def dfg_for_group(sub, group_name):
    # Build a pm4py-parseable log string; each line = one case's activity sequence.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved case risk within complexity levels.** Orion may still get the *hardest* cases inside each label. *Most valuable addition:* a continuous risk/priority score (or claim amount, prior-appeal flag) recorded at Classify, enabling finer matching and a Rosenbaum-style sensitivity analysis.

2. **Group–shift collinearity (positivity).** Orion is 92.9% night, Nova mostly day, so the group effect cannot be cleanly separated from shift for most cells. *Most valuable addition:* deliberate cross-assignment (some Orion-day, Nova-night) via a routing pilot to create overlap.

3. **Selection on completed cases only.** Abandoned/still-open cases are censored, so Nova's fast completed sample may hide slow/dropped hard cases. *Most valuable addition:* records of open/abandoned cases with arrival and last-event timestamps for a survival/censoring model.

4. **Reviewer skill and true per-visit service.** review_service bundles skill and rework; equal stratum service could hide offsetting effects. *Most valuable addition:* reviewer ID and per-review-visit timestamps to compute within-reviewer, per-visit service times.

5. **Queue-state and load definition.** wait_before_review mixes queueing and compliance waiting, and arrival_load is only a 3-level label. *Most valuable addition:* event-level timestamps and concurrent queue length at review entry to reconstruct actual queue state and isolate the compliance-wait component.
