## # 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic Scenario (15–20% improvement in cycle time, low rework)
- **Handover pattern**:
  - Nominal handovers reduced by 12–15% (fewer local-specialist loops).
  - Central queues stabilize; cross-team handovers drop to 3 per 100 cases.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**:
  - Median wait after handover increases slightly (e.g., +0.8h for technical advice), but tail latency remains stable.
  - Shared approval queue wait time <3.5h (vs. 2.4h local).
- **Cycle-time distribution**:
  - Median cycle time drops to ~5.5d (from 6.8–8.6d).
  - 90th percentile cycle time drops to ~14d (from 16.4–22.8d).
- **Rework and quality**:
  - Rework rate drops to ~7% (from 8.2–11.5%).
  - SLA miss rate drops to ~5%.
- **Role clarity and accountability**:
  - Clear shared-service roles; standardized decision criteria.
  - Accountability maintained via shared queue ownership.
- **Fairness implications**:
  - All teams improve; East benefits most (relief of E_Tech overload).
  - No significant unfairness across customer segments.
- **Early warning signs**:
  - None in first 8–12 weeks.

### Central Scenario (5–10% improvement, mixed outcomes)
- **Handover pattern**:
  - Nominal handovers reduced by 8–12%.
  - Central queues form; cross-team handovers drop to 4–5 per 100 cases.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**:
  - Median wait after handover increases by 1–2h (e.g., technical advice wait ~4.5h).
  - Shared approval queue wait time ~3.2h.
- **Cycle-time distribution**:
  - Median cycle time ~6.0–6.5d.
  - 90th percentile cycle time ~15–17d.
- **Rework and quality**:
  - Rework rate ~8–9%.
  - SLA miss rate ~6–7%.
- **Role clarity and accountability**:
  - Shared roles clear, but local staff feel "disconnected."
  - Accountability diluted; some cases "fall through cracks."
- **Fairness implications**:
  - North/South see minor improvements; East improves significantly.
  - High-complexity cases in North/South experience longer waits.
- **Early warning signs**:
  - Local staff report reduced informal advice.
  - East team reports relief; North/South report frustration.

### Adverse Scenario (cycle time stable or worse, rework up, fairness issues)
- **Handover pattern**:
  - Nominal handovers reduced by <5% (centralization creates new handovers).
  - Cross-team handovers rise to 8–10 per 100 cases due to misrouting.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**:
  - Median wait after handover increases by 3–5h (e.g., technical advice wait ~6h).
  - Shared approval queue wait time >4h; tail latency >24h.
- **Cycle-time distribution**:
  - Median cycle time ~7.0–7.5d (no improvement).
  - 90th percentile cycle time >20d (worse than current).
- **Rework and quality**:
  - Rework rate >10%.
  - SLA miss rate >10%.
- **Role clarity and accountability**:
  - Roles unclear; shared approvers overwhelmed.
  - Local staff bypass rules; informal handovers return.
- **Fairness implications**:
  - East benefits; North/South suffer.
  - High-complexity and high-value customer segments face longer waits.
  - Legal/risk specialists become bottleneck; wait times >12h.
- **Early warning signs**:
  - Local staff report "workarounds" and "going around the system."
  - East team reports relief; North/South report delays and rework.
  - Customer complaints spike for high-value segments.

## # 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency for high-complexity cases**:
   - Average cycle time may improve, but high-complexity variants (top 10%) may see 20–30% longer waits due to shared-service routing.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.filter_variants` by `case_complexity`; compare 90th percentile cycle time before/after.

2. **Local knowledge loss and rework rebound**:
   - Early informal triage by embedded specialists prevents 6–9% of downstream rework; loss may cause rework to rise after 3–6 months.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` by team; monitor `local_expertise_flag` frequency.

3. **Fairness degradation for high-value segments**:
   - High-value customers may get routed to shared queues with longer waits; fairness issues masked by average metrics.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` by `customer_segment` and `priority`; compare wait times.

4. **Accountability dilution and exception handling**:
   - Shared approvers may deprioritize "edge cases"; exceptions handled informally, increasing tail latency.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` for ad hoc transfers; monitor `reassignment_count`.

5. **Learning loss and skill atrophy**:
   - Local staff stop developing technical skills; future hiring and training costs rise.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` for reduced co-occurrence; survey staff on skill confidence.

6. **Resilience to absence**:
   - Shared-service queues become single points of failure; absence of one specialist causes cascading delays.
   - *Detection*: `pm4py.discover_dfg` during absence periods; monitor queue length spikes.

## # 5. Pilot design

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Pilot Scope** | Two teams: North and East (representing high and low current utilization). Include all case types and customer segments. |
| **Comparison Group** | South team (unchanged) as control; compare North/East post-change to South. |
| **Pre/Post Observation Window** | 12 weeks pre-change; 16 weeks post-change (to capture tail effects). |
| **Segmentation** | By team, `case_complexity` (low/medium/high), `required_specialty`, and `customer_segment` (standard/high-value). |
| **Guardrail Metrics** | - Median cycle time 7.5d (no worse than current East).<br>- 90th percentile cycle time 20d.<br>- Rework rate 9.5%.<br>- SLA miss rate 8%.<br>- Specialist utilization 90%.<br>- No increase in cross-team handover frequency. |
| **Staff Feedback Collection** | Weekly pulse surveys (5 questions) on role clarity, workload, and informal advice availability. Monthly focus groups. |
| **Rollback or Adjustment Triggers** | - Cycle time 90th percentile >20d for 2 consecutive weeks.<br>- Rework rate >10% for 3 consecutive weeks.<br>- Specialist utilization >90% for 2 consecutive weeks.<br>- Staff feedback: >30% report "unclear role" or "can't get advice" for 2 consecutive weeks. |
| **Adjustment Options** | - Reintroduce one embedded specialist in North.<br>- Adjust routing rules to allow local triage for medium-complexity cases.<br>- Add "fast lane" for high-value customers. |

---

## # 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation**: **Pilot first**, with conditional rollout.

**Rationale**:
- The restructuring has plausible benefits (specialist utilization, standardization), but risks (tail latency, local knowledge loss, fairness) are material and may not be visible in early averages.
- East team may benefit, but North/South may suffer; a pilot with North and East allows testing of both scenarios.
- Management should **not infer success** from early average cycle time improvements alone; must monitor tail latency, rework, role clarity, and fairness.
- If pilot meets guardrail metrics for 12 weeks, proceed with phased rollout (South next, then Legal/Risk integration).
- If pilot triggers rollback triggers, modify routing rules or reintroduce embedded specialists before full rollout.
