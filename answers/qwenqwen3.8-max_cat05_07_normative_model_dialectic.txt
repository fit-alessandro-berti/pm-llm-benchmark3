## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that the operation materially departed from the controlled standard CS-9, and that M0 remains the best available formal expression of those mandatory controls.

**1. CS-9 is the governing approved standard.**  
CS-9 is controlled, formally approved, and effective throughout the relevant period. It requires sanctions and quality review before activation and manual procurement approval before activation. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 undercuts any claim that PD-3 amended the standard. **2. Some deviations are clear, high-risk, and unauthorized.**  
- **SUP-703**: HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, operator selected pilot despite ineligibility, no manual approval, quality after activation, EUR 180,000 first purchase. This is not a harmless technical deviation. It is misuse of an exception.

- **SUP-712**: The requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through aliases. CS-9 explicitly requires different humans. M0’s silence is not a defense because the resource rule exists in CS-9.  
- **SUP-714**: Auto_Approve used on 2026-08-02, after PD-3 expired. Good faith or belief in imminent permanent approval does not create authority.  
- **SUP-705**: The same emergency pattern continued after FD-7 expired. A factual emergency does not replace written authority where the directive expressly expired.

**3. Expiry matters.**  
Both PD-3 and FD-7 were time-boxed. FD-7 expired automatically. PD-3 ended on 2026-07-31. Continued use of Auto_Approve in August, and continued use of emergency-style activation after 2026-07-12, show normalization of exceptions. The aggregate evidence supports this: six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in the first week of August.

**4. The pilot was not uniformly clean.**  
Even if many Auto_Approve cases were eligible, 14 were clearly ineligible and 9 lack eligibility attributes. The exception cannot be accepted where eligibility is not proven. The 9 missing-attribute cases are especially important because exception authority depends on case-level eligibility.

**5. Notification evidence deteriorated.**  
After CRM migration, notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. In **SUP-706**, the onboarding log has no notification and the CRM link is a mutable text field entered two days later. Notification is not a trivial administrative step; it is part of the normative closure control. Weak notification evidence undermines auditability.

**6. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.**  
SUP-707’s `Request_More_Info` cycles may have improved decision quality, and the aggregate evidence shows no activation before requested evidence arrived. But M0 has no loop and no such activity. Compliance may agree that the behavior is desirable, yet desirability is not authority. If the organization wants RMI, it should amend the model and procedure.

**7. Model and data defects are real but do not excuse substantive risk.**  
The prosecution should acknowledge that M0 is defective in several ways: it lacks RMI, lacks resource constraints, lacks reactivation scope, lacks rejected-lead scope, does not handle label equivalence, and does not reflect CRM notification capture. SUP-711’s duplicate registration and SUP-713’s label rename are genuine data/model problems. But these defects do not explain or justify SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, or SUP-705. They show that M0 needs revision; they do not show that high-risk unauthorized activation is compliant.

**Prosecution conclusion:**  
The operation is materially noncompliant where it used auto-approval outside eligibility, ignored expiry, activated before sanctions, or violated segregation of duties. M0 should be enforced as to its core CS-9 controls until formally amended.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is that M0 is obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, and applied to an event log that no longer reliably captures the actual process.

**1. M0 is semantically incomplete.**  
M0 assumes a simple onboarding path with no iterative information requests. But operations commonly uses one or more `Request_More_Info` cycles, and Compliance agrees this is desirable. SUP-707 shows the problem: two RMI events obtained missing certificates before approval. M0 cannot express this. Treating SUP-707 as substantive noncompliance confuses model omission with process misconduct.

**2. M0 does not represent authorized overlays.**  
M0 has no low-risk auto-approval route and no emergency provisional route. Yet PD-3 and FD-7 were documented governance artifacts. SUP-702 was a LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group, within the PD-3 window, with sanctions before activation, quality review next business day, and stable CRM notification evidence. SUP-704 was a critical pump supplier activated during FD-7, with sanctions before activation, spend below the cap, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality and approval. These are not rogue cases; they reflect temporary overlays that M0 failed to model.

**3. M0’s concurrency semantics already allow some apparent order differences.**  
In M0, sanctions and quality are concurrent. Therefore, sanctions before quality is not itself a deviation. The real issue in SUP-708 is activation before sanctions, not the order between quality and sanctions. The prosecution should not treat every interleaving as a violation.

**4. Scope is genuinely ambiguous.**  
- **Rejected leads:** CS-9 says rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen process but does not say whether those leads are M0 cases. SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen and was rejected without quality review. If rejected leads are intake cases, M0 should not require full quality review.  
- **Reactivations:** SUP-710 reactivated a supplier closed six months earlier, with prior quality certification still valid. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. It is unfair to treat the absence of `Register_Supplier` and `Quality_Review` as clear noncompliance when the standard is silent.  
- **Withdrawals and closures:** M0 does not distinguish withdrawn, rejected, and completed onboarding cases.

**5. The log is inadequate after CRM migration.**  
From 2026-07-01, notifications moved to CRM. Approximately 70% were mirrored. SUP-702 has a stable `notification_id`, which is better evidence than the missing onboarding-log event. SUP-706 has a CRM message but a mutable text link. The fair inference is not necessarily that notification failed to occur, but that the logging architecture is defective. The organization should not use its own data-collection failure to manufacture noncompliance where stable evidence exists.

**6. Some apparent deviations are data or vocabulary artifacts.**  
- **SUP-711**: duplicate `Register_Supplier` caused by migration, with same source transaction ID. The underlying process appears compliant.  
- **SUP-713**: `QA_Clearance` is the renamed `Quality_Review`. Mapping was approved and audit evidence shows equivalent content. This is a label-ontology defect, not a substantive control failure.  
- **SUP-706**: missing notification in the log may be a CRM linkage defect.  
- **SUP-710**: missing register event may reflect reactivation scope, not process omission.

**7. Trace deviation is not the same as business misconduct.**  
SUP-707’s RMI cycles did not cause activation before evidence. SUP-709’s rejected lead did not activate a sanctioned supplier. SUP-710’s supplier had valid prior quality certification. SUP-715 has no explicit prohibition on the spousal relationship, although disclosure was required. These cases require governance refinement, not automatic condemnation.

**8. The defense must acknowledge real limits.**  
Informal practice cannot permanently replace CS-9. SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-705 present genuine risk or authority problems. Ineligible high-risk auto-approval, activation before sanctions, same-human approval, expired pilot usage, and undisclosed conflicts cannot be cured by saying M0 is obsolete. The defense should accept that exceptions require clear authority, eligibility evidence, and compensating controls.

**Defense conclusion:**  
M0 is not a valid standalone enforcement model. It is obsolete, incomplete, and applied to incomplete data. Many deviations are model/scope/data artifacts. But the defense concedes that high-risk unauthorized conduct and expired-authority conduct require corrective action.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should be layered. POWL should express stable control-flow structure. Declarative constraints and data checks should express time, amount, eligibility, identity, and authority conditions. Organizational controls should govern exceptions, conflicts, and evidence.

**Layer 1: Case-type and scope layer**

This layer determines which normative path applies.

- **Full onboarding case**: new supplier requiring activation.
- **Pre-screen rejected lead**: supplier rejected before acceptance into full onboarding.
- **Reactivation case**: previously closed supplier being reactivated.
- **Withdrawal/cancellation case**: case closed before activation.

Explicit scope rules:

1. **Rejected leads**  
   - If a lead is rejected during pre-screen, the case is a pre-screen case.  
   - `Sanctions_Check` is required.  
   - `Quality_Review` is not required unless the lead was accepted into full onboarding.  
   - `Reject_Supplier`, `Notify_Requester`, and `Close_Case` are required.  
   - This resolves SUP-709 as a scope issue.

2. **Reactivations**  
   - If prior quality certification is valid and risk classification has not materially increased, reactivation may omit full `Quality_Review` but must include:  
     - fresh `Sanctions_Check`;  
     - `Manual_Procurement_Approval` or a valid auto-approval overlay;  
     - notification before closure.  
   - If certification is expired, risk is high, or material changes occurred, full onboarding applies.  
   - A distinct `Reactivate_Supplier` or `Register_Reactivation` event should be logged to avoid missing `Register_Supplier` ambiguity.

3. **Withdrawals**  
   - If the requester withdraws before activation, the case may close after `Notify_Requester`.  
   - No activation controls apply, but the closure path must be logged.

This layer belongs in **case master data and pandas/data checks**, not in POWL.

**Layer 2: Core POWL control-flow layer**

The POWL model should express:

- standard manual route;
- low-risk auto route;
- emergency provisional route;
- rejection routes;
- notification before closure;
- optional/repeatable RMI as far as POWL can approximate.

POWL should not encode:

- spend caps;
- risk scores;
- country eligibility;
- effective dates;
- actor identity;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- delegation authority.

Those are data and organizational controls.

**Layer 3: DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints**

Declarative constraints should handle dynamics that POWL cannot express well.

Examples:

- `Sanctions_Check` must precede `Activate_Supplier` in all routes.
- `Quality_Review` must precede `Activate_Supplier` in the standard route.
- Under pilot/emergency overlays, `Quality_Review` must occur within two business days after activation.
- Under FD-7, `Manual_Procurement_Approval` must occur retrospectively within two business days.
- `Auto_Approve` is absent unless an active low-risk overlay applies.
- `Request_More_Info` may repeat zero or more times.
- No activation before requested evidence is received, if an RMI request is open.
- `Notify_Requester` must occur before `Close_Case`.

This layer belongs in **DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints**.

**Layer 4: Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules**

Pandas/data checks should enforce:

- LOW risk;
- DE supplier;
- approved corporate group;
- pilot effective date;
- emergency effective date;
- spend cap;
- cumulative spend before retrospective approval;
- quality-review deadline;
- retrospective-approval deadline;
- notification timestamp;
- stable notification ID;
- duplicate-event detection;
- label mapping;
- missing eligibility attributes.

This layer belongs in **pandas/data checks** and **data-quality controls**.

**Layer 5: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules**

Resource rules should be enforced outside POWL:

- requester and manual approver must resolve to different canonical humans;
- aliases must resolve to canonical person IDs;
- delegation must be explicit, time-boxed, and approved;
- quality reviewer and manual approver relationships must be screened;
- conflicts of interest must be disclosed and reviewed;
- spousal or same-reporting-line relationships should trigger either recusal or documented mitigation.

This layer belongs in **identity master data, HR/COI data, pandas checks, and organizational controls**.

**Layer 6: Policy-overlay governance**

Pilots and emergencies should be governed by a formal overlay register.

Each overlay must contain:

- overlay ID;
- rule version ID;
- authority document;
- effective start and end timestamps;
- approval authority;
- risk assessment;
- eligibility conditions;
- compensating controls;
- monitoring requirements;
- expiry behavior;
- ratification or termination decision after expiry.

Rules:

- A pilot may temporarily deviate from a standard only if the standard owner or delegated exception authority approves it.
- A monitored exception does not permanently weaken the standard.
- Emergency extensions must be written and time-boxed.
- Post-expiry use is noncompliant unless retroactively ratified by the competent authority.
- Permanent low-risk auto-approval requires amendment of CS-9, not operational practice.

This layer belongs in **organizational controls and policy governance**, supported by data tables.

**Layer 7: Evidence and audit layer**

Event collection must be stable:

- stable `notification_id`;
- immutable event timestamps for occurred-at and recorded-at;
- source transaction IDs;
- migration batch IDs;
- canonical activity labels;
- actor aliases;
- eligibility snapshots;
- emergency/pilot authority IDs;
- delegation evidence;
- conflict disclosures.

This layer belongs in **data architecture and audit controls**.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
# Revised POWL model M1 and normalized policy/data scaffolding.
# This code does not read external files.

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Request_More_Info phase
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# POWL limitation: unbounded repetition is awkward in a strict partial order.
# Best-effort approach:
#   - use Operator.LOOP if the PM4Py POWL runtime supports it;
#   - otherwise fall back to an optional single Request_More_Info occurrence.
# The unbounded business rule is carried by DECLARE constraint DEC-009.

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")

try:
    rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[request_more_info, SilentTransition()]
    )
    rmi_phase = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), rmi_loop]
    )
except Exception:
    # Safe approximation: zero or one Request_More_Info occurrence.
    # Repeated occurrences are allowed by the declarative layer, not by POWL.
    rmi_phase = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), request_more_info]
    )


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Standard manual-approval route
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Sanctions and quality are concurrent before manual approval.

s_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
q_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_std, q_std])

manual_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
notify_std = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_std = Transition(label="Close_Case")

standard_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_std, manual_std, activate_std, notify_std, close_std]
)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(checks_std, manual_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(manual_std, activate_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(activate_std, notify_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(notify_std, close_std)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Low-risk auto-approval route
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# This route is only lawful when an active policy overlay permits it.
# POWL does not encode risk, country, corporate group, or dates.
# Those conditions are in POLICY_OVERLAYS and data checks.

s_lr = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
md_lr = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_lr = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate_lr = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
q_lr = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
notify_lr = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_lr = Transition(label="Close_Case")

low_risk_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[s_lr, md_lr, auto_lr, activate_lr, q_lr, notify_lr, close_lr]
)

# Sanctions and master-data validation are concurrent before Auto_Approve.
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(s_lr, auto_lr)
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(md_lr, auto_lr)

# Auto_Approve precedes activation.
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(auto_lr, activate_lr)

# Quality review and notification may occur after activation in either order.
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(activate_lr, q_lr)
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(activate_lr, notify_lr)

# Closure requires both quality review and notification.
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(q_lr, close_lr)
low_risk_branch.order.add_edge(notify_lr, close_lr)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Emergency / provisional route
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# This route is only lawful under an active emergency overlay.
# Sanctions must still precede activation.
# Quality review and retrospective manual approval occur after activation.

s_em = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
activate_em = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
q_em = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_em = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
notify_em = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_em = Transition(label="Close_Case")

emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[s_em, activate_em, q_em, manual_em, notify_em, close_em]
)

emergency_branch.order.add_edge(s_em, activate_em)

# Post-activation obligations may interleave.
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate_em, q_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate_em, manual_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate_em, notify_em)

# Closure requires quality, retrospective approval, and notification.
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(q_em, close_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(manual_em, close_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(notify_em, close_em)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Rejection routes
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Two rejection routes are modeled:
#   A. Abbreviated pre-screen rejection for rejected leads.
#   B. Full-check rejection for accepted onboarding cases later rejected.

# 5A. Abbreviated pre-screen rejection
s_pre = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject_pre = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify_pre = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_pre = Transition(label="Close_Case")

reject_pre_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[s_pre, reject_pre, notify_pre, close_pre]
)
reject_pre_branch.order.add_edge(s_pre, reject_pre)
reject_pre_branch.order.add_edge(reject_pre, notify_pre)
reject_pre_branch.order.add_edge(notify_pre, close_pre)

# 5B. Full-check rejection
s_rf = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
q_rf = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks_rf = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_rf, q_rf])

reject_rf = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify_rf = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_rf = Transition(label="Close_Case")

reject_full_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_rf, reject_rf, notify_rf, close_rf]
)
reject_full_branch.order.add_edge(checks_rf, reject_rf)
reject_full_branch.order.add_edge(reject_rf, notify_rf)
reject_full_branch.order.add_edge(notify_rf, close_rf)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Route choice and top-level model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# The XOR represents mutually exclusive route selection.
# Route selection must be justified by eligibility snapshots and overlays.

routes = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_branch,
        low_risk_branch,
        emergency_branch,
        reject_pre_branch,
        reject_full_branch,
    ],
)

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, rmi_phase, routes])
M1.order.add_edge(register, rmi_phase)
M1.order.add_edge(rmi_phase, routes)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Normalized policy-overlay table
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

# Those conditions are represented here and should be enforced by data checks.

POLICY_OVERLAYS = [
    {
        "overlay_id": "OV-CS9-2025-01",

        "authority_document": "CS-9",
        "type": "BASE_STANDARD",
        "effective_from": "2025-01-01T00:00:00Z",
        "effective_to": None,
        "recorded_at": "2024-12-15T00:00:00Z",
        "approved_by": "Compliance Standards Board",
        "approval_status": "APPROVED",
        "routes": ["standard", "reject_full"],
```
