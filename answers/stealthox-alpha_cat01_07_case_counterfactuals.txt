## 1. Feasibility model

**Partial-order relation set**

```text
Base order (all cases):
  Register -> Triage
  Triage -> Identity_Check
  Triage -> Document_Check                        [originals NOT required]
  Triage -> Originals_Receipt -> Document_Check   [originals required]
  Identity_Check AND Document_Check -> Assessment
  Decision -> Notify

Conditional additions:
  [high-risk OR high-value]:
      latest relevant Assessment -> Compliance_Review(final) -> Decision
  [Request_More_Info issued by Assessment or Compliance_Review]:
      Request_More_Info -> Document_Check(new) -> Assessment(new)
         -> Compliance_Review(new or reaffirmed) [if high-risk/high-value] -> Decision
  [optional preliminary track, K-104]:
      Triage -> Escalation_Advice(S1) -> Preliminary_Compliance_Screen
      Preliminary_Compliance_Screen || {Identity_Check, Document_Check}   (overlap permitted)
      Preliminary_Compliance_Screen -> Compliance_Review(final)           (prep only, never a substitute)
  [K-411 workaround]:
      Triage -> {Identity_Check || Document_Check}                        (permitted: no originals)
  [K-337 override]:
      Assessment(2) -> Compliance_Review(final) -> Decision               (RMI_2 deleted;
      residual discrepancy must be explicitly dispositioned in Decision)
```

**Conditional and declarative constraints**

```text
D1  Precedence: no activity starts before all predecessors complete; queue forecasts are
    expectations with roughly +/-30% error, not commitments.
D2  Mutual exclusion: a resource performs at most one interval at a time (S1 advisory exempt;
    it may overlap verification checks).
D3  Four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
D4  Certification: final Compliance Review only by C1; preliminary screens never substitute.
D5  Reservation semantics: a provisional C1 slot holds capacity only; it does not authorize an
    early final review and does not transfer automatically.
D6  Loop closure: every Request_More_Info obliges a new Document Check and new Assessment
    before any Decision; high-risk/high-value cases need a new or reaffirmed review.
D7  Concurrency permission: parallel Identity/Document Checks only when no originals required.
D8  Logging integrity (K-411): concurrency claims require real-time timestamps; batch backfill
    voids the parallelism claim and triggers validation rework.
D9  Decision-policy override (K-337 only): Decision on documented incomplete evidence allowed,
    but the residual discrepancy must be explicitly approved-with-conditions or rejected.
D10 Calendar gating: external responses arrive mainly on business days; M1 signs predominantly
    in business-day windows, so completion between windows waits for the next window.
```

**Classification of interventions.** K-219 alters **routing only** (resource reassignment D1D2; timing effects emerge indirectly). K-104 alters **routing and timing** (adds parallel advisory/preliminary-screen paths plus a capacity reservation) but leaves the decision policy intact. K-411 alters **timing and control-flow enablement** (concurrency plus logging discipline); policy unchanged. K-337 alone alters the **allowed decision policy** — it deletes loop iterations and legalizes deciding on known-incomplete evidence; it is not merely a time saving.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The critical path was queue-dominated, not work-dominated: D1's Monday queue (13h forecast), then A1's Tuesday queue, then an ~18h C1 wait. The sanctions ambiguity was discovered late (Assessment, Tue evening), so C1 read the file from scratch (4.5h) and S1's advice arrived only after Assessment.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage (Mon 08:50), emit the escalation packet to S1 and book a provisional C1 slot; add a short C1 preliminary screen. Registration, Identity, Document Check, and Assessment timings are deliberately left unchanged — this is a minimal edit.

**Temporal propagation:** S1's Monday-morning memo (09:30–11:30, overlapping checks legally) reframes the sanctions question, so A1's Assessment is unchanged or slightly shorter. The decisive change is downstream: C1's final review enters a reserved slot at Tue 19:30 instead of exiting an ~18h queue at Wed 13:10, and its effort drops from 4.5h to ~2.5–3h because list screening and pre-reading occurred Tuesday. Decision pulls from Wed 18:00 to Tue night (or Wed 08:00 under a strict M1 calendar). The saving is deleted queue wait, not compressed work.

**Process-feasibility check:** All precedence holds: final CR follows the final Assessment; four-eyes intact (A1 vs C1); the preliminary screen never substitutes for the final review (rule 10); the reservation is a legal capacity booking that consumes scarce C1 hours someone else needed.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best 38h (packet complete, M1 signs Tuesday night). Base 44–49h (M1 signs Wednesday morning). Worst 90–140h: an incomplete packet causes C1 to issue RMI at the reserved slot, forcing a new Document Check, new Assessment, and reaffirmed review — worse than factual.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Timestamps showing C1 re-read everything despite the packet; reserved slots repeatedly wasted by Assessment slippage; displaced urgent compliance cases breaching their own targets; packet-triggered RMI rates above ~30%.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Routing to D1 for handwriting expertise produced a ~16h wait between originals receipt (Mon 17:30) and start (Tue 09:30), then a clean 4h check. Completion was then gated by the A2 queue and by M1's Wednesday-morning signing window — the factual case was ready Tuesday 22:30 yet decided Wednesday 08:30.

**Minimal intervention:** Reassign the Document Check to D2 effective at originals receipt; nothing else changes.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's shorter Monday-evening queue (~5h vs ~16h) advances the check's start by ~10–11h, but D2's slower service on handwriting (+2–5h) claws much of it back: completion Tue ~05:30–08:30 vs factual 13:30. Assessment then joins the A2 queue ~7h earlier (start ~Tue 12:00–14:30 vs 20:00). Whether cycle time improves hinges on a calendar threshold: if Assessment finishes inside M1's Tuesday working window, decision occurs Tue ~15:30 (cycle ~31h); if it finishes Tuesday evening or later, the gain is absorbed and decision stays Wed 08:30. Earlier readiness converts to earlier completion only when it crosses a working-window boundary.

**Process-feasibility check:** Precedence fine (Originals Receipt precedes the check; Identity proceeded earlier, as permitted). No compliance constraints bind (not high-risk/high-value). The quality risk is the clarification branch: a D2 query acts like a rule-7 loop, requiring a new Document Check and new Assessment.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best 30–34h (fast D2, Tuesday-window decision), ~35% of runs. Base 46–50h, ~35%. Worst 95–170h if clarification triggers an 8–36h external round trip, ~30%. The probability-weighted mean exceeds the factual 48h: the intervention trades a realized good outcome for a high-variance lottery.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** M1 demonstrably signs Tuesday afternoons when ready; D2's clarification rate on handwriting below ~10%; or conversely, D2's Monday queue longer than forecast, which would make the swap strictly harmful.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The tail from Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) to Notify (Wed 09:10, week 2) took ~119h, dominated by a weekend-spanning external response (Fri 10:30  Mon 09:00), a D1 queue re-entry, and one more Assessment — not by work content. Total cycle: 214h.

**Minimal intervention:** At Assessment 2 completion, prohibit RMI2; require the Decision to disposition the known discrepancy explicitly (conditions or rejection). DocCheck3, RMI2, and Assess3 are deleted from the path; everything upstream is untouched.

**Temporal propagation:** Compliance can enter Friday midday (C1's queue was clearing; ~0–2h wait) instead of Tuesday week 2. The review runs 3–6h — somewhat longer than routine because defensibility of deciding with a known discrepancy must be documented. Decision follows Fri 15:30 if M1 is available, else Mon 08:30. The 48–113h saving comes almost entirely from removing external-latency exposure, a weekend boundary, and one full D1/A1 queue-reentry cycle.

**Process-feasibility check:** Precedence and four-eyes hold (final Assessment = Assess2 by A1; review by C1). But this is a decision-policy change: deciding on unconfirmed supplier totals is admissible only under the intervention's override, and the "no silent ignoring" clause forces an explicit conditional approval or rejection. Critically, C1 retains rule-6 authority to issue RMI independently, so the suppressed loop can resurrect after sunk preparation cost.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best 101h total (Friday conditional approval sticks). Base 130–166h (Monday decision). Worst  factual ± a few hours: C1 reviews Friday, issues its own RMI, and the loop returns with the weekend penalty intact plus lost preparation effort.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Appeals or credit losses traced to the unconfirmed schedule; auditor classification of such conditional approvals as control breaches; C1 overriding via RMI in a majority of treated cases; evidence the residual discrepancy was material, not cosmetic.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The legacy lock serialized verification: the document task was created only after Identity completed (Tue 13:20), adding ~1.7h of pure serialization plus task-creation lag before D2 started at 15:00. Assessment then faced a grown queue, becoming the binding constraint.

**Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks at Triage (09:35), enable concurrent ownership via a controlled workaround, and require real-time timestamps. No service times or resources change.

**Temporal propagation:** D2 can start at ~13:45 once its own queue clears — only ~1.25h earlier than factual, because D2's queue, not the lock, set the factual start. The check finishes Tue ~18:15 vs 19:30. Assessment joins the A2 queue slightly earlier, but with the queue grown, its start improves only from ~09:30 to ~09:00 Wednesday: an hour saved upstream buys minutes downstream. Real gains require evening assessment capacity (best case: Assess Tue 19:00–21:30, decision Wed 08:30, cycle 24h).

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 3 explicitly permits concurrency when no originals are required, so the workaround aligns with normative flow; the lock was a legacy artifact. Four-eyes and compliance steps are not applicable (low-risk). The genuine risk is evidentiary: if timestamps are batch-entered after the fact, the "parallelism" is unfalsifiable fiction, and an audit finding can force validation rework.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best 24h (evening assessment). Base 28–29h (save 0.5–1.5h). Worst 40–54h if false concurrency is detected and the document check is redone, pushing Assessment and Decision a full day.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Event-log gaps or backfilled timestamps; no measurable change in Assessment start times across piloted cases (queue absorption); any rise in document-check error rates under simultaneous workload.

**100-case weekly rollout.** Holding all other traffic fixed, each intervention looks locally attractive; at 100 similar cases per week the accounting changes qualitatively. Compliance capacity: C1 is a single certified reviewer; at 3–6h per final review, weekly capacity is roughly 35–50 high-risk reviews before any preliminary screens. One hundred reservations cannot create capacity — they only allocate it. Honored reservations ration by click order rather than urgency, systematically delaying genuinely urgent non-pilot cases; soft reservations degenerate into ordinary queue positions and the benefit evaporates. Preliminary screens add 1–1.5h each, so "prepared" cases can consume *more* total C1 time than unprepared ones — a Jevons effect in which the bottleneck migrates firmly to compliance and aggregate high-risk throughput falls even as each treated case finishes sooner. Document substitution: shifting standard or handwritten work from D1 to D2 relieves the complex-corporate specialist but lengthens D2's queue for everyone, and each D2 clarification injects a full rule-7 loop — new document check, new assessment, renewed compliance attention — so avoided D1 hours can be repaid with interest. Rework: suppression policies remove D1/A1/C1 hours deterministically but export cost to appeals, losses, and audit findings that sit outside the event log; preparation policies occasionally add loops when packets are incomplete. Assessment: advancing verification completion synchronizes arrivals into A1/A2; with one assessor in training early week, assessment — not verification — becomes binding, exactly as K-411 previews locally. Weekends and externals: loops straddling a weekend are the most expensive; suppressing them captures disproportionate savings, while any added loop inherits the 8–36h external hazard. Timestamps: at scale, unlogged workarounds manufacture phantom concurrency; batch-entered timestamps make parallelism unfalsifiable, inflate measured gains, and invite rework. Real-time capture is not overhead — it is the control that makes the policy evaluable.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-104. It preserves the decision policy, spends abundant advisory capacity (S1) to economize scarce certified capacity (C1), and its benefit — deleting queue wait rather than compressing work — survives wide timing noise. Its failure modes, incomplete packets and wasted reservations, are observable early and correctable.

The most dangerous is K-337. It buys two to four days by deleting an independent confirmation step, converting process latency into credit, legal, and audit risk. The decision content itself changes — conditional approval or rejection replaces a clean approval — and notification is irreversible. Worse, C1's retained authority to request more information means the loop can return after preparation costs are sunk, capturing the downside of both worlds.

The least transportable local result is K-104's slot reservation. For one case, reserving C1 is nearly free allocation of slack; for one hundred, it is pure rationing of a fixed resource, so aggregate completion time cannot improve — only allocation and fairness change. K-219 similarly presumes D2 slack and a benign clarification rate that vanish under volume, and K-411's gains dissolve entirely against a congested assessment queue.

The highest-value additional data are: (i) resource calendars and true availability windows, especially M1's signing patterns and C1's interruptions, since several counterfactuals hinge on whether work lands inside a working window; (ii) empirical service-time distributions by case type and resource, replacing the coarse ranges used here; (iii) external-response hazard curves including weekend effects, which dominate K-337's economics; (iv) rework-trigger rates by document type and checker, which decide K-219; (v) timestamp entry-lag distributions separating recorded from actual concurrency; and (vi) arrival and priority processes feeding the compliance and assessment queues, without which displacement from reservations cannot be priced.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural** — The twelve normative rules fully capture admissible control flow; no unstated mandatory activities exist in this window.
2. **Temporal** — Internal processing continues outside business hours (overnight document checks are observed), but external suppliers respond mainly on business days, creating weekend stalls.
3. **Temporal** — Published queue figures are forecasts with roughly ±30% error, refreshed every 8–16h; they are expectations, not commitments.
4. **Resource** — C1 is the sole certified final compliance reviewer; C1 time is non-substitutable and ultimately bounds all high-risk/high-value completions.
5. **Resource** — Service-time ranges hold irrespective of local queue composition; no congestion-induced slowdowns (fatigue, context switching) beyond the stated bands.
6. **Behavioral** — M1 signs decisions predominantly within business-day windows (observed decisions at 08:30–18:30, none overnight), so downstream readiness between windows waits for the next window.
7. **Quality** — Preliminary escalation packets reduce final compliance effort by 1–3h only when the underlying evidence set is stable; incomplete packets add rework rather than saving time.
8. **Interference** — Single-case analyses hold other cases' arrivals, priorities, and queues fixed; at 100-case scale, reservations and substitutions propagate, invalidating this ceteris-paribus assumption.
