# 1. Equivalence Notions

Duplicate visible labels are distinguished **by occurrence position in a trace**, not by a persistent activity identity. Thus `Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Analyze Revision` contains two occurrences of the same label; they are ordered occurrences, but there is no claim that they concern the same revision object beyond the segment structure stated in P1.

Let `O = Open Case`, `A = Analyze Revision`, `C = Check Revision`, `Ap = Approve Revision`, `R = Rework Case`, and `Cl = Close Case`. Silent steps are written `tau`.

## Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`

For a model `M`, let `L_vis(M)` be the set of complete finite visible traces obtained from complete executions by deleting every `tau`. Models are visibly trace equivalent iff

\[
L_{vis}(A)=L_{vis}(B).
\]

This retains label order, interleavings, repetitions, and counts of every visible label. It does not retain silent-branch identity: a missing `Approve Revision` may result from choosing `tau`, and the visible trace alone does not identify that choice except through the absence of `Ap`.

## Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3

Let

\[
L^{P3}(M)=\{w\in L_{vis}(M): |w|\le 12,\ \#A(w)\le 3\}.
\]

Models are P3-bounded equivalent iff `L^{P3}(A)=L^{P3}(B)`. This is an equivalence of a finite bounded language only. Equality there would not prove unbounded equality; inequality there, when witnessed by complete traces satisfying the bounds, does disprove unbounded visible equivalence as well.

## Business-relevant equivalence under P1

P1 observes all visible labels, hides only `tau`, and additionally evaluates the compliance segmentation induced by `O`, successive `R` separators, and `Cl`. A complete trace is P1-compliant iff every analysis segment—starting after `O` or after an `R`, and ending before the next `R` or before `Cl`—contains its own `C` before that segment boundary. Equivalently, if a trace has `k` occurrences of `A`, compliance requires at least `k` occurrences of `C`, with at least one `C` in each `A`-segment separated by `R`/`O`/`Cl`.

P1-equivalence requires: first, the same P1-visible traces after hiding `tau`; second, the same compliance status trace-by-trajectory, in particular no trace admitted by one model violates the per-analysis check rule while the corresponding behaviour is admitted by the other. Retaining `Ap` positions matters because P1 says approval number and placement remain visible and potentially relevant.

## Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2

Define the dashboard abstraction `alpha` on a complete visible trace `w` by

\[
alpha(w)=(\text{first}=O,\ \text{last}=Cl,\ \#A(w),\ \mathbf{1}[\#C(w)\ge1],\ \mathbf{1}[\#R(w)\ge1]).
\]

`alpha` deletes `Ap` and `tau`, collapses any positive number of `C` occurrences to “check occurred”, and forgets the exact interleaving of `A` and `C`. P2-equivalence means equality of the image sets `alpha(L_vis(A))` and `alpha(L_vis(B))`, not equality of the underlying traces.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | The parallel `+(Analyze Revision, Check Revision)` is inside the revision loop body. | Only `Analyze Revision` with optional approval is inside the loop; `Check Revision` is outside and parallel to the whole loop. | A repeats the check with every revision attempt; B performs the check once for the whole case revision phase. |
| Minimum and maximum `Check Revision` occurrences | Minimum 1, maximum unbounded: exactly one per loop iteration. | Minimum 1, maximum 1: exactly one total, because the single `C` branch must complete before the parallel join. | A has `#C = #A` for complete traces. B has `#C = 1` regardless of `#A`. |
| Possible placement of `Check Revision` | In each iteration, `C` is concurrent only with that iteration’s `A`; after hiding interleaving inside the iteration, `C` occurs before that iteration’s optional `Ap`, before the next `R`, and before `Cl`. | `C` can be interleaved anywhere in the parallel composition after `O` and before the join preceding `Cl`: before the first `A`, between `A` and `Ap`, between iterations, after the last approval, etc., provided the loop has finished before `Cl`. | B admits placements impossible in A, e.g. a sole check after an approval or after later analyses. A admits repeated checks impossible in B. |
| Optional approval occurrences | In every iteration, `X(Approve Revision, tau)` is executed after the `A || C` pair; approval is optional independently per iteration. | In every iteration, `X(Approve Revision, tau)` follows that iteration’s `A`; approval is optional independently per iteration, but is not ordered relative to the single global `C`. | Both allow zero or more visible approvals, at most one per analysis iteration. Their placement relative to `C` differs: in A an approval in an iteration is after that iteration’s check; in B an approval may precede the only check. |
| Concurrency | Concurrency is local: each `A` is parallel with its paired `C`. No concurrency between different revision attempts because `R` and loop sequencing separate them. | Concurrency is global: the one `C` is parallel with the entire loop, hence with all analyses, approvals, and reworks until the join. | B’s check is not tied to a particular revision attempt; A’s check is tied by sequencing to the current attempt, though inside the pair `A` and `C` are unordered. |
| Join before `Close Case` | After exiting the loop, sequence proceeds directly to `Cl`; the final iteration’s `C` and optional `Ap` have already completed. | The parallel `+` must join: both the single `C` branch and the loop branch must finish before `Cl`. | B cannot omit `C`, but may postpone it until just before `Cl`. A cannot postpone an iteration’s check beyond that iteration’s approval/rework boundary. |

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | not equivalent | High | A guarantees one check per analysis segment; B admits multiple analyses with only one check and admits a first segment ending at `Rework Case` with no preceding check. | If the business rule were weakened to “at least one check somewhere before close”, B would satisfy that weaker rule; that is not P1 as stated. |
| P2 | equivalent only under stated abstraction | High | Under `alpha`, both models generate exactly: `O` first, `Cl` last, `#A = k  1`, check-occurred = true, rework-occurred iff `k > 1`, for every integer `k  1`. | This is not trace equivalence. Counts of `C`, `Ap` behaviour, and `A/C` interleaving are deliberately erased. |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

All traces below are complete: they start with `Open Case`, end with `Close Case`, contain no `tau` after projection, and satisfy the P3 bounds. “Admitted” means membership in the visible language after hiding `tau`.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Close Case` | admitted | not admitted | The second `Check Revision`: A requires one check in the second iteration; B has only one check total. | P1, P3; two analysis attempts |
| A2 | `Open Case  Check Revision  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Check Revision  Analyze Revision  Close Case` | admitted | not admitted | Again the second `Check Revision` is decisive; within each A-iteration `Check` may precede `Analyze`, but B cannot produce two checks. | P1, P3; two analysis attempts |
| B1 | `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Approve Revision  Check Revision  Close Case` | not admitted | admitted | In B the sole check is after the approval; in A, when `Approve Revision` is visible in an iteration, that iteration’s check must already have occurred before the approval. | P1 placement sensitivity, P3 |
| B2 | `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Close Case` | not admitted | admitted | The first segment ends at `Rework Case` with no check for the first analysis; B’s only check is deferred until after the second analysis. A rejects because iteration 1 lacks its paired check before `Rework Case`. | P1, P3; two analysis attempts |
| B3 | `Open Case  Check Revision  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Close Case` | not admitted | admitted | B uses its only check before the first analysis and then performs a second unchecked analysis; A requires a fresh check in iteration 2. | P1, P3; two analysis attempts |

No inclusion direction holds. A1/A2 refute `L_vis(A)  L_vis(B)` because A can repeat `C` once per iteration whereas B cannot repeat `C` at all. B1/B2/B3 refute `L_vis(B)  L_vis(A)` because B can place its sole check after an approval, after a rework-separated later analysis, or before all analyses while leaving later analyses unchecked.

Optional approval and hidden `tau` affect the verdict as follows. In both models, each iteration independently chooses `Approve Revision` or `tau`; after projection, an iteration with no visible approval is consistent with the silent branch having been chosen. That does **not** make approval placement irrelevant: when `Ap` is visible, its order relative to `C` is observable. Hence B1 is decisive even though traces with approval omitted can coincide. Conversely, absence of `Ap` in A1, A2, B2, and B3 is explained by choosing `tau` in the relevant iterations; the decisive difference there is check count/placement, not hidden approval. Hiding `tau` therefore removes branch labels but does not erase the visible consequences of sequencing around the choice.

Under P2 these counterexamples collapse: for example A1, A2, B2, and B3 all have `#A = 2`, check-occurred = true, rework-occurred = true, so `alpha` maps them to the same dashboard record. That is exactly why the P2 verdict is only abstraction-relative.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following is exhaustive bounded breadth-first exploration over Petri-net markings, not random playout. It explores silent transitions without increasing visible length, prevents repeated `(marking, visible trace)` states, enforces the P3 visible-length and analysis-count bounds, and records a trace only when the current marking equals the final marking.

```python
from collections import deque

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYZE = 3
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"

TREE_A = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

TREE_B = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""


def marking_key(marking):
    return frozenset((place, count) for place, count in marking.items() if count > 0)


def fire(marking, transition):
    """Return the marking after firing transition, assuming it is enabled."""
    new_marking = Marking(marking)
    for arc in transition.in_arcs:
        weight = getattr(arc, "weight", 1)
        new_marking[arc.source] -= weight
        if new_marking[arc.source] <= 0:
            del new_marking[arc.source]
    for arc in transition.out_arcs:
        weight = getattr(arc, "weight", 1)
        new_marking[arc.target] = new_marking.get(arc.target, 0) + weight
    return new_marking


def bounded_visible_traces(tree_string, name):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

    sound_result = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
    # pm4py versions differ: check_soundness may return bool or (bool, diagnostics).
    if isinstance(sound_result, tuple):
        is_sound, diagnostics = sound_result[0], sound_result[1:]
    else:
        is_sound, diagnostics = bool(sound_result), ()
    print(f"{name}: sound={is_sound}, diagnostics={diagnostics}")

    start = (im, ())
    queue = deque([start])
    seen_states = {(marking_key(im), ())}
    complete_traces = set()

    while queue:
        marking, trace = queue.popleft()

        # Complete traces are recorded only at exact final-marking equality.
        if marking == fm:
            complete_traces.add(trace)
            # Do not extend beyond acceptance for the complete-trace language.
            continue

        for transition in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            label = transition.label  # None for silent Petri-net transitions.
            if label is None:
                new_trace = trace
            else:
                if len(trace) >= MAX_VISIBLE:
                    continue
                if label == ANALYZE and trace.count(ANALYZE) >= MAX_ANALYZE:
                    continue
                new_trace = trace + (label,)

            new_marking = fire(marking, transition)
            state = (marking_key(new_marking), new_trace)
            if state in seen_states:
                continue
            seen_states.add(state)
            queue.append((new_marking, new_trace))

    return complete_traces


traces_a = bounded_visible_traces(TREE_A, "Model A")
traces_b = bounded_visible_traces(TREE_B, "Model B")

only_a = sorted(traces_a - traces_b)
only_b = sorted(traces_b - traces_a)

print(f"|A bounded traces| = {len(traces_a)}")
print(f"|B bounded traces| = {len(traces_b)}")
print("Traces unique to Model A:")
for trace in only_a:
    print("  ", " -> ".join(trace))
print("Traces unique to Model B:")
for trace in only_b:
    print("  ", " -> ".join(trace))
print("Bounded equivalent under P3:", traces_a == traces_b)
```

This BFS is exhaustive **relative to the stated bounds and to the converted accepting Petri nets**: every firing sequence whose visible projection stays within the bounds is either explored or pruned only when it repeats an already seen `(marking, visible trace)` state, exceeds the visible-length bound, exceeds the analysis bound, or passes acceptance. It is not a proof about traces longer than 12 visible events or with more than three analyses.

If `pm4py.play_out(...)` were used instead, the result would be a sampled set of executions. Unless playout is explicitly configured as exhaustive enumeration—which ordinary stochastic/ heuristic playout is not—absence of a differing trace in samples would not establish bounded equality, and presence of samples would still require checking completeness, bounds, and final-marking acceptance.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

Footprints and stochastic-language distances can add evidence, but neither is a substitute for trace-language comparison.

`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` summarises local relations such as which activities can follow, precede, be parallel to, or be unrelated to others. Here it could corroborate that in A, `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` are locally parallel inside an iteration, whereas in B, `Check Revision` is parallel to a larger loop region. However, footprints typically abstract away exact repetition counts, global segment obligations, and the distinction between “one check per iteration” and “one check somewhere concurrently”. Equal footprints would therefore not imply equal visible languages: two models can share pairwise relations while differing in how many times a label must occur. Different footprints can refute some strong behavioural equivalences, but footprint equality is only necessary evidence for certain relations, not sufficient evidence for trace equivalence.

A stochastic comparison could be coded as follows, but only after declaring extra probabilistic assumptions that are **not** present in either process tree: probabilities for choosing `Approve Revision` versus `tau`, for exiting versus repeating each loop, and for resolving concurrency if a simulator requires weights.

```python
import pm4py

# Assumes tree_a/tree_b or net_a/net_b etc. have already been constructed.
# The process trees themselves specify no probabilities. Any numeric language
# below depends on simulator defaults or on an explicitly declared stochastic map.

fp_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
print("Footprints equal:", fp_a == fp_b)

# Version-dependent signature: inspect pm4py.get_stochastic_language in your
# installed pm4py version before use. Do not interpret the result unless the
# branching/loop probabilities used by the simulator are declared.
lang_a = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
lang_b = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
emd = pm4py.compute_emd(lang_a, lang_b)
print("Earth Mover's Distance under declared simulator assumptions:", emd)
```

A low Earth Mover’s Distance would still not establish trace equivalence. EMD compares probability mass over trace variants under an assumed stochastic semantics. It can be small even when the supports differ, if the differing traces have low assumed probability; it can also be dominated by frequent traces while rare compliance violations remain. Conversely, a large EMD might reflect only different assumed loop-exit probabilities rather than different possible behaviour. Since the given trees declare no probabilities, stochastic evidence must be reported as conditional on an external probability assignment, never as a property invented from the tree syntax.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

The exact process-tree counts below are derived by enumerating the given strings. Depth is measured in edges from the root operator to the deepest leaf; root depth is therefore 0 edges. A duplicate-label leaf count means extra leaf occurrences beyond the first leaf with the same visible label; both originals have each visible activity as exactly one leaf, even though loop execution can repeat labels at runtime.

| Measure | Model A | Model B | Simplified model in Section 8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Process-tree node count | 12 | 12 | 7 |
| Operator nodes | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Leaf nodes | 7 | 7 | 5 |
| Visible leaves | 6 | 6 | 5 |
| Silent leaves | 1 (`tau`) | 1 (`tau`) | 0 |
| Distinct visible labels | 6 | 6 | 5 |
| Duplicate-label extra leaf occurrences | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Depth in edges | 4 | 5 | 2 |

For Model A the nodes are: root sequence; `Open Case`; loop; `Close Case`; inner sequence; parallel; exclusive choice; `Analyze Revision`; `Check Revision`; `Approve Revision`; `tau`; `Rework Case`. For Model B the nodes are: root sequence; `Open Case`; parallel; `Close Case`; `Check Revision`; loop; inner sequence; `Analyze Revision`; exclusive choice; `Approve Revision`; `tau`; `Rework Case`. Both have five operators and seven leaves. B is deeper because the path root  parallel  loop  sequence  choice  approval has five edges, whereas A’s deepest corresponding path has four.

The converted-Petri-net simplicity value is not asserted numerically here; it should be computed in the installed environment rather than invented. Use structural counting code such as:

```python
import pm4py


def tree_stats(tree):
    nodes = []

    def walk(node, depth_edges):
        nodes.append((node, depth_edges))
        for child in node.children:
            walk(child, depth_edges + 1)

    walk(tree, 0)
    leaves = [node for node, _ in nodes if not node.children]
    visible_leaves = [
        node for node in leaves
        if getattr(node, "label", None) not in (None, "tau")
    ]
    silent_leaves = [
        node for node in leaves
        if getattr(node, "label", None) in (None, "tau")
    ]
    labels = [getattr(node, "label", None) for node in visible_leaves]

    return {
        "node_count": len(nodes),
        "operator_count": len(nodes) - len(leaves),
        "leaf_count": len(leaves),
        "visible_leaves": len(visible_leaves),
        "silent_leaves": len(silent_leaves),
        "distinct_visible_labels": len(set(labels)),
        "duplicate_label_extra_leaves": len(labels) - len(set(labels)),
        "depth_edges": max(depth for _, depth in nodes),
    }


for name, tree in [("A", tree_a), ("B", tree_b), ("simplified", simplified_model)]:
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    print(name, tree_stats(tree))
    print(name, "petri-net simplicity:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm))
```

Node count alone does not capture semantic complexity: A and B have the same node count, but B’s wider concurrency scope creates a larger set of possible check placements, while A’s repeated in-loop check creates unbounded check counts tied to iterations.

# 8. Simplified Model

I choose **P2** as the target semantics for simplification. P1 is the safer operational semantics, but precisely because P1 retains per-revision obligations, approvals, and interleavings, a materially simpler model cannot preserve P1 exactly while also being simpler in the relevant sense. The simplification below is therefore a dashboard surrogate, not an operational replacement.

## Compact expression

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'),
  'Check Revision',
  'Close Case'
)
```

Under the loop semantics, `Analyze Revision` occurs at least once; each further analysis is preceded by `Rework Case`; exactly one `Check Revision` occurs after the analysis loop and before `Close Case`.

## PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'), "
    "'Check Revision', 'Close Case')"
)
```

## Preservation relative to the originals

| Property | Status for simplified model | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `Open Case` first and `Close Case` last | exact | The root sequence enforces this in all models. |
| At least one `Analyze Revision` | exact | The loop body executes at least once. |
| At least one `Check Revision` | exact at P2 Boolean level | The simplified model has exactly one check; P2 only records that a check occurred. |
| Number of `Analyze Revision` occurrences | exact | For every `k  1`, the simplified model admits an execution with `k` analyses separated by `k-1` reworks, matching the dashboard count of both originals. |
| Whether any `Rework Case` occurred | exact at P2 Boolean level | Rework occurs iff the loop repeats, iff `#A > 1`, as in both originals under `alpha`. |
| P2 dashboard equivalence | exact | `alpha` of the simplified language is `{ (O first, Cl last, k, true, k>1) : k  1 }`, the same as for A and B. |
| Per-analysis check before next rework/close, P1 | lost | The single check is after all analyses; an intermediate analysis can be followed by `Rework Case` before any check. |
| Check count | abstracted in P2, lost in P1/P3 | Simplified has exactly one check. A has one per analysis; B has exactly one but concurrently placed. |
| `Approve Revision` visibility and placement | lost | The simplified model has no approval activity. P2 hides approvals anyway; P1 and P3 do not. |
| Exact `Analyze`/`Check` interleaving | abstracted in P2, lost in P3 | P2 forgets it by design. The simplified model fixes check after the loop, unlike either original’s full trace language. |
| Concurrency | lost | No parallel operator remains. This is acceptable only because P2 erases interleaving. |
| Silent behaviour | changed | No `tau` leaf remains because approval, the source of the silent choice, is removed. |

The simplified model is structurally simpler than both originals: 7 nodes versus 12, 2 operators versus 5, 5 leaves versus 7, 0 silent leaves versus 1, and depth 2 edges versus 4 for A and 5 for B. It is not simpler than A or B in P1/P3 semantics; it is simpler only after the P2 abstraction has discarded the features that make A and B complex.

## Regression traces for the simplified model

Positive traces admitted by the simplified model and having the required P2 images:

1. `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Close Case`  
   Dashboard image: `#A = 1`, check occurred, no rework.

2. `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Close Case`  
   Dashboard image: `#A = 2`, check occurred, rework occurred.

3. `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Rework Case  Analyze Revision  Check Revision  Close Case`  
   Dashboard image: `#A = 3`, check occurred, rework occurred.

Negative traces not admitted by the simplified model:

1. `Open Case  Check Revision  Close Case` — rejected because at least one `Analyze Revision` is required before the check/close part.

2. `Open Case  Analyze Revision  Close Case` — rejected because the required `Check Revision` before `Close Case` is missing.

# 9. Final Recommendation

For **P1 operational compliance**, use **Model A**. It is the only original whose loop scope enforces a check for every analysis attempt before the next rework or close. Model B should not be used as the operational compliance model: its single concurrent check can be consumed by an early placement while later revisions proceed unchecked, and its check can occur after approvals in a way A forbids.

For **P2 executive dashboarding**, either original is adequate after abstraction, but the simplified model is preferable as a dashboard surrogate because it preserves exactly the dashboard tuple while removing approval, concurrency, and repeated-check detail that the dashboard discards. It should be labelled as a P2 abstraction and must not be presented as the operational process.

For **P3 bounded testing**, neither original should be declared equivalent to the other. The bounded witnesses in Section 4 already separate them within 12 visible events and at most three analyses. If the test oracle encodes compliance intent, Model A is the suitable reference; Model B will generate non-compliant bounded traces such as B2 and B3.

The simplified model should replace **neither** original as an operational or bounded-testing model. It may replace direct dashboard use of A or B only where consumers contractually receive the P2 abstraction and nothing finer.

Warning: bounded comparison is asymmetric evidence. Finding a bounded counterexample is decisive for non-equivalence, provided the trace is complete and satisfies the bounds. Finding no bounded counterexample proves only equality of the bounded slices; longer traces, more analyses, different silent-branch resolutions, or behaviour outside the converted-net assumptions could still differ.
