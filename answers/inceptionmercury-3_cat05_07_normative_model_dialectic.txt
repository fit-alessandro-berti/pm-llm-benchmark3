## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Supported facts.** The evidence establishes the following without serious dispute:

- M0 is a PM4Py POWL model with a strict partial order: `Register_Supplier`  concurrent `Sanctions_Check`/`Quality_Review`  XOR (`Manual_Procurement_Approval`  `Activate_Supplier` OR `Reject_Supplier`)  `Notify_Requester`  `Close_Case`.
- M0 has no loop, no data-aware route selection, no resource constraints, no exception overlays, and no label synonym mapping.
- CS-9 is the controlled compliance standard from 2025-01-01 onward. It requires both checks before activation and manual approval before activation, and it requires requester and manual approver to be different humans.
- PD-3 was a steering-committee pilot decision effective 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, allowing low-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups to use `Auto_Approve`; it was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0.
- FD-7 was an emergency directive effective 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, allowing provisional activation before quality review and manual approval for critical relief suppliers, subject to sanctions before activation, a EUR 20,000 spend cap, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days.
- CRM migration from 2026-07-01 moved `Notify_Requester` to CRM; only about 70% of notifications are mirrored into the onboarding log.
- Aggregate evidence shows 312 pilot auto-approvals in June/July, of which 289 appear eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack eligibility attributes.
- Forty-seven cases contain `Request_More_Info`; none activated before requested evidence arrived.
- Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after CRM migration.
- Six post-pilot cases used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August.

**Disputed propositions.**

- Whether PD-3 was a valid derogation from CS-9 despite not being incorporated into CS-9 and despite the compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9.
- Whether FD-7’s expiration automatically extinguished the authority to continue the same emergency pattern after 2026-07-12.
- Whether rejected leads are in scope of M0 or belong to a separate pre-screen/intake process.
- Whether reactivation is a form of supplier onboarding governed by M0.
- Whether a CRM notification with a mutable text link satisfies the `Notify_Requester` requirement.
- Whether `QA_Clearance` is the same activity as `Quality_Review`.
- Whether alias resolution in SUP-712 is reliable enough to conclude that requester and approver are the same person.
- Whether the spouse/COI case in SUP-715 is a normative violation or an unresolved organizational-control gap.

**Unproved or insufficiently evidenced propositions.**

- The nine pilot cases with missing eligibility attributes cannot be classified as either eligible or ineligible.
- The exact authority of the steering committee to override CS-9 is not documented.
- Whether the flood emergency continued to exist factually after FD-7 expired is asserted by operations but not documented.
- Whether SUP-706’s CRM message really belongs to that case is not established by a stable identifier.
- The exact content of the CRM notification in SUP-706 is not supplied.
- Whether SUP-708 had a transaction in the 40-minute window before sanctions clearance is not directly evidenced, though no transaction is reported.
- The approved mapping for `QA_Clearance` was approved one week after SUP-713; whether that mapping is purely retrospective or reflects an established synonymy is a matter of interpretation.

**Burden-of-proof approach.** I will treat noncompliance as established only when the evidence, after resolving reasonable data and scope ambiguity, shows by a preponderance that a real process event violated an applicable, valid normative rule. A party invoking an exception must show formal authority, temporal coverage, satisfaction of all conditions, and reliable evidence. Informal practice, operational normalization, or a desirable outcome does not retroactively amend a controlled standard.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that CS-9 and M0 are the controlled baseline, and every departure from them is a control failure unless an authorized exception is proved. The exceptions are narrow, time-limited, and contested. They do not become permanent by repetition.

**Formal authority.** CS-9 is a controlled and formally approved compliance standard. PD-3 and FD-7 are exceptions, not amendments. They were not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. A steering-committee pilot can authorize a monitored experiment, but it does not rewrite a compliance standard. The compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 confirms that the authority of PD-3 is at least disputed. Therefore, the default comparator remains CS-9 and M0.

**Expiry is real.** FD-7 expired automatically on 2026-07-12. PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. SUP-705 used the emergency pattern on 2026-07-13 with no written extension. SUP-714 used `Auto_Approve` on 2026-08-02, after the pilot ended. Six additional post-pilot auto-approvals occurred in the first week of August. Factual emergency and belief that permanent approval was imminent are not normative instruments. If operations wanted to continue, it needed a written extension or a new policy. It had neither.

**High-risk cases are not model artifacts.** SUP-703 is the clearest violation. A high-risk new foreign supplier was not eligible for the pilot, yet the operator selected `Auto_Approve`. The first purchase was EUR 180,000. This is not a logging problem or a model gap; it is a deliberate or reckless bypass of eligibility control. SUP-708 is equally severe: a high-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before the sanctions check. No emergency or pilot applied. The fact that no transaction occurred before clearance does not cure the exposure; the supplier was already active and could have transacted.

**Deviations hidden by M0.** M0 contains no resource or conflict-of-interest rules. SUP-712 fits M0’s control flow, but requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through aliases, violating CS-9’s separation-of-duties rule. SUP-715 fits M0, but the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line and no conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded. M0’s conformance is therefore insufficient; CS-9 and broader organizational policy still apply.

**Normalization of exceptions.** The aggregate data show the pilot was already being normalized: 312 auto-approvals, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility data, and six cases after expiry. Internal Audit identified SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe. The fact that many other deviations are attributable to obsolete model features does not justify ignoring these cases.

**Desirable outcomes do not amend policy.** SUP-707’s repeated `Request_More_Info` may be sensible, and SUP-714’s low-risk auto-approval may be a good future policy. But desirability is a reason to amend the model through governance, not a reason to declare the existing deviation compliant. M0 cannot express the loop; that is a defect in M0, but it does not make the current normative standard retroactively different.

**Conclusion for prosecution.** The operation is materially noncompliant. The formal baseline should be enforced while any desired changes are made prospectively through the proper process.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is that M0 is an obsolete and incomplete representation of the process that actually exists and that many “deviation” findings are artifacts of a poor model and degraded data.

**Valid overlays are not deviations.** PD-3 and FD-7 created real, documented exceptions. SUP-702 is a low-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group, activated during the pilot window, with quality review the next business day and a stable CRM notification. SUP-704 is a critical relief supplier activated during FD-7, with sanctions before activation, spend under the cap, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality and approval the next business day. These are not violations; they are the expected behavior of the exceptions. M0 simply cannot represent them.

**M0 lacks the required loop.** `Request_More_Info` is not in M0, but operations uses it and compliance agrees it is desirable. Among 47 such cases, none activated before the requested evidence arrived. That is a control-strengthening behavior, not misconduct. M0’s inability to express a repeatable loop is a model defect, not an operational failure.

**M0 lacks the auto-approval branch.** The pilot allowed `Auto_Approve`. Of 312 pilot cases, 289 appear eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 have missing eligibility attributes. The dominant pattern is a functioning pilot, not systemic abuse. The correct response is to incorporate the auto branch into the revised model and enforce eligibility with data checks, not to treat every auto-approval as noncompliance.

**Emergency continuity.** FD-7 expired on a calendar date, but operations says the flood emergency factually continued. SUP-705 used the same sanctions-first, capped, monitored, and retrospectively approved pattern as SUP-704. A rule that expires while the emergency continues is incomplete. The operation’s behavior is better explained by a missing transition rule than by misconduct.

**CRM migration explains missing notifications.** After 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` moved to CRM and only 70% of notifications are mirrored. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours; SUP-702 has a stable CRM notification ID. The absence of a mirrored log event is a data-integration defect, not proof that notification did not occur.

**Scope ambiguities should not be penalized.** SUP-709 is a rejected lead that failed sanctions pre-screen. The process owner says rejected leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance. The scope document is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier; CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure, and the prior quality certification remained valid. It is not fair to call these cases noncompliant when the normative scope is undefined.

**Label equivalence matters.** SUP-713 used `QA_Clearance`. The procedure and UI renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance`, and audit documents show equivalent content. A conformance check that treats a synonym as a violation is formalism over substance.

**Trace deviation is not business misconduct.** SUP-711’s duplicate `Register_Supplier` has the same source transaction ID and is a migration artifact. SUP-712 depends on alias resolution that M0 cannot express. SUP-715 is a conflict-of-interest disclosure issue, not a control-flow issue, and no rule explicitly prohibits spouses. Many deviations are model-data problems rather than actual control failures.

**Conclusion for defense.** M0 is obsolete and incorrectly scoped. Applying it mechanically produces false noncompliance. The operation should be assessed against a revised model, a policy-overlay registry, and better event data.

**System-level judgment.** The operation is not compliant with the current formal baseline. ## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised system should be layered.

**Layer 1: Core control-flow POWL model.** This is the structural skeleton. It should express the standard route, the low-risk auto-approval route, the emergency provisional route, the repeatable `Request_More_Info` mechanism, rejection, and closure. It should not attempt to enforce data conditions such as risk, country, amount, or date; those belong in the policy overlay and data-check layers.

**Layer 2: Policy-overlay governance.** Every exception, pilot, emergency directive, and permanent amendment should be recorded as an overlay with:
- a policy version ID;
- the issuing authority;
- an effective window;
- precise eligibility conditions;
- the route it activates;
- the controls it changes;
- an explicit statement of whether it derogates from CS-9.

Overlays should never silently expire into permanent practice. Extensions must be written. If an overlay is rescinded or expires, operations must return to the baseline unless a new overlay is issued.

**Layer 3: Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time checks.** These are implemented as pandas/data checks, not POWL nodes. Examples:
- auto-approval eligibility: supplier risk = LOW, country = DE, corporate group = approved, snapshot timestamp present;
- emergency eligibility: directive ID = FD-7, case date inside window, critical relief flag;
- amount cap: no spend above EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval in emergency route;
- time lag: quality review must occur within two business days after provisional activation in auto and emergency routes;
- notification: a `Notify_Requester` event must exist either in the onboarding log or in CRM with a stable `notification_id` before `Close_Case`.

**Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest controls.** These are organizational controls and should be enforced outside POWL:
- canonical person IDs and alias resolution;
- requester  manual approver;
- delegation records;
- conflict-of-interest declarations;
- spouse and reporting-line checks where required by policy.

**Layer 5: Explicit scope rules.**

- Rejected leads: define whether the pre-screen rejection is part of M0. The recommended rule is that leads rejected before acceptance are intake cases, not M0 onboarding cases.
- Reactivation: define a separate reactivation process. If prior quality certification remains valid, quality review may be waived, but sanctions and manual approval should still be required unless a specific rule says otherwise.
- Withdrawal: define a terminal withdrawal event. M0 currently has no withdrawal path.

**Placement of constraints.**

- **POWL**: control-flow structure, including concurrency, XOR routing, loops, and the common notify-before-close tail.
- **DECLARE/log-skeleton style constraints**: useful as global safety nets, for example “`Sanctions_Check` must precede `Activate_Supplier` in all routes” and “`Notify_Requester` must precede `Close_Case`.”
- **Pandas/data checks**: eligibility, amounts, dates, directive windows, notification IDs, missing data, and route selection.
- **Organizational controls**: actor identity, separation of duties, delegation, COI disclosure, and governance authority.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The code below constructs a revised POWL model. It is a structural template. Route selection is data-driven: the policy overlay must choose which XOR child applies for a given case.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Common start and end
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# ---------------- Standard route ----------------
sanctions_standard = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_standard = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

# Request_More_Info: repeatable, optional loop
request_more_info_body = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
request_more_info_redo = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
request_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[request_more_info_body, request_more_info_redo]
)
request_optional = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[request_loop, SilentTransition()]
)

# Standard checks are concurrent; the information-request loop may occur while checks run
standard_checks_with_requests = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_standard, quality_standard, request_optional]
)

manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_standard = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
approved_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate_standard])
approved_branch.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate_standard)

reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approved_branch, reject])

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[standard_checks_with_requests, decision])
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_checks_with_requests, decision)

# ---------------- Low-risk auto-approval route ----------------
sanctions_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
master_data_validation = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_pre_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_auto, master_data_validation])

auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
quality_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_pre_checks, auto_approve, activate_auto, quality_auto]
)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_pre_checks, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate_auto, quality_auto)

# ---------------- Emergency provisional route ----------------
sanctions_emergency = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
activate_emergency = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
quality_emergency = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_emergency = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_review = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality_emergency, manual_emergency])

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions_emergency, activate_emergency, emergency_review]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_emergency, activate_emergency)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_emergency, emergency_review)

# ---------------- Route selection and common tail ----------------
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route]
)

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
M1.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Limitations of the POWL model.** POWL cannot evaluate supplier risk, country, corporate group, directive dates, spend amount, or actor identity. The model therefore makes the three routes structurally available but does not decide which route applies. The policy overlay and data-check layers must make that decision. The `Request_More_Info` construction is an approximation using an optional LOOP; the exact rule that no activation occurs until requested evidence arrives must be enforced by a data check.

**Normalized policy-overlay table.**

| Overlay ID | Authority | Effective window | Route | Conditions and effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BASE-CS9/M0 | CS-9 | permanent | standard | Default route. Sanctions and quality before activation; manual approval before activation; notify before close; requester  approver. |
| PD3-V1 | Steering-committee pilot | 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 | auto | LOW risk, DE supplier in approved corporate group. Auto_Approve may replace manual approval. Sanctions and master-data validation before activation. Quality may occur within two business days after activation. |
| FD7-V1 | Emergency directive | 2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12 | emergency | Critical relief supplier. Sanctions before activation. Provisional activation may precede quality and manual approval. Quality and manual approval must occur retrospectively. Spend capped at EUR 20,000 until approval. Daily monitoring required. |
| REQINFO-V1 | Proposed normative amendment | prospective | standard | Optional repeated Request_More_Info before decision. No activation until the requested evidence has been received. |
| LABELMAP-V1 | Approved synonym mapping | prospective | all | `QA_Clearance` maps to `Quality_Review` before conformance checking. |
| SCOPE-REJECTED-V1 | Governance clarification | prospective | intake/pre-screen | Leads rejected at pre-screen are not M0 onboarding cases. |
| SCOPE-REACTIVATION-V1 | Governance clarification | prospective | reactivation | Reactivation is a separate process. Registration may not be repeated. Quality may be reused only if prior certification is valid. |
| NOTIFY-CRM-V1 | Data-collection standard | from 2026-07-01 | all | Notifications may be recorded in CRM, but must be linked by an immutable `notification_id`. Mutable text links are not sufficient. |

**Event/data-collection proposal.**

- Every event should have an immutable record timestamp and an immutable effective timestamp.
- Every case should record the policy overlay and rule version that governed it.
- Eligibility snapshots should be taken at case opening: risk level, country, corporate group, approved-list status, and source references.
- `notification_id` should be generated by CRM and stored as a stable foreign key in the onboarding log.
- Actor aliases should be resolved through a canonical identity registry. Each event should store a canonical actor ID.
- Delegation evidence should be stored when an approver acts through a deputy.
- Emergency and pilot authority should be recorded as directive IDs, including the issuing role and the effective window.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations should be linked to the case and actor IDs.
- All event links should be append-only and immutable. Mutable text fields should not be accepted as evidence for conformance.
