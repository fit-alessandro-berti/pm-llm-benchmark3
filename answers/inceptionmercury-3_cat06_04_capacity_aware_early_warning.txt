## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility gate**  
A case is eligible for a swarm only if:

- Current stage is **Triage** or **Diagnosis Started**. Once the case reaches **Diagnosis Complete**, the swarm is treated as having ~0 effect.  
- The remaining time to SLA is **at least 90 minutes**, so the 45-minute swarm still has a plausible window to change the trajectory.  
- The alert is not suppressed by the repeated-alert policy in Section 4.  
- If the alert is driven by an imputed or missing field, the case is placed in **data-validation hold**. It becomes eligible again only after the field is restored or the risk is manually confirmed.

**Calibration robustification**  
The raw `p_now` is not used as an unbiased risk estimate. I define `p_cal`:

- Strategic Enterprise: `p_cal = max(0.05, p_now  0.085)` as a midpoint of the stated 0.05–0.12 overprediction.  
- SME: `p_cal = min(0.95, p_now + 0.065)` as a midpoint of the stated 0.04–0.09 underprediction.  
- Public Sector: `p_cal = p_now`.  
- Non-Native-Language Support: `p_cal = p_now`, but the credible interval is widened and the lower-bound effect is used for the go/no-go check.

The predicted probability `p_now` is observational. It is not the intervention effect. The effect estimates come only from the supplied risk-reduction ranges.

**Prioritization score**  
For each eligible case, compute:

```
Score = _mid × V  €450  (1  p_cal) × B  SlotCharge
```

where:

- `_mid` is the midpoint of the estimated absolute risk-reduction range.  
- `V` is the value of avoiding a breach.  
- `B` is the false-alarm burden.  
- `SlotCharge = €1,800` is an initial policy parameter representing the external spillover harm of using one slot. It is based on six untreated diagnosis-stage cases, a 0.01–0.03 breach-probability increase, and an assumed average avoided-breach value of about €20,000.

For high-uncertainty segments, I also require:

```
_low × V  €450  (1  p_cal) × B > 0
```

before a slot may be used. This prevents an uncertain effect range from being used at face value.

**Operational allocation**  
- Sort eligible cases by Score, highest first.  
- Select the highest positive-score cases until slots are exhausted.  
- Hold one slot in reserve until 13:30 for the possible regulatory incident.  
- If no regulatory case has arrived by 13:30, release the slot to the highest-scoring eligible case that is still before Diagnosis Complete.  
- Never use a slot just because `p_now` is high if the stage, effect, or net expected value does not support it.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down**  
After a swarm, the same case is normally ineligible for another swarm for 24 hours. A repeat swarm is allowed only if a materially new event occurs and a senior manager approves. In practice, this means most cases receive at most one swarm.

**Suppression**  
Alerts are suppressed when they stop carrying new information.

- A first or second alert is allowed to trigger review.  
- For a third or later alert, the case is suppressed unless:
  - a materially new event has occurred, or  
  - `|p_cal_now  p_cal_at_last_review|  0.08`.  
- Routine 30-minute refreshes do not count as new evidence.

**Escalation**  
If a suppressed case has very high value, `V  €50,000`, or carries regulatory/public-sector obligations, it is escalated to a human reviewer. The human may authorize one swarm if they see a risk not captured by the model.

**Re-entry**  
A case re-enters the queue if one of the following occurs:

- New customer diagnostic log or document uploaded.  
- Missing/imputed data field restored.  
- New triage or diagnosis workstream event.  
- Stage change.  
- `p_cal` moves by at least 0.08.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization may starve lower-value cases**  
The score is heavily value-weighted. Lower-value cases, especially SMEs and public-sector cases, will rarely win slots if high-value Strategic Enterprise cases are present. Safeguards:

- Reserve one “safety-net” slot per day for eligible cases in underserved segments whose lower-bound expected direct benefit is positive.  
- Use value bands rather than raw value in score review so one €100,000 case does not automatically consume all slots.  
- If no lower-value case clears the hurdle, do not force a slot; but the rule exists to prevent systematic starvation.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support**  
I-09 has sparse calibration and uncertain intervention effects. It would be unfair to exclude it solely because its uncertainty interval is wider. Safeguards:

- Use the lower-bound effect for the go/no-go check, not the midpoint alone.  
- Track I-09 outcomes separately and report them with wide credible intervals.  
- If the uncertainty remains high, consider a randomized or matched pilot for non-native cases to build evidence quickly.

**Differential calibration across segments**  
SME risk is underpredicted; Strategic Enterprise risk is overpredicted; Public Sector is broadly acceptable; Non-Native support is uncertain. Raw `p_now` is therefore not comparable across segments. Safeguards:

- Apply segment-specific `p_cal` adjustments before scoring.  
- Re-estimate the adjustments monthly or after workflow changes.  
- Never use a single raw-risk threshold across segments.

**Selective intervention changes future training labels**  
If intervened cases are treated as natural outcomes, the model will learn a mixture of baseline risk and intervention effects. Safeguards:

- Flag every intervened case in the training data.  
- Train or validate models on untreated cases whenever possible.  
- Use inverse-propensity weighting or include intervention status as a feature.  
- Periodically run a small randomized holdout/no-swarm wave to preserve clean outcome labels.

**Spillover harms may fall systematically on untreated groups**  
Untreated lower-value cases, non-native cases, and public-sector cases are the most likely to bear queue delays. Safeguards:

- Monitor delay and breach outcomes by untreated segment.  
- Set a maximum acceptable spillover burden, e.g., no more than 0.05 added breach probability per untreated case per day from swarm activity.  
- If one segment is consistently harmed, increase the SlotCharge or reduce the number of slots during peak congestion.  
- Pause interventions if average queue delay per slot exceeds 20 minutes.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**  
Record for every swarm:

- Case ID, stage, time to SLA, and alert history.  
- Swarm start/end time and specialists involved.  
- Queue delay created for other cases.  
- Whether the case was before Diagnosis Complete.  
- Customer disruption and false-alarm burden.

Review these weekly with operations leadership.

**Predictive calibration monitoring**  
- Compute empirical breach rates in probability bins for each segment.  
- For each segment, compare `p_cal` with observed breach frequency.  
- If the empirical rate differs from `p_cal` by more than 0.05 in a bin, update the segment adjustment.  
- Re-estimate the Strategic Enterprise and SME offsets monthly.

**Causal-effect monitoring**  
The intervention effect cannot be inferred from `p_prev` to `p_now` changes. Instead:

- Use the reserve/waitlist as a natural control group: compare eligible cases that received swarms with eligible cases that did not because capacity was unavailable.  
- Use a regression-discontinuity design around the priority score cutoff: cases just above and just below the cutoff can be compared.  
- Where possible, randomize among tied cases to generate cleaner effect estimates.  
- Estimate the average risk reduction per segment after at least 30–50 swarm observations. Update the  ranges accordingly.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring**  
- Track average delay per slot.  
- If average delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot, reduce the number of daily slots or restrict swarms to lower-congestion periods.  
- Track breach rates among non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases in periods with and without swarm activity.  
- Update SlotCharge from observed spillover harm rather than the initial €1,800 assumption.

**Fairness monitoring**  
- Track selection shares by segment and value band.  
- Calculate the ratio of swarm share to eligible-case share for each segment.  
- If any segment falls below 0.7 for two consecutive months, investigate.  
- Track false-alarm burden and spillover harm by segment.  
- Run a quarterly fairness audit.

**When to change the threshold or allocation rule**  
Change the decision rule when:

- Calibration adjustment errors exceed tolerance, e.g., empirical calibration slope moves more than 0.2 from 1.  
- Estimated causal effects are materially different from the supplied  ranges.  
- Observed spillover harm changes the SlotCharge by more than €500.  
- A fairness audit shows that a protected or underserved segment is systematically harmed or starved.  
- The adverse scenario begins to appear: queue delays rise, selected cases show little benefit, and spillover harm moves to the upper end. In that case, pause new interventions and re-run the entire evaluation.
