KYC-clearance and SoD checks are preconditions in the generation flow.
**Gate 2:** shadow-mode diff rate on terms below Legal tolerance; zero unresolved-control cases auto-issued; legal-ops exception queue within capacity; rule-library owner and change control operating.

**Phase 3 — Scale, redeploy, decide Year 2 (months 10–12).**
Scale A4/A5/A6; redeploy released analyst hours into second-pair review and High-exposure judgment; use back-office afternoon slack for document chasing and mismatch evidence prep. Benefits audit on end-to-end P90 by variant, not activity labor. Year-2 backlog decision informed by measured residual waits: **A7/A8** if valuation lateness and packaging rework still cause committee-cadence misses; **A10** once the KYC gate is proven; **A2** if misrouting persists; **A1** only after constraint capacity has landed; **A11** last.

**Standing monitoring set:** per-activity P50/P90 wait (per branch); end-to-end P90 by segment × product; first-pass yield and rework rate (doc review, mismatch, normalize, packaging, contract); committee-cadence catch rate; KYC-before-sign violations (target 0); SoD violations (target 0); contract remediation % overall and renewal; analyst touch-hours per case; specialist escalation queue age and senior-concentration index; override rate and override-correctness; model accuracy/drift; customer complaints and abandonment; bot-failure queue age.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend P4 — the hybrid bottleneck portfolio: A5  A4  A6 (Wave 1)  A9 (Wave 2), preceded by Phase-0 control fixes.**

The highest-frequency activity should **not** be automated first. `Receive  Register` is 11,880 events, but registration sits at position 2 in 98% of cases with a 1.5h wait, 72% of intake is already digital, and the responsible pool has 12 FTE falling below 65% utilization after 15:00. That is a **visible queue, not a binding constraint**. Automating A1 first converts slack into faster arrivals at the two pools that actually govern throughput: 3 compliance specialists (two of whom carry the handover network, facing 1,750 mismatches at 55 min with 38% rework and a 31h wait, on the critical path in 82% of affected cases) and 9 credit analysts who carry roughly 45% of their recorded touch-time in document review and normalization before judgment even starts. The predictable result of A1-first is a shorter registration queue, a longer 28h credit-analysis queue, more month-end rush, and *more* four-eyes violations — local efficiency purchased with systemic risk.

P4 instead spends the four slots on the causal chain: **A5** stops the 3,3002,800 document loop that both compounds 8h customer waits and degrades the PDFs A6 must read; **A4** attacks the low-frequency, high-criticality blocker that gates KYC *and* signing; **A6** expands the only pool that can absorb the resulting clean inflow; **A9** then generates contracts from the authoritative decision record with control-rule checks, satisfying Compliance's demand for a genuine control module and relieving legal ops' month-end peak and the 14% renewal remediation loop. Downstream automation is never enabled ahead of its prerequisites: A9 waits for A4-supported KYC clearance and a hard clearance gate, and A10/A11 are explicitly deferred rather than shipped into unreliable controls.

The trade-off is honest. P4 does not buy modules for the two longest single waits — 58h external valuation and 44h customer signing — nor for the four-way packaging join. Those are addressed in Year 1 by **process rules that cost no module slot**: request valuation at classification for Equipment finance (71% required) so it overlaps credit analysis instead of following it, tier valuer SLAs, enforce a committee pre-read cutoff so a 15%-rework pack does not cost a full week of cadence, and standardize RM instructions. If the month-6 and month-10 gates show that valuation lateness or packaging rework still dominates P90, A7/A8 displace Year-2 candidates ahead of A1/A10 — a pre-committed swap rule rather than a post-hoc justification. Similarly, if the Phase-0 audit shows classification error above Compliance tolerance, A2 takes the reserved second H1 deep-integration slot and A5 slips, because an unreliable exposure label undermines every control rule downstream.

What management gets politically is a real, demonstrable Wave-1 win — fewer document chases, fewer stalled KYC cases, measurably released analyst hours — without pretending that the busiest activity is the most valuable one. What it gets structurally is capacity placed *before* flow, controls placed *before* downstream bots, and freed human hours reinvested in the second-pair review and specialist depth that the control findings demand. Compliance responsibility is unchanged: automation proposes, logs and routes; named humans clear KYC, own credit judgment, and approve contract terms.
