# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Mechanism common to all clusters: naive KPIs assume `service = complete  start` is touch time, `waiting = start  arrival` is pure queueing, and resource intervals are disjoint. Each cluster violates a different subset of these assumptions.

- **G1 (fixture batch).** Waiting (2–6 min to the 09:00 release) is real *batch-formation* wait — mislabeled if treated as ordinary queueing. Service per item appears as 3 min but effective touch is 45 s (4 items / 3 min)  **capacity understated ~4×** if read serially. Utilization summed per event inflates 4× (naive 400% during the cycle). Bottleneck detection: the robot falsely appears saturated.
- **G2 (start-group).** Waiting (18/14/11/6 min) mixes true wait with group-release policy — early arrivals look penalized. Reported services (8/14/21/27 min) grow artificially; implied touch from completion deltas is 6–7 min each  **later items' service overstated up to ~4×**. Naive utilization = 70 min of "work" in a 27min window = 259% — a built-in impossibility check. Bottleneck: inspection looks congested when it is really a reporting convention.
- **G3 (end-batch).** Waits (22/18/19/19 min) look plausible *if* starts are genuine. Services (28/20/13/6 min) have a fabricated descending shape; start deltas imply 7–8 min each  first item overstated ~3.5×, last understated. Throughput shows a fake burst of 4 completions at 11:30. Utilization: union = 28 min  100%, but true touch is lower. Bottleneck: end-of-hour spikes are artifacts.
- **G4 (serial chain).** Essentially unbiased: waits 7–19 min and 4min services are genuine; utilization 100% during 12:05–12:25 is real saturation. This is the reference cluster against which the others deform.
- **G5 (shared account).** Waits (15–16 min) may be fine; services are wall-clock occupancy, not touch time. Utilization is **undefined**: naive sum = 70 min over a 30min envelope = 233%, i.e., between 1 and 3 FTE. Bottleneck detection is meaningless until headcount per interval is known.
- **G6 (bulk submission) — the requested dual-bias case.** Because **one submission event supplies both lifecycle stamps**, the same act produces two opposite distortions: (i) *inflated waiting*: arrival17:00 spans of 35–175 min are attributed to queueing although much of that time the ticket may have been worked, parked by policy, or simply unrecorded; (ii) *near-zero service*: every duration collapses to exactly 0, so flow time  waiting and the process looks like "pure queue, no work." Downstream: utilization  0% all afternoon punctuated by a phantom burst of 7 completions at 17:00; bottleneck detection inverts (station looks idle-yet-backlogged); SLA/conformance breaches are manufactured by the logging practice, not the operation. Any queueing model fitted to G6 will trade service capacity for arrival-rate error in unidentifiable proportions.
- **G7 (coarsening).** Every metric carries ±15 min (or wider, given the undocumented rounding rule) error bars; the negative waits (1 to 11 min) demonstrate signs cannot be trusted. Naive utilization = 75 min / 15 min = 500%. Bottlenecks below 15min scale are invisible.
- **G8 (post-downtime drain).** Waits of 38–58 min are *real*, but their cause is the 15:00–15:35 unavailability; if downtime is not segmented, triage is misread as a slow bottleneck. Services (7 min) and utilization (100% during 15:38–16:13) look genuine. Residual risk: the safety system's clock offset shifts the downtime boundary by an unknown amount.

Conformance note: zero-duration events (G6) and synchronized completions (G3) can pass token-based replay while hiding rework and parallelism, yielding over-fit models; batch-aware filtering should precede discovery.

def overlap_rate(sub):
    """Pairwise fraction of intersecting [start, complete] intervals.
    Endpoint-touching positive intervals do NOT count; coincident zero-length
    points DO count (so G6 is not silently scored as non-overlapping)."""
    s = sub["start_ts"].values.astype("datetime64[s]").astype(np.int64)
    e = sub["complete_ts"].values.astype("datetime64[s]").astype(np.int64)
    num = den = 0
    for i in range(len(s)):
        for j in range(i + 1, len(s)):
            den += 1
            lo, hi = max(s[i], s[j]), min(e[i], e[j])
            if lo < hi or (lo == hi and (s[i] == e[i] or s[j] == e[j])):
                num += 1
    return round(num / den, 2) if den else np.nan

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource IDs trusted (each ID = one worker/machine).** G1 becomes a genuinely parallel machine (divide load by 4 slots); G2/G3/G4/G8 are single-server stories and the Section 1 diagnoses stand; G5 yields a logical contradiction (one identity at 233% load), which itself is evidence for either multitasking measurement failure or account sharing; G6 becomes one actor performing seven instant closures — i.e., a logging artifact.

**Resource IDs treated as possibly shared.** Every cluster weakens: G4's perfect chain could be two packers alternating seamlessly (identical temporal footprint, halved per-worker utilization); G2's staggered completes could be parallel inspectors (undermining the start-group story); G8's chain could be a relay; G6's 17:00 mass could be several closers (still bulk). Per-resource utilization becomes a *lower bound on workers × per-worker load* and queueing models assuming one FCFS server break. G5 is already interpreted under this regime.

**Timestamps exact.** Ties are then strong evidence: G1's batch is confirmed at 1s resolution; G3's common end and G6's point-mass are deliberate acts; G4/G8 chains are exact. Conclusions as in Section 1.

**Timestamps interval-censored by stated precision** (each stamp t  an interval of width p, rule unknown). G1 (±1 s) unchanged. G2/G3/G5/G6 (±60 s): G3's "simultaneous" end becomes "within a minute" — bulk submission remains most likely given testimony, but exact simultaneity is no longer provable; G6 durations become 60 s, still absurd against a 140min arrival spread. G4/G8 (±1 s): sequencing robust, tiny gaps/overlaps possible. **G7 (±15 min): all five intervals overlap regardless of ground truth; batch vs. serial is unidentifiable, and any PM4Py label on G7 measures the censoring grid, not the process.**

**Same pattern, multiple physical explanations (five instances):**
1. *Common start + common end* (G1 vs. G7): fixture batch vs. grid collapse vs. group-commit logging. Discriminators: precision metadata, lag profile, fixture context.
2. *Perfect start = previous-complete chain* (G4 vs. G8): pure sequential batching vs. post-downtime drain vs. multi-worker relay on one terminal. Discriminators: external downtime logs, arrival clustering, capacity data.
3. *Staggered completes from a common start* (G2): begin-group button + serial finishing vs. parallel finishes vs. retroactive common start stamp. Discriminators: UI design, per-item sub-step logs.
4. *Interleaved overlapping spans* (G5): team behind a shared login vs. one multitasker vs. session-relogin artifacts. Discriminator: badge/session IDs only.
5. *Point-mass completions* (G6 vs. G3): end-of-shift bulk entry vs. policy-synchronized closure vs. mid-session bulk submit. Discriminators: arrival spread, lag uniformity, source type, testimony.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritized (P0 = blocks correct analysis today):

1. **P0 — Execution timestamps at the point of action.** Capture per-item start/complete from device signals (scanner reads, button edges, PLC I/O) as non-null mandatory fields; G1's PLC is the model, G6's bulk form the anti-model. For group UIs (G2), emit per-item events, not only group events.
2. **P0 — Unique actor identity.** Badge/SSO per action; eliminate shared logins or bind each session to a badge; store `session_id` on every event. Directly resolves G5 and hardens G2/G4/G8 against relay ambiguity.
3. **P0 — Clock governance.** NTP-sync PLCs, tablets, MES, scanners, and the safety system; log per-source offset/drift daily; one timezone/DST policy. Removes G8's cross-clock ambiguity and G7's rounding opacity.
4. **P1 — Native batch/group identifiers.** Fixture/load ID for G1-style cells; `group_session_id` whenever a "begin group" button is pressed; `submission_id` linking bulk forms with per-item completion captured before submit (converts G3/G6 artifacts into structured batching facts).
5. **P1 — Workstation capacity and state telemetry.** Slot counts and states (idle/busy/down/changeover, with reason codes) so downtime (G8's safety stop) and saturation (G4) are first-class, enabling downtime-segmented queueing.
6. **P1 — Pause/resume and interruption events** for knowledge work (G5), capturing switch-away/switch-back to separate touch time from elapsed time.
7. **P2 — Raw-resolution storage with precision metadata as data.** Store unrounded timestamps; apply rounding only in reporting; publish per-event `precision`/`accuracy` fields (makes G7-type failures detectable automatically).
8. **P2 — Pipeline-latency monitoring.** Alert on `recorded_at  event_time` thresholds and on bulk inserts (many rows, same instant) — mechanical detection of G3/G6-style submissions.
9. **P2 — Queue-side events.** Enqueue/dequeue/pick-up markers and WIP snapshots to separate batch-formation wait from processing wait (G1–G4).

# 7. Final adjudication

**Interpretable with high confidence (usable now, with stated caveats):**
- **G4** — clean serial drain; waiting (7–19 min) and service (4 min) usable as-is; the reference cluster.
- **G1** — physical 4-item fixture batch, corroborated by 1s precision, 2–3 s lag, and context; use batch-aware aggregation (per-cycle, not per-item, service; capacity = items per cycle ÷ cycle time).
- **G3** — bulk submission, corroborated by testimony and the exact end-tie; use starts for waiting; censor completes; do not report the fabricated descending service profile.
- **G2** — start-group artifact; treat 10:00 as group start; estimate per-item touch from completion deltas; validate the button semantics.

**Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis:**
- **G6** — exclude from service-time and waiting statistics entirely; retain only as a data-quality incident. If it must be kept, right-censor completions at 17:00, flag rows, and never interpret the 0min durations or 35–175min waits as operational quantities.
- **G7** — interval-censor every stamp to its 15min box; exclude from minute-level queueing and bottleneck detection; usable only for coarse counts. Its negative waits prove the stamps cannot support sign-sensitive metrics.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion:**
- **G5** — mandatory badge/session audit; until then report only bounds (headcount 1–3; utilization anywhere between union/span and sum/span ÷ headcount). No bottleneck or staffing conclusion is defensible from these rows alone.
- **G8** — reconcile the repair-queue and safety-system clocks and confirm the 15:00–15:35 stoppage; afterwards, waiting decomposes into downtime (30–35 min) plus genuine queue drain, and triage is exonerated as a bottleneck.
- **G1 (light)** — confirm fixture capacity = 4 and that PLC stamps are edge-triggered per cycle, not group-committed.
- **G2 (light)** — confirm "begin group" semantics and whether parallel inspection is physically possible.
